Another useful differentiation of any concept is the characteristics that constitute the concept, while the extension is the number of referents belonging to the sign, *extension and intension*. The intension refers to the number of referents... Kabakate and Pusch 2011, 124; Pawlowski 1980, 53–54; Escandell Vidal 2012, 23–25). The intension of the sign *novel* should be the characteristics of the concept "novel", while the extension is the number of all instances belonging to the novel in the world. A possible inductive analysis of a genre is the study of the intension of e.g. adventure novels (their common characteristics), which is achieved by collecting the whole extension of adventure novels and analyzing their characteristics.

An important differentiation in philosophical definitions (but rarely used in linguistics or lexicography) is between nominal and real definitions. A nominal definition is a "terminological determination", it is "[...] what we accept on the basis of an explicit terminological convention or linguistic usage [...]" (Pawlowski 1980, 29, my translation). In contrast, a real definition "depicts a generalization" (Pawlowski 1980, 30, my translation). The humanities, and literary studies in particular, have usually not been constrained by empirical methods, partly due to the lack of digitized data, and partly because researchers tend to use selected excerpts from a smaller sample range for hermeneutic processes. From this perspective, I assume that the definitions of the genres discussed in section 2.1 by previous scholars are nominal definitions, because none of them assumed an empirical perspective, used a shared set of characteristics, formulated hypotheses or tested hypotheses (although some researchers used rigorous methods on a larger range of texts, e.g. Rivalan Guégo 2008, Ferreras 1988, or Lara López 2000, all cited in section 2.1). One of the aims of this work is to propose a method for a real definition of the sub-genres analyzed, which will be given in section 8.7.

### 2.3.2 Genre models

In this section, I will introduce four models describing the interaction between instances: the Aristotelian-structuralist model, the mixed model and the prototype.

### 2.3.1 Classic Aristotelian-structuralist model

The Aristotelian-structuralist model consists of genus proximum and specifica differentia (Atkins and Rundell 2008, 41).
