## 13.5 Current Challenges and Future Trends

Today, environmental monitoring is a hot topic. The use of low-cost sensors and open-source microcontrollers (such as `Arduino` and `Raspberry`) has enhanced the possibilities for the scientific community and general users to monitor pollutants.

In cities, pollution sources often migrate outside the city. For example, electric vehicles. Some European cities restrict fossil fuel vehicles entering the city. However, the battery charging of these vehicles depends on the power grid, which in most countries still uses fossil fuels for power generation. Overall, green energy or cold fusion technology is expected to replace fossil fuels in the future, eliminating emissions in the energy production process.

As mentioned earlier, developing countries have air quality monitoring networks to monitor the presence of pollutants in the air. Monitoring external air quality is crucial for taking protective measures (such as wearing masks, issuing health alerts, or implementing environmental policies). However, internal atmospheres may have harmful concentrations of pollutants that are not monitored. In industrial environments, monitoring of the internal atmosphere is part of occupational risk prevention plans; but in homes, monitoring of the internal atmosphere is lacking.

Regarding water bodies, a distinction can be made between monitoring drinking water and monitoring water bodies. Drinking water monitoring in developed countries is very comprehensive, capable of quickly detecting chemical or biological contaminants. However, in emerging countries, drinking water contamination is a challenge for achieving sustainable development goals. Using sensors to monitor biological contaminants remains a challenge. Current technology relies on culturing bacteria and DNA replication, requiring expensive laboratory equipment. It is expected that the cost of these technologies will decrease, while the economic strength of emerging countries increases, making the widespread application of these technologies possible.

Soil pollution monitoring is difficult to improve. Soil is a heterogeneous environment, and pollutants may be absorbed in different parts, making monitoring complex. Future sensor monitoring technology is expected to improve. Although the use of satellite images has limitations in soil monitoring, it helps monitor vegetation.

Finally, governments at all levels are committed to governance. Today, the use of Information and Communication Technologies (ICT) allows city or national governments to get closer to citizens, promoting citizen participation in decision-making. But to truly improve its effectiveness, more people need to understand its value and improve the implementation process. Most elderly people do not use ICT, which means their opinions need to be fully considered in the urban governance process.
