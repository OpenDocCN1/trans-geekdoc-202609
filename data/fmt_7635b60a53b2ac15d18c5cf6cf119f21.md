
After discussing how blockchain can improve existing government systems and processes, let us shift perspective and examine the technology's second impact on public administration or governance: the possibility of changing the basic premise of government.

Blockchain can not only improve government processes and systems but also has the potential to pioneer entirely new forms of government. It may end representative government as we know it, replacing it with a model where citizens have more direct participation rights, particularly a say in day-to-day operations. After all, the basic premise of a legislature is that not all citizens can gather to deliberate on issues affecting their lives. In the past, this was indeed impossible because the logistical conditions to support such assemblies were lacking. Therefore, we elect representatives from among ourselves to represent us in government decisions.

However, times have changed, and technology continues to turn what seemed impossible a few years ago into reality. If you had the chance to travel back to the early 20th century and tell people then that video conferencing and similar functions would be realized in less than 100 years, they would likely have thought you were describing magic, not reality.

Blockchain offers a new option to electing legislative and executive bodies and hoping they do the right thing. It provides a mechanism for citizens, as a collective, to assume responsibility for most day-to-day government functions and directly perform at least a portion of them. This was unimaginable ten years ago. At first glance, this may seem impractical or absurd, but after delving into blockchain's capabilities and its already tested applications in governance structures, the concept of direct government is not far-fetched.

### The DAO Model

In April 2016, German blockchain startup Slock.it announced a new project called the "Decentralized Autonomous Organization" (DAO). This was an investment organization with no leadership structure or team of employees. Instead, the DAO was managed through a system of smart contracts, or protocols, on the Ethereum blockchain. All decisions were made by a consensus mechanism achieved through direct voting by members^(9). Simply put, a DAO is a company with shareholders but no board of directors, CEO, or any other employees.

This idea was highly attractive, exciting, and popular, raising over $150 million during a 28-day funding period, with investors thereby becoming shareholders. Through the smart contract protocol or system, shareholders would vote on projects undertaken by the organization and hire service providers to act on their behalf. Shareholders could propose plans and required budgets, then persuade others to approve them through voting. Service providers could also pitch business ideas to the DAO and attempt to convince shareholders they could deliver results beneficial to the organization. Once members approved a project, the smart contract would lock the required funds in an escrow account. The service provider would then commence work; upon completion, the smart contract guiding execution would release payment from the escrow account.

However, the DAO was never fully implemented. In June 2016, just two months after launch, a hacker discovered a vulnerability in the code of its main smart contract and attempted to exploit it to steal approximately $50 million from the DAO wallet. This triggered one of the most significant hard forks in the crypto space. Since payments had to be completed on the Ethereum blockchain, the community faced the choice of rolling back transactions to deny the hacker access to the DAO funds. The community was deeply divided: some supported rolling back transactions to stop the attacker from obtaining funds, while others argued this would jeopardize the blockchain's promise of immutability, and that $50 million was not a sufficient reason.

Unable to reach consensus, the Ethereum network was forced to split into two independent blockchain networks. Those supporting the transaction rollback retained the name Ethereum (ETH), while opponents adopted the new name Ethereum Classic (ETC). The hacker eventually cashed out a portion of the funds in Ethereum Classic tokens. Shortly after, the DAO shut down and returned funds to investors.

Nevertheless, the DAO concept did not die; several subsequent projects have adopted this governance model. One of the most successful projects is the DASH blockchain network, where members select contributions they can make to the network and persuade the community to fund them. If the community is satisfied, it approves the budget into an escrow account, the proposer begins work, and funds are released as milestones are reached. Another project adopting the DAO model is MakerDAO.

Similarly, a form of DAO could replace government, making all citizens shareholders. Voting rights could be obtained by paying taxes into a treasury wallet. Both contractors and citizens could propose necessary public projects, which would be approved by citizen vote, and contractors selected. The winning contractor's budget would be placed in an escrow account, and payment would be made according to the smart contract guiding project execution upon completion of work. Notably, review, discussion, and voting can all be conducted via a portal or mobile app, allowing citizens to participate anytime, anywhere.

Of course, we cannot say we are ready to convert the entire government into a DAO. But transforming partial functions is possible, and this could improve service delivery, increase transparency, reduce corruption, and enhance public participation.

### Challenges Facing the Use of Blockchain

Now, let us focus on the challenges blockchain technology must overcome before government adoption, whether for improving existing processes and systems or creating entirely new governance structures.

The fact that blockchain has value does not mean it will be automatically accepted and used. Even with numerous advantages in public service delivery, we may never witness its large-scale application due to insurmountable challenges.

The primary challenge facing blockchain is compatibility with existing laws. For most processes on the blockchain to be accepted, they must be legally recognized. For example, agreements executed via smart contracts are only legally binding if the law recognizes them; otherwise, courts can overturn the execution results of smart contracts at any time. Therefore, laws must be amended to fully unleash the potential of applications.

第二个障碍来自现行治理体制受益者的抵制。政府是一个庞大的企业，拥有许多受益者。事实证明，从政府运作方式中获益最多的人，同时也掌握着变革的钥匙。例如，政客们以竞选公职、在立法、行政和司法机关中担任人民代表为生。要从代议制政府转变为直接政府，他们（政客）必须修改相关法律。然而，预计他们会阻挠任何威胁其地位的变革。

另一个挑战是缺乏足够大的专家库来协助设计、构建和实施政府区块链应用。自早期以来，这一直是一个重大挑战，尤其是大型私营企业也在组建区块链开发团队。

区块链有巨大潜力改变政府的运作方式。我们为这一目的拥抱区块链的程度，取决于我们打破对政府固有认知的准备程度。这也取决于我们是否具备克服道路上可能出现的挑战的能力和决心。
