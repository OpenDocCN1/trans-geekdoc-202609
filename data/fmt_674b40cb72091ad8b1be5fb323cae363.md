### 递归与循环

`for` 循环和 `while` 循环允许程序员重复一组指令。**递归** 也是重复指令的过程，但以 **自相似** 的方式进行。我知道这听起来有点奇怪。让我用以下例子来解释。

##### 示例 1

想象你站在一条非常长的队伍里，想知道队伍前面有多少人，但你看不到所有人。如果你问前面的人有多少人在他的前面，他会转向他前面的人问有多少人在他的前面，以此类推，最终这条链条会到达排队第一个人。这个人会转向他身后的人说：“没有人”。然后会发生有趣的事情。第二个人会转向第三个人说：“有一人”。第三个人会转向第四个人说：“两人”，以此类推，直到最前面的人回应他前面有多少人。这种创造性的解决方案就是递归算法的一个例子。

一个 **递归** 方法是调用自身的方法。递归是一种编程技术，可以用于解决一个问题，其解决方案可以通过对相同问题进行 progressively 小的迭代来找到。使用得当时，它可以是一个极其优雅的解决方案。顺便说一下，递归解决方案可以用迭代来编写，但我们仍需了解它，因为它使某些问题的编写和理解变得更加容易。

我们知道一个方法可以调用另一个方法，对吧？如果方法调用自身呢？如果你停下来思考，第一次调用方法后，程序将永远不会结束，因为该方法会继续调用自身（想象电影 *Inception*）。

### 基准情况

递归方法的 **基准情况** 是方法参数与程序员有策略性选择的预定义值之间的比较。基准情况的比较决定了方法是否应该再次调用自身。回到本概念介绍中的例子，排队第一个人的回答 “没有人” 就是基准情况。它是递归过程的逆向答案。

每次递归方法调用自身且基准情况不满足时，该方法会暂时把该调用保存 aside，然后再对自身发起新的调用。直到基准情况满足为止。当最终有一个调用使基准情况为真（基准情况满足）时，该方法停止自我调用，并开始为之前的每个调用返回值的过程。由于调用是放在 `stack` 上的，返回值的执行顺序与调用顺序相反。这个顺序称为 最后-先出，先-入（LIFO），意味着最后一次递归调用是第一个返回值的。

此外，每一次递归方法的调用应该使参数 *更接近* 使基准情况为真的状态。这意味着每次调用方法时，参数的值应该 *越来越接近* 使基准情况为真的，而不是远离它为真的。

如果递归方法不包含有效的基准情况比较，那么它可能会无限自我调用。这将是不好的。

那我所说的“差”是什么意思？记住每次递归方法被调用时，它必须暂时把该调用保存，直到基准情况满足。这个过程称为 *把调用压栈*，计算机将这个栈存储在其 RAM 中。如果基准情况 *永远* 不满足，那么方法调用会堆积，计算机最终会耗尽内存。这将导致 `stack overflow error`（栈溢出错误），并且在出现 `infinite recursion`（无限递归）时会发生。

![Image](img/power.jpg)

### 基准情况

基准情况是方法参数与程序员预先选定的特定值之间的比较。当基准情况为真时，方法停止自我调用，并开始返回值的过程。每个递归方法都需要一个有效的基准情况，否则它会无限自我调用（无限递归）并导致内存不足错误，称为栈溢出错误。

# 合并排序

**Merge Sort** 是一种“分治”算法，使用递归。Merge Sort 算法会反复将列表分成两组，直到无法再分（这就是递归的作用）。随后，算法将较小的组合并一起，在合并的同时进行排序。此过程会一直进行，直到所有组合并成一个已排序的组。

Merge Sort 是相对较快的排序算法，且在大数据集上比插入排序或选择排序更高效。虽然它看起来是这三种排序中最好的，但其缺点是执行时需要更多的内存。

### 示例

使用合并排序算法对一组数字进行排序。

步骤 2：将每组再分为两组：8、5 和 2、6 以及 9、1 和 3、4

步骤 3：重复直到无法继续：8 和 5 和 2 和 6 和 9 和 1 和 3 和 4

步骤 4：合并两组，合并时排序：5、8 和 2、6 和 1、9 和 3、4

步骤 5：合并两组，合并时排序：2、5、6、8 和 1、3、4、9

步骤 6：重复上一步，直到得到一个已排序的组：1、2、3、4、5、6、8、9

## 二分查找

到目前为止，我们唯一知道的在列表中查找目标的算法是顺序查找。它从列表的一端开始，向另一端工作，比较每个元素与目标。虽然它可行，但效率不高。**二分查找**算法是查找目标项的最高效方法，前提是列表已经排序。二分查找算法从已排序的数组或 `ArrayList` 的中间开始，在每次迭代中消除数组或 `ArrayList` 的一半（要么是“低”侧，要么是“高”侧），直到找到所需值或所有元素被消除。

## 快速回顾

• 递归方法是指方法调用自身的方法。

• 当你想解决的问题可以通过不断缩小问题直到一个简单的最终问题来解决时，选择递归而不是循环。

• 递归方法通常有返回类型（而不是 void），并且必须接受参数。

• 方法的再调用应该使你更接近基例。

• 许多问题可以在不使用递归的情况下解决；但是，如果做得正确，递归可以是一种极其优雅的解决方式。

• 一旦递归过程开始，唯一结束的方式是满足基例。

• 基例是方法内部进行的比较。当这个比较为真时，方法返回一个值，开始返回过程。

• 如果你编写递归方法并忘记加入基例，或者后续对方法的调用没有使你更接近基例，你会导致无限递归，进而引发栈溢出错误。

• 为了追踪递归调用，可以在每次调用时写出方法的调用及参数值。当达到基例时，用结果替换每个方法调用。这会在逆序发生。

## 合并排序

• Merge Sort 使用一种算法，反复将数据分成两组，直到无法再分。算法在合并较小的组时对它们进行排序。此过程会一直进行，直到所有组合并成一个已排序的组。

• Merge Sort 使用分治算法和递归。

• Merge Sort 比选择排序和插入排序更高效。

• Merge Sort 需要的内部内存（RAM）比选择排序和插入排序多。

• 二分查找算法要求列表在搜索前已排序。

• 二分查找算法在每次遍历中消除搜索列表的一半。

## Free-Response Scoring Guidelines

When grading, AP readers will compare your code against the scoring guidelines for each question. After completion, if any of the following penalties apply, the corresponding points will be deducted. Each penalty can only be assessed once per problem (even if the same error occurs multiple times), and the total score cannot be negative. These guidelines were used for the 2019 exam and may be modified subsequently. Please note that changes may occur.

**General Penalties** (assessed only once per problem)

![image](img/f0461-01.jpg)

**No Penalties** (*these may change from year to year*)

• Extraneous code that does not cause a side effect

• Spelling errors, such as "wile" instead of "while"

• Using `private` or `public` on local variables

• Using a local variable but forgetting to declare at least one

• Forgetting `public` when writing a class or constructor header

• Misusing math symbols as operators (×, •, +, ≤, ≥, <>, ≠)

• Confusing `[ ]` with `< >` with `( )`

• Misusing `=` instead of `==` and vice versa

• Confusing `length` vs `size` for String, ArrayList, or array

• Incorrectly writing size in array declaration, e.g., `int[25] myArray = new int[25];`

• Missing semicolons if most of the code is missing semicolons but appropriate spacing is provided

• Missing braces if indentation is correct and braces are used elsewhere

• Forgetting parentheses on no-argument method or constructor calls

• Forgetting parentheses after `if` statement or `while` loop (if indentation is correct)

*Note: e.g., declaring "Bunny bunny = new Bunny()" then using "Bunny.hop()" instead of "bunny.hop()". This error is not considered a syntax error, but because the reader cannot assume you know the difference between "Bunny" and "bunny", you cannot get credit for that problem.*

### Teacher's Manual Introduction

Today, teachers have a wealth of resources for the AP Computer Science A classroom, no longer limited to the teacher and textbook. Today's teachers can use online simulations, apps, computer-based assignments, video lectures, and more. Even the College Board itself provides a vast amount of material for the AP Computer Science A exam, which can overwhelm teachers and students with the sheer volume of teaching materials and resources.

This book is an important resource for your classroom because it explicitly states in concise language what students need to master for the AP Computer Science A exam, and provides a program for students to use for exam review. This teacher's manual will guide you on how to use this book in your classroom and provide suggestions for best practices and best resources.

This teacher's manual will walk you through the 5 steps of teaching AP Computer Science A. These 5 steps are:

1. **Develop a Strategic Plan for the Course**

2. **Teach Engaging Lessons Every Day**

3. **Assess Student Progress**

4. **Prepare Students for the AP Exam**

5. **Become a Better Teacher Every Year**

I will discuss each of these steps in turn and provide suggestions and ideas I use in my classroom. I list them here because over the years I have found *they work*. But I hope you find something helpful in this teacher's manual.

### STEP 1

### Develop a Strategic Plan for the Course

The College Board's Course and Exam Description (CED), available at [`apcentral.collegeboard.org/pdf/ap-computer-science-a-course-and-exam-description.pdf?course=ap-computer-science-a`](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/pdf/ap-computer-science-a-course-and-exam-description.pdf?course=ap-computer-science-a), is designed by the College Board so that topics and skills build progressively throughout the school year. Over the years, working with teachers at AP Summer Institutes, AP Workshops, and AP Readings, I have recommended following the College Board's suggested scope and sequence.

After you have taught for a few years and are comfortable with the material, you may want to adjust the topic order to better suit your classroom needs. For example, in Unit 2, I introduce the concept of classes through methods defined in the `String`, `Math`, `Integer`, and `Double` classes. I leave the task of actually writing classes and calling their methods until Unit 5, when classes can be introduced completely. This allows students to use pre-written classes before writing their own. This makes me more comfortable in Unit 5 when students use conditional statements and loop structures.

The table below shows the suggested time for each unit in the CED. Class periods are based on a typical 45-minute class. If your school uses block scheduling or other non-typical schedules, you will need to adjust the pacing accordingly.

When planning your year, make sure to leave ample time for review. I like to reserve at least one month of dedicated review time before the exam. Although you should have students practice released multiple-choice and free-response questions at the end of each unit, giving students a full month of comprehensive review helps them prepare for the question types they will encounter on the exam.

### STEP 2

### Teach Engaging Lessons Every Day

AP students should love coming to your class. Because you should provide many opportunities and strategies to help students understand the material and internalize what they have learned. I usually follow one of two patterns each day. When I introduce a new unit or concept, I use Direct Instruction or the Flipped Classroom model. On subsequent days, I use the Inquiry-Based model.

**Bell Ringer/Warm-Up**. At the beginning of the year, I give students a booklet called "AP Question of the Day". This booklet contains released AP multiple-choice questions translated into Java since the first AP Computer Science exam was administered in 1984. I arrange the translated questions in unit order, from Unit 1 to Unit 10. While I take attendance and prepare the classroom, students answer the assigned question and then discuss it with classmates. Then I introduce the question and answer. This takes no more than 5 minutes per day. You can also use AP Classroom questions or questions from this review book as sources.

**Direct Instruction**. I upload my notes to the students' learning management system, and students must download or print these notes themselves. This saves time because I can focus in class on the important or commonly misunderstood parts of the notes, skipping content students can learn on their own. I *almost never* lecture for more than 15 minutes a day.

**Flipped Classroom**. I assign students to watch AP Daily videos on AP Classroom or other sources as homework. Like Direct Instruction, I focus the next day in class on important or commonly misunderstood topics.

**Inquiry-Based**. This model is used when students complete programming assignments on computers. Depending on the complexity of the assignment, a programming assignment may be completed in one class period or may take multiple periods. The purpose is to give students hands-on programming experience. Remember Benjamin Franklin's words: "Tell me *and I forget*. Teach me *and I remember*. Involve me *and I learn*." Involve your students in the class, and they will not only enjoy the class but also excel on the AP exam.

If you have the *5 Steps to a 5: AP Computer Science A* textbook, you can assign students to read a few pages corresponding to the current teaching topic. You can also assign the review questions at the end of each chapter as homework. The book covers every unit and topic in the CED, and all review questions are aligned with learning objectives.

Looking for a fun way to prepare students for Free-Response Questions (FRQs)? Here are a few activities that have worked well in my classroom.

![Images](img/arrowa.jpg) **FRQ Mocktail Party:** Give each student a copy of the same FRQ. Holding only the question (no pens or pencils allowed), have them walk around the classroom, talking to classmates, asking for their thoughts on the solution approach. After 10 minutes, students sit down and complete the solution individually. As a bonus, provide snacks and drinks in the classroom for students to enjoy while walking and talking.

![Images](img/arrowa.jpg) **Program Relay Race:** Give each student a copy of the same FRQ and give them 15 minutes to write a solution. Divide students into teams (5-6 per team works best). Each team goes to a different area of the classroom equipped with a whiteboard or large paper roll. One student from the team goes up and writes one line of code or corrects another student's code on the board/paper, then goes to the back of the team line. The next team member then writes or corrects one line of code, then goes to the back of the line. And so on, until the team believes the FRQ solution is complete. The first team to correctly write the FRQ solution wins. Participants may not talk or help teammates, and may not look at other teams' solutions until the race ends.

![Images](img/arrowa.jpg) **Write FRQ Code on the Computer:** Give each student a copy of the same FRQ and give them 20 minutes to write the solution on paper. After they finish, have them type the code into the computer. This requires you to prepare skeleton code, drivers, and data in advance so students can run the program immediately after completing parts a and b. Several websites online have these codes pre-prepared.

![Images](img/arrowa.jpg) **Practice Grading FRQs:** First, you grade the students' FRQs using the College Board scoring guidelines, then return the papers to students and discuss how each point was gained or lost. Once students are familiar with the scoring guidelines, they should start evaluating their own solutions. Collect their graded papers and discuss any necessary discrepancies. You can also have students grade sample student responses found on the AP Central exam page, and have them grade their peers' responses. Whatever grading technique you use, or all of them, the more familiar you and your students are with the grading process, the more likely students are to score points on the exam FRQs. This is one of the most effective preparation activities you can do. The time spent on this activity will greatly improve how students handle the actual AP exam FRQs.

Providing timely feedback to students is very important. Feedback on FRQs is especially difficult, especially with large class sizes. If students have to wait for results, the value of feedback is diminished. Students need to gain confidence and develop learning and test-taking strategies. You need to know students' strengths and weaknesses to adjust teaching accordingly. One way to provide immediate feedback is: have students complete part of an FRQ (e.g., part a on day 1, part b on day 2), then collect their solutions after 5-8 minutes. Quickly scan solutions and assign a grade of 100, 80, or 60. Correct or nearly correct solutions get 100, indicating the student has mastered the material and is likely to get full credit on the full solution. 80 indicates the student has potential for full credit but needs to review some concepts. 60 indicates the student needs deeper concept review to earn credit.

I recommend including College Board released questions (available in AP Classroom) in your unit tests. You can also include questions from the textbook, from the *5 Steps to a 5 AP Computer Science A* book, or create your own. However, there is no better resource than the test writers themselves; AP Classroom has hundreds of questions available for use.

If you have a classroom set of *5 Steps to a 5 AP Computer Science A*, you can pick an FRQ from the book that corresponds to the topic you are teaching and assign it to students. You can go over the solution and scoring guidelines for that question in class. The solutions also come with a sample driver program that students can use to test their solutions on the computer. After running the program, they can see if their code works, and if it doesn't, they have the opportunity to debug and fix it.

In each of your unit tests, make sure to include an FRQ. Having students practice answering open-ended questions in tests is the best way they can prepare for the AP exam. Remember, FRQs account for 50% of the student's exam score, so practicing FRQs in every unit test is crucial.

### STEP 5

### Become a Better Teacher Every Year

A great AP teacher constantly strives to improve, however success is measured. If something didn't work as expected this year, there's always next year to try to fix it or try a different approach. Whether you're a new teacher just starting out or an experienced veteran, your goal is to get better every year.

A passing score of 3 or higher is considered qualified. I tell teachers that 3 or higher means "completed college course". The highest score they get will be considered for college credit at most colleges, and they won't need to take the course again. But what does a 2 mean? I say 2 means "college ready". This means a student who scores a 2 has a solid foundation of knowledge entering a college computer science course, and with just a little more time and practice, can get an A in the college course. Remember this when you receive score reports in July; you can be proud of students who earned qualifying scores, and celebrate those who are college ready!

How to judge success? There is no right or wrong. We teach different students at different schools. Some students enter the classroom with strong science and math abilities, and honestly, they might pass the exam on day one, with or without you. Most students may lack some of the skills or knowledge needed to complete the AP exam. These are the students we need the most, and when they succeed, it's precisely because of their effort and our support.

For all AP teachers (new and experienced), the best way to improve scores is to use the Instructional Planning Report you receive after student scores are released. You can access this document in your AP Classroom. You will receive a score breakdown by unit, by question type (MCQ and FRQ), etc. This is exactly the information you need to adjust your curriculum for the next school year. If you find students struggling with a particular unit, that's where you need to adjust. Maybe you need to spend more time on that unit, or find new activities to use in the classroom. Maybe you'll review that unit in class before the AP exam next school year. If students perform well on multiple-choice but poorly on free-response, how do you get extra professional development opportunities to teach them writing better? This might take a few years to see results, but as long as you pay attention to Instructional Planning Reports and continuously adjust your curriculum, your scores will soar!

Also, it's very important to attend an AP Summer Institute when you start teaching the course and for several years after. To get new ideas and insights, I recommend you attend summer workshops with different instructors. I also encourage all AP teachers to apply to become AP Exam Readers after completing three years of teaching AP courses.
