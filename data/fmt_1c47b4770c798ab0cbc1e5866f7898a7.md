
$$H = \begin{pmatrix} {\widetilde{S} - \lambda_{g}\left( {1 - {\hat{p}}_{b}} \right)I} & {\lambda_{g}{\hat{p}}_{1}I} & {\lambda_{g}{\hat{p}}_{2}I} & \ldots & {\lambda_{g}{\hat{p}}_{b - 1}I} \\ {\lambda_{g}{\hat{p}}_{b - 1}I} & {\widetilde{S} - \lambda_{g}\left( {1 - {\hat{p}}_{b}} \right)I} & {\lambda_{g}{\hat{p}}_{1}I} & \ldots & {\lambda_{g}{\hat{p}}_{b - 2}I} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ {\lambda_{g}{\hat{p}}_{2}I} & {\lambda_{g}{\hat{p}}_{3}I} & {\lambda_{g}{\hat{p}}_{4}I} & \ldots & {\lambda_{g}{\hat{p}}_{1}I} \\ {\lambda_{g}{\hat{p}}_{1}I} & {\lambda_{g}{\hat{p}}_{2}I} & {\lambda_{g}{\hat{p}}_{3}I} & \ldots & {\widetilde{S} - \lambda_{g}\left( {1 - {\hat{p}}_{b}} \right)I} \\ \end{pmatrix}.$$

Note that, `H` is cyclic and hence, if `π` is the stationary probability vector of `H` satisfying

`πH = 0`, `πe = 1`,

then using the uniqueness of the invariant vector `µß(-S)^(-1)` of the generator `S̃`, it can be easily verified that $\pi = \frac{\mu}{b}\left( {e^{\prime} \otimes \beta{( - S)}^{- 1}} \right).$

The stability condition of the blockchain queueing model under study is provided in Theorem 8.1 as follows.

**Theorem 8.1:** *The queueing model M*^(\[*X*\])*/M*^(\\[*b*]*/*1 under study with the generator given in (8.1) is stable if and only if*

(8.2)

*`λ < bμ`.*

**Proof:** Following the results from the `M/G/1`-type queues (see, Refs. [31, 32 and 33]), the stability condition is given by $\pi\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kH_{k + 1}e < \pi H_{0}e.$ This implies, (see the generator matrix in (8.1))

$$\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kH_{k + 1} = \lambda_{g}\begin{pmatrix} {\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kp_{kb}\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kp_{kb + 1}\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kp_{kb + 2}\cdots\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kp_{kb + b - 1}} \\ {\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kp_{kb - 1}\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kp_{kb}\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kp_{kb + 1}\cdots\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kp_{kb + b - 2}} \\ {\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kp_{kb - 2}\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kp_{kb - 1}\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kp_{kb}\cdots\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kp_{kb + b - 3}} \\ {\vdots \vdots \vdots \ddots \vdots} \\ {\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kp_{(k - 1)b + 1}\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kp_{(k - 1)b + 2}\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kp_{kb + 3}\cdots\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kp_{kb}} \\ \end{pmatrix} \otimes I,$$

from which, we see that $\pi\sum_{k = 1}^{\infty}kH_{k + 1}e = \frac{\mu}{b}(\frac{\mu_{1} + \mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}})\lambda_{g}\mu_{BS} = \frac{\lambda}{b}.~Since~\pi H_{0}e = \mu,$ we get the stated result on the stability condition. □

### 8.4.3 The Steady-State Probability Vector

Let `y` be the steady-state probability vector for the generator matrix `Q`. It is well-known that (see, Refs. [31, 32 and 33]) the computation of the vector `y` requires the knowledge of key matrix, say, `G`. This matrix is obtained as follows. Note that `Q` is positive recurrent and irreducible. The stochastic matrix `G` is determined as the minimal non-negative solution to

(8.3)

$$\sum_{r = 0}^{\infty}H_{r}G^{r} = 0$$

Note that the (*j,k*)th entry of `G` provides the conditional probability that the system under study will eventually visit the state (*i, k*) for the first time starting in state (*i* **+** 1,*j*), for *i =* 1 (away from the boundary). The vector `g` is the invariant vector of `G`, i.e., `g` satisfies

(8.4)

`gG = g`, `ge = 1.`

The matrix `G` and the vector `g` play a crucial role in the computation of the vector `y`. The full details including the algorithmic procedures for all these quantities are spelled out in Refs. [32, 33] and are omitted here. It is worth pointing out that one of the structural properties (see, Ref. [33]) of `G` indicates that `G` has all even numbered columns to be zero due to `H`[0] possessing this property. The following result, which is intuitively clear, can be utilized as an internal accuracy check in numerical computation.

**Lemma 8.1:** *We have*

(8.5)

$$\frac{1}{\rho}\sum_{j = 1}^{\infty}y_{j}(e \otimes I) = \frac{1}{\mu_{1} + \mu_{2}}\left( {\mu_{2},\mu_{1}} \right).$$

`Proof:` The steady-state equation for the vector `y` partitioned as `y = (`y`[0],`y`[1],`y`[2], ...)` is obtained by solving

(8.6)

```
\begin{matrix} {y_{0}C_{0} + y_{1}F = \mathbf{0},} \\ {y_{0}C_{k} + \sum_{j = 1}^{k + 1}y_{j}H_{k - j + 1} = 0,k \geq 1,} \\ \end{matrix}
```

subject to the normalizing condition

(8.7)

```
$$y_{0}e + \sum_{j = 1}^{\infty}y_{j}e = 1.$$
```

Post-multiplying the first equation in (8.6) by (`I` ⊗ `β`) and adding to this the second equation over *k* from 1 to ∞, and after interchanging the summations, we get (note that *H*[0] **=** `F`(`I` ⊗ `β`)),

(8.8)

```
$$y_{0}C_{0}(I \otimes \beta) + y_{0}\sum_{j = 1}^{\infty}C_{j} + \sum_{j = 1}^{\infty}y_{j}H = \mathbf{0}.$$
```

Post-multiplying Equation (8.8) by (`e` ⊗ `I`) and noting that $\left\lbrack {C_{0}(I \otimes \beta) + \sum_{j = 1}^{\infty}C_{j}} \right\rbrack(e \otimes I) = \mathbf{0},$, we see

(8.9)

```
$$\sum_{j = 1}^{\infty}y_{j}H(e \otimes I) = \sum_{j = 1}^{\infty}y_{j}(e \otimes \widetilde{S}) = 0,$$
```

which implies, due to the uniqueness of the invariant vector of `S̃`, that

(8.10)

```
$$\sum_{j = 1}^{\infty}y_{j}(e \otimes I) = d\left( {\mu_{2},\mu_{1}} \right),$$
```

where *d* is a constant obtained by using the normalizing condition given in (8.7) along with the fact that `y`[0]`e =` 1 **-** ρ. It is simple to assert that $d = \frac{\rho}{\mu_{1} + \mu_{2}}$ and this produces the desired result right away. **□**

### 8.4.4 Quasi-Birth Death Approach

Assume that the maximum batch size is fixed at *K.If b*≧ *K*, then we can use quasi-birth death (QBD) approach, which will simplify a number of computational aspects. In this case, we write down the generator as

(8.11)

```
$$Q^{(1)} = \begin{bmatrix} B_{1} & B_{0} & & & & & \\ E & A_{1} & A_{0} & & & & \\ & A_{2} & A_{1} & A_{0} & & & \\ & & A_{2} & A_{1} & A_{0} & & \\ & & & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots & \\ \end{bmatrix}.$$
```

The matrices `E`, `B[0]` and `B[1]` have dimensions (*2b x b*), (*b x 2b*) and (*b x b*), respectively. The matrices `A[0]`, `A[1]` and `A[2]` are square matrices of order *2b.*
