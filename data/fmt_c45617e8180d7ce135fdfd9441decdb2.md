## 20.1 引言

在许多实际应用中，虽然数据位于高维空间，但真正的维度（称为**内在维度** `intrinsic dimensionality`）可能要低得多。我们在第 9 章稀疏建模的背景下遇到过这种情况。在那里，虽然数据位于高维空间，但已知许多分量为零。任务是学习零的位置；这等同于学习由非零分量位置决定的特定子空间。在本章中，目标是在更一般的设置下处理该任务，并假设数据可以位于任何可能的子空间（不仅仅是由坐标轴移除形成的子空间）或流形上。例如，在三维空间 `ℝ³` 中，数据可能聚集在一条直线周围，或圆周周围，或抛物线图周围，任意放置在 `ℝ³` 中。在所有前述情况下，数据的内在维度均等于一，因为这些曲线中的任何一条都可以用单个参数等价地描述。图 20.1 说明了这三种情况。学习与给定数据集相关的低维结构，在**大数据** `big data` 处理和分析中具有重要意义。一些典型例子包括计算机视觉、机器人学、医学成像和计算神经科学等学科。

In all three cases, the intrinsic dimensionality of the data is equal to one. 本章的目标是向读者介绍该主题遵循的主要方向，从更经典的技术开始，如主成分分析 `PCA` 和因子分析方法，包括它们的标准形式和概率形式。典型相关分析 `CCA`、独立成分分析 `ICA`、非负矩阵分解 `NMF` 和字典学习技术也将被讨论；在后一种情况下，数据通过过完备字典中的原子展开来表示，并利用稀疏性相关论据来检测字典中最相关的原子。最后，介绍了用于学习（非线性）流形的非线性技术，如核 `PCA`、局部线性嵌入 `LLE` 和等度量映射 `ISOMAP` 技术。还介绍了包括矩阵补全、低秩矩阵分解和鲁棒 `PCA` 方法在内的较新趋势，并讨论了两个相关的应用示例。在本章末尾，描述了一个关于 fMRI 数据分析的案例研究。

## 20.2 内在维度

A dataset $\mathcal{X} \subset \mathbb{R}^l$ is said to have **intrinsic dimensionality** $m \le l$ if $\mathcal{X}$ can be described (approximately) by $m$ free parameters. As an example, consider the generation of vectors in $\mathcal{X}$ as a function of $m$ random variables, i.e., $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{g}(u_1, \dots, u_m)$, where $u_i \in \mathbb{R}, i = 1, \dots, m$. The corresponding geometric interpretation is that the observed vectors will lie along a manifold whose form depends on the vector-valued function $\mathbf{g} : \mathbb{R}^m \mapsto \mathbb{R}^l$. Let us consider the case:

$$
\mathbf{x} = [r\cos \theta, r\sin \theta]^T,
$$

where $r$ is a constant and the random variable $\theta \in [0, 2\pi]$. The data lies on a circle of radius $r$, and a single free parameter suffices to describe the data. If a small amount of noise is now added, the data will cluster around the circle (e.g., Fig. 20.1B), and the intrinsic dimensionality equals 1. From a statistical perspective, this implies that the components of the random vector are highly correlated. One sometimes says that the "effective" dimensionality is lower than the apparent dimensionality of the "ambient" space in which the low-dimensional manifold is embedded.

In more general cases, the data may lie on a collection of manifolds, or even clusters, or follow specific spatial or temporal structures. For instance, in the wavelet domain, most coefficients of an image are close to zero and can be ignored, but the larger (non-zero) coefficients have a specific structure characteristic of natural images. This structured sparsity has been exploited by the JPEG2000 coding scheme. Structured sparse representations are common in many big data applications (e.g., Ref. [42]). This chapter will focus solely on identifying manifold structures, starting with linear (subspace/affine subspace) and subsequently discussing non-linear ones.

Learning the manifold on which a dataset lies can be used to provide a compact, low-dimensional encoding for high-dimensional datasets, which can subsequently be used to perform processing and learning tasks more efficiently. Furthermore, dimensionality reduction can be used for data visualization.

## 20.3 Principal Component Analysis

**Principal Component Analysis** (PCA), or the **Karhunen–Loève Transform**, is one of the oldest and most widely used dimensionality reduction methods [105]. The basic assumption behind PCA, and any dimensionality reduction technique, is that the observed data is generated by a system or process driven by a (relatively) small number of **latent** (unobserved) variables. The goal is to learn this latent structure.
