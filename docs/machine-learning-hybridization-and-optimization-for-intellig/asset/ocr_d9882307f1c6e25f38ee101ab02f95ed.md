With the increasing use of wearable ECG devices and other monitoring equipment, there is a growing need for advanced tools and techniques to manage and analyze this vast amount of data. By leveraging big data technologies such as machine learning, data mining, and predictive analytics, researchers and health professionals can identify patterns and trends in ECG data that can provide more accurate diagnosis and tailored treatment plans, and improve patient outcomes.

One approach to performing the test is to have a prolonged record of the cardiovascular activity during a person's typical daily activity. The major issue with long-term recording from individuals is that the ECG signal can be significantly affected by external noises, potentially resulting in incorrect data classification. To improve the signal quality, noise cancellation must be implemented through the application of signal processing filtering algorithms. Power line interference (PLI) is a major culprit that affects the quality of the acquired signal, typically falling within the range of 50–60 Hz. In addition to PLI, the signal can also be affected by high- and low- frequency noises across various frequency ranges. As previously discussed, the recorded signal can be heavily impacted by noises such as high- and low-frequency noise caused by PLI, muscle tremor, and electrode movement.

Adaptive filtering techniques work by estimating the noise present in the input ECG signal. This is achieved by conducting the filtering operation based on the provided input and estimating the noise within the input, which includes both the information source and the noise. As a result, the output produced by the adaptive filter will be free from noise and maintain the integrity of the data. The most widely accepted adaptive filtering technique is the normalized least mean square (NLMS) filter, which is a modified version of the LMS algorithm that overcomes the instability issues associated with the LMS filter. This stability is achieved by applying a normalization process to the input power [2, 3, 4, 5].
