In 2013, Rai et al. proposed the automatic classification of ECG signals using a combination of DWT and various NN classifiers. Three types of classifiers – BPNN, FFNN, and multilayer perceptron (MLP) – were employed and achieved accuracies of 97.8%, 97.8%, and 100%, respectively [15]. In 2016, Shadmand et al. suggested a new ECG classification algorithm using a block-based neural network (BBNN) and PSO to categorize five heartbeats with an accuracy of 97% [16]. In 2017, Xiong et al. [17] developed a 16-layer one-dimensional CNN to predict atrial fibrillation with an accuracy of 90%. Gautam et al. [21] recommended wavelet analysis for feature extraction and BPNN as a classifier with 93.3% accuracy. Gutiérrez-Gneccchi et al. [45] introduced a combination of wavelet transform and PNN for arrhythmia detection of eight heartbeat types with an accuracy of 92.74%. In 2020, Yang et al. [46] used a combination of parametric and visual pattern features of ECG morphology for automatic diagnosis of arrhythmia using three types of classifiers – NN, SVM, and KNN – to detect 15 types of arrhythmias. Venkatesan et al. [47] proposed arrhythmia beat classification using SVM to classify normal and abnormal subjects, achieving an accuracy of 96%. In our proposed work, the accuracy obtained is significantly higher compared to the SVM classifier. Marinho et al. [48] used a novel method for arrhythmia classification, focusing mainly on different feature extraction methods and classifications. HOS and SCM are among the techniques utilized in this work, and a Bayes classifier is used to predict five types of arrhythmias with a classification accuracy of 94.3%. Furthermore, recent technologies in machine learning models such as K-means [49], ML techniques in healthcare in the context of security and privacy [50], and explainable AI [51] have evolved. From the experimental results, it is evident that PNN performs well compared to other existing models in the literature.
