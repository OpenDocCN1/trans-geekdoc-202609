### 4.1 INTRODUCTION

Agriculture is a crucial industry that provides food for the world’s expanding population. However, traditional agricultural practices face obstacles such as soil erosion, climate change, and inefficient resource utilization. These deficiencies have resulted in low yields, high expenses, and environmental degradation. To address these issues, modern agriculture must immediately adopt data-driven strategies that employ sensors, analytics, and machine learning. This chapter concentrates on the agricultural applications of these technologies, specifically how the Internet of Things (IoT) can revolutionize agricultural practices. It aims to introduce AgTech, a data-driven approach to agriculture that utilizes sensors and machine learning to optimize resource utilization, boost yields, and reduce environmental impact. By monitoring critical parameters such as plant health, soil quality, and irrigation needs, producers can make informed decisions that result in more efficient and sustainable agricultural practices. It describes the essential components of AgTech, such as Raspberry Pi, Pi Camera v1.3, NoIR Camera, Arduino UNO, DHT Sensor, Soil NPK Sensor, and NodeMCU.

Using these technologies, AgTech facilitates real-time data collection, analysis, and decision-making that have the potential to revolutionize the way farmers approach agriculture. By automating plant health monitoring, soil analysis, and irrigation scheduling, AgTech reduces the need for manual labor and provides producers with the necessary insights to optimize their operations. AgTech can predict optimal planting, fertilizing, and harvesting times with the aid of machine learning algorithms, thereby enhancing efficiency and yield. This chapter explores the potential for AgTech to revolutionize agricultural practices and create a more sustainable future for agriculture. The integration of sensors, analytics, and machine learning provides producers with the data required to make informed decisions, optimize resource utilization, increase yields, and reduce environmental impact. The agriculture industry can continue to feed the world's growing population in a more efficient, sustainable, and profitable fashion by adopting these technologies.

### 4.2 LITERATURE SURVEY

The authors in [1] highlighted the importance of smartphone applications to provide useful functionality to farmers using the Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) framework. It includes the guidelines for policy makers to follow while digitizing farming applications. The authors in [2] detailed a comparative study on data collection for agriculture fields and evaluated the potential of Agriculture Mobile Crowd Sensing to provide data collection schemes and important insights for agriculture data. A high plant growth productivity ecosystem using nano-electronic technologies like ZnIn2S4(ZIS) nanosheets as the kernel sensing media using ZIS-based flexible sensor to monitor humidity parameters is proposed by [3, 4]. Plant health is monitored using near-infrared fluorescent H2O2 nano-sensors [9]. Nano letters [5, 6] detail the study of various machine learning models to identify plants. The recognition methodologies include scanning leaf digital fingerprints using laser technology and tracing a plant’s outline using camera technology.

The studies [7, 13, 16] detail the advancement of IoT [35] in the field of agriculture, examining issues and challenges associated with the deployment of IoT in farming. Various case studies are explored for better improvement of farming. The studies [8, 12, 20] are about the identification of disease in crops by computer vision techniques using deep learning methods. A deep convolutional neural network is used to train the public data set consisting of both diseased and non-diseased crop species. The study [10] details the usage of IoT technology for pepper production, proposing the use of a No InfraRed (NoIR) camera and Global Positioning System (GPS) module with Raspberry Pi to analyze the health of black pepper plants and track the coordinate location of the plants. The NoIR camera has a filter on the lens which allows it to use infrared photography and take pictures in low light (twilight) environments. The IoT is designed to transfer data over a network without requiring human-to-human or human-to-computer interaction. The utilization of the Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) is included in the prototype to determine the health of pepper plants.

In [14, 15], a system has been proposed to adopt precision agriculture using wireless sensor networks, which enable remote monitoring of soil fertility and other parameters, namely soil moisture, pH, and temperature. This data is transmitted to the cloud, and the corresponding values are displayed on a mobile application. In [11, 22] a Raspberry Pi–based IoT [34] device is proposed which sends images of plants to classify diseases and updates environmental parameters like air temperature, humidity, soil moisture, and pH in a MySQL database in real-time. A multi-class support vector machine (SVM) is used to categorize diseases by utilizing 14 types of features related to color, texture, and shape. These features are derived from the implementation of a gray-level co-occurrence matrix, enabling the system to classify the diseases effectively.

In [17, 18, 21, 24, 25], emerging technologies such as plant wearables and wireless sensor networks, and unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV)-based technologies are also discussed in terms of their applications in precision agriculture. The study [29] proposes a GeoSense model, a decision support system for precise farming in India. The research directions and challenges of soil sensors and intelligent agriculture are also presented. [30, 31] contend that decision support systems (DSS) can improve farm management decisions and offer the opportunity to improve productivity and limit environmental degradation. Practical suggestions are proposed to encourage a more effective user-centered design. The normalized [19] difference vegetation index (NDVI) and normalized difference red edge (NDRE) were obtained from five spectral band images (red, green, blue, infrared (NIR), and red edge (REDGE)) that were processed by software into a full image map. The image map obtained can be used to analyze the farmland and identify variations in the greenness of plants. These observations demonstrate the importance of using NDRE, a sensitive index for monitoring plant chlorophyll content.

In [23], the authors discuss how to improve yield production and nitrogen management while reducing operating costs and environmental impact. This paper discusses research developments conducted within the last 15 years on machine learning based techniques for accurate crop yield prediction and nitrogen status estimation. The paper concludes that the rapid advances in sensing technologies and ML techniques will provide cost-effective and comprehensive solutions for better crop and environment state estimation and decision making. The authors in [26] reviewed existing sensors and data analytics techniques used in different areas of agriculture. It classified agriculture into five categories and reviewed the state-of-the-art technology in practice and ongoing research in each of these areas. It also presented a case study of a Korean scenario compared with projects in other developed nations. In [27], a low-power wireless sensor network based on LoRaWAN protocol was designed with a focus on the IoT’s low-cost precision agriculture applications, such as greenhouse sensing and actuation.
