# 15.1 INTRODUCTION

Sign languages are forms of communication that convey meaning using manual gestures or visual cues. Sign language, often known as SL, is the principal mode of communication utilized by persons who are deaf or hearing impaired when attempting to convey their thoughts or emotions to hearing people in everyday life [1]. A collection of gestures, hand motions, facial expressions, or head movements can be used to represent letters or symbols in SL, and each letter of the alphabet has a corresponding sign. There are between 138 and 300 distinct forms of sign language used throughout the world now due to the fact that there are more varieties of sign language being used around the world and that these variations are being improved as a result of various groups of people connecting with each other [2]. The visual means of communication utilized by the hand-talk community and ordinary people to transmit the meaning include hand gestures, signals, body movements, facial expressions, and lip motions; we know this language as a sign language. The process of sign language recognition (SLR) is difficult and intricate, and the current state of the art in artificial intelligence makes possible a wide variety of study prospects.
