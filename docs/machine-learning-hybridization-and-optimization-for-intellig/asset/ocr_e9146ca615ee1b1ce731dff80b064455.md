**Weather Sensors:** These sensors measure temperature, humidity, and other weather-related data points that can impact crop growth.

**Soil Nutrient Sensors:** These sensors measure the health of soil by detecting soil macronutrients such as nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium along with its pH.

**Crop Sensors:** These sensors measure the health and growth of crops, including plant height, leaf area, and chlorophyll levels.

Once the sensors are installed, the data they collect is analyzed using advanced analytics techniques. This data can help farmers make more informed decisions about crop management, including when to plant, when to water, and when to harvest. By analyzing historical data, predictive analytics can help farmers to forecast crop yields and plan accordingly. Machine learning algorithms can be used to analyze sensor data in real time and make recommendations to farmers about optimal planting, watering, and harvesting schedules. With the help of connected sensors and analytics, farmers can remotely monitor their crops and receive alerts if any issues arise, such as low soil moisture or pest infestations. The PlantVillage dataset by Hughes et al. [59] consists of about 87,000 healthy and unhealthy leaf images divided into 38 categories by species and disease. Here we provide a subset of our experiments on working with this data. We also ended up using transfer learning from MobileNet and the weights from pre-training on ImageNet.

The following are augmentations to the images:

+   - width_shift and height_shift are ranges (as a fraction of total width or height) within which to randomly translate pictures vertically or horizontally.
+   - rescale is a value by which we will multiply the data before any other processing. Our original images consist of RGB coefficients in the 0–255, but such values would be too high for our models to process (given a typical learning rate), so we target values between 0 and 1 instead by scaling with a 1/255 factor.
+   - shear_range is for randomly applying shearing transformations.
+   - zoom_range is for randomly zooming inside pictures.
+   - fill_mode is the strategy used for filling in newly created pixels, which can appear after a rotation or a width/height shift.

We used flow_from_directory() to generate batches of image data (and their labels) directly from our images in their respective folders.
