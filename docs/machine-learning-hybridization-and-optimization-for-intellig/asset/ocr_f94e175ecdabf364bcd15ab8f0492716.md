# 15.2 PROPOSED WORK

Problem statement: The preceding studies [15, 16, 17, 18] have shown, among other things, that methods that depend their accuracy on the user’s behavior are worse when it comes to recognizing signs in a language other than English. This is due to the high cost and complexity of the necessary hardware installations [15]. For this reason, most studies have focused on vision-based models. The creation of sign language recognition models is evidently still a challenging subject of research, despite the many studies that have been undertaken in this area.

In order to solve continuous sign issues that are independent of the signer, an appropriate model has to be developed [16]. Since each signer’s duration, pace, and context will be unique, it is difficult to create a model that is both highly accurate and consistent among all models [17]. Several academics have made suggestions on where CNN-related model research could go next. The dependency of models with regard to the optimization of tuning parameters adds another layer of difficulty to the task of selecting the most appropriate model to adopt and enhance [18]. However, advancements in British sign language detection using the CNN and LSTM models have received little attention.
