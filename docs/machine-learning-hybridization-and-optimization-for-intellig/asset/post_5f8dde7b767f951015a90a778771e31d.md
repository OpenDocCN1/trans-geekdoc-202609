## 4.6 CONCLUSION

AgTech's use of sensors and analytics to revolutionize agricultural techniques has the potential to transform conventional agriculture. The inefficiencies of conventional agricultural methods, such as soil erosion, climate change, and inefficient resource utilization, have led to low yields, high costs, and environmental degradation. To enhance agricultural practices and profitability, there is a pressing need for modern agriculture to adopt data analytics and machine learning. Our initiative has demonstrated how IoT-based agriculture applications can streamline operations such as plant health monitoring and irrigation, allowing farmers to save time and resources while improving the overall efficiency and sustainability of agriculture. By utilizing critical parameters such as temperature, humidity, soil moisture, and nutrient levels, along with machine learning and predictive analytics, we can maximize resource utilization and increase yields with minimal environmental impact. We hope that our initiative will serve as an example of the transformative power of technology in the field of agriculture and will contribute to the sustainable feeding of the world’s growing population.

## 4.7 FUTURE WORK

Even though the AgTech solution significantly improves plant illness diagnosis and soil health analysis, there is still room for system improvement and development. The scalability of the prototype created for this project is an important factor that needs further thought.

The current prototype is designed for small to medium-sized farms, and it may not be suitable for larger farming operations. Therefore, future efforts could concentrate on expanding the system’s scalability to meet the requirements of larger farms. This could involve developing more robust hardware, improving software algorithms, and optimizing the user interface to handle larger datasets.

Furthermore, investigating the application of machine learning and artificial intelligence algorithms to enhance the accuracy of disease detection may be a promising direction for future research. A system could learn to recognize and diagnose a greater variety of plant illnesses by examining a big dataset of plant pictures, thereby increasing agricultural yields and lowering the need for pesticides.

Additionally, deeper integration with precision agricultural methods would make it possible for farmers to optimize their irrigation and land-use strategies, resulting in higher productivity and sustainability.
