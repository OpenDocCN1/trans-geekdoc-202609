This section includes related research on classifying remote sensing images using both conventional and machine learning classifiers.

Parul Singh et al. [1] have proposed an assessment of urban growth in Delhi using geospatial techniques. The satellite data has been preprocessed, which includes atmospheric correction. For generating broad urban land cover classes, a maximum likelihood classifier (MLC) and supervised classification have been used. According to the 2011 census (Census of India, 2011), Delhi City has a population of roughly 16 million people. As of 2018, the number of residents was anticipated to be close to 29 million (World Urban Development Prospects, 2018). One of the major driving factors for urban expansion in the region is the region’s growing population. Two Landsat series satellite imageries were used in this study to detect LULC changes. Satellite imagery was downloaded for temporal analysis from the USGS website (http://glovis.usgs.gov) (1992, 2019). The broad steps involved are the acquisition of temporal datasets (1992 and 2019) from the USGS website, layer stacking, study area extraction, and atmospheric correction to improve imagery classification accuracy. The broad LULC classes have been delineated using supervised classification. The classification accuracy of the two classified images was assessed using 280 cross-verified random points. For the years 1992 and 2019, the classification accuracies were 85% and 88%, respectively.

SVM HLS IP is initially produced with the use of a Vivado HLS tool, and then it is incorporated with the Zynq SoC design. The intended Zynq system’s bitstream is produced after the Xilinx SDK tool’s synthesis, place, and route operations. This tool assists in running the application on Zynq’s onboard for classification. A test is carried out using two HSI datasets from the airborne visible infrared imaging spectrometer (AVIRIS). Using the available directives in the Vivado HLS tool, the proposed HLS IP design is optimized. To enhance and optimize the synthesis outcomes, a number of experiments employing different optimization directives were conducted. This process will help in the creation of straightforward, low-complexity hardware.
