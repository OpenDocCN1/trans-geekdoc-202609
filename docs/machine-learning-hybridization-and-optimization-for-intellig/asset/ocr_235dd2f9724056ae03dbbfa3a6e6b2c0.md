# 9.2.3 DENOISING

The raw ECG signal downloaded from the PhysioNet database will have imposed noises within it. The preprocessing of the signal is a crucial step to remove the artifacts present in the raw signal. There are many works of literature available on filtering ECG signals. The filters have to be designed by considering the frequency component required for feature extraction [29]. Vibrations and sudden movements by the subjects may cause motion artifacts, resulting in peak amplitude and duration differentiation. This type of movement during real-time ECG signal recording will lead to a baseline shift of the signal. By denoising the input ECG pulse, the quality of the ECG pulse has been enhanced and quantified using certain parameters like the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR).

For noise cancellation applications [30], adaptive filters are widely employed, which are usually designed using finite impulse response (FIR) filters. These filters make use of a minimization criterion to update the filter coefficients. Thus, the weighted sum of current and previous input samples will be the corresponding output of the filter. The NLMS adaptive filtering technique is aimed at artifact removal from ECG data.
