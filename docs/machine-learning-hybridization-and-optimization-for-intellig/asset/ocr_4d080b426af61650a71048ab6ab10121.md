There are other additional areas of research where LULC data is crucial, and LULC data extraction has long been a significant and active area of study. The most effective way to obtain LULC data is to classify the images that sensors have captured [4]. The process of classifying an image entails categorizing pixels based on a variety of criteria, including spectral signatures, indices, contextual data, and so on [18].

Remote sensing (RS) data is expanding at a rapid pace because of recent improvements in computer technology and RS. Pixels are classified and given land cover classifications through the LULC classification procedure, for example, water, urban areas, wooded areas, horticulture, structures, woodlands, agricultural areas, highlands, mountains, and grasslands [22, 23]. The main goal of image grouping is to logically classify all of the pixels in an image into land cover-related groups or themes. In other words, depending on their intrinsic alien reflectance, various component types display a varied mix. At the local, regional, and national levels, LULC maps are crucial in the design, management, and monitoring of programs. It is critical to monitor the evolution of LULC patterns throughout time. The act of acquiring, processing, interpreting, and permanently storing pictures from ground-based, aerial, or space-based equipment is known as remote sensing, and it may be used for a variety of things, including identifying the land's many uses [52].
