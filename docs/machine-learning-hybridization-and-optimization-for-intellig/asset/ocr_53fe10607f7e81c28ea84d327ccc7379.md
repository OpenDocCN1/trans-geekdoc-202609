Images’ original intrinsic structural information and non-local information are preserved by implementing high-order clustering tensors and are tested on the simulated synthetic aperture radar (SAR) dataset consisting of four real SAR images.

An integrated feature selection algorithm and an innovative technique have been introduced [112] in which both the independence criterion of Hilbert–Schmidt and the linear discriminant analysis of the Kernel Fisher technique have been combined to extort the uncorrelated, low-dimensional, and low-distinctive features. The extracted low-dimensional features are classified using a Naive Bayes classifier. The proposed approach is tested on two typical circuits and successfully detects and classifies most analog circuit faults.

An extended framework [17] has been proposed to mine the inherent features which help in classifying the PolSAR land dataset based on a tensor embedding approach. Every pixel image is represented as an intrinsic feature and the statistical image properties are symbolized in a matrix form. Algorithms based on a vector are then applied to obtain the projection matrices, and the algorithms’ effectiveness seems to be outstanding when tested on three different datasets that cover the regions of San Francisco, Xi’an, and Flevoland.

Feature extraction frameworks [84] that consist of using a feature-centric technique to extort the unique information from a research study have been introduced. To extract the logical outline from articles, a four-stage novel approach is introduced called FLAG-PDFe. The proposed model is built upon an SVCML model and trained on the textual as well as the geometric feature information to extract the unique sections in an article, and comprehensive generic feature evaluations are then done.

A discriminant correlation feature fusion analysis has been proposed [119] to extract features of three unrelated matching types and to differentiate scenes together. The results of one or more classifier models are merged, and finally the best one is identified using the majority voting method; this can further be improved to achieve substantial improvement in classification performance for remote sensing.
