In 1992, Jay Wilpon and a team of researchers at AT&T designed and developed automated speech recognition (ASR) using HMM. This was a significant development as it saved a tremendous amount of time and money.

In the same year, AT&T researchers also solved the problem of large classification using support vector machines (SVM). And in 1996, Patrick Haffner, a researcher at AT&T, developed convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for large-scale application recognition

.In 1997, another group of AT&T researchers developed the AdaBoost algorithm, which utilizes decision trees and unstructured data for decision classification.In that same year, Garry Kasparov lost a game of chess to the IBM supercomputer Deep Blue. This marked the first time a computer had defeated a skilled chess player, causing great alarm within the chess community. It was a historic event that showcased the superior performance of AI systems

in challenging tasks. The world now understands that humanity has developed its own adversary—an artificial intelligence that can learn and evolve on its own. This signifies a miraculous turning point in machine learning.

Natural language understanding was developed by combining learning techniques such as SVM, HMM, and AdaBoost, resulting in the creation of the first interactive voice response (IVR) in 2001.
