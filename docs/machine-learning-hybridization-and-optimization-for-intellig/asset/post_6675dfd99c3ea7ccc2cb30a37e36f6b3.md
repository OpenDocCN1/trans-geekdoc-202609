## 19 History of Machine Learning

Deepak K. Sinha and Garima Sinha

DOI: 10.1201/9781003465775-19

### 19.1 INTRODUCTION

Artificial intelligence and machine learning are the demand of this era, and they have left very few applications untouched; otherwise, most of the domains are applying ML for prediction of the results very precisely and accurately without the interference of human skills, experience or even interference [1, 2, 3, 4].

Although ML is in huge demand nowadays, this field gradually seasoned since the early 1950s with the development of the Turing test somewhere in the 1950s by Alan Turing. ML is considered to be part of computer science, but if we look at the history of its development, we may find evidence that in its early days, mathematicians and learning algorithm developers had contributed a lot to its present stage [5, 6, 7, 8, 9].

### 19.2 ML IN EARLY YEARS

We can find historical evidence of the development of ML in the early 1950s with the Turing test and Turing machine by Alan Turing. The first goal of the Turing machine was to develop a model that can compute anything that a human can compute. Before the invention of electronic computers, the term "computer" actually referred to a person whose line of work is to calculate numerical quantities! The second goal of the Turing machine was to develop a model that is so simple that it can actually prove interesting epistemological results, and finally, it was developed as the Turing machine in 1950. This can be considered as the first step towards the development of ML models [10, 11, 12, 13, 14].

The second significant contribution to ML came from Arthur Samuel, who developed the first game of checkers and implemented machine learning for the first time while developing checkers [15, 16]. The learning algorithm of the game of checkers was used to find the moves responsible for the winning strategies, and subsequently, the computer improved the game by determining those strategies and incorporating them into the game, which was developed in 1952 [17, 18].

Later, Frank Rosenblatt worked on simulating human neurons for the thought process of the human brain and designed neural networks for the first time in 1957. This was a breakthrough in the field of ML and made many things easier and possible, especially in deep learning, image processing, NLP, sequence learning, and other applications.

In 1974, backpropagation was introduced to aid neural networks in learning to recognize patterns. It has also been used in various machine learning applications, such as performance optimization and generalizing from datasets to new instances. Backpropagation aims to increase a model's accuracy by adjusting its weights to predict future outputs more accurately. The nearest neighbor algorithm was written in 1967 and was initially used for recognizing similar patterns and later used to find paths for the traveling salesman problem. Nowadays, it is widely used for classification problems using KNN.
