## FIGURE 7.2 Technology for virtualization

There are different types of virtualization, including complete virtualization, paravirtualization, and software and hardware virtualization [12]. Technology for virtualization includes the technique for software-level virtualization implementation. Overall, it can be split into two categories: open source and proprietary. Xen, KVM, VMware, Hyper-V, and Docker containers are typical examples.

- Xen: Based on the x86 architecture, Xen is one of the open-source virtualization technologies with the fastest growth, most stability, and least resource-intensive characteristics [13]. Based on the x86 architecture, the open-source virtualization technology Xen has become well-known for its dependability, speed, and resource effectiveness. Xen is an open-source project that was first created by the University of Cambridge and is currently managed by the Linux Foundation. The ability to deliver near-native performance for virtualized workloads is one of Xen’s core advantages, making it a popular option for high-performance computing and mission-critical applications. Additional advanced security measures provided by Xen include support for hardware-based virtualization extensions like Intel VT and AMD-V as well as the capacity to isolate virtual machines from one another and the underlying host system. A well-liked option for a variety of use cases and sectors, Xen is a mature and reliable virtualization technology that has built a reputation for its performance, security, and resource efficiency [14].

- **KVM:** Open-source full virtualization KVM, which is a part of the Hypervisor paradigm and supports a range of hardware platforms, uses QEMU (Quick Emulator) to offer device virtualization. KVM (kernel-based virtual machine) is a hypervisor-based open-source complete virtualization system that works with a variety of hardware platforms. Numerous Linux distributions contain KVM as a standard feature, which enables the operation of multiple virtual computers on a single physical host. The usage of QEMU to provide device virtualization is one of KVM’s core features. A variety of hardware devices, including CPUs, network cards, storage devices, and input/output devices can be simulated by the quick and effective emulator QEMU. Because of this, KVM can deliver almost native performance for virtualized workloads, making it suitable for usage in industrial settings. KVM is a potent and adaptable virtualization solution that works well across a variety of use cases and sectors. It is a well-liked option for enterprises looking for a dependable and effective virtualization solution due to its usage of QEMU for device virtualization and support for hardware-based virtualization extensions.

- **VMware:** A leader in virtualization infrastructure and cloud computing, VMware offers a variety of solutions to assist businesses increase their IT’s productivity, adaptability, and speed. The solutions from VMware are made to make IT operations simpler, management more efficient, and services more rapidly and securely. One of VMware’s flagship products is the vSphere virtualization platform, which offers a dependable and secure cloud computing foundation and enables businesses to operate their workloads and applications on a virtual infrastructure. High availability, data security, and automation are just a few of the features and functionalities that vSphere offers, all of which assist enterprises in enhancing their IT operations and agility. Along with vSphere, VMware also provides a number of other cloud computing services, such as VMware Cloud on AWS, which enables businesses to run their VMware workloads on the AWS cloud; and vCloud Suite, which offers a comprehensive cloud infrastructure platform. Overall, VMware’s solutions are made to assist businesses of all sizes and sectors in streamlining their IT processes and infrastructure while also lowering costs and increasing agility [15].

- **Hyper-V:** Microsoft has developed a hypervisor virtualization technique called Hyper-V that allows for desktop virtualization. Desktop virtualization is possible because of Microsoft’s Hyper-V hypervisor-based virtualization technology. Microsoft Windows Server and the Pro and Enterprise editions of Windows 10, all come with Hyper-V, a native hypervisor. With Hyper-V, many virtual machines can run on a single physical host, consolidating workloads and maximizing server efficiency. Other capabilities include support for hardware-based virtualization extensions like Intel VT-x and AMD-V as well as live migration, which enables the quick switching of virtual computers across physical hosts. By utilizing remote desktop services (RDS) and virtual desktop infrastructure (VDI), Hyper-V also offers desktop virtualization in addition to server virtualization. While VDI offers a method to build and administer virtual desktops in a centralized and scalable manner, RDS enables users to access virtualized desktops and apps from anywhere. It is a well-liked option for enterprises wishing to enhance desktop administration and boost user productivity because it supports desktop virtualization with RDS and VDI.

- Docker container: The lightweight and highly portable Docker containers were created with this in mind. They are smaller and more effective than virtual machines due to the fact that each container has its own set of system files that are shared with the host operating system and other containers. Additionally autonomous of one another, processes between containers are distinguishable and manageable with the use of container orchestration tools. Docker containers are also intended to be platform-agnostic, which means they can function on a variety of hardware platforms and operating systems. In contexts where portability and scalability are crucial, such as cloud computing systems and other distributed computing environments, they are thus well suited for utilization. Compared to Docker containers, traditional virtual machines are less portable. Each container has its own system files, and processes between containers can discriminate between various computer resources and are independent of one another.

## 7.4 CLOUD COMPUTING: SECURITY CONCERNS

### 7.4.1 NETWORK AVAILABILITY

The importance of network connectivity and bandwidth is directly related to cloud computing. Since cloud services are often accessed online, dependable and quick network connectivity is essential to guaranteeing that cloud applications and data are always accessible. Cloud services may become unavailable or operate poorly if network connectivity is sluggish, inconsistent, or unavailable, which can have serious repercussions for businesses. For instance, any interruption in network access could cause a firm that depends on cloud-based software to run its operations to lose productivity and income. Cloud-based data loss or corruption, which can have major repercussions for organizations, especially those in regulated industries, may occur if information is not accessible when it is needed. Before implementing cloud computing services, it is crucial for enterprises to confirm that their network connectivity and bandwidth fulfill their bare minimum requirements. This may necessitate installing redundancy measures, upgrading network infrastructure, and making sure that network availability and performance are covered in service level agreements (SLAs) with cloud providers.

### 7.4.2 DISASTER RECOVERY AND BUSINESS CONTINUITY

The assurance that their operations and services will continue in the event of a catastrophe or disruption in the cloud provider's production environment is essential for tenants and consumers of cloud services. In multi-tenant cloud settings where numerous customers share the same physical infrastructure and resources, this is especially crucial. In such settings, a single catastrophe or outage can affect numerous clients, causing extensive service interruptions and data loss. To combat this, cloud service providers commonly have business continuity and disaster recovery mechanisms in place to make sure that their services are available and functional in the case of a disaster. This could involve techniques like data backup and replication, failover to different data centers or cloud regions, and the use of redundant network connectivity and equipment. Service level agreements (SLAs), which guarantee a specific level of uptime and availability and give compensation in the event of service interruptions or outages, may be offered by cloud providers. Ensuring the continuity and availability of cloud services in the event of a disaster is a crucial component of cloud computing, and cloud service providers have a duty to implement effective disaster recovery and business continuity measures to safeguard their clients' operations and services.

### 7.4.3 TRANSPARENCY

Cloud users and tenants frequently have to put their trust in the security assurances made by cloud providers, especially when those companies withhold information about their internal policy or technological implementation. Cloud providers must be open and honest about their security and privacy policies as well as how they respond to security issues in order to earn and keep customers’ trust. Transparency can take many different forms, such as making available to the general public the provider's security and privacy policies, practices, and standards as well as specifics regarding their incident management protocols and methods. Tenants and users may find this information useful in understanding how the provider safeguards their data and applications as well as the measures taken to stop, identify, and address security incidents.
