## 12.4.2 Ant Colony Optimization

Ant colony optimization (ACO), a population-based meta-heuristic, can be employed to roughly solve challenging objective functions. In ACO, a group of computer organisms, or artificial ants, search for appropriate solutions to optimization problems. To apply ACO, the optimization approach is transformed into the task of finding the optimal path on a symmetrical graph. The artificial ants, also known as ants, explore the network to progressively build solutions [37]. A pheromone paradigm, which is a set of parameters related to network elements (either vertices or connections) and modified by the ants during runtime, influences the process of generating diverse solutions. In the 1990s, Marco Dorigo introduced the ACO approach, which draws its inspiration solely from the behavior of ant colonies and birds. Ants are highly social creatures that prioritize collective survival over individual species survival [38]. They use pheromones, touch, and sound to communicate with each other. Pheromones are bioinorganic substances generated by ants that induce other individuals of the same species to act socially. These substances can affect the behavior of other individuals by functioning like pheromones that are released into the body of the individual secreting them. As most ants are ground-dwelling, they use the land surface to deposit pheromone trails that other ants can follow (smell).

## 12.4.4 GENETIC ALGORITHM

The genetic algorithm (GA), a meta-heuristic search technique, performs in a way that is comparable to natural selection. It belongs to the larger category of evolutionary algorithms used to address optimization problems. The performance of the current implementation, which is not determined by utilizing the previous or original solution, completely determines the genetic algorithm's final solution. Like this, a mobile robot chooses its course based on how the environment reacts while avoiding barriers and avoiding collisions to accomplish a task. High computational challenges and the local minima problem are avoided with the evolutionary algorithm [40].

The extent of mobility and reverse kinematic problems, which arise whenever a particle moves without any force, are also solved using the evolutionary method. Consider the genetic algorithm's approach first from source to destination [41]; when the robot encounters an obstacle, it travels three steps away from its present position and advances in the direction of the goals. Robot positions change based on the obstacle's fitness functions [42]. A set of solutions for an artificial neural network is used to determine the variables. An objective answer is the route that leads from the location of the current impediment to the desired outcome. The mobile robot is considered a candidate solution if it never encounters any impediments and successfully completes the task. It is not the best option for a mobile robot to travel in if the target solution is better than the candidate solution [43].
