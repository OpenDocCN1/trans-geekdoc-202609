## 8.4 SECURITY CHALLENGES OF IOT DEFENSE SYSTEMS

### 8.4.1 GATHERING BATTLEFIELD INFORMATION

The gathered information is processed through command, control, intelligence, surveillance, and observation systems that process and distribute the most strategic information, such as the location of an inbound threat [20, 21]. This information can be coordinated and utilized to create a common operational picture, which supports decision-making by commanders and improves coordination and control in the operational area. IoT can play a vital role in collecting, analyzing, and delivering real-time blended data for quick decision-making. This enhances situational awareness for acquiring data on adversary's intent, capability, and actual position [3]. IoT can enhance battlefield recognition from the global, company, platoon, and crew authorities down to a single officer's level.

A specific challenge for detecting coordinated storms against IoT devices used to launch distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks is difficult to identify and can take weeks or months to establish before the attacks actually begin. A commonly used software application programmed to conduct DDoS attacks is a "bot." The "bot" software can be discreetly present on any vulnerable web structure and activated in a sequence. Numerous DDoS attacks occur when multiple bot-infected structures are activated and coordinated against specific targeted systems on the IoT.

### 8.4.2 HEALTH SURVEILLANCE AND MONITORING

A combination of IoT sensors monitoring things like temperature, blood pressure, heart rate, cholesterol levels, and blood glucose throughout the body will permit the well-being of the soldier to be checked in real time. Soldiers can be alerted of unusual states like dehydration, sleep deprivation, elevated pulse, or low glucose, and, if necessary, a clinical response team in a base medical clinic can be notified. The soldiers are equipped with special head protectors like helmets with integrated control sensors to detect force and other traumas [4]. Small, intelligent health-monitoring and medical service devices are increasingly being used frequently in battle, and therefore, medical aid/medical care can be provided to soldiers without the need for personnel. Over the years, the speed of development in the defense sector in India has remained low, and the creation of innovative weapons is still a challenge. This is primarily due to a declining defense budget, years of speculation and innovation, and the government's reluctance to award defense contracts to India's private sector. India still relies on imported products for innovative weapons, thus hindering the development of the domestic industry [24].

### 8.4.3 Fleet Management for Equipment and Vehicles

The harsh conditions and increased deployments take a toll on equipment. IoT can enable improved equipment maintenance and management through monitoring, optimizing, and properly allocating various resources and processes, such as manpower, material, financial resources, and maintenance staff. Smart bases that incorporate commercial IoT technologies in buildings, facilities, etc. ensure security at bases as well as maritime and littoral environments, health and personnel monitoring, and monitoring and just-in-time equipment support.

In geographical situations, the Indian military needs to remain functionally prepared to face threats. Therefore, the military's combat capabilities must be continuously enhanced, and technology in weapons and equipment must be upgraded. To address these issues locally, there is a need for proper assessment and planning for the military's requirements and investment in years of weapon development.

### 8.4.4 Smart Bases

Adversaries may attempt to infiltrate army installations by using stolen identification or pretending to be regular civilians. By using IoT fingerprinting and other biometric data, a person's actual identity can be determined. Other IoT sensors can be integrated into army installations to enhance the performance, efficiency, and convenience of services on a secure army installation. Automated screening of resources, along with the smart management of water and power, can all help improve operations.

#### 8.4.4.1 Lack of Technology Transfer

All the protective materials that are imported do not include an innovative component. This supports dependence on imported goods and hinders domestic manufacturing.

#### 8.4.4.2 Insufficient Quality

The materials provided by certain manufacturers do not conform to international quality standards and are compromised for cost reduction. This reduces their market potential and jeopardizes the reputation of Indian products globally.

### 8.4.5 REMOTE TRAINING

IoT can assist military staff in preparing for genuine combat zone battles. Development sensors, acoustic sensors, and more can monitor the staff during readiness or practice and send information and insights to the mentors who prepare them. A remote-controlled weapon station (RCWS) or remote weapon station (RWS), also known as a remote weapon framework (RWF), is a remotely controlled robust framework often equipped with a shoot control system for light- and medium-type weapons, which can be installed on ground combat vehicles or sea- and air-based combat platforms. Such equipment is used on modern military vehicles as it allows a heavy weapon specialist to remain within the overall security of the vehicle.

#### 8.4.5.1 Budgetary Issues

A lack of capital expenditure on defense manufacturing and research and development has been a significant challenge to India's defense objectives. While India's defense budget has increased in the long term, a significant portion of it is spent on personnel costs such as salaries and pensions, thus reducing the funds available for defense production. Additionally, of the total defense budget for 2020–2021, 58.6% was allocated to salaries, pensions, and salaries, while only 22.7% had been allocated for capital expenditure. Furthermore, India's budget allocation for research and development was only 4% of the entire defense budget for 2020–2021.
