In [14], the virtual machines rented computing resources from the cloud
layer to increase the capacity of the edge boundary and reduce the cost and
processing overheads at the edge, the cost of virtual machines on demand,
and the overheads associated with reversed virtual machines. Users, the
edge, and the cloud participate in a three-tier device–edge–cloud
collaboration model. The device layer, also known as the user layer,
includes a variety of end devices such as unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs,
also known as drones, mobiles, surveillance cameras, IoT devices, smart
devices, and other equipment. Several devices have limited storage and
processing capacity. These devices generate and store large amounts of
data. Depending on the client’s needs, these data sets can be processed
locally or offloaded to the edge and the cloud.

According to [15], in which the client employs numerous intelligent
devices, the authors investigated the computation processing offload issue
in UAVs, and the non-convex optimization problem was solved using a
distributed intelligence algorithm (DIA) [16]. Task dispatching algorithms
are essential in distributed computing environments, where tasks must be
assigned to various computational resources (servers, processors, or virtual
machines) efficiently. These algorithms aim to balance the load, optimize
resource utilization, and ensure that tasks are completed within their
required time frames and that task scheduling is highly efficient. In their
experiment, the online learning dispatching algorithm significantly reduced
the waiting time or average task response time in terms of the efficiency
and fairness of task dispatching. In the real world, unplanned offloading
tasks, constantly changing network resources, and a scarcity of computing
processing resources are the primary causes of the edge computing
network’s ineffective scheduling issues. Users may receive services with
