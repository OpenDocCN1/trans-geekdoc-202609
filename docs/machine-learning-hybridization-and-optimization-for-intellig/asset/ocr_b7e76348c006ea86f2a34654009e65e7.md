Malavika et al. [14] used logistic regression for forecasting cases for the
short-term interval, which provided results with 95% accuracy where the
model was trained in states with high daily cases. The SIR model was used
for the prediction of cases in the worst-case scenario so that the necessary
steps could be taken.

Anastassopoulou et al. [15] used the SIRD model (susceptible, infected,
recovered, dead) and tried to find the reproduction rate of the virus in two
different scenarios. The first one obtained the reproduction rate based on the
official count provided, but since there are high chances of cases that are
asymptomatic, the work has increased the count of infected by 20 and the
recovered by 40 and calculated the reproduction rate of the virus. The
average of the reproduction rate for both scenarios was taken as the final
reproduction rate based on which the forecasting of the possible total
number of infected cases in China was carried out for an interval of the
upcoming 30 days.

AlJame et al. [16] used a combination of various models to predict if a
person is COVID-19 positive based on the blood test provided. The
ensemble model provided in the chapter is ERLX which comprises logistic
regression, diverse classifiers, and extra trees, the outputs of which were
combined by the XGboost classifier. The pre-processing was done by
KNNImputer with isolation forest for outlier detection in the model with
synthetic minority oversampling technique (SMOTE) for balanced data
distribution. The above-proposed model provided far better accuracy
(99.8%) compared to various state-of-the-art models; due to the high
accuracy obtained from the model, it was considered to be one of the most
robust and reliable models for diagnosis of COVID-19 based on blood
samples.
