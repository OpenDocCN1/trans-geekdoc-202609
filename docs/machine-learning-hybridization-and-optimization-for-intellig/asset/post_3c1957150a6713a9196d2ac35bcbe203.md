## 5 Developing an AI-based Multi-Task Transfer Learning Framework for Automating Judicial Contracts

R V Aswiga

DOI: 10.1201/9781003465775-5

### 5.1 INTRODUCTION

The lists of pending cases are huge in number in Indian courts, out of which some have remained pending for more than decades. These pending cases will affect the efficiency of courts and will ultimately slow down the process of giving justice. The study of National Judicial Data Grid (NJDG) results shows 3,89,41,148 district level cases, and 58,43,113 Taluk level cases are still pending. These pending cases reduce people's access to justice and are not handled effectively. The delay in our justice delivery system may give enough time for the perpetrators to erase all evidences because of its slow process of analyzing cases.

Fortunately, the foremost top courts have already started using e-filing, and most court proceedings are conducted through online video conferencing. Artificial intelligence (AI) and newer technologies like transfer learning can now help in reducing the number of pending cases significantly. Therefore proposing any innovative approach towards digitization of law processes will facilitate the speeding up of the justice delivery process. Therefore it is understood that AI will impact the coming two or three decades and will influence the Indian judiciary, which has a largely unstructured dataset. The usage of such technologies in various facets of its implementation will benefit the Indian judicial system. This automation process will help higher grade officials to focus on only important responsibilities and the timely delivery of justice. Similarly, the accuracy of legal research can also be enhanced. Along with this, case hearings, several administrative obligations, official communications, organizing trials at top courts, and planning can be managed flexibly and fully automated with the help of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). The foremost objective of AGI, ML, and deep learning technology is to reduce the workload of lawyers and high court judges in documenting, managing, and researching cases easily.

Further, as generating legal documents is a time-intensive process and takes a lot of effort on the part of experts, AGI models combined with transfer learning are being used to automate document generation and processing. Leveraging such technologies in the Indian judiciary will reduce dependence on humans and will minimize the wrong information being given as input. Considering the current state of affairs, the Indian judiciary undoubtedly will benefit from smart contracts that are automatically created using technology. This can be viewed as a unique way to reduce the burden and administrative load in courts and to avoid disputes.

In fact, lengthy adjournments happen in India due to conflicting hearings, which means that lawyers cannot be physically present in courtrooms due to a lack of time. Therefore enabling hybrid courtrooms offline as well as online could be a solution for conflict hearing matters. This will enable justice to be delivered earlier without any further delay due to the absence of lawyers. Referencing law is considered essential for winning a case. For lawyers, gaining a victory in a case requires considerable effort. Normally, lawyers research plenty of materials to confer and argue about their case. Competent case handling will eventually result in an increase in budget that is, income will benefit and along with this will come fame and reputation for lawyers. Therefore, there is an immediate and demanding need to adopt ML and AI with deep learning techniques like transfer learning to augment the competence of the delivery of justice in India.
