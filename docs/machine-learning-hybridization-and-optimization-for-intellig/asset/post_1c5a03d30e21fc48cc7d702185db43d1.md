## 9.5 DISCUSSION

As discussed earlier in Section 9.2.5, various algorithms are used to extract features from the ECG signal. Table 9.5 depicts the list of feature extraction methods used in the literature. Based on this comparison, it is convenient to select the best method depending on the dataset, network architecture, nature of the input signal, and so on.

TABLE 9.5 Comparison of different feature extraction methods

| S. No. | Feature extraction methods | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. | ICA [11, 39] | - Provides high performance in voluminous data<br>- Signal will be decomposed as spatially fixed components and temporally independent | - Inapplicable for undecided cases<br>- More calculations required |
| 2. | PCA [40] | - Best method for dimension degradation<br>- Lower data loss | - PCA fails for complicated manifold<br>- Linear and continuous data |
| 3. | WT [41] | - Through variable window size – analyses signal with discontinuity<br>- Analysis in both time and frequency domains | - Lacking method to remove pervasive noise<br>- Limited performance |
| 4. | AR [42] | - Best for short-duration data<br>- Better frequency resolution<br>- Reduces spectral loss | - Not applicable to nonstationary signals like EEG |
| 5. | WPD [43] | - Good for nonstationary signals | - High computation time |
| 6. | FFT [44] | - Frequency domain analysis | - Only for stationary signals<br>- Greater sensitivity to noise<br>- Poor time localization |

Once the features are extracted, the final step is classification. Table 9.6 compares the accuracy of existing methodologies with the proposed study.

In 2013, Rai et al. proposed the automatic classification of ECG signals using a combination of DWT and various NN classifiers. Three types of classifiers – BPNN, FFNN, and multilayer perceptron (MLP) – were employed and achieved accuracies of 97.8%, 97.8%, and 100%, respectively [15]. In 2016, Shadmand et al. suggested a new ECG classification algorithm using a block-based neural network (BBNN) and PSO to categorize five heartbeats with an accuracy of 97% [16]. In 2017, Xiong et al. [17] developed a 16-layer one-dimensional CNN to predict atrial fibrillation with an accuracy of 90%. Gautam et al. [21] recommended wavelet analysis for feature extraction and BPNN as a classifier with 93.3% accuracy. Gutiérrez-Gneccchi et al. [45] introduced a combination of wavelet transform and PNN for arrhythmia detection of eight heartbeat types with an accuracy of 92.74%. In 2020, Yang et al. [46] used a combination of parametric and visual pattern features of ECG morphology for automatic diagnosis of arrhythmia using three types of classifiers – NN, SVM, and KNN – to detect 15 types of arrhythmias. Venkatesan et al. [47] proposed arrhythmia beat classification using SVM to classify normal and abnormal subjects, achieving an accuracy of 96%. In our proposed work, the accuracy obtained is significantly higher compared to the SVM classifier. Marinho et al. [48] used a novel method for arrhythmia classification, focusing mainly on different feature extraction methods and classifications. HOS and SCM are among the techniques utilized in this work, and a Bayes classifier is used to predict five types of arrhythmias with a classification accuracy of 94.3%. Furthermore, recent technologies in machine learning models such as K-means [49], ML techniques in healthcare in the context of security and privacy [50], and explainable AI [51] have evolved. From the experimental results, it is evident that PNN performs well compared to other existing models in the literature.

## 9.6 CONCLUSION

In this work, an efficient and accurate method for the automatic classification of arrhythmia is obtained by using PNN and KNN classifiers. The initial steps, such as preprocessing and feature extraction, are discussed in detail. The advantage of using PNN classification is proven by the performance metric graph. By achieving higher accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity values, the PNN classification method is the best among the other existing methods of classification. The combinational effect of the PNN network’s computational capability and flexible nature plays an important role in achieving better performance in classification. The arrhythmia prediction accuracy of PNN can be further improved by increasing the combination of classifiers.
