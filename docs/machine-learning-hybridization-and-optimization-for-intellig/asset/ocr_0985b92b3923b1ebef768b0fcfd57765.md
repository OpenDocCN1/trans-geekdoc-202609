# 9.1 INTRODUCTION

Cardiovascular diseases are a major concern, causing a high mortality rate every year. According to a survey [1], hypertension, obesity, and diabetes are the major reasons for congestive heart failure, with 1.3–4.6 million incidents every year, according to statistics. The automated detection and classification of ECG have been broadly focused on analyzing cardiac diseases. ECG signals are used to diagnose various heart conditions, and as such, collecting and analyzing large amounts of ECG data can be valuable in identifying trends and patterns in cardiac health. Big data refers to large, complex datasets that are too big to handle using traditional data processing techniques. The analysis of ECG data poses significant technical challenges that require advanced computing tools and algorithms to process and analyze. Collecting the big data of ECG signals can involve the continuous monitoring of large numbers of patients using wearable ECG devices or other monitoring equipment. The collected data can then be stored in databases and analyzed using advanced machine learning and data analysis techniques to identify patterns and trends. One example of the use of big data in ECG signals is to analyze the data from patients with arrhythmias. By comparing the ECG signals of many patients with arrhythmias, researchers can identify commonalities in the data that can help identify specific types of arrhythmias or potential risk factors.
