[21] presented a multilayer task off-loading decision-making technique that simultaneously takes into account the following four factors: (i) choosing the task to be off-loaded, (ii) choosing the robot to off-load the work, (iii) choosing the place to off-load/perform the task, and (iv) choosing the access point for the task to be off-loaded. A paradigm for Internet-connected multi-robot networks is offered where the off-loading process can be improved by the lead robot receiving assistance from other nearby robots. A multilayer evolutionary algorithm approach is developed to handle the off-loading decision for each job as a constituent of a joint optimization problem that considers the motion of the robot, access to the network, and local collaboration.

In very recent surveys by Cai et al. and Sariff et al., the authors reviewed several research works that are presented on mobile robot path planning algorithms. In the studies [22, 23], the classification of path planning algorithms was discussed in a very detailed way. The authors explained each algorithm in individual sections, discussing the advantages and disadvantages of each approach and the workspaces to which they have been applied in past experiments. First, they went through the local path planning algorithms followed by the global path planning algorithms. They outlined all the algorithms with the workspace they can be applied to and also compared different parameters in various environments.
