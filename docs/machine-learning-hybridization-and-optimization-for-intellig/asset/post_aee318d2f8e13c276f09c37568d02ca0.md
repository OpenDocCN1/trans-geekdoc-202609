
## 3.1.1 WHY ML/AI IN COVID-19?

If we need to understand why ML algorithms are being used for COVID-19, we need to first understand how useful they can be for helping in predicting COVID-19's various factors so that we can stop another pandemic like this if it occurs again. For COVID-19, some of the approaches which involved machine learning algorithms were

- i. Identifying who has a high potential risk of getting infected;
- ii. Diagnosing affected patients;
- iii. Faster development of drugs/medicines;
- iv. Predicting the spread of the disease across the globe;
- v. Understanding the complexity of virus;
- vi. Understanding the source of virus origin and their mapping;
- vii. Predicting the next pandemic (second/third wave).

As you can see, we have enlisted many possibilities in which ML algorithms can be used to help in the identification of viruses, prediction of pandemics, isolation, and methods to eradicate the virus, although the study is at an early stage for a new problem like COVID-19, bringing in the concept of ML and its subfields can push forward research by leaps and can help to provide a faster solution to this new problem. In the above factors mentioned, each factor alone would not provide much help to research, but a combination of them would help in providing a faster solution to new problems. In the upcoming sections, this chapter will look into how a few ML algorithms that have been used have helped provide insights into the problem of the COVID-19 pandemic and how we can use the features to take precautionary measures.

## 3.1.2 CHALLENGES IN COVID-19 DATA

The major challenges in a new problem like that of COVID-19 are discussed below.

Availability of data: The availability of data for training various models is very limited as it has only been a year since COVID-19 data is made available; the low amount of data makes it hard for the model to train upon.

### 3.1.2.1 Reliability of Data

The data provided is not reliable because not everyone who was symptomatic has been tested and only those who have been tested are taken into account while those who are actually COVID-19 positive but do not have access to health care are considered not positive as they haven’t been tested. In addition to this, a chunk of data is obtained from social media due to the lack of scale available for testing and, as we know, depending on data from social media is not the best.

Even with the above issues we have to work on the model based on the data available with the assumption that the data is reliable as there aren’t alternate options for data abstraction for COVID-19.

## 3.2 BACKGROUND AND LITERATURE SURVEY

### 3.2.1 THE ORIGIN

Coronavirus, as we know, has been widespread, but before we get into its prediction, let us talk about how this pandemic occurred and changed everyone’s lives. So, before we look into how it has spread, let’s discuss the various factors affected by COVID-19, the precautions taken against it, and so on. We have to take a deeper look into how the virus originated so that we get a better idea about how to proceed. The current pandemic is caused by a coronavirus known as SARS-COV-2. Coronavirus is a large group of viruses that causes various respiratory diseases, some of which are serious, like Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome (SARS) or Middle East Respiratory Syndrome (MERS), which are known for high mortality rates and were discovered in 2003 and 2012. Coronavirus comes under four different viruses: alpha, beta, gamma, and delta. The coronavirus which is known to infect humans was in the category of alpha and beta. The coronavirus spreads among animals as well, causing respiratory diseases, but the ones which transmit between animals and humans are called zoonotic viruses and the event is known as a spill-over event.

The reason for finding the origin of the virus is that the best way to kill the virus is if it is isolated it automatically dies; moreover, we could understand the dynamics of its spread so that the public health authorities can take the necessary action to control its spread as much as possible. One of the ways to find out the origin of the virus is to look at its genetic makeup; the reason to do so is that in the case that there are viruses present of similar genetic makeup, we would be able to restrict the geographic locations of that virus and thereby control its spread. SARS-COV-2 had a genetic makeup similar to SARS-COV, isolated among the bat population, which was the reason why there were initial speculations that zoonotic events could have happened from bats to humans. We did come to know that the zoonotic event occurred but we were not able to find out the first person who got infected by this spill-over; however, the first case of coronavirus was registered on 19 December 2019 in Wuhan, China.

After the first case was registered, the genetic makeup was looked into, which, as mentioned, was similar to SARS-COV originally discovered in 2003 and isolated among bats; however, this virus occurred among bats only in a few places like Asia, Africa, the Middle East, and Europe. The reason why SARS-COV-2 was able to affect humans was that it was able to easily adapt to human cell receptors and exploit the cells.

Once the first case of COVID-19 was detected, investigations began to find the point at which spill-over had occurred so that a minimum amount of geographical restrictions would need to be put into place. As the first case was encountered in Wuhan, China, it was soon found that the epicentre for the virus was the local seafood market called Huanan Seafood Wholesale Market. The rise in cases from the people in the market, vendors and regular consumers, led to the shutdown of the market; however, it was too late, as the spread of COVID-19 had begun across China at this point. The main issue to date is that no one was able to find who was infected first and how the spill-over occurred, even though the first case was registered around December 2019. Reports are saying that it could be very much possible that the first infected case of COVID-19 was in around November of 2019, which is why it has become really hard to control the virus. Until the source of this virus is identified, there are chances of re-introduction of the virus back to the human population even after passing through this pandemic.

Even though researchers were able to figure out the origin point and tried to stop it from eventually spreading since it was a communicable disease, it spread quickly across China and to the rest of the countries across the globe.

### 3.2.2 THE SPREAD

The reason for the spread of COVID-19 across the globe was at the time the virus was first discovered, around December; a lot of people from various parts of the globe came to China to welcome and celebrate the new year, which is why Wuhan became a hotspot for the disease. The disease spread among people and, when they flew back home, they became one of the initial cases, which in turn created new hotspots in those countries.

### 3.2.2.1 China

As we mentioned previously, the outbreak of the novel coronavirus was reported on 19 December 2019 at a seafood market in Wuhan, and the first death was reported on 11 January 2020; by this time, cases were being reported across China and one or two cases across the globe. China managed to control the virus the fastest and also most effectively because of the strict restrictions in lockdown, which other countries weren’t able to follow.

### 3.2.2.2 USA

The first case from the USA was registered on 21 January and was found to have travelled to China; this led to the further spread of COVID-19 across the country, making total number of cases in the USA one of the fastest growing and causing several deaths. By the end of July, the USA was at the top in the total number of confirmed cases, and various protests took place during the pandemic, causing an increase in the case count.

### 3.2.2.3 India and Other Countries

This chapter will focus more on the effects COVID had in India and how in that situation, India was the leading country providing the maximum number of positive cases around the globe every day. The first case in India occurred on 27 January 2020 in Kerala. India tried to handle it by initially applying various lockdowns, but due to a financial crisis, the country had to start unlocking, which worked well initially but later led to a second wave.
