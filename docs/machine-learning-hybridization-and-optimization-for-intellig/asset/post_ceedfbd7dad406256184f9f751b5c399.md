## 5.2 RELATED WORK

There are many instances where AI is already very successful in helping judges to arrange bail and parole hearings. More complex architectures like RNN [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6] and LSTM [7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12] have been proposed by several researchers for processing words and sentences [13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18] and for improving query and document representation [19, 20, 21]. Most law firms in India are leveraging the benefit of deep learning technologies to make their work as efficient as possible. The use of technology for e-filing and virtual hearings saw a dramatic rise during the period of the Covid-19 pandemic. But the current advancements in the judicial field are not exclusively the result of the pandemic. The progressions of digitizing applications for Indian courts started at the beginning of 2007. The announcement of the ‘eCourt Project’ was initiated and executed in 2007. The eCourt project is the initiative taken by the Indian government to develop the National eGovernance Plan. The eCourt Project is being developed in three phases. The eCourt Project’s Phase I primarily focuses on digitization, that is, details like how to set up hardware with an internet connectivity, along with the details covering how to digitize case records; how to operate the e-Courts platform is the focus of Phase I of the project. Phase II of the eCourt Project is aimed at granting service delivery to all concerned stakeholders like litigants and lawyers by developing projects like the National Judicial Data Grid (NJDG) platform and an eCourt service app for virtual courts. Phase III of this project is to simplify court procedures and create a digital infrastructure wherein multiple diverse systems can communicate with each other, and this Phase III approach establishes a new governance framework.

In 2019, the Indian Supreme Court formed an AI expert committee to discuss and launch its first AI portal called ‘SUPACE’. This provides assistance by leveraging powerful machine learning approaches to process vast amounts of case data, and it helps in increasing the courts’ efficiency. Likewise, ‘SUVAS’, an AI-based language translation software, was launched by the Indian Supreme Court and helps in translating the original judicial documents from English into all state languages. But unfortunately each of these software programs serves only its unique purpose and there is no one-for-all software in the law industry. Several initiatives were taken by researchers [26, 27, 28, 29, 30, 31] for supporting prediction and decision-making, which will be discussed here. Also, nowadays, numerous law firms are eagerly trying out new technologies to allow quick reference of similar cases with legal concerns at stake. ‘Riverus’, a Mumbai-based legal software development company, is developing an application based on ML that reads thoroughly troves of cases to understand them better and then pares those cases that are very similar in content. The results are delivered very quickly in minimum time and the outcomes are as accurate as a human expert would achieve. One similar application by Dipankar et al. is proposed which is based on a concept of AI that serves a double purpose. One part of this application serves tax law, and the other part is used to prepare contracts. This application, once it has been trained on a huge number of historical precedents, will highlight similar key ideas in contracts.

Another intelligent software program called ‘Kira’, which is based on AI, can handle multiple tasks at a time. This application saves time and effort in analyzing and identifying legal critical papers and extracts provisions from multiple legal documents. In addition to this, another AI-driven proposed work called ‘Mitra’, which is a legal research platform, has been introduced purely for the purpose of research.

Other intelligent software has been proposed for the purpose of predicting the ideological direction of cases handled in the Supreme Court. But decisions are made mainly based on the facts of the case arguments. Third-party stakeholders or those who are very much interested in an upcoming case could obviously use this tool as a guide to assess their prospects for success by consulting a case that is very similar to their own and plan accordingly. In the proposed work, we aim to see if the combination of justice traits shows a strong preference for dealing with certain tax and civil rights issues. Topic modeling concept plays a major role in automating Supreme Court decisions. With the help of text mining, the Judge Opinion software will analyze judges’ opinions on a particular concern. Law-making software based on ML is another important development, which takes any text as input and returns the most similar congressional bills. This is implemented using the similarity model and the word embeddings model. Multilabel classification using Bert is used as an online prediction tool with a fine-grained sentiment analysis technique. To reduce the small case matters happening in court, an online dispute resolution (ODR) tool is recommended by Government as an alternative tool. These systems will directly impact the efficiency of the delivery of justice. However, such systems are not documented properly and not made public because these systems are masked due to secrecy. Therefore any framework that promotes and brings transparency in rules, internal policies, regulations, and functionalities is much warranted at the current time.

### 5.2.1 SIMILAR GLOBAL INITIATIVES TAKEN BY FOREIGN NATIONS

Technological advancements and tools are already very popular, becoming integral to justice delivery systems. Several countries like the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada, Singapore, and China have taken initiatives in the digitization process. This risk score is calculated based on different predetermined parameters.
