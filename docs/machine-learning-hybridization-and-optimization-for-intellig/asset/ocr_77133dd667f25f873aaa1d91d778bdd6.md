# TABLE 12.2 Pros and cons of all the path planning algorithms

| S. No. | Algorithm | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Particle swarm optimization | No need for high computation and exhaustive search | Will not work with dynamic and concave obstacles |
| 2 | Ant colony optimization | Finds optimal path in both simple and complex environments | Results are not that much effective |
| 3 | Glowworm swarm optimization | Better cost results | Need to enhance the coordination between multi-robots |
| 4 | Artificial bee colony optimization | Fast convergence Both exploitation and exploration | Search space limited by initial solution |
| 5 | Firefly optimization | High convergence rate Finds good optimum solution in less numbers | High computational complexity Slow convergence speed |
| 6 | Cuckoo search | Aims to speed up convergence Deals with multi-criteria optimization problems | Slow search speed Low convergence accuracy |
| 7 | Grey wolf | Easy to implement Less storage requirement and faster convergence | Easy to fall into the local optimum Convergence speed is very less |
| 8 | BAT | Accurate and efficient Used in engineering design and classification | Easy to fall in the local extremum |
| 9 | A* | Ability to identify a goal with minimal probability of collision and is straightforward to comprehend | Branching factor must be finite, and every action must have a fixed cost |
| 10 | RRT | Memory efficient and simple to understand and implement | Solution may not be optimal |
| 11 | PRM | Efficient and effective will result in a smooth path, given that the generated graph is dense | Generating an adequate number of points is necessary, as reducing the gap between points will lead to an increase in iterations |
| 12 | D* | The ability to replan a path if the path previously calculated meets any obstacle | It selects cubes and singular covers arbitrarily during test generation |
