### 20.1 INTRODUCTION

The evolution of the start-up environment's information-handling paradigms and digital data storage results in developing the latest technologies. The most crucial sector that requires restructuring with the help of modern science and technologies is a start-up. This will ease the pressure brought on by expanding the population and enabling the provision of high-quality products and services. Information and communication technologies developed in the 1990s in response to these demands increased access, the standard of almost every process, and the effectiveness [1] of start-ups. The characterization and description of terms are changing due to the daily growth in technology. Industry 4.0 is the current version. The government is nevertheless dedicated to advancing technological culture and enabling legislation [2]. Thus, while corporate management, legal framework, and training are all included in development strategies, technologies are also related. Technically speaking, this is sometimes referred to as the cyber-physical systems centered on Industry 4.0, which depends on several technologies. One such technology is the Internet of Things (IoT) [3]. The evidence of rapid technological advancement coupled with global governmental conflicts leads to the conclusion that Industry 4.0 significantly affects the start-up sector.

IoT is crucial for start-ups, their management, and future growth [4]. IoT refers to linked, wireless, or wired gadgets and things on the Internet. Kevin Ashton first used the term in 1999 to advocate the "radio frequency identification (RFID)" concept, which includes embedded "actuators and sensors." The IoT concept was first introduced to enhance supply-chain operations. However, the IoT's many features contributed to its rapid prominence in 2010. With the arrival of "wearable technology," "smart energy meters," and "connected homes," the massive boom began in 2011 [5]. IoT's explosive growth has benefited start-ups in various ways. Similar to this, IoT-enabled automated services have improved people's quality of life. Unfortunately, this unchecked growth has increased the challenges with privacy and security [6]. Lax security measures increase data leaks and other risks. Most security experts consider IoT a source of risk for cyberattacks because of lax security rules and regulations [7].

IoT-enabled devices have been used in start-ups. These businesses can gain a competitive advantage over their rivals due to these devices. Most enterprises are now extremely anxious about privacy and data breaches due to the extensive usage of smart gadgets that exchange and combine information, as they interrupt the working process, regular activities, and communication networks. To handle these threat challenges and develop detailed security rules and regulations to protect their company's assets and ensure the continuity and reliability of their services, professionals are necessary [8]. New technologies are developed daily, and old ones change as well. Take, for instance, the most recent developments in the 5G network. The IoT systems and applications are anticipated to rely on 5G heavily. Researchers are becoming more interested in potential security and privacy issues as a result of its high frequency and bandwidth.

As a result, we want to give a general outline of the security and privacy of IoT applications in start-ups. Through this action, regulatory bodies will be instructed to continue implementing laws and educating end users, enterprises, and IoT stakeholders on developing and executing better privacy and security precautions. Existing research has talked about IoT applications, privacy, and security in general, as well as supply chains, healthcare, and so on [3]. Not many studies were conducted on the privacy and security of IoT start-ups. The uniqueness of this study comes from the thorough and organized literature review it provides, which explains the state of the art for IoT applications in start-ups in terms of privacy and security.

There are five sections in this chapter. Section 20.1 explains the goal of the study. In Section 20.2, a review of prior literature is discussed. Section 20.3 discusses the approach used. The analysis of the research literature and how it is limited, and what areas need more investigation, are discussed in Sections 20.4 and 20.5. The final section of the investigation draws a conclusion.

### 20.2 LITERATURE REVIEW

#### 20.2.1 INTERNET of Things (IoT)

The term IoT was first defined by Kevin Ashton as "distinct and operational interconnected things/objects identified with RFID devices that assist enterprises manage supply-chain difficulties." Nevertheless, distinct research groups have different definitions of IoT [9, 10]. With IoT, anybody, everywhere, anytime, and anything can link to any item or thing. Researchers then come to three primary conclusions to explain the IoT to address these discrepancies. The three primary conclusions are globally connected devices, linked communication systems, and globally integrated network topologies that contain physical properties [11]. The Internet has quickly become more ubiquitous than any other technology in recorded history [7].

Its network supports the IoT. It speaks about things that are distinctive and digitally identifiable in a manner akin to the Internet. The state of network technology is evolving toward an independent, context-aware network [12]. IoT components comprise technological, social, and socio-economic factors [4]. IoT's advent has greatly impacted several industries, including businesses. In particular, it has changed business models on an industrial and small-enterprise scale [13].

To expand their market and assist businesses in implementing and improving IoT technologies more rapidly, the majority of technology companies provide IoT solutions. For organizations that did not hire engineers from diverse IoT industries, these platforms become an essential resource [14]. To completely comprehend complex and methodical business model innovation, more study of IoT in an innovative business environment is still needed [15].

IoT, which connects a variety of devices to the Internet and generates enormous amounts of data, has been evolving quickly. Big data management, storing, and analyzing the data produced by these devices have become significant challenges for enterprises with the emergence of IoT. IoT devices offer unique qualities that make managing and analyzing the data difficult, such as real-time data, high volume, and variety. Many new developments in IoT big data have been noticed in recent years, and they are changing how data is gathered, stored, processed, and evaluated.

#### 20.2.2 Start-Ups

Some start-ups have grown extremely quickly, while others are about to. To deal with the influx of new businesses and start-ups, more Silicon Valleys have been established throughout the world [16]. Start-ups are frequently viewed as newly formed businesses founded in the backyard by creative young individuals developing low-cost portable devices and high-tech gadgets. Many definitions can be used to describe start-ups [17, 18]. Start-ups are always looking for a firm notion and work in search mode, producing something out of nothing, in opposition to established companies that operate in well-known marketplaces aiming to maximize effective and optimal execution [19]. Since they develop innovative products and services and transform business models using new technology, start-ups also serve as a source of innovation. Start-ups are seen as a source of outside innovation even more by companies that use an open innovation process. As a result, start-ups and their creative methods and outcomes are inextricably linked [18].
