## 19.3 ML IN LATE YEARS

In 1985, Gerald DeJong introduced explanation-based learning, which brought historical training data to ML. This data is used to create rules based on training data and remove non-relevant data. This was also the time when exploratory data analysis (EDA) came into existence, and explanation-based learning became an integral part of ML.

Machine Learning Research Group (MLRG) was created by AT&T in 1985 as a research group for ML, which eventually evolved into NIPS, a leading conference on ML. This group tackled real-world problems and aimed to replace humans in tasks like recognizing digits, zip codes, and checking amounts.

In 1992, Jay Wilpon and a team of researchers at AT&T designed and developed automated speech recognition (ASR) using HMM. This was a significant development as it saved a tremendous amount of time and money.

In the same year, AT&T researchers also solved the problem of large classification using support vector machines (SVM). And in 1996, Patrick Haffner, a researcher at AT&T, developed convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for large-scale application recognition.

In 1997, another group of AT&T researchers developed the AdaBoost algorithm, which utilizes decision trees and unstructured data for decision classification. In that same year, Garry Kasparov lost a game of chess to the IBM supercomputer Deep Blue. This marked the first time a computer had defeated a skilled chess player, causing great alarm within the chess community. It was a historic event that showcased the superior performance of AI systems in challenging tasks. The world now understands that humanity has developed its own adversary—an artificial intelligence that can learn and evolve on its own. This signifies a miraculous turning point in machine learning.

Natural language understanding was developed by combining learning techniques such as SVM, HMM, and AdaBoost, resulting in the creation of the first interactive voice response (IVR) in 2001.

## 19.4 DEEP LEARNING AND ONWARDS

In 2006, deep learning was introduced to achieve more accurate results using neural networks. Geoffrey Hinton published "A Fast Learning Algorithm for Deep Belief Nets," the first paper on deep learning. In this paper, he demonstrated how a deep belief network could be used to train a computer to recognize patterns in photos.

The concept of deep neural networks (DNN) was introduced in 2011 by a group of researchers who were inspired by Hinton's work. DNNs were capable of being trained on millions of examples and brought significant advancements to image processing, speech recognition, and computer vision. In 2011, Google Brain, a research team focused on AI and machine learning, was established by Google. The team collaborated with other Google AI research teams, including the DeepMind team behind AlphaGo, an AI that defeated the Go world champion. Their objective was to create robots that could think rationally, interpret language, learn from facts, and respond to queries in natural language.

As of 2021, the group is led by Geoffrey Hinton, Jeff Dean, and Zoubin Ghahramani, focusing on deep learning—an artificial neural network model capable of automatically understanding complex patterns from data without explicit programming. Deep Fake, a deep learning algorithm developed by the "Meta" corporation in 2014, is a framework built on a deep neural network. It consists of layers of synthetic neurons and weights that connect each layer. The algorithm is trained on a dataset of annotated images indicating the age of each participant. The group has published numerous articles based on their productive years of study. They have also trained deep neural networks that excel in machine learning and pattern identification.

## 19.5 AREAS OF ML

In 2014, ML was introduced as a business model to Google and Facebook by ex-AT&T researchers. It was first used for customer care in 2015. AI-based social media emerged in 2017 and has become very popular. It enables brands to filter, respond, and interact with followers on social media platforms. Nowadays, ML is widely used in robotics, education, healthcare, and almost every field where it can be applied.
