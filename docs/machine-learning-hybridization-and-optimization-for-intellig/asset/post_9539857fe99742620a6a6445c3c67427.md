
Once the signal is preprocessed without any noise, the next step is to extract the features from the denoised data. A variety of feature extraction techniques are discussed in the literature. The most prominent features of the input ECG waveform are peak-to-peak intervals, peak amplitudes, and the QRS complex. By applying CWT and a neural network, a study [6] developed a new technique to detect and classify the QRS complex in ECG signals. This technique results in QRS complex detection among various morphologies by applying four wavelet basis functions. Signal denoising and feature extraction are also achieved using thresholding, resulting in 97.2% accuracy in terms of classification. In [7], a complex QRS identification with different decision rules for event vectors is implemented. Experts have proven a wavelet transformation method to extract these features accurately. To obtain the morphological features [8], DWT is applied to each heartbeat. RR interval data is used as the dynamic feature. Independent component analysis is applied for the reduction of the dimensions of DWT sub-bands, and 12 coefficients are chosen as morphological features. Principal component analysis (PCA) and dynamic time warping (DTW) are used to extract morphological features from P-QRS-T waves, and ECG features are acquired from the input ECG segment [9]; finally, support vector machine (SVM) is used over the extracted features, and the results of automated diagnosis are presented. In studies [10, 11], R-peaks are detected using a multi-resolution approach combined with adaptive thresholding. Denoising is done by removing the D1 and D2 detailed coefficients. In addition, real-time electrocardiogram wave peak detection is also possible [12]. To improve the accuracy of the current classification algorithm, categorization of an input ECG is done by fusion feature under multi-classification conditions. Multilayer CNN and RNN are used in the algorithm [13]. The most traditional and simplest method for extracting the QRS complex is discussed in [14], where the authors describe an algorithm to observe the QRS complex by enabling the input ECG signal through a sequence of processes. Primarily, the input ECG signal is passed through low-pass and high-pass filters, designed with appropriate cutoff frequencies to locate the main energy of ECG. Following the filtering process, differentiation, squaring, and averaging of the signal are performed. To complete the averaging process, a sliding window has to be chosen.

The final step in arrhythmia detection or classification is to choose the best classifier. Artificial neural network (ANN) is one such classifier that provides accurate predictions. Different types of arrhythmia classifiers are discussed in the literature [15, 16, 17]. A standard 12-lead ECG recording system and ANN-based classification technique can be implemented to identify the cardiac arrhythmia diseases discussed in the publication [18], and also to predict multiclass classification using a multilayer perceptron (MLP) model [19]. Neural networks are used to identify diseases [20]. The Levenberg–Marquardt back-propagation algorithm is utilized to train the network. The outcomes achieved are more efficient than the methods previously proposed. This study [21] investigates feature extraction parameters from ECG signals such as Poincaré plot, Lyapunov exponent, and spectral entropy. This research utilizes an artificial neural network to identify heart disease abnormalities. A novel method for input ECG signal classification and feature extraction is achieved by employing wavelet packet decomposition (WPD); the performance evaluation is done in reference to GA-BPNN and is discussed in the publication [22]. In journal [23], an automated approach is used for initial seed discovery, and the regressive learning-based neural network classifier (RLNNC) is discussed for heart disease classification. To enhance the patient output considerably in instances, the identification established from a large volume of data, particularly atrial fibrillation, myocardial infarction, cardiac arrhythmias, and electrophysiology, is discussed in [24, 25, 26, 27, 28] using a machine learning algorithm.

When the electrical movement of the heart is disrupted due to certain factors, it results in abnormal ECG waves with altered waveform characteristics. One major abnormality of the heartbeat is arrhythmia. The average resting heart rate for a person is 60–100 beats per minute (BPM). Arrhythmia is a condition where a person's heartbeat is too fast or too slow, exceeding normal levels; such conditions are categorized as tachycardia and bradycardia, respectively.

The MIT-BIH arrhythmia database used in this study was downloaded from PhysioNet ATM. PhysioNet is an open-source website that provides a range of biosignals recorded under different conditions. The signals can be downloaded at specific time intervals, as the original signals are recorded over a longer duration. The advantages of using PhysioNet ATM include its availability of a vast number of signals, user-friendliness, and flexibility in signal format. The downloaded signal can be in the required format for the work, such as Excel, CSV, MAT-file, and so on. The data used in this study can be accessed at https://archive.physionet.org/cgi-bin/atm/ATM [38]. The dataset includes 48 records of 2-channel ambulatory recorded ECG signals taken from 47 people between 1975 and 1979, and it has been inspected by the BIH Arrhythmia Lab. The signals were recorded with a sampling frequency of 360 Hz, covering a range of 10 millivolts with 11-bit resolution.

### 9.2.3 DENOISING

The raw ECG signal downloaded from the PhysioNet database will have imposed noises within it. The preprocessing of the signal is a crucial step to remove the artifacts present in the raw signal. There are many works of literature available on filtering ECG signals. The filters have to be designed by considering the frequency component required for feature extraction [29]. Vibrations and sudden movements by the subjects may cause motion artifacts, resulting in peak amplitude and duration differentiation. This type of movement during real-time ECG signal recording will lead to a baseline shift of the signal. By denoising the input ECG pulse, the quality of the ECG pulse has been enhanced and quantified using certain parameters like the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR).

For noise cancellation applications [30], adaptive filters are widely employed, which are usually designed using finite impulse response (FIR) filters. These filters make use of a minimization criterion to update the filter coefficients. Thus, the weighted sum of current and previous input samples will be the corresponding output of the filter. The NLMS adaptive filtering technique is aimed at artifact removal from ECG data.
