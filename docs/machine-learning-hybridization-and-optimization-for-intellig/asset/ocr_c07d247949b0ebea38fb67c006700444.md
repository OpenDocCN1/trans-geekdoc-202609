and so on. An innovative contribution of the present work is the use of the intelligent transfer learning technique, which clearly confirms that this multipurpose model can be implemented in several other different domains that suffer from a sparsity of data. Therefore, since most of the information in the judicial system is confidential and moreover the details about cases are highly imbalanced, the proposed two-level multipurpose frameworks are suitable for law processes that suffer from a sparsity of dataset problems. The future work mainly focuses on reporting the results of the BLTL target domain and the LSTM framework, and further discovering the likelihood of extending this architecture and fine-tuning it to other domains where data is not openly available in the judiciary court system. Undoubtedly, this dual framework will increase the competence of the justice delivery system and it will be useful for several purposes like the prediction of decisions, assessing the risk and consequences of decisions, solving disputes, managing files, document translation, legal assistance in research, automating processes, and, finally, recognizing different languages. This would help the judges and all stakeholders to conduct legal research and benefit from computer aided detection (CAD) systems.
