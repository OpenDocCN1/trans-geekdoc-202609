# 9.2.6 DWT

In this methodology, the features of the denoised signal are extracted by applying an LBP algorithm and DWT. The wavelets possess different scaling and shifting properties, and each wavelet has different sizes and shapes. The wavelet has to be chosen based on the input signal used, computational complexity, wavelet characteristics, and the required output of the application. Among the wide variety of wavelet families, the most commonly used wavelets are Haar, Daubechies, Symlet, and so on. When applying a discrete wavelet transform to the denoised signal, the Daubechies mother wavelet is chosen. As shown in Figure 9.4, the denoised signal is divided into lower and higher frequency components. At each level of decomposition, the data is down-sampled by 2 (↓2). Specifically, the significant features of the input signal are decomposed into DWT coefficients as an approximation and detailed coefficients, whose lengths are smaller than the original signal length.
