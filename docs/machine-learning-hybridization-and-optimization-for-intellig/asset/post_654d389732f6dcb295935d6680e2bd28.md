
The ECG signal has a definite pattern of five waves, where the Q, R, and S wave components together make up the QRS complex as shown in . Additionally, the ECG waveform has specific characteristics as seen in Table 9.1. Therefore, an ECG signal is a signal that has repetitive patterns of all wave components, but it varies with time. The signal that continuously varies in time must have some special filters designed to work on those variations. So, an adaptive filter is chosen; the filter parameters change according to the signal variations. This means that the filter adapts itself to the variations occurring in the ECG signal. The adaptive algorithm works by weight adjustments achieved through an adaptive control mechanism [31]. The major advantage of using adaptive denoising techniques is that high-level noise reduction can be achieved, which is difficult through direct filtering techniques. Also, the error signal used should be chosen as required by the application.

Here, the normalized least mean square adaptive filtering is chosen based on the limitations of the LMS algorithm as discussed in [32]. An adaptive filtering technique is used to apply the self-correcting leaky NLMS algorithm to the ECG signal in order to remove the EMG artifact. This adaptive filter works by adjusting its step size and leakage coefficient. The efficiency of the filter output is tested by calculating the SNR, which was found to be between 16.53 and 28.56 dB, and low mean square error for the MIT-BIH database.

The NLMS adaptive filter is used here to eliminate artifacts such as AC and DC noise present in ECG data. The overall noise cancellation is clearly shown in Figure 9.3. The filter parameters are adjusted to eliminate baseline wandering, motion artifacts, and power line interference. NLMS accomplishes this by normalizing the power of the input. ### 9.2.5 FEATURE EXTRACTION

The resultant data of the NLMS filter is a noise-free ECG with all the required information. The noise-free ECG data is always recommended for extracting features, as it plays a major role in the classification of abnormalities. It is the most important stage prior to classification. The features should be properly extracted from the denoised signal to achieve more accurate classification results.

Generally, there are various techniques designed for feature extraction from the ECG signal [34, 35, 36, 37], such as fast Fourier transform (FFT), adaptive autoregressive (AAR) parameter, multivariate AAR, bilinear AAR, PCA, ICA, genetic algorithm (GA), wavelet transform (WT), CWT, DWT, and WPD. All these methods have their own significance in feature extraction with respect to signal characteristics. Among them, PCA, ICA, WT, AR, WPD, and FFT are mostly used, considering their advantages in feature extraction from biosignals. In the proposed work, DWT and LBP are utilized to extract the dominant characteristics from the ECG signal.

### 9.2.6 DWT

In this methodology, the features of the denoised signal are extracted by applying an LBP algorithm and DWT. The wavelets possess different scaling and shifting properties, and each wavelet has different sizes and shapes. The wavelet has to be chosen based on the input signal used, computational complexity, wavelet characteristics, and the required output of the application. Among the wide variety of wavelet families, the most commonly used wavelets are Haar, Daubechies, Symlet, and so on. When applying a discrete wavelet transform to the denoised signal, the Daubechies mother wavelet is chosen. As shown in Figure 9.4, the denoised signal is divided into lower and higher frequency components. At each level of decomposition, the data is down-sampled by 2 (↓2). Specifically, the significant features of the input signal are decomposed into DWT coefficients as an approximation and detailed coefficients, whose lengths are smaller than the original signal length.

![](img/8c9c25929b8bfe55c61ba059d5462e42_242_0.png)

FIGURE 9.4 DWT using filters

In the proposed work, the input ECG signal is passed through DWT and decomposed into eight levels of approximate and detailed coefficients. By validating the frequency of each decomposed level, the db8 sub-band of the signal reflects the original ECG signal. Based on the obtained detailed and approximate wavelet coefficients, the P and R peaks of the ECG signal are extracted to estimate the RR interval and PR interval. The extracted features mentioned above are further used to train and test the dataset for classification purposes.

### 9.2.7 LBP METHOD

In LBP, the histogram features are extracted. Finally, the mean and standard deviation statistical features are extracted from LBP. The workflow of the LBP method is shown in Figure 9.5. The steps involved in extracting histogram features before classification are as follows: divide the window into cells. Compare each pixel in a cell with each of its eight neighbors. Follow the pixels in either a clockwise or counterclockwise direction around a circle. Write "1" where the value of the neighboring pixel is higher than the value of the center pixel. Otherwise, write "0" in the box. This gives us a binary number with eight digits. Calculate the histogram of the frequency of each "number" occurring in each cell. Normalize the histogram if necessary. This gives us the window's feature vector.
