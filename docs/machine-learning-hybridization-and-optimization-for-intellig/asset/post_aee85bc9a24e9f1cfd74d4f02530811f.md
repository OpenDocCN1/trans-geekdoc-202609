## 6 Analysis of Deep Learning Methodologies for Handling Non-Medical Big Data and Very Limited Medical Data with Feature Extraction and Annotation Techniques

R V Aswiga

### 6.1 INTRODUCTION

One of the prevalent and major research areas in medical imaging is diagnosis using Computer Aided Detection (CAD) techniques. This has several breakthrough records in diagnostic radiology too. These techniques require extensive big data to build reliable and accurate models. However, it is extremely difficult to collect images in huge volumes from the latest modalities as they are not available to the public. Especially in the medical domain, collecting huge amounts of data is difficult. Thus, maximizing the utilization of medical images that are very limited for the purpose of interpretation and analysis appears to be a significant field for research. This work focuses on building frameworks for the classification and annotation of images with limited medical image datasets. For classifying a limited medical dataset, the proposed model should be first trained on abundantly available non-medical big datasets. The proposed work is organized as mentioned below. Section 6.2 explains recent advancements in CAD methods and advanced machine learning techniques that have made breakthroughs in handling medical images. A thorough review of the masking, segmentation, and classification of rare as well as abundantly available medical images is presented in Section 6.3. The transfer learning concept techniques to overcome the problems that are associated with the medical domain classification as well as non-medical datasets that have insufficient training data are summarized in Section 6.4. With the advancements in feature extraction techniques and with several other advanced image processing techniques, the classification of both non-medical and medical images has been explored, and this is discussed in Section 6.5. The significance of the decoder-encoder models for generating captions for medical and non-medical images is presented in Section 6.6. Finally, Section 6.7 summarizes the issues and challenges based on the literature survey.
