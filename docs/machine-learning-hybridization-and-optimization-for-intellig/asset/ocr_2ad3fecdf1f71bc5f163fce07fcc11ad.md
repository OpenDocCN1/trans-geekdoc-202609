By combining centralized and decentralized planning [13], this model applies the concepts of collaborative progression to produce pathways which are best in terms of timing, and they also outlined the paradigm and problems associated with multispeed, map-based route planning for numerous robots. This involves the application of the evolutionary approach's wait for robot feature.

Duchoň et al. [14] worked with the mobile robot in a grid map. The necessary presumption for this is having SLAM and functional reactive movement. The A* technique is improved upon, and numerous adjustments (Basic Theta*, Phi*) are introduced. The changes are particularly concerned with computational efficiency and path maximization. Sharma et al. [15] tackled the problem of cooperative path planning for multiple robots in a warehouse setting. However, in such a multi-robot environment, these two issues are interconnected and must be addressed simultaneously to execute any operation successfully. It is crucial to manage each parameter carefully while optimizing the others. Gurji et al. [16] compared various path-finding techniques and also suggested the time-efficient A* algorithm. For both algorithms (A* and Modified A*), the percentage increase in path distance and the percentage decrease in computation are detected and measured. In this study, it is found that the updated A* approach decreases processing time by at least 65% with a maximum path length rise of 3.4%. Arora et al. [17] employed genetic algorithms for optimal path planning. The initial population consists of 20 random points. Among these, the points that fall within any barrier are identified. These points are assigned a very high destination length, rendering them unsuitable for selection. The final choice is based on the stored solutions with the lowest values. Chen et al. [18] discussed the issue of multi-robot path planning and outlined the fundamental components of a smart warehouse and also provided a graphical representation of the entire system. The genuine warehouse scenario involves consistent robot driving, so it is suggested that the architecture of the allocation column in the WHCA* method be adjusted. The persistent time of each location is reserved, and this information is used as storage.
