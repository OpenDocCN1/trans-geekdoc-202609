## 20.5 DISCUSSION

The above analysis shows the importance of privacy and security in IoT applications. This section discusses the challenges and some recent advances in privacy and security IoT application in start-ups.

### 20.5.1 IoT PRIVACY AND SECURITY NEEDS IN START-UPS

A person’s right to privacy is their ability to decide how much information they want to share with others [25]. The effective implementation of IoT at the root level depends on several significant issues. The security solutions must be portable, meaning that they can run on low-cost, low-memory, and low-processing-power devices and objects. Even still, there are various security options for confined objects, but these are only considered for standalone limited devices, not for incorporation into the IoT [25]. Some of the privacy and security requirements are the following:

- (a) User privacy: Some procedures must be observed for the data controller to avoid monitoring a user’s use of the retrieval system. Improving user security and privacy at every level is now a top priority.

- (b) Access control: The information service provider shall apply any accessibility management technique to protect data from unauthorized access and damage to a user’s private communication. Data must only be available to the people to whom it belongs.

- (c) Identity management: This is a broad administrative topic that deals with system-wide item identification utilizing a number of ways and access control by coupling user rights and restrictions to the identified identity.

- (d) Secure data communication: A significant component of IoT security is protecting data transmission. This entails preserving the identity of communication objects, ensuring the integrity and confidentiality of sent data, and validating communicating items.

- (e) Resilience to attacks: In the open and interconnected IoT environment, attackers can quickly identify and take advantage of any vulnerabilities. Any defense mechanism for the system must prevent such assaults. In the event of a node failure, it must adjust and be protected against single points of failure. It should be able to defend itself from a variety of assaults.

- (f) Trust: As trust is largely dependent on the quality of data, this is also one of the fundamental requirements for IoT. Gadget trust, institution trust, and data trust are the three components of trust.

### 20.5.2 RECENT ADVANCES IN IOT PRIVACY AND SECURITY IN START-UPS

If IoT alters how we live and work, it will depend on how we handle its security. Security issues plagued the conventional Internet, but the IoT environment created new security problems. It should be a primary goal to solve these issues and guarantee the security of IoT goods and services.

Integrating security measures with consumer approval is the start-up industry’s biggest problem. Instead of feeling that the system controls them, the user should believe that they are in charge of any information that relates to them. To the best of our knowledge, this integration creates fresh requirements that have never been considered. Security in the IoT refers to the safeguarding of networks and linked devices. IoT security guards against “malicious attacks,” “illegal access to services,” “theft or alteration of data,” “denial-of-service assaults,” “Internet risks,” and “network security.” But the IoT’s capacity to link numerous networked autonomous devices and its support for remote communication and management make it more probable that malicious attacks will proliferate [26]. IoT devices gather, process, and send private information across the network. The user must know what private information is being handled, while this data needs to be well protected from attackers. New IoT big data trends are reshaping how data is gathered, processed, stored, and evaluated. Some of the key themes influencing the IoT big data landscape are “edge computing,” “blockchain,” “5G networks,” and “cloud computing.” Businesses can get a competitive edge in the IoT market and promote innovation and growth by wisely utilizing these trends.

### 20.5.3 COUNTERMEASURES MITIGATING IOT VULNERABILITIES IN START-UPS

Designers and developers must recognize that users can be knowledgeable about the components of a system to include them in the design process. We examine precautions that can be taken well in advance of a cyberattack and what can be done. In contrast, a hacking attempt emphasizes the role users can play in safeguarding their privacy and reducing their risk. Before using an IoT device, individuals’ characteristics should be taken into account to protect their privacy and security. Users’ perceptions of their security, their willingness to take proactive steps to secure their data, and their level of trust in smart devices should all be considered by designers and developers because these aspects will influence how users engage with their gadgets.

The deployment of IoT technology depends on access and permission methods. As a result, while creating IoT systems, systems’ access to permitted requests must be considered. The effectiveness of authorization procedures depends on validating the two objects involved in the communication. “SMARTIE,” an integrative client platform for the effective yet secure distribution of IoT data in smart cities, was proposed by [26]. The authors discussed the implementation of the “IoT-ARM” to create this system. This platform’s major objective is to give consumers the power to control their access and privacy choices to manage devices. The “SMARTIE,” based on the “IoT-ARM” safety and scaling principles, offers architectural artifacts that make enforcing user access control regulations simple and effective [27] suggested security procedures to facilitate data sharing among objects and integrated it with a security architecture to improve integrated computers’ safety, trustworthiness, and anonymity. IoT devices should be able to deter attackers using intrusion detection software and other capabilities. Any unusual events or circumstances ought to eventually lead to a decline in service quality and a slow end. However, it is important to inform users of important developments. “Single-sign-on (SSO) techniques,” which require users to identify just once to interact with numerous devices, can be helpful in some IoT scenarios. Users no longer need to input numerous passwords to access all resources to have the appropriate access. IoT implementation requires trust. It includes how people experience IoT interactions. Implementing IoT-based products and services might be seriously hampered by feelings of powerlessness and being subject to unidentified external control.

The authors used real data to describe the effects of not guaranteeing IoT in [28]. They used the factory default passwords or no password to access IoT equipment accessible to the general public. According to the documented data, many of these devices were truly accessible. People could do more harm than good if they continue to employ lax security measures, such as the default password that goes with the item. If one of the devices in the network is vulnerable, hackers can attack the entire network. Privacy by design, which gives people the means to control their data, is one workable alternative. The present reality is not far from the answer. Users currently can use dynamic consent mechanisms whenever they create a data fragment, allowing specific services to acquire as little or as much of the data as requested.
