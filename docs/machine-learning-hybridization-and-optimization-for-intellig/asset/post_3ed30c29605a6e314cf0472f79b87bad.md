- Infrastructure: Today’s smart infrastructure, which includes “smarter cities,” “smart grid,” “smart buildings,” “smart homes,” “intelligent transport systems (ITS),” and “ubiquitous healthcare,” among others, requires safety as a crucial element in its infrastructure. Safety must be reliable, portable, dispersed, beneficial, and a strong facilitator for these implementations.

- Computer attacks: The most frequent dangers in an IoT environment are these assaults. Malware can be distributed through IoT devices via attacks, such as “denial of service (DoS)” attacks. Such threats may involve hacker vulnerabilities, invasions of privacy protection, or even manipulating the device’s electronic components.

- Data information: The largest hurdle for service providers in an IoT ecosystem is dealing with the vast volume of data. Big data is extremely significant and beneficial to businesses. To do that, we must overcome obstacles like storing data securely and through a method that would support IoT service providers in analyzing this data and identifying pertinent trends and patterns.

- Data interception: Cybersecurity is now available to stop communication interceptions. Implementing conventional security procedures to protect against (some) dangers, such as session kidnapping, communication protocols, and network data eavesdropping, is crucial.

- Data privacy: IoTs are expanding the scope of data gathering, processing, and evaluation procedures. The connection, the application, the system, and the gadget are among the various components that need protection when using an Internet-connected device.

- Software vulnerabilities: The security risks associated with IoT applications and software flaws are also significant. To avoid security issues, these programs must be properly examined, verified, and upgraded (regularly) (i.e., in the platform and backend).

- Technical vulnerabilities: IoT work with devices (of various types) linked to the Internet and use their tools to gather user data and store it in the cloud. Here, we must work carefully on the authentication processes to guarantee user privacy and defend user data from threats.

- Complex system: The danger to data security increases when there are more devices, users, connections, and platforms.

- Lack of common standard: There are numerous protocols for IoT devices, but there isn't one specific specification accessible.

## 20.2.4 BIBLIOMETRICS

Pritchard [20] created bibliometric analysis, which has grown in favor of a tool for thoroughly examining the scientific development of a studied subject through time [21]. Bibliometrics is a quantitative analysis technique that measures the interactions and impacts of publications in a certain field of study using mathematical and statistical methods [22]. This strategy effectively identifies influential research, authors, journals, organizations, and nations across time by providing a macroscopic picture of a large body of academic literature [23]. Bibliometrics, a powerful tool for big data processing, is frequently used to qualitatively assess the state of the discipline's development and its influence on academia [24]. As a result, the emphasis of our research is understanding the general privacy and security of IoT applications in start-ups.

## 20.3 METHODOLOGY

### 20.3.1 Study Design

A bibliometric analysis was conducted to get an overview of the privacy and security of IoT applications in start-ups.

### 20.3.2 Study Selection and Extraction

The search engine of choice was Scopus because it is the most widely used database for examining scholarly works. The study’s data was searched from Scopus to conduct a thorough analysis. The terms included TITLE-ABS-KEY (“Privacy and Security”) (7,458 documents); TITLE-ABS-KEY (“Privacy and Security” AND “IoT”) (975 documents); TITLE-ABS-KEY (“Privacy and Security” AND “Start-ups”) (6 documents); and TITLE-ABS-KEY (“Privacy and Security” AND “IoT” AND “Start-ups”) (0 documents). The term searched includes quotation marks. For bibliometric analysis, we took 981 documents with the search term TITLE-ABS-KEY (“Privacy and Security” AND “IoT” OR “Start-ups”). The range of dates was decided to be from 2003 to 2022. The following were the inclusion requirements: (i) research on “privacy and security” AND “IoT” OR “Start-ups” in the title, keywords, or abstract and (ii) English-language publications. Following this step, the study summaries were examined, duplicate articles and articles of non-relevance were eliminated, and a total of 970 publications were used in this study. The Scopus database’s “Save in other file formats” option provided all of the bibliographic data used to construct the bibliometric map. The main information of the data is shown in Table 20.1.
