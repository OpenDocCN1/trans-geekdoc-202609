IoT is crucial for start-ups, their management, and future growth [4]. IoT refers to linked, wireless, or wired gadgets and things on the Internet. Kevin Ashton first used the term in 1999 to advocate the “radio frequency identification (RFID)” concept, which includes embedded “actuators and sensors.” The IoT concept was first introduced to enhance supply-chain operations. However, the IoT’s many features contributed to its rapid prominence in 2010. With the arrival of “wearable technology,” “smart energy meters,” and “connected homes,” the massive boom began in 2011 [5]. IoT’s explosive growth has benefited start-ups in various ways. Similar to this, IoT-enabled automated services have improved peoples’ quality of life. Unfortunately, this unchecked growth has increased the challenges with privacy and security [6]. Lax security measures increase data leaks and other risks. Most security experts consider IoT a source of risk for cyberattacks because of lax security rules and regulations [7].

IoT-enabled devices have been used in start-ups. These businesses can gain a competitive advantage over their rivals due to these devices. Most enterprises are now extremely anxious about privacy and data breaches due to the extensive usage of smart gadgets that exchange and combine information, as they interrupt the working process, regular activities, and communication networks. To handle these threat challenges and develop detailed security rules and regulations to protect their company’s assets and ensure the continuity and reliability of their services, professionals are necessary [8]. New technologies are developed daily, and old ones change as well. Take, for instance, the most recent developments in the 5G network. The IoT systems and applications are anticipated to rely on 5G heavily. Researchers are becoming more interested in potential security and privacy issues as a result of its high frequency and bandwidth.
