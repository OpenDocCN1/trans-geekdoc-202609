## 5.4 EXPERIMENTS

### 5.4.1 DATASET

In the framework, the initial source domain model is validated on the basis of 25,000 ImageNet dataset images [34]. The proposed BLTL framework’s first source domain network is useful for extracting substantial features, and it is given as one of the key inputs to the LSTM model. In this chapter, only source domain details are reported, whereas in the future the target domain along with LSTM model dataset details will be reported as implementation is going on for judicial and law-related image and text datasets.

### 5.4.2 EVALUATION METRICS

For evaluating the proposed BLTL framework’s source model, the metric chosen is ‘Accuracy’. It is believed that the higher the BLTL framework accuracy, the better the model in giving the classification output and caption generation.

### 5.4.3 EXPERIMENT RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

### 5.4.3.1 BLTL framework

The 25,000 ImageNet dataset is used for feature learning at the source domain. The first source model of the BLTL framework consists of just five convolutional layers; then max pooling layers are used which in turn are followed by three dense layers. This proposed BLTL source CNN network, with adequate training and validation, results in a significant accuracy of 0.90, as demonstrated in Figure 5.3.

## 5.5 CONCLUSION

The proposed work has put forward an innovative approach to effectively design a two-level BLTL and LSTM architecture for several purposes like the generation of judicial documents, legal research prediction assistance, and so on. An innovative contribution of the present work is the use of the intelligent transfer learning technique, which clearly confirms that this multipurpose model can be implemented in several other different domains that suffer from a sparsity of data. Therefore, since most of the information in the judicial system is confidential and moreover the details about cases are highly imbalanced, the proposed two-level multipurpose frameworks are suitable for law processes that suffer from a sparsity of dataset problems. The future work mainly focuses on reporting the results of the BLTL target domain and the LSTM framework, and further discovering the likelihood of extending this architecture and fine-tuning it to other domains where data is not openly available in the judiciary court system. Undoubtedly, this dual framework will increase the competence of the justice delivery system and it will be useful for several purposes like the prediction of decisions, assessing the risk and consequences of decisions, solving disputes, managing files, document translation, legal assistance in research, automating processes, and, finally, recognizing different languages. This would help the judges and all stakeholders to conduct legal research and benefit from computer aided detection (CAD) systems.
