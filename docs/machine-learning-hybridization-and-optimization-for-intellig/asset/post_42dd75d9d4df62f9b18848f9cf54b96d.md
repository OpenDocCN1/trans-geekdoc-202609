## 3 A Brief Study on Understanding and Handling COVID-19
Test Bed for Forecasting with Deep Learning and Machine Learning Algorithms

M. Premalatha, G. Anusooya, G. Suganya, M. Braveen, and Venkatasubramanian Adhinarayanan

DOI: 10.1201/9781003465775-3

### 3.1 INTRODUCTION

The COVID-19 pandemic originated in Wuhan, China, and its first case was registered at the end of December 2019. The disease, which was initially thought to be the common flu, has become a human-killing machine that has spread across the globe and has caused an economic crash to a level that had never before been witnessed in human history. COVID-19, also named SARS-COV-2 by the World Health Organization (WHO), is a communicable disease, which means that people must stay apart from each other to stop its spread. The disease has diverse effects on the human body, initially starting with the symptoms of the common cold and headaches to the adverse effects of pneumonia and acute respiratory diseases. One of the major issues is that there could be cases that might be asymptomatic, which means a person could have COVID-19 and yet still not know that he has been infected by it, which could lead to an even faster spread of the virus among others. Since the spread of the virus had been exponential, the leaders of the country could not find any other way apart from activating lockdowns in specific regions to stop its spread as a measure of precaution. We look into various countries’ approaches to using lockdowns effectively and how they were able to control the spread of the disease. In some countries, the lockdown was not very effective, while others successfully bent the curve. Identifying which strategies from these successful countries could apply to nations with high daily case counts, like India, is crucial.

ML algorithms will be used to help in better decision-making for handling such a pandemic situation. This chapter attempted to predict future cases of COVID using ML algorithms that have been used in the prediction of various problems such as stock price prediction, cardiovascular disease prediction, breast cancer prediction, and many others. This chapter focuses on comparing various algorithms based on their performance and accuracy in the prediction of future cases.

The chapter’s main goal is to predict future cases so as to take necessary measures against the further spread of the virus and take necessary precautions. In this chapter, various machine learning algorithms, such as linear regression, lasso regression, and support vector machines; and various types of recurrent neural networks, such as LSTM, Bi-Directional LSTM, and Encoder-Decoder LSTM with the addition of univariate and multivariate forms of LSTM, were trialed for the analysis of COVID-19. This section compares the results obtained from the above models to evaluate their performance and accuracy obtained from them. A case study is performed on how various countries focus on bending the curve and considers the features that could be used to reduce the curve.

All the above-mentioned details are elaborated on in the upcoming sections. First, let’s look into data abstraction as the problem of COVID-19 is relatively new, and the places to obtain data are not abundant, followed by which we dive into the exploratory data analysis to find out how each parameter present in the table affects the forecasting of the cases and also present various visualizations, after which we look into how each model works and why we choose these models over others. Finally, we examine the features that make it possible for other countries to reduce the number of cases and remove the implications of lockdowns as much as possible within one year or less. In short, we compared the algorithms for predicting the cases and identified the features for bending the curve.
