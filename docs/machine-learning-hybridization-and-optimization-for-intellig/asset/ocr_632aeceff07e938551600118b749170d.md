# 12.2 RELATED WORK ANALYSIS

Al-Jarrah et al. [8] presented the path planning of multi-robot and collision-free coordination using a probabilistic neuro fuzzy network. The work employs subducted clustering along with least square calculation, which helps in creating fuzzy rules to understand the correlation between input and output data. The authors incorporate a leader-follower approach. A neural network was implemented to train membership function attributes, and ANFIS was used to tune fuzzy rules. Mathew et al. [9] adopted a traveling salesman problem approach with two variations including enumeration and reduction in several static warehouses. The objective of this research is to overcome the limitations of the heterogeneous delivery problem and formulate it as an optimal path planning concern. With this method, we can observe a 50% reduction in distance traveled by the truck and fuel consumption. In their study, Yang et al. resolved the collaborative path planning [10] issue using ant colony optimization (ACO). They considered the stationary obstacles in an unknown environment for simulation purposes. To enhance the confluent speed of the proposed method and smoothness of the initial path, they incorporated a multifactor heuristic function. Nasrollahy et al. addressed the problem of route planning with variable barriers in a dynamic environment. This strategy is straightforward and effective in both dynamic and static contexts and reduces overall costs [11]. This approach can be used to overcome any type of obstacle, independent of its size and shape, even if in this research, we consider the obstacles to have a circular form. In the study by Yu et al. [12], a polynomial-time algorithm called "split and group" is employed for path planning of multiple robots in well-connected environments. This approach addresses grid-based challenges and consistently produces a globally optimal schedule for all instances of the problem.
