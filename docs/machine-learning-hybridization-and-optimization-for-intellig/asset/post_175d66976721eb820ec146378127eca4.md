## 15.4 CONCLUSION

The fundamental characteristics of human communication and interaction are embodied in human language. Many people have trouble communicating verbally due to hearing loss; in these cases, sign language is used. One area of this field is research into sign language interpretation. Research into automatic sign language recognition aims to remedy this problem. In this chapter, we build a sign detector using Python’s OpenCV and Keras modules to recognize numbers between 1 and 10, with the potential for future expansion to recognize a wider range of signs and hand gestures (such as the alphabet). The project’s development cycle included three phases: (1) data creation, (2) data collection, and (3) data-driven CNN model training. During testing, this model achieved a success rate of 89.99%, while during training, it achieved a success rate of 95.98%, and during validation, it achieved a success rate of 63%. Deaf and speech-impaired people may benefit from the proposed model if it is used to develop automated editors that recognize hand gestures as input.

## 15.5 FUTURE SCOPE

The goal of this project for the sign language recognition (SLR) system is to create a method for identifying the numbers 0–9 by creating our own dataset, although it is also possible to get the dataset from the Internet, such as the MNIST dataset or ASL dataset. One way that gesture recognition might prove its worth is by facilitating effective human–machine and human–human interactions for those who have trouble hearing or speaking. This work can be extended in the future to include the recognition of alphabets and other hand motions. Specifically, the image processing part of the system needs work so that it can convert spoken language into sign language and vice versa, allowing for bidirectional interaction. A similar ideology can be implemented and extended using the clustering [22, 23]. The scope of the upcoming work also provides several opportunities to increase precision. In the future, this can be incorporated into block chains if there are sufficient numbers of large datasets available.

## 16.1 INTRODUCTION

NFTs, short for “non-fungible tokens,” are digital assets that stand in for other forms of intellectual property or creative works such as songs, paintings, games, gifs, and videos. Each NFT token is a one-of-a-kind entity representing a single thing, thus the term “non-fungible.” Tokens cannot be traded for one another. These tokens are pieces of digital information – music, video, or images – whose worth may be measured in cryptocurrency. Although both NFTs and Ethereum coins are technically a part of the Ethereum blockchain, the former are fungible, meaning they may be traded for other cryptocurrencies with the same characteristics.
