However, there is no guarantee that no challenges will arise in terms of the implementation of these technologies in the real world. The implementation of an all-in-one infrastructure for use in courts is a challenging task. The reason for this is that there would be a relatively limited level of understanding about the promising technologies, and obviously very poor ground-level training is given in such lower courts. Another critical concern that may emerge while establishing digital frameworks is that there is no robust protection law in India to safeguard data. This may cause several privacy concerns, especially when collecting and processing datasets. However, integrating AI into the Indian judiciary requires a wide-ranging framework that adopts legal, ethical, and regulatory issues. Therefore the proposed AI based all-in-one transfer learning framework helps in delivering justice, avoids decisions being biased in multiple cases, translates contracts, and analyzes different cases effectively.

Features and attributes are often missing from law documents. This is due to the fact that there is no uniform format for processing the dataset. Due to the data imbalance problem and due to infrequent law usage, prediction of such laws is often considered tricky using traditional machine learning algorithms. Transfer learning can be used in situations where there is a scarcity of data and missing values. By transferring the learned knowledge from numerous frequent cases to rare ones, the proposed work undoubtedly saves a lot of time for legal advisors by automatically increasing the understanding level of law articles for cases that they seldom deal with. The proposed work is expected to outperform most of the existing state-of-the-art few-shot prediction techniques.

This technique is called named-entity linking (NEL). Initially the proposed model can be trained on publicly available datasets.
