# ## 20.2 LITERATURE REVIEW

# ### 20.2.1 INTERNET of Things (IoT)

The term IoT was first defined by Kevin Ashton as “distinct and operational interconnected things/objects identified with RFID devices that assist enterprises manage supply-chain difficulties.” Nevertheless, distinct research groups have different definitions of IoT [9, 10]. With IoT, anybody, everywhere, anytime, and anything can link to any item or thing. Researchers then come to three primary conclusions to explain the IoT to address these discrepancies. The three primary conclusions are globally connected devices, linked communication systems, and globally integrated network topologies that contain physical properties [11]. The Internet has quickly become more ubiquitous than any other technology in recorded history [7].

Its network supports the IoT. It speaks about things that are distinctive and digitally identifiable in a manner akin to the Internet. The state of network technology is evolving toward an independent, context-aware network [12]. IoT components comprise technological, social, and socio-economic factors [4]. IoT’s advent has greatly impacted several industries, including businesses. In particular, it has changed business models on an industrial and small-enterprise scale [13].
