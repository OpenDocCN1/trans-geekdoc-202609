# ## 17.1 INTRODUCTION

The majority of today’s most popular 2D games either feature intensive visuals or else require a lot of storage space and memory to run. Having a smaller file size for a game means lesser quality visuals and gameplay. However, games with high-resolution sprites cause excessive RAM utilisation and PC overheating. It is obvious that either the CPU’s performance or the game’s quality has suffered greatly. On the other hand, games with high-quality visuals cannot be played on PCs with little RAM. If your computer does not have enough RAM, the game will run poorly with frequent pauses and frame drops. If your RAM is really low, the game might not even load. When discussing what elements are most important in making a convincing virtual environment, developers, designers, and reviewers almost universally agree that visuals and animation are at the top of the list [1]. More attention is paid to less dramatic parts of reality, such as lighting effects and shadows. The gaming industry has yet to achieve its elusive yet profitable objective of immersing players in convincing and immersive logical virtual worlds with their own physics and surroundings. Due to the gaming industry’s and the public’s fixation on cinematic and spatial realism, we may create our own implementation that mimics the cinematic behaviour of a movie while maintaining its plot.
