Joint decision-making capability in a network of UAVs allows them to manage their limited resources like battery, processing power, and so on and to improve the area of coverage. It also enables a user to control multiple robots at the same time. Joint decision-making assures the action of each UAV proves to be useful for the entire network of UAVs. Any uncoordinated behavior will result in a waste of resources and redundant coverage of the environment.

In cooperative multi-UAV searching, UAVs are typically coordinated to reduce the time to search for the location(s) of one or more fixed points. Exchanging the regional charts among each other assists the robots in understanding their surroundings and planning their routes in a better and more effective manner. Data is generated in a continuous manner and big data technologies are required to process the huge amount of generated data [3].

Cooperative searching is expressed by three parameters: (i) capturing the environment and making updates on the chart by each robot, (ii) communicating about regional data, and (iii) making collaborative judgments regarding search activities based on whatever information is available. Hence, the task of designing a network of UAVs requires intensive study and is still an open research problem.
