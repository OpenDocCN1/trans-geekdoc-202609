## 9 An Efficient and Accurate Classification Algorithm for ECG Signals Using PNN and KNN

R. Anandha Praba, L. Suganthi, and E.S. Selva Priya

### 9.1 INTRODUCTION

Cardiovascular diseases are a major concern, causing a high mortality rate every year. According to a survey [1], hypertension, obesity, and diabetes are the major reasons for congestive heart failure, with 1.3–4.6 million incidents every year, according to statistics. The automated detection and classification of ECG have been broadly focused on analyzing cardiac diseases. ECG signals are used to diagnose various heart conditions, and as such, collecting and analyzing large amounts of ECG data can be valuable in identifying trends and patterns in cardiac health. Big data refers to large, complex datasets that are too big to handle using traditional data processing techniques. The analysis of ECG data poses significant technical challenges that require advanced computing tools and algorithms to process and analyze. Collecting the big data of ECG signals can involve the continuous monitoring of large numbers of patients using wearable ECG devices or other monitoring equipment. The collected data can then be stored in databases and analyzed using advanced machine learning and data analysis techniques to identify patterns and trends. One example of the use of big data in ECG signals is to analyze the data from patients with arrhythmias. By comparing the ECG signals of many patients with arrhythmias, researchers can identify commonalities in the data that can help identify specific types of arrhythmias or potential risk factors. Another example is using big data to identify the risk of cardiac events, such as heart attacks or strokes. By analyzing large amounts of ECG data from patients with a history of cardiac events, researchers can identify patterns in the data that may be predictive of future events. Overall, collecting and analyzing the big data of ECG signals can provide valuable insights into cardiac health, potentially leading to earlier detection and better treatment of heart conditions.

With the increasing use of wearable ECG devices and other monitoring equipment, there is a growing need for advanced tools and techniques to manage and analyze this vast amount of data. By leveraging big data technologies such as machine learning, data mining, and predictive analytics, researchers and health professionals can identify patterns and trends in ECG data that can provide more accurate diagnosis and tailored treatment plans, and improve patient outcomes.

One approach to performing the test is to have a prolonged record of the cardiovascular activity during a person's typical daily activity. The major issue with long-term recording from individuals is that the ECG signal can be significantly affected by external noises, potentially resulting in incorrect data classification. To improve the signal quality, noise cancellation must be implemented through the application of signal processing filtering algorithms. Power line interference (PLI) is a major culprit that affects the quality of the acquired signal, typically falling within the range of 50–60 Hz. In addition to PLI, the signal can also be affected by high- and low- frequency noises across various frequency ranges. As previously discussed, the recorded signal can be heavily impacted by noises such as high- and low-frequency noise caused by PLI, muscle tremor, and electrode movement.

Adaptive filtering techniques work by estimating the noise present in the input ECG signal. This is achieved by conducting the filtering operation based on the provided input and estimating the noise within the input, which includes both the information source and the noise. As a result, the output produced by the adaptive filter will be free from noise and maintain the integrity of the data. The most widely accepted adaptive filtering technique is the normalized least mean square (NLMS) filter, which is a modified version of the LMS algorithm that overcomes the instability issues associated with the LMS filter. This stability is achieved by applying a normalization process to the input power [2, 3, 4, 5].
