The author Mohamed [3] analyzes the effectiveness of a machine-learning method for classifying LULC in a boreal region using data from Sentinel-2. Some of the most significant changes in the environment that occur at the landscape scale and that directly impact ecosystem processes include deforestation, increased agricultural production, and urbanization. Two satellites make up the Sentinel-2 Multispectral Instrument (MSI), which uses three different spatial resolutions to study the earth. The results produced by SVM had the greatest overall accuracy as compared to Xgboost, Random Forest, and Deep Learning. The corrected areas of the classes within a 95% confidence interval for SVM, Xgboost, and RF were comparable. In contrast to other algorithms, DL overestimated coniferous forests and open land while underestimating all other groups except for water. The four analyzed algorithms’ overall accuracies, which ranged from 0.733 to 0.758, were comparable. However, the McNemar test revealed that class-wise predictions were just 62% accurate. When classifier accuracies were compared using the Z-test, it was discovered that proposed algorithm combinations were providing better results.

In the study [4], the author proposes a methodology to evaluate changes in LULC in the Rahuri watershed basin, India, using machine learning techniques on the Google Earth Engine platform. The authors of the study make use of images from the two satellites Landsat 5 and Sentinel-2, which may be used to observe the earth’s surface features. In order to identify LULC components, satellite images are used. The authors of this study have developed a machine learning method for comprehending LULC based on satellite data. The author created maps of LULC for 2010, 2015, and 2020 using the random forest model. Overall, the three years of land use and land cover gave highly accurate results, with an overall accuracy of 85.53–94.34%. Degraded acreage increased overall from 19.42% in 2015 to 26.79% in 2020. The number of water bodies has decreased due to the construction of study facilities. In 2010, 2015, and 2020, the total amount of built-up land expanded by 34.96 ha, 37.08 ha, and 42.58 ha, respectively. In 2010, 2015, and 2020, the water bodies were seen to have reduced annually by 0.96 ha and 0.73 ha, respectively.

The work on machine learning algorithms for mapping of LULC using airborne hyperspectral images has been proposed by the authors Ozlem Akar et al. [5]. One of the most crucial applications of remote sensing is the mapping of LULC. Both spectral and spatial resolutions are necessary for LULC mapping in order to reduce the spectral ambiguity of various land cover types. Airborne hyperspectral images are one of the data types that are best for these kinds of applications due to their large number of spectral bands and ability to observe fine characteristics in the field. It is investigated as to how texture information is used to classify high-resolution hyperspectral pictures using machine learning techniques like support vector machine (SVM) and random forest (RF). The RF classification method was used to classify images that had been reduced using the DWT method to produce the soil class’s highest user accuracy of 92.86%, and the method was also used to classify images that had been reduced using the Factorial approach to produce the soil class’s greatest producer accuracy of 93.75%. For the vegetation class produced, the user accuracy was 86.13% and the producer accuracy was 92.19%.

The work [6] demonstrated a novel learning technique for enabling convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to recognize patterns in LULC and extract features to break down socioeconomic aspects. The authors developed an elementary CNN model to recognize LULC, and characteristics are extracted that describe the range of human behaviors. The input feature frame encompasses 99 channels in total, 1 from DNB/VIIRS, 6 from bands 2–7 of Landsat 8 OLI, and 1 from DEM. As training labels, the LULC type of the center pixel was retrieved from the GLC30. The identified built-up land area was 20.78% of the overall area, which was greater than the labeling map’s 13.63% but on par with the reference map’s 20.85%. The detected agricultural land area made up 26.10% of the entire region, which was less than the labeling map’s 32.45% but identical to the reference map’s 23.80%.

Vivekananda et al. [7] have used a quantitative method of change detection analysis. Each satellite image is assigned a category when using the change detection approach. The data obtained has been categorized as satellite and auxiliary information. The data was employed to create a subject-specific map of the LULC of the research area and evaluate temporal changes. The result assessment and validation employed the auxiliary data. The Landsat 8 OLI-Thermal Infrared Sensor (TIRS) data collection, path 143, and row 51 were used to produce the 2018 satellite image. The land cover classifications in the research area were examined and identified using Landsat imagery from various periods. A supervised classification method called maximum likelihood classifier (MLC) was used in the present study. The user accuracy for three categories (water bodies, developed terrain, and forest) was greater than 80%. The user accuracies in the barren and agricultural land were 68.25% and 77.77%, respectively.

Andrea et al. [8] performed a test on object-oriented classification using the combination of a simple non-iterative clustering (SNIC) algorithm and a gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) method. The machine learning techniques random forest (RF) and support vector machine (SVM) are combined. In order to perform object-oriented classification, change different parameters (such as choosing the input bands and classification algorithm, and testing various segmentation scales), and compare it to a pixel-based approach; the proposed method is implemented in a user-friendly, open-source Google Earth Engine code. Data from Landsat 8 were used in a 154 km² study area close to Lake Trasimeno (middle Italy) to carefully evaluate the proposed methodology. Various experiments on various datasets in the research region produced intriguing results.

A technique to examine the land use and land cover mapping of Shiraz, Iran, using machine learning classifiers has been developed by Jamali et al. [9]. It is important to note that recent droughts brought on by climate change have caused people to move from rural to urban areas. Iran’s population growth has, nevertheless, caused the rate of urbanization to increase. Two well-known machine learning models, complex tree (CTree) and support vector machine (SVM), were used to map the land cover in this work. Three separate scenarios using Landsat 8 images with spatial resolutions of 30 m and 15 m are established in order to examine the influence of data preprocessing on the final expected LULC maps. The DNN classifier outperformed the other supervised object-based classifiers using Landsat 8 of 15 m spatial resolution with kappa index and overall accuracies of 88.57% and 91.28%, respectively.

In order to estimate changes in LULC in Bangladesh, Abdulla-al Kafy et al. [10] have conducted a study on remote sensing methods. The LULC and LST shifting trends for 1999, 2009, and 2019 were calculated using the Landsat thermal bands and the SVM technique. The 2019 LULC and LST simulated and estimated data were used to assess the artificial neural network (ANN) and cellular automata (CA) models. An accumulated kappa value of 0.82 indicated that the CA model was accurate. The validation of the ANN model uses correlation coefficient (R) and mean square error values. With respect to the summer and winter, these numbers – 0.523 and 0.796, respectively, and 0.6023 and 0.831, respectively – provided positive results.

In order to map the LULC classes, Singh et al. [11] have presented a work to classify the Landsat images using machine learning techniques. For each state in India, LULC maps have been created, with a total area of 3,287,469 km². The map, which spanned the whole nation of India, was made using remotely sensed data, and an ML method used for Landsat images from 2005, 2006, 2007, and 2016. The accuracy across the four years ranged from 80% to 86%, which is a respectable level. A kappa statistic, or K (hat), was attained, denoting a respectably high degree of precision.

Schulz et al. [12] have conducted a study on land use mapping of the Sahel region of Niger using Sentinel-1 and -2 satellite data. An author will produce a precise, high-resolution land use map of Niamey, the capital of Niger. The authors analyzed the effectiveness of three well-known land use classifiers: support vector machine (SVM), maximum likelihood (ML), and random forest (RF). In order to take advantage of each classifier’s incentives, they developed a classifier ensemble (CE) map. When compared to the current worldwide earth cover products in the area of the study, the ensemble map was determined to be more accurate (72.9%) overall, especially when it comes to populated areas.
