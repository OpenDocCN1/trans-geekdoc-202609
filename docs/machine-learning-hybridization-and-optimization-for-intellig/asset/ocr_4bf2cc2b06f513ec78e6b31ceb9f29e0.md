There are many instances where AI is already very successful in helping judges to arrange bail and parole hearings. More complex architectures like RNN [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6] and LSTM [7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12] have been proposed by several researchers for processing words and sentences [13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18] and for improving query and document representation [19, 20, 21]. Most law firms in India are leveraging the benefit of deep learning technologies to make their work as efficient as possible. The use of technology for e-filing and virtual hearings saw a dramatic rise during the period of the Covid-19 pandemic. But the current advancements in the judicial field are not exclusively the result of the pandemic. The progressions of digitizing applications for Indian courts started at the beginning of 2007. The announcement of the ‘eCourt Project’ was initiated and executed in 2007. The eCourt project is the initiative taken by the Indian government to develop the National eGovernance Plan. The eCourt Project is being developed in three phases. The eCourt Project’s Phase I primarily focuses on digitization, that is, details like how to set up hardware with an internet connectivity, along with the details covering how to digitize case records; how to operate the e-Courts platform is the focus of Phase I of the project. Phase II of the eCourt Project is aimed at granting service delivery to all concerned stakeholders like litigants and lawyers by developing projects like the National Judicial Data Grid (NJDG) platform and an eCourt service app for virtual courts.
