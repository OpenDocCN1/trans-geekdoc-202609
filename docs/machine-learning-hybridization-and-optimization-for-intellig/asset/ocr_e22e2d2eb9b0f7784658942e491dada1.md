Wu et al. [15] have submitted research on high-resolution mapping using deep learning and object-oriented techniques. An important area of geography research is LULC. The main issues with LULC mapping brought on by deep learning (DL) are as follows: big historical data (BHD) (such as vector data) is underutilized in DL, large-scale remote sensing mapping (LSRSM) studies are uncommon, and classification of remotely sensed data based on DL frequently lacks adequate geometric limits for ground elements. Typically, indoor visual interpretation is used to annotate training and testing samples for DL rather than field surveys. The testing results of one Yangtze River Basin county show an overall accuracy of 90.20% when compared to 75.60% for structure recognition.

For the mapping of crop types, Hutt et al. [16] have proposed FOSS and open data to combine Sentinel-1 with permitted topographic and cadastral geodata. Mapping of crop-type maps with more accuracy is essential to utilize as input data for a variety of applications. Planning will be improved, and it will encourage resource use that is more sustainable. Remote sensing is the greatest way to deliver this data. Sentinel-1 satellite multitemporal microwave remote sensing images were used. A high-resolution, high-precision digital elevation model (DEM) supported the preprocessing. Prior to classification, it was used to establish the boundaries of the agricultural region and to create LULC maps that contained crop types that change annually but are often not present in official geodata. An overall accuracy of 96.7% was shown by validation utilizing an independent validation dataset with 11 different crop groups.

Accurately classifying LULC is crucial for sustainable resource management and for understanding how climate change is altering the landscape. To create LULC maps for the years 2017–2021, the ArcGIS tool has been used with classifiers of support vector machine (SVM), random forest/random tree (RF/RT), and maximum likelihood (ML).
