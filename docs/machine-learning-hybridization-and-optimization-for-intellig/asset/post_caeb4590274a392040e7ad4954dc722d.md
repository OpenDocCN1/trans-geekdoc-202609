The study for the performance analysis of several classifiers for remote sensing applications was carried out by Mahendra et al. [14]. Due to the complexity of the earth’s surface and the selection of an appropriate classification technique, it has long been a challenging task to categorize remotely sensed data on specific maps, but it is also one of the goals of contemporary remote sensing. In order to categorize the multispectral data from the Udupi district in Karnataka, India, a number of classifiers were utilized in this study, including SVM, MLC, Mahalanobis distance, and minimum distance classifier. Data with a resolution of 3980 × 3201 pixels were obtained from a Landsat 3 satellite. The outcomes show that SVM, when compared to other classifiers, will yield an accuracy of 95.35% and kappa value of 0.9408, making it a suitable option for categorizing remotely sensed data.

Wu et al. [15] have submitted research on high-resolution mapping using deep learning and object-oriented techniques. An important area of geography research is LULC. The main issues with LULC mapping brought on by deep learning (DL) are as follows: big historical data (BHD) (such as vector data) is underutilized in DL, large-scale remote sensing mapping (LSRSM) studies are uncommon, and classification of remotely sensed data based on DL frequently lacks adequate geometric limits for ground elements. Typically, indoor visual interpretation is used to annotate training and testing samples for DL rather than field surveys. The testing results of one Yangtze River Basin county show an overall accuracy of 90.20% when compared to 75.60% for structure recognition.

For the mapping of crop types, Hutt et al. [16] have proposed FOSS and open data to combine Sentinel-1 with permitted topographic and cadastral geodata. Mapping of crop-type maps with more accuracy is essential to utilize as input data for a variety of applications. Planning will be improved, and it will encourage resource use that is more sustainable. Remote sensing is the greatest way to deliver this data. Sentinel-1 satellite multitemporal microwave remote sensing images were used. A high-resolution, high-precision digital elevation model (DEM) supported the preprocessing. Prior to classification, it was used to establish the boundaries of the agricultural region and to create LULC maps that contained crop types that change annually but are often not present in official geodata. An overall accuracy of 96.7% was shown by validation utilizing an independent validation dataset with 11 different crop groups.

Basheer et al. [17] have presented a study mapping the LULC classes. Accurately classifying LULC is crucial for sustainable resource management and for understanding how climate change is altering the landscape. To create LULC maps for the years 2017–2021, the ArcGIS tool has been used with classifiers of support vector machine (SVM), random forest/random tree (RF/RT), and maximum likelihood (ML). The SVM in ArcGIS Pro specifically displays accuracy with Sentinel, Landsat, and Planet at 89%, 91%, and 94%, respectively. The SVM in Google Earth Engine displays similar outcomes, with accuracies with Landsat 8 and Sentinel 2 being 87% and 92%, respectively.

Christos et al. [19] investigated the effects of several spectral indices on the detection of LULC change using change vector analysis (CVA) in their research on the Greek island of Crete. This study’s primary objective was to assess the influence of several spectral indices on CVA’s capacity to identify LULC changes on Crete over the previous two decades. Utilizing Landsat satellite images from 1999, 2009, and 2019, these indexes were assessed. The results obtained were contrasted with precisely comprehensive visualizations of land cover produced by the supervised classification of the corresponding illustrations. According to the outcomes from the two periods under consideration, the five index combinations performed well in terms of overall accuracy and kappa index.

A method to identify the LULC change in the Lower Yenisei River has been developed by Kelsey et al. [20] in the Google Earth Engine platform using dense stacking of Landsat imagery. This study uses Landsat thick stacks covering the period of 32 years from 1985 to 2017, and 1,641 images are referred to examine LULC change in the Lower Yenisei River basin of arctic central Siberia. The random forest classification provided land cover maps for the Lower Yenisei research area, covering 11 distinct 3-year intervals over the 32-year period from 1985 to 2017. The accuracy ratings of the 11 classed maps produced kappa calculations ranging from 90.83% to 98.5%.

A technique for Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 time series object-based land cover classification using Google Earth Engine has been developed by Stromann et al. [21]. Google Earth Engine and Google Cloud Platform’s capacity for processing have been utilized by the author of this work to generate an excessive feature set. Once the significance of each feature has been identified, the effect of dimensionality reduction techniques on object-based land cover classification with support vector machines is taken into consideration. The results of the study indicated that the most prominent features, in particular Sentinel-2 NDVI, NDWI, and Sentinel-1 VV temporal means, enhanced the ability to differentiate between multiple types of urban land cover and land use. In Stockholm and Beijing, the overall accuracies of classification were 94% and 93%, respectively.

Mahendra et al. [27] have suggested using a deep convolutional neural network to evaluate Mysuru City’s vegetation cover from 2009 to 2019. The authors have performed the LULC image classification for the assessment of LULC class and vegetation cover of Mysuru City. The assessment results of the vegetation cover and other LULC classes in this work will help to carry out the development activity within the city. A convolutional neural network (CNN)-based multiple training round (CNN-MTR) deep learning model has been proposed to classify visuals from imagery collected remotely. The classified findings were examined to determine how the city’s vegetation cover had changed over a 10-year period. According to estimates, the area covered by Mysuru City Corporation’s vegetation in 2019 was expected to be 39.09%, down from 43.32% in 2009. These findings show that Mysuru City’s vegetation cover has declined by 3.43% over the past 10 years. According to estimates, the suggested CNN-MTR model’s overall classification accuracies were 95.20% in 2009 and 94.17% in 2019.
