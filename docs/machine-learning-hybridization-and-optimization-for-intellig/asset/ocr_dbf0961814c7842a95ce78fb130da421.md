Several advanced deep networks have been engaged in [21] that are very useful for extracting the shape of the liver, and then the model is fine-tuned and refined on the basis of new sets of data with structure-preserving and noise-removal techniques.

Segmenting the liver automatically based on a fusion approach is proposed by the authors in [28] by combining traditional methods that include threshold based active contour with probability density along with the neural networks, and it is tested on heterogeneous CT data. The proposed work [102] is an improved method for isolating the liver with the help of contours extracted from all three views, namely, the sagittal, coronal, and cross-section views. The method proposed by the authors [51] extracts specific ROI from the liver with the help of CNN and is tested on the computer-assisted intervention (MICCAI) 2007 grand challenge dataset. In recent times, three deep models to segment multitypes of CT abdominal images have emerged. The three models segment the liver only from high contrast CT images. The method proposed [21] extracts the liver with the help of residual networks, and it is further fine-tuned with structure-preserving components.

An advanced ensemble-based classifier [120] for predicting prognosis has been proposed recently. Different features have been successfully classified within gene expressions, and auto encoders are used to assess the outcomes of breast lesions. An Efficient Adaboost Algorithm based on advanced deep learning (DLA-EABA) for identifying and classifying breast malignancy with an efficient advanced mathematical computational technique has been proposed by the authors in [61]. In addition to the conventional computer vision approach, tumor-classifying approaches using deep convolutional networks have also been designed.

An IoT for health (IoHT) using an ML method was proposed [32] for recognizing medical images. The proposed computational intelligence framework creates folders and places all the recognized online medical images in cloud storage. The proposed approach is tested on several medical datasets, for example, for classifying strokes, for classifying malignancy, and for classifying skin-related melanocytic lesions.
