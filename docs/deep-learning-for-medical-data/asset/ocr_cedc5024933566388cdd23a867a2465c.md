# 6.3 RESULTS

Quantifying the quality of uncertainty estimates of models remains an open problem. Various methods have relative entropy (Louizos and Welling, 2017); Gal and Ghahramani, 2015a), reliability calibration, and proper scoring rules (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017). Although interesting in their right, these metrics do not directly measure a good ranking of predictions, not indicate applicability in high-risk domains. Proper scoring rules are the exception, but a model with good ranking does not necessarily exhibit good performance on proper scoring rules any score that provides relative ordering suffices and does not have to reflect well calibrated probabilities. In fact, well-ranked scores can be re-calibrated (Niculescu-Mizil and Caruana, 2005) after training to improve performance on proper scoring rules and calibration metrics.

In order to evaluate the applicability of the model in high-risk fields such as medicine, we want to quantify how models perform under a desired risk guarantee. We propose to use the Selection with Guaranteed Risk (SGR) method¹
