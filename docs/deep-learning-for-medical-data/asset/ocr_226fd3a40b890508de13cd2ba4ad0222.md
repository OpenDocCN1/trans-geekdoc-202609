In summary, the SGR method uses a confidence score, e.g. the estimated probability of the prediction, and rejects the prediction if this score is below a certain threshold. This threshold is optimized on a held-out set to the tightest possible value that still guarantees a minimal error rate with high probability (e.g. 99%). SGR is formally proven, and empirically shown, to ensure this minimal error rate under i.d. assumptions. We propose to use SGR as evaluation metric for confidence estimation methods. Specifically, on a hold-out set we measure the percentage of predictions that are accepted by SGR under a specific guarantee, ie the coverage. To illustrate, one can imagine that a trivial solution to guarantee no errors is to reject all data-points, with 0% coverage. By lowering the confidence threshold, coverage goes up, but the accuracy of the method goes down. SGR finds the optimal threshold that ensures the accuracy does not drop below a predetermined threshold, and by measuring the coverage under this method we can effectively compute the real value of a confidence estimation methods.

To limit the influence of hyper-parameters on the comparison, we use an expensive hyper-parameter scheme on all variants and baselines. Specifically we use the automated optimization method TPE (Bergstra et al., 2011) over a maximum of 100 evaluations per model. The hyper-parameters are optimized for coverage at 2% risk (δ=0.01) on fashionMNIST (Xiao et al., 2017), and we evaluate the optimal hyperparameters on another dataset, notMNIST, to measure how sensitive the hyperparameters are to a change in the data-distribution. Larger models are evaluated on Street View Housing (SVHN) (Netzer et al., 2011) using the same optimization scheme. For the details on the evaluated search space and chosen hyper-parameters we refer to the appendix.
