## Blockchain (BC)

BC refers to the process by which new transactions are encapsulated in a data block and then appended to an existing chain of previous transactions. A Blockchain can be described as an organized, solid, incremental & digital block of cryptographically connected data.

This technology is a departure from the traditional model in which a single person or entity is responsible for all the system’s data. In terms of lowered costs, increased efficiency, and improved security, this technology has completely changed how supply chains are managed.

Patient satisfaction can be improved by using a blockchain-based technique for preserving health information, which meets all of the standard requirements for a healthcare record-keeping system while improving patient access to their records and strengthening security against privacy violations. BC has several advantages, as well.

Maintaining an average healthcare information system necessitates numerous processes, including but not limited to conducting backup storage services, having recovery mechanisms in place, and ensuring that fields have the most recent information.

This is where blockchain technology comes into play. There is no single point of failure in a blockchain. Hence there is no built-in method for backup. Additionally, each blockchain node has a copy of the same data.

As a result, the healthcare ecosystem is relieved of the stress caused by the volume of data exchanged between systems. Patients take control of their healthcare data by becoming the platform they own and control. All barriers to patients obtaining copies of their medical report records or sending these to other Health Providers (HP) have been eliminated by this measure.

No single institution can be looted or hacked to get vast numbers of patient records because data is maintained on a decentralized network. The patient’s private key is required to decrypt data stored in the blockchain. In addition to using tamper-proof timestamps and account IDs, the data is also added to the blockchain using mechanisms that can detect if the data has been altered.

## BC application in the health sector

Verification of the identity of the product BC is being used. Validating a single product identifier is much simpler and faster (for example, in case of product failure). Industries have applied blockchain to manage the chain of product supply. Blockchains are making their way into the healthcare industry, where they're being used to track the flow of pharmaceuticals, clinical supplies, blood, and medical gadgets. When a product's authenticity is verified by BC, the public and private sectors are better equipped to identify products that may be fake, unauthorized, or even harmful. BC alerts and responds to inquiries.

Data storage considerations and data throughput limits would accompany a distributed blockchain containing health information, papers, or photos. Clinical and research efficiency can be significantly improved through cross-institutional exchange of data from various healthcare facilities and the continuity of care can be maintained across various healthcare facilities if providers have consistent access to patient information.

Because of this, a health care blockchain built after the Bitcoin blockchain would include a copy of each individual's health record in the United States and this would be impractical from a data storage standpoint.

Replicating all health records to every member of the network would be bandwidth expensive, wasteful of network resources, and raise data throughput concerns due to the dynamic and expansive nature of health data. In order for blockchain to benefit health care, it must act as a manager of health records and data access rights. Individuals and organizations (for example, hospitals, pharmacies, academics, and other research institutes) must be accurately and verifiably identified in order to achieve positive outcomes in the health sector.

The usage of a blockchain can help to ensure the integrity and transparency of an individual's identity, as well as battle the issue of several versions of an individual's identity. As wearable medical devices and the Internet of Things (IoT) begin to generate more data, it will become increasingly vital to ensure that these data, for example, are linked appropriately with the electronic medical records of individuals.

It's possible to share patient-generated data between institutions via the IoT. Wearable device health data might be managed by a blockchain IoT infrastructure. BC technology helps healthcare institutions to share health care data through “health information exchange networks, application programming interfaces and the usage of standards.”

An automated disseminated archive with a BC tracking component can assist manufacturers, distributors, and service providers in verifying relevant data. One area of hospitals that can profit from the irreversibility, openness, and traceability of data recorded on the blockchain is health insurance claims.

With regards to blockchain's potential in the healthcare insurance claim processing industry. Encrypted and immutable medical insurance data is provided to the insurance sector via blockchain-based medical insurance systems.

Monitoring of a patient from afar. Mobile devices, body area sensors, and IoT devices can all be used to collect medical data on patients being monitored from a distance. Biomedical data obtained via remote means can be safely stored, shared, and retrieved via blockchain technology. Blockchain has also been shown to be useful in the pharmaceutical industry.

Inadequate or counterfeit medications can have serious consequences on a patient’s well-being. A potential solution to this issue has been identified in the form of blockchain technology. The immutability of data is one of the primary goals of *Modum.io AG*, a blockchain-based start-up. This firm provides public access to the temperature records of pharmaceutical products during their transportation in order to check compliance with quality control temperature criteria.

It is possible to protect and track the supply chain of pharmaceuticals using blockchain technology. Disease patterns and their transmission mechanisms can be tracked down faster with blockchain’s unique attributes for real illness tracking and reporting. These materials can even be monitored for labor expenses and carbon dioxide emissions during their production.

When items or transactions fail to meet regulatory requirements, a safe system can alert the appropriate authorities. Managing Electronic Medical Records (EMRs), protecting healthcare data, managing Personal Health Records (PHRs), and managing Point-of-Care Genomics (POCG) and Electronic Health Records (EHRs) are five major areas in which blockchains in healthcare might be anticipated.

## BC adoption in the health sector

BC technology is being used by the healthcare sector to facilitate the sharing and use of medical data. Transactions in the health industry, such as procurement and delivering operations in distribution networks for surgical supplies and medications; and monitoring authorization and accessibility, medical information, or other health data, may also necessitate transparent and immutable record keeping.

With its everlasting, autonomous, and entirely open nature that makes it a feasible solution to health data protection, Blockchain stands out. While the system is solid, people’s personal information, such as their identification and medical records, will be kept secret using Blockchain. Constraints imposed by duplicating the blockchain among all network participants make the blockchain unsuitable for high-volume data archiving (node).

It would be a waste of time and money to store huge records on the blockchain, such as electronic medical records or genetic data sets. The inability to easily search for data within a blockchain restricts its usefulness for clinical, statistical, and research purposes.
