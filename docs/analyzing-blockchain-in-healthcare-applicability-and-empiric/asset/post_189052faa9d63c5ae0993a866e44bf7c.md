
## Insurance and disaster relief

Lockdowns and social distancing policies throughout the globe have enormous economic consequences for enterprises.

When it comes to lending money to companies, governments and financial institutions have an obligation. Traditional paper-based techniques, on the other hand, are time-consuming and ineffectual when it comes to helping a big number of enterprises. As a result, agencies are looking for solutions that are quick, dependable, and scalable.

Smart contracts on the blockchain may be used to streamline the cumbersome loan and insurance application procedures. By replacing paper-based rules with smart contracts, processing delays may be avoided entirely.

As a result, blockchain may eliminate the need for third-party facilitators. Such a method has several advantages, including less operational risk and speedier settlement for all parties.

## Sharing of health-related data of patients

When dealing with the COVID-19 pandemic, it is critical that healthcare colleagues share important data. Developing powerful data sets for COVID-19 research could be made possible by sharing data across the international research community.

National and international data-sharing regulations must be followed when using these data-sharing tools. Medical data sharing is hampered by concerns about patient privacy, which is a major roadblock to its widespread implementation and use. Controls such as the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) and data access standards are enforced by national and international agencies. Medical IoT (MIoT) devices may collect additional patient data, such as blood oxygen levels, heart rates, and prescription dosages, via the use of an integration strategy.

Healthcare data could benefit greatly from the decentralized storage provided by a blockchain. With the elimination of the costly middleman in the form of centralized databases, patients and hospitals will be able to better control their own data.

As a result, blockchain might break down the conventional silos of medical data and make the process of sharing between hospitals and medical experts throughout the nation and even across the globe substantially simpler, as well. It is possible to share real-time data using Blockchain Technology. Data forging and mutation may be avoided if MIoT data is uploaded directly to a blockchain-based system. Data collection, storage, and sharing should be more transparent to ensure that patients’ privacy is protected.

## Immigration and emigration procedures

There have been several nations that have shut down all ports of entry due to the extraordinary worldwide spread of COVID-19. These limitations will either be relaxed or entirely abolished at some point in the future. Immigration and emigration, then, will be critical in the near and distant future. According to government rules, passengers are expected to communicate their medical history and travel history and their real-time location with one another.

Furthermore, this information must be shared with immigration officials while traveling across foreign boundaries. Immigration and emigration can be handled more efficiently with the creation of cross-country data exchange systems driven by the blockchain. Immutability, control, non-repudiation, and auditability are all essential aspects of blockchain systems with stringent smart contracts that may be used in these kinds of applications. With these key features in place, an immigration ecosystem that is safe, decentralized, and collaborative can be created.

Blockchain-based immigration systems may soon become commonplace around the globe, connecting all immigration checkpoints. Such systems will be able to communicate with each other in a specified way.

## Management of the supply chain

As a result of the continuing epidemic, supply chains throughout the globe are being severely disrupted. In either case, industrial production operations are at a stop because of a lockdown mandated by law or because factories are not equipped and/or constructed to meet the new paradigm of social distance and working with limited physical touch. It has also been impacted by import and export restrictions. This year’s COVID-19 supply and demand problems have been severe, but it’s impossible to quantify just how much damage has been done.

There is either a strong demand for certain items or a large supply of certain goods. As a consequence of panic purchasing, the price of everyday necessities has risen sharply. Health care organisations are also struggling to keep up with the increased demand for medical supplies and pharmaceuticals. The development of a more robust supply chain may be aided by the use of Blockchain Technology.

The blockchain may be used to create an unreliable ecosystem by bringing together all parties without their knowledge. Immutable logs allow auditability and provenance while smart contracts give a high degree of automation and access limitations. We may expect a combination of consortium and public blockchain technologies in this specific use case.

## Secure certification and online education

Several nations have shut down their educational institutions for a lengthy period. No matter how dire things become, education, which is essential for both personal development and a nation’s progress, must continue.

There is little doubt that online education has emerged as the most feasible option, but the systems that support it are still under development and face several obstacles. In addition, there is a risk that students’ and instructors’ data will be stolen, as well as a lack of cross-platform cooperation and difficulties in ensuring students’ credentials (degrees, transcripts, and other certifications) are accurate.

Online education systems built on the blockchain have the potential to address a broad variety of these problems.

They allow for the safe cross-platform exchange of online material while also promoting uniformity among educational institutions. It is possible to design a payment system based on the precise use of content using smart contracts and tokens. Data stored on a decentralised blockchain may be more secure while still being accessible to authorised users. It is also possible to issue and share valid educational certificates in a timely, efficient, and secure manner using the blockchain.

Once a credential is uploaded to a blockchain-based system by an institution’s issuing authority, the entitled student has complete management of the credential. As a result, students have the option of granting further viewing access to recruiters, teachers, or colleges.

## Contactless delivery and automated surveillance systems

COVID-19 made us cultivate behaviors such as social distance, using facemasks, and monitoring symptoms such as a fever, cough, and trouble breathing. These new habits, on the other hand, seem to be difficult to acquire since they don’t fit with human nature.

For notifications to save many lives, a certain degree of constant and automated monitoring must be in place. Even in locations with strong transmission rates, the present scenario necessitates the use of contactless (robotic) delivery systems for necessary goods (food, medication, and so on.) to those under lockdown. This use case is well-suited for **Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs)** and robotics powered by blockchain.

Many nations now employ UAVs for various purposes, including public announcements, surveillance, medical diagnosis (such as taking a temperature), and contactless delivery of goods. Even though unmanned aerial vehicles UAVs are capable of performing complex tasks autonomously, they are vulnerable to cyberattacks. As a result of these assaults, the intended function may be altered, and/or data may be stolen. UAVs may be equipped with security, limited data access, digital payment integration, and other advantages provided by blockchain and smart contracts tailored to government or healthcare programs.

## Manufacturing management

In conjunction with COVID-19, there has been a significant increase in the demand for a number of critical commodities, including hand sanitizers and disinfectants. Due to a shortage of in-house manufacturing capacity, many businesses are turning to third parties to help them keep up with demand.

Because these third parties may not satisfy quality or sanitary compliance criteria, this raises major concerns. Furthermore, COVID-19 drugs and vaccines will be key commodities in the near future. Global demand will need the simultaneous and collaborative operation of many tiny plants and micro-manufacturing facilities.
