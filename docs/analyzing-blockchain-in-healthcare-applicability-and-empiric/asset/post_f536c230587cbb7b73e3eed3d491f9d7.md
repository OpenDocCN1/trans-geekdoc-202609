## BC in health care

A wide range of industries has now used blockchain technology to reap the benefits it offers in their professions. Although blockchain technology has been widely used in different industries, including the healthcare sector, it has earned a major following in the last few years. Much more than a technology infrastructure is required for the blockchain to work. Blockchain technology enables new ways of regulating data linkages and networks. With the rise of big international consortia and numerous interesting case studies, blockchain technology is already being used in healthcare.

One of the most obvious applications of the blockchain is in the pharmaceutical and healthcare supply chains, where data traceability and transparency are critical components. Thus, blockchain can function as an economic driver and a vehicle for social benefit. Patients will be able to access and leverage their own data with better outreach to underserved communities.

According to academic publications like Jahankhani and Kendzierskyj, “blockchain can be a significant driver of the digital revolution in healthcare. An intricate web of relationships is created throughout the entire supply chain”. However, there is an asymmetry in the way that information is shared, and as a result, network stockholders often receive changes after the fact. Due to the silos, tasks and information are often duplicative. According to Singhal and Carlton from McKinsey & Company, due to its promise to increase efficiency, blockchain has the potential to upend the healthcare supply chain. Several blockchain-based solutions were created to respond to the specific needs created by the epidemic, while others were created to expand the functionality based on existing demands (such as supply chain monitoring). Contact tracing, vaccination passports, public health surveillance, pandemic supply chains, and other use cases have all been addressed by blockchain-based solutions.

## Benefits of blockchain in the health care sector

To meet the demand for a transparent, decentralized financial system, blockchain technology was developed. For starters, blockchain ensures data integrity in real-time. As the number of transactions increases, the blockchain’s size will also increase. As a result of this exponential expansion, storage prices are increasing, and the speed at which BCs are distributed around the network is slowing down. According to Pirtle and Ehrenfeld, blockchain technology has the potential to solve some of healthcare’s interoperability problems and play a key role in putting patients at the center of the ecosystem.

Furthermore, the usage of blockchain in healthcare ensures data interoperability and security. Interoperability in healthcare refers to the capacity to share and access data across several systems. As a result of this improved interoperability, blockchain technology has the potential to improve medical services greatly.

## Blockchain technology and patient engagement

First and foremost, BC technology makes it possible to keep a complete record of all medical care provided to patients. For example, a patient's information would be readily available to their doctor if they visited a hospital in another nation while on vacation. After the patient leaves a healthcare provider, it is likely that they already have a follow-up appointment arranged for when they return home to the location where their primary care physician is situated.

Transaction records and medical records are kept in blocks in an Ethereum-based blockchain. After all essential data has been entered, finished blocks are appended to preceding blocks, and a new block is produced for the following data entry. A global network of medical equipment suppliers and buyers might use a decentralized blockchain that allows for an unlimited number of members. As new blocks are uploaded to a blockchain, the existing copy is automatically updated and synchronized across all participants. The term “node” refers to a computer or other device that stores or allows access to a blockchain copy. All participants in a blockchain network have access to all records in the blockchain. Mobile applications and remote monitoring devices are critical for patient care in today’s technological age. Numerous technologies can benefit from the use of blockchain. Cognitive-behavioral therapy for insomnia, for example, was developed and tested by a team of researchers as a mobile health (mHealth) smartphone app. Customers may be able to follow the progress of their purchases from the point of manufacture until delivery. Patients could use this to make sure they aren’t obtaining fake medications.

Data sharing and access permissions in biomedical research are being improved by using blockchain technology for patient consent and approvals. Another method to get involved is through remote monitoring. To keep track of the patient’s status, remote patient monitoring uses mobile devices, body area sensors, and IoT devices to collect medical data. BC plays a significant role in the storage of biomedical data collected remotely. Also, lately, blockchain technology has been used to store medical insurance policies and clinical studies. BC’s transparency allows for quick detection of fake data, and smart contracts on the blockchain can be utilized to safeguard clinical trial data. Patients may be able to set their own rules for how their medical records are handled and shared using blockchain technology, which is currently not possible in the existing healthcare system. Patients, for example, can make their current medication list available to all providers, so they don’t have to carry around their list of drugs or so each clinician already knows what they are taking.
