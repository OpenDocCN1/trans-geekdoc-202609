## BC

So many people now believe in BC that it is being hailed as the next big disruptive technology. Since its inception, it has been likened to the Internet and the World Wide Web. To begin with, BC is nothing more than an information-storage technique, but it has the potential to be so powerful because of its ability to facilitate asset interaction among different parties without the need for a centralized power to monitor and manage them. It is common knowledge that the BC serves as a secure archive of past transactions; it is organized in chronological chunks and dispersed across multiple servers for maximum provenance. Multipurpose technology BC is considered to be one of the most important trends of the next few years, when it comes to business and society. BC has evolved as a potentially disruptive, general-purpose technology for organizations and governments to support information transmission and transactions that necessitate trust and authentication.

Business data is increasingly being considered a potential benefit of BC technology in today’s modern-day world. Medical data interoperability is seen as a holy grail in the medical field. Unrestricted (‘permissionless’) or restricted (‘permissioned’) access to this ledger is possible in terms of reading and writing. ## Types of BC

Since each block of data is encrypted, a blockchain serves as a chronological single source of truth for all the data that it contains. There are public, private, consortium, and hybrid BCs in this system.

### Public BC

The first type of blockchain technology was public blockchain. This is where cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin were born, helping to popularize the concept of Decentralized Ledgers (DL). This type of blockchain technology can assist organizations based on transparency and trust, such as social support groups or non-governmental organizations. It eliminates the drawbacks of centralization, such as a lack of security and openness. Anyone can join a public blockchain network and use blockchain ledgers in any way they see fit. Bitcoin and Litecoin are examples of cryptocurrencies that use public blockchains. A public blockchain, which is permissionless and completely decentralized, can be joined by anybody. All nodes can access the blockchain, create new blocks of data, and validate blocks of data in public blockchains. Coin trading and mining have been the primary uses of public BC to date. Anyone with an internet connection can become a node on a public blockchain network, which is non-restrictive and permissionless. Access to current and previous data, as well as the sophisticated computations needed to verify transactions and add them to the ledger. When a public blockchain is established, it is fully independent of any one organization. As long as there are computers connecting to the public blockchain, it will continue to function.

### Private BC

Only those users who have been allowed access to the blockchain can make use of a private blockchain. Private BC, which may also be known as administered BC, is a permissioned BC system operated by a single enterprise.

A private blockchain’s nodes are chosen by a central authority. If the network works in a closed network or is controlled by a single entity, it is a private blockchain. While it leverages peer-to-peer connectivity and decentralization to operate like a public blockchain network, this sort of blockchain is substantially smaller in scale. Instead of allowing everyone to join and contribute computer power, private BCs are usually run within a business or enterprise. Additionally, the central authority does not always offer each node identical powers to fulfill duties. Because of the restrictions on public access, private BC only provides partial decentralization. Ripple, a business-to-business virtual currency exchange network, and Hyperledger, an open-source blockchain application umbrella project, are two instances of private BC. Permission levels, security, authorizations, and accessibility are all set by the controlling organization. It is possible to control who can see, contribute, or alter data in a private blockchain network; for example, it can also prohibit third parties from getting their hands on specific data.

### Hybrid BC

To meet specific business demands, a hybrid of public and private blockchains can be used. The majority of the supply chain use cases demonstrated in practice use a private blockchain environment where only known users with regulated access can exchange data.

Hybrid BCs are BCs that are controlled by a single corporation but also have a level of control provided by the public blockchain, which is required to complete certain transaction verifications. When businesses desire the best of both worlds, they can use hybrid blockchain, a type of blockchain technology that combines elements of both private and public blockchains. It enables businesses to set up a private, permission-based system alongside a public, permissionless system, allowing them to choose who has access to what data on the blockchain and what data is made public. IBM Food Trust is an example of a hybrid BC, which was created to improve efficiency across the entire food supply chain.
