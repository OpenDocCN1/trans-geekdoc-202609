Unfortunately, the absence of security safeguards necessary for these devices makes them open to hacking. Threats might include anything from the theft of a person's data and personal information to the malfunction of intra-body monitoring. They all pose a threat to those who choose to employ them. Because many IoT devices are restricted in-memory storage and processing capacity, the security systems must be able to adapt to the limited space available in these devices. However, the wireless networks to which these devices are linked are exposed to an increasing number of attacks, including Denial of Service (DoS), spoofing, and message injection. The dynamic nature of these vulnerabilities makes them more difficult to foresee than device-specific flaws. Heart rate monitors and glucose meters, as well as the signals they provide, are susceptible to assaults that might be fatal. Because of their limited resources, biomedical sensors and other medical equipment are particularly susceptible to cyberattacks and other forms of misuse. In the event of a security compromise, gadgets might fail, create misleading information or enable individuals to access the data being captured by them.

# # Applications of IoMT in the healthcare industry

The Internet of Medical Devices (IoMT) is an essential component of today's modern healthcare system, as it helps to enhance the precision, dependability, and overall effectiveness of today's electronic equipment. Combining the many medical resources and services already accessible is one way in which researchers contribute to the digitalization of the healthcare system.

In the realm of medical care, the Internet of Things is primarily concerned with the following:

+   - Standard critical processing and connection to machines, people, and data that may be presented on a computer or in the cloud;
+   - Critical treatment of grave risks to life;
+   - Medication and regular assessment of patients;
+   - Standard critical processing of life-threatening emergencies

The IoT is now being used in the healthcare industry in two distinct ways. The first step is to locate, inspect, and keep up the condition of the assets, and the second is to enhance the effectiveness of the service levels.
