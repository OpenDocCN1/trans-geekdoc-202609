E-Government: Electronic government, also known as digital government, refers to the extensive use of Information and Communications Technology (ICT) to facilitate the delivery of public and government services to a country’s population. Services, including water, gas, electricity, sewage, and other essential public utilities, payroll, tax collection, marriage and divorce, land registration, elections, and visa processing, are all examples of this. The idea of e-government envisions digitizing all or portions of these functions.

It is possible to enhance security in E-Government by strengthening integrity, immutability, secrecy, and data consistency amongst companies via blockchain. In addition, the use of this technology reduces processing delays and lowers operational costs, resulting in improved efficiency. When it comes to incorporating various operational aspects in e-government systems, blockchain’s major capabilities like as notarization, shared databases, and workflow automation come in handy.

It is feasible to identify faults and counterfeiting efforts with such methods.

Agriculture and food distribution: Agricultural and food distribution are critical to human survival and must continue to operate efficiently. This has led to a number of difficulties for farmers, suppliers, manufacturers, retailers, and consumers because of severe disease control measures such as travel bans, export restrictions, and the closure of informal labor sectors. In order to get a decent price for their crops, farmers are having difficulty selling their harvests since producers aren’t able to operate their facilities at full capacity. It is becoming more difficult for distributors to move across state and national boundaries due to the lengthy process of obtaining governmental clearance. For humanitarian organisations like the World Food Program (WFP), COVID-19 has triggered a major financial crisis and has made it impossible to maintain the food distribution cycle.
