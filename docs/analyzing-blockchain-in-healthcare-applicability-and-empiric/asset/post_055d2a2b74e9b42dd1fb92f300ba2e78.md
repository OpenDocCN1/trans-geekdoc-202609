## CHAPTER 5

Medical Device Implementation in Blockchain

## Abstract

The term “Internet of Medical Things” (IoMT) refers to a network of medical devices that may be implanted in a patient or worn by the patient in order to continuously gather medical data of the patient’s present state of health. These devices may also communicate with one another over a wireless connection. These files are significant in size, contain sensitive information, and need a high level of security because of this combination. In the context of clinical studies, Blockchain technology might be used to establish a decentralized ledger for the collection of data while also protecting the patient’s right to have their personal information kept secret. Given the recent advancements in blockchain technology, researchers are focusing their efforts on the development of blockchain-based solutions that, if successful, might provide a higher degree of security to apps used in the healthcare industry. However, due to the fact that these two systems have extremely different requirements, the integration of the two is very difficult and demanding.

## Introduction

The healthcare industry is a data-intensive clinical field that generates huge quantities of data, accesses those data, and faces major challenges because of the sensitive nature of the data and restricting factors such as privacy and security. Sharing data in a way that is Safe, Secure, and Scalable (SSS) is of the utmost importance in the diagnostic process and in the process of making connected clinical decisions in the healthcare industry and clinical frameworks. In order for clinicians to be able to share their patient’s clinical data in a manner that is acceptable and respectful of their privacy, data sharing is considered important. This helps to ensure that both participants have complete and up-to-date information regarding the health conditions of their patients.

## Synopsis of chapter

Researchers have lately shown a strong interest in enhancing the health sector as a response to human demands by digitizing and decentralizing healthcare facilities as well as offering continuous and remote medical monitoring. This desire arises at the same time when wireless communication and sensors that may be implanted or worn are undergoing rapid development. The newly created medical data are highly significant and need to be treated with the utmost care in order to prevent any sort of data manipulation from occurring. In this context, blockchain technology has emerged as the platform that provides the best degree of security while also providing the largest amount of decentralization. To name just a few of the significant capabilities that may be accessed without the participation of a third party: immutability, tamper-proof, data integrity, traceability, secrecy, and privacy are just a few examples.

*E-health and Telemedicine* are two sectors that are actively utilized to transmit clinical data to a technician located in a remote place remotely, for instance, by requesting an expert’s opinion. In recent years, telemedicine and online health care have gained a more significant foothold in the healthcare industry. These healthcare services transmit the patient’s data either via a “store-and-forward” method or real-time clinical monitoring using technologies such as telemonitoring and telemetry. Another option is to take advantage of the technology “store-and-forward.”

Patients may be diagnosed and operated remotely by medical experts if they employ these clinical services and use the clinical data that has been sent. Because of the sensitive nature of patient data, the data’s security, sensitivity, and privacy are some of the most important aspects that need to be materialized throughout all of these healthcare systems. This is only one of the many reasons, why the previously mentioned mechanisms are so vital. Therefore, the capability to convey data in a fashion that is safe, secure, and scalable is very important for the preservation of trustworthy clinical communications, especially in circumstances involving patients who are located at a great distance. Researchers have been putting in a lot of effort over the course of the past few years to develop applications for **Machine Learning (ML)**, **Artificial Intelligence (AI)**, **computer vision** and the **Internet of Things (IoT)**. These applications are intended to assist clinical practitioners and physicians in the diagnosis and treatment of a wide variety of diseases that have persisted for a considerable amount of time.

There are many different applications for blockchain technology, but one that has recently garnered notice is its use in the healthcare industry. Blockchain technology offers a broad variety of potential uses. Blockchain technology was developed to establish trust, accountability, traceability, and the integrity of data exchange, all of which are critical components of a successful healthcare system.

Safeguarding data that has been disseminated within an organization or even across national borders is a primary objective of IoT healthcare systems using blockchain technology.
