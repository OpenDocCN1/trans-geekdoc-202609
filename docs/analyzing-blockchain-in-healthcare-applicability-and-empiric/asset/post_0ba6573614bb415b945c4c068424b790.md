
## Benefits that might be derived from using the blockchain with COVID-19

Because Blockchain Technologies may be used in a variety of industries, they are rising in popularity.

Healthcare professionals are also looking at its possibilities, as are researchers. However, in 2011, a shared database for physicians, nurses, pharmacists, and other sector players was built using blockchain in the healthcare area. Because of COVID-19, several institutions and corporations throughout the globe have begun using blockchain in their solution space. This integration's primary goal is to bring together as many reliable and trustworthy data sources as possible. In the battle against COVID-19, blockchain's unique ability to continually verify data even in real-time is crucial.

Bell et al., McGhin et al., and Kumar et al. have noted the following current healthcare issues:

### Nationwide interoperability

Legacy healthcare systems often store patient data in a centralized system in pieces that prevent simple data exchange among stakeholders.

### Healthcare data interchange

There must be some kind of safeguard in place for patient data that is shared across several parties.

### Supply chain management

Stock control and authenticity are major issues in healthcare since timely delivery and authenticity are critical to treatment effectiveness.

### Mobility

Mobile technology has made it increasingly likely that patients will demand that their medical records follow suit, which means they need to be accessible in real-time from any device, no matter where they are.

### Fraudulent transactions involving electronic funds transfer

During pandemics like COVID-19, double payments and insurance frauds pose a significant danger to the health care system.

### Tracking drugs

In the medical field, patient medicine mix-ups are common, as is the existence of fake medications.

A secure end-to-end solution is provided to the end-users by blockchain, which may address the aforementioned difficulties.

## Opportunities brought by blockchain to the COVID-19 pandemic

Blockchain is a promising new technology that has a wide range of potential uses. We’ve looked at how blockchain’s unique traits may help us combat the COVID-19 epidemic.

### Decentralization

It is possible for any member of the blockchain network to see the whole network and participate in transaction verification and validation because of blockchain’s decentralized peer-to-peer architecture (P2P). In the case of the COVID-19 pandemic, the benefit of such an architecture is that it allows independent parties to work without handing over total power to a single authority. In addition, its decentralized design does not have the same vulnerability to a single point of failure as centralized systems.

Nodes in a decentralized system are not impacted if one of them goes down. A functional healthcare system is essential in these situations. If the system is unable to accomplish this, it might be fatal. The patient retains control of their data since there is no central authority to oversee it. As a result of this distributed design, data is saved using hash values rather than real patient names.

### Increased capacity

As opposed to being held on a small number of centralized servers, all of the data in P2P networks is instead distributed throughout a huge network of geographically dispersed computers. This is the primary benefit of P2P networks. These are the kinds of networks whose capacities are always expanding; once a new node is added to the network, it immediately begins adding to the overall capacity of the network as well. This additional capacity may be essential in the fight against this pandemic in order to store the large quantity of patient data that is always expanding. Because of the increased processing capacity, the time it takes to validate transactions may be cut down, allowing for a greater number of transactions to be processed per second. By assisting with run-time simulations, the computing capacity may also contribute to the development of a vaccine to combat this pandemic.

### Transparency

Transparency is something that the blockchain networks provide in a way that no other network does. In these types of networks, the ledger is distributed across all of the member nodes and may be accessed by any of them at any time.

Nugent et al. described a blockchain-based smart contracts system for medical clinical trials that were deployed on the Ethereum network. It is common for such studies to have their credibility called into question as a result of missing data, data loss as a result of being transmitted from one institution to another, or data discrediting as a result of data spying. Also, in the healthcare industry, there is a trade-off between giving openness and safeguarding the patient’s privacy due to the fact that the miner has access to a full ledger of all the information in the network.

### Autonomy

Since there’s no one central authority that governs the transactions, all of the member nodes of the blockchain are able to access the data and upload more data without any outside source keeping an eye on them. Because the patients may access the data on the network, and because they can also connect with other patients located anywhere in the world who may be afflicted with the same medical condition, this is a very beneficial resource. Patients will be able to exercise full control over their data and decide with whom they wish to share it thanks to the design, which guarantees this.

### Persistency

The blockchain network is able to swiftly verify all of the transactions, and it only permits the transactions that are legitimate to join the network. Once a transaction has been accepted into the network, the network does not let the transaction be rolled back or deleted. This quality makes it possible to make decisions more quickly and accurately, both of which are necessary for dealing with the COVID-19 scenarios. The management of the supply chain for COVID-19 vaccines may also benefit from the features of blockchain.

### Immutability

Once a record has been added to the blockchain network, it is impossible to take it out or erase it. This feature guarantees that a record of each and every transaction will be kept in the blockchain indefinitely. One of the benefits of using this strategy to combat the COVID-19 epidemic is that if a patient is sent to another doctor, the new physician will have access to the patient’s complete medical history. The 51 percent attack, on the other hand, reveals a possible weakness in this argument. During these types of assaults, a single party takes control of more than 51 percent of the total nodes, giving them full access to the network and the ability to make modifications.

Contact tracing: Symptoms of SARS-CoV-2 infection often begin to appear after 5.5 days of infection, and it is anticipated that a large number of cases go unrecognized. To limit the transmission of COVID-19, it is essential to quickly and efficiently identify all social contacts that occurred during this infectious incubation period.

To do this, use contact tracing to keep tabs on those who have been in close vicinity to an infected person for a certain period of time.

All of a person’s closest friends and family members should be notified when they are diagnosed with COVID-19. Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) technology has been used to produce a number of mobile contact tracing apps. Close interactions between mobile phones and wearables and IoT devices may be recorded thanks to BLE. This strategy, however, raises the most serious issues about data security and privacy for consumers. As a result, consumers may lose control of their data since their data is kept and processed in a centralized cloud.

Data security and privacy problems may be addressed with the use of Blockchain Technology. Blockchain can provide a decentralized database alternative that enables users to maintain complete control over their data. Patients and users may set time-bound access to their data using smart contracts. In addition, pseudo-anonymity may be used to preserve patients’ privacy.

The blockchain-based system may allow the usage of a unique digital fingerprint (public key or hash) for each patient, instead of disclosing the patient’s genuine identity.
