
- **Distribution**: Participants in the case of blockchain are physically separated from one another but are linked through a network. Each user running a node has a copy of the ledger that is updated as new transactions take place.

- **Encryption**: Blockchain captures the information in the blocks securely and semi-anonymously utilizing technologies like public and private keys. Participants can maintain their identities and other personal information private and only divulge the information required for the transaction.

- **Immutability**: Firstly, the transactions which are completed are cryptographically signed. Then they are time stamped after the cryptographical signature, and at last they are combined in the ledger. Without the participants’ consent, records cannot be altered or distorted in any other way.

- **Tokenization**: These secure exchange of value occurs during different transactions and other needful interactions occurring in the blockchain. Tokens are used to represent value, which can be anything from intangible to tangible assets. Tokens also give users the ability to manage their personal data, which is a key component of the blockchain.

- **Decentralization**: Due to a consensus mechanism, nodes in the decentralized system are responsible for maintaining both network data and the rules that govern how the network functions. Decentralization refers to the fact that no single organization is in control of all the computers, information, or rules.

## Different categories of blockchain

Nowadays, there are various forms of blockchain technology, each of which has a specific use and resolves a specific problem or group of problems.

In order to maximize the benefits, numerous businesses are employing them in their operations. While distributed technology eliminates the negative aspects of centralization, it also creates a number of new issues that must be addressed while using blockchain technology in various contexts.

It wasn't an issue at first, but the time and effort needed to solve those mathematical equations likewise increased, as the difficulty raised. ### Public blockchain

The permission-less distributed ledger technology known as a public blockchain allows anybody to join and conduct transactions. It is a non-restrictive variant in which a replica of the ledger is copied by each peer.

The Bitcoin blockchain was one of the first public blockchains to be publicly accessible to the public. It made it possible for anyone with an internet connection to conduct decentralized transactions.

Due to its decentralized structure, some mechanisms of authenticating data must be utilized. By using a consensus mechanism, users in the blockchain can agree on the ledger’s current condition. Acting nodes must perform the hard lifting, involving handling transactions, in order for the public blockchain to function. A public blockchain will quit functioning if it doesn’t have the necessary peers to solve transactions.

There are no constraints in a public blockchain. Anyone with internet access can access the network, evaluate the blocks, and then submit the transactions. Such networks often provide some type of reward to users who evaluate the blocks. You don’t need anyone’s consent to download the protocol while using public blockchain architecture. The public blockchains represent the ideal paradigm that makes this blockchain so lucrative. As a result, the system is entirely decentralized and not under the authority of any one entity. A private blockchain, however, can be modified and updated by the organization that owns it.

Public blockchain architecture has a few specific features, which are as follows:

- The ledger is accessible to each node both for reading and writing.
- Anybody can access and download nodes in the network.
- The system is entirely decentralized in its structure.
- It provides anonymity, so nobody can link your transactions to each other.
- Compared to the private blockchain, it is a little bit slower.

Some of the examples of public blockchains are NEO, Bitcoin, Litecoin, Ethereum, and so on.

### Private blockchain

Private blockchain is established and maintained by a private company that owns the consensus algorithm and mining process. The private entity determines who can access the system and download nodes. Private blockchains are excellent when a privately held firm or organization wishes to use a blockchain for internal use cases. You can use the blockchain effectively and restrict access to the blockchain network by doing this. Accessibility, authorization, and other parameters can all be established by the organization of the network. The term “permissioned blockchain” is frequently used to refer to a private blockchain.

Only the parties involved in the transaction will know about it in this type of blockchain; others won’t be able to access it, making the transaction private. The Use cases for Private Block can be seen in the following Figure 8.4:

Private blockchain architecture has a few specific features which are as follows:

- Only certain individuals or entities are permitted to view the network’s nodes.
- Only some individuals are allowed to conduct reviews and audits.
- P2P transactions are allowed and are decentralized.
- Most of them are unchangeable.

### Hybrid blockchain

Organizations sometimes utilize hybrid blockchain, a form of blockchain technology that includes components of both private and public blockchain, to get the best of both worlds. In order to regulate which data is made publicly accessible and who has rights over it, it enables enterprises to develop. In a hybrid blockchain, transactions and records are usually private but can be validated as necessary, for example by granting access via a smart contract. The hybrid blockchain may be owned by a private organization, but it cannot change transactions. Dragonchain, XinFin’s hybrid blockchain, is the type of a hybrid blockchain.

Hybrid blockchain architecture has a few specific features which are as follows:

- Works in a closed loop system without requiring that anything be made public
- Rules can be modified based on circumstances
- In addition, hybrid networks are resistant to 51% of attacks
- It prevents unauthorized access while remaining a part of a public network
- It isn't entirely transparent
- It can be difficult to upgrade to the hybrid blockchain
- Participating in and making contributions to the network are not rewarded in any way

### Consortium blockchain

When an organization needs both public and private blockchain functionality, a consortium blockchain (sometimes referred to as federated blockchains) is a novel way to address the issue. This is also similar to hybrid blockchain. A consortium blockchain essentially functions as a private blockchain with restricted access to a certain group, removing the concerns associated with having just one entity control the network on a private blockchain. The consensus processes in a blockchain consortium, blockchain are managed by predetermined nodes. It has a validator node that performs transaction initiation, receipt, and validation. Member nodes have the ability to send or receive transactions. Consortium blockchain architecture has a few specific features which are as follows:

- It offers enhanced customizability and resource management.
- Consortium blockchain are more scalable and secure.
- Additionally, it is more effective when compared to public blockchain networks.
- It provides access restrictions.
- Less transparency can be seen
- Additionally, compared to other kinds of blockchain, it is less anonymous.
