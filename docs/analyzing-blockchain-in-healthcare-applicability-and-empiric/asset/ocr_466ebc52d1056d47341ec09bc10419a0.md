# Benefits of blockchain in healthcare

In the previous sections, we have also discussed the blockchain, health care, its types, capabilities and so on. Now, in this section we will discuss the benefits of blockchain in healthcare.

+   **Decentralization:** Depending on the type of medical care, a decentralized management structure is necessary for healthcare because of the spread of stakeholders. Blockchain has the potential to become the decentralized health data management foundation that would allow all stakeholders without anybody acting as the global health data's governing body.

+   **Improved data security and privacy:** Once the data is saved to the blockchain cannot be degraded, edited, or recovered, this property of blockchain significantly enhances the security of the health data kept on it. Each and every piece of health information on the blockchain is time-stamped, encrypted, and added in chronological sequence.

+   **Health Data ownership:** Patients must be the owners of their data and in charge of how it is utilized. Patients should have the ability to notice instances of data misuse and require the confidence that their health information won’t be used improperly by third parties. Blockchain’s robust cryptographic protocols and clearly specified smart contracts help it to meet these needs.

+   **Availability and robustness:** The availability of the health data saved on blockchain is ensured since records are duplicated across numerous nodes, making the system resistant to data loss, data corruption, and some security attacks on data availability.

+   **Transparency and trust:** Blockchain end up in distributed healthcare applications because of its openness and transparency. This makes it easier for healthcare stakeholders to accept such applications.

+   **Data verifiability:** The authenticity and integrity of the records kept on blockchain can be confirmed even without access to their plaintext. This capability is especially helpful in healthcare settings where record verification is necessary, such as in the processing of insurance claims and pharmaceutical supply chains.
