# Traceability

The traceability capability provided by blockchain allows tracking a product’s provenance. It is possible to track down a product’s provenance and history through traceability. The blockchain is an immutable database to trace any asset or transaction data. The distributed ledger is the source of all transactions. The supply chain, for example, can greatly benefit from this feature.

# Distributed ledger

Distributed ledgers, often known as blockchains, are an emerging technology that may play an important role in the Industry 4.0 paradigm. Transactions generated by autonomous and cyber-physical devices can be stored on blockchains. Blockchain technology is characterized as a secure and reliable means to store and share data using distributed ledgers. It is possible to connect all supply chain entities using blockchain ledgers. Different sorts of supply chain transactions can be digitalized, such as the history of materials and products, information on shipping, financial papers, and agreements. Organizational performance can be improved through the implementation of green transportation and distribution practices. Supply chains are embracing blockchains because of lower transaction costs, improved efficiency, security, and transparency. Organizations and their supply chains confront major obstacles and will require enablers to overcome these barriers, but these motives can be significant in themselves. Based on David, here are the benefits of using a distributed ledger:

+   • Participation: “Every node participates in maintaining the record and database. Each node takes part in the validation process.”

+   • No Tempering in Data: “Each node has a copy of data; a single node cannot temper the past data. Any malicious activity is noticed easily”.

+   • Equal Privileges: “All nodes in the distributed network go through the same process of transaction verification.”

+   • Fast Response: “As the network is distributed with no central power, the transactions take less time.”
