Blockchain is one of the greatest solutions for supply chain management for businesses worldwide. First, it can effectively link all included stakeholders through a decentralized universal network, and it can securely display data from silos attributable to its property of transparency. In any industry or application, blockchain technology allows for faster data handling, faster processing, and lower risk in operations which can extensively be used by businesses that transform into digital operations post-Covid.

Blockchains use cryptography, a branch of computer science and mathematics; to sign every transaction. It includes information related to the transfer of financial assets from one individual to another, with a unique digital signature belonging to the user who initiated it. These signatures are kept private but are publicly verified. This implies that if identity A delivers an asset to identity B; anyone can verify that A transferred the asset, but they will not be able to use A's signature for carrying out their own transactions. This cryptographic method establishes complete security and accountability while avoiding any instances of identity fraud: if someone transfers money or alters any information on a blockchain network, they cannot later claim they were not a party to it or absolve themselves of responsibility.

Blockchain is thought to be the enabling technology that has the ability to radically transform practically the entire way that all businesses and economies function. By 2030, it is anticipated that blockchain would generate USD3 trillion in annual company value. According to the World Economic Forum (WEF), by 2025, 10% of global GDP will be kept on the blockchain, and blockchain is one of seven technologies expected to alter various parts of our life.

In July 2020, an international blockchain start-up, BelfricsBT established a collaborative research venture with IIIT Bangalore to integrate and build India’s first COVID-19 blockchain platform, BelYo, alongside YoSync, a business incubated at IIITB-IMACX Studios. Successful experiments like these have resulted in a positive response to the platform.

Better procurement and contract administration increased reliability, accountability, and quality control throughout supply chains, and authority being disseminated in a decentralized manner in decision-making are all possible advantages of blockchain in India. For example, blockchain has the potential to completely alter India’s agricultural sector by improving the utility function of eNAM by generating an audit trail of all farmer product transactions and thus eradicating the distrub between farmers and mandi intermediaries. Blockchain technologies can be used to investigate the origins of organic produce, thereby boosting its marketability in international markets as well.
