## The road ahead with blockchain

By 2025, blockchain is anticipated to increase firm value by $176 billion, rising to $3.1 trillion by 2030. These data demonstrate that blockchain technology has huge potential in India. As per a Gartner report, blockchain technology will be used to build new innovative enterprises, and businesses developed utilizing this advanced technology will be valued at billions of dollars. Automated customs enforcement, exchange of land records and compliance, public service delivery, health information, crypto wallet, e-sign solution, digital certificate governance, motor registrations, pharma supply chains, charity/trust donations, agriculture distribution/supply chains, smart grid management, e-notary services, and other areas hold tremendous potential for the application of blockchain technology in India and for blockchain development organizations. Existing programs like DigiLocker, ePramaan, and eSign, as well as existing infrastructure and services, can be seamlessly integrated with blockchain technology. This also will ensure that state governments will no longer need to launch separate efforts to integrate blockchain technology because these activities will now be merged at a centralized national level, igniting the next wave of information technology transformations.

Along with the numerous advantages of blockchain technology, its implementation and securing end-user computing environments present numerous problems. The most significant challenge possible is the implementation scalability, as transaction processing speeds now vary for various reasons. The scalability and performance of blockchain networks will be the primary focus; security will be a major concern, as it is with other revolutionary technologies. In contrast, blockchain development companies will continue to make ongoing attempts to produce innovative products and design models that will increase the security and accuracy of transactions and data.

Interoperability is another area of concern in the country, which is still in its initial phases but has a huge amount of potential. Another issue that requires a lot of attention and investigation is data localization. Countries have passed new legislation to ensure that data centers are located within their borders, limiting data movement beyond the country and localizing data.
