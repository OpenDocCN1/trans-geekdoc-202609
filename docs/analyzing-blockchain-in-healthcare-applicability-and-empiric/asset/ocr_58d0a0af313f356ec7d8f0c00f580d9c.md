Even if technically possible, metadata on personal health data poses privacy concerns due to its ability to reveal patient identities and the fact that the data can’t be removed. Pharmaceutical supply chain and health insurance claims are just four examples of how blockchain is being used to involve patients. Patients might use the same information on the blockchain to unlock and share their medical records with other doctors and organizations using a shared private key. Health Information Technology (HIT) could benefit from this, making it more interoperable and collaborative.

Doctors and patients both sign document releases and opt into programs and channels to support the patient’s therapy onboarding treatment. The blockchain is updated with the details of these transactions. There’s an immediate exchange of information between the insurance company and the patient’s medical practitioner via the blockchain. Also, the manufacturer has direct access to the patient data, allowing them to accept them into support programs upon request. A healthcare system that relies on a multidisciplinary team, financial resources, and payment methods can benefit from the use of blockchain technology. Healthcare data now has a higher level of integrity because of the intrinsic qualities of cryptography, public and private key access, proof-of-work, and distributed data. A drug’s journey from the maker to the patient is made much simpler with blockchain technology. As a result, the supply chain can be traced more efficiently, reducing the risk of counterfeiting drugs.

Individuals can safely communicate their personal health information with others over the blockchain using their unique credentials and encryption key. Access to their medical records and other data may be allowed for various reasons, including direct medical care, research, statistical analysis, or other relevant actors (such as social workers and researchers). You can provide others secure and auditable access to your personal health information by using a unique password and encryption key. Part of this includes allowing healthcare practitioners, service providers, and other relevant actors (such as researchers and social workers) to access and use information from your medical records and other sources.

On top of that, blockchain is being hailed as a tool for the gig economy and the sharing economy, where consumers are increasingly becoming prosumers and independent contractors and freelancers are the norm, as well as for the next generation of online labor marketplaces. When service providers aren’t bound by a central authority, blockchain platforms can allow them to offer more flexible options, and service transactions can take place in an open and transparent environment.
