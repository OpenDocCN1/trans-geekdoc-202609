In recent years, blockchain has emerged as a novel and safe technique for storing tamper-proof records. When it comes to establishing trust and trustworthiness in a situation, blockchain has been utilized and proposed in a variety of ways. Blockchain and financial transactions were the primary focus at first, but it is also increasingly being used in healthcare and governance.

A worldwide pandemic like the present lock-up and large-scale instability caused by the Corona Virus Disease (COVID-19) crisis would need a blockchain-based information management platform to monitor, store, and manage diverse data sets across many businesses and supply chain activities.

There are few things more important than medical therapy in this scenario, and that is a strong belief in information sharing. The confined community has several challenges, including cooperating, monitoring remote places, monitoring essential medical kits and pharmaceuticals, spotting suspicious instances and linkages, and monitoring patients.

Several IoT-based smart solutions have been launched to monitor and collect patient data. Despite this, IoT-based apps and cloud storage’s inherent security vulnerabilities, which may be remedied using a blockchain-based architecture, pose a substantial hurdle to adopting these technologies.
