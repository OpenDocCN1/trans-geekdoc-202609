# Private BC

Only those users who have been allowed access to the blockchain, can make use of a private blockchain. Private BC, which may also be known as administered BC, is a permissioned BC system operated by a single enterprise.

A private blockchain’s nodes are chosen by a central authority. If the network works in a closed network or is controlled by a single entity, it is a private blockchain. While it leverages peer-to-peer connectivity and decentralization to operate like a public blockchain network, this sort of blockchain is substantially smaller in scale. Instead of allowing everyone to join and contribute computer power, private BC are usually run within a business or enterprise. Additionally, the central authority does not always offer each node identical powers to fulfill duties. Because of the restrictions on public access, private BC only provides partial decentralization. Ripple, a business-to-business virtual currency exchanges network, and Hyperledger, an open-source blockchain application umbrella project, are two instances of private BC. Permission levels, security, authorizations, and accessibility are all set by controlling organization. It is possible to control who can see, contribute, or alter data in a private blockchain network, for example, it can also prohibit third parties from getting their hands on specific data.

# Hybrid BC

To meet specific business demands, a hybrid of public and private blockchains can be used. The majority of the supply chain use cases demonstrated in practice use a private blockchain environment where only known users with regulated access can exchange data.

Hybrid BCs are BCs that are controlled by a single corporation but also have a level of control provided by the public blockchain, which is required to complete certain transaction verifications. When businesses desire the best of both worlds, they can use hybrid blockchain, a type of Blockchain Technology that combines elements of both private and public blockchains. It enables businesses to set up a private, permission-based system alongside a public, permissionless system, allowing them to choose who has access to what data on the blockchain and what data is made public. IBM Food Trust is an example of a hybrid BC, which was created to improve efficiency across the entire food supply chain.
