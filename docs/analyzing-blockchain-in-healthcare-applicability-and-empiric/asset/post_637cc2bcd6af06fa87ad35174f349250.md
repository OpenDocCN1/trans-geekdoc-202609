### Blockchain

Cryptography techniques are used to record all digital transactions in chronological order in a distributed public ledger known as a "blockchain". The *blockchain genesis block* (the first block in the chain without a parent block to link to) was created when Nakamoto issued the first 50 bitcoins on January 3, 2009, and the currency was first utilized. The Bitcoin blockchain is made up of a sequence of transactions that all lead back to the first one. It’s worth emphasizing that BC maintains a transparent, decentralized data store that keeps track of every transaction from its inception. Because of its immutability, a blockchain relies on a cryptographic link between data that make changes more difficult as the chain length grows longer, as well as a decentralized network in which honest nodes are expected to outnumber potential attackers.

Indeed, blockchain technology has numerous uses in the financial and supply chains, like maintenance of engineering and healthcare systems, and the **Internet-of-things** (IoT). There are a wide variety of applications that can benefit from blockchain technology. It also provides a platform for the development of entirely new ones. It was a combination of various fields, including cryptography, distributed consensus algorithms, computer programming, mathematics, and game theory economics that first led to this breakthrough. Blockchain technology can be utilized in a variety of ways, but at its most basic level, it is a means for securely storing and exchanging data. Without a central authority, diverse agents can share asset transactions with indisputable transparency. The cryptographic operation of blockchain allows participants to securely trade value without the requirement of a middleman, hence creating trust. In a nutshell, BC technology is a means of storing data that makes it difficult or impossible to alter, hack, or cheat. The underlying principles of blockchain technology are straightforward, but the technology itself can be difficult to grasp.

### Features of BC

The unique qualities of blockchain technology, such as data provenance, transparency, **Decentralized Transaction Validation (DTV)**, and immutability, can help clinical trials overcome rigorous data management difficulties (patient recruitment, persistent monitoring, data management, and data analytics and accurate reporting). Non-localization (decentralization), security, auditability, and smart execution are all features unique to blockchain technology. As a result of blockchain technology’s features such as data immutability, data traceability, and smart contracts, trustless settings with fewer intermediaries are being created. These are the fundamental qualities of blockchain that distinguish it from other systems.

### Capacity expansion

The ability to scale a network using blockchain is one of the most important features of this new technology. It is possible to have more computing power when a large number of computers operate together. When it comes to Blockchain technology, the most notable aspect is that it raises the overall network’s capacity significantly. Because many computers are working together, the combined power is greater than that of a few centralized devices. Stanford University’s creation of a supercomputer capable of simulating protein folding for medical research is an excellent illustration of this improved computing power.

### Smart contracts

According to the definition provided by the phrase "smart contracts," these are computer programs or codes that run on the blockchain when certain circumstances are satisfied. It’s a self-executing contract, with the agreement’s terms written straight into code. To avoid the participation of an intermediary, these contracts allow transactions between two parties who may be distant or anonymous to take place. IBM explains the advantages of smart contracts as speed, efficiency, accuracy, security, and trustworthiness.

### Anonymity

Anonymity, according to Angelis et al., is an effective method of concealing users’ identities and ensuring their privacy. To keep private information secure, no central authority is required. As a result, the real-world identity cannot be determined from the transaction information, thereby protecting some privacy. However, in systems run and managed by known entities, such as private and permissioned Blockchains, identity is frequently essential. In the end, it’s crucial to keep in mind that blockchain technology has another important property dubbed anonymity.

In a blockchain network, a participant’s genuine identity is hidden from view.

- The distributed network’s participants are all identified by an address.
- Instead of a real name, this address serves as the entity’s identifier.
- The user’s identity is protected by the addresses.
