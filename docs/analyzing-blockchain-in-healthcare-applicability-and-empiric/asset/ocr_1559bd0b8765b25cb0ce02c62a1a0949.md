Healthcare interoperability is the ability of various health information technology systems (both between and among organizations) to connect data for better healthcare delivery. Foundational, structural, and semantic (vocabulary / terminology) requirements, as well as organizational ones, go into interoperability. BC technology is currently being used to assist organizations in their interoperability efforts even though standards for interoperability are increasing in use. The technology provides some protection against some sorts of attacks, but it is not completely safe. They are still vulnerable to zero-day attacks and technical flaws because they are essentially simply coded.

As a result, the system is vulnerable to social engineering, one of the most serious threats to information technology. Consequently, the technology, particularly BC in healthcare, requires the same level of information security examination as any other network or software.

According to Prewett et al. (2019), the risk is connected with developing and administering contracts to govern all blockchain-related connections. The blockchain administrator’s interactions with all of the participating nodes must be defined and managed through **Service-Level Agreements (SLAs)**, in addition to agreements for managing vendor relationships. Also keep in mind that the rights and privileges of the blockchain players can be vastly different, necessitating separate SLAs for each interaction. An effective SLA monitoring and enforcement system are essential to successful blockchain implementation. It’s difficult to get things done when senior and middle management isn’t invested.
