## Consortium BC

Consortium blockchains sometimes referred to as federated chains, are the fourth and final type of blockchain to be discussed here. In contrast, several members of an organization work together on a decentralized network. In contrast to the private BC, which is run solely and exclusively by a single corporation, consortium BCs are administered by a collection of organizations. Consequently, consortium BC has more decentralization, resulting in greater security than private BC. Establishing consortiums, on the other hand, involves a variety of problems, including logistical issues and the possibility of antitrust issues (which we will discuss further). Some supply chain members may lack the requisite technology or infrastructure to use BC tools and those who can decide that the upfront costs of digitizing their data and connecting with other healthcare providers are too expensive.

It is important to note that the hazards related to a single organization regulating the system on a private blockchain are all but eliminated by the consortium blockchain.

Nodes in a consortium blockchain have authority over the consensus methods. Validator nodes are responsible for initiating, receiving, and verifying transactions on the network. Transactions can be initiated or received by member nodes. As opposed to a public blockchain network, a consortium blockchain is more secure, scalable, and efficient. Similar to private and hybrid blockchains, it provides access controls for users. As compared to the public blockchain, a consortium’s blockchain is less transparent. BC’s own restrictions can hinder the network’s ability to function if a member node is breached. This form of blockchain can be used for healthcare payments as well. Health clinics and hospitals can form a consortium and decide which nodes will be responsible for authenticating the transactions. Research groups and food-tracking organizations might use the same model. It’s perfect for food and medical supply networks, as well as other supply chain operations.

## Characteristics of BC

Distributed consensus, secure information, traceability, verification, and openness are all essential features. Many firms, like Walmart, have incorporated BC technology into their supply chains in an effort to increase efficiency and performance.

## Auditability

There is a record of adequate information about the transactions to lead to any verification of the transactions and their validity. Bitcoin blockchain holds data about user balances based on the **Unspent Transaction Output (UTXO)** model: Any transaction must make a reference to some previously unspent transactions. When the latest data is entered into the ledger, the status of the referred unexpended transactions changes from unspendable to spendable. As a result, transactions may be easily verified and traced.

## Consensus

A consensus technique, once again, allows each member of a group to make and support a decision that benefits all members. Consensus is needed to ensure that the blockchain is just and equal. Bitcoin’s original method, known as **Proof of Work (PoW)**, requires solving a complicated mathematical challenge that, once solved, can be easily replicated in the remainder of the network’s nodes. While PoW is a highly valuable consensus technique, it requires a significant amount of computational power (hardware) to run in a reasonable length of time, making it challenging to implement in chains with high transaction rates and short transaction commit times. As a result, a number of consensus algorithms have been devised to reduce the complexity and, as a result, the computational power and time necessary to address these problems using mostly private networks. Consensus procedures are used to ensure that a majority of the network’s participants approve each new block before it is added to the blockchain.

## Decentralization

As with traditional centralized transaction systems, the central trusted agency (for example, the Central Bank) must validate each transaction, which invariably leads to high costs and performance bottlenecks at the main servers. In contrast to the centralized option, blockchain eliminates the need for a third party. Consensus techniques in the blockchain maintain data consistency on a distributed network.

## Persistency

Honest miners will not accept faulty transactions because they can be swiftly validated. Once a transaction has been recorded in the blockchain, it is nearly impossible to remove or reverse it. Invalid transactions could be spotted instantly in blocks. Because of the ledger’s decentralized design and use of cryptographic algorithms, the data saved in the chain can be retained indefinitely and cannot be modified without detection.

Due to the inherent limitations of the blockchain, anonymity cannot be guaranteed to the fullest extent. Pseudonymization or anonymization, as suitable, is delivered by cryptographic functions so that the true uniqueness of the contributors in the BC is not recognized. ## Security

A public ledger and built-in security mechanisms have made BC a popular choice in a wide range of industries. Blockchain Technology cannot function without cryptography. As a result, data saved in the blockchain and data exchanged between nodes of the network are secure. An operational blockchain requires a set of legitimate and up-to-date asymmetric keys to be used in order to function on this platform. The asymmetric key format used by different BC is not the same. All transactions in a blockchain are signed by the private key of the issuer.
