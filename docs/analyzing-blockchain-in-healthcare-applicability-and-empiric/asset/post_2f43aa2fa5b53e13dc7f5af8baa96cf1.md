## Examination of security measures used during COVID-19

The epidemic has forced several businesses and organizations to let employees work from home. As a result of the pandemic’s unexpected start, most enterprises lack an acceptable cybersecurity policy. Employees often utilize their own devices to do their duties. Malware, phishing, and **Denial-of-Service (DoS)** assaults have been on the rise due to this trend. The frequency of successful cyber-attacks has surged by 600 percent during the epidemic. Additionally, healthcare businesses are in danger of hackers. During the epidemic, there have been several ransom-motivated assaults on hospitals.

In the wake of the assault, the hospital’s management was unable to access its computer systems, which put patients in danger. To restore access, vast sums of ransom have to be paid.

Some strategies have been implemented to lessen the likelihood of such cyberattacks, including but not limited to:

### Virtual Private Network

These assaults may target company computers connected through home networks; however, Virtual Private Networks (VPN) may encrypt communications between those computers and the corporate network.

### Multi-factor authentication

This adds an extra level of safety to the system. In addition to the username and password, a one-time code sent to a mobile phone is needed to log in using multi-factor authentication.

**Awareness amongst employees:** In order to keep their workers safe, companies should provide seminars on cybersecurity procedures, such as, the significance of secure passwords.

### System updation

The most vulnerable systems are those that are no longer maintained and do not get security updates. A further layer of security may be added by updating outdated systems and firmware.

### Antivirus software

It is essential to keep antivirus databases up to date in order to combat the latest threats.

### Blockchain

Notably, the European Parliamentary Research Service has named blockchain as one of the 10 major technologies that may be used to combat COVID-19. The majority of the restrictions that are associated with centralized computing ecosystems may be eliminated thanks to Blockchain Technology, which offers a decentralized computing service architecture. The blockchain is comprised of a group of computational nodes that are linked in a Peer-To-Peer (P2P) fashion and mutually verify any transactions that are carried out inside the network.

A hash-based (cryptographic) chain of blocks is formed by the blockchain, with each block cryptographically securing a set of previously recorded transactions and being connected to the block that came before it. Blockchain is a viable answer to the healthcare industry’s growing number of data breaches and other security concerns, such as supply and supplier verification, online payments, and so on. Blockchain is a new technology that helps to keep track of transactions that cannot be tampered with, in a decentralized manner.

Patients’ personal and sensitive information may be shared in a safe and trustworthy manner thanks to this method. Decentralization, immutability, and transparency are three of the most important aspects of the blockchain. Information about transactions that have been started by one party and validated by other members of the network may be stored in a chain of blocks that are linked together logically.

The blockchain is then updated to reflect these transactions. As the chain grows, so does the security of the data it holds. The attacker has to know the cryptographic hash value of the block in question, as well as the hash value of the next block, in order to modify the contents of a block. As a result, in order to commit a cyberattack, the attacker would have to alter every subsequent block in the chain, which would take a significant amount of effort and time and be almost impossible.

All network users have access to the data contained in the blocks and their current condition, but the identities of individual users are protected by pseudonymity. To prevent unauthorized users from accessing data that is not intended for them, blockchain also offers access control. For example, physicians should only be able to access a patient’s past medical information while using the blockchain, which has an extra feature that is data permanence. All transactions are logged and may be retrieved at any time by a person with the proper permissions.

## Taxonomy of blockchain

Blockchain networks are categorized into four categories: public, private, consortium, and hybrid.

### Public blockchain

This type of blockchain provides a totally open network in which all participants may participate in the consensus process.

### Private blockchain

Every member of this blockchain network is required to have the authorization to join, and such members are referred to as “known participants.”

### Consortium blockchain

A consortium blockchain is used by more than one company. For the member organizations, it serves as a public blockchain.

### Hybrid blockchain

Hybrid blockchains incorporate the best aspects of both private and public blockchains. Consensus is achieved by a small number of predetermined nodes, similar to a private blockchain, but transactions are available to everyone on the network, similar to a public blockchain.
