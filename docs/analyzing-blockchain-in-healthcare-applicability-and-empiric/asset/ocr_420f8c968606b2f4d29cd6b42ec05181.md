Even though a private entity owns the hybrid blockchain, it is not allowed to change transactions. An individual who joins a hybrid blockchain has complete access to the network. The identification of the user is hidden from the other users until they complete a transaction. The opposite party is then made aware of their identity. One of the biggest benefits of hybrid blockchain is that, it functions in a closed environment, which means outside hackers can’t launch a 51 percent attack on the network. It also guarantees privacy but enables communication with external parties. It’s cheaper and faster than a public blockchain network, and it’s more scalable. Due to the possibility of information being protected, this sort of blockchain cannot guarantee perfect transparency. When it comes to upgrading, the process can be difficult, and there is little incentive for network members to participate or contribute. Real estate is one of the many compelling use cases for hybrid blockchain. Using a hybrid blockchain, companies can operate systems secretly while still making some info, such as listings, available to the public. Highly regulated industries like finance, can potentially benefit from employing hybrid blockchain to streamline their procedures. According to a study, a hybrid blockchain can be used to store medical records. Random third parties are unable to access the record, but smart contracts give individuals control over their own information. Governments may also utilize it to securely store and exchange private information about their populations.

# ## Consortium BC

Consortium blockchains sometimes referred to as federated or federated chains, are the fourth and final type of blockchain to be discussed here. In contrast, several members of an organization work together on a decentralized network. In contrast to the private BC, which is run solely and exclusively by a single corporation, consortium BCs are administered by a collection of organizations. Consequently, consortium BC has more decentralization, resulting in greater security than private BC. Establishing consortiums, on the other hand, involves a variety of problems, including logistical issues and the possibility of antitrust issues (which we will discuss in further). Some supply chain members may lack the requisite technology or infrastructure to use BC tools and those who can decide that the upfront costs of digitizing their data and connecting with other healthcare providers are too expensive.

It is important to note that the hazards related to a single organization regulating the system on a private blockchain are all but eliminated by the consortium blockchain.
