## Blockchain

Each and every transaction that occurs on a network of computers on the blockchain is recorded and stored in a database known as the blockchain. Backward referencing in the blockchain ensures that all of the blocks are stored sequentially. For as long as there is no ‘genesis’ block, each block on the blockchain contains the transaction data of the preceding block. The hash value of the preceding block is also included in each new block on the blockchain. Each block also contains a timestamp, the root hash value of all transactions, and a cryptographic (random) number. As far as the transaction’s root hash value is concerned, almost no modifications may be performed.

It is possible to completely modify a hash value by altering only one piece of data. Decentralization, nonrepudiation, and immutability are all features of the blockchain. The immutability of the blockchain is properly protected. Using timestamps and hash values connected with each transaction, the blockchain makes it feasible to keep track of all transactions. The fact that data on the blockchain cannot be challenged lends credence to its nondeducibility. In a decentralized system, the majority of network peers are able to authorize transactions.

## Potential applications of blockchain technology in the healthcare industry

Scalability, data privacy, and access security are the three pillars of any healthcare blockchain. It must be open to the public. Data storage considerations and data throughput limits would accompany a distributed blockchain including health information, papers, or photos. It’s not viable from a data storage perspective to have a copy of every health record, for example, on the distributed healthcare blockchain network. Health records and data will be protected by the blockchain.

Because of its increased network resiliency and supply chain security, blockchain has shown to be an adaptable solution in a variety of business areas. Some applications in the healthcare business serve to emphasize the significance of the blockchain.

When several persons require access to the same information, the benefits are obvious. There are times when a therapist or other medical practitioner is confronted with an inconsistency between an IT interface or other medical health records while treating patients with chronic conditions, for example. When anything goes wrong, it may need lengthy authentication procedures. As a result, a distributed database might provide several benefits in terms of gaining access to the same data. Using Ethereum Blockchain technology, a new startup named Gem has established Gem Health Network, which provides professionals with easy access to the same data while also resolving other operational issues. As a result, errors in medicine caused by out-of-date information will be less common in the future. In addition, the doctor-patient relationship will not be overlooked, and the treatment process will be defined by openness.

Demand for data-intensive health applications has sparked interest in blockchain-based medical research, which offers a variety of methods to track characteristics that might point to health issues. Data transactions and how personal data are exchanged are handled by a Swiss health start-up called Health Bank using a platform that is securely kept and controlled. Health Bank has become a popular tool for storing and analyzing medical data. By using blockchain technology to strengthen its potential to address challenges in the healthcare business, Health Bank may be seen as a symbol.

Electronic Health Records (EHRs) are another factor to consider when discussing the benefits of blockchain. This system is built to handle such records. Interoperability issues created by data transmitted between multiple business partners may be solved using blockchain technology. Security issues arise as a result of the increased use of patient data. Data sharing may benefit significantly from the implementation of a new strategy, such as one based on blockchain technology. Interoperability has the potential to improve communication and cut down on administrative time. Hospitals and private clinics gain the most economically from a well-organized healthcare delivery system. Due to the sheer volume of data, compiling it all might be a challenge. Using blockchain technology, all of the relevant information is securely stored. Doctors will have a better understanding of a patient's medical history and have easier access to a greater volume of data this way. In addition to the patient's identity, this technology provides another benefit. The Public-Key Infrastructure (PKI) is used to identify transactions on the blockchain.

## The Internet of Medical Things and blockchain

Blockchain-enabled IoMT architecture has four layers. As you go from bottom to top, these layers look like this:

- The medical gadgets, sensors, RFID tags, scanners, and other equipment utilized in the medical profession are part of the device layer, which is the first layer.
- Nodes installed at IoT access points collect data using edge computing. A blockchain network controls the various resources at each tier.
- Artificial intelligence, deep learning algorithms, cloud computing, and data storage servers are all examples of data analytics.
- The IoT powered by blockchain connects all of the data on the blockchain.

## Challenges faced by the Internet of Medical Things and blockchain

When it comes to the safety of the internet of medical things, the use of blockchain technology is unquestionably advantageous. Despite this, merging the two technologies is not at all a simple task and is fraught with several difficulties as a result of the competing needs imposed by the two technologies:

- **Processing:** The mining process and the complex cryptography in blockchain are resource-hungry, requiring intensive computation and high energy consumption. This is something that resource-constrained IoMT devices, which already suffer from a shortage of resources and energy limitations, cannot afford to do because it requires a large number of resources.
- **Storage:** The Internet of Things and mobile devices create a massive quantity of data at a fast rate. To protect this data and guarantee its authenticity, the blockchain will need to be processed and used to store them. This presents a considerable difficulty. Blockchain technology depends on its nodes to offer distributed storage, which is outside the financial means of IoMT devices due to their restricted storage capacities.
- **Mobility:** Blockchain was built to operate on a fixed network architecture. On the other hand, implanted and wearable medical devices move about all the time, which results in a persistent alteration of the topology.
- **In Real-Time:** Most applications for IoMT are time-sensitive and need an instant and real-time response. Nevertheless, the production of blocks takes a considerable amount of time. Every ten minutes, 1 megabyte (MB) is added to each block that is part of Bitcoin. It is challenging to meet the real-time criterion while simultaneously grouping these data streams into chunks.
