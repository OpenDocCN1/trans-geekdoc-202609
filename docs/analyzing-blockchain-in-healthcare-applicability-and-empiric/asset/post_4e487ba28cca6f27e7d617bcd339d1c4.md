## The whole processing time from start to finish

It is a common selling point for DLT because it enables quicker transaction resolution in a single arrangement. DLT has the potential to simplify workflows by lowering the barriers associated with information exchange. It is essential to keep in mind that potential improvements in end-to-end processing speed are being addressed at the ecosystem level (across the value chain), and transaction settlement within the infrastructure itself may be slower than expected. This is something that should be kept in mind at all times. Settlements using DLT could take more time than those using RTGS systems. It's possible that using DLT will make it more difficult, from a technical standpoint, to validate a transaction and reach an agreement than it would be using a centralized organization.

Comparisons must be made between the costs of maintaining and updating a distributed ledger and the costs associated with existing practices and alternative options. The world of business is looking at DLT agreements to see whether or not they may lower costs in different stages of the value chain. In addition to this, the costs to society and the market as a whole need to be taken into consideration. Expense distribution among members could be different if the agreement is based on DLT. A decentralized arrangement in which players contribute to maintaining and updating a shared ledger enables participants to share maintenance costs rather than having a single organization, such as an FMI, charge fees to participants. This is in contrast to a model in which the costs of maintaining the ledger are borne by a single organization. Participants in the distributed system keep the ledger updated. As a result of this division of labor, certain of the members running certain nodes in an arrangement could have to bear greater direct expenses.

## Carrying out of the reconciliation with speed and care

When reconciling, you must ensure that the internal records of all relevant parties are similar to one another. Because it requires capturing and storing information in numerous distinct forms as well as reconciling information on several separate ledgers, this procedure is laborious and time-consuming. The use of DLT may lessen the likelihood of data inconsistency, quicken the pace of reconciliation, and eliminate or cut down office chores. This is accomplished by all parties participating in the transaction providing transaction information in a standard manner. It is possible that all of the reconciled data, or only a portion of it, may be shared with other market players in order to ease reporting or to promote greater market transparency.
