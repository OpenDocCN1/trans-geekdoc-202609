# Barriers to BC adoption in the health care system

Despite the great promise that BC technology has, there are many roadblocks in the way to its widespread adoption. As a result of this constant communication, there is a substantial amount of overhead traffic. Bandwidth-constrained IoT devices can’t afford this.

In addition, distributed ledgers are difficult to search for specific phrases or information, as opposed to traditional databases. They are therefore of limited use as clinical and research data repositories. The user interfaces for BC-enabled ledgers are still in the early stages of development, limiting their deployment at a large scale. When BC is still a relatively new technology, new technological remedies may arise as the technology is used more frequently and in a wider variety of industries.

Complex cryptography in BC is resource-expensive, necessitating intensive processing and significant energy consumption, both of which medical devices with limited resources simply cannot afford to consume. As a result, such solutions cannot be implemented since standard protocols for adapting and promoting interoperability across heterogeneous technology are not defined. Medical devices, BC, cloud computing, and end-users all need to work together, therefore it’s critical that universal, platform-agnostic solutions are available.

In addition, while BC technology offers built-in protection against some forms of assault, it is vulnerable to others. Social engineering and zero-day attacks are both possible because of the BC code. Because of this, information security is extremely important, especially in the healthcare industry. Regulation is another concern. Regulatory agencies may find it difficult to trust BC and hence criticize it as a new governance technology that competes with other established institutions of capitalism and networks.

Policymakers and regulators are concerned about the possibility that bitcoins could be used for money laundering. Aside from that, national rules differ, with some governments preferring to regulate new technology cautiously, while others are outright hostile to their use.
