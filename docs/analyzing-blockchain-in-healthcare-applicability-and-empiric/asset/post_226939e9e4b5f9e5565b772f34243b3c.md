## Blockchain as an enabling technology

Blockchain technology is a decentralized innovation with distinctive features such as an impenetrable data infrastructure, and the highest levels of built-in, confidential cryptographic software based on the highest security levels. It is an integrated network of peer-to-peer computing nodes that are assigned together to efficiently validate financial transactions. The blockchain platform is decentralized to become tamper-resistant and resistant to fraud or malpractice due to the cryptographic infrastructure that is used to authenticate network members. Consequently, it can be utilized in virtually every industry.

The dictionary defines blockchain as “a chain of blocks linked by a link.” The group of legitimate transactions that can be identified is known as a block structure. Any node which is a part of the network can initiate the transaction, which is subsequently broadcast to all connected nodes.

Then, using old transactions and cryptographic techniques, a validation process can be carried out. The transactions being carried out will be uploaded to the already existing blockchain once these two basic steps are completed. The number of transactions within each block varies. When Satoshi invented Bitcoin, the block size was around 1 MB. It reached 8 MB in March 2018. The larger the block required to execute more transactions, the faster the processing.

In binary context, the reference between the blocks is known as the hash, and it belongs to the previous block, which is known as the parent block. The genesis block is the initial block without a parent.

All transactions are also viewable to anyone with the right authorization on the public blockchain. As a result, no user or entity can exercise control. In a private blockchain system, on the other hand, everyone who wants to join the blockchain must first obtain authorization. The transactions in this system are only accessible to permitted parties.

While hybrid blockchains offer flexible options, specific data can also be at the same time kept private or made public, and this is referred to as **Consortium** in certain research studies.

According to them, the consensus protocol is the fundamental technique that permits the blockchain’s dependability, security, and efficiency. Numerous consensus strategies have been thought of to enhance the blockchain network’s overall workability or meet specific application requirements, ranging from further tweaks to the Nakamoto consensus algorithm to novel alternative consensus processes.

For instance, blockchain experts are open to new proposal methods such as proof of stake, proof of authority, and proof of elapsed time, which are less computationally intensive to mine and so waste less energy.

Blockchain’s technology incorporates a variety of algorithms. For transaction validation and authentication, asymmetric cryptographic encryption is employed. And to ensure the ultimate security of the transaction environment, digital signature verification is used.

The initial user who initiated the transaction generates the digital signature, which miners can validate. Each functional node in the blockchain network possesses a pair of public and private keys for these two systems.

Hash function, public-private keys, and digital signature are the three fundamental algorithms and functions utilized by blockchain. The hash function effectively connects the blockchain system block to prevent any severe manipulation, while the digital signature verifies the identity.

It may therefore be confirmed with complete certainty that it safeguards the system against non-repudiation. During the global lockdown due to the pandemic, smart contracts were delayed converting any physical contracts that missed out on a stable system to manage them; the paper-based system is also no longer efficient when considering an application-based system.

Smart contracts on the blockchain represent a comprehensive alternative to paper contracts. The customer’s physical presence is not required as one of the key components. Additionally, it eliminates the need for any third-party intermediaries and associated processing delays. Consequently, all parties engaged in a transaction gain from a quicker, less expensive, and faster settlement. Blockchain is one of the greatest solutions for supply chain management for businesses worldwide. First, it can effectively link all included stakeholders through a decentralized universal network, and it can securely display data from silos attributable to its property of transparency. In any industry or application, blockchain technology allows for faster data handling, faster processing, and lower risk in operations which can extensively be used by businesses that transform into digital operations post-Covid.

Blockchains use cryptography, a branch of computer science and mathematics; to sign every transaction. It includes information related to the transfer of financial assets from one individual to another, with a unique digital signature belonging to the user who initiated it. These signatures are kept private but are publicly verified. This implies that if identity A delivers an asset to identity B; anyone can verify that A transferred the asset, but they will not be able to use A's signature for carrying out their own transactions. This cryptographic method establishes complete security and accountability while avoiding any instances of identity fraud: if someone transfers money or alters any information on a blockchain network, they cannot later claim they were not a party to it or absolve themselves of responsibility.

Blockchain is thought to be the enabling technology that has the ability to radically transform practically the entire way that all businesses and economies function. By 2030, it is anticipated that blockchain would generate USD 3 trillion in annual company value.
