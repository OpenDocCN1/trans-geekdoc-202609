## Cloud computing

Cloud computing provides users with a network-based view of their environment, allowing them to share computations and resources no matter where they are. Cloud computing, according to the **National Institute of Standards and Technology** (NIST), is “a template for providing the appropriate and when needed access to the internet, to a collective pool of programmable grids, storage, servers, software, and amenities that can be rapidly liberated, with little communication and supervision from the provider.” Processor features include self-service on-demand, high-performance network connectivity, rapid elasticity, resource pooling, and metric service quality. Hybrid, community, private, and public cloud deployment models add three service models: **Platform as a Service (PAAS)** for the platform, **Infrastructure as a Service (IAAS)** for infrastructure, and **Software as a Service (SAAS)** for shared services. These shared properties, such as virtualization, homogeneity, geographic dispersion, and service orientation are all outlined in NIST’s definition of cloud computing.

## Blockchain and cloud computing

Despite the technology's many advantages, two recurring issues in existing blockchain-enabled cloud systems have been found. For starters, while attempting to use blockchain in cloud applications, it often runs into technological problems.

The majority of issues arise as a result of blockchain’s technological characteristics, some of which are seen as advantages. However, the absence of central control in a purely decentralized context (such as a public blockchain) is also a drawback in many practical scenarios. Many factors, such as legal considerations or governmental responsibilities, prevent a complete abandonment of centralization-based governance.

Another major problem is developing/conducting blockchain service models. When Bitcoin was initially presented to the public, the term “blockchain” was used as a synonym for “Bitcoin,” even though blockchain technology was developed a few years before Bitcoin’s inception. There has been a wave of blockchain-based digital currency and financial services, but the success of blockchain in cryptocurrencies has seldom be replicated in other sectors. Even though several efforts have been made over the years, the lack of suitable service models remains a significant factor restricting blockchain applications.

## Blockchain and cloud security

Because of the exponential growth in the quantity of data, the capacity of conventional storage devices is no longer sufficient to fulfill the requirements of modern life. In this respect, the Cloud Storage Systems, abbreviated as CSS, has been upgraded to accommodate more significant data. Block-based Cloud Storage Systems (BCSSs), Object-based Cloud Storage Systems, and File-based Cloud Storage Systems are the three categories that make up CSS. Each category is differentiated from the others based on the kind of data that is stored. The least expensive and most easily scalable of these Cloud Storage Systems are the Basic Cloud Storage Services (BCSSs), which is why they are now the most often used. Because the data is kept in a block structure inside the BCSS’s structure, the resources are comprised of several tiny block spaces. When a user transfers a data file to a BCSS, the systems first divide the file into segments and then convert each of those segments into property blocks.

## Remote healthcare

In a pandemic, the need for social isolation grows as the number of ill-infected people rises. In addition to the pandemic, there is also a rapidly aging population and a limited supply of medical resources to contend with. There are limitations on the number of individuals permitted in specific places. Thus, arrangements must be made to ensure that the requirements of everyone seeking medical attention are met despite these constraints.

Patients no longer need to visit the hospital since their clinical data may be monitored and analyzed in real-time by a third-party system and then sent to the cloud for later use.

We must protect a vast volume of sensitive data in the IoMT since it is a high-data-density domain that is constantly expanding. Blockchain is a peer-to-peer digital ledger that has been tested and shown to be secure. Non-trusting members may communicate without the need for an intermediary, thanks to the blockchain.

Patients and healthcare workers may attend appointments remotely from distant places, reducing the number of people waiting in a doctor’s office. Health care providers may offer patients the option of staying at home or attending a doctor’s appointment without coping with an overcrowded waiting room. With so many IoMT wearables with sensors, patients may participate in remote healthcare while still in their own homes. This new technology may alleviate some of the pressure on medical resources.

As a result, many healthcare providers are reluctant to employ remote systems because of concerns about the security of their patient’s confidential information. When blockchain is enabled patient data, video conferencing, and online medications will be safeguarded from illegal access. It is possible to give additional treatment outside of the doctor’s office or hospital with the extra security afforded by blockchain.

## Future implementations

The future implementation of medical device in blockchain includes various perspectives such as scalability, Machine, and Deep Learning implementation, Backend-as-a-Service, and so on. The following are their brief advantages:

### Scalability

As a first step, the issues posed by blockchain technology must be addressed. Scalability is the primary issue with blockchain-enabled devices. The present state of blockchain scalability is appalling. Blockchain solutions can only manage a small fraction of the transactions per second (tps) that other systems can. For example, millions of people rely on the VISA payment system at all hours of the day and night. The capacity of this system is 2,000 tps. Whereas, only 7 tps can be handled by Bitcoin. The enormous volume of data included in medical records is a significant problem for blockchain storage.

Massive transactions may be handled in the IoMT system offered by blockchain by boosting its throughput.

### Machine learning/Deep learning and IoMT

There is a vast quantity of data that IoMT gathers. The difficulty occurs in collecting usable information from the data gathered.

It is possible to collect significant and useful information from the IoMT device, such as analyzing the data and identifying system issues. The variety of IoMT data, on the other hand, creates difficulties for data analysis. This can be solved by the developments in the Machine Learning (ML) approach to deep learning.

### Blockchain IoMT and the Cloud /Backend-as-a-Service

Data security is a crucial issue when delivering data through networks. When IoMT apps rely on a centralized cloud, there is a security problem. IoMT data that has been stored in cloud storage may be able to be authenticated and authorized using blockchain and AI.

To secure data, federated learning algorithms may also be used to develop a method of storing data outside of a cloud server. The data gathered at edge nodes may be used to train AI models through these techniques. There are IoT gateways where these nodes may be discovered in an approximation to the user.
