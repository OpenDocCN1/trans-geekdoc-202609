## Discussions

BC is a creative, broad-sense technology that provides new ways of organizing transactions, events, certifications, and ownership in various areas. This type of distributed computing is characterized by the introduction of consensus mechanisms that allow for operations to take place. In contrast to the numerous private-sector examples of BC in action, BC has the potential to assist the e-government sector as well. An interdisciplinary study that goes beyond current technology-driven approaches to BC applications is needed to better understand the consequences for the government. The strategic, organizational, economic, informational, and technological advantages that could be gained are discussed in detail in this study.

Even though these benefits may be more challenging to attain than first assumed, we argue they are worth it. In addition, BC-based healthcare systems face significant challenges in terms of scalability, particularly when it comes to the sheer volume of medical data involved. Healthcare data must be stored off-chain (that is, BC) to avoid performance deterioration because of the enormous volume of data it generates. Transaction processing speed and off-chain data load contribute to system delay in a BC-based system. Finally, because of the immutability and self-execution of code in BC smart contracts, they may be exposed to hackers, which is the system’s final flaw.
