Clinical trials around the world have seen an upsurge in the use of patient participation during the past decade. **Patient engagement** (**PE**) has the potential to improve research; due to many advantages and widespread use of patient involvement in government, academic, and pharmaceutical research, the practice has grown in popularity.

Even though it is becoming more widely used, no standard practices or criteria for evaluating the outcomes have been established for the engagement process. Right now, the level of involvement and contribution can range from the most minimal to the most sophisticated. Organizational goals are frequently formed and defined before participation in cases of little engagement. Patients have limited powers to influence the research and development of drugs. Patients, on the other hand, are given equal decision-making power in more active and purposeful research. Working with patients, researchers determine priorities, set agendas, and clinical design studies on the higher end of the spectrum.
