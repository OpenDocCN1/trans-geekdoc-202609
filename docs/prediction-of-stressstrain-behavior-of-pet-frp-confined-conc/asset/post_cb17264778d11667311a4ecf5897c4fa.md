## 5 Comparison and Discussion

### 5.1 Performance Evaluation of ML Models

This section presents an overview of the performance of three widely recognized ML models: XGBoost, KNN, and RFR. The performance metrics of three different ML models for predicting stress–strain curves of square and rectangular specimens are summarized in Table 5. The evaluation criteria include $R^2$-values for both training and testing datasets, $RMSE$-values for training and testing, and $MAE$-values for training and testing.

#### 5.1.1 Circular Specimens

The evaluation of results for circular specimens is presented in Table 5. XGBoost demonstrated strong performance for circular specimens with high $R^2$-values for both training 0.971 and testing 0.965, indicating an excellent model fit. It also exhibited the lowest $RMSE$-values for training 1.461 and testing 1.988 among the other ML models, highlighting its precision in prediction. Furthermore, XGBoost had the smallest $MAE$-values for training 0.770 and testing 1.140, suggesting superior predictive accuracy with minimal absolute errors.

RFR also performed well, showcasing a high $R^2$-value for testing 0.922, although slightly lower than that of XGBoost. It had higher $RMSE$-values for both training 2.071 and testing 3.129 compared to those of XGBoost, indicating a slightly less precise fit than XGBoost. Moreover, the $MAE$-values for RFR were also higher for both training 1.161 and testing 1.600 than the corresponding values of XGBoost.

KNN exhibited the lowest performance among the three models, with lower $R^2$-values for both training 0.689 and testing 0.677, indicating potential overfitting. It had notably higher $RMSE$-values for both training 4.342 and testing 5.049, suggesting a less accurate prediction. KNN also had relatively high $MAE$-values for both training 2.450 and testing 2.970, implying less accurate predictions with higher absolute errors as compared to XGBoost and RFR.

In summary, XGBoost demonstrated the most promising performance among the models for predicting stress–strain curves of circular specimens. It offered superior predictive accuracy and minimal errors, making it a suitable choice for accurate stress–strain curve prediction in this context. RFR also performed well but slightly lagged behind XGBoost, while KNN showed the least accuracy and higher absolute errors, indicating its limited suitability for this predictive task for PET FRP-confined circular specimens.

#### 5.1.2 Noncircular Specimens

A comparison is also made among the three ML models to compare their performance in predicting the stress–strain behavior of PET FRP-confined concrete for noncircular specimens’ cases.

Among the three ML models, XGBoost exhibited the highest $R^2$-values for both training 0.994 and testing 0.899, indicating excellent model fit. XGBoost also demonstrated the lowest $RMSE$-value for training 0.292 and competitive $RMSE$-value for testing 1.039. Additionally, XGBoost yielded the smallest MAE-values for both training 0.106 and testing 0.650, highlighting its superior predictive accuracy.

RFR showed reasonably good performance with a respectable R²-value for training 0.924 and testing 0.790, although it fell behind XGBoost. However, it showed higher RMSE-values for both training 1.324 and testing 1.756 compared to those of XGBoost, signifying a slightly less precise fit. The MAE-values for RFR were also found to be higher for both training 0.781 and testing 1.080 than XGBoost. On the other hand, KNN demonstrated a high R²-value for training 0.994 but a lower R²-value for testing 0.814, indicating potential overfitting. Furthermore, the RMSE-value for testing 2.339 is also higher. However, KNN has relatively low MAE-values for both training 0.091 and testing 1.060, implying good predictive accuracy in terms of absolute errors.

The assessment metrics demonstrated that the XGBoost model achieved superior prediction accuracy and effectively balanced performance between the training and testing datasets for the noncircular specimens. On the other hand, RFR showed good performance but slightly lagged behind XGBoost, while KNN exhibited overfitting tendencies but still demonstrated competitive accuracy in terms of MAE.
