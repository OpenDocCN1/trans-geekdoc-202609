+   1. For circular specimens, XGBoost demonstrated the most promising performance with high R²-values for both training and testing datasets, low RMSE, and MAE-values, indicating excellent accuracy and minimal errors in predictions. RFR also performed well but slightly lagged behind XGBoost, while KNN showed the least accuracy with higher absolute errors.
+   2. For noncircular specimens, XGBoost again exhibited superior performance, with high R²-values, low RMSE, and MAE-values, showcasing its accuracy and consistency. RFR performed well, but KNN showed potential overfitting tendencies.
+   3. For both circular and noncircular specimens, the XGBoost model showed impressive accuracy in predicting various parameters of the stress–strain curves, with low MAE and RMSE-values. The predictions closely aligned with the experimental values, highlighting the model’s precision.
+   4. The XGBoost model outperformed a mechanism model proposed by [57] in accurately capturing essential curve points, such as ultimate stress and strain, for both circular and noncircular specimens. The XGBoost model showed greater consistency and reliability in predicting the stress–strain curves across a diverse range of specimens.
+   5. The comparative analysis of predictive performance for compressive stress and strain revealed that the XGBoost model exhibited superior accuracy, consistency, and reliability in predicting ultimate compressive strain and strain for PET FRP-confined concrete, outperforming several other mechanism models considered in the comparative analysis.
+   6. Feature importance analysis revealed that parameters like the number of PET FRP layers, fiber thickness (t_f), and corner radius significantly influenced stress–strain behavior. The feature dependency analysis highlighted linear relationships between these input features and output parameters, providing insights into their impact.

In conclusion, the XGBoost model proved to be a robust and accurate tool for predicting the stress–strain behavior of PET FRP-confined concrete specimens. Its superior performance, consistency, and precision make it a valuable asset in the field of composite materials. Additionally, feature importance and dependency analysis shed light on the key factors driving the behavior of these specimens. This research contributes to the advancement of predictive modeling in civil engineering and materials science, providing a foundation for further studies in this area.
