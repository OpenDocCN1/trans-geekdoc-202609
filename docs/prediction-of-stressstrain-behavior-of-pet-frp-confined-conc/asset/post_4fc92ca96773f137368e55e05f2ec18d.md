To further assess the performance of the XGBoost in predicting ultimate compressive stress (f_cu) and strain (ε_cu) for PET FRP-confined concrete, a comprehensive comparison was made. In addition to the selected mechanism model proposed by Pimanmas et al. [57], the predictions generated by XGBoost were compared with several other mechanism models [37, 53, 54, 56]. The objective was to provide a thorough evaluation of the XGBoost model’s accuracy and consistency in the predictive task.

Table 8 provides detailed insights and a comprehensive view of this comparative analysis. The table offered a meticulous examination of the results, allowing for a comprehensive evaluation of the XGBoost model’s performance in the context of f_cu and ε_cu. The data in each cell of the table represent the ratio of predicted value by the model to the experimental value. A ratio of 1.00 indicates accurate predictions, while values greater or less than 1.00 indicate overestimation or underestimation, respectively. Notably, the XGBoost model in the present study demonstrates strong performance, with an average ratio close to 1.00, indicating accurate predictions. When compared to the other mechanism models, the XGBoost model stands out as a reliable performer, with a relatively high degree of accuracy and consistency. The coefficient of variation (COV) values offer insights into the relative variability of predictions, highlighting the XGBoost model’s advantage in terms of stability and reliability.

## 6 Feature Importance and Dependency Analysis

### 6.1 Feature Importance

The utilization of SHAP feature importance offers significant insights into the learning process of complex models and the significance of input characteristics. In order to achieve consistency across characteristics, the calculation of relative importance was conducted through normalization. The relative relevance ranking of input characteristics for both circular and noncircular specimens is depicted in Fig. 8.

Figure 8a illustrates the importance of several features in predicting the stress–strain behavior of PET FRP-confined concrete for circular specimens. The f_co was identified as the most significant parameter, followed closely by the number of PET FRP layers. The significance of the number of PET FRP layers in influencing the stress–strain characteristics was clear. Moreover, the parameter number of layers was followed by the parameter t_f representing the thickness of the fiber, which signifies that change in the fiber thickness influences the properties of the stress–strain characteristics. Lastly, it was noticed the area parameter has a comparatively lesser influence on the stress–strain characteristics when compared to other variables in circular specimens.

On the other hand, Fig. 8b presents an overview of feature importance for noncircular specimens. It is noteworthy that the corner radius occupied the primary position as the most relevant component, followed by t_f. The parameter t_f played a substantial role in both circular and noncircular specimens. The t_f was followed by the parameter representing the number of layers of PET FRP, which signifies that changes in the number of layers magnify the impact of fiber thickness on the stress–strain characteristics. Finally, it was observed that the parameter f_co had the least impact on noncircular specimens among the parameters that have been addressed.

### 6.2 Feature Dependency Analysis

### 6.2.1 Circular Specimens

In this section, the feature dependency analysis was delved specifically tailored to circular PET FRP specimens. The focus of this investigation lay in understanding how various input variables influenced the specimens’ behavior. To achieve this, SHAP analysis, a powerful technique for unveiling the intricate relationships between input features and model predictions, was employed. The illustrations in Fig. 9 show the feature dependency of circular specimens. The SHAP values of layers for the output f_cu increased with the increase in the layers of PET FRP indicating a linear relationship between them. Similarly, Fig. 9c shows the SHAP values of layers for the output of ε_cu increases with the increase in the number of layers of PET FRP indicating a linear relationship between these two.

These analysis results were found to be aligned with the notable findings presented by [17]. It was found that the circular specimens’ f_cu and ε_cu increased linearly with the increase in the PET FRP layers. Furthermore, f_c1 was also increased approximately linearly with PET FRP layers [17].

### 6.2.2 Noncircular Specimens

Firstly, a notable positive correlation was found between the number of PET FRP layers and the f_cs for noncircular specimens. Figure 10a presents that as the layers increased, f_cs exhibited a consistent and linear improvement. Similarly, a linear relationship emerged between the number of layers and the f_cu, indicating that increased layers positively influenced f_cu. Furthermore, the same linear relationship was found between the number of layers and the ε_cu.
