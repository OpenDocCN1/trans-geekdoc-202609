# 5.3 Comparison of Stress–Strain Curves

After predicting the mechanical properties by the ML models, the complete stress–strain response of PET FRP-confined concrete for both circular and noncircular specimens was plotted for the most accurate ML model, XGBoost. The stress–strain response of PET FRP-confined concrete predicted by the XGBoost was compared to the experimental stress–strain curve. Furthermore, a mechanism model proposed by Pimanmas et al. [57], emerged as the standout choice among several mechanism models and was selected as the benchmark for comparative analysis due to its exceptional performance compared to other mechanism models.

Figures 6 and 7 provide a visual comparison of stress–strain curves, representing the relationship between axial strain (X-axis) and axial stress (Y-axis) for both circular and noncircular specimens. These figures include the experimental curve, XGBoost’s prediction, and the selected mechanism model’s projection.

In Fig. 6, it became evident that the mechanism model fell short in accurately capturing ultimate stress (f_cu) and strain (ε_cu) for circular specimens. Furthermore, its prediction of the point f_c2 lacked the precision found in XGBoost’s projections.

Figure 7, which focuses on noncircular specimens, reveals significant distinctions. For instance, in specimen SROL1, the mechanism model’s predictions of ε_cu lacked accuracy compared to the XGBoost’s predictions. Similarly in the specimen RROL1, the prediction made through the mechanism model lacked accuracy in the case of f_cs as well as f_c2 and ε_c2.
