
# The Accountable Layer

The accountable layer is ultimately accountable for all major decisions of the consortium, and it also appoints the board and agrees to the board's boundaries and year plans. To avoid an inefficient structure, a responsible layer is introduced.

## BOARD

When the consortium grows beyond six participants, the responsible layer is introduced to guarantee efficiency in governance. The responsible layer is embodied by the board and consists of six participants. The board is appointed by the consortium from among its participants, and can operate freely on a daily basis, within the assigned boundaries, and can delegate the daily operations to the management team. This guarantees efficiency in handling. The main responsibilities are:

1.  Supervision of the management team
2.  Managing and maintaining relationships with the observers' community and reporting to the consortium.

For the appointment of board participants the following criteria will apply: the participants should be geographically spread, meaning that all major continents must be represented. Also, a member of the board may not be affiliated with any of the other participants, to guarantee the strict neutrality of the board.

To guarantee continuity board participants are appointed for three years; every year two board participants are replaced, which minimizes the risk of political re-election bias and thus loss of efficiency as a complete new board needs time to start up – and it reduces the risk that during the last part of their term, the board is more occupied with re-election than their daily tasks.

## NAVIPORTA OPERATIONS MANAGEMENT TEAM

The daily operations (operational layer) are managed by the management team under the responsibility of the board based on defined key performance indicators. The management team takes care of all the execution of day-to-day tasks, ranging from development of the core infrastructure and software, participation management (KYC, etc) to marketing and sales. The management team is hired by and reports to the board on a weekly basis, and should present any major decisions to the board. The board can decide if the consortium needs to have the final say in the matter (at a general assembly). Those involved in the management team will either be employees of the Naviporta consortium or can be hired from external solution or services providers.

## QuayConnect

Each year around €360 billion of goods are exported from the EU to the UK. Since Brexit, the UK has sharply tightened the customs formalities, resulting in a strong increase of administrative workload and additional costs for exporting goods to the UK.

Before leaving the EU, goods moved freely between the continent and the UK, with little or no formalities. For example, goods produced or imported into the EU could be sold within the UK without the need for additional health and safety checks at the border of the member country or payment of import duties. On 31 January 2020 the EU and UK agreed on the terms and conditions under which the UK would leave the EU and the new arrangements between the partners in the so called Withdrawal Agreement and Political Declaration. A transition period, keeping most of the pre-Brexit arrangements in place, officially ended on 31 December 2020. On Christmas Eve 2020 the EU and the UK struck a last-minute deal on how to regulate future trade. This new regulation, as laid down in the Border Operating model (UK Government, 2021), constitutes a phased approach towards tariff-free, quota-free access to each other's markets for goods – but not services. However, the implementation of the model comes with a considerable administrative burden for both exporter and importer. Not only are they required to declare their goods upon import, but also these goods are now subject to more extensive checks from UK customs (HMRC)[^3], as well as other authorities, such as Border Force[^4] and the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs (DEFRA)[^5]. As part of this phased approach, new regulation was introduced on 1 January 2022. Among other things, it sees to it that all goods being moved between the EU and the UK now require both an export and an import declaration. The exact implementation differs per port. Add to that the complex rules of origin, the individual trade deals that exist between the UK and the rest of the world, the pandemic and global supply chain issues and it is easy to see why many importers and exporters have trouble in meeting these new custom requirements. Companies that do not meet these complex requirements are faced with delays, additional paperwork and even fines. It was for this reason that a new service called QuayConnect was developed on Naviporta, the neutral blockchain platform for the logistics industry.

### FIRST SERVICE ON THE NAVIPORTA PLATFORM

QuayConnect is developed in collaboration between Blocklab[^6], Azarc[^7], and the operator of CCS-UK[^8], British Telecom[^9], the Dutch Fresh Produce Association[^10] and ABC Logistics[^11]. Built on Naviporta, QuayConnect provides a seamless and direct interaction between the system of the exporter, the Dutch port community system (PCS) 'Portbase'[^12] and the UK Cargo Community System (CCS). CCS connects the various members of the cargo community in a digital and distributed way. This community includes HMRC, but also freight forwarders, importers and hauliers. This enables secure, reliable and fast verification of information and creates a seamless and fully automated flow of information between all involved parties, resulting in considerable savings in both time and money. The following steps have been integrated in the solution:

*   Preparing, submitting, receiving and handling of the required custom documents in a digital fashion
*   Management of the required underlying documentation such as packing list, sales order, etc
*   Tracking and tracing of the shipment using an intuitive user interface
*   Creating and signing e-CMR, providing immediate digital proof of shipment pick-up and delivery

### COMMON FRAME OF REFERENCE FOR ALL INVOLVED PARTIES

The blockchain infrastructure supported by Naviporta[^13] allows for assets such as documents and datasets originating from the often siloed systems operated by the various cargo community members, to be uniquely registered based on their cryptographically generated hash key, without revealing any sensitive data on the blockchain; it does not require users of the Naviporta infrastructure to store any of their data on the infrastructure, ensuring strict neutrality and making monetization of data by Naviporta impossible. All this results in a common frame of reference over the siloed systems for these assets, which in turn enables the generation of several proofs. These are:

*   Proof of existence – by publishing the hash of the asset on the Naviporta blockchain, the company from which the asset originated can provide proof that this document existed at a given point in time.
