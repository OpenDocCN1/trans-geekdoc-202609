
Neutrality should be enforced by the governance structure, built around a consortium of, by nature, neutral partners, guaranteeing the neutrality of the infrastructure and providing trust for service providers and end-users to use the platform without fear of vendor lock-in and monetization of their data. Furthermore, the infrastructure should guarantee data-sovereignty, meaning that 1) end-users can always verify the existence, origin and integrity of data, 2) other users of the platform (including Naviporta) are in no way able to access each other’s original data, unless a party has been authorized to do so and 3) consortium partners are not able to create a revenue model based on the end-users’ data. Finally, 4) there should be no infrastructure lock-in, meaning that an end-user can integrate a service from providers outside the platform. The infrastructure should be there for the sole reason to create interoperability and enable ‘connect once, use many’, with the aim to grow cooperation in the ecosystem and optimize the primary processes of the end-users.

## Core values

The Naviporta infrastructure is founded on the following core values:

1.  **Data sovereignty**: Naviporta stores no master data, business logic and transactional data on the blockchain, apart from the data’s fingerprint (hash). Each user of the blockchain network is empowered to control how their data is used and stored. Hence, Naviporta is not able to monetize any data.

2.  **Technology agnostic**: decentralized apps (`Dapp`) development; Naviporta doesn’t prescribe any protocol, programming language or proprietary technology to third-party service developers. They are encouraged to develop their services any way they want as long as they adhere to the `SDK` standards to ensure interoperability with the platform and successfully pass the services’ onboarding process.

3.  **Enable best-of-breed 2.0**: in today’s siloed world even companies that adopt a ‘best-of-breed’ approach when it comes to their supply chain management software must compromise. For example, one electronic bill of lading service provider is stronger in Asia-Pacific, while the other has a stronger presence in Latin-America. Naviporta allows consumers to mix and match their services at a more granular level, even at the shipment level.

4.  **Neutral**: Naviporta is underpinned by a governance structure that doesn’t favour a specific producer, consumer or core ecosystem member. This is achieved by partnering with (port) ecosystems that are committed to the core values and actively grow the partner’s Naviporta ecosystem, by adding new services, consumers, producers and trade lanes.

## Ecosystem

While the Naviporta ecosystem still resembles Deliver ecosystems, there has been a fundamental shift towards producers as the key focal point of the Naviporta platform. This is primarily a result of clearly separating the `Dapps` from the core services as described in the technology paragraph. This has several benefits:

*   core services and `Dapps` can each have their own business case and governance;
*   a clear distinction in operating model: Infrastructure-as-a-Service (`IaaS`) for the core and Software-as-a-Service (`SaaS`) for the `Dapps`;
*   enable faster scaling of the platform as producers can potentially on-board members of their own ecosystem that want to make use of other services being offered on the Naviporta platform;
*   safeguard the technology agnosticism of both `Dapps` and core services.

In the Deliver era, a tradelane-based strategy between South Korea and the Netherlands was adopted to build the ecosystem, based on the rationale that the three partners were able to mobilize significant traffic on this tradelane alone because of their respective headquarters, the total volume of trade between the two countries and proximity to key stakeholders such as authorities and regulatory bodies. With ABN-AMRO and Samsung SDS no longer part of the consortium, Naviporta had to abandon this tradelane and overall reassess the suitability of the trade lane strategy. As the QuayConnect `Dapp` (see ‘Supported use cases’) offers tremendous scaling opportunities on those tradelanes connecting the UK, we almost gravitated effortlessly towards a tradelane-based strategy between the European Union (`EU`) and the United Kingdom (`UK`).

The proposed design of the governance of the consortium consists of three layers, an accountable layer, a responsible layer and an operational layer.

### CONSORTIUM

The consortium represents the accountable layer and hence has the highest authority in the consortium. It gathers at least once a year in the general assembly, or more often when called upon by consortium participants or the board (see responsible layer). Decisions are made according to absolute or relative majority, depending on the kind of vote.

Consortium participation is designed to be as open as possible without compromising the neutrality of Naviporta. Therefore, three categories for member submission are determined:

1.  **Directly admissible** – organizations in this category can become a member of the consortium without the approval of the current participants, as long as they adhere to the minimum requirements for participation (regulatory compliance). These are any (semi-)governmental organization, like port authorities or organizations without any direct profit or commercial interests in the primary processes that the Naviporta platform supports.

2.  **Excluded organizations** – these organizations are allowed to use the Naviporta platform and offer their services via the platform to their customers but cannot become a member of the consortium. These are all organizations that have a direct profit or commercial interest in the primary processes supported by Naviporta and all organizations that are suppliers to the consortium and thus are paid directly by the consortium for their services.

3.  **To be debated** – the third category is for all organizations that cannot be immediately placed in the first or second category. For these organizations to become a member of the consortium, they will need approval from the current participants by majority decision of the consortium at a general assembly.

Consortium participants are expected to either run and maintain a Naviporta node or pay a yearly participation fee. Participants receive one vote in the general assembly as a reward.

If a subsidiary of an existing member wants to join the consortium, they won’t receive additional voting rights. This guarantees openness and decentralization on the one hand and neutrality on the other.
