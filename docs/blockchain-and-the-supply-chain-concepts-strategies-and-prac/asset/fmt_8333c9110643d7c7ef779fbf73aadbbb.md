## Overview

We present the basics of blockchain technology and its applications in this chapter. We begin with the classic Bitcoin protocol, the first-ever blockchain, designed for the specific use case of a peer-to-peer digital currency. We explore it in some detail, including some of the key cryptographic building blocks and innovative new mechanisms that enable Bitcoin to operate in a completely decentralized and secure manner. We then introduce Ethereum, the second-generation protocol providing for general-purpose programmable smart contracts. This has ushered in a revolution in terms of a vast array of decentralized applications that utilize fungible and non-fungible tokens, including decentralized finance, creation and management of digital assets, decentralized autonomous organizations, and more. As the domain and applications have matured, some performance challenges have been identified such as high energy consumption and low transaction throughput, and are being addressed through new innovations.
