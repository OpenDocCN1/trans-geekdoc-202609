Wen and Patrick (Shiau & Chau, 2016) and Safad & Eydgahi (Ashtari & Eydgahi, 2017) statistical modelling for EDM. Statistical methods are not able to support the change in population and size. Also, it was difficult to handle lead time biases.
Fernando et al. (Koch, Assunção, Cardonha & Netto, 2016) used Partial Least method and proved that it was sensitive to the choice of parameters. The parameters were few.
Janice et al. (Gobert, Kim, Pedro, Kennedy, Bailey, 2015) and Anjana, Smija & Kizekhethotottam (Pradeep, D. & J, 2015) used Decision trees in EDM. Limited features were used while predicting the performance. As well tree structure was prone to sampling. The accuracy was affected by biased data.
Costa, Fonseca, Santana, Araújo, & Rego, 2017 used Naive Bayes, Decision Tree, SVM and Neural Network to predict the performance of the students. The data was collected from distance learning and on-campus students. Performance data per week for the four weeks was collected and analysed for the effectiveness of the algorithms.
Wanli et al. (Xing, Guo, Petakov, & Goggins, 2015) used genetic programming for predicting students' performance. The genetic algorithm produced an optimised relation. While predicting, less consideration was given to the qualitative aspects. They monitored classroom learning of students identified the factors which affect the performance. The participation of students in various activities was major considered.
Xin Chen, Vorvoreanu, & Madhuvan, 2013) used social data to identify the factors which affect the behaviour or performance of students-study-life balance, lack of sleep, lack of social engagement, and lack of diversity.
Michael N. Giannakos et al. (Giannakos, et al., 2017) identified various cognitive factors like academic performance, attendance etc., and its effects on students' performance.
Hijazi & Naqi (Hijazi & Naqi, 2006) and Shoukat (Shoukat, 2013) studied the impact of various cognitive and non-cognitive on students' performance.
Mushaq & Khan (Mushaq & Khan, 2012) proved that communication, learning facilities, proper guidance and staff history have a direct impact on students' performance. As well Omar & Dennis (2015) used many factors for study and identified which factors played a vital role in students' performance.
Suryawan & Putra (Suryawa & Putra, 2016) did a detailed survey to identify the factors which affect students GPA. Also, regression tests and correlation analysis were done on various factors. It was proved that the entrance exam and attendance in the class were important factors. Lecturer quality was also important and had an effect on GPA.
In an interesting article in 2016, Pooja Mondal (2016) identified various factors like intellect, learning, mental, social and economic factors which affected students' behaviour and performance.
Most of the research is centred on the application of Data Mining and Machine Learning techniques in the classification task for students' performance. Classification and prediction task wisely classification methods based on learning from examples, such as Decision Tree, Artificial Neural Networks and Support Vector Machines. Although hybrid algorithms gained popularity for solving complex problems, they are not cited as the other methods in students' performance classification and prediction (Vora & Kamatch, EDM – Survey of Performance Factors and Algorithms Applied, 2018).
