In this work, the comparative study was carried out between some models while using transfer learning and without using this technique, as is, training or not the networks from scratch in the classification of leukocytes.

# Models Trained with Transfer Learning

In the case of the training with transfer learning, the original top of the networks was excluded. That way it is possible to use the weights from previous training with Imagenet, that is, the knowledge of some characteristics of objects applicable to the identification of leukocytes. A small network with three layers was created (the new top), a dense layer with the ReLU activation, a dropout layer and an output layer with the softmax function. The code needed to program this network in Keras as follows:

```
model = models.Sequential()
model.add(layers.Dense(256, activation='relu', input_dim=7*7*512))
model.add(layers.Dropout(0.5))
model.add(layers.Dense(4, activation='softmax'))
```

The network weights related to the training features and were then to classify leukocytes in 4 categories. The initial training lasted 100 epochs, and a batch size of 16 examples was used. The average training time of the 8 NN ('resnet50', 'vgg16', 'vgg19', 'inceptionV3', 'xception', 'densenet121', 'densenet169', 'densenet201') for 100 epochs was 9 minutes and 59 seconds on the computational architecture achieved. The fastest, VGG19, completed the training in 5 minutes and 47 seconds, and the shortest, Xception, took 16 minutes and 57 seconds. The networks that achieved the best results with lower values with lower losses were the Xception, the Denseset201, the Densenet169, the Densenet121 and InceptionV3. The network that converged faster to (approximately) 0 was the Xception. The worst results were obtained by the VGG and ResNet50 networks.

Although the networks were initially presented by their authors to operate with optimizers, in this work, all the used the SGD optimizer, for comparing purposes (with the consequent of penalizing the performance of some of networks used), with a learning rate of 1⁻⁵ and decay of 0.001.

Figure 14 shows that the accuracy is closely related to loss, and the models that obtained the best accuracies were the same as that had obtained the losses with lower values. Thus, the Xception network was the one that converged to 1 (with overfitting), and the ResNet50 was the one that obtained the worst results with a training accuracy of about 40%. The poor ResNet results may be related to the SGD.

The results for the validation process are close to the ones obtained during the training, with the losses being slightly lower (and the accuracies slightly higher) than training as expected. In the case of transfer learning, the ResNet50 would be expected to perform better, because it is one of the newer networks, with an average of only 3.57% in ILSVRC'15. However, the established training conditions, was not able to track the network with better results.
