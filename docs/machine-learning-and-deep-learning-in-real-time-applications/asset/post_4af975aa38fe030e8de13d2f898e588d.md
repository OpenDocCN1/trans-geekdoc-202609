### ABSTRACT
The field of audio forensics has seen huge advancement in recent years. This chapter focuses on the authentication of audio recordings submitted as evidence. The authors provide an overview of the techniques used in the field and the main objective of this chapter is to introduce the basics of audio processing.

### INTRODUCTION
Authentication of digital audio is a complex process. The digital era has made manipulation of audio recordings easy. There is a need for forensic experts to verify the authenticity of audio recordings. This section provides a brief introduction to the fundamentals of digital audio authentication.

### Structure and Format of an Audio Signal
Digital audio is composed of a sequence of discrete samples taken from an analog signal. The sampling frequency and bits per sample are important parameters. A sampling frequency of 44.1 kHz and 16 bits per sample are common. There are different audio formats available depending upon the application:
- Uncompressed formats: PCM, WAV, AIFF.
- Lossy compressed files: MP3, AAC, WMA.
- Lossless compressed files: FLAC, ALAC.

### Audio Recording Setup
The components of a digital audio recording setup are shown in Figure 1. It consists of a microphone, a preamplifier, an analog-digital converter and a digital recorder. The incoming sound is an analog signal which is converted into an electric signal by the microphone. There are different types of microphones available which can be chosen mainly depending upon the directional pattern. The output signal of a microphone is weak which is amplified by a preamplifier to a voltage of amplitude around 1.5V. The electrical signal to this point is an analog signal which is converted into a digital by the A/D converter. The recorder converts the electrical signal of the A/D converter into a physical form which can be stored or played back. If the signal has to be played back, the player such as a loudspeaker conveys the signal back into pressure waves.
