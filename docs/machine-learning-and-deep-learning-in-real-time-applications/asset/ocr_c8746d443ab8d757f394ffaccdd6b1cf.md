# Deep Convolutional Neural Network-Based Analysis for Breast Cancer Histology Images

Staining is one of the most important steps in the preparation of tissue because most cells are colour and transparent. Therefore, histological sections should be stained to highlight important features of the tissue as well as increase tissue contrast. Microscopic evaluation of such tissues provides invaluable information for pathologists to diagnose and classify pathological conditions. H&E staining is the most commonly used staining technique in hematopathology. Hematoxylin stains the nucleus blue, counterstain eosin cytoplasm and non-nuclear components in various shades of pink. H&E staining is not specific because it drains most of the cells in the same way. Specific staining methods are those that select specific chemical groups or molecules in cells.

Histopathological analysis of breast tissue almost always begins with a careful examination of H&E stained specimens. Despite the introduction of powerful and highly specialized procedures over the last 40 years, H&E remains the most widely used tissue stain for determining the diagnosis and prognosis of breast cancer patients. The pathological classification of breast enlargement depends on the molecular morphology and structural patterns of cells and glands. In vascular tumors are routinely classified by the assessment of tube/gland formation, nuclear pleomorphism, and mitotic count. Due to different pathological criteria, interverbal variability, and differential tumor growth pattern, classification and grading are challenging due to time-consuming. Computerized analysis can solve the problem of inappropriate components and subjectivity, and the lack of widespread access to robust digitizing glass slides has hindered the use of computerized analysis techniques.
