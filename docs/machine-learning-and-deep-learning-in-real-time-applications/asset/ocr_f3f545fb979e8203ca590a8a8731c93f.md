x(t) = \sin\left[-\frac{\omega_T}{\omega_s}\ln\left(\frac{\omega_s}{\omega_t}\right)\right]$

where $\omega_s$ is the angular start frequency of the sweep which is considered to be 20Hz and $\omega_t$ is the angular stop frequency to 20 KHz. $T$ is the duration of the sine sweep.

This generated sine sweep is now played and recorded in an environment. Therefore, reverberation characteristics of the recorded environment are captured in the recorded signal. If the signal recorded at the microphone is represented by $y(t)$ and the system's(environment's) response is $h(t)$, let $f(t)$ be an inverse filter which is just a time reversal of the test signal. The impulse response is obtained by the convolution of $y(t)$ and $f(t)$.

For the above experiment, sweep signals are synthetically generated as discussed in the previous section and convolved with the already available impulse responses from the Multichannel Acoustic Reverberation Database at York (MARDY) database. This gives an effect that the sweep signals are recorded in that particular environment.

From the obtained impulse response, features are extracted and applied to classifiers for classification.
