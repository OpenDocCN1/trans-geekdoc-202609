Although the early stages of tumor progression and the exact underlying mechanism have not fully elucidated, it is also known that almost all invasive breast cancers originate from in situ carcinoma. Multiple pathological and biological features differentiate between in-situ carcinoma, invasive ductal carcinoma and benign lesions. Accurate diagnosis of breast enlargement is crucial in determining the optimal treatment plan. At present, the diagnosis of these conditions is largely based on careful examination of breast tissue under the pathologist's microscope. Histological procedures are direct to provide the best quality components for microscopic evaluation of human tissue changes in immune or induced diseases. Typically, the tissue was fixed with paraffin, preferably with normal formalin 10%, and then divided by a microtome to obtain 4-5um thick paraffin sections. Devoured sections are marked with H&E and are used for several purposes (Slaoui & Fiette, 2011). These steps are usually performed by a Pathology Lab technician, who then sends the sample to a pathologist for clarification.
