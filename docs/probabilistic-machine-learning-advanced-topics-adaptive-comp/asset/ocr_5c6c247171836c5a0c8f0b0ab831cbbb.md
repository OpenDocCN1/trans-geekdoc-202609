# 15.5.2.2 Non-centered parameterization

One problem that frequently arises in hierarchical models is that the parameters be very correlated. This can cause computational problems when performing inference. Figure 15.17a gives an example where we plot p(\(\beta_j, \sigma_B\) | \(\mathcal{D}\)) for some specific county \(j\). If we believe that \(\sigma_B\) is large, then \(\beta_c\) is "allowed" to vary a lot, and we get the broad distribution at the top of the figure. However, if we believe that \(\sigma_B\) is small, then \(\beta_j\) is constrained to be close to the global prior mean of \(\mu_\beta\), so we get the narrow distribution at the bottom of the figure. This is often called Neal's funnel, after a paper by Radford Neal [Nea03]. It is difficult for many algorithms (especially sampling algorithms) to explore parts of parameter space at the bottom of the funnel.
