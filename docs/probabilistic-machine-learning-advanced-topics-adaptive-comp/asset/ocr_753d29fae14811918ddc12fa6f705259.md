# ## 10.3.6.1 The variational posterior

We will use the standard mean field approximation to the posterior: $q(\theta, z_{1:N}) = q(\theta) \prod_n q_n(z_n)$. At this stage we have not specified the forms of the $q$ functions; these will be determined by the form of the likelihood and prior. Below we will show that the optimal forms are as follows:

$q_n(z_n) = \text{Cat}(z_n|r_n)$

$q(\theta) = \text{Dir}(\pi | \widehat{\alpha}) \prod_k \mathcal{N}(\mu_k | \widehat{m}_k, (\widehat{\kappa}_k \Lambda_k)^{-1})\text{W}(\Lambda_k | \widehat{L}_k, \widehat{\nu}_k)$

where $r_n$ are the posterior responsibilities, and the parameters with hats on them are the hyperparameters from the prior updated with data.

# ## 10.3.6.2 Derivation of $q(\theta)$ (variational M step)

Using the mean field recipe in Algorithm 10.4, we write down the log joint, and take expectations over all variables except $\theta$, so we average out the $z_n$ wrt $q(z_n) = \text{Cat}(z_n|r_n)$:

\(\log q(\theta) = \log p(\pi) + \sum_n \mathbb{E}_{q(z_n)} [\log p(z_n|\pi)]\)

\(+ \sum_k \left[ \log p(\mu_k, \Lambda_k) \sum_n \mathbb{E}_{q(z_n)} [z_{nk} \log \mathcal{N}(x_n|\mu_k, \Lambda_k^{-1})] \right] + \text{const}\)

Since the expected log joint factorizes into a term involving $\pi$ and terms involving $(\mu_k, \Lambda_k)$, we see that the variational posterior also factorizes into the form

$q(\theta) = q(\pi) \prod_k q(\mu_k, \Lambda_k)$

For the $\pi$ term, we have

\(\log q(\pi) = (\alpha_0 - 1) \sum_k \log \pi_k + \sum_k \sum_n r_{nk} \log \pi_k + \text{const}\)

Exponentiating, we recognize this as a Dirichlet distribution:

$q(\pi) = \text{Dir}(\pi | \widehat{\alpha})$

\(\widehat{\alpha}_k = \alpha_0 + N_k\)

\(N_k = \sum_n r_{nk}\)
