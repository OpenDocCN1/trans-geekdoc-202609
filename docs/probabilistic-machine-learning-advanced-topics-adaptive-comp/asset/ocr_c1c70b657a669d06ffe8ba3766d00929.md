# 4.3.5 Gaussian MRFs

In Section 4.2.3, we showed how to represent a multivariate Gaussian using a DPGM. In this section, we show how to represent a multivariate Gaussian using a UPGM. (For further details on GMRFs, see e.g., [RH05].)

# 4.3.5.1 Standard GMRFs

A Gaussian graphical model (or GGM), also called a Gaussian MRF, is a pairwise MRF of the following form:

p(x) = \frac{1}{Z(\theta)} \prod_{i \sim j} \psi_{ij}(x_i, x_j) \prod_i \psi_i(x_i)
\psi_{ij}(x_i, x_j) = \exp(-\frac{1}{2} x_i \Lambda_{ij} x_j)
\psi_i(x_i) = \exp(-\frac{1}{2} \Lambda_{ii} x_i^2 + \eta_i x_i)
Z(\theta) = (2\pi)^{D/2} |\Lambda|^{-\frac{1}{2}}

The \(\psi_{ij}\) are edge potentials (pairwise terms), and each the \(\psi_i\) are node potentials or unary terms. (We could absorb the unary terms into the pairwise terms, but we have kept them separate for clarity.)

The joint distribution can be rewritten in a more familiar form as follows:

p(x) \propto \exp[\eta^T x - \frac{1}{2} x^T \Lambda x]

This is called the information form of a Gaussian; \(\Lambda = \Sigma^{-1}\) and \(\eta = \Lambda \mu\) are called the canonical parameters.

If \(\Lambda_{ij} = 0\), there is no pairwise term connecting \(x_i\) and \(x_j\), and hence \(x_i \perp x_j | x_{-ij}\), where \(x_{-ij}\) are all the nodes except for \(x_i\) and \(x_j\). Hence the zero entries in \(\Lambda\) are called structural zeros. This means we can use \(\ell_1\) regularization on the weights to learn a sparse graph, a method known as graphical lasso (see Supplementary Section 30.4.2).

Note that the covariance matrix \(\Sigma = \Lambda^{-1}\) can be dense even if the precision matrix \(\Lambda\) is sparse. For example, consider an AR(1) process with correlation parameter \(\rho\). The precision matrix (for a graph with \(T=7\) nodes) looks like this:

\Lambda = \frac{1}{\tau^2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -\rho & & & & \\ -\rho & 1+\rho^2 & -\rho & & & \\ & -\rho & 1+\rho^2 & -\rho & & \\ & & -\rho & 1+\rho^2 & -\rho & \\ & & & -\rho & 1+\rho^2 & -\rho \\ & & & & -\rho & 1 \end{pmatrix}
