number of datapoints \( N \). For \( \sigma^2 \), we work instead with the sum of squares: the posterior sum of squares, \( \tilde{\nu}\tilde{\tau}^2 \), is the prior sum of squares \( \tilde{\nu}\tilde{\tau}^2 \) plus the data sum of squares, \( \sum_{n=1}^N (y_n - \overline{y})^2 \), plus a term due to the discrepancy between the prior mean \( \tilde{m} \) and the MLE \( \overline{y} \). The strength of this posterior, \( \tilde{\nu} \), is the prior strength \( \tilde{\nu} \) plus the number of datapoints \( N \);
The posterior marginal for \( \sigma^2 \) is just

\( p(\sigma^2|\mathcal{D}) = \int p(\mu, \sigma^2|\mathcal{D}) d\mu = \chi^{-2}(\sigma^2 \mid \bar{\nu}, \tilde{\tau}^2) \) (3.125)

with the posterior mean given by \( \mathbb{E}[\sigma^2|\mathcal{D}] = \frac{\tilde{\nu}}{\tilde{\nu}-2} \tilde{\tau}^2 \).

The posterior marginal for \( \mu \) has a Student distribution, which follows from the fact that the Student distribution is a (scaled) mixture of Gaussians:

\( p(\mu|\mathcal{D}) = \int p(\mu, \sigma^2|\mathcal{D}) d\sigma^2 = \mathcal{T}(\mu \mid \widehat{m}, \tilde{\tau}^2 / \hat{\kappa}, \tilde{\nu}) \) (3.126)

with the posterior mean given by \( \mathbb{E}[\mu|\mathcal{D}] = \widehat{m} \).

# 3.4.3.4 Posterior of \( \mu \) and \( \sigma^2 \): uninformative prior

If we “know nothing” about the parameters a priori, we can use an uninformative prior. We discuss how to create such priors in Section 3.5. A common approach is to use a Jeffreys prior. In Section 3.5.2.3, we show that the Jeffreys prior for a location and scale parameter has the form

\( p(\mu, \sigma^2) \propto p(\mu)p(\sigma^2) \propto \sigma^{-2} \) (3.127)

We can simulate this with a conjugate prior by using

\( p(\mu, \sigma^2) = NI\chi^2(\mu, \sigma^2 \mid \widehat{m}=0, \tilde{\kappa}=0, \tilde{\nu}=-1, \tilde{\tau}^2=0) \) (3.128)

With this prior, the posterior has the form

\( p(\mu, \sigma^2|\mathcal{D}) = NI\chi^2(\mu, \sigma^2 \mid \widehat{m}=\overline{y}, \widehat{\kappa}=N, \tilde{\nu}=N-1, \tilde{\tau}^2=s^2) \) (3.129)

where

\( s^2 \triangleq \frac{1}{N-1} \sum_{n=1}^N (y_n - \overline{y})^2 = \frac{N}{N-1} \hat{\sigma}^2_{\text{mle}} \) (3.130)

\( s \) is known as the sample standard deviation. Hence the marginal posterior for the mean is given by

\( p(\mu|\mathcal{D}) = \mathcal{T}(\mu|\overline{y}, \frac{s^2}{N}, N-1) = \mathcal{T}(\mu|\overline{y}, \frac{\sum_{n=1}^N (y_n - \overline{y})^2}{N(N-1)}, N-1) \) (3.131)

Thus the posterior variance of \( \mu \) is

\( \mathbb{V}[\mu|\mathcal{D}] = \frac{\tilde{\nu}}{\tilde{\nu}-2} \tilde{\tau}^2 = \frac{N-1}{N-3} \frac{s^2}{N} \rightarrow \frac{s^2}{N} \) (3.132)
