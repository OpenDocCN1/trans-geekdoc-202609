```
Algorithm 6.1: Forward-mode automatic differentiation (JVP) on chains
input: \( f : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m \) as a chain composition \( f = f_T \circ \cdots \circ f_1 \)
input: linearization point \( \boldsymbol{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n \) and input perturbation \( \boldsymbol{v} \in \mathbb{R}^n \)
\( \boldsymbol{x}_0, \boldsymbol{v}_0 := \boldsymbol{x}, \boldsymbol{v} \)
for \( t := 1, \dots, T \) do
   \( \boldsymbol{x}_t := f_t(\boldsymbol{x}_{t-1}) \)
   \( \boldsymbol{v}_t := \partial f_t(\boldsymbol{x}_{t-1})[\boldsymbol{v}_{t-1}] \)
output: \( \boldsymbol{x}_T \), equal to \( f(\boldsymbol{x}) \)
output: \( \boldsymbol{v}_T \), equal to \( \partial f(\boldsymbol{x})[\boldsymbol{v}] \)
```

By contrast, we can transpose Equation (6.29) to consider a VJP against an output perturbation \( \boldsymbol{u} \in \mathbb{R}^m \) :

\( \partial f(\boldsymbol{x})^\top[\boldsymbol{u}] = \partial a(\boldsymbol{x})^\top [\partial b(a(\boldsymbol{x}))^\top [\partial c(b(a(\boldsymbol{x})))^\top[\boldsymbol{u}]]] \)

Transposition reverses the Jacobian maps relative to their order in Equation (6.29), and now the bracketed evaluation corresponds to reverse-mode automatic differentiation. To carry out this VJP, we can compute the original chain prefixes \( \boldsymbol{x} \), \( a(\boldsymbol{x}) \), and \( b(a(\boldsymbol{x})) \) first, and then read them in reverse as successive linearization points:

\( \partial c(b(a(\boldsymbol{x})))^\top, \partial b(a(\boldsymbol{x}))^\top, \partial a(\boldsymbol{x})^\top \)

Extending this idea to arbitrary chain compositions gives Algorithm 6.2.

```
Algorithm 6.2: Reverse-mode automatic differentiation (VJP) on chains
input: \( f : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m \) as a chain composition \( f = f_T \circ \cdots \circ f_1 \)
input: linearization point \( \boldsymbol{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n \) and output perturbation \( \boldsymbol{u} \in \mathbb{R}^m \)
\( \boldsymbol{x}_0 := \boldsymbol{x} \)
for \( t := 1, \dots, T \) do
   \( \boldsymbol{x}_t := f_t(\boldsymbol{x}_{t-1}) \)
\( \boldsymbol{u}_T := \boldsymbol{u} \)
for \( t := T, \dots, 1 \) do
   \( \boldsymbol{u}_{t-1} := \partial f_t(\boldsymbol{x}_{t-1})^\top[\boldsymbol{u}_t] \)
output: \( \boldsymbol{x}_T \), equal to \( f(\boldsymbol{x}) \)
output: \( \boldsymbol{u}_0 \), equal to \( \partial f(\boldsymbol{x})^\top[\boldsymbol{u}] \)
```
