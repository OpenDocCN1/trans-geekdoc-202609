$$\mathcal{K}(r) = \mathcal{K}_1(r) + \mathcal{K}_2(r) + \mathcal{K}_3(r) + \mathcal{K}_4(r)$$ (18.189)

where $\mathcal{K}_i(t, t') = \mathcal{K}_i(t - t')$ for the $i$'th kernel.

To capture the long term smooth rising trend, we let $\mathcal{K}_1$ be a squared exponential (SE) kernel, where $\theta_0$ is the amplitude and $\theta_1$ is the length scale:

$$\mathcal{K}_1(r) = \theta_0^2 \exp \left( - \frac{r^2}{2 \theta_1^2} \right)$$ (18.190)

To model the periodicity, we can use a periodic or exp-sine-squared kernel from Equation (18.18) with a period of 1 year. However, since it is not clear if the seasonal trend is exactly periodic, we multiply this periodic kernel with another SE kernel to allow for a decay away from periodicity; the result is $\mathcal{K}_2$, where $\theta_2$ is the magnitude, $\theta_3$ is the decay time for the periodic component, $\theta_4 = 1$ is the period, and $\theta_5$ is the smoothness of the periodic component.

$$\mathcal{K}_2(r) = \theta_2^2 \exp \left( - \frac{r^2}{2 \theta_3^2} - \theta_5 \sin^2 \left( \frac{\pi r}{\theta_4} \right) \right)$$ (18.191)

To model the (small) medium term irregulariritires, we use a rational quadratic kernel (Equation (18.20)):

$$\mathcal{K}_3(r) = \theta_6^2 \left[ 1 + \frac{r^2}{2 \theta_7^2 \theta_8} \right]^{-\theta_8}$$ (18.192)

where $\theta_6$ is the magnitude, $\theta_7$ is the typical length scale, and $\theta_8$ is the shape parameter.

The magnitude of the independent noise can be incorporated into the observation noise of the likelihood function. For the correlated noise, we use another SE kernel:

$$\mathcal{K}_4(r) = \theta_9^2 \exp \left( - \frac{r^2}{2 \theta_{10}^2} \right)$$ (18.193)

where $\theta_9$ is the magnitude of the correlated noise, and $\theta_{10}$ is the length scale. (Note that the combination of $\mathcal{K}_1$ and $\mathcal{K}_4$ is non-identifiable, but this does not affect predictions.)
