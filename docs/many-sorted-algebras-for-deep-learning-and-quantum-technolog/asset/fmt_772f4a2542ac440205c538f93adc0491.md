
## Universal quantum gate set

Intuitively a universal quantum gate set (`UQGS`) is a fixed, finite number of unitary operators that, when applied in succession, can approximate any unitary operation in `SU(2, C)`. The operators are applied in composition, from left to right. However, they are illustrated as the concatenation of strings of operations, labeled in order. Let `G = {g[1], g[2], …, g[m]}` be a finite subset of `SU(2, C)`. A word of length `n` is `w[n] = g[1] g[2] …, g[n]`, with `g[i]` in `G` and `n` greater or equal to 1. The set of all words from `G` with length `n` or less is denoted by `G[n]`. The union of all `G[n]` for `n < ∞` is denoted by `<G>`. A set of gates is `UQGS` whenever `<G>` is dense in `SU(2, C)`. That is, for any `U` in `SU(2, C)`, and `ϵ > 0`, there exists a string of operators `g` in `<G>` such that the distance `d(U, g)` is less than `ϵ`. The metric is induced by the trace norm, `‖A‖ = tr[(A* A)^(1/2)]`. The trace norm satisfies the equational identities and inequalities:
1. Triangle Inequality: `‖A + B‖ ≤ ‖A‖ + ‖B‖`.
2. Submultiplicative: `‖A B‖ ≤ ‖A‖ ‖B‖`.
3. Unitary Invariance: `‖U A V‖ = ‖A‖` for all unitary operators `U` and `V`.

The operator norm is also used; it is `‖A‖ = sup |A v|`, for `‖v‖ = 1`. For `U` and `V` in `U(2, C)`, `U` approximates `V` that means `d(U, V) = sup ‖(U − V) v‖ < ϵ`, for `‖v‖ = 1`. For two strings of quantum gates `U = U[1] U[2] …, U[n]` and `V = V[1] V[2] …, V[n]`, then `d(U, V) ≤ Σ d(U[i], V[i])`. This follows using `n = 2` and can be proved by mathematical induction. For `n = 2`, `d(U[1] U[2], V[1] V[2]) = sup ‖(U[2] U[1] – V[2] V[1]) v‖ = sup ‖(U[2] U[1] – V[2] U[1] + V[2] U[1] – V[2] V[1]) v‖ ≤ sup ‖(U[2] U[1] – V[2] U[1]) v‖ + sup ‖(V[2] U[1] – V[2] V[1]) v‖ = sup ‖(U[2] – V[2]) U[1] v‖ + sup ‖V[2] (U[1] – V[1]) v‖ = d(U[2], V[2]) + d(U[1], V[1])`. Note that a maximum could be used here in place of `sup` and that the order of operation of operators is backward from the string notation.

A most popular `UQGS` for `SU(2, C)` consists of the Hadamard gate `H` and the `π/8` gate `T`. The proof makes use of the Pauli rotation operators. [Section 16.3](#Ch016_291-305_B9780443136979000060.xhtml#s0020) illustrates that the Hadamard gate is a product of Pauli rotation operators. Also, `T = R[Z](π/4)`. The `T` gate and the `HTH` string of gates are successively utilized in showing `{H, T}` is a `UQGS`. An important identity in producing the approximation with any degree of accuracy is the identity `T H T H = R[Z](π/4) R[X](π/4)`. More general `UQGS` is explained in [Lloyd (1995)](#Ch016_291-305_B9780443136979000060.xhtml#bib4).

## 16.7 The Haar measure

Haar measure `u` is best understood as a left `G` invariant measure ([Nachbin, 1965](#Ch016_291-305_B9780443136979000060.xhtml#bib5)). Here `G` is a locally compact topological group. For the binary operation of multiplication, an invariant measure is such that `u(x V) = u(V)`, where `x` is an element of the group `G` and `V` is a Borel measurable set. Appendix A.2 provides an outline of relevant measure theoretic concepts.

Historically, Haar measure was first expressed as an approximation of an absolute measure based on a unit measure. The unit measure for the Haar measure uses `E` as a Borel subset of `G`, and `V` is a nonempty open subset of `G`. Then let `(E: V)` denote the smallest number of left translates of `V` that cover `E`. Thus, `(E: V) = inf {cardinality of A such that E is a subset of the union of x V, for x in A, A a subset of G}`. Hence `(E: V)` can be viewed as a relative measure of `E`.

Fix a precompact open subset `Eo`, and assign its measure as one. Precompact means that the closure of the set is compact. Then consider as an approximation to the absolute measure of `E` the following: `(E: V) / (Eo: V)`. Notice for all `x` in `G`, `(E: V) / (Eo: V) = (E x: V) / (Eo: V)`, and this operation is left `G` invariant. Taking the limit as `V` shrinks to the identity element shows that the Haar measure is defined as the limit. So, `u(E) = limit [(E: V) / (Eo: V)]`, as `V → I`.

For `G`, a locally compact topological group, Haar’s theorem states that there is up to a positive multiplicative scalar a countably additive measure on Borel subsets of `G` such that
1. The measure is left invariant as mentioned earlier.
2. For a compact subset `K` of `G`, `u(K) < ∞`.
3. The measure is both outer regular on Borel sets in `G` and inner regular on open sets `U` in `G`, respectively, `u(S) = inf {u(U) such that S is a subset of an open set, U in G}`, and `u(U) = sup {u(K) such that K is a subset of U and K is compact}`.

### Example 16.14

Consider the carrier set for `G` to be the group of positive real numbers `R+`, under multiplication, with the usual topology on the real line. Then for any Borel subset, `B`, in `R`, a Haar measure can be created using the usual Cauchy-Riemann, Riemann, or Lebesgue integral: `u(B) = ∫[B] 1/x dx`. For any Borel set, say the interval `[2, 4]`, the corresponding measure `u([2, 4]) = ln(4) − ln(2) = ln(2)`. Also for any positive real number in `R+`, say three, and multiplying the interval by three, then the Haar measure `u([6, 12]) = ln(12) − ln(6) = ln(2)` again. This illustrates the invariance of the Haar measure using the multiplicative group involving elements in `R+`.

## 16.8 Solovay–Kitaev theorem

The notion of unitary operators in `SU(2, C)` being approximated by a string of unitary operators, `S[1] S[2] … S[n]`, is described in [Section 16.6](#Ch016_291-305_B9780443136979000060.xhtml#s0035). This string forms a `UQGS` and was explained with emphasis on `{H, T}`, the Hadamard, and the `π/8` gate set. In this section, the degree of closeness in the approximation is related to the number of elements `n`, in the string of unitary operators.

The Solovay–Kitaev theorem (`SK`) gives a constructive procedure to approximate arbitrary unitary 2x2 matrices with determinant one by a string of physically realizable unitary gates. The approximation is to within `ϵ > 0`. The string is a finite product of gates from a universal gate set `S`. The `SK` result shows that the length of the string of gate products needed is of the order `O(log⁴(1/ϵ))`. Required, for these approximations to hold, the set of gates `S` must be dense in `G`. More precisely, consider a finite subset `S`, of `SU(2, C)`, which contains inverses and is dense in `SU(2, C)`. Then there is a constant `c`, in `[1, 4]`, such that for any such `S` and `ϵ > 0`, `n` can be chosen where `n = O(log^c (1/ϵ))` such that `S^n` is an `ϵ` net for `SU(2, C)`. For any `U` in `SU(2, C)`, there is a string of gates `S[1] S[2] … S[n]` in `S^n`, where `d(S[1] S[2] … S[n], U) < ϵ`. The distance metric `d` is induced by the trace norm. That is, `d(A, B) = ‖A − B‖`. So for `A, B` in `SU(2, C)`, `‖A‖ = Tr[(A A*)^(1/2)]`, this is also equal to the sum of the absolute value of the two eigenvalues of `A`. In short, an `ϵ` net for `SU(2, C)` means that `SU(2, C)` is a finite subset of the union of open balls of radius `ϵ` centered about strings `S[1] S[2] … S[n]` in `S^n`. Thus, for every unitary operator in `SU(2, C)`, there exists a string of gates from `S^n` that come `ϵ` close to producing the same results.
