The spectral theorem for self-adjoint bounded operators on a Hilbert space is almost exactly the same as the one mentioned right before the last example. The major difference is that in place of the saltus, a measure is employed. The measure is described in detail in Appendix A.2. As in the previous cases, the integrals are the Stieltjes integrals over the spectrum. The need for a spectral measure is that the continuous spectrum, as well as limit points arriving from clusters of discrete spectral points, can also be included in the spectral integration. This is somewhat like the integration process illustrated in Example 13.29.
For T self-adjoint and bounded with spectrum spT, there corresponds a spectral measure u. It is such that, for all v and w in the Hilbert space H, the inner product  < *v*, *T**w* >  = ∫[*s**p**T*]*λ**d* < *v*, *u*[*λ*]*w*\>![Equation](Images/F000278si310.png).
Generalization of the aforementioned concepts leads to the projective-valued measure (PVM). These measures have values that are self-adjoint projections. As usual, measures are defined on a measurable space. The spectral theorem involving PVM associates a PVM with any self-adjoint operator T in B(H,H). Here, *T* = ∫*λ**d**E*[*λ*]![Equation](Images/F000278si311.png). The integral is understood as a Stieltjes or Lebesgue integral on the real line. A rigorous well-explained description of PVM, along with direct integrals, can be found in [Hall (2013)](#Ch019_349-364_B9780443136979000278.xhtml#bib5).
