# Chapter 13
## Spectra for operators

### Abstract

The criteria for showing the existence of a spectrum are reviewed. Spectral applications are provided of the Lumer-Phillips theorem for adjoint operators and dual operators in Banach space. Descriptions, as well as examples, are given for the point spectrum, the continuous spectrum, along with the residual spectrum in both Banach and Hilbert spaces. The Hellinger-Toeplitz theorem is stated and illustrated for unbounded operators, including the momentum and distance operators. The numerical range is described in reference to spectral values along with dissipative and accretive operators. Conditions for which symmetric operators are self-adjoint are provided. The approximate eigenvalue concept is described along with applications to compact operators. The Cauchy integral theorem is utilized for determining the resolvent set. The chapter ends with an introduction to spectral measures and spectral theory. Additionally, in the last section, an example of Stieltjes integration is illustrated. Also, projective-valued measures are described in reference to the spectral theorems.

#### Keywords

Lumer-Phillips theorem; Hellinger-Toeplitz theorem; one-parameter semigroups; dissipative operator; accretive operator; Stieltjes integral; residual spectrum; approximate spectrum; saltus

This section is a continuation of [Sections 8.5](#Ch008_145-164_B9780443136979000059.xhtml#s0030) and [8.6](#Ch008_145-164_B9780443136979000059.xhtml#s0035). The necessary and sufficient condition is repeated here. For an operator `T`, in `B(H,H)`, `spT={c, such that c in C, and (c I−T) is not invertible iff both 1) and 2) hold}`:

1. 1) The image of `T` is dense in `H`, and
2. 2) There is a scalar, `a> 0` such that for all `v` in `H` `‖Tv‖>a‖v‖`.

In the second reference given earlier, criterion (1) is referred to as weakly onto, and the second criterion number (2) is implied to be strongly one to one.

For `T`, in `B(H,H)`, it was previously shown that the spectrum of `T` is nonempty and forms a compact set in `C`. Moreover, the resolvent set, `RsT`, is an open set in `C`. This set `RsT` = {`λ`![Equation](Images/F000138si3.png), where `λ`![Equation](Images/F000138si4.png) is in `C`, and such that (`T−λI`) is one to one and onto, (`T−λI`) ^(−1)![Equation](Images/F000138si5.png) is bounded}. The boundedness of the inverse follows also from the bounded inverse theorem. This theorem can be found in Appendix A.6. Additionally, a partition of the `spT` is provided by three disjoint sets `sppT`, `spcT`, and `sprT`. Briefly, the spectra will be described for `T`, a bounded operator on a complex Banach space, `X` differing from `{0}`. The inner product is not necessary for this development; thus a Banach space is utilized. Numorous examples are provided, and in the unbounded case they are presented in a formal manner. Domain issues are not considered.
