
$\\begin{array}{r} \\left. NC\\;\\bot\\; PA\\mspace{500mu}\\Longrightarrow\\mspace{500mu}\\frac{NC}{\\parallel NC \\parallel} \\cdot \\frac{PA}{\\parallel PA \\parallel} = 0 \\right. \\\\ \\end{array}$

The lines OA, OP, and ON represent the unit-normalized embedding vectors for the anchor (`f_a`), positive (`f_p`), and negative (`f_n`), respectively. C is the midpoint of PA and OC represents the average embedding vector `f_c` (not unit-normalized).

Analyzing this in more detail, it turns out that incorporating the cosine similarity between the vectors Anchor-Positive and Anchor-Negative helps achieve this objective. (We are skipping the proof for brevity, please refer to [Mohan et al. (2020)](#Ch004_59-79_S0169716123000032.xhtml#bb0050) for more details.) This can be written as

$\\begin{array}{r} {Cos(AN,AP) = \\frac{1 - \\mathbf{f}\_{p} \\cdot \\mathbf{f}\_{a}}{\\parallel \\mathbf{f}\_{n} - \\mathbf{f}\_{a} \\parallel \\parallel \\mathbf{f}\_{p} - \\mathbf{f}\_{a} \\parallel}} \\\\ \\end{array}$

![si54_e](images/S0169716123000032/si54_e.png)  (32)

We find that minimizing this cosine similarity term helps to satisfy the condition in Eq. ([31](#Ch004_59-79_S0169716123000032.xhtml#fo0165)). Since the current metric learning losses lack explicit enforcement of the orthogonality of the negative sample with respect to the anchor-positive pair, when incorporated as a regularization term, it helps create a much more robust embedding space. The following equations provide definitions and the intuition on how easily this regularization term can be adapted into any standard metric learning loss function. The modified triplet loss can be written as:

$\\begin{matrix} {\\mathcal{L}\_{apn} =} & {{\\parallel \\mathbf{f}\_{a} - \\mathbf{f}\_{p} \\parallel}^{2} - {\\parallel \\mathbf{f}\_{a} - \\mathbf{f}\_{n} \\parallel}^{2} + \\alpha - \\gamma\\; Cos(\\mathbf{f}\_{n} - \\mathbf{f}\_{a},\\mathbf{f}\_{p} - \\mathbf{f}\_{a})} \\\\ \\end{matrix}$

![si55_e](images/S0169716123000032/si55_e.png)  (33)

Similarly, the modified Multi-Similarity loss can be written as:

$\\begin{matrix} \\mathcal{L} & {= \\frac{1}{B}\\sum\\limits\_{i = 1}^{B}\\left\\{ {\\frac{1}{\\alpha}\\;\\text{log}\\left\\lbrack {1 + \\sum\\limits\_{}e^{- \\alpha(S\_{ip} - \\lambda)}} \\right\\rbrack} \\right.} \\\\ & {\\mspace{144mu}\\left. {+ \\frac{1}{\\beta}\\;\\text{log}\\left\\lbrack {1 + \\sum\\limits\_{}e^{\\beta(S\_{in} - \\lambda - \\gamma\\; Cos(\\mathbf{f}\_{n} - \\mathbf{f}\_{a},\\mathbf{f}\_{p} - \\mathbf{f}\_{a}))}} \\right\\rbrack} \\right\\}} \\\\ \\end{matrix}$

![si56_e](images/S0169716123000032/si56_e.png)  (34)

In the case of Multi-Similarity loss, the hardest positive to the anchor is used in the regularization term.

In the case of Proxy-NCA ([Movshovitz-Attias et al., 2017](#Ch004_59-79_S0169716123000032.xhtml#bb0055)), the direction of forces is modified to push negative samples in the direction of their corresponding proxies. It is given by :

$\\begin{array}{r} {\\mathcal{L} = \\sum\\limits\_{a \\subset N} - \\text{log}\\left( \\frac{e^{( - {\\parallel \\mathbf{f}\_{a} - p(a) \\parallel}^{2})}}{\\sum\\limits\_{n}e^{\\lbrack{- {\\parallel \\mathbf{f}\_{a} - p(n) \\parallel}^{2} - \\gamma\\; Cos(p(n) - \\mathbf{f}\_{a},p(a) - \\mathbf{f}\_{a})}\\rbrack}} \\right)} \\\\ \\end{array}$

##  6: Conclusion

DML approaches provide objective functions that help to create highly discriminable embedding spaces. In this chapter, we have provided a brief review of some of the important DML methods and elaborated on the uniqueness of each approach compared to each other. Currently, owing to the recent advancements in the area of Cross-modal retrieval methods such as CLIP ([Radford et al., 2021](#Ch004_59-79_S0169716123000032.xhtml#bb0060)), DML methods which primarily focused on a single modality are being adapted toward multimodal representation learning. Recent works, such as [Wei et al. (2020)](#Ch004_59-79_S0169716123000032.xhtml#bb0095) and [Jawade et al. (2023)](#Ch004_59-79_S0169716123000032.xhtml#bb0030), show the ability of these methods to create more robust feature representations for multimodal tasks such as text–image retrieval.
