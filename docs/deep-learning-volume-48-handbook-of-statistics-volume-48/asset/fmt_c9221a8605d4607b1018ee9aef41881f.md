
The tasks in facial attribute prediction involve predicting an attribute corresponding to the person in the face image. These attributes are provided as ground truth annotations in different datasets and may be binary in nature. Some examples of binary attributes include `**`**`varName`**`**, smiling, blonde hair, and wearing makeup. Other attributes can correspond to facial expressions such as happy, sad, or disgust. Some datasets include sensitive attributes such as gender or ethnic subgroups, which have also been used for training and evaluation in the literature. The performance of attribute prediction is measured via the prediction accuracy corresponding to the attribute. In case of certain databases, the average prediction accuracy across all attributes is also reported.

##  3: Facial analysis databases for bias study

Several facial analysis databases have been proposed in the literature. Popular databases employed for studying face recognition and attribute prediction have been summarized in [Table 3](#Ch007_169-221_S0169716123000020.xhtml#t0020). Studying bias in facial analysis requires databases that contain annotations corresponding to demographic subgroups such as gender, skin tone, and ethnicity. Initially, existing databases with demographic information were used to study the effect of bias. While these datasets were not developed with the intention of ensuring fairness, they are utilized for fairness analysis. However, these databases often consist of imbalanced data distribution across ethnic and gender subgroups. Some of them are listed below.

*   • [Rawls and Ricanek (2009)](#Ch007_169-221_S0169716123000020.xhtml#bb0675) proposed the MORPH database with subjects belonging to different gender subgroups and ethnicity within the age range of 16–77 years. It is one of the largest databases for studying the effect of age on face recognition algorithms.

*   • The Indian Movie Face Database (IMFDB) proposed by [Setty et al. (2013)](#Ch007_169-221_S0169716123000020.xhtml#bb0740) consists of face images of Indian actors and actresses. The database is annotated with age, gender, pose, expression, and the amount of occlusion present in an image.

*   • [Eidinger et al. (2014)](#Ch007_169-221_S0169716123000020.xhtml#bb0250) proposed the Adience database containing face images labeled with age and gender information. The database is mainly used for age estimation.

*   • The Cross-Age Celebrity Dataset (CACD) proposed by [Chen et al. (2015)](#Ch007_169-221_S0169716123000020.xhtml#bb0140) contains images of celebrities with ages ranging from 16 to 62 years.

*   • The LFWA ([Huang et al., 2008](#Ch007_169-221_S0169716123000020.xhtml#bb0325)) and CelebA ([Liu et al., 2015](#Ch007_169-221_S0169716123000020.xhtml#bb0495)) databases contain 40 annotated facial attributes and are commonly used for attribute prediction tasks. These databases are used for recent research that focuses on analyzing the performance of attribute prediction across protected attributes (male and young), followed by developing algorithms for bias mitigation ([Tan et al., 2020](#Ch007_169-221_S0169716123000020.xhtml#bb0780)).

*   • The AgeDb ([Moschoglou et al., 2017](#Ch007_169-221_S0169716123000020.xhtml#bb0555)) and All-Age Faces (AAF) ([Cheng et al., 2019](#Ch007_169-221_S0169716123000020.xhtml#bb0145)) databases are used for analyzing the performance of algorithms across age subgroups.

*   • The IJB-C database ([Maze et al., 2018](#Ch007_169-221_S0169716123000020.xhtml#bb0535)) is one of the largest databases with skin-tone information on the Fitzpatrick scale used for studying bias.

With the increased attention on understanding different aspects of bias in model prediction and developing fair algorithms, multiple databases have been proposed specifically for studying bias. Some of these databases are listed below.

*   • [Zhang et al. (2017)](#Ch007_169-221_S0169716123000020.xhtml#bb0935) proposed the UTKFace database with more than 20K images having variations in pose, illumination, expression, occlusion, and resolution.

*   • [Buolamwini and Gebru (2018)](#Ch007_169-221_S0169716123000020.xhtml#bb0110) proposed the Pilot Parliaments Benchmark (PPB) database for studying the effect of bias in gender classifiers w.r.t different skin tones. The PPB database consists of 1270 subjects from three African and three European countries. The authors labeled the skin tones of the subjects using the Fitzpatrick six-point labeling system.

*   • Racial Faces in the Wild (RFW) database proposed by [Wang et al. (2019a)](#Ch007_169-221_S0169716123000020.xhtml#bb0850) is an unconstrained testing database for studying racial bias in face recognition. The database consists of Caucasian, Indian, Asian, and African racial subgroups.

*   • [Wang and Deng (2020)](#Ch007_169-221_S0169716123000020.xhtml#bb0830) proposed four different training databases, namely BUPT-Balancedface (race-balanced), BUPT-Globalface (racial distribution approximately equal to the real distribution of the world's population), BUPT-Transferface containing labeled data for Caucasian and unlabeled data for other subgroups (created for unsupervised domain adaptation), and MS1M-wo-RFW containing non-overlapping subjects of MS-Celeb-1M database for studying the effect of race on face recognition algorithms.

*   • [Morales et al. (2020)](#Ch007_169-221_S0169716123000020.xhtml#bb0550) proposed the DiveFace database with annotations for gender and ethnicity (Caucasian, European, Asian) subgroups.
