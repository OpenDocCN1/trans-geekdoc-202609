
[Lin et al. (2020)](#Ch003_29-58_S0169716123000019.xhtml#bb0230) investigate whether or not leveraging the underlying manifold information can boost model robustness, in particular against novel adversarial attacks. They consider the scenario when the manifold information of the underlying data is available. First, an “On-manifold ImageNet” (OM-ImageNet) dataset where all the samples lie exactly on a low-dimensional manifold is constructed. This is achieved by first training a StyleGAN on a subset of ImageNet natural images, and then projecting the samples onto the learned manifold. With this dataset, they show that on-manifold adversarial training (i.e., adversarial training in the latent space) could not defend against standard off-manifold attacks and vice versa. They proposed dual manifold adversarial training (DMAT), which mixes both off-manifold and on-manifold AT (see [Fig. 2](#Ch003_29-58_S0169716123000019.xhtml#f0015)). AT in the image space (i.e., off-manifold AT) helps improve the robustness of the model against L[p] attacks, while AT in the latent space (i.e., on-manifold AT) boosts the robustness of the model against unseen non-L[p] attacks.

####  3.2.1: On-manifold ImageNet

One major difficulty in investigating the potential benefit of manifold information in general cases is the inability to obtain such information exactly. For approximate manifolds, the effect of the distribution shift between ℳ![si15_e](images/S0169716123000019/si15_e.png) and $\\overset{-}{\\mathcal{M}}$![si16_e](images/S0169716123000019/si16_e.png) is difficult to quantify, leading to inconclusive evaluations. To address the issue, [Lin et al. (2020)](#Ch003_29-58_S0169716123000019.xhtml#bb0230) propose a novel dataset, called On-Manifold ImageNet (OM-ImageNet), which consists of images that lie exactly on-manifold.

The OM-ImageNet is built upon the Mixed-10 dataset introduced in the robustness library ([Engstrom et al., 2019](#Ch003_29-58_S0169716123000019.xhtml#bb0100)), which consists of images from 10 superclasses of ImageNet. A total of 69,480 image-label pairs are manually selected as 𝒟[*t**r**]^(*o*) = {(*x*[*i*], *y*[*i*])}[*i* = 1]^(*N*)![si17_e](images/S0169716123000019/si17_e.png) and another disjoint 7200 image-label pairs as 𝒟[*t**e**]^(*o*) = {(*x*[*j*], *y*[*j*])}[*j* = 1]^(*M*)![si18_e](images/S0169716123000019/si18_e.png), both with balanced classes. The approach presented in [Lin et al. (2020)](#Ch003_29-58_S0169716123000019.xhtml#bb0230) first trains a StyleGAN ([Karras et al., 2019](#Ch003_29-58_S0169716123000019.xhtml#bb0190)) to characterize the underlying image manifold of 𝒟[*t**r**]^(*o*)![si19_e](images/S0169716123000019/si19_e.png). Formally, the StyleGAN consists of a mapping function *h*:𝒵→𝒲![si20_e](images/S0169716123000019/si20_e.png) and a synthesis network *g̃*:𝒲→𝒳![si21_e](images/S0169716123000019/si21_e.png). The mapping function takes a latent code *z* and outputs a style code *w* in an intermediate latent space 𝒲![si22_e](images/S0169716123000019/si22_e.png). Then, the synthesis network takes the style code and produces a natural-looking image *g̃*(*w*)![si23_e](images/S0169716123000019/si23_e.png). The authors of [Lin et al. (2020)](#Ch003_29-58_S0169716123000019.xhtml#bb0230) then follow ([Abdal et al., 2019](#Ch003_29-58_S0169716123000019.xhtml#bb0010)) and consider the extended latent space of StyleGAN. In [Abdal et al. (2019)](#Ch003_29-58_S0169716123000019.xhtml#bb0010), it has been shown that embedding images into the extended latent space is easier than 𝒵![si24_e](images/S0169716123000019/si24_e.png) or 𝒲![si22_e](images/S0169716123000019/si22_e.png) space. Therefore, in the following, *g*:𝒲^+→𝒳![si26_e](images/S0169716123000019/si26_e.png) is the generator function which approximates the image manifold for 𝒟[*t**r**]^(*o*)![si19_e](images/S0169716123000019/si19_e.png).

In order to obtain images that are completely on-manifold, each image *x*[*i*] in 𝒟[*t**r**]^(*o*)![si19_e](images/S0169716123000019/si19_e.png) and 𝒟[*t**e**]^(*o*)![si29_e](images/S0169716123000019/si29_e.png) is projected onto the learned manifold by solving for its latent representation *w*[*i*] ([Abdal et al., 2019](#Ch003_29-58_S0169716123000019.xhtml#bb0010)). A weighted combination of the learned perceptual image patch similarity (LPIPS) ([Zhang et al., 2018a](#Ch003_29-58_S0169716123000019.xhtml#bb0480)) and *L*[1] loss is used to measure the closeness between *g*(*w*) and *x*[*i*]. LPIPS is shown to be a more suitable measure for perceptual similarity than conventional metrics, and its combination with *L*[1] or *L*[2] loss has been shown to be successful for inferring the latent representation of GANs. This strategy is adopted to derive the latent representation as in:

$\\begin{array}{r} {w\_{i} = \\underset{w}{\\arg\\min}\\;\\text{LPIPS}(g(w),x\_{i}) + \\parallel g(w) - x\_{i} \\parallel\_{1}.} \\\\ \\end{array}$

![si30_e](images/S0169716123000019/si30_e.png)  (4)

In summary, the resulting on-manifold training and test sets can be represented by 𝒟[*t**r**] = {(*w*[*i*], *g*(*w*[*i*]), *x*[*i*], *y*[*i*])}[*i* = 1]^(*N*)![si31_e](images/S0169716123000019/si31_e.png) and 𝒟[*t**e**] = {(*w*[*j*], *g*(*w*[*j*]), *x*[*j*], *y*[*j*])}[*j* = 1]^(*M*)![si32_e](images/S0169716123000019/si32_e.png), where *N* = 69, 480 and *M* = 7200. The total number of categories is 10. Notice that for OM-ImageNet, the underlying manifold information is exact, which is given by {*g*(*w*), *w* ∈ 𝒲^+}![si33_e](images/S0169716123000019/si33_e.png). Sample on-manifold images *g*(*w*[*i*]) from OM-ImageNet are presented in [Fig. 3](#Ch003_29-58_S0169716123000019.xhtml#f0020). On-manifold images in OM-ImageNet have diverse textures, object sizes, lightening, and poses, which is suitable for investigating the potential benefits of using manifold information in more general scenarios.
