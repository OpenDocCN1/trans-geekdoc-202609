
When a system has areas of technological risk and uncertainty, however, requirements become much more of a challenge. It is traditional to think of requirements as independent of the design or implementation of the systems. Requirements are intended to be statements of need, not of design. It becomes difficult to write requirements, beyond the obvious and trivial, without assuming some aspects of the design of the system. Requirements provided to a government contractor for the construction of a defense system often run into the many thousands of shall-statements. It's clear that the government has something very specific in mind, but the provided requirements statements masquerade as pure statements of need, not of design and thus they fail to communicate all of thes with little uncertainty and little risk of change later.
