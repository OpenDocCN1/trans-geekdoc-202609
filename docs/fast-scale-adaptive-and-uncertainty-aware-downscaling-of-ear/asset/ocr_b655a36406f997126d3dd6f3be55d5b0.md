# Network architectures and training

We use a two-dimensional U-Net$^{18,35}$ from the Diffusers library to train both score and consistency networks from scratch, with four down- and upsampling layers. For these four layers, we use convolutions with 128, 128, 256 and 256 channels, and 3 $\times$ 3 kernels, sigmoid linear unit activations, group normalization and an attention layer at the architecture bottleneck. The network has, in total, around 27M trainable parameters.

We train the core network with the Adam optimizer$^{46}$ for 200 epochs, with a batch size of 1, a learning rate of 1 $\times$ 10$^{2}$ and an exponential moving average over the model weights with a decay rate of 0.999 (Supplementary Information).

The CM model is trained for 150 epochs$^{23}$ with the RAdam optimizer$^{47}$ and the same batch size, learning rate and exponential moving average schedule (with an initial decay rate of $\alpha_0=0.9$) as the score network. We find that the loss decreases in a stable way throughout the training (Supplementary Fig. 1). The training of 150 epochs takes around six and a half days for the CM and four and a half days for the SDE on an NVIDIA G102 32 GB graphics processing unit. A summary of the training hyperparameters is given in Supplementary Table 1.

# Scale-consistent downscaling

As shown in other work$^{45,48}$, adding Gaussian noise with a chosen variance to an image (or fluid dynamical snapshot) results in removing spatial patterns up to a specific spatial scale associated with the amount of added noise. The trained generative model can then replace the noise with spatial patterns learned from the training data up to the chosen spatial scale.

In principle, the spatial scale can be chosen depending on the given downscaling task, for example, related to the ESM resolution or variable. Hence, our method allows for much more flexibility after training in which the optimal training scale could be defined with respect to any given metric. In general, ESM fields are too smooth at small spatial scales, which presents a key problem for Earth system modelling in general and impact assessments in particular. More specifically, when comparing the frequency distribution of spatial precipitation fields in terms of spatial PSDs, it can be seen that ESMs lack the high-frequency spatial variability, or spatial intermittency, which is a key characteristic of precipitation$^{49}$. Hence, a natural choice for the spatial scale to be preserved in the ESM fields is the intersection of PSDs from the ESM and the ground-truth ERA5 (ref. 17) (Fig. 3), that is, the scale at which the ESM fields become too smooth.

```
w(k) = exp(s0 log($\alpha_0$) / N(k))

```

where $w_0 = 0.9$ is the initial decay rate$^{50}$. For the distance measure $d(\cdot, \cdot)$, we follow another work$^{35}$ and use a combination of the learned perceptual image patch similarity$^{51}$ and $l^p$ norm:

```
$d(x, y) = \text{LPIPS}(x, y) + \|x-y\|_l$
(10)
```

Thus, the training of the CMI is self-supervised and closely related to representation learning$^{52}$, where a so-called online network $\hat{\text{f}}: \theta$ is trained to predict the same image representation as a 'target network' $\text{f}: \hat{\theta}$ (ref. 44). Importantly, the CMI is, therefore, not trained explicitly for the downscaling tasks, which are purely performed at the inference stage.

For Gaussian noise, the variance as a function of time $t$ can be related to the PSD of a given wavenumber $k$ and the grid size $N$ by$^{47}$

```
$\sigma^2(t) = N^{p}\text{PSD}(k)$
(11)
```

Using equation (11), we choose $k^* = 0.0667$ (Supplementary Fig. 2), such that it represents the wavenumber or spatial scale at which the PSDs of ESMs and ERA5 precipitation fields intersect. This corresponds to $t^* = 0.468$ for the CM variance schedule and $t^* = 0.355$ for the SDE bridge.

The diffusion bridge$^{47}$ starts with the forward SDE in equation (1), initialized with a precipitation field from the POEM ESM. The forward SDE is then integrated until $t = T$. Then, the reverse SDE (equation (2))—initialized at $t = T$—denoises the field again, adding structure from the target ERA5 distribution.

For the CM approach, at inference, we apply the 'stroke guidance' technique$^{48,53}$, where we first sample a noise ESM field $\mathbf{x}^{\text{ESM}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ with variance corresponding to $t^*$:

```
$\mathbf{x}^{\text{ESM}} \sim \mathcal{N}(\textbf{x}^{\text{ESM}}, \sigma^2(t^*)\mathbf{I})$
(12)
```

which is then denoised in a single step with the CM:

```
$\mathbf{x} = f(\mathbf{x}^{\text{ESM}}, t^*, \theta)$
(13)
```

thereby highly efficiently generating realistic samples $\mathbf{x}$ that preserve unbiased spatial patterns of the ESM up to scale $k^*$.
