# Reaction Chemistry & Engineering

Despite the high yield of CO2 cycloaddition reactions catalyzed by imidazolium-based and pyrazolium-based ILs, their application is hindered by stringent reaction conditions. Then there is an increasing demand for new catalysts that can function under mild conditions. Designing new imidazolium-based and pyrazolium-based ILs through theoretical calculations is expensive and challenging to maintain high success rates. The application of machine learning (ML) to predict such reactions has gained interest recently.$^{12}$ ML is based on the induction from historical data, cost-effective and high-throughput prediction of chemical properties.${13}$ Researchers have made many explorations in predicting properties of ILs. For instance, Huang et al.${14}$ established the universal-chemical functional activity coefficient (UNIFAC) method to improve the solubility prediction of liquid-water mixtures, with $R^2$ of 0.99. Liu et al.${15}$ established a SVR-based model to predict the solubility of CO2 in ILs, achieving $R^2$ exceeding 0.98.

However, research on the reactivity of ILs using ML methods remains scarce compared to physical properties of ILs. Notably, Li et al.${16}$ explored this area by establishing a ML model to predict the yield of cycloaddition reactions catalyzed by ILs. The best ML model achieved $R^2$ of 0.80 on a subset of imidazolium-based ILs, but ML showed lower performance on others. Thus, further exploration is needed in the study of ML for the reactivity of CO2 cycloaddition reactions catalyzed by ILs. Due to the limitation in computational power, traditional algorithms struggle to improve model accuracy.${17}$ In contrast, deep learning algorithms exhibit strong fitting capabilities, demonstrating superior performance in prediction.$^{18,19}$ For instance, Gong et al.${20}$ employed dimensional molecular structure graphs to establish a deep learning model capable of predicting selectivity.

This achieved outstanding results with minimal labeled data across three different chemical datasets, showing superior prediction ability compared to traditional algorithms.

The main aim of this work is to develop a deep learning model that could predict the yield of CO2 cycloaddition reactions. Using ML, we aim to design efficient imidazolium-based and pyrazolium-based ILs under mild conditions. The research scheme is illustrated by hybrid fingerprint to describe the structural features of molecules in CO2 cycloaddition reactions. Using SHAP analysis, the model is interpreted to explore the effects on yield. By basing on a molecular chemical scheme, hundreds of ILs are generated. Finally, imidazolium-based ILs are generated using a screened-two screening strategy yielding prediction and DFT calculations of energy barrier. This work presents much contributing to advancing sustainable chemical processes and CO2 utilization technologies.
