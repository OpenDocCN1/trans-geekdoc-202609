# An accurate and interpretable deep learning model for yield prediction using hybrid molecular representations†

Yingwang Wang,abc Xiny Sun,abc Yuyuan Li,abc *abc Li Wang *abc and Jing Zhang abc

In recent years, imidazolium-based ionic liquids (ILs) and pyrazolium-based ILs have shown efficient catalytic activity in CO2 cycloaddition reactions. However, these catalysts require stringent conditions to facilitate the reactions in the absence of co-solvents, limiting their applicability. There is an increasing demand for new ILs capable of operating under milder conditions. Traditional methods for designing ILs through theoretical calculations or experimental exploration are both costly and challenging. This study presents a deep learning model for predicting yield of CO2 cycloaddition by imidazolium-based and pyrazolium-based ILs. The Model utilizes hybrid features to capture the structural information of molecules achieved in squared correlation coefficient (R²) value of 0.98. The Shapley Additive explanations (SHAP) technique is established to create new structures. Through a two-step strategy yield prediction using the deep learning model and energy barriers via functional theory (DFT), 4 promising imidazolium-based ILs are identified as potential catalysts for CO2 cycloaddition reactions with epichlorohydrin under mild conditions. This work introduces a novel learning approach for designing imidazolium-based ILs and pyrazolium-based ILs, aimed at reducing experimental burden and costs associated with catalyst development.

# 1. Introduction

In the past few decades the use of CO2 as a renewable resource has received widespread attention from scientists. The CO2 cycloaddition reaction can convert CO2 into high-value cyclic carbonates with 100% atom economy, making it one of the most promising CO2 conversion strategies. Various carbamates used as solvents, electrolytes and organic synthesis have numerous applications. However, the thermodynamic stability and kinetic efficiency of catalysts are required to ensure the completion of cycloaddition reactions under mild conditions. Homogeneous and heterogeneous catalysts have been reported, including metal-organic frameworks (MoFs), metal complexes, ionic liquids (ILs),†

Among these ILs, we have shown exceptional low vapor pressure, excellent thermal stability, and wide temperature range, making them ideal catalysts for CO2 cycloaddition reactions. Imidazolium-based are the most common employed ILs-based catalysts to facilitate functionality and affinity to CO2 molecules. For instance, Wang et al. synthesized poly cyclic carbonate 92% at the temperature of 120 °C, CO2 pressure of 1.5 MPa, catalytic yield of 0.5 mol%, reaction time of 2.0 h. Ye et al.⁸ synthesized series of imidazolium-based ILs with dual functionality hydroxyl and amine groups, which showed high catalytic activity at the reaction temperature of 90 °C and time of 12.5 h. Zhang et al.⁹ synthesized, which share a high structural similarity with imidazolium-based ILs, also demonstrate excellent catalytic potential. For instance, Zhang et al.¹¹ synthesized a carboxylmethyl pyrazolium ILs (CMPEbz), achieving a yield of over 90% at 70 °C and an initial CO2 pressure, of 1.0 MPa.

Despite the high yield of CO2 cycloaddition reactions catalyzed by imidazolium-based and pyrazolium-based ILs, their application is hindered by stringent reaction conditions. Then there is an increasing demand for new catalysts that can function under mild conditions. Designing new imidazolium-based and pyrazolium-based ILs through theoretical calculations is expensive and challenging to maintain high success rates. The application of machine learning (ML) to predict such reactions has gained interest recently.$^{12}$ ML is based on the induction from historical data, cost-effective and high-throughput prediction of chemical properties.${13}$ Researchers have made many explorations in predicting properties of ILs. For instance, Huang et al.${14}$ established the universal-chemical functional activity coefficient (UNIFAC) method to improve the solubility prediction of liquid-water mixtures, with $R^2$ of 0.99. Liu et al.${15}$ established a SVR-based model to predict the solubility of CO2 in ILs, achieving $R^2$ exceeding 0.98.

However, research on the reactivity of ILs using ML methods remains scarce compared to physical properties of ILs. Notably, Li et al.${16}$ explored this area by establishing a ML model to predict the yield of cycloaddition reactions catalyzed by ILs. The best ML model achieved $R^2$ of 0.80 on a subset of imidazolium-based ILs, but ML showed lower performance on others. Thus, further exploration is needed in the study of ML for the reactivity of CO2 cycloaddition reactions catalyzed by ILs. Due to the limitation in computational power, traditional algorithms struggle to improve model accuracy.${17}$ In contrast, deep learning algorithms exhibit strong fitting capabilities, demonstrating superior performance in prediction.$^{18,19}$ For instance, Gong et al.${20}$ employed dimensional molecular structure graphs to establish a deep learning model capable of predicting selectivity.

This achieved outstanding results with minimal labeled data across three different chemical datasets, showing superior prediction ability compared to traditional algorithms.

The main aim of this work is to develop a deep learning model that could predict the yield of CO2 cycloaddition reactions. Using ML, we aim to design efficient imidazolium-based and pyrazolium-based ILs under mild conditions. The research scheme is illustrated by hybrid fingerprint to describe the structural features of molecules. Using SHAP analysis, the model is interpreted to explore the effects on yield. By basing on a molecular chemical scheme, hundreds of ILs are generated. Finally, imidazolium-based ILs are generated using a screened-two screening strategy yielding prediction and DFT calculations of energy barrier. This work presents much contributing to advancing sustainable chemical processes and CO2 utilization technologies.
