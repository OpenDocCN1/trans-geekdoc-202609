# 8.2.1 Flooding

One of the primary challenges in urban areas during rainfall is flooding. Intense development often leads to increased impervious surfaces, reducing natural drainage. Combined with inadequate storm water management systems, heavy rainfall can result in flash floods and inundation of streets, residential areas, and even commercial zones. This poses significant threats to public safety, property, and the overall functionality of the urban landscape.

# 8.2.2 Traffic Disruptions

Rainfall significantly contributes to traffic disruptions in urban environments. Flooded streets, reduced visibility, and slippery road conditions can lead to traffic jams, accidents, and delays. Urban transportation systems, already strained by the volume of vehicles, face additional challenges during rainfall. Efficient traffic management strategies and infrastructure improvements are essential to alleviate these issues.

# 8.2.3 Infrastructure Damage

Urban infrastructure, including buildings, roads, bridges, and drainage systems, is susceptible to damage caused by rainfall. Excessive water accumulation can weaken foundations, erode soil, and compromise the structural integrity of buildings and transportation networks. The economic toll of repairing and maintaining infrastructure post-rainfall underscores the importance of resilient urban planning and construction practices.

# 8.2.4 Waterlogging and Public Health

Persistent rainfall may lead to waterlogging in low-lying areas, creating breeding grounds for disease vectors. Stagnant water can contribute to the spread of waterborne diseases, posing health risks to the urban population. Proper sanitation and drainage systems are crucial to mitigating these health concerns and ensuring the well-being of residents.
