## Smart Farming: Empowering Agriculture in Smart Cities

A. Sumathi, S. Santhoshkumar, S. Meganathan, R. Sujarani, and R. Mathi

DOI: 10.1201/9781003476047-16

### 16.1 Introduction

From the point of view of intelligent agriculture, the sharing of data and resources of IoT devices in the agricultural network can share its computing resources as needed [1]. For example, during peak computing times (such as during pest outbreaks or weather events), nearby devices can work together to efficiently handle the computing load. This technology enables decentralized decision-making while IoT devices are operating and generating data. IoT devices can have built-in intelligence and decision-making capabilities. For example, a smart irrigation system can analyze soil moisture data and weather forecasts to independently control irrigation and optimize water based on real-time conditions [2,3]. The scalability of the technology depends on the technology used. Additional devices can be easily integrated as the agricultural IoT network grows. Osmotic computing and scalability ensure that the system can adapt to the growing number of IoT sensors and actuators required for larger agricultural operations [4,5]. Blockchain technology has the potential to revolutionize agriculture by enhancing supply chain traceability, ensuring quality assurance and certification, streamlining farm management and compliance, promoting fair trade and supply chain ethics, enabling decentralized marketplaces and trade, and facilitating data sharing and collaboration. As the adoption of blockchain continues to grow, it will play an increasingly important role in creating a more transparent, efficient, and sustainable agricultural ecosystem.

### Key Components of Smart Farming:

- 1. Sensors and IoT Devices: These devices collect data on various parameters like soil moisture, temperature, humidity, and crop health. They provide real-time insights into the condition of crops and soil, enabling timely interventions.

- 2. Data Analytics and AI: Advanced analytics and AI algorithms process the data collected from sensors to make predictions, identify patterns, and offer actionable recommendations. This helps farmers in decision-making processes such as crop selection, irrigation scheduling, and pest management.

- 3. Precision Irrigation: Smart irrigation systems use sensors and weather data to deliver the right amount of water directly to the roots of plants. This reduces water wastage and ensures optimal moisture levels for plant growth, resulting in higher yields and water conservation.

- 4. Remote Monitoring with Drones: Drones equipped with cameras and sensors are used to survey large agricultural fields quickly and efficiently. They can detect crop health issues, monitor livestock, and assess the condition of infrastructure like fences and irrigation systems.

- 5. Livestock Monitoring: Smart farming techniques extend to livestock management, with wearable sensors tracking animal health, activity levels, and location. This data helps farmers detect diseases early, improve breeding programs, and optimize feeding schedules.

- 6. Blockchain Technology: Blockchain technology is being applied to enhance transparency, traceability, and trust in the agricultural supply chain. By recording transactions and data in a decentralized and immutable ledger, blockchain ensures the authenticity and integrity of agricultural products from farm to fork. Smart contracts enable automated agreements and payments, while tokenization facilitates fractional ownership and trading of agricultural assets. Blockchain also promotes fair trade, supports decentralized marketplaces, and incentivizes sustainable farming practices.
