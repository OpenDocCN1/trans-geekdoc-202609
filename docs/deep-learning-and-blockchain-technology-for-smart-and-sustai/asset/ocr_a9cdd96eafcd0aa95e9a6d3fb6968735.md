The model used is LSTM (long short-term memory). The data was taken from the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA). The dataset chosen contains 30 years of data. This information is used to monitor the nature and characteristics of earthquakes. Six seismic attributes were used to train the LSTM model. The sample training dataset is taken from South America. It is an area with large and frequent earthquakes. The greatest earthquake in South America was the Valdivia earthquake in Chile (magnitude 9.5) in 1960. Nicaragua (1992) experienced a magnitude of 7.0. Colombia was hit by earthquakes in 1994 and 1999. El Salvador (2001) experienced a magnitude of 7.6. Peru experienced a magnitude 7.9 earthquake in 2007, a magnitude 7.1 earthquake in 2021, and a magnitude 7.1 earthquake in 2018. The research results come from the US Geological Survey. Due to the exponential distribution (Gutenberg–Richter rule), magnitude is chosen as one of the training attributes of the model. A dataset is a time series dataset. If the model gives a false alarm, the public is afraid. However, if the model does not raise an alarm in time, the loss is dramatic. The chosen model should be very efficient and give good results. The damage can be controlled by earthquake prediction [2]. LSTM is suitable for time series forecasting. The effectiveness of forecasts improves.

In recent days, artificial intelligence everywhere has become a great superpower [3]. The hyperparametric nature and nature of randomness make artificial intelligence and deep learning useful in earthquake prediction. Artificial intelligence offers many advanced techniques for predicting earthquakes. LSTM and composite LSTM models are also part of these advanced techniques. These techniques provide accurate and efficient forecast output. These models are very efficient and work with long-term dependencies of seismic data. They can work with very complex datasets. LSTM belongs to RNN (Recurrent Neural Network). When predicting large earthquakes, LSTM methods work effectively. This LSTM is designed to overcome the limitations of traditional RNN. Temporal spatial earthquake prediction by providing a single binary result of whether an earthquake affects a region fulfills the purpose of our work.

The main goal of this research work is to generate composite LSTM and use those LSTM to analyze the computational complexity of simple and complex LSTMs [4]. A complex LSTM performs better than a simple LSTM. Both models were trained on a dataset of earthquakes in South America with six attributes. Datasets are time series and sequential datasets.
