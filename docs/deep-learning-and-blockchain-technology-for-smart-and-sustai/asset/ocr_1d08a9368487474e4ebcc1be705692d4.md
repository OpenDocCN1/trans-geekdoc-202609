# 12.7 Future Directions and Challenges

# 12.7.1 Evolving Threat Landscape

In a smart city, cyber threats can be classified into three main types depending on their target: threats against devices, threats against systems, and threats against networks. Despite the introduction of numerous techniques aimed at enhancing cybersecurity in smart cities, the advancement of attack methods poses a constant vulnerability. Contemporary cyberattacks like spam attacks, Sybil attacks, and identity attacks are particularly concerning in this regard (Al-Taleb et al., 2020).

As smart cities continue to evolve, so does the threat landscape. Future directions in smart city cybersecurity must remain adaptive to emerging cyber threats. The integration of artificial intelligence, machine learning, and quantum computing into smart city infrastructures poses both opportunities and challenges. Research and development efforts should focus on preemptive strategies to anticipate and mitigate novel threats, ensuring the resilience and security of smart cities against an ever-changing threat landscape.

# 12.7.2 Interdisciplinary Collaboration

The future of smart city cybersecurity lies in interdisciplinary collaboration. The integration of deep learning and blockchain technology is just one aspect of a broader landscape that involves urban planning, public policy, law enforcement, and cybersecurity. Collaboration among professionals from diverse fields is crucial for developing holistic and effective strategies. Future research directions should emphasize interdisciplinary approaches to address the multifaceted challenges faced by smart cities.

# 12.7.3 Privacy Concerns and Ethical Considerations

The frequency and complexity of attacks on interconnected devices are rapidly increasing, posing significant security risks. Beyond security, privacy concerns also loom large in the realm of smart homes in smart city. These concerns span from unauthorized access to personal information to various dimensions of privacy, including solitude, anonymity, and intimacy. There's also the threat of physical breaches where unauthorized parties gain access to the home itself raising serious privacy issues (Hall et al., 2020).
