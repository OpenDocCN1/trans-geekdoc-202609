### 7.1 Introduction

The idea of a “smart city” is quickly developing since it has radically changed how people interact with devices in urban environments. By definition, the smart city depends primarily on elements like a wide range of electronic and digital applications, embedded ICTs (information and communication technologies) for administrative settings, ICTs for communication and information in operational systems, and ICTs that help people and ICTs to enhance information and innovation.

The challenge of managing an ever-growing population moving into metropolitan areas has been resolved by the concept of the smart city. Nowadays, a sizable portion of the global population resides in cities. It is anticipated that each user will have approximately seven devices actively communicating over the internet in the future. Approximately 70% of the world’s population is predicted to live in cities by the end of 2050. Therefore, the strategic development of urban areas will be the main task in the future decades. In order to fulfill the growing need for higher-quality services, urban settings must be managed by modifying them in accordance with smart city ideals. In general, a smart city is defined as one that incorporates the use of ICTs and the implementation of strategies that undoubtedly have an impact on the quality of life for the people who live there. The creation of massive datasets is among the most important results of smart city applications and implementation. Significant processing is needed to handle this massive volume of data for the integration of different AI and machine learning (ML) techniques.

Many people support the idea of a “smart city,” which improves the quality of life for people living in cities by integrating several fields, such as smart parking, smart transit, smart healthcare, and smart communities. Real-time data processing and intelligent decision-making capabilities provide a substantial barrier to the continuous evolution of complex metropolitan networks. From a social perspective, the most crucial goal of the smart city–smart community concept is the sustainable development of cities, which guarantees the social well-being of community members. For this reason, the study looks at smart city and sustainable development best practices. In addition, the pandemic’s current scope provides justification for looking into urban development initiatives in search of smart city-smart community solutions. The most important objective of the smart city is health-related. Radiology is one of the smart health domains where AI and ML are most successfully applied. Every year, more than two billion medical exams and scans, including chest X-rays, are performed worldwide. One well-known deep learning algorithm is Deep Neural Networks (DNN), which is applied to a variety of health-related images such as medical scans, including images of the bones to check for fractures and breaks. Many important applications, such as the management of water or electricity resources, intelligent card services payment and verification, have adopted a data-centric approach recently. Both traffic efficiency and CO₂ emissions can be reduced by using smart mobility. The applications of smart cities and their utility are entirely dependent on data connectivity, compilation, and pervasiveness. The India Smart City Mission's major statistics highlights are as follows:

- 100 cities were chosen out of the total.
- Committed capital in total: $98 billion.
- Projects submitted: over 5,000.
- Projects finished: more than 2,000.
- Focus areas include: energy efficiency, waste management, water management, smart infrastructure, sustainable transportation, e-governance, citizen involvement, education, healthcare, safety, and security.

Among the smart city projects having a big influence are:

- **Intelligent Traffic Management Systems (ITMS)**: These systems make use of cameras and sensors to keep an eye on traffic patterns, adjust traffic signals, and give residents up-to-date travel information.
- **City Surveillance Systems**: The use of CCTV cameras and video analytics helps to monitor traffic infractions, promote public safety and security, and discourage crime.
- **Smart Networks**: These networks maximize energy distribution, minimize losses, and enhance energy efficiency through the use of sensors and automation.
- **E-governance Platforms**: To increase transparency, expedite procedures, and give individuals access to government services, online portals and mobile apps are being utilized.
- **Public Wi-Fi**: To encourage digital inclusion and economic activity, several smart cities are providing free or reasonably priced Wi-Fi connectivity in public areas.
