# 12.3.5 Critical Infrastructure Resilience (CIR)

Smart cities depend on interconnected and interdependent critical infrastructure systems, including communication, water, power, sanitation, and healthcare, to deliver vital services and improve the well-being of their residents. However, these CI are also exposed to various vulnerabilities and threats, such as cyberattacks, natural disasters, climate change, and human errors, which can disrupt their normal functioning and cause cascading impacts across sectors and scales. Therefore, enhancing the resilience of CI in smart cities is a key challenge and opportunity for urban planners, policymakers, and researchers. Resilience refers to a system’s capacity to endure, adjust, and bounce back from various disruptions and pressures, all while preserving its fundamental operations and essence. Resilience can be achieved through a combination of technical, organizational, and social measures, such as risk assessment, contingency planning, redundancy, diversity, flexibility, learning, and collaboration (Ali et al., 2018). Smart cities need to adopt a comprehensive and proactive approach to CIR, which involves developing and promoting an organizational resilience culture, identifying and managing cross-sectoral dependencies, providing timely and high-quality policy advice, implementing effective cybersecurity measures, and supporting collaboration and coordination among various stakeholders. By enhancing CIR, smart cities can improve their operational efficiency, social and economic well-being, and national security.
