### 12.1 Introduction

Imagine living in a city where technology seamlessly weaves through every aspect of daily life, making things more efficient and improving our overall experience. These are the promises of smart cities—urban spaces enhanced by advanced technologies. However, this interconnected web of smart devices and systems, while offering numerous benefits, also brings with it a host of security concerns. As smart cities become increasingly integral to our lives, understanding and addressing these vulnerabilities is crucial.

This research explores the various challenges smart cities face, such as vulnerabilities, cyber threats, and security issues. It further explores the possibility of employing blockchain technology to enhance the security of smart cities. Blockchain, known for its decentralized and tamper-resistant nature, holds promise in addressing the security challenges these cities encounter. This introduction sets the stage for a deeper dive into how blockchain can be seamlessly integrated into the fabric of smart cities, with the aim of making them more secure and resilient.

### Key Objectives:

- Investigate the vulnerabilities and threats in smart city setups.
- Explain the basics of blockchain technology and how it can enhance smart city security.
- Explore practical ways to integrate blockchain into different aspects of smart city living.
- Propose a tool called the *smartVAST framework* to make smart cities more secure.
- Share real-world examples to illustrate how the *smartVAST framework* can be applied.

As we navigate the intricate relationship between smart cities and blockchain technology, the insights gained from this research contribute to the ongoing conversation about creating cities that are not only smart but also secure, resilient, and sustainable for everyone.

### 12.2 Background Study

### 12.2.1 Smart City Components and Vulnerabilities

Smart cities encompass a diverse range of components, including, but not limited to, transportation systems, energy grids, healthcare infrastructure, and data management systems. These interconnected elements facilitate real-time data exchange, enabling efficient city management. However, this connectivity comes at a cost—an increased susceptibility to vulnerabilities. As an example, incorporating Internet of Things (IoT) gadgets within smart urban environments can create vulnerabilities due to their frequent lack of robust security measures. Transportation systems, relying on interconnected networks, are susceptible to cyberattacks, impacting the flow and safety of urban mobility. While the threat of cyberattacks on smart cities remains persistent, collective efforts to collaborate and share strategies for safeguarding these urban areas are crucial for the collective benefit (Lupton et al., 2022). Understanding the vulnerabilities inherent in each smart city domain is crucial for developing effective security measures.

### 12.2.2 Security Challenges in Smart Cities

The rapid deployment of smart city technologies has outpaced the development of robust cybersecurity measures. The global landscape is witnessing the emergence of smart cities, driven by advancements of information technology, while these innovations offer new economic and social prospects, they also present security challenges and raise concerns regarding privacy expectations (Elmaghraby & Losavio, 2014). This has led to a multitude of security challenges, ranging from unauthorized access to critical infrastructure to data breaches compromising citizen privacy. The centralized nature of many smart city systems amplifies these challenges, creating single points of failure that can be exploited by malicious actors. Moreover, the sheer volume of data generated by smart city components poses challenges in terms of secure storage, transmission, and analysis. There are five major security challenges in smart cities, which encompass maintaining privacy amidst complex datasets, securing expansive networks, fostering trust in data sharing, effectively deploying artificial intelligence, and addressing cascading failures within the smart network (Braun et al., 2018).

### 12.2.3 Introduction to Blockchain Technology

Blockchain technology, originally created as the foundational structure for digital currencies such as Bitcoin, has attracted interest due to its potential beyond financial uses. Essentially, a blockchain functions as a decentralized and distributed database that securely records transactions across a network of computers in a transparent and tamper-proof manner.

A blockchain, alternatively referred to as a Chain of Blocks, links each block to its precursor by retaining the hash data of the preceding block. This hash is derived from the data within the block, and any adjustments made to the block result in a new hash. As a result, even slight modifications within a block disrupt the entire chain because subsequent blocks are unable to pinpoint the tampered block due to inconsistencies in the hash. In these instances, the longest chain is recognized as the most legitimate, resulting in the eventual disregard of the altered blockchain (Imteaj et al., 2021).

### 12.2.4 Previous Research on Blockchain in Smart Cities

The intersection of blockchain and smart cities has been a subject of exploration in recent research. Previous studies have highlighted the potential of blockchain in enhancing the security and trustworthiness of smart city applications. This section reviews and synthesizes key findings from existing literature, providing a foundation for the proposed integration of blockchain technology into the fabric of smart cities.

In the paper by Treiblmaier et al. (2020), the authors delved into the potential implications of blockchain technology on smart cities, examining its underlying technologies and protocols. They pinpointed nine crucial sectors where blockchain technology could play a significant role in advancing urban development, encompassing healthcare, logistics, transportation, energy, governmental processes, electronic voting, industrial operations, household management, and education. The authors analyzed ongoing developments in these sectors highlighting the impact of blockchain and suggesting directions for future investigation.

The research conducted by Salha et al. (2019) sought to investigate the possible role of blockchain technology within smart cities. Their study involved an extensive review of scholarly literature concerning blockchain, focusing on its fundamental concepts and practical applications. Furthermore, it synthesized global instances of successful blockchain implementation while also delving into the associated challenges and opportunities within the context of smart urban environments.

The study conducted by Barella Migliorini et al. (2021) sought to illustrate how smart cities could offer an alternative pathway toward achieving comprehensive sustainability across political, economic, social, and environmental realms. Through extensive surveys of smart city rankings and indexes, the research pinpointed cities excelling in various dimensions. It was found that blockchain technology played a crucial role in driving significant progress toward establishing self-regulating and sustainable smart cities. An analysis of blockchain's applicability, carried out in consultation with market experts well-versed in its implementation, revealed a predominant focus on the cognitive aspect, with comparatively less attention directed toward the operational infrastructure of cities.

The paper by Gade & Aitha (2020) explored the integration of blockchain technology into smart cities, highlighting its potential impact on the development of such urban areas. They addressed the need for reliable and secure online operational processes in various applications within smart cities and proposed a secure framework leveraging blockchain technology. Through thorough research and the extensive literature review, they identified multiple applications and process areas that stand to benefit significantly from this technology, enhancing their intelligence, reliability, and suitability for implementation across smart cities.
