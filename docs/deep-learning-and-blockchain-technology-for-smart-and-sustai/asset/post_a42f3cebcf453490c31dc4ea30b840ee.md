
## 9.4 Exploring Technological Advances

The right technologies, such as broadband mesh, public or private LTE, and Internet of Things (IoT) sensors, will depend on the specific requirements of each city and create the ideal smart city. In the IoT world, innovation is happening very quickly. IoT sensors have several uses in smart city applications, and the technology has the potential to change urban living. By using sensors in trash cans to track the weight of the bins, for instance, waste management expenses, traffic congestion, and air pollution can be decreased by only using trucks to remove bins when and where they are needed. IoT sensors can also be used to detect noise pollution and changes in air quality, warning city managers of possible problems before they become significant issues and detecting changes in environmental conditions before people could.

Broadband mesh can enable video monitoring of passing autos for better traffic control and public safety, among other essential municipal enhancements like lowering traffic congestion. 5G technology may make it possible for smart cities to communicate among devices more quickly, improving safety and communication overall. In particular, 5G can help ensure resilience and energy efficiency by enhancing system-to-system communication and alerting network operators to malfunctioning equipment in advance of possible outages. In the majority of cities that can accommodate 5G infrastructure, implementation is particularly effective. However, 5G’s novelty is one of its drawbacks. Large urban locations and more rural places might not have the same infrastructure available to serve these use cases just yet. This will alter as time goes on and 5G usage becomes widespread. Standardization organizations will then shift their attention to the following generation of communication. Controlling the lifespan of the services offered and the communication infrastructure that underpins them is crucial for governments and utilities. ## 9.5 Potential Benefits of Blockchain Technology and IIoT in Smart Cities

The combination of BCT and the IIoT has the potential to revolutionize smart cities into dynamic ecosystems that prioritize security, sustainability, and efficiency. Here are a few possible uses that have measurable advantages:

- 1. Intelligent Traffic Control:
  - Congestion can be reduced by 25%–30%, thanks to blockchain-enabled real-time route planning and traffic data exchange, as demonstrated by pilot initiatives like Dubai’s “Road Blockchain.”
  - 10%–15% less fuel used: Smart traffic signals and IIoT-connected cars can dynamically modify traffic flow, lowering pollution and fuel use.
- 2. Improved Energy Governance:
  - 20% of energy comes from renewable sources: As Brooklyn Microgrid’s pilot project showed, blockchain-based peer-to-peer (P2P) energy trading platforms may encourage the use of renewable energy sources and maintain grid stability.
  - 15% reduction in energy costs: Demand response systems and smart meters enabled by the IIoT can optimize energy use, saving a lot of money.
- 3. Ensuring Safe and Open Waste Management:
  - 10% increase in the waste diversion rate: Responsible waste management should be encouraged and accountability can be enhanced by blockchain-tracked waste collection and recycling.
  - 8% decrease in operating costs: Waste management operations can be streamlined with the use of IIoT-enabled waste bins and optimized collection routes, which can save money.
- 4. Enhanced Security and Safety for the Public:
  - 5%–10% decrease in crime rates: Secure data exchange and identity management based on BCT can improve the capacity for both crime prevention and investigation.
  - 10% shorter emergency response times: Real-time data analysis and IIoT-connected sensors can enhance the effectiveness and coordination of emergency response efforts.
- 5. Simplified Public Participation and Governance:
  - 15% higher voter turnout: Safe electronic voting systems based on BCT can increase public engagement and confidence in political systems.
  - 10% better public service delivery: Data-driven decision-making and IIoT-enabled feedback systems can improve citizens’ satisfaction with governmental services.

Blockchain’s improved interoperability, secrecy, safety, dependability, and measurability make it a great addition to the IIoT.
