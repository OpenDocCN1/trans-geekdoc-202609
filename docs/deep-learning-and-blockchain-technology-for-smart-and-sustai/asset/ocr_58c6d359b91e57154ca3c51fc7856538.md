The emerging trend of ecologically friendly smart cities—where cutting-edge technology like AI, the IoT, and big data come together to address issues like urbanization and climate change—is examined in the paper by Bibi et al. [22]. It maps research trends and driving factors using a combination of evidence synthesis and bibliometric analysis. The main conclusions of the suggested model discuss the following topics: the rapidly expanding trend, dynamic research agendas, SDGs and fragmentation, ICT dominance, possibility for convergence, environmental costs, and ethical risks. The paper recognizes the need to address ethical and environmental issues while highlighting the potential of big data and AIoT (the convergence of AI and IoT) for creating sustainable cities. Jagatheesaperumal et al. [23] devised a system that uses an ultrasonic sensor mounted on a vehicle’s tire rim and a microphone to gather acoustic data—the noises made by moving cars on various types of roads. ML algorithms such as Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), Support Vector Machine (SVM), and Random Forest are then used to analyze this data and classify the various road conditions (smooth, slippery, grassy, and rough). When it came to determining road conditions, the system performed best with MLP, with an accuracy of 98.98%. With regard to enhancing road condition evaluation and guaranteeing safer and more effective transportation systems, this study highlights the potential of AI and ML for smart city infrastructure management.

Rao and Deebak [24] investigate the privacy and security issues that arise from the integration of cutting-edge technology like the IoT in smart cities and industries. These technologies bring weaknesses that can be used for evil purposes, even as they offer efficiency, sustainability, and an enhanced quality of life. The suggested work tackles challenges, technology, privacy problems, and security difficulties. The paper also emphasizes the necessity of a thorough strategy for privacy and security in smart businesses and cities. This entails creating strong security protocols, putting data governance frameworks into place, and educating the public about the value of data protection. Using IoT technology, Karn et al. [25] suggested an integrated strategy for a sustainable wastewater treatment and management system in smart cities. This method effectively treats wastewater for possible reuse, addressing the growing issue of water scarcity. The article discusses a particular architecture known as SMARTreat, which has been applied to a trial project with success. This building shows how well the suggested strategy works to clean and manage wastewater in a sustainable manner. The paper also offers a viable way for smart cities to deal with the problems associated with water scarcity: effective and sustainable wastewater treatment using IoT technology.
