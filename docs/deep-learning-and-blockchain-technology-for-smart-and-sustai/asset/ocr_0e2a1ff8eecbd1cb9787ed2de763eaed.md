Recent advancements in healthcare, propelled by the integration of IoT technologies, have heralded a transformative era in patient care and medical services. Among these breakthroughs, remote patient monitoring systems have emerged as a cornerstone, allowing healthcare providers to remotely track patients’ vital signs, health parameters, and treatment adherence in real time. Such systems enable proactive intervention, early detection of anomalies, and personalized care plans, thereby minimizing hospital visits and enhancing patient convenience and autonomy. Telemedicine solutions, powered by IoT, facilitate virtual consultations, enabling patients to access healthcare services remotely [26]. These solutions not only bridge geographical barriers but also ensure timely medical assistance, especially in remote or underserved areas. Artificial Intelligence (AI) applications in healthcare leverage IoT-generated data for predictive diagnostics, aiding in early disease detection and personalized treatment recommendations [6]. AI-driven algorithms analyze vast datasets to discern patterns, aiding clinicians in making more accurate diagnoses and formulating tailored treatment plans. These technological advancements have led to substantial improvements in disease management and healthcare outcomes [13]. Patients receive more personalized and precise care, resulting in reduced hospital readmissions, improved treatment adherence, and better health outcomes. Additionally, IoT-based healthcare solutions optimize resource allocation within healthcare facilities, streamlining operational workflows and enhancing overall efficiency [12]. Moreover, IoT-driven innovations foster increased patient engagement through interactive platforms, empowering individuals to actively participate in their healthcare journey, leading to higher satisfaction rates and improved overall well-being. However, the medical industry faces challenges like fragmented data systems, limited interoperability between devices, and the need for enhanced cybersecurity [27]. IoT can address these problems by offering interoperable systems, secure data sharing, and efficient management of healthcare data. By ensuring seamless connectivity among various medical devices and platforms, IoT in healthcare promises to overcome these challenges, promoting a more integrated, efficient, and patient-centric healthcare ecosystem [14].
