The authors of this study [19] suggested a methodology for assessing smart city projects according to their price and effect on urban growth. The study comes to the conclusion that cost-effective solutions typically yield the greatest results with the least amount of administrative and financial strain. The research that is being presented highlights how crucial it is to begin developing smart cities with workable and affordable solutions. Future initiatives that are more ambitious and revolutionary may be made possible by these early triumphs. In their paper, Shi et al. [20] suggested a way to increase city energy efficiency by utilizing a method known as non-intrusive load monitoring (NILM). Without adding additional sensors to each appliance, NILM calculates the energy usage of each one in a home. People can use this information to better understand how much energy they consume and implement energy-saving actions. The accuracy of traditional NILM systems is limited because they only use data from one house. This paper suggested a cooperative NILM method that enhances model accuracy by merging data from multiple residences. This technology trains the models without disclosing specific household data by using a privacy-preserving method known as federated learning. The system was shown to be more accurate than conventional NILM systems by the authors of the paper after evaluating it on a real-world dataset.
