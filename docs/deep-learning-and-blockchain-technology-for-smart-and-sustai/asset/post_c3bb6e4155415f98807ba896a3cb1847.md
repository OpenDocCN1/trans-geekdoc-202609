### 13.1 Introduction

Particularly in heavily populated metropolitan areas, outdoor air quality is a crucial factor in determining both the health of the population and environmental sustainability. An essential tool for evaluating outdoor air quality and its possible effects on both the environment and human health is the Air Quality Index (AQI). To guide decision-making processes about environmental legislation, public health initiatives, and urban planning, accurate AQI level forecast is crucial.

Air pollution happens when things like airborne particles, gases, and biological molecules are released into the atmosphere of Earth in hazardous or excessive proportions (Zhu et al., 2018). Pollutants that produce air pollution include nitrogen dioxide (NO₂), sulfur dioxide (SO₂), carbon monoxide (CO), ozone (O₃), and particulate matter. The contaminants and index concentrations taken into account by the AQI are displayed in Table 13.1. The quality of the air in a number of Indian cities has declined significantly over time (Singh and Chauhan, 2020). Monitoring the region’s general air quality and issuing warnings when necessary are extremely important. When it comes to analysing air pollution, unprocessed raw data is hard for humans to comprehend. So, the research community has established the AQI, a quantitative measurement, to solve this problem (Bao and Zhang, 2020).

The national air quality monitoring network in our nation periodically measures these air contaminants. The outcome AQI is posted frequently on this center’s website. The main contributors to air pollution are heating, industry, traffic, and transportation pollutants (Li et al., 2021). Furthermore, the development of urbanization negatively impacts the quality of the air. There are several suggestions to protect the public from extreme pollution. Some of these include (i) limiting public outdoor activity and (ii) encouraging patients with serious illnesses like cardiovascular diseases, respiratory conditions, pregnancy, and children to spend more time at home (Almetwally et al., 2020). Anticipating air pollution is essential for both alerting people to pollution and minimizing it in their daily lives.

The rise in smart city projects in recent years has highlighted how critical it is to use cutting-edge technologies to address urgent urban issues, such as air quality control. Conventional techniques for AQI prediction sometimes depend on oversimplified machine learning (ML) algorithms (Narasimhan and Vanitha, 2021) or statistical models, which may make it difficult to represent the intricate dynamics of airborne contaminants in dynamic metropolitan contexts. Furthermore, the high processing costs associated with these technologies sometimes limit their ability to scale and real-time usefulness in smart city scenarios.

To get beyond these drawbacks, this work suggests a novel hybrid deep learning (DL) model-based method for outdoor AQI estimation in smart and sustainable cities. DL is a branch of artificial intelligence (AI) that simulates the neural networks found in the human brain. It has shown impressive results in managing intricate data structures and producing precise forecasts. Our goal is to improve prediction accuracy while cutting down on computing time by integrating different DL architectures, such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), into a hybrid framework.

The main aim of this study is to create an effective and scalable AQI prediction system that supports the objectives of sustainable and smart cities. Our method goes beyond only predicting AQI levels; it also aims to clarify the complex connections among human health, environmental sustainability, and air quality. An extensive range of detrimental health outcomes (Turner et al., 2020), including respiratory disorders, heart problems, and even early mortality, have been connected to elevated air pollution levels. Furthermore, air pollution has a major role in aggravating global warming and ecosystem disruption by degrading the environment.

Smart cities may take steps to tackle air quality challenges, reduce health risks, and advance environmental sustainability by implementing precise and effective AQI prediction algorithms. Decisions about land use, transportation, and emissions reduction measures can be made with greater knowledge by citizens, legislators, and city planners, thanks to continuous monitoring and predictive analytics. In addition, increasing public knowledge of the negative impacts of air pollution encourages environmental stewardship and group efforts to create resilient and healthier metropolitan areas (Sharma et al., 2018).

In summary, by utilizing hybrid DL approaches to achieve improved accuracy and computational efficiency, this study pushes the boundaries of outdoor forecasting of air quality indicators in the framework of smart and sustainable cities. ### 13.2 Related Work

Zhou et al. (2019) constructed an AQI prediction model using GRU, an attention mechanism, and a CNN. The input data was processed using CNN to extract features, and the AQI was predicted using a GRU. To demonstrate the greater accuracy of the GRU prediction model, weather and air quality measurements from 1 January 2017 to 30 September 2020 were gathered and applied to the Chinese city of Shijiazhuang. The results were compared with the performance of other models, and the suggested model performed the best overall.

In a busy highway field, Sharma et al. (2019) tracked the horizontal concentrations of air pollutants. They noted that there is an inverse relationship between particle air pollution concentrations and distance.

The researchers found that maintaining air quality stability may help stabilize the environment and has a significant relationship with raising air purity. Using a variety of ML-based models, including Decision Tree (DT), Random Forests (RF), and Extreme Gradient Boost (XGBoost) (Van et al., 2023) developed a strategy for processing air pollution data and improving the AQI forecasting process. The results are examined using MAE, RMSE, and R² approaches. With an RMSE of 0.03684, XGBoost outperformed the other algorithms in AQI value prediction.

Campbell-Lendrum and Prüss-Ustün (2019) demonstrated how to apply ML to estimate the PM₂.₅ μm level based on meteorological information in a high-elevation, non-metropolitan city (Quito, Ecuador). Based on historical PM₂.₅ μm levels, auto-regression was employed to predict PM₂.₅ values. With an accuracy score of approximately 89%, SVM algorithm and DT were capable of predicting PM₂.₅.

Pant et al. (2022) used supervised ML techniques to estimate the air conditions in Dehradun, Uttarakhand. The test findings showed that the DT exhibited a greater precision and accuracy level. By analyzing the levels of contaminants and particles and calculating the air quality rating using SVR (Support Vector Regression) and Radial basis function (RBF), Castelli et al. (2020) demonstrated that SVR provided the most reliable and accurate forecasts with an accuracy rate.
