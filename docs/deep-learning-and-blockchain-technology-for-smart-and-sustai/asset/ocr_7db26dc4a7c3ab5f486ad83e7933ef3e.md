# 2.5 Conclusion

Numerous methods exist for filtering internet spam. Offering efficient processes and putting in place capable software packages to control spam is crucial, given the daily rise in spam and spammers. Email classification can be done using a variety of machine learning-based classification algorithms. In order to categorise the emails as spam or ham, this study uses four classification algorithms: SVM, Multinomial Naive Bayes, Gaussian Naive Bayes, and Bernoulli Naive Bayes. The subject and substance of the emails are used to build the classification model. Using RBSA and SBFS, a classification model is constructed based on the email subject and content attributes. It has been suggested to use RBSA to determine spam term weight and determine if spam phrases are present in the subject. To minimise features, SBFS has been used. The algorithms SVM, Multinomial Naive Bayes, Gaussian Naive Bayes, and Bernoulli Naive Bayes are used to evaluate this suggested methodology, and their respective performances are compared. The same classification algorithms were also used, but without the use of RBSA and SBFS algorithms, on the Enron dataset. The accuracy of the suggested approach was contrasted with that of the conventional approach. The findings highlight the potential of integrated RBSA and SBFS approaches in enhancing the efficiency of email categorization within smart city frameworks, thereby facilitating more streamlined communication and decision-making processes. According to the study, RBSA and SBFS with SVM provide the maximum accuracy (97%), while also reducing false positives. When compared to the suggested methodology, the accuracy level is lowest when RBSA and SBFS are not used.
