Scalability tests were conducted to identify the necessary requirements for the efficient operation of the system on a large scale. The outcomes of these tests verified that conducting fall detection at the Edge can lead to substantial reductions in the size of stored and transmitted data, thus improving efficiency and reducing resource consumption [4].

■ (Hashim et al. 2020). Detecting falls among elderly individuals with Parkinson's disease (PD) and epilepsy is crucial, as such incidents can lead to severe injuries and even fatalities, significantly impacting their quality of life. Despite the importance of fall-detection systems, they often face challenges, particularly in accuracy. This study aims to tackle these challenges by developing and deploying a wearable fall-detection system (WFDS) specifically designed for PD patients, utilizing the low-power ZigBee wireless sensor network (WSN). The WFDS employs data event algorithm (DEA) results from two wireless sensor nodes—an accelerometer and Myoware—attached to the patient’s body to accurately identify falls. Additionally, a direction fall event (DFE) algorithm in the receiver node precisely determines the direction of the fall for PD patients. Experimental results showcase that the WFDS achieved remarkable accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity, boasting a 100% accuracy rate in detecting patient falls. These findings suggest that the experimental WFDS surpasses existing methods in terms of fall detection sensitivity, accuracy, and specificity [5].
