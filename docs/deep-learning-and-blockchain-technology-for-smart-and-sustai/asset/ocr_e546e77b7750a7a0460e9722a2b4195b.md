Dwivedi et al. [26] suggest automating the medical diagnosis of monkeypox, a novel pandemic concern, by the use of deep learning models. Promising findings are obtained when comparing the efficiency of the ResNet50, EfficientNetB3, and EfficientNetB7 algorithms on a small dataset. According to the paper’s findings, ResNet50 attained an accuracy of 83%, EfficientNetB3 generated 86%, and EfficientNetB7 first performed well but saw a sharp decline in Accuracy when more epochs were added.

The use of deep learning algorithms for the automated diagnosis of skin lesions linked to monkeypox is discussed in Singh and Songare [27]. Out of the four examined models, GoogLeNet scored 88.27%, indicating that it may help with early diagnosis and surveillance for this illness. Such computer-aided detection techniques could lead to a decrease in the number of incorrect diagnoses and better public health results in relation to global epidemics of monkeypox. Rabaan et al. [29] address the recent outbreak of monkeypox and how surveillance could be improved by computer-assisted detection. Additionally, it presents the MSLD. This new dataset uses deep learning models to reliably classify skin lesion photos of chicken pox, measles, and monkeypox that were gathered from websites and news portals.

Bengesi et al. [30] investigate public opinion regarding the most recent monkeypox outbreak in order to assist policymakers.
