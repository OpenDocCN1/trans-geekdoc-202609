## 19.1.12 Conclusion

In conclusion, our system represents a significant advancement in enhancing communication for people having hearing and speech impairments with regard to smart cities. By leveraging machine learning algorithms and CNNs, we have achieved practical and meaningful outcomes in accurately interpreting hand signs and translating them into understandable text. Despite the successes of our system, it is essential to acknowledge its limitations. One such limitation is the finite number of gestures trained within the system. While our current dataset bound to a variety of hand signs, there is also place for further expansion to accommodate a broader array of gestures commonly used in communication. Additionally, our system exhibits reduced accuracy when interpreting moving gestures. This shortfall underscores the complexity inherent in capturing and processing dynamic hand movements. However, we remain optimistic that through continued training and refinement, our system overcomes these challenges and optimizes the performance. Looking ahead, we envision ongoing efforts to improve and refine our system, leveraging advancements in machine learning techniques and data collection methodologies. By expanding the scope of trained gestures and enhancing the system's adaptability to dynamic movements, we aim to achieve even greater accuracy and usability in facilitating real-time communication for people having hearing and speech impairments within smart cities. In essence, while our system has made crucial headway in bridging communication barriers, there is still room for growth and optimization. Through iterative refinement and continuous innovation, we remain committed to advancing the accessibility and inclusivity of communication technologies within the dynamic urban landscapes of smart cities, ultimately enriching the lives of residents and fostering greater social cohesion and connectivity (Figure 19.6,19.7,19.8).
