### 12.5.4 Decentralized Identity Management

Decentralized Identity Management (DID) relies on blockchain technology to create and verify self-sovereign identities that are independent of any centralized authority. DID has various benefits, such as enhancing privacy, security, and portability of identity data, as well as enabling diverse applications and use cases across domains. However, DID also faces several challenges, such as interoperability, scalability, and governance (Singla et al., 2022).

Traditional identity management systems are often centralized, making them susceptible to security breaches and identity theft. Blockchain technology enables decentralized identity management, where individuals have control over their personal information. Each user's identity is securely stored on the blockchain, and access is granted through cryptographic keys. This decentralized approach reduces the risk of a single point of failure, enhances data privacy, and provides citizens with greater autonomy over their identity information.

### 12.5.5 Supply Chain Security and Transparency

Supply chain security and transparency are crucial aspects of smart cities that require careful attention and management. Smart cities rely on a complex network of interconnected devices, sensors, and systems that generate and exchange vast amount of data. This data is often sensitive and valuable, and its security and privacy must be protected from theft, sabotage, or disruption. Blockchain technology can provide a solution to this challenge by enabling secure and transparent data sharing among different stakeholders in a smart city ecosystem (Sodhi et al., 2018).

Blockchain can enhance the security and transparency of supply chain processes within smart cities. From the sourcing of materials for construction projects to the delivery of goods and services, blockchain ensures an immutable record of transactions. This transparency not only prevents fraud but also provides citizens and businesses with the ability to trace the origin and journey of products. By integrating blockchain into supply chain management, smart cities can strengthen the security of critical infrastructure projects and improve the overall efficiency of resource allocation.

### 12.5.6 Environmental and Energy Initiatives

In the context of sustainability, blockchain technology can be integrated into smart cities to monitor and incentivize environmentally friendly practices. Blockchain-based systems can trace the origin and impact of energy consumption, track emissions, and facilitate transparent carbon trading. Additionally, decentralized energy grids powered by blockchain can enable more efficient energy distribution, reducing waste and promoting the use of renewable energy sources. The integration of blockchain in environmental and energy initiatives contributes to the overall resilience and sustainability of smart cities.

## 12.6 smartVAST Framework

### 12.6.1 Introduction to smartVAST

In response to the evolving landscape of smart city vulnerabilities and the integration of blockchain technology, the smartVAST framework is introduced as a strategic tool for assessing, addressing, and fortifying the security of smart city ecosystems. smartVAST stands for Vulnerability, Attacks, Security, and Threats, encapsulating a comprehensive approach to mitigating risks in the context of blockchain integration. This framework is designed to provide a structured methodology for smart city stakeholders, including urban planners, cybersecurity professionals, and policymakers, to analyze and enhance the security posture of their cities.

The goal is to identify weak points in the system that could be exploited by malicious actors.
- **Attack Analysis**: Building upon the identified vulnerabilities, the framework conducts an analysis of potential cyberattacks that could exploit these weaknesses. This includes studying historical attack patterns, understanding emerging threats, and assessing the potential impact of attacks on various smart city domains.
- **Security Enhancement**: With a clear understanding of vulnerabilities and potential attack vectors, the smartVAST framework proposes security measures and enhancements. This may involve the integration of blockchain technology to secure data transactions, implement smart contracts for automation, and strengthen overall cybersecurity measures across smart city components.
- **Threat Mitigation**: The final component involves developing strategies to mitigate identified threats. This may include the implementation of proactive measures, incident response plans, and continuous monitoring to detect and respond to potential security incidents in real time.
