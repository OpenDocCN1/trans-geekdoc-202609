Common voice modulation symptoms, such as dysphonia and dysarthria, can be evaluated through sustained phonation or running speech tests, providing a realistic measure of impairment and facilitating early diagnosis at stage 0 [3]. Once detected early, physicians can implement therapeutic solutions or deep brain stimulation to reactivate dopamine-producing neurons, thereby slowing PD progression. Despite its complexity, a cure for Parkinson's remains elusive; however, timely identification followed by appropriate medication can alleviate tremors and imbalance symptoms, enabling patients to lead a relatively normal life. This innovative approach underscores the significance of audio as a non-invasive biomarker for PD detection. Preliminary results indicate that a random forest classifier model, trained on 22 attributes of MDVP audio data, achieves a minimal accuracy of 91.83%, outperforming KNN, Support Vector Machine (SVM), and logistic regression models. Recognizing the mobility challenges faced by PWP [4], who find it challenging to travel for health check-ups, the proposed remote detection technique offers a lifeline by classifying PD severity using speech data recorded on mobile phones. The research compares and contrasts various ML models for disease classification, emphasizing their memory efficiency and speed compared to deep neural network learning models. The promising outcomes of this study aim to spur advancements in telemedicine for PD. Through the comparison of various ML algorithms like logistic regression, decision tree, k-nearest neighbor (KNN) as some “Ensemble” learning techniques where attempt to enhance the accuracy by combining several models [5], the ML model has been implemented to significantly improve diagnosis method of PD. In this study, it indicates that the ensemble techniques Boost classification (Extreme gradient boosting) algorithm achieved the high test accuracy rate (95%) compared to other classification algorithm.
