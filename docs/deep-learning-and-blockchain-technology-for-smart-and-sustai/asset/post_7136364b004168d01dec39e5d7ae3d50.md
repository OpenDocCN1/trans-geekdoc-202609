## 11.6 Experimental Results

Hardware: The hardware implementation of the wheelchair fall detection with IoT system involved integrating the system directly into the wheelchair itself. The wheelchair is equipped with an ESP32 microcontroller, a gyroscope sensor, a buzzer, and a secure button. These components work in conjunction to detect falls and transmit relevant data for analysis and alerting purposes. The gyroscope sensor and buzzer are affixed to the inner left-hand side of the wheelchair. These sensors are linked to a breadboard. Once a person is seated in the wheelchair, the sensors activate. The collected data from the wheelchair fall detection system are then sent to the Blynk mobile application. Both the user and authorized individuals can monitor the wheelchair’s status using the application. The system operates when powered by a 10,000 mAh power bank or by a power supply connected to the microcontroller. It is essential for the system to connect to mobile data or Wi-Fi networks to ensure smooth operation.

Software: Blynk is a hardware-agnostic IoT platform with white-label mobile apps, private clouds, device management, and data analytics.

The GUI of the system encompasses three primary functions: Google Maps integration, email notifications, and a virtual LED indicator. Upon receiving and analyzing data from the sensors, the mobile application instantly displays the results. Fall detection occurs when values from the gyroscope sensor surpass predetermined thresholds. In such cases, the virtual LED activates, signalling a fall event, and an email notification is dispatched to the registered email address, alerting them to the user’s fall from the wheelchair.

One of the key strengths of wearable IoT sensors is their ability to provide real-time monitoring and immediate response in the event of a fall. The sensors are designed to accurately detect the specific motion patterns associated with falls, allowing for timely alerts to caregivers, healthcare professionals, or emergency services. This rapid response can significantly reduce the potential consequences of a fall, such as injuries or delayed medical attention. Moreover, the portability and non-intrusive nature of these wearable sensors ensure user comfort and compliance. The sensors are seamlessly integrated into the user’s daily routine, fostering user acceptance and long-term adherence to the technology. This ease of use is crucial in promoting widespread adoption and maximizing the impact of fall detection systems. The implementation of machine learning algorithms in conjunction with IoT sensors further enhances the accuracy of fall detection, minimizing false alarms and optimizing the system’s reliability. Continuous refinement and updates to these algorithms contribute to the ongoing improvement of the technology, ensuring its effectiveness in various real-world scenarios. In addition to fall detection, these sensors can also collect valuable data on users’ daily activities and health metrics, offering a holistic approach to healthcare monitoring. This comprehensive data can be leveraged by healthcare providers to gain insights into users’ behaviour, potentially identifying trends or patterns that may inform personalized care plans.
