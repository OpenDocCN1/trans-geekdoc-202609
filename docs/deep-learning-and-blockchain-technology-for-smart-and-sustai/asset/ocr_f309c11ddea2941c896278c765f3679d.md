The study conducted by Ćirić et al. (2019) explored the various ways blockchain technology could be applied in smart cities, drawing from existing literature and use cases. Their objective was to categorize potential applications based on the different aspects of smart cities to offer a more comprehensive analysis.

The paper by Rotuna et al. (2019) presents a blockchain-based model tailored for smart city ecosystems. By integrating self-sovereign identity (SSI) and smart contracts, the authors aim to foster secure and transparent interactions among citizens, public authorities, and IoT devices. Their analysis delves into the potential applications of blockchain technology across several domains crucial to smart city development. These domains include digital identity management, bolstering IoT security, facilitating private messaging systems, and enhancing the functionality of autonomous vehicles within urban environments. Through their exploration, the authors highlight the transformative potential of blockchain in revolutionizing various facets of smart city infrastructure and services.

Alnahari and Ariaratnam (2022) proposed a framework for applying blockchain technology to smart city infrastructure in various sectors such as energy, water, transportation, construction, and government. They explored the benefits and challenges of blockchain for enhancing data security, transparency, efficiency, and sustainability in urban systems. They also presented a case study of Tempe, Arizona, as an example of a hypothetical city that could implement blockchain in its infrastructure and operations. They concluded that blockchain has the potential to transform the entire infrastructure into a corporate entity, but also acknowledged the regulatory, technical, and cost barriers that need to be overcome.

Dzyatkovskii (2022) discussed the applications and prospects of AI and blockchain technology in smart cities, focusing on the domains of energy, transport, waste management, and air quality. It reviewed the current literature on how these technologies can improve the efficiency, sustainability, and security of urban systems, as well as presented some case studies and examples of existing or proposed projects. It also identified some challenges and limitations of implementing AI and blockchain solutions, such as data availability, scalability, interoperability, and public acceptance. The paper concluded that AI and blockchain can have a significant impact on smart city development, but they should not be seen as the only or optimal solutions for every problem.

Năsulea and Mic (2018) summarized the potential applications of blockchain technology for smart cities and smart communities, such as smart contracts, smart assets, digital identity, and voting. The paper also proposed a model for blockchain integration in smart cities, based on the interactions between the smart city, the smart community, and the individual.
