iii. Smart Manufacturing: Automated production is giving way to “smart manufacturing” in the manufacturing sector [35]. During this upgrade procedure, BDA (big data analytics) on manufacturing data is crucial. Every stage of the life cycle of a product, from designing to supplying raw materials to manufacturing, distribution, retail, and after-sales service, produces enormous amounts of data. Data analytics and data aggregation are challenging, nevertheless, because manufacturing data is extremely fragmented. By enabling data exchange between industrial sectors and linking IoT systems via peer-to-peer networks, BCIoT may solve the interoperability problem. For example, it is possible to build multiple distributed blockchains, each of which serves one or more sectors. Smart manufacturing security can also be enhanced by BCIoT. The IoT systems’ centralized maintenance is the key of obstacles preventing factories from being upgraded. For example, to address security vulnerabilities, IoT firmware must be updated on a regular basis. However, the majority of firmware updates on IoT devices are manually deployed after being downloaded from a central server. Installing and updating firmware updates for distributed IoT devices is costly and inefficient. An automatic firmware upgrading method based on smart contracts and blockchains is presented in the work of Christidis and Devetsikiotis [10].

The entire industrial network is specifically equipped with smart contracts that specify the firmware upgrading procedures (such as the times and locations for firmware upgrades). The firmware hashes can then be downloaded and installed on devices through automatically running smart contracts. Consequently, there can be significant savings on security maintenance expenses.
