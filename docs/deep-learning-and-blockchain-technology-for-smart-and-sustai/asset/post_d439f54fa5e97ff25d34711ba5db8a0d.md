### 19.1.2 Literature Survey

Sunitha et al., in their paper, cover the elucidation for the deaf-mute communication interpreter [7]. As of now for the deaf-mute communication, there are two methods: one is physically wearable hearing aids and the other is learning system through internet. In wearable device, the user wears a glove with keypad with a micro-controller or a touch screen [8]. But in this method, an external device is utilized for communication between the deaf/non-deaf people. In learning systems, the usage of an extra device can be eliminated. Learning system based on online has five subdivided methods: SLIM module, TESSA, WI-See Technology, SWI_PELE System, and Web-Sign Technology [9]. Anand et al. devised an ISLR system that uses a pattern recognition and classification with the use of Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT), and the nearest neighbor classifier recognizes the hand gesture with a 92.23% accuracy. Another model by Mandeep Kaur Ahuja and Amardeep Singh uses the skin color and template matching for recognizing the hand signs [1]. The hand is first segmented by the difference in skin color, and thresholding is used to separate the foreground and the background. Finally, it is compared with the templates to recognize the hand sign [10]. The system used here applies digital image processing to recognize the hand gestures, using the SIFT algorithm at the edges that are invariant to scaling, rotation, addition of noise. Nagpal et al. proposed a system to communicate with the computer that can recognize 36 different hand gestures given by the user. Shirbhate et al. used a system to aid the communication between deaf and dumb people with others by using Indian Sign language (ISL) [11]. Khandge has presented their gesture language recognition consisting of three steps: preprocessing, feature extraction, and classification with neural network (NN) and Support Vector Machine (SVM) as some of the methods. Pratibha Pandey and Vinay Jain have presented application that helps the communication by a gesture to text conversion. The processing steps include gesture extraction, gesture matching, and conversion to speech [12]. Yun, in his paper, segmented the data of each sign into sub-words having three sub-divisions with shape, color, and orientation. Delliraj and Vijayakumar proposed a system with a flex sensor to give a voice output for the recognized sign gesture. Choudhury et al. presented a Conditional Random Field (CRF) for complex backgrounds based on ISL. Hee-Deok proposed an additional hand appearance-based verification method and a subsign reasoning method to increase the accuracy of spotting rate to 87.0% and recognition rate of 93.5%. Chao et al. proposed the Microsoft Kinect system that spots the hand gestures using the hand-tracking feature of the device.
