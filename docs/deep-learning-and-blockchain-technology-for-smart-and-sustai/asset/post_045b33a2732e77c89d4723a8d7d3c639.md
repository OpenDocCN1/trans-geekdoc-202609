### 9.6 Literature Survey

The authors of this study, Gai et al. [1], suggested a unique method for enhancing healthcare in smart cities by fusing federated deep learning and edge computing. The MDP-FDRNN method is the central concept; it combines Federated Deep Recurrent Neural Networks with Markovian Decision Processes to achieve effective resource allocation at the network edge. This methodology offers significant advantages for healthcare applications in intelligent urban environments, as it facilitates real-time data processing with minimal latency and processing time. The article emphasizes the value of edge computing in overcoming processing and bandwidth constraints, and it shows how the MDP-FDRNN algorithm successfully overcomes these obstacles to improve the standard of healthcare services for urban residents.

The growing popularity of the IoT and IIoT has been addressed in this article by Bansal and Singhrova et al. [2]. IoT and IIoT provide convenience and security problems. Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) provide a way to defend these systems against many types of attacks. The present status of IDS research in relation to IoT and IIoT is reviewed in this article. It examines several methods of detection, such as hybrid, anomaly, and signature-based techniques. The paper emphasizes the need for advancement in areas such as real-time performance, scalability, and accuracy. It also recognizes scientific trends, such as the application of deep learning and machine learning methods. In conclusion, it suggests unresolved issues and potential paths for IDS research in the IoT/IIoT space.

A novel authentication methodology was presented in the paper by Jain et al. [3] to secure communication between IIoT devices and servers. Because of its straightforward, lightweight, and secure architecture, this protocol is appropriate for IIoT devices with little resources. It does this by utilizing hash functions and XOR, two fundamental cryptographic procedures. According to the protocol, the server receives an authentication request from the IIoT device. Session keys are then returned by the server and are used to encrypt any further communications. Periodically, the session keys are changed to ensure security. The authors assert that their protocol is immune to man-in-the-middle, replay, and guessing attacks, among other types of assaults. To bolster their arguments, they also conducted a formal verification tool analysis of the procedure.

A flexible and secure edge computing strategy for AI-driven Industrial IoT (AIoT) was presented by Zhao et al. [4]. It uses distributed computing and a software-defined industrial control architecture to overcome the security and flexibility issues in edge systems. Through industrial modeling and virtualization, the architecture decouples hardware from software, increasing programmability and flexibility. The use of resources and real-time performance are enhanced via distributed computing. Data availability, integrity, confidentiality, and privacy are further guaranteed by a security domain-based task offloading method and a decentralized service discovery and distribution paradigm. Experiments show that the suggested plan is effective. All in all, this plan presents a viable strategy for adaptable and safe edge computing for the IoT.

Gateways serve as vital links between devices and the larger network in the context of the IIoT. But these gateways are also easy targets for cyberattacks, which can take down the entire system if they are compromised. The authors of the paper, Fröhlich et al. [5], suggested a unique Secure IIoT Gateway Architecture that makes use of Trusted Execution Environments (TEEs) in order to address this. To improve security, TEEs—secure sections inside processors—isolate sensitive functions from the rest of the system. In order to protect the TEE from outside threats, this architecture assigns crucial responsibilities like device onboarding, data encryption, and authentication to them. The approach is also more adaptable because it doesn’t require prior trust between devices.

Using a K-harmonic clustering algorithm and BCT, Baalamurugan et al. [6] established a new framework for safe data sharing and storage in IIoT systems. Ineffective sharing and data breaches are two problems with traditional IIoT security. Simulations are used to assess the framework, and the results demonstrate benefits over current approaches in terms of data sharing efficiency, storage capacity, and processing time. This integrated strategy could improve data management for IIoT applications in terms of security and efficiency.

The difficulty of dynamically orchestrating service function chains (SFCs) in edge intelligence-powered IIoT systems, where quick response times and adaptability are critical, is examined by Huang et al. [7]. In order to overcome this difficulty, it suggests using Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL), which takes delay restrictions into account and strives to maximize resource allocation and service placement choices while guaranteeing prompt service delivery. This strategy makes use of the intelligent edge to make IIoT services effective and flexible.

A novel “Deep Progressive Neural Network” (DPNN) approach was developed in the article by Sharma et al. [8] to solve security concerns in the ever-expanding IIoT ecosystem. This novel algorithm efficiently detects and categorizes different risks within an IIoT network by utilizing the advantages of both progressive learning and deep learning. Through transfer learning, the DPNN effectively integrates new attack types while avoiding the loss of previously acquired knowledge. This leads to an extremely precise system that achieves 94% accuracy in threat detection against a baseline of 69.7%, demonstrating its potential to greatly improve IIoT security.

Smart factories are undergoing a revolution thanks to 5G networks and the IIoT, but security and privacy remain major obstacles. These problems are investigated at the physical, data, and application layers by Lin et al. [9]. It showcases cutting-edge techniques including edge computing for quicker reaction times, blockchain for safe data management, and deep learning for anomaly detection. Although these developments seem promising, there are still issues to be resolved, such as scarce resources, a lack of AI that protects privacy, and intricate multi-stakeholder settings. The article’s conclusion emphasizes the necessity of continued investigation and cooperation in order to provide private and secure 5G-IIoT smart factories.

In order to ensure secure transaction management in IIoT applications, Alahmari et al. [10] proposed a novel approach. The Streebog cryptographic hash function is recommended by the authors as a means of augmenting security when used in conjunction with the Davis Mayer blockchain infrastructure. To further streamline the suggested method for effectively managing and controlling network resources, they provide Software-Defined Networking (SDN) as a crucial component. The goal of integrating these technologies is to improve the overall security of industrial systems by addressing the difficulties associated with safe transaction processing in IIoT contexts.
