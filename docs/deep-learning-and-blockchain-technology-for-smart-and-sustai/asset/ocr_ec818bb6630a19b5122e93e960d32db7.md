A two-phase model was presented by Ikram et al. [16] to forecast both regular and anomalous IIoT traffic. A random forest classifier is used in the second phase to forecast traffic patterns based on the features that were picked in the first phase, which used a modified whale optimization method to identify the most significant features from the data. On a variety of IIoT datasets, this method outperformed existing benchmark approaches in terms of accuracy, true positive/negative rates, and ROC curve area.

Using the Interpretive Structural Modeling (ISM) technique, Malhotra et al. [17] developed a hierarchical framework to assess the difficulties of deploying the IIoT in manufacturing. The framework finds and groups problems into technical, organizational, and social categories. It then examines how these categories relate to one another and creates a hierarchical structure. Important conclusions include the following: (1) the most significant technical constraints are insufficient resources and high investment costs; (2) the most significant organizational obstacles are insufficient resources and a skilled labor force; and (3) the most significant social barriers are privacy and data security concerns. With the purpose of successfully implementing IIoT in manufacturing, the hierarchical structure aids in the prioritization of problems and the design of focused solutions.

In order to improve data security in IIoT, Mathew et al. [18] investigated the intriguing combination of BCT and collaborative intrusion detection systems (CIDS). It draws attention to the shortcomings of conventional, centralized security techniques in the intricate world of IIoT and underscores the necessity of a more decentralized strategy. The immutability, transparency, and trustless collaboration of BCT are thought to be complementary to CIDS’s multi-device threat detection capabilities. By examining different integration options, analyzing potential risks, and identifying research needs, the paper paves the path for an IIoT ecosystem that is more dependable and secure.

For IIoT manufacturing segments, Li et al. [19] developed a “Redundant Collaboration Secure Communication Scheme” (TS-RCSCS). This approach makes use of a “redundant-synergy” concept with several auxiliary pathways to address the shortcomings of single-path communication. In the event that a single node fails, this guarantees the authenticity, secrecy, and integrity of the data. It uses a “redundant-synergy transmission method” in conjunction with threshold signatures to guard against data loss and information interception.
