
## 11.4 Related Work

As the elderly population in Japan continues to grow, there is a pressing need for the advancement of welfare infrastructure aimed at reducing the burden of care. In this study, researchers have devised an intelligent power wheelchair designed to minimize the caregiving requirements for frail elderly individuals. This intelligent power wheelchair boasts four key functions: gesture recognition for approach, assistance with standing up, walking assistance, and operation assistance through posture recognition. The study also presents experiments demonstrating the practical utility of three of these assistance functions [1].

As per the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), falls stand as the primary cause of injury and fatality among the 160 million senior citizens presently residing in Europe and the United States. Current technologies predominantly concentrate on identifying falls rather than preventing them. However, merely notifying authorities after a fall occurs does little to mitigate the overall fall rate among the elderly population. This section delves into an architectural framework and a straightforward algorithmic approach that display potential in predicting instances where an unattended elderly individual in a wheelchair might attempt to rise without assistance, thus risking a fall. Our cloud-based infrastructure offers a real-time notification system that is both scalable and redundant, capable of managing extensive network traffic across numerous facilities, each with multiple monitored wheelchairs. Additionally, the authors elaborate on how multidimensional notification techniques are employed to promptly alert remote, preoccupied, or otherwise engaged caregivers [2].

The realm of fall detection through image processing has seen extensive exploration. However, the efficacy of such systems can be compromised by the variability in customers and environments, leading to potential inaccuracies with the pre-existing training data. Hence, the development of a fall detection system capable of autonomously adapting to its surroundings becomes imperative. A viable solution involves the system’s ability to dynamically select the most suitable model for fall detection and utilize everyday images captured from users to refine the model when mis-detections occur. This study delves into the methodology of identifying the optimal model for fall detection. Furthermore, in the event of a mis-detection, the erroneously identified frames are uploaded to a cloud server, where the model undergoes retraining before being redeployed to the edge node for enhanced fall detection capabilities. Deep learning techniques are employed to assess the training data, with GoogLeNet demonstrating the most impressive performance among the models tested [3].

Detecting falls among elderly individuals with Parkinson's disease (PD) and epilepsy is crucial, as such incidents can lead to severe injuries and even fatalities, significantly impacting their quality of life. Despite the importance of fall-detection systems, they often face challenges, particularly in accuracy. This study aims to tackle these challenges by developing and deploying a wearable fall-detection system (WFDS) specifically designed for PD patients, utilizing the low-power ZigBee wireless sensor network (WSN). The WFDS employs data event algorithm (DEA) results from two wireless sensor nodes—an accelerometer and Myoware—attached to the patient’s body to accurately identify falls. Additionally, a direction fall event (DFE) algorithm in the receiver node precisely determines the direction of the fall for PD patients. Experimental results showcase that the WFDS achieved remarkable accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity, boasting a 100% accuracy rate in detecting patient falls. These findings suggest that the experimental WFDS surpasses existing methods in terms of fall detection sensitivity, accuracy, and specificity [5].

## 11.5 Proposed Work

Figure 11.1: The proposed system’s main flowchart depicts the operation of the smart wheelchair, initiated by pressing the start button. Upon activation, the system engages all on-board sensors, each with its predefined threshold. Utilizing accelerometer, gyroscope, and ESP32 dev Module, the system monitors for values surpassing the thresholds, triggering the fall detection mechanism. Upon detection, the user can verify the fall’s legitimacy; if deemed false, the snooze button deactivates the alert. However, in case of a genuine fall, failure to press snooze within 5 seconds activates the buzzer, signalling nearby assistance. Simultaneously, the GSM module initiates a call for aid. Registered individuals can access the wheelchair’s fall detection via the IoT platform, facilitating prompt assistance.
