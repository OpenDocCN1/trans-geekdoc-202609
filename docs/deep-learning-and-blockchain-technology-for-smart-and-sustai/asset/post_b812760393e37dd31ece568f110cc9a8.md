## 17.12 ADA Boost

AdaBoost, alternatively referred as Adaptive Boosting, is an ensemble ML technique primarily applied to classification tasks. It amalgamates numerous weak learners, commonly decision trees or stump classifiers, to form a robust classifier [28]. The key idea behind AdaBoost is to iteratively train weak classifiers on various segments of the training dataset, assigning higher weights to misclassified data points in each iteration. By doing so, subsequent weak classifiers focus more on the data points that were classified incorrectly by previous classifiers, effectively “boosting” their performance. AdaBoost presents a compelling approach to harness the collective strength of weak classifiers for enhanced accuracy. Initially, identical weights are allocated to all vocal frequency samples, setting the stage for iterative training [29]. A weak learner, such as a decision tree stump, is chosen as the base classifier, capable of discerning subtle patterns within the vocal frequency features. Through iterative training, the weak classifier is repeatedly trained on the dataset, with a focus on misclassified samples weighted more heavily to iteratively refine the model’s performance. This process continues for a predetermined number of iterations or until the error rate is minimized, ensuring continual improvement in the classifier’s predictive capabilities. In the final stage, the individual weak classifiers are amalgamated into a robust ensemble model [30]. Each weak classifier contributes to the ensemble based on its accuracy, with higher-performing classifiers receiving greater weightage. Ultimately, the final classification decision is made through a weighted majority vote, synthesizing the insights gleaned from multiple weak classifiers.
