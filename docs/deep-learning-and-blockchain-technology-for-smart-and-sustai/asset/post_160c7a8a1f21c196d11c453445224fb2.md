The sensors are classified into in-body sensors, on-body sensors, wearable and implant sensors. In-body sensors are positioned inside the body, whereas on-body sensors are placed on the skin surface [10]. The LM35 is a type of temperature sensor that is used to measure temperature. The operating frequency is 1 Hz, which supports a low bit rate, i.e., less than 100 bps [11]. The heart rate sensor, blood pressure sensor, oximeter and blood glucose sensors are operated at the frequency of 1 Hz and a bit rate of less than 100. These are used to measure heart activities, pressure oxygen level and glucose level from the human body. The electrocardiogram signals are sampled at high frequency, i.e., 1,000 Hz with a precision of 24 bits, resulting in a dataflow of 24 kbps [12]. The motion sensors like gyroscope, accelerometers and magnetometer are operated at different sampling frequencies ranging from 10 to 2,000 Hz and bit rates ranging from 1 to 200 kbps.

### 14.3.1.1 Requirements for Sensors

The sensors should satisfy the following requirements [13]:

### 14.3.1.1.1 Wearable

Growth in technology reduces the size of batteries and integrated circuits that makes the sensors small in size, less weight and easy to wear. The weight and size of the battery determine the size of the sensor.

### 14.3.1.1.2 Reliable

Reliable communication is enabled by processing sensor signals throughout the network. It depends on communication standards, battery life and energy consumption.

### 14.3.1.1.3 Security

Safe and secure communication is an important requirement for body sensors. This can be achieved using encryption algorithms. The devices are also protected using authentication schemes.

### 14.3.1.1.4 Interoperability

Medical devices will communicate with other devices. It allows users to build a robust application.

The sensor nodes are organized in different topologies like tree, mesh and star topology [14]. The wireless standards are responsible for communication between various layers in WBAN. Near-field communication is a low-frequency standard that operates at 13.56 MHz, transmits from 0.1 to 1 m and supports narrowband modulation, making it suitable for short-range networks. The data rate is 423.75 kbps. Zigbee is a widely used standard in WBAN that operates at 0.9/2.4 GHz and transmits in the range 10–20 m. Zigbee accommodates large number of nodes, organized in either a tree or mesh topology with low data rates, i.e., 20–250 kbps. Bluetooth operates at 2.4 GHz frequency, designed using a star topology and transmits up to 100 m. Wi-Fi supports data rates of 11 and 54 Mbps, transmits up to 10 m and is organized in point-hub topology. Wi-Fi is suitable for fast networking and is inexpensive [15].

### 14.3.3 Challenges

Cyber-physical system enables the continuous and non-invasive monitoring of health data by using WBAN sensors, networks and cloud.

### 14.3.3.1 Sensitivity

In harsh environments like fire situation, the device sensitivity is considered as an important one. Sweat and water droplets also affect the performance of the sensors. Care should be given for designing high sensitivity and requiring less maintenance devices.

### 14.3.3.2 Energy

Sensors are battery-powered and energy consumption devices. It is very difficult to recharge the batteries. The design requires low power and green energy enabled devices.

### 14.3.3.3 Problems in Compatibility

Wireless body area networks consist of multiple sensors, working in different frequency levels. The integration of these devices requires different bandwidths and protocols, which creates compatibility problem. So, the design of healthcare systems should ensure compatibility between the devices.

### 14.3.3.4 Scalable

The system offers efficient migration among networks and uninterrupted service.

### 14.3.3.5 Device and System Level Security

Security algorithms are used to ensure device safety, although it is the main challenge in cyber-physical systems.

## 14.4 Attacks

The attacks are divided into two categories known as physical and cyberattacks. The physical attack causes damage to the system components. Table 14.2 describes the attacks occurring in various layers of cyber-physical system.

## Table 14.2 Attacks in CPS Layers

| Layer | Equipments | Function | Attacks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perception layer | Sensors<br>RFID<br>GPS<br>Actuator | Collecting physiological signals from patient's body and aggregating the signals | Capturing the nodes and leaks information<br>Passing malicious data to the nodes<br>Flooding more packets causes disruption in network<br>False measurements |
| Transmission layer | Network devices | Transmitting data to cloud for analysis | Data leakage<br>Delay in transmission<br>Jams wireless channel to introduce noise<br>Selective forwarding |
| Application layer | End-user devices | Monitoring the application<br>Decision-making | Overflow occurs in buffer<br>Malicious code |

Some attacks affect all layers such as eavesdropping, man-in-the-middle, denial of service, spoofing and replay attack. Spoofing is the process by which attackers read the data and then modify it by adding or deleting the data. Denial of service makes the network unavailable by blocking the traffic.

Vulnerability is a condition that can be exploited by an adversary to damage systems. A vulnerability assessment involves identifying weaknesses and finding solutions to reduce the vulnerability. These are classified into three types such as management, network and platform. A lack of security policies introduces management vulnerability. Network vulnerability includes hardware, configuration and monitoring vulnerabilities. Platform vulnerability involves database and hardware vulnerabilities. The reason for these vulnerabilities is the use of USB, poor programming skills, the inclusion of third-party components, and the increase in connections.

Cyber-physical systems not only suffered from vulnerabilities and threats, but also affected by failures in the system. The failures occur due to inaccurate information, delay in transmission, sensor node fault, lack of messages to transfer, increased implementation cost and errors in service upgrades.

## 14.5 Security Mechanisms

The open issues in healthcare systems are data security and privacy. It is a tedious job to maintain a secure environment due to challenges, limitations and issues that occur during integration, lack of accuracy and privacy. Based on criticality, the system is classified into mission, business, security and safety critical. Environment conditions such as flood and fire can cause damage to the system and lead to loss of life. This is called safety critical. In business critical, an attack can cause severe financial losses. The failure in the total or partial system due to attack is known as mission critical. Safety critical defines that an attack creates security gap in the system. The solutions proposed to ensure security are shown in Figure 14.3 [16].

The software-based security solution is achieved by Tinysec [17]. It implements authentication and encryption techniques in the link layer. This chip supports CBC-MAC authentication, CCM encryption plus authentication and counter mode encryption and decryption.
