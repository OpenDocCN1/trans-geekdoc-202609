# 12.5.4 Decentralized Identity Management

Decentralized Identity Management (DID) relies on blockchain technology to create and verify self-sovereign identities that are independent of any centralized authority. DID has various benefits, such as enhancing privacy, security, and portability of identity data, as well as enabling diverse applications and sue cases across domains. However, DID also faces several challenges, such as interoperability, scalability, and governance (Singla et al., 2022).

Traditional identity management systems are often centralized, making them susceptible to security breaches and identity theft. Blockchain technology enables decentralized identity management, where individuals have control over their personal information. Each user's identity is securely stored on the blockchain, and access is granted through cryptographic keys. This decentralized approach reduces the risk of a single point of failure, enhances data privacy, and provides citizens with greater autonomy over their identity information.

# 12.5.5 Supply Chain Security and Transparency

Supply chain security and transparency are crucial aspects of smart cities that require careful attention and management. Smart cities rely on a complex network of interconnected devices, sensors, and systems that generate and exchange vast amount of data. This data is often sensitive and valuable, and its security and privacy must be protected from theft, sabotage, or disruption. Blockchain technology can provide a solution to this challenge by enabling secure and transparent data sharing among different stakeholders in a smart city ecosystem (Sodhi et al., 2018).

Blockchain can enhance the security and transparency of supply chain processes within smart cities.
