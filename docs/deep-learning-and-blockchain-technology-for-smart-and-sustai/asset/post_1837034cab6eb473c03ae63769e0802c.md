## 17.3 Machine Learning

ML constitutes a segment of artificial intelligence (AI) focused on enabling computers to learn from data and make predictions or decisions without being explicitly programmed. In contrast to conventional programming, where humans dictate precise instructions to computers, ML entails computers learning without explicit programming directly from the data they encounter. ML is vital in predicting PD by analyzing diverse data types such as clinical evaluations, imaging scans, genetic data, patient demographics, and even through monitoring patient voice frequency in smart urban environments. By employing ML algorithms, it is possible to create predictive models that can detect patterns and correlations within this data, potentially signaling the onset or advancement of PD. Overall, ML shows significant potential for improving the early detection and management of PD by leveraging the vast amounts of data available to identify patterns and relationships that may not be apparent to human observers [9]. By developing accurate predictive models, ML has the potential to revolutionize the identification and management of PD leading to better outcomes for patients. ML, encompassing deep learning methodologies, has found extensive applications in medical research, including the exploration of PD. An illustration of this is provided by Shahid et al., who employed a deep neural network model to forecast the advancement of PD. The ML model acquires the ability to discern patterns within the data that correlate with the manifestation of PD, empowering it to forecast the disease status of fresh individuals by analyzing their features [4]. In essence, ML harnesses the richness of voice patterns and frequencies, alongside multidimensional datasets, to advance the early detection and management of PD. By unlocking hidden patterns and relationships within these datasets, ML holds promise in revolutionizing the identification and management of PD, ultimately improving patient outcomes. One approach involves utilizing supervised learning algorithms, wherein models are trained on annotated datasets containing individuals both afflicted and unaffected by PD [5]. These datasets encompass diverse features like motor function scores, demographic details, and medical histories. By learning from these labeled examples, the model can detect subtle patterns indicative of PD and forecast the disease status of new individuals based on their unique features.

## 17.4 Data Mining

Data mining involves delving into patterns, trends, and insights within vast datasets [10]. It entails deriving significant insights from raw data to unveil concealed patterns, relationships, and anomalies essential for decision-making and predictive purposes. The dataset utilized for data mining in PD prediction encompasses a comprehensive array of characteristics derived from voice recordings of individuals [11]. These features encompass fundamental frequency, jitter, shimmer, noise-to-harmonics ratio, and various nonlinear measures. Each feature holds significance in characterizing the voice patterns and frequencies associated with PD. By using data mining techniques such as clustering, classification, and regression, researchers analyze these features to unveil concealed patterns, associations, and irregularities, which can assist in the early identification and forecasting of PD within smart urban environments. The dataset provides an invaluable asset for researchers and clinicians to delve into the complexities of diagnosing and predicting PD, ultimately leading to improved patient outcomes and the advancement of more efficient treatment approaches [12]. The research seeks to add to the expanding wealth of knowledge in PD research and promote progress in utilizing data mining for healthcare purposes.
