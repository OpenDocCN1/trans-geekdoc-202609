# 17.9 Support Vector Machine

SVM, a supervised learning algorithm, is employed for tasks involving classification and regression. Renowned for its effectiveness in handling high-dimensional datasets, it is applied across diverse domains such as text categorization, visual pattern recognition, and computational biology [20]. In the initial phase, comprehensive datasets are gathered and meticulously preprocessed to extract relevant features, which may involve normalization, feature engineering, and dimensionality reduction techniques to enhance signal clarity and reduce noise. After dataset is being readied, it is split into distinct subsets for training and testing, guaranteeing an impartial evaluation of the predictive model’s efficacy [20]. The SVM classifier is then educated using the training data, with particular emphasis given to selecting the appropriate kernel functions like linear, polynomial, or radial basis function (RBF) that best captures the inherent connections within the data. Furthermore, hyperparameter optimization methods, such as grid search or randomized search, are employed to fine-tune parameters like the regularization strength (C) and kernel-specific parameters (e.g., gamma for RBF kernel), optimizing the model’s ability to discern between PD cases and healthy controls. Following training, the effectiveness of the SVM model is thoroughly evaluated through a peak of performance measures, which encompass accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and the area beneath the ROC curve. Furthermore, cross-validation methods are utilized to validate the model’s reliability and adaptability across different cohorts of patients [21].
