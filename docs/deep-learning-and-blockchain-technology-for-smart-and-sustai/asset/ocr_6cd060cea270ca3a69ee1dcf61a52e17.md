# ## 20.1 Introduction

The globe is changing routine at a price that has actually never been seen before, with the three biggest issues facing human societies in the coming years likely to be biodiversity loss, rising resource demand, and climate change [1,2]. The primary causes of these worldwide issues are increasing numbers of people and developing human activity, which keeps civilization itself constantly changing. The global food system is undergoing significant changes due to megatrends like globalization of trade and travel, urbanization, changing consumer behavior, and use changes, and it is brought on by a rise in the demand for animal-based proteins worldwide. Livestock systems are not excused from these changes [3].

In order to facilitate the shift to sustainable, wholesome, ecologically nutritious foods with strict animal welfare regulations, animal health will be essential. In addition to reducing poverty, preventing climate change, and diminishing biodiversity, the wellness of livestock is crucial for economic growth, global health, food safety, and the integrity of food [4,5,6]. One of the top priorities for achieving sustainable livestock systems is lowering the burden of animal diseases, particularly zoonoses, and controlling pandemic threats, emerging diseases, and antibiotic and antiparasitic resistance appropriately [7,8]. There are few specific tools available to control many animal diseases, and the existing animal health solutions must be perpetually invented to cope with problems such as evolving livestock breeding methods, client demands, remnants in food or the environment, resistant to drugs diseases, and effective end-user implementation [9].
