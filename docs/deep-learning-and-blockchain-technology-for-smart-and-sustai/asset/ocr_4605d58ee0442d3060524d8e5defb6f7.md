# 20.3.3 Dataset Description

The combined dataset used in this work includes photos from HAM10000 (2019) and MSLDv2.0. The images cover a wide variety of skin lesions that are divided into 14 different categories. These lesions provide a thorough depiction of dermatological disorders, ranging from common benign kinds to perhaps dangerous ones. An administered understanding of category troubles is made easier by meticulously tagging every image in the dataset with the appropriate lesion type. The dataset also contains extensive metadata, which expands the amount of information available for analysis. Examples of this metadata include lesion characteristics and patient demographics. To protect patient privacy, reduce bias, and ensure fair research techniques, ethical protocols are strictly enforced. By utilizing this unified dataset, scientists can create dermatological diagnostic designs that are a lot more exact and equitable, which will advance therapeutic applications pertaining to the category of skin sores and ultimately lead to better patient outcomes. # 20.3.4 Existing Methodology

Polymerase Chain Reaction, or PCR, has been a total game-changer in molecular biology and all sorts of other scientific fields. It lets researchers copy and amplify specific DNA sequences, which is super useful for studying genes and all kinds of other genetic stuff. But even though it’s super helpful, PCR isn’t perfect. A couple of obstacles occur when matched with it.

First off, PCR can be expensive. It requires fancy equipment, special chemicals, and other materials that cost a pretty penny. This can be a real barrier for scientists who work in places with limited resources. And that suggests they could not have access to PCR technology, which can limit the kinds of research they can conduct.
