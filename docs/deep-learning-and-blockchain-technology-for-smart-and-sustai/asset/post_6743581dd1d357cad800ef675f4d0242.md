The number of cyber threats in 2018 is observed as 208,456, which is doubled by 2022 to 1,402,810. They have also proved that the number of various cyber-related issues had been increased in the last 4 years particularly during the Covid-19 lockdown period. This is because people mainly worked from home. During this time, cyber-attackers masquerade as known person or products for confusing employees and internet users. In 2018, India ranked 47th out of 193 countries in cyberattacks. In 2020, India moved to 10th place.

The increase in cyberattacks will make a great loss for countries in terms of information and money. Worldwide, countries are creating new laws against cyber-attackers to punish them. Institutes and universities are also enlightening their employees on types of cybercrime attacks. Even though various crime detection mechanisms had been proposed, cybercriminals find efficient ways for committing cyberattacks. So, these cyberattacks need to be detected. In this paper, text-based email phishing detection mechanism has been proposed using the Bag of Words model and machine learning algorithms. The Bag of Words model is used as a feature reduction technique. Once the emails are preprocessed for removing stop words, numerals, special characters, etc., Rule-Based Subject Analysis (RBSA) and Semantic-Based Feature Selection (SBFS) techniques have been applied to the subject feature of email to compute weight, which represents the number of spam words in a subject. The computed weight is combined with sparse matrix for detecting the spam emails. Support Vector Machine (SVM), Multinomial Naive Bayes, Gaussian Naive Bayes, and Bernoulli Naive Bayes classifiers have been applied on the publicly available Enron dataset. Among all the classifiers, SVM gives the highest accuracy and the lowest FP rate.

## 2.2 Literature Review

For the purpose of detecting spam, the authors in 2019 suggested a hybrid water cycle optimisation algorithm that included simulated annealing. In order to maximise the result, this system focuses on minimising the feature set for spam identification and enhancing feature selection accuracy. For training and validation datasets, cross-validation is employed. Seven distinct datasets are used to test the approaches. For classification, the SVM classifier is used. The hybridization algorithm outperforms the particle swarm, genetic, and harmony search algorithms, according to the findings. A 96.3% accuracy rate is attained. In this work, the characteristics are decreased by more than 50% [5].

In 2018, the authors suggested using SVMs to classify spam emails. The email dataset is classified using a nonlinear SVM classifier. This methodology took into account emails from Gmail inboxes and the SpamAssassin public dataset, using both linear and Gaussian kernel functions. When compared to the Gaussian kernel function, it is discovered that the linear kernel yields the maximum level of accuracy for the test dataset. Using these two kernel functions to categorise the test dataset takes the same amount of time [6].

Using Natural Language characteristics, an anti-spam filter comprising five classifiers has been suggested in 2015 and tested on the Enron-spam and Ling spam datasets. To create binary classifiers, the real valued and natural language properties are taken out of the email content. Using five different learning methods, the classifiers are investigated and assessed using the non-personalized email datasets from CSDMC2010, SpamAssassin, Enron-Spam, and LingSpam. In this work, the relevance of the traits is measured using the BORUTA algorithm, which is also used to generate spam attribute scores. This study finds that ADABOOSTM1 and BAGGING outperform Random Forest and suggests that readability attributes are less significant than Natural Language features [7].

Fruit fly optimisation and the clustered negative selection method were proposed by the authors in 2017 for the detection of email spam. To increase classification accuracy, the fruit fly optimisation and K-means algorithms are coupled with the Negative Selection algorithm [8].

The authors had put forward an improved methodology in 2020 for email classification based on semantic features. This approach performed a semantic analysis of the email dataset’s text properties. There are two stages to this semantic-based text analysis. All of the emails are categorised into distinct domains at the first level, such as finance, education, and health. In order to identify spam emails, the second level combines the physically defined and often extracted semantic aspects of emails. This domain classification aids in offering a subject for the spam email description. This research uses the Naive Bayes, Decision Tree, Random Forest, Ada Boost, and K closest neighbour algorithms to categorise emails as spam or ham. Performance is estimated using the Ling-spam dataset, CSDMC2010, and Enron. This approach led to the conclusion that the suggested methodology performed better than the current methodology [9].

In 2020, the authors proposed the creation of multi-view email classification using semi-supervised learning algorithms, specifically for the Internet of Things. In this study, a multi-view strategy for spam email prediction is combined with semi-supervised learning methods. Data that are equally marked and unlabelled are used to assess the suggested system’s performance. Following preprocessing, the email dataset is used to extract the email body, routing, and forwarding properties. To build the models, a semi-supervised technique based on disagreement is implemented on both labelled and unlabelled datasets. In order to categorise emails as authentic or spam, various machine learning classifiers are finally applied to the email properties. This study found that the semi-supervised method based on disagreement produces higher accuracy levels (88.4%) [10].

By examining textual traits, it is possible to identify spam threats and contents in an email spam corpus. This research uses content text analysis with spam terms to classify different kinds of spam emails. This algorithm recognised many kinds of spam content. Chain-E-letters are recognised as an uncommon kind of spam thread among these, while advertisement messages are recognised as a frequent kind of spam threat [11]. In 2018, robust natural language processing (NLP) techniques had been implemented for phishing email detection. The technique of NLP is used to identify phishing emails. This method of detecting spam emails achieved an 80% accuracy rate [12].

The 2020 authors developed a number of machine learning algorithms for the classification of phishing emails. They conducted studies on machine learning-based phishing tactic evaluation. They demonstrated how effective the KNN, Naïve Bayes, and SVM algorithms are in classifying emails into phishing or genuine. Every algorithm’s accuracy has been analysed and compared with various performance metrics [13].

It is noted in [14] that phishing threats and counter-phishing techniques have been created for mobile devices and social media platforms in addition to emails and websites. The taxonomy that has been supplied covers communication channels, targeted settings, countermeasures, and phishing attack strategies, providing a thorough approach to the issue.
