## 14.6 Privacy Preserving Using Deep Learning Techniques

The widespread use of IoT devices has resulted in unparalleled connectivity and data gathering capacities, transforming a multitude of facets of our everyday existence and sectors. But as these gadgets are always gathering and sending private data about people, places and activities, the growing use of IoT also poses serious privacy issues. Privacy-preserving techniques are essential to ensure that IoT deployments respect users’ privacy rights and comply with data protection regulations. Deep learning, a subset of artificial intelligence (AI), offers promising solutions for preserving privacy in IoT environments by enabling intelligent data processing and analysis while minimizing the exposure of sensitive information.

The architectural diagram (Figure 14.5) provides an overview of the deep learning procedure in an IoT.

Data gathered from sensors is sent via a gateway to the cloud [22]. Because healthcare data are sensitive, secure data transmission is necessary. One of the main goals of security requirements is authentication, which guarantees that the right person will use the data [23]. Differential privacy, homomorphic encryption, Federated Learning, privacy-preserving data aggregation and other deep learning techniques are used to achieve it. Differential privacy assurances are achieved by introducing noise into data using algorithms like the Gaussian, Laplace and exponential mechanisms. This ensures that individual records cannot be distinguished.

The homomorphic encryption approach protects privacy during computation by enabling computation on encrypted material without first decrypting it [24]. Privacy-preserving machine learning uses methods like partially homomorphic encryption (PHE) and fully homomorphic encryption (FHE). Federated learning involves training models on a number of decentralized servers or devices that hold local data, and then centrally aggregating the model changes [25]. This method maintains the privacy and decentralization of the training data while enabling cooperative model training. Deep learning algorithms have the potential to improve the security posture of IoT devices and networks in addition to protecting user privacy. The following are a few applications for deep learning techniques: Threat intelligence, malware detection, intrusion detection systems (IDS), malware detection, malware security and behavioral analysis [26]. An IDS is a security mechanism designed to monitor and analyze network or system activities for signs of malicious behavior or policy violations [27]. IDS plays a crucial role in cybersecurity by providing early detection and response to security threats, helping organizations protect their assets, data and networks from unauthorized access, attacks and breaches. IDS comes in two main varieties: Network-based Intrusion Detection System (NIDS) and Host-based Intrusion Detection System (HIDS). Convolutional neural networks (CNNs), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), auto encoders and generative adversarial networks (GANs) are the main elements of deep learning based IDS in the IoT. CNNs are very useful for identifying anomalies in IoT network traffic because they can analyze intricate patterns in data. CNNs are able to identify minute variations in sensor and device raw data that could be signs of possible security risks. RNNs are perfect for identifying temporal trends of assaults in IoT contexts because they are skilled at processing sequential data. Through the examination of the device interactions or network traffic sequence, RNNs are able to detect suspicious activities that change over time.

## 14.7 Blockchain in Healthcare

A decentralized digital ledger system called blockchain technology keeps track of transactions over a network of computers. Because every transaction is kept in a “block,” which is then connected to other blocks in a chronological “chain,” the term blockchain refers to this arrangement of data. Important data can be exchanged while maintaining security and confidentiality due to this technology. It is the ideal solution for securely keeping all the relevant documents in one place. Blockchain also expedites the process of searching a single patient database for candidates who meet particular trial requirements [28]. Blockchain technology would be essential to changing the healthcare industry. Furthermore, the health system’s terrain is shifting toward a patient-centered strategy that prioritizes two key elements: always-available healthcare resources and easily accessible services [29,30]. The blockchain improves the ability of healthcare companies to offer proper patient care and top-notch medical facilities. Another laborious and repeated procedure that raises expenses for the healthcare sector is health information exchange, which may be swiftly resolved with the use of this technology. Data security and integrity, interoperability, patient control and consent management, clinical trials and research, supply chain management, and healthcare payments and billing are the advantages of utilizing blockchain technology in the healthcare industry.
