# ## 11.1 Introduction

By integrating blockchain with Internet of Things (IoT), artificial intelligence, and other emerging technologies, smart living ecosystems can be built to enhance sustainability, improve quality of life, and promote inclusive economic development. Blockchain-based electronic health records (EHRs) provide patients with secure access to their medical data while maintaining privacy and control over their information. IoT devices such as wearable sensors and medical implants can collect real-time health data, which is securely recorded on the blockchain, enabling personalized treatment plans and remote monitoring by healthcare providers. Blockchain can facilitate secure and efficient communication and data sharing among IoT devices involved in fall detection. Wheelchair-mounted sensors, along with other IoT devices such as smartphones or home automation systems, can seamlessly exchange data and trigger appropriate actions in the event of a fall. Smart contracts deployed on the blockchain can automate emergency response procedures, such as alerting caregivers or emergency services, based on predefined conditions. IoT sensors are embedded in different objects, devices, and environments, enabling them to collect data, communicate with each other, and respond to commands or triggers. These sensors can be utilized in numerous applications, such as home automation, healthcare monitoring, environmental sensing, and industrial automation.
