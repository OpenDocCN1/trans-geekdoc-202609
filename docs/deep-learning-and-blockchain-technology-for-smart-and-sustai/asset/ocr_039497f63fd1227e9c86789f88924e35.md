Through a network of interconnected sensors, machines, and data analytics, IIoT enables seamless coordination between production units, supply chains, and distribution networks. This streamlined connectivity fosters proactive maintenance, predictive analytics, and agile responses to changing demands or challenges [7].

In smart manufacturing, IIoT drives the convergence of physical and digital realms, revolutionizing production workflows [8]. Connected machinery equipped with sensors gathers real-time data, facilitating predictive maintenance, reducing downtime, and enhancing overall productivity [9]. These insights empower manufacturers to optimize operations, minimize waste, and tailor production to meet dynamic market demands swiftly. Moreover, IIoT’s impact extends beyond manufacturing, permeating sectors such as energy, healthcare, transportation, and infrastructure within smart cities. For instance, in energy management, IIoT enables smart grids that optimize energy distribution, reduce wastage, and promote sustainable practices. In healthcare, it fosters remote patient monitoring, predictive diagnostics, and personalized medicine, revolutionizing healthcare delivery. In transportation, IIoT ensures efficient logistics, enhances public mobility, and supports the evolution of autonomous vehicles, redefining urban mobility. In essence, IoT and IIoT stand as pivotal pillars in shaping the evolution of smart cities, fostering a connected ecosystem where data-driven insights, efficiency, and innovation converge to redefine urban living and industrial landscapes [6].
