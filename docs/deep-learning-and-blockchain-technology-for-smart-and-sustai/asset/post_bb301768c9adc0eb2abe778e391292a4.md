### 15.3.1 Data Exploration Analysis

- **Latitude**: The distribution of earthquake occurrences according to their latitudinal places is shown on the latitude box plot graph. The latitude values' central tendency, distribution, and skewness are all visually summarized by the box plot. Knowing the latitude distribution can help identify the regions with the highest frequency or concentration of earthquakes.

- **Longitude**: The distribution of earthquake occurrences according to their longitudinal positions is depicted in the longitude box plot graph. It provides information on the longitude values’ distribution, dispersion, and central tendency, much like the latitude box plot. Finding fault lines or locations vulnerable to seismic activity is made easier by analyzing the longitude distribution.

- **Depth**: The distribution of earthquake events according to their depths below the Earth’s surface is displayed in the depth box plot graph. It gives an overview of the depth distribution with central tendency and spread measurements. Determining the depth distribution facilitates determining the vertical extent of the seismic activity and its possible effects on the Earth’s surface and crust.

- **Magnitude**: The distribution of earthquake magnitudes, which represent the seismic energy released during earthquakes, is depicted in the magnitude box plot graph. It provides information on the frequency distribution, central tendency, and variability of earthquake magnitudes. Understanding the frequency and intensity of earthquakes in a particular area is made easier by analyzing the magnitude distribution, which supports risk assessment and disaster preparedness initiatives.

### 15.3.2 LSTM

An RNN architecture called LSTM models was created to overcome the shortcomings of conventional RNNs in identifying long-range dependencies in sequential data. In contrast, conventional RNNs have trouble during training with the vanishing gradient issue. Because of their distinctive design, LSTMs are ideally suited for time series prediction because they can efficiently identify temporal patterns and correlations in sequential data.

- **Data Preprocessing**: When preparing data for training a LSTM model, there are usually several preprocessing steps to ensure that the data is in an appropriate format so that the model can learn effectively. Here is a step-by-step description of the preprocessing methods used on the dataset.

- **Random Sampling Imputation**: This method substitutes observed values from the same feature that are randomly sampled for missing values. It preserves the variable’s distribution and is helpful when data is absent arbitrarily. The mean of the corresponding feature is used to fill in missing values in numerical features. Although the procedure is straightforward, bias may be introduced if data is not missing randomly.

- **Outlier Detection and Removal**: The distribution of continuous data can be seen and outliers can be found using boxplots. Observations that lie above the third quartile plus 1.5 times the IQR or below the first quartile minus 1.5 times the IQR are considered outliers. Next, outliers are set to a threshold value and capped or winsorized.
