## Enhancing Healthcare in Smart Cities: The Fusion of MCPS and IoT

P. Vimala Devi and V. Kalaichelvi

### 14.1 Introduction

A cyber-physical system is a means of integrating processes from the physical world into the virtual one. CPS uses sensors to measure signals from different settings, and then networks are used to transfer and process these signals. The use of cyber-physical systems is growing in a number of industries, including healthcare, smart grid, aerospace and automotive systems, thanks to recent technological breakthroughs. Among these, healthcare is crucial to day-to-day living. Medical Cyber-Physical System (MCPS) refers to the cyber-physical system used in healthcare applications. The integration of cyber-physical system with Internet of Things (IoT) creates a new dimension in healthcare applications, which is called Internet of cyber-physical systems [1]. The services in healthcare system consist of physical information, networking and computation. The services offered for healthcare using Internet of Things (IoT) are divided into six categories [2]. The categories are services provided during an emergency, services to help people with disabilities, services for medical professionals, personalized medical advisor, services for sports persons and medication management. The method used to process healthcare information is known as electronic health records (EHR) [3]. EHR is a structured information that includes image, text or both. It increases the interoperability between patient information and healthcare organizations, even though the system is suffered from security and privacy issues.

### 14.2 Background

Ashish Singh and Kakali Chatterjee introduced a model for healthcare to ensure the privacy of the data [4]. They also explained to avoid unauthorized access. The most important problem in the rural area is a lack of healthcare centers and specialists'. People in the rural area are not aware of the medical facilities, which causes different issues. To avoid this, a cloud-based healthcare system is developed by Kashfia Sailunaz et al. [5]. The patient's age, weight, height, blood pressure and temperature are collected by sensors and analyzed. After analysis, patients are classified into any of the three categories such as emergency, healthy and alarming. Identity-based encryption is proposed for secure storage in the cloud.

Yosef Ashibani and Qusay H. Mahmoud proposed a three-layer architecture for cyber-physical systems [6]. The layers are perception layer, transmission layer and application layer. These layers are responsible for data collection, transfer and analysis. They also explained possible attacks in each layer and measures. During data transmission, it is necessary to ensure that the data is transmitted from the proper node or device. The methods used to provide authentication for devices are encryption algorithms and biometric techniques. Abdullah Alhayajneh et al. explained the biometric authentication techniques for wireless body area networks [7]. Based on the availability of devices, the methods are categorized into hand wearable devices, body portable devices and implantable medical devices. The methods are compared in terms of sample size, accuracy and requirements. Syed Taha Ali et al. highlighted a scheme to authenticate the data in WBAN using hash tree and digital signature [8]. The hash tree is formulated using the samples collected from the sensor and then signed using ARQ protocol. The simulation shows that the 99% of data is authenticated with fewer transmission overheads.

### 14.3 Medical Cyber-Physical System

The architecture of cyber-physical systems is designed using components and layers. There are two types of components, one is for sensing and the other is for controlling. Sensors, actuators and aggregators are used as sensing components. It consists of a four-layer architecture [9]. The group of sensors present in the first layer, typically known as wireless body area network, is used to capture information. Because the sensors have minimum power for computation, a device, either concentrator or cloudlet, is required to aggregate the data (Figure 14.1).
