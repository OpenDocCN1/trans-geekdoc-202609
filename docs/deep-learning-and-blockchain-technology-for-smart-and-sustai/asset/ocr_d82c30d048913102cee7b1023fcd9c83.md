In the research, Alam [10] presented a novel method for limiting access to IoT device communication in smart city settings. This method takes advantage of the difficulties associated with access control in IoT connectivity, as well as the advantages of big data and blockchain technologies. Blockchain technology is used in the suggested approach to provide transparent and safe access control. To efficiently process and evaluate access control data, it also makes use of big data analytics. A potential solution to the problems with access control in IoT connectivity for smart cities is presented in this article. The suggested method aims to provide safe, transparent, and effective data exchange between IoT devices by merging the advantages of big data with blockchain technology. A methodology and optimization strategy for assessing the life cycle cost (LCC) of photovoltaic (PV) systems in Indian smart cities were proposed in the paper by Beniwal et al. [11]. The authors optimize the system architecture and compute the LCC using the Artificial Bee Colony (ABC) method, taking into account variables including energy generation, savings, efficiency, and carbon credits obtained. Four smart cities—Srinagar, Jaipur, Pune, and Chennai—that reflect various climatic conditions and energy demands are examined in the study. According to the data, Srinagar has the lowest LCC (INR 4,125 million) and the highest energy production (435 MWh), with an 8.9% return on investment. The suggested methodology exhibits a 50.24% decrease in LCC in contrast to conventional techniques [19].

Müller-Eie and Kosmidis's article [12] describes how sustainable mobility is developing in mid-sized Nordic smart towns. In ten mid-sized Nordic cities—Aarhus, Finland; Espoo, Finland; Gothenburg, Sweden; Helsinki, Finland; Kista, Sweden; Linköping, Sweden; Lund, Sweden; Oulu, Finland; Tampere, Finland; and Umeå, Sweden—the writers carried out a document analysis of mobility projects. According to the report, the cities are pursuing sustainable mobility in a number of ways, such as by boosting electric cars and public transportation, building infrastructure for cyclists, increasing walking, and enhancing traffic control. According to the study’s findings, mid-sized Nordic cities are significantly further along in their development of sustainable mobility. A blockchain-based system is proposed by Miloud Dahmane et al. [13] to provide data integrity and access management in smart cities. This is important because smart cities are susceptible to data breaches and unauthorized access because they are built on a massive network of interconnected sensors and devices. To provide integrity and access control in smart cities, the framework addresses tasks including determining the important parameters, enacting laws, and storing sensitive data on a blockchain. The paper also notes certain difficulties, such as the necessity for effective systems to handle access control regulations and possible scalability concerns with blockchain technology. The significance of robots and AI in combating the COVID-19 pandemic is examined in the paper by Lauri et al. [14], with a focus on smart cities. It draws attention to the different uses, such as helping medical professionals, improving public health initiatives, and bolstering supply chains and logistics. In order to effectively use robotics and AI in the event of a pandemic, the essay highlights how critical it is to build resilient organizations inside smart cities.
