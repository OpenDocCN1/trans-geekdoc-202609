By leveraging advanced sensor technologies, these systems can distinguish between normal wheelchair movements and irregular patterns associated with falls, ensuring a high level of accuracy. One of the key advantages of wearable sensors in wheelchair fall detection is their adaptability to various wheelchair types and user preferences. These sensors can be integrated into the wheelchair itself or worn on the user’s body, providing flexibility in deployment. This adaptability caters to the diverse needs of wheelchair users and fosters a user-friendly experience, encouraging widespread adoption of such technologies. In addition to fall detection, wearable sensors can contribute valuable data for healthcare professionals and caregivers. Continuous monitoring of movement patterns, daily activities, and environmental factors can aid in assessing the user’s overall well-being. This data-driven approach enables personalized care and intervention strategies, ultimately improving the quality of life for wheelchair users. Despite the promising potential of wearable sensor-based fall detection systems, challenges such as false alarms, privacy concerns, and user acceptance must be carefully addressed. Striking a balance between sensitivity and specificity is crucial to minimize false positives and negatives, ensuring the reliability of the system. Moreover, safeguarding user privacy through secure data handling practices is imperative to encourage user trust and compliance.
