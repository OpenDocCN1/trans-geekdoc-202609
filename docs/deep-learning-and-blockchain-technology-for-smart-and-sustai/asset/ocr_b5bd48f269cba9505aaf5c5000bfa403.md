# 13.1 Introduction

Particularly in heavily populated metropolitan areas, outdoor air quality is a crucial factor in determining both the health of the population and environmental sustainability. An essential tool for evaluating outdoor air quality and its possible effects on both the environment and human health is the Air Quality Index (AQI). To guide decision-making processes about environmental legislation, public health initiatives, and urban planning, accurate AQI level forecast is crucial.

Air pollution happens when things like airborne particles, gases, and biological molecules are released into the atmosphere of Earth in hazardous or excessive proportions (Zhu et al., 2018). Pollutants that produce air pollution include nitrogen dioxide (NO₂), sulfur dioxide (SO₂), carbon monoxide (CO), ozone (O₃), and particulate matter.
