## 17.14 Conclusion

The successful demonstration of the efficacy of utilizing vowel phonation data for PD classification in smart cities achieves an impressive accuracy of 98.305% with the KNN model. Additionally, the SVM model yielded a commendable accuracy of 94.915%, with both models exhibiting no false positives in the results. But the highest accuracy is achieved through KNN algorithm. Notably, the KNN model performed exceptionally well on a balanced dataset, further solidifying its potential as a reliable and accurate technique for PD prediction. According to the findings, the KNN model will be recommended for classifying the progression of PD. Its non-invasive nature, simplicity, and high accuracy make it a viable option for providing long-term relief to individuals living with PD on a global scale. By leveraging vowel phonation data, healthcare practitioners can utilize this approach as a potent tool for early detection and monitoring of the disease, thereby facilitating timely interventions and personalized treatment strategies. Looking ahead, the future scope of PD prediction in smart cities holds immense promise. With advancements in technology and ML algorithms, there is potential for further refinement and optimization of predictive models. Subsequent research efforts may concentrate on exploring additional features and biomarkers beyond vowel phonation data, incorporating multimodal data fusion techniques to enhance predictive accuracy. Furthermore, the integration of wearable devices and remote monitoring technologies could enable real-time monitoring of PD symptoms, facilitating proactive healthcare interventions and improving patient outcomes. Collaborative efforts between healthcare professionals, researchers, and technology developers will be instrumental in driving innovation and advancing the field of PD prediction. In conclusion, the investigation underscores the importance of harnessing ML methods, such as KNN and SVM, for PD classification using vowel phonation data. By tapping into the potential of data-driven methods, we can make considerable advancements in early diagnosis, tailor-made treatment, and enhancing the quality of life for those affected by PD.
