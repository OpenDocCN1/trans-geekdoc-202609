For privacy preservation, a Lightweight Consensus Proof-of-Work (LCPoW) approach is employed to reduce computational burden. Data features are transformed by an Authentic Intrinsic Analysis (AIA) model to enhance attack detection efficacy. Three tiers make up the AILBSM framework: AI-based attack classification, data authentication/attack prevention (via the use of blockchain), and trust evaluation. This method offers superior privacy protection over previous approaches, with high accuracy (99.8%) and quick detection (0.6 seconds). But the issue of storage costs is still unresolved. Using deep learning and a particular focus on the Modbus protocol, Gueye et al.’s research [12] presented a novel technique for detecting cyberattacks in IoT/IIoT devices. This method examines the values in the registers of the device, as opposed to conventional approaches that depend on network traffic analysis. In order to enable the model to characterize the sort of attack as well as determine whether an attack has happened, the authors incorporate an embedding layer into a neural network design. Deep learning has the potential to improve IoT/IIoT security at the device level, as this technology outperforms previously published models in terms of performance. The “Smart Collaborative Framework” (SCF) was presented in the paper by Ai et al. [13] in order to solve the problem of multi-task offloading in IIoT-MEC networks. This system makes use of a two-part model: the Fine-grained Resource Scheduling (FRS) module allocates resources taking into account aspects such as game theory and queuing theory, while the Hierarchical Spatial-Temporal Monitoring (HSTM) module predicts task urgency based on historical data. Through the integration of strategic resource allocation and deep learning for prediction, SCF seeks to accomplish effective and dynamic multi-task offloading in IIoT environments. A novel safety-as-a-service (Safe-aaS) plan for IIoT that makes use of federated machine learning and blockchain was proposed by Hasan et al. [14]. This plan seeks to enable efficient safety decision-making while simultaneously addressing privacy and security issues. The solution guarantees the openness and immutability of safety-related data by utilizing blockchain, while federated learning permits machine learning models to be trained on local data without jeopardizing privacy. Increased confidence among members of the IIoT ecosystem, lower costs, and enhanced safety are just a few advantages of this combination. MeAEG-Net is a revolutionary technique that was proposed by Wang et al. [15] for identifying anomalous traffic in –IIoT networks. This approach uses a generative adversarial network (GAN) that has only been trained on normal traffic to address the problem of limited labeled aberrant data.
