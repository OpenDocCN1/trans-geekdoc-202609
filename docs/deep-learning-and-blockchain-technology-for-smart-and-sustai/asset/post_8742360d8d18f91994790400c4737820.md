### 10.1 Introduction

Coal is the world’s primary source of energy. The development of society and industry has led to coal’s analytical quality that has become increasingly important in terms of production efficiency and pollution. The economic viability of coal-based industries depends on the availability of high-quality coal. Accurate classification supports economic assessments, cost-effectiveness analyses, and investment decisions in coal-related projects. Coal classification provides important guidance for production planning and resource assessment in the coal mining and production process. The classic categorization of coal approach, on the other hand, relies heavily on artificial experience categorization in the current mining operation. This necessitates a large amount of people and material resources, and automation is challenging to do. As a result, one of the most important research topics in coal mining and use is how to detect many types of coal fast, correctly, and in real time. Coal is categorized into three groups based on the degree of coalification: lignite, bituminous coal, and anthracite. Different varieties of coal are used in different ways at different times. Coal usage is differed based on their types and quality. The places where coals are used are thermal power plants, as well as boiler design and manufacturing. There are two standard conventional methods for classifying coal: the first is human classification, which is relatively rapid but has low classification accuracy and is challenging to automate; and the second is chemical analysis, which has high recognition accuracy but is difficult to automate. The downsides of this approach include its high cost and extended detection time. The ability to accurately and efficiently determine the kind of coal is critical for lowering classification costs and increasing classification efficiency. As a result, determining the type of coal fast and properly is a pressing issue that current mineral processing technologies must address [1].
