For privacy preservation, a Lightweight Consensus Proof-of-Work (LCPoW) approach is employed to reduce computational burden. Data features are transformed by an Authentic Intrinsic Analysis (AIA) model to enhance attack detection efficacy. Three tiers make up the AILBSM framework: AI-based attack classification, data authentication/attack prevention (via the use of blockchain), and trust evaluation. Using deep learning and a particular focus on the Modbus protocol, Gueye et al.'s research [12] presented a novel technique for detecting cyberattacks in IoT/IIoT devices. This method examines the values in the registers of the device, as opposed to conventional approaches that depend on network traffic analysis. In order to enable the model to characterize the sort of attack as well as determine whether an attack has happened, the authors incorporate an embedding layer into a neural network design. Deep learning has the potential to improve IoT/IIoT security at the device level, as this technology outperforms previously published models in terms of performance. The "Smart Collaborative Framework" (SCF) was presented in the paper by Ai et al. [13] in order to solve the problem of multi-task offloading in IIoT-MEC networks. This system makes use of a two-part model: the Fine-grained Resource Scheduling (FRS) module allocates resources taking into account aspects such as game theory and queuing theory, while the Hierarchical Spatial-Temporal Monitoring (HSTM) module predicts task urgency based on historical data. Through the integration of strategic resource allocation and deep learning for prediction, SCF seeks to accomplish effective and dynamic multi-task offloading in IIoT environments. A novel safety-as-a-service (Safe-aaS) plan for IIoT that makes use of federated machine learning and blockchain was proposed by Hasan et al. [14]. This plan seeks to enable efficient safety decision-making while simultaneously addressing privacy and security issues. The solution guarantees the openness and immutability of safety-related data by utilizing blockchain, while federated learning permits machine learning models to be trained on local data without jeopardizing privacy. Increased confidence among members of the IIoT ecosystem, lower costs, and enhanced safety are just a few advantages of this combination. MeAEG-Net is a revolutionary technique that was proposed by Wang et al. [15] for identifying anomalous traffic in IIoT networks. This approach uses a generative adversarial network (GAN) that has only been trained on normal traffic to address the problem of limited labeled aberrant data. The generator of the GAN makes an effort to reconstruct typical traffic, and anomalies are found by analyzing its reconstruction mistakes for real traffic. A "memory augmentation" module is added to boost the model's capacity to identify aberrant traffic by increasing the reconstruction errors for anomalous patterns. This semi-supervised method is appropriate for real-world IIoT settings since it efficiently finds anomalies without requiring a large amount of annotated anomalous data.

A two-phase model was presented by Ikram et al. [16] to forecast both regular and anomalous IIoT traffic. A random forest classifier is used in the second phase to forecast traffic patterns based on the features that were picked in the first phase, which used a modified whale optimization method to identify the most significant features from the data. On a variety of IIoT datasets, this method outperformed existing benchmark approaches in terms of accuracy, true positive/negative rates, and ROC curve area.

Using the Interpretive Structural Modeling (ISM) technique, Malhotra et al. [17] developed a hierarchical framework to assess the difficulties of deploying the IIoT in manufacturing. The framework finds and groups problems into technical, organizational, and social categories. It then examines how these categories relate to one another and creates a hierarchical structure. Important conclusions include the following: (1) the most significant technical constraints are insufficient resources and high investment costs; (2) the most significant organizational obstacles are insufficient resources and a skilled labor force; and (3) the most significant social barriers are privacy and data security concerns. With the purpose of successfully implementing IIoT in manufacturing, the hierarchical structure aids in the prioritization of problems and the design of focused solutions.

In order to improve data security in IIoT, Mathew et al. [18] investigated the intriguing combination of BCT and collaborative intrusion detection systems (CIDS). It draws attention to the shortcomings of conventional, centralized security techniques in the intricate world of IIoT and underscores the necessity of a more decentralized strategy. The immutability, transparency, and trustless collaboration of BCT are thought to be complementary to CIDS's multi-device threat detection capabilities. By examining different integration options, analyzing potential risks, and identifying research needs, the paper paves the path for an IIoT ecosystem that is more dependable and secure.

For IIoT manufacturing segments, Li et al. [19] developed a "Redundant Collaboration Secure Communication Scheme" (TS-RCSCS). This approach makes use of a "redundant-synergy" concept with several auxiliary pathways to address the shortcomings of single-path communication. In the event that a single node fails, this guarantees the authenticity, secrecy, and integrity of the data. It uses a "redundant-synergy transmission method" in conjunction with threshold signatures to guard against data loss and information interception. It also incorporates a technique to anticipate missing communication data in the event of a scheme failure, preserving data integrity. Experiments demonstrate how well the plan works in the IIoT to achieve high communication accuracy and data prediction.

## 9.7 IIoT Applications for Smart Cities with BCT

The combination of blockchain with IIoT offers a wide range of fascinating possibilities for smart cities, from efficiently managing garbage and energy grids to optimizing traffic and protecting citizen identities. Imagine a city where the use of energy is recorded openly, waste management is rewarded and free from fraud, traffic is efficiently managed with the help of real-time data, and water conservation is promoted with awards. The safe data exchange capabilities of blockchain promote departmental trust and cooperation, give citizens access to information, and even protect medical records and academic qualifications. These two technological innovations have the capacity to completely transform a number of facets of urban life and open the door to the development of safe, effective, and sustainable smart cities in the future. Popular applications are:

- Smart Grid Management
- Transportation Management
- Water Management
- Identity Management
- Environmental Monitoring

### 9.7.1 Applications of Smart Grid Management

The potential for transforming smart cities is enormous as Smart Grid Management, IIoT, and BCT come together. By collecting data from different grids, residences, and commercial spaces, IIoT sensors enable effective energy management via real-time monitoring and analysis. Blockchain guarantees safe transactions and makes P2P energy trading within the smart grid possible with its transparent and safe data storage. This integrated strategy streamlines grid operations for utility companies, enables consumers to make informed decisions about their energy consumption, and lays the groundwork for the smooth integration of renewable energy sources. In the end, this cooperation helps to create future smart cities that are safe, effective, and sustainable.
