# 10.2 Literature Survey

The authors of [4] identified the Coal and coal gangue using image processing and pattern recognition techniques. The coal gangue identification techniques have been developed and tested in different types of coal and coal gangue in various wavelengths. They also have concluded that the gray scale distribution and peaks of the coal images are entirely different. The authors made a study on the texture of coal and gangue and implemented it in coal gangue separation process in the electric power industry. The similarities and differences in texture of coal and coal gangue images have also been studied. The required features are extracted by applying the technique that combines both gray scale and coal texture. A gray scale co-occurrence matrix has been developed to attain automatic classification of coal and gangue texture features. This proposed model has also been improved using CNN. The neural network extracts image enrichment, data development, and convolutional layer from coal and gangue images.

The images are classified using softmax regression function, and the recognition results are trained and tested on tensor flow. The proposed method shows that the neural network discovered the characteristics of coal and gangue effectively. The problem of low coal and gangue recognition rates caused by standard image processing and feature extraction has been solved in this work. An essential stage within the coal mining process involves the classification of both coal and gangue materials. This pivotal step is instrumental in distinguishing between the valuable coal resource and the accompanying non-coal gangue. Through this classification process, mining operations gain valuable insights into the quality and composition of the extracted materials, enabling them to optimize resource utilization and enhance overall efficiency. The accurate differentiation between coal and gangue is paramount for effective resource management, ensuring that the extraction process aligns with quality standards and environmental considerations. This research has abstract relevance for the recognition and cataloging of coal and gangue, as well as laying the groundwork for the future growth of coal and gangue categorization robots.

In coal mine engineering [5,6], effectively identifying and separating coal gangue from raw coal is a critical challenge, as it significantly impacts the combustion quality of coal and mitigates the emission of harmful gases. Addressing this concern, a support vector machine (SVM) algorithm has been employed, utilizing the dielectric properties of coal and gangue for precise classification. This innovative approach not only enhances the accuracy of distinguishing between coal and gangue but also improves the efficiency of feature extraction.
