A method for forecasting urban air quality was proposed by Aggarwal and Toshniwal (2021). The dataset containing 15 Indian locales is used to run their model. They contrasted the suggested method’s performance with that of other forecasting models that are currently in use. They came to the conclusion that, in comparison to other approaches, LSTM model performs better forecasting.

Wu et al. (2019) developed a more advanced mixed-model approach for estimating the AQI in Beijing and Guilin by integrating the LSTM with the optimization algorithm and secondary decomposition.

Ma et al. (2019) highlighted how governing bodies, research institutions, and the general public are beginning to take air pollution seriously. The authors also stressed how numerous studies and research projects have used neural networks, deterministic approaches, ML, and statistical techniques to anticipate air quality. However, while these strategies are only applied in the vicinity of the stations of monitoring, the area lacking of monitoring stations receives little or no attention. As a result, they employed a mixed DL network to concentrate on the region without monitoring stations.

The authors (Gorai and Mitra, 2017) used BP optimization along with a multi-layer feed-forward. The top findings were divided into three distinct groups. The simplest Artificial Neural Network comes in first, followed by the best ANN for predicting every scenario, and finally, the variations of each neural network. They created the mode in a way that allows it to forecast every upcoming interval of 1, 2, 3, and 6 hours. By contrasting the outcomes of neural network techniques with observed data, the model prediction capability is evaluated.

# 13.3 Proposed Work

DL technologies are being used extensively in big data analysis to solve a wide range of problems related to regression and classification, including computer vision (Bengio, 2009), speech recognition, natural language processing, image processing, time series prediction, and particularly in the area of air pollution prediction. DL technologies have been used widely in big data analysis to solve a variety of classification and regression problems. In addition to categorizing pollutant levels and making long-term predictions for several days in the future, they are also capable of predicting particular amounts of pollutant concentrations.

While DL shows the potential to enhance air quality forecasting precision, a single network’s accuracy may be restricted in the face of more complicated issues. Numerous academics have looked into combining several DL networks.
