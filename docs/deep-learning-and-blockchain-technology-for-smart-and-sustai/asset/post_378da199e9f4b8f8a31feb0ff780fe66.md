### 20.1 Introduction

The globe is changing routine at a price that has actually never been seen before, with the three biggest issues facing human societies in the coming years likely to be biodiversity loss, rising resource demand, and climate change [1,2]. The primary causes of these worldwide issues are increasing numbers of people and developing human activity, which keeps civilization itself constantly changing. The global food system is undergoing significant changes due to megatrends like globalization of trade and travel, urbanization, changing consumer behavior, and use changes, and it is brought on by a rise in the demand for animal-based proteins worldwide. Livestock systems are not excused from these changes [3].

In order to facilitate the shift to sustainable, wholesome, ecologically nutritious foods with strict animal welfare regulations, animal health will be essential. In addition to reducing poverty, preventing climate change, and diminishing biodiversity, the wellness of livestock is crucial for economic growth, global health, food safety, and the integrity of food [4,5,6]. One of the top priorities for achieving sustainable livestock systems is lowering the burden of animal diseases, particularly zoonoses, and controlling pandemic threats, emerging diseases, and antibiotic and antiparasitic resistance appropriately [7,8]. There are few specific tools available to control many animal diseases, and the existing animal health solutions must be perpetually invented to cope with problems such as evolving livestock breeding methods, client demands, remnants in food or the environment, resistant to drugs diseases, and effective end-user implementation [9].

Within the poxviridae family, the infectious disease known as monkeypox is caused by the Zoonotic Orthopoxvirus, which is closely linked to smallpox and cowpox and infamous for possessing extremely intricate double-stranded DNA [10]. Primates and monkeys are the main carriers of the disease; however, the spread from person to person is also incredibly predominant [11].

The virus may also proliferate through secretions from the body. Numerous anatomical traits shared by other orthopoxviruses are present in the monkeypox virus. A dumbbell-shaped core, surface tubules, and an envelope make up its 200–250 nm size. The variola virus and the core region of the monkeypox virus genome, which encodes essential enzymes and structural proteins, have 96.3% sequence similarity. Still, the area of the genome that codes for host range factors and virulence factors is extremely different. Variola minor (1%) and variola major (30%) have greater instance death prices than monkeypox [12].

In a Copenhagen, Denmark laboratory, the virus was initially discovered in a monkey's body in 1958 [13]. As efforts to eradicate smallpox intensified in 1970, the Democratic Republic of the Congo identified the first human case of monkeypox [14]. People who live close to humid rainforests are frequently afflicted with monkeypox, which is primarily found in central and western Africa [15]. In close proximity to another sick person, animal, or item, the virus itself spread. It can be spread straight across another person, animal bites, respiratory droplets, or mucus from the mouth, nose, or eyes [16]. Those who have monkeypox may have a high temperature, muscle pain, and exhaustion at the onset of the illness. A reddish patch on the skin is the long-term result [17].

The number of cases of monkeypox is increasing even though it is not as infectious as Coronavirus Disease (COVID)-19 that has been documented thus far. In West and Central Africa, there were just 50 cases of monkeypox in 1990 [18]. Nonetheless, in 2020, there were 5,000 cases. Although monkeypox was previously thought to exist solely in Africa, numerous non-African nations in Europe and the United States revealed in 2022 that they had identified the infected persons [19]. As of December 21, 2022, 94 countries had recorded cases of monkeypox, with an overall of approximately 83,424 people, based on the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) [20]. People's extreme dread and anxiety are thus gradually increasing, and this is frequently reflected in their opinions on social media [21].

As immunity against smallpox vaccination wanes, there has been a sharp rise in confirmed cases of the virus in recent years, together with its geographic spread. The monkeypox outbreak in West Africa in 2017 was deadly, with a 6% mortality price in Nigeria. In September 2018, individuals from Nigeria brought instances of monkeypox to the United Kingdom, leading to infections among workers. The virus was also brought to America in 2003 by mice that had been transferred from Ghana. After being housed with puppies, people are ultimately infected. Monkeypox viruses are labeled into clades: West Africa and Central Africa. While human-to-human transmission has certainly now not been recorded formerly, communication within the clade has been documented, resulting in a 11% fatality fee. The virus responsible for the US outbreak was identified because the variant displayed better mortalities, rates of human transmission, and blood viral tiers than its counterpart from West Africa. Although there were no deaths reported in the US, adults experienced greater extreme impacts than youngsters in the course of this outbreak. A West African clade determined in Nigeria is responsible for the bulk of deaths in Africa, in which rates vary from 1.5% to 17% [22]. This variable can suggest the decline within the general immunity following the cessation of smallpox vaccination. Congenital monkeypox can potentially be transmitted from mother to child through the placenta or long, close contact following delivery [23].

In the clinical differential diagnosis of a rash, all conditions ought to be assessed. The occurrence of lymphadenopathy during the prodromal stage of the illness is one clinical indicator that could help distinguish monkeypox from smallpox or chickenpox. Obtaining a representative sample and sending it to a lab equipped with the required tools is imperative for medical practitioners who suspect monkeypox. Its severity and potential for treatment are determined by the laboratory test and specimen used to confirm monkeypox. Vital patient data that needs to be sent with specimens includes (i) the patient’s age, (ii) the fever beginning date, (iii) the time of specimen acquisition, (iv) the date of collection, and (v) the current state (rash stage) of the patient [24].
