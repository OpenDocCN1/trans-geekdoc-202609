As rainfall data is continuously collected and processed in real time, the smart infrastructure responds dynamically to changing weather conditions. When heavy rainfall is detected, automated responses are triggered within the smart drainage systems to optimize water flow and prevent potential flooding. The automated responses extend beyond drainage systems to include other facets of smart infrastructure, such as traffic management and emergency services. This integration with smart infrastructure contributes to the overall efficiency of urban systems. By leveraging technology and real-time data, cities can enhance their adaptive capacity to weather-related events. This approach not only improves the management of rainfall-induced challenges but also lays the foundation for a more resilient and sustainable urban landscape. Recent studies have explored the use of ML models, particularly the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) neural network, in urban flooding prediction. Chen et al. [16] proposed a method that combines a numerical model with an LSTM model to achieve high prediction accuracy and fast calculation speed. The limitations of the study include potential error accumulation due to the use of calculated results of the numerical model as training samples, as well as the need for improvement in accuracy by using measured storm flood data as the driver. The reduction in sample requirements due to the increasing amount of measured rainfall flood data and the progressive development of the neural network model algorithms [12] is also a limitation.
