# 9.4 Exploring Technological Advances

The right technologies, such as broadband mesh, public or private LTE, and Internet of Things (IoT) sensors, will depend on the specific requirements of each city and create the ideal smart city. In the IoT world, innovation is happening very quickly. IoT sensors have several uses in smart city applications, and the technology has the potential to change urban living. By using sensors in trash cans to track the weight of the bins, for instance, waste management expenses, traffic congestion, and air pollution can be decreased by only using trucks to remove bins when and where they are needed. IoT sensors can also be used to detect noise pollution and changes in air quality, warning city managers of possible problems before they become significant issues and detecting changes in environmental conditions before people could.

Broadband mesh can enable video monitoring of passing autos for better traffic control and public safety, among other essential municipal enhancements like lowering traffic congestion. 5G technology may make it possible for smart cities to communicate among devices more quickly, improving safety and communication overall. In particular, 5G can help ensure resilience and energy efficiency by enhancing system-to-system communication and alerting network operators to malfunctioning equipment in advance of possible outages. In the majority of cities that can accommodate 5G infrastructure, implementation is particularly effective. However, 5G’s novelty is one of its drawbacks. Large urban locations and more rural places might not have the same infrastructure available to serve these use cases just yet. This will alter as time goes on and 5G usage becomes widespread. Standardization organizations will then shift their attention to the following generation of communication. Controlling the lifespan of the services offered and the communication infrastructure that underpins them is crucial for governments and utilities.
