Digitizing raw material information from origins to each food processing sector is necessary for traceability. The provenance and traceability of data related to the food business can be guaranteed during this process via blockchain technology. This aspect has multiple proposals. For instance, the study of Tian [37] suggested building a supply chain platform in China from agricultural to food production using RFID and blockchain technology. This approach has proven to ensure that data on the food supply chain is traceable. However, research by Sander et al. [38] demonstrates how blockchain technology might enhance food safety by enabling the supply of traceable food items. Further evidence that the incorporation of blockchain technology into the food supply chain enables consumers to monitor the entire food production process can be found in Bettín-Díaz et al. [39]. A blockchain application for the Colombian organic coffee sector was also provided by the authors. Additionally, Lin et al. [40] suggest an IoT and blockchain-based food safety traceability system using electronic product codes (EPCs) tags. Through smart contracts, this system can specifically stop data manipulation and privacy exposure. To show how effective it is, a prototype is suggested for implementation.

+   v. Supply Chain Management: A product frequently comprises several pieces that are supplied by various producers located in different nations. Nonetheless, the supply chain might come into interact with some forged or subpar items. The cost of integrating anti-fraud systems throughout a product’s entirety is high. This issue can be resolved by combining BC technology with IoT. Specifically, each component will be linked to a distinct ID throughout the creation process. Along with this ID, an unchanging timestamp is also attached. A blockchain, which is traceable and impervious to tampering, can then contain the identification of each component. For instance, research by Konstantinidis et al. [41] demonstrates how a blockchain-based system might be used to authenticate a product’s partial ownership. Moreover, Kim and Laskowski [42] suggest a traceability ontology with the integration of blockchain and Internet of Things technologies, based on the Ethereum BC platform. Provenance of data in the supply chain can be guaranteed by the suggested architecture.
