# 16.2.1 IoT

The term “Internet of Things” (IoT) describes a network of physically connected objects that are interconnected and communicate and share information online. Equipped with sensors, actuators, and other embedded technologies, devices can collect and exchange information that allows them to interact with their environment and make their own decisions in certain situations. The IoT is revolutionizing many industries and changing the way we work, live, and interact with the outside world [6,7].

Devices and Sensors: IoT devices include a wide range of physical objects, from everyday things like thermostats and home appliances to industrial machines and wearables. These devices are embedded with sensors that collect information about their environment. Connectivity: IoT relies on robust network infrastructure to facilitate communication between devices. This can include various connectivity options such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular networks, and low-power wide area networks (LPWAN). Data processing and analysis: The large amount of data generated by IoT devices needs to be processed and analyzed for meaningful insights. Cloud computing and edge computing are important in managing and analyzing this data [8,9]. Triggers: IoT devices are often able to take actions based on the information they receive. Actuators allow devices to perform tasks or trigger responses, such as adjusting settings, sending alarms, or starting certain processes. User interface: Many IoT systems provide a user interface, either through mobile apps or web applications, that allows users to monitor and control connected devices. This interface often provides real-time information, alerts, and the ability to configure device settings remotely.

# 16.2.1.1 IoT Applications

Smart Homes: IoT devices improve home automation by connecting devices, security systems, lighting, and heating/cooling. Users can remotely monitor and control their homes to increase comfort, energy efficiency, and security. Industrial IoT (IIoT): In manufacturing and industry, the IoT is used for predictive maintenance, asset tracking, process optimization, and overall efficiency improvements. IIoT facilitates “smart factories” with interconnected machines and systems.
