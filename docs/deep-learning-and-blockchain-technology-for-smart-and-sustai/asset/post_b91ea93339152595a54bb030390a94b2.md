Their objective was to categorize potential applications based on the different aspects of smart cities to offer a more comprehensive analysis.

The paper by Rotuna et al. (2019) presents a blockchain-based model tailored for smart city ecosystems. By integrating self-sovereign identity (SSI) and smart contracts, the authors aim to foster secure and transparent interactions among citizens, public authorities, and IoT devices. Their analysis delves into the potential applications of blockchain technology across several domains crucial to smart city development. These domains include digital identity management, bolstering IoT security, facilitating private messaging systems, and enhancing the functionality of autonomous vehicles within urban environments. Through their exploration, the authors highlight the transformative potential of blockchain in revolutionizing various facets of smart city infrastructure and services.

Alnahari and Ariaratnam (2022) proposed a framework for applying blockchain technology to smart city infrastructure in various sectors such as energy, water, transportation, construction, and government. They explored the benefits and challenges of blockchain for enhancing data security, transparency, efficiency, and sustainability in urban systems. They also presented a case study of Tempe, Arizona, as an example of a hypothetical city that could implement blockchain in its infrastructure and operations. They concluded that blockchain has the potential to transform the entire infrastructure into a corporate entity, but also acknowledged the regulatory, technical, and cost barriers that need to be overcome.

Dzyatkovskii (2022) discussed the applications and prospects of AI and blockchain technology in smart cities, focusing on the domains of energy, transport, waste management, and air quality. It reviewed the current literature on how these technologies can improve the efficiency, sustainability, and security of urban systems, as well as presented some case studies and examples of existing or proposed projects. It also identified some challenges and limitations of implementing AI and blockchain solutions, such as data availability, scalability, interoperability, and public acceptance. The paper concluded that AI and blockchain can have a significant impact on smart city development, but they should not be seen as the only or optimal solutions for every problem.

Năsulea and Mic (2018) summarized the potential applications of blockchain technology for smart cities and smart communities, such as smart contracts, smart assets, digital identity, and voting. The paper also proposed a model for blockchain integration in smart cities, based on the interactions between the smart city, the smart community, and the individual. The paper discussed the advantages of blockchain technology in terms of security, privacy, efficiency, and innovation, as well as the challenges and limitations of its implementation. The paper concluded that blockchain technology could enable new forms of decentralized governance and coordination for smart cities and communities.

### 12.3.1 Vulnerabilities in Transportation Systems

Smart city transportation systems represent a convergence of traditional infrastructure with modern technologies such as IoT devices, sensors, and connectivity. These advancements, while increasing effectiveness, also create weaknesses that can be manipulated by malicious individuals. The interconnected nature of traffic control systems, autonomous vehicles, and public transportation infrastructure creates potential points of entry for cyberattacks. Threats may range from unauthorized access to traffic management systems, leading to traffic disruptions, to compromising the safety protocols and autonomous vehicles, risking public safety. As smart cities heavily rely on efficient and secure transportation networks, understanding and addressing these vulnerabilities are critical to maintaining the reliability and safety of urban mobility.

Smart transportation systems rely on ICTs to improve the efficiency, safety, and sustainability of urban mobility. However, these systems also face various vulnerabilities, such as cyberattacks, natural disasters, human errors, and sabotage. These vulnerabilities can compromise the functionality, reliability, and security of the transportation infrastructure and services, and cause negative impacts on the economy, environment, and society. Therefore, smart transportation systems need to adopt appropriate measures to prevent, detect, and respond to potential threats, as well as to ensure resilience and recovery in case of disruptions (Cristéa et al., 2016).

### 12.3.2 Energy Grid Vulnerabilities

Smart city energy grids leverage digital technologies to optimize energy distribution, monitor consumption, and integrate renewable energy sources. However, this connectivity opens the door to vulnerabilities that can have severe consequences. Cyberattacks on smart grids can disrupt the flow of electricity, leading to power outages that not only affect the functionality of the city but also pose risks to critical infrastructure and services. The decentralized nature of energy sources also introduces challenges in securing the entire energy grid. Safeguarding against potential threats is essential for ensuring a stable and resilient energy infrastructure within smart cities.

Smart grid networks, which combine conventional power grids with information and communication technologies, face susceptibility to a range of cyber threats that could jeopardize the reliability, accuracy, and privacy of both the power infrastructure and user data. Some of the main vulnerabilities include customer privacy, physical security, implicit trust, outdated equipment, and diverse stakeholders (Aloul et al., 2012).

### 12.3.3 Healthcare System Vulnerabilities

The digital transformation of healthcare in smart cities introduces vulnerabilities that impact patient data privacy, service delivery, and the overall integrity of healthcare systems. Smart healthcare systems rely on interconnected devices, electronic health records (EHRs), and telemedicine solutions. These systems are susceptible to cyberattacks that compromise patient data, disrupt critical healthcare services, and even manipulate medical records. Ensuring the security of these interconnected healthcare components is paramount to maintaining public trust and the confidentiality of sensitive health information.

Healthcare systems are vulnerable to cyberattacks due to the sensitive and valuable data they store and transmit, such as personal health information, financial data, and medical records. Cyberattacks can compromise the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of healthcare data, as well as the safety and quality of healthcare services. Some of the common cyber threats facing healthcare systems include ransomware, malware, denial-of-service, data breaches, and phishing. To prevent and mitigate these threats, healthcare systems need to adopt security standards, protocols, and technologies that can ensure data protection, privacy, and trust. Blockchain technology is one of the promising solutions that can enhance healthcare security and interoperability (Safavi et al., 2018).

### 12.3.4 Data Management and Privacy Concerns

The vast amount of data generated by smart city components, including IoT devices, sensors, and surveillance systems, poses significant challenges in terms of secure storage, transmission, and protection of citizen privacy. Unauthorized access to sensitive data can lead to identity theft, privacy breaches, and erode public trust in smart city technologies. The centralized storage of large datasets becomes a potential target for cyberattacks, necessitating robust data management practices and encryption protocols. Addressing these privacy concerns is essential for building and maintaining public confidence in the adoption of smart city technologies.

Smart cities rely on big data to provide various services and solutions for urban challenges, such as transportation, energy, health, and security. However, big data also poses significant risks to the privacy and security of the citizen’s data, especially when it is collected, stored, and analyzed by different entities without proper consent or protection (Atoum & Keshta, 2021). Some of the vulnerabilities and threats that smart cities face include data breaches, cyberattacks, unauthorized access, data misuse, identity theft, and surveillance.
