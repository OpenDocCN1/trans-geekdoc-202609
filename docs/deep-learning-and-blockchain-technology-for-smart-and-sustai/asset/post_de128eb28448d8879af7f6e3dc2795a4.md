Dwivedi et al. [26] suggest automating the medical diagnosis of monkeypox, a novel pandemic concern, by the use of deep learning models. Promising findings are obtained when comparing the efficiency of the ResNet50, EfficientNetB3, and EfficientNetB7 algorithms on a small dataset. According to the paper’s findings, ResNet50 attained an accuracy of 83%, EfficientNetB3 generated 86%, and EfficientNetB7 first performed well but saw a sharp decline in Accuracy when more epochs were added.

The use of deep learning algorithms for the automated diagnosis of skin lesions linked to monkeypox is discussed in Singh and Songare [27]. Out of the four examined models, GoogLeNet scored 88.27%, indicating that it may help with early diagnosis and surveillance for this illness. Such computer-aided detection techniques could lead to a decrease in the number of incorrect diagnoses and better public health results in relation to global epidemics of monkeypox. This work demonstrated the correct identification of skin lesions linked to monkeypox by deep learning algorithms. This study shows that more investigation into computer-assisted detection techniques is necessary to lower the rate of misdiagnosis and enhance public health outcomes in relation to global epidemics of monkeypox.

Sahin et al. [28] presented a MobileNetv2-based system that successfully classified photos with 90.12% accuracy. Encouraging those who are infected to act quickly and seek professional guidance for a definitive diagnosis will help lower the rate at which the infection spreads. Given that it can be trained using many datasets using a similar deep transfer learning technique, it may also be used in treating other skin conditions. When evaluated using the Monkeypox Skin Lesion Dataset (MSLD) database (a publicly available dataset), the results presented in the literature outperformed other methods concerning measures.

Rabaan et al. [29] address the recent outbreak of monkeypox and how surveillance could be improved by computer-assisted detection. Additionally, it presents the MSLD. This new dataset uses deep learning models to reliably classify skin lesion photos of chicken pox, measles, and monkeypox that were gathered from websites and news portals.

Bengesi et al. [30] investigate public opinion regarding the most recent monkeypox outbreak in order to assist policymakers. It involved gathering multilingual tweets on monkeypox, utilizing TextBlob and VADER for sentiment analysis, and developing, testing, and evaluating 56 classification models for performance evaluation based on F1 Score, Accuracy, and other metrics. Approximately 500,000 tweets discussing the monkeypox Twitter posts from a variety of languages have been gathered for this article. It used the VADER and TextBlob annotation methods for positive, negative, and neutral feelings. The researchers developed 56 classification models for vocabulary normalization and included vectorizations.

Reference [31] describes a novel detection system called Human Monkeypox Detection (HMD), which uses AI techniques to identify monkeypox patients early on. The two main parts of this HMD are the Selection Phase, which selects the best attributes, and the Detection Phase, which uses an Ensemble Diagnosis model and a weighted vote mechanism to combine three distinct diagnostic algorithms into one diagnosis. The suggested HMD technique consists of two fundamental components: “Selection” and “Detection”.

An Improved Binary Chimp Optimization technique is used in the Selection phase to choose important characteristics prior to going on to the DP phase of learning an Ensemble Diagnosis model. In this IBCO algorithm, the Wrapper Selection Layer is in charge of creating the Binary Chimp Optimization Algorithm’s starting population, while the Filter Selection Layer is in charge of quickly choosing important components.

A study on the current monkeypox outbreak is presented in [32]. The 2022 MPXV B1 lineage and its offspring lineages were identified via Panel 2. In real-time PCR assays, both panels demonstrated great specificity for distinguishing distinct strains of the monkeypox virus, with no cross-reactivity with other viruses. Other poxviruses were not the subject of the article; only MPXV detection and distinction were covered. It did not assess the tests’ potential as a diagnostic tool or how well they perform in clinical samples.

Reference [33] carried out an extensive search of the literature and analysis of the historical background, vaccine and treatment developments, regulatory policies, host cell evolution and mutation, and vaccination. According to recent studies, the pox strain that is now in circulation has many genetic changes linked to the control of APOBEC3. Sexual contact has also been suggested as a possible means of spreading the already widespread disease.

It is imperative that immunizations epidemics administered and that an anti-pox drug be developed quickly. To ensure that epidemics are eradicated, further study is needed.

Reference [34] addresses the transmission, pathophysiology, and clinical manifestation of the virus, as well as the recent monkeypox outbreak and its probable causes. Furthermore, we investigate if the virus that causes monkeypox could leave Africa and establish itself there as an endemic. Despite data indicating that disconnected clusters and human-to-human transmission may still be occurring, many strategies have been implemented to tackle this epidemic, such as active case identification, contact tracing, isolation, and postexposure immunization.

In order to find the monkeypox virus (MPXV) DNA in 154 human samples, Reference [35] employed four different approaches in their study. The NovaplexTM MPXV Assay, a real-time PCR assay that employs certain primers and probes to identify viral genetic material in clinical specimens, was the first technique used. The second approach used the Bio-Speedy® Monkeypox Virus qPCR Kit, which has its own set of primers and probes specifically made for monkeypox detection. It makes use of real-time PCR technology.

The CDC's guidelines state that there is currently no effective treatment for monkeypox virus [36]. However, in response to the pressing need, the CDC authorized the use of two oral medications, Brincidofovir and Tecovirimat, which were previously primarily used to treat the smallpox virus, to treat the monkeypox virus. The most effective way to combat the monkeypox virus is by vaccination. Although there are vaccines against the monkeypox virus that have been licensed by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), they have not yet been given to humans in the US. Smallpox vaccinations are also utilized to treat monkeypox in other nations [37,38].

Early detection of the peculiar features of the skin lesions and the background of exposure are important elements of the monkeypox diagnosis process. However, employing electron microscopy to assess skin lesions is the only surefire method for identifying the virus. Furthermore, PCR is a useful diagnostic tool for COVID-19 patients. PCR can confirm the visibility of the monkeypox virus [39,40,41].

A new area of machine learning (ML) called transfer learning (TL) has shown promise in the identification and imaging of medical conditions, for example, it works with deep Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)-based methods to automatically identify malaria parasites in blood cell pictures in Reference [42]. To diagnose malaria from microscopic pictures, Reference [43] recommended combining models based on Support Vector Machine (SVM) and Visual Geometry Group (VGG)-19. In 93.1% of cases, their suggested algorithm correctly identified photos with malaria. To enhance the CNN model's performance in diagnosing breast cancer, Reference [44] developed a shallow-deep CNN-based model; their suggested techniques produced an accuracy of 85%. References [45,46] achieved an accuracy of 73.1% when they built a modified inception-based model utilizing 453 Computed Tomography (CT) scan pictures. In order to identify skin conditions including psoriasis, melanoma, lupus, and chickenpox, Reference [47] suggested using a low complex CNN. They demonstrate that skin illness can be detected accurately by 71% with picture analysis when VGGNet is used. By obtaining an accuracy of about 78%, their suggested approach, in contrast, shows the best outcomes. According to Reference [48], who presented a smartphone-based method for identifying skin diseases using MobileNet, they were able to identify individuals with chickenpox symptoms with an accuracy of 94.4%. To identify skin conditions such as acne, candidiasis, cellulitis, and chickenpox, Reference [49] used a range of segmentation techniques.
