# 17.1 Introduction

Parkinson's disease (PD) exists as a prevalent neurological disorder profoundly impacting motor functions, speech, and posture as neurons die off, and there's a decrease in dopamine levels in the brain, resulting in dopamine levels within the brain [1]. This deficiency disrupts synaptic communication, resulting in impaired motor functions marked by tremors, muscle rigidity, and bradykinesia. The progression of symptoms varies among individuals, but balance issues and tremors remain widespread manifestations of dopaminergic neuron degeneration. Regrettably, no cure for PD exists, necessitating patients with Parkinson's (PWP) to rely on early identification and personalized treatments to alleviate the disease's advancement. The disease is categorized into five stages, with vocal cord injuries emerging as a prevalent symptom in stage 0 for approximately 90% of PWP [2]. Severe lesions were observed in key brain regions, including the nucleus of the stria terminalis, components of the hypothalamus, and non-thalamic nuclei projecting to the cerebral cortex.
