# ABSTRACT

The execution traces generated by an application contain information that the developers believed would be useful in debugging or monitoring the application, it contains application states and significant events at various critical points that help them gain insight into failures and identify and predict potential problems before they occur. Despite the ubiquity of these traces universally in almost all computer systems, they are rarely exploited because they are not readily machine-parsable. In this paper, we propose a Multi-Agents approach for prediction process using Blockchain technology, which allows automatically analysis of execution traces and detects early warning signals for system failure prediction during executing. The proposed prediction approach is constructed using a four-layer Multi-Agents system architecture. The proposed prediction approach performance is based on data prepossessing and supervised learning algorithms for prediction. Blockchain was used to coordinate collaboration between agents, and to synchronize prediction between agents and the administrators. We validated our approach by applying it to real-world distributed systems, where we predicted problems before they occurred with high accuracy. In this paper we will focus on the Architecture of our prediction approach.

# 1 INTRODUCTION

With recent developments in computing, we have witnessed a rapid increase in levels of reliance on computing technology for a variety types of daily use software, as it has become dependent on a series of heterogeneous computing systems. For example, banking systems, health systems, transportation systems, etc. Therefore, we must work to keep these systems working and reduce their downtime because it is very important and has many effects on the functions of society. There are many examples that have shown the effects of computing failure. For example, in 2012, 6 million customers of the Royal Bank of Scotland Group in the United Kingdom and Ireland found they had no access to their accounts for weeks [5]. Also, in March 2008, 140,000 people were affected by a computing failure in the baggage system at Heathrow Airport and the cost of the failure was estimated at 32 million dollars [5]. Hence, this highlights the need to ensure continuity of the system. Each system has its own database containing the history of the different operations performed at each moment. These data are called logs. logs are a rich source of useful information that helps developers debug or monitor app errors to ensure system dependability. In general, logs are unstructured text generated by logging statements in system source code. Looking at relevant researches on log analysis, we find that it focused on three distinct management problems; (i) Forensic analysis, which aims to identify the root cause of system failure after an occurrence [13, 30]. (ii) Fault detection, which aims to quickly detect signs of system failure when they occur [29]. (iii) System failure prediction, which aims to provide early warning of a system failure before it occurs [28, 31]. One of the challenges of log-based failure prediction is the number of features required or the length of time period required to make the prediction [28, 31]. For example, we find that current research [28] uses nearly 400 features extracted from system logs. While [31] he uses a certain period of time to make prediction.
