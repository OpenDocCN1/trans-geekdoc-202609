
## A. Category Theory Basics

The natural notion of ‘sameness’ for categories is equivalence:

**Definition A.1 (Equivalence).** An equivalence between two categories $C$ and $D$, written $C \xrightarrow{\sim} D$, consists of a pair of functors $F : C \to D$ and $G : D \to C$ with natural isomorphisms (natural transformations where every component has an inverse) $F \circ G \cong 1_C$ and $G \circ F \cong 1_D$.

**Definition A.2 (Initial).** An object $I$ in a category $C$ is called **initial** if for every $X \in C$ naturally holds that $\mathcal{C}(I, X) \cong 1$, meaning that there is only one map of type $I \to X$.

**Definition A.3 (Terminal).** An object $T$ in a category $C$ is called **terminal** if for every $X \in C$ naturally holds that $\mathcal{C}(X, T) \cong 1$, meaning that there is only one map of type $X \to T$.

**Definition A.4 (Limit).** For categories $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{J}$, a diagram of shape $J$ in $\mathcal{C}$ is a functor $D : J \to \mathcal{C}$. *Definition B.6.* For a given endofactor $F$, its algebra and algebra homomorphism we denote by $\text{Alg}_{Endof}(F)$; For $(F, \sigma)$ a well-pointed endofactor we denote by $\text{Alg}_{Endof}(F)$ the category of algebras and morphisms thereof. *Lemma B.7.* Suppose $F$ is an endofactor on a category $\mathcal{C}$ with coproducts. Then there is an equivalence of categories $\text{Alg}_{Endof}(F) \simeq \text{Alg}_{Pre}(F + id)$.

*Definition B.8.* Given $F : C \to C$, an algebraically free monad on $F$ is a monad $\text{Free}(F)$ together with an equivalence of categories $\text{Alg}_{Endof}(F) \simeq \text{Alg}_{Mnd}(\text{Free}(F))$ which preserves the respective functors to $C$ that forget the structure.

### B.2. Kelly's Unified Transfinite Construction

The existence for algebraically free monads is Kelly's unified transfinite construction (Kelly, 1980).

**Definition B.9 (Reflective subcategory).** A subcategory $\mathcal{D}$ of a category $\mathcal{C}$ is **reflective** if the inclusion functor $F : D \to \mathcal{C}$ admits a left adjoint. A reflective subcategory is **locally presentable**.

*Example B.10* (Reflective categories are graphs). Nearly every category encountered in practice is a locally presentable category: The categories of monads, groups, vector spaces, and modules. As the categories of topological spaces, metric spaces. While beyond the scope of this document to explain much, local presentability is a powerful notion of what it means for objects to be defined by equalities.

**Definition B.12 (Accessible category and accessible functor).** For an ordinal $\kappa$, a $\kappa$-accessible category $\mathcal{C}$ is a category such that:

- $C$ has $\kappa$-directed colimits;
- there is a set of $\kappa$-compact objects which generate $C$ under $\kappa$-directed colimits.

A $\kappa$-accessible functor $F : C \to D$ is a functor between $\kappa$-accessible categories which preserves $\kappa$-filtered colimits.

*Remark B.13.* The accessibility of a functor can be thought of as an upper-bound on the arity of the operations it abstracts. For example finite sums are finite sums.

**Definition B.14.** Let $C$ be a $\kappa$-locally presentable category and $F : C \to C$ be a $\kappa$-accessible endofunctor. $F^\kappa$ be the $\kappa$-directed colimit of the diagram

$F^0 \to F^1 \to F^2 \to \dots \to F^\kappa$

where $F^0 = id_C$ and $F^{\alpha+1} = F \circ F^\alpha$ for $\alpha < \kappa$ and $F^\kappa = \lim_{\alpha < \kappa} F^\alpha$ as a limit.

**Lemma B.15.** For $C$ and as above, $F^\kappa$ is a monad. The canonical inclusion of $id_C$ into the colimit $F^\kappa$ and the multiplication comes from the preservation of $\kappa$-filtered colimits by $F$.

**Theorem B.16.** Assume the hypotheses of Lemma B.15 with $\kappa$ the ordinal in those hypotheses. Then $\text{Alg}(F)$ is equivalent to $\text{Mod}(F^\kappa)$ i.e. $F^\kappa$ is an algebraically free monad for $F$.

Remark B.17. For the endofunctors we study here, $Free(F) \cong (F + id)^*$ is the underlying endofunctor of an algebraically free monad for $F$, $Free(F)$.

The above formula allows us to derive the explicit formula for free monads, which has a dual formula in the case of cofree monads (Ghani et al.).

**Proposition B.18 (Cofree monads).** Let $F : C \to C$ be an endofunctor. Then the free monad on $F$ is given by $\text{Free}(F)(X) = \text{Fix}(X \to F(X))$. Dually, the cofree monad on $F$ is $\text{Cof}(F)(X) = \text{Fix}(F(X) \to X)$.

**Example B.19** Free monad on $1+A$. Free on the endofunctor $1+A$ is $\text{List}_{+1}(A)$ of lists of elements $A$ whose last element is not $1$; i.e. an element of type $1$, instead an element of type $Z$. That is, lists which end with an element of $Z$.

**Example B.20** Free monad on $A(-)^2$ is given by $\text{Tree}(A(-)) : \text{Set} \to \text{Set}$, mapping a set $Z$ to the set of trees with $A+Z$ labelled leaves.

**Example B.21** Cofree monad on $(I \times -)$. The cofree monad of the endofunctor $(I \times -)$ is $\text{Fix}(X \to I \times X)$, mapping an object $Z$ to the set of streams whose outputs are $O \times Z$.

**Example B.22** Cofree comad on $(I \to O \times -)$. The comad of the endofunctor $(I \to O \times -)$ is $\text{Fix}(O \times X \to X)$, outputting an additional element of $Z$ at each step does not depend on $I$.

## C. Categorical Deep Learning Examples

To illustrate the power of categorical deep learning as a framework that subsumes geometric deep learning (Bronstein et al., 2021), we make the reader see three additional examples demonstrating equivariance constraints of established geometric deep learning architectures, leveraging the framework of CDL.

All of these examples are faithful to Geometric DL practitioners, and are covered in prior papers (Maron et al., 2018; Cohen et al., 2018; Thomas et al., 2018; Cohen & Wells, 2016), we believe that relegating their exact derivations to appendices is appropriate in our work.

Before we begin, recall the core template of our approach: that we represent neural networks $f : A \to B$ as monad homomorphisms between two algebras $(A, a)$ and $(B, b)$, for a monad $(M, \eta, \mu)$.

```
M(f)   M(B)
|       |
v       v
A  ----->  B
```

and that geometric deep learning is recovered by making our group the group action monad $M(X) = G \times X$.
