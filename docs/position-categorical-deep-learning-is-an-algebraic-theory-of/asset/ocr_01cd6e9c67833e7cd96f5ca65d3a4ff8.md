Our framework also offers a proactive path towards equitable AI systems. GDL already enables the architectural imputation of protected classes (Choraria et al. (2021) for example). With CDL, we hope to enable even finer control. By way of categorical logic, we hope that CDL will lead to a deeper understanding of the relationship between architecture and logic, in particular clarifying the logics of inductive bias. Our framework will eventually allow us to specify the kinds of neural networks and constructions we wish, permitting thereby for assessing biases, fairness in reasoning by DL models at example. We believe that this is the right path to AI clarity and safety, not merely explainable, but verifiable AI.

# 4. New Horizons

Our framework gives the correct definition of various architectures of neural networks as universal parameters known in computer science. This methodology potentially opens up new avenues for research.

Firstly, any results of categorical deep learning as presented here rely on choosing the right category to order; in like manner deep learning relied on the choice of group symmetry. We have seen that many algorithms—general equivariance constraints—can be parametric, and as a consequence, the kind of constraints we can learn more: neural networks that learn not merely correct but verify correct logical argument, or order. This has ramifications for code synthesis: we can, for example, specify neural networks that learn well-tuned functions by choosing appropriate biases as their domain and codomain.
