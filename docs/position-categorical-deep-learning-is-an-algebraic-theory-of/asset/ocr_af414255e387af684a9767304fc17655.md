# D. Lawvere Theories and Syntax

de Haan et al. (202a) expanded the theory of equivariant layers in neural networks using the abstraction of natural transformations of functions. In this section, we will explain how to understand morphisms of monads, and in the semantics of algebras in these terms.

Definition 1.1. If C is a category, a presheaf on C is a functor $C^{op} \rightarrow Set$. The category whose objects are presheaves and morphisms are natural transformations is denoted P(C).

Fix a monad T on Set and $C_T$ denote its category of algebras and homomorphisms.

Given an algebra $A \in C_T$, the way to interpret A as a functor is via the Yoneda embedding $C_T \rightarrow P(C_T)$, which identifies A with the presheaf $[-, A]$. It is a standard result that the Yoneda functor is faithful, which means that we can identify algebras with morphisms as presheaves by the definition of limits and colimits.

Furthermore, these presheaves have a special property. If $\lim j$ is a small colimit in C, then $[\lim j, A] = [\lim j, A]$, essentially to + to the class of all, and 0 to the reader of empty

Definition D.2. If C is a category and J is a class of small colimits in C, then a J-continuous presheaf on C is a presheaf $F \in P(C)$ such that $F(\\lim j) = \lim F(j)$ for all $j \in J$. The corresponding full subcategory of PSh(C) is denoted PSh(C), or simply PSh(C) in the case that J is the class of all small colimits. We will use $[\ ]$ to refer to the class of all colroducts, and 0 to the reader of empty

It turns out that C has a nice property: it is strongly compact, meaning that every continuous presheaf is representable as above by an object of $C$. In other words, we have the identification $C = PSh(C).

However, it is impractical to work with the entire category C. When possible, we want to reason in terms of a more tractable vocabulary. This will provide us with syntax for monads. Following Ehrenmann's concept of a "column sketch" (Brandenenburg, 2021), a category equipped with a restricted set of classes. Rather than giving the general definition, we will describe few special cases of interest here.

Definition D.3. The Kleisli category $K_T$ is the full subcategory of $C_T$ on the free algebras. The finitary Kleisli category $K_T^F$ is the free algebras TS for a finite set. The unary Kleisli category $K_T^U$ is the full one-object subcategory on the free algebras T1.
