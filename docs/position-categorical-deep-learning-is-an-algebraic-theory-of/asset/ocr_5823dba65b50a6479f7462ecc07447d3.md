# B.1. Well-pointed Endofactors and Algebraically Free Monads

In this subsection we'll relate endofactors and their algebras to monads by way of well-pointed endofactors and the transfinite construction for an algebraically free monad on such an endofactor.

# Definition B.3. A well-pointed endofactor $(F, \sigma)$

comprises an endofactor $F : C 	o C$, and a natural transformation $\sigma : id_C \Rightarrow F$. A pointed endofactor is said to be well-pointed if $F$ and $\sigma$ either give the same from the left or the right or in others, the natural transformations $\sigma \circ F$ and $F \circ \sigma$ (which are of type $F \Rightarrow F$) are equal.

*Remark B.4.* Well-pointedness is a property, not structure, algebras for well-pointed endofactors are just algebras for the pointed endofactors. Morphisms of algebras for pointed endofactors are morphisms of algebras for the underlying endofactor.

*Example B.5* (Monads and pointed endofactors, idempotent monads and well-pointed endofactors). The unit and underlying endofactor of a monad constitute a pointed endofactor. If moreover monad is idempotent, then that pointed endofactor is well-pointed.

*Definition B.6.* For a given endofactor $F$, its algebra and algebra homomorphism we denote by $	ext{Alg}_{Endof}(F)$; For $(F, \sigma)$ a well-pointed endofactor we denote by $	ext{Alg}_{Endof}(F)$ the category of algebras and morphisms thereof. For $(F, \mu)$ an algebra and algebra homomorphism then we denote by $	ext{Alg}_{Mnd}(F)$.

*Lemma B.7.* Suppose $F$ is an endofactor on a category $\mathcal{C}$ with coproducts. Then there is an equivalence of categories $	ext{Alg}_{Endof}(F) \simeq 	ext{Alg}_{Pre}(F + id)$.

*Definition B.8.* Given $F : C 	o C$, an algebraically free monad on $F$ is a monad $	ext{Free}(F)$ together with an equivalence of categories $	ext{Alg}_{Endof}(F) \simeq 	ext{Alg}_{Mnd}(	ext{Free}(F))$ which preserves the respective functors to $C$ that forget the algebraic structure.

# B.2. Kelly's Unified Transfinite Construction

The existence for algebraically free monads is Kelly's unified transfinite construction (Kelly, 1980).

*Definition B.9* (Reflective subcategory). A subcategory $\mathcal{D}$ of a category $\mathcal{C}$ is **reflective** if the inclusion functor $F : D 	o \mathcal{C}$ admits a left adjoint. A reflective subcategory is **locally presentable category**.

*Example B.10* (Reflective categories are reflective in graphs). Nearly every category encountered in practice is a locally presentable category: The categories of monads, groups, rings, vector spaces, and modules are locally presentable. As are the categories of topological spaces, metric spaces, and uniform spaces. While beyond the scope of this document to explain too much it, local presentability is a powerful notion of what it means for objects and morphisms to be of things defined by equalities of set-expressions.

*Definition B.12 (Accessible category and accessible functor). For an ordinal $\kappa$, a $\kappa$-accessible category $\mathcal{C}$ is a category such that:

+   - $C$ has $\kappa$-directed colimits;
- there is a set of $\kappa$-compact objects which generate $C$ under $\kappa$-directed colimits.

A $\kappa$-accessible functor $F : C 	o D$ is a functor between $\kappa$-accessible categories which preserves $\kappa$-filtered colimits.

*Remark B.13.* The accessibility of a functor can be thought of as an upper-bound on the arity of the operations which it abstracts. For example finite sums of finite sums are finite sums.
