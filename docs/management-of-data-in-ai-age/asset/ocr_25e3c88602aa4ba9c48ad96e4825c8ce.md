The loss of this ability to monitor how the system works implies a risk of also losing the understanding of how it does the work – it just works, and the process becomes black-boxed. In the eBiz case we have reported in this paper they deal with this challenge through in-house development of the AI system. This strategy forces them to constantly make sure that they understand what the system does, how it operates, and how it develops. Accordingly, it’s an active strategy that both continuously aligns the development of the AI-system with the development of their core business model, at the same time as it is a strategy that constantly makes sure that there is no disconnect between an understanding of their core business, and a close-up understanding of how their core business AI-system operates on their behalf.

The 2ⁿᵈ challenge is about *the lack of transparency*. This challenge comes as an effect as the AI-solution scale to business level. With small-scale AI-solutions (e.g. if machine-learning is applied to a small data set to check for errors) it is possible to have an understanding of the data set, the algorithms, and the output from the system. When the data set grows, when the algorithms re-configure themselves, and when the complexity grows in terms of the sets of multiple variables that the system handles there is a good chance that the transparency is lost – here it is not about being disconnected as in the 1ˢᵗ challenge, but as a 2ⁿᵈ effect, being disconnected might also lead to a situation where it is hard to reestablish an understanding for how the AI-system works.
