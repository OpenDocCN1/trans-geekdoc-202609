## C. Collaboration of Artificial Intelligence and Big Data in Business Innovation

Until before the evolution of Big Data, organizations were completely unaware of the power of data being generated by them on a continuous basis. The traditional business methodologies adopted by organizations were overlooking the potential of data in formulating business strategies. With the advent of new technologies to cater to the potential of data - both unstructured and structured, organizations are now adopting in-house data analytics tools to improve their business models. [1]

Big Data is much more than a mere collection of data as articulated by many researchers. The notion of three Vs: Volume, Velocity, and Variety defines Big data in a sophisticated way to gain insights that result in better decision making and improved business strategies.

In 2001, after investigating the challenges, issues and potential generated from tremendous amount of data, Gartner developed the ‘3V model’ that characterizes big data in context to three attributes:

> “Big Data is high-volume, high-velocity, and/or high-variety information assets that demand cost-effective, innovative forms of information processing that enable enhanced insight, decision making, and process automation.”

Thus, the term Big data analytics (BDA) was coined to better express the potential of Big data in examining large amounts of data to reveal hidden patterns, trends, correlations, opportunities and other useful analysis. That, in turn, results in smarter business moves, more efficient and effective operations, increased profitability and sustainability along with higher customer satisfaction. This awareness of trends in the data pool and advantage of hidden information has completely redefined the business moves in this digital epoch which can be overwhelming to markets and organizations around the globe [21].

Furthermore, the advances in the field of AI have taken the potential of BDA to a next level in businesses. The amalgamation of AI and BDA has resulted in framing innovative business models. In order to make effective futuristic decisions and predictions in businesses using AI, Big data plays a significant role. The efficient use of AI depends highly on the scope and quality of the available data used for training and testing algorithms [3]. Due to the momentum generated by AI and BDA, data has now become a valued commodity in businesses and organizations are competing to gather the most remunerative data pool. Moreover, with the advancements in AI and data mining, it’s now possible to collect and store data generated by users and customers efficiently [1].

For instance, in order to predict the customer churn Moz used AI based deep learning neural networks for analyzing customer actions and behaviors. Efficient business insights were collected based on the history of actions performed by customers to decrease churn among existing customers, which otherwise could have impeded the company growth [1].

The interplay between BDA and AI in view of businesses has also paved the way to the collection of data from the physical environment. In contrast to earlier online data collection techniques based on search queries, orders placed or purchased, clicks made by customers, many retail companies are now looking forward to gathering data offline as well for efficient decision making.

For instance, Amazon has incorporated corporate surveillance strategies in grocery stores to collect data related to customer preferences and behaviors. Actuators and advanced sensors have been installed in stores to accomplish offline data collection tasks. Furthermore, Internet of Things (IoT) based tools, Drones, and AI-based personal assistants are being used to transform customer actions into valuable data. This collected data acts as a driving force behind the price-setting algorithms that incorporates responses as per customer demands and reacts accordingly [1].

The potential showcased by AI and Big Data to generate new insights to facilitate product, process, and business innovations have made the adaptation of these technologies, a business priority.

## D. Machine Learning in Business

Machine Learning is a subset of AI that incorporates the capability of automatic identification and detection of patterns, relationships, correlations and trends in the given data. Once these relationships and patterns are identified, they are further used to make assumptions about the behavior of new patterns. Essentially, it’s comparable to the way how humans learn, think and derive conclusions on the basis of regular experiences gained by interacting with environment and people. Humans then use these derived conclusions to deal with new situations [6].

The rise of Artificial Intelligence, in this era, has marked an unprecedented boom in application of Machine Learning (ML) techniques in numerous fields which include healthcare, banking, automobile, financial sector, manufacturing, retail etc. Although ML techniques have been known to be operating from quite a long time but due to recent technological advancements their use has become widespread in the business areas where it was limited previously. Even the enterprises that were not using ML at all due to higher implementation costs and lack of relevant manpower are also adapting ML based solutions [12].

Organizations dealing with retail business are using ML techniques to make analysis of purchase histories of the customers to understand their interests, demands and affordability to generate personalized product recommendations for the next purchase [1]. Another application of ML in business is target marketing wherein based on the information provided like gender, income, age, browsing history, purchase history, locations etc., the system will predict whether the concerned person is interested in a particular product or not. Based on these predictions, marketing department makes decisions accordingly whether to consider a person for promotional offers or not [6].

Deep Learning, a variant of Machine Learning, has gained enough popularity in recent years due to its potential to embody hierarchical processing using different layers of architecture. Moreover, deep learning has been identified to be best suited for high dimensional and dynamical data handling used in Bioinformatics, vision and speech recognition and sensor web.

Even deep learning has been found to work miraculously in e-commerce business for personalized product recommendations. The deep learning algorithm deployed in an e-commerce website traces the customer’s journey and activities. Based on the specifications of products viewed, duration of time spent on viewing each product, the customer activity before and after viewing each product, time of the day when the customer logged into the e-commerce website, his location, gender and other relevant factors, the e-commerce website then offers the customer personalized purchase option that would appeal the customer ultimately. Thus, deep learning is considered to create more opportunities for improved business conversion rate and eventually helps to improve brand image by targeting customers with personalized content.
