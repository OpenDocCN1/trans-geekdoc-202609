By focusing on a real-world business and how they have applied AI as a core business component we have in this article been able to illustrate “AI in action”. This means that we have in this article demonstrated a proof-of-concept of what Applied AI can look like at the business level. In doing so we also present this case as evidence of 3<sup>rd</sup> wave AI beyond speculations on AI as “the next big thing”. In relation to the statement made by Holloway and Hand (1988) we can now say for sure that “*AI is no longer an academic term, but a reality*”.

In this article we have also presented a conceptual frame for businesses to think strategically about AI as a core business component. We have done so by presenting the implications and challenges that are associated with applying AI on a business level. For sure, the strategic business value of IT has been known for a long time (see e.g. Strassmann, 1990; Melville, et al., 2004). What we can add to this is that IT might not only work as a recourse that can support other structures and processes in the company, but it can also be a structure and process in the development of the core business model. In short, we have in this paper offered a complimentary perspective on the role of technology and in particular how AI might work as an applied technology, and as a core business component. This contribution is well-aligned with the perspective proposed by Rahwan, et al. (2019) who suggests that the machine behavior of AI needs to be studied empirically, i.e. to apply an applied perspective on AI.
