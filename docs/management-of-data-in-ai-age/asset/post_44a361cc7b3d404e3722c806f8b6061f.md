## The eBiz AI-platform in action

The AI-system developed by eBiz is a digital platform that their clients can use when building and developing their online webshops. The AI-modules run in the background, and it scrapes the web for competing products as to automatically adjust prices on the products their clients are selling over the web. At the current moment the platform has algorithms for data scraping and processing, and the system can act based on this (i.e. implement “intelligent pricing” for their clients). At the current moment the platform handles automatic and intelligent pricing of 600.000 items (web shop products), and these adjustments are made continuously in relation to competitors pricing strategies, and in relation to a wide set of factors.

The AI-system operates along a simple cycle of 1) input to the system (the e-retailers product feed), 2) scraping of the web, and downloading of selected competitors’ products and prices, and 3) the processing of this data (to automatically adjust the e-retailers prices, and the further processing of this data as input to the e-retailers ERP systems).

In implementing this cycle eBiz says that they enable their clients to 1) follow their competitors by tracking and matching comparable products, at the same time as their system supports 2) dynamic and intelligent pricing and rule setting (e.g. “Adjust my price to be higher or lower price, in part of the business model it can both ‘run the business’ (Holloway & Hand, 1988) and at the same time serve the online customer.”).

However, to make AI work also comes with some conditions and necessities. In our project we have focused on making it work for consumers, and as a core business component, by relying on the following simple model for applying AI in this context including the cycle of 1) monitoring, 2) acting/reacting, and 3) re-configuring. Here we briefly outline these three components:

### 1) Monitoring

The AI system we have focused on in this business case is configured to monitor changes online from the viewpoint of price changes for similar products. To make this work, the AI system is dependent on a set of variables that describe each product, and the system can then train its algorithms to constantly monitor these similar products online.

### 2) Acting/reacting

The AI system we have focused on can also act. Given any changes in the pricing of similar products online, the AI system can also take some actions. It can act/react on changes in the pricing of similar products, and it can instantly and dynamically adjust the prices of the online shops articles based on an analysis of how similar products are priced.

### 3) Reconfiguring

Not only can the AI system act and react on the data it collects through scraping of other websites, but since the AI system is also configured to train its underlying algorithms it can also adapt to changes (e.g. adapt to changing pricing strategies in the market (e.g. “Black Friday sales” or other campaigns).

To successfully apply AI in a business context is about designing AI solutions that incorporate these three aspects in a continuous cycle. That is, it should not only be configured to monitor a particular data set, and be configured to act upon this data, but the true power comes from making use of AI’s capability of also training its algorithms to achieve higher precision, or its ability to constantly, at high speed, and with increased precision, adjust and reconfigure itself given changes in the data landscape it is set to monitor.

Of course, there are also challenges associated with applying AI to real-world use contexts. In the research project reported in this article, which is specifically oriented towards applying AI in a business context, we have identified two such overarching challenges.

The 1st challenge has to do with “*the risk of getting disconnected*” (Borgmann, 1984). When we apply automatic technologies, and in this case even self-reconfiguring technologies we also introduce these as a mediator between ourselves and the task it autonomously executes. In the case of the eBiz business, there is no way to either do what the system does by hand, or even follow what the system does. It is too many subtasks being done, at too high speed for any human to monitor. The loss of this ability to monitor how the system works implies a risk of also losing the understanding of how it does the work – it just works, and the process becomes black-boxed. In the eBiz case we have reported in this paper they deal with this challenge through in-house development of the AI system. This strategy forces them to constantly make sure that they understand what the system does, how it operates, and how it develops. Accordingly, it’s an active strategy that both continuously aligns the development of the AI-system with the development of their core business model, at the same time as it is a strategy that constantly makes sure that there is no disconnect between an understanding of their core business, and a close-up understanding of how their core business AI-system operates on their behalf.

The 2nd challenge is about *the lack of transparency*. This challenge comes as an effect as the AI-solution scale to business level. With small-scale AI-solutions (e.g. if machine-learning is applied to a small data set to check for errors) it is possible to have an understanding of the data set, the algorithms, and the output from the system. When the data set grows, when the algorithms re-configure themselves, and when the complexity grows in terms of the sets of multiple variables that the system handles there is a good chance that the transparency is lost – here it is not about being disconnected as in the 1st challenge, but as a 2nd effect, being disconnected might also lead to a situation where it is hard to reestablish an understanding for how the AI-system works. In relation to this 2nd challenge there is a whole strand of research on “explainable AI” (XAI). However, most of this work is related to small-scale systems, and in relation to individuals. For the business we have studied it offers no solution here! (it is in fact rather pointless, and in fact impossible to monitor and make sense of the actions the AI system takes as it scrapes the web and adjusts 600.000 articles every day). Accordingly, this calls for other solutions for how to review how the AI works. As formulated by Xu (2019):

> “Explainable AI (XAI) enables users to understand the algorithm and parameters used, which is intended to address the AI black-box problem. If a medical-diagnosis intelligent system predicts the likelihood of cancer based on a patient’s personal data, XAI should provide the reasons for the prediction to the patient through a UI. Previous research on XAI had mainly been done in two ways: visualization of ML [machine-learning] processes and explainable ML algorithms. However, these approaches may be biased in explaining how ML algorithms work and rely mainly on abstract visualization methods or statistical algorithms, which may further increase complexity.” (Wei Xu, 2019)

As illustrated here, current XAI solutions are only offered at the level of the individual (as a “walk-through” XAI model, and there is an implicit assumption made here that the AI-system operates at the same pace as the individual so that he/she can monitor what the system does in a step-by-step fashion). That is not the case when it comes to the business AI-solution we have reported on in this article.

Indeed, and when it comes to large-scale AI-system other examination models are needed. In our research collaboration with the eBiz company we are exploring explanation models that offers explanations of the inner logic of the system, and descriptions of the decision model so that both customers and the eBiz company can examine a particular output from the system and see if it makes sense (i.e. if it corresponds to that explanation model). By focusing on these alternative solutions, we propose “examinable AI” as a complement to explainable AI, and we note that this might serve as a call for research aimed at resolving this 2nd challenge associated with black-boxed AI-system, and how to increase transparency on how these systems work in real use contexts.
