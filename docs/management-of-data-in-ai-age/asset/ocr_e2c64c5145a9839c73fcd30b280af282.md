As further described by Miller (2019), these researchers expanded the concept of a computer’s functional architecture to reflect the way the brain is put together, that is the brains cognitive functional architecture. The analogy with the human brain was the dominant model.

> “Like a computer, the brain has storage for information or facts – memory – and ways of retrieving information and working on it, using rules for problem solving – algorithms.” (Miller, 2019).

This first set of human-like developments was the result of close collaborations between several scientific disciplines (including collaborations across cognitive science, psychology, neuroscience and computer science). It was in this sense multidisciplinary, but they all shared the same focus – to inform the design of an intelligence computational machine.

Since then, the multi-disciplinary tradition of research collaborations around AI has continued, but the outcome is nowadays more diverse. Today, researchers are interested in not only the technical challenges associated with the design and implementation of intelligence machinery, but also in understanding its philosophical, social, cultural, ethical and moral implications.
