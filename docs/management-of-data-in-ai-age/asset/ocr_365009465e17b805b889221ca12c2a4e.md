If we now map this development to the three waves of AI as proposed by Xu (2019) we can see how the 1st wave of AI lasted for approximately 20 years (1950s-1970s), and how it was mostly driven by academia with a focus on technological solutions, symbolism and connectionism. In moving forward, we can also see how the 2nd wave of AI (1980s-1990s) was still academia driven and technology oriented, and as indicated above it was a clear focus on speech recognition, machine translations, artificial neural networks, and research on pattern recognition. Still, it was not only the late 1990s that the focus started to shift towards the use of AI for different purposes, and according to Xu (2019) if was not until the beginning of 2000 that we could see the beginning of a 3rd wave of AI (2006).

With the 3rd wave of AI came an important shift – from being a research lab product, it started to become widely applied. As suggested by Xu (2019) it was a breakthrough in applications (including functional systems built on deep learning, speech recognition, pattern recognition, and big data) and it was enabled by high-performance computers. As such it started to prove itself useful and it is increasingly applied to real-world problems.
