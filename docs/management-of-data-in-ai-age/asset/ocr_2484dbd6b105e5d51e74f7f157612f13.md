Learning is a key cognitive function of the human brain and the link to managerial performance. It is a process of acquiring or modifying knowledge, behaviors, skills, values, or preferences. The brain “learns” and the cerebrum is most important device for learning as it is here where higher-ordered functions like memory and reasoning occur. Each area of the cerebrum specializes in a function – sight, hearing, speech, touch, short-term memory, long-term memory, language and reasoning abilities are the most important for learning. Through a network of neurons, sensory information is transmitted by synapses along the neural pathway and stored temporarily in short-term memory, a volatile region of the brain that acts like a receiving center for the flood of sensory information we encounter in our daily lives Once processed in short-term memory, our brain’s neural pathways carry these memories to the structural core, where they are compared with existing memories and stored in our long-term memory, the vast repository of our life long experiences and the, prime point of reference within managerial behavior (Ford , 2011) (UCERI, OECD, 2008).

Brain learning could be explicit or implicit. Explicit learning connotes conscious awareness and articulation of what is being learned. Implicit learning is without conscious access. (Roman F. Loonis, et al, October 2017).
