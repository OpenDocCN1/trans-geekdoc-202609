
Learning could also be deep, i.e., models patterns in data as complex, multi-layered networks; it could also be “generic,” building a mathematical model based on sample data or “training data,” in order to make predictions or decisions without being explicitly programmed to perform the task.

## III. How does all of that translate into problem solving?

Analysis provided above could lead to the hypothesis that problem solving is one of the core cognitive functions of the human brain. And a deep learning-like brain configuration guides the process.

Problem solving is seen, then, as a higher level brain activity that connotes interaction with many other cognitive processes such as abstraction, searching, learning, decision making, inference, analysis, and synthesis (Wang et al., 2008).

The brain performs this cognitive function when it seeks a solution for a given problem or a potential route to a goal. Problem solving can be perceived by the brain, then, as a learning and analysis process.

Problem situations, in many cases, could be structured or unstructured, functional or multi-functional, conceptual or applied, short- or long-term, high-risk or low-risk, etc.

Is it plausible that the human brain is adopting its own version of deep learning in its search for an answer to the myriad of problem situations that it confronts? It resorts to a process of “management” of memory and learning. Memory provides the ability to encode, store, retain and subsequently recall information and past experiences. The brain consequently learns. A learning that depends on several criteria: from time, long- and short-term learning, to transparency, explicit or implicit learning, and depth of analysis, deep or generic learning.

These cognitive processes of the brain contribute to problem solving. Drucker’s self-management problem provides an illustration.

## Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning and Data Science as Iterations of Business Automation for Small Businesses

Pooja Sharma  
Managing Partner – Business Automation, SKS Consulting & Advisors, India  

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence and machine learning, the two iterations of automation, are based on the data, small or large. The larger the data, the more effective an AI or machine learning tool will be. The opposite holds for the opposite iteration. With a larger pool of data, the large businesses and multinational corporations have effectively been building, developing and adopting refined AI and machine learning based decision systems.

The contention of this chapter is to explore if the small businesses with small data in hand are well-off to use and adopt AI and machine learning based tools for their day to day business operations.

**Keywords:** AI for small business, machine learning for small business, Data Science, Big Data

The modern age automation tools are being developed using artificial intelligence and machine learning. The basis of development of an AI or machine learning tool is entirely based on the availability and quantity of data. The more data we have, the more effective our automation tools will be.

Now, we come across the issue if the mere availability and quantity of data are the only basis of an effective AI or machine learning tool. Not entirely but largely yes. To train an AI based tool, we need more and more buckets of data. This data needs to be fetched from a gamut of processes and operations taking place across a particular industry or business organization. Why are we stressing here on the data coming from a particular industry or a business organization? The answer lies in the concept of relevance of data that is further based on the effectiveness of data science or analytics capabilities of a business unit.

Here come the small businesses in the discussion of this chapter. Small businesses are obviously having fewer market offerings, generally operating in niche markets, and thus have small data in hand, especially in the infancy stages of their business. Even small businesses with established business presence and market for their offerings still have small data with them.

The small data may not be sufficient to build, develop and train an AI system. Furthermore, a small business may not have data for some business processes entirely that other small businesses may have. There are a larger number of small businesses not having HR departments, legal compliance divisions and other functional divisions that large businesses have. The absence of data related to all processes of a business or the availability of small data with the small business requires re-working or re-thinking on the fact if a small business still can embrace the business automation tools that are basically based on big data driven AI systems.

## AI vendors and small businesses

It is evident that insufficient data in the hands of small businesses, coupled with small investment outlays they may have, makes it infeasible to build and develop their own AI systems independently. Lesser data and limited financial outlays with small businesses hinder the in-house development of AI based automation tools.

But the small businesses can still adopt the AI based automation tools procured from AI vendors. AI vendors are the full-time developers of AI systems for the industry segments they have specializations in. AI vendors pool small data from the business processes from different small businesses they work with, and then the collected data is aggregated to build and train a standalone AI system for all the businesses falling into a particular market segment.

This way, a small business can benefit from the automated data of the HR department of another small business even if the former doesn’t have an HR function division. An AI system procured from an AI vendor may further help a small business to adopt automation in their business decisions and processes without investing in in-house AI development.

## Does it solve the AI adoption problems?

The same were built using years of experience of these large corporations. All this means that using platforms provided by these large corporations, small businesses are helping themselves, benefitting from the years of experience and AI development. Of course, using these platforms comes with a price, but this is far lesser than what it takes to build in-house or procuring from an AI vendor.

## How can data science help small businesses?

Small businesses have small data. Generally, it isn’t difficult to manually process the lesser quantity of data, but making this data—however smaller it can be—work for one or more objectives at the same time may save time and efforts of the small businesses generally having small teams to focus on relatively more critical business processes.

Having data analytics people or just having an understanding of data relevance and analysis can help small businesses get an edge in their market segments.

## Small business functions having prospects for AI, machine learning and data science

The AI based automation tools and data analytics can be used for real-business problems in marketing, sales, customer relations, billing & accounting, after-sales customer experience, etc. All these business functions are vital and pervasive in every small business, and there are a number of large businesses providing AI-powered support services for all these functions that small businesses may use.
