The turing test was developed by Alan Turing in 1950, and it is a test of a computer’s ability to show intelligent behavior equivalent to, or indistinguishable from, that of a human. In this strive, the notions of human-like AI, or human-level AI has been recurring notions (see e.g. Miller (2019) and Kaplan& Haenlein (2019).

In going back to the roots for this human-like model for the development of Ai we should of course mention the Eliza system. This system was designed by Joseph Weizenbaum in 1966, and it is said to be the first computer program pass the Turing test. The Eliza program worked by examining a user’s typed comments for keywords, and responded to the user in a dialogue fashion. By relying on this model, Eliza could respond either with a generic phrase or by repeating an earlier comment left by the user. The Eliza program was in this was designed to replicate the behavior of a psychotherapist. In passing the Turing test, Eliza was able to fool some people into believing that they were talking to a real person, instead of a computer.
