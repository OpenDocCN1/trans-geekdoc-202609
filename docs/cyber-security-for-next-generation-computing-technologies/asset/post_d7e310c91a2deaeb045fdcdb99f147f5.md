## 11.7 ITS ATTACKER MOTIVES

Find the reason that ITS attackers have for digging in certain data your company might hold for them [117]. Knowing the right reasons might make your organization technically enough to defend against those attacks as well. When attackers hijack systems, their intentions can be identified according to the type of data they steal. You may fortify your defenses and be ready for anything the threat landscape throws your way if you can clearly understand why hackers hack (their motivations) and who will most likely target your particular company. (It should be emphasized that the word “crime” is a legal term and an attack may or may not be regarded as one, it is not used here. the term “attack” is utilized.) There are typically six reasons for trying to launch a cyber attack, according to Mark Hepat [118], which are discussed in the following sections. ### 11.7.1 FINANCIAL SUCCESS

Hackers are primarily motivated by money, and there are numerous methods to acquire it [19]. They might hack financial websites, gain access to your bank accounts, and move money to one of their accounts. Or they could attack your entire business with malware. They can also use a complex phishing scam to trick an employee into sending money [120]. Despite the abundance of choices, most hackers seek to make money. A well-known illustration of the financial motivation behind cyber attacks is the theft of personally identifiable information (PII), which is then sold. This motive accounts for many cyber attacks against retailers and healthcare facilities, typically carried out by organized criminal gangs.

### 11.7.2 ACHIEVEMENT

Some hackers are motivated by the feeling of accomplishment from breaking into an extensive system. Everyone desires to be noticed, whether they work alone or with others. This is connected to the notion that cyber criminals are challenging individuals who enjoy the task. Now, their actions frequently motivate one to perform more difficult attacks [122].

### 11.7.3 INTERNAL THREATS

People with access to crucial information or systems may abuse it, which would be bad for them. These dangers are among the greatest threats to a company’s cyber security because they could come from partners, vendors, contractors, or internal workers. Not all insiders are intentional; according to Research, the majority (51%) are the consequence of negligence or fabricated credentials. However, harm might still happen in unforeseen circumstances. Many different kinds of data, such as economic data, data on sales, customer details, automobile tracking data, intellectual property, business operations, personally identifiable information (PII), and other types of data, are susceptible to theft and monetization. Data can be used to perpetrate crimes and threats linked to identity theft, privacy breaches, industrial espionage, and more [123].

### 11.7.4 INFORMATION WARFARE AND "HACKIVISM" WITH POLITICAL MOTIVATION

Using their expertise in hacking, specific criminal organizations go after giant firms. They are often motivated by a cause, such as bringing attention to human issues or alerting a significant company to system flaws. They might also go against groups whose viewpoints conflict with theirs. These organizations may claim they are exercising their right to free expression when they steal data. Still, they always use DDoS (distributed denial of service) attacks to saturate a website with traffic and bring it to a halt [125]. They launch a distributed denial-of-service attack (DDoS) against the ITS infrastructure to down the systems and cause chaos on the roads [126] and use company apps or websites to post political, critical or prank remarks. Another goal is to harm the reputation of the ITS firm and cause financial damage for it [128]. Hacking dynamic message signs to display satirical, political, or other messages is another goal. Cooperative autonomous vehicles are a reality on public roads; thieves could be able to send V2V messages to disrupt traffic. In the future, dishonest companies might pay criminals to taint V2V channels with V2V poisoning so that autonomous vehicles pass by their destinations [129].

### 11.7.5 THEFT IN VEHICLES AND SYSTEMS

The theft of commodities or valuables within vehicles, or even the vehicles themselves, would be one of the most alluring profit-making models for criminals. Certain people can try to take advantage of ITS systems to avoid paying fees. A number of assaults have been found [130]:

- Hacking autonomous trucks and rerouting them to a remote place (like a deserted parking lot out of town) so that thieves can break in and take the cargo.
- Clever using compromised autonomous vehicles to convey illegal goods like drugs and weapons.
- Hacking driverless cars to give them instructions to reroute to a secret place, where thieves can take goods from the passengers or possibly kidnap the passengers.
- Delivering and stopping autonomous vehicles to steal them or their parts.
- Using compromised ITS systems to get out of paying fees for services like parking, bridge tolls, congestion charges, etc.

## 11.8 CASE STUDIES OF SECURITY ATTACKS ON TRANSPORTATION SYSTEMS

### 11.8.1 UKRAINE POWER GRID ATTACK (2015)

One of the most well-known cyber security assaults on a transportation system occurred in 2015 when the power grid in Ukraine was attacked. In the attacks of December 2015, malware called Crash Override specifically targeted the Ukrainian power infrastructure [135]. Three power distribution firms in Ukraine had their systems breached by hackers and were able to seize the electrical grid and turn off the electricity for more than 225,000 consumers. It was the first time a cyber attack on power infrastructure. The attackers employed various strategies to access the management systems, including spear-phishing emails, malware, and remote access tools. Once they gained entry, they took over the systems and sent orders to turn off the electricity. Although the Russian government denied any participation, it thought that a group of Russian hackers were responsible for the assault. The assault had a significant economic and social effect because it left homes and businesses without electricity during the winter. Additionally, it sparked worries about how susceptible vital infrastructure is to cyber attacks and the possibility that other nations could experience similar attacks.
