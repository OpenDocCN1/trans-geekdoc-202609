On the other hand, in single-hop broadcasting, vehicles do not flood the information packets. Instead, when a vehicle receives a packet from a single hop of broadcasting, it keeps this information to itself on the database board. The second broadcast information is received in the next cycle. Ultimately, the previous information is transferred to the next neighbor in one hop. In this way, broadcasting relies on vehicle mobility for spreading information [41].
