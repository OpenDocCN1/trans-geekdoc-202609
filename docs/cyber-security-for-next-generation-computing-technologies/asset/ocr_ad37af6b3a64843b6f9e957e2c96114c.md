# 12.5 PRIVACY AND MANAGEMENT IN THE INTELLIGENT TRANSPORTATION SYSTEMS

The intelligent transportation system works dynamically in nonlinear sophisticated systems. In these types of networks, they depend on a source of information, which is provided on a real timeline. Multi-agent cooperators work in ad-hoc-based networks, which may mislead the information related to the task and put ITS management and privacy at risk [53].

Attackers could be tempted to the authorized supervision and fairly confidential information. These malicious actors can enable attackers to easily access top-secret records internally and externally. An external attacker may constantly control the connection and examine traffic information, but the information cannot be interpreted. On the other hand, internal attackers, such as malicious agents or employees, have full authority to control public and private information of transportation networks. Consequently, if governors showed compromised behavior, they can become strong attackers. The following attacking scenarios were a part of the study [55].

+   1. Impersonation of Genuine Staff: The attacker appears to be a staff member and access confidential information.
2. Malicious Staff: Malicious actions can lead to unauthorized access to information and serious emergencies.
3. Greedy Staff: Greedy staff takes advantage of their privilege and generate needless trouble such as traffic jams, lane blocking, etc.

For reason, intelligent privacy and security technologies are needed to obfuscate things for attackers, or strategies are needed to find these connected entities. These steps are necessary to detect malicious content. Significantly, researchers have been working on how to achieve privacy using public key infrastructure (PKI) [56]. Two types of encryption rules allow the implementation of privacyaware policies.

+   1. Policy-Based Encryption: This cryptographic primitive needs data for encryption regarding policies.
2. Policy-Based Signature: These cryptographic primitives create digital signatures based on policies agreed upon between establishments. These cryptographic benefits include privacy policy creation, automatic trust negotiation, trust generation, access control, etc. [57].
