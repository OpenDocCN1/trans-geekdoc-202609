# Cyber Attacks Against Intelligent Transportation Systems

Telematic systems are installed in vehicles to deliver services like fleet administration and insurance monitoring. With roadside infrastructure, vehicles are increasingly sharing warning information.

When developing and creating transportation products and services, it's important to take into account the various attack points that can be targeted. These attack points include everything from local car communications, charging and battery management systems, motors and actuators, and passenger/pedestrian devices like cellphones, tablets and laptops connected to Internet, cloud, and data communications [162]. Particularly susceptible to assault are Wi-Fi hotspots and connections, as well as 3G/4G/5G cellular connections. The services providers also be a source of attack because they might not be adequately protected. Other potential targets for hackers include vehicle communications like Bluetooth connections and vehicle communication [178]. Charging and battery management systems, which are becoming increasingly important as electric vehicles hit the market, can also be targeted. Hackers may try to manipulate the battery management system to cause a fire or explosion, for example. Motors and actuators control various functions and are potential attack points. Other potential targets are pedestrian devices, smartphones, tablets, and laptops. Hacker may attempt to steal personal information or install malware through these devices. Aftermarket devices, such as GPS trackers and diagnostic tools, also represent potential vulnerabilities [179].

To attack the aforementioned, attackers search for engineering flaws. Consequently, it is essential to recognize human fallibility in engineering while creating systems. Engineering design complacancy exists while the attackers search for. In engineering, it is well-known that fallibility exists in various systems. One of the most common examples is the occurrence of bugs in software. Over several decades, engineers have faced multiple issues, such as backdoors, user errors, wrong configurations, and poor engineering practices. These issues can significantly damage the system, leading to downtime, financial loss, or security breaches [180]. Backdoors are a type of vulnerability that allows unauthorized access to a system, compromising its integrity. User errors, on the other hand, occur due to mistakes, as incorrectly entering data or making incorrect decisions. Wrong configurations can result system failures, leading to significant downtime. Vulnerabilities are security flaws that hackers can exploit to gain unauthorized access to a system, causing substantial damage [181]. Poor designing practices, such as failing to consider all use cases or not following proper design and testing procedures, lead to system failures and others. It is essential to understand that these issues are not unique to any particular system or technology and can occur in any engineering project. As such, it is crucial to employ best practices and rigorous testing procedures to identify and mitigate potential issues in early development. How should we approach these problems in terms of transportation cyber security? Engineers working on transportation networks are retaliating. They employ cyber security experts, and automakers have set up bounty testing. Penetration testing is performed on automobiles to identify any flaws that are subsequently rectified [182].
