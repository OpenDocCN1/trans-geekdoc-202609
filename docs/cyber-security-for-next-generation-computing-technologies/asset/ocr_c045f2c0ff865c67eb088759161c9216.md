# 5.4.4 SECURITY MANAGEMENT

The preceding solutions can be quite complex and expensive to implement for small businesses; however, there are solutions that are quite simple and accessible to all. For example, we can follow the recommendations of various organizations such as ANSI (Agence Nationale de la Securite des Systems d’Information) or ASD (Australia’s Signals Directorate). Here are some examples: (1) Use a white list of applications to prevent the execution of malware, scripts, etc.; (2) apply the latest patches for Java, Web browser, and Microsoft Office; (3) keep your operating system up-to-date; and (4) Use multifactor authentication to connect to VPN, SHH, or any remote access.

To ensure these recommendations are properly implemented within an organization, it is important to create a PSSI (information system security policy), which governs the company’s information system. This is an integral part of ISMS (information security management system), which provides a framework for how security is managed globally in the company. In particular, it addresses the three principles of security: confidentiality, integrity, and availability.

Finally, the implementation of an ISMS is based on ISO standards, such as ISO27001, which defines the requirements.

# 5.5 CONCLUSION

In our analysis, we aimed to present the current security landscape. We presented different types of threat actors to explain how one works. In the future, the ransomware threat will probably continue its upward trend. The business of ransomware is too lucrative unless international governments and technology innovations can fundamentally alter the attacker’s cost-benefit calculation.

While there have efforts to disrupt operations and hold threat actors accountable, cyber criminals sign up with another platform—as part of the ransomware-as-a-service model—continue to operate. Nation-state actors will continue to organize attacks to serve their geopolitical interests. Above all, with the Ukrainian war, Russia has maintained an aggressive posture throughout 2022 and 2023, with a sustained emphasis on targeting NATO, Eastern Europe, Ukraine, Afghanistan, and the energy sector.

As the business of ransomware is not going anywhere, and neither are espionage and information operations, new solutions exist to protect yourself or your organization. Therefore, following best practices in cyber security is more necessary than ever as the menace continues to increase.
