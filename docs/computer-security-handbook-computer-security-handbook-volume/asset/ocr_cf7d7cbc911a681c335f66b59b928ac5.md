# YIELD AND ITS RELATION TO CORN BORER

BY J. H. EVANS

AGRICULTURAL EXTENSION SERVICE

THE OHIO STATE UNIVERSITY

Columbus 10, Ohio

Does the European corn borer lower the yield of corn? This question has been asked many times. Both plant pathologists and entomologists have studied the relationship of the corn borer to yield with varying results.

In Ohio, in 1946 and 1947, considerable data were obtained in two fields and in every case the findings were similar. Table 1 gives the results obtained in 1946.

**TABLE 1.-Effect of corn borer populations on yield of corn**

| Number of borers per 100 plants | Yield per acre (bushels) |
|---|---|
| 640 | 96.2 |
| 1550 | 92.7 |
| 2650 | 89.9 |

With each increase of borers there was a decrease in yield. From these data it would appear that the European corn borer materially lowers the yield of corn.

The number of borers per 100 plants, 640, 1550, 2650 and yield per acre (bushels) 96.2, 92.7, 89.9 seem to indicate that each 1000 increase of borers lowers the yield approximately 2.3 bushels per acre. In other words, the loss varies with the infestation. These data are insufficient to draw definite conclusions. They are given merely as a matter of interest and information.

# Summary

+   1. The European corn borer lowers the yield of sweet corn from 10 to 25 per cent.
+   2. Damage by the European corn borer results in lower yields and poor quality.
+   3. Even one borer per plant will reduce yield.
