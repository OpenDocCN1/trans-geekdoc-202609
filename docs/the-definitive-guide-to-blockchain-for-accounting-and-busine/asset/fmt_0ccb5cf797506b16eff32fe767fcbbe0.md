
## Token Offering Terms

### Redemption Right
- Curzio Research established the right to redeem the CEO tokens in certain circumstances; the potential blockages were under scenarios in which potential investors failed to properly complete the KYC/AML/Accreditation process, or did not sign the SAFE agreement.

### Token transfer restrictions
- The SAFE and tokens were subject to a standard 12-month lock-up period, during which secondary trading of the CEO token would be restricted to trades permitted under existing securities regulations.

### Use of token proceeds
- The CEO token proceeds were designated to be used for general corporate purposes, such as capital expenditures, acquisitions, infrastructure and personnel, development of products and services, and offering, legal and accounting expenses. Specifically, Curzio Research planned to:
  1. Increase its front-end reader file to 100k+.
  2. Further monetize its existing podcasts.
  3. Build a world-class investor education platform.
  4. Add new podcasts and reader-requested newsletters.
  5. Hire more expert analysts and other key personnel.
  6. Collaborate with large external media outlets to increase brand awareness.

### Token format
- The CEO token would be ERC20-compliant and embedded in the Ethereum blockchain as a smart contract.

### Defining security token
- At a basic level, a token (or “coin”) is a digital identity for something that can be owned.
- A security token is a tradable asset. It can pay investors dividends, interest or represent an equity stake in the underlying asset. Since these tokens are backed by a secured asset, and are tradable, they’re subject to federal securities regulations just like stocks.
- Security tokens provide a way for asset owners to sell off a portion of their assets (real estate/art/collectibles/companies) to investors with the speed and security of blockchain, while providing investors with regulatory protection.

### The difference between a security token and a utility token
- The first generation of cryptocurrencies was mainly utility tokens. They weren’t created to be an investment. These were more like app coins or user tokens and allowed access to a company’s products or services.
- An example of a utility coin is Filecoin, which raised $257 million. These tokens will give users access to its distributed cloud storage platform.

A STO has many advantages over a private placement, these are:
- The security token market creates an avenue for any company to sell a portion of an asset to investors – including real estate, art, businesses or any of the hundreds of trillions of dollars’ worth of assets around the world – using the speed and security of blockchain.
- These are cost effective. A STO has smart contracts embedded. Smart contracts (or cryptocontracts) are self-executing blockchain contracts that eliminate intermediaries (investment banks), which in turn cuts down costs of service delivery. This makes STOs a much cheaper alternative than the traditional private placement route.
- Important security checks like KYC and AML are automated, which makes it much quicker to sell assets to accredited investors. These steps also ensure that STOs are fully compliant with regulatory authorities.
- Cryptotrading outshines traditional trading because it’s continuous. Most STEs are open 24 hours a day… so you can trade any time of day or night.
- Security tokens are being quickly adopted since they lead to greater liquidity and less administration. They’re already being adopted by Hollywood (individual investors can now fund movies), artists (Andy Warhol recently tokenized and auctioned a 31.5% stake in one of his paintings) and real estate (the St. Regis Aspen Resort recently tokenized its hotel giving investors an ownership stake).
- With a traditional private placement, investors’ money can be locked up for a considerable amount of time. It takes five years for the average venture-backed company to be acquired … and over eight years to go public (IPO). But STO participants can convert their tokens to cash after a 12-month lock-up period.

## 8.5.2 The Issuance Phase
As mentioned earlier, the CEO token offering was limited to United States accredited investors and those designated to be qualified non-US investors. With the assistance of Securitize, it was then confirmed that each prospective investor:
- Met all applicable investor suitability criteria, including those imposed by that investor’s country of domicile.
- Completed KYC verification.
- Conducted AML due diligence and screening.
- Executed and delivered the SAFE Agreement and an accompanying questionnaire that was available on the website.

Curzio Research then used Securitize’s standard smart contract as a framework for issuing the CEO tokens. The attorneys confirmed that all necessary Reg D, Rule 506(c) restrictions and related global compliance requirements were programed into the Ethereum blockchain as a smart contract. The resulting ERC20-compliant token took into account the investor limits, lock-up periods, and secondary trading restrictions, among other compliance mandates. The CEO token smart contract was also customized to allow for the distribution of future potential dividends that Curzio Research intends to pay its token holders.

The purchase price of the CEO token was designated in USD. Payment was accepted in USD, Bitcoin (BTC) or Ether (ETH). Payments in BTC and ETH were delivered to a designated digital custodian, who converted the BTC and ETH into USD on the payment date. Five investors chose to purchase the CEO tokens using cryptocurrency (two used BTC and three used ETH). In accordance with the requirements defined in the setup phase by Curzio Research, Securitize assisted with both the collection of investor subscriptions and the issuance of the CEO tokens to each investor’s designated digital wallet.
