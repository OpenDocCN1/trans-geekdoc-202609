## 10.7. DIGITAL SECURITIES ECOSYSTEM

The security token ecosystem continues to expand, presently made up of primary issuance, trading and security platforms. Strategic resource and knowledge sharing adds significant value for all parties, as data-enhanced services and products are coming online.

The primary market mostly includes digital security platforms, such as Securitize. These providers facilitate the full lifecycle of digital securities ownership, beyond issuance, as well as the goals of each stakeholder: issuers, investors and exchanges. Securitize’s platform is powered by its DS Protocol (Digital Securities Protocol), a fluid and adaptive ownership architecture detailed extensively in the Curzio Research chapter. The DS Protocol creates a regulatory compliant internet ecosystem built atop a smart contract infrastructure of Digital Securities Services (DS Services) supporting third party Digital Securities Applications (DS Apps). All interactions between the security tokens and other smart contracts are standardized, enabling proper adherence to applicable securities laws and regulations throughout the lifecycle of each digital security.

The digital security secondary market is made up of multiple licensed Security Token Exchanges (STEs), among other marketplaces and liquidity providers. STEs are classified as alternative trading systems (ATS) under US regulations, and must be approved by the SEC. ATS, known as proprietary trading systems in Japan, are computer systems that match buyers and sellers to find counterparties for executing securities transactions. By disintermediating the services of traditional brokers and exchanges, ATS offer lower trading costs to investors seeking an alternative method for accessing liquidity.

Our Curzio Research chapter examined Open Finance Network, an ATS with a protocol for standardizing alternative assets, as well as the reporting, compliance, clearing and settlement processes related to those assets. The resulting interoperability creates a mechanism for tokenized trading to connect off-chain “traditional” assets and on-chain “digital” assets. Furthermore, market participants no longer need to rely on manually reconciled ledgers, making real-time verifications of asset ownership and funding availability possible. The instant settlement of tokenized assets will transform the typical securities transaction lifecycle.

Agents of the digital securities market have obtained the necessary licenses and regulatory approval to operate as investment service providers. Coupled with technological know-how, the bar has been raised for both quality and execution within the digital assets industry. Maturation has led traditional financial services firms to partner with the security token ecosystem, as seen through Securitize’s recent financing round that raised more than $14M from Santander InnoVentures, MUFG, Nomura Holdings and Sony Financial Ventures.[⁶](#CH010.html#fn6) Collaborations with trusted institutions like these further legitimizes digital securities, and should help accelerate security token adoption.

## 10.8. REAL ESTATE MARKET INEFFICIENCIES

The real estate industry is currently littered with inefficiencies; disparate distribution of reliable property data, ad hoc pricing, opaque ownership structures, fragmented information systems, analog (paper and other non-electronic) records, localized markets and siloed networks, among countless other deficiencies contribute to market transparency issues. Markets with lower levels of information transparency hinder investor confidence when making decisions, leading to adverse selection and inefficient pricing. Digital securities can provide a framework for modernizing the processes and procedures of an asset class notorious for its lack of real-time data.

Tokenized assets issued and transferred on DLT systems would increase data transparency and broaden market access, ultimately improving real estate liquidity and asset pricing efficiency. Through careful financial structuring techniques, ownership interests in real estate assets, as well as their underlying cash flows can be customized. Security tokens can be packaged together to represent a claim on cash flows from multiple properties, or they can represent fractional ownership in an individual property. Digital securities offer the flexibility of securitization, while also leveraging the open access of web-based platforms.

The flexibility of tokenization provides unique advantages to both issuers and investors. Protocol-based financial engineering allows tokens to be created from any type of cash flow, including those that would be economically inefficient under current structuring techniques. These tokens have a clearly defined mandate, serving as a financial intermediary between borrowers and investors. Issuers have a cost-effective method of leveraging assets on their balance sheets, thus providing access to lower-cost global funding. Capital providers can enjoy an array of options for investment deployment, diversifying portfolio risk. Moreover, tokens offering direct asset class exposure provide better risk calibration, helping investors form customized risk-return profiles.
