## 10.10. TOKENIZING JAPANESE REAL ESTATE INVESTMENTS

Security tokens operating within DLT systems are designed to improve efficiencies of the price discovery process. Inefficiencies in the system currently restrict the Japanese real estate market; specifically those challenges that relate to investment data transparency, access and liquidity.

### 10.10.1. Data Transparency

Real estate investment opportunities are often complex, requiring intensive due diligence by large teams of analysts and lawyers; significant up-front costs are necessary to properly evaluate prospects and assess the technicalities of related assets. These costs are often fixed in nature, resulting in a competitive advantage for those deploying capital at scale. As such, a threshold exists where large asset managers have a distinct edge over those with less capital.

Digital securities can help lower the initial capital deployment threshold, by leveraging DLT ecosystems and security token history to increase data transparency surrounding real estate assets. Enhancing data transparency would disseminate informational inputs that go into the investment decision-making process, encouraging investors with more modest resources to deploy capital. As a result, the global investor pool for Japanese real estate would expand, creating additional fund flow and consequently higher prices.

According to JLL’s “Global Real Estate Transparency Index 2018,” Japan ranked 19th out of the 100 countries surveyed; Japan rated toward the top of the “Transparent” group, but was still far from making the “Highly Transparent” tier ([Fig. 10.12](#CH010.html#fig12)). Since the last survey in 2016, 85% of countries saw an increase in real estate transparency, many of which outpaced Japan. The most notable increases were in those jurisdictions that relied on technology.

JLL’s survey revealed an uptrend in technology used to increase real estate data transparency. One example was seen in Dubai, where a building classification initiative leveraged an official government app for property broker and operator registration. The Netherlands was also recognized for utilizing real estate technology to encourage data sharing; the switch to open data made it easier to verify transaction prices and other registered information.

JLL’s 2018 report highlighted the recent rise of 250+ proptech (property technology) startups, which collectively raised over $6 billion in funding since its 2016 Transparency Index report. These tech-enabled platforms are generating significant amounts of fresh data and creating new metrics, fueling investor and business expectations for a significant leap in real estate transparency. However, the industry is failing to keep pace with the higher anticipated standards, as there are challenges around data access, quality and consistency. We believe that the security token ecosystem can assist with these areas of concern, particularly within the Japanese real estate market.

JLL researcher, Yuto Ohigashi commented on Japan’s need to act, stating:

*transparency in the Japanese market has improved, but other counties are achieving even more significant improvement. Japan will be left behind the global trend unless it increases the speed of improvement.[^(14)](#CH010.html#fn14)*

As noted earlier, many Japanese real estate fund managers are seeking to develop a better understanding and gather information before they allocate more capital to the market. This statement coincides with Japan ranking 35th in “Transaction Process” on JLL’s survey, which was mostly attributed to lack of transparency and validity of transaction price information. Japan’s embracement of blockchain technology is an effort to enhance transparency of real estate prices. Additionally, its government supports open data initiatives making it an ideal sandbox for tokenization solutions.

One proposal for increasing real estate transparency focuses on creating a real-time transparency index, employing a network of DLT systems and security tokens to aggregate and share Japanese market data among users. With the JLL Global Real Estate Transparency Index (GRETI) serving as a framework, we believe that technology is at the point where most of JLL’s 186 individual transparency measures can be compiled and displayed on a continuous basis; this would be a significant upgrade from the current methodology, where the index is updated every two years. The quantitative data can be collected from a combination of (1) proptech platform APIs (application programing interfaces); (2) real estate blockchain technology application initiatives, such as land title and deed records, property sale and title assignment, and investor/tenant identity among others; and (3) the underlying data collected throughout each security tokens lifecycle.

The qualitative factors can be aggregated from a consortium of industry experts using the DLT network; these practitioners would add granularity for actionable data, leveraging insights from their respective area of competence. The timeline for qualitative inputs would be decided by the participating members through consensus (IE: Daily, Weekly, Quarterly, etc.). Our proposal would satisfy the growing demand from investors and businesses for higher transparency standards; technology is raising aspirations for quality information to be delivered in a timely manner. However, a wide gap exists between expectations and reality, as made evident throughout JLL’s 2018 GRETI report.

Users of our proposed network would be incentivized to contribute data in exchange for access to real-time real estate transparency information, as opposed to waiting two years for JLL updates. The JLL GRETI currently serves as an industry benchmark for real estate fund managers and other decision-makers. As per Joseph L. Pagliari, Clinical Professor of Real Estate, Booth School of Business, University of Chicago:

*The Global Real Estate Transparency Index has long been the gold standard by which many in the international real estate investment community have attempted to assess and price risk.[^(15)](#CH010.html#fn15)*

A real-time transparency index would prove invaluable to international investment decision-makers, such as those looking to deploy capital into the Japanese real estate market from abroad.
