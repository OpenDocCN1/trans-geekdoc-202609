
Broad distribution of the support and control structure is needed for the blockchain network. This distribution is about control not about geography. Distributed control is a key tenet of blockchains. This is because a weakness of blockchain networks is from their consensus algorithms. Blockchain consensus algorithms determine validity by voting. The majority of network nodes determines whether a block or transaction is valid. Therefore, if many nodes fall under the control of one entity, then the blockchain is vulnerable. A 51% attack is when more than half of the participants in a blockchain consensus computation are compromised. That is, such a compromise can vote improperly giving the wrong consensus.

One of the important contributions of blockchains is their clever use of cryptography maintains previous transactions and locks in distributed consensus of disinterested parties. Quantum computing aside, 51% attacks do not compromise the cryptography. Rather, a 51% attack is a direct attack on the consensus mechanism. This means blockchains with few participants are more vulnerable than larger blockchains with large numbers of participants since small numbers of participants are easier to capture and control.

## 8.1. INTRODUCTION
Conventional financing mechanisms, such as initial public offerings (IPOs), have been under pressure in recent years, as burgeoning internet technologies facilitate cumbersome processes and eliminate unnecessary participants. The rise of online platforms coincided with private placement growth, helping entrepreneurs connect with a large audience of potential investors at the often critical early business stages. Now, as blockchain technology reinvents the fabric of the internet, the private placement fundraising method is being transformed with Security Token Offerings (STOs).

The following real world case study describes the Curzio Equity Owners (CEO) token issuance of a recently formed financial newsletter provider, Curzio Research. The case highlights the costs, benefits, requirements and timeframe for issuing a digital security to raise capital. The chapter generalizes to STOs in the mining industry, a traditional, archaic and conservative sector.

## 8.2. COMPANY BACKGROUND – CURZIO RESEARCH
Founded in 2016, Curzio Research is a financial newsletter publisher that provides individual investors with access to curated investment research and analysis. Within the first 18 months of business, Curzio Research amassed about 40,000 readers. A publisher’s success is largely dependent on its ability to convert its reader list into paid subscribers. Curzio Research has done well in this area, generating $6.4 million within the first two years of launch. For comparison, over the same period, their largest competitors booked $2 billion in sales, while distributing to millions of readers.

As Curzio Research’s readership expands, revenue growth will coincide: in the newsletter industry, list size means everything. After analyzing the data from their existing list and getting the proper management team in place, Curzio Research was ready for strategic expansion. Since capital serves as the fuel for scaling a business, the company set out in 2019 to raise $3–12 million with a private offering in mind.

## 8.3. PRIVATE OFFERING – ALTERNATIVES AND CHALLENGES
Under federal securities laws, a company may not offer or sell securities unless the offering has been registered with the SEC or an exemption from registration is available. An offering that meets exemption requirements is typically referred to as a private placement or an unregistered offering. Private placements can be offered under the following exemptions:
* Regulation A+;
* Regulation CF;
* or, most commonly, Regulation D (Reg D).

[Table 8.1](#CH008.html#tab1) provides a simplified summary of these exemptions, as well as their rules.

Curzio Research wasn’t eligible for the Regulation CF exemption because of its $1M maximum raise limit. The company also wasn’t interested in a Regulation A+ offering, due to the increased reporting requirements and 60 day waiting period. By process of elimination, Regulation D was the logical exemption; when deciding between Rule 506(c) or Rule 506(b), general solicitation was the main factor. Since sales and marketing are specialties of Curzio Research, a Reg D 506(c) offering was chosen.

While in the process of preliminary planning, Curzio Research started to see the downsides of traditional private offerings. The intermediaries wanted to extract exorbitant fees for simply making introductions to potential investors. Moreover, brokers sought rent without guaranteeing adequate liquidity for Curzio Research’s potential financial instrument. It was apparent that Curzio Research’s traditional private placement was going to generate more value for the middlemen than Curzio Research’s stakeholders.
