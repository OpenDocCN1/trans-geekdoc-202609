
The above simple product had many advantages over the traditional insurance products and processes. There are no exclusions or uncertainty with the claim processing – in case of delay there is always a payment. The passenger doesn’t even have to file a claim form. The insurance proceeds are deposited to the passenger’s bank account directly upon landing, there are no more trips to the bank to deposit a check. There are no manual intervention from the insurance company whatsoever, reducing the cost of operations for the insurance company.

Unfortunately, two years after its launch, in November 2019 Fizzy was discontinued, as it did not reach the commercial targets. AXA asserted that there was not sufficient market appetite for a blockchain-based consumer product as of that time. However, AXA has committed to continue to test similar insurance products and has gained valuable experience with building and deploying smart contracts by launching Fizzy.

In general blockchain powered decentralized insurance has many advantages that can yet be exploited. Smart contracts enables complete automation, including quoting of the premium, determining when the claim has to be settled, and auto-payments, thus reducing operating costs. Lower costs enable insurance companies to access developing economies and low-income businesses thereby increasing their market reach. Moreover, with reduced costs, insurance companies could offer low margin insurance products such as crop insurance to farmers, etc. The entire contract is transparent, and can be subject to public audit, hence increasing confidence and trust in the industry. As with AXA’s experience with Fizzy, perhaps the society isn’t ready yet but the potential exists and the concept has been tested.

### 11.7. ESTABLISHING PROVENANCE[⁸](#CH011.html#fn8)

With increased consumer literacy and their increased demand for socially responsible behavior on part of the corporations, establishing the provenance of retail products is gaining momentum. For example, when a consumer buys a product that is labeled “Made in U.S.A.,” how do they know what percentage of the raw materials were sourced in the United States. Similarly, when buying Brazilian coffee, how does the consumer know that the coffee beans were indeed grown in Brazil.

Blockchain technology through its ability to enhance traceability, and its property of immutability, allows consumers to ascertain the origin of products or that of its ingredients. The traceability is achieved by entering every detail and process in the manufacturing of the product on a blockchain ledger. Once coded into the blockchain, the record of the product is “permanent, chronically ordered and available to each actor on the network” (Gupta, 2017). This implies that the origin of the product can be traced back to its sources and is permanently stored, thus subject to future scrutiny.

As supply chains become more complex and increasingly global, many organizations contribute through the various stages of the product’s lifecycle. With consumer expectation around transparency increasing as well, there is pressure on large corporations to become more transparent about their supply chain.

The assurance consumers are seeking about a product are of four types: assurance about origin; assurance about authenticity; assurance about custody; and assurance about integrity as illustrated in [Fig. 11.3](#CH011.html#fig3). Blockchain technology can deliver these assurances by providing traceability, certifiability, trackability, immutability and verifiability (Montecchi et al., 2019).

Assurance regarding origin involves identifying whether the product was manufactured under ethical and safe environment without employing child labor with ingredients that were legally sourced from ethical producers. The country of origin of the key ingredients, such as coffee beans or cocoa, could influence consumer opinion and intent to purchase. Authenticity assurance is important for brand name products and luxury goods. For example, when a consumer purchases a Rolex watch or a Prada handbag, they are paying a premium for the brand name and would not like a similar product from a fake manufacturer. Luxury brand manufacturers use various types of trademarks to certify the products’ authenticity, which are typically costly requiring trusted third party. Blockchain technology offers alternatives certifications that are secure and transparent without reliance on intermediaries (Tucker & Catalini, 2018). For expensive products, consumers may demand information on the chain of custody through the transportation and storage phases. Assurance on integrity entails the product’s meeting customers’ expected level of performance and reliability. Blockchain can support this by providing transparency on the supply chain processes, which previously was difficult for consumer to access. This is usually important in exotic fashion products, which claim to use expensive raw materials, like Mongolian cashmere or crocodile leather or hand-woven silk. Providing consumers with a guaranteed assurance on the raw material, integrity of processes and assurance on the proper chain of custody could all be achieved through blockchain.

A British company aptly named Provenance,[⁹](#CH011.html#fn9) teamed up with a London-based fashion designer to offer fully traceable garment, where the source of each raw material was recorded and made available to the end consumer. The key raw material was Suri Alpaca fleece which was sourced from a national park. Through the blockchain, it allows the downstream consumer to navigate all the way upstream and be assured of the origin of the fleece.

Another similar example is of an Italian vineyard, Catina Volpone that partnered with EY to develop wine case certified by blockchain technology. This ensures complete transparency of the production process. The end consumer will have access to every minute detail including the dates of harvesting, bottling, etc., and other production details.

Implementing a blockchain solution to the supply chain requires all partners to agree to collaborate and share information. If any of the many parties from the alpaca farmer (as in previous example) to the tanner and the deliverer do not consent to participate, the solution cannot be implemented. Any break in the supply chain would defeat the purpose. Consent from all partners is necessary but not sufficient. The financial commitment from each partner to build and maintain the blockchain is mandatory. Most of the processes will have to be automated to eliminate the possibility of human error. The end customer should be aware and interested in accessing the provenance information and be willing to pay a premium for it to justify all of the upstream costs incurred.

Another area where provenance of the product is valued is in the agricultural supply chain. Blockchain technology can provide a mechanism for immutable record of the product from its harvesting to the retail store, including time-stamp for determination of freshness. Such a system will not only give the consumers trust and confidence in the product they buy but also an opportunity to reward the producers who employ good agricultural practices to cultivate the product. Potentially, this could lead to sustainable farming practices and responsible consumption. Barilla, an Italian manufacturer of pasta sauce had teamed up with IBM to tackle transparency and traceability of its production cycle. The supply chain starts at cultivation, harvest to storage and end at the retail store. For assurance on integrity, it provides detailed quality control information throughout its supply chain. Not only does it ensure food safety, but also it increases brand reputation.
