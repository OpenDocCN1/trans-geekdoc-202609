In the 1960s, many believed that cryptography would be the main countermeasure for attacks. Although that view has proven to be too limited, cryptography remains a very important security countermeasure. Additionally, cryptography is a part of many other countermeasures, so we begin the discussion of countermeasure technology with cryptography in this chapter and the next.

# Encryption for Confidentiality

A common security goal is **confidentiality**,¹ which means that people who intercept messages cannot read them. Figure 3-1 shows that confidentiality requires a type of cryptography called **encryption for confidentiality**. Encryption for confidentiality was the original purpose of cryptography.²

> Confidentiality means that people who intercept messages cannot read them.
