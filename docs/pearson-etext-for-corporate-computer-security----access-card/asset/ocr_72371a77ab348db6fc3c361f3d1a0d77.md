# In the News

# Costly Climate Disasters

A recent report¹ by the National Climatic Data Center (NCDC) stated that since 1980 there were more than 250 weather events (hurricanes, droughts, tornadoes, or other severe weather events) that caused more than $1 billion in damages, with a total cost over $1.7 trillion. In the first 6 months of 2019 there were six weather events that caused at least $1 billion in damages. The prior year, 2018, was the fourth most destructive year on record with 14 major events and damages totaling more than $91 billion.

# WikiLeaks Founder Arrested

In 2019 Julian Assange had his asylum revoked from the Ecuadorian Embassy and was arrested by law enforcement in the United Kingdom. Multiple countries are looking to prosecute the WikiLeaks founder for a variety of crimes, mostly connected with data leaks through WikiLeaks. Shortly after his arrest, Assange was charged by the U.S. government for violating the Espionage Act of 1917.² The U.S. government is seeking Assange's extradition, but he is currently being held in a UK jail.

Part of the U.S. case against Assange surrounds the help he provided well-known hackers. Prosecutors showed chat logs between Assange and hacker Jeremy Hammond talking about how to more easily parse stolen e-mails from security firm Stratfor.³ Hammond pled guilty to conspiracy to engage in computer hacking in 2013 and is serving out his 10-year sentence for the theft of account information of 860,000 Stratfor clients. In addition to attacking Stratfor, Hammond was also implicated in attacks on the FBI, the Arizona Department of Public Safety, Brooks-Jeffrey Marketing, Special Forces Gear, Vanguard Defense Industries, the Boston Police Patrolmen's Association, and Combined Systems.⁴

# Evolving Honeypots

Researchers in China developed a new honeypot that dynamically changes as it's being attacked. The dynamically changing environment is designed to distract hackers and prevent them from achieving their goal or realizing they're caught in a honeypot.⁵ Traditionally hackers have been the innovators in the cat-and-mouse game between hackers and systems administrators. However, new adaptive defense systems should make hacking much more difficult.
