This project uses an Enigma® machine simulator. It functions like the Enigma machines used during World War II. This example has been included to help you better understand how encryption worked in the early days. It’s a great learning tool when you first start exploring the subject of cryptography. Enigma machines provided fairly good encryption strength for their day. Modern cryptographic systems are much more secure than Enigma machines.

Pay attention to the colored paths as you type. The red path goes through the three rotors, bounces off the reflector, becomes green, and then goes back through the three rotors. The right rotor moves with each keystroke. If it completes one full cycle, it will advance the middle rotor and subsequently the left rotor.

## Case Study

### The Battle for Privacy

Bloomberg LP provides traders on Wall Street terminals that are used for a variety of purposes including market research, reading news, communicating with other traders, and making deals. Bloomberg's ability to maintain their client's confidentiality when using their terminals has come under attack.43 In one case, a Bloomberg reporter questioned an executive at Goldman Sachs about the possible departure of an employee because the employee hadn't logged into his terminal in a while.

It turned out that a built-in function (UUID) allowed Bloomberg reporters to see general usage statistics about every terminal user including traders, bankers, economists, and researchers. These statistics included when users used the terminal, and which of the built-in 15,000 functions they were using. The UUID function had been available since the initial implementation of the system 30 years ago.

Subsequent reports indicated that Bloomberg messaging data from their terminal systems had been found online.44 The data contained user information, trading data, and sensitive communications. Bloomberg messenger is widely used by traders to make deals. Bloomberg admits that messages are scanned for relevant deal information and sent back to their clients.

However, with recent privacy concerns and the revelation of past messaging data being found online, many are questioning if the content of messages between traders is being used inappropriately. Cyrus Sanati at CNNMoney notes, "There is also concern that the company may be using that information to help Bloomberg Tradebook or Bloomberg Pool, the company's growing broker-dealer and dark pool trading outfits, to gain an informational advantage over their clients."

Bloomberg executives have said they have disabled the UUID function and are appointing an executive to investigate any possible inappropriate use of confidential data.

This case illustrates the importance of privacy and confidentiality both within and between businesses. Companies are becoming increasingly interconnected and reliant on services provided by external vendors. Privacy and confidentiality violations can affect a business's bottom line.

The Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) has released its annual Who Has Your Back? report listing companies that have taken significant steps to ensure your privacy.45 The EFF report "examined the policies of major Internet companies—including ISPs, e-mail providers, cloud storage providers, location-based services, blogging platforms, and social networking sites—to assess whether they publicly commit to standing with users when the government seeks access to user data."
