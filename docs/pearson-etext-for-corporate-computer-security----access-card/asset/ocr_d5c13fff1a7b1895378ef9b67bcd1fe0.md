A small but significant fraction of all people who receive phishing messages respond to them because these messages seem so authentic. The 2019 Data Breach Investigations Report (DBIR) found that 32 percent of all data breaches included some form of phishing.38 That number jumps to 78 percent if the target is a nation-state. About 4 percent of users will click on a phishing campaign. More recently, the FBI estimated phishing losses at $1.3 billion for 2018.39

# SPEAR PHISHING

Normal phishing attacks tend to appeal broadly to many people so that they can dupe as many victims as possible. By contrast, spear phishing attacks are aimed at single individuals or small groups of individuals. For instance, if an attacker’s goal is to get the CEO of a corporation to download a Trojan horse, the attacker may craft an e-mail message that deals with a pressing issue for the CEO, appears to come from a trusted person, and contains specific details that only the trusted person is likely to know.

# HOAXES

Some e-mail messages contain hoaxes. In some cases, these hoaxes simply make the victim feel stupid when they tell other people what they “learned.” In other cases, the hoaxes try to persuade the victim to damage their own system by deleting critical system files.

# 1.5 Hackers and Attacks

1.5 Describe traditional external hackers and their attacks, including break-in processes, social engineering, and denial-of-service attacks.

In the 1970s, malware writers were joined by external hackers, who began to break into corporate computers that were connected to modems. Today, every firm is connected to the Internet, which harbors millions of external hackers. Hackers are able to break into corporate networks, steal confidential data, or do damage to critical infrastructure from thousands of miles away.
