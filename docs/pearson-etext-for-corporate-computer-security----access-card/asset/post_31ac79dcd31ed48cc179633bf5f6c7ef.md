
- *Information and Communication*—Relevant information is identified, captured, and communicated in a form and timeframe that enable people to carry out their responsibilities. Effective communication also occurs in a broader sense, flowing down, across, and up the entity.
- *Monitoring*—The enterprise risk management system is monitored and modifications are made as necessary. Monitoring is accomplished through ongoing management activities, separate evaluations, or both.

## COBIT

COSO is a general control planning and assessment tool for corporations. For IT controls, there is a more specific framework, **Control Objectives for Information and Related Technologies (COBIT)**. In addition to creating the broad control objectives framework, the IT Governance Institute also has developed detailed guidance for implementing the COBIT framework.

## THE COBIT FRAMEWORK

Figure 2-28 illustrates the COBIT 2019 framework. This framework has five major domains, which follow the general systems development life cycle:

- *Evaluate, Direct and Monitor (EDM)*—The EDM domain has five high-level control objectives that evaluate strategic alternatives and help guide senior leadership on the monitoring and achievement of that strategy.
- *Align, Plan and Organize (APO)*—The APO domain has 14 high-level control objectives that cover the organization of IT, how it can be used strategically, and how it should be supported.
- *Build, Acquire and Implement (BAI)*—After planning, companies need to make their own systems, acquire systems from external sources, and then implement those systems. This domain has 11 high-level control objectives.
- *Deliver, Service and Support (DSS)*—Most of an IT project’s life takes place after implementation. Consequently, the COBIT framework has six high-level control objectives for delivery, service, and support.
- *Monitor, Evaluate and Assess*—Finally, firms must monitor their processes, evaluate the adequacy of internal controls, obtain independent assurance, and provide for independent auditing. These are four control objectives.

Beneath the five major domains of COBIT, then, there are 40 high-level governance and management objectives. Beneath these are many detailed governance practices. COBIT also includes many documents that help organizations understand how to implement the framework.

## DOMINANCE IN THE UNITED STATES

The IT Governance Institute was created by the Information Systems Audit and Control Association (ISACA). ISACA, in turn, is the primary professional association for IT audit professionals in the United States. The Association’s certified information systems auditor (CISA) certification is the dominant certification for U.S. IT auditors, so it is not surprising that COBIT has become the dominant framework for auditing IT controls in the United States.

## The ISO/IEC 27000 Series

While COBIT focuses on the governance of the IT function broadly, the ISO/IEC 27000 series of standards focuses specifically and in detail on IT security. The first standard in this series was originally called ISO/IEC 17799. When it was decided to have all security standards begin with 27000, this standard was renamed ISO/IEC 27000.

## ISO/IEC 27002

This standard divides security into 14 broad areas (listed below) and offers best practices for information security controls within an organization.

1. Information security policies
2. Organization of information security
3. Human resource security
4. Asset management
5. Access control
6. Cryptography
7. Physical and environmental security
8. Operations security
9. Communications security
10. Information systems acquisition, development, and maintenance
11. Supplier relationships
12. Information security incident management
13. Information security aspects of business continuity management
14. Compliance

## ISO/IEC 27001

In 2005, ISO and the IEC released ISO/IEC 27001. This standard specifies how to *certify* organizations as being *compliant* with ISO/IEC 27000. This is important because by demonstrating compliance, companies can assure business partners (and their management) that the company’s security is well managed. In other frameworks, including COSO and COBIT, companies certify themselves, sometimes with the concurrence of an external auditor. They lack ISO/IEC 27001’s third-party certification process, which external parties may value highly.

However, certification does not necessarily perfect security—only that the IT security management function follows ISO/IEC 27000. IT security, as noted at the beginning of this chapter, cannot guarantee that no security breaches will occur.

## OTHER 27000 STANDARDS

ISO and IEC are continually working on other standards for the 27000 family. Below are a few examples of the other ISO 27000 standards.

- *ISO/IEC 27004* will define how to *manage* the security function via monitoring, measurement, analysis, and evaluation.
- *ISO/IEC 27005* focuses on *risk management*.
- *ISO/IEC 27007* focuses on information security *systems auditing*.
- *ISO/IEC 27039* focuses on *intrusion prevention*.
- *ISO/IEC 27043* focuses on *incident investigation*.
