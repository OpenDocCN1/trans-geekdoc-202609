Keys, with lengths less than 112 bits, are considered weak symmetric keys. Public key/private key pairs (discussed later in the chapter) must be much longer to be considered strong because of the disastrous consequences if a private key is cracked and because private keys cannot be changed frequently. Public keys and private keys must be at least 512 to 1,024 bits long.

National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) recommends symmetric keys need to be at least 112 bits long to be considered strong.⁶ As the power of cryptanalysts' computers continues to grow, even longer symmetric keys will be needed for strong encryption. By 2030, NIST estimates that keys will need to be at least 128 bits long.⁷
