Newer naval boats may have systems that can store a lifetime of detailed information. The same may be true of cell phones, ground vehicles, photocopiers, and so on. Security policies must change with advances in technology.

PricewaterhouseCoopers (PwC) 2018 Global State of Information Security Survey collected more than 9,500 responses from “9,500 CEOs, CFOs, CIOs, CISOs, CSOs, VPs, and directors of IT as well as security practices from 122 countries.” The following are a few of the key findings from their annual report that yield insights about the state of information security at corporations, and the driving forces behind security policy creation within those corporations.24

48 percent of organizations said they did not have a security awareness program in place.
54 percent said they did not have an incident response plan in place.
45 percent said they performed vulnerability assessments.
40 percent said disruptions of operations and manufacturing was their biggest concern.
67 percent said they have an IoT security strategy in place or are currently implementing one.
29 percent reported loss or damage of internal records as

a result of a security incident.
35 percent reported the loss of customer records.
30 percent said current employees represent the top source of security incidents.
