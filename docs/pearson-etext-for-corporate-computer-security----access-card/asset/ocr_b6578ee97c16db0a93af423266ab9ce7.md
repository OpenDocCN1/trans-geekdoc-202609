The NCSC report illustrates the real and persistent cyber threats U.S. corporations are facing. Most corporations focus the majority of their resources and efforts on maintaining their competitive advantage and, most importantly, profitability. The question becomes do corporations have the resources, knowledge, personnel, and skills to fend off attacks from state-sponsored entities with nearly unlimited budgets?

Managed IT security services may be part of the answer. Corporations may be able to delegate part of their IT security function to external security firms in the same way they would outsource their payroll function. A report by Aberdeen Group looked at the trend toward managed IT security services. Following are seven key findings from the Aberdeen report on the move toward managed services for network security:

+   1. More than half (53 percent) of all respondents have outsourced at least one of their IT security solutions, up from about one-third (36 percent) in Aberdeen's study from mid-2011.
+   2. Current investments in IT security continue to be driven by risk (e.g., avoid negative publicity; actual security-related incidents, or vulnerabilities and threats; business disruptions) and compliance (e.g., government or industry regulations; industry standards and best practices; actual audit- or compliance-related issues).
+   3. The most commonly experienced consequences of actual security-related incidents, on the other hand, were loss of end-user productivity and unplanned downtime or system outages.

+   4. Overall, network security solutions appear to reflect very high penetration (between 60 percent and 100 percent, depending on the specific solution category) but relatively modest growth (between 3 percent and 24 percent).
+   5. A closer look, however, shows very high growth—between 30 percent and 95 percent—for network security in outsourced/managed models.
+   6. Aberdeen's analysis of estimated total annual cost shows an annual advantage of up to 50 percent per year for deployments using managed security services providers:
+      - Network firewalls—57 percent lower operational costs, 37 percent lower security costs, 49 percent lower total annual costs
+      - Intrusion detection/prevention—3 percent lower operational costs, 22 percent lower security costs, 11 percent lower total annual cost
+      - 24×7×365 security monitoring services—45 percent lower operational costs, 4 percent lower security costs, 35 percent lower total annual costs
+   7. Operating and maintaining a secure, compliant, and available IT infrastructure are not necessarily core competencies, and can represent significant opportunity costs for organizations of any size—a point that sheds some additional light on why the research findings on growth and cost are so strongly in favor of the outsourced/managed security services model.
