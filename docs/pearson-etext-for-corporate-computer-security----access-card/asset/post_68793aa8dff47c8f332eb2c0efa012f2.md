## Case Study

### Security Policy, Leadership, and Training

A retired Japanese Coast Guard boat (Takachiho) was sold to a pro-North Korean organization without having assurances that navigational data was deleted. The decommissioned patrol boat could have had as many as 6,000 locations recorded over 250 days of use.²²

The vessel was presumably sold to be turned into scrap. Weapons and radio equipment were removed, but no procedures were in place to ensure that navigational data were securely deleted. In fact, there are no secure data deletion procedures in place for naval vessels prior to disposal. It is unknown if navigational data were recovered from vessels disposed of through past sales.²³

The loss of Japanese military data to North Korea may be a serious security breach. This is especially troubling during times of heightened tensions between the two countries. Secure disposal policies will likely be applied to all craft, systems, and technology in the name of national security.

This case points out the importance of periodically reviewing security policies and procedures. Past Japanese navy boats may have been unable to store any navigational data.

Newer naval boats may have systems that can store a lifetime of detailed information. The same may be true of cell phones, ground vehicles, photocopiers, and so on. Security policies must change with advances in technology.

PricewaterhouseCoopers (PwC) 2018 Global State of Information Security Survey collected more than 9,500 responses from “9,500 CEOs, CFOs, CIOs, CISOs, CSOs, VPs, and directors of IT as well as security practices from 122 countries.” The following are a few of the key findings from their annual report that yield insights about the state of information security at corporations, and the driving forces behind security policy creation within those corporations.24

48 percent of organizations said they did not have a security awareness program in place.
54 percent said they did not have an incident response plan in place.
45 percent said they performed vulnerability assessments.
40 percent said disruptions of operations and manufacturing was their biggest concern.
67 percent said they have an IoT security strategy in place or are currently implementing one.
29 percent reported loss or damage of internal records as a result of a security incident.
35 percent reported the loss of customer records.
30 percent said current employees represent the top source of security incidents.
