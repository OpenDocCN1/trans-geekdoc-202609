## Project 2

In this project, you will create two simple firewall rules in Windows Advanced Firewall. This may be the first time you have made a modification to the firewall on your computer. The first rule will block all ICMP traffic. This will effectively prevent you from using the ping command to send ICMP packets to other computers. You will use a command prompt to verify the rule was effective.

The second rule will block all outgoing Port 80 traffic. Port 80 is traditionally associated with web traffic (HTTP). Once you create and enable the rule, all outgoing Port 80 traffic will be blocked. You will use a web browser to verify the rule was effective. However, secure web traffic (HTTPS) running over Port 443 will still be accessible.

Both of the rules in this project will apply to outgoing traffic only. It is important to remember to disable the rules at the end of the project so your ICMP and Port 80 traffic will not be blocked.

- 1. Click Start.
- 2. In the search box, type cmd.
- 3. Press Enter.
- 4. Type ping www.google.com
- 5. Press Enter. (This will ping www.Google.com.)
- 6. Take a screenshot.
- 7. Click Start and type Windows Defender Firewall.
- 8. Click on Windows Defender Firewall.
- 9. Click Advanced settings.
- 10. Click Outbound Rules.
- 11. Click New Rule (right-hand pane).
- 12. Click Custom, Next, and Next.
- 13. Change the dropdown box to ICMPv4.
- 14. Click Next, Next, Next, and Next.
- 15. Name the rule YourName_Block_ICMP. (Replace YourName with your first and last names. In this case, it was RandyBoyle_Block_ICMP.)
- 16. Click Finish.
- 17. Return to your command prompt.
- 18. Type ping www.google.com
- 19. Press Enter. (This will ping www.google.com. You should get a “General failure” error.)
- 20. Take a screenshot.
- 21. Open a web browser.
- 22. Browse to www.Google.com. (This will verify that you do have Internet access.)
- 23. Return to the Windows Defender Firewall window.
- 24. Click Outbound Rules.
- 25. Click New Rule (right-hand pane).
- 26. Click Port and Next.
- 27. Type “80” into the text box for Specific remote ports. (This will effectively block all outgoing web traffic from your computer. You will disable [or delete] this rule later.)
- 28. Click Next, Next, and Next.
- 29. Name the rule YourName_Block_Port_80. (Replace Your Name with your first and last names. In this case, the rule was named RandyBoyle_Block_Port_80.)
- 30. Click Finish.
- 31. Return to your web browser.
- 32. Browse to any nonsecure (not HTTPS) website of your choosing. (You can browse to any website as long as it does not make an HTTPS connection (Port 443). The rule you made only blocks Port 80 web traffic.)
- 33. Take a screenshot of the blocked website. (In this case, it was www.microsoft.com.)
- 34. Return to the Windows Defender Firewall window.
- 35. Select both of the rules you created.
- 36. Right-click the selected rules.
- 37. Click Disable Rule. (If you don’t disable the rules, your ICMP and web traffic will still be blocked.)
- 38. Take a screenshot of your disabled rules.

## R&D versus Espionage

Funding R&D projects for new drugs, technology, aircraft, and clean energy can be extremely costly. For some corporations and governments, these costs are too high. But there is a way to bypass R&D costs—espionage. Why develop a new drug when you can steal it at 1/100th the cost?

A 2018 report from the National Counterintelligence and Security Center (NCSC) detailed several threats to U.S. corporations in the form of economic espionage by foreign collectors.⁹ The report notes that there is little risk of getting caught by their targeted corporations. The use of proxies, routing through multiple networks/hosts, and custom malware make identifying the crooks difficult. Even if the theft was noticed and the cyber crooks identified, complications with international extradition would make prosecution nearly impossible.

⁹ National Counterintelligence and Security Center, 2018 Foreign Economic Espionage in Cyberspace, Office of the Director of National Intelligence, July 26, 2018. www.dni.gov/index.php/ncsc-newsroom/item/1889-2018-foreign-economic-espionage-in-cyberspace.

The NCSC notes that the United States is a leader in the development of new technologies and that “foreign economic and industrial espionage against the United States continues to represent a significant threat to America's prosperity, security, and competitive advantage." NCSC says the following industries have been and will continue to be primary targets of foreign economic espionage:

- Energy/alternative energy
- Biotechnology
- Defense technology
- Environmental protection
- High-end manufacturing
- Information and communications technology

The NCSC report illustrates the real and persistent cyber threats U.S. corporations are facing. Most corporations focus the majority of their resources and efforts on maintaining their competitive advantage and, most importantly, profitability. The question becomes do corporations have the resources, knowledge, personnel, and skills to fend off attacks from state-sponsored entities with nearly unlimited budgets?

Managed IT security services may be part of the answer. Corporations may be able to delegate part of their IT security function to external security firms in the same way they would outsource their payroll function. A report by Aberdeen Group looked at the trend toward managed IT security services. Following are seven key findings from the Aberdeen report on the move toward managed services for network security:

- 1. More than half (53 percent) of all respondents have outsourced at least one of their IT security solutions, up from about one-third (36 percent) in Aberdeen's study from mid-2011.
- 2. Current investments in IT security continue to be driven by risk (e.g., avoid negative publicity; actual security-related incidents, or vulnerabilities and threats; business disruptions) and compliance (e.g., government or industry regulations; industry standards and best practices; actual audit- or compliance-related issues).
- 3. The most commonly experienced consequences of actual security-related incidents, on the other hand, were loss of end-user productivity and unplanned downtime or system outages.
- 4. Overall, network security solutions appear to reflect very high penetration (between 60 percent and 100 percent, depending on the specific solution category) but relatively modest growth (between 3 percent and 24 percent).
- 5. A closer look, however, shows very high growth—between 30 percent and 95 percent—for network security in outsourced/managed models.
- 6. Aberdeen's analysis of estimated total annual cost shows an annual advantage of up to 50 percent per year for deployments using managed security services providers:
  - Network firewalls—57 percent lower operational costs, 37 percent lower security costs, 49 percent lower total annual costs
  - Intrusion detection/prevention—3 percent lower operational costs, 22 percent lower security costs, 11 percent lower total annual cost
  - 24×7×365 security monitoring services—45 percent lower operational costs, 4 percent lower security costs, 35 percent lower total annual costs
- 7. Operating and maintaining a secure, compliant, and available IT infrastructure are not necessarily core competencies, and can represent significant opportunity costs for organizations of any size—a point that sheds some additional light on why the research findings on growth and cost are so strongly in favor of the outsourced/managed security services model.
