## Security @ Work

### Exhaustive Cheating

Any automotive enthusiast will tell you that the past several decades have had a tremendous impact on cars. Not long ago most car troubles could be fixed with just some basic tools and a warm Saturday afternoon. Today, those same repairs require specialized equipment and a certified technician. While many of us enjoy the new functionality that contemporary vehicles provide (e.g., enhanced GPS navigation, real-time traffic updates, and innovative safety features), the proliferation of computers in vehicles has turned them into black boxes. A black box is a system with internal workings that we don’t know or understand; we can only interpret the inputs and outputs of the system.

The black box nature of cars has made it difficult for even certified technicians to completely understand their inner workings. In fact, it’s possible to manipulate the inner workings of a vehicle without the changes being noticed. Even potentially damaging, harmful, or illegal system manipulations can be difficult to detect.

Recently, a global car manufacturer was found exploiting the black box nature of cars to improve the emissions performance of its vehicles, effectively circumventing industry regulations.

### VW Busted

Emissions testing is performed when a vehicle’s registration must be renewed. In order for the testing to occur, you drive your car to an emissions testing facility where various sensors and equipment are attached to it. Employees run diagnostics, and if your vehicle’s emissions fall within government-regulated limits, you can proceed with registering your vehicle. Due to strict emissions testing, vehicle manufacturers are forced to develop cars that adhere to emissions regulations. However, forcing cars to adhere to these regulations may affect vehicle performance. In other words, in order to make cars “clean,” manufacturers may have to give up some power.

It is still unclear who was involved in the emissions cheating at Volkswagen and to what extent the initiative was backed by upper management. What is clear is that someone, at some level, decided to create deceptive software designed to cheat standardized emissions testing procedures. This software was detected when a test was conducted to see if a Volkswagen vehicle could be manipulated to reduce emissions. The software was designed such that regular operation of the car resumed when the emissions test was complete.

Experts believe the embedded software was designed to temporarily improve fuel savings and reduce torque and acceleration in order to pass the emissions test. When the emissions test was complete, fuel usage, torque, and acceleration performance levels returned to normal. When normal performance resumed, emissions output rose well above legal levels.^10

The scope of this violation is substantial. This software was installed on an estimated 11 million vehicles worldwide.^11 Volkswagen apparently deployed the deceptive software during testing in order to conform to government emissions standards and to appeal to vehicle owners with the appearance of improved performance.

### Man versus Machine

The decision of some employees to create deceptive software tarnished the Volkswagen brand and created a substantial drop in stock prices. But what if intelligent machines had the ability to autonomously carry out this type of deceptive action? What if intelligent machines could manipulate their own software to deceive other systems or even humans?

Technologists and researchers speculate about the capabilities of future computers and the ways they may benefit society. Hollywood movies portray powerful artificial intelligence (AI) in a positive way (e.g., Jarvis in *Iron Man*). However, some of the most prominent scientists and innovators warn of the possible dangers of powerful AI systems.

Elon Musk (cofounder, CEO, and product architect of Tesla Motors) and Stephen Hawking (renowned physicist) both recently won the Luddite Award, an award given to individuals thought to be in opposition to technological advancements. How could these two experts, often considered on the cutting edge of their respective disciplines, win such an award? Because they have spoken out publicly about the potential risks of developing systems with AI equal to or exceeding human capabilities.

Musk and Hawking have warned that such systems could ultimately harm us. Hollywood has also portrayed the downside of powerful AI many times (e.g., *The Matrix, 2001: A Space Odyssey*, and *Ex Machina*). Is it possible that these movies could be glimpses of the future? Is it possible that one day powerful AI systems will manipulate their own code and operations in order to deceive? Perhaps… just be sure to keep your computer’s power cable within easy reach!
