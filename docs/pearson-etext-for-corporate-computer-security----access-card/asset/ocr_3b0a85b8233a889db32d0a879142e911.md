# Antivirus Industry

The dominant leader in the antivirus industry is Microsoft Windows Defender. It’s estimated that about 50 percent of all Windows desktops now rely on Windows Defender as their primary antivirus scanner. In 2011 Microsoft integrated Windows Defender® into its operating system at the time, Microsoft Windows 8, and gained market share very quickly. Windows Defender comes preinstalled in all versions of Windows and cannot be removed.

The integration of a free antivirus scanner into Microsoft’s dominant operating system had many antivirus vendors worried they would become redundant and eventually forced out of business. Microsoft Windows has about 90 percent of the desktop market share, so their fears were understandable.16

# Future of Antivirus Software

With dozens of vendors, hundreds of million viruses, mobile devices, IoT devices, ever-increasing bandwidth, and new propagation vectors, it is likely that the antivirus industry will remain strong and profitable. The substantial number of competitors in the marketplace may encourage mergers, acquisitions, and consolidation within the industry.

Antivirus vendors will also need to adapt to and provide protection for hosts that may not be traditional targets. These may include smartphones, tablets, mixed-reality devices, watches, social networks, private clouds, commercial equipment, and even cars. In the near term, smartphones may prove to be the next big target for virus writers. And Microsoft may not be a threat when it comes to mobile devices. Android (70 percent) and iOS (28 percent) dominate the mobile market.18
