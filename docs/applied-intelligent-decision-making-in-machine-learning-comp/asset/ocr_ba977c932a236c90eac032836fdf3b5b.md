OCR development has a rich history [8, 30, 37]. Starting from OCR machine to modern software-based recognition systems, the main aim is to recognize and classify characters irrespective of position, font size, and their orientation in the visual field. With modern devices, the detection font style independent has also become an interesting field of research. Broadly an OCR system has two steps, namely, character extraction and character detection. The extraction process involves image enhancement [1], skew and error correction, binarization [18, 19, 42–44], line and word separation, and character separation. The image enhancement and error removal techniques are aim to make the image ready for the OCR. These steps are regarded as the pre-processing step as a whole, which can be followed by a font detection process [31]. Font detection helps improve accuracy by utilizing font-specific detection algorithms. So little work has been reported in recognize Odia character recognition, and in most of these cases only primary characters [7, 29] have been considered. The study of basic and compound characters in Indian languages like Bangla [6, 11] and Devnagari [34] has been carried out by many researchers. Chaudhuri et al. [6] have studied 35 and modified characters of the Bangla language and achieved 96% accuracy. Following this, 300 numbers of allographs of the Bangla script were studied by Garain et al. [17] using the run-number-based method and showed 99.69% accuracy over a self-collected dataset, while in an accuracy of 96.00 was earned for Devnagari character recognition. However, the performance is yet to be significantly improved for other Indian scripts like Kannada [33], Gurmukhi [21], Telugu [46], Tamil [9], Gujarati [2, 16] and Odia [7, 29].

Many studies have carried out handwritten Odia OCR. Padhi et al. [32, 33] designed features based on size and statistical information, and used ANN to classify 44 allographs of Odia script, with an accuracy of 94% achieved on a self-collected handwritten dataset. Pal et al. [36] studied 52 Odia characters using gradient and curvature features with a quadratic classifier to achieve an accuracy of 91.11. Kumar et al. [20] proposed a method using an Ant miner-algorithm on 50 classes of Odia handwritten characters and achieved 90% accuracy. Basa et al. [4, 25] demonstrated a two-classification system. They proposed a tree based method to classify handwritten Odia characters into two groups and further used discriminant features of each group learned by two different ANNs. They formed a handwritten Odia character dataset of 51 classes and showed that their method provided an overall accuracy of 90%. Das et al. [10] proposed a recognition model for Odiacars using an extreme learning machine (ELM). They analyzed parameters of ELM and their effect on accuracy for various datasets.
