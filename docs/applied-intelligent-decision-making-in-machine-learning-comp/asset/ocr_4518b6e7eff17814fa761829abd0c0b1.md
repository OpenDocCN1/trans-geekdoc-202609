The weighted average of fields is computed after normalizing the representation of each field to get the document embedding, $D_d$. The corresponding equation for normalizing is given in Equation 5.2. Here, we use the title, abstract, and keywords of a document D:

```
$D_d = \lambda^{Title} \frac{FV_{D[Title]}}{\|FV_{D[Title]\|^2} + \lambda^{Abstract} \frac{FV_{D[Abstract]}}{\|FV_{D[Abstract]\|^2} + \lambda^{Keywords} \frac{FV_{D[Keywords]}}{\|FV_{D[Keywords]\|^2}} \quad (5.2)$
```

where $\lambda^{Title}$, $\lambda^{Abstract}$, and $\lambda^{Keywords}$ are scalar model parameters. The parameters of embedding model, that is, $\lambda^*$, $d^{dir*}$, and $d^{mag*}$, are learned using a training set $T < S_d, D^{cited}, D^{notcited}$ where $S_d$ is a seed document, $D^{cited}$ is a document cited in $S_d$, and $D^{notcited}$ is a document not cited in $S_d$. Wang et al. describe in their work that the goal of the model predictor is to train a high cosine similarity for the pair $(S_d, D^{cited})$ and a low cosine similarity for the per-instance triplet loss (Wang et al. 2014). The representing loss function is given in Equation 5.3.

```
$Loss = \max(\alpha + s(S_d, D^{cited}) - s(S_d, D^{notcited}), 0) \quad (5.3)$
```

where $s(S_d, D_j)$ is defined as the cosine similarity between document embeddings cos-sim$(D_d, D_j)$. Here $\alpha$ is the hyper-parameter of the model and tuned to get the best result. Choosing a positive sample for training is straightforward, any pair of $(S_d, D^{cited})$ is selected, where a document $S_d$ in the training set cites $D^{cited}$. But, a careful selection of the not-cited sample is needed for training the model to show better performance. The documents that are not cited in a document but are near to it in the embedding space are chosen as negative samples to train the model.

# 5.3.4 PHASE 2: RANKING CANDIDATE JOURN

This phase aims at training the model, which takes a pair of documents $(S_d, D_j)$ and estimates the probability as whether $D_j$ should be recommended or not.
A vital goal of this work is to evaluate the viability of recommending journals without metadata. $FV_{default}$ is a feature vector, which is calculated for different fields (title, abstract, and keywords) of each document.
For the document under consideration, a subset of words types in the title are identified by conducting intersection, and the sum of their weights are computed as an additional feature, for example, $\sum_{\omega} d_i^*$; Along with this, we also consider the log of the output connection (the number of other documents cited) of $D_p$, that is, $log(D_{(cited)})$; the cosine similarity between $S_d$ and $D_j$ in the embedding space, that is, $cos\_sim(D_{er}, D_{ej})$, is used.
