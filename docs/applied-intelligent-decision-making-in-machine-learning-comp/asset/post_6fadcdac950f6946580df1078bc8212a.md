## 9.4 CONCLUDING REMARKS

The development of a robust, high-quality, and accurate model for forecasting carbon emissions is a prerequisite for providing insights into environmental policies for achieving the Paris Agreement on climate change. Research comparing the forecasting ability of different ML algorithms, by focusing on carbon emissions and more specifically on Australia's carbon emissions, warrants further research. This research has applied many ML techniques, such as DT, RF, XGBoost, and SVR, to model Australia's carbon emissions. The findings indicate that the DT has a high coefficient of determination (R²) of 99.71%, followed by the RF with an R² of 99.14%, XGBoost with an R² of 98.88%, and SVR with an R² of 97.42%. Overall, the tree-based models had the lowest MAPE and RMSE. Overall, the DT model produced the most accurate predictions. In the other hand, the kernel-based model, RBF-SVR, had comparatively higher errors. For computational efficiency, the DT and SVR models were more efficient than XGBoost and RF. The DT ranked first among the other ML techniques utilized in this study, by the performance assessment metrics. The implication is that for accurate prediction and planning of policies to mitigate change, Australia's environmental planners should include a decision tree as one of the techniques for modeling and forecasting carbon emissions. Some ML algorithms could have performed better in terms of their computational efficiency; however, the combination and choice of parameters for such algorithms during the grid-search process could be computationally laborious in general and hence, to a marginal extent, could limit our results in those aspects. Robust and novel algorithms could be employed to explain the 100% variance in the dataset. This study was for regression, future research could investigate the classification problems pertaining to Australia's carbon emissions.
