# 4.1 INTRODUCTION

A landslide is a natural disaster, which causes a loss of social as well as economic property. In recent decades human civilization has been increasing, but not in a scientific way and in the process destroying of mountainous regions and enhancing the likelihood of landslides. A landslide is one type of slope instability process and also a mass movement (such as a rock fall, earth flow and debris fall) (Borgatti and Soldati 2010). It is also the outcome of the relationship between shear stress and the materials' shear strength on the slope (Ycin 2007). In hilly and mountainous regions, landslides are very common and frequent in nature, but the time of their happening is not yet possible to determine (Gla and Crozier 2005). The presentation of landslide susceptibility mapping is an area where identified capacity according to various environmental factors (like slope, aspect, elevation, profile curvature, plan curvature, topographicness index (TWI), stream power index (SPI), geology, lithology, soil, distance to truce, linear density, land use and land cover (LULC), the normalized vegetation index (NDVI), road density, rainfall, and morphology) and present landslide features. Fell et al. 2008 that shows the spatial distribution, the known and unknown landslide areas based on local terrain. The preparation of a landslide susceptibility mapping of any region helps to forecast future landslide prone areas or vulnerable zones (Ahmed 2015; Pal et al. 2019). Traditionally, statistically approaches were used for large areas and recently trend machine learning models have also been widely used with their ensembles for clusters and also for validation (Bu et al. 2016). The statistical methods include various approaches, such as logistic regression (LR), multivariate regression, a statistical method, an analytical hierarchy process (AHP), an evident belief function (EBF), and certainty factors (Chakraborty et al. 2018, 2020; Chowdhury et al. 2020; Das and Pal 2019a, 2019b, 2020; Das et al. 2019a, 2019b; Pal et al. 2019). Therefore machine learning is an artificial intelligence branch which considers computer-based algorithms for making accurate predictions from invariant data. Previous literature reviews informed about used machine learning models for landslide prediction areas. These studies included an analytical neural network work (ANN); a neural network model; decision trees (DTs); a support vector machine (SVM); boosted regression trees (BRTs); random forest (RF) geography-based optimization (BBO); eco-geography-based optimization (EBO); data mining; neuro-fuzzy and hybrid neuro-fuzzy, decision-making models; and big data analysis methods (Das et al. 2015, 2018, 2019, 2020a, 2020b, 2020c, 2020d; Dey et al. 2018, 2019; Rout et al. 2020).

Nowadays, hybrid machine-learning models are exploring the problem in a scientific way. The hybrid models used in previous studies are ANN-fuzzy logic, step weight assessment analysis ratio (SWARA), an adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system with frequency ratio (FR), EBF-fuzzy logic, and our recent research work consider hybrid geography-based optimization ensembles with a main operator, which are migrations and mutations.
