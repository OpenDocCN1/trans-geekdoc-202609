In hilly regions every year many reports are published about landslides. If we check the global scale, we can see 66 million people in highly landslide-prone areas, where 17% are affected by this natural disaster (Sassa and Canuti 2008). Sikkim is a small hilly state in India in its southern Eastern Himalaya, where most of the topography has developed by erosional process through a number of perennial and non-perennial springs and springs (Pal and Chowdhury 2019; Pal et al. 2019; Rawat et al. 2016; Tambe et al. 2012). Of four districts, the east district is more populated and civilized, where 79.55% of the total population lives in an urban area. The trend of rapid urbanization is increasing day by day and is concentrated around the capital of Gangtok. The major landslides in Sikkim are 1968, 1997, 2000, 2011 and 2018 (Kaur et al. 2019). Choubey (1995) has reported that about 36,000 people were killed by the 1968 landslide alone. In Sikkim in 1997, June 7 was a black day. Due to heavy rain on that day 5000 houses were washed away, 50 people were injured, and National Highway 31A was damaged. In 2007 landslides happened in the area of Takse. In 2011, Sikkim faced landslides due to an earthquake (Kaur et al. 2019). In the current area East Sikkim is more susceptible to landslides because of population pressure, rising urbanization, and/or effective environment conditioning factors; and this hilly region is very vulnerable to landslides (Chakraborty et al. 2011). Some landslide susceptibility works have been done in East Sikkim well as Sikkim but the many landslide causative factors and ensemble learning methods have not applied to landslide susceptibility mapping. The objective of this present research is to prepare a susceptibility map of East Sikkim. Hybrid BBO and its ensembles were used to show more vulnerable zone of landslides from which the Sikkim Government can take administrative steps to reduce unplanned rustic civilians and also manage increasing urban development in a scientific way.
