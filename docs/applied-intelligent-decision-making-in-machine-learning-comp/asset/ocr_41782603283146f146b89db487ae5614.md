# 9.2.4 OPTIMIZATION OF HYPERPARAMETERS

As the goal of this study is to evaluate predictive ML models, the need is to develop optimal models suitable for comparison purposes. We performed a grid search with ten-fold cross-validation on the hyperparameters of each model. This technique shuffles and resamples the training data into ten equal folds, fits the model on a combination of one set of hyperparameters, and tests the model on the remaining fold (Bannor & Acheampong, 2019). A carefully tuned model is at lower risk of overfitting and overfitting problems. The best score function returns a combination of hyperparameters and associated arguments for developing the model. For all models, we assessed their mean cross-validation scores (MCVs). The highest HCV was used as the base to select the ideal combination of hyperparameters.

# 9.2.4.1 Parameter Tuning for the DT, RF, and XGBoost Algorithms

In prior experimentation, certain parameters were deemed to influence the performance of the models significantly and hence were used in tuning the ML algorithms; in the case of the DT algorithm, five maximum leaf nodes (none, 2, 3, 5, and 7), and five minimum samples for a leaf node (1, 3, 5, 7, and 9) over the ten folds of data resulted in 1250 models. We used a random seed for the DT regression. For the RF algorithm, five numbers of estimators/trees (100, 150, 200, 250, and 300), five tree depths (1, 3, 5, 7), and five minimum samples for a leaf node (1, 3, 5, 7, and 9) were specified. This also led to 1250 models. The hyperparameter arguments of the XGBoost, such as the number of estimators (100, 150, 200, 250, and 300), the number of maximum tree depths (1, 3, 5, 7, and 9), and the learning rates (0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3), were also tuned, totaling 1250 models.
