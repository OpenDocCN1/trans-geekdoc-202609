## 8.1 INTRODUCTION

A massive number of research papers on disease treatment, prevention, and diagnosis have been published. The medical text data provide the origin of information for biomedical study and research. However, these research papers are scattered across a huge informatics literature which have been published by specialist doctors. It is difficult for doctors to read all of these publications and discover new knowledge. The need is to accumulate all the information in a single place so that the specialist doctor may obtain guiding information for the most effective treatment and prevention. Health care professionals keep patient details, as their past medical history, signs and symptoms of diseases, tests and treatments, and medication, in clinical records are discharge summaries and patients' prescriptions. These clinical records are in the form of unstructured or semi-structured texts. Extracting medical knowledge from an unstructured clinical dataset is a real challenge.

## Applied Intelligent Decision Making in Machine Learning

The Center of Biomedical Computing, named i2b2 (Informatics for Integrating Biology and Others), organized different challenges in natural-language processing (NLP) for extracting useful knowledge from clinical texts. One of the challenges was organized in 2010, in which task focused on extracting clinical concepts such as problems, tests, and treatments from clinical records [1]. Medical concept detection is also called entity recognition. A named entity recognition is an essential part of clinical NLP, because it is a crucial step for extracting knowledge from semi-structured or unstructured texts. Named entity recognition has two phases: boundary detection and entity classification. Previously, many systems have been developed for recognizing clinical entities using a machine learning approach [1], a rule-based approach [2], a dictionary look-up method [3], and a hybrid method [4]. Entity boundary detection is a type of sequence labeling problem, resolved by a BIO model, where B refers to “beginning,” I to “inside,” and O to “outside.” Other models like IOBW and IOBEW, where E refers to “end” and W refers to “word” [5]. Some others are available but not precisely. MetaMap is an information tool for medical texts which also uses chunking method, but its precision and recall are both worse. single word is easy to find and classify, but boundary detection of entities is a vital issue in clinical text. For example, “oxycodone-acetaminophen,” “descending artery,” and “pacemaker.” The work of solving boundary identification of clinical concepts is explored. The proposed system uses Part of Speech (POS) as a feature for training the model. The system is based on a matrix model and performs multi-pattern matching. The matched patterns are converted to their corresponding words and mapped with unified language (UMLS) for entity classification. The rest of this chapter is organized as follows: Section 8.2 illustrates the background of entity recognition, Section 8.3 describes the proposed method and dataset, Section 8.4 presents the experimental results and discussion, and Section 8.6 concludes and indicates some directions for research.

## 8.2 BACKGROUND

Many NLP challenges have organized and focused on medical concept extraction tasks such as i2b2 2010 challenge for clinical notes [8] and the CLEF Shared Task [9], BioNLP/nwak 2004 with the GENIA corpus for identifying entities [10], and the BioCreative challenge [11] for recognizing biological concepts [12]. In previous works of clinical entity identification, different methods have been used such as dictionary-up method, rules-based and machine learning. The dictionary-up method used in [3], in which the authors identified clinical entities using dictionaries from the corpus, performed on i2b2 2010 dataset and obtained the average F score of 48% for the dataset and 50% for the Patients. The rule-based method is also used in [13, 14], in which some rules are created on corpus words and occurrences, and words are then found in the corpus and mapped to a corresponding category and provided 42% F score.
