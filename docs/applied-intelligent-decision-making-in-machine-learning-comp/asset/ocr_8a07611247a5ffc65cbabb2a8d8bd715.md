# 9.2.2 METHODOLOGY

# 9.2.2.1 Decision Trees

Decision trees (DTs) are non-parametric learning techniques applied to regression and classification problems (Das, Naik, & Behera, 2020a; Das et al. 2020). These tree-based models are popular and advantageous in handling smaller datasets than neural network models. Through a repetitive process of splitting, the regression trees can yield a set of rules that can be used for prediction (Das et al., 2020b; Tso & Yao, 2007). The splitting process divides the sample into two or more nodes derived from the most important differentiator among the input variables. In case of classification problems, metrics such as cross-entropy and the Gini index are used to decide strategic splits for DTs (Das et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2005). For regression problems, DTs normally use the mean squared error (MSE) for splitting a node into two or more sub-nodes. That is, on each subset of data, the algorithm computes an MSE value and the tree with the least MSE is selected as a point of split. The resulting outcome comprises decision nodes and leaf nodes (Das, Naik, & Behera, 2020a, 2020b). By contrast with black-box models such as deep learning models, DTs are easy to understand and interpret because their rules can be visualized. These algorithms have been applied and attained success in many fields due to their efficiency and interpretability (Tsai & Chiou, 2009; Wu, 2009).
