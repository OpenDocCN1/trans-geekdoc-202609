The rule-based method is also used in [13, 14], in which some rules are created on corpus words and word occurrences, and words are then found in the corpus and mapped to a corresponding category and provided a 42% F score. Machine-learning-based approaches like SVM (support vector machine) and CRF (conditional random field) have been used for entity boundary identification and entity classification [1, 15], which is based on the beginning, inner, and outside (BOI) model for sequence labeling. An unsupervised approach has also been used to extract entities from biomedical texts [16], in which others have developed a noun phrase chunker followed by a filter based on inverse document frequency. The classification of multiword entities is carried out by using the context of distributional semantics.

A number of systems have been developed for medical entity recognition, such as MetaMap, which is used for concept extraction [17]. It uses UMLS for medical-term identification [18], but its results are worse than other approaches. MetaMap 2013v2 gave a 40% F score and MetaMap 2010 [6] gave a 21.8% F score; various reasons [7] gave an average 15.5% F score. Other systems have used entity boundary identification, like Pereine (F 46.8%), OpenNLP chunker (F 70.0%), and StanfordFer (F 76.8%) [6]. A of previous work has been identified for boundary detection of medical data. In [5, 19], the authors used OpenNLP chunker for name entity recognition (NER) with IO, IOBW models, and for getting correct boundaries, post-processing is performed with boundary adjustments. In [7], the authors compared methods like MetaMapPlus, a rule-based method, and obtained an F score of 52.28%; the CRF method, with an F score of 45.33%; and an SVM method, with an F score of 76.17%.

For pre-processing, several NLP tools were used, like Lingpee, Tree Tagger, OpenNLP, c-TAKES, Stanford parser [20], splitta, SPECIALIST, and Stanford CoreNLP. Evaluation of boundary detection of these tools is carried out in [21], where the authors discussed and identified different errors, such as the detection of splitters like semicolons and colons, these errors were worse from their context. As per their evaluation, except for c-TAKES, other tools performed on clinical notes than on general domain text.
