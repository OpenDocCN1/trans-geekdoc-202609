The main objective was to use hydrostatic-seasonal-temporal and hydrostatic-temperature-temporal models to interpret the nonlinear deformation at the dam wall through the ground measurements together with SAR time-series analysis. In this regard, the authors could find that most of the deformation at the dam can be explained by taking into account only thermal seasonal dilation and hydrostatic pressure. Di Martire et al. (2014) proposed a new DInSAR technique for ground displacement monitoring of an embankment dam called the Conza Dam in Italy. The authors utilized 51 SAR images belonging to ENVIRonmental SATellite-Advanced Synthetic Aperture Radar (ENVISAT-ASAR) to extract the SAR-based ground displacements. Because the ground displacements recorded by a network of conventional ground-based sensors installed in the dam were also available, they could perform a comparative study in terms of statistical analysis to realize the superiority of remote sensing over the traditional contact-based sensing. Gama et al. (2020) exploited SBAS and PSI techniques for relatively short-term deformation monitoring of the Brumadinho Dam located in Brazil before its collapse via 21 SAR images of Sentinel-1 in the descending orbit from 03 March 2018 to 22 January 2019. During such a relatively short-term monitoring program, the direct analysis of the 21 displacement samples regarding the crest, middle, and bottom sectors of the dam indicated serious variability, which was an alarm of an adverse change (collapse). Jänichen et al. (2022) implemented a study on long-term deformation monitoring of the Moehne gravity dam located in Germany and also the radial deformation of the dam. In their gravity, the authors exploited the PSI technique for extracting the deformation samples from SAR images of Sentinel-1 between 2015 and 2020. They concluded that the displacements or deformations of the dam followed a seasonal pattern and the PSI technique could achieve reasonable accuracy for health monitoring of the dam of interest.

Due to the important influences of environmental and operational changes (EOCs) on civil structures, some authors studied these conditions by using SAR images. In this regard, Qin et al. (2017) assessed the impacts of air temperature on displacements of two arch bridges with different materials using several SAR images from COSMO-SkyMed, TerraSAR-X, and Sentinel 1. Haung et al. (2017) utilized 29 SAR images acquired by Sentinel 1 for the health monitoring of a long-term bridge by analyzing longitudinal displacement samples alongside thermal analysis under temperature fluctuations. Ma et al. (2019) analyzed the dynamic behavior of a high-speed railway bridge and investigated the feasibility of measuring settlement and uplift in the unit of millimeter by applying SAR images from COSMO-SkyMed. They also modeled the temperature variability and attempted to separate the thermal expansion and linear deformation. Schlögl et al. (2021) analyzed a relatively large displacement data samples of 719 SAR images from Sentinel-1 in both ascending and descending orbits regarding a side harbor bridge in Austria (i.e., called Seitenhafenbrücke). In their research, the authors performed structural deformation monitoring between October 2016 to October 2019 by using many post-processing tools such as cleaning and smoothing for reducing outliers in measured displacement samples and also seasonal trend decomposition for separating deformation trends from seasonal deformation.
