Based on these motivations, the main objective is to improve the classical SAR-aided SHM in three categories and aspects:

+   - Data augmentation (expansion) for supplying a wealth of information from small displacement points
+   - Removal and mitigation of the EOC effects from augmented displacement data
+   - Feature discrimination by innovative and advanced machine learning methods for SHM, especially pre-collapse prediction

For the sake of convenience, Fig. 1.1 illustrates the general flowchart of the proposed SAR-aided SHM method. On this basis, this book proposes seven hybrid methods in an unsupervised learning fashion along with four hyperparameter optimization algorithms to not only address the key demanding issues in the SAR-aided SHM but also provide a practical strategy for other long-term SHM projects with small data. The first method called NNS-EVT proposes a probabilistic hybrid unsupervised learner based on the concepts of unsupervised nearest neighbor searching (NNS) and extreme value theory (EVT) for SHM under relatively large data and EOC. The second technique called HMC-MD relies on a data augmentation approach based on the Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) sampling and feature discrimination through the tried-and-tested Mahalanobis distance (MD) it its squared form. The other methods are divided into two groups of either offline learning or online learning frameworks. Accordingly, the third method named as HMC-DAE-MD is an improvement of the second technique by adding a data normalization approach based on a DAE for minimizing the EOC influences on the augmented or expanded displacement samples. After the procedure of data normalization, the normalized augmented features are applied to the MD-based novelty detector for feature discrimination. In this technique, a simple but effective iterative algorithm for hyperparameter optimization is designed to choose the neuron numbers of the hidden layers of the DAE with the focus on mitigating the EOC effects. The fourth method namely HMC-UTSL-MD suggests an innovative unsupervised teacher-student learning (UTSL) by merging an undercomplete DAE with seven hidden layers (teacher) and an overcomplete shallow AE (student). Both the teacher and student models need hyperparameters to configure their networks. Hence, a new hyperparameter optimization is proposed to determine their unknown elements. The fifth method named as HMC-DTL-MD develops a deep transfer learning (DTL) strategy by developing consecutive DAEs. Although this method utilizes some prior information (i.e., transferred hyperparameters), it needs to determine the number of consecutive neural networks. For this issue, a new hyperparameter optimization is also designed. All the previous methods rely upon offline learning within a fixed training period. In order to perform real-time SHM, the sixth and seventh methods HMC-ODTL-EMD and SLS-ODTL-EMD consider the HMC and SLS to initially augment small data, subsequently develop an online deep transfer learning (ODTL) approach, and finally propose a damage indicator by computing the Euclidean norm of the MD values. The first two proposed techniques, NNS-EVT and HMC-MD, are validated by simulated numerical displacement data from the I-40 Bridge (Rezaiee-Pajand et al. 2021).
