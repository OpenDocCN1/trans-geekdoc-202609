### Category III.

The underlying goal of an SHM process is to determine the difference between at least two structural state conditions (i.e., an undamaged state versus a current state, which can be undamaged or damaged). Accordingly, one should only find the discrepancy between these conditions via various novelty detectors (Sarmadi and Karamodin 2020; Sarmadi and Yuen 2021, 2022; Daneshvar and Sarmadi 2022; Entezami et al. 2020d; Bigoni and Hesthaven 2020; Turrisi et al. 2022) or statistical distance metrics (Deza and Deza 2013; Entezami 2021; Entezami and Shariatmadar 2018; Entezami et al. 2022d, e). The main objective of these methods is to yield discriminative novelty indices or damage indicators for discriminating the possible damaged state from the undamaged one. Having considered a relatively large displacement data points, this book initially proposes a probabilistic novelty detector with the aid of the NNS, EVT, and quantile modeling. The great innovation in this methodology (NNS-EVT) is to define a new probabilistic novelty index that is a quantile value from some extreme samples. One of the great benefits of this methodology is that one can develop different novelty detectors by using various extreme value distribution models, their quantile values, and two different techniques for extreme value modeling, that is, block maxima and peak-over-threshold. This means that although the NNS-EVT presents a single algorithm for novelty detection, it is possible to use it with different extreme value distributions and frameworks. In relation to small displacement data, although the proposed hybrid learning methods in offline manners (i.e., HMC-MD, HMC-DAE-MD, HMC-UTSL-MD, and HMC-DTL-MD) utilize the tried-and-tested Mahalanobis distance metric, the online hybrid learning methods (i.e., HMC-ODTL-EMD and SLS-ODTL-EMD) propose a new novelty index. The key innovation pertains to the mechanism of deriving a univariate distance value from a multivariate distance metric.

## 1.5 Organization of This Book

The organization of this book can be described as follows. Chapter 2 presents the full discussions of all proposed methods. In Chap. 3, the proposed NNS-EVT and HMC-MD techniques are evaluated and verified by a numerical model of a full-scale bridge. In this numerical study, the displacement data is simulated by a finite element method. To validate the other proposed methods, Chap. 4 presents a real-world civil structure with a realistic partial collapse. For this reason, small displacement data points extracted from limited SAR images are used to verify the proposed hybrid learning methods via real-world data. Eventually, the conclusions of this book along with some suggestions for further research are mentioned in Chap. 5.

## 1.6 Chapter Summary

This chapter has aimed to present the introduction to health monitoring of civil structures by using the technology of remote sensing and SAR images. The main focus has been on the collapse phenomenon. After mentioning the advantages of the SAR-aided SHM by remote sensing, the major challenges and limitations in this strategy have been discussed in detail. For better explanation, the main motivations for conducting this book have been mentioned, which highly aid readers to better realize the aim and scope of this book. In the following, brief descriptions of all proposed methods have been presented. Eventually, the major innovations of this work have been disclosed properly. With these descriptions and literature reviews discussed in this chapter, one can conclude that the classical SAR-aided SHM techniques may not provide reliable frameworks for damage assessment of large-scale civil structures by directly analyzing displacement data. For long-term monitoring programs via SAR images, it is essential to develop robust machine learning techniques for alleviating the negative effects of the EOCs.
