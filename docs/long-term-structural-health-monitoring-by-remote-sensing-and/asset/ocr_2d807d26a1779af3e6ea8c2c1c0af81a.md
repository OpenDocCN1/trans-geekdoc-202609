\(d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{xi}) = (\mathbf{e}_{xi} - \mathbf{v}_e)^T \mathbf{S}_e^{-1} (\mathbf{e}_{xi} - \mathbf{v}_e), \quad \forall i = 1, \dots, n \quad (2.39)\)

where \(\mathbf{e}_{xi}\) is the \(i\)th column vector of \(\mathbf{E}_x\). Using Eqs. (2.38) and (2.39), it can be obtained \(n\) distance quantities, either \(\{d_{MD}(\mathbf{x}_1), \dots, d_{MD}(\mathbf{x}_n)\}\) or \(\{d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{x1}), \dots, d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{xn})\}\) in the baseline phase. Such \(n\) distance quantities are then considered to estimate a threshold limit for feature discrimination. Having considered \(\mathbf{Z}\) (i.e., the augmented test data points) or \(\mathbf{E}_z\) (i.e., the residual matrix from one of the data normalization algorithms), their column vectors are inserted into Eqs. (2.38) and (2.39) by considering \(\mathbf{v}_x\) or \(\mathbf{v}_e\) and \(\mathbf{S}_x\) or \(\mathbf{S}_e\). Hence, in the testing phase, the MD can be rewritten in the following forms:

\(d_{MD}(\mathbf{z}_j) = (\mathbf{z}_j - \mathbf{v}_x)^T \mathbf{S}_x^{-1} (\mathbf{z}_j - \mathbf{v}_x), \quad \forall j = 1, \dots, m \quad (2.40)\)

or

\(d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{zj}) = (\mathbf{e}_{zj} - \mathbf{v}_e)^T \mathbf{S}_e^{-1} (\mathbf{e}_{zj} - \mathbf{v}_e), \quad \forall j = 1, \dots, m. \quad (2.41)\)

In this regard, one can obtain \(m\) distance values, either \(\{d_{MD}(\mathbf{z}_1), \dots, d_{MD}(\mathbf{z}_m)\}\) or \(\{d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{z1}), \dots, d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{zn})\}\) concerning the testing phase. Applying \(n\) and \(m\) distance quantities, the process of feature discrimination is based on comparing each of these quantities by the threshold limit, which is determined by using the novelty indices of the training data points related to the undamaged structural state. In this regard, \(\{d_{MD}(\mathbf{x}_1), \dots, d_{MD}(\mathbf{x}_n)\}\) or \(\{d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{x1}), \dots, d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{xn})\}\) should fall below the threshold confirming the healthy condition of the structure. For the distance values of the test data points, it is possible to reach two general states (i.e., see Fig. 2.4):

+   (1) If \(d_{MD}(\mathbf{z}_j)\) or \(d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{zj})\) is larger than the threshold, this means that the structure suffered from damage and the SHM method triggers the emergence of damage.

+   (2) If \(d_{MD}(\mathbf{z}_j)\) or \(d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{zj})\) is smaller than the threshold, this means that the structure is still healthy and no damage is available.

In the process of feature discrimination, threshold limit can be estimated by two general methodologies based on central limit theory (CLT) and extreme value theory (EVT). The former assumes that the novelty indices of the training data points conform to a Gaussian distribution, in which case standard confidence interval under various significant level (the probability of false alarm) are applicable. The former is based on the selection of some maximum novelty indices that are the extreme values. Using these samples, one can model an extreme value distribution and estimate a quantile value under a small significant level. Generally, the EVT-aided threshold estimator outperforms the CLT-aided technique in case of addressing the issue of EOCs or the presence of low variability conditions. More details can be found in (Sarmadi and Karamodin 2020; Sarmadi et al. 2022; Daneshvar and Sarmadi 2022; Sarmadi 2021; Sarmadi and Entezami 2021; Entezami et al. 2019) regarding the EVT-aided threshold determinators and (Entezami et al. 2019; Entezami 2021) for the CLT-aided threshold determinator.
