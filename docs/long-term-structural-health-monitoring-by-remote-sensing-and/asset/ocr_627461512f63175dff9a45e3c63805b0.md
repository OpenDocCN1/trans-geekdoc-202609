According to the estimated EVIs, the quantile values for the training and test samples are determined under the 5% significance level. The result of damage detection by using the proposed NNS-EVT method is shown in Fig. 3.6, where the first 280 quantiles pertain to the undamaged structural state, the quantiles of the samples 281–350 relate to the validation data, and the remaining quantile values from 351 to 500 belong to the triple damaged cases with individually 50 quantiles. Moreover, the horizontal line refers to the threshold limit estimated by the 280 quantile values of the normal condition in the baseline phase. For this, the recent EVT-based method proposed by Sarmadi and Yuen (2021) is considered to estimate the threshold of interest. This technique lies in the EVT, generalized Pareto distribution modeling, and peak-over-threshold algorithm.

From Fig. 3.6, it is observed that all (absolute) quantile values of the normal condition, considered in either the normal phase or the monitoring phase, are placed below the threshold line accurately implying the undamaged condition. Furthermore, one can discern that all quantile values of the damaged cases are over the alarming threshold accurately indicating the damaged conditions of the bridge. A remarkable result is that the quantile values of the first damaged case are appropriately and accurately discriminated from the normal condition. Accordingly, one can confidently make a decision that this case refers to the damaged state of the bridge. The same conclusions are observable for the second and third cases. On the other hand, the variations in the quantile values of these three cases are distinguishable without any ambiguity about the severity of damage. In the samples 351–500 of Fig. 3.6, one can observe that the magnitudes of the quantile values increase from DC1 to DC3.
