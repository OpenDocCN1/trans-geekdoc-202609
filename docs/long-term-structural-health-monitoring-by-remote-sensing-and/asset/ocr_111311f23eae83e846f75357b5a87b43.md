In contrast, if the false positive error is substantial or its rates to be larger than \(\beta\), the online learning process is terminated and the new data should be transformed into the next stage to use as the test data. In this case, this kind of data contains damaged information of the structure. For simplicity, the procedure of the ODTL algorithm can be summarized as:

+   (1) If the false positive error is smaller than \(\beta\), this means that the premise about the validation data and the healthy state of the structure is correct. Hence, the online learning (the first stage of the ODTL) should be kept on. In addition, the Euclidean norm of the MD values of the validation data is computed to obtain a result at each online step for real-time SHM (see Sect. 2.4.2).

+   (2) If the false positive error is greater than \(\beta\), this means that the premise about the validation data and the healthy state of the structure is incorrect. Accordingly, it needs to stop the online learning and begin the second stage of the ODTL concerning online damage detection. In this regard, the augmented validation data treats as test data. Accordingly, the last updated DAE from the first stage is called to extract the new residual matrix and compute a new novelty index by the EMD. This process continues for any new data.

# 2.4.2 Feature Discrimination for Real-Time SHM

The final step for real-time SHM is feature discrimination by a different novelty detector compared with the previously proposed techniques. Apart from this issue, the other difference relates to how to perform the process of feature discrimination. In the previously proposed techniques, one needs to calculate the novelty indices of all training and test points via the MD-based novelty detector and compare them with a threshold. In a different manner, the proposed online hybrid learning methods exploit the threshold within their algorithms; that is, online learning and online damage detection. Moreover, the shift from the first stage to the second one infers the existence of an abnormal change in the structure. Eventually, both stages give scalar novelty indices by computing the Euclidean norm of the MD amounts of the residual data points in an online way.

Let \(\mathbf{E} = [\mathbf{e}_1, \dots, \mathbf{e}_n] \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times n}\) denotes the matrix of \(n\) residual data points gained by the ODTL algorithm from one structural state (i.e., one image from satellite). To determine a scalar novelty index, it suffices to estimate the mean vector and covariance matrix of \(\mathbf{E}\), which are defined here as \(\mathbf{b_e} \in \mathbb{R}^p\) and \(\mathbf{C_e} \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times p}\), respectively.
