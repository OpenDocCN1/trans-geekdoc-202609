For this issue, many research studies on different civil structures were conducted to analyze the effects of EOCs (Moser and Moaveni 2011; Zhou and Sun 2019; Zonno et al. 2019; Entezami et al. 2019, 2022c, e; Sarmadi and Karamodin 2020; Jang and Smyth 2020; Sousa Tomé et al. 2020; Sarmadi 2021; Sarmadi et al. 2021a; Sarmadi and Yuen 2021; Daneshvar and Sarmadi 2022).

Moster and Moaveni (2011) studied the effects of temperature on modal data of a steel footbridge in order to analyze the structural behavior under temperature fluctuations in steel frame, concrete deck, and the footbridge piers. Their study demonstrated that ambient temperature could change structural responses (natural frequencies) by 4–8%. Zhou and Sun (2019) evaluated the influences of both environmental and operational variability caused by wind loading, structural temperature (i.e., the effect of ambient temperature on structural elements), humidity, sea levels, and traffic on modal frequencies of a long-span sea-crossing bridge. Their investigations indicated that structural temperature and traffic loads were the main factors for significant changes in the vibration responses of the bridge so that the influence of temperature on yearly scale was greater than the traffic. In contrast, the traffic load was dominant on the weekly and daily scales. Zonno et al. (2019) assessed the structural behavior of adobe buildings under long and short-term impacts of temperature and humidity. They concluded that the modal frequencies of the building in the long-term monitoring varied up to 8% due to the environmental variations, especially relative and absolute humidity. In short-term monitoring, the rates of variability was about 2.6–3.8%. Jang and Smyth (Jang and Smyth 2020) performed a numerical study on the effect of temperature distribution on structural responses of a full-scale finite element model of a cable-stayed bridge and modeled two approaches for this study by considering thermal prestress imposed at an element level and temperature-dependent Young's modulus. Their research demonstrated that the thermal prestress at the structural elements was the key reason for significantly variability in the natural frequencies of the bridge. In a similar manner, Teng et al. (2021) analyzed the mechanism of the influence of temperature on the frequency of a steel arch bridge in terms of internal force, geometric size, elastic modulus, and boundary condition. On the daily scale, the temperature distribution on the steel arch and gider behaved differently, while the frequencies of both elements decreased by increasing temperature on the yearly scale. Sarmadi (2021) conducted a comparative study on the effect of various EOC conditions on different structural responses. In that study, he utilized two types of features including time-series-based features obtained from time series modeling and dynamic features from operational modal analysis. He also suggested an effective approach to predict the level of EOCs by using the median absolute deviation. The results of that study indicated that EOCs had more impacts on the dynamic features rather time-series features. Maes et al. (2022) studied the influences of different environmental variability on the structural responses of a retrofitted steel bridge. They observed sudden increases in the natural frequencies of this steel bridge when the temperature reached around 0 °C.
