The other methods contain three main parts including: (i) augmentation of small displacement data by either HMC sampling or slice sampling (SLS), (ii) normalization of augmented data for removing the EOCs, and (iii) detection of damage in terms of unsupervised feature discrimination via the MD metric. These methods are divided into two groups: (i) those are based on offline learning, and (ii) those rely upon online learning frameworks. (3) Third method called HMC-DAE-MD adds a data normalization approach to the proposed HMC-MD technique by exploiting a deep autoencoder (DAE). In this regard, a simple but effective iterative hyperparameter optimization is presented to choose the neuron sizes of the DAE hidden layers with an emphasis on mitigating the EOC effects. (4) The fourth method called HMC-UTSL-MD suggests an innovative unsupervised teacher-student learning (UTSL) algorithm by combining an undercomplete DAE and an overcomplete shallow autoencoder (AE). (5) The fifth method called HMC-DTL-MD presents a deep transfer learning (DTL) strategy by developing consecutive DAEs. All the mentioned methods rely upon offline learning within a fixed training period. (6, 7) In order to perform real-time SHM, the sixth and seventh methods, HMC-ODTL-EMD and SLS-ODTL-EMD, utilize the HMC and SLS to initially augment small data, subsequently develop an online deep transfer learning (ODLT) approach, and finally propose a novelty indicator or damage index by computing the Euclidean norm of Mahalanobis distance (EMD) values. In total, this book proposes several innovative methods including seven hybrid unsupervised learners as well as four hyperparameter optimization algorithms. The first two proposed techniques, NNS-EVT and HMC-MD, are validated by simulated numerical displacement data from a full-scale bridge known as the I-40 Bridge, located in the USA. The remaining techniques are verified by a set of real-world long-term displacement data points of the Tadcaster Bridge in the UK obtained from a small number of images of TerraSar-X. Results show that the proposed hybrid techniques can promisingly tackle the demanding issues in the SAR-aided SHM and these are influential tools for real-world problems.
