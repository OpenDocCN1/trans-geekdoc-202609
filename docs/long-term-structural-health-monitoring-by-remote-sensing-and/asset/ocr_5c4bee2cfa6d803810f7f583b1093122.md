\( H(y) = \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} 1 - \left(1 + \frac{\xi_p}{\delta_p}(y-u)\right)^{-\frac{1}{\xi_p}}, & \xi_p \neq 0 \\ 1 - \exp\left(-\frac{1}{\delta_p}(y-u)\right), & \xi_p = 0 \end{array} \right. \)

where \( u \) is the threshold value for distinguishing the main extreme samples from the other data points. Furthermore, \( \xi_p \) and \( \delta_p \) are the shape and scale parameters of the GPD, respectively. An important note about this probability distribution is that it is modeled by these two parameters, whereas the GEVD requires the three parameters (i.e., the shape, scale, and location). This makes sense that the extreme value modeling via the GPD needs fewer parameters than the GEVD. As the other note, similar to the GEVD, it is feasible to reach the classical extreme value distributions based on the shape parameter of the GPD. Accordingly, if \( \xi_p = 0 \), \( \xi_p > 0 \), and \( \xi_p < 0 \), the GPD is equivalent to the Gumbel, Fréchet, and Weibull models, respectively (Coles et al. 2001).

Maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) is one of the main and important techniques for estimating the parameters of the extreme value distribution models. This technique is based on defining maximum likelihood estimators and maximizing likelihood functions of the mentioned distribution models. For this purpose, logarithmic transformations of likelihood functions are often incorporated. Having considered the \( b \) maximum samples \( X_1, ..., X_b \) from \( b \) blocks, the log-likelihood function of the GEVD under \( \xi_G \neq 0 \) is written as follows (Coles et al. 2001):

\( L_G(\lambda_G, \delta_G, \xi_G) = -b \log(\delta_G) - \left(1 + \frac{1}{\xi_G}\right) \sum_{i=1}^b \log\left(1 + \xi_G \left(\frac{X_i - \lambda_G}{\delta_G}\right)\right) - \sum_{i=1}^b \left(1 + \xi_G \left(\frac{X_i - \lambda_G}{\delta_G}\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{\xi_G}} \)

provided that

\( 1 + \xi_G \left(\frac{X_i - \lambda_G}{\delta_G}\right) > 0 \)

where \( i = 1, ..., b \). On the other hand, when \( \xi_G = 0 \), the log-likelihood function is formulated as follows (Coles et al. 2001):

\( L_G(\lambda_G, \delta_G) = -b \log(\delta_G) - \sum_{i=1}^b \log\left(\frac{X_i - \lambda_G}{\delta_G}\right) - \sum_{i=1}^b \exp\left(-\left(\frac{X_i - \lambda_G}{\delta_G}\right)\right) \)
