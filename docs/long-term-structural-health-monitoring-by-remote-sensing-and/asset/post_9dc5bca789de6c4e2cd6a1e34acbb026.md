## Chapter 2
Advanced MIL Methods: Bridging SAR Images and Structural Health Monitoring

**Abstract** The classical SAR-aided SHM directly analyzes displacement data obtained from SAR images. Although the extraction of displacement responses from SAR images via various interferometric techniques is of paramount importance to remote sensing-based SHM, the direct use and analysis of SAR-based displacement responses for monitoring and damage assessment of complex and critical civil structures is not a reliable and effective approach, especially in long-term monitoring with various uncertainties and variability in measured data. On this basis, this chapter proposes various machine learning methods in terms of unsupervised learning for SHM under environmental and operational conditions and different types of structural displacement responses retrieved from SAR images in terms of their sizes. In case of a large set of displacement data, an innovative probabilistic unsupervised learning method is proposed. For a small set of displacement data, this chapter proposes two unsupervised hybrid learning methods and mainly consists of three parts of data augmentation for expanding the size of small displacement responses, data normalization for removing the effects of environmental and operational conditions, and anomaly detection.

**Keywords** Machine learning · Unsupervised learning · Hybrid learning · Online learning · Data normalization · Markov Chain Monte Carlo · Anomaly detection

### 2.1 Introduction

Most of the SHM programs via remote sensing are based on direct analyses of structural displacements retrieved from SAR images. Although this strategy is simple and efficient, it may not be reliable and effective for damage assessment of important and complex civil structures under EOCs (Behkamal et al. 2023a, b). To address the major limitations and shortcomings of this strategy, this book intends to propose more rigorous methods for health monitoring of civil structures, with the focus on bridges, by the same SAR images and displacement data used in the classical SAR-aided SHM technique. On this basis, depending upon the size of displacement data, this book proposes two types of innovative methods for SHM under relatively large data and small data. In this regard, the proposed NNS-EVT is developed to assess the health of civil structures by considering a relatively large displacement set. Although the preparation of a large number of SAR images may encounter the Big Data problem and storage limitations, some research studies exploited many images for SHM tasks (Farneti et al. 2022; Bayik et al. 2021; Militello et al. 2016). The great advantage of the proposed probabilistic machine learning method is to simultaneously deal with the challenges of variability in measured (displacement/deformation) data and reliable feature discrimination by a new novelty index or damage indicator. Therefore, Sect. 2.2 describes this method. One of the great advantages of the proposed probabilistic method is its capacity for developing various anomaly detectors based on different extreme value distributions. However, in this book, the proposed methodology is based on the semi-parametric concept of the EVT and the quantile modeling of the semi-parametric extreme value distribution.

For the SAR-aided SHM applications with a small number of images, leading to few displacement data, this book mainly concentrates on this challenge by proposing various offline and online hybrid learning methods; that is: (i) HMC-MD, (ii) HMC-DAE-MD, (iii) HMC-UTSL-MD, (iv) HMC-DTL-MD, (v) HMC-ODLT-EMD, and (vi) SLS-ODLT-EMD. Accordingly, Sects. 2.3 and 2.4 fully describe the offline and online hybrid learning methods, respectively, along with some hyperparameter optimization algorithms needed for learning these techniques.

### 2.2 Tackling Big Data Challenges: A Probabilistic Unsupervised Learning Method

The proposed probabilistic machine learning method called NNS-EVT aims to perform a long-term SHM process when the size of data is relatively large. The main elements of this method consist of a feature selection approach based on NNS and a novelty detector under the extreme value machine (EVM) and semi-parametric EVT. The feature selection approach is based on the nearest neighbor search for choosing the most appropriate and relevant samples, called representative features. For this reason, this step can be considered as a dimensionality reduction strategy and also a data normalization method. The second element of the proposed machine learning method is to propose a novelty detector for distinguishing normal samples, regarding the undamaged condition of the structure, from abnormal ones, associated with the possibly damaged state. In this method, a novel probabilistic method is proposed by considering the concepts of the EVT, semi-parametric extreme value distribution, and quantile modeling.

#### 2.2.1 Extreme Value Theory and Its Applications

In statistics, the EVT offers a well-known and useful probabilistic technique for studying on unusual (rare) events and samples rather than usual ones. In other words, this technique intends to analyze and model extreme data samples in terms of maximum and minimum samples and rare events (Coles et al. 2001; Kotz and Nadarajah 2000; Castillo 1988). In contrast to the central limit theory (CLT), which concentrates on the mean of randomly sampling data or the center of probability distributions, the fundamental principle of the EVT lies in the extreme samples and the tails of probability distributions. This makes sense that the most relevant statistics for modeling the right and left tails of a probability distribution include the maximum and minimum samples. The great advantage of the EVT is to only focus on a few assumptions about the distribution of data rather than the modeling of the whole data distribution.

To model the extreme values, there are generally two techniques of block maxima (BM) under Fisher-Tippet theorem and peak-over-threshold (POT) under Balkema-de Haan-Pickands theorem (Résillat et al. 2018; Sarmadi and Yuen 2022; Sarmadi et al. 2023a). The first technique is based on dividing randomly sampling data into some blocks (segments) and choose the maximum or minimum sample of each block as the extreme value (Sarmadi and Yuen 2022; Sarmadi and Karamodin 2020). The second technique sets a threshold for selecting extreme (only maximum) samples (Sarmadi et al. 2023a, 2022). On this basis, the maximum data points over the threshold of interest are chosen as the main extreme values for the extreme value modeling. Given n random samples X1, ..., Xn, Fig. 2.1 illustrates the graphical representations of the BM and POT techniques, where the BM divides the sampling data into b blocks and the POT sets a threshold for the extreme sample selection. This means that both techniques require hyperparameters (i.e., the number of block b and the threshold level) that should be determined properly. Once the extreme values have been selected, one should consider an EVT distribution for modeling. Different applications of the extreme value theory to SHM estimation can be found in (Sarmadi and Yuen 2022; Sarmadi et al. 2023a) for anomaly detection and (Sarmadi and Karamodin 2020; Sarmadi et al. 2022; Sarmadi and Yuen 2021; Daneshvar and Sarmadi 2022; Entezami and Shariatmadar 2018) for threshold estimation.

Because this research intends to take advantage of the EVT for feature discrimination, the term “extreme” refers to the maximum value. This is due to the fact that the occurrence of any abnormal change in a civil structure (damage) increases the deviation of an anomaly detector. In the context of SHM, the process of anomaly detection is based on finding maximum novelty indices. Hence, all extreme value models are based on the maximum values. Given the independent random data points (or variables) X1, ..., Xn, a general extreme value distribution sets out to model the statistical behavior of the maximum data in the following form:

```
$$M_n = \max(X_1, ..., X_n)$$
```
