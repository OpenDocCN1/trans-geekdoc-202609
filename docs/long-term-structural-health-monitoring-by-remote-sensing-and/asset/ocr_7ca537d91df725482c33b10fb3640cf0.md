# Chapter 4
From Theory to Reality: Advanced SHM Methods to the Tadcaster Bridge

**Abstract** Experimental validation of SHM methods is a critical process in evaluating the accuracy and reliability of such methods for damage assessment, especially in long-term monitoring programs. In this chapter, real-world applications of the proposed unsupervised learning methods (i.e., HMC-DAE-MD, HMC-UTSL-MD, HMC-DTL-MD, HMC-ODTL-EMD and SLS-ODTL-EMD) developed for coping with the limitation of small data are investigated by using a small set of displacement responses of a masonry bridge called the Tadcaster Bridge located in Tadcaster, UK. Because this bridge experienced a partial collapse, it is an appropriate case study for verifying the proposed methods. This chapter also indicates how a data augmentation process can help to better observe the EOCs in augmented displacement responses.

**Keywords** Pre-collapse detection • Masonry bridge • Small data • Data augmentation • Machine learning • Unsupervised learning

# 4.1 The Tadcaster Bridge: A Masonry Bridge

The real-world application of the other proposed methods is based on the valuable small displacement dataset of the Tadcaster Bridge, Tadcaster, UK (Selvakumaran et al. 2018). For some important reasons, we believe that this dataset provides the best condition for validating the proposed methods. The major motives for considering this structure are: (i) it brings a realistic condition of a partial collapse event in a real structure, (ii) the sample displacement data comes from a continuous long-term monitoring plan, (iii) similar to the installation of contact-based sensors, appropriate target points were incorporated and distributed to extract the displacement points.
