Let \(\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times 1}\) denotes a set of displacement points of \(p\) target points from a SAR image. Note that each \(\mathbf{d}\) is equivalent to \(\mathbf{x}\). On this basis, the underlying goal of the HMC sampler is to forecast the vector \(\mathbf{d}\) under \(C\) chains and \(N\) sample numbers. This means that the HMC technique makes attempt to generate \(N\) points of the vector \(\mathbf{d}\) from a target distribution (i.e., Gaussian distribution in this research) in an effort to make a set of augmented displacement points in a new matrix \(\mathbf{D} \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times N}\), under \(C\) chains in an iterative algorithm. In other words, for the \(j\)th chain and \(k\)th sample, where \(j = 1, ..., C\) and \(k = 1, ..., N-1\), one can derive an augmented matrix \(\mathbf{D}_{k+1}^{(j)}\). Using a hypothesis based on the Gelman-Rubin statistic (Gelman and Rubin 1992), if the statistic converges on the hypothesis criterion, one can fix the simulated parameter at the \((k + 1)\)th iteration; unless, one should choose the simulated parameter of the previous at the \(k\)th iteration. After determining all \(C\) augmented matrices, their average is used as the final set of the augmented displacement points. Eventually, the matrix of the augmented displacement points (**D**) is divided into the training set (matrix) \(\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times n}\) and test set (matrix) \(\mathbf{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times m}\), where \(n < N\) and \(m < N\).

# 2.3.1.2 Slice Sampling

The SLS is the other algorithm of Markov chain Monte Carlo for generating random data points on the basis of their previous states. The fundamental principle of the SLS lies in the idea that a random variable can be sampled uniformly from the area under the graph of a uniform probability distribution and then only observe the horizontal coordinates of the samples. For this purpose, one needs to construct a Markov chain that can meet the uniform distribution of interest via uniformly sampling from the vertical interval represented by the density at the current sample and from the union of intervals that comprises the horizontal slice.
