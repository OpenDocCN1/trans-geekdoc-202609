
Regarding the other types of civil structures, Zhu et al. (2018) explored slow ground surface movements regarding building-type structures by extracting and interpreting information from InSAR images. In their research, the authors intended to directly analyze the persistent scatterer deformations in an effort to find anomalies for detecting potential instability risks affecting buildings and infrastructures. Macchiarulo et al. (2021) and Giardina et al. (2019) performed damage assessment analyses for identifying early signs of tunnelling-induced damage to adjacent buildings with different structures, foundations, and position relative to the tunnels by using SAR images from COSMO-SkyMed between 2011 and 2015. They extracted the vertical displacement/deformation samples from multi-temporal InSAR techniques and analyzed them in order to detect the settlement-type damage patterns. Cigna et al. (2014) exploited the InSAR methodology based on persistent scatterer interferometry (PSI) and the technology of remote sensing via SAR images of COSMO-SkyMed for deformation monitoring of historical buildings in the Historic Centre of Rome, Italy. Di Carlo et al. (2021) merged multi-temporal DInSAR products and historical survey data for health monitoring of building structures in Italy by applying SAR images of COSMO-SkyMed between 2011 and 2019. In their work, the authors aimed to follow three main tasks including:

- the displacement extraction from SAR images collected over the city of Rome during 2011–2019
- accurate three-dimensional geometrical modeling of the external volume of the buildings under monitoring and consistent positioning of the identified and investigated persistent scatterers
- analysis and interpretation of the displacement samples of some buildings for possible damage detection

They demonstrated that the analysis of displacements and mean deformation velocity values enables them to identify possible critical conditions for buildings to be monitored.

In relation to dam structures, Bayık et al. (2021) conducted long-term SAR-aided SHM on the Atatürk Dam in Turkey by using different images from ERS, ENVISAT, and Sentinel-1. In their long-term monitoring study, the authors utilized 24 and 33 images of ERS and ENVISAT in the descending orbit between 1992–1999 and 2003–2010, 114 and 111 SAR images in both ascending and descending orbits between 2016–2019. As Sentinel-1 could provide images in both orbits, the main objective of their research was to estimate the surface displacements of the dam in the vertical and east–west directions by utilizing both line-of-sight displacements. Moreover, they studied the relationship between the measured displacements and water levels. As their results indicated, there was no considerable relation between these parameters and most of the variations in displacements occurred at the dam crest. Mura et al. (2018) exploited the remote sensing technology and DInSAR techniques for risk assessment and vulnerability analysis of some tailings dams in Brazil after a catastrophic collapse in a tailings dam in the vicinity of mining structures on November 2015 causing spilling around 32.6 million cubic meters of mining waste. In their research, the authors merged small baseline subset (SBAS) and persistent scatterer interferometry (PSI) to detect linear and nonlinear ground displacements in these structures. For this procedure, they used 48 SAR images from TerraSAR-X between 11 November 2015 to 15 May 2017. Miliello et al. (2016) carried out continuous long-term monitoring on a dam (i.e., the Pertusillo Dam in Italy) by 198 SAR images from

The main objective was to use hydrostatic-seasonal-temporal and hydrostatic-temperature-temporal models to interpret the nonlinear deformation at the dam wall through the ground measurements together with SAR time-series analysis. In this regard, the authors could find that most of the deformation at the dam can be explained by taking into account only thermal seasonal dilation and hydrostatic pressure. Di Martire et al. (2014) proposed a new DInSAR technique for ground displacement monitoring of an embankment dam called the Conza Dam in Italy. The authors utilized 51 SAR images belonging to Environmental Satellite-Advanced Synthetic Aperture Radar (ENVISAT-ASAR) to extract the SAR-based ground displacements. Because the ground displacements recorded by a network of conventional ground-based sensors installed in the dam were also available, they could perform a comparative study in terms of statistical analysis to realize the superiority of remote sensing over the traditional contact-based sensing. Gama et al. (2020) exploited SBAS and PSI techniques for relatively short-term deformation monitoring of the Brumadinho Dam located in Brazil before its collapse via 21 SAR images of Sentinel-1 in the descending orbit from 03 March 2018 to 22 January 2019. During such a relatively short-term monitoring program, the direct analysis of the 21 displacement samples regarding the crest, middle, and bottom sectors of the dam indicated serious variability, which was an alarm of an adverse change (collapse). Jänichen et al. (2022) implemented a study on long-term deformation monitoring of the Moehne gravity dam located in Germany and also the radial deformation of the dam. In their study, the authors exploited the PSI technique for extracting the deformation samples from SAR images of Sentinel-1 between 2015 and 2020. They concluded that the displacements or deformations of the dam followed a seasonal pattern and the PSI technique could achieve reasonable accuracy for health monitoring of the dam of interest.

Due to the important influences of environmental and operational changes (EOCs) on civil structures, some authors studied these conditions by using SAR images. In this regard, Qin et al. (2017) assessed the impacts of air temperature on displacements of two arch bridges with different materials using several SAR images from COSMO-SkyMed, TerraSAR-X, and Sentinel-1. Huang et al. (2017) utilized 29 SAR images acquired by Sentinel-1 for the health monitoring of a long-term bridge by analyzing longitudinal displacement samples alongside thermal analysis under temperature fluctuations. Ma et al. (2019) analyzed the dynamic behavior of a high-speed railway bridge and investigated the feasibility of measuring settlement and uplift in the unit of millimeter by applying SAR images from COSMO-SkyMed. They also modeled the temperature variability and attempted to separate the thermal expansion and linear deformation. Schlögl et al. (2021) analyzed a relatively large displacement data samples of 719 SAR images from Sentinel-1 in both ascending and descending orbits regarding a side harbor bridge in Austria (i.e., called Seitenhafenbrücke). In their research, the authors performed structural deformation monitoring between October 2016 to October 2019 by using many post-processing tools such as cleaning and smoothing for reducing outliers in measured displacement samples and also seasonal trend decomposition for separating deformation trends from seasonal deformation.

Despite many successful applications of SAR-aided SHM on different civil structures, particularly bridges, this technology has its limitations and challenges (Behkamal et al. 2023a, b). One of these challenges is the impossibility of providing rich and adequate information (measurements) the same as vibration-based SHM (Entezami et al. 2022a, b). To put it another way, any vibration-based SHM may contain many contact- and non-contact vision-based sensors to measure vibration responses (e.g., acceleration time histories or displacements from images or videos) in a long-term monitoring program. Under such circumstances, one can obtain a huge volume of useful information and responses regarding the behavior of the structure under study. In contrast, this strategy is not feasible in SAR-aided SHM. For example, Sarmadi et al. (2021b) exploited 5956 natural frequencies of a three-span bridge to detect damage in a one-year monitoring scheme. Entezami et al. (2020a) applied the total of 1,010,880,000 time series data concerning a cable-style bridge for only 12 days. Because the preparation of such large data is not often possible in SAR-aided SHM, the major challenge is the problem of small data. Simply speaking, even though one can implement a long-term monitoring program for SHM of civil structures via remote sensing, a limited number of SAR images are usually applied to find out displacement data points. On the other hand, extremely large sizes of SAR images due to covering wide areas in the unit of km, and memory space restrictions for collecting many images are other limitations of this process. The other key challenge of SAR-aided SHM pertains to directly analyzing displacements extracted from satellite images regarding bridges (Selvakumaran et al. 2018; Miliello et al. 2019, 2016; Qin et al. 2017, 2018), buildings (Zhu et al. 2018; Macchiarulo et al. 2021; Giardina et al. 2019; Cigna et al. 2014; Di Carlo et al. 2021), and dams (Bayik et al. 2021; Mura et al. 2018; Miliello et al. 2016; Di Martire et al. 2014; Gama et al. 2020; Jänichen et al. 2022). An important note is that the direct utilization of measured data is not a robust way for SHM. Undoubtedly, apart from the size and complexity of civil structures compared with other structural systems, those are expensive and critical. This means that any erroneous decision on SHM by directly analyzing measured data may cause irreparable events such as human deaths and injuries and economic losses. Therefore, the direct application of small data is not a reliable and robust technique for SHM of prominent civil structures, especially in long-term monitoring.

Apart from this limitation, most of the unsupervised learning methods are susceptible to erroneous results due to severe variability in data and damage-sensitive features regardless of the type of sensing and features. In reality, ambient and environmental changes are inevitable in every civil structure built in outdoor environments. Daily and seasonal temperature fluctuations, wind speed and direction changes, humidity and moisture variations, and excitation amplitude alternations are prevalent environmental variability conditions. Moreover, operational variations in outdoor and indoor environments significantly affect civil structures. Dead and live load alterations, excessive loading, sudden accidents and collisions, explosions, human and vehicle traffic are some common operational variability conditions (Farrar and Worden 2013: Chapter 12; Wang et al. 2022). Even though the EOCs are not adverse phenomena like structural damage, those can alter the mass, stiffness, and damping properties of a structural system, as well as its materials, geometry, connectivity, and responses.
