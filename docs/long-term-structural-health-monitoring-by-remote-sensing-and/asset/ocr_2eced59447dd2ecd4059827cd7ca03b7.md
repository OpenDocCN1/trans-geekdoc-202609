Regarding the other types of civil structures, Zhu et al. (2018) explored slow ground surface movements regarding building-type structures by extracting and interpreting information from InSAR images. In their research, the authors intended to directly analyze the persistent scatterer deformations in an effort to find anomalies for detecting potential instability risks affecting buildings and infrastructures. Macchiarulo et al. (2021) and Giardina et al. (2019) performed damage assessment analyses for identifying early signs of tunnelling-induced damage to adjacent buildings with different structures, foundations, and position relative to the tunnels by using SAR images from COSMO-SkyMed between 2011 and 2015. They extracted the vertical displacement/deformation samples from multi-temporal InSAR techniques and analyzed them in order to detect the settlement-type damage patterns. Cigna et al. (2014) exploited the InSAR methodology based on persistent scatterer interferometry (PSI) and the technology of remote sensing via SAR images of COSMO-SkyMed for deformation monitoring of historical buildings in the Historic Centre of Rome, Italy. Di Carlo et al. (2021) merged multi-temporal DInSAR products and historical survey data for health monitoring of building structures in Italy by applying SAR images of COSMO-SkyMed between 2011 and 2019. In their work, the authors aimed to follow three main tasks including: (i) the displacement extraction from SAR images collected over the city of Rome during 2011–2019, (ii) accurate three-dimensional geometrical modeling of the external volume of the buildings under monitoring and consistent positioning of the identified and investigated persistent scatterers, and (iii) analysis and interpretation of the displacement samples of some buildings for possible damage detection. They demonstrated that the analysis of displacements and mean deformation velocity values enables them to identify possible critical conditions for buildings to be monitored.

In relation to dam structures, Bayık et al. (2021) conducted long-term SAR-aided SHM on the Atatürk Dam in Turkey by using different images from ERS, ENVISAT, and Sentinel-1. In their long-term monitoring study, the authors utilized 24 and 33 images of ERS and ENVISAT in the descending orbit between 1992–1999 and 2003–2010, 114 and 111 SAR images in both ascending and descending orbits between 2016–2019. As Sentinel-1 could provide images in both orbits, the main objective of their research was to estimate the surface displacements of the dam in the vertical and east–west directions by utilizing both line-of-sight displacements. Moreover, they studied the relationship between the measured displacements and water levels. As their results indicated, there was not considerable relation between these parameters and most of the variations in displacements occurred at the dam crest. Mura et al. (2018) exploited the remote sensing technology and DInSAR techniques for risk assessment and vulnerability analysis of some tailings dams in Brazil after a catastrophic collapse in a tailings dam in the vicinity of mining structures on November 2015 causing spilling around 32.6 million cubic meters of mining waste.
