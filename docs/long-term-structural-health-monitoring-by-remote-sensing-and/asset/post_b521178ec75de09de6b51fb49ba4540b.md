- Data augmentation (expansion) for supplying a wealth of information from small displacement points
- Removal and mitigation of the EOC effects from augmented displacement data
- Feature discrimination by innovative and advanced machine learning methods for SHM, especially pre-collapse prediction

On this basis, this book proposes seven hybrid methods in an unsupervised learning fashion along with four hyperparameter optimization algorithms to not only address the key demanding issues in the SAR-aided SHM but also provide a practical strategy for other long-term SHM projects with small data. The first method called NNS-EVT proposes a probabilistic hybrid unsupervised learner based on the concepts of unsupervised nearest neighbor searching (NNS) and extreme value theory (EVT) for SHM under relatively large data and EOC. The second technique called HMC-MD relies on a data augmentation approach based on the Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) sampling and feature discrimination through the tried-and-tested Mahalanobis distance (MD) in its squared form. The other methods are divided into two groups of either offline learning or online learning frameworks. Accordingly, the third method named as HMC-DAE-MD is an improvement of the second technique by adding a data normalization approach based on a DAE for minimizing the EOC influences on the augmented or expanded displacement samples. After the procedure of data normalization, the normalized augmented features are applied to the MD-based novelty detector for feature discrimination. In this technique, a simple but effective iterative algorithm for hyperparameter optimization is designed to choose the neuron numbers of the hidden layers of the DAE with the focus on mitigating the EOC effects. The fourth method namely HMC-UTSL-MD suggests an innovative unsupervised teacher-student learning (UTSL) by merging an undercomplete DAE with seven hidden layers (teacher) and an overcomplete shallow AE (student). Both the teacher and student models need hyperparameters to configure their networks. Hence, a new hyperparameter optimization is proposed to determine their unknown elements. The fifth method named as HMC-DTL-MD develops a deep transfer learning (DTL) strategy by developing consecutive DAEs. Although this method utilizes some prior information (i.e., transferred hyperparameters), it needs to determine the number of consecutive neural networks. For this issue, a new hyperparameter optimization is also designed. All the previous methods rely upon offline learning within a fixed training period. In order to perform real-time SHM, the sixth and seventh methods HMC-ODTL-EMD and SLS-ODTL-EMD consider the HMC and SLS to initially augment small data, subsequently develop an online deep transfer learning (ODTL) approach, and finally propose a damage indicator by computing the Euclidean norm of the MD values. The first two proposed techniques, NNS-EVT and HMC-MD, are validated by simulated numerical displacement data from the I-40 Bridge (Rezaiee-Pajand et al. 2021). ## 1.4 Novel Approaches and Contributions

Despite valuable studies on SHM of various civil structures by using the remote sensing technology and SAR images, this book mainly intends to address the major limitations and shortcomings of the classical SAR-aided SHM and particularly concentrates on long-term monitoring by small displacement data. In this regard, various innovative methods in terms of machine learning are proposed as discussed above. Here, it is attempted to clarify the major innovations of these methods in an effort to facilitate readers to better realize the significance and novelty of this book. As the implementation of the classical SAR-aided SHM strategy in a long-term monitoring manner consists of three major challenges of small displacement data, the negative effects of EOCs, and discriminative damage or novelty2 indices, the underlying innovation of this book is to propose hybrid learning methods in terms of unsupervised learning. Based on the three major challenges, the innovations of these methods can be divided into three categories:

- Category I—Innovations for dealing with the challenge of small data
- Category II—Innovations for dealing with the challenge of EOCs

- Category III—Innovations for dealing with the challenge of novelty indices

### Category I

Although HMC and SLS are well-known Markov Chain Monte Carlo algorithms for the generation of random samples, the first parts of the proposed hybrid learning methods exploit these algorithms for expanding the small displacement data. The important innovation of data augmentation via HMC and SLS is that this process not only expands the small set of displacements but also reveals the variability in extracted displacement data samples better than the original data. In this case, it is possible to better analyze the level of variability and decide whether data normalization is needed or it can be ignored.

### Category II

Without any doubt, one can declare that the great innovations of the proposed hybrid learning method are related to various novel data normalization algorithms. The central core of data normalization is based on deep and shallow AEs, especially DAEs. As the first innovation, the proposed hybrid learning technique called HMC-MD is developed by adding an undercomplete DAE with three hidden layers for data normalization. The key novel part of this process is to propose a hyperparameter optimization for finding the optimum neuron numbers of the three hidden layers of the undercomplete DAE with the focus on alleviating the negative effects of the EOCs. Although different hyperparameter optimization algorithms such as cross-validation are applicable, this book suggests a new algorithm based on an engineering justification; that is, the removal or reduction of the EOC influences. On this basis, the hyperparameters obtained from this algorithm allow the undercomplete DAE to exclusively address the challenge related to the EOCs. Concentrating on this challenge, this book proposes the other data normalization method based on the concept of teacher-student learning. Even though this concept is well-known, we develop two novel ANNs for creating teacher and student models in an unsupervised learning fashion. The key innovative part of the second data normalization method is related to proposing an undercomplete DAE with seven hidden layers as the teacher model and an overcomplete shallow AE as the student model. In other words, the proposed data normalization method creates a novel hybrid ANN strategy. The other innovation of this method is to propose a new hyperparameter optimization for selecting the neuron numbers of the seven hidden layers of the DAE and the encoding layer of the shallow AE. In the following, the other data normalization method is proposed by taking advantage of the idea of transfer learning (Yang et al. 2020; Weiss et al. 2016). Unlike the other well-known transfer learning algorithms, which mainly rely upon supervised deep neural networks in the configuration of convolutional neural networks (Dhillon and Verma 2020), this book suggests a novel deep transfer learning algorithm that is based on an individual ANN in terms of an undercomplete three-hidden-layer DAE. The key novel part of the third data normalization method is to design an iterative algorithm using consecutive DAEs. For this algorithm, the other hyperparameter optimization is suggested to find the optimum number of consecutive DAEs without any effort for selecting the neuron numbers. In order to take advantage of the capacity of online learning for real-time SHM, this book provides the other innovation in data normalization.
