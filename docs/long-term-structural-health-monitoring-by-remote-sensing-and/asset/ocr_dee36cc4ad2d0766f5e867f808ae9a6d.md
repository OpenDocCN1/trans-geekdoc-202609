The main premise lies in the fact that if adequate environmental, operational, and structural data is measurable, this enables civil engineers to model the most appropriate machine learning model in a fixed training period, in which case it is possible to cover all possible EOCs. On the other hand, it is supposed that no damage emerges in the training stage. However, this strategy is confronted with two major obstacles. Firstly, there is no guarantee that the same variability pattern caused by the EOCs occurs in new measurements, even the structure is undamaged. In other words, it is probable that newly unpredictable EOCs will occur out of the learning period, which do not capture and consider by the trained model. In this case, the false alarm is a probable consequence. Secondly, it is not logical to assume that no damage or abnormal change occurred in the structure during the training period.

Accordingly, online/incremental learning provides a more realistic and reliable condition for real-time SHM of civil structures. Here, the term real-time means implementing the data analysis, updating the trained model, and making a decision in an online fashion as the new measurement becomes available. In the online learning, the training procedure is carried out in an incremental manner by continually utilizing new data and updating the trained model. In most cases, an online learning strategy begins by applying an initial small set of measured data to develop an initial model. Once the new data is measured, it feeds into the initially trained model for updating or making decision. On this basis, in the problem of SHM, it is necessary to define a criterion or standard for terminating the updating step and starting a new step for decision-making. If the standard makes sure of a healthy or undamaged structural state, it needs to continue the learning procedure by updating the developed machine learning model; unless, this procedure should be ended by choosing the new measurement as test data. In other words, it is not necessary to update the model. Hence, any new data is only fed into the last updated model to make a decision.

In relation to the applications of online learning to SHM, one can mention the study by Krishnan et al. (2018) who proposed an online damage detection method with the aids of the recursive PCA and a time-varying linear autoregressive (AR) model. In their work, the main components of the recursive PCA including the eigenvector (principal component) and eigenvalue matrix were updated by receiving new data (i.e., acceleration time histories from accelerometers). The time-varying AR model was then fitted to the principal components of the recursive PCA in order to track the changes in the AR coefficients as an indicator for damage detection. Yu (2017) presented an online learning fault detection in mechanical machines via adaptive hidden Markov model along with a healthy index that measured the similarity between two density distributions regarding the baseline and current system conditions. Jin et al. (2015) proposed an output-only data normalization technique based on a novel adaptive PCA in an online manner. The main objective was to capture the intrinsic structural behavior by continuously updating a reference (PCA) model before damage detection.
