# Chapter 5 Conclusions and Prospects for Structural Health Monitoring

**Abstract** In this book, it has been attempted to address some major challenging issues related to long-term health monitoring of civil structures based on the technology of remote sensing and machine learning. For this reason, various unsupervised learning methods have been proposed to detect any abnormal conditions in civil structures under unknown EOCs using small and large sets of displacement responses obtained from SAR images. This chapter aims to mention the main conclusions of the proposed methods in both numerical and experimental validation stages. Due to the importance of SHM and the benefits of remote sensing, some suggestions are given to further evaluate in future research activities.

**Keywords** Long-term health monitoring • Conclusions • Environmental and operational changes • Remote sensing • Machine learning

# 5.1 Introduction

The main motivation for conducting this research is to improve the classical SAR-aided SHM and address the major limitations of this process. On this basis, new hybrid methods in an unsupervised learning fashion were developed to alarm the emergence of any abnormal change and damage, especially collapse, under unknown and unpredictable EOCs. In case of apply relatively large displacement data, this research proposed a hybrid probabilistic unsupervised learning method based on the NNS and EVT. When a small number of displacement samples is available, the proposed hybrid learning methods entailed data augmentation via either HMC or SLS techniques, different novel DAE-based data normalization approaches, and feature discrimination by the MD and EMD. In the second part of the hybrid learning methods for small data, this research exploited various AE in deep and shallow configurations. In the HMC-DAE-MD method, a direct application of the three-hidden-layer DAE was considered to mitigate the EOC influences from the augmented data.
