Therefore, the numerical model of this part is incorporated here to verify the proposed NNS-EVT and HMC-MD methods.

The part of interest comprised three spans of the total length of 129.5 m including the middle span of the length of 49.7 m and two side spans of the lengths of 39.9 m. The finite element model of this part of the I-40 Bridge was built in MATLAB by utilizing the shell and 3D beam elements (Rao 2011). Figure 3.2 displays the schematic forms and element numbers of the bridge regarding the deck, girder webs and the piers. For the concrete deck and girders, the numerical modeling is based on 144 and 48 shell elements with four nodes in terms of the membrane and rectangular plate elements. More precisely, as Fig. 3.2a indicates, the elements 1–144 are concerned with the deck shell components. Additionally, in Fig. 3.2b and c, the elements of the girder webs in the north and south sides are labeled by 145–168 and 169–192. The 3D beam elements are considered to build the top and bottom piers, which are labeled by the numbers 193–204. Steel and concrete were the major materials for the numerical modeling of the I-40 Bridge. To simulate damage, it is assumed that the stiffness values of Elements 47, 54, 152, 153, and 194 in the vicinity of the second pier are uniformly reduced by 2%, 10%, and 15% leading to total reductions in the structural stiffness equal to 10%, 50%, and 75%, respectively (i.e., the total stiffness reduction 10% is determined by considering 5 x 2%). This damage pattern is a realistic simulation of the detachment between the deck and the second pier causing increases in displacements at those areas. Based on Fig. 3.2, the target point “F” is the location of damage.

# 3.3 Simulating Structural Displacements

To simulate displacements of the normal and damaged conditions, one assumes that different static loads are vertically applied to all nodes of the deck, see Fig. 3.2a, at different times. Based on the simple concept of structural analysis and finite element method, the relationship between the applied load $f$, structural stiffness $K$, and displacement $\delta$ is written in the following equation:

```
$f_i = K \delta_i$ (3.1)
```

where $i = 1,...,T$ denotes the number of measurements. In the field of the SAR-aided SHM, this value is equivalent to the number of SAR images.
