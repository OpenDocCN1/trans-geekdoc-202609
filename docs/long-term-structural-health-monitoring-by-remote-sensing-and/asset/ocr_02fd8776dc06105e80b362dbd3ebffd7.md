# 2.3 Tackling Small Data Challenges: Proposed Hybrid Learning Methods

Apart from the HMC-MD technique, the other proposed hybrid learning methods entail three key steps of the data augmentation by a Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithm (i.e., HMC or SLS), (ii) data normalization by ANN-based approaches, and (iii) feature discrimination by the MD and EMD. Once the small set of displacement samples has been augmented, the expanded data is decomposed into training and test subsets. The subsets can be either directly applied to a novelty detector (e.g., MD) or fed into a data normalization tool (e.g., DAE) or algorithm (e.g., UTSL, DTL, and ODT L) for removing any source of variability. Regarding the HMC-MD technique, no data normalization is considered in cases that the level of EOCs is low (Sarmadi 2021).

# 2.3.1 Data Augmentation by Markov Chain Monte Carlo

In statistics, Markov Chain Monte Carlo methods are comprised of a class of probabilistic algorithms for systematic random sampling from a probability distribution. In contrast to Monte Carlo sampling techniques that are capable of drawing independent samples from the distribution of interest, Markov Chain Monte Carlo methods can draw samples where the next sample is dependent on the existing sample, called a Markov chain. Markov Chain Monte Carlo algorithms, as their names indicate, consists of two parts of Monte Carlo and Markov Chain. In probability theory, Monte Carlo is an approach to randomly sampling a probability distribution and approximating a desired quantity. On this basis, Monte Carlo techniques typically suppose that it is possible to efficiently draw samples from a target distribution, in which case, from the samples that are drawn, one can estimate the sum or integral quantity as the mean or variance of the drawn samples. On the other hand, Markov Chain is a systematic stochastic procedure for generating a sequence of random samples, where the current sample is probabilistically dependent on the value of the previous sample. In case of combining these two methodologies (i.e., Markov Chain and Monte Carlo), it allows us to draw random samples for high-dimensional distributions that hold the probabilistic dependence between samples by constructing a Markov Chain that comprises the Monte Carlo sample. For this aim, it suffices to build a Markov chain that contains the desired distribution as its equilibrium distribution and then gain a sample of that distribution by recording states from the chain (Van Ravenzwaaij et al. 2018). The successful performance of data augmentation in SHM has been studied by Entezami et al. (2022) regarding damage localization and threshold estimation with a small number of anomaly indices.
