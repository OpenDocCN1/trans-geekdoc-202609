# 1.3 Motivations

Based on the major challenges in SHM and its new technique via SAR images, this book aims to propose innovative methods to deal with these challenges and develop a more reliable and robust manner for long-term health monitoring of civil structures based on SAR images. Hence, one initially attempts to clarify the main motivations of this book and then discuss the proposed method.

**Motivation I**: In most of the SAR-aided SHM applications, long-term monitoring is preferable to evaluating the stability and health of civil structural systems. Unlike vibration-based procedures for SHM, a limited number of displacement points can be extracted from a few SAR images during a long period of years (e.g., one, two, five, or even ten years). Although, this strategy can help civil engineers to observe variations in displacements and detect any abnormal condition in the structures, it is not an effective and robust way. Normally, the EOC is dominated in long-term SHM. Due to their direct and significant effects on structural behavior and responses, any change in displacement sample cannot be interpreted as an abnormal condition or a serious risk. It may be caused by the EOCs that are not naturally adverse situations. However, these conditions may lead to false positives that directly relate to the economic issue. On the other hand, minor damage may cause smaller changes in displacement data than the EOCs. In this case, it is difficult to detect minor damage. Accordingly, the decision-making tools (i.e., feature discrimination techniques) cannot precisely alarm the emergence of damage (i.e., false negative or Type II), thereby gradually expanding and occurring major damage as partial and then global collapses. The other challenging issue is that the EOCs in small data obtained from a long-term monitoring strategy are often hidden or invisible. Therefore, in the first motivation, one attempts to propose an effective approach to extract or reveal the EOCs. For this purpose, it is suggested to augment or expand the small data by some Markov Chain Monte Carlo algorithms.
