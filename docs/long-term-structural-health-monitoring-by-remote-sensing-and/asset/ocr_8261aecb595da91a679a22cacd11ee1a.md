**Motivation II:** There is no doubt that the EOCs have significant influences on the structural responses and behavior. These can lead to either extra costs or time for detecting damage when the structure is in its normal or undamaged state (i.e., false positive or Type I error) or immense costs and human losses when the structure really suffered from damage but it is assumed to be undamaged (i.e., false negative or Type II error). On this basis, the removal of the EOCs from the main data for long-term SHM (i.e., time, frequency, or modal data, displacements, etc.) is a major challenging issue. Hence, it is indispensable to pay much more attention to this challenge for small data. For this problem, there are two tasks that should be considered and dealt with properly. First, it is necessary to remove or mitigate all possible EOCs from either small or expanded data. Second, one should increase the reliability of decision-making by decreasing the rates of false positives and false negatives as well as increasing damage detectability. For these tasks, this book proposes new methods based on machine learning, ANNs, and deep learning to remove any EOC from the augmented data.

**Motivation III:** Most of the parametric machine learning methods are based on influential unknown parameters that directly affect their general performances. This means that any inaccurate choice or estimation of these parameters may lead to an incorrect learning process and imprecise results with large errors (e.g., false positive or false negative). Accordingly, hyperparameter optimization is mandatory (Sarmadi 2021; Yang and Shami 2020). In some cases, the optimum choice of hyperparameters a parametric model (e.g., the neurons associated with hidden layers of an ANN) may be time-consuming and laborious, particularly if the hyperparameter optimization is based on a manual tuning approach. Although it seems that the optimization-based methods outperform the manual tuning techniques, the definition of an objective function for a particular problem (e.g., the removal of the EOC condition available in real or augmented data) may not be trivial. Hence, the development of an effective but fast machine learning method can be a major challenge in continuous SHM. To deal with this problem, this study aims to propose a deep transfer learning method to not only enhance the accuracy and reliability of feature discrimination but also make a simpler learning process than the common learning strategies under rigorous hyperparameter optimizations.

**Motivation IV:** Despite the effectiveness of many SHM methods based on the offline learning framework, it may not suffice for performing a long-term monitoring scheme. As mentioned earlier, one cannot make sure that the structure is undamaged during the offline learning process. Furthermore, it is difficult to assume that the same variability conditions in the offline learning period emerge once again. The major limitation is that the learned model confronts new data with different variability pattens. To address these problems, this study sets out to design online hybrid learning algorithms.
