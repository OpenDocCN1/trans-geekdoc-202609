# 1.4 Novel Approaches and Contributions

Despite valuable studies on SHM of various civil structures by using the remote sensing technology and SAR images, this book mainly intends to address the major limitations and shortcomings of the classical SAR-aided SHM and particularly concentrates on long-term monitoring by small displacement data. In this regard, various innovative methods in terms of machine learning are proposed as discussed above. Here, it is attempted to clarify the major innovations of these methods in an effort to facilitate readers to better realize the significance and novelty of this book. As the implementation of the classical SAR-aided SHM strategy in a long-term monitoring manner consists of three major challenges of small displacement data, the negative effects of EOCs, and discriminative damage or novelty2 indices, the underlying innovation of this book is to propose hybrid learning methods in terms of unsupervised learning. Based on the three major challenges, the innovations of these methods can be divided into three categories:

+   - Category I—Innovations for dealing with the challenge of small data
+   - Category II—Innovations for dealing with the challenge of EOCs

2 In machine learning, the term “novelty” refers to an abnormal change in data. In some literature, it is defined as “anomaly” or “outlier” (Pimentel et al. 2014). However, in this research, a novelty value is representative of a damage index, which can represent the difference between two datasets or structural state conditions.
