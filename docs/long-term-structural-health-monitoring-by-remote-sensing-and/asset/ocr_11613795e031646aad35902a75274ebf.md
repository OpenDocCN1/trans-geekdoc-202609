# Preface

Continuous health monitoring of civil structures is an effective approach to assess their health and safety. In most applications to structural health monitoring (SHM) grounded on contact-based sensors, long-term strategies contain a large volume of measured data from relatively dense sensor networks. Under such circumstances, the course of dimensionality from big data is one of the major challenges in SHM. Thanks to developments of satellite systems and the technology of remote sensing via synthetic aperture radar (SAR) images, it has been possible to take advantage of its capacities in health monitoring of civil structures via satellite images. The great advantage of a SAR-aided SHM method is the possibility of obtaining prior information or archived images concerning structures in their undamaged states. Furthermore, it is feasible to perform a long-term process using SAR images. Accordingly, one should extract displacement data from such images and analyze them. However, some issues restrict providing an adequate set of displacement points, in which case the SAR-aided SHM is confronted with the challenge of *small data*. On the other hand, the other major challenge in most of the long-term SHM is related to *environmental and/or operational changes* (EOCs). This issue is challenging because such variations can lead to unpredictable changes in civil structures similar to damage. Hence, serious false positives and false negatives emerge during the long-term monitoring process. This issue will be worse in the SAR-aided SHM when a small number of data is present. Because the false positives and false negatives are directly associated with economic and human issues, it is essential to consider all possible EOCs, visualize their variations in data, and remove them from displacement points.

Therefore, the main objective of this book is to propose **novel hybrid unsupervised learning methods** for addressing the aforementioned challenges; that is, long-term SHM under small data and EOCs. Due to the importance of these issues, this study proposes **seven hybrid methods** under the concepts of artificial intelligence, data sampling, and probabilistic and statistical analyses. (1) The first method suggests a probabilistic hybrid unsupervised learner based on the ideas of unsupervised nearest neighbor searching (NNS) and extreme value theory (EVT) for SHM under relatively large data and EOCs. (2) The second technique called HMC-MD relies on a data augmentation approach based on Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC)
