Even though Figueiredo et al. (2011) achieved to obtain reliable outputs of data normalization through this neural network in their problem, Sarmadi et al. (2021a) indicated that this network may not be reliable for any engineering issue with various EOCs. To address this drawback, this study proposes a consecutive three-hidden-layer DAE based on the concept of transfer learning. Unlike some well-known deep transfer learning techniques, the proposed method does not make a single ANN. In contrast, a sequence of three-hidden-layer DAEs is developed to generate a new deep neural network (DNN). Moreover, the proposed neural network differs from some well-known stacked DNNs (e.g. stacked autoencoders). This is because the proposed DNN does not implement any stacking process. By contrast, the main idea is to compute the direct difference between the input and output fed into any DAE and use this difference (residual) as a new input to the next network.

Figure 2.11 indicates the graphical representation of the proposed DNN under transfer learning. As can be seen in this figure, the consecutive ANN learning process is an iterative algorithm of k iterations or steps. For the first iteration, the input is the expanded training data X, which is fed into the first DAE. The main objective is to extract the residual matrix of the input and output. On this basis, the extracted residual data is set as a new input (i.e., Input 2) in the second iteration and this input is applied to the next (second) DAE. This process continues until the last (k) iteration. Hence, the final residual extraction is performed to determine the last or kth residual set, which serves as the final normalized expanded feature matrix (Ex) of the undamaged state. This set is then used to compute its novelty scores for decision-making. It should be mentioned that the iterative procedure illustrated in Fig. 2.11 should be implemented by using both the augmented training and test data sets. Nonetheless, the learning process is only performed via the training set. This means that the trained neural networks (i.e., along with their estimated weights and biases) are only employed to obtain the residuals of the augmented test data.

### 2.3.4.1 Hyperparameter Optimization in DTL

In contrast to the previous proposed methods (i.e., HMC-DAE-MD and HMC-UTSL-MD), which their performances significantly depend on the neuron sizes of the hidden layers, the proposed HMC-DTL-MD method does not require any strategy for finding the neuron numbers. The only prerequisite of this technique is to define a stopping condition in its iterative procedure. In fact, this condition declares how many consecutive DAEs is needed to perform the DTL algorithm. Therefore, the major hyperparameter optimization in this section is to determine the number of consecutive DAEs.

This section presents a technique by computing the accuracy of feature discrimination under some sample numbers of neural networks and the rate of misclassification. Because finding the number of consecutive DAEs in the proposed DTL method is

## 2.3.5 Feature Discrimination via Mahalanobis Distance

Using the augmented training and test samples, these are considered in a novelty detector for feature discrimination. In the context of SHM, the MD is a tried-and-tested novelty detector that was widely applied to SHM applications (Sarmadi and Karamodin 2020; Sarmadi et al. 2020, 2021, 2023b; Yeager et al. 2019; Sarmadi et al. ; Mosavi et al. 2012; Nguyen et al. 2014; George et al. 2018; Entezami et al. 2023 ). This is due to some great advantages such as simplicity, computational efficiency, and non-parametric behavior. In some conditions that the variability level is not considerable, this method yields reasonable results of feature discrimination with high detection accuracy. In this regard, Sarmadi (Sarmadi 2021) demonstrated that the MD-based novelty detection is a reliable method under low EOCs. However, it fails in providing satisfactory results in high variability conditions. In this case, data normalization is mandatory before applying the MD or other novelty detectors with poor performances in high EOCs.

The process of feature discrimination via the MD-based novelty detector is generally implemented in the baseline (training) and monitoring (testing) phases. The former requires the features of the undamaged state (i.e., training data) and the later needs some features of the undamaged condition (i.e., validation data) and all available features associated with the unknown (current) structural state. Assume that the augmented training matrix X comes from an undamaged condition of the structure with inconsiderable EOCs. In this case, it is not necessary to use a data normalization strategy similar to the proposed HMC-MD technique. Hence, the underlying goal is to utilize sample estimators for estimating the mean v_x in R^p and covariance S_x in R^{p x p} of X. It should be pointed out that as the augmented data obtained from either HMC or SLS conforms to the multivariate Gaussian/normal distribution, it can be ensured that the uses of the sample estimators are reasonable. With these descriptions, the MD for the ith column vector of X is expressed as follows:

d_MD(x_i) = (x_i - v_x)^T S_x^{-1} (x_i - v_x), forall i = 1, ..., n (2.38)

When the data normalization is necessary (i.e., the level of EOCs is considerable), the MD needs normalized features. In this study, it is supposed that such features are the residual samples extracted from the difference between the input and output concerning one of the data normalization algorithms (i.e., DAE, UTSL, and DTL). In the baseline phase, one assumes that E_x refers to the normalized (augmented) features of the normal structural condition originated from the real feature matrix X. Similarly, the mean v_e in R^p and covariance S_e in R^{p x p} are estimated from E_x. Accordingly, the MD-based novelty detector can be rewritten as follows:

\(d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{xi}) = (\mathbf{e}_{xi} - \mathbf{v}_e)^T \mathbf{S}_e^{-1} (\mathbf{e}_{xi} - \mathbf{v}_e), \quad \forall i = 1, \dots, n \quad (2.39)\)

where \(\mathbf{e}_{xi}\) is the \(i\)th column vector of \(\mathbf{E}_x\). Using Eqs. (2.38) and (2.39), it can be obtained \(n\) distance quantities, either \(\{d_{MD}(\mathbf{x}_1), \dots, d_{MD}(\mathbf{x}_n)\}\) or \(\{d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{x1}), \dots, d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{xn})\}\) in the baseline phase. Such \(n\) distance quantities are then considered to estimate a threshold limit for feature discrimination. Having considered \(\mathbf{Z}\) (i.e., the augmented test data points) or \(\mathbf{E}_z\) (i.e., the residual matrix from one of the data normalization algorithms), their column vectors are inserted into Eqs. (2.38) and (2.39) by considering \(\mathbf{v}_x\) or \(\mathbf{v}_e\) and \(\mathbf{S}_x\) or \(\mathbf{S}_e\). Hence, in the testing phase, the MD can be rewritten in the following forms:

\(d_{MD}(\mathbf{z}_j) = (\mathbf{z}_j - \mathbf{v}_x)^T \mathbf{S}_x^{-1} (\mathbf{z}_j - \mathbf{v}_x), \quad \forall j = 1, \dots, m \quad (2.40)\)

or

\(d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{zj}) = (\mathbf{e}_{zj} - \mathbf{v}_e)^T \mathbf{S}_e^{-1} (\mathbf{e}_{zj} - \mathbf{v}_e), \quad \forall j = 1, \dots, m. \quad (2.41)\)

In this regard, one can obtain \(m\) distance values, either \(\{d_{MD}(\mathbf{z}_1), \dots, d_{MD}(\mathbf{z}_m)\}\) or \(\{d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{z1}), \dots, d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{zn})\}\) concerning the testing phase. Applying \(n\) and \(m\) distance quantities, the process of feature discrimination is based on comparing each of these quantities by the threshold limit, which is determined by using the novelty indices of the training data points related to the undamaged structural state. In this regard, \(\{d_{MD}(\mathbf{x}_1), \dots, d_{MD}(\mathbf{x}_n)\}\) or \(\{d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{x1}), \dots, d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{xn})\}\) should fall below the threshold confirming the healthy condition of the structure. For the distance values of the test data points, it is possible to reach two general states (i.e., see Fig. 2.4):

- (1) If \(d_{MD}(\mathbf{z}_j)\) or \(d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{zj})\) is larger than the threshold, this means that the structure suffered from damage and the SHM method triggers the emergence of damage.

- (2) If \(d_{MD}(\mathbf{z}_j)\) or \(d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_{zj})\) is smaller than the threshold, this means that the structure is still healthy and no damage is available.

In the process of feature discrimination, threshold limit can be estimated by two general methodologies based on central limit theory (CLT) and extreme value theory (EVT). The former assumes that the novelty indices of the training data points conform to a Gaussian distribution, in which case standard confidence interval under various significant level (the probability of false alarm) are applicable. The former is based on the selection of some maximum novelty indices that are the extreme values. Using these samples, one can model an extreme value distribution and estimate a quantile value under a small significant level. Generally, the EVT-aided threshold estimator outperforms the CLT-aided technique in case of addressing the issue of EOCs or the presence of low variability conditions. More details can be found in (Sarmadi and Karamodin 2020; Sarmadi et al. 2022; Daneshvar and Sarmadi 2022; Sarmadi 2021; Sarmadi and Entezami 2021; Entezami et al. 2019) regarding the EVT-aided threshold determinators and (Entezami et al. 2019; Entezami 2021) for the CLT-aided threshold determinator.
