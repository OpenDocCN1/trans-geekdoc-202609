## 4.3.2 HMC-UTSL-MD

The central core of the proposed HMC-UTSL-MD method concentrates on data normalization by the proposed UTSL algorithm. As explained in §0, this algorithm consists of a teacher model consisting of an undercomplete seven-hidden-layer DAE and a student model based on an overcomplete shallow AE. To remove the EOC effects, it suffices to feed the augmented training data into these models to initially train them and subsequently extract the augmented residual matrix concerning the training data. In the next step, the expanded test data points are used to the trained teacher and student models for residual extraction. Both residual matrices of the training and test datasets are considered in the MD-based novelty detector for feature normalization.

The first step of data normalization by UTSL is to gain the neurons of the seven hidden layers of the undercomplete DAE (teacher) and the single encoding layer of the overcomplete shallow AE. According to the proposed hyperparameter optimization presented in Sect. 2.3.3.1 and the sample neuron collections from Table 2.1, Fig. 4.11 displays the AIC values of 10 sets of the sample neurons. As this figure discloses, the AIC values of the 4th, 5th, and 6th sets are smaller compared to the other ones. This means that each of them can yield reliable outputs. However, the average of these values calculated to apply this averaged number to be the optimum set of the neurons. Hence, the seven hidden layers of the DAE need 10, 8, 6, 3, 6, 8, and 10 neurons, respectively. For the overcomplete shallow AE, the number of neurons of the encoding layer is equal to 51.

By graphically comparing the novelty indices obtained from HMC-UTSL-MD and HMC-DAE-MD, it can be deduced that the UTSL is superior to the DAE in terms of less variability and higher detection rate.

## 4.3.3 HMC-DTL-MD

Similar to the previous hybrid methods, HMC-DTL-MD undertakes to detect early damage or predict collapse before its occurrence under unpredictable EOCs. To do this, it needs to gain the optimum number of consecutive DAEs so that each of them entails three hidden layers of the sizes 10, 2, 10 neurons for mapping, encoding, and de-mapping layers, respectively. According to the proposed hyperparameter optimization (see Sect. 2.3.4.1), it is only necessary to determine the number of iterations (i.e., a stopping condition) in the iterative algorithm of this process. Figure 4.14 indicates the amounts of accuracy by using 10 trials for the sample neural networks. As can be observed, the seventh sample provides the best accuracy rate close to 100%. Hence, the DTL algorithm for data normalization requires seven DAEs. In this regard, the first input fed into the first DAE is the real augmented training data X and remaining inputs (E_x1, ..., E_x6) fed into the next six DAEs are the residual samples between each input and output of the previous DAE.
