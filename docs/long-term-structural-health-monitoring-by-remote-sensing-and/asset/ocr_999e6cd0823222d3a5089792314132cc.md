Yan et al. 2005; Soo Lon Wah et al. 2019; Deramaeker and Worden 2018; Reynders et al. 2014), nearest neighbor searching (Sarmadi and Karamodin 2020; Sarmadi et al. 2021a, 2022, 2023a; Sarmadi and Yuen 2022), and some linear approaches based on singular value decomposition (SVD) and Gaussian mixture model (GMM) (Figueiredo and Cross 2013; Figueiredo et al. 2011) are some well-known statistical output-only data normalization techniques. The ANN-based data normalization lies in learning an unsupervised ANN by using the structural responses or damage-sensitive features and computing the residuals between inputs applied to the network (i.e., the same structural responses or damage-sensitive features) and the network outputs (predictions). This algorithm can be carried out by shallow neural networks (Figueiredo et al. 2011; Chang et al. 2018; Sarmadi 2021) or deep neural networks (e.g., autoencoders) (Wang and Cha 2021; Shang et al. 2021; Silva et al. 2021). The other approaches are based on advanced machine learning methods. In this regard, Daneshvar et al. (2023b) proposed a novel locally unsupervised learning method through a discriminative reconstruction-based dictionary learning model for removing the environmental effects from modal data. Sarmadi et al. (2023b) proposed a novel hybrid feature weighting-selection algorithm in conjunction with a new nearest neighbor searching call natural nearest neighbor selection to implement an unsupervised (output-only) data normalization on modal data of full-scale bridge structures. Entezami et al. (2023a) put forward the idea of unsupervised meta-learning for eliminating the effects of the EOCs from dynamic data of concrete and steel bridges with missing data. In another study on advanced machine learning, Entezami et al. (2023c) proposed an innovative unsupervised multi-task learning method for unsupervised data normalization. All of these techniques can be classified as non-parametric and parametric algorithms. Although the first algorithm does not need any unknown components that should be estimated before the model development, parametric methods can yield more reliable outputs regarding the removal of the variability conditions by estimating their hyperparameters (Sarmadi 2021).

The other challenge in machine learning-aided SHM is related to the learning strategy. Depending upon the type of processing and acquiring measured data, the learning process can be carried out in offline or online manners. The central idea behind the offline learning is to handle and analyze an approximately large set of measurements at once. Indeed, this strategy brings a systematic approach to process numerous data points, which are gathered over a period of time. One of the advantages of this approach is the possibility of capturing all potential EOCs in long-term monitoring. In this regard, one supposes that adequate training data is available to develop a plausible computational/statistical model in terms of machine learning. By contrast, online learning is the process of being able to almost instantaneously analyze data, update the initial model, and the learning strategy in an online manner by receiving new data (Hoi et al. 2021).
