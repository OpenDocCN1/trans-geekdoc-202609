Despite many successful applications of SAR-aided SHM on different civil structures, particularly bridges, this technology has its limitations and challenges (Behkamal et al. 2023a, b). One of these challenges is the impossibility of providing rich and adequate information (measurements) as same as vibration-based SHM (Entezami et al. 2022a, b). To put it another way, any vibration-based SHM may contain many contact- and non-contact vision-based sensors to measure vibration responses (e.g., acceleration time histories or displacements from images or videos) in a long-term monitoring program. Under such circumstances, one can obtain huge volume of useful information and responses regarding the behavior of the structure under study. In contrast, this strategy is not feasible in SAR-aided SHM. For example, Sarmadi et al. (2021b) exploited 5956 natural frequencies of a three-span bridge to detect damage in a one-year monitoring scheme. Entezami et al. (2020a) applied the total of 1,010,880,000 time series data concerning a cable-style bridge for only 12 days. Because the preparation of such large data is not often possible in SAR-aided SHM, the major challenge is the problem of small data. Simply speaking, even though one can implement a long-term monitoring program for SHM of civil structures via remote sensing, a limited number of SAR images are usually applied to find out displacement data points. On the other hand, extremely large sizes of SAR images due to covering wide areas in the unit of km, and memory space restrictions for collecting many images are other limitations of this process. The other key challenge of SAR-aided SHM pertains to directly analyzing displacements extracted from satellite images regarding bridges (Selvakumaran et al. 2018; Miliello et al. 2019, 2016; Qin et al. 2017, 2018), buildings (Zhu et al. 2018; Macchiarulo et al. 2021; Giardina et al. 2019; Cigna et al. 2014; Di Carlo et al. 2021), and dams (Bayik et al. 2021; Mura et al. 2018; Miliello et al. 2016; Di Martire et al. 2014; Gama et al. 2020; Jänichen et al. 2022). An important note is that the direct utilization of measured data is not a robust way for SHM. Undoubtedly, apart from the size and complexity of civil structures compared with other structural systems, those are expensive and critical. This means that any erroneous decision on SHM by directly analyzing measured data may cause irreparable events such as human deaths and injuries and economic losses. Therefore, the direct application of small data is not a reliable and robust technique for SHM of prominent civil structures, especially in long-term monitoring.

Apart from this limitation, most of the unsupervised learning methods are susceptible to erroneous results due to severe variability in data and damage-sensitive features regardless of the type of sensing and features. In reality, ambient and environmental changes are inevitable in every civil structure built in outdoor environment. Daily and seasonal temperature fluctuations, wind speed and direction changes, humidity and moisture variations, and excitation amplitude alternations are prevalent environmental variability conditions. Moreover, operational variations in outdoor and indoor environment significantly affect civil structures. Dead and live load alterations, excessive loading, sudden accidents and collisions, explosions, human and vehicle traffic are some common operational variability conditions (Farrar and Worden 2013: Chapter 12; Wang et al. 2022).
