this regard, the optimal neuron numbers should force the HMC-DAE-MD method to
provide smooth novelty indices for the training data with the lowest rate of variability.
Therefore, the optimal neurons are those that have the minimum variance value. In
Table 4.1, it can be observed that the minimum variance coincides with the 28th row
and 4th column of the stored variance matrix (Step 7 of Algorithm 1). This means
that the DAE with 28 neurons for the mapping and de-mapping layers and 4 neurons
for the encoding layer can satisfy the condition of the lowest rate of variability and
can properly mitigate the EOC effects. Therefore, one should design a DAE using
the three hidden layers and the selected neuron sizes. It is worth remarking that the
learning process of this DAE is based on the Levenberg–Marquardt backpropagation
under 1000 epochs for estimating the model-parameters (i.e., the weight matrices
and bias vectors).

Using the learned DAE, the augmented training and test matrices are fed into this
neural network to extract their residual matrices E$_\mathbf{x}$ and E$_\mathbf{z}$, which are the main inputs
of the MD-based novelty detector for feature discrimination. To better demonstrate
the positive effect of data normalization by the DAE, the initial result of feature
discrimination ignores any threshold limit. As a comparison, Fig. 4.8 evaluates the
evolution of the novelty indices gained by HMC-MD and HMC-DAE-MD. As can
be seen, despite high variability of the distance values in the training data points
of Fig. 4.8a, the DAE highly assists HMC-MD in reducing the influences of the
EOCs and providing smoother outputs with lower variability. This makes sense that
HMC-DAE-MD significantly outperforms HMC-MD. The same conclusion can be
achieved concerning the validation data. In Fig. 4.8b, one can discern that the sudden
gap and difference is not available and both groups of validation data regarding
Images 42 and 43 have similar novelty indices. In relation to the test data related
to Images 44 and 45, it is seen that their distance values in Fig. 4.8b are more
distinguishable from the distances of the undamaged condition (i.e., training and
validation points) compared to Fig. 4.8a. Thus, it can be deduced that the DAE-aided
data normalization not only mitigates the EOC effects but also helps the MD metric
to increase the detectability of damage.
