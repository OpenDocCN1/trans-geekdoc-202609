\( d_E = \sqrt{(d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_1))^2 + \dots + (d_{MD}(\mathbf{e}_n))^2} \quad (2.43) \)

Hence, \( d_E \) is the only scalar novelty index for representing the status of the structure in both stages of online learning and online damage detection. This means that any \( d_E \) value in the first stage declares the undamaged state of the structure and any \( d_E \) value in the second stage triggers that the structure is damaged. This kind of feature discrimination in the proposed online hybrid techniques is the major improvement against the previously proposed techniques.

# 2.5 Chapter Summary

This chapter has fully discussed the main proposed methods in this book for the enhanced SAR-aided SHM. These methods have consisted of two categories of relatively large displacement data and small displacement data. For the first category, this work has proposed a new probabilistic machine learning method on the basis of the concepts of NNS and EVT. At first, the fundamental principles and theoretical basis of the EVT and all possible extreme value distribution models have been described in detail. Subsequently, a new algorithm for the NNS-EVT method based on the semi-parametric quantile modeling has been developed to establish a probabilistic novelty detector for SHM. For the second category, the proposed hybrid learning methods in offline and online manners have been discussed elaborately.

In this chapter, it can be deduced that the proposed NNS-EVT method has good diversity for developing many novelty detectors for SHM. Although, this book has considered one type of novelty detector based on the semi-parametric extreme value distribution model and its quantile function for novelty index definition, it is possible to exploit the other (parametric) extreme value distributions such as the GEVD based on the BM technique and the GPD based on the POT technique for designing new novelty detectors. This means that the proposed NNS-EVT method develops a general methodology by high diversity of applicability with other models (i.e., GEVD and GPD) and techniques (i.e., BM and POT).

In relation to the hybrid learning methods, it can be concluded that the unsupervised DAE is a powerful and effective approach to designing various data normalization approaches. With the aids of advanced machine learning algorithms such as teacher-student learning, transfer learning, online learning, one can design different configurations of the DAEs for data normalization. Moreover, the random data generation based on Markov Chain Monte Carlo algorithms can provide non-parametric strategies for data augmentation. Finally, when the challenge of the EOCs is solvable, non-parametric novelty detectors based on the statistical distance metrics, especially Mahalanobis distance, can be the first choice.
