
## Chapter 5 Conclusions and Prospects for Structural Health Monitoring

**Abstract** In this book, it has been attempted to address some major challenging issues related to long-term health monitoring of civil structures based on the technology of remote sensing and machine learning. For this reason, various unsupervised learning methods have been proposed to detect any abnormal conditions in civil structures under unknown EOCs using small and large sets of displacement responses obtained from SAR images. This chapter aims to mention the main conclusions of the proposed methods in both numerical and experimental validation stages. Due to the importance of SHM and the benefits of remote sensing, some suggestions are given to further evaluate in future research activities.

**Keywords** Long-term health monitoring • Conclusions • Environmental and operational changes • Remote sensing • Machine learning

### 5.1 Introduction

The main motivation for conducting this research is to improve the classical SAR-aided SHM and address the major limitations of this process. On this basis, new hybrid methods in an unsupervised learning fashion were developed to alarm the emergence of any abnormal change and damage, especially collapse, under unknown and unpredictable EOCs. In case of apply relatively large displacement data, this research proposed a hybrid probabilistic unsupervised learning method based on the NNS and EVT. When a small number of displacement samples is available, the proposed hybrid learning methods entailed data augmentation via either HMC or SLS techniques, different novel DAE-based data normalization approaches, and feature discrimination by the MD and EMD. In the second part of the hybrid learning methods for small data, this research exploited various AE in deep and shallow configurations. In the HMC-DAE-MD method, a direct application of the three-hidden-layer DAE was considered to mitigate the EOC influences from the augmented data. For this purpose, a hyperparameter optimization with an emphasis on addressing the challenge of the EOCs was developed. In the HMC-UTSL-MD method, a new UTSL algorithm based on an undercomplete DAE with seven hidden layers for the teacher model learning and an overcomplete shallow AE for the student learning model were proposed. A new hyperparameter optimization was designed to gain the neurons of the seven hidden layers of the DAE and the only encoding layer of the shallow AE. The proposed HMC-DTL-MD method took advantage the idea of the transfer learning by considering the DAE with the pre-defined 10, 2, and 10 neurons for the mapping, encoding, and de-mapping layers, respectively. For this technique, an iterative hyperparameter optimization was designed to find the number of consecutive DAEs. Since the central core of the aforementioned hybrid methods concentrated on the idea of offline learning, it was attempted to propose two new methods under online learning. Accordingly, this book also suggested the HMC-ODTL-EMD and SLS-ODTL-EMD methods for real-time SHM. The main improvements of these methods pertained to the DLT algorithm, which was improved to design its online version (ODLT) for data normalization, and feature discrimination by the EMD.

To validate the effectiveness and reliability of the proposed methods, two full-scale bridge structures were considered. For the NNS-EVT and HMC-MD methods, a numerical study based on the simulation of displacement points of the I-40 Bridge was conducted. Furthermore, the other methods were verified in a real-world case study by using a small number of displacement data points associated with the Tadjaster Bridge. These data points were extracted from a limited number of SAR images of TerraSar-X.

### 5.2 Main Conclusions

Drawing from the extensive research presented in the monograph, it becomes evident that the traditional direct analysis of displacement data is suboptimal for health monitoring of civil structures, particularly long-term SHM. The unpredictability in data variation and inherent uncertainties make these conventional techniques unsuitable for a reliable and long-term analysis. In contrast, the proposed methods in this monograph address these challenges and offer innovative, machine-learning based alternatives.

The NNS-EVT and HMC-MD methods perform well under low variability conditions. For larger datasets, NNS-EVT shines as an effective approach. However, when subjected to high levels of Environmental and Operational Changes (EOCs), the HMC-MD method showed limitations, emphasizing the crucial role of data normalization in yielding reliable results. This sets the stage for the introduction of hybrid unsupervised learning methods, which combine data sampling, artificial intelligence, and statistical analysis.

The monograph strongly recommends data augmentation techniques such as Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) and Slice Sampling (SLS) for smaller displacement samples to better understand the impacts of EOCs on structural responses (i.e., displacements). While both approaches demonstrated their ability to account for EOCs, HMC exhibited superior performance when assessed through graphical comparisons via Box plot. Nonetheless, when evaluated numerically based on the MD measure, both HMC and SLS proved effective. Therefore, it is recommended to consider the MAD-based data analysis for the EOC evaluation.

The advanced hybrid methods, such as HMC-DAE-MD and HMC-UTSL-MD, demonstrated their robustness in tackling the challenging aspects of EOCs. The HMC-DAE-MD method could deal with the challenge of the EOCs by obtaining discriminative novelty indices between the normal and damaged conditions. It was discerned that the DAE-aided data normalization along with its hyperparameter optimization minimized the influences of the EOCs. In this regard, this method was superior to the HMC-MD.

In case of considering two threshold estimators based on the CLT and EVT, it was perceived that the HMC-DAE-MD gave fewer false positives via the EVT-aided threshold determinant in comparison with the CLT-based technique. Although this method with the aid of the EVT yielded more false negatives than the CLT, its output was reasonable. Totally, the EVT-aided threshold determinant is superior to the CLT-based technique. However, it contains more details and parameters, in which case the CLT-based threshold determinant is more efficient.

Adding another layer of complexity and effectiveness, the HMC-UTSL-MD and HMC-DTL-MD methods achieved remarkable results in the early detection of structural damage, even under high levels of EOCs. The proposed UTSL-based data normalization in HMC-UTSL-MD could better minimize the EOC influences compared to HMC-DAE-MD and HMC-MD. For this achievement, HMC-UTSL-MD succeeded in accurately detecting early damage or predicting the occurrence of collapse even by CLT-aided threshold determinant. The proposed HMC-DTL-MD method through the DAE with pre-determined neurons for its three hidden layers and the concept of transfer learning could better address the problem of the EOC. Even under the classical standard confidence interval, this method yielded smaller rate of the false positive and misclassification errors.

Finally, the proposed online hybrid learning methods not only designed a new SHM strategy in an online learning manner but also provided reasonable results of early damage detection by stopping the learning process and accurately detecting the emergence of damage and predicting the partial collapse. The online hybrid methods reasonably triggered the occurrence of damage and the risk of collapse before its event.

In summary, this monograph delivers a comprehensive and rigorous investigation of long-term SHM, integrating insights from remote sensing and machine learning techniques. By critically assessing the limitations of conventional approaches, this work introduces pioneering methodologies that represent a noteworthy advancement in the field of health monitoring of civil structures, particularly long-term SHM.
