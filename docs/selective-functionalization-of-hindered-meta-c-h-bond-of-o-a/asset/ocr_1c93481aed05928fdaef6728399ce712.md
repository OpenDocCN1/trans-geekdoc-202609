In reality, for a well-established reaction, predicting the outcome of an unseen substrate by inference is a challenging task for an experienced chemist. Therefore, developing prediction model for unseen substrates would have tremendous performance to help chemists adopt this methodology in synthetic chemistry; moreover, it could significantly lower the bar for non-experts to use this methodology.

With the experimentally measured 1032 reaction yield data points, we developed a new, communicative message neural network for reaction yield prediction (CMPRY). Our model was based on the communicative message passing neural network (CMPNN). First, we partitioned the dataset into training and external data. We selected 860 reactions, including 20 ketones (1a-1h, 1p-r, 1t, 1v, and 1w), 43 A&BFk (3a-e) as the training set. To evaluate the model performance for external data, we used the 172 test identities (1l, 1s, 1u, and 43 with A&BFk) as the independent test set. We pretrained the model on a large dataset (USPTO49⁴) by following the previous study that proves the equivalence of molecules to chemical reactions in the embedding space³⁵ and fine-tuned it on the training. By a random 80/20 split of the test set followed by 5-fold cross validation, our model achieved R² of 0.750 and MAE in yield of 7.26% (Figures 5B and 5C), which are essentially the ones (R² of 0.736 and MAE in yield) on the independent test (Figures 5B and SD). It is worth noting that 4 ketones were not included in the independent test set. Achieving such good performance for external data demonstrated that our model was capable of predicting reaction yield in this tandem reaction. We also evaluated the relationship of model performance with regards to the number of ketones in the training dataset. As expected, the R² was 0.503 with 172 training points (4 ketones) and gradually increased to 0.735 with 688 points (20 ketones). Although R² grows with the training size, it tends to converge (Figure 5E).

For comparison, we have employed four used baselines. As Figure 5B, the performance of retaining causality caused good R² of 0.643. The change from CMPNN to graph convolutional networks (GCNs) resulted in a small but significant decrease in R². Although the Machine Learning-based method extreme gradient boosting (XGB) and gradient boosting (GBM) achieved acceptable result in the S-5 fold cross validation, which lacked sufficient generalization capability, R² of 0.606 and 0.626 in the independent test.
