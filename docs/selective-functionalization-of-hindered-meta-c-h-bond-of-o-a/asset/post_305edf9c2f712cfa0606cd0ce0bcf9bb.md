Next we used to study C-H arylation/decarboxylation process using the carboxylated compound (2a) and potassium nitrilylfluoroborate (PhBF,K, 3a) as model substrates to make the final product (5a) (Figure 2B). As of the acetic acid directed C-H arylation, the hindered meta-C-H arylation compound (4a) was formed. It was proved that the mono-N-protected amino acids (MPAAs) could accelerate the Pd(II)-catalyzed C(sp³)-H/boron cross-coupling. Then 46 kinds of MPAAs and 2 bases were selected for the initial condition screening. In total, 92 conditions were evaluated by HTE. Then, further HTE were used to optimize MPAAs, bases, benzoquinones (BQ), temperature and method (see supplementary information). It is worth mentioning that certain level of decarboxylation product (5a) was observed in this process. With L12, KPO₄, and BQ2, the yield was improved to 92% (5a = 4a = 6:86). Finally, meta-C-H arylation final product (5a) was obtained in 77% with decarboxylation performed in a microwave at 150 °C for 0.5 h.

## Reaction scope

With the optimized conditions in hand, we began to explore the scope of o-alkylaryl ketones and potassium nitrilylfluoroborate (ArBF,K) via HTE (Figure 3). Thanks to the ¹H-NMR shift of the CH-aryl group, we identified the reaction yield via high-throughput method. Therefore, the HTE enabled scope exploration on the tangent C-H functionalization, i.e., photo-induced carboxylation/carbonyl group decoration. A substituted ketones array of structurally diverse substrates, consisting of 24 ketones (1a-14) and 43 ArBF (3a-32z), was selected to use micro-scale reactions using HTE (Figure 3).

Subsequently, a dataset with 1032 reaction data were analyzed (Figure 4a). It was that over 70% of the reactions gave desired products, which demonstrated that our strategy was generally applicable for a wide range of substrates. Generally, o-tolyl ketones (1a-u) afforded the corresponding products in notable yield with many ArBF,K. Substrates such as fluoro (1c, 1g, 1n), and chloro (1d and 1h), and tert-butyl (1f and 1p) on the aromatic ring were loaded. According to the literature, compounds with substantial steric hindrance at the benzyl position were challenging.²⁶ To our surprise, substrates with Me, Et, Bn, and Ome at the benzyl position (1q-1t) were also amenable to this process. In the case of o-tolyl ketone (1u), such products were afforded much. It appears that o-phenyl ketones (1y) yielded high yield or no product. 41% overall yield of using tandem C(sp³)-H activation/C(sp²)-H arylation was obtained when it reacted with p-OMePhBF,K (3j). In the C-H carboxylation step, aryl alkyl ketones (1v, 1x) were generally considered to have lower reactivity than bromophenyl ketones; however, many reactions gave good overall yield. Prediction of C(sp³)-H bonds with two adjacent substituents was extremely challenging due to the steric hindrance. Substituted o-phenyl ketones (1e, 1p) and para-substituted o-tolyl methyl ketone (1w) gave low yield or no product; however, p-OMePhBF,K (3j) still gave 23% overall yield with para-chloro o-tolyl ketone (1p). In terms of the substrate scope of ArBF,K, electron-donating or electron-drawing groups at ortho-, meta-, or para-positions were possible in this reaction (3a-3ze).
