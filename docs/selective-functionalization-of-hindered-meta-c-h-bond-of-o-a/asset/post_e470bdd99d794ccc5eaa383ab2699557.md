For a stricter test, we attempted to make an independent test set with both ketones and ArBFKusen. We selected 4 ketones (1l, 1s, 1u, and 1w) and 8 ArBFK (3k, 3n, 3p, 3r, 3s, 3w) and we re-trained the basic reaction data from the training set (SA and 5F). The performance achieved by using the shrunk set and independent test set showed a reasonable R² test of MAE in yield of 8.46, indicating the generality and the robustness of CMPRY (Figure 5G). The performance could be considered superior because of the complexity of predicting trends, in regards with which most models reported in the literature [44–51] were dealing with one-step reaction.

To interpret our DL-based model, we employed the gradient-weighted activation mapping (CAM-GRAD) to identify critical atoms for predictions. As shown in Figure 6, CMPRY highlighted the new (csp³)–(csp⁴) bond formation, indicating that the model learned the basic reaction information. For the reaction 6C that gave no product, CMPRY highlighted the ortho-substitution, methylsulfonyl group, with the reason that the steric hindrance is harmful for the C-H bond activation. Interestingly, CMPRY found that the carboxylic acid group was important at all successful reactions, such as information was not input into the predictions. Mechanistically, carboxyl group is needed to direct palladium catalysis for C-H activation. But not the least, there are peculiar structural fragments highlighted in reactions where oxygen atoms in the aldehyde and acetyl groups were considered important atoms, but only oxygen in the acetyl group was highlighted as an important atom in products.

Following the photo-induced C–H carbonylation, the unpurified intermediate was subjected to carboxyl group directed Pd-catalyzed C–H arylation with ArBF,JK, and then, it was transferred to a MW reactor for decarboxylation for the desired product. Generally, this one-pot procedure furnished the hindered meta-C–H carbonylation products in good yield (up to 76% for three substrates, ranging up to 91% yield for each step). The synthetic applicability and efficiency transfer were demonstrated by these results. More importantly, CO2 was acted as a relay director in the control.

In summary, this methodology achieved selective functionalization of hindered meta-C–H bond of o-alkyl ketones. A tandem transformation, involving photo-induced C–H carbonylation, carboxyl group directed Pd-catalyzed C–H functionalization, MW-assisted decarboxylation, was developed to streamline the access of multiply substituted are that would otherwise require extra steps. Further application of this strategy is under investigation in our laboratory. Moreover, several DL models, which are capable of predicting reaction yield over a range of substrates for this one-reaction, have been developed. Overall, we hope that the use of HTE-DL paradigm in the development of hindered meta-C–H arylation will stimulate its further application in the development of other synthetic methods.
