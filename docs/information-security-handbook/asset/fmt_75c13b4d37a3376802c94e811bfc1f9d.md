## 10.9 Steps Involved in the Infrastructures of the SMS

As information security is the constitutive part of any organization's operating environment and commercial culture; following the same concept, there are various steps that cover the building of SMS. There are some things to be taken into consideration while performing these steps \[3\]; first is to take input from all concerned stakeholders in the organization and afterward discuss the results to follow the agreed-upon pathway. Central repository for SMS should be maintained and updated, which could be done through a security manual. It is the confidential and reliable document usually sustained by the chief security officer. Let's analyze the several steps incorporated in the establishment of SMS \[[23](#ch10.xhtml#ref10_23)\].

1. **The first step** starts with the definition of the scope of the SMS in the context of business aspects, the firm, its place, present resources, and expertise about technology. The scope is based on the association of any interface with other systems, organizations, or any supplier. It also responds to security requirements satisfied by SMS.

2. **The second step** starts with the orderly approach of risk assessment. Here risk assessment method for an SMS is identified, with acknowledged business security and other legal requirements. The goal is to set policies and objectives for identification of threats and vulnerabilities and pay attention to control and reduce risks to some extent. By going along with the above procedure, we can, now, determine risk criteria and classify the acceptable level of risk by management approval by identification of impacts of confidentiality, integrity, and availability lacks on the organization's resources.

3. **The third level** is all about the top-down approach because information security is a management issue, not only an IT issue. Top management of the organization must play a critical role and take ownership in the maintenance of SMS. It is the core responsibility of management to motivate other employees in the enterprise to adhere to security principles.

4. **The fourth level** is the definition of functional roles. Once management decides to act on SMS principles, all functional roles must be definite. The type of role to be assigned to any entity depends upon the type and magnitude of the firm, and then roles could be varied. An owner of the SMS who serves as chief information security officer must be appointed. Other functional roles, including data agents, security awareness mentors, etc., must also be assigned.

5. **The fifth step** is to outline the policy in the same terms as we defined the scope of the SMS, but it includes the framework for setting its aims, which clears the vision of information security direction and principles. The security management stratagem is a type of document that elaborates the enterprise's information security plans at a high level. Here, some regulatory requirements also be noted, requirements that are derived from risk assessment. In this stage, a criterion is established to handle risk involved, evaluate risk, and define assessment, and that criterion must be affiliated with business significances and objectives.

6. **The sixth step** is to write the standards. These are fixed necessities that an organization must follow to support security policy and its measurement. The best approach is to document what the consequences are and where we lack behind.

7. **The seventh step** is to find out and evaluate choices for treatment of risks, after we have set policies and standards. These are useful actions, such as establishing proper controls, accepting risks empirically, providing the way to fully follow the organization's policy and criteria for accepting threats, and then avoiding risks.

8. **The eighth step** is to select the control target and other oversights for dealing with risks. One thing that must be under consideration is to choose the control that will be cost effective. For instance, the cost of treatment must not exceed the cost of impact of risks that are planned to be reduced. Risks are not always of financial type; they could be related to safety, legal and personal information, regulatory compulsion, and reputations. So, these are effects must be considered.
