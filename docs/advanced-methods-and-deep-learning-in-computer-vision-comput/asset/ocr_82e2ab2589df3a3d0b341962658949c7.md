Then, for each particle (·ⁿ), a KF is employed to predict the continuous variables X̃_t which depend on the expected superstate as pointed out in Eq. (13.13) and can be expressed in terms of conditional probability P(X̃_t|X̃_{t-1}, S̃_tⁿ). The posterior probability associated with the predicted state is defined as:

π(X̃_t) = P(X̃_t, S̃_t|Z̃_{t-1}) = ∫ P(X̃_t|X̃_{t-1}, S̃_t) P(Z̃_{t-1}|X̃_{t-1}) / λ(X̃_{t-1}) dX̃_{t-1}. (13.17)

Accordingly, once a new evidence Z̃_t is observed, a message back-ward propagated from the bottom-level towards the higher levels can be exploited by the MJPF to estimate the posterior probability P(X̃_t, S̃_tⁿ|Z_t) in the following way:

P(X̃_t, S̃_t|Z̃_t) = π(X̃_t) λ(X̃_t). (13.18)

Consequently, the weights of the corresponding particle can be updated according to:

W_t^n = W_t'^n λ(S̃_t) (13.19)

and then normalized following the Sequential Importance Resampling (SIR) approach. In Eq. (13.19), λ(S̃_t) is the diagnostic message propagated from bottom towards the top level of hierarchy to update the belief in hidden variables at that level and can be calculated as:

λ(S̃_t) = λ(X̃_t) P(X̃_t|S̃_t) = P(Z̃_t|X̃_t) P(X̃_t|S̃_t) (13.20)

where P(X̃_t|S̃_t) ~ N(μ_{S̃_k}, Σ_{S̃_k}) denotes a Gaussian distribution with mean μ_{S̃_k} and covariance Σ_{S̃_k}. While, λ(X̃_t) ~ N(μ_{Z_t}, R) denotes a Gaussian distribution with mean μ_{Z_t} and covariance R. The multiplication between λ(X̃_t) and P(X̃_t|S̃_t) can be obtained by calculating the Bhattacharyya distance (D_B) as follows:

D_B(λ(X̃_t), P(X̃_t|S̃_t = S̃_k)) = -ln ∫ √[λ(X̃_t) P(X̃_t|S̃_t = S̃_k)] dX̃_t (13.21)
