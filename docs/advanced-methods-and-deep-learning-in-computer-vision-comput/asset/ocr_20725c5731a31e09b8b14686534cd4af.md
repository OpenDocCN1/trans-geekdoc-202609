# 1.4.8 Summary: the value of invariants

Sections 1.4.2–1.4.6 have aimed to give some insight into the important subject of invariants and their application in image recognition. The subject takes off when ratios of ratios of distances are considered, and this idea leads in a natural way to the cross ratio invariant. While its original manifestation lies in its application to recognition of the spacings of points on a line, it generalizes immediately to angular spacings for pencils of lines, and also to angular separations of concurrent planes. A further extension of the idea is the development of invariants which can describe sets of noncollinear points, and two cross ratios suffice to characterize a set of 5 noncollinear points on a plane.

Many other theorems and types of invariant exist, but space prevents more than a mention being made here. As an extension to the point and line examples discussed above, invariants have been produced which cover conics; a conic and two coplanar nontangent lines; a conic and two coplanar points; two coplanar conics.
