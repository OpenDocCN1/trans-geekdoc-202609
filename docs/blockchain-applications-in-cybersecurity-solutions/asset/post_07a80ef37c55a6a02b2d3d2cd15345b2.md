## CONCLUSION AND FUTURE SCOPE

Blockchain is the future of technology. It has the potential to transform the economy as well as reinvent the way we see operations. Of course, we have yet to fully comprehend this technology, which is why more Indian state governments are collaborating with start-ups from around the world, such as Chroma Way, to conduct proof-of-concept tests within their jurisdictions. Our understanding of Blockchain has greatly benefited from Chroma Way's expertise and previous implementation in countries such as Sweden.

As you can see, there are a lot more blockchain applications than simply cryptocurrency, and they can affect a lot more industries than just finance. We purposefully avoided depicting the impact of Blockchain on the banking sector in order to keep the list as non-fintech as feasible.

A smart contract can declare an almost limitless number of business use cases involving the immutability of data in multi-organizational decision-making. A smart contract developer's job is to convert a current business process, such as one that governs financial prices or delivery conditions, into a smart contract using a programming language like JavaScript, GOLANG, or Java. Smart contract auditors are gradually practising the legal and technological skills required to adapt decades of legal language into the programming language.

In a non-trustable distributed ecosystem, the primary use of blockchains is to establish a creditworthy ecosystem among independent members. The peer-to-peer nodes, consensus-based ledger mechanisms, chained blocks, anonymous accounts, self-regulated data ownership, and programmable smart contracts make up a blockchain system that is secure and autonomous. Only a few blockchain projects are necessary, viable, efficient, and long-lasting. Furthermore, whereas blockchains provide a creditworthy framework for data-level storage and activities, rigorous credit procedures are required to develop creditworthy blockchain systems.
