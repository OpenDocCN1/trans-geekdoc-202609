
## e. Mining Pool Attacks

Individual miners can no longer profit from large cryptocurrencies, such as bitcoin, so miners pool their processing resources to form mining pools. This allows them to mine more blocks and distribute the prize evenly. BTC.com, AntPool and ViaBTC are currently the top three Bitcoin mining pools. According to Blockchain.com, they account for over 52% of the total hash rate of the Bitcoin network. Mining pools are a tempting target. By exploiting vulnerabilities in the blockchain consensus system, malicious miners attempt to gain internal and external control of mining pools. The following are the most frequent mining pool assaults.

### Selfish Mining

Malicious miners attempt to raise their part of the reward by not delivering the blocks to the network for a set length of time and then releasing several blocks at once, resulting in other miners losing their blocks. Randomly assigning miners to different branches of the pool, preferring a block with the latest timestamp, and creating blocks within the maximum time allowed are all possible countermeasures. Block holding is another name for this type of attack.

Miners lost 300 BTC in 2014 due to a selfish mining assault on the Eligius pool. Selfish mining has a high success rate and may occur with any coin. Only trustworthy miners will be registered, and the existing Bitcoin protocol will be modified to hide the distinction between partial and complete proof of work are two possible preventative methods against selfish mining [39].

### Fork After Withholding

FAW (Fork After Withholding) is a form of selfish mining that seems more profitable for attackers. During a FAW attack, a hostile miner conceals the winning block and steals it and, depending on the situation, either rejects it or publishes it later, creating a fork. A team of researchers led by Ugin Kwon has elucidated the concept of this attack in detail [39].

## CONCLUSION

Blockchains have emerged as a new paradigm in distributed systems and allow the secure exchange of assets between companies with competing interests. Blockchain technology is rapidly gaining ground, particularly in the form of cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin and Ethereum, which together have a market capitalization of more than $850 billion. Due to its huge market capitalization, opponents often target blockchain-based cryptocurrencies to make money. The attack surface of the blockchain was thoroughly examined in this chapter. We found that the attack surface of the blockchain can be divided into three types of attacks.

Authentication and encryption technologies can help prevent assaults, but they aren't the sole way to keep the system safe. Outdated security and defence methods can't handle complex new threats like zero-day malware campaigns, which have changed the scope of cybercrime.

Finally, this chapter contributes to the security of distributed systems by exposing a series of novel vulnerabilities related to application-specific regulations and the complexity of the network layer of blockchain systems. Our theoretical modeling, measurements, attacks, and countermeasures pave the way for new approaches to assessing blockchain security.

The attack surfaces for blockchain technology have been explored. Attacks on cryptographic designs, peer-to-peer network architecture, and blockchain technology applications are taken into account. The report reviewed the latest security research on decentralized systems based on blockchain technology and identified the main weaknesses of decentralized systems based on blockchain. Although despite current countermeasures, some attacks can be carried out with a high degree of probability, the work also sheds light on the connection between different attack sequences.
