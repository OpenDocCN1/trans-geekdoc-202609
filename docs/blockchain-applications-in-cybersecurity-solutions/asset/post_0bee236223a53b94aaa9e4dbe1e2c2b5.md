## ELIMINATING HUMAN FACTOR FROM AUTHENTICATION

Organizations can verify devices and clients without the requirement for a password with the assistance of blockchain innovation [8]. This eliminates human intervention from the authentication process, thus avoiding turning into a potential attack vector.

The use of a combined design and simple logins is a major drawback of standard frameworks. Regardless of how much money an organization invests in security, all of these efforts are pointless if employees and clients use passwords that are simple to steal or crack. Blockchain provides reliable verification while also resolving a single point of failure. A security framework used in an organization can use a distributed public key infrastructure for authenticating devices and customers with the assistance of blockchain [9]. Instead of a password, this security framework assigns a unique SSL certificate to each device. Because the management of certificate information is performed on the blockchain, it is practically impossible for attackers to use counterfeit certificates.

### Decentralized Storage

Blockchain users can keep their data on their PC in their system. In this way, they can ensure that the chain does not break. For example, if someone who isn't the owner of a piece of information (for example, an attacker) tries to tamper with a block, the entire framework examines every single data block to discover the one that differs from the rest [10]. If this type of block is found by the framework, it simply removes the block from the chain, marking it as fraudulent.

Blockchain is designed in such a way that there is no central storage area or central authority. On the system, each client has a task to perform in terms of storing some or all of the blockchains [11]. Everyone in the blockchain network is responsible for verifying the information that is transmitted as well as maintaining it to ensure that existing information is not removed and fake information is not incorporated.

### Traceability

Each transaction that is uploaded to a private or public blockchain is meticulously timestamped and tagged. This means that companies can trace each trade back to a certain timeframe and locate the counterparty on the blockchain using their public address [12]. This aspect is associated with non-repudiation: the assurance that someone cannot deny the veracity of their signature on a document, or the initiation of a transaction that they started. As each transaction is cryptographically linked to a client, the current blockchain's utility improves the framework's reliability. Any new transaction that is added to a blockchain changes the global state of the record. This means that with each new cycle of the framework, the previous state will be saved, resulting in a fully observable history log.

Blockchain's audit capability provides companies with a level of security and transparency over each transaction. From the standpoint of cyber security, this provides entities with an additional level of assurance that the information hasn't been tampered with and is correct [13].

Due to their decentralized nature, blockchains provide no 'hackable' entrance or a single point of failure and, as a result, provide more security when compared with other current database-driven value-based structures [14].
