# e. Mining Pool Attacks

Individual miners can no longer profit from large cryptocurrencies, such as bitcoin, so miners pool their processing resources to form mining pools. This allows them to mine more blocks and distribute the prize evenly. BTC.com, AntPool and ViaBTC are currently the top three Bitcoin mining pools. According to Blockchain.com, they account for over 52% of the total hash rate of the Bitcoin network. Mining pools are a tempting target. By exploiting vulnerabilities in the blockchain consensus system, Malicious miners attempt to gain internal and external control of mining pools. The following are the most frequent mining pool assaults.

# Selfish Mining

Malicious miners attempt to raise their part of the reward by not delivering the blocks to the network for a set length of time and then releasing several blocks at once, resulting in other miners losing their blocks. Randomly assigning miners to different branches of the pool, preferring a block with the latest timestamp, and creating blocks within the maximum time allowed are all possible countermeasures. Block holding is another name for this type of attack.

Miners lost 300 BTC in 2014 due to a selfish mining assault on the Eligius pool. Selfish mining has a high success rate and may occur with any coin. Only trustworthy miners will be registered, and the existing Bitcoin protocol will be modified to hide the distinction between partial and complete proof of work are two possible preventative methods against selfish mining [39].

# Fork After Withholding

FAW (Fork After Hold) is a form of selfish kicking that seems more profitable for attackers. During a FAW attack, a hostile miner conceals the winning block and steals it, depending on the situation, either rejects it or publishes it later, creating a fork. A team of researchers led by Ugin Kwon has elucidated the concept of this attack in detail [39].
