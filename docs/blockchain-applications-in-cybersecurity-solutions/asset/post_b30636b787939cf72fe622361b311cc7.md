## How Smart Contracts in Multichain Redefine Value Exchange

Smart Contracts [14] are self-executing code on a blockchain that automatically implements the conditions of an agreement between parties, thereby expediting procedures that are presently dispersed across multiple databases and ERP systems.

They are lines of software programmes, not contracts in the legal sense, that extend the utility of blockchains beyond just keeping track of financial transaction entries to automatically enforcing multiparty agreements. This is accomplished through Consensus protocols, which allow users to agree on the highlighted lines of action in response to an event.

### The following are some of the advantages of smart contracts:

- **Speed:** As smart contracts automate manual procedures, they increase the speed with which various business processes are completed.
- **Accuracy:** They assist in the automation of manual developments as well as the reduction of errors.
- **Lower Execution Risk:** Decentralized and immutable processes eliminate the possibility of manipulation, and the possibility of erroneous execution is too remote.
- **Lower cost:** When compared to traditional business procedures, contract implementation costs are lower since there is less human participation and fewer intermediaries.
- **New business models:** The development of novel business models, ranging from trustworthy peer-to-peer sharing to trading in a safe environment, is aided by new means of transactions and contract execution.
- **Avoiding the use of third-party intermediaries:** Smart contracts eliminate or reduce the use of third-party intermediaries and provide an alternative technological trust factor.

Smart contracts may be used for a variety of purposes, from determining loan eligibility to completing trade agreements and transfer agreements, thanks to the aforementioned advantages. Due to the need to maintain separate databases, all of these sorts of smart contracts were previously unfeasible. All of the parties involved can now validate the results in real-time, without the need for a third party to intervene.

**Smart contracts** make the most sense when a network of participants conducts frequent transactions and the counterparties undertake manual or redundant processes or duties for each transaction. Smart contracts, in a nutshell, automate the exchange of values between parties without the need for an intermediary or human intervention in the event of a contract.

Agents were heavily reliant on **traditional contracts** and agreements to complete paperwork and other legal requirements. The primary trust criteria for transferring money or other valuable assets were agents and go-betweens. Smart contracts, which are coded into the system to automatically execute activities based on a predetermined set of discussions between the parties, have replaced agents with the help of Blockchain. The trust is built up through the use of secure blockchain technology. The expense of managing the flow of value is dramatically reduced, as is the time it takes to complete the agreement. Similarly, when 'value' is exchanged using the Blockchain's distributed ledger technology, new blockchain chains are formed and kept permanently without the possibility of manipulation.
