## 11.1 INTRODUCTION

The emerging technique in the field of microarray technology helps scholars to determine a number of genes simultaneously, achieving essential information about the functions of cells [1]. The information collected during the study can be utilized in the prognosis and diagnosis of cancer. However, to extract characteristics of gene expression, data from large datasets and gene selection process remain a difficult process due to the presence of noise. To overcome the difficulty, a technique is required to select an appropriate set of genes having high classification accuracy [2].

This technique will save computational expenses as well as permit physicians to recognize a small fraction of the genes that are biologically specific to particular cancers. As shown in Figure 11.1, blood is composed of plasma along with three distinct cells (white, red, and platelets). Each one is responsible for a different task. The red blood cells (RBC) carry oxygen from lungs to the tissues and vice versa [4], whereas the white blood cells are responsible for fighting against disease. The platelets are responsible for controlling bleeding. On the right side of Figure 11.1, leukemia is a blood cancer where the growth of the white blood cells increases rapidly and interferes with the operation performed by platelets and RBC [5]. There are two main categories of leukemia that appear in the blood known as lymphocytic leukemia, which is caused due to lymphoid cells, and other one is myelogenous leukemia, which appears due to myeloid cells. The growth of these cells increases rapidly. Therefore, it is essential to treat these cells immediately.
