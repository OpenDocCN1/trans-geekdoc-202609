## B. Fine Grained Co-occurrences for Behaviour Fraud Detection

The authors in Wang *et al*, 2022 presented enhancement for behavioural modelling is the primary focus in utilizing fine-grained co-occurrences for behaviour-based fraud detection in digital payment systems. The system identifies complex correlations among transactional attributes utilizing data map that an organized manner facilitates a deeper analysis of how attributes interact, uncovering intricate patterns that may signal fraudulent activity. Additionally, the model employs heterogeneous embedding to enhance the representation of these relationships, improving the understanding of how different elements within the data are interconnected. Looking forward, an exciting opportunity for research lies in broadening enhancement methods to encompass a wider array of models. This could include generalized-based models, alongside population-level frameworks, enhancing detection accuracy across diverse user interactions.

## C. Heterogeneous Graph Transformer (HGT)

Tang et al. 2021 presented fraud detection in online review systems through HGT. This approach tackles issues of inconsistency and camouflage frequently. Label-aware Heterogeneous Graph Transformer (FAHGT) employs feature mapping developed to clear out noisy neighbours. This system uses a combined mechanism to integrate two neural modules, computation of final feature aggregation, where heterogeneous features are subsequently combined to form a more informative representation. FAHGT has demonstrated significant performance enhancements compared to baseline methods across various datasets, highlighting its effectiveness in capturing relationships within the data. Looking ahead, there is an aim to further develop the model's capabilities in dynamic graph where relationships evolve over time, allowing for its application in diverse fields, increasing its overall impact and utility.

## E. CC Fraud Detection Using Neural Factorization Autoencoder

CC fraud detection using deep learning models offers numerous advantages. These advanced algorithms can figure out various numbers of transaction data with remarkable speed, identifying intricate patterns and anomalies shows fraudulent activities. The efficiency of deep learning algorithms to learn from large datasets enables continuous enhancement in detection accuracy, making them particularly effective in adapting to evolving tactics. Moreover, improved architectures, such as CNNs and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), can capture both spatial and temporal relationships in the data, further enhancing manual oversight. The automated nature of these models also reduces the need for extensive manual, streamlining the fraud detection process. However, there are significant drawbacks to consider. The accuracy of deep learning simulations largely depends on the quality and quantity of training data; inadequate or biased datasets can lead to inaccurate predictions. High false positive rates, weakening sincere transactions and potentially trusting customers are some of the factors which leads to suffering the models. The computational complexity of Deep Learning (DL) algorithms face different issues such as interpretability which makes it difficult to understand the decision-making process. At last, the maintenance and implementation of the models are expertise specialized, intensive resource making and stabilized updates to praise new approaches. Balancing these advantages and disadvantages is essential for effective fraud detection (Sulaiman et al, 2024).

## F. Fraud Detection Method for Low-Frequency Transaction

Introducing novel approach to construct individual behavior, aimed at enhancing accuracy of low frequency users' behavior by integrating present transaction group behavior and transaction status. Initially, leverage of user's historical transactions exclusively, employing an optimal risk determination baseline. Subsequently, applying Density Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise (DBSCAN) clustering algorithm, it extracts behavioural characteristics from both current normal and fraudulent samples, culminating in the formulation of the collective behavior within the current transaction group. Finally, leveraging a sliding window mechanism for the creation of a refined transaction behavior profile for the user. The method presented in this paper demonstrates significant efficacy, particularly for low-frequency users, as it adeptly discerns fraudulent transactions while maintaining a minimal misjudgement rate for normal transactions. However, moving forward the focus will be directed towards addressing the challenge of online model updating, with the aim of further enhancing the effectiveness of the method (Zhang et al, 2020).
