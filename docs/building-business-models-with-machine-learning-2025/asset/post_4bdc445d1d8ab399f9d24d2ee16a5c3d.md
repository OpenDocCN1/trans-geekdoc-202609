
# How Business Model Transformation Has Changed

The rapid evolution of digital technologies has led to significant transformations that have a significant impact on businesses and organizations. For all businesses to secure long-term sustainable competitive positions in current markets, digital transformation of business processes and all other business segments is essential.(Hunger, D. L et al., 2008)

The following are some of the factors that are driving business transformation:

- 1. **Innovation in Technology**: Businesses must stay competitive by keeping up with the constant improvements in technology.
- 2. **Dynamic Markets**: Globalization, high competition, and evolving industry trends may prompt enterprises to modify their operations to maintain their competitiveness.
- 3. **Rules and Regulations**: Law and regulation changes may compel businesses to alter their operations and business procedures.
- 4. **Culture Within the Organization**: Businesses that wish to increase productivity, creativity, and performance frequently need to change the organizational culture.
- 5. **Strategic Objectives**: Businesses may start business transformation projects in order to accomplish strategic objectives including diversifying their company, breaking into new markets, or introducing new goods.

# Introduction to Business Analytics

There is an enormous amount of data out there, and more will come along in the future. Numerous businesses are already quite lost in a sea of data. Rather, smart organizations see this growth as a key element of the competitive advantage. Indeed, business analytics is one of the 'hot' topics nowadays. It is referred to as business analytics, and it is the application of data, information, statistical and quantitative methods to help management enhance their understanding of work and make more effective decisions.

# Definition

Business analytics is a process of transforming data into actions through analysis and insights in the context of decision making and problem solving. It is a process of ongoing exploration and analysis of both historical and current data to discover insights. This is done by applying a particular set of techniques, competencies, and procedures to gathering and examination of business-related data with the aim of spotting trends, patterns, and consumer behavior.

There are various procedures in business analytics:

- 1. Identifying the objectives.
- 2. Determining the most effective analytical approach.
- 3. Acquiring extensive data from several different systems.
- 4. Cleansing the information and combining it into a single location, like a data warehouse (Figure 2).

Business analytics can be defined more precisely with some common examples:

- 1. **Customer Segmentation**: Identifying key customer groups in retail, insurance, and credit card industries.

# Importance of Business Analytics

Business analytics (BA) includes several moving factors, but it is vital for the company in the initial place. Tools for business analytics assist in understanding what a company requires to make judgments. These choices will help boost market share, increase profitability, and give investors a better return. Technology undoubtedly affects lot of businesses, but when applied properly, business analytics (BA) may have a positive effect on your entire company even if some are unclear on what to do with massive amounts of data (Johnson, W. et al., 2008). - 1. Business analytics can spot vulnerabilities in a business's system.
- 2. Implement preventative actions to identify risk factors.

- 4. Using data-driven recommendation engines to move revenue from in-store to online.

# Roles and Responsibilities of Business Analytics

Business analytics has its own roles and responsibilities to analyze data to make decisions. Some of them include:

- Recognizing trends in data to find strategic possibilities.
- Recognizing potential challenges the company might encounter.
- Preparing a budget.
- Tracking the performance of business activities.
- Updating stakeholders on the status of business objectives.

# Elements of Business Analytics

Business analytics leverages the following elements:

- 1. **Data Aggregation**: To derive summary statistics such as mean, maximum, sum, and count of the data.
- 2. **Data Mining**: For data-driven decision-making, data mining uses a variety of statistical approaches including clustering, regression, and classification to solve issues, find patterns and trends in large data sets.
- 3. **Text Mining**: To find patterns and use text mining for evaluating competitor performance, examine text data from social media posts, blog comments, customer communications, and more.
- 4. **Data Visualization**: Construct meaningful visualizations to identify patterns and links within datasets for modeling, forecasting, and exploratory analytics.
- 5. **Forecasting**: Using past data to forecast future occurrences. For example, using historical data to predict retail sales or energy utilization to plan budget or project spending.
- 6. **Optimization**: To find the best-case scenario, use simulation techniques such as what-if analysis.
