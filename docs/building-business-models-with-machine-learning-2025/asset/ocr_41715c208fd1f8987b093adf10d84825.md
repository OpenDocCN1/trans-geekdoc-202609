In the modern world, data has turned into one of the huge commodities with an enormous potential for monetization (Cappa et al., 2021). Both businesses and individuals generate substantial data through online activities, which holds significant value across industries, necessitating monetization efforts. Data monetization is a process that leverages insights, patterns, and trends derived from material data to develop new products, sell data directly, or to drive strategic decisions. In this respect, data monetization provides a number of advantages in the field of businesses, such as revenue generation, improvement in decision-making via insights obtained from data analysis, operational efficiency enhancement, and innovations in product development (Farouki et al., 2020a; Johnson et al., 2017; Ritter et al., 2024). It also enables good consumer interaction and personalization, increasing customer satisfaction and loyalty (Johnson et al., 2017; Oofule & Benoycef, 2022). However, ethical concerns about privacy, consent and fairness must be paramount in data monetization deanders (Farouki et al., 2020a; 2020b; Oofule & Benoycef, 2022). Therefore, in the next section, we will investigate the issues that affect the data monetization process.

# Issues Affect Data Monetization

To be successful, data monetization must first address a number of issues. Organizations need consideration and controls of critical variables like the issue of privacy, data quality, and governance, complications arising out of legal and ethical concerns, and security-related to data (Farouki et al., 2020a; 2020b; Oofule & Benoycef, 2022). Some other issues that need consideration include fairness, openness, and potential for bias or discrimination in decisions based on data (Quach et al., 2022). In this sense, building consumer and stakeholder trust requires balancing business goals with moral obligations (Oofule & Benoycef, 2022). In addition, data security and cybersecurity must also be addressed, since the fact as data has become a valuable asset, it has become target for criminal activity and threats (Mahmood et al., 2024).

Alongside privacy and security issues, an essential aspect of data monetization is also data quality (Cappa et al., 2021). Poor, incomplete, and outdated data will result in erroneous insights and monetization decisions that are wrong. The organization, therefore, has to invest in the data governance frameworks that ensure integrity, correctness, and consistency of its data (Quach et al., 2022). The new data economy laws are straightforward and adhere to the fundamental concept that data owners are the ones who own it (GDPR, California Consumer Privacy Act).
