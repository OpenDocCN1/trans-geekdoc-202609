ESG is a comprehensive taxonomy that takes into account an organization's non-financial needs in relation to the environment, society, and governance, according to Johnson Jr. et. (2020). There are two main variables that contribute to ESG. Businesses all across the globe must abide by rules that represent a strong emphasis on meeting requirements and providing knowledge in subjects unrelated to finance (Krishnamorthy, 2021). Climate change, carbon emissions, and the use of finite resources like trash, water, and air are all of environmental concerns. In the continuous struggle, societal issues including employment, racial and social justice, child labor, health and safety, inclusion and diversity, human privacy, and the general well-being of workers and humanity are taken into account. The following are listed as governance factors by Johnson Jr. et. (2020): organizational goal; social impact; issues with compensation and corruption; the board's and management's independence, control, and evaluation. Technological solutions to address or amelior the fundamental problems at the core of the ecological crisis have emerged as a result of the increased global awareness and urgency surrounding (Falk & van Wynberg, 2023).

According to the UN (1987), sustainable development is the process of meeting present-day needs without endangering the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. With the 17 sustainable development goals, the UN created Agenda 2030 (Schrijver, 2008). It takes a strong commitment to corporate social responsibility, CSR, to achieve sustainable development. According to Zhao and Farihas (2023), corporate social responsibility (CSR) includes socially conscious investing, protecting stakeholders', interests, advancing sustainable development, and improving governance. Artificial intelligence (AI) has the potential to improve corporate social responsibility (CSR) programs by increasing their effectiveness and efficiency (Naqi, 2021). As the value of social and environmental responsibility has grown, sustainability has emerged as a top priority for businesses. A company that does not understand the importance of sustainability, will struggle to follow. A good example of an ESG case study is Unilever's Sustainable Living Plan, which was launched in 2010. Three fundamental tenets of sustainability were served as the foundation for the strategy: raising the quality of life for people inside the company's value chain, reducing the negative environmental consequences of products, and improving the health and well-being of one billion people. Unilever's broad sustainability project demonstrates the company's commitment to incorporating social, governance, and environmental considerations into its basic strategy. In order to make sure board membership and leadership matched the company's commitment to sustainability as stated by its governance practices, Unilever encouraged openness and appropriate hiring. This project shows how a large international consumer products company may successfully strike a balance between profitability and a steadfast dedication to environmental preservation, social responsibility, and open governance. According to Carney (2021), Unilever is the top company for exemplifying ESG initiatives.
