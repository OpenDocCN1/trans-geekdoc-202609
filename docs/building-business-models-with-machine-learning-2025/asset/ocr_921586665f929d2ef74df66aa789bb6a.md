# How to Extract Value From Big Data

As previously mentioned, an enterprise's ability to extract value from big data has turned a matter of competitiveness in the digital economy. These strategies range from strong collection and storage of data to using a host of analytics techniques and embedding the culture of data in the organization (Dubey et al., 2019; Mikalef et al., 2019). Crucially, the foundation for extracting value from big data lies in efficient data gathering and storage infrastructure capable of handling vast volumes from diverse sources (Ghashemhaei & Calic, 2019). In this sense, big data analysis requires modern analytics techniques to uncover insights and trends effectively (Mikalef et al., 2019).

Value extraction from big data and data-driven decision-making are closely intertwined (Wamba et al., 2015). Establishing a culture that values and supports data-insights is essential, ensuring decision-makers have the necessary skills and resources for effective data exploration (Frisk & Bannister, 2017). In this sense, incorporating data into the decision-making process enhances operational effectiveness, resource allocation, and business process identification, fostering agility and responsiveness in a dynamic business environment (Cappa et al., 2021; Erevelles et al., 2016; Mikalef et al., 2019). A data-centric culture involving a collaborative environment for data sharing and exploration, promoting data literacy across the organization, and providing personnel with data analytics training (Messeni Petruzelli et al., 2022). Ultimately, businesses that harness the capabilities of analytics and business intelligence systems enterprise-wide can seize upon significant value and seize precisely unnoted opportunities (Ghashemhaei & Calic, 2019).

In sum, businesses are on the outlook for an opportunity to get data that may turn out to be value to some other party, ensuring ownership and, consequently, control over the data. Confronted with growing expectations to place personal data under an umbrella of their control, governments and technology companies alike are adjusting their orientation to this expectation.
