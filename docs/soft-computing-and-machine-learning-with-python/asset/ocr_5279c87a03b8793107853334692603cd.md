According to Cohen (1988), the effect size is considered small when it is between .20 and .79 and large values are over .80. Only the difference in the Rasch score of the inductive reasoning development presented a large effect size (d = .88, p < .05).

As pointed before, the common language effect size indicates how often a score sampled from one distribution is greater than the score sampled from the other distribution if both are randomly selected (McGraw & Wong, 1992). Then, considering the common language effect size, the probability that a TDRI taken at random from the high achievement group is greater than a TDRI score taken at random of the low achievement group is 73.41. It means that out of 100 TDRI scores from the high achievement group, 73.31 will be greater than the TDRI scores of the low achievement group. The Rasch scores of the other tests have moderate effect sizes. Their common language effect size varied from 64.92% to 70.10%, meaning that the probability of a score taken at random at the high achievement group is greater than a score taken at random in the low achievement group is at least 64.92% and at most 70.10.
