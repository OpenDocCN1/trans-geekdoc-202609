# Introduction

Our human worth and values are defined by symbols and virtual identities – for example, email address, social media accounts, bank accounts, bitcoins, and so on. In the contemporary interconnected society; these symbols are represented by the “numbers” ($) in bank accounts, by “likes” in social media accounts, grades in the university systems, or scores maintained by the credit unions and background check agencies. These symbols and identities are translated and represented by data and information – whether individually or collectively – and the bits and bytes of data that reside on information systems in cyberspace. Due to connectivity and the user-friendly nature of the networking environment, data and information are left vulnerable and transparent in cyberspace, which leaves our human rights to privacy, worth, and values subject to manipulation and attack.

Cyberspace is that ubiquitous environment where communication occurs and is enabled through interconnected technologies.
