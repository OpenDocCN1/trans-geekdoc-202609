# 3.3.2 Zero-Knowledge Proof

One of the most interesting concepts in applied cryptography currently is the zero-knowledge proof. A zero-knowledge proof is a powerful example of cryptographic creativity, with applications ranging from unilateral proliferation to providing anonymous and stable transactions for public Blockchain networks. Although Blockchain is a reliable option for reaching the required privacy and protection in exchanging information, it has one drawback: transparency. As a result, zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) has become one of the most common methods for meeting transaction confidentiality requirements. ZKP is a procedure whereby the prover (P) will send a proof to the verifier (V) stating that he is aware of secret value without revealing other details. ZKF is modest in demonstrating declaring someone knows something by merely disclosing it. The task is to defend such ownership without disclosing information or any other details.
