# ANSWERING THE 7 WHS OF CRYPTOCURRENCIES

To start things off, we need to establish the groundwork by understanding what cryptocurrencies are and what you can do with them. We will go over the basics of cryptocurrencies in this chapter. ## WHAT IS CRYPTOCURRENCY

At a glance, cryptocurrency is just like any other digital currencies for your online banks. You are partly right. Cryptocurrencies do not have a physical presence. The entire system is completely online, supported by the users.

You see, cryptocurrencies are within decentralized networks. These networks use blockchain technology. Blockchain is a peer-to-peer (P2P) distributed ledger system that is upheld by the user’s computers. More on its function and inner workings later.

Cryptocurrency is classified as a virtual currency by the United States government and it is secured by cryptography. In other words, the major cryptocurrencies are virtually impossible to replicate or counterfeit.

The thing that sets cryptocurrencies apart from other fiat currencies, or government-issued currencies, is the fact that these do not usually come from any government or financial institution. While certain governments and banks are experimenting with cryptocurrencies of their own, the original and major cryptocurrencies do not come from any central authorities. Instead, they come from the users.

This defining feature is the main point of contention nowadays between crypto supporters and skeptics. Cryptocurrencies are technically immune to government interference or manipulation, but some argue that this lack of control is dangerous, to say the least.

The name “cryptocurrency” is derived from the encryption techniques implemented to protect the entire network from hackers. Blockchain technology powers the entire system, which is why you often hear the two terms used together. Think of it this way. Cryptocurrency is the money that you send and receive whereas blockchain technology is the entire financial system that allows the transaction to happen.

Since cryptocurrencies are independent of government and financial institutions, there have been concerns surrounding the subject. After all, the latest 2020 Bitcoin halving and its subsequent price surge gave many people the impression that Bitcoin is the digital gold. There is a lot of money here, so you can bet that many people are interested in the coins.

Many financial experts believe that blockchain and other relevant technology will ultimately revolutionize how transactions work. But their applications reach far beyond just financial technology. Blockchain is an information technology. In other words, it gives you a new way to store information. Sure, the technology is not new. It just implements technologies differently and that happens with a very secure and effective method. This technology has the potential to change how information is stored and disrupts many industries such as healthcare, law, and finance.

Cryptocurrencies have been bashed many times in the past and the arguments against them are largely the same. They always point to the potential use for illegal activities, volatility in the exchange rate, and the vulnerabilities of the underlying infrastructure, especially the cryptocurrency exchanges where cryptocurrencies can be bought and sold. While the blockchain network itself is virtually impenetrable, the crypto exchanges are particularly vulnerable and that is where most of the money is at.

Despite all of this, cryptocurrencies are praised for their transparency, divisibility, resistance to inflation, and portability.

## TYPES OF CRYPTOCURRENCY

The first-ever cryptocurrency was Bitcoin, which kickstarted it all. Bitcoin is still the most valuable and popular cryptocurrency today. This is closely followed by Ethereum, Bitcoin Cash, Litecoin, etc. Altcoin is a blanket term for all alternative cryptocurrencies besides Bitcoin.

Not all cryptocurrencies are created with the same purpose in mind. While Bitcoin is created to become a medium of transaction, Ethereum is also used to fund startups with their Initial Coin Offering (ICO) events. Investing your Ether into ICO is a viable way to make more money, and we will talk more about this later.

Bitcoin was born in 2009 developed by one Satoshi Nakamoto. It is not known who this person is or if it is a group of people, but we do know that their cryptocurrency is digital gold. As of March 2021, there were over 18 million Bitcoins in circulation. This means that the total market cap of Bitcoin is around $927 billion. The total market cap of all cryptocurrencies in existence is about $1.5 trillion, and Bitcoin holds over 60% of that share.

**Advantages**

Cryptocurrency makes transactions fast and cheap. This is possible because of the blockchain network which allows fund transfer between two parties without the need for an intermediary. For a normal bank transfer, your bank would verify the transaction before sending your money off to the other party. The way it works now is people are still required at the helm, especially when a large amount of money is being transferred internationally. This means higher transaction costs and more time needed to verify the transaction.

Cryptocurrency does not have such a tedious system. Sure, the transaction still needs to be verified but this job falls onto the miners, a common term when one discusses cryptocurrency. These miners contribute their computing power to verify transactions and they get paid a small amount of cryptocurrency for their work. The thing is that they get paid similar amounts regardless of how much money is being sent. The fee on crypto transactions is minuscule compared to the fees from your bank or other financial institutions.

Plus, the blockchain network allows for transactions to occur within minutes. Bitcoin takes about nine to ten minutes to verify, and other cryptocurrencies can do the job faster because the developers optimized their network.

**Disadvantages**

That said, the biggest perceived disadvantage for cryptocurrency is its anonymity. You see, users on the blockchain network do not use their real names. They use pseudonyms or usernames, and tracking down any specific user is a challenging task even for the United States government.

This blanket of anonymity means that criminals can engage in illegal activities without the government’s knowledge. They might use it to launder money or avoid tax. On the flip side, there have been cases where people would send their life savings into the blockchain network by buying up a cryptocurrency, and then move out of their dangerous country, only to get back their riches. That way, they don’t have to risk all their wealth by carrying it with them.

Certain journalists and whistleblower websites and outlets require an alternative source of income since they may be denied financial services by major financial institutions. WikiLeaks is one such website. These websites are located on the Dark Web, where anonymity is guaranteed. That means, cryptocurrency benefits whistleblowers and activists living under repressive governments.

It is also worth mentioning that the best way to stop crime would be to give up all forms of privacy. If the government knows everything, they can stop illegal activities long before it happens. But would you want to live in such a society? Evidently, any government should not have this much power. In fact, the NSA scandal with Edward Snowden illustrates just that.

The case with the TOR browser that allows you to access the Dark Web shows why anonymity has to be accessible for everyone or no one at all. The TOR browser is intended to be used by United States officials. It allows them to remain anonymous as they conduct their investigation. However, if only they have access to the technology, the website’s host would know that an FBI agent is checking out their website if they see an anonymous user. So, the U.S. government is forced to release the technology to the public.

And that is just the sad truth of anonymity. Either no one has it at all or everyone has to have it if we want anonymity to work. Unfortunately, that also includes criminals. That said, not all cryptocurrencies are equally private.

For instance, Bitcoin is not a good option for illegal businesses. The way the blockchain network is set up allows digital forensic analysts to track down criminals. This happened with the Silk Road website in the Dark Web, which was basically Amazon for drugs.

**Special Considerations**

The entire appeal behind cryptocurrencies is blockchain technology, which provides a new way to store information. It is used to keep an online ledger of all the transactions that happened within the network and recorded everything in chronological order. In addition, the ledger is distributed among all the users in the blockchain network.

These ledgers are independent of one another, but they would arrive at the same conclusion thanks to the consensus algorithm. In other words, the ledgers are a way to check and verify each other’s validity so that to alter an entry, one needs to alter every entry on the network, which is nigh impossible.

All transactions are recorded and added to a “block”, which would be added to the blockchain after it is confirmed by a miner. After the block has been confirmed, it is impossible to change it unless you alter every block in the blockchain. That means, it is not possible to forge transaction histories.

Many experts, though divided on the future of Bitcoin and Altcoins, all agree that blockchain technology has a serious potential for other uses where anonymity is guaranteed and forgery or fabrication is impossible. That means online voting, crowdfunding, etc.

However, since cryptocurrencies are completely virtual, you can lose your expensive coins simply by hardware failure. If you store your coins in your hard drive without a backup copy, they are gone if your hardware fails. If you were to lose your credit card or forgot your PIN, your bank can restore your access easily. The same cannot be said for your crypto wallet since no one else can access it.

**Criticism of Cryptocurrency**

The price for every product on the planet is based on supply and demand. High supply and low demand mean low prices. Low supply and high demand mean high prices. Cryptocurrencies are designed to be scarce so that their price would go up exponentially over time if they are successful.

The perfect illustration of this is Bitcoin. Bitcoin experienced a “halving” back in 2016. This halving is a way to control how much Bitcoin would be released into circulation. You see, there is a hard cap on how many Bitcoins there would be in the end. That cap is 21 million Bitcoins. New Bitcoins are created to reward the Bitcoin miners and the amount changes after every halving.

As the name suggests, Bitcoin halving is when the flow of Bitcoin being released into circulation is cut in half. This occurs every four years or so and is a way to control the price of the coins. When the halving happens, miners receive only half the coins they used to get.

This, in turn, creates scarcity and drives the price way up. The 2016 halving saw the coins going as high as $19,000 each before dropping back down to $7,000. However, the price before the halving was much lower than $7,000. In other words, just because you receive fewer coins from mining, their worth is more than doubled, so it’s a win-win for everyone.

Other than that, cryptocurrency is controlled by the people. There is no central authority to oversee the flow other than what’s been hardcoded in the network. That also means that the crypto trading market is highly volatile.

In foreign exchange or Forex, the major financial institutions exert influence over a currency’s value trends. So, the retail traders, the ordinary people, generally trade alongside the financial institutions in hope that they can cash in on the trend. Retail traders only trade in small amounts, so they don’t have any influence over the trends.

In cryptocurrency, there is no central authority. So, people who trade cryptocurrencies directly influence their prices. And since there are plenty of people in the system hoping to make money off cryptocurrencies, they cause a lot of swings in the price.

In short, cryptocurrencies are highly volatile and experts agree that this is a risky investment option. This is exactly what happened in the 2016 halving where people would flock to Bitcoin, buy up every coin they could get, and hope to sell when the price is at its peak. However, most people who joined the craze were not savvy traders and they bought up the coins when the price was already at the highest. Many saw their investments going down the drain and refused to sell, hoping to catch the next big break that never came. For this reason, cryptocurrencies are often regarded as a short-term fad or little more than a speculative bubble.

Another concern surrounding cryptocurrencies is that they are not tied to any material goods. Before the gold standard was obsolete, gold was the store of value. Then came fiat currencies, which is money issued by the government and its value is tied directly to the government’s economic and political power.

Cryptocurrencies possess neither of those so their value remains questionable to this day. One possible “material good” that cryptocurrencies are tied to is the production cost, or the energy and hardware needed to mine cryptocurrencies.

But there is yet another problem. Mining cryptocurrencies is a power-intensive process and the upfront cost can be too steep for many people. You need to have a beefy computer or a specialized tool to mine Bitcoin efficiently. Mining Bitcoin is a race, as in whichever miner gets to verify the transaction first would be awarded. This is the proof of work system.

This competition leads to the creation of ASIC, which is a piece of specialized equipment that allows miners to outmine all other miners who do not have this hardware, and there have been controversies around this expensive device. Casual miners looking to make a few bucks with their computers end up losing money because they could not compete, and they would still have to pay for the high power bill every month.

If you’re interested in mining Altcoins such as Ethereum, you might have a better shot since some cryptocurrencies use a different method called proof of stake, where you stake some of your coins to show your willingness to verify the transaction. Everyone gets their fair share of work, even the small-timers, and everyone is happy.

Power consumption is a serious issue and mining crypto is not helping the issue here. At first, people used graphics cards and processors to handle the mining. But these tools are not optimized for the job since they are created for other purposes.

The Bitcoin craze created a market that arguably left everyone worse off. Certain tech companies such as Nvidia started creating dedicated GPU for miners. It is a GPU with no output port and is intended to be used for mining. The problem with this move is that it puts more strain on the global supply of silicone and chips, which is already stretched thin as it is. This affected even car manufacturers.

Then there is the problem of security. Of course, the blockchain network behind every cryptocurrency is highly secure, but they are not the only thing within the cryptocurrency ecosystem. There are crypto exchanges where you can buy and sell cryptocurrencies and you can store your coins in digital wallets. While the blockchain network is almost impervious to hacking, the exchanges and wallets are not.

Crypto exchanges are online platforms where you can buy and sell cryptocurrencies. These websites are usually the target of hackers and there have been successful cyberattacks that resulted in millions of dollars lost. Crypto wallets are not 100% safe either unless you use hardware wallets, which in itself brings some other risks.

Regardless, many observers believe that cryptocurrencies have the potential to revolutionize the financial world. Even if it fails to do so, blockchain technology would be beneficial, nonetheless.

**Blockchain Use Cases**

Blockchain technology allows for data storage on a peer-to-peer network that is very resilient against cyberattacks. This seemingly simple function gives blockchain technology a wide range of applications that we did not know we needed until now.

**Decentralized Finance**

One of the most common uses for blockchain technology is decentralized finance, which is a breath of fresh air from traditional and centralized financial systems. Decentralized finance (DeFi) uses peer-to-peer finance that is enabled by decentralized technologies created on a blockchain network.

This is where all the cryptocurrencies come from. Interestingly enough, it does not have to apply to cryptocurrencies alone. Banks and other financial institutions can make use of blockchain technology to enable the transfer of fiat currencies with all the benefits that blockchain technology provides.

The most cited use case for DeFi would be on the Ethereum network where you could run smart contracts to automatically execute certain protocols exactly as programmed, without the need of a third party overseeing the whole operation. DeFi has no centralized infrastructure and is instead powered purely by codes running on decentralized infrastructure on a blockchain network.

There are a number of benefits that DeFi provides that centralized financial systems just cannot. For one, it makes use of smart contracts that automate certain processes. The blockchain network is also tamper-proof, and security is something everybody wants when it comes to money.

The blockchain network also allows for interoperability as protocols and applications on the network can interact with one another. Therefore, we are looking at a robust ecosystem of apps and smart contracts on a given blockchain network.

It is also very accessible. Another label for it would be “permissionless”, but this does not mean people can access your data without your permission. This just refers to the fact that anyone with a crypto wallet and internet connection can access the blockchain network, oftentimes without any minimum amount of crypto in their wallet, from anywhere across the globe. Some contents are not accessible because of government censorship or it is just locked to certain regions, so blockchain allows you a greater level of accessibility. Plus, you can access it without giving anyone permission. No one can bar you from accessing the blockchain network.

The way the blockchain network is designed allows for a great level of transparency as well. Every transaction is broadcasted and verified by the miners and recorded on the ledgers that anyone can access. Moreover, DeFi protocols are created with open-source that anyone can see, so that people can have a complete understanding of what really is going on behind the scene for apps on the blockchain network.

**Non-Fungible Tokens**

Non-fungible tokens (NFTs) are tokens that are used to represent ownership of items with unique qualities. That means, intellectual properties or real estate. Only one person can hold this token and this ownership is recorded and protected by the blockchain network. No one can alter the ownership record or fabricate a new NFT into existence.

On the flip side, fungible items are defined by their value, not their unique properties. Suppose that a single Bitcoin is worth 1,000 USD. Both Bitcoin and USD are fungible because one Bitcoin or 1,000 USD is exchangeable for another one Bitcoin or 1,000 USD.

Each NFT has a unique identifier. That means, it is digitally unique and so no two NFTs are the same. Every NFT has to have an owner, and the ownership record is on the public ledger that anyone can check to verify. On the Ethereum network, NFTs can be used with apps on the platform. You can sell an NFT ticket to an event in exchange for another NFT. Plus, content creators can sell their work and retain ownership rights over their own work. They can claim royalty over resold work directly because they have the tokens to verify their ownership of the content. Another interesting application for NFT is that you can use it as collateral if you use a decentralized loan service.

Each NFT has a special identifier and it is not interchangeable with another token, at least at a 1:1 ratio. One Bitcoin is exactly the same as another Bitcoin, but 1 NFT is not equal to another NFT. You can buy and sell NFT, but the original creator would claim some resale royalties. Alternatively, you can hold onto your NFT forever. Your digital or physical asset that is tied to the NFT would be yours forever because you can use your NFT to prove your ownership.

That said, NFTs are primarily used for digital content. Ownership over digital content outside of blockchain is stored on servers that are controlled by various centralized institutions and you have no choice but to hope that they keep their words. With NFTs, you have full control over your intellectual property.

The list of applications goes on and on. But you can easily see how useful NFTs could be in protecting content creators’ revenues and intellectual properties. It also has interesting applications for physical items as well, namely real estate. The possibility is quite literally endless for NFTs.

What are these decentralized servers? Those are miners’ devices. Miners would then be rewarded in the form of tokens or cryptocurrency.

**Smart Contracts**

Smart contracts are essentially programs slapped onto a contract. It is created to take in certain data and execute certain clauses of the contract should certain conditions be met. At its core, smart contracts are the if-then Excel function. Nothing more, nothing less.

Again, this application is very simple in nature. But its utility is very versatile. You can use it to trade product movement in a supply chain, to confirm identity digitally, for voting, healthcare, food safety, intellectual property, the list goes on and on.

The reason why smart contracts are so desirable is that it operates on the blockchain network. That means, tampering with the data is nigh impossible unless you break the whole thing. It takes away a third party who has to oversee the whole thing. It just automates the entire process for you. With a bit of tinkering, you just need to feed it some input and it will spit out the right output. It is that simple.

## HOW CRYPTOCURRENCIES WORK

In this section, we will get into the details of the workings behind cryptocurrencies.

We will start with the ledger. As the name suggests, the ledger records all transactions that happen within the blockchain network and organizes them all into chronological order. The entries are completely public, meaning that anyone can view them. Anonymity here comes in the form of username or pseudonyms. So, while you can see which user sent how many coins to whom, you do not know the real person behind those usernames. This gives you the perfect balance between anonymity and transparency.

The entries on the ledger cannot be changed, either. The only way to do that would be through a 50% + 1 confirmation from other ledgers in the system. Having a majority of ledgers that agree would automatically update all ledgers to have the same entries. This is difficult to pull off since each user has their own independent ledger that uses a consensus algorithm. While all ledgers agree on updating their entries after a transaction has been verified by a miner, fabricating a transaction is nigh impossible.

We also need to talk about transaction verification. Suppose that you want to dabble in cryptocurrency. You buy some Bitcoins through a crypto exchange. Then, you decide to spend it. The entire process is completed within minutes, but a lot happened behind the scenes.

When you decided to spend your coins, the transaction requires verification. In other words, the transaction is not official yet and it is up to the miners to verify it. The miners’ computers or devices have to solve an algorithm problem to complete the verification. They are looking for the key to confirm the transaction. Once that is completed, the transaction is recorded in the ledger and becomes part of the blockchain. The miners would be awarded a modest sum of Bitcoin for their work.

In other words, they hold onto the coins and hope that the price will go up so they can cash out when the price is much better. It’s riskier than converting the coins at a flat rate, but there is a lot of money to make even if the price difference is only $10. For this reason, companies are slowly adopting cryptocurrencies into their payment methods. This also expands their reach to the cryptocurrency community. ## ON THE SUBJECT OF COINS AND TOKENS

When you talk about cryptocurrencies, you might hear the term “token” being used now and again, and it can be confusing when you learn that you can buy and sell these tokens as well. It’s even more baffling when people sometimes use the term “coins” and “tokens” interchangeably. However, both are totally different things. So, what’s the difference?

Let’s start with coins. Coins or cryptocurrencies are digital assets that have their own blockchain. So, each cryptocurrency, or coin, has its own blockchain network that they operate in. Bitcoin only works on the Bitcoin blockchain. Litecoin only works on the Litecoin blockchain. Each blockchain is independent of one another.

You can send and receive coins but there is nothing physical about it other than the computers working to send the data around a gigantic global database. This database is the blockchain network that keeps track of all transactions.

You can use your coins the same way you spend money. You can send and receive it from someone. You can also hold onto it and sell it off at a later date and pocket the profit. Or, you can also buy products and services using cryptocurrency.

While Bitcoin is intended to be used as money, Ether and other cryptocurrencies are created for other purposes.

For instance, Ether is used to facilitate transactions on the Ethereum network. This is important since most tokens are developed on Ethereum. So even though you are sending or receiving tokens, the sender still has to spend a bit of Ether, which goes to the miners.

NEO is a stake in a wallet to earn a dividend. This is also known as GAS. Tokens built on NEO can be sent and received the same way on Ethereum, and the GAS is a transaction fee.

Then there’s DASH. It’s a coin, but if you own enough of them, you can vote on important decisions for the entire Dash network. Let’s say that someone posted an idea to upgrade the DASH network. So, those who own a large amount of Dash coins can decide whether to upgrade or not. So, owners of Dash coins can decide what direction the project goes.

So, how about tokens? Tokens are different from coins. They are created on an existing blockchain. Thanks to smart contracts, the most popular platform for tokens would be Ethereum. All tokens created on Ethereum are called ERC-20 tokens.

Smart contracts are computer programs or transaction protocols created to automate the execution, control, or documentation of legally relevant events and actions. This is all based on the terms and agreement of the contract.

As mentioned before, Ethereum is not the only platform for creating tokens. You have NEO, Lisk, Waves, and Stratis. They have different names for their tokens. For instance, tokens created on the NEO platform are known as NEP-5 tokens.

Creating tokens is very easy, although you still need some coding experience. You just have to have some native coins to get started so the miners can facilitate the transaction process.

Most tokens are created to be used with decentralized apps such as dApps. Developers creating the tokens have complete control over how many tokens they want to have and where they would go after they are created. Upon creation, they would need to spend some native coins.

After the tokens are created and distributed, token owners can use them to activate features on specific apps. For instance, Musicoin gives you the ability to access their library of music, videos, and songs.

A crypto exchange called Binance also has its own token which gives users a 50% discount on their fees.

Tokens can also be used to represent a physical object. Suppose you want to use a smart contract to sell your house. Of course, you cannot put your house into the smart contract, at least not physically. So, an alternative would be to use a token to represent the house.

WePower or WPR reflects that usage perfectly. Their project is a dApp and their tokens represent electricity. It’s not entirely physical, but you get the idea. Users can buy and sell electricity using smart contracts. Each WPR token represents a certain amount of energy.

The question then is, why use tokens?

First of all, it’s the simple fact that developers do not have to create an entire blockchain. They can piggyback on an existing blockchain and use their limited resources on something else. They benefit from the security and resilience of the native blockchain they are on, such as Ethereum.

If they were to create their own blockchain, it would be expensive and it can take forever. You see, if you create a blockchain, you would need miners. Lots of them. And since miners flock to the more popular cryptocurrencies, you can see how that is not happening.

Plus, a blockchain’s resilience against cyberattacks depends entirely on the number of users. You want to have many computers connected to a blockchain rather than having thousands of weak and centralized blockchain networks. Overall, it’s just expensive, tedious, and nigh impossible.

This problem can be solved if you bite the bullet and pay a bit of coin of the native blockchain network and work on your tokens from there.

With all that said, how does a token work in practice? Let’s take Civic. Civic uses CVC tokens and is an app that contains your private information. This information is fully encrypted so that even if your device is stolen, your private information remains safe.

The Civic app is intended for those who would have their identities checked often. It can be a hassle having to hold onto the paperwork and other things, so the Civic app puts everything in one convenient place for you. It is cheaper, more reliable, and efficient.

Suppose that you go to South-East Asia on a holiday, then you might need to confirm your identity more than once. The first time would be the airport. If Civic happens to be a partner of that airline, you would have the option to scan a QR code. The app would then send all the necessary details to the company. No more fumbling with papers. It’s all inside your phone. Of course, to confirm that the information indeed comes from you, you are required to do a fingerprint or an iris scan.

You can do this whenever you need to verify your identity. So long as the destination is a partner with Civic, then the entire process is a breeze. Your information can be verified via the blockchain network.

You might be thinking that not many companies would partner up with Civic, and you would be correct. It is still rather small in scope right now, but as the app continues to be used, the more trust it gathers. Eventually, it will reach a point where everyone has heard about the app.

But that’s not the entire system behind the CVC tokens. They have another role to play. CVC is used up in the verification process. The verifiers of IDs such as governments, banks, and other trusted sources would be paid CVC tokens for their KYC (know-your-customer) checks. The records would be stored on the blockchain.

The user also receives a certain number of CVC when that happens. The idea is to encourage you to continue using Civic as the companies that need to confirm your identity need to buy more tokens from you, the user. So, this system creates an interesting economy where everyone is rewarded for their participation.

Since CVC tokens are developed on the Ethereum blockchain, the transaction requires some Ether to fuel the transaction process. That said, the Civic app uses CVC tokens exclusively. You cannot use Bitcoin, Ethereum, or NEO to access their services.

## CRYPTOGRAPHY MADE SIMPLE

Cryptocurrency got its name as a cross between currency and cryptography. Although cryptocurrency boasts bulletproof encryption to protect data, not many people understand what cryptography is and how is it linked to cryptocurrency.

Let’s start with the basics. Cryptography is the practice of encrypting information in a way that only the people “in the know” would be able to read it. That way, even if the information falls into the wrong hands, no one from outside would know what it means. It’s an ancient technique that is still used today, from Julius Caesar communicating with his generals using ciphers, to the Allied forces using encrypted messages so the Axis could not read it, even if they could intercept the message.

Cryptography is needed to protect sensitive information or data from being stolen by someone who isn’t supposed to see it. The data can be anything from finance to military information. There are plenty of reasons why people would want to keep their private information private.

As you can probably tell, cryptography can take on many forms. There are many ways to scramble data or information in a way that the recipient can decipher it. Before modern technology, people came up with creative ways to encrypt information. For instance, BSELUEA can go through a cipher and give you BLUE SEA. It’s a simple task of rearranging the letters in a specific way. Some would go as far as to create an entire alphabet.

Nowadays, cryptography becomes much more complex thanks to computers. They can apply very complex mathematical technology to encrypt data. These technologies are now used for cryptocurrencies. But no matter how complex it gets, it always works on the same basic principle. That is to hide the actual information so that only authorized people can access it.

So, how does cryptography apply in cryptocurrency?

Cryptography is used in the blockchain network to protect the transactions, control the registration of new transactions, and verify the transfer of assets. To accomplish these goals, cryptocurrencies rely on public key cryptography.

Public key cryptography gives users two separate keys. One is called “public key”, which is used to receive the coins. To access the coins, you would need a “private key”. Think of it like one of those blue mailboxes you find in public places. The public key would be the box itself since anyone can put mail in there. However, to access the mail in the box, you would need the key, and only the mailman has that key. This key to the box is the private key.

The keys are normally around 30 characters long and are a jumbled mess of letters and numbers. It does not mean anything. The public key is also called the public address since that jumbled mess serves as an address so people can send you coins.

These keys are linked to your digital wallet, where your coins are actually stored. So, anyone can send you coins into your wallet using your public key. However, to access the wallet, you would need a private key, which you should not share with anyone.

Public key cryptography is a simple concept, but pulling it off is an impressive technological advancement. Even if cryptocurrency fades into obscurity in the near future, the technologies it leaves behind would benefit everyone. This cryptography is one of those technologies that will change the online payment industry.

Though there are vulnerabilities with public key cryptography, it is all just human errors and there is not much technology can do to compensate for that. For instance, people could lose their private keys. If so, they might have no way of recovering the funds in their crypto wallet. Sometimes, they might accidentally reveal their private key to someone else and they could just access the wallet and steal everything.

Again, this is just human errors but there are ways to prevent this from happening by setting up two-factor authentication, backing up the digital wallet, and keeping spare private keys. You can write your private keys on a piece of paper stick it somewhere that you know will be safe. Have a couple of copies in different places just to be safe.

## INVESTING CRYPTO AT YOUR OWN RISK

Before you invest in cryptocurrencies, you need to understand the associated risks.

**Price Volatility and Manipulation**

As mentioned before, cryptocurrencies experience very wild price swings. There are major price surges that are soon followed by crashes. These seemingly random events leave experts and investors alike amazed and baffled. Your profit today could be your loss tomorrow. The price swings so wildly that it is difficult for anyone to have the same confidence as the stock market or Forex. The volatility is oftentimes random and unpredictable, so even the strategies you meticulously planned out might not work because of factors outside your control.

Speaking of factors, the volatility in crypto prices comes from three sources: market manipulation, speculation, and sentiment. Since the cryptocurrency market is largely unregulated and anonymous, the price becomes very volatile. Feelings run high. Influencers can manipulate prices just by tweeting. Price manipulation hasn’t been widely proven yet because of the anonymous nature of cryptocurrency, but it is not hard to see that it is there. The most common price manipulation tactics would be pump and dumps and shilling.

**Lack of Regulations**

The lack of a regulatory framework contributes to the problem. Governments across the globe are on the fence about the issue. Some outlawed cryptocurrencies whereas others are starting to accept them.

For one, having a legal framework would be a sign that the government views cryptocurrencies favorably. It is the missing link that Bitcoin and Altcoins need to achieve widespread adoption. However, in the business world, you want to have as few regulations as possible so you can trade and make a profit easily. Too many laws mean more restrictions that could cut into your margins.

At the same time, if there’s a lack of regulation, then the legal outlook would be uncertain. It means that the government does not officially support the coins and they may remain obscure and function as little more than speculative vehicles.

The current legal framework for cryptocurrencies is complex, haphazard, and disorganized. The biggest concern right now would be taxes, as expected. It is all about the money, after all. The lack of regulations in this area or a “regulatory greyness” means that investors are uncertain of what tax obligations are required. They want to know how much they should set aside for taxes as well as what records they need to keep. Failing to meet these obligations comes with a very hefty price and investors do not want to make the IRS unhappy. As the Joker once said, he’s more willing to fight Batman rather than fight the IRS.

Though regulations are slow to catch up to technology, when there is a lot of money on the line, the pacing is much better. In this case, regulations are catching up quickly to cryptocurrencies. Authorities in many countries are taking appropriate steps to introduce regulations. Research papers are being written, standards are set, etc.

The first country to start building up a robust regulatory framework for cryptocurrency is Switzerland. Their idea is to create as few rules as possible so that the market remains free, but provide enough rules that go with other legal frameworks so that companies know what obligations they need to fulfill through a “sandbox” so that startups can play around with their innovations within a controlled environment.

Singapore and Britain are also starting to experiment with their blockchain and crypto regulatory environment. This environment allows companies to experiment under relaxed regulations and licensing requirements. For the United States, there is a study on exchanges created by the New York Attorney General’s Office.

**Market Adoption**

Bitcoin strives toward widespread adoption, but there are a number of things that are holding it back such as regulatory concerns, market volatility, the lack of understanding from the public, among other things. That and the fact that cryptocurrencies and the blockchain technology that powers them are still new, there is a chance that cryptocurrency would not be broadly adopted. By that point, cryptocurrencies would lose all of their value. While no one really knows when the deadline would be, cryptocurrencies need the law, technological improvements, and institutions to back them up as soon as possible if they were to grow in scale.

**Security, Custody, and Consumer Rights**

Although the blockchain network is secure, there are other points of vulnerability in its ecosystem. Users need to take all measures to store their cryptocurrencies and other crypto assets or risk losing it all.

Crypto exchanges are viewed as one of the most unsafe places to store your cryptocurrencies. Most exchanges give you a wallet to store your coins, but there have been cases where they have been hacked and all the coins were stolen. Of course, most exchanges you see today would talk about their top-notch security and that your coins would be safe with them. But it is better to be safe than sorry and store your coins in a hardware wallet.

To make matters worse, lost or stolen coins or assets cannot be recovered. Tracking down where your coins went is nigh impossible. If you send your coins to someone by mistake, there is no way to reverse the transaction either. The best you can do is to ask them nicely to send you back the coin. That is, assuming that you know who got your coins in the first place.

Because there is no intermediary, cryptocurrencies do not have central or official safeguards or insurances. How much you get from rebates on lost investment depends entirely on the organization you are doing business with.

Thankfully, there are now custody solutions that allow financial institutions to act as an intermediary in such a transaction. This is one of the things that cryptocurrencies desperately need to gain trust and receive widespread adoption.

The system works by having a third party holding onto the cryptocurrencies on behalf of the trading clients. Once the products or services are given and all are satisfied, then the coins would be sent to the provider. The basic system includes three parties. The client, the provider, and the mediator. The client purchases goods or services from the provider, but place the coins with the mediator until the provider delivers the goods or services. Once done, it takes two out of three approvals from the parties involved to release the funds. If there are any disputes, then the mediator may step in and resolve the conflict.

This gives investors an added level of security when investing their expensive coins. If things go south, they can get their coins sent back to them. One may argue that the client can abuse their power and demand their coins returned to them after they got the product or service. Of course, the mediator has to be trusted by both parties to make a sound judgment of the situation.

The custody solutions are believed to be the leap cryptocurrencies need to kickstart the entry of institutional capital into the cryptocurrency industry. Once institutions start to invest using cryptocurrencies, it will start to gain trust and approval and retail investors would soon follow suit.

Coinbase and a few other crypto exchanges are starting to offer their custody products. A multinational investment bank, Citigroup, also said that they would provide crypto custody solutions to institutional investors. They created Digital Asset receipt, which allows institutional investors to invest in cryptocurrencies while still following regulations and have a safe way to pull out if the investment goes awry. Fidelity and their subsidiary Fidelity Digital Asset Services also aim to do the same thing.

**Exiting the Market**

Of course, when you enter a market, you have to think of your exit strategy well in advance. You want to know when you want to get out so you can cash in on your profits. The crypto market’s off-ramps pose another problem for many investors. It is with the exchange rate. Most exchange only allows you to withdraw in USD. If you’re in the United States, that is fine. Other exchanges might offer JPY, GBP, or EUR, but your options are nonetheless limited.

The problem is that while blockchain allows for cheap and fast transactions, you are still subject to the inefficiency and slow speed of major financial institutions once you are out of the system. Most exchanges have a high minimum amount if you want to withdraw your fiat currencies.

To make matters worse, they also only accept major cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin or Ethereum. Once you get past that hurdle, you also need to wait on the exchange to send the money over to your bank. This is outside the blockchain network, so you have to put up with the tedious process that can take up to months to complete.

To this day, some exchanges are allegedly withholding funds without a clear reason and most banks are still wary of accepting cash from the sale of cryptocurrency. All this factor means is that investors have to put up with the exchange rates, fees, and other risks if they happen to be using exchanges that lack transparency.

Of course, if they were to invest using fiat currencies directly, they would still have to deal with similar hurdles, but they won’t have to worry about the initial steps they had to do with cryptocurrencies.

## GET YOURSELF A CRYPTO WALLET

We have mentioned this time and again since the start of the book: digital wallet/crypto wallet. What is it? The wallet is an app that allows you to store your coins. It also doubles as a way to receive coins.

Cryptocurrency wallets are apps that you can run on your phone or computer. But if the idea is too abstract for you and you want to have a little something more tactile, you can get a hardware wallet or a device that runs the wallet app for you.

Digital wallets do not work the way you think they do. You might think that your coins would be stored in your crypto wallet as if they are files on a USB drive. But that is not the case. The data stored on the wallet just points to where the actual coins are in the blockchain network and the public ledger.

That said, why do you need a wallet? The whole idea behind Bitcoin and Altcoins is to act as a decentralized currency so that the common people have full control over their money. The money you put into the bank would be invested into something else to turn a profit. Basically, your bank is borrowing money from you so they can do other businesses. Except, this time, they control how much they have to give back in interest.

So, what you get is an IOU or a promise that the bank would give back your money by demand. The only problem is that’s not always the case. There were instances when the economy reaches a point where banks just do not have enough money to give back to their customers. So, a lot of people ended up losing money.

Bitcoin and Altcoins give you more power over your money. Since they are also steadily increasing in value, investing in cryptocurrencies might not be a bad idea. Plus, with such a valuable digital asset in your hands, you want to make sure that you have a safe place to store your coins.

You might be wondering that since your crypto exchange would give you a wallet anyway, would it be adequate enough. You can store your coins there, but it is best if you do not. Exchanges can be hacked and all the coins there could be whisked away in an instant.

Ideally, you want to have two wallets: hot and cold. A hot wallet is connected to the internet and a cold wallet is usually offline. Having a wallet on an exchange is fine, but you should only have coins in there when you want to use them. Once done, you should move all your coins to a cold wallet.

A cold wallet is a hardware wallet and is disconnected from the internet. This is perhaps the safest way to store your coins. Hackers cannot hack what is not connected to the internet, after all. That said, maintaining a hardware wallet can be difficult. You need to keep your private keys safe and accessible only to you.

Other than that, you need to practice all the online safety procedures such as activating two-factor authentication and not clicking on suspicious links. We have covered this extensively in the first book.

Another thing to keep in mind is that certain wallets can only hold a certain cryptocurrency. A Bitcoin wallet, for example, can only hold Bitcoins, not Ethereum. So, you can only send and receive Bitcoins. There are wallets out there that allow you to store different coins, but make sure that they are compatible with the coins you intend to own.

## MINING CRYPTOCURRENCY

If you are not into trading cryptocurrencies or investing in them, then you can always try to dabble in cryptocurrency mining. As mentioned before, miners are always in demand as they are the ones who allow the whole blockchain network to function. Since there is no central authority to verify transactions or anything, the task falls to them.

Every blockchain network has to have miners. They use their computers to solve complex mathematical problems to verify transactions. As a token of appreciation, the miners are awarded a small amount of cryptocurrency. How many coins you get from mining depends on the coin.

We have mentioned Proof of Work before, but there are two types of mining. There is the Proof of Work (PoW), and Proof of Stake (PoS). These two types of mining verify transactions and update the ledger using consensus algorithms.

**Proof of Work**

To put it simply, the Proof of Work consensus model requires miners to solve mathematical problems. This is the system for Bitcoin, but it requires a lot of computational power. What are they calculating for? Miners generate what is called “hash”, which turns an input into a random string of letters and numbers. The idea is to find the right string to match the target.

As you can see, the probability is pretty low. However, given how fast computers perform these calculations in a given second, it is not impossible. In fact, miners’ rigs can generate trillions of computations every second. So, it takes them an average of nine to ten minutes to find the right one.

The problem is that it only awards the people who completed the verification first. That means, whoever generates the right one first takes the coins, so not everyone is a winner here. And you can say that the competition is fair, but it isn’t between humans and their abilities. It depends completely on people having better and more expensive hardware.

For instance, there are ASICs in the Bitcoin network and they are the point of controversy. ASICs are specialized devices that do a very good job in mining coins. They are expensive, sure, but they mine coins at a rate with which no other machine can really compete. So, their presence in the ecosystem means that no one other than the people who own ASICs can make a living off mining coins.

Then, there are other problems with the system. The first and most obvious one would be energy consumption. Mining Bitcoin consumes the same amount of energy on average as that of Switzerland because of the PoW system. As more miners join the ecosystem to mine for coins, the energy use is only going to go up.

Then there is the centralization element of this system, despite the fact that cryptocurrencies are all about decentralization. For one, if a mining entity can take over 50% +1 of the entire blockchain network’s hash rate, then the owner can double-spend, block transactions, or bend the rule temporarily. That is highly unlikely for larger cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin, however.

Sometimes, the problem isn’t concentrated on one individual. The system is somewhat centralized because a few mining pools can control up to around 50% of Bitcoin’s computational power already. As of now, developers are working to alleviate this issue.

**Proof of Stake**

Proof of Stake works by having the miners, in this case “validators”, stake some of their Ether. That amount would be frozen and cannot be used for anything while the validators participate in the proof-of-stake process. When they stake their Ether, they make themselves available to be chosen to propose a new block to be added to the chain.

The selection process is entirely random, of course. Once chosen, other validators attest that they have seen the block. Once enough attestations for the block are gathered, the block is added to the blockchain.

Validators are awarded when they attest to a new block and say that it is accurate and it follows the rule. That is it. Then, they would be awarded a small number of coins. The one chosen to propose a block would also be awarded.

Of course, one can easily see how this system can be abused. This is why there are punishments in place for breaking the rule. If the validator proposes a block with false data history, or transaction, their stake would be slashed and they would be banned from the network. But for smaller infractions such as going offline when they are chosen to propose a block, there are equally smaller penalties.

The biggest advantage of the PoS consensus model is the fact that it consumes less energy. In fact, you do not have to worry much about the initial investment in hardware or the power bill. This makes this mining method a lot more accessible to an average person. You just need a working computer and a solid internet connection.

The only investment you need to make is to invest a certain amount of crypto to be qualified to be a validator. But if it is still too much for you, there are even staking pools. These are groups of people who put together their coins and this pool would stake on everyone’s behalf. The reward would then be distributed proportionally to the percentage of their contribution to the pool. So, you can just chip in a little bit of coin and you can get started with them. Just don’t expect to get paid much if you only contribute a little bit of coin to the pool.

This also opens up the door for participation and allows many people a new way to generate revenue. Considering that you do not need much to get started in PoS-based cryptocurrency, many people would jump into mining here. Since everyone is chosen to propose and attest to new blocks at random, everyone has an equal chance to earn their keep.

Another thing worth mentioning is that it is scalable, meaning that you stand a higher chance of being chosen if you stake more of your coins. Of course, if something goes wrong, you would lose your coins, but you shouldn’t run into this problem if you follow the rules.

Increasing how many coins you stake is also interesting. For Ethereum at least, the starting cost would be 32 ETH or Ether. Staking this many Ethers would give you “one” validator. If you want to stake more, you need to dump in another 32 ETH, which would give you another validator. You cannot stake 33, or 34, or 63 ETH. You need to do it in multiples of 32, each of which gets you one validator. If you have multiples of validators, your chance increases accordingly. Having 10 validators boosts your chance of being picked tenfold compared to having only one validator.

This is also another reason why staking pools are popular. People chip in bits of Ether and the aggregate Ether the pool has can get them many validators. This means, a staking pool can have tens or even hundreds of validators running at the same time, which massively improves the chance for the pool to get picked.

GPU mining is possibly the most popular crypto mining method today. This is because GPU rigs can churn out coins at a respectable speed without spending too much on them. A quick Google search for cryptocurrency mining and chances are that you will find GPU rigs among the top results.

GPU mining is far more efficient and cheaper than CPU mining. Of course, the initial investment in a GPU mining rig can be costly, but it can process a lot more than your computer’s processor alone.

The introduction of a new line of mining graphics cards caused outrage among gamers who are looking to upgrade their computers. The problem here is that, at the moment of writing, there is a global shortage of chips that are needed to build GPUs.

Nvidia’s 3000 series of graphics cards are already snatched up by scalpers who would resell them at marked-up prices. So, the supply for those graphic cards is already running low. Instead of putting more supplies into manufacturing more graphics cards, Nvidia decided to create mining graphic cards instead with the little supply they had. This is seen as a move to latch onto the latest cryptocurrency trend and people are not very happy about it.

Nvidia is also working to discourage people from using their latest 3000 series graphic cards to mine by simply reducing the hash rate, which basically slows down the mining speed of those cards. So, if you are in the market for mining graphic cards, make sure to look at the hash rate.

**Cloud Mining**

But let’s say that your computer sucks. Your Intel processor from the early 2000s can’t run the latest Windows Media Player, Google Chrome, and Microsoft Word at the same time without it reaching boiling temperature. You also happen to be in a bad spot and don’t have enough money to spare for a mining rig. Yet, you want to get into the crypto mining game. What are your options?

There is cloud mining. It is the most popular way to mine cryptocurrencies with little to no effort on your part. The idea is simple. Some people who have a lot of money decided to make a business out of mining cryptocurrency. So, they bought up a bunch of GPU mining rigs and let people borrow them to mine cryptocurrencies. It’s that simple.

Many cloud mining companies normally have massive mining facilities with multiple farms located in areas where power is cheap. Their farms usually have hundreds of GPU rigs stacked and working simultaneously. The people maintaining the farms and offering cloud mining services are tech-savvy and know full well how to mine cryptocurrencies.

Given the fact that you can just borrow their rig, it is not hard to see why it is as popular as it is today. If you don’t have enough money, your computer is just inadequate, or you are not interested in owning a crypto mining rig, then cloud mining is your best bet. All you really need is some cash and an internet connection.

Cloud mining can be free if you use certain providers. You might think that it would be a better deal, but it usually comes with very slow mining speeds, extra conditions, etc. Plus, they are designed in a way that you would be familiarized with their platform and is more willing to pay to get better service from them.

Your cloud mining provider should provide plans going from the cheapest to the most expensive one, with different perks. Some hosts might give you the ability to tinker with your cloud mining plan and get a custom price. You are looking to pay from anywhere from $500 to $5,000.

It might sound expensive, and it kind of is. You do not own the mining rig permanently. There is a term and it usually lasts a few years. You might get a lifetime deal with your provider too, but you might have to fork over some extra cash for that to happen. Such is the price for convenience.

Speaking of price, how would you get paid? You just have to register an account with your provider, pay them, and put in your cryptocurrency wallet code, and you are good to go. Throughout your rental period, all the earnings that your rig generates would be sent to your wallet. At least, the remaining value after electricity and maintenance costs are deducted.

On average, it is expected that cloud mining would pay itself in about six months to one year. From that point, you start to rack up profit. But this is just an average estimate since the prices for coins tend to fluctuate.

**ASIC Mining**

Finally, we have ASIC mining. ASIC is Application-Specific Integrated Circuit. These are special devices created specifically to mine cryptocurrency. These are the most powerful rigs to mine cryptocurrencies. They are so fast that they leave GPU and CPU rigs in the dust.

Now, you might be wondering why aren’t they are popular as GPU rigs if they are so good. That is because it is just too good.

When the ASIC company announced the introduction of their mining rig, there was an uproar among those in the crypto community. They wanted these things banned because they are so powerful that other miners just have no way to compete. That means ASIC owners can hoard all the coins because other miners just cannot keep up with the high hash speeds.

Think of it this way. If the majority of earnings just go to a few miners who have enough money to build an ASIC farm, what kind of chaos do you think would ensue? Not only that they have all the coins to themselves, but they can also potentially control the cryptocurrency blockchain network as well since they can verify over 50% of all transactions on the network and therefore could technically control the entire network.

Although, nowadays, the general sentiment around ASICs isn’t that harsh anymore and it is slowly becoming more affordable. In fact, more and more people are migrating to using ASICs. There are many ASIC devices out there with specific tasks. This is its strength and also weakness.

You see, ASIC devices are built to mine a specific coin. Depending on how they are designed, they can either mine a number of different coins or only one coin. It all comes down to the algorithm it uses. So, if your ASIC miner can only mine a cryptocurrency, but then there is an update to the mining algorithm, then you end up with a very expensive paperweight with no resale value.

ASIC miners also consume quite a lot of power, even the most efficient device, can run pretty hot and loud as well. The upfront expense, niched application, and lack of resale value make it a difficult recommendation as a mining rig.

For GPU mining rig, you can at least use the graphic cards for your computer when you are done mining or resell it to gamers out there looking to upgrade their computers. You cannot say the same for ASIC miners.

There is another danger on the horizon, though. Some people are starting to mine cryptocurrencies other than Bitcoin. More specifically, they are flocking toward Ethereum because of its updated consensus model, to a Proof of Stake (PoS) system, which does not require much computing power to mine. That means, less power is consumed and less money is spent on a pricey mining machine.

But perhaps this would create an opportunity for Bitcoin miners who have ASIC mining rigs because there are fewer competitors and they can start to rake in profit when people start to migrate. Only time will tell.

**What Cryptocurrency to Mine?**

As mentioned before, you want to squeeze as much value out of your mining rig as possible. That means mining the right coins so you can sell them off and make bank. But what coins to mine?

Well, that’s easy. You go for the most valuable coins to mine. That means Bitcoin, Dash, Ethereum, Litecoin, etc. Just go to Coinbase or Binance and look at the most valuable coins on the list. That should give you a good idea where to start mining.

Another interesting thing to note is what rig you use to mine cryptocurrency. For instance, if you have a mining rig or got yourself a deal with a cloud mining service provider, you can definitely start mining Bitcoin as they use the PoW consensus model. That said, mining Bitcoin is probably going to be difficult since it is so popular that everyone wants a piece of that Bitcoin pie. Miners across the globe would go into the few mining pools on the network and try to snatch a small amount of coin. You might have to wait for hours until you start to see the first Bitcoin trickle in.

However, if you are not interested in owning and maintaining a rig or cannot afford one, you can look for coins that use the PoS consensus model. Ethereum would then be the perfect option, but you want to hold onto that thought for now since they are still undergoing a renovation. Ethereum 2.0 would incorporate the PoS consensus model into their network and it is set to fully launch in 2021 or 2022, so keep an eye out and jump in once everything is set up. This is probably your best bet.

Now, you might be wondering what if you go for the less popular coins and start mining them since there would be fewer competitors. You can definitely do that and you can perhaps churn out quite a number of coins without using up too much energy. The problem then would be the coin prices.

Bitcoin alone takes up over 50% of the total market cap and is worth over $55,000 for each coin. Ethereum comes in second place with only over $2,000 for each coin. You can clearly see the price disparity between the two coins and it only gets lower the further you go down the list.

And maybe you can tolerate the low price. So long as you make a lot of coins, you can still rake in all the profit anyway. But then there is the problem of power consumption, time, and security to consider, depending on whether the cryptocurrency uses the PoW or PoS consensus model.

In a cryptocurrency that uses the PoW consensus model, you would need to consider power consumption since your device would need to perform a lot of calculations. The problem with mining small cryptocurrencies with this consensus model is that there aren’t many transactions here, so chances are that you won’t be making a ton of coins every second.

You might still be making more coins than Bitcoin, but their cumulative value would be very small and you might have problems selling them off. Because, again, the coin you are mining is not popular so there will not be many people who would want to trade these coins.

Again, you might be fine with that. So long as you get to dump all your coins off eventually, that would be okay. But then there is the power bill to consider. Unfortunately, although the coins are cheap and you can mine a lot of them, your miner would still run at full power and consume a lot of energy. The coins you make are most likely not going to cover even the power bill, let alone generate a profit for you. For this reason, you want to make the most out of your power consumption by mining more popular cryptocurrencies.

But what about the cryptocurrencies that use the PoS consensus model? For that, power consumption will not be an issue. Time could be an issue since everyone is chosen randomly, but you can also increase your odds of being chosen by getting more validators. Since there aren’t many people around and that the cryptocurrency is cheap, you do not have to spend much in order to stand a good chance of getting picked. But then there is the security problem.

We need to understand that the blockchain networks that power popular cryptocurrencies are virtually impenetrable because of the sheer number of users on the network. As mentioned before, each user and miner would be given a ledger that updates itself independently from the rest. These ledgers check and validate each other’s data to ensure that there won’t be any counterfeiting. The alteration of data is only possible when someone controls a majority of the node.

In popular PoS cryptocurrencies, this is not possible because of the number of users and validators on the network. It would require way more money than you think to take over the blockchain that way. It is very much possible with not-so-popular cryptocurrencies, however.

You can buy many cheap coins and use them to stake so you have a lot of validators to your name. Since the coin is not so popular, there won’t be many users on the network, so you will eventually reach a critical point where you have a majority of control over the network. Then, you can just alter data and double-spend at your leisure. That is fine for people who want to watch the world burn, but not so fun for miners.

To be clear, this threat still applies to small PoW cryptocurrencies, except it is even more expensive to carry out that attack. You would need to have a lot of money and have the mining rigs powerful enough for the attack. It is expensive and really not worth it for the attacker, most of the time. In PoS cryptocurrencies, you just need to have money.

For all the reasons above, it is not recommended that you mine smaller cryptocurrencies. It’s just not worth all the trouble.

**Crypto Mining Legality**

One of the most common questions people ask is whether mining cryptocurrency is legal. Generally, yes. But that really depends on your government and your mining method. Unfortunately, governments across the globe view cryptocurrency differently, so the answer won’t be a solid “yes” across the board.

Certain governments ban the possession of cryptocurrency, let alone mining or using it. They are usually quite vocal about it, so a quick search on the internet should give you a pretty solid idea of what your government’s stance is on the coins.

When it comes to the mining method, it means you should not use illegal means to mine coins. You see, some hackers would use Javascript on browsers or any other means to install malware on your computer. They are not after your data, but they want your computer’s processing power. This is called cryptojacking, which is highly illegal.

Then, there are taxes to consider. After all the fees are cut by your broker or exchange, and the bank transfer, you may have to deal with another tax from your government. It is money, after all, so you can guess that your government would be interested in having a share of your spoils.

**Crypto Mining Pool**

The reward for verifying the block is granted to the miner who found the right hash first in the PoW system. However, given how large the Bitcoin blockchain network is, your chance of getting there first is highly unlikely.

Your probability of finding the valid hash is equal to the portion your mining rig contributes to the overall mining power on the network. So, if your machine only contributes 0.001% to the total mining power, that is your chance of finding the hash first. That is not a good probability.

So, miners with weak rigs stand a very small chance of actually getting any coin. Therefore, they band together to create a mining pool. That means, miners would pool their computational power over a network to mine the coins. In this case, the aggregate computational power might go up to 15% share if you band together with a few thousand others, and suddenly your odds of making money are pretty good.

That said, the pool mines for every member. So, the reward is divided proportionally according to the amount of computational power you contribute to the pool. If there are a few thousand people in the pool, everyone gets their fair share. For you, that means you won’t get a lot, but it is better than your 0.001% probability. So, you should definitely join a crypto mining pool.

## CRYPTO TRADING

Cryptocurrency trading wasn’t the hottest thing when Bitcoin just launched. That is because there wasn’t any other cryptocurrency in the market yet. Right now, that is a different story. There are over a thousand cryptocurrencies in the market, so the possibilities are endless here.

Trading cryptocurrency is simple. You exchange one cryptocurrency for another and hope that the coins you have increase in value. You can do this at your crypto exchange and they will charge you a small fee for doing this. Crypto trading is probably the next hottest thing right now as billions of dollar’s worth of coins are being traded every day.

Savvy traders, with a little bit of luck, can make bank just by trading cryptocurrency. There are plenty of people who do this full-time from the comfort of their own homes. But to do so would require a lot of skill and knowledge of the market.

For this, traders use a number of tools to monitor the market and price movement. This is so that they can choose to grab the right coin at the right time. Among their repertoire of tools, they have software to monitor previous price trends among others.

This might sound intimidating to you already, but worry not. Everyone has to start somewhere, after all. So long as you trade with the money you are willing and can afford to lose, there is no danger in giving this a shot.

There are two ways to trade cryptocurrency. You can put together a short-term strategy or a long-term strategy.

**Short-Term Trading**

As the name suggests, short-term trading involves the purchase of cryptocurrency that you plan on holding onto for a short time before selling it off again. How long? That depends on you. It can be from minutes to a few months.

A short-term strategy is effective when you expect a temporary rise in price in the near future. If that is true, then you can sell the coins for a quick profit before the price drops again.

Short-term trading has a number of advantages. For one, it provides a good opportunity to have a high return on investment. In forex, or foreign exchange, the prices for the currency pair hardly move by more than 1% every day. So, the only way to make a lot of money from that would be through leveraging, which is basically a multiplier that amplifies your profits (and losses).

In cryptocurrency, their prices can double (or cut in half) overnight. With over 1,000 currencies to choose from (compared to 100 something of fiat currencies), you have a lot of options. That also means that there are large trading volumes for many coins.

This is important because you can usually find buyers or sellers for a certain cryptocurrency. There are a lot of coins flowing in and out in the exchange across all the cryptocurrencies.

Of course, you have a few other things to consider as well. Again, volatility rears its ugly head. It’s a double-edged sword. If you time it right, you can win big. If not, you would lose a lot of money. The key here would be to carefully analyze the market, have a solid entry and exit strategy and maybe a couple of contingency plans, and commit through it without panicking.

Keep in mind that you need to have a cool head if you are to pull this off successfully. Remain calm as you weather through the choppy sea of the crypto market. One thing you will learn right off the bat is that you do not always win. There are factors outside your control that no one can anticipate. It gets very stressful very quickly when you see how prices move against you.

So, you sometimes just have to bite the bullet, take the L, and pull out before losses mount. Bail out and try again later. Even the best trader on the market do not make a profit 100% of the time, so chances are that you won’t either.

Also, understand that short-term crypto traders are only after the small price movement. So, you have to see the subtle details on the charts to make the most out of it. If you are a complete beginner, this can be tricky.

Another drawback is that you need to make large investments if you want to see a chunky return on investment. This is something that many people aren’t very comfortable with, especially beginners. You either put in a bit of money and make almost no profit or put in a fair chunk of your money and risk losing it over a couple of bad trading decisions.

**Long-Term Trading**

Long-term trading involves a lot of waiting time, which might also involve a lot of nail-biting moments. The term “HODL” is pretty common in this context and only the beginners do not know what it means.

HODL stands for “Hold On for Dear Life”. As you might have guessed, this simple plan involves holding onto the coins for over a year and weather the volatility storm. That said, the price swings are to be expected, but you are expecting to see a slow increase in value over a long time. It will take a while, but you are going to get there and you can cash in. The whole plan hinges on you doing absolutely nothing, which is easier said than done.

A good example of this would be the early investors in Bitcoin. Sure, people would laugh in your face if you invested in Bitcoin back when you needed over 10,000 of those to get a pizza. Around 2011, the price was just $0.35 a pop. If they held onto the coins until 2017, they would be able to sell them all off for around $20,000 each. In other words, you would get around 57,000 times of your initial investment. You have to wonder what those people are doing right now.

So, what kind of benefits does this long-term strategy bring? For one, all it takes is time. You buy some coins and sit on them for a while. It is that simple. That means, you do not have to understand the charts or graphs since you just hold onto the coins for a long time.

Plus, you do not need to waste time anxiously checking the price movement every five minutes. You can do it when you are free or whenever you want. It does not really matter much since you are not doing anything else anyway.

You can also treat this like investing in stock, not to grow wealth, but rather to develop good habits. The idea is simple. You start off with whatever cash you can afford to give away and put that forward to buy a couple of coins. Whenever you have some cash to spare, put that into buying more coins. You can even say that you would spare just $100 every month and you would put that into a cryptocurrency. You do that every month over a long period of time until it is time to reap your harvest when you are near retirement or when you need some emergency cash. This strategy lets you avoid the stress of market volatility since there is no need to fuss about short-term price movements.

Of course, no plans are without their own flaws. The biggest one is that you will miss opportunities that would otherwise allow you to make short-term profits. Because of price volatility, sometimes it may jump up momentarily before settling back down again. Short-term traders would wait for this moment to make a quick profit.

You may also notice another major flaw in this strategy. It is the fact that you are betting on the price going up in the future. Given how unpredictable the future is, you can miss out on some bad news and have your investment go down the drain. If there is bad news relating to your coins, the price may drop and never rise again.

This is why you should always have a solid main plan and some contingency plans when things go south. In this case, you should keep an eye on cryptocurrency news. If the outlook is bad, pull out and move your wealth to another cryptocurrency and try again.

But before you even begin, you should do your research. This is probably repeated many times in literally every investment advice you get, but its importance could never be overstated. Do your due diligence and at least try to understand if the coin would grow in the future.

**What to Be Careful of?**

That said, there are a few other tips to help you navigate the chaotic yet lucrative world of crypto investments.

**You Don’t Always Win**

The most important thing to keep in mind is that you do not always win. Sometimes, everything goes south because of factors outside of your control, without prior warning. Sometimes, you may lose a portion of your investment. Sometimes, you lose everything. It can happen.

For this reason, traders are advised to not invest more than what they can afford to lose. Plus, traders should not set up their trade in a way that would cost them more than 2% of their outstanding balance on a given position. That way, if things do go south hard, you only lose 2%.

How you deal with losses as they happen can also be the difference between a good trader and a bad one. This tip is also important: don’t try to earn your losses back. You might think you can do that by putting in more money. In reality, you could recoup your losses. The problem is that people try it without having a solid plan going in, which is a very bad idea. They lack planning because they let their emotions get the best of them. So, if you go in just because you are upset and you do not have good entry and exit strategies, what you are doing is basically gambling, which ends up causing people to lose a lot more money.

**Fear, Uncertainty, and Disinformation**

Commonly referred to as FUD, this occurs when you start to doubt your investments just because you heard other people’s opinions. They may tell you that the coin is a scam or that it would crash in the foreseeable future. They may even have some solid reasoning as well.

But, again, do not panic. Always do your own research before making a decision. Information is everything here, remember that. A quick Google search would prove whether what you hear from other people is correct. More often than not, they parrot other people’s opinions and pass them off as facts.

**Persuasion**

Speaking of facts, you should always differentiate between facts and opinions, and value them differently. Keep in mind that there are other people who want to make a profit from trading cryptocurrencies as well. Therefore, you should not allow yourself to be influenced by other people’s opinions.

This is important if you get your information from YouTubers or any social media. Some people are paid by the developer of a certain cryptocurrency to promote their coin. The influencer would then “shill” by touting to their audience how great the coin is. This would, of course, lead to an increase in that coin’s price since people are buying in, but this only lasts for a while. It will decrease over time.

This is called shilling. Influencers would be paid to say nice things about a certain product, service, or company, by following a script or guideline, but they themselves do not actually believe in what they are saying.

This doesn’t just apply to cryptocurrency. Some people are transparent and would tell you that they are sponsored by a certain company and go the extra length to tell you that their options would remain honest. How true it is will depend entirely on the influencer’s reputation. If they keep touting one product after another without really having anything concrete to back up their claims, chances are that they are a shill.

Regardless, thorough research should give you a better idea. They can say what they want, but nothing beats the truth. Do your research and stick to the facts, and you should be fine.

**Ponzi Scheme**

Cryptocurrencies got a bad rep because there have been some malicious individuals who successfully pulled off a Ponzi scheme in the past. The idea is simple. You put together a legendary sales pitch, promising huge upsides to investors and little to no risk. It does not have to be difficult if you pack your pitch full of jargon and technicality and put it toward people who are desperate and not too knowledgeable in the field.

With the money, the scammer could run away, but that would be a simple scam. Nothing special about it. A Ponzi scheme goes a level deeper by taking the money from recent investors and giving some of it to the investors that came before them. You don’t have to pay for everything. Just cover the interest and you will be fine.

When you do it this way, you can keep fooling people into believing that your company or project is making a lot of money, when the only money you make is that from other investors. The pool of investors grows until it is no longer sustainable, usually around the time when there are no more new investors to fool and people start to ask more and more questions. So, you can go down the classic scam route and run off with the rest of the money.

Luckily, you can spot this kind of scam from a mile away. If they come up to you, usually unsolicited, with a crypto project that offers 10% returns on investment a year or even more, every month, it’s a scam.

Sometimes, they may go down the pyramid scheme and ask you to find new investors. If you do, you will get a bonus of some kind. That is also a scam.

**The Exit Scam**

This kind of scam is also pretty common in the crypto circle. This happens quite often in ICOs and small crypto exchanges. The plan is simple. You overhype a scheme, get the investment, and run. It is simple in principle, but people have added extra levels of sophistication to make it even more convincing.

Some people would put together a fully functional crypto exchange. That means, they can take your coin and you can trade with them. At least, that is what you are seeing on your screen. Behind the scenes, scammers would sell off your coins and manipulate the exchange’s accounting system to make you think that you are actually trading coins.

The balance you see does not reflect the actual wallet on the exchange, which has already been emptied out. Keep in mind that you need to transfer coins into the exchange’s wallet in order to start trading. If you do, the scammers will empty it out the instance the coins come in.

The jig is up when you make a withdrawal request. Now, some scammers are willing to part with some coins to keep up the game. But, if the withdrawal gets too close for comfort for the scammers, they will just shut down the website. Plain and simple.

Of course, just because a website is down does not mean it is a complete scam. Sometimes, the exchange’s wallet came under cyberattacks. Maybe there was a bug. Your coins might be liquidated to pay for the bill. Regardless, the outcome is the same. The coins are gone and so is the website. All you get is a vague error page, maybe an error 404, and no way to recover your coins.

Sometimes, they have another variation. That is scamming via KYC form. This is where you fill out your personal information. They would simply open up a crypto exchange that allows everyone to sign up without filling out a KYC form, which involves sharing private information that people aren’t comfortable with. Since this is cryptocurrency we’re talking about, people are more sensitive about privacy and anonymity.

So, people would come in without the need to fill out a KYC form. But once they make enough money and go to make a withdrawal, they are suddenly required to submit a KYC form or keep all their spoils on the exchange, basically forfeiting everything to the scammers. How could this happen?

Most people would fail to read the fine print in the terms of service or any agreement they inadvertently agree to regarding the KYC requirement upon withdrawal. Legitimate crypto exchanges let people know well in advance of their policy change that would require KYC upon withdrawal. But for scammers, they don’t have to complicate things. They can put it in the fine print, sneak in a policy change without notifying the users, or simply spit an error message whenever people try to submit the KYC form to get their money.

Regardless, the result is the same. You can’t submit the KYC form and all your coins on the exchange are gone. They will have all sorts of excuses, but nothing in the way of reparation.

So, how can you stop that from happening? First of all, have your own wallet. Putting all of your coins on your crypto exchange wallet is just asking for trouble. Have a mobile app on your phone or computer to store your coin, or better yet, have a hardware wallet. A cold wallet that is disconnected from the internet would be your best bet in protecting your investment.

Of course, as mentioned before, you would need to have coins in your crypto exchange wallet in order to trade. So, the safest method to approach this would be to have only the amount you need in your crypto wallet and transfer everything out at the end of the day. At the very least, only keep the coins you need to use the next day on your crypto exchange wallet. Yes, it is a hassle having to do it every day, but you never know when it will end up saving your investment.

In other words, you should never trust any exchange, ever. Sure, some popular exchanges such as Coinbase are trustworthy and they would never steal your coins since their reputation is on the line. But crypto exchanges work like banks.

It does not really matter how trustworthy or regulated they are, or even how much money they have, there will come a time when they stumble and they may go bust. Crypto exchanges function largely similarly to banks since they only have a small share of all the coins that move in the blockchain networks.

Plus, there are a few things that work behind the curtain that you have to understand. First of all, when you trade one coin for another, the accounting system will update your balance accordingly. The coins that move out of your crypto exchange wallet would be held in a cold wallet elsewhere, which is separate from the accounting system. So, when you go and make a withdrawal, be it cash or coin, you would get it from a new account, usually separate from the exchange itself. The accounting system would also update to reflect this change.

In simple terms, a crypto exchange works very much like a casino, except the odds aren’t skewed against you that much. You go to the person who handles the exchange from cash to chips and vice versa. You give them money and they hand you back some chips. You play with those chips and after you are done, you go back to the same person and they will take your chips and give you money.

This disconnect between the actual amount of coins the crypto exchange has to the coins you use to trade with allows the crypto exchange to work somewhat like a reserve bank. They never have enough cash to pay everyone if they all simultaneously request a withdrawal. If they do, the crypto exchange would just capitulate and disappear.

And the crypto exchange may disappear, especially when they no longer make a profit and going forward would not yield any economic benefit for the owners. If the crypto prices suddenly yet slowly rise without any explanation and there are slow payments from the exchanges, it is time to leave.

So, you should always make it a habit to move all your wealth from your crypto exchange at the end of the day. You should also only use the most popular crypto exchange such as Coinbase. There may be newer ones that you want to try out since they offer better security and other handy features, but if you do go there, keep an eye out for two things.

The first is how fast they pay you when you request a withdrawal. It should not take more than a few days. If the new crypto exchange needs a week or two just to make a withdrawal, that’s a red flag.

Also, you should test out how fast they respond to you. A solid crypto exchange should have a very active customer support network. It usually does not take them more than a business day to respond. If they are slow to respond or do not respond to your email at all, you should not be using that crypto exchange.

**The Stop Drive**

This is another sly tactic that some crypto exchanges pull to make a quick buck. Suppose that you are fast asleep at night, dreaming of how your positions at the end of the day would yield a profit when you get up tomorrow. You invested in Bitcoin and set up a stop-loss order to cover your bases in case the price drops in the middle of the night.

That night, the price did drop and Bitcoin fell $2,000 in under two minutes. It triggered your stop-loss order and your crypto exchange closed your position for you. Your balance is saved somewhat but you still lost the trade to the tune of $1,000. You might think that it is fine since you did not lose any more money. That’s what a stop-loss is for and it protected your investment just fine.

The funny thing is that the price suddenly goes up significantly. Since your position is closed, you are cut out of the game and you just missed that opportunity to make a profit. That’s not something you want to see in the middle of the night. Sometimes, it’s just the volatility of the market. But some other times, other nefarious things are going on behind the curtain.

This trick is called the stop drive. It can happen when your crypto exchange knows where you set your stop-loss and they decided to crash the price with fake moves. Then, they move in to take the profits.

It works by the exchange selling the coins, therefore causing the price to go down. They do this because they know that they would make more money on the stop-loss than they would lose to sell the coins to cause the price crash, only to buy them back later. The good thing about driving a stop is that you get your coins back from covering the stop. In simple terms, it is basically a way for people to print money.

This strategy is not new. In fact, it has been done many times in history by brokers in various markets. It’s not just the crypto exchange. Traders also play the game sometimes, but it is normally the “house” that uses this strategy since they have all the data they need to pull this off.

Though this is highly illegal, it doesn’t stop it from happening. It still occurs from time to time in the stock and commodities markets, though rare. But it is starting to become pretty common in the cryptocurrency market.

So, what can you do to stop it? Here, we need to talk about leverage. It is basically a way to amplify your profits if your trade goes well, or loss if it does not. It is a multiplier. Crypto exchange offers various levels of leverage. In Forex, it can go up to 1:1000, meaning that you can get 1,000 times your profit if you utilize leverage.

Leverage is created so that traders can benefit from large volume trading without actually having to invest much money. This is particularly important in forex when the profit you make is in thousandths of cents, so leverage pumps the numbers up so that your profit is more tangible. Traders only need to invest a small fraction of the position to make use of the leverage. A portion of their balance would be frozen by their platform so that they can cover their losses if the trade goes south.

In crypto trading, it is a different story. You should learn the charts and trade without leverage or even without stops if you can keep an eye on the charts. Crypto exchange platforms that do not provide leverage or stop-loss features do so to protect their users. Those that do, do so because of their own interest.

Novice traders that go into crypto trading platforms every day have little idea of how trading works and would use all the features to help them trade, be it leverage and stop-loss, without really knowing the intricacies of crypto trading. They often end up losing a lot of money and then some more since they are novice traders and let their emotions get the best of them.

All of this would give the exchange more and more money, and that is exactly what they want. This just goes to show that trading is usually a zero-sum game. Nobody wins it all and the losers have to pay for it. At least some people care and understand the risk behind leveraging and stop-loss, and decided to offer a platform that does not have these features, which is something to look for.

If you have to use stops, just make a mental note or write it down somewhere so your platform does not know where your stops actually are. Also, try your best not to use leverage. It will take some time to bring in the profits, certainly, but it is a safer route. Even if you do, keep it as low as possible. If you have high leverage, monitor every price movement. Better yet, buy and hold onto the coins, and also buy the dips (buy the coins when the price goes down), but this is a very risky move and no one wants to be in that position. This is why there aren’t many successful traders. It’s high risk and high reward.

# STORAGE OPTIONS

In this chapter, we will go over how you can store your coins, in detail. You need a place to store your coins and you can’t just place them on your crypto exchange wallet unless you want to lose all your coins in a cyberattack or a scam. Therefore, it is imperative that you choose a secure wallet to hold all of your coins.

## HOW CRYPTOCURRENCY WALLETS WORK

To quickly summarize, a crypto wallet is basically a piece of software that allows you to send and receive coins. While all wallets allow you to store different coins at once, most of them only allow you to do so for a small number of coins. They usually cover the most popular coins such as Bitcoin, Litecoin, Ethereum, etc.

To be clear, the wallets do not store your coins in any shape or form. They simply contain the address to which your coins belong in the blockchain network. To access these coins, you would need to use your private key, which is a string of numbers and letters. Everyone uses their public key as a way to send and receive coins, but a private key is needed to access the coins in the wallet.

Private key and public key are generated as a pair. Your private key needs to match with your public key before you can access your coins and spend them. To be clear, “match” here does not mean duplicate. Private key and public key have different combinations of letters and characters, so no one can access your wallet just because they know your public key. It is just that the private key would only work with a specific public key, which would then allow you access to your coins.

Creating a cryptocurrency wallet is quite simple. Most wallet providers would give you detailed step-by-step instructions on how you can set up a wallet with them. Before you do, just make sure that the wallet actually supports the coins you plan to have. Upon creation, you would be given a private and public key to your wallet, and you should write them down somewhere.

## TYPES OF CRYPTOCURRENCY WALLETS

There are many types of cryptocurrency wallets, and they can be categorized based on how they are used. They are grouped into two main categories: hot or cold, and software or hardware. Ideally, you should be using a cold hardware wallet.

The difference between hot and cold wallets depends on internet connectivity. Hot wallets are connected to the internet and are less secure, prone to cyberattacks, but convenient to use. On the other hand, cold wallets do not need to be connected to the internet in order to be used.

Cold wallets work because of how cryptocurrency wallets work in the first place. To reiterate, the wallet themselves do not physically hold the coins. They only hold the data that point to where the coins are on the cryptocurrency network. So, they do not need to be online in order to function. They would still need to go online to receive coins from others, but they just go off the grid once that is complete.

There are many forms of cold wallets. The most user-friendly option would be USB wallets such as that from Ledger, KeepKey, or TREZOR. There used to be paper wallets as well, which is a document sent to an offline printer that would create private and public keys for the owner. But ever since hardware wallets became commonplace, paper wallets started to become obsolete.

The idea behind cold wallets is that private keys are generated offline. For a USB wallet, you just plug it into your computer and launch a certain app to access and store your coins. If you use a USB wallet, make sure to keep it safe as any damage or loss means losing access to your wallet.

There are also offline software wallets. This works similarly to hardware wallets except it involves two separate platforms, which makes the whole process a bit more complicated and overwhelming for an average user. An offline software wallet uses two platforms: an online platform where the public key is stored and an offline platform that contains the private key.

So, the online portion creates new unsigned transactions and sends the user’s address to the sender or recipient on the other side. Then, this unsigned transaction would be moved to the offline wallet where the private key would be used to sign. The transaction, now signed, would be moved back to the online wallet and be broadcasted on the blockchain network to confirm the transaction. Since the offline wallet is never connected to the internet, the private keys remain protected. If you are interested in having an offline software wallet, consider Electrum and Armory. They are known to have the best offline software wallet.

Now is a good time to mention hardware and software wallets. These are pretty self-explanatory. Hardware wallets are special devices that store your private keys whereas software wallets do so on your computer.

You want to have hardware or at least a cold wallet to store your coins. You see, when you register with a crypto exchange, you would need to create a wallet with them. Unfortunately, they also handle the generation of private and public keys. Therefore, they could, in theory, use the private key to steal your money.

So, you should look for well-known crypto wallet providers that have cutting-edge technology. There are many options out there, so look around and evaluate the pros and cons of each provider before you decide.

## WHAT WALLET TO CHOOSE

That said, if you are new to the world of cryptocurrency, maybe you are undecided and do not know where to start. Worry not. We’ve got you covered. There are a number of things to consider when choosing a wallet provider.

**Reputation**

This is self-explanatory, you should only consider companies that are well-known. These companies understand that they are better off providing their customers with good quality products instead of scamming them out of their money. Their reputation is worth more than a quick buck.

Look for companies that have been around for a long time and see what their customers say about them. Understand what features they offer and how those can help protect your coins. More often than not, you can find online reviews about these companies so you know what to expect going in.

**Technology**

Given the anonymous, decentralized, yet transparent features of cryptocurrencies, people would want the same from their crypto wallet provider. In this case, your provider should have open-source wallets. That means, other people can see how the wallets are developed by looking at the codes that are made publicly available.

That way, if there is anything amiss, people would know about it. Knowing the codes that power these wallets is very important because they basically tell you how the wallet works in its entirety. They tell you how the private key is stored; is it with you or your provider, also is the key stored on their server? The second path is riskier. Two-factor authentication is also something to look out for.

In addition, privacy is also something that a lot of people care about. A few of them care about it a little too much, but you can’t fault them for that given all the private data leaks in the past. If that is you, consider checking out whether the crypto wallet would work over a TOR Network in case you really want to operate in the dark.

**Backup Functionalities**

This is one of those things that may seem whimsical and insignificant at first until you realize a little too late just how important it is. There are a lot of problems that you might face in the future that may jeopardize your investment. Maybe your computer broke down and that computer happens to be the only place where you’ve installed your software offline wallet. Maybe the external environment shortens your hardware wallet’s lifespan faster than you expect, and it suddenly dies one day. If any of those happen, your investment will go down the drain.

To prevent this from happening, make sure to back up your wallet. Again, since wallets hold data showing where your coins are on the blockchain network, backing up your wallet does not duplicate the coins you have in your wallet. Backing up just duplicates the treasure map to your spoils just in case anything happens to the original copy.

So, when you are out looking for a proper crypto wallet, make sure that they have a solid backup procedure. It should have a rescue link, password, and some passphrases to restore your access. Two-factor authentication is also important here.

**Multi-Coin Support**

Most wallets these days allow you to store a few types of Altcoins, in addition to Bitcoin. What coins they choose to support may differ between manufacturers, so make sure you know what coins you are going to trade in the future, and then choose the right wallet for them.

Having a crypto wallet that can support different coins is handy. You do not have to own multiple wallets that only support one type of coin each. You won’t have to go through so much hassle just to access different coins.

**Cryptocurrency Conversion**

This is optional, but handy, nonetheless. It is a nice little integration that can be built right into your crypto wallet. Of course, they would need to be working with a crypto exchange or have other means to buy and sell your coins for you if the manufacturer is to be able to offer such a feature. In this case, Exodus works with ShapeShift to create a crypto wallet to convert coins to simplify cryptocurrency arbitrage.

**Shared Account**

To be clear, you should not be sharing your crypto wallet with another user, for obvious reasons. You cannot really trust anyone. You can do that just as another form of protection against lost keys or similar situations. Luckily, there is a way to ensure that your coins are safe. You can do this by creating a MultiSig, or Multiple Signature, account.

It is a trust system in which transactions cannot take place unless all parties tied to the account give their approval. So, multiple private keys would be generated and tied to the same wallet. This feature is very useful for business partners or families.

**Platforms/Devices**

This is something worth considering. Having multiple devices connected to the same wallet is convenient, albeit a bit risky. Ideally, you want to have a wallet per device only. It would be a hassle to manage all of them, but if any of your devices get lost or compromised or something, at least you won’t lose all of your coins.

**QR Scanner**

This one is pretty important. Scanning the QR code lets people share their public key without the need to type everything out. Since keys are just a jumbled mess of letters and numbers, it is very easy to misread or mistype a certain character. Just a single mistake means that your coins would be sent to the wrong person, and there is no way to recover them. So, any crypto wallet worth a penny can generate and scan QR codes to facilitate coin transfers.

**Customer Service**

Just like crypto exchanges, crypto wallets should have a solid infrastructure for their customer service. They should be very responsive and can answer most of your questions. This is doubly important when you are operating in the cryptocurrency environment, where market values go up and down very quickly.

**Liquidity Risk**

Funding or cash flow liquidity risk is another thing to consider as it can affect your ability to trade off your coins quickly during peak trading hours. In this context, liquidity refers to how fast or easily you can buy or sell your coins, including the speed at which cash would be converted to coins and vice versa. Funding or cash flow liquidity risk refers to whether the firm or company has enough assets or funds to support its liabilities.

**Blockchain Evolution**

There is also the question of future-proofing features. Blockchain technology is continuing to develop by the day. There may be new breakthroughs or updates, and your wallet needs to be able to keep up with the changes to the blockchain network. This applies not just to crypto wallets, but also to crypto exchanges as well.

Normally, the developers are pretty vocal about their upcoming update that can break the current platform, and they also give people plenty of time to prepare. So, keep an eye out for that and see what your crypto exchange or crypto wallet provider is doing in preparation for that update.

When the update hits, there might be some issues. Programmers and developers would know that when one fixes a bug, they may inadvertently introduce a bunch of new ones. Something may break, and it will take a while to fix.

So, your best bet would be to keep your coins in a hardware wallet in cold storage. Once everything is patched up and ready to go, you can jump straight back in.

**Custodial Wallet**

Finally, and most importantly, go for non-custodial wallets. What are they? Simply put, they are wallets that users have full control over. In other words, no one, not even the company that provides the crypto wallet, has control or access to the wallet. You are the owner of the wallet and only you can access it. Its importance can never be overstated.

To be fair to custodial wallets, they are highly convenient. People who are new to the whole cryptocurrency world can start with custodial wallets first. Once they get a feel for how everything works, they can then move to non-custodial wallets.

A non-custodial wallet gives you the ability to interact directly with a blockchain network, cutting out the middleman, which is in line with the spirit of cryptocurrency in the first place.

There are two main types of non-custodial wallets: software and hardware.

Software wallets are apps on your computers or phone. They generate a private key and store it as a file on your computer. The key (pun intended) here is that a non-custodial wallet does not store your private key on the provider’s server. It is all on your device, so your provider cannot access your money.

But having a file on your computer can be risky. For this reason, you should find a non-custodial wallet provider that also allows you to protect your private key with a password and encryption. Again, maybe a two-factor authentication as well. This might be a bit of a hassle, but they serve as an additional layer of security.

As mentioned before, you want a wallet that has the ability to generate and scan QR codes. If you install the software app on your computer, you lack that ability. For this reason, mobile wallets are also created, usually to pair with their computer app counterpart. Both apps work largely the same, but the mobile apps are more convenient in the sense that you can make quick everyday payments and scan QR codes. In addition to password and encryption, your phone may also come with other security features such as TouchID or FaceID.

However, if you do not have that much confidence in the security of your everyday devices, you have the option to go for hardware wallets. These devices contain all the features you would want in a crypto wallet and are considered to be the safest option for coin storage.

As always, make sure that you back up your private key in case something goes wrong and you lose access to your device.

# INVESTING IN ETHEREUM

## ETHEREUM STRENGTHS AND WEAKNESSES

Ethereum refers to the blockchain network and Ether refers to the currency on said network. There are a number of benefits and considerations you need to go through when deciding whether to invest in Ethereum.

**Advantages**

The first and most important thing about Ethereum is its complexity. Ethereum uses smart contracts, which may seem very technical and full of jargon to a layperson because of how complex it can get, but there is value in this complexity.

Ethereum provides this value in ways that Bitcoin could not. Transactions using Ether utilizes what is basically a self-executing contract that is slowly proving its value to companies. They can use this to enforce contracts without the need for a third party to oversee the whole thing.

This creates a firm confidence in Ethereum, something that Bitcoin rather lacks. Even if the whole cryptocurrency market falls apart in the next few years, Ethereum still has some utility values in its smart contract. So, if the crypto market collapses, Ethereum stands a good chance of surviving the fallout because there is still a need for smart contracts.

Speaking of utility, Ethereum is also more than just a cryptocurrency. Other than smart contracts, the platform itself is a breeding ground for groundbreaking ideas. Many startups and tokens are created on the platform and Ether is needed to fuel these projects. The platform itself supports Decentralized Finance (DeFi) projects, dApps (apps on the blockchain), which gives Ethereum a lot of utility.

Ethereum can be used in different sectors such as medicine, energy, or even gaming. The platform allows developers to create tokens, which can foster the tokenization of art, patents, voting history, ID verification, medical records, and other complex tasks.

Plus, Ethereum is also very affordable. It is still the most expensive Altcoin in the market today, but it is far cheaper than Bitcoin. So, you can get into the Ethereum market a lot easier than Bitcoin. Add to that the ability to invest in startups with Ether, and you can see why Ethereum comes as a close second to Bitcoin. If Ethereum was created before Bitcoin, it could very well be the leading cryptocurrency right now.

Another benefit to Ethereum is that its coin, Ether, has no maximum supply. The developer has no plan to put a hard limit on how many Ether there can be in circulation. That means, the price for Ether would remain more or less stable since Ether would be needed to execute contracts and transactions.

**Drawbacks**

The first problem could come from the fact that it may struggle with scaling. Bitcoin is created to be the digital gold, or a medium of exchange just like fiat currencies. It has only a single purpose.

Ethereum, on the other hand, has to fill many shoes. It needs to be a ledger, a platform for smart contracts, etc. While the broad scope may be appealing, it can also lead to breakdowns, hacks, and bugs.

The platform itself can be used to create new apps and tokens. Developers do not need to focus much on creating an entire blockchain network that is small and vulnerable just to power their dApp. Instead, they can piggyback the Ethereum blockchain and go from there.

The only problem here is that Ethereum uses Solidity, which is another programming language that can be pretty tricky to learn. Since it is a relatively new programming language, coupled with the fact that its application is limited to Ethereum, Solidity has very little support.

It is not easy to find beginner-friendly tutorials. Most of the guides you find out there are usually for people who know coding like the back of their hands. It will definitely slow down the token development process, but it gets better over time once the developers figure out how to navigate this new programming language.

Also, the fact that Ether is used to fuel transactions might not be appealing to some people. Bitcoin is considered to be a crypto gold but Ether is more like gas used to fuel smart contracts. As mentioned before, GAS fees are basically the cost needed to execute a transaction. You do not need much, but it still cuts into your margin. For some investors who are interested in holding onto the coins, losing them in some small transaction is a big no-no for them.

Finally, Ether’s unlimited supply can be both a blessing and a curse. When we talk about price, we need to understand supply and demand. Low supply and high demand mean high prices. High supply and low demand mean low prices.

The developers behind Bitcoin decided to put their feet down and put a hard cap on Bitcoin and control how many coins can be released into circulation. Plus, every four years or so, there would be a halving in which the rate of Bitcoin is given to miners (which is the only way to inject Bitcoin into circulation) is cut in half. Whenever this has happened, Bitcoin’s price skyrocketed for a moment before settling down again at a price higher than that before the halving. It is believed that the last coin to be released into circulation would be around the year 2140.

There can never be over 21 million Bitcoins in circulation, so this effectively controls the supply, which influences its price. Sure, the fact that cryptocurrency is almost infinitely divisible can alleviate the supply problem, but the presence of that hard cap allows Bitcoin to retain its value once the maximum number of coins is reached.

No one really knows what would happen when Bitcoin reaches that cap. Maybe users would need to use Bitcoin as GAS fees just like Ethereum since miners need to be compensated, after all. Maybe the Bitcoin limit would be raised.

Going back to Ether, the fact that there is no limit and the fact that it is used up to fuel transactions means that Ether might not rise in price all that much. Though Ether is the second biggest cryptocurrency in the market, its value is not even in the same ZIP code as Bitcoin. That means, Ether is probably not a good investment for those who are looking to pull a HODL strategy.

That depends on how you plan to use Ethereum and the risks/rewards associated with the cryptocurrency. If you buy Ether to fund startups or projects via ICOs, then Ether is definitely worth it. Plus, Ether has more utility than Bitcoin, which ensures that there is always demand for the coin, despite the fact that there won’t be a limit on supply.

However, Ether is probably not a worthwhile investment if you are trying to execute a HODL strategy. Its value is minuscule compared to Bitcoin. There is an infinite supply of coins. If you want to trade Ether as if it is a stock, you are better off investing in Bitcoin and HODL.

## ETHEREUM VS. BITCOIN

A lot of people ask what is the difference between Ethereum and Bitcoin, and which one they should invest in because all the resources out there say that both are good investment options. Here, we will quickly go over the key differences. But before that, we need to grasp the basics of these two major cryptocurrencies.

Bitcoin was launched in early 2009 by Satoshi Nakamoto. The idea behind Bitcoin’s creation is to create a secured cryptocurrency that is independent of any central authority. Bitcoin has no physical form and its blockchain network records all transactions on secured public ledgers. Though Bitcoin was not the first-ever attempt in creating a virtual currency of this type, its launch was the most successful. It laid the foundation for all other cryptocurrencies that came after it. Over the years, its value continues to go up and is now starting to gain acceptance among regulators and government bodies.

Ethereum was introduced in mid-2015, and it is the largest and most well-known and well-established open-ended platform for decentralized software. Ethereum has a different goal in mind. It provides people with a platform to develop apps, and its coins Ether have more utility beyond just a digital currency.

As mentioned before, Ether is used to make payments, as collateral, and as a store of value. It is also needed to fuel transactions on the platform and dApp developers use it to build and run their dApps on the platform.

**Key Differences**

With the basics in mind, let’s talk about how they are different, to help you understand which one is worth investing in if you have to choose one.

**History**

Bitcoin was the first cryptocurrency, so you can blame it for starting all of this. The creator, Satoshi Nakamoto, remains anonymous to this day. No one knows if it is a person or a group, or even if they are alive or dead. Ethereum was created in 2015 by Vitalik Buterlin, a researcher and programmer. He looked at Bitcoin and its blockchain network, understood their flaws, and set out to work on a new platform that improved upon Bitcoin. His platform provides a lot more functionality and it is used for the development and use of distributed applications (dApp) as well as smart contracts.

**Concepts**

Here, while both Bitcoin and Ethereum allow peer-to-peer (P2P) transactions and they can be used as a payment method, their similarities end here. Bitcoin functions as a replacement for fiat currencies without the issues associated with the latter. The transaction fee is low and there is no central authority to dictate the value.

For Ethereum, its application goes beyond just being a currency. It also provides developers with a platform to create dApps and smart contracts. Therefore, Ethereum is the platform where groundbreaking blockchain-based technology is likely to be developed. If such a thing were to happen, it would take place on the Ethereum network.

**Mining**

Bitcoin uses a Proof of Work validation method. In short, miners race to solve a mathematical problem in order to validate the transaction. Whoever gets it first wins some coins. Since this is a race, not many people would win and many people end up losing money because they have to pay their utility bills while earning nothing from mining Bitcoin.

Ethereum used to have the same validation method. However, it is moving to a new validation system called the Proof of Stake. Simply put, miners would be randomly chosen to propose a block and other miners would also be randomly chosen to attest that they have seen the block. Once enough consensus has been gathered, the block is added.

The difference here is that in PoS consensus model, everyone has a good chance of being chosen either to propose a new block or to provide their attestations. Moreover, the hardware requirement for mining Ethereum is much lower since there isn’t a lot of processing to do. Bitcoin mining is a very power-intensive process and the hardware requirement is very high if you are willing to get yourself a mining rig. Even then, you are not guaranteed to make a profit.

**Fees**

Then there is the question of transaction fees. In Bitcoin, miners are awarded a small amount of coin when they successfully verify a transaction. This coin does not come from anywhere. It is created. It also happens to be the only way to generate Bitcoin, meaning that this is the only way that new Bitcoin is introduced into circulation.

Verifying transaction on the Bitcoin blockchain is free for the users, although you can choose to pay the miner a bit more money so they prioritize going through your transactions first. The fees are completely optional, so even if you don’t pay them anything, the transaction would go through, nonetheless. It just takes a little bit more time.

Ether is also generated via mining. The difference here is that Ether is needed to fuel all transactions on the Ethereum blockchain. This is called the GAS fee and Ether would be used to verify transactions, run apps, etc. Some may frown upon the fact that they need to pay a small transaction fee in Ether to grease the engine, but this also promotes Ether circulation within the network.

**Time**

When it comes to the average amount of time needed to verify transactions and add the block to the blockchain network, Bitcoin takes around nine to ten minutes whereas Ethereum is a lot faster. Usually, it takes about 15 seconds to do so on the Ethereum network.

**Hashing Algorithms**

Hashing algorithms are how blockchain networks protect their data and ensure that everyone on it is anonymous. Bitcoin uses a hashing algorithm called SHA-256 whereas Ethereum uses Ethash.

**Numbers**

There is more Ethereum in circulation compared to Bitcoin. Despite the fact that there is more Ether, it is Bitcoin that reached the $1 trillion market cap milestone first. Ethereum is lacking behind by a country mile. What Ethereum has more than Bitcoin is its number of blocks, which is at over 660,000 at the moment of writing, while Bitcoin is around 540,000. Ethereum has more blocks in its blockchain because it takes a lot less time to verify transactions.

# DIVERSIFYING

One of the first things you will hear when you get into the investment game is that you should diversify your portfolio. The same theory applies here. ## ALTCOINS

Altcoins, or alternative coins, is an umbrella term that refers to every other cryptocurrency except for Bitcoin. They share characteristics that make them a cryptocurrency, but they offer different features from Bitcoin. For instance, Ethereum is an Altcoin and it provides a platform for app developers.

There are over 9,000 cryptocurrencies to date, although Bitcoin still controls a majority of the market share. Altcoins are derived from Bitcoin in one way or another. They all take the lessons from Bitcoin and improve upon its shortcomings in their own ways. For this reason, the prices of Altcoins tend to mimic that of Bitcoin, although they lack the magnitude.

**Types of Altcoins**

What distinguishes one Altcoin from another comes down to its functionalities and consensus mechanism. They usually fall under these four categories, some fit into more than one category.

**Mining-Based**

As the name suggests, mining-based coins refer to the cryptocurrencies that are created by mining. Mining here has two main methods: Proof of Work (PoW) and Proof of Stake (PoS) systems that we have already discussed in Chapter 3. Some examples include Ethereum, Zcash, Monero, and Litecoin. Some cryptocurrencies, such as Ripple’s XRP, are premade or pre-mined. Meaning, they are not produced via mining but are created and distributed before their listing on the crypto market.

**Stablecoins**

Some people would tout Stablecoins as the best compromise that would secure the future of cryptocurrencies. Cryptocurrency trading has been plagued by volatility since its inception. Stablecoin aims to remedy this problem by tying its value to goods such as fiat currencies, precious metals, or other cryptocurrencies. That way, their value remains relatively stable, earning confidence from potential investors. Some examples of Stablecoins include MakerDAO and USDC.

**Security Tokens**

Security tokens are basically securities that are traded in the stock market, except these are traded in the crypto market. They provide similar benefits to traditional stocks and these tokens promise their owners equity in terms of ownership or a dividend based on profit.

The idea that a company would be successful and that their security token would skyrocket in value makes them an appealing prospect for investors. These tokens are offered to investors through ICOs or initial coin offerings. That said, security tokens are subject to heavy scrutiny and they have to satisfy all legal requirements before the company can hand them out.

**Utility Tokens**

Utility tokens work a bit like security tokens, but they aren’t subject to all the legal scrutiny. These tokens are used to provide services to the owners. They can use utility tokens to redeem rewards or to purchase goods and services. Utility tokens do not give owners a share of the company that issued them, however.

**Are Altcoins Good Investment?**

It’s not just Ethereum. There are many other promising Altcoins out there. Should you invest in them?

You see, the market is still in its infancy. There are almost 10,000 Altcoins on the market today and the number is still on the rise. This attracts hordes of retail investors. They simply speculate on the price movement and make short-term profits.

The crypto market has yet to mature and there isn’t a good way to define investment criteria or metrics to evaluate cryptocurrencies. More often than not, Altcoins are purely speculative vehicles and only a fraction of the coins are actually worth trading. The rest are dead cryptocurrencies, coins that failed to gain traction or simply faded into obscurity.

So, if you are looking for long-term gains, you are probably better off buying Bitcoins and hold onto them and sell them at a later date. But if you are interested in funding startups, you can use Ether or other supported cryptocurrencies for ICOs. Using other Altcoins for long-term investment is not viable yet and you should be trading them only as speculation.

**Bitcoin Cash (BCH)**

Bitcoin Cash was created as the hard fork from Bitcoin. It is actually one of the earliest and most successful hard forks from Bitcoin. What is a hard fork? For that, we first need to understand the community behind each cryptocurrency.

You see, because of the decentralized nature of digital currencies, any changes made to the blockchain network or the cryptocurrency itself have to be made with general consensus. The developers would take it to the community and ask for their opinions and the developers themselves would debate among themselves what direction should their coins go. The process is different for each cryptocurrency.

The discussion here is important since the cryptocurrency’s value depends entirely on the users. More specifically, the miners, who actually confirm transactions, generate coins and bring them into circulation in the first place. Major changes to the codes behind the coins would have to go through a consensus.

It is all well and good when everyone agrees. But when there is a conflict, a hard fork may occur. A hard fork can be initiated by developers or the members of the crypto community who are dissatisfied with the status quo and their proposed changes went unheard. Such a hard fork requires users to upgrade to the latest protocol software version and this results in certain invalid blocks and transactions becoming valid and vice-versa.

And a hard fork was exactly what happened that led to Bitcoin Cash. There was a split in the community when they could not come to an agreement on Bitcoin’s direction going forward in late 2017.

The disagreement that caused all of this came down to Bitcoin’s problem with scalability. You see, the Bitcoin network has a limit on the block’s size, which is only one megabyte (MB). The block size is one of the things that contribute to the slow transaction speed in Bitcoin in the first place.

There were actually two solutions proposed for this problem. The first one would be to increase the block size, which was promptly shut down by the developers. The second solution was to implement Segregated Witness (SegWit) protocol, which would optimize the data storage in the block.

But the push for SegWit implementation was controversial, to say the least. Miners weren’t happy because of the radical changes it requires. Without going into the technical details, the solution is seen as ineffective and unprofessional, due in part because the push was done without community consensus or an honest discussion to understand the associated benefits and the costs.

So, the fork happened and Bitcoin Cash was created. Each block is eight megabytes big and there is no SegWit protocol. Larger blocks mean that they can hold more transactions within them and the transaction speed would be improved.

## HOW TO DIVERSIFY

As with anything in life, diversifying your cryptocurrency investment portfolio is not that simple. If you’re just trading cryptocurrencies the way you trade the Forex market, then this probably won’t be useful to you. In that case, simply jump between cryptocurrencies and make use of the price swings to make a profit.

Here, we are assuming you want to make long-term investments in the coins and are wondering how you can improve your odds. As you might have guessed, it is not as simple as going to Coinbase and buying one of the top ten coins and HODL.

Just like stocks, you want to diversify your portfolio. The idea here is that you mitigate your risks by mixing a variety of different investments. That way, you have your investment in as many different areas as possible so reduce the overall risk. If one area of your investment were to fail, you would still have other investments to keep you afloat.

For instance, suppose that you have 90% of your investment in Bitcoin and 10% in Ethereum. There are a couple of problems right off the bat. First of all, it is very poorly distributed. You only have two investments. Not to mention, both of these are heavily correlated. If Bitcoin’s price dips, Ethereum’s would too. That means, your entire portfolio would be at serious risk.

For this reason, you want to invest in other Altcoins that aren’t really tied to Bitcoin. How can you tell? You can look up the correlation coefficient of a certain Altcoin compared to Bitcoin. The range goes from 1 to -1. The higher the value, the more closely tied the Altcoin is to Bitcoin.

Of course, all Altcoins are correlated one way or another to Bitcoin, but it’s on a spectrum. The number hardly goes into the negative, but you should have investments in coins that hover around zero correlation coefficient. So, if Bitcoin does crash one day, you can weather the storm and come out on top.

**Learning the Fundamentals**

If you are completely new to the whole world of cryptocurrency and even more so, trading, then do not feel bad about it. Everyone has to start somewhere. To get you up to speed quickly, we will go over some fundamentals.

Let’s start with the portfolio. Portfolio is a term referring to the collection of all your investment assets. If you invest in ten types of coins or tokens, then your portfolio consists of those ten coins or tokens.

Then there are risks to consider. What are they? Risk represents the possibility of loss that traders or investors are willing to take in exchange for the possibility of making a profit or gain from their investment. In the cryptocurrency market, there are two main types of risks. Systematic or market risk, and coin-specific or idiosyncratic risk.

Market risks are the risks associated with the overarching performance of the crypto market. This is the kind of risk that is inherent and inevitable. So, the only thing you can do is minimize risk. This is down to the fact that all cryptocurrencies are more or less tied to Bitcoin and each other. Just like the Bitcoin crash following its halving in 2016, almost all coins and tokens are almost worthless.

Idiosyncratic risks are a lot easier to deal with since they are tied to a specific coin or token. These risks are influenced by factors that directly relate to the project or coin itself. For instance, suppose that the project experiences a network failure, a member of the development team quits, the entire development team runs away with the investors’ funds or any other negative event, then the coin holder would lose money.

What is interesting in the investment world is that risks are both a blessing and a curse. Conventional wisdom in the financial world dictates that the greater the risk, the better the reward.

**Trading vs Investing**

We have gone over this before, but it’s worth mentioning it here again. Given how many cryptocurrencies there are on the market, coupled with the fact that cryptocurrencies are inherently volatile, you should know that you are setting yourself up for a particularly bumpy ride and you should know what you are doing before you jump in. This involves analytics. There are two main types of analysis.

The first is fundamental analysis. This involves a very basic research process and analysis of the data you gather. You would first look for the coin’s whitepaper or a detailed proposal by the development team. It should contain all the information you need to establish a basic understanding. You should look for information regarding the industry it operates in, what problem it seeks to solve, how it aims to achieve its goals, the intrinsic value of the company, its financial statements, and other economic variables.

But whitepaper could get technical quickly, so you can also seek out alternative sources. You should do this because a salesperson worth their paycheck would not write anything negative about themselves that would dissuade potential buyers or investors. For this, you want to look at blog posts or community forums to access the general sentiments around the coin, how the developers interact with the community, etc.

Most people would have already gone over the technical jargon and explain it in detail for other people who come before you. If not, then all you have to do is ask and someone will gladly explain it to you. Alternatively, you can also Google the term and add “ELI5”, which means “Explain it to me like I’m 5 years old”. Don’t mind the condescending tone of the term.

Another form of analysis is technical analysis. This one requires a bit more data because you need to forecast the price movement in the near future, which requires you to look at market data from the past, especially volume and prices. All of this involves a lot of charts and number crunching, which is understandably not for everyone. Unlike fundamental analysis, technical analysis does not evaluate the coin’s underlying value. It just looks at the price charts, understands the trends and patterns, and formulates a strategy from there.

Now, you need to ask yourself whether you want to invest or trade cryptocurrencies. Both are nuanced in their own way.

The trading path involves short-term but frequent investments, so it is better suited for traders who like living life on the edge. Traders would use technical analysis to identify price movement patterns and time their entry and exit accordingly.

On the other hand, the investment path is more risk-averse. Your plan would be to hold onto the coins long-term and reap your profits by either selling off your coins several months or even years into the future or by holding onto security tokens and getting dividends. Because you don’t have to care too much about minute price movements in the immediate future, your focus should be on the coin’s underlying value. That means, you would need to do some fundamental analyses.

Regardless of which path you choose, you should always do enough research so you know what kind of person you are, be it a daredevil or a scholar, as well as the risks associated with the coins you are looking into and whether you can tolerate those risks.

**Personal Competency**

Another thing to consider is your experience and overall competency. The idea here is that you should focus on your strengths. For this, you should ask yourself what coins and tokens do you research, what sector or industry you are most familiar with, and what you understand better between utility tokens or security tokens.

As mentioned before, everyone has to start somewhere, and there is no better place to start than familiar grounds. It would work best for you and you can build up competency. In doing so, you can learn to call the shots and maximize your profits.

**How to Get Started**

There are a number of strategies that you can use to diversify your portfolio. This is why we went to great lengths to explain what each Altcoin does in the last section. Your strategy here involves looking at what the Altcoin provides and how it goes about doing it. With this in mind, there are a number of strategies to go over.

**Industry Diversification**

The idea is simple. You just look at what industry the Altcoin is catering toward and try to diversify by investing in Altcoins in as many industries as possible. So, when an industry takes a hit, your portfolio can survive the blow. Some of the main industries that Altcoins fall into include finance, medical, supply chain, data and data analysis, and innovation (such as AI or machine learning).

You can also take it up a notch by looking within each industry and diversify your investments again based on the projects behind the coins. That means, you can support well-established projects, emerging projects, those that solve existing problems, or those that bring innovation.

From here, it is up to you how you want to invest. Each sub-strategies above have their own risks and strengths. That said, you should never invest in a project that you do not know much about. This is even more so if you do not understand their values or the industry they are operating in.

As always, do your own research. More often than not, you can find most information on the project’s page. If it’s filled with jargon and complex languages, chances are that it’s a bad investment.

**Solution Method**

The second strategy depends on the kind of solution or products the Altcoin provides. That would involve looking at how the blockchain for that Altcoin works. Some would provide new tools or services such as digital wallet, a dApp development platform, a new protocol, etc.

Not all business ventures would work out. Not all solutions would end well. So, spreading your investment this way would mitigate the risk as well.

**Types of Cryptocurrency**

As mentioned before, there are four types of cryptocurrency. Mining-based, stablecoins, security tokens, and utility tokens. Because each type of cryptocurrency has its own strengths and weaknesses, by spreading your investment this way, you can also minimize risks.

You can also categorize cryptocurrencies into five groups: transactional tokens, smart contract tokens, yield-earning tokens, utility tokens, and stablecoins. Transactional tokens are coins that are used up in transactions, like Bitcoin. Smart contract tokens are protocols that allow developers to create dApps on their blockchain network, run smart contracts, etc. Yield-earning tokens are basically security tokens gained by funding projects and earning dividends so that you can get more coins than you put in.

That said, make sure to look at the different possibilities and how you want to spread your investments around.

**Geographical Diversification**

Finally, invest in coins based in different regions. Asian blockchain projects are particularly popular since it covers a good chunk of the globe. But you can also think about investing in projects based in America and Europe as well to have a geographically balanced portfolio.

**Time Diversification**

Another way to diversify is through time. The idea is that you do not invest all your money outright. Instead, you would just invest over time. If you have $100,000 to invest, you don’t go all in. You just put in $5,000 every month, over 20 months, and see where the wind takes you. In doing so, you limit the risk of having to time your entry into the market to make the best return on investment.

Let’s say that you started investing in Bitcoin starting in January. You put in $5,000 every month and you didn’t care much about the price movement. You just spent that five grand on however many Bitcoins it can buy. Six months later, you would have put in $30,000 into Bitcoin. But let’s say that the price moves favorably and the price for Bitcoin increases. Then, you can exit the market with more than $30,000.

But what would happen if the price dips? Then your investment would take a hit and you end up with less money than you put in. That is not so good. But this is the risk with trying to time the market. At least, by diversifying through time, you would not lose a lot of money.

If you put in all your money upfront and the market goes sour, you could lose everything. At least with this strategy, you only stand to lose some cash, so the damage is contained.

**The Four-Step Diversification Strategy**

Finally, we have this four-step strategy. There is nothing super specific in this strategy other than the fact that you would group the coins loosely based on how they can provide you value.

**The Foundation**

The first part of your portfolio should consist of some core cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin, Ethereum, and XRP. These should make up from 10 to 25 percent of your portfolio because of their inherent values. Bitcoin is the digital gold. Ethereum is the most popular cryptocurrency thanks to a myriad of features it provides. XRP is a payment network. Each of these three coins has specific and separate use cases.

**The Passive Earners**

Once you have the foundation laid out, consider branching out to Altcoins that generate money for you passively, literally. Mining is not passive income in this context because you need to put your computer to work. Passive earners here refer to security tokens, yield-earning tokens, or interest-bearing coins (they are the same thing) that would generate revenue for you by the virtue of you having the coins in your possession.

Other than their inherent values, these tokens would distribute dividends monthly, quarterly, or bi-annually. Stellar, Bankera, and NEO are good examples here. Although NEO is the most popular out of the bunch, Stella is almost criminally underrated and is worth looking into.

Another form of passive income from coins is airdrops and hard forks. Airdrops are similar to pre-mined coins in the sense that the developers would just give away free coins. The difference here is that airdrops are done as a marketing maneuver. Hard forks are also beneficial since the developer of the newly created coin usually gives people the same or equivalent amount of coins if they migrate to the new cryptocurrency.

With a good selection of passive earners, you would start to earn coins and money just by holding onto them. This would form the launch pad to propel you into newer and potentially riskier investing ventures that might yield better rewards down the line.

**The Anchor**

The crypto market is notorious for its volatility. For this reason, you want to have something to fall back to when the sea is getting choppy and you see dark clouds on the horizon. For this, you want to have stablecoins in your portfolio to help you weather the storm. Pax, USDC, TrueUSD, and Tether are some good options here since they are tied to fiat currencies. That way, you are more shielded from wild price swings.

Stablecoins offer a great way to protect your portfolio from the unpredictable volatility of the crypto market. They provide you with on-demand liquidity. In simple terms, because they have a relatively stable value, you can put money into them knowing that their prices won’t change much in the future and you can sell them off to get some money for a rainy day.

So, when the market takes a hit, at least you still have your investment in stablecoins while the rest of your portfolio might be going down the drain. But since their prices are relatively stable, you probably won’t get any long-term benefit from them. That said, they have another interesting use.

Suppose that the price for a particular cryptocurrency plummets and you want to buy the dip. You know that the price would skyrocket in the near future and you want to catch the trend before it happens. The problem is you don’t have much money on you. No problem, you can just sell your stablecoins and put the money into the cryptocurrency while the price is low. When it jumps back up, you can sell off some of your coins and put them back into your cash reserve, and pocket the rest.

**The Fringes**

To be honest, you can stop at the last stage and sit quite comfortably with your investment. However, there is no harm in branching out if you know what you are doing. In this case, you want to be looking for smaller, niche protocols that have potential. You just look at upcoming projects that you believe will be successful in the future and you buy into their ICOs or their coins.

There are two main ways you can benefit from this if the projects are successful. You can buy into the projects while their tokens or coins are cheap and sell them off at a later date, or hold onto them if they’re offering security tokens that grant you dividends now and then. For the latter, you would be filling up your portfolio with more passive earners.

As always, your portfolio should be nicely diversified. In this case, you want each category of your digital assets to be diverse either in terms of industry, solution, types, or geographical. Of course, you probably do not have enough money to diversify it across all fronts, but try your best to diversify.

**Putting Diversity into Practice**

What we’ve covered are some of the ways you can diversify your coins. But the question remains: how? How much money should I put into each coin? That really comes down to your risk tolerance and how much money you have at hand, so it is impossible to give you exact numbers. Instead, here are some basic diversification models so you have an idea of what would work best for you.

**The Newbie**

This is a good strategy if you are just starting out with cryptocurrency and do not know much about investment or crypto. If you are just new to the whole thing, you should start here. In this model, you just pick out a varied group of coins as we’ve mentioned above, and just allocate equal portions of your capital to each asset. Depending on which coin you chose, your risk level is medium to low.

**The Gambler**

Though this strategy is not for beginners, they usually do this because of how simple it is. There is not much in the way of diversification since you would be going all-in on one cryptocurrency and not consider any other. At most, you would only leave a fraction or 10% of your portfolio on other digital assets. It is high risk and high reward. It sometimes works out well, but it does not more often than not. Your target coins would be Bitcoin and Ethereum.

**The Conservative**

This model is also quite simple. You look at the top ten cryptocurrencies and allocate equal capital for each of them. You do not have to invest in all ten, of course. That depends on how much money you have. Five or three would be enough as the starting point. For that, you can invest in Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Litecoin or Ripple.

The term “whale” refers to people who have a lot of money to spare. This term usually comes up in the gaming community, especially games with microtransactions, which would allow you to purchase certain things using real money. These people usually spend a lot of money on microtransactions either because they are addicted or because they have a lot of money to spare.

In this case, the term simply means that you have quite a bit of cash to spare and you want to put it to good use. With this model, you distribute large portions of your capital between a few cryptocurrencies and then acquire small volumes of others. Again, you want to invest in Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Litecoin or Ripple. How much you should invest is up to you. But if you really want to have a number, you can just give each of the three major cryptocurrencies 25% of your capital, and leave the other 25% for other smaller cryptocurrencies.

This strategy is employed primarily to combat inflation. Unless you live in Zimbabwe, or Venezuela where hyperinflation is rife, chances are that you do not really know or understand the effects of hyperinflation. To be honest, no one really wants to find out the hard way either.

## ALTERNATIVES TO CRYPTOCURRENCY

The blockchain technology that powers cryptocurrencies have a wide range of uses. What Ethereum has brought to light is the fact that developers can create decentralized applications that work on the blockchain network for data storage and analysis without security risks, as well as smart contracts that enforce themselves once certain conditions are met.

Among those applications are ICOs or initial coin offerings. Though some would say that ICOs are just glorified GoFundMe campaigns or Patreon because of their similarities, ICOs are another great way to invest your cryptocurrency. In simple terms, investing in ICOs is very much like investing in a startup and earning yourself some share in the company. If you pick the right projects or startups to back, the payout will be astounding.

You spend some money to buy a cryptocurrency and hope that their prices go up in the future so you can sell it for more money. And you can stop there, but you can go down another level by spending cryptocurrency to make cryptocurrencies.

In this section, we will be expanding upon the idea of “passive earner” coins, especially security tokens, and cover how you can put your money in the right projects. But before we do that, let us talk about the basics, again.

**What is an ICO?**

We have mentioned ICOs in the past. It works like this. Suppose that you are a Silicon Valley entrepreneur with big ideas. That does not narrow it down that much, but let’s go with it.

You’ve got big ideas, but unlike Elizabeth Holmes’ Theranos or Adam Neumann’s WeWork, both of which are disastrous startups that are little more than a complete scam, your ideas actually solve problems and they are actually feasible so long as you get enough funding. But how do you get funding?

You can go to the local bank and take out a loan or try to get venture capitalist investors on your side. But this means you have to give up some ownership of your own company. This is where ICO comes in.

ICO stands for Initial Coin Offering. It works like this. First, you create a document that explains in detail how your solution would work. This is called a white paper. Then, you design a sleek and professional-looking website, as is the current trend, and explain why your project is worth backing.

Then, you just ask people to send you money in cryptocurrency. Most of the time, that would be Ethereum if you design your projects on their blockchain. This is also where you need to create your own tokens, which you would send to your backers. You get the funding you need and your backers hope you are successful. If you bag the investment and run, that would be a scam.

Keep in mind that in ICOs, you would be distributing utility tokens or cryptocurrency, not security tokens. Therefore, the owner of the company does not have to give up on the ownership stake of the company.

For an investor, you won’t be getting any source of passive income. But the utility tokens you have might come in handy. For instance, if the company creates a new crypto exchange platform that offers token holders discounts on their trades, this would also benefit you since you can start trading on the exchange with reduced transaction fees.

Different companies or projects grant token holders different privileges, some of which are very useful. Even if you are not interested in getting those benefits, you can also sell the tokens off at a later date when the project is successful to make more money.

But if you want to get your hands on security tokens, you would need to look for STO or Security Token Offerings.

**What is an STO?**

The process is largely the same compared to ICO, except there are a lot more regulations to deal with since owners of the tokens are entitled to dividends of the company’s profit. A security token represents the holders’ ownership or shares in the company. In other words, STOs are very much like IPO, or initial public offering, where the company offers its shares to the public.

**STO vs. ICO**

As mentioned before, the process is largely the same, but the token characteristics are different.

For one, STOs are asset-backed and have to comply with regulations. So, it is quite difficult to launch an STO because the idea is to offer an investment contract under securities law. So, the people behind STO projects have to make sure they comply with all relevant regulations. Plus, they normally would have to raise funds only through accredited investors who meet certain requirements themselves.

ICOs are not as difficult to get started. This is down to the simple fact that their utility tokens are for usage, not investment. Therefore, they can weasel their way around the strict regulations. So, the barrier to entry for startups is much lower if they just launch ICOs. They just need to focus on working on their project and developing their utility tokens.

**STO vs. IPO**

Again, the process is mostly the same. The difference is that STOs issue tokens on a blockchain network while IPOs provide certificates on the traditional markets. Both are also well-regulated since they fall under the securities law.

That said, IPOs are only used when private companies want to go public. They can raise funds by giving away shares to accredited investors through their initial public offering process.

On the other hand, STOs developers would provide security tokens. These tokens represent an underlying asset that is issued to accredited investors via the blockchain network. Though the token can represent the company’s share, it can also represent any other asset that is expected to turn a profit, such as property ownership, investment funds, fine art, etc.

**STO Regulations**

As you probably expect, this depends on the jurisdiction. In the United States, the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is pretty vocal on how a security token is defined. They are also debating on whether certain utility tokens should be reclassified as security tokens that need to be regulated.

Although the line between security tokens and utility tokens is pretty clear, some are operating in the grey zone. Certain businesses hand out utility tokens that are very similar to security tokens. That way, they can attract more people by the promise of periodic dividends payouts without having to deal with all the regulations relevant to security tokens.

According to the SEC, ICOs would be reclassified as a security if they fit the definition of an investment contract. The definition was established by the Supreme Court from a case between the SEC and The Howey Company, which is now known as the Howey Test.

Without using all the technical terms commonly found in legal stuff, we can say that an investment contract is an investment of money in a company with an expectation of profit that would be deducted from the company’s revenue. Although, the “investment of money” part here does not only apply to money alone. It does not have to be cash.

The United Kingdom also created consultation papers to help people decide whether their crypto asset is an exchange token, a utility token, or a security token. Switzerland also joined in with their ICO guidelines.

As expected, governments across the globe do not have the same opinion on STOs. In fact, certain countries outright ban STOs. Some have regulations in place, but they are a patchwork of vague laws.

**Advantages of STOs**

Let’s first compare STOs to ICOs. STOs have lower risks because of the security laws they have to comply with before they can start issuing security tokens. As a potential owner of security tokens, you would be pleased to know that there will be transparency and accountability.

Plus, a security token is tied to real-world assets. Meaning, it is more accessible even if it is not priced fairly compared to its underlying asset. Pure tokens are more difficult to evaluate because they are not tied to any physical asset. Therefore, it is difficult to judge whether or not your utility tokens are priced fairly.

What about IPOs, then? STO is a cheaper alternative because there is no need for a middleman like banks or brokerages. Companies can make use of smart contracts to limit reliance on expensive lawyers and the storage of data on the blockchain network limits the need for paperwork. So, STOs are just cheaper and faster.

**Challenges of STOs**

As with anything in life, there are a couple of challenges or drawbacks that STO platforms face. That is primarily the increased regulations that put a bigger administrative burden on them.

The people behind STO projects now have to have proper processes for custodianship, a way to track ownership, a way to approve the exchange, KYC forms, and many other things. They have to go through all of these to ensure that they comply with relevant security laws. Though it is cheaper than IPOs, the work needed to launch an STO is costlier than ICOs, which raises the barrier of entry. For an investor, that means it is harder to find STO than ICO, let alone STOs that are worth investing in.

In addition, the lack of a middleman like banks, brokerages, and lawyers means that the responsibility of doing those jobs fall to the company, which also increases the administrative burden for the company.

Finally, there is the question of legality to consider. Depending on where you live, you may not even get to invest in STO since the jurisdiction limits who can invest in STOs. So, this also limits the investor pool.

**Which ICO/STO Should I Invest in?**

The best you can do is to do your own research and determine for yourself whether the project would be successful. You would need to read the white paper, know the team behind the project, and make sure that they have at least some experience in cryptocurrency.

You are safer investing in STOs if you can because of all the regulations they have to comply with before they can even launch. Their security tokens would be quite valuable if the company does well because you would then be entitled to a share of their spoils, and this is a great source of passive income.

For ICOs, you would need to be extra careful. Keep in mind that scams can be pulled off quite easily here. Cryptocurrency payments cannot be reversed unless you write the recipient a strongly worded letter and hope for the best.

## ON LEGAL MATTERS

As mentioned before, governments across the globe have a different view on cryptocurrency. Although you may have seen it coming from a mile away, it is usually the not-so-democratic countries that are banning cryptocurrencies whereas free states are finding ways to introduce regulations to this new digital asset as a way to integrate it into the free market.

**The United States**

The United States, for example, allows cryptocurrencies to operate in their jurisdiction. Although, the states do not have a consistent or uniform legal approach to cryptocurrencies. The laws change between states and even the federal law could not decide what cryptocurrency is.

While discussing what something is or isn’t might not be important to an average Joe, it can make or break an entire legal framework if you don’t get the definition right. For instance, the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network views cryptocurrencies as money transmitters, and the IRS sees them as property. It really does not help the situation here.

Crypto exchanges also have to deal with uncertainty because the government is undecided on the matter. For one, they do not know what law they need to comply with and which authority they should answer to. Different regulators have various jurisdictions and they get to call the shots. The problem is that many different regulators are claiming jurisdictions although there is no cohesive approach yet. The policy is still unclear.

As of now, there really is no uniform definition of “cryptocurrency” because states have different views on the matter. It is often referred to as a virtual currency, digital asset, digital tokens, crypto assets, or just crypto. Many jurisdictions in the United States adopt a more tech-agnostic, broader definition and some want to come up with detailed definitions. The latter would have to regulate when technology evolves, but they would have to keep up.

Although everything is muddled with confusion, at least the United States government is working hard toward creating some sort of overarching crypto regulation so people at least can say for sure how the government defines it and what laws would apply to it.

**The European Union**

The European Union has a different problem in regards to cryptocurrency. Although it is widely considered legal throughout the EU, the rules for exchanges are different depending on the states they are based in. That also means different taxes where you pay 0% in one state but 50% in the other. Crypto is also subject to capital gains tax. Looking at it now, the EU Parliament seems pretty content with the status quo as they have not passed any more specific legislation regarding cryptocurrencies.

Exchanges need to register with their local financial authority. Once that is done, they can operate within the EU’s jurisdiction. In addition, they also have to follow the EU’s anti-money laundering regulations. Some crypto exchanges allow users to convert from fiat currencies to cryptocurrencies without VAT.

**The United Kingdom**

The United Kingdom, since it separated from the EU a while ago, have created their own set of regulations for cryptocurrencies. The United Kingdom does not consider cryptocurrency to be legal tender but crypto exchanges are allowed to operate. The taxability of cryptocurrency depends on the parties and activities involved, but gains or losses are subject to capital gains tax.

Crypto exchanges need to first register with the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) before they are allowed to operate. Some exceptions apply where registrations are permitted online. The FCA also has the authority to supervise how businesses that deal in cryptocurrency handle terrorist financing and money laundering.

**Russia**

Russia has a complex relationship with cryptocurrency, but it appears that they have finally decided what to do about it. They are now taking action against the use of cryptocurrencies as they are classified as money substitutes. And a recent law outlawed money substitutes, so cryptocurrency is illegal under its classification under Russia’s jurisdiction. It still remains unclear what cryptocurrency can be used for then.

There are also proposals on the horizon that might allow the confiscation of cryptocurrencies. These proposals can be implemented as laws. Although it is unclear how the Russian government plans to go about confiscating cryptocurrencies since everything is anonymous and decentralized.

**China**

China’s stance on the issue is pretty clear. They reject cryptocurrencies and are working hard to purge them from their country. They have been at it for quite a long time now, in fact. The People’s Bank of China banned financial institutions from dealing with Bitcoin transactions since 2013. In 2017, they banned crypto exchanges and ICOs.

Back then, there were some workarounds, but those loopholes are quickly closed. Mining used to be rampant in China because it sorts of operates in a grey area, but the government is also looking to put a stop to that as well.

In short, the Chinese government has the toughest anti-crypto laws in the world and it is only going to get worse for them. Recent government crackdowns have seen some crypto exchanges based in China closed down.

**Japan**

Surprisingly enough, Japan has been one of the most progressive states in the world when it comes to regulations regarding cryptocurrencies. Japan used to be the biggest market for Bitcoin in 2017. Though it lost that title now, it still holds on well enough to be regarded as one of the top countries in terms of market size.

Cryptocurrencies are considered to be legal properties in Japan and they are subjected to tax as miscellaneous income. The regulations on crypto exchanges are also way ahead of their time, although there are a number of crypto exchange security breaches in the past. Since then, Japan is enforcing stricter rules for crypto exchanges to protect both the consumers and the companies that run the exchanges.

Japan is considered to be one of the friendliest countries toward cryptocurrency. Although, the cyberattacks on the crypto exchanges grabbed the attention of regulators. As a response, they now have a separate regulatory body that deals with crypto exchanges specifically. This is a move to restore the balance between progress and regulation.

**India**

It is a different story in India. Although cryptocurrencies are readily adopted by the Indian government, there is a clash between them and the Reserve Bank of India in court because the latter banned crypto. Although cryptocurrency is not banned in India, the court battle is still going on.

With this in mind, the future of cryptocurrency is still uncertain and only time will tell how it plays out. Much of the dispute revolves around how cryptocurrencies can be used to launder money and to fund terrorists. But, with the right regulations in place, measures can be implemented to combat those problems and foster progress.

## LOWERING YOUR TAXES

Believe it or not, there are a few ways you can lower your taxes. It’s not tax avoidance, of course. You would just be using the law to shave off some extra expenses that you could do without. Is it ethical? That is up to the philosophers.

But you may find comfort in knowing that large companies, especially transnational corporations, are always looking for ways to reduce taxes. The common folks are not aware of the intricacies of tax law, so they usually spend more than they have to. A few tips here and you should be able to cut off a few percentages of your hard-earned cash.

**Tax Loss Harvesting**

It sounds more ominous than it actually is. It works like this. If you see that some of your crypto holdings go down in value, you can sell them off to incur a loss. This will reduce your overall tax bill. It is not illegal or unethical. It is totally legal to balance capital gains with capital losses. So, if you have some crypto that has depreciated a lot in value, consider selling them off to reduce the tax on the gains you have made earlier in the fiscal year. You can offset it by up to $3,000, but always make sure to check with your local legislation.

**Long-Term Investment**

The IRS sees cryptocurrency as property. So, playing by their rule, its capital gains rate is lower if you hold onto your investment over a year. Therefore, if you buy and sell your crypto asset after nine months, your capital gains tax would be higher than if you sold your investments after 13 months.

Of course, there are other considerations to be made here. Because of the market volatility, you might be better off selling your crypto asset now rather than wait until the 12-month period has passed. Because, by then, your investment may decrease in value, and selling it off, even with the reduced tax, would still give you less money than if you were to sell it earlier.

So, this is something to think about. You might not know how the value of your investment would fluctuate in the future, so it is purely speculation.

The tax can reach 0% under certain conditions. The eligibility for which depends on your filing status, annual income, and how long you have been holding onto the coins before you sell them. You can find more details about the conditions listed in the U.S. tax code.

The 0% tax rate applies if you are single and make up to $40,000 a year. If you are married and file taxes separately with a $40,000 annual income, you are also entitled to a 0% tax rate. If you are the head of household and make up to $53,600 a year, that is also a 0% tax. If you are married, file your tax together, and make $80,000 a year, you are also eligible for 0% tax.

You can also donate your long-term crypto assets to qualified charities. Again, the asset needs to be in your possession for over 12 months. When you donate it to charity, you can get a tax deduction equivalent to its fair market value when you make that donation. Plus, you do not have to pay taxes on the capital gains for the donated crypto asset.

**Give Away Cryptocurrency**

Did you know that gifts under a certain amount are not be taxed? Otherwise, all Christmas gifts would be subject to taxes. That amount would be $15,000 every year. Of course, it might seem like a bit of a stretch to go out of your way to avoid tax, but if you want to give away money as Christmas gifts anyway, you can just send them some crypto as gifts.

**Use your IRA or 401-K**

Your tax would be reduced or outright omitted if you use your retirement account to buy cryptocurrencies. This is because all the income and gains from your retirement account would be returned into your account with a reduced tax or with no tax applied at all. This means that your crypto investment can continue to grow without any hindrance.

**Use a Crypto Tax Software**

We went over this before, but you can save yourself a couple of bucks here and there by using crypto tax software. With it, you can calculate exactly how much money you need to pay for the tax. The problem with crypto and tax is that, since cryptocurrencies’ values fluctuate over time, it can be difficult to keep track of exactly how much money you make and lose. This can quickly snowball into an impossible task. A crypto tax software can track everything for you.

**Get a Crypto Specialized CPA**

You might not think that this is a worthwhile investment, but getting a solid crypto accountant can save you a lot of money in the long run. They not only handle your crypto tax, but all your taxes for you, so you don’t have to worry about crunching numbers anymore.

Of course, hiring an accountant may be expensive, but their fees will pay for themselves over time because of the reduced tax. A certified public accountant not only handles the paperwork for you, but they are also familiar with relevant regulations so they can give you some clever advice on how you can lower your taxes even further. Not only that, if there are any changes to regulations, they will let you know about it.

# GETTING TECHNICAL WITH INVESTING

As you might have guessed, investing is a whole can of worms to get into. There are many nuances and caveats that you need to understand before you can consider yourself a mildly competent trader. The investment world is complex, which goes to show why many people decided to give up and pursue something a little less boring.

You can do what many users on SatoshiBets have been doing and just look at the charts and buy low and sell high. ## START SMALL

When you are looking to invest in a certain cryptocurrency or project, you need to find out a few things about them. For this, there are two analysis models: fundamental and technical. We went over these two briefly in Chapter 6 and we will discuss them in detail here.

Although the crypto market is unpredictable, you are better off knowing what causes prices to rise, fall, or stabilize. With some sound analysis, you can at least have a good idea of what to expect and set up your trade from there. A savvy trader would know when to get into the market and when to get out. They can see market trends and identify certain instances where something would happen. You can only really do that with the aforementioned analysis models.

**Fundamental Analysis**

The thing about this analysis model is that it works on all trading vehicles, be it crypto, forex, commodity, etc. It is compatible with all types of trading. The underlying concept is very simple. If you know that an asset has an intrinsic value that is higher than its current market price, then you trade based on your analysis and make a profit.

In other words, if you see projects that have a good chance of success, you want to get in early. When you invest and the people behind the project prove their capabilities, other investors would notice and jump in on the action. This would improve the overall price. For you, that means more profit.

That is the basic idea behind fundamental analysis method. You just do your research to determine whether the investment would increase in value in the future. But you can also profit from the investment becoming less valuable. For that, you would do what is called shorting, which we have already covered.

You might be skeptical about this analysis model since it makes everything so simple. But it works out more often than not.

If you know that the project has future potential and that its future value would be much higher than the present, you can buy in right now and ride the upward trend toward profit. But fundamental analysis does not only look at the big picture. Sometimes, you would need to think of the smaller details as well.

For instance, back in 2015, crypto developers would rebrand frequently by changing the name and logo. Doing so would increase the price for that cryptocurrency. However, this soon no longer has any significant impact.

As a trader, you need to understand how changes like this can influence how a project is viewed by market participants. More often than not, it does not really matter what you feel about the project. Rather, how the collective, the market, feels about it.

Sometimes, small changes can make a huge impact on the project as a whole. When performing fundamental analyses, always keep a lookout for what is happening and how the market is going to react to those changes. Understanding these, and also knowing the future potential for assets will put you several steps ahead of an average Joe.

**How to do Fundamental Analysis in Crypto**

Most crypto projects you see are very different from the companies you see nowadays. More often than not, you do not have a load of data to dive into the same way you would with normal stock investments. Because the cryptocurrency world is still very young, most of it is pure speculation. So, when you are scoping out a new investment venture, always make sure to look for the following things:

**Target Market**

Every project has a target market. That means, you would need to look at the size of the target market. You might assume that a larger market would be good, but it isn’t always the case. A large market could attract a lot of businesses already, and that means an oversaturated market. So, this decreases the likelihood of adoption. While niche markets are small, the customers are a lot more receptive to new solutions to a problem.

**Competition**

Competition is pretty much everywhere in any given industry. New industries can only go for a short time before more companies jump in on the action. This is a good thing for consumers because competing businesses mean lower prices and greater quality products. As a crypto investor, you can use competition to gauge the effectiveness of a crypto project.

First, you would need to consider competitors. How many are there? If there are a lot of businesses in the same industry, then it would be more difficult for your project to achieve adoption.

You also need to compare your project to its competitors. In doing so, you can see the strengths and weaknesses behind your project and you can even have an idea if your project stands a good chance of beating the competitors. Look at your project and evaluate the level of competition. Gauge whether the project is in good standing compared to the competitors. If the project offers something unique, it is a great sign that it would be successful.

**Team**

Any successful project has to have a very solid team behind it. At the very least, know who is on the senior management team, where they are from, what their experience is in crypto. The more information you have about the team, the better. A good project would have a skilled and experienced team on the wheel.

**Roadmap**

A roadmap is basically a guideline on what direction the project is going to go. It details future contents, features, or things that will be done as the project goes forward. This is to be expected since any project needs to have a plan. The development team would lay out this roadmap and you can take a look and see if they have a solid plan for their project. One thing you want to keep an eye out for is how ambitious their plan is. A bit of ambition is good, but too much means that the project would fail to meet its lofty goals.

**Development and Releases**

On the flip side, development and releases highlight how much progress the project has made and how it has performed since its inception in terms of development. Normally, the development team or the community manager would come out to give everyone an update and what to expect next. Communication between the investors and the development team is important, so regular updates would be a good sign of an effective project.

**Partnerships**

Partnerships are also a good way to determine the value of a project. However, it all comes down to the finer details, so make sure to look into those before you pass judgment.

**Demand, Token economics, and Utility**

The fundamental rule of economy dictates that supply and demand drive price and value. For an investor, you would need to consider demand first, then supply. The higher the demand, the higher the price. And demand is influenced by token economics or tokenomics as well as utility.

For token economics, you need to consider the economy surrounding the token. The token itself should be useful, as in having a wide range of applications so that there would be demand for it. When it comes to utility, you want to know if the coin has enough utility to create future demand. Roadmap also influences the project’s utility, so factor that in your decision.

**Status**

This refers to the status of the project, which is mostly related to the team behind it and the reputation the team has from previous projects if they have any. Unfortunately, though crypto projects may start from scratch, that does not translate into a level playing field.

If you launch a new cryptocurrency tomorrow and Amazon decided to do the same the very next day, people would flock to Amazon’s amazing tokens. This is one more reason to do a thorough background check on the team behind a project and this track record in the crypto world.

If some members of the team came from some popular cryptocurrencies and they have created many successful projects in the past, chances are that you are looking at a valuable project.

**White Paper**

We have mentioned this quite a few times in the past. Any project would have a white paper attached to it. It goes into the details of the projects. They are technical in nature, so it won’t be a very readable paper unless you have some experience in the crypto world. However, once you get over this problem, you would find that it is a very important piece of document.

In short, the paper outlines everything you need to know about the project. This can give you a much clearer picture of the whole situation and help you make an investment decision. If there is only one thing you have to look at when conducting fundamental analysis, it would be white papers. Always read it before deciding whether to invest.

**Community and Reviews**

This one is simple. Doing your own analysis would subject your findings to a certain bias that you are not entirely aware of. This is okay. You can get other people’s opinions on the matter by diving into the community and reading reviews on the project. This should give you a more objective view of the whole thing before you decide to put your money in.

**Real-World Use Case**

Just because you can does not mean you should. Many people get caught up with all the hype at the moment and forget to stop and ask the important question: why? Why does the project have to do things this way? Why does it have to have its own blockchain?

For instance, we have watches that can track how many steps we have taken today and it is connected to our phone via Bluetooth. That is fine and dandy. But what do you think of shoes that need to be connected to the internet, and you need to download an app that also needs to be connected to the internet in order to monitor your steps. It is a pretty roundabout way to doing things and is completely necessary.

So, always ask why. If there is no good or practical reason to do something a certain way, chances are that it is a solution in search of a problem. In other words, it’s probably a bad investment.

**Price History and Age**

Just like empires, cryptocurrencies rise and fall. There are some decisions you have to make here, and unfortunately, there is no way to tell if the answer is right or wrong unless backed up by other factors we have covered here.

You see, an established project would be easier to evaluate because you can look at its values since its beginning and compare that to others. A project will have longevity if it manages to maintain a consistent value over time. This would provide you with a stable investment, but the payout might not be that great.

On the other hand, you can invest in upcoming projects that are not well-known. It is a riskier investment, but if it is successful, you get to be an early joiner and get larger returns on investment.

**Liquidity and Volume**

Simply put, how many coins or tokens are there for this project? How often are they traded? You want to know how easily you buy or sell them off because all assets are worthless if no one is buying or selling them. If there is a lot of interest around a project, then there should be a lot of trading going on, and this is an indication that the cryptocurrency or token is in high demand.

**Market Cap**

You would need to look at the market capitalization or market cap. It is the total market value of the project or company or underlying cryptocurrency. In this case, the values would be derived from the supply of the cryptocurrency or token behind the project. Normally, a large market cap would have smaller growth potential whereas lower cap markets have more room for growth.

**Regulation**

We went over this before, but its importance can never be overstated. Make sure the project actually abides by the relevant laws and regulations. Otherwise, there could be adverse consequences on your investment in the future.

With that said, there are so many nuances and other little details that would need an entire book for itself. But what we have discussed above are some of the most important factors to consider. Over time, you will pick up on some other things you want to keep an eye on when you are scoping out for future investment.

**Technical Analysis**

Technical analysis is a whole other can of worms because it involves drastically different strategies than what was outlined in fundamental analysis. Here, you would crunch numbers, data, and deal with a lot of colorful charts that would leave beginners frustrated within the first 30 seconds unless they know how to interpret them. Luckily, it is not super complex and it should become second nature once you get into the groove of things.

Traders would look at price movement patterns and use various analytic tools to help them understand the strengths and weaknesses of each cryptocurrency. Previous price movements can help them predict future ones too. Just like fundamental analysis, technical analysis is also used in many markets such as stocks, futures, currencies, and forex. Luckily, the process is mostly the same so you can still apply this technique to other markets if you are interested in trading them.

**The Basic Ideas for Technical Analysis**

A few ideas from the Dow Theory give a basic understanding of technical analysis. First off, the theory says that pricing in the market reflects many things that can influence the price. In the crypto world, that “many things” include the regulations, traders’ expectations, demand in the past, present, and future, etc. Technical analysis would give traders an idea of the general sentiment around a market.

Technical analysis also makes the basic assumption that history often repeats itself, at least in the crypto world. For this reason, technical analysis seeks to predict market psychology and cryptocurrency based on data from the past.

Technical analysis also makes another assumption that price movement is not always random, even in the volatile crypto market. Price movements usually follow short or long-term trends. More often than not, when a cryptocurrency follows a certain trend, it will follow an opposite one soon after. Traders who use technical analysis can use this to identify price movement patterns and make profits.

Overall, technical analysis does not really look at the underlying value of the cryptocurrency. Instead, it only considers what is happening and why it happens.

**Reading the Charts**

So, with this in mind, let us start technical analysis by reading the charts. The most basic elements are candlesticks, trend lines, support and resistance levels, trading volumes, market caps, relative strength index, moving averages, and time frames.

**Candlesticks**

These are either red or green bars with a little line sticking out from above and below them, with varying lengths. These are called candlesticks although they sometimes look more like hammers.

Every element of the candlestick, from the color to the length of the lines at the top and bottom as well as the length of the “body”, or the bars tells a lot of stories. First of all, the top and bottom of the candlestick’s rectangle indicate the opening and closing prices of the cryptocurrency at that instance.

Green means that the crypto rises in value, so the closing price is at the top and the opening price is at the bottom. If it is red, then it indicates that the crypto price drops in value and the closing price is at the bottom and the opening price is at the top.

The little lines poking out from the top and bottom are called wicks. They indicate the lowest and highest prices that the coin managed to reach within the same time period. The wick can give you an idea of market volatility.

And this is the most basic level of technical analysis. You can use the candlesticks to guide you in making future predictions. For instance, if you can see that the wicks are long, you know that the market is volatile and the waves will be choppy going forward. You can take this time to set up your trades so that you can buy the dip and sell at the peak, given that you time everything properly. Other than that, it also tells you that the market will correct itself by tomorrow.

If the wicks are short, then there could be a change in the market on the horizon. The potential change depends on the wick. If the top wick is short, this indicates that the coin reached a peak price and one that is possibly one of its highest in its history, hence a price ceiling or resistance level. If the top wick is very long, it tells us that the coin was much higher in price at a certain point in the day before traders cashed in for profit. This type of pattern occurring at the peak of an upward trend can indicate that the trend is coming to an end and a downward trend is imminent.

If the bottom wick is short, it means that people continue to sell the coin. This would bump up the coin supply, further driving the price down. A long bottom wick suggests that the price used to dip in the past and that general sentiment is that the price could not go any lower, hence a price floor or a support level. You can usually spot this at the bottom of a downward trend right before an upward trend. Because by then, people believe that this is the best time to buy the coins and they would do just that. In doing so, supply would dip and the price would go up, further encouraging more people to buy the coin.

**Trend Lines**

Along with the candlesticks, you might also see a line that vaguely follows the candlestick patterns. This is the trend line. Trend lines simply point out the direction that the crypto price moves. But they also contain some other information only perceivable to a trained eye.

This is especially true in the volatile crypto market. Traders need to understand the overarching trend that goes up or down with all the little highs and lows. To make matters worse, trends do not just move up or down. They can also move sideways, which indicates that the cryptocurrency has neither significantly moved up or down. But if it stays this way for long, then it could lead to some explosive breakouts that can see massive gains or losses.

Most exchanges or trading platforms would come with a trend line built into their charts. These are done for you automatically, though some allow you to tweak the settings to make the readings more sensitive to changes. You can normally get away with the default settings, but for those who want to squeeze a bit more efficiency out of their trading, they have that option to change the trend line settings. How the platform draws the trend line is different based on the platform you use.

**Support and Resistance Levels**

We have mentioned this a bit earlier. Both of these are horizontal lines that you can draw on your trading chart to better understand the price movement for that particular coin. Some would argue that it would be better to see these levels as areas rather than lines. Some trading software allows you to draw a line, but if yours doesn’t have that feature, you can just eyeball it.

The support level is basically a price floor. You can see this with the candlestick and the trend line. They never drop below a certain point. They get close to it a few times, but never below it. That would be your support level. This is the perceived lowest price point for that coin and this is usually where a lot of traders would buy in. This will either stop the coin price from going even further down or reverse the trend entirely and jumpstart an upward trend.

The resistance level is the opposite. It is a line or an area where prices just would not go any higher. This is an indication that buyers feel that the price is already quite high, so they are not going to buy into it anymore. This also means that buyers are tempted to sell off their coins because the prices are as good as they can get, therefore increasing supply and drive the price back down.

When the price reaches either of these levels, it is testing the line. The more it is tested, the weaker it becomes. Support and resistance levels are not permanent, however. Prices will break through these levels eventually and will end up establishing new support and resistance levels.

A few key indications of the trend’s strength include a breakout of either support or resistance levels. Sometimes, the resistance level can become the new support level or vice versa, which further reinforces the strength of the current trend. Of course, a false breakout, or a fakeout, can also occur. Sometimes, a fakeout can occur at such a magnitude that it triggers your stop-loss or take-profit, much to the frustration of traders. Because of that, traders are advised to put their stop-loss and take-profit a little bit further and to look at different indicators to help them define trends.

**Trading Volumes**

Trading volumes also help the trader know whether a trend is significant. If there is a high trading volume, it means that the trend is significant. On the other hand, if there is a low trading volume, it means that the trend is weak and will pass by quickly.

So, check the trading volume if there is a drop in price for the coin. You want to check if there is low volume if the price drops and high volumes if the price goes up. That means there is a healthy trend going on with long-term growth. But, if the trading volume goes down during an upward trend, that means the trend will end soon. The opposite applies to downtrends.

**Market Caps**

You can look at the market cap to give you an idea of a coin’s stability. Normally, the larger the market cap is for that cryptocurrency, the more stable it is. Calculating the market cap is pretty simple if your exchange does not provide it to you already. You just take the number of coins in circulation and multiply it by their current price.

**Relative Strength Index**

You can only get so far with eyeballing the charts, looking at the candles, and following the trend lines. Sometimes, you would need to use indicators, and there are a lot to choose from. One of them is called the RSI indicator, or relative strength index indicator.

There is a bunch of math involved here. Luckily, you don’t have to crunch numbers since most platforms do that for you. You can enable the RSI indicator on the charting software. It is displayed as a line or two lines, on the spectrum from 0 to 100. If the RSI is at 30 or lower, that means the crypto is undervalued and the price is likely to go up very soon. If the RSI is 70 or above, that means the crypto is overvalued, which may indicate that people would start to sell off their coins, and the price would drop very soon.

**Moving Averages**

There is an indicator for this as well. As the name suggests, a moving average is the crypto’s average price over a certain period. The default calculation is based on the trading prices of the coin in the last 20 days. You can draw a line with the moving averages to help you make predictions about future price movements.

Most traders use an exponential moving average indicator to help them trade. EMA involves a little more math since it is more sensitive to recent price movements. You can use different moving averages at the same time, over different time frames. For instance, if one short-term moving average crosses over a long-term moving average, that could mean that an upward trend is imminent.

**Time Frame**

Then, there is also the time frame to consider. There is a lot to choose from such as one, five, fifteen, and 60 minutes, all the way to 24 hours and more. You should choose your time frame based on the way you trade.

For instance, if you are a day, intraday, or short-term trader, you should close all your positions within the day. That means, you should select a shorter time frame than 24 hours. Many traders would opt for 5, 15, or 60 minutes. You might be able to work with a larger time frame, but it is not recommended.

Long-term traders usually can afford to keep their positions open for a long time. This can be as long as several years. For this reason, long-term traders are better off looking at larger time periods such as 4 hours, 24 hours, or weekly charts.

## FACTORS THAT INFLUENCE INVESTMENT

It is also worth noting external factors that can change the price of your cryptocurrency. Given how volatile it is, a single event can negatively impact your investment. So, it pays to keep an eye out for a few things and get ready to take action when something happens. So, what should you look out for?

**Government**

As of now, governments across the globe do not have a uniform view of cryptocurrencies. Some of them are working to regulate cryptocurrencies whereas others outright ban them. Many jurisdictions are still on the fence about the matter and whichever stance they take can influence the price of your digital asset. For instance, if a government decided to ban cryptocurrency or impose heavy tax on it, users in that country would be discouraged from using it and its price would drop. If a government accepts crypto, there may be more users coming into the market, which would drive prices up.

**Financial Institutions**

Major financial institutions such as banks and credit card companies are still your money outlet when you want to get coins from your cash. Therefore, they are among the most powerful players that influence the future of cryptocurrency. If they decide to co-exist with crypto, or even better, integrate crypto into their banking systems, then crypto’s values would rise significantly. However, if they go out of their way just to stifle crypto’s growth, then its price would suffer accordingly.

**Businesses**

Major and popular companies or businesses that adopt crypto payments can also increase the value of cryptocurrencies across the board. That can be a restaurant chain, a hotel, or online retailers. Their inclusion of crypto payment would help legitimize cryptocurrency for customers.

**Trading Platforms**

Then, there are the platforms that offer traditional assets such as stocks or forex. If they announce that they would also deal in crypto trading, this would promote cryptocurrencies to their users.

**Crypto Economy**

The above are factors outside the cryptocurrency sphere. In the crypto world, several things can alter the price of a certain cryptocurrency. That could be a successful cyberattack, a new cryptocurrency that positions itself as a direct competitor to the coin you are investing in, etc.

In short, many things influence the price of a cryptocurrency, most of which are outside your control. Therefore, traders are advised to keep an eye out for crypto-related news and act accordingly. Information is key here.
