Let a strictly positive integer k be given.

+   - φ is said to be Σ₁,ₖ in ℳ iff there exists positive formulas ψ₁, ..., ψₖ such that:
+     - for all p ∈ {1, ..., k−1}, ψₚ₊₁ logically implies ψₚ in ℳ;
+     - φ is logically equivalent to ∨₁≤p≤k, p mod 2=1 (ψₚ ∧ ∧ₚ<q≤k ∼ψ_q) in ℳ.
+   - φ is said to be Π₁,ₖ in ℳ iff ∼φ is Σ₁,ₖ in ℳ.

Figure 7.2 illustrates the kind of picture that is associated with a formula that is Σ₁,₈ in ℳ. We would have that ψ₁ is a positive formula that defines the outermost disk, ψ₂ a positive formula that defines the second outermost disk... and ψ₈ a positive formula that defines the innermost disk, the whole formula defining the union of all black rings: you are in the first and not in the second outermost disk, unless you are in the third and not in the fourth outermost disk, unless you are in the fifth and not in the sixth outmost disk, unless you are in the seventh and not in the eight outermost disk. More generally, as the value of k increases in Σ₁,ₖ, the number of disks in the associated picture increases, with the outermost ring being filled for any value of k, and with the innermost ring being filled for odd values of k, and not being filled for even values of k.
