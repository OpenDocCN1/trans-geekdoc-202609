Let X' be the set of atoms in X. Define S_σ as the set of members of 𝔞 that either belong to X', or that are conjuncts in some of the conjunctions in X \ X'. We infer from Proposition 40 that there exists σ ∈ T with 𝔐 ⊨ σ || r. Obviously, S_σ ⊂ S. Moreover, it is easy to verify that for all S' ⊆ S with S_σ ⊆ S', if 𝔐' is the interpretation such that for all p ∈ 𝔞, 𝔐' ⊨ p iff p ∈ S', then 𝔐' ⊨ σ ||_T r, hence ℓ⟨𝔐'⟩ = ℓ⟨𝔐⟩, in contradiction with the assumption that (T, ℓ) recognises ⊕_{1 ≤ i ≤ n} p_i. Therefore, since there are 2^n many subsets of 𝔞, T must have at least 2^n many elements.
