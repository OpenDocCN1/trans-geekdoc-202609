# Proposition 66.

Let a formula φ be Σ₁,k but not Π₁,k in ℳ, or Π₁,k but not Σ₁,k in ℳ. Then some parity-based classifying 𝒜^∧-gradation graph of depth k + 1 recognises φ, that is negative by default or positive by default, respectively.

Proof. Without loss of generality, assume that all atoms occur in φ. Given a finite set D of atoms, let \widetilde{D} denote D ∪ {¬ψ | ψ ∈ 𝒜 \ D}. Inductively define sets X₀, …, X_k of finite subsets of A and a binary relation E on ⋃_{i≤k} X_i, setting X₀ to {∅} and E to ∅ to start with. Let i < k be given, and assume that X_i has been defined and E has been appropriately extended. For all D ∈ X_i, put in X_{i+1} all ⊆-minimal subsets D' of A such that \widetilde{D'} ⊨_ℳ φ iff \widetilde{D} ⊨_ℳ ∼φ, and extend E with (∧D, ∧D'). Set V = {∧D | D ∈ ⋃_{i≤k} X_i}. It is immediately verified that (V,E) is an 𝒜^∧-gradation graph that satisfies the claim of the proposition.
