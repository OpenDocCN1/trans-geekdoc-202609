# Key Components of DeFi

Decentralized Finance (DeFi) is a financial ecosystem built on blockchain technology that eliminates intermediaries like banks and brokers. Instead, it uses **smart contracts**, protocols, and decentralized applications (dApps) to provide open access to financial services such as lending, borrowing, trading, and investing. DeFi’s rapid growth stems from its modular and transparent design, where various components interconnect to create a flexible and permissionless financial network.

**Smart contracts** are the backbone of DeFi. These self-executing agreements automatically enforce terms and conditions without requiring a trusted third party. For example, when a user deposits collateral into a DeFi lending platform, a smart contract manages the loan, interest calculations, and repayments. Smart contracts are deployed on public blockchains like Ethereum and Binance Smart Chain, making their code visible to anyone. This transparency ensures that users can verify how their funds are being managed, though it also requires developers to write secure and error-free code.

**Decentralized exchanges (DEXs)** are critical components of the DeFi ecosystem. Unlike centralized exchanges, DEXs allow users to trade cryptocurrencies directly with one another using liquidity pools and automated market makers (AMMs). Popular DEXs like **Uniswap**, **SushiSwap**, and **PancakeSwap** enable peer-to-peer trading without requiring custody of funds. Users maintain control of their private keys throughout the process, reducing counterparty risk. Liquidity pools on DEXs are funded by other users, who earn fees in return for providing liquidity. This incentivizes participation and ensures that trades can occur even without traditional market makers.

**Lending and borrowing protocols** are another cornerstone of DeFi. Platforms like **Aave**, **Compound**, and **MakerDAO** allow users to lend their cryptocurrencies and earn interest or borrow against their holdings by depositing collateral. Borrowers can access funds without credit checks, as loans are overcollateralized to mitigate risk. For example, if a user wants to borrow $1,000 in stablecoins, they might need to deposit $1,500 worth of Ether as collateral. This system ensures that the protocol remains solvent even if the borrower defaults. However, volatile markets can lead to liquidations if the value of the collateral falls below a certain threshold.

Unlike volatile cryptocurrencies, **stablecoins** are pegged to fiat currencies or other assets, ensuring predictable value.
