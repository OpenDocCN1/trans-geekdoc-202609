While DEXs are gaining popularity, they remain less user-friendly than CEXs. Managing wallets, understanding AMMs, and dealing with gas fees can be intimidating for beginners. However, the growing adoption of tools like **WalletConnect** and improved interface designs are making DEXs more accessible to a broader audience.

The choice between CEXs and DEXs ultimately depends on user priorities. CEXs offer convenience, speed, and services tailored to professional traders, but they come with security and privacy risks. DEXs prioritize decentralization, control, and anonymity but require a higher level of technical knowledge and have limitations in liquidity and scalability. Many traders use a combination of both, leveraging CEXs for fiat on-ramps and larger trades, while turning to DEXs for privacy, niche tokens, and decentralized finance opportunities.

# Understanding Trading Pairs

In cryptocurrency trading, a **trading pair** refers to the two assets being traded against each other. When you see pairs like **BTC/USD** or **ETH/BTC** on an exchange, it means you can trade Bitcoin for US dollars or Ethereum for Bitcoin, respectively. Trading pairs are essential because they establish the exchange rate between two assets, allowing traders to determine how much of one currency they can get in exchange for another.

Most cryptocurrency exchanges categorize trading pairs into two main groups: **fiat-to-crypto** and **crypto-to-crypto**. Fiat-to-crypto pairs, like BTC/USD or ETH/EUR, allow users to trade cryptocurrencies against traditional currencies. These pairs are particularly useful for onboarding new users into the crypto space, as they provide a direct way to buy digital assets using cash. Crypto-to-crypto pairs, such as BTC/ETH or ADA/USDT, involve trading one cryptocurrency for another. These pairs are ideal for users already holding cryptocurrencies who want to diversify or speculate on price movements.

Stablecoins like **USDT (Tether)** and **USDC (USD Coin)** provide a stable reference point for pricing cryptocurrencies, acting as a substitute for fiat currencies on many exchanges. For instance, instead of trading BTC/USD, a trader might use BTC/USDT. Stablecoins reduce the need for frequent fiat conversions, especially on exchanges that do not support direct fiat trading.

The order of a trading pair matters. In a pair like BTC/ETH, Bitcoin is the **base currency**, and Ethereum is the **quote currency**. This means the price displayed represents how much of the quote currency (ETH) is required to purchase one unit of the base currency (BTC). If the price of BTC/ETH is 15, it means one Bitcoin can be exchanged for 15 Ethereum. Understanding this distinction is key to reading price charts and placing orders correctly.
