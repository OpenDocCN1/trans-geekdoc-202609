**Compound** is another popular platform, known for its algorithmic approach to interest rates. Interest rates on Compound adjust dynamically based on supply and demand within each market. When many users lend an asset, the interest rate decreases to encourage borrowing. Conversely, when borrowing demand is high, interest rates rise, incentivizing lenders to provide liquidity. This self-regulating mechanism keeps markets efficient without the need for human intervention.

Stablecoins like **USDC**, **USDT**, and **DAI** dominate lending and borrowing markets due to their price stability. Borrowers often prefer stablecoins to avoid the risk of holding volatile assets, especially when they need liquidity for real-world expenses or trading strategies. For lenders, stablecoins provide predictable returns without exposure to significant price fluctuations. Platforms like Aave and Compound also support a wide variety of cryptocurrencies, including Bitcoin, Ethereum, and altcoins, allowing users to diversify their portfolios.

Interest rates in crypto lending platforms are typically higher than those in traditional savings accounts, attracting both individual and institutional investors. However, the rates vary widely depending on the asset and market conditions. For instance, during a market rally, borrowing demand often surges, driving up interest rates for popular assets. Conversely, during bearish periods, borrowing demand may decline, leading to lower returns for lenders.

Borrowers on these platforms often use loans to leverage their trading positions. For example, a trader might deposit Ethereum as collateral to borrow stablecoins, which can then be used to buy more Ethereum. This strategy, known as **leveraged trading**, amplifies potential gains but also increases the risk of liquidation. If the collateral value falls below a specified threshold due to market volatility, the platform automatically liquidates part or all of the collateral to repay the loan. Liquidation events are common in crypto lending and highlight the importance of maintaining a safe margin to protect against sudden price drops.

Another innovative feature of crypto lending platforms is **flash loans**, pioneered by Aave. Flash loans allow users to borrow assets without collateral, provided the loan is repaid within the same blockchain transaction. These loans are useful for arbitrage, refinancing, or exploiting temporary price inefficiencies. However, flash loans have also been exploited in high-profile attacks, where malicious actors use them to manipulate DeFi protocols and drain funds. While these incidents underscore the need for better security, they also highlight the flexibility and power of blockchain-based financial tools.

For instance, Aave’s native token, **AAVE**, and Compound’s token, **COMP**, give holders voting rights over protocol changes, such as adjusting interest rate models or adding new assets.
