**Ethereum’s move from PoW to PoS,** completed in 2022 with "The Merge," is one of the most notable examples of this transition. Under the PoW system, Ethereum relied on miners using GPUs to solve cryptographic puzzles, similar to Bitcoin. However, the increasing environmental impact and inefficiency of mining led the Ethereum community to develop Ethereum 2.0, a PoS-based network. With the upgrade, validators replaced miners, and staking became the new mechanism for securing the blockchain. Validators are selected to propose and validate blocks based on the amount of ETH they stake, incentivizing participants to act honestly. Malicious behavior, such as attempting to validate fraudulent transactions, results in penalties, including losing part or all of the staked ETH—a process known as **slashing.**

The shift to staking offers several benefits over mining. First, PoS is **far more energy-efficient**. Mining requires vast amounts of electricity to power hardware, but staking significantly reduces this demand. For example, after Ethereum’s transition, the network’s energy consumption dropped by over 99%. This reduction aligns with broader efforts to make blockchain technology more environmentally sustainable, addressing criticisms that cryptocurrencies contribute to global energy waste.

Staking also lowers the barriers to entry for participation. In mining, users need expensive hardware, technical expertise, and access to cheap electricity to remain competitive. PoS networks, however, require participants to hold and lock up cryptocurrency, which is more accessible to a broader audience. This democratization of participation helps decentralize networks further, as more users can become validators without needing specialized equipment. Some networks, like **Cardano** and **Polkadot**, have implemented staking pools, allowing individuals with smaller holdings to combine their resources and share rewards, further enhancing accessibility.

Another advantage of staking is its scalability. PoW networks often struggle with transaction throughput and confirmation times due to the limitations of mining. PoS networks, by contrast, streamline block validation, enabling faster and more efficient consensus. Protocols like **Solana**, which use a PoS variant combined with **Proof of History**, achieve transaction speeds of thousands per second, making them competitive with centralized systems like Visa. This scalability is essential as blockchain adoption grows and networks face increasing demands.

The transition to staking has also introduced **liquid staking**, a feature that addresses one of the key drawbacks of traditional staking: the inability to access staked funds. In standard staking systems, participants lock up their cryptocurrency for a set period, which limits liquidity and flexibility.
