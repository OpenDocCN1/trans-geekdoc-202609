Despite its achievements, Ethereum has faced challenges, particularly around scalability and transaction costs. During periods of high demand, gas fees can spike to hundreds of dollars, pricing out smaller users and limiting accessibility. These limitations prompted the transition from **proof of work** to **proof of stake** with Ethereum 2.0, a series of upgrades aimed at improving scalability, energy efficiency, and transaction throughput. The shift to PoS reduces energy consumption by over 99% and introduces shard chains to distribute transaction loads across multiple parallel blockchains.

Ethereum’s programmability also exposed it to **security vulnerabilities**, leading to high-profile incidents like the **DAO hack** in 2016. The DAO was an Ethereum-based investment fund governed by smart contracts, but a flaw in its code allowed an attacker to drain a significant portion of its funds. This event highlighted the risks of deploying unaudited or poorly designed contracts and led to the controversial Ethereum hard fork, which reversed the hack’s effects and split the network into Ethereum and Ethereum Classic.

Ethereum’s influence extends beyond the blockchain space, inspiring experiments with decentralized governance, tokenized assets, and programmable money. Governments and corporations are exploring Ethereum-based solutions for supply chain management, identity verification, and carbon credit tracking. Its open-source nature invites collaboration, enabling innovators to build on its foundation and push the boundaries of what’s possible with blockchain technology.

# ## Real-World Applications

Smart contracts are driving innovation across a range of industries by automating processes, reducing costs, and ensuring transparency. One of the most transformative applications is in **finance**, where smart contracts underpin decentralized finance platforms. Lending protocols like **Aave** and decentralized exchanges such as **Uniswap** allow users to borrow, lend, and trade without intermediaries. By automating these services, smart contracts make financial tools accessible to a global audience, especially those excluded from traditional banking systems.

In **real estate**, smart contracts simplify property transactions by automating escrow services, title transfers, and payment processing. A smart contract can ensure that ownership is transferred only after payment is verified on the blockchain, reducing the need for intermediaries like lawyers or title companies.
