# Overview of Popular Altcoins

Altcoins, short for "alternative coins," are cryptocurrencies other than Bitcoin. They were created to improve upon Bitcoin's limitations or to serve specific use cases. Some focus on faster transactions, others on privacy, and many introduce entirely new functionalities. The altcoin market is vast, with thousands of coins in circulation, but a few stand out due to their popularity, innovation, or widespread adoption.

**Ethereum** is by far the most prominent altcoin, often regarded as the second-largest cryptocurrency by market capitalization. Unlike Bitcoin, which is primarily a store of value and payment network, Ethereum was designed as a platform for building **decentralized applications**. Its core innovation is the use of **smart contracts**, self-executing agreements coded onto the blockchain that run when predefined conditions are met. These contracts eliminate the need for intermediaries, enabling decentralized finance, non-fungible tokens (NFTs), and countless other applications.

Another well-known altcoin is **Cardano (ADA)**, which positions itself as a more sustainable and scalable version of Ethereum. It uses a PoS consensus mechanism called **Ouroboros** and emphasizes academic research and peer-reviewed development. Cardano focuses on creating a secure, scalable ecosystem for smart contracts and dApps, targeting applications in finance, education, and governance. Its layered architecture separates the settlement layer from the computation layer, allowing for flexibility and upgrades without disrupting the network.

**Binance Coin (BNB)** originated as the utility token for the Binance exchange, one of the world's largest cryptocurrency trading platforms. Initially built on Ethereum, BNB later migrated to its own blockchain, the Binance Chain. While it started as a way to reduce trading fees on the Binance platform, BNB has grown into a multi-purpose token. It powers transactions on Binance Smart Chain (BSC), a blockchain known for its low fees and fast transaction speeds, making it a popular choice for DeFi projects and dApps.

**Ripple (XRP)** is a cryptocurrency designed for cross-border payments and international remittances. Unlike Bitcoin and Ethereum, which rely on mining, Ripple uses a consensus algorithm known as the **XRP Ledger Consensus Protocol** to validate transactions. This allows XRP to process thousands of transactions per second with minimal energy usage. Ripple works closely with banks and financial institutions, positioning itself as a bridge currency for global transactions. Its partnerships with companies like Santander and American Express showcase its potential in modernizing traditional finance.
