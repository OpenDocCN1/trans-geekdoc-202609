# What Are Smart Contracts?

Smart contracts are self-executing agreements written in code and deployed on a blockchain. They automate processes by executing specific actions when predefined conditions are met. Unlike traditional contracts, which require intermediaries to enforce terms, smart contracts rely on cryptographic algorithms to ensure trust and accuracy. They are foundational to blockchain ecosystems, enabling decentralized applications and various use cases in cryptocurrency, finance, and beyond.

The concept of smart contracts was first introduced in the 1990s by computer scientist **Nick Szabo**, who envisioned digital agreements that could enforce themselves without human intervention. However, blockchain technology, particularly **Ethereum**, brought this vision to life by providing a platform where smart contracts could operate securely and transparently. Each contract is deployed on the blockchain and becomes immutable, meaning it cannot be altered once written. This ensures fairness and trust for all parties involved.

At their core, smart contracts function through **if-then logic**. For instance, a simple smart contract might state, "If Bob sends 1 Ether to the contract, then release the ownership of a digital asset to Bob." When the condition is fulfilled, the contract automatically executes the specified action. This eliminates the need for intermediaries like banks, lawyers, or escrow agents, reducing costs and delays.

Smart contracts operate on decentralized networks like **Ethereum**, **Solana**, and **Binance Smart Chain**, leveraging blockchain’s security and transparency. When a smart contract is created, its code and logic are stored on the blockchain, making it accessible to anyone for verification. The decentralized nature of the blockchain ensures that no single entity can manipulate or censor the contract.

The **Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM)** is one of the most prominent environments for running smart contracts. It acts as a decentralized computer that processes the contract’s code across all Ethereum nodes. Smart contracts on Ethereum are written in programming languages like **Solidity** or **Vyper**, designed specifically for creating and managing contracts on the blockchain. Once deployed, the contract interacts with the network, receiving and sending data or tokens based on its programmed logic.

Smart contracts are used in a variety of applications, with **decentralized finance** being one of the most prominent. In DeFi, smart contracts power platforms for lending, borrowing, trading, and yield farming.
