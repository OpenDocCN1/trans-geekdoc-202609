
- **Altcoin**: Any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin.
- **AML (Anti-Money Laundering)**: Regulations designed to prevent illicit financial activities using cryptocurrencies.
- **Arbitrage**: Profiting from price differences of the same cryptocurrency across different exchanges.
- **ASIC (Application-Specific Integrated Circuit)**: Specialized hardware designed for efficient cryptocurrency mining.
- **Atomic Swap**: A smart contract that allows two parties to exchange cryptocurrencies without intermediaries.
- **Bear Market**: A prolonged period of declining cryptocurrency prices.
- **Bitcoin (BTC)**: The first and most well-known cryptocurrency, created by Satoshi Nakamoto.
- **Block**: A unit of data containing transaction information, added to a blockchain.
- **Block Explorer**: A tool used to view and analyze blockchain transactions and data.
- **Block Reward**: Cryptocurrency awarded to miners for successfully validating a block of transactions.
- **Blockchain**: A decentralized, immutable ledger that records transactions in chronological order.
- **Burning**: The process of permanently removing cryptocurrency from circulation to reduce supply.
- **Bull Market**: A sustained period of rising cryptocurrency prices.
- **Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC)**: A digital version of fiat currency issued by a central bank.
- **Cold Wallet**: An offline wallet used to securely store cryptocurrencies.
- **Consensus Mechanism**: The method used to achieve agreement on the blockchain, such as Proof of Work or Proof of Stake.
- **Crypto Wallet**: A tool for storing private keys and accessing cryptocurrencies.
- **Cryptography**: The practice of secure communication, foundational to blockchain and cryptocurrency.
- **DAO (Decentralized Autonomous Organization)**: A blockchain-based organization governed by smart contracts and token holders.
- **dApp (Decentralized Application)**: An application that runs on a blockchain rather than centralized servers.
- **Decentralization**: The absence of a central authority, with control distributed across a network.
- **DeFi (Decentralized Finance)**: Financial systems built on blockchain technology without intermediaries.
- **Difficulty**: A measure of how hard it is to mine a block in a blockchain network.
- **Double Spending**: Fraudulent activity where the same cryptocurrency is spent twice.
- **Dumping**: Selling off large amounts of cryptocurrency, causing its price to drop.
- **DYOR (Do Your Own Research)**: A term encouraging individuals to independently research crypto projects before investing.
- **ERC-20**: A standard for tokens built on the Ethereum blockchain.
- **Ethereum (ETH)**: A blockchain platform known for its smart contract capabilities.
- **Exchange**: A platform where users can buy, sell, and trade cryptocurrencies.
- **Fiat Currency**: Government-issued currency like USD, EUR, or JPY.
- **Fork**: A split in a blockchain resulting in two separate chains.
- **FOMO (Fear of Missing Out)**: A feeling of urgency to buy cryptocurrency due to rising prices.
- **FUD (Fear, Uncertainty, and Doubt)**: Negative sentiment spread to influence markets.
- **Gas**: Fees paid to execute transactions on the Ethereum blockchain.
- **Halving**: A scheduled event that reduces the reward for mining cryptocurrency, typically in Bitcoin.
- **Hard Fork**: A blockchain split requiring all participants to upgrade to a new protocol version.
- **Hash**: A cryptographic function used to secure blockchain transactions.
- **Hash Rate**: The computational power used in mining cryptocurrencies.
- **HODL**: A slang term for holding cryptocurrency rather than selling.
- **ICO (Initial Coin Offering)**: A fundraising method where new cryptocurrencies are sold to investors.
- **Immutable**: A characteristic of blockchains that prevents data from being altered once recorded.
- **Interoperability**: The ability of different blockchains to communicate and share data.
- **KYC (Know Your Customer)**: A process requiring users to verify their identity.
- **Ledger**: A record-keeping system, such as a blockchain.
- **Lightning Network**: A Layer 2 scaling solution for Bitcoin that enables faster and cheaper transactions.
- **Liquidity**: The ease of converting a cryptocurrency to cash or other assets.
- **Market Cap**: The total value of a cryptocurrency, calculated by multiplying price by circulating supply.
- **Mempool**: A temporary storage area for unconfirmed transactions waiting to be added to the blockchain.
- **Merkle Tree**: A data structure used to efficiently and securely verify blockchain transactions.
- **Metaverse**: A virtual world integrating blockchain, cryptocurrencies, and NFTs.
- **Mining**: The process of validating transactions and creating new cryptocurrency.
- **Mining Pool**: A group of miners combining computational resources to share rewards.
- **NFT (Non-Fungible Token)**: A unique digital asset verified on a blockchain.
- **Node**: A device that maintains a copy of a blockchain and validates transactions.
- **Nonce**: A value used in mining to create a valid block hash.
- **Oracles**: Services that provide real-world data to smart contracts on a blockchain.
- **Paper Wallet**: A physical copy of private and public keys for offline storage.
- **Private Key**: A secret code granting access to a cryptocurrency wallet.
- **Proof of Stake (PoS)**: A consensus mechanism requiring validators to stake cryptocurrency.
- **Proof of Work (PoW)**: A consensus mechanism requiring computational effort to validate transactions.
- **Protocol**: A set of rules governing blockchain operation.
- **Public Key**: A cryptographic code used to receive cryptocurrencies.
- **Pump-and-Dump**: Coordinated manipulation to inflate a cryptocurrency’s price before selling off.
- **Satoshi**: The smallest unit of Bitcoin, equal to 0.00000001 BTC.
- **Scalability**: A blockchain’s ability to handle increasing transaction volume.
- **Seed Phrase**: A series of words used to recover a cryptocurrency wallet.
- **SHA-256**: A cryptographic algorithm used in Bitcoin mining.
- **Sharding**: A scalability technique dividing blockchain data into smaller partitions.
- **Sidechain**: A separate blockchain connected to a main chain for scalability or functionality.
- **Smart Contract**: Self-executing agreements coded onto a blockchain.
- **Soft Fork**: A blockchain update that remains compatible with older versions.
- **Stablecoin**: A cryptocurrency pegged to a stable asset, such as fiat currency.
- **Staking**: Locking cryptocurrency to participate in network validation and earn rewards.
- **Supply Cap**: The maximum number of coins that can ever exist for a cryptocurrency.
- **Testnet**: A blockchain used for testing and experimentation.
- **Token Burn**: The act of destroying tokens to reduce supply and increase scarcity.
- **Tokenomics**: The economic design of a cryptocurrency or token.
- **Transaction Fee**: Fees paid to miners or validators for processing blockchain transactions.
- **Total Value Locked (TVL)**: The total value of assets locked in a DeFi platform.
- **Utility Token**: A cryptocurrency granting access to a product or service.
- **Volatility**: The degree of price fluctuation in a cryptocurrency.
- **Wallet**: A tool for storing and managing cryptocurrencies.
- **Web3**: The next generation of the internet, emphasizing decentralization and blockchain integration.
- **Whale**: An individual or entity holding a large amount of cryptocurrency.
- **Whitepaper**: A document outlining the purpose and technology of a cryptocurrency project.
- **Wrapped Token**: A tokenized version of a cryptocurrency, allowing use on a different blockchain.
- **Yield Farming**: Earning rewards by lending or staking cryptocurrency in DeFi platforms.
- **Zero-Knowledge Proof**: A cryptographic method for verifying data without revealing it.
- **Zombie Chain**: A blockchain no longer actively maintained or developed.
- **Zk-SNARKs**: Cryptographic proofs enabling private transactions on blockchains.
