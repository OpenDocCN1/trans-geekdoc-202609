Entering my personal email address into PayPal, I am presented with four different ways to verify my identity. The first option is text message verification. Figure 17.20 shows the PayPal screen, which displays five digits of my phone number.

Comparing this with what we learned from Gmail, we can identify that the phone numbers both end in 68. This is a pretty good indication that phone numbers are the same. We now have the full last digits of the number, and the first digit of the area code. This information in itself can be used to verify an identity on other sites. How many times have you been asked to enter the last four digits of your phone number?

The next option is an email verification check. This is similar to verification on other sites—you will be shown a portion of the email address and asked to receive an email (shown in Figure 17.21).

Looking at the details in Figure 17.21 we can safely assume the recovery domain is mac.com, and know that the last two letters of the email address are “ia.” Since there are eight asterisks (for a total of nine letters) it would not take much to guess that my recovery email is my name, vinnyia@mac.com.

The last option in Figure 17.22 is to verify the credit card number on file. Most sites do not offer this for a verification option, but in case it comes up, we now know that I have a Visa that ends in 10.
