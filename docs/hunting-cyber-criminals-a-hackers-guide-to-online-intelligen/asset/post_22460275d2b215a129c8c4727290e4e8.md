## Cross-Checking All Information

I can't tell you how many times I have forgotten to do. This takes discipline and a tremendous amount of organization. If you are investigating a threat actor, getting into this mode of documenting and storing all in an organized system will quickly pay off. Remember, most (if not all) threat actors have multiple aliases. As you start to get into the weeds with a particular alias, you will most likely forget clues you found elsewhere. A good system of documentation will solve this dilemma.

When we searched Johnny Framerson's name, we found 37 other domains that he owns or owned at one point. Threat actors rarely use their own name, but there is a good chance that they will reuse other pieces of information like an address, phone number, or email address. To illustrate my point, Figure 9.29 shows the results of a reverse WHOIS search for Johnny Framerson's email address, johnnyboy@gmail.com.

| Domain Name | Create Date | Registrar |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ... | 2014-07-10 | DOMAIN.COM, LLC |
| whitepacket.com | 2015-10-18 | NAME.COM, INC |

Figure 9.29

Because our search results dropped from 37 to 2, we can conclude that our target uses different email addresses for domain registration. As part of a normal investigation, we would need to look through each of the other domains to find email addresses we use. Once we have those, we document them and perform more searches to find additional domains that can potentially be registered under those emails that we missed. As you can tell, the entire process can quickly turn into one giant rabbit hole.

> **NOTE** This is when using a mind mapping tool will really come handy. If you don't have a method for noting and expanding each of these different trails, I highly suggest looking into a mind map. Plenty are available. Mindnode is a great tool for Mac users. If you prefer an app, Coggle or Lucidchart are both good. You have so many to choose from. Just pick something because writing everything in OneNote is not going to cut. I can guarantee you will end up losing tracking or overlooking important clues many times. Save yourself the pain of having to go back and research data points and get into the history of properly documenting things from the start. Take screenshots of everything (another lesson I had to learn the hard way).
