What this means is any string you search for will appear in any part of the domain name.

For example, searching for “vinny” may show any combination of domains with the word Vinny in it:

+   - Vinny.com

This is significant for a few reasons. First, as I mentioned earlier, never estimate the value (or lack of awareness) of a threat actor, especially in the early days when OPSEC was just another fancy word to learn.

Because of the site’s wide searching abilities, Nameddroppers also has the added benefit of quickly finding domains related to your organization—this may include competitors (or most likely) phishing domains.

Nameddroppers will show you domains that are registered and provide you with the ability to view WHOIS information on any of those search results.

This is ultimately a paid service, but the first 50 results are free. That might not sound like a lot, but it is enough to give you an idea of what else might be out. If you need more than 50, for example, let’s say, a customer project, the report isn’t very expensive. Let's look at some examples.
