# Moral Gray Areas

During the course of an investigation, you will inevitably come to a number of morally gray areas. Whether it's looking for public data leaks, investigating threat actors, or infiltrating specific threat groups, there is always a line that you may have to cross in order to achieve your goals.

Threat actors and criminals will never respect moral or legal boundaries, so if they see that you are trying to uphold some moral standard... Something to keep in mind—crime is their livelihood. Cyber criminals are there to make money, so someone legitimately in the sense of those reasons will inevitably make the conversation towards something illegal. This is typically the first and most obvious red flag, and why so many investigators are discovered immediately. These scenarios come up quite a bit, especially during social engineering, which by definition means you are lying. I discuss several of my own scenarios in Chapter 15, when we dive more into social engineering. For now, here is a very interesting viewpoint by John Strand.

> EXPERT TIP: JOHN STRAND

When a target, there is nothing more powerful than hate. Where, if you start trolling, like they're a very religious person and you come to them and say, "Well, I'm an atheist and everything you believe in is wrong. Jesus was a manufactured idea by the Roman Empire to control the Jews," people get worked up.

You get 'em worked up and you give them links and you can get like, "Here's a link at this website, refutes everything you believe in." That person is going to click that link.

The downside is on that, most of our customers would never, ever, let us go far, far because that's nasty territory.

It is definitely crossing a line and I think if you can talk about it in a book, we do this professionally. We do very evil things with a contract and when you start talking about social engineering, when you start talking about recon, specifically on individuals, you are really starting to get close to a line, where it blurs out of the engagement and starts blurring into something personal.

Being cognizant of that line exists is something many people in the industry don't have, until they cross it. They don't understand what they did wrong, and I think that's an important concept. So, you know, that's number one.

Number two, I am a big fan of recon against evil people. How can you recon against an adversary? Cyber attribution. There's some tools that I love to use if we're trying to get some level of cyber attribution. Honey badger, a meta tool written by Tim Tom while he was working at the HIS, is an amazing tool if you can enter an adversary to an applet, to write a Word document or an Excel spreadsheet with a macro, we can locate that person within 20 meters.

If you go to Canary Tokens, they have amazing things that you can create in Word documents, PDFs, AWS keys. Set up everything if an adversary clones over your website, it'll beacon back and this is also a form of recon. It's a whole form of recon against adversaries and cyber attribution with being how many people in the industry don't think about it. A lot of the tools and techniques that we have as red teamers, they can be very effective in the blue team recon, to get a high degree of confidence in cyber attribution against an attacker, if you know how to leverage them properly.
