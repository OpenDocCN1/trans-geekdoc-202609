# Prologue xxix

# Education

One day I was working for the Department of Defense, I heard from a senior leader that he was going to bring someone onto his team that recently completed his “ethical hacking” certification.

Certification? I bet I could do that, seeing as how I already had the skills to hack into things and had done it all my life. It sounded like a great career path doing something that I really enjoyed, so I started looking into it.

By this point, I had already earned a bachelor's degree. I started working a tech-support job while I was in high school, then only took a semester of college before dropping out. It was not much later that I decided to go back and finish my online bachelor's degree.

After some research, I found a master's program at Western Governor’s University (WGU) that specialized in information security and identified the Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH) and Certified Hacking Forensic Investigator (CHFI) certifications as part of the coursework. I decided to get my master's.

After a few years, I finished my master's and all of the certifications I wanted. Thinking back, I guess I felt a lot like Forrest Gump when I was running across the country: I already made this far, so I might well keep going, right? So I decided to skip the customary CISSP certification and went for my PhD.

I spent about four years taking online classes and wrote my dissertation on the “perceived effectiveness of the cybersecurity framework among CISOs of varying industries.” I received my PhD in 2018.

# Starting Night Lion Security

Working with a number of large organizations, including director of security services for RSM (a top-five accounting firm), I felt that I had a unique perspective on how other organizations performed penetration testing and risk assessments, and I knew I could provide something better.

Being a startup security consulting firm is difficult enough on its own. Being a security startup and competing against giants like Optiv, KPMG, SecureWorks, and AT&T has been brutally difficult.

I feel that I was able to stand out in such an oversaturated market by being heavily active in the news and media. I feel that being on TV is one of the core reasons why companies were willing to take their chance with a small startup that no had ever heard of. I don't think I would have been able to make it this far without that.
