
Apple made its first patent application for a head-mounted display in January 2007—the very month the iPhone was publicly unveiled, nearly 5 years before Apple co-founder and CEO Steve Jobs’s death, and 18 years before the debut of the Vision Pro.^([59](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr59)) A May 2008 patent application depicted a user connecting to a pre-iPhone iPod, as well as an Entertainment System Connector Box that “may include power/video/audio connects as well as power the circuit board and optical modules of the goggle system.”^([60](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr60)) Use cases for the HMD included mobile communications, computing, gaming, and television, which could be provided through traditional 2D or as “3-D media.”^([61](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr61)) The patent also details mechanisms to correct for a user’s visual impairments, and the use of a retina or fingerprint scan, as well as voiceprint analysis.[62](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr62) ¶ The team that produced the Vision Pro was established in late 2015, with Apple CEO Tim Cook trying a prototype of the device around that same time—one he called a “monster” of an “apparatus.”[63](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr63) “It wasn’t wearable by any means of the imagination,” Cook told Vanity Fair in a piece published days before the Vision Pro released to the public.[64](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr64) Instead, Vanity Fair’s Nick Bilton reported, the device was “crude, like a giant box, and it’s got screens in it, half a dozen of them layered on top of each other, and cameras sticking out like whiskers.… And it’s whirring, with big fans—a steady, deep humming sound—on both sides of his face. And this apparatus has these wires coming out of it that sinuate all over the floor and stretch into another room, where they’re connected to a supercomputer.”[65](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr65) “I’ve known for years we would get here,” Cook added. “I didn’t know when, but I knew that we would arrive here.”[66](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr66) US Patent and Trademark Office, Apple

## The Future of HMDs

In 2022, Tim Sweeney, who in 2016 imagined that Oakley-sized supercomputers would be possible by 2023, told The Verge, “Well, I think that augmented reality is the platform of the future. But it’s very clear from the efforts of Magic Leap and others that we need not just new technology but, to some extent, new science in order to build an augmented reality platform that’s a substitute for smartphones. And it’s not clear to me whether that’s coming in 10 years or in 30 years. I hope we’ll see it in my lifetime, but I’m actually not sure about that.”^([67](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr67)) This uncertainty is reflected in the go-to-market strategies and products of big tech.

Amazon, Apple, Facebook, Google, and Microsoft have all entered the market at different times (2014 for Google Glass, 2024 for Vision Pro), with different starting products (optical AR for Google, VR for Facebook, audio-only glasses for Amazon, optical MR for Microsoft, passthrough MR for Apple), different prices (from $250 for the Echo Frames to $3,500 for the Vision Pro), and prioritization of different customers (Facebook targets everyone; Microsoft, enterprise users; Apple, high-end households and designers).

There are also fundamental differences in what each company believes to be the “minimum viable product” in a given category. With the Vision Pro, Apple opted to add an externally facing multicolor display that would show a reproduction of the user’s face and eyes (“EyeSight”). This decision substantially increases the device’s costs, battery usage, heat generation, and computational needs, while also forcing front-facing cameras to the side rather than center front of the device, which makes accurate passthrough video harder to produce and even more demanding on computing resources.^([¶](#chapter9.xhtml#footnote-015)) Apple insists that the feature is needed to avoid social alienation and for the device to be embraced in semipublic environments such as the office. Facebook obviously does not share this view. Apple also believes that an externally tethered battery pack is an acceptable trade-off for more power, despite the aesthetic implications and potential challenges and even dangers that come with wires, whereas Facebook again disagrees (Apple’s longtime chief design officer, Sir Jony Ive, who led or co-led the design of the Apple Newton, the iPod, the iPhone, the iPad, and the Apple Watch, apparently agrees with Facebook on this issue).^([#](#chapter9.xhtml#footnote-014)) With its first HMD, Apple also decided to add a small external wheel (not unlike the “crown” on an Apple Watch) that enables users to instantly shift to (or phase in to) passthrough mode even when they are inside a purely VR-based experience. Such a component has never appeared on a Facebook device, which instead activates passthrough mode based on hand gestures or button presses, but Apple believes it essential to users’ feeling comfortable in their headset.

The Vision Pro boasts 12 cameras that varyingly support face, eye, and hand tracking, while the Quest Pro has 5 total cameras for face, eye, and hand tracking, and the Quest 3 has only 4 cameras, all dedicated to hand tracking. The Vision Pro’s larger and higher-quality fleet of cameras reflects Apple’s belief that HMDs should not require handheld controllers for non-specialized use cases (such as graphic design, data entry, etc.). In fact, Apple does not even produce such controllers, whereas Facebook’s HMDs ship with and require the use of Quest controllers. But as a result, the Vision Pro must be capable of precise and reliable eye- and hand-based inputs. The Vision Pro’s external cameras can also be used to scan a user’s face to help generate a photorealistic 3D avatar (or “persona,” in Apple’s terms). In contrast, Facebook’s avatars are created through an avatar maker and offer a more cartoonish aesthetic, rather than aspiring to realism.

Apple has even departed from its customary approach to product rollout, too, as the release of the Vision Pro will be the first time Apple has ever debuted a “Pro” model before offering a standard edition. The first Plus or Pro model iPhone launched seven years after the first standard-issue iPhone and a year after Apple introduced a low-end model. The iPad Pro came five years after the first iPad and three after the low-cost iPad Mini, while the Apple Watch Ultra was seven years after the first and two after the Apple Watch SE. The AirPod Pros came two years after the AirPods, with the AirPods Max debuting after three. Apple’s inverted approach to the Vision HMD doubtless reflects its belief that, in 2024, releasing a more affordable model would require so many concessions that the device would not be worth selling. Facebook, which continues to sell its 2020 Quest 2 for $300, clearly disagrees.
