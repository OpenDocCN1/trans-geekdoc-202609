
In 2017, Epic Games launched `Fortnite`, which quickly established itself as a platform for mass culture, rather than just a virtual pastime. In 2018 and 2019 (the last years with publicly available data), `Fortnite` collected more than $9 billion in net revenue—mostly through the sale of virtual apparel that had no performance-related or competitive value. These sales exceeded those of storied fashion houses and brands such as Dolce & Gabbana, Saint Laurent, J.Crew, Prada, and Lacoste. Unlike these physical labels, however, `Fortnite` had only design costs, rather than manufacturing and shipping/fulfillment expenses, making Epic one of the most profitable apparel makers globally. By the end of 2023, brands such as Nike, Balenciaga, LVMH, Moncler, and Ralph Lauren had released exclusive virtual apparel inside `Fortnite`, as well as complementary physical editions with prices that ran into the thousands. Before passing away from cancer at the age of 41, Virgil Abloh, the founder and CEO of Off-White and creative director of Louis Vuitton menswear, announced the creation of a virtual-only fashion label, saying, “I want to make virtual clothes to paint pictures physical clothes cannot, and let buyers access a new dimension of their personal style—no matter who they are, where they live, and the virtual worlds they love.”^([20](#notes_split_003.xhtml#ch3fr20))

In 2018, Epic launched `Fortnite Creative`, its own riff on `Minecraft`’s and `Roblox`’s world-building modes, with brands such as Nike and Coachella, as well as the Estate of Martin Luther King Jr., establishing a presence. The following year, `Fortnite` hosted a live, interactive, and canonical prologue to the Star Wars film `The Rise of Skywalker`, which also included a live interview with director J. J. Abrams. The year after that, hip-hop star (and Kardashian family member) Travis Scott hosted a concert that was attended live by over 12 million players, with millions more watching live on social media. As a point of contrast, fewer than 9 million Americans attended `The Avengers: Endgame`, which holds the record for highest single-day ticket sales in American history, during its first 24 hours of release. The track Scott premiered during the concert, which featured Kid Cudi, debuted at #1 on the `Billboard` Hot 100 charts a week later, was Cudi’s first #1 track, and finished 2020 as the third-largest US debut of the year. In addition, several of the tracks Scott performed from his two-year-old `Astroworld` album returned to the `Billboard` charts after the concert, with the official event video accumulating more than 200 million views. `Fortnite` would go on to host live shows with artists such as Steve Aoki, Ariana Grande, and J Balvin; exclusively premiere trailers to films such as Christopher Nolan’s `Tenet`; host film festivals; and tie in with the 75th anniversary of the NBA.

In the history of the Metaverse from MUDs to `Fortnite`, a few points are clear. Efforts to build a parallel virtual existence span decades rather than just a few buzzy years. As graphical capability, experiential diversity, and the number of internet users grew, so did the popularity of the top virtual worlds. Moreover, growth seems likely to continue in the years to come. But to understand why so many companies declared for the Metaverse in 2020–22, it’s necessary to examine the technical side of the history detailed above. Recall Sweeney’s 2021 observation: not only has a “critical mass of working pieces started coming together,” it is “coming together rapidly.” The best place to start is with live graphical simulation technologies.

## Real-Time Graphical Simulation

The first attempts to build a “computer game” or “video game” emerged in the 1940s. The 1947 “cathode-ray tube amusement device” is considered the first interactive electronic game, but it did not use a computer or any form of memory device or program. Instead, the player adjusted various knobs to change the trajectory of a CRT beam so that it would align with plastic targets overlaid on the device’s screen.^([21](#notes_split_003.xhtml#ch3fr21)) In 1948, the celebrated mathematician, computer scientist, and cryptanalyst Alan Turing began development of Turochamp, which was intended to play a full game of chess with a human player, but the code proved too complex for even military computers of the era.

In 1950, the Canadian inventor Josef Kates unveiled Bertie the Brain, a nearly four-meter-tall computer that, for all its immensity, could play just a single game that spanned only two different inputs and nine total decisions: tic-tac-toe. Bertie was incapable of producing computer graphics; the moves of both the player and Bertie’s AI were represented by the turning on and off of various lightbulbs.^([‡](#chapter3.xhtml#footnote-045)) `OXO`, a 1952 tic-tac-toe title which was developed at the University of Cambridge as part of a research study, added on-screen computer graphics and ran on a general-purpose computer rather than purpose-built one (though this meant the computer was the size of the room, rather than an everyday storage cabinet).
