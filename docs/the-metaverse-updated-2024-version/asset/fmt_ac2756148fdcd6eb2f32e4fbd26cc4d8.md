
# Smart Contracts and NFTs

The simplest implementation of smart contracts are “non-fungible tokens” (NFTs). In this case, a smart contract is written that publishes (or “mints”) data to a blockchain and assigns ownership of this data to an individual address (“wallet”) on the blockchain. This data might be a record that the owner of that wallet had bought a ticket to the county fair—or it could be the deed to a home in that county. Once this NFT is minted, the receiving address has full control over this record and can freely reassign (i.e., sell or transfer) it to any other address on the blockchain. While the underlying code to this NFT “smart contract” can easily be copied, as can the data it relates to (e.g., a ticket receipt or a house deed), the actual NFT and its record can be neither copied nor substituted—hence “non-fungible”—as these are unique and immutable identifiers written to the blockchain. As such, proponents of NFTs believe that they can serve as a worldwide system that irrefutably and automatically validates ownership of both virtual and physical goods and that can be accessed by any application or user in the world.

# Smart Contract Interactions

Smart contracts can also interact with other smart contracts. A smart contract might be written to recognize ownership of an NFT in order to permit some activity, such as signing into a members-only Discord channel or entering a local fair. Many NFTs include code that automatically activates a second smart contract whenever the NFT is sold. The latter smart contract is typically used to send the NFT’s original creator (or some other predesignated address) a portion of the proceeds (a “royalty”), often between 5% and 10%, which enables artists to directly participate in the appreciation of their works—a luxury not typically afforded to painters, photographers, designers, and so on, and which was even harder to collect, audit, and enforce.

# NFT Market Overview

During 2021 and 2022, roughly $65 billion was spent on NFTs globally.^([3](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr3)) These purchases were diverse. NBA Top Shots from Dapper Labs turned individual moments from various NBA seasons into collectible, trading-card-like NFTs that ran on Dapper’s own blockchain, Flow. Artists such as Damien Hirst also released paintings and photographs as NFTs (which sometimes included rights to a physical work).^([4](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr4)) Larva Labs, a crypto-focused design studio, released an NFT collection of “Cryptopunks,” which spanned 10,000 algorithmically generated 24-by-24-pixel 2D avatars that many owners used as profile pictures across the internet. Bored Ape, another profile-picture NFT series, provided owners with select IP rights to the underlying artworks and the ability to use their “Ape” to gain access to the affiliate social club, the Bored Ape Yacht Club, in addition to select rights to the artwork. In April 2022, a record $78 million was being paid to NFT creators each week as royalties.

# Decentralized Applications (dapps)

Collections of smart contracts have been used to produce decentralized apps (“dapps”) that can execute complex tasks akin to traditional applications. Examples help bring this concept to life and demonstrate some of the prospective benefits of blockchain-based applications.

## Uniswap

Uniswap enables users to exchange hundreds of different tokens with other users, all without the need for contracts, brokers, clearinghouses, delays, or fears of nondelivery, and at a modest fee of 0.3%—far lower than the 1%–3% spread on currency exchanges that traditional banks typically demand. In the five years following its November 2018 debut, Uniswap has managed more than $1.5 trillion in trades, processing as much as $90 billion in its biggest month (November 2021) and $14 billion on its biggest day (March 10, 2022).^([5](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr5)) Despite its enormous operating scale, Uniswap has only a few dozen employees.^([6](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr6))

## OpenSea

OpenSea is a marketplace focused on the exchange not of cryptocurrencies or tokens but of NFTs, taking an average of 2.5% on sales. The platform allows users to display their own collection of NFTs, as well as browse those of others, and to search, bid on, and purchase individual NFTs or to make their own NFTs available for sale. OpenSea’s staff, which peaked at roughly 300 in 2022, also provides editorial and curatorial functions (such as featured collections), verification services (confirming the legitimacy of NFTs made available for sale), and blocking the sale of stolen NFTs. In the six years since OpenSea’s December 2017 launch, it has processed over $35 billion in NFT sales, with a peak of roughly $5 billion traded in January 2022.^([7](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr7))

## Mirror

Mirror has used smart contracts and token systems to reimagine the independent journalism and blogging business model. On the platform, authors can finance their research, investigations, and writings by preselling NFT copies of the finished works or issuing tokens that would be needed to access the finished work or would entitle owners to a portion of revenues generated by this work. In other cases, a collective of authors has issued tokens to fundraise for a new magazine that is then made exclusively available to token holders. Some writers use smart contracts to automatically share tips with those who helped or inspired them.

## Axie Infinity and Zed Run

*Axie Infinity* and *Zed Run* have used blockchain to build a “Web3” version of the video game *Pokémon* and virtual racing, respectively. In both cases, these games issue NFT-based creatures that can be collected, bred, traded, trained, battled, rented, or gambled upon, with smart contracts managing profit sharing and genetic inheritances while also operating the in-game odds and combat. As NFTs are minted to the blockchain, not recorded on the private database of their makers, outside developers are able to access and use these creatures, too—and not necessarily to build games. In 2020, the collective known as Yield Guild Games (YGG) was founded primarily to buy “Axies” that would then be lent out to *Axie Infinity* players in exchange for a portion of their winnings and/or rental fees. YGG, in other words, was making money enabling Sky Mavis’s own players to play Sky Mavis’s own game.

## Otoy and RNDR

In a few cases, dapps and smart contracts have been used to establish and operate large-scale and decentralized infrastructure networks, with these dapps and smart contracts often issuing their own cryptocurrencies on top of the blockchain upon which they operate. One leading example is Otoy, a cloud-based rendering company that in 2020 publicly launched the Ethereum-based RNDR network, protocol, and token. Specifically, RNDR establishes a token-based marketplace where idle computers can make their GPUs available to those in need of additional computing power. All of the negotiation and contracting between parties is handled by RNDR’s protocol in a matter of seconds; neither side has any knowledge of the identity or specifics of the task being performed; and all transactions occur using RNDR cryptocurrency tokens. According to the company, RNDR can perform tasks at a fraction of the cost offered by centralized databases, while also increasing the global supply of available computing resources by several orders of magnitude.^([8](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr8))
