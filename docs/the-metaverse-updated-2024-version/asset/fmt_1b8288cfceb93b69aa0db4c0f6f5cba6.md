
And for all his reluctance on using the word “Metaverse,” Cook has been unafraid to declare that humanity’s future is one surrounded by augmented, virtual, and mixed reality. Only a few days before he discussed his reservations about “Metaverse,” Cook gave an off-the-record to an Italian university in which he said, “I’m super excited about augmented reality. Because I think that we’ve had a great conversation here today, but if we could augment that with something from the virtual world, it would have arguably been even better. So I think that if you, and this will happen clearly not too long from now, if you look back at a point in time, you know, zoom out to the future and look back, you’ll wonder how you led your life without augmented reality. Just like today, we wonder, how did people like me grow up without the internet.”^([40](#notes_split_000.xhtml#introfr40)) Though these remarks were leaked, they were not a new position for Cook, nor a newly public one, either. As early as 2016—long before the Metaverse hype cycle—he promised that eventually, a “significant portion of the population … will have AR experiences every day, almost like eating three meals a day. It will become that much a part of you.… VR I think is not going to be that big, compared to AR. I’m not saying it’s not important, it is important.”^([41](#notes_split_000.xhtml#introfr41))

When Apple finally unveiled its Vision Pro headset in June 2023, the company chose to brand the device a “spatial computer,” while declaring “the era of spatial computing” had begun.^([42](#notes_split_000.xhtml#introfr42)) The term “spatial computing” was coined around the same time as Neal Stephenson developed “Metaverse” in the late 1980s and is typically attributed to Bob Jacobson, who co-founded the Human Interface Technology (HIT) lab at the University of Washington in 1989, and/or Tom Furness, another HIT co-founder and pioneering VR researcher. By 1992, Jacobson was using “spatial computing” as part of his start-up, Worldesign.^([43](#notes_split_000.xhtml#introfr43)) In January 2024, two weeks before the Vision Pro was made available for sale, Apple publicly released its guidelines for Vision Pro developers, stating that developers should “refer to your app as a spatial computing app. Don’t describe your app experience as augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), extended reality (XR), or mixed reality (MR).”^([44](#notes_split_000.xhtml#introfr44))

Roblox’s Shifting Language

At the time of its October 2020 IPO filing, the term “Metaverse” had appeared in only 17 filings with the US Securities and Exchange Commission since January 1, 2001.^([45](#notes_split_000.xhtml#introfr45)) In its filing, Roblox wrote “Metaverse” 16 times, writing that it operates in a category sometimes known as the “metaverse,” and which Roblox believed was “materializing,” with its “business plan \[assuming\] … the adoption of a metaverse.”^([46](#notes_split_000.xhtml#introfr46)) During the company’s pre-IPO roadshow, Roblox founder and CEO David Baszucki wrote an opinion piece as part of Wired magazine’s 2021 predictions column titled “The Metaverse Is Coming,” in which he predicted “billions would come to \[Metaverse\] platforms such as Roblox” and that the “Metaverse is arguably as big a shift in online communication as the telephone or the internet.”^([47](#notes_split_000.xhtml#introfr47)) Then, on the day of the company’s March 2021 IPO, Baszucki tweeted, “To all those who helped get us one step closer to fulfilling our vision of the #Metaverse-thank you. #RobloxIPO.”^([48](#notes_split_000.xhtml#introfr48)) In early October 2021, Baszucki continued to talk about the popularity of Roblox’s “Metaverse.”^([49](#notes_split_000.xhtml#introfr49)) However, the company largely stopped using the term following Facebook’s rebrand to Meta in late October 2021. Two years later, Baszucki was asked about reports that the company was distancing itself from “metaverse connotations.” Baszucki demurred, arguing the company had always used other terms such as “human co-experience” and had “evolved the terminology we use,” and claimed, “We’ve never really used the term metaverse a lot.”^([50](#notes_split_000.xhtml#introfr50)) Twitter

The future of the Metaverse may be a rebrand—to spatial computing, as Apple imagines it, the spatial internet, the 3D internet, to hyper-digital reality, as Tencent imagines it, or to a “human co-experience” platform, per Roblox. Alternatively, the concepts and ideas powering the Metaverse will just be absorbed into a term that’s already in use: the internet. But however uncertain the Metaverse’s terminology may be, the trends are clear. The number of virtual worlds made each year, the number of people inside them, as well as the money and time they spend there and the cultural influence of those spaces, all continue to grow. More importantly, the deployment of real-time 3D-simulation and game-engine technology continues to increase, as do their capabilities and business applications. These tools are increasingly used in healthcare, industrial design, civic management, automotive, aerospace, and more.

The goal of this revised edition of *The Metaverse* is to clarify what the Metaverse is, explain what’s needed to build it, why it matters—and how the way it is built, and those who build it, will affect our lives for decades to come.
