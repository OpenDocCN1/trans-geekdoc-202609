
As points of contrast, consider Rockstar’s `Grand Theft Auto V` (`GTA:V`) and `Red Dead Redemption 2` (`RDR2`). `GTA:V` is the second-best-selling game in history, with over 200 million copies sold (`Minecraft` ranks first with nearly 300 million). `RDR2` was the best-selling title made for the eighth generation of consoles (i.e., PlayStation 4, Xbox One, Nintendo Switch), with 65 million copies sold. The two games are also believed to be among the most expensive game productions ever, with final budgets estimated at $250–$300 million and $400–$500 million, respectively, which includes more than half a decade of development each, plus extensive marketing and publishing costs. Or compare Roblox’s R&D budget to that of Sony’s PlayStation group, which was estimated at $2.17 billion in 2023 and spanned close to a dozen gaming studios, its cloud gaming division, live services group, and hardware division (including VR).^([17](#notes_split_007.xhtml#ch7fr17)) In 2019, the last year for which data is publicly available, Epic Games’ Unreal Engine generated less than $100 million in revenue (it’s likely this figure is considerably higher in 2024).^([18](#notes_split_007.xhtml#ch7fr18)) Unity’s engine brought in much more but still came short of Roblox’s R&D spend.^([19](#notes_split_007.xhtml#ch7fr19))

Roblox’s R&D investments are diverse, spanning improvements in developer tools and software, server architecture to synchronize high-concurrency simulations, machine learning to detect harassment, artificial intelligence, rendering for virtual reality, motion capture, and more. That Roblox can invest so much into its platform is astonishing. In theory, every additional dollar enables developers to produce more compelling virtual worlds, which attracts more users, leading to more revenue—which enables more R&D not just by Roblox but also by the independent developers who make these worlds, investment that again drives more user engagement and spending on `Roblox`, leading to more R&D by the company.

## Many Virtual Platforms and Engines, Not Many Metaverses

[Chapter 4](#chapter4.xhtml) provided a “full-spec” description of the Metaverse: “A massively scaled and interoperable network of real-time-rendered immersive virtual worlds that can be experienced synchronously and persistently by an effectively unlimited number of users with an individual sense of presence, and with continuity of data, such as identity, history, entitlements, objects, communications, and payments.” Some might read this description and consider `Roblox` to be an imperfect, but still fairly close, match. It cannot be experienced synchronously and persistently by an effectively unlimited number of users; no real-time-rendered virtual world can, at least for now. And when that does become possible, it will surely be true for `Roblox`. And yet `Roblox` is unlikely to meet my definition in one key way: most virtual works will exist outside of it.

But could `Roblox` eventually become the Metaverse? What if Epic’s IVWP `Fortnite Creative`, game engine Unreal, and live services suite Epic Online Services, along with its other special projects, were all combined—would the result be the Metaverse? If one squints, it’s possible to imagine these companies, or one like it, subsuming all virtual experiences, thereby becoming a Metaverse-sized Metagalaxy. And it is notable that some form of this process is how the Metaverses of `Snow Crash` and `Ready Player One` come into existence.

The current state of technological progress suggests another outcome. Why? Because as fast as these virtual giants are growing, the number of virtual experiences, innovators, technologies, opportunities, and developers are all growing faster.

While `Roblox` and `Minecraft` are among the most popular games in the world, their reach is modest when considered in the broadest terms. These two supposed titans have but a small fraction of the global internet population of 4.5–5 billion. In effect, they are still at the ICQ stage of virtual worlds; billions of users and millions of developers have yet to even try them. It’s easy to assume that `Roblox` or `Minecraft` will be the sole or at least primary beneficiaries of this growth. History provides reason for optimism.

When Microsoft acquired `Minecraft` in 2014, the title had over 25 million monthly active players. By mid-2020, this playerbase had more than quintupled—even though `Minecraft`’s primary competitor, `Roblox`, had seen its usership grow from fewer than 5 million to more than 200 million over that same time, and many other IVWPs had launched to success, too. In 2018, `Fortnite` launched `FNC`, and throughout the decade, Rockstar had progressively transformed its 2013 title `Grand Theft Auto V` into the makeshift IVWP in `Grand Theft Auto Online`.

As long as there are billions, or even tens of millions, of players left to adopt IVWPs, more will come to market and traditional games will launch their own creative modes, just as `Fortnite` did in 2018, with `Free Fire` following suit in 2021. It’s likely that `GTA 6`, which is expected to release in 2024 or 2025, will incorporate many of the lessons learned from the success of `Roblox`, `Minecraft`, and `FNC`. Rockstar’s August 2023 acquisition of a leading `GTA` and `RDR2` “modding platform,” Cfx.re, which converted Rockstar’s combat-based games into “role-playing” simulations enabling complex role-playing, is a case in point.^([20](#notes_split_007.xhtml#ch7fr20)) It was not long ago that Rockstar had been calling the role-play companies—including Cfx.re—“\[violators\] of our terms of service,” whose products were “designed to facilitate piracy.” According to some reports, Rockstar even sent private investigators to physically shut down servers operated by these supposed malfeasants.^([21](#notes_split_007.xhtml#ch7fr21)) But as markets change and customer behavior evolves, even the most successful of platforms need to evolve, too.

Many new IVWPs are being developed around different technical premises, too. At the end of 2022, even the largest of the blockchain-based IVWPs, including `Decentraland`, `The Sandbox`, `Cryptovoxels`, `Somnium Space`, and `Upland`, had less than 0.05% of `Roblox`’s and `Minecraft`’s daily active users. But by allowing users greater ownership over their in-world items and even giving them a say in how the platform is governed and a right to share in its profitability, these blockchain IVWPs will be able to grow far more quickly than traditional IVWPs (more on this theory in [Chapter 11](#chapter11.xhtml)). In early 2024, Krafton, which makes the blockbuster title `PUBG`, and Naver, another South Korean digital media giant, are expected to launch an NFT-based IVWP titled `Overdare`, which will run on its own blockchain, Settlus.^([22](#notes_split_007.xhtml#ch7fr22))

Facebook’s `Horizon Worlds` is not limited to immersive VR and AR, but it is focused in those areas, which contrasts with `Roblox`, which is available in immersive VR but prioritizes traditional screen interfaces, such as an iPad or PC screen. Upstarts such as `Rec Room` and `VRChat` are also centered on immersive VR world creation, are rapidly accumulating users and boast orders of magnitude more users than `Horizon Worlds`—even on Facebook’s own Quest line of devices. Niantic, the maker of `Pokémon Go`, is working on its own augmented reality and location-based virtual world platforms.
