
# The formation of the Metaverse will help regulators force the major operating systems to unbundle their stacks

Despite this, the companies that own these OSs and ecosystems will still thrive because their unbundled offerings are still market leading and the emergence of the Metaverse will increase the size and importance of most of these markets. Overall, the structure of the Metaverse is likely to resemble that of the digital world today—a handful of horizontally and vertically integrated companies will control a substantial share of the digital economy, with their influence even greater. This outcome is likely regardless of whether the Metaverse is built on centralized or decentralized technologies. The underpinnings of the World Wide Web are nearly all decentralized—and it grew to popularity at a time in which the devices used to access the web were “open,” such as Windows, Mac, and Linux PCs. Even so, consumer usage of the internet rapidly consolidated around the ecosystems of Microsoft, Google, and Facebook throughout the 1990s and 2000s. There are many nontechnological feedback loops that concentrate power, such as branding and IP, user habits, data-center investments, commercial partnerships and sister products, profit margins, and so on. The key concern for the Metaverse is not whether centralization or decentralization “wins”—neither can—but where along the spectrum it sits, and how that placement changes over time. And here, I’m hopeful.

# Whatever one believes about crypto, there is a clear want for better user rights and openness

Many early leaders in the Metaverse are responding to this desire. When Mark Zuckerberg announced his Metaverse strategy in October 2021, he also announced the end of a year-old policy that required Quest users to have a Facebook account^([7](#notes_split_012.xhtml#confr7)) and committed to supporting WebXR, an open-source API collection for browser-based AR and VR apps, and OpenXR, an open-source API collection for installed AR and VR apps, rather than forcing its own proprietary alternatives.^([8](#notes_split_012.xhtml#confr8)) Zuckerberg also said Quest devices would continue to allow users to directly install apps, including third-party app stores, and that developers would be able to choose their own in-app payment solutions.^([9](#notes_split_012.xhtml#confr9)) A few weeks later, Facebook began to open up many of the APIs that it had closed a decade earlier, including those that enabled users to cross-post Instagram photos on Twitter and other social networks.^([10](#notes_split_012.xhtml#confr10)) A month after announcing its Activision Blizzard acquisition, Microsoft unveiled a new, 14-point policy platform for its Windows operating system, and the “next-generation marketplaces \[the company\] builds for games.” This included a pledge to support third-party payment solutions and app stores (and not disadvantage developers who choose to use them), the right for users to set these alternatives as default options, and the right for developers to directly communicate with the end user (even if the point of that communication is to tell the user they can get better pricing or service by cutting out Microsoft’s store or services suite), and the statement that Microsoft was “committed” to bringing these same principles to its Xbox console (though no details or timing was provided). Epic Games’ ecosystem and ambitions are still developing, but its controls remain few and its promises largely met despite the extraordinary costs.

It’s fair to argue that Facebook, Microsoft, Epic, and many proponents of the blockchain ecosystem are fans of openness because they are stymied by their closed competitors. Were any of these companies or their leaders in control of iOS or Android, or less affected by those same platforms, they might hold different views. The current support for openness may or may not be sustained, either. Prior to leaving Facebook, John Carmack admitted that Facebook had to increase the price of Quest 2 because the most popular games on the device were free-to-play titles that rarely monetized and were made by independent developers.^([11](#notes_split_012.xhtml#confr11)) When Microsoft vowed to open up Xbox, it offered no roadmap or timeline to doing so, admitted its business model could not presently support it, and offered no ruminations on how it might. With every service it gives away, Epic’s de facto control likely grows. During the 2021 trial between Epic and Apple, Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers seemed sympathetic to many of Epic’s arguments, but also declared, “Let’s be clear. Epic is here because, if relief is granted, it could turn the multibillion-dollar company into maybe a multitrillion-dollar company. They aren’t doing it out of the kindness of their heart.”^([12](#notes_split_012.xhtml#confr12))

# The Metaverse will intensify many of the societal challenges of the internet today

Not only will these problems intensify, the lessons learned over the past 20 years will not always apply. Moderating asynchronous texts is quite different from live and haptically integrated 3D gestures. When the Islamic State (also known as ISIS/ISIL) began to use social media in the mid-2000s, the terrorist group hoped to radicalize Muslims around the world and then recruit them to parts of the Middle East for training. In response, governments around the world restricted the ability of foreigners and even their own citizens to enter their countries after visiting various “hot spots.” Such an approach no longer applies when candidates are trained through immersive platforms running on decentralized networks. And Alexa listening for a keyword trigger is a lot less invasive than optical AR glasses that scan, digest, and simulate the real world around them.

# I worry comparatively less about virtual isolation

This is not an endorsement of spending more time inside and less out in the world but, instead, a recognition of how we currently spend our time. Including naps and bouts of sleeplessness, the average American over 18 sleeps for 8.7 hours per day and works for 5.6 hours, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics.^([13](#notes_split_012.xhtml#confr13)) Of the remaining 9.7 waking hours, more than half (or 5–5.5 hours total) is spent watching television, movies, and user-generated video online.^([14](#notes_split_012.xhtml#confr14)) Some of this time is spent multitasking—the viewer is cooking, on the treadmill, watching their kids, or surfing the internet—but at least two-thirds of viewing occurs without such distractions.^([15](#notes_split_012.xhtml#confr15)) Sixty percent of total viewing is also done on a solitary basis,^([16](#notes_split_012.xhtml#confr16)) and nearly 90% of viewing is done while sedentary.^([17](#notes_split_012.xhtml#confr17)) Shifting any of this time to social, interactive, and more engaged entertainment is likely a positive outcome, not a negative one, even if we’re all still indoors. This is particularly true for the elderly. The average senior in the United States spends 7.5 hours per day watching TV. Few among us dreamed of retiring and living long in order to spend half of each of our remaining days watching TV. The Metaverse may offer no substitute for physically sailing in the Caribbean, but captaining a virtual sailboat alongside old friends is likely to beat midday cable news and offer its own perks, too.
