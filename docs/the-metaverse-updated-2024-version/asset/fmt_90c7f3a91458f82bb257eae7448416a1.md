
# iPhone 与 Metaverse 的技术普及与经济影响

When it debuted in 2007, the iPhone retailed for $500 (or roughly $730 in 2024 dollars). In 2024, the cheapest model sold by Apple, the iPhone SE, is only $429—15% less in nominal terms and over 40% cheaper in real terms, even though it boasts several hundred times more computing power, a display that’s 67% larger and with 100% greater pixel density, front- and back-facing cameras that can produce images that have 3.5–6 times more pixels than the first iPhone’s sole rear camera and leverage AI and neural-network technologies, and so on.^([1](#notes_split_012.xhtml#confr1)) Though a comparison of computational specs shows that Samsung’s entry-level budget Galaxy devices are less powerful than the iPhone SE, as of Q4 2023, they were available for $160^([2](#notes_split_012.xhtml#confr2)) and each one could outperform a rack of Apple’s groundbreaking first smartphone.^([3](#notes_split_012.xhtml#confr3)) It is likely that it will take longer for HMDs to become affordable than it did for smartphones to do so, but this very slowness means that adoption of these devices and use of their revolutionary software will also take time.

## 企业家与技术普及的时间滞后

We typically underestimate how much time is required for entrepreneurs, companies, and even tech giants to make use of advances in networking, computing, AI, interoperability, hardware, and so on. All of the technologies required to build Facebook were widely available years before Mark Zuckerberg created the social network in 2004. In hindsight, it seems that the holdup was Zuckerberg and his peers. It took a child of the 1990s internet—characterized by text-based message boards, chat clients, and web-hosting platforms—to build Facebook, and he relied on his similarly minded peers to populate it. An analogous story played out with Evan Spiegel, a child of the 2000s mobile internet, who founded Snapchat in 2011 based around “ephemeral messages” that would dispose of sexts and spicy texts—a use case millennials and Gen Zs embraced en masse. In [Chapter 3](#chapter3.xhtml), I recounted the rise of the iPad native generation and the way it closely mapped (i.e., fueled) `Roblox`’s growth. The oldest of users is just now turning 20—in between the ages of Zuckerberg and Spiegel at the advent of Facebook and Snapchat—and so the new wave of entrepreneurs may soon be upon us, propelled not just by `Roblox` but by `VRChat` (which debuted in 2014), `Rec Room` (2016), `Pokémon Go` (2016), and the first consumer-release Oculus (2016).

## Metaverse 的怀疑论与历史先例

Even as the Metaverse’s underlying technologies become more popular and capable, skepticism will persist and often grow. In 1995, Clifford Stoll, an American astronomer and former systems administrator at the US Department of Energy’s Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, wrote the now-infamous book `Silicon Snake Oil: Second Thoughts on the Information Highway`. In an editorial for `Newsweek` around the book’s publication, he stated, “After two decades online, I’m perplexed … uneasy about this most trendy and oversold community. Visionaries see a future of telecommuting workers, interactive libraries and multimedia classrooms. They speak of electronic town meetings and virtual communities. Commerce and business will shift from offices and malls to networks and modems. And the freedom of digital networks will make government more democratic. Baloney. Do our computer pundits lack all common sense? … What the Internet hucksters won’t tell you is that the Internet is one big ocean of unedited data, without any pretense of completeness.”^([4](#notes_split_012.xhtml#confr4)) Three years after Stoll’s opinion piece, Paul Krugman, who would win the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences a decade later, wrote an (unintentionally) ironically titled article, “Why Most Economists’ Predictions Are Wrong,” in which he stated: “The growth of the Internet will slow drastically, as the flaw in ‘Metcalfe’s law’—which states that the number of potential connections in a network is proportional to the square of the number of participants—becomes apparent: most people have nothing to say to each other! By 2005 or so, it will become clear that the Internet’s impact on the economy has been no greater than the fax machine’s.”^([5](#notes_split_012.xhtml#confr5)) Notably, Krugman’s prediction preceded the launch of nearly all of the internet’s enduring social platforms, such as Skype (2003), Facebook (2004), `Roblox` (2006), Twitter (2006), Snap (2011), and TikTok (2014), and the companies that indexed the billions of pages made by internet users, including Google (founded three months later in 1998). In December 2000, the `Daily Mail` published a news piece headlined “Internet ‘May Just Be a Passing Fad as Millions Give Up on It,’ ” backed by research that estimated Britain was set to lose 2 million of its 15 million internet users.^([6](#notes_split_012.xhtml#confr6))

## Metaverse 的定义演变与未来展望

Though skeptics will shape the discourse, I’d guess that by the end of the current decade, many if not most of us will agree that the “Metaverse” has begun (though even in hindsight, there will be no precise date of its start, nor will we be able to predict when it will arrive in full). Furthermore, the term “the Metaverse” will probably have been discarded—perhaps for the “immersive internet” or “spatial internet” or “3D internet.” Or maybe we just keep saying “the internet.” After all, the younger generations will be first to adopt “the Metaverse,” and they have no interest in what social 3D spaces are called—nor any other labels for their behavior. I’d guess that for decades to come, we’ll continue to use many of the digital and mobile products we do today—real-time 3D rendering is not the best way to perform many tasks or experience all forms of content—even when the products are supporting Metaverse use cases.

## Metaverse 的财务影响

The financial impact of the Metaverse’s construction, development, and realization will be considerable. “Trillions” is a large, yet reliable, number. The internet and computer are among the most powerful platforms humanity has ever created—and both will evolve and be redesigned in support of the Metaverse. Estimating the actual value of the Metaverse, however, will never be possible—which makes current forecasts unfalsifiable and distracting. Although it’s common to refer to the “digital economy,” there is no agreed-upon, nor particularly important, answer to what, exactly, its size might be. The answer depends purely on allocation. Imagine, as an example, attributing a portion of the purchase price of this book to “digital” or “the internet.” What is the right share and how would you know? Presumably the allocation differs if it’s a physical book, audiobook, or e-book, but how much? How would the answer change if you found out about the book on social media versus browsing a bookstore? Or based on whether I wrote it on a local versus cloud-based word processor, or even physical paper? What matters is that the Metaverse will be one of the propulsive global forces in the coming decades, with companies emerging, growing, shrinking, or dying as a result.
