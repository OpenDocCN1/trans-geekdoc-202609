
The law provides a way to support the budding Metaverse. Epic Games’ Unreal license to developers is written such that licensees have indefinite rights to one specific Unreal Engine build.^([88](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr88)) Epic can still change its license for subsequent builds and updates—and giving away such a right would be financially impractical and probably harmful to developers over the long run. But the result of this policy is that developers need not worry that by choosing to use Unreal, they’re forever reliant upon the whims, desires, and leadership of Epic. And as the Unreal Engine license allows developers nearly free rein for customizations and third-party integrations, developers can choose not to use future updates and instead build their own in lieu of whatever Epic adds in future releases.

In 2021, Epic made another important modification to its Unreal Engine license: it gave up the right to terminate that license, even in instances where a developer had failed to make an outstanding payment or violated the agreement outright. Instead, Epic would need to take its customer to court to mandate payment or win an injunction that would allow it to suspend support of the developer’s app.^([89](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr89)) This makes it harder, slower, and costlier for Epic to enforce its rules, but the policy is designed to build trust with developers, which Epic hopes will be good business overall, while placing pressure on “powerful companies” that use “termination of contracts to exert control” and act as “judge, jury, and executioner.”^([90](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr90))

## Blocking and Tackling

By the start of 2024, venture capital funds, private equity firms, and hedge funds have invested over $80 billion directly into crypto and blockchain start-ups since 2012, a sum that excludes the purchase of NFTs, DAO and dapp tokens, and cryptocurrencies that were made through secondary trading platforms, marketplaces, and exchanges such as Coinbase, OpenSea, and Uniswap; retail investment is similarly excluded.

Expecting world-changing solutions from such world-changing figures is reasonable, though these sums need to be put in context. Over half of crypto investment occurred after December 2021, and at the start of 2024, barely one in six dollars has been at work for more than four years. Rebuilding the internet, and society with it, takes time. Eighty billion dollars is probably close to Facebook’s and Apple’s combined Metaverse investments, which likewise have yet to take off or, in some cases, debut. At the same time, there is not much evidence that the next great technology companies are emerging from the Web3 community—let alone those which might rival and force change from giants such as Apple and Google.

Some analysts now speculate that “tokenomics” may actually be a bug, not a feature, of crypto entrepreneurship.^([91](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr91)) It’s possible that such structures reward hype and quick financial returns to the detriment of the quiet, long-term efforts that have, on balance, created the world’s most enduring corporations—and its most significant nonprofits, too. Another issue relates to market feedback. By providing early users, customers, and partners with large volumes of tokens, it’s theoretically easier for an entrepreneur to drive adoption and set in motion a “flywheel,” but it can also be hard to determine how much of their product’s usage reflects “product-market fit” versus speculation from those who don’t even care what the product does or doesn’t do, or why.

It’s also clear that tokenomics alone cannot differentiate a platform—and might not start its flywheel, either. Integrated virtual world platforms such as `Decentraland` (which opened publicly in February 2020, with parcels available for sale since 2017) and `The Sandbox` (2021 and 2017) promised that their blockchain foundations would allow them to rapidly accumulate users and developers and ultimately outmaneuver Web 2.0 giants such as `Roblox` and `Minecraft`. In 2022, `Sandbox` reported that it had 1.3 million total players throughout the year, with an average of 200,000 monthly players, and 30,000 to 40,000 daily players, while `Decentraland` claimed 57,000 average monthly users.^([92](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr92)) As Apple’s iOS, Microsoft’s Xbox, Nintendo’s Switch, and Sony’s PlayStation all prohibit crypto, blockchain IVWPs face a lower user ceiling than non-blockchain IVWPs. Even so, the gap between these services and Web 2.0 IVWPs is astounding—in 2022, an average of 39,000 users logged on to `Roblox` each minute.^([93](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr93)) Reports also suggest that most of those that log on to `Sandbox` and `Decentraland` do so not to play these titles but instead to access their wallets or trade game-related NFTs and other tokens.^([94](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr94)) Developer investment is equally lackluster (and partly responsible for the poor user numbers). In March 2024, Sandbox announced more than 1,000 user-generated experiences were available on the two-year old platform.^([95](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr95)) In contrast, `Fortnite Creative` had added nearly 100,000 new worlds in the previously year alone, reaching an average of 600 each day.^([96](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr96)) And during 2022, Roblox reported it was growing by `15,000` experiences per day.^([97](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr97))

In 2024, most enthusiasm for the blockchain now focuses on financial use cases, which are broadly termed “DeFi,” as in “decentralized finance.” There are obvious benefits to reducing the cost of capital formation and improving payment delivery. At the same time, regulators spent much of 2023 arguing that many token offerings are securities—including those of major Layer 2s like Polygon and centralized Layer 1s such as Solana and Top Shot maker Dapper Labs’ Flow—alongside many of the tokens used to fund or participate in dapps and DAOs, and even some NFT collections.^([98](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr98)) Securities that are available to the public require their issuers to register their offerings, provide various public disclosures, agree to audits and oversight, and face legal consequences for misrepresentations and other gaps. These issuers, and those that sell these supposed securities, such as Robinhood or Coinbase, must also ensure that they “know their customer” to comply with federal provisions designed to limit money laundering and facilitate tax collection, and cannot sell some of these securities to unaccredited investors. In turn, Web3 proponents (among others) point out that these requirements often benefit the already wealthy and discriminate against the poor, represent a roadblock for start-ups trying to raise money, and are out of date in the digital era (especially in the case of crypto). In either case, at least part of DeFi’s scale reflects regulatory arbitrage, if not outright legal violations, rather than a technical unlock. Furthermore, the lack of oversight has had consequences.
