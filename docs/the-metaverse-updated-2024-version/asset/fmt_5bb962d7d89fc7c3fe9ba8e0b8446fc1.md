
# Polygon 与 Solana 的验证器网络分析

Polygon has a capped validator network of only 100, and the top 10 holders (mostly the blockchain’s founders, foundation, employees, and advisors) own roughly 66% of total token supply.^([58](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr58)) In contrast, there are more than 500,000 Ethereum validators—who can join permissionlessly—and the top 10 holders own less than 10%.^([59](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr59)) In 2022, a prominent investor alleged that Polygon’s “admin key” is controlled by a multisignature contract that requires approval from only five of the eight eligible signees (four of whom founded the network, with the remaining four selected by the foundation) and could therefore be used to hijack billions in tokens.^([60](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr60)) In 2021, Polygon paid a $2 million bounty to a white hat hacker who identified a security exploit that would have enabled a hacker to steal $850 million. Solana’s network has averaged roughly 3,000–3,500 validators since Q1 2022,^([61](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr61)) with the top ten holders estimated to own roughly 10%^([62](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr62)) (though some investors sued, alleging that de facto ownership is as much as 48%^([63](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr63))). In part due to Solana’s low fees to validators, the network crashed more than half a dozen times between September 1, 2021, and September 30, 2022, with the longest outage lasting 20 hours.^([64](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr64)) In March 2022, hackers stole over $600 million from wallets stored on the Ronin sidechain, which had been developed by Sky Mavis so that *Axie Infinity* could support high volumes of low-cost transactions, despite being based on Ethereum.^([65](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr65)) But Ronin operated with only nine validators, four of which were run by Sky Mavis, whose centralized server was hacked. Unfortunately, one of the five independent validators had loaned their key to Sky Mavis in order to speed up transactions as the game’s userbase spiked; this was enough to give hackers control of over half the network and the ability to validate fraudulent transactions.^([66](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr66))

# TPS 限制与共识机制

It’s also essential to note that TPS is not the only constraint to rapid transaction speeds, as TPS measures throughput, not the time required to establish consensus. The larger a validator network is and the more spread out it is globally, the more time is required—the speed of light endures as a constraint. This could be addressed by establishing local validator networks, but consistent with the blockchain trilemma, such a model would decrease network security as it makes it easier for said validator network to collude and harder for each validator to remain anonymous and thus unknown to would-be hackers.

Due to the many limitations of modern blockchains, dapps continue to place most of their data and operations “off chain.” Games, for that matter, simply must; there is no way to run a game engine, much less a multiplayer service, on a decentralized validator network. But with its reliance on centralized databases, critics maintain that much of the blockchain ecosystem remains under centralized control.

# NFT 所有权的法律与技术局限

Recall from [Chapters 7](#chapter7.xhtml) and [8](#chapter8.xhtml) that for data to be used, it must be run by code. An NFT may provide a user with immutable ownership of a Michael Jordan avatar, but this doesn’t assure that a game will always recognize this avatar or any of its properties. Even the game that issued that NFT might change its rules unilaterally so that the user’s ability to use that NFT is limited or effectively disabled. Take *Sandbox*, the crypto-focused integrated virtual world platform owned by blockchain conglomerate Animoca Brands. Section 4(a) of Sandbox’s terms and conditions lists 22 “Prohibited Activities.” Section 4(b) warns that:

“If we reasonably believe that you are engaged in any of the category b prohibited activities, in addition to our right to immediately suspend or terminate your user account and/or delete your premium NFT’s images and descriptions from the platform, we also reserve the right, at our sole and absolute discretion, without notice or liability to you, to take any of all of the following actions: (a) to deem any transaction that look place via or as the result of such activities to be void ab initio; and/or (b) to immediately confiscate any premium NFTs (including their underlying NFTs) that were purchased or acquired as the result of such activities.”^([67](#notes_split_011.xhtml#ch11fr67))

While the terms of use list specific “prohibited activities” whose potential harm is evident (such as distributing viruses), at least in theory, Sandbox could change that list tomorrow to prohibit any conduct it so chooses. Certainly, Sandbox does not benefit from taking such steps, but the mere fact that it has the legal right to take those steps, even to the point of confiscating an NFT, makes the technical distinction between decentralized NFTs and centralized assets seem trivial—either way, it’s trust, not “immutability,” that keeps the system together. Some virtual games are constantly changing how their code interprets various NFTs so that their games can stay fresh and reward new forms of play. This was the approach used by Sky Mavis, which frequently alters the skill sets or powers of its Axies and tweaks what they can earn, too. It’s hard to say ownership is immutable if one’s ability to use a good that one owns is so easily and fundamentally changed.

# Dapp 竞争与 DAO 治理

Applications such as *Sandbox* and *Axie Infinity* also point to the limits of dapp-versus-dapp competition, such as that between Uniswap and Sushiswap. These latter two apps boast little custom code, have negligible server costs, and involve no proprietary IP, with the business primarily concerning assets developed by outside parties. But the code of *Sandbox* and *Axie Infinity* is protected, the costs of operations are high, and their IP cannot legally be repurposed even though their NFTs can be read by other developers. Building a comparable offering with a comparable userbase is not easy. In this regard, competing with a hit blockchain game is not much different from, nor much easier than, competing with those of Activision Blizzard or Warner Bros. Interactive—yes, a developer could offer a game that’s “better” or “cheaper” or that “offers better rights,” but that’s just part of the challenge.

These examples expand into DAOs, as well. When CityDAO passed a motion to reduce voting stakes to the rounded-down square root of the number of tokens held, it forcibly diluted 12.3% of the other members of its DAO—and by varying degrees! A member that had 25 votes was diluted to 5, a net loss of 80%, whereas a user that had 4 diluted to 2, a net loss of 50%. And while CityDAO’s developmental lease for its Colorado site is clear that the DAO maintains the deed and right to rescind the NFT, such clauses show that the NFT is primarily ceremonial and, more often than not, subordinate to classical property law.
