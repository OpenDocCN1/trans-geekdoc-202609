
# Vision Pro 设备分析

The Vision Pro’s 12 total cameras and two depth sensors support rich color passthrough, facial, eye, and hand tracking, and reliable boundary and obstacle detection. As with its display, most reviewers consider the Vision Pro’s sensors far more capable and reliable than those available on any other device available for consumers to purchase. However, passthrough cameras struggle to capture the full color of the world around them—and the display supports just under half of the colors the average human eye can see. The cameras also produce blurriness or inaccurate passthrough video tinting when capturing environments in low, high, or inconsistent light.^([50](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr50)) According to The Verge, Vision Pro’s OS “feels also designed for an eye tracking system that’s just slightly more precise than it actually is.”^([51](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr51)) The device’s external cameras and computer vision can accurately map the dimensions of a room and anchor virtual objects (e.g., a virtual screen against a living room wall), though it is unable to understand most of what it sees (i.e., identify an object or place it in context), let alone what it does or how the user might interact with it—core requirements for many industrial passthrough MR use cases, as well as for optical MR glasses.

## 消费者成本

The consumer costs of the Vision Pro’s advances are also evident and considerable. As discussed earlier in this chapter, the entry-level version of the device costs US$3,500 (in many countries, the sales tax on the device will exceed the price of a Quest device).^([§](#chapter9.xhtml#footnote-016)) Users who require corrective lenses will need to spend another $99–$149, and if the device is intended for multiple users, Apple recommends each user purchase their own $199 “Light Seal,” which comes in 28 different sizes and shapes, and “gently conforms to your face and helps deliver a precise fit while blocking out stray light.”^([52](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr52)) The user may also need a different version of the four “Light Seal Cushions,” which cost $29 each.^([53](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr53))

## 重量与电池

Depending on the head strap used, the Vision Pro also weighs between 600 and 650 grams, equivalent to 3.7 iPhone 15s and just 60 grams less than the largest and heaviest iPad Pro, the 12.9" sixth-generation iPad Pro, released in 2022. In contrast, Facebook’s Quest 3 is 515 grams, while the Quest Pro, Facebook’s highest-end HMD at the time of the Vision Pro’s debut, is 722 grams. And while Facebook’s headsets have an integrated battery, the Vision Pro requires the wearer to physically connect to a separate, pacemaker-like battery that is roughly as thick as two iPhones and weighs twice as much as well (or 350 grams) or stay plugged into an electrical outlet. Though the Quest Pro headset is 100 grams (0.22 lbs.) heavier than the Vision Pro headset, many reviewers have reported that the Quest Pro feels lighter (or at least more comfortable). This perception stems from Facebook’s decision to place the Quest Pro’s battery inside the back of the device’s headband. As such, the Quest Pro is more balanced than the Vision Pro, with its weight more centered on the wearer’s spine, rather than pulling their head downward.

Apple’s decision to use an external, rather than integrated, power source for the Vision Pro was reportedly controversial within the company,^([54](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr54)) but it was no doubt required to power the device’s brightly illuminated high-resolution screens and robust processors and sensors, and to avoid overheating the user’s face. Even though the Vision Pro’s battery is 39.5 watt hours versus the Quest 3’s 18.88 and Quest Pro’s 20.58, the Vision Pro supports up to 2 hours of general use and 2.5 hours of video playback—less than Facebook’s HMDs.^([55](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr55)) After the Vision Pro was unveiled, Zuckerberg told employees in a company-wide meeting, “From what I’ve seen initially, I’d say the good news is that there’s no kind of magical solutions that they have to any of the constraints on laws of physics that our teams haven’t already explored and thought of.”^([56](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr56))

## 其他光学 AR 设备

As one might expect, mainstream optical AR devices are even further from release in 2024. Snap’s Spectacles 4, released in 2021, cost only $500, weighed 134 grams, and resembled boxy, somewhat stylish sunglasses. The device also augmented less than 10% of the wearer’s FOV and on a barely 0.5K display that supported 30–120 fps depending on simulation complexity, could not scan, much less understand, what it saw, lacked both eye and hand tracking, and had a battery that lasted no more than 30 minutes.^([57](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr57)) Microsoft’s 2019 HoloLens 2 cost $3,500 and weighed over 550 grams (making it a headset, not glasses), but augmented only 20% of a wearer’s FOV on a 2K display at a max of 60 fps and had limited environmental scanning functionality, though it was capable of both eye and hand tracking.^([58](#notes_split_009.xhtml#ch9fr58))
