One of the main challenges of finding the catalytic reaction path is the subtle trade-off between exploitation and exploration. Exploitation in this work means that the model claims the rewards based on the current strategy, while exploration means finding a better strategy to produce more plausible intermediate states along the reaction path. The goal is to find a strong decision-making strategy so that our model has the highest probability of taking the optimal action. On the one hand, the optimal action trajectory guarantees reaching the end state and maximizes rewards given the observed states. On the other hand, the high probability for those optimal actions will increase the stability of constructing complex reaction networks and is crucial to confidently determine the most plausible reaction path given the start and final states. During the training stage, if we constantly choose the best action known so far, the model will not have sufficient chance to

explore other plausible actions given the same state, so the model is most likely to follow semioptimal policies leading to small rewards or even bad policies that cannot reach the final state. Moreover, unlike many other problems, the catalytic reaction path found by the DRL model is expected to become significantly shorter in the late training stage compared with that in its early stage. As a result, the total number of samples from a rollout may decrease dramatically with further training. These pose great challenges for exploring this complex environment with sparse rewards, many competing actions, and a huge number of states.

To tackle the exploitation–exploration challenge, we employed several novel strategies. We applied the curriculum learning algorithm to environment building. During training, the model explores multiple environments of varying complexity associated with low to high skill levels and eventually interacts with the realistic environment. This also significantly helps to improve the stability and speed of model convergency.
