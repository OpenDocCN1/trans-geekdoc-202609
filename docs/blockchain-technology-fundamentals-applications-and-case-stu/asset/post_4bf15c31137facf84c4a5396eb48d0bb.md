# 9.1 BLOCKCHAIN

# 9.1.1 BLOCKCHAIN

Blockchain is a decentralized computation and information sharing shared platform that enables multiple authoritative domains that do not trust each other to cope, coordinate, and collaborate in a rational decision-making process. In a typical blockchain architecture, every node (the system/computer of each part) maintains a local copy of the global datasheet. The system ensures consistency among the local copies by ensuring that (i) the local copies at every node are identical and (ii) the local copies are always based on the global information.

The local information stored on each part's computer is called a public ledger, which can be considered as a database available with everyone, containing the historical data that can be used for futures computations. The public ledger stores all the global transactions that are synchronized. For example, consider four parties named Anna, Ben, Dav, and Emma who are involved in distributed banking. They have Rs. 5,000 in each of their accounts, and all four have a public ledger at their local systems. Now, consider the following transactions:

- 1. Anna sends Rs. 2,000 to Ben – accepted and updated in everyone's ledger
- 2. Ben sends Rs. 4,000 to Dav – accepted and updated in everyone's ledger
- 3. Anna sends Rs. 4,000 to Emma – transaction rejected

The sample led of Anna, Ben, Dav, and Emma after the above transactions are shown in Figure 9.1. Here, the first two transactions are successful; they were verified and accepted by all the parties. But the third transaction, Anna → Emma: Rs. 4000 was not successful. When Anna broadcasts her request, other parties check the balance in her account by checking transactions committed in their public ledger. From they learn that Anna does not have the required balance for the transaction. Hence, all the other parties reject the third transaction mentioned above, and this transaction is not recorded in their public ledger.

FGChain: Blockchain Application Framework

The basic requirements for blockchain are as follows:

- Protocols for commitment: There should be proper mechanism to ensure that every valid transaction from the clients is committed and included in the blockchain within a finite time. For example, the first two transactions mentioned in the above have to be verified one after the other and committed before the next transaction starts.
- Consensus: There should be mechanism to ensure that the local copies of the public ledger are consistent and updated.
- Security: When one party broadcasts the information, other parties involved should be able to validate that the information is tamperproof. If a client acts maliciously or if a client is compromised, it should be identified by the other parties immediately.
- Privacy and authentication: As a number of parties are involved in blockchain, authenticity and privacy of the data should be maintained. According to Lansiti and Lakhani (2017), blockchain can be defined as “an open distributed/decentralized ledger that can record transactions between the parties efficiently and in a permanent way”. The public ledger is accessible to all and is not controlled by a single party. The transactions between the parties should be fast and scalable, and everyone involved check the validity of information. They have also briefed the working of blockchain. The five basic principles of blockchain technology mentioned byiti and Lakhani (2017) is shown in Figure 9.2.

The cryptographic primitive used to build the blockchain is a hash function. Every block stores the hash value of the previous block and thus the hash function acts as a link to the chain of blocks, resulting in a blockchain. This type of using values to form a chain of blocks is derived from Haber and Stometta (1990). The Merkle trees used by Merkel, developed in 1979 (Merkle, 1982), is combined with the work of Haber and Stometta (1990) to develop efficient sampling of documents to form one block (Bayer et al., 1993). This invention led to the discovery of bitcoin by Nakamoto (2008), which is built on the foundation of blockchain technology.

Bitcoin is a completely decentralized peer-to-peer cryptocurrency. As it is permissionless, anyone without any identity or login credentials can use it anywhere, without any access control. This leads to legal issues in many countries, the technology behind it, blockchain, can be used for a number of useful applications. Blockchain technology can be used not only in the financial sector but in almost all the distributed, decentralized applications. Governments, industries, academia, and agriculture domain are moving towards blockchain, and this revolution is called Blockchain 2.0.

# Distributed Database/Public Ledger

Each party involved in the computation of blockchain has access to the entire database/public ledger and its complete history. No single party controls the data or the information. Every party can verify the records of its transaction partners directly, without an intermediary or any central controller.

# Peer-to-Peer Transmission

Communication between the peers takes place directly, without using a central node. Every node/ party involved have the capacity to store and forward the information to all other parties.

# Transparency with Pseudonymity

Anyone has access to the system can view the transactions and the values associated. A unique 30+ address is assigned to each party involved in blockchain, which is used for transactions. Users/parties can remain anonymous or provide proof of their identity to others.

# Irreversibility of Records

The records cannot be altered after the transaction is verified and entered in the database. All transactions are linked and updated in the blocks of the blockchain. Multiple algorithms and approaches are used to ensure permanent recording in database in a chronological order, available to all others over the network.

# Computational Logic

The public ledger is maintained in digital format. This makes the transactions linked to computational logic, which can be programmed. Algorithms and rules can be programmed in such a way that transactions between the nodes are automatically triggered.
