# Distributed Database/Public Ledger

Each party involved in the computation of blockchain has access to the entire database/public
ledger and its complete history. No single party controls the data or the information. Every party
can verify the records of its transaction partners directly, without an inerymediary or any central
controller.

# Peer-to-Peer Transmission

Communication between the peers takes place directly, without using a central node. Every
node/ party involved have the capacity to store and forward the information to all other parties.

# Transparency with Pseudonymity

Anyone has access to the system can view the transactions and the values associated.
A unique 30+ address is assigned to each party involved in blockchain, which is used
for transactions. Users/parties can remain anonymous or provide proof of their identity to
others.

# Irreversibility of Records

The records cannot be altered after the transaction is verified and entered in the database. All
t transactions are linked and updated in the blocks of the blockchain. Multiple algorithms and
approaches are used to ensure permanent recording in database in a chronological order, available
to all others over the network.

# Computational Logic

The public ledger is maintained in digital format. This makes the transactions linked to
computational logic, which can be programmed. Algorithms and rules can be programmed in such
a way that transactions between the nodes are automatically triggered.

The Blockchain 2.0 involves smart contracts to achieve decentralization without a
middle. Smart contract introduced by Szabo in the late 1990s (Szabo, 1997) is an
automated computerized protocol used for digitally facilitating, verifying, or enforce-
t negotiation on performance of a legal contract by using intermediaries and
directly validating the contract over a decentralized platform in a faster, cheaper, and
more secure. Smart contracts are:

+   i. Immutable: That means no party will be able to change the contract once
it is fixed and written to the public ledger – blockchain.

+   ii. Distributed: That means there will be all the steps of the
contract can be validated by every participating party. Hence, no one
claim later that the contract was not validated.

These properties of smart contract make it suitable to be used in blockchain, as the
blocks in it are immutable and the information is open to all the parties involved;
anyone can check and validate the information.
