## 12.7.5 DOUBLE-SPENDING PROBLEM SOLVED BY BLOCKCHAIN

The inherent drawback with digital money is the double-spending problem, which is because of the centralized ledger. Double-spending indicates “spending the same money twice”. If Alice has physical cash and once the cash is handed over to Bob in exchange of goods, the money goes to Bob and cannot be used by Alice again. Digital money is easy to copy as it is a string of alphanumeric characters.

![](img/5197498995d15589ead293656bce20db_226_0.png)

FIGURE 12.7 Solution to double spending problem.

Let us assume that Alice has $50, and she is attempting to transfer $50 to the wallets of both Bob and Charles simultaneously. Both transactions would enter the pool of unconfirmed transactions. The miners now get action and if the transaction to Alice is confirmed, then the transaction is verified, confirmed, and linked to the previous block. However, the transaction to Charles would be recognized as invalid by the confirmation process and would be rejected. If both transactions are dragged out from the pool concurrently, the transaction that gets the highest number of confirmations will be included in the blockchain, while the other one will get discarded.

The general recommendation is to wait for a minimum of 6 confirmations to ensure that the transaction is valid. Here, “6 confirmations” implies that after a transaction has been added to the blockchain, since more blocks containing several other transactions are to be added after it. All these confirmations and transactions are timestamped and linked with the previous block, making them irreversible and impossible to tamper with.
