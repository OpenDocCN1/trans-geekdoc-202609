## V. DISCUSSION

Machine learning techniques were used in this research to generate predictive models to help identify possible cases and predictors of mental health conditions among the general population in the Philippines. There were 3 approaches executed in this study in order to experiment multiple test cases of the data.

Approach A did not use the DASS-21 and IES-R survey answers as possible features (see Table III), unlike approach B and C. All the results were low, with an overall average accuracy of 67.61%. The data gathered from the 8 parts of the created survey were not good stand alone predictors. Considering this, approaches B and C included the other target variables as predictors as well.

Compared to A, approaches B and C had its results increase, having accuracies of 81.40% and 82.45% respectively. This was due to the fact that the DASS-21 and IES-R survey results were taken into account in these approaches. This suggests that when predicting for the presence of a mental illness, say Stress, the other target variable results for the other mental illnesses (In this case Anxiety, Depression, Impact of Event) were significant additions as predictors. The status of the presence of the mental illnesses in the respondent are good predictors for each other. That being said, other independent variables that came from the created survey (*) still had good enough correlation to the dependent variable being predicted at that time to be counted as a significant predictor.

Results show that how an individual rates their own health, and presence of a pre-existing neuropsychiatric condition were good predictors for stress. Depression had similar predictors as stress, but an addition was that age was a good predictor as well. Individuals actively seeking information on the symptoms of the virus, being at home longer, and experiencing symptoms such as body pain and difficulty breathing, contribute to anxiety levels. The study also found that respondents who actively seek information on the pandemic on social media, want to know about an effective medicine against the virus, have concern for their family, have feelings of being discriminated, and experience cough are connected to adverse psychological impact from the pandemic (Impact of event).

## VI. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK

This research aimed to help identify the possible cases for Stress, Anxiety, Depression, and assess the impact of event of the general population in the Philippines using machine learning techniques. Manual efforts can be time consuming and it might not show all possible results at first glance, especially considering the large amount of data present. The results from this research help support the growing popularity of machine learning in the field of detection and diagnostics of mental health conditions. With COVID-19 spreading all over the world, techniques like the one used in this research can contribute significantly to find the possible contributing factors to mental health issues caused by the pandemic with high accuracy. Psychologists and researchers can pursue further studies to validate and describe the effect of these identified factors. Once established, improved policies and guidelines can be created by stakeholders to help society avoid further mental harm.
