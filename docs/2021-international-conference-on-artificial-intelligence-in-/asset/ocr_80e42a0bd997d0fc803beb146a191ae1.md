Another research conducted by [11] assessed the psychological distress among Korean Immigrants in the United States using artificial neural networks (ANN). In a study done by [12], they did research on the psychological impact of COVID-19 on the general population of the Philippines by looking into the psychiatric symptoms present and determined the factors contributing effects. Online surveys were distributed from March 28, 2020 to April 20, 2020 and gathered data including socio-demographics, health status, contact history, COVID-19 knowledge and concerns, precautionary measures, and information needs. In addition, ratings for the Depression, Anxiety, and Stress scale (DASS-21) and the Impact of event scale-Revised (IES-R) were taken as well. The researchers used SPSS statistic 24.0 to perform statistical analysis on the data. Results from their research showed that factors such as Female gender; youth age; single status; students; specific symptoms; recent imposed quarantine; prolonged home-stay; and reports of poor health status, unnecessary worry, concerns for family members, and discrimination were connected with substantial psychological impact and elevated levels of depression, anxiety, and stress. In contrast, a sufficient amount of health information, having grown-up children, perception of good health status and confidence in doctors’ abilities were linked to less adverse impact and decreased levels of depression, anxiety and stress. Further investigation of their data could be done using machine learning models and feature selection algorithms to verify or discover more possible predictor variables to the mental health issues found among the participants.

# III. METHODOLOGY

# A. Data set

Data gathering was done through online means, as the government of the Philippines recommended to minimize face-to-face interactions. The online survey platform Survey Monkey was used, and the created questionnaire was distributed first to students and alumni of the University of the Philippines. Afterwards, they passed it on to others after they accomplished the questionnaire. This snowball strategy that was implemented helped to reach a total of 2652 respondents.

1) Predictor Variables: The survey was divided into 7 parts: (A) Demographics; (B) Symptoms and physical health status; (C) Contact history; (D) Knowledge and belief about COVID-19; (E) Pre-cautionary measures in past 14 days; (F) Additional information for COVID-19; (G) Pre-existing condition.

The demographics collected from the respondents were gender, age, education, country of residence, marital status, employment status, if the he was a health care professional, parental status, household size, and if the he traveled outside of the country of residence in the past 14 days.

Presence of the following symptoms in the past 14 days included fever, chills, headaches, myalgia, cough, difficulty breathing, dizziness, coryza, sore throat, nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea. In addition to, if they saw a doctor in the past 14 days, admitted to the hospital in the past 14 days, gotten tested for COVID-19 in the past 14 days, quarantined in the past 14 days, if they have medical insurance, if they suffer from a chronic illness, and how they self-rated their physical health status.

The extent of the contact history with COVID-19 patients was taken as well, namely close contact, indirect contact, and contact with suspected cases and infected materials.

To get the respondents’ knowledge of COVID-19, the variables included known transmission routes, satisfaction on health information available and how they obtain it, heard numbers on the different types of cases, hours spent on social media to obtain COVID-19 information, confidence in their own doctor on the virus, self-rated likelihood of contracting and surviving the virus, concerns about their family, and whether or not the feel discriminated.

The variables on precautionary measures included covering of mouth, sharing of utensils, hand washing, and wearing masks. The respondents were also asked if they feel that COVID-19 has resulted to unnecessary worry, and how many extra hours per day did they stay at home because of the virus.

The respondents’ desire for different types of additional information on COVID-19 was taken note of as well. These types included: details on symptoms, advice on prevention and treatment, regular updates, advice for people who need tailored information, availability and effectiveness of a vaccine, number of people affected, travel advice, how the virus is spread, and how other countries are doing.

The respondents were also asked if they had a pre-existing neuropsychiatric condition and if they were taking medications for it.
