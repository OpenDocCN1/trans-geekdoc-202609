### Abstract

The COVID-19 pandemic has created a massive impact in the economy, healthcare, education and other aspects of society in each and every respect. Also greatly affected is the mental health of individuals. This study aims to determine the possible contributing factors to stress, anxiety, depression, and adverse psychological impact on the general population of the Philippines using machine learning approaches. The data gathered from 2119 participants who answered an online survey were analyzed using feature selection methods and machine learning classifiers to determine contributing factors to the aforementioned mental health issues. The results show that longer hours at home, on social media, age, how people rate their own health, pre-existence of a neuropsychiatric condition; wanting information on availability and effectiveness of a medicine or vaccine, being concerned for their family, feeling discriminated; and symptoms of body pain, difficulty breathing, and cough were good predictors of individuals being adversely impacted psychologically by the pandemic and others having elevated levels of stress, anxiety, depression.

### Index Terms

Mental Health, Stress, Anxiety, Depression, Impact of Event, Machine Learning, Feature Selection

## I. INTRODUCTION

On December 31st 2019, cases of pneumonia of unknown etiology were detected in Wuhan City, Hubei Province of China, and authorities identified a new type of coronavirus (2019-nCoV) shortly after [1]. The World Health Organization (WHO) named the virus COVID-19, and it was declared a pandemic on March 11th 2020 [2], [3]. As of February 5 2021, there are over 102 million confirmed cases and over 2.2 million deaths worldwide spanning 222 countries and territories [4], with the Philippines having a total of 531 thousand confirmed cases and over 10 thousand deaths [5].

Just like any pandemic, it has created a massive impact in various aspects of society, from economy to healthcare, even to education. Psychological impact is also a matter of concern, since the spread of the virus plays a critical role in the occurrence of emotional distress and social disorder among the population affected [6], [7]. In a recent study performed in Italy of 18147 respondents, among several other outcomes, 17.3% reported severe depressive symptoms, 20.8% reported severe anxiety symptoms, and 7.3% reported severe insomnia symptoms. The study also states that conditions caused by the pandemic that were associated with the results were independent from other considerations such as previous mental illnesses or childhood traumas [8]. In dealing with mental health issues among members of the population, contributing factors must be determined for immediate intervention and to prevent further damage. Recent years have seen advances in research in the field of machine learning for mental health, and it is clear that it can significantly aid in the detection and diagnosis of mental health conditions. [9].

This study aims to determine the possible contributing factors to stress, anxiety, depression, and adverse psychological impact on the general population of the Philippines resulting from the pandemic using machine learning approaches.

## II. RELATED WORK

To date, a few studies have been done on the psychological impact the COVID-19 pandemic has caused worldwide. As shown in these studies, finding the possible contributing factors is a key step in mitigation and prevention of further damage

A recent study [10] examined the psychological impact of COVID-19 and the restrictions set by their government on the perceived stress of Italian adults. Their research also targeted to verify some contributing factors to the impact as stated in another recent study. Data from a total of 2053 participants was used and statistical analysis was performed on it through the JASP software. Further analysis through feature selection and machine learning models was done using the WEKA 3.9 software. From the questions answered by the participants, 18 were considered as predictors of perceived stress. Of those 18, 7 were chosen by the correlation-based feature selection algorithm as the best predictors for perceived stress, while the rest were considered redundant and irrelevant. To measure the predictive performance of these 7 predictors, machine learning models were generated using the classifiers Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machines, Naive Bayes, and Random Forest. Their results show considerable ROC area values ranging from 0.70 to 0.78, however the Random Forest classifier having low recall compared to the others makes it weak in terms of predictive purposes in their case.

Another research conducted by [11] assessed the psychological distress among Korean Immigrants in the United States using artificial neural networks (ANN). Their data was gathered through a distributed online survey among Korean immigrants above the age of 18, collecting 790 total participants as a result. Their predictors consisted of discrimination-related variables, coping mechanisms, and socio-demographic factors. Among the factors associated with these predictors, the most connected to the participants' psychological distress were their own resilience, experiences of discrimination, and the mindset of racial discrimination towards Asians has increased in the country.

In a study done by [12], they did research on the psychological impact of COVID-19 on the general population of the Philippines by looking into the psychiatric symptoms present and determined the factors contributing effects. Online surveys were distributed from March 28, 2020 to April 20, 2020 and gathered data including socio-demographics, health status, contact history, COVID-19 knowledge and concerns, precautionary measures, and information needs. In addition, ratings for the Depression, Anxiety, and Stress scale (DASS-21) and the Impact of event scale-Revised (IES-R) were taken as well. The researchers used SPSS statistic 24.0 to perform statistical analysis on the data. Results from their research showed that factors such as Female gender; youth age; single status; students; specific symptoms; recent imposed quarantine; prolonged home-stay; and reports of poor health status, unnecessary worry, concerns for family members, and discrimination were connected with substantial psychological impact and elevated levels of depression, anxiety, and stress. In contrast, a sufficient amount of health information, having grown-up children, perception of good health status and confidence in doctors' abilities were linked to less adverse impact and decreased levels of depression, anxiety and stress. Further investigation of their data could be done using machine learning models and feature selection algorithms to verify or discover more possible predictor variables to the mental health issues found among the participants.

## III. METHODOLOGY

### A. Data set

Data gathering was done through online means, as the government of the Philippines recommended to minimize face-to-face interactions. The online survey platform Survey Monkey was used, and the created questionnaire was distributed first to students and alumni of the University of the Philippines. Afterwards, they passed it on to others after they accomplished the questionnaire. This snowball strategy that was implemented helped to reach a total of 2652 respondents.

1) Predictor Variables: The survey was divided into 7 parts:
- (A) Demographics
- (B) Symptoms and physical health status
- (C) Contact history
- (D) Knowledge and belief about COVID-19
- (E) Precautionary measures in past 14 days
- (F) Additional information for COVID-19
- (G) Pre-existing condition

The demographics collected from the respondents were gender, age, education, country of residence, marital status, employment status, if he was a health care professional, parental status, household size, and if he traveled outside of the country of residence in the past 14 days.

Presence of the following symptoms in the past 14 days included fever, chills, headaches, myalgia, cough, difficulty breathing, dizziness, coryza, sore throat, nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea. In addition to, if they saw a doctor in the past 14 days, admitted to the hospital in the past 14 days, gotten tested for COVID-19 in the past 14 days, quarantined in the past 14 days, if they have medical insurance, if they suffer from a chronic illness, and how they self-rated their physical health status.

The extent of the contact history with COVID-19 patients was taken as well, namely close contact, indirect contact, and contact with suspected cases and infected materials.

To get the respondents' knowledge of COVID-19, the variables included known transmission routes, satisfaction on health information available and how they obtain it, heard numbers on the different types of cases, hours spent on social media to obtain COVID-19 information, confidence in their own doctor on the virus, self-rated likelihood of contracting and surviving the virus, concerns about their family, and whether or not they feel discriminated.

The variables on precautionary measures included covering of mouth, sharing of utensils, hand washing, and wearing masks. The respondents were also asked if they feel that COVID-19 has resulted to unnecessary worry, and how many extra hours per day did they stay at home because of the virus.

The respondents' desire for different types of additional information on COVID-19 was taken note of as well. These types included:
- details on symptoms
- advice on prevention and treatment
- regular updates
- advice for people who need tailored information
- availability and effectiveness of a vaccine
- number of people affected
- travel advice
- how the virus is spread
- how other countries are doing
