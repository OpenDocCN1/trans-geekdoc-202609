**Abstract**—The COVID-19 pandemic has created a massive impact in the economy, healthcare, education and other aspects of society in each and every respect. Also greatly affected is the mental health of individuals. This study aims to determine the possible contributing factors to stress, anxiety, depression, and adverse psychological impact on the general population of the Philippines using machine learning approaches. The data gathered from 2119 participants who answered an online survey were analyzed using feature selection methods and machine learning classifiers to determine contributing factors to the aforementioned mental health issues. The results show that longer hours at home, on social media, age, how people rate their own health, pre-existence of a neuropsychiatric condition; wanting information on availability and effectiveness of a medicine or vaccine, being concerned for their family, feeling discriminated; and symptoms of body pain, difficulty breathing, and cough were good predictors of individuals being adversely impacted psychologically by the pandemic and others having elevated levels of stress, anxiety, depression.

**Index Terms**—Mental Health, Stress, Anxiety, Depression, Impact of Event, Machine Learning, Feature Selection

# I. INTRODUCTION

On December 31st 2019, cases of pneumonia of unknown etiology were detected in Wuhan City, Hubei Province of China, and authorities identified a new type of coronavirus (2019-nCoV) shortly after [1]. The World Health Organization (WHO) named the virus COVID-19, and it was declared a pandemic on March 11th 2020 [2], [3]. As of February 5 2021, there are over 102 million confirmed cases and over 2.2 million deaths worldwide spanning 222 countries and territories [4], with the Philippines having a total of 531 thousand confirmed cases and over 10 thousand deaths [5].

Just like any pandemic, it has created a massive impact in various aspects of society, from economy to healthcare, even to education. Psychological impact is also a matter of concern, since the spread of the virus plays a critical role in the occurrence of emotional distress and social disorder among the population affected [6], [7]. In a recent study performed in Italy of 18147 respondents, among several other outcomes, 17.3% reported severe depressive symptoms, 20.8% reported severe anxiety symptoms, and 7.3% reported severe

insomnia symptoms. The study also states that conditions caused by the pandemic that were associated with the results were independent from other considerations such as previous mental illnesses or childhood traumas [8]. In dealing with mental health issues among members of the population, contributing factors must be determined for immediate intervention and to prevent further damage. Recent years have seen advances in research in the field of machine learning for mental health, and it is clear that it can significantly aide in the detection and diagnosis of mental health conditions. [9].

This study aims to determine the possible contributing factors to stress, anxiety, depression, and adverse psychological impact on the general population of the Philippines resulting from the pandemic using machine learning approaches.

# II. RELATED WORK

To date, a few studies have been done on the psychological impact the COVID-19 pandemic has caused worldwide. As shown in these studies, finding the possible contributing factors is a key step in mitigation and prevention of further damage

A recent study [10] examined the psychological impact of COVID-19 and the restrictions set by their government on the perceived stress of Italian adults. Their research also targeted to verify some contributing factors to the impact as stated in another recent study. Data from a total of 2053 participants was used and statistical analysis was performed on it through the JASP software. Further analysis through feature selection and machine learning models was done using the WEKA 3.9 software. From the questions answered by the participants, 18 were considered as predictors of perceived stress. Of those 18, 7 were chosen by the correlation-based feature selection algorithm as the best predictors for perceived stress, while the rest were considered redundant and irrelevant.
