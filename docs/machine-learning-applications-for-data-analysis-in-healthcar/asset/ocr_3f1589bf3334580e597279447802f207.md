In 2009, Kumar and Ravikanth [4] developed a system using a digital camera to acquire finger-back surface images. The imaging system is focused against a white background under uniform illumination and the camera was fixed at a 20-cm distance from the imaging surface. The nonuniform illumination of objects at the hand boundaries reduces the performance of the acquired image. This is achieved by using a fixed light source above the hand. The resolution was 1600 × 200 pixels of the acquired FKP image. The subject is asked to place their hand on the support with the backhand facing the sensor.

There is a six-finger geometry and each has four fingers, resulting in 24 fingers being used to extract features. The features include finger length, widths, perimeter, and area along with the knuckle feature. The knuckle feature is extracted by applying the subspace analysis method onthe finger back surface and using these features for person classification. Although these methods were able to identify the unique features of the outer finger surface, they were not able to provide a practical solution for implementing an efficient system.

The proposed method [3] focuses on the 3D shape information of FKP and not fully on the texture information while subspace analysis methods used in Ref. [4] may not be able to extract efficient features of FKP.

# 12.2 FKP RECOGNITION ALGORITHMS

The state-of-the-art method of FKP recognition algorithms may be classified into four categories, namely, (1) subspace-based methods, (2) coding methods, (3) other methods, and (4) recent trend method. Some of the algorithms are discussed as follows.
