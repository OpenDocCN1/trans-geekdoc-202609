## 7.2.3 SBDD APPROACH

Most of the progress has been done in the field of molecular biology and structural advancement has been made for the determination of the spectroscopic structures. These methodologies have obtained the three-dimensional structures of more than 1 lakh proteins [12]. Besides the storage and retrieval of data, there has been a sufficient development of sophisticated techniques for the analysis of biological data computationally. Improvement in bioinformatics and Human Genome Project completion has paved out the way for the development of drugs due to the accessibility of innumerable target proteins. Three-dimensional structures of those proteins that are known to be therapeutic in nature are favoring the binding cavities identification and have formed the basis for drug designing according to its structure. SBDD is a speedy process for the lead optimization and discovery as it governs the three-dimensional structure of the receptor protein and houses the information about the illness at a molecular basis [13]. Most of the computational techniques like molecular simulations, molecular docking, and SBDD are most commonly used in SBDD.

SBDD is a recursive process as it progresses through more than one cycle leading to an enhanced drug molecule to medical tribunals. Generally, any detection prior to the drug involves four steps: phase 1: discovery; phase 2: development; phase 3: clinical; phase 4: registry. During the first phase, a potent healing target and a dynamic ligand is being recognized. Numerous computational systems can be utilized for binding the vast records of the data comprising small molecules into the binding site of the receptor protein. Ranking of the molecules is performed rendering the system of scoring on the basis of the electrostatic forces with the binding site. In-depth examination of the properties of the electrostatic forces of attraction for the binding site, with the existence of cavities, allosteric pockets, and clefts can be accomplished by utilizing the three-dimensional structure of the molecule to be targeted. Currently used SBDD methods contemplate the significant characteristics of the binding cavity of the therapeutic target for the competent designing of novel ligands [14, 15]. The second phase involves the synthesis and optimization of top hits [16]. Moreover, the compounds which are ranked on the top with high kinship for discerning intonation of the protein which is to be targeted are examined in vitro in the biochemical assays. These molecules as ligands interfere with the vital cellular pathways, thus leading to the development of drugs with the anticipated pharmacological and therapeutic effects [17]. Experimental methods are used for the evaluation of the biological properties like affinity, potency, and efficacy of the discerning complexes [12]. The subsequent step involves the determination of the three-dimensional structure of the receptor protein in complex with the capable ligand attained in the first phase. The three-dimensional structure delivers comprehensive evidence about the intermolecular forces responsible for the molecular recognition and ligand binding. Structural acumen into the ligand and protein complex aids with the analysis of several binding conformities, binding pockets identification of the unidentified ones, and ligand–receptor protein interaction; explication of the alterations in the conformation which results from the ligand binding; and thorough mechanistic studies [18]. In due course, multiple recurrences intensify the usefulness and meticulousness of the potential drug molecule. The third phase includes the clinical trials of the developmental candidates which are further taken into consideration. The compounds which pass the clinical trial phase are then further being taken to the last phase for the distribution of the drug in the market subjected for the clinical use.

## 7.2.4 STRUCTURE PREDICTION TECHNIQUES EMPLOYED IN SBDD

Many techniques are used in structure prediction during SBDD, and it includes comparative modeling (also known as homology modeling), threading, and ab initio modeling. The technique used on a wider scale for predictive modeling of three-dimensional structure of proteins is homology modeling [19]. In homology modeling, we predict the structure of the unknown query sequence based on selecting a template protein whose structure is known and has 40% or above sequence similarity.

Homology modeling is very useful in predicting protein structure when only sequence data is available. When a target protein of an unknown structure has a structural homolog in the database, the structure of the target protein can be modeled by using a template. This method is composed of six basic steps:

- Template selection: FASTA and BLAST are used for sequence alignment to find the template protein for the target protein. Minimum 35% sequence similarity should be there.
- Target-template sequence alignment: After finding a template sequence, alignment of the query sequence with the template sequence is carried out by using software T-coffee and PRALINE [20].
- Model building: It has two substeps known as the backbone model building and loop modeling.
- After model building, Side-chain refinement is done using algorithms like SCWRL.
- Built model is optimized by model refinement procedures. Model refinement is done by using energy minimization functions. We use software like GROMOS for model refinement.
- Model evaluation is carried out by PROCHECK and WHAT IF.

After the prediction of three-dimensional structure of the target, it is essential to correlate the model by checking the stereochemical properties and this is done by assessing the prediction structures using Ramachandran plot [21]. This helps in determining torsion angles ψ and φ and help in providing insight about the peptides structure. After applying Ramachandran plot, predicting the active site of the target protein where the ligand will bind, is an essential step. Multiple freely available tools and software are used for active site or binding cavity residues prediction. These methods are based on the evaluation of the interaction energy and van der Waals forces. Q-site finder is one such tool which is used for active-site prediction. Q-site finder is an energy-based method commonly used for binding-site prediction [22]. In this method, van der Waals interaction energies of proteins are calculated with a methyl probe. Those that have favorable energy are employed. These probe clusters are placed accordingly to their total interaction energies additionally interacting protein residues are functionally annotated to determine the binding site [23].

## 7.2.5 ONLINE TOOLS FOR IMPLEMENTATION OF SBDD

The compound database preparation containing small molecules is frequently accessible via synthesis or purchase, which owns necessary characteristics like solubility, stability in aqueous media, presence of apposite functional groups for the interaction with the target bioactive compounds, and the absence of noxious and unwanted moieties. Various rules have been applied for ensuring the “likeliness of the drug” with the “Lipinski's rule of five” [24] which states that the drug compound should have a molecular weight less than 500, log P value which is the lipophilicity to be lesser than 5, must possess less than five hydrogen bond donors and less than 10 hydrogen bond acceptors. Further, ADMET studies which is an amalgamation of selecting the chosen pharmaceutical distribution, absorption, excretion, metabolism, and toxicologic properties which is clearly advised in the earlier stages of the drug design process. In order to efficiently screen the collection of compounds against the said criteria, various online servers have been technologically evolved.

Chembioserver is one of the publicly available online applications which aids in the filtering and assortment of molecules which are smaller in size [25]. The aim of this online application is in the preparation of the component by utilizing the main segments of it including basic search, filtration on the basis of toxicity, advance filtering, clustering, modified pipeline, and use of the property graphs for the visualization of the properties of the compound which will thus upsurge the efficacy and the compound’s quality that proceeds to in vitro assays. The FAF-Drugs2 server is a tool which is publicly available for filtering the compounds computationally by keeping in view the physiochemical properties and identifying the vital functional and unwanted moieties [26].
