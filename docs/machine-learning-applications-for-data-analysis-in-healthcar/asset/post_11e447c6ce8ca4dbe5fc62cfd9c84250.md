## 4.3 CHALLENGES

- Other body sounds, such as muscle activity, were difficult to distinguish from articular cartilage sounds. Knee radiographic images are extremely sensitive to unintended defects, which can complicate the study of bone structures. It is possible that the experts will take more time to analyze the knee X-ray and determine the presence of OA.

- Hand-drawn contours and joint area cropping are used for the region of interest segmentation, which is tedious and time-consuming.

- The primary cause of geometric distortions of the cartilage region in knee X-ray images is the progression of OA, which may be misrepresented due to image acquisition filming, handling, and digitization.

- Extraction of significant regions from such distorted images may become difficult.

- It is also difficult to recover the relevant region when images are distorted due to geometric deformation.

- Current imaging methods, such as X-ray, MRI, and ultrasound, have low sensitivity in early disease, and as a result, OA is already advanced at the time of diagnosis, and understanding of its cause and progression is still limited.

## 4.4 PROBLEM STATEMENT

OA is one of the most common chronic joint diseases, with a large number of risk factors that are directly linked to it. When compared to other types of OA, KOA is the most common and occurs most frequently in people over the age of 55, with the disease’s prevalence increasing as people reach the age of 65. KOA is difficult to diagnose in the early stages of the disease. To address this issue, we are developing an ensemble approach early detection deep learning-based system that will use an X-ray/MRI dataset of KOA patients to provide more accurate results than previous work.

## 4.5 PROPOSED METHOD

The prevalence of OA is increasing, which is partly due to our aging population. OA is currently diagnosed using patient-reported symptoms and X-rays. The alternative is an MRI, but this comes at a high cost and is rarely used until symptoms worsen and patients are referred for a surgical opinion from a specialist. As a result, effective management and early detection of KOA is a critical public health issue that concerns the general public as well as a variety of clinicians and healthcare executives.

Many researchers have attempted to diagnose OA using X-rays and patient medical data, but the results have been inconsistent. Through this proposed idea/work, we will design and implement an ensemble methodology for early detection of KOA using a hybrid dataset of X-ray and medical data of patients through which doctors can predict the OA and its level more accurately and in less time through this proposed idea/work.
