
As Andrew Beck, Director of Bioinformatics at the Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center Cancer Research Institute, points out: “Determining the presence or absence of metastatic disease in a patient’s lymph nodes is a routine yet critically important task for pathologists. Examining thousands of normal cells under a microscope to identify just a few malignant ones can be extremely tedious and challenging using traditional methods.” That is the reason Beck and his group constructed a mechanized symptomatic instrument utilizing a profound learning calculation prepared to separate among destructive and noncancerous cells. In a 2016 assessment, a digital diagnostic tool achieved a success rate of 92%, only four percentage points lower than that of human pathologists. Also, when Beck’s group consolidated human pathologists’ investigations with the examinations of the mechanized apparatus, the indicative achievement rate increased to a noteworthy 99.5%. Ultimately, Beck believes that his analysis only begins to scratch the surface of what a hybrid approach—combining human expertise and algorithmic methods—can offer to clinical diagnostics. He states, “Our results show that what the computer is doing is quite intelligent, and that the combination of human and machine interpretations will lead to more accurate and clinically meaningful diagnoses to guide treatment decisions.”

## 6.2.4 IMPROVING HEALTHCARE MARKETING

At the point when a patient has been examined, the accompanying stage is to find a treatment that will fix or potentially assuage the effects of their condition. Healthcare professionals (HCPs) clearly have a significant influence over which supportive regimen a patient adopts; however, the importance of direct-to-patient communication—such as asking about Drug X—should not be underestimated. Unfortunately, the pharmaceutical industry often ends up speaking past its core audiences. In fact, one study found that up to 45% of patients believe that drug companies do not understand their real needs. Much like the challenges faced in everyday clinical diagnostics, this disconnect is primarily a matter of scale. In the old age, monitoring patient behavior is the first step toward delivering relevant, personalized guidance. This involves collecting information from a wide range of datasets, including clinical data—such as hospital records, lab results, and healthcare provider notes—as well as general data like media preferences, online activity, and demographic information. Healthcare marketers must then extract meaningful narratives and insights from the data they have gathered—“connecting the dots,” so to speak—not just once, but continuously throughout the course of a campaign. Effectively executing such a data-driven strategy at scale demands an extraordinary level of computing power. Even the most skilled and experienced marketing teams are not capable of organizing and analyzing the vast volume of data points required for effective patient targeting. This is where AI-driven predictive analytics tools become invaluable. By leveraging well-trained predictive algorithms, marketing teams can gain deep, data-driven insights into their target patient populations. These insights move beyond generalized segmentation, enabling communication strategies based on nuanced analyses of patients’ past behaviors, personal characteristics, and current positions in the care journey—patterns often too complex or subtle for the human eye or mind to detect [11].

## 6.2.5 REDUCING MEDICATION NONADHERENCE

Between 1988 and 1994, approximately 38% of adults in the United States were taking at least one prescription medication. Over the following two decades, this figure rose to 49%, largely driven by a 100% increase in the number of adults taking three or more prescribed drugs. According to research presented to the American Hospital Association in October 2016, total annual spending on prescription medications had reached $309.5 billion, making it the fastest-growing component of the US healthcare economy. Alarmingly, a significant portion of this spending is wasted. It is estimated that up to 50% of the 3.2 billion prescriptions written each year in the United States are not taken as directed—if they are taken at all. Medication nonadherence results in over $250 billion in unnecessary healthcare costs annually, accounting for approximately 13% of the United States’ total yearly healthcare expenditure. Just as pharmaceutical companies use algorithmic tools to enhance patient targeting, healthcare providers (HCPs) can leverage similar AI-based systems to address this issue by identifying patients most at risk of nonadherence. Factors such as socioeconomic status, insurance coverage, out-of-pocket costs, and the prescribing physician’s specialty all influence a patient’s likelihood of deviating from their treatment regimen. AI algorithms offer a powerful solution by analyzing these complex variables to help HCPs proactively support patients who may need additional intervention to stay on track with their prescribed therapies. Outfitted with the insightful yields of such structures, HCPs can pinpoint which patients need additional assistance to keep focused with their treatment. In reality, improved and more focused communication is not, by itself, a comprehensive solution to the problem of medication nonadherence, nonetheless, research dispersed in clinical care suggests that poor HCP-patient correspondence achieves a 19% higher threat of nonadherence.

## 6.2.6 VIRTUAL HEALTH ASSISTANT

Virtual health assistants (VHAs) can proactively help patients differently, for one, VHAs can support dementia patients by providing medication reminders, helping them stay on track with their prescribed treatments. Additionally, VHAs can offer guidance on managing common illnesses and provide personalized dietary recommendations tailored to patients' specific health needs. VHAs can also monitor patients using data-driven insights, enabling healthcare professionals to engage with both patients and pharmacies by sending reminders for medication refills and pickups. They can even recommend preventive health screenings. AI has also been employed to enhance clinical diagnostics—for instance, AI-assisted medical imaging from the Beijing-based company Infervision is currently used to improve the interpretation of CT scans and X-rays. Virtual Health Assistants represent a fusion of artificial intelligence and advanced decision-support systems, leveraging big data, NLP, and voice recognition technologies. These features contribute significantly to their ability to effectively engage and assist patients. Voice-empowered VHA will change the conveyance of medical services to patients through [9]:

- Navigate electronic health records
- Navigate diagnosis and prescription options
- Improve access to data health records
- Improve patient discharge and follow-up treatments
- Improve communication between providers

Coming up next are five different ways how VHA advances can upgrade patient commitment inside medical services associations.

### 6.2.6.1 AUTOMATED CALLING SYSTEMS AND INTERACTIVE VOICE RESPONSE SYSTEMS (IVRS)

VHAs are increasingly being used to monitor patients before and after treatment. By receiving text and voice confirmations of upcoming appointments, VHAs help reduce cancellations and minimize outstanding bills. In clinical settings, healthcare organizations are leveraging Voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP) technologies alongside VHAs, driven by the growing adoption of AI, deep learning, NLP, and speech recognition applications on mobile devices.

### 6.2.6.2 M-HEALTH APPS

Healthcare providers are encouraging patients to use smart devices to monitor their wellness progress, make payments, and manage many aspects of their daily routines. Some health applications available on platforms like Amazon Echo, equipped with VHAs, prompt patients to use their smartphones to request prescription refills, confirm appointments, track health metrics, and complete forms prior to visits. Wearable fitness devices, such as Fitbit, allow patients to monitor vital signs like heart rate and blood pressure. These health parameters are uploaded to patient portals, which can be easily accessed through virtual assistants.

### 6.2.6.3 PATIENT PORTALS AND HEALTH KIOSKS

The adoption of patient health portals continues to grow at a modest pace. Ideally, these online portals—enhanced with virtual assistants—would serve as centralized platforms where patients can confirm appointments, complete medical and social history forms, update health records, view lab results, and settle outstanding balances using voice and text recognition technologies. Kiosks also hold significant potential for collecting patient information through integrated VoIP systems. Although such technologies are available in various forms, the healthcare IT infrastructure must still address key concerns related to data security and the integrity of collected information.
