# 6.4.3 PRIVACY CONCERNS

Another set of risks arises around patient privacy. The need for large datasets creates incentives for developers to collect extensive data from various patients, which can raise concerns about potential privacy violations. In fact, legal cases have already been filed over data-sharing practices between major healthcare systems and AI developers [5]. AI also poses unique privacy challenges: it can infer sensitive personal information that was never explicitly provided. For instance, an AI system might detect early signs of Parkinson's disease based on subtle tremors in a user's computer mouse movements—even if the individual never disclosed this information. Patients may view such inferences as breaches of privacy, particularly if those predictions become accessible to third parties such as banks or life insurance companies.

# 6.4.4 BIAS AND INEQUALITY

There are significant risks of bias and disparity in healthcare AI systems. These systems learn from the data on which they are trained, and if that data reflects existing biases, the AI can perpetuate or even amplify them.
