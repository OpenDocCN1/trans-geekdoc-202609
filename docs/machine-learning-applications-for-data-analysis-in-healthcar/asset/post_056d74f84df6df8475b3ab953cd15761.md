### ABSTRACT

People experience significant health discomfort as a result of rheumatoid arthritis (RA) and osteoarthritis (OA). ### 4.1 INTRODUCTION

Artificial intelligence (AI) computing has altered the dynamics of development and is playing an important role in improving people’s quality of life. Healthcare solutions are one such domain where the scope of application for AI has become an urgent need. There are distinct sets of solutions where AI's role is prominent, ranging from formulation planning to research to clinical care.

Predictive modeling solutions are becoming more constructive for some of the current AI applications in the healthcare domain, from diagnosis to treatment care, and they help improve the accuracy of diagnosis and treatment. Rheumatic or musculoskeletal conditions cause more than 150 diseases, and the syndrome is usually progressive and painful for those who suffer from it. Rheumatic conditions can be divided into three categories: joint diseases, spinal disorders, and conditions that lead to traumatic conditions. In general, such rheumatic conditions cause morbidity and disability in certain conditions, as well as significant healthcare expenditures [1].

In terms of the dynamics of applying machine learning (ML) models, one of the key areas of healthcare where the use of ML models can be seen as essential is the accurate prediction of rheumatoid arthritis (RA).

RA is a chronic systemic disease that affects joints, fibrous tissues, connective tissues, and other tissues, according to a brief overview of the RA and osteoarthritis (OA) outline. It primarily affects people between the ages of 20 and 40, during their most productive years of adulthood. More often than not, if undiagnosed for an extended period of time, it can impair the healthy movement of people with RA [2].

OA is a degenerative joint disease that primarily affects the articular cartilage. More often than not, the problem manifests itself in the cases of aged people and physically stressed people as a result of excessive physical work. According to statistics, OA is one of the 10 most disabling diseases in developed countries [3, 4].

The fact remains that there are various types of arthritis, with OA and RA being two of the most common. Because the symptoms of both types of arthritis discussed are similar, there is a need for more accurate and appropriate treatment to decide on the best treatment to address the issues [5].

Knee osteoarthritis (KOA) is a condition of the human knee joint that primarily affects the cartilage. Cartilage plays an important role in leg movement. The top layer of cartilage disintegrates and deteriorates in OA, resulting in excruciating pain. OA is a chronic joint disease that causes pain, swelling, stiffness, and loss of function. OA is a chronic disease whose origin is unknown.

It is the most common type of arthritis and is distinguished by cartilage degradation and bone changes [30]. It is difficult to diagnose OA in its early stages because it has been suggested that the first changes occur in the subchondral bone [31] before the occurrence of joint space narrowing and osteophytes. As a result, early detection, diagnosis, and intervention are highly desired in order to overcome this severely disabling disease, as early-stage treatment could prevent cartilage and bone breakdown.

Thus, focusing on the implications of similarities and the potential scope of human errors being significant to the problem, there is a need for a strong model that can assist healthcare professionals in having more accurate diagnostics and an effective system in place for quick turnaround analysis with the appropriate type of detection for the type of arthritis. As a result, the focus of this chapter is on providing a detailed review of the current solutions or models used in ML and non-ML practices that could be useful in developing a more appropriate type of system [6].

The focus of the other sections of this chapter is on understanding the literature's possible set of solutions for using ML models for arthritis form detection.
