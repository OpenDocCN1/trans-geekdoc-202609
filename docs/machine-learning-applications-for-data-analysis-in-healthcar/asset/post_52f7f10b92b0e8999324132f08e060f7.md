### 8.4.1 HEALTHCARE SUPPLY CHAIN MANAGEMENT

Healthcare supply chain management ensures that hospitals, clinics, and pharmacies have the right things at the right time. It involves lots of steps, like ordering, storing, and delivering medical stuff, while making sure everything is safe and of good quality. Various kinds of integration that define health supply chains are as follows:

- Process coordination and integration.
- Coordinating and integrating information flow.
- The blending and coordinating of planning procedures.
- The blending of internal and external organizational processes.
- Integration of a market-based strategy.
- Market development integration.

In the administration of the healthcare supply chain, blockchain transactions are a crucial monitoring tool for the entire process of moving medicines and medical products. Due to the fact that every transaction is recorded on the ledger and updated by every node in the blockchain, it is easy to instantly validate the drug’s origin, the vendor, and the distributor. Additionally, the distributed ledger of the blockchain enables officials and medical professionals to confirm and validate the credentials of providers. In this sense, the development of a reliable vendor network that enables healthcare managers to protect patients from shady vendors shows significant promise for blockchain technology. Furthermore, blockchain technology promises to significantly improve transactional security, data provenance, demand forecasting, and fraud prevention. ### 8.4.1.1 TRACEABILITY OF DRUGS

- Step 1: When a new treatment or medication is developed, a process begins involving patent protection and extensive clinical testing. These data are recorded in a digital ledger, serving as a transaction entry.
- Step 2: After completing clinical trials, the manufacturing facility obtains a patent for prototype testing and large-scale production. Each product receives a unique identification linked to additional blockchain data.
- Step 3: Post mass production and packaging, medicines are stored in warehouses for future distribution. The blockchain records details like timestamps, lot numbers, barcodes, and expiry dates.
- Step 4: Transportation specifics, such as travel duration between warehouses (in), mode of transport, authorized agents, and related particulars, are also logged in the blockchain.
- Step 5: Third-party distribution networks transport drugs and medical supplies to healthcare providers or retailers. Utilizing in and out warehouses linked to all distribution endpoints, blockchain updates with new transactions.
- Step 6: Healthcare facilities like hospitals and clinics input data (batch and lot numbers, product owners, and expiry dates) into the blockchain to authenticate and prevent counterfeiting.
