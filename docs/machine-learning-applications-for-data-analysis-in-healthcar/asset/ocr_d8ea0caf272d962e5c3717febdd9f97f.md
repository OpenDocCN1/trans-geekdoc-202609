Those who are elderly or disabled can receive support at home using smart homes. These are specialized homes or flats that monitor the tenants’ bodily indications and surroundings using sensors and actuators that are incorporated into the residential infrastructure of the building. The activities that can be carried out also contribute to an enhanced quality of life. About 54% doctors suggested that home automation and health monitoring are the two primary facets that make up the function that smart homes play in the healthcare industry. People who require care are now able to become less reliant on healthcare professionals while simultaneously improving the quality of their lives at home thanks to the ability of these technologies to perform some basic services while simultaneously gathering health data (83%).

Apps and a health information platform enable patients to take charge of their own healthcare and self-manage their conditions.

The traditional method of determining the likelihood of an individual contracting a disease is collecting relevant data, comparing the information with the regulatory state health guidelines, and then diagnosing accordingly. This approach is time-consuming, requires monitoring constantly, and fails to give accurate and precise findings. In the context of smart healthcare, disease risk prediction is reported to be both dynamic and individualized. Around 67% said that it makes it possible for patients and their doctors to take part, to proactively monitor their illness risk, and to engage in preventative efforts that are tailored according to the findings of their own monitoring. The latest model for predicting disease risk collects data using wearable watches (e.g., Apple, Mi) and through interconnected apps, data are stored onto the cloud, and then results are analyzed using neural networks and algorithms (72%).
