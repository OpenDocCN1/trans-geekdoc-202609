# 12.1 INTRODUCTION

The finger knuckle print (FKP) image is an inherent skin pattern available at the backside ofthe finger joint, recently this pattern has been quite popular and attracts the biometric research community due to its high acceptability and reliability [1]. The popularity gained by FKP is not only the cause of acceptability and ease of use, apart from that, it is not rugged like a fingerprint and easily can be captured using a normal resolution imaging device [2].

Woodard and Flynn [3] are the first scholars to make use of the finger knuckle surface to recognize a person. They used the Minolta 900/910 sensor to create a 3D finger back database. This sensor simultaneously captures and register image into size 640 × 480 and 640 × 480 of 24-bit color intensity, respectively. The sensor measures 213 mm × 413 mm × 270 mm and weighs around 11 kg. Due to its large size and weight, it is not suitable for commercial use of the biometric system. At the time of data collection, the sensor is placed on a flat wall at positioned approximately 1.3 m and is covered with a black cloth. Before the data collection was conducted, the subject was asked to remove all jewelry to ensure that the sensor does notscatter when in contact with the reflective surface of the jewelry. During the experiment, thesubjects were asked to place their right hand against the wall and keep it there as the image is captured. After that, they remove their hand from the wall and return it to the same position. The researchers collected a total of 1191 hand range images which are available publicly. In this image one is taken a sample of hand color images of size 640 × 480 pixels.
