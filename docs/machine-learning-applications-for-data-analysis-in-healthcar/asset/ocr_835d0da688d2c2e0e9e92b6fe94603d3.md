French geophysicist Jean Morlet introduced the Wavelet in 1982.

The term “Wavelet” refers to a little wave, and research into the wavelet transform is a new method for analyzing seismic signals.

Alex Grossmann theoreticians studied the wavelet transform inverse formula [28].

An analytical tool for transitory, nonstationary, or time-varying signals,
a wavelet is a tiny wave with energy concentrated in time [31].

There are many different wavelets that can be used in a wide range of
situations. Some things about them that make them helpful are as follows:

+   • Wavelets have a confined frequency and temporal domain.
+   • Nonstationary signals like ECG exhibit frequent level changes
+   and uneven characteristics.
+   • Wavelets split signals into multiresolution components.

A time-scale representation known as the wavelet transform has proven
useful in several applications, most notably signal compression. The signal
is divided into several scales related to frequency components, and each
scale is subjected to a linear process that examines it with a specific resolu-
tion [32]. Another benefit of the wavelet technique is the availability of a
variety of wavelet functions, which allows choosing the optimal function
for analyzing the signal as opposed to Fourier analysis, which is limited to
one feature morphology, the sinusoid.
