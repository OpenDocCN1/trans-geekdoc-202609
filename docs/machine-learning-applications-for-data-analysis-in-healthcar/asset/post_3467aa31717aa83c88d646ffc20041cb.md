
### 6.2.6.4 DISCRETE DATA

VHAs can interact with patients and digitally capture their information, which can then be validated and updated in real time using cloud technologies and IoT-connected devices. When a patient engages with a virtual assistant, queries to a pharmacy hub can be cross-checked against existing records in the facility’s database. Once all information is verified, it is seamlessly processed and integrated into the EHR system—eliminating the need for paper-based documentation.

### 6.2.6.5 ANYTIME ACCESS FROM ANYWHERE

For some individuals in the maturing populace, it is undeniably more viable to have verbal correspondence alongside visual collaboration, rather than a call or electronic correspondence. VHAs can be accessed through websites, smartphones, tablets, and computers. These systems are capable of recognizing returning patients and recalling recent interactions [10].

### 6.2.7 HEALTHCARE BOTS

Healthcare bots are primarily designed to support patient engagement and accountability. These bots, often integrated into adaptive learning applications, can assist patients by delivering targeted messages and responding to health-related inquiries. They also help manage medications by providing information about drug types and recommended dosages. Recent advancements in healthcare bots include the ability to learn and mimic human conversations, detect emotions to enable empathetic interactions, and incorporate technologies such as natural language processing, sentiment analysis, and intent mining into chatbot scripts. Some bots can even perform complex image recognition tasks to analyze medical images, handwritten notes, and standardized identifiers. Beyond chatbots, other AI applications in healthcare are being developed to assess heart sounds, provide companion robots for the elderly, mine medical records, design treatment plans, assist with repetitive tasks, conduct consultations, aid in drug development, and use visual data for medical training.

## 6.3 POTENTIAL BENEFITS

AI is rapidly making its way into healthcare, playing significant roles—from automating repetitive and routine tasks in clinical practice to managing patients and medical resources. As developers design AI systems to handle these responsibilities, several risks and challenges arise. These include potential harm to patients from system errors, threats to data privacy, issues related to AI accountability, and more. Addressing these concerns requires complex solutions, including the development of better evaluation frameworks, representative datasets, community-driven oversight by regulatory bodies like the Food and Drug Administration, and reforms in medical education to prepare providers for evolving roles in a technology-driven environment. Although still an emerging field, AI is poised to play at least four major roles within the healthcare system [1].

### 6.3.1 BREAKING THE BOUNDARIES OF HUMAN CAPABILITIES

The most significant use of clinical AI is to do things that even the most prominent healthcare providers cannot. For example, Google Health is developing a program capable of predicting severe kidney damage up to two days in advance. This early warning system contrasts with current clinical practices, which often detect such damage only after it has occurred [2]. By identifying risks earlier, this AI-driven approach has the potential to significantly enhance patient care—surpassing what is typically achievable through conventional observation alone.

### 6.3.2 DEMOCRATIZING MEDICAL KNOWLEDGE AND EXCELLENCE

AI can also help extend the expertise and performance of specialists to providers who may otherwise lack that level of proficiency. Fields such as ophthalmology and radiology have become key areas of focus, particularly because AI-based image analysis has seen significant advancement in these domains. Some systems can analyze images of the eye to provide diagnostic insights that would traditionally require an ophthalmologist. With these tools, general practitioners—or even patients—can reach similar conclusions. This kind of democratization is crucial, especially given the shortage of highly skilled specialists in many regions [3].

### 6.3.3 THE MONOTONIC AI OF AUTOMATED MEDICAL PRACTICE

AI can automate many PC operations that now require a lot of clinical practice. Suppliers have invested a lot of energy to track electronic medical records, receive display, and perform operations on the console, even in the test room [4]. When AI systems organize key information into patient records, and facilitate documentation, narration, and coordination of data, they can significantly reduce the administrative burden on providers. This not only saves time but also enhances the provider–patient relationship by allowing more focus on meaningful clinical interactions. Ultimately, it improves the quality of care and the overall clinical experience for both parties.

### 6.3.4 PATIENT AND MEDICAL RESOURCE MANAGEMENT

Finally, AI often operates behind the scenes, yet it plays a powerful role in resource allocation and shaping healthcare operations. For example, AI systems can predict which tasks may require additional support staff, identify patients who could benefit most from limited clinical resources, or—even more controversially—optimize billing strategies to align with favorable reimbursement models.

### 6.3.5 AI FACILITATES ACCESS TO HEALTHCARE

Unfortunately, many underdeveloped countries still lag behind global technological progress in many areas, and healthcare remains their biggest challenge. People living in the most remote areas are at greater risk of being left behind, as it is difficult for them to access and manage the full range of healthcare services and technological advancements. WHO claims that lopsided admittance to wellbeing administrations brings about an 18.1-year hole in future between the most extravagant and least fortunate nations. AI innovations can play a crucial role in building an efficient and responsive healthcare ecosystem. Such digital systems help patients understand their symptoms and access appropriate treatment. In recent years, numerous applications have been developed to enhance collaboration between national and international healthcare organizations, enabling faster assistance to people in need. For instance, the Ada app, powered by intelligent technology, is available in over 140 countries and expands access to medical guidance for vulnerable populations. Notably, Ada played a significant role during global pandemics by enabling individuals worldwide to conduct self-assessments for infectious diseases, improving early detection and response.

### 6.3.6 AI TOOLS HELP IDENTIFY DISEASE RISKS EARLY

Because AI can collect and store information about people in quarantine locations, it can use that data to view past and current illnesses. This correlation of infection complexity allows clinicians to draw more accurate conclusions. The application has arranged various indications, results, and specific cases, all of which take into account the anticipation of a person’s future diseases. A notable application developed by Verily, a subsidiary of Google, specializes in diagnosing both noncontiguous and inherited genetic disorders. Such applications empower wellbeing specialists to expect possible dangers and keep away from them later on, taking proper measures today. Additionally, prescient examination improves the operational administration of medical care offices.

Another great advantage of using AI in clinical examination systems is that it reduces the time and effort required to analyze and diagnose patients. Identifying biomarkers that signal the presence of certain diseases is critical to early diagnosis and treatment. Advanced computing enables the automation of much of the manual work involved in biomarker detection, significantly reducing the time required—potentially saving lives. Calculations are more useful than traditional cost estimation methods. As these technological advances are integrated into healthcare systems, patients may no longer need to undergo costly laboratory tests.
