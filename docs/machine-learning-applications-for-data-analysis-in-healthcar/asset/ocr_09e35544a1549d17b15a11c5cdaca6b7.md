# 10.1 INTRODUCTION

Electrocardiogram (ECG) measures heart activity in the electrical form. It is a visual representation of the biopotential's variation throughout time. Leads are attached to certain parts in body to record (ECG) on the graph of the chapter or a computer monitor, depending on the application. Human heart has four chambers: right, left atrium, left, and right ventricular [1, 2]. The upper two chambers are atria, and the bottom two are ventricles. The sino atria node in the right atrium, where the heartbeat normally starts, is responsible for transmitting electrical signals across the heart. The atrio-ventricular (AV) node receives this signal as it travels from the atria [3–5]. The lower chamber’s ventricles, which contain a network of fibers that channel electricity and convey impulses to all of its parts, are connected to the AV node.
