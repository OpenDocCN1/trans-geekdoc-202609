# ABSTRACT

Sophistications in scientific domains have repeatedly highlighted the challenges in the drug discovery and designing pathways. Deciphering the significant lead molecules, which have potential to be effective therapeutic molecules, take time of 10–14 years and the average cost of 500–650 US million dollars when experimental techniques are deployed for drug discovery processes. In order to reduce time span, and accelerate the cost-effective approaches with high accuracy for finding novel lead molecules, computer-aided drug designing methods have been developed which make the process of hit to lead identification much easier. In this chapter, we will highlight various computational approaches, advancements in these approaches, algorithms involved in target optimization, validation and molecular docking, 2D and 3D QSAR for computer-aided drug designing. Further, we will review the inhibitors of protein–protein interactions and its design through the implementation of the pharmacophore computationally which will present its role in the discovery of the drugs.
