## ABSTRACT

The real challenge posed to in-hospital mortality study is from heterogeneous data from nonhomogeneous class of patients. This motivates the study, which uses traditional machine learning (ML) algorithms to analyze the heterogeneous data containing 48 features and classify in-hospital mortality. The retrospective cohort was focused on heart failure patients. Twelve ML algorithms have been deployed on a publicly available dataset for binary classification. The dataset used for the study was highly resource constrained and unbalanced; hence a novel data preprocessing unit has been designed. In addition to standard implementations, hyperparameter tuning and stacked implementations have also been done. Post cross-validation, the accuracy value observed was 95.45% for histogram-based Gradient Boost algorithm. Hyper-parameter-tuned model accuracy was observed at 96.55% for the Gradient Boost classifier. Accuracy for the stacked model was 96.43%, with an ROC-AUC score of 0.977, a recall value of 0.964, and an F1-Score of 0.977. The false negative share for the stacked model was at 0.56%. Since mortality analysis is the subject of study, we claim that the false negative value validates the robustness of the classification. The performance of all the ML algorithms has been compared with the existing literature.

## 1.1 INTRODUCTION

Heart failure is a complicated clinical ailment resulting in hindrance of ventricular filling or emission of blood to the systemic circulation due to structural or functional heart disorder. By definition, it is failure to meet the systematized demands of circulation. It implies that there is some anomaly present in the systolic or diastolic phases of the heart which is hampering the heart's performance and in turn it can lead to huge consequences later on. It has affected many people globally and the death rate associated with it is increasing day by day. It has an estimated ubiquity of around 26 million people globally and it contributes to an escalated healthcare cost worldwide. Heart failure is a common, expensive, and potentially lethal condition having a mortality rate of around 4–7% [1], and it is one of the major causes of both hospitalization and readmission. The heart-failure rate in adults and senior citizens is around 2–3% and 6–10%, respectively, and is bound to increase in the coming years. Also, the death rate is about 35% after the diagnosis of heart disease after one year.
