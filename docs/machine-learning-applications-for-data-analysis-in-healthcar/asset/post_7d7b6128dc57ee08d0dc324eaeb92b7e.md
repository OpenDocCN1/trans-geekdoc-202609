## 6.4 CHALLENGES AND RISK

While AI offers different expected benefits, there, furthermore, are a couple of risks.

### 6.4.1 INJURIES AND ERROR

A significant risk associated with AI in healthcare is the potential for errors that could lead to patient harm or other medical complications. For example, if an AI system recommends an inappropriate medication, fails to detect a tumor in a radiological scan, or incorrectly prioritizes patients for emergency care, the consequences can be severe. While medical errors already occur in today's healthcare systems without the involvement of AI, mistakes made by AI may raise unique concerns.

First, patients and healthcare providers may respond differently to injuries caused by software compared to those resulting from human error. Second, as AI systems become more widely adopted, a critical flaw in a single system could potentially harm a large number of patients simultaneously—unlike the more limited scope of harm caused by individual provider mistakes.

### 6.4.2 DATA AVAILABILITY

Preparing AI frameworks requires a lot of information from sources, for example, electronic wellbeing records, drug store records, protection claims records, or purchaser created data like wellness trackers or buying history; yet, wellbeing information is regularly divided across various frameworks. Indeed, even beside the assortment just referenced, patients commonly see various suppliers and switch insurance agencies, prompting information split into different frameworks and numerous configurations. This fragmentation increases the risk of errors, reduces the scope of available datasets, and raises the cost of data collection—further limiting the types of organizations that can effectively develop healthcare AI solutions.

### 6.4.3 PRIVACY CONCERNS

Another set of risks arises around patient privacy. The need for large datasets creates incentives for developers to collect extensive data from various patients, which can raise concerns about potential privacy violations. In fact, legal cases have already been filed over data-sharing practices between major healthcare systems and AI developers [5]. AI also poses unique privacy challenges: it can infer sensitive personal information that was never explicitly provided. For instance, an AI system might detect early signs of Parkinson's disease based on subtle tremors in a user's computer mouse movements—even if the individual never disclosed this information. Patients may view such inferences as breaches of privacy, particularly if those predictions become accessible to third parties such as banks or life insurance companies.

### 6.4.4 BIAS AND INEQUALITY

There are significant risks of bias and disparity in healthcare AI systems. These systems learn from the data on which they are trained, and if that data reflects existing biases, the AI can perpetuate or even amplify them. For example, if most of the training data comes from academic medical centers, the resulting AI may be less effective at treating patients from populations that rarely access such institutions.
