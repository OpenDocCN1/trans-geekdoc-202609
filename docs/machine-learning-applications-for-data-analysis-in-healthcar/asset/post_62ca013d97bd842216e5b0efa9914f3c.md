### ABSTRACT

The human being has several biometric traits that can be used to identify a person such as fingerprint, finger vein, hand vein, hand geometry, face, iris, finger knuckle print, palm print, ear, gait, retina, and so on but the hand-based biometric system is highly acceptable due to easy acquisition, ease-to-use, and leading recognition performance. The fingerprint-based biometric system is an older one and is often used type for person recognition/identification. Apart from various advantages, the fingerprint trait has three major drawbacks. First, the fingerprint ridges are rugged in the hard-working people like cultivator, industrial labor, machine mechanics, rickshaw pooler, and so on. Second, the skin elasticity is not constant throughout the seasons; and third, applied thumb pressure is not always the same, cause dispersion of thumb skin is varied, resulting the extracted feature sets are also varied. Hence, intraclass variation occurs in the matching score and system performance degrades. The biometric domain researchers consider the above issues about the fingerprint biometric and nowadays person identification using finger knuckle print biometric is very popular among the biometric research community. This chapter presents an overview of this emerging biometric and available recognition methodology to identify people.

### 12.1 INTRODUCTION

The finger knuckle print (FKP) image is an inherent skin pattern available at the backside of the finger joint, recently this pattern has been quite popular and attracts the biometric research community due to its high acceptability and reliability [1]. The popularity gained by FKP is not only the cause of acceptability and ease of use, apart from that, it is not rugged like a fingerprint and easily can be captured using a normal resolution imaging device [2].

Woodard and Flynn [3] are the first scholars to make use of the finger knuckle surface to recognize a person. They used the Minolta 900/910 sensor to create a 3D finger back database. This sensor simultaneously captures and register image into size 640 × 480 and 640 × 480 of 24-bit color intensity, respectively. The sensor measures 213 mm × 413 mm × 270 mm and weighs around 11 kg. Due to its large size and weight, it is not suitable for commercial use of the biometric system. At the time of data collection, the sensor is placed on a flat wall at positioned approximately 1.3 m and is covered with a black cloth. Before the data collection was conducted, the subject was asked to remove all jewelry to ensure that the sensor does not scatter when in contact with the reflective surface of the jewelry. During the experiment, the subjects were asked to place their right hand against the wall and keep it there as the image is captured. After that, they remove their hand from the wall and return it to the same position. The researchers collected a total of 1191 hand range images which are available publicly. In this image one is taken a sample of hand color images of size 640 × 480 pixels. Second image taken as a pseudo intensity of the same hand image of size 640 × 480 using a polygonal mesh and third image is a surface detail detected near a knuckle surface representation. The normalized correlation coefficient was then used to compare the similarity of the images.

In 2009, Kumar and Ravikanth [4] developed a system using a digital camera to acquire finger-back surface images. The imaging system is focused against a white background under uniform illumination and the camera was fixed at a 20-cm distance from the imaging surface. The nonuniform illumination of objects at the hand boundaries reduces the performance of the acquired image. This is achieved by using a fixed light source above the hand. The resolution was 1600 × 200 pixels of the acquired FKP image. The subject is asked to place their hand on the support with the backhand facing the sensor.

There is a six-finger geometry and each has four fingers, resulting in 24 fingers being used to extract features. The features include finger length, widths, perimeter, and area along with the knuckle feature. The knuckle feature is extracted by applying the subspace analysis method on the finger back surface and using these features for person classification. Although these methods were able to identify the unique features of the outer finger surface, they were not able to provide a practical solution for implementing an efficient system.

The proposed method [3] focuses on the 3D shape information of FKP and not fully on the texture information while subspace analysis methods used in Ref. [4] may not be able to extract efficient features of FKP.
