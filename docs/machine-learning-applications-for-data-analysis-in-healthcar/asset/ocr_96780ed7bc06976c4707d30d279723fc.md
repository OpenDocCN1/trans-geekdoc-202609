# 6.2.6.1 AUTOMATED CALLING SYSTEMS AND INTERACTIVE VOICE RESPONSE SYSTEMS (IVRS)

VHAs are increasingly being used to monitor patients before and after treatment. By receiving text and voice confirmations of upcoming appointments, VHAs help reduce cancellations and minimize outstanding bills. In clinical settings, healthcare organizations are leveraging Voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP) technologies alongside VHAs, driven by the growing adoption of AI, deep learning, NLP, and speech recognition applications on mobile devices.

# 6.2.6.2 M-HEALTH APPS

Healthcare providers are encouraging patients to use smart devices to monitor their wellness progress, make payments, and manage many aspects of their daily routines. Some health applications available on platforms like Amazon Echo, equipped with VHAs, prompt patients to use their smartphones to request prescription refills, confirm appointments, track health metrics, and complete forms prior to visits. Wearable fitness devices, such as Fitbit, allow patients to monitor vital signs like heart rate and blood pressure. These health parameters are uploaded to patient portals, which can be easily accessed through virtual assistants.

# 6.2.6.3 PATIENT PORTALS AND HEALTH KIOSKS

The adoption of patient health portals continues to grow at a modest pace. Ideally, these online portals—enhanced with virtual assistants—would serve as centralized platforms where patients can confirm appointments, complete medical and social history forms, update health records, view lab results, and settle outstanding balances using voice and text recognition technologies. Kiosks also hold significant potential for collecting patient information through integrated VoIP systems. Although such technologies are available in various forms, the healthcare IT infrastructure must still address key concerns related to data security and the integrity of collected information.
