## 9.4 ANALYSIS AND THE ROAD AHEAD

The advent of intelligent healthcare has resulted in the development of fully developed ideas and structures. Despite this, there is still a significant amount of potential for improvement, and many new difficulties are currently coming into play because of the introduction of new technologies and new problems. Now, there is a lack of macrodirection and programmatic guidelines in the field of smart healthcare. This results in ambiguous development goals, which eventually leads to a waste of resources. In addition, medical institutions do not have similar standards across different locations and different organizations, and there is a need for changes in the process of ensuring that data integrity is maintained [2]. Because of the sheer volume and complexity of the data, both the exchange of information and the transmission of messages can be challenging. Compatibility issues between the various operating systems and hardware platforms are another source of frustration. The patient feels smart healthcare is lacking in applicable legal norms, and there are dangers around the disclosure of personal information and breaches of privacy [5]. Even for some experienced users, these technologies might be challenging. Some technological advances linked with astute healthcare are still in the exploratory stages from a technological perspective and they ask for a significant amount of financing to be maintained and updated. If it is used carelessly, there is also a possibility of undiscovered danger [7].

Therefore, if we wish to receive answers to the previously identified challenges, we must focus on two distinct factors: regulations and innovative thinking. For a start, the process of associated technologies reaching maturity and stability may gain impetus by implementing updates. Improving one’s capacity to derive meaning from massive amounts of data is likewise an essential goal. The establishment of a common technological standard as a means of achieving the highest possible level of interoperability between various platforms and devices is the second key step. It will be possible to increase data integrity and remove obstacles to the sharing of information using this method. Lastly, the utilization of methods such as blockchain is going to be necessary to guarantee the highest level of data security and transmission stability. Regarding the issue of regulation, experts from the many sectors that are pertinent to the topic might collaborate to define the growth goals of the industry. Legislation is necessary for ensuring the confidentiality of patients and enhancing the safety of intelligent healthcare delivery systems [18].

In conclusion, the possibilities for intelligent healthcare are quite promising. Better health self-management is something that can be facilitated for individual users of smart healthcare. When they are required, patients will have access to timely and appropriate medical care, and the services themselves will have a greater degree of personalization. Smart healthcare has the capability to lower the cost for healthcare facilities, alleviate the burden of manpower, enable streamlined organization of data and services, and enhance the experience of patients. Smart healthcare has the potential to lower the costs of conducting research, shorten the amount of time spent conducting research, and increase the overall productivity of research institutes. Smart healthcare may alleviate the conventional wisdom of medical wealth disparities, push medical transformation, encourage the implementation of preventive techniques, and diminish community medical discretionary spending.
