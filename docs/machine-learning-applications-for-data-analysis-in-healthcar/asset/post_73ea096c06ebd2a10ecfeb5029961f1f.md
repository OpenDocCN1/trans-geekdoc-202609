
### 8.1.2 ISSUES AND CHALLENGES ASSOCIATED TO SMART HEALTHCARE SYSTEMS

Design and implementation of a smart healthcare system may face various issues and challenges. Some most challenging points are as follows:

- 1. Funding: The smart healthcare system has a number of challenges, including funding. IoT infrastructures are expensive and difficult to acquire, which makes their implementation challenging. A smart healthcare system also costs money to maintain.

- 2. Bureaucracy: Bureaucracy stands as a significant challenge in the smart healthcare system. Bureaucracy in healthcare refers to the complex administrative structures, regulations, and processes that can sometimes impede the efficiency of healthcare systems. While bureaucracy serves the purpose of ensuring compliance, quality control, and standardization, an excessive bureaucratic framework in healthcare can lead to various challenges. Changes in organizational and cultural norms are frequently more challenging than changes in technology.

- 3. Healthcare practitioners' limited computer proficiency: The absence or lack of computer proficiency among healthcare practitioners can affect the adoption of telemedicine, data analytics, and other digital healthcare initiatives. Without adequate computer knowledge, healthcare professionals may struggle to efficiently interpret and use digital health data or leverage technology-driven patient management tools.

- 4. Intermittent power supply: The success of remote healthcare applications may be hampered by an epileptic power supply. Inconsistent power supply poses a significant challenge to successful remote healthcare delivery. The irregularity in power availability could disrupt crucial healthcare services, affecting patient consultations, medical device functionality, and data transmission. This unpredictability impedes efforts to provide consistent and reliable healthcare services to remote or underserved areas.

- 5. Security and privacy: This is a severe problem in IoT-based healthcare systems because numerous devices and protocols are used to access critical patient information that is stored in the cloud. Security and privacy in healthcare are paramount, ensuring the protection of sensitive patient data from unauthorized access, breaches, or misuse.

### 8.1.3 PIVOTAL INFRASTRUCTURE

It is possible to monitor the physiological conditions of the body with wearable sensors. Vital indications including body temperature, heart rate, and breathing rate can all be measured by the sensors. It is possible to add more blood pressure and blood oxygen sensors. A specialized central node that only performs one function is preferred to reduce power consumption.

AI is made up of a variety of distinct technologies. Digital data can be shared and recorded via blockchain technology without being altered.

## 8.2 CURRENT STATUS OF RESEARCH AND DEVELOPMENT IN SMART HEALTHCARE SYSTEMS

Lots of research work is going on in the field of smart healthcare systems and their development. Chen et al. [1] worked on handling the emergency situation of patients and proposed the edge-cognitive-computing-based (ECC-based) smart healthcare system. Using cognitive computing, this system can track and examine the physical health of its users. Additionally, it adjusts each user’s health-risk grade when allocating computer resources throughout the entire edge computing network. The results of the trials demonstrate that the ECC-based healthcare system offers a better user experience, efficiently utilizes computing resources, and dramatically increases patient survival rates in an unexpected emergency. Uddin [2] suggested a smart system to monitor the patient’s activities without hospitalization and it is possible with the use of new technologies such as the Internet of Things (IoT), Edge of Things, and Cloud of Things. Recurrent neural network (RNN) technology on an edge device is used in this work to present a wearable sensor-based system for activity prediction (i.e., personal computer or laptop). The system’s input data come from a variety of wearable healthcare sensors, including magnetometer, accelerometer, gyroscope, and electrocardiography sensors. An RNN is then trained using the features. The RNN that has been trained is utilized to forecast the activities. In order to provide older persons, patients, and individuals with disabilities with a simple control system, Ismail et al. [3] presented an effective method based on voice recognition. To quickly access IoT devices put in smart homes and hospitals without relying on a centralized supervisory system, a low-cost speech recognition system is being developed. The suggested system employed a Raspberry Pi device to provide wireless smartphone management of home appliances. This system’s major objective is to make it easier for users to connect with household appliances using IoT communications that are based on voice commands. The proposed framework improves voice recognition by combining a hybrid support vector machine with a dynamic time-warping algorithm. Ahad et al. [4] presented a survey on the use of IoT in smart healthcare systems. Tripathi et al. [5] examined the social and technological impediments to the adoption of smart hospital system (SHS) by examining user perception and cutting-edge expert opinions. Haque et al. [6] suggest a smart healthcare system to resolve the problems of current healthcare systems. Smart contracts built on blockchain technology are used to protect confidential data and uphold privacy. Smart contracts enable a considerable number of actions to be performed automatically. As a result, the system becomes more resource-friendly because it depends less on physical labor. It has been established that the information kept is accurate, unchangeable, and always necessitates proper authority approval. Additionally, healthcare has become universally accessible, safe, decentralized, and automated. The use of machine learning techniques for big data analysis in the healthcare industry has been thoroughly reviewed by Li et al. [7] in their research. Additionally, the strengths and weaknesses of the currently used approaches as well as numerous research difficulties are emphasized. Their research will help government organizations and healthcare professionals stay up to date on the latest developments in machine learning–based big data analytics for smart healthcare. Li et al. [8] proposed a federated learning-based special three-layer (i.e., user, client, cloud, etc.) architecture, ADDetector which achieves privacy-preserving by addressing privacy breaches that exist in data, feature, and model levels. Sujith et al. [9] developed blockchain, deep-learning-based healthcare system. The blockchain framework’s integration improved patient confidentiality and data privacy, preventing data misuse against patients. Syeda et al. [10] discussed smart healthcare and its applications, they covered the significance and function of developing technologies, including AI, the IoT, blockchain, 3D printing, and 5G networking (AIB35). Hameed et al. [11] proposed a theoretical autonomous healthcare model that separates hospital–patient collaborations from the overall healthcare system. The suggested concept makes use of numerous blockchains, including a global blockchain consortium (GBC), which is used for hospital collaboration building a global healthcare system where a patient is not constrained to a certain group of hospitals and physicians located in a particular region. For small issues that do not need much doctor engagement or consultation, other blockchains (regional blockchain consortium [RBC]) are tailored to the regions of the patients. RBCs are permissioned, but the GBC is a permissionless blockchain. The suggested paradigm promises a decentralized framework that makes it easier for users and other parties to share, exchange, and integrate information.

## 8.3 TECHNOLOGIES AND ARCHITECTURE TO IMPLEMENT SMART HEALTHCARE SYSTEMS

Machine learning and IoT open whole new doors for healthcare. The collection and analysis of physiological data from anybody, anywhere, at any time has been made possible by the development of transformational technologies. The implementation of smart healthcare systems involves various technologies and architectural frameworks. These systems encompass cutting-edge advancements such as artificial intelligence (AI), IoT, blockchain technology, and cloud computing. The combination of these technologies creates an intricate architecture to revolutionize healthcare delivery.

AI-driven algorithms analyze patient data, enabling accurate diagnoses and personalized treatment plans. IoT devices, including wearable sensors, monitor vital signs, fostering continuous health tracking. Blockchain technology ensures secure and immutable data sharing among healthcare stakeholders, maintaining patient privacy and data integrity. Cloud computing facilitates vast data storage and seamless access to information across healthcare networks.

The synergy of these technologies forms a robust architecture enhancing healthcare services. It enables remote patient monitoring, streamlines administrative tasks, and facilitates predictive analytics for better health outcomes. Embracing these advancements in healthcare architecture promises improved efficiency, accessibility, and quality of medical services for patients.
