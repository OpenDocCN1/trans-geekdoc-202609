# ## 7.1 INTRODUCTION

Earlier novel drugs for known diseases were identified using random screening and observations with effectiveness of natural products. Until the 1980s, this random screening approach resulted in the discovery of numerous significant chemicals. High-throughput screening (HTS), which is ideal for independent screening of multiple drugs against a specific molecular target or cellular test, is currently improving this technique. The essence of HTS in drug discovery was emphasized with the discovery of cyclosporine A as an immunosuppressant [1]. Progressively, another successful paradigm of application of computer-aided drug design (CADD) was the finding of gefitinib using ALLADIN [2] software after screening a library of 1500 chemicals.

To unravel the mechanism of drug resistance, search for novel antibiotics, and design them for both known and unknown targets, CADD can be integrated with wet lab techniques in the design of new antibiotics. CADD approaches are computationally faster, accurate, and economical [3].

Deciphering novel targets that exhibit distinct pathways needed for bacterial life is a key option to solving antibiotic resistance. Researchers are still looking for novel antibiotics to combat established targets, and computational tools have been utilized effectively in a number of studies to do so. The fundamental CADD procedure begins with the biological identification of a hypothetical target for which ligand binding resulted in antibacterial action. In SBDD, the target’s 3D structure can be determined via X-ray crystallography, nuclear magnetic resonance, or homology modeling. CADD-SBDD screening is built on this base [4]. The major focus is the development of structure–activity relationship which yields further improvements in the compounds, and LBDD is utilized in the absence of the availability of the desired 3D structure.

CADD provides for greater experiment focus, which saves time and money while discovering novel medications. The most popular and extensively used method in this area is the *in silico* methodology.
