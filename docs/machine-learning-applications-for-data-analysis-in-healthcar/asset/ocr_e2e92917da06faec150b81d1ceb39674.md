# ### 6.2.6.4 DISCRETE DATA

VHAs can interact with patients and digitally capture their information, which can then be validated and updated in real time using cloud technologies and IoT-connected devices. When a patient engages with a virtual assistant, queries to a pharmacy hub can be cross-checked against existing records in the facility’s database. Once all information is verified, it is seamlessly processed and integrated into the EHR system—eliminating the need for paper-based documentation.

# ### 6.2.6.5 ANYTIME ACCESS FROM ANYWHERE

For some individuals in the maturing populace, it is undeniably more viable to have verbal correspondence alongside visual collaboration, rather than a call or electronic correspondence. VHAs can be accessed through websites, smartphones, tablets, and computers. These systems are capable of recognizing returning patients and recalling recent interactions [10].

# ### 6.2.7 HEALTHCARE BOTS

Healthcare bots are primarily designed to support patient engagement and accountability. These bots, often integrated into adaptive learning applications, can assist patients by delivering targeted messages and responding to health-related inquiries. They also help manage medications by providing information about drug types and recommended dosages. Recent advancements in healthcare bots include the ability to learn and mimic human conversations, detect emotions to enable empathetic interactions, and incorporate technologies such as natural language processing, sentiment analysis, and intent mining into chatbot scripts. Some bots can even perform complex image recognition tasks to analyze medical images, handwritten notes, and standardized identifiers. Beyond chatbots, other AI applications in healthcare are being developed to assess heart sounds, provide companion robots for the elderly, mine medical records, design treatment plans, assist with repetitive tasks, conduct consultations, aid in drug development, and use visual data for medical training.
