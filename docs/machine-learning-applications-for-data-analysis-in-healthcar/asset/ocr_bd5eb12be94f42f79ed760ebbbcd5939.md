# 9.3 THE ENABLING TECHNOLOGIES

Multiple stakeholders, including medical professionals and patients, healthcare facilities, and academic research organizations, are involved in the delivery of cognitive medical care. It is an organic totality that incorporates numerous dimensions, such as the prevention and monitoring of diseases, the diagnosis and treatment of illnesses, the administration of hospitals, the formulation of decisions regarding healthcare, and the conducting of medical research. Ref. [3] advocated in their research that together with contemporary biotechnology, there are others that also form the foundation of smart healthcare for example, IoT, cloud computing, 5G, artificial intelligence (AI), and so on. Liu et al. [1] advocated that these technologies are utilized extensively across the board in relation to smart healthcare. The concept of “smart aging,” that is, the patient is able to wear a device like an accessory which continuously monitors the vital statistics and if there is any deviation, it alerts to seek medical assistance through virtual assistants, and remote services can be taken advantage of within no time at all [15]. A doctor sees the same as a smart support system which improves their diagnosis and prescription. They get the vital figures within no time at all and even if it might not be very accurate, it still gives them a time advantage. An integrated information platform allows for the regular record of patient’s data by medical professionals. Ninety percent doctors agreed that using surgical robots and mixed reality technologies, more accurate surgery is now possible. Forty-eight percent respondents report that radiofrequency identification technology is also being effectively utilized to manage equipment and supply chain in hospitals.
