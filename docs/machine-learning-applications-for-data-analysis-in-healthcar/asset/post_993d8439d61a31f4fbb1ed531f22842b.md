## 4.2.2 RELATED WORK

The authors of Ref. [7] concentrated on the dynamics of how ML models can be useful in identifying rheumatoid problems. The study discussed various potential solutions, such as supervised learning models and nonsupervised learning models, that could be useful for implementation. The study served as a comprehensive review of the possible applications, focusing on the dynamics of how various ML models and classifiers can assist. Another important aspect discussed in the study is the development of a systematic review, which aids in understanding the dynamics of how ML solutions can work as a systematic outcome for deciding on treatment conditions for patients based on data.

The authors of Ref. [8] discussed how ML models can be useful for the early detection of arthritis problems. Despite this, the study outlined the purpose of how the ML models can be viewed as an effective solution for arthritis detection. Most of the inputs discussed in the study are about the pros and cons of ML models, how the solutions can be resourceful for individuals, and the steps to be taken for the critical application of the ML models. The chapter review provided two key insights into various aspects of ML models, such as image-based detection and data-centric detection, structure recovery, significant data compression, and so on. Another important takeaway is a detailed review of references citing how ML models are used in other areas of diagnosis such as cardiology, oncology, endocrinology, and hematology. The study’s findings can be used to consider how solutions can be pragmatic for improving the overall outcome of using ML for medical diagnostics.

Kataria and Ravindran [9] provide a detailed overview of how the e-health system can be a profound solution for dealing with business dynamics. Considering how various application systems, wearable devices, and tracking solutions can assist healthcare professionals in tracking the arthritis levels of various patients, it refers to pragmatic ways of using technology for better monitoring and treatment. The model also emphasizes the use of augmented data analysis patterns, which can aid in gaining insights into the health conditions of each individual patient, as well as the use of data in macroassessment to focus on the dynamics of data analysis for research and treatment care.

A research paper on digital health advocacy [10] discusses how various emerging technologies, such as wearable devices, pattern studies, big data analysis, real-time tracking sensors, and so on, can help improve the overall quality of healthcare management. The study emphasizes the importance of using sensor networks in the treatment of RA. These wearable devices can help track patient data, which can help improve clinical trials and conditions, resulting in better patient care. Despite the fact that the study is more of a comprehensive review model on digital health, the emphasis is on understanding how the technology scope improves the underlying conditions of patient data monitoring and related ways of applying the diagnosis models for detection accuracy.

The authors of the study [11] discussed the conditions of mathematical models and the treatment conditions significant for RA treatment, with a focus on quantitative predictive modeling applications for RA. The authors of the study emphasize the importance of identifying the problem of RA at an early stage in order to provide more effective treatment. The mathematical models discussed in the study demonstrate how statistical models such as the stochastic and progressive analysis models can be useful.

The study discussed ordinary differential equations (ODEs) and partial differential equations (PDEs) models, highlighting how the solutions are used in the RA progression analysis over two decades. The study emphasizes the absence of more focused studies on biological mechanisms for disease progression. The main point to consider from the condition above is to look into a possible solution in which the mathematical models can be adapted to the ML models. If this is the case, the number of ML models from the literature exploring the application of ODEs and PDE solutions to the detection of RA in patients will increase.

Sharon et al. [12] discuss the classification of medical disorders based on RA and orthopedics datasets using ensemble methods, where a modern model is proposed to determine RA using an ensemble model. A white blood cell classification was used to generate RA datasets. The models’ feature extraction was based on lymphocytes obtained from a digital microscope using an electronic image sensor. Orthopedic datasets were used as the primary benchmark image datasets for training the classifiers and testing the proposed model.

The proposed model incorporates three ensemble algorithm models: Adaboost, Bagging, and Random subspace. The KNN (K-nearest neighbor) and random forest (RF) algorithms were modified. In the study, data classification is modeled using 10-fold cross-validation and a holdout set-up.

According to the study, the accuracy ranges from 97.50% to 94.84% across a variety of ensemble classifiers. Bagging RF is advocated as an effective algorithmic model for the system’s successful implementation. The study clearly shows that the ensemble classifier model is regarded as important in the management of arthritis conditions.

The studies in Ref. [13] focused on tertiary-care RA patients. The wrapper feature selection algorithm was used in the study to determine the attribute core set. Four distinct sets of algorithms were used to validate the model’s dataset-based performance. The model’s feature system focuses on DAS28 (Disease Activity Score on 28 joints) remission as the critical factor for analyzing the model. The ML models used in the model are KNN, extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), RF, and logistic regression (LR).

Despite this, the model has focused on specific and niche feature segments. However, the study refers to the conditions in which the model was successful to the extent of 72.7% in the case of the XGBoost algorithm, and the other algorithms used for analysis have fetched a far lower level of accuracy. To summarize, the model is the most effective solution; however, the solution is less significant in terms of accuracy, which is critical in the model.

The study [14] discusses a magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) report-based imaging-centric ML model, with the authors emphasizing training the algorithms to perform significantly better than the proposed conditions. Understanding the conditions of bone marrow edema and tenosynovitis is the focus of the proposed features. The system’s data study involves using 1,700 MR scans from hospital records as anonymous data. One of the model’s critical implications is that, rather than adhering to any specific model in which the key inputs are used for analysis, the model encourages a qualitative understanding of the datasets. As a result, it is clear that the model under consideration requires more concrete validation of the precision, sensitivity, and specificity required to carry out the accurate analysis.

Another critical area where research has been conducted is disease activity prediction. The study authors considered using ML models to understand the level of RA problems faced by the patients in Ref. [15]. The study’s significance lies in describing how the application of ML problems has exploded in RA segments. One of the key challenges discussed as an impacting factor in the model is understanding the gaps in the quality of data used for the analysis and the implications that could be encountered if the right kind of data is not used for training. In practical terms, the model is more qualitative and discusses how ML is a potential solution and the benefits of ML.

One of the most difficult challenges in RA treatment is how patients may experience a flare-up of the condition. Although the likelihood is low, it may have an adverse effect in cases where such issues are prevalent. Thus, in the study [16], the authors focused on training datasets derived from electronic medical records using various classifiers such as KNN, RF, LR, stacking meta classifier, and naive Bayes classifier. Kaggle datasets are used in the model’s experimental analysis to ensure controlled implementation and understanding of clinical disease activity (DAS28 ESR). In order to keep the study more focused and exclusive, the impact in the study is limited to disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD) therapy. As a result, the study did not concentrate on synthetic DMARDs.

Lin et al. [17] have focused on using electronic medical records to assist users in gaining the right kind of insights into the early and automated prediction of potential RA issues in people. The study used medical records and the Apache Clinical Text Analysis and Knowledge Extraction System to gain the right insights into the data using the support vector machine (SVM) classifier. Based on the chi-square test and other statistical analyses performed on the model, the experimental study of the model discussed in the study indicates a moderate accuracy factor.
