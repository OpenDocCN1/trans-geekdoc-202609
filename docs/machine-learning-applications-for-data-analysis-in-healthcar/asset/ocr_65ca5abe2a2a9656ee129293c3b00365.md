# 6.2.5 REDUCING MEDICATION NONADHERENCE

Between 1988 and 1994, approximately 38% of adults in the United States were taking at least one prescription medication. Over the following two decades, this figure rose to 49%, largely driven by a 100% increase in the number of adults taking three or more prescribed drugs. According to research presented to the American Hospital Association in October 2016, total annual spending on prescription medications had reached $309.5 billion, making it the fastest-growing component of the US healthcare economy. Alarmingly, a significant portion of this spending is wasted. It is estimated that up to 50% of the 3.2 billion prescriptions written each year in the United States are not taken as directed—if they are taken at all. Medication nonadherence results in over $250 billion in unnecessary healthcare costs annually, accounting for approximately 13% of the United States’ total yearly healthcare expenditure. Just as pharmaceutical companies use algorithmic tools to enhance patient targeting, healthcare providers (HCPs) can leverage similar AI-based systems to address this issue by identifying patients most at risk of nonadherence. Factors such as socioeconomic status, insurance coverage, out-of-pocket costs, and the prescribing physician’s specialty all influence a patient’s likelihood of deviating from their treatment regimen.
