# ## 9.1 INTRODUCTION AND LITERATURE REVIEW

The age that we live in is the age of information. Traditional medicine has gradually begun to get digital especially in recent years because of research-oriented advances in technology and scientific theory. There has been an emergence of intelligent and smart healthcare that makes use of the most recent inventions of information technology [9]. Smart healthcare is not simply a simple technology improvement; rather, it is also a transformation that affects healthcare on a comprehensive and multilevel scale [17]. Changes in the healthcare model (moving away from diagnosis to prevention and care), information construction changes (moving away from patient's data to other related family factors) [4], changes in mindset (moving away from general care to personal care), and changes in the idea of support and systems are all manifestations of this shift (from prescription to preventive medicine). The mantra is to act smart and move with agility toward a smart society 5.0 [16].

These adaptations place an emphasis on catering to the specific requirements of individuals while also boosting the effectiveness of healthcare coverage [10]. As a result, the overall experience of receiving medical and health services is significantly improved, and they point to the direction in which modern medicine will continue to develop in the future [8].
