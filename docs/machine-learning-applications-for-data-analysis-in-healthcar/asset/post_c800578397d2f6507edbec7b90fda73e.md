## Bow-Tie Construction from Accident Narratives: A Text-Mining Approach

ASHISH GARG, SOUVIK DAS*, AMARDEEP KUMAR, and J. MAITI

Indian Institute of Technology, Kharagpur, West Bengal, India

### ABSTRACT

In this chapter, we propose an integrated natural language processing (NLP) and the Bow-tie-based risk assessment method to analyze the accident or incident reports for providing preventive and protective actions to the organization. Over 500 accident/incident text narratives of distinct scenarios of workers at different working sites in an organization were collected. We first applied the custom-trained named entity recognition model and found out the objects involved and the potential root cause of the accident or incident from each of the text narratives. Second, using the elbow method, it is found that 4–6 clusters of root causes can be created. Then, the root causes are classified into four categories namely, human factors, component factors, management factors, and environmental factors, using term frequency–inverse document frequency vectorizer and linear support vector classifier. In order to visualize these accidents/incidents, root causes, and their categories, bow-tie methodology is applied. The preventive and protective barriers have been suggested for each potential top event. Thus, this work provides a qualitative risk assessment method to manage future risk events.

### 11.1 INTRODUCTION

Accidents or incidents are common in manufacturing and other industries. As stated by the International Labor Organization, more than 27.8 million occupational workers die every year due to accidents [10]. One of the major reasons which lead to fatal accidents is the availability of hazardous materials like oil, flammable gas, and so on [6, 9, 11]. These occupational accidents affect the entire organization in different ways; hence stakeholders’ safety should be of utmost priority in any industry.

Nowadays in the era of Industry 4.0, industries are collecting ample amounts of data through various means. However, it is difficult to get structured data easily. Also, the value of the data comes from the analysis and insights generated. In manufacturing industries, it is common to find accident or incident reports. These reports are prepared after the accidents by the inspecting agency in conjunction with the witnesses of the accident or incident. Analysis of these accidents or incident reports may provide some great insights or hidden causes of accidents or incidents that may be helpful in reducing the risk in the future. For example, suppose after analyzing accidents reports, it is found that irregular inspection of machines before their day-to-day operation is one of the measure causes of the accidents. These findings may lead to making regular inspection as a compulsory element of standard operating procedures of operations on machines.

The research on the analysis of the accident/incident reports in the manufacturing industries is at its infancy. These researches are mainly focused on natural language processing (NLP)-based tools and techniques. However, integration of quantitative risk assessment techniques such as bow-tie analysis with the NLP is rarely been observed in the literature. Bow-tie is a graphical tool combining fault tree and event tree. A fault tree is drawn from top to bottom and quantitatively analyzed from bottom to top, whereas an event tree is useful in analyzing the risk control measures, that is, safety barriers and the accident consequences. Bow-tie is a highly useful to identify the root causes of any accident and finding the connections between risk control systems. Hence, the motive for this work is to analyze the accident reports with the help of the NLP and the bow-tie analysis. The combination of the results of the text narratives analysis and the bow-tie model provides an improved analysis and better insights. Here, first the preprocessing of the text data such as converting to lowercase, punctuation removal, stop word removal, tokenization, stemming, and lemmatization is done to bring the unstructured text information into a desirable format so that risk assessment can be done easily [7]. To find the root cause of accidents, a custom-trained NER (named entity recognition) model is used. Basically, NER model is a method to extract the key entity from the text. But before applying the NER model, it is necessary to annotate the text data. Over 450 text narratives are annotated. Further, two desired entities, namely, root causes and objects, involved in an accident/incident are labeled. The causation sentences are categorized into four levels by identifying the number of clusters using the elbow method and linear support vector classifier (SVC). It is found the most frequently occurring words in whole corpus, called top event related to accidents are fire, fall down, slipped, and gas leakage. Along with this, the bow-tie diagram is plotted using the open-source BowtieXP software. Bow-tie diagram gives an effective risk assessment process to systematically assess the potential risks.
