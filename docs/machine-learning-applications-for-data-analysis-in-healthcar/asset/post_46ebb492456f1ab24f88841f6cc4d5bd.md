## ABSTRACT

Depression is a widespread mental health issue affecting over 264 million people worldwide. It is characterized by fluctuating mood changes and emotional responses that can be short-term or long-lasting. When untreated, depression can significantly disrupt daily functioning and reduce a person’s potential, sometimes becoming as debilitating as a physical illness. Timely diagnosis and treatment are crucial for managing depression. While there are several mental health apps available, many of them have premium features, making them inaccessible to users who cannot afford the costs. One such app, Mood Path, tracks mood patterns and offers health assessments to help users manage negative thoughts, focusing on mindfulness skills rather than physical exercises. In contrast, our app takes a more comprehensive approach by analyzing user inputs and recommending tailored treatments. It is free to use, serving as an accessible eHealth tool for individuals dealing with depression. The app emphasizes treatment and recovery, offering a user-friendly interface that allows easy interaction in everyday language. To ensure the app remains beneficial, it includes scheduled treatments to help users manage their anxiety and negative thoughts. This approach minimizes the risk of overuse while promoting long-term mental health improvement.

### 3.1.2 METHODOLOGY

This app opens with the logo of our app, that is, “worry not” and later shows a motivational quote to motivate the users. After the motivation quote comes to the login page where the user inputs “name,” “email address,” and “password.” This is done so that the app can differentiate between users and this way the user will have their data secured. After the login page comes the questionnaire pages where the app asks the user certain questions that will help in the detection of the severity of the user’s depression. The determination of the level of depression is done through a point (here “stars”) system, that is, each answer the user gives will have certain points allocated to them.

In our app, we have made a point (or star) system such that if the user gets a point (or star) ranging from 0 to 3 then the user will be detected to have mild depression, if the user gets points (or star) ranging from 4 to 9 then the user has intermediate depression and if a user gets points (or stars) more than 9 then the user has extreme depression. This point (or star) system is not visible to the user so as not to affect their mood. After the determination of the level of depression by our app, the app recommends the user tasks like listen to songs, or do meditation, and so on. This helps the user to take their mind off the depressive or sad thoughts they have in the present time. Also, as mentioned in paper [5], apps should be friendly and colorful so they can enjoy using them and also more affordable than the books that help you improve depression and our app once done will be tried to be made free of cost. Our app also recommends users the personalized diet chart that can keep them healthy if the users follow it.
