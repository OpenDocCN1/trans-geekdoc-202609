## 7.3 MOLECULAR DOCKING

Molecular docking refers to fitting in the potential ligands in the active site of the target protein and generates the least energy, best-fit molecular conformation. Various molecular parameters are analyzed during docking followed by multiple iterative rounds to minimize the energy using algorithms like Newton–Raphson, steepest descent, and conjugate gradient methodologies [36]. Molecular docking generates a plethora of docked structures and conformations which are then categorized using scoring function features. Scoring function is based on multiple parameters such as free energy, topological properties, electronic structure, stability of complexes, binding energy, root mean square deviation, and so on [37].

The accurate ligand-binding conformation depends on two rules which are as follows:

- Conformation space which helps in finding suitable binding regions.
- Binding energy of each prediction is correlated with each conformation.

Molecular docking is a multiphase process and is done when a state with minimum energy is achieved, in which ligand binding is checked by various scoring functions. The goal of molecular docking is to examine feasible binding of genome having specific ligand with a target whose 3D structure is known [38].

There are three basic features that need to be accomplished in docking:

- depiction of the binding site,
- ligand fixing with the specific binding region, and
- assessing the energy of interaction between a specific ligand–receptor complex [39].

Pharmacophore modeling is required as a preliminary step in many cases before going for molecular docking. Pharmacophore is a 3D arrangement of functional groups having a molecular structure. Pharmacophore modeling refers to generating a model of the specific conformation of active functional groups which is derived from a set of known ligands that are selected for a target [40]. This model depends upon steric and electronic features. It uses algorithms that encompass the following steps: input, conformational search, feature extraction, representation of structure, pattern identification, and scoring [41]. It is generally used to select hits for docking studies. Scoring functions are mathematical tools by which binding affinity of two molecules is predicted after they are docked.

### 7.3.1 DOCKING PARAMETERS

Docking has a large number of input parameters based on its functionality. We need to check accessible surface area, force field parameters acting on each molecule, dipole moment, steric complementary between receptor and ligand, physicochemical properties of the ligand and receptor. Docking can be carried out by varying different parameters listed below:

- Flexible ligand: Used to relax an input conformation; have parameters such as anchor search, anchor size, and torsion drive [42].
- Orient ligand: Helps in orientation search; done by matching.
- Score ligand: Does scoring of orientation.
- Minimize ligand: It minimizes conformations and improves the efficiency.
- Multiple ligand
- Chemical screen: Allows rapid filtering of data based on 3D structure.
- Random seed: Figure to seed the random number generator [43].

### 7.3.2 DOCKING ALGORITHMS

Docking uses different search algorithms, which help in predicting possible conformation of complexes. These algorithms are based on certain parameters. On the basis of flexibility of the receptor and ligand, the search algorithms are divided into two types.

*Rigid body docking*: In this algorithm, molecular conformation remains rigid and ligand is fitted in by varying the orientation of ligand in various angles. It is also the first slant used in docking and it compiles the below-mentioned functions such as it fixes the protein and ligand and helps in selecting molecules with moderate energy orientation and protein–protein docking is done by this.

*Flexible docking*: It is the most used form of docking. This ligand is flexible but the receptor is rigid. First ligands are selected to become flexible by moving firm parts through optimization. In this bond angle, torsion angles are modified and internal geometry is changed.

- Systematic search algorithms provide variations in the structure, and rapidly change ligand conformation. Examples of some systematic search algorithms are conformational search, database, fast shape mappings, and so on. It is further categorized into fragment and exhaustive search algorithms. Exhaustive searches clarify ligand forms by systematically rotating all possible bonds and torsion angles in a given period of time. The fragment-based algorithm is done by incremental construction of ligand conformations from fragments obtained by selecting ligands of interest [44].
- The Monte Carlo algorithm creates a random initial configuration of ligand in the active site which is scored on the basis of energy and other parameters. After this, small new changes are made and the novel conformation is again subjected to scoring. The better conformation with higher scoring is selected, also known as the initiation minimization program [45].
- Genetic algorithms are built on the principle of population and biological evolution [46]. There is a set of parameters that decides the arrangement of protein and ligand based on translation, rotation, and conformation.
- Tabu search algorithm: In this, a small change is made to the current configuration of ligand and on the basis of fitness, they are ranked [47].
