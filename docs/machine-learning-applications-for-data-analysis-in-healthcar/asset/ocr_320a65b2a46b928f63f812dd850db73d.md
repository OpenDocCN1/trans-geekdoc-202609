All sensors are possibly compact, transportable, and wearable through advanced technology. Patients now have a nonintrusive, comfortable solution that can track their health continuously. Patients may be more open to employ health monitoring technology through smart healthcare systems. Such systems can be made more useful if cameras or implantable sensors are attached to take updated information about patients. Short-range connections, like Bluetooth, are recommended by current models for sending sensor data to smartphones for processing. The patient’s processed information can subsequently be transferred to the healthcare practitioner, usually a doctor, using SMS or the Internet using long-range connections like long-term evolution. The main drawback of using cell-phones for healthcare updates is the battery life. Cellphones frequently need to be recharged because of their short battery life; a patient with a dead battery would not be connected to health practitioners.

Patient physiological data are monitored and collected using sensors and the patient is identified using a radio frequency identification (RFID) tag. This IoT-based RFID healthcare monitoring device allows for the collection of physiological data from hospital patients and elderly people. Ambient sensors can detect the physical parameters of the surroundings, like the presence of toxic agents, humidity, and temperature could be used to assess the environmental parameters and analyze their relation with the person’s health (Figure 8.1).
