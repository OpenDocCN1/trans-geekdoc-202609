A novel method called weighted linear embedding (WLE) was proposed [5] to recognize FKP. This method combines the Fisher criterion with the manifold learning criterion to extract features from a linear model. The concept of the manifold learning theory states that the importance of local information is more significant than nonlocal information. This concept is implemented and both local and nonlocal features are extracted from the data sets. The author uses Gaussian weighing to mapping procedure that combines nonlocal and local information. It is commonly used to find a mapping vector that is suitable for estimating the weighted class scatters maximized. The classification is done on the base of the nearest neighbor classifier. The authors have also tested the proposed algorithm on palmprint and have made a comparative study of PCA, LDA, LDE, and WLE. The WLE is tested on the right index finger of 1000 persons and achieved a recognition rate of 78.2%.

In Ref. [6], the author proposed a new FKP recognition work in which the author uses wavelets for image analysis and pattern recognition and also uses PCA to reduce the Gabor feature into low-dimensional space. Further, the author applies orthogonal linear discriminant analysis (OLDA) transformation in PCA subspace to classify the image using nearest neighbor and achieve 98% accuracy. This chapter also compares the individual finger performance and shows that the left index finger has better performance than the right index finger.

An author presented a method that simultaneously measures distances and angles between theimage data vectors to study the similarities between them [7]. An image angle measurement method that is focused on a shifted image space is proposed to highlight the distinction between the angles of different data. Both the distance and angle are obtained through a parallel fusion procedure that uses the complex locality-preserving projections to extract low-dimensional features. Orthogonal complex locality preservation projections or OCLPP is a subspace method that can minimize the redundant information in each image and four images were randomly selected for the recognition process and achieved a recognition rate up to 88%.
