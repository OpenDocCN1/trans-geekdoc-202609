
Skin disease diagnosis depends heavily on visually inspecting lesions for certain characteristics indicative of melanomas, basal cell carcinomas and more. This involves subtle judgments general practitioners in remote areas may lack resources to make consistently. Deep convolutional networks trained on datasets of hundreds of thousands of images classified common conditions with impressive accuracy, sensitivity and specificity when validated on independent test sets. Some such systems have received regulatory approval for use on smartphone photos to assist initial screening decisions. This represents a promising method for boosting accessibility of expert-level dermatological evaluations anywhere visual data can be captured and transmitted.

### Pathology Applications

Pathologists study tissue slides under microscopes, a labor-intensive process for comprehensively characterizing cancer stages, grades and biomarkers. Convolutional neural networks demonstrate human-level or better performance evaluating tumor histopathology scans.

#### Digitization of Histopathology

As technology has advanced, entire glass pathology slides can now be digitized into high-resolution whole-slide images (WSIs) viewable on any device. This digitization enables remote consultation, education and large-scale research through image archives. It also lays the foundation for applying computer vision to automation.

#### Tumor Detection and Localization

CNNs trained on large histopathology datasets demonstrate accuracy comparable to pathologists in identifying whether a WSI contains cancer and precisely localizing tumor regions. Models can highlight suspicious zones to guide further review under microscopes. This aids workload triage and prioritization.

#### Cancer Classification

Deeper models go beyond detection to classify cancer types and grades using morphological features. They analyze cell types, growth patterns and other histopathological criteria pathologists rely on for diagnosis. Automated classification supports initial screening and worklist organization.

#### Biomarker Analysis

Looking beyond visual appearance, models show potential for prognostically-relevant biomarker analysis tasks like quantifying tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes or detecting specific gene expressions from stained scans. This adds an objective layer complementing pathologists' examinations.

#### Standardization

As digital archives and AI decision support proliferate globally, models may help standardize diverse pathologists' diagnostic practices and education. This benefits ensuring consistent, high-quality cancer care especially in underserved regions with clinician shortages.

### Model Development Best Practices

Several considerations ensure AI applications for healthcare achieve their potential safely and equitably.

#### Data Collection and Annotation

Rigorous protocols involving patient consent are followed to amass representative datasets. Experts provide iterative annotations addressing diagnostic ambiguities.

#### Prospective Validation

Models undergo thorough testing against independent held-out datasets as well as real clinical usage, quantifying performance stability over time.

#### Mitigating Bias

Techniques like `Grad-CAM` and `SHAP` are leveraged to understand model reasoning and detect potential biases in decisions. Fairness is ensured.

#### Regulatory Compliance

Models meet applicable regulatory standards through controlled validation and monitoring after any approval for clinical decision support use.

#### Addressing Dataset and Covariate Shift

Performance degradations are addressed through continued monitoring and retraining as clinical practice, demographics and other factors change over many years.

#### Model Explainability

Tools provide explanations to build confidence in model interpretations and identify overreliance on non-diagnostic cues that could lead to errors.

#### Ethical Oversight

Multidisciplinary governance ensures patients’ interests are protected and AI benefits are maximized in a safe, responsible manner respecting medical ethics.

#### Continuous Improvement

An evidence-based culture of ongoing evaluation and enhancement optimizes model safety, effectiveness and proper integration into care over the long term.

### Integration into Clinical Workflows

Rather than replacing specialists, deep learning tools are meant to augment their expertise, especially for general practitioners in resource-scarce environments.

#### Flagging of High-Risk Cases

Computer vision systems can be designed to automatically flag high-risk cases like melanomas, advanced carcinomas or abnormal pathology slides that require immediate secondary review by a specialist. This helps prioritize critical cases.

#### Broad Categorization

Models may classify findings into broad categories like benign vs malignant to guide general practitioners. This aids initial screening without replacing specialist diagnoses.

#### Differential Diagnosis Generation

Systems can provide a ranked list of potential conditions for clinicians to consider, along with visual explicability of predictions. This supports decision-making without direct replacement.

#### Performance Monitoring

Regular audits ensure models continue performing acceptably against new data as clinical practice evolves. Retraining addresses dataset or protocol changes over time.

#### Specialist Augmentation

Rather than exclusively aiding generalists, tools may benefit specialists by filtering caseloads or providing computer-annotated scans during tumor boards.

#### Multidisciplinary Integration

Close cooperation between computer scientists, clinicians and ethicists maximizes benefits while preventing over-reliance on any one component of the diagnostic process.

#### Telehealth Enablement

With remote guidance via high-quality medical images, augmented diagnostics aim to expand access globally especially in underserved areas facing clinician shortages.
