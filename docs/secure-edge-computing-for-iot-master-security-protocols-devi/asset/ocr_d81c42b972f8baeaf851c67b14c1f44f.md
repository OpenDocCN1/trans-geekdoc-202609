Edge Data Centers: In some cases, edge data centers may be established in strategic locations to support edge computing. These smaller, localized data centers help reduce latency, and support the processing needs of IoT applications. The micro data centers are small and modular reach-level systems that help in installing and managing the edge network computing that can be deployed globally in any environment. Due to their size, they are more suitable than traditional data centers to be used in many places.

Edge Gateways: Edge gateways are devices that serve as intermediaries between edge devices and the cloud or data center. They aggregate data from multiple IoT devices, preprocess it, and transmit only relevant information to the cloud. Edge gateways can also run analytics locally.

5G and Edge: The rollout of 5G networks significantly enhances the capabilities of edge computing by providing high-speed, low-latency connectivity. 5G enables real-time data processing and communication for IoT devices in a way that was previously unattainable with 4G or older networks.

Cloudlets: Cloudlets are small-scaled cloud data centers with enhanced mobility, located at the edge of a network. They stand as a representation of the second of the three-tiered hierarchy of IoT or edge devices. Cloudlets help in improvising resource-intensive and interactive mobile applications, by lowering the latency rate in mobile devices within nearby geographical proximity.
