
## Anomaly Detection and Behaviour Analysis

Forensic analysis in the realm of edge computing presents a distinct set of challenges and opportunities. Edge computing involves processing data closer to its source, often on devices at the edge of the network. Forensic analysts must contend with the distributed nature of this infrastructure, which spreads across a multitude of interconnected devices, such as sensors, smartphones, and IoT devices. The challenge lies in effectively collecting, preserving, and analyzing digital evidence in this decentralized and diverse environment.

In edge computing, forensic investigators need to consider the vast volume of data generated and stored on various edge devices. Traditional forensic approaches may not be sufficient due to limitations in storage, processing power, and network bandwidth. Therefore, specialized forensic techniques are essential to extract relevant data, while considering the constrained resources and intermittent connectivity characteristic of edge devices. This necessitates the development of lightweight forensic tools and methodologies tailored for edge environments.

Another critical aspect of forensic analysis in edge computing is ensuring data integrity and authenticity. Edge devices are often exposed to physical vulnerabilities and attacks, making it crucial to establish a robust chain of custody for digital evidence. Techniques such as cryptographic hashing and secure data transmission protocols are employed to maintain the integrity and verifiability of the evidence collected from these distributed edge devices.

Furthermore, the dynamic and heterogeneous nature of edge computing environments calls for real-time or near-real-time forensic analysis capabilities. Investigative processes need to adapt to the fast-paced, constantly evolving edge landscape. Forensic analysts can leverage automation and AI-based tools to expedite data analysis and anomaly detection, enabling rapid responses to security incidents and breaches in these distributed edge environments.

Collaboration among forensic experts, cybersecurity professionals, and edge computing specialists is fundamental to developing effective forensic strategies for distributed edge environments. Sharing knowledge, expertise, and best practices helps in staying ahead of emerging threats and evolving technologies.

As edge computing continues to proliferate, a proactive approach to forensic analysis is essential to secure and uphold the trustworthiness of data and systems at the edge of the network.

## Challenges in Edge Continuous Monitoring

One of the primary hurdles is the inherent heterogeneity of edge devices and systems. Edge environments encompass a diverse array of hardware, operating systems, protocols, and applications. Integrating and standardizing monitoring solutions across this heterogeneous landscape is a formidable challenge, and requires careful consideration of compatibility, scalability, and interoperability.

Another key challenge revolves around the volume and velocity of data generated at the edge. Edge devices produce an immense amount of data in real-time, making it difficult to efficiently process, analyze, and act upon this data in near real-time. Moreover, transmitting all data to a centralized monitoring system for analysis is often impractical due to bandwidth constraints and latency issues. Balancing the need for comprehensive monitoring with the limitations of edge network capabilities is a constant struggle.

Finally, managing the lifecycle of monitoring applications and algorithms at the edge is a critical challenge. Over time, updates, patches, and improvements are necessary to enhance the monitoring capabilities and address vulnerabilities. However, updating and maintaining monitoring applications on numerous distributed edge devices can be a logistical challenge, and requires thoughtful strategies to ensure the consistency, reliability, and security of monitoring operations.
