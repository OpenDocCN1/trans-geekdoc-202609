# Denial of Service (DoS) and Distributed DoS Attacks

Denial of Service (DoS) and Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attacks are malicious attempts to disrupt the normal functioning of a targeted server, service, or network. In the context of edge computing, these attacks pose a significant threat due to the distributed and decentralized nature of edge infrastructure.

In a Denial of Service (DoS) attack targeting edge computing, a malevolent actor attempts to overwhelm a specific edge node or service with a high volume of traffic. The goal is to exhaust the resources of the targeted edge component, rendering it unavailable to handle legitimate requests. The attacker might exploit vulnerabilities in the software of the edge node, or flood the network connecting to the edge node, making it impossible for genuine users to access the services hosted there.

A more sophisticated and potent form of attack in the realm of edge computing is the Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attack. In a DDoS attack, multiple compromised devices, often forming a botnet, are coordinated to flood the edge infrastructure with an overwhelming volume of traffic. These devices could be located across various geographical locations, making it challenging to mitigate the attack centrally. The distributed nature of edge computing exacerbates this challenge, as it becomes more difficult to filter and detect malicious traffic in real-time across the entire edge network.
