Smith [38] suggested that if |R| > 0.8, it can be expected that there is a strong correlation between observed and predicted values. It should be recognized that even if R is close to 1, the predicted and observed values may not match each other; they only tend to vary similarly. To compensate this limitation, coefficient of determination R<sup>2</sup> can be used. To have precise results, R<sup>2</sup> values should be close to 1. In all cases, the RMSE and MAE should be close to zero. As shown in Table 22.4, the MARS model outperformed the GMDH and MNLR models in term of accuracy for both training and testing data sets. For example, the MARS model decreased the RMSE value by 40.6% and 49.2% and increased R<sup>2</sup> values by 13% and 22.2% in respect to GMDH and MNLR models, respectively. It should be noted that the performances of developed GMDH and MNLR models are also acceptable and GMDH outperformed the MNLR model. Lower accuracy of MNLR can be attributed to its limitations in predicting the phenomenon with a highly nonlinear relationship or with multiple criteria.
