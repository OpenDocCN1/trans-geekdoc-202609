Since in each earthquake damage depends on the dynamic characteristics of the earthquake, each record is identified by two important characters, namely p.g.a. and the main frequency. Main frequency of an earthquake is defined as the frequency which transfers the most energy of the earthquake. In order to calculate the main frequency, the frequency content of the earthquake should be computed by Fourier transform. The procedure is performed by transferring the ground motion from time domain into the frequency domain. Finite Fourier Transform of an accelerogram $a(t)$ is defined as [16]:

```
$F(\omega) = \int_{0}^{T} a(t) e^{-i\omega t} dt, \quad i = \sqrt{-1}$ (11.2)
```

where $T$ is the duration of the record. Fourier amplitude spectrum $FS(\omega)$ is defined as square route of the sum of the squares of real and imaginary parts of $F(\omega)$, as it is shown below:

```
$FS(\omega) = \sqrt{\left[\int_{0}^{T} a(t) \sin \omega t dt\right]^2 + \left[\int_{0}^{T} a(t) \cos \omega t dt\right]^2}$ (11.3)
```
