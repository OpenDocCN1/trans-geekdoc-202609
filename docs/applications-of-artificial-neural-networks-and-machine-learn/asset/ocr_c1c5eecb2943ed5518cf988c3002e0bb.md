HSS materials have distinct manufacturing methods and material properties that lead to various structural behaviors concerning material and geometric imperfections compared to NSS. The field of structural engineering has seen significant advancements in recent years, particularly in the application of machine learning methods to predict the behavior of various structural elements. Although the importance of experimental evaluation of HSS members is not negligible, in relation to recent developments in the field of machine learning (Especially ANNs), it has become possible to predict the behavior of a variety of natural phenomena, including buckling behavior or flexural buckling of columns and beam-columns.

Artificial neural networks (ANNs) are a branch of machine learning techniques that simulate the learning mechanism of biological organisms. ANNs are characterized by a large number of processing elements called neurons, which are interconnected through axons and dendrites forming synapses. ANNs resemble the structure of a human brain, consisting of many processing elements that can establish an accurate relation between some inputs and outputs of a given dataset for which no specific solution exists. ANNs are fairly efficient at finding complex relationships between influencing variables. They have been increasingly employed to solve complex and time-consuming problems in the field of structural engineering. Some of the pioneering works in employing ANN in structural engineering were carried out by Kaveh et al. [3], who utilized the prediction capability of neural networks in various fields of structures.

A number of studies have investigated the use of ANNs and other machine learning techniques to predict the behavior of structural elements. The use of ANN to predict the strength of structural components and the buckling columns when subjected to axial compression is a topic of ongoing research. However, there needs to be more research on utilizing ANN to forecast buckling loads from experimental data. This is mainly due to the requirement of a large amount of experimental data to train the network, which incurs significant costs. Nonetheless, Waszczyszyn and Bartezak [4] have successfully operated Backpropagation Neural Networks (BPNN) to forecast the buckling load of cylindrical shells under axial compression.

The experimental data of some previously published papers was utilized for training artificial neural networks using the Resilient Backpropagation learning algorithm to predict buckling load factor based on the imperfection data of shell specimens. Markopoulos et al. [5] used ANN with the Levenberg-Marquardt and Backpropagation algorithms as training functions to predict the collapse mode of PVC cylindrical shells under axial compression, using the shell thickness-to-diameter ratio and length-to-diameter ratio as inputs to the network. Ferreira et al. [6] focus on developing a prediction model for the lateral-torsional buckling resistance of steel cellular beams using ANNs. They demonstrated that ANNs are a promising tool for predicting lateral-torsional buckling resistance of steel cellular beams with high accuracy and efficiency. Also, Kaveh and Khavaninzadeh developed ANN-based models to predict FRP strength [7].
