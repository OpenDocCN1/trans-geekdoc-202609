First of all, RBF networks are simpler than MLP networks, which may have more than three architectural layers, so the training process is generally faster than MLP networks. Second, RBF networks act as local approximation networks because the network outputs are determined by hidden units specified in specific local input fields. While MLP networks initially use randomly generated parameters. Last and most importantly, the classification mechanisms are different for RBF networks and MLP networks: RBF clusters are separated by hyperspheres. While in neural networks, hypersurfaces with arbitrary shape are used for separation.
