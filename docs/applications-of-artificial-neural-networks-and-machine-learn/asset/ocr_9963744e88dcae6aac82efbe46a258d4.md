# # Chapter 20

# New Predictive Models for Prediction of Bond Strength Between FRP Reinforcements Externally Glued on Masonry Units

**Abstract** Interest in FRP composite bars as reinforcement to concrete has increased over the years as it has shown solutions to the drawbacks of steel such as its corrosion issues and vulnerability when employed in adverse environmental conditions. However, it is still not widely incorporated as a replacement to conventional steel primarily due to the complexity of its bond strength mechanism. This, therefore, imposes the need to establish a comprehensive relationship for the bond property of the FRP reinforced concrete. This chapter presents a novel Artificial Neural Network (ANN) bond strength prediction model for FRP reinforced concrete using 184 hinged beam database from various existing experiments.

# ## 20.1 Introduction

This chapter discusses the derivation of new model for the bond behavior of NSM FRP systems in concrete [1]. Recently, Fibre Reinforced Polymer (FRP) sheets or laminates have been widely employed as externally bonded reinforcement (EBR) systems in civil engineering constructions. In particular, the use of FRP EBR system for strengthening and retrofitting the existing masonry buildings like monumental heritage is significantly increasing. The reason for Increasing the use of FRP materials for retrofitting masonry buildings is due to the favorable properties of FRP EBR techniques such as: high strength and resistance to corrosion, high durability, non-magnetic, high fatigue resistance, and no increment in mass and stiffness of the structure [2]. In common practice, the FRP EBR technique is designed based on several empirical and semi-empirical design equations. Design procedure of FRP EBR technique including installation, loads, safety requirements and acceptable features of materials has been coded in several guidelines [3–6]. Different in-plane or out-of-plane failure modes of retrofitted masonry units have also been investigated in recent years [7–9].
