where [K] is the stiffness matrix, and {F} is the normal force vector. For equilibrium U is negative and the sum of U and Uc is zero.

Apart from the above formulation, one can optimize a structure with a different approach, without the need for the formation of U, and utilizing only Uc. As we observed before, Uc can be written in the form of Eq. (15.12), and in analysis the objective is its minimization. Differentiating this equation leads to the following relationship before obtaining Eq. (15.13).

\(\frac{\partial U^c}{\partial q} = 0 \Rightarrow [[\mathbf{H}_{qp}]]\{\mathbf{p}\} + [[\mathbf{H}_{qq}]]\{\mathbf{q}\} = \{0\}\)

Therefore q should be selected such that the Eq. (15.19) holds (as a replacement to the selection of q such that it minimizes Eq. (15.15)). However, the right-hand side of Eq. (15.19) is a zero vector and should be changed to scalar. The best is to calculate the norm as the square root of the squared sum of its entries. If this norm becomes zero, then all the entries should be zero. In the previous method the scalar value presented the complementary energy, for becoming zero we had to combined it with energy U. Here, if the aim is the optimization of cross sections {A} and geometry {D}, then we can write:

\(F(q, A, D) = W(A, D)(1 + \alpha \text{norm}([[\mathbf{H}_{qp}]]\{\mathbf{p}\} + [[\mathbf{H}_{qq}]]\{\mathbf{q}\})\)

where {q}, {A} and {D} are the input data. It is sufficient the number of variables to be equal to the degree of statistical indeterminacy of the structure, because the remaining forces can be obtained considering the geometry and topology of each chromosome as given in Eq. (15.9). The submatrices [[H_qp] and [[H_qq] are functions of the selected cross sections, geometry and topology. Having these two, F can be obtained from Eq. (15.20), and its minimum value for a big \(\alpha\) means corresponds to minimization of the weight W. If the structure contains other constraints, it is sufficient to normalize these constraints and then with the help of a penalty coefficient to add to the above equation. Thus the final formulation is obtained as

Find q, A, D; \(A \in \{S_d \text{ or } S_c\}\)
Min \(F(q, A, D) = W(A, D)(1 + \alpha[\text{norm}([[\mathbf{H}_{qp}]]\{\mathbf{p}\} + [[\mathbf{H}_{qq}]]\{\mathbf{q}\})\)
\(+ \sum_{m=1}^{nc} \max[0, g_m(q, A, D)])\)

In this relationship, \(S_d\) and \(S_c\) are the discrete and/or continuous sections, respectively. \(g_m(q, A, D)\) represents the magnitude of the constraint violation and nc is the number of constraints consisting of the stress constraints, displacement constraints and buckling constraints. The main advantage of this formulation is that the term corresponding to the analyst is included in the objective function.
