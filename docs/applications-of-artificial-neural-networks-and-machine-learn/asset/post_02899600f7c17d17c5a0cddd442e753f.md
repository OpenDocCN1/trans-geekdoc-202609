Since in each earthquake damage depends on the dynamic characteristics of the earthquake, each record is identified by two important characters, namely p.g.a. and the main frequency. Main frequency of an earthquake is defined as the frequency which transfers the most energy of the earthquake. In order to calculate the main frequency, the frequency content of the earthquake should be computed by Fourier transform. The procedure is performed by transferring the ground motion from time domain into the frequency domain. Finite Fourier Transform of an accelerogram $a(t)$ is defined as [16]:

```
$F(\omega) = \int_{0}^{T} a(t) e^{-i\omega t} dt, \quad i = \sqrt{-1}$ (11.2)
```

where $T$ is the duration of the record. Fourier amplitude spectrum $FS(\omega)$ is defined as square root of the sum of the squares of real and imaginary parts of $F(\omega)$, as it is shown below:

```
$FS(\omega) = \sqrt{\left[\int_{0}^{T} a(t) \sin \omega t dt\right]^2 + \left[\int_{0}^{T} a(t) \cos \omega t dt\right]^2}$ (11.3)
```

Based on the above-mentioned theory, the main frequencies of the earthquake records are calculated. Table 11.1 shows the earthquake records and their p.g.a and the calculated main frequency.

## 11.3 Artificial Neural Networks

A model of an artificial neuron is shown in Fig. 11.1, where a set of input signals (X1, X2, ..., Xn) are applied to the neuron [17]. Each signal is multiplied at a proportional weight before arriving at summation unit (Σ). All weighted inputs are summed and make the output NET, then activation function is applied to NET and produces the signal OUT. In this research, two important activation functions, namely Sigmoid and Hyperbolic Tangent are employed which have most application in ANN problems. Fig. 11.2.

Sigmoid function maps the value of NET onto the range (0, 1) and Hyperbolic Tangent maps NET value onto the range (−1, 1). In this research, Back Propagation Network (BPN) is used, since it is the most applicable type of ANN. In this network, the output values are propagated backward, which can be compared to the previous values, and the error can be minimized.

### 11.3.1 Evaluation of Learning and Performance of the Networks

If an ANN can present acceptable response to the inputs present or not present in the training set, it is said that the ANN has learnt well and it has good performance. This state is evaluated by various mathematical methods. In the following, two important parameters for the measurement of learning and performance of ANNs are described.

#### 11.3.1.1 Root Mean Square of Errors (RMS)

RMS error is the square root of sum of squares of errors between actual and desired outputs of the network. This parameter is considered as target function in back propagation network and the weights of the network are considered as function variable. During the training of a network, RMS reduction indicates the progress in network learning. As the RMS is reduced, the network learns better and gives more accurate answers to the existent inputs in the training set, but this procedure does not indicate that the network can give accurate answers to the nonexistent inputs in the training set. In other words, it is possible that value of RMS is very little, but the network can’t present acceptable answers to new inputs. On the other hand, if RMS is reduced more than required limit, it is possible that the network recites the existent data in the training set and loses the ability of generalization and response to new inputs; so one cannot absolutely certify the good learning and performance of a network according to low RMS value.

#### 11.3.1.2 Correlation Factor

Harmonic variation of two variables is called correlation. When two variables change in agreement with each other and in the same direction, it is said that they have positive correlation, e.g. size and weight of some people have positive correlation, because it is very probable that somebody who is taller is heavier, vice versa if two variables change in agreement with each other but in opposite directions, they have negative correlation. Finally if variables don’t change in agreement with each other, they are called uncorrelated. Correlation factor can be calculated according to Eq. (11.4).

```
r(x, y) = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{[\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2 \sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2]^{1/2}} (11.4)
```

where:
x̄: Average of x variables
ȳ: Average of y variables.

The value of \( r \) always is between -1 and 1. If \( r = 1 \), correlation is perfect and if \( r = -1 \), correlation is perfect and negative, and if \( r = 0 \), the variables are uncorrelated. Also in ANN, the correlation factor between the actual and desired outputs from training and testing sets together can be used as a criterion for evaluating performance and learning of the network. The closer the value of this factor to 1 for both data existing in training and testing set, better the learning and performance of the network. It is notable that correlation factor between the actual and desired outputs from training set, can never be used for the evaluation of the performance of a network, because may be the network would recite existent data in the training set and the correlation factor between actual and desired output is close to 1 but the network cannot present acceptable answers to new inputs (inputs that do not exist in the testing set).

### 11.3.2 Data Classification for Training and Testing of ANNs

The data are divided into 3 groups for training and testing of the ANNs as follows:

- Group A: Network for assessment of damage in 3, 4, and 5 stories structures
- Group B: Network for assessment of damage in 6–10 stories structures
- Group C: Network for assessment of damage in 3–10 stories structures.

### 11.3.3 Training of the ANNs for Predicting the Vulnerability of the Structures

In order to train the ANNs, the “Neural Works” program has been employed [17]. In all the networks, the training set includes seven parameters which are: the ratio of number of stories to number of bays of structure, period of first vibration mode of structure in terms of second, peak ground acceleration (p.g.a.) in terms of ground gravity acceleration (g), earthquake main frequency (Hz), scaled earthquake acceleration, maximum displacement of top of the structure, and Park and Ang damage index, respectively. Testing set includes six parameters which are as the same as the above-mentioned parameters except the damage index.

In order to train the networks, back propagation algorithm is used. Architecture of the networks consists of one input layer, one (or two) hidden layer(s), and one output layer. Both Hyperbolic Tangent and Sigmoid functions are employed in training the networks and their performances are compared. The number of neurons in hidden layer are increased until the best prediction is achieved. The results of training of group A networks are briefly shown in Table 11.2.
