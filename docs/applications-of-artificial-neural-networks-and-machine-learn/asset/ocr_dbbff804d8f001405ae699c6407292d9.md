# 11.3.1 Evaluation of Learning and Performance of the Networks

If an ANN can presents acceptable response to the inputs present or not present in the training set, it is said that the ANN has learnt well and it has good performance. This state is evaluated by various mathematical methods. In the following two important parameters for the measurement of learning and performance of ANNs are described.

# 11.3.1.1 Root Mean Square of Errors (RMS)

RMS error is the square root of sum of squares of errors between actual and desired outputs of the network. This parameter is considered as target function in back propagation network and the weights of the network are considered as function variable. During the training of a network, RMS reduction indicates the progress in network learning. As the RMS is reduced, the network learns better and gives more accurate answers to the existent inputs in the training set, but this procedure does not indicate that the network can give accurate answers to the nonexistent inputs in the training set. In other word, it is possible that value of RMS is very little, but the network can’t present acceptable answers to new inputs. On the other hand, if RMS is reduced more than required limit, it is possible that the network recites the existent data in the training set and loses the ability of generalization and response to new inputs; so one cannot absolutely certify the good learning and performance of a network according to low RSM value.

# 11.3.1.2 Correlation Factor

Harmonic variation of two variables is called correlation. When two variables change in agreement with each other and in the same direction, it is said that they have positive correlation, e.g. size and weight of some people have positive correlation, because it is very probable that somebody who is taller is heavier, vice versa if two variables change in agreement with each other but in opposite directions, they have negative correlation. Finally if variables don’t change in agreement with each other, they called uncorrelated. Correlation factor can be calculated according to Eq. (11.4).

```
r(x, y) = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{[\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2 \sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2]^{1/2}} (11.4)
```

That:
x̄: Average of x variables
ȳ: Average of y variables.
