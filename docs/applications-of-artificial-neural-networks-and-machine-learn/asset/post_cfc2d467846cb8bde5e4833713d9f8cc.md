## 22.6 Conclusions

The robustness of group method of data handling (GMDH) and multivariate adaptive regression splines (MARS) as alternative approaches was investigated and assessed in prediction of the shear strength of slender HSC reinforced concrete beams. An available database including 250 experimental tests of shear strength has been employed to develop the MARS and GMDH model. The proposed models related the shear strength to longitudinal steel ratio, the shear span-to-depth ratio, compressive strength of concrete, the size of the beam specimens, and the size of coarse aggregate. The predictive abilities of GMDH and MARS models were examined by comparing their predictions with those obtained from the most common formulae and MNLR regression approach. - Among the existing equations in literature, there is a good agreement between measured and predicted maximum shear strength using AS 3600-2009 equation. However, a notable number of its predictions were categorized in unsafe region based on Collins criteria. In contrast, the CEB-FIP model represented the safest predictions according to this criterion while its accuracy is remarkably limited.

- The newly proposed GMDH and MARS models outperformed the existing design equations in terms of both accuracy and safety. The errors of developed GMDH and MARS models also showed a symmetrical and predictable behavior. The MARS model also outperformed GMDH and MNLR models in both accuracy and safety aspects.

- The results of statistical measures show that the proposed MARS model outperformed the other design equations in literature. The proposed models yield an R² = 0.94 and RMSE = 0.18 kN, this represented a 65.5% improvement in RMSE and 88% improvement in term of R² in respect to AS 3600-2009 equation as the best equation among the other design equations.

- The sensitivity analysis based on GMDH and MARS models indicated that shear capacity is mostly affected by effective depth whereas the aggregate size-to-depth ratio holds the least importance. The longitudinal steel ratio was the second-important parameter and followed by the compressive strength of concrete and the shear span-to-depth ratio.

- The robustness of GMDH and MARS models in capturing the underlying physical behaviors of shear strength was verified through parametric study. Results of parametric analysis were also confirmed the outcomes of sensitivity analysis.

In general, results indicated that the GMDH and MARS algorithms can be successfully used as reliable alternative approaches to predict shear strength of HSC slender beams without web reinforcement.
