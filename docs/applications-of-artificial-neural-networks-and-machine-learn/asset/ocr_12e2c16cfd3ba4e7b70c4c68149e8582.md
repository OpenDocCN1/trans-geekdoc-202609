It has been found that several mechanisms including the shear transfer in the compression zone, aggregate interlock across crack face, stirrups crossing the shear crack, and dowel action of longitudinal reinforcing bars crossing the crack can be involved to provide shear resistance of RC beams [3]. In beams without web reinforcing stirrups, crossing the shear crack mechanism cannot be considered as internal contributing forces to shear resistance. It has been found that the shear failure mechanism in concrete members reinforced longitudinally and but without transverse reinforcement under bending varies significantly and the size of member and shear span-to-depth ratio are the two main parameters that influence on the shear failure mechanism. In fact, the shear span to depth ratio (a/d) determines the failure modes of RC beams and divides them into deep beams (<2.5) and slender beams (>2.5). For deep beams with a/d approximately less than 2.5, the arch action occurs [4]. For the slender beams with a/d greater than 2.5, the shear strength of reinforced member with longitudinal steel bars is a function of a/d as well. For these members, a/d represents the interacting effect of the moment and shear at a section on the shear strength. Accurate prediction of the shear behavior of reinforced concrete (RC) beams unlike to flexural behavior, which can generally be predicted well, is a challenging work due to the complexity of the shear transfer mechanism.

Furthermore, there are remarkable differences between NSC and HSC beams without web reinforcing [5–10]. It has been reported that the fracture surface in HSC beams is smooth and develops along the transition zone between the matrix and aggregates whereas it is rough in NSC beams. Also internal shear transferring mechanisms show different contributions for different concrete strengths. At higher concrete strengths, the aggregate interlocking does not contribute greatly because of the smooth fracture planes and the straight cracks, which do not go around the aggregate particles [11, 12].

Shear strength prediction of HSC slender beams without web reinforcement is still contentious. There is no unified rational theory explaining the interaction of the three internal contributing forces to shear resistance, especially for HSC. Various design equations are available to estimate shear strength of reinforced concrete beams without stirrups. Their accuracy, however, seems limited for slender reinforced HSC beams as these equations were empirically developed using predefined forms and with experimental data mainly generated for a limited number of influential parameters.

Recently, machine learning approaches have been successfully employed to overcome these limitations in many different problems of civil engineering (e.g. [13–23]). In this regard, particularly, Elsanadedy et al. [12] very recently applied regression models and neural networks for predicting the shear strength of HSC slender RC beams without stirrups. Their results showed that ANN approach generally provides better predictions than regression approach in term of accuracy. However, ANN implementation suffers from several drawbacks.
