
## Chapter 11 Estimation of the Vulnerability of the Concrete Structures Using Artificial Neural Networks

### Abstract

In this study, the aim is to use the combination of meta-heuristic algorithm with two different types of artificial neural network structure to optimize the parameters of the feed forward backpropagation and radial basis function networks. In this chapter, Particle swarm optimization (PSO), Genetic algorithm (GA), Colliding bodies optimization (CBO), Enhanced colliding bodies optimization (ECBO) algorithms are used to combine with ANNs.

### 11.1 Introduction

This chapter is based on the research by Kaveh and Khavaninzadeh [1]. Damages in concrete structures are generally induced by different factors such as improper construction management and maintenance, overloading, corrosive environment and earthquake loads. Among these factors, earthquake needs special attention since it is the most destructive factor and its destruction mechanism is of different type, because it can impose extra damage to structures within few seconds. Many researchers have applied different methods for damage detection based on dynamic characteristics of structures. Rytter [2] distinguishes four levels of damage identification: level 1. (Detection) the method gives a qualitative indication that damage might be present in the structure; level 2. (Localization) the method also gives information about the probable position of the damage; level 3. (Assessment) the method gives an estimate of the extent of the damage; level 4. (Consequence) the method offers information about the safety of the structure, e.g., estimate a residual life. Doebling and Farrar [3] classified the structural damages in two categories: linear and nonlinear. Linear damage indicates that a structure with linear elastic behavior keeps its linear-elastic behavior after the damage. In this type of damage, changes in characteristics of the structure can be defined based on the geometrical and mechanical specifications of the structure. Nonlinear damage indicates that a structure with linear elastic behavior, shows nonlinear behavior after the damage. Marvala and Hunt [4] presented a committee of neural networks technique, which employs frequency response function and modal data simultaneously to identify faults in structures. They tested the method on simulated data from a cantilever beam. Worden [5] applied neural network to diagnose damage in a simple simulated lumped-parameter mechanical system. It was shown that the system transmissibility provides a sensitive feature for the detection of small stiffness changes. Xia et al. [6] proposed a statistical method with combined uncertain frequency and mode shape data for structural damage identification. They updated a finite element model by comparing the measured vibration data before damage or analytical finite element model of the undamaged structure with those measured after damage, so that the vibration characteristic changes of the finite element model are equal to the changes in the measured data as closely as possible. They applied the proposed method to a laboratory tested steel cantilever beam and frame structure. Hung and Kao [7] presented a two-steps approach for damage detection. The first step is system identification, and involves using neural system identification networks (NSINs) in order to identify the undamaged and damaged states of a structural system. The partial derivatives of the outputs with respect to the inputs of the NSIN, which identifies the system in a certain undamaged or damaged state, have a negligible variation with different system errors. This loosely defined unique property enables these partial derivatives to quantitatively indicate system damage from the model parameters. The second step, structural damage detection, involves using the Neural Damage Detection Network (NDDN) to detect the location and extent of the structural damage. The input to the NDDN is taken as the aforementioned partial derivatives of NSIN, and the output of the NDDN identifies the damage level for each member in the structure.

Huang et al. [8] identified the dynamic characteristics of a building and diagnosed whether earthquakes have damaged the building, using a back-propagation neural network approach. The dynamic characteristics were directly evaluated from the weighting matrices of the neural network trained by observed acceleration responses and input base excitation. Whether the building is damaged under a large earthquake is assessed by comparing the modal parameters and responses for this large earthquake with those for a small earthquake that has not caused this building any damage. They demonstrated the feasibility of the approach through processing the dynamic responses of a five-story steel frame, subjected to different strengths of the Kobe earthquake, in shaking table tests. Tsai and Hsu [9] employed displacement time history of the existing structures and back-propagation neural network technique to assess the severity and location of defects for reinforced concrete structures. They presented the theoretical analysis of a simply-supported reinforced concrete beam with assumed defects by a finite-element program was applied to generate training and testing examples which were needed for neural network assessing task. Examples were generated according to the displacement time history of the defected beams due to a dynamic force at the center of the beam. Kirkegaard and Rytter [10] considered the potential of using back-propagation neural network for damage assessment of a free-free cracked straight steel beam based on vibration measurements. Ziemianski and Piatkowski [11] applied neural networks for damage detection in rod structures. The identification method was based on the analysis of propagation of wave in solids. In order to simulate wave propagation in a rod element, they used finite element method. Zang and Imregun [12] employed measured frequency response functions (FRFs) as input data to artificial neural networks, for structural damage detection. They applied the methodology to the measured FRFs of a railway wheel. Harpula and Ziemianski [13] applied neural networks for damage detection in bar structures based on changes of dynamic characteristics of an 8-storey steel structure model. They simulated crack damage by changes of stiffness of columns.

In this research the damage index defined by Park and Ang [14] is used in order to assess the seismic damage of reinforced concrete structures quantitatively. Then the collection of calculated damage indices, obtained from nonlinear dynamic analyses, is used for training the required neural networks for predicting structural damages.

### 11.2 Nonlinear Dynamic Analysis and Damage Assessment

In order to estimate the seismic damage of the structures quantitatively, an appropriate damage assessment method is needed. For this purpose, several damage indices have been presented by researchers. Park and Ang [14] damage index is one of the most suitable indices for evaluating the seismic damage of concrete structures. This index is defined as follows:

D.I. PA = \frac{\delta_m}{\delta_u} + \frac{\beta}{\delta_u F_y} \int dE (11.1)

In the above equation:

- D.I. PA Park and Ang Damage Index
- \delta_m Maximum deformation of structure during an earthquake
- \delta_u Ultimate deformation capacity of structure under monotonic loading
- \beta Dimensionless strength reduction parameter
- \int dE Hysteretic energy absorbed by structure during response history

Park and Ang damage index is calibrated as follow:

- 0 \le D.I. PA < 0.4 Represents repairable damage
- 0.4 \le D.I. PA < 1.0 Represents damage beyond repair
- 1.0 \le D.I. PA Represents total collapse of structure.

In order to perform dynamic analyses, IDARC program is employed. This program is capable of calculating the Park and Ang damage index for structures. Park and Ang damage index can be obtained either for a structural element (local damage index), or for the entire structure (global damage index). In this research, the global damage indices are calculated for the structures.
