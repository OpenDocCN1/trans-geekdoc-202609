Debonding process is very complex due to several preeminent parameters involved in this process including mechanical properties of masonry blocks, mortar joints, adhesive, and FRP reinforcement. This mechanism involves the presence of cracks a few millimeters underneath the bond line inside masonry units [10, 11]. In fact, FRP debonding will initiate in the substrate if bond strength directly depends on the tensile strength of masonry units.

Various experimental studies have been conducted to investigate the effect of different influential parameters on debonding failure and a number of empirical and analytical models for predicting debonding failure have been developed. As examples of such studies one can refer to [12–22]. However, due to the complexity and the brittle characteristics of debonding failure mechanism, a remarkable scatter has been observed between predicted maximum bond strength and the measured ones [23]. Many of these models are highly empirical, and their predictive abilities are limited by the corresponding data sets from which they were derived, and do not provide a reliable prediction of maximum transferable load. Recently, soft computing methods have been successfully used to develop predictive models for different problems in civil engineering [24–30]. In particular Mansouri and Kisi [23] evaluated the applications of neuro fuzzy and neural network approaches for estimation of debonding strength for masonry elements retrofitted with FRP composites using eight available experimental datasets consisting of altogether 109 data points. Gene expression programming (GEP) has been used by Mansouri et al. [31] to predict the debonding strength of retrofitted masonry members using ten available experimental databases [22] consisting of 134 data points. Results showed that ANN and ANFIS approaches can be successfully used to predict the bond strength. However, these methods do not give sufficient insight into the generated models and are not as easy to use as the empirical formulas. Although GEP has successfully been used to predict the bond strength and results in an explicit formulation of maximum load but it is well known that compiling more comprehensive database is requisite in generating exact predictive models.

The main purpose of this study is to employ M5′ and MARS algorithms to develop transparent models to predict maximum bond strength and determine the most effective parameters. For this aim a new comprehensive database collected in the present study from different sources in literature (231 data points from 23 available experimental studies). The M5′ model tree is a robust data-based method that provides understandable formulas that allows users to have more insight in the physics of the phenomenon [32, 33]. The MARS algorithm is also known as a self-organized predictive approach that can discover complex behaviors between input and output parameters and determine the most effective parameters in predicting maximum bond strength [34]. Five different predictive variables that characterize the mechanical and geometrical properties related to FRP rods and support including the reinforcement width, ratio between the widths of FRP reinforcement and masonry unit, tensile strength of support, axial strength of reinforcement and bond length are considered as input variables.
