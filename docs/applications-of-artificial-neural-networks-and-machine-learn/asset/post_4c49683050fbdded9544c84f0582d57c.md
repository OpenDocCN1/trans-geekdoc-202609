
\( K = \sum_{i=1}^{NE} k_i \) (7.9)

where NE is the number of finite elements in the structure. P and U are the load vector and displacement vector, respectively. The symmetric stiffness matrix is a function of the design variables defining the structure. The load vector P may also be a function of design variables if we include, for example, self-weight of the structures. Solving for displacement as

\(\mathbf{U} = \mathbf{K}^{-1} \cdot \mathbf{P}\) (7.10)

The element stiffness matrix \( k_i \) is created from the stiffness

\( k_i = T^T k_i^o T \) (7.11)

Matrix \( k_i^o \) in the element's local coordinate system and a geometric transformation matrix T.

The displacements associated with each element can be transformed to the local co-ordinate system by

\( u^0 = Tu \) (7.12)

The element forces are

\( f = k_i^0 u^0 \) (7.13)

Finally, the stress constraints are computed from these forces. Differentiating Eq. (7.8) implicitly with respect to design variable Xi, i = 1, 2, ..., n.

\(\frac{\partial K}{\partial X_i} U + K \frac{\partial U}{\partial X_i} = \frac{\partial P}{\partial X_i}\) (7.14)

or

\(\frac{\partial U}{\partial X_i} = K^{-1} \cdot \left[ \frac{\partial P}{\partial X_i} - \frac{\partial K}{\partial X_i} U \right]\) (7.15)

Assuming the loads to be independent of Xi, the first term in the brackets becomes zero. Also, from Eq. (7.9),

\(\frac{\partial K}{\partial X_i} = \sum_{j=1}^{NE} \frac{\partial k_j}{\partial X_i}\) (7.16)

Once the gradients of displacements with respect to design variable Xi are obtained, these are used at the element level to calculate the gradient of forces and finally stresses with respect to Xi. As an example, for the first element of a truss, the following two constraints are imposed

\[
g_1 = \frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma^+} - 1 \quad (7.17)
\]
\[
g_2 = \frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma^-} - 1 \quad (7.18)
\]

where \(\sigma^+\) and \(\sigma^-\) are the limiting tension and compression stresses. The gradient of the constraint with respect to the design variable Xi is computed as follows:

\[
\frac{\partial u_1^0}{\partial X_1} = T \frac{\partial u_1}{\partial X_1} \quad (7.19)
\]

Now, the gradient of constraints of the first element with respect to design variable \( X_1 \) is obtained:

\[
f_1 = \frac{A_1 E}{L_1} u_1^0 \quad (7.20)
\]
\[
\frac{\partial f_1}{\partial X_1} = \frac{f_1}{A_1} + \frac{A_1 E}{L_1} \times \frac{\partial u_1^0}{\partial X_1} \quad (7.21)
\]
\[
\frac{\partial \sigma_1}{\partial X_1} = \frac{\partial}{\partial X_1} \frac{f_1}{A_1} = \frac{1}{A_1} \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial X_1} - \frac{f_1}{A_1^2} \quad (7.22)
\]

This procedure is automated in the developed program in order to compute all constraints and their relevant gradients.

### 7.4 The Structure of Neuro-Optimizer

The basic idea of neuro-optimizer is to extend the range of applicability of ANN to structural optimization by employing CPN. The strategy consists of three phases. In the first phase, the structural design variables are generated randomly in the user-specified domain of search. The constraints and the relevant gradients are computed analytically for each set of generated design variables. In the neurocomputing phase, two CPNs are trained on such data (i.e., the constraints and the relevant gradients). Weight matrices are computed automatically in the training process. In the final phase, neurocomputing models are used as a fast interpolator to predict the constraints and the relevant gradients in the optimization process in order to minimize the computation time.
