
This study aims to develop machine learning-based models that can predict the ultimate strength of I- and H-shaped sections under compressive loading with consideration of initial imperfection accurately and reliably. Four ML methods, namely Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Colliding Body Optimization (CBO), and their developed version by applying Genetic Algorithm (GA), Particle Swarm Optimization–Genetic Algorithm (PSO-GA), and Colliding Body Optimization–Genetic Algorithm (CBO-GA), are implemented in this study to train ANNs efficiently. Firstly, a finite element method (FEM) model was created using ABAQUS software for proof of concept in FEM Modeling; then, a database consisting of 114 FEM specimens was created, and their statistical characteristics were analyzed. Secondly, the fundamentals of the four adopted ML methods were briefly presented to illustrate how they solve complex regression problems. Thirdly, a model training procedure was implemented to obtain the optimized and trained models. Finally, the performance of the trained ML models was evaluated and compared.

## 2.3 Metaheuristics-Trained Neural Network Model

Metaheuristics are a class of algorithms employed to solve complex optimization problems that are complicated to solve operating traditional optimization techniques [10]. Unlike exact optimization methods, metaheuristics do not guarantee the optimal solution but aim to find a suitable solution within a reasonable amount of time. Meta-heuristics are especially beneficial for solving large-scale optimization problems with a large number of variables and constraints. Metaheuristics are inspired by natural and physical phenomena such as genetic evolution, swarm behavior, and simulated annealing. These algorithms use various techniques such as randomization, heuristic search, and local search to explore the search space and find a solution that satisfies the objective function. There exist many optimization algorithms, while Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Colliding Body Optimization (CBO), and their developed versions, which are equipped with Genetic Algorithm (GA), are considered in this work.

### 2.3.1 Particle Swarm Optimization

Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) is a popular computational technique used in optimization problems [11]. PSO is based on the behavior of social organisms such as birds, fish, and insects. In PSO, a set of particles is used to search for the optimal solution in a given search space. Each particle represents a potential solution, and its movement in the search space is guided by the best-performing particle in the swarm and its own past movements. The goal of PSO is to find the global optimum by iteratively updating the position and velocity of each particle based on its own and the swarm’s best performance. It is a robust and efficient technique that can handle both convex and non-convex optimization problems with multiple local optima.

### 2.3.2 Colliding Body Optimization

Colliding Body Optimization (CBO) is a relatively new optimization algorithm that has gained significant attention in recent years [12]. The algorithm is inspired by the physics of collisions, where particles in a system interact with each other by colliding and exchanging energy. CBO uses this concept to find optimal solutions to complex optimization problems by simulating collisions between a set of bodies. Each body represents a solution in the search space, and the collision between bodies corresponds to a new solution that is a combination of their features. This approach makes CBO highly effective in solving optimization problems that involve multiple objectives or constraints.

### 2.3.3 Genetic Algorithm (GA)

A Genetic Algorithm (GA) is an optimization technique that draws inspiration from natural selection and genetics [13]. It is a heuristic method that is commonly employed to find the best solutions for problems with a large search space and intricate fitness functions. The algorithm operates on a population of potential solutions, and it uses genetic operators like selection, crossover, and mutation to produce new generations of solutions that more closely approach the optimal solution. GA has been successfully applied in numerous fields, including engineering, finance, and computer science, and is recognized as a powerful tool for solving complex optimization problems.
