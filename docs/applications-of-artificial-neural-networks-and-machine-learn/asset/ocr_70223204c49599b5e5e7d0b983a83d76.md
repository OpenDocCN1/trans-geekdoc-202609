# Chapter 22
Development of Predictive Models for Shear Strength of HSC Slender Beams Without Web Reinforcement Using Machine-Learning-Based Techniques

Abstract The present chapter presents two robust methods to predict bond strength between FRP reinforcement and masonry units. The M5’ algorithm as a rule based method is used to develop practical equations for estimating bond strength. The MAR S algorithm besides its high predictive ability is used to determine the most important parameters in predicting bond strength. To develop the models, a comprehensive database including 575 test series is collected from different sources in literature for the first time. The reinforcement width, ratio between widths of FRP reinforcement and masonry unit, tensile strength of support, axial strength of reinforcement and bond length are the five influential parameters considered to estimate the maximum bond strength.

# 22.1 Introduction

This chapter is based on the predictive models for shear strength of HSC slender beams without web reinforcement using machine-learning based techniques developed by Ref. [1]. In the last 30 years, the application of high strength concrete (HSC) have been remarkably increased in field due to significant development in building and materials technology. Several advantages including having better mechanical properties, structural efficiency and economically beneficial in terms of both structural and aesthetic aspects made the HSC as a widely used material in the construction of high-rise buildings and long-span bridges in all over the world. The definition of HSC has been changed parallel to the developments of its properties in recent years. HSC can be defined as a concrete with compressive strength significantly beyond and more than double from what is used in normal practice (normal strength concrete, NSC). Different design codes and researchers stated different limits for compressive strength of concrete for demarcating between NSC and HSC materials. In the present study, the definition of HSC by ACI 363R-10 [2], which is defined as a concrete with compressive strength more than 40 MPa, has been followed).
