The histograms of discrepancy ratio between measured and predicted maximum bond strength ($DR = F_{max,meas}/F_{max,pre}$) by equations of Tanaka [12], Yang et al. [18], Neubaer and Rostasy [14], Khalifa et al. [15], and the proposed ones (M5’ and MARS) are illustrated in Fig. 20.7. The errors of a good predictive equation should be close to one and also be symmetrical around their mean values. A wider distribution generally leads to more uncertainty. As can be seen from Fig. 20.7, the distribution errors of mentioned empirical equations are remarkably wide and their predictions generally underestimate the values of maximum bond strength. On the contrary, the distribution errors of proposed M5’ and MARS models concentrate around 1 and these just slightly underestimate the bond strength.

The variations of DR values of different empirical equations and the proposed ones as a function of axial rigidity ($E_pt_p$) are shown in Fig. 20.8. A good model has errors that are independent of the input parameters [61]. Tanaka [12], Yang et al. [18], Neubaer and Rostasy [14], and Khalifa et al. [15] formulas are very sensitive to change of $E_pt_p$. However, according to Fig. 20.8, the model trees had a better performance than the others in this aspect. In addition, this figure shows that $E_pt_p$ is included in a better way in the developed models. A similar process has also been observed (not shown) for other input parameters, such as bond length, the width of reinforcement, the ration between the width of reinforcement and support, and the tensile strength.
