In the present chapter, a fast computing learning algorithm namely as Kernel Extreme Learning Machine (KELM) is employed to predict bond strength between FRP plates and concrete substrates for the first time. To the best knowledge of authors, the ELM has not been used as predictive method in structural engineering domain. Several advantages of ELM method in comparison with neural network method are reported in literature. For instance, the intervention of humans in developing process of algorithm is the least in comparison with other neural networks [33]. The unknown function between input and output variables in ELM algorithm is estimated based on single-layer feed-forward (SLFN) architecture. Unlike other conventional methods of neural networks, the output weights of SLFN are determined analytically by ELM theory. Favorable universal capability, fast learning speed and suitability for activation and kernel functions are the other pros of ELM method [34]. In order to develop KELM models for bond strength, a comprehensive datasets containing 342 experimental data was collected for the first time from different sources in literature. The relative importance of significant parameters dealing with bond strength of FRP-reinforced members is investigated through sensitivity analysis done by Gamma test. Furthermore, in the present study, the ANN and ANFIS models are also developed to evaluate the robustness of KELM model in aspects of performance and physical analyses. The robustness of proposed KELM model was also verified through a parametric analysis.

# 21.2 Shear Bond Strength Between FRP and Concrete Substrate

Debonding of the FRP reinforcement due to shear stresses is often one of the crucial failure modes of composite elements, which externally bonded with FRP. The externally bond strength between FRP and concrete substrate have been tested and analyzed generally on the basis of a single pull-off test. In the test, the direct tensile force P_app is applied to the FRP plate bonded to concrete substrate in order to determine the maximum transferable load (P_max). A sketch of single pull-off test for FRP to concrete is presented in Fig. 21.1. The shear bond strength is generally estimated as the maximum transferable load by applying a coupled model of a shear stress-tangential slip law and normal stress-displacement law [35].
