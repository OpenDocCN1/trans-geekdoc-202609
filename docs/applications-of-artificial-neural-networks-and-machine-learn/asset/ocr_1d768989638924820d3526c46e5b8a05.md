The mean field equation for bisection is given by:

$$v_i = \tanh \left( \sum_{j=1}^N (t_{ij} - \alpha) \frac{v_i}{T} \right).$$ (17.4)

where $T$ is the temperature. This equation is used to compute a new value of u\(_i\) asynchronously. Initial values for the elements in vector V are assigned using small continuous random values. The temperature is lowered using a cooling factor (0.90) after one complete iteration of Eq. (17.4).

At the beginning of the optimization process, the spines (state of neurons) average stay at or near zero indicating that the bisection is maximally disordered. As the temperature is decreased, the system reaches a critical temperature where the spines begin to converge, causing each element of the mesh to move into one or another of the two bisections. At sufficiently low temperatures, the state of each neuron reaches a value close to +1 or -1, indicating the stable state of the system being reached. This state indicates the fulfillment of the bisection criteria.

For different sets of initial parameters, different bisections may be obtained. However, if the sets are close to each other, then the number of interface nodes and the number of elements in each bisection will not differ much.
