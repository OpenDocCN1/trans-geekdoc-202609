The $ald$ ratio determines the behavior of deep and slender beams and also the failure modes of them. In deep beams, it can be expected that marginal enhancement of resistance occurs by the beam beyond first diagonal crack due to arch action with redistribution of stresses. On the other hand, failure occurs after the first diagonal crack in slender beams. It indicates that there is possible meager reserve strength beyond diagonal cracking. The similar effect of ald ratio on shear strength of RC beams also reported in literature.

The effect of longitudinal steel ratio ($\rho$) on shear strength is presented in Fig. 22.4c. It can be seen that shear strength increases as the percentage of longitudinal reinforcement increases. This contribution of longitudinal reinforcement in increasing shear resistance can be attributed to dowel action and can be about 30% in slender member like slabs. It should be noted that in HSC beams, it demands higher percentage of longitudinal reinforcement to enhance the dowel force than beams made of normal strength concrete (NSC).

From Fig. 22.4d, it can be seen that the compressive strength of concrete increases the shear strength when all other parameters are constant. The shear strength in RC slender beams significantly depends on the diagonal cracking strength. The diagonal cracking strength of a NSC beam without web reinforcement often expresses as a function of square root of compressive strength of concrete [27]. However, the shear strength of RC beams made of HSC tends to vary differently from the square root of compressive strength of concrete. From these observations, it can be concluded that the developed GMDH and MARS models are in good agreement with the physical characteristics of shear strength phenomenon and previous findings.

The data used in parametric analysis were also employed to explore and quantify the relative importance of model inputs to its output, by measuring the effects on the output when the inputs are varied between their minimum and maximum in the dataset used. The quantification of this process was determined using data obtained from holding all input variables at their average values except one output that was varied between its ranges ($x_i$ {${x_1, x_2, ..., x_n}$}). The output $y_i$, for $n$ levels of particular input, $x_i$, was used to identify which variables have more influences using the sensitivity measure, $S_g$, of the average gradient over all intervals, as follows [40]:

```
$S_g = \sum_{i=1}^{n} |y_i - y_{i+1}| / (n - 1)$ (22.26)
```

In fact, this approach investigates how output would change if the variables in the trained MARS and GMDH models are perturbed in their ranges.
