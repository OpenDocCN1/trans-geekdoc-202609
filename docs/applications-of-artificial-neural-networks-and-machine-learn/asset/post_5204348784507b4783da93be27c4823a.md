In this chapter a genetic algorithm is utilized for optimization of transmission towers. The energy method is combined with the force method to reduce the design unknowns and also to remove the need for the operation corresponding to matrix inversion. In transmission towers with small number of elements this algorithm is easily employed, however, for the towers encountered in practice which contain many elements, this reduces the speed of the computation. In order to overcome this difficulty neural networks are trained to take part of the computational load as an analyzer. Examples are provided to illustrate the important role of the neural networks in reducing the computational time.

## 15.2 Loads and Design

The ultimate strength design philosophy is used for transmission structures. Therefore, all the strength design equations for transmission structure components refer to conditions near failure in yielding or buckling.

Design loads and load factors typically considered for transmission structures include legislated loads, additional climatic loads, security loads and construction and maintenance loads. Reference [1] provides a detailed discussion of available methods for selecting loads.

Legislated loads are those specified by state or local authorities [2]. The NESC (National Electric Standard Committee) specifies two load cases, a district load case with load factors ranging from 1.5 to 4 (depending on direction of load and material type), and an extreme wind case with a minimum load factor of 1. The combination of wind, ice and temperature of the district load case depends on the line location. The wind velocity of the extreme wind case is selected from a contour map of 50-year return period winds. The magnitude of the loads and load factors specified by the NESC are based on experience, and it is good practice to consider additional climatic load case to control the reliability of a line [1, 3].

Combinations of extreme wind, wind and ice, or extreme ice alone with the coincident temperatures are also considered with the appropriate load factors. Other loads consist of Security loads, Construction/Maintenance loads, Galloping and Vibration.

Steel latticed towers for alternating-current electrical systems are normally designed to carry one or two circuits. Where right-of-way is restricted, three or more circuits can be used. Each circuit consists of three electrical phases. For line over 100 miles long, direct current systems are also used. Structures for these systems support two phases. In all areas except those that have no incidence of lightning, the line must be shielded by ground wires.

The design tension stress on the net section of concentrically loaded members is taken to be the yield stress \(F_y\), but if the bolted connection is eccentric to the centroid of an angle, the design stress on the net section is \(0.9F_y\), Refs. [4–7].

The design compression stress \(F_a\) on the gross section of angles in compression recommended in Ref. [5] is given by:

For \(KL/r < Cc \quad F_a = \left[ 1 - \frac{(KL/r)^2}{2Cc^2} \right] F_y \quad (15.1)\)

For \(KL/r > Cc \quad F_a = \frac{\pi^2 E}{(KL/r)^2} \quad (15.2)\)

The magnitude of \(Cc\) can be calculated from the following relationship:

\(Cc = \pi \sqrt{\frac{2E}{F_y}} \quad (15.3)\)

The above relationships can be used when the ratio of the width to thickness of the members do not exceed the following specified values.

\(\left( \frac{w}{t} \right)_{lim} = \frac{80}{\sqrt{F_y}} \quad (15.4)\)

\(E = 29,000\) ksi Elastic modulus.

\(F_y\): Yield stress of steel in ksi.

\(\frac{KL}{r}\): Maximum slenderness ratio of the unbraced member.

When \(w/t\) exceeds the above mentioned value, the following relationships are used:

If \(\left( \frac{w}{t} \right)_{lim} < \frac{w}{t} < \frac{144}{\sqrt{F_y}} \quad F_{cr} = \left[ 1.677 - 0.677 \frac{w/t}{(w/t)_{lim}} \right] F_y \quad (15.5)\)

If \(\frac{b}{t} > \frac{992}{\sqrt{F_y}} \quad F_{cr} = \frac{0.0332\pi^2 E}{(w/t)^2} \quad (15.6)\)

The magnitudes of \(KL/r\) for different end conditions of the members can be found in [5].

## 15.3 Optimal Design of Towers

In this chapter the genetic algorithm is used for optimal design. For optimization of small towers one can use the force method together with energy approach as described in [8]. Using energy, force method and genetic algorithms result in eliminating the need for the formation of the inverse of matrices. For large scale towers such a mixed method can be slow and occasionally leads to local optima. In order to speed up the process of analysis, here a neural network is utilized. Also the genetic algorithm employed, is migration genetic algorithm used which is more suitable than simple GA for large scale problems. For towers with small number of elements the first method is used and for the towers with high number of members the second approach is employed. In the following the formulation for both methods is presented.

First a short introduction is provided for the energy methods. The nonlinear behaviour of the material is modeled as ε = f(σ) or σ = g(ε) representing the stress strain relationship. The strain energy, complementary strain energy and total potential energy can be represented as:

```
U = ∬ g(ε)dεdV, Uᶜ = ∬ f(σ)dσdV (15.7)
V = U - P′u (15.8)
```

where U is the strain energy, Uᶜ is the complementary strain energy, V is the total potential energy, P is the applied load vector and u is the nodal displacements vector. Considering the first Castigliano's theorem for an elastic (linear or non-linear) system the potential energy is minimum for each system in equilibrium. Similarly in the second theorem, if the internal forces satisfy the compatibility, then the complementary energy becomes minimum. In general, U corresponds to the stiffness method and Uᶜ corresponds to the flexibility approach. In the first method we look for the displacements while in the latter we seek the unknown forces. In a statically indeterminate structure once the unknown forces are calculated the remaining member forces can easily be obtained. The use of Uᶜ corresponds to smaller number of unknowns and in what follows the force method will be employed.

Suppose {p} = {p₁, p₂, ..., pₙ}ᵗ is the vector of nodal forces, {q} = {q₁, q₂, ..., qₘ}ᵗ contains m redundant forces, and {r} = {r₁, r₂, ..., rⱼ}ᵗ comprises of the internal forces of the members. From equilibrium:

```
{r} = [B₀]{p} + [B₁]{q} = [B₀ B₁] [p/q] (15.9)
Uᶜ = 1/2 {r}′[Fₘ]{r} (15.10)
```

where [Fₘ] is the unassembled flexibility matrix of the structure, [B₀]{p} is the particular solution, and [B₁]{q} is the complementary solution. Now {q} should be calculated such that the complementary energy, \( U^c \), becomes minimum. Substituting {r} from Eq. (15.9) into Eq. (15.10) leads to:

[\mathbf{H}] = [\mathbf{B}_0 \ \mathbf{B}_1]' [\mathbf{F}_m] [\mathbf{B}_0 \ \mathbf{B}_1], \quad U^c = \frac{1}{2} [\mathbf{p} \ \mathbf{q}]' [\mathbf{H}] \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{p} \\ \mathbf{q} \end{bmatrix} (15.11)

Decomposing the matrix [H] into four submatrices [Hqq], [Hqp], [Hpq], and [Hpp], the complementary energy is obtained as:

\( U^c = \frac{1}{2} (\{p\}' [\mathbf{H}_{pp}] \{p\} + \{p\}' [\mathbf{H}_{pq}] \{q\} + \{q\}' [\mathbf{H}_{qp}] \{p\} + \{q\}' [\mathbf{H}_{qq}] \{q\}) (15.12) \)

In the classical method, the derivative of \( U^c \) with respect to {q} is found and equated to zero, leading to:

\( \{q\} = -[\mathbf{H}_{qq}]^{-1} [\mathbf{H}_{pq}] \{p\} (15.13) \)

Since [H] is symmetric, therefore [Hqp]' = [Hpq]. In the present approach, finding the inverse of [Hqq] is not required. Instead, \( U^c \) from Eq. (15.11) is minimized by genetic algorithm. The first term of \( U^c \) in Eq. (15.12) is constant and the second and third terms are equal, therefore

\( U^c = \frac{1}{2} (\{p\}' [\mathbf{H}_{pp}] \{p\} + \{q\}' [\mathbf{H}_{qp}] \{p\} + \{q\}' [\mathbf{H}_{qp}] \{p\} + \{q\}' [\mathbf{H}_{qq}] \{q\}) (15.14) \)

Substituting {q} from Eq. (15.9), the last two terms are eliminated and we have

\( U^c = \frac{1}{2} (\{p\}' [\mathbf{H}_{pp}] \{p\} + \{q\}' [\mathbf{H}_{qq}] \{p\}) (15.15) \)

Here we want to present a suitable formulation for finding minimum structural weight. For a structure in equilibrium and compatible condition, for a structure with linear behavior, the complementary energy \( U^c \) and strain energy \( U \) should be zero. Therefore the objective function is selected as

\( F = W(1 + \alpha(U + U^c)^2) (15.16) \)

Here \( \alpha \) is selected as a big number. It can be observed that the penalty function is selected such that its minimum value becomes zero. For calculating \( U \) if \( B_0 \) and \( B_1 \) corresponding to all degrees of freedom are constructed, then we have

\( \{d\} = [\mathbf{B}_0' \ \mathbf{B}_1'] [\mathbf{F}_m] ( [\mathbf{B}_0]\{p\} + [\mathbf{B}_1]\{q\} ) (15.17) \)

\( U = \frac{1}{2} \{d\}' [\mathbf{K}] \{d\} - \{d\}' \{F\} (15.18) \)
