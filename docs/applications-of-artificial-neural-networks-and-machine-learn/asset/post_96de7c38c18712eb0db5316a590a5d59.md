## Chapter 2

Buckling Resistance Prediction of High-Strength Steel Columns Using Metaheuristic-Trained Artificial Neural Networks

**Abstract** This chapter aims to develop practical metaheuristic-trained Artificial Neural Networks to predict the ultimate buckling load of High Strength Steel columns. The section height, flange width, web thickness, flange thickness, and steel yield strength are considered the input variables, while the ultimate buckling load was assumed to be the only output variable. ANN models are trained through algorithms such as LM, BR, and SCG; Four metaheuristic algorithms, namely Particle Swarm Optimization, Colliding Body Optimization, and their developed versions are used for efficient training of the ANNs.

### 2.1 Introduction

In this chapter buckling resistance prediction of high-strength steel columns is performed using metaheuristic-trained artificial neural networks [1]. The recent advances in steel production procedures have provided the engineering community with steel grades known for better characteristics, such as excellent toughness or high nominal yield stress (higher than 420 MPa), thus allowing for the greater use of high-strength steel (HSS). The use of HSS results in a significant decrease in the weight of structures; however, their stability requires to be thoroughly checked, particularly for compression elements where flexural buckling is commonly the major factor in design. The current design codes for HSS columns are based on experiments on normal-strength steel (NSS) materials because there were no tests done for HSS materials when the codes were established. As an example, EN 1993-1-12 (2007) regulations [2], which are for steel grade materials up to S700, rely on buckling resistance curves based on the Ayrton-Perry type formulation that was developed through an extensive experimental program for NSS members conducted by the European Convention for Constructional Steelwork (ECCS), theoretical formulations obtained from the analysis of experimental results, and a reliability assessment employing Monte Carlo simulations.

The present regulations in Eurocode 3 for HSS columns [2] are basically an extended version of the design rules for NSS columns, and the buckling resistance they provide may not be precisely applicable. HSS materials have distinct manufacturing methods and material properties that lead to various structural behaviors concerning material and geometric imperfections compared to NSS. The field of structural engineering has seen significant advancements in recent years, particularly in the application of machine learning methods to predict the behavior of various structural elements. Although the importance of experimental evaluation of HSS members is not negligible, in relation to recent developments in the field of machine learning (Especially ANNs), it has become possible to predict the behavior of a variety of natural phenomena, including buckling behavior or flexural buckling of columns and beam-columns.

Artificial neural networks (ANNs) are a branch of machine learning techniques that simulate the learning mechanism of biological organisms. ANNs are characterized by a large number of processing elements called neurons, which are interconnected through axons and dendrites forming synapses. ANNs resemble the structure of a human brain, consisting of many processing elements that can establish an accurate relation between some inputs and outputs of a given dataset for which no specific solution exists. ANNs are fairly efficient at finding complex relationships between influencing variables. They have been increasingly employed to solve complex and time-consuming problems in the field of structural engineering. Some of the pioneering works in employing ANN in structural engineering were carried out by Kaveh et al. [3], who utilized the prediction capability of neural networks in various fields of structures.

A number of studies have investigated the use of ANNs and other machine learning techniques to predict the behavior of structural elements. The use of ANN to predict the strength of structural components and the buckling columns when subjected to axial compression is a topic of ongoing research. However, there needs to be more research on utilizing ANN to forecast buckling loads from experimental data. This is mainly due to the requirement of a large amount of experimental data to train the network, which incurs significant costs. Nonetheless, Waszczyszyn and Bartezak [4] have successfully operated Backpropagation Neural Networks (BPNN) to forecast the buckling load of cylindrical shells under axial compression.

The experimental data of some previously published papers was utilized for training artificial neural networks using the Resilient Backpropagation learning algorithm to predict buckling load factor based on the imperfection data of shell specimens. Markopoulos et al. [5] used ANN with the Levenberg-Marquardt and Backpropagation algorithms as training functions to predict the collapse mode of PVC cylindrical shells under axial compression, using the shell thickness-to-diameter ratio and length-to-diameter ratio as inputs to the network. Ferreira et al. [6] focus on developing a prediction model for the lateral-torsional buckling resistance of steel cellular beams using ANNs. They demonstrated that ANNs are a promising tool for predicting lateral-torsional buckling resistance of steel cellular beams with high accuracy and efficiency. Also, Kaveh and Khavaninzadeh developed ANN-based models to predict FRP strength [7].
