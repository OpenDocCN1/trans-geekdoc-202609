## Chapter 15 Optimal Design of Transmission Towers Using Genetic Algorithm and Neural Networks

Abstract In this chapter a migration genetic algorithm is used for optimization of transmission towers. The energy method is combined with the force method to reduce the design unknowns and also to remove the need for the operation corresponding to matrix inversion. In towers with small number of elements this algorithm can easily be employed, however, for the towers encountered in practice which contain many elements, this reduces the speed of the computation. In order to overcome this difficulty neural networks are trained to take part of the computational load as an analyzer.

### 15.1 Introduction

This chapter is based on optimal design of transmission towers using genetic algorithm and neural networks developed by Kaveh et al. [1]. Transmission towers are the most important part of systems for transferring the electrical power and its distribution. These structures are designed to support the conductors and ground wires of electrical power lines. A linear analysis is generally sufficient for the analysis of a transmission tower. In these structures, the secondary or P-Δ effects are not expected to exceed a few percent of the primary load effects.

Transmission structures can be classified in many different ways. One classification, based on the insulator type is as follows:

A suspension structure supports the conductors with suspension insulators. There are no longitudinal loads if the cables are in an intact condition.

A strain structure is where the conductors are attached to the structure with strain insulators, i.e., the conductors are dead ended on the structure. Unlike true dead-ends, these structures are designed to support intact, basically equal, longitudinal cable tensions on both sides of the structure.
