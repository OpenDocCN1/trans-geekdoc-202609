# ## 1.1 Artificial Intelligence Background

The background of the question about artificial intelligence dates back to 1956, marked by the coining of the term “artificial intelligence” (AI) during a Dartmouth College conference. AI represents an interdisciplinary field shaped by the convergence of computer science, cybernetics, information theory, psychology, linguistics, and neurophysiology.

The overarching aim of the AI is to replicate and execute intelligent functions similar to the human brain, thereby enabling the creation of technological innovations and theories. The evolution of artificial intelligence can be divide to distinct stages: the 1950s saw the dawn and decline of AI, followed by a resurgence in the late 1960s to 1970s with the advent of expert systems. The 1980s witnessed significant advancements due to the emergence of the fifth-generation computers. Subsequently, the 1990s ushered in a new era of AI research characterized by the integration of network technologies, particularly the internet, leading to the explosion of distributed AI models. This era not only focused on goal-oriented distributed problem-solving but also delved into multi-agent problem-solving, rendering AI more pragmatic.

Moreover, the introduction of Hopfield’s multilayer neural network model catalyzed an upsurge in artificial neural network research across various spheres.
