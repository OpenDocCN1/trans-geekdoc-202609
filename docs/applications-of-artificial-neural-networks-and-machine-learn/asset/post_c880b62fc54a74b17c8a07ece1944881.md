## Chapter 1
Artificial Intelligence: Background, Applications and Future

**Abstract** In today’s world, which has witnessed unprecedented advances in technology and computer science, artificial intelligence has emerged as a top field captivating global attention. This technology, often referred to as AI, stands apart from other disciplines as it aims to design machines and systems that exhibit intelligence, learn autonomously, and make decisions akin to humans. Artificial intelligence introduces a transformative element to our reality, fostering significant breakthroughs and innovations.

### 1.1 Artificial Intelligence Background

The background of the question about artificial intelligence dates back to 1956, marked by the coining of the term “artificial intelligence” (AI) during a Dartmouth College conference. AI represents an interdisciplinary field shaped by the convergence of computer science, cybernetics, information theory, psychology, linguistics, and neurophysiology.

The overarching aim of AI is to replicate and execute intelligent functions similar to the human brain, thereby enabling the creation of technological innovations and theories. The evolution of artificial intelligence can be divided into distinct stages: the 1950s saw the dawn and decline of AI, followed by a resurgence in the late 1960s to 1970s with the advent of expert systems. The 1980s witnessed significant advancements due to the emergence of the fifth-generation computers. Subsequently, the 1990s ushered in a new era of AI research characterized by the integration of network technologies, particularly the internet, leading to the explosion of distributed AI models. This era not only focused on goal-oriented distributed problem-solving but also delved into multi-agent problem-solving, rendering AI more pragmatic.

Moreover, the introduction of Hopfield’s multilayer neural network model catalyzed an upsurge in artificial neural network research across various spheres. Key theories and methodologies in AI encompass symbolism, behaviorism, and connectionism. Since its inception, artificial intelligence has evolved from a niche domain to a cornerstone technology, poised for continuous development. The convergence of AI with cloud-based technologies, the Internet of Things, and 5G networks holds promise for enhanced possibilities and applications in the future, [1] and [2].

### 1.2 Types of Artificial Intelligence

### 1.2.1 Evolutionary Computation

Evolutionary computing (EC) is a subfield of artificial intelligence that uses an iterative process, often inspired by biological mechanisms of evolution, to evolve a population of solutions to achieve a desired goal. EC has been applied in the field of civil engineering for decades, mainly serving as an effective method for solving complex optimization problems [3].

### 1.2.2 Swarm Intelligence

A meta-heuristic based on swarm intelligence, which simulates a population of simple individuals that evolve their solutions by interacting with each other and with the environment, has shown promising performance on many difficult problems and in recent years, they have become a very active research field [3].

### 1.2.3 Fuzzy Systems

The term “fuzzy” means vague and unclear. In normal life, we sometimes face situations where we do not know which is the right or wrong decision and the correct action is hidden from our eyes. At this point, “fuzzy logic” offers a unique and valuable proposition. In this way, the amount of uncertainty can be determined for each situation. For this reason, fuzzy logic is sometimes called doubtful logic because its results are created with doubt. Fuzzy logic has helped to solve many decision-making problems today, so that most of the time, it produces the best decision based on the inputs. Fuzzy logic is based on human decision-making and can be seen as a development of “Aristotelian logic” or “Boolean logic”. Today, based on fuzzy logic, “fuzzy numbers”, “fuzzy calculations” and even fuzzy statistical methods such as fuzzy clustering have been created. Devices and computers have been produced that have far better performance than similar devices with Boolean logic. The concept and study of fuzzy logic began in 1920, but the term fuzzy logic was first used by Professor Lotfi Asgarzadeh (1921–2017) in 1965 at Berkeley University.

### 1.2.4 Expert System

An expert system relies on human experts based on the existing knowledge of the stored knowledge system. The expert system creates the first and most effective system in the field of artificial intelligence research. The expert system is widely used in roads and bridges, construction engineering, geotechnical engineering, underground engineering, disaster prevention projects, materials engineering, geological exploration and petrochemical industries, etc. A system expert is in a specific domain and has relevant knowledge and experience in system programming. The expert system simulates the human expert’s problem-solving thinking process and reaches or approaches the level of experts.

### 1.2.5 Chaos Theory

Chaos theory is a branch of mathematics that investigates the behavior of dynamic systems that are very sensitive to initial conditions. Chaos is an interdisciplinary theory that states: in the randomness of complex chaotic systems, there are basic patterns, constant feedback loops, repetition, self-similarity, fractals, and self-organization. According to the definition, the word Chaos means “a state of complete confusion and disorder; complete lack of organization or order”. Chaos theory is an interesting contrast. The science of predicting the behavior of “inherently unpredictable” systems. In fact, chaos theory is a mathematical tool that allows us to derive beautiful structures from chaos. The main foundation of chaos theory is the idea that order and chaos are not always opposites. Chaotic systems are a fascinating combination of order and chaos. When we look at them from the outside, they behave unpredictably and show disorder, but inside these systems we see a set of deterministic equations that work with order.

Chaos is a name that is often applied to a non-linear dynamics. This expression is used to explain the complex behavior of so-called simple, linear and well-behaved systems. Chaotic behavior appears irregular and often random, similar to the behavior of a system strongly affected by random external noise. The mathematical definition of chaos is the unpredictable long-term behavior of a deterministic dynamical system due to sensitivity to initial conditions (commonly known as the butterfly effect). Chaos theory is defined as the qualitative study of unstable periodic behavior in deterministic nonlinear dynamical systems. Chaos occurs in very simple systems that are often free of noise. In fact, these systems are essentially deterministic; That is, with accurate knowledge about the initial conditions of the system, its future behavior can be predicted. As a result, chaos may be defined as a bounded, non-periodic and noisy oscillation [4].

### 1.2.6 Machine Learning

In the science of machine learning, the issue of designing machines that learn by using the examples given to them and their own experiences is discussed. In fact, in this science, an attempt is made to design a machine in such a way that it can learn and act without explicitly planning and dictating each and every action by using algorithms. In machine learning, instead of programming everything, data is fed to a generic algorithm, and it is this algorithm that builds its own logic based on the data it is fed. Machine learning has various methods, including supervised learning, unsupervised learning, and reinforcement learning. Algorithms used in machine learning are among these three categories. Machine learning is a field of study that emerged from artificial intelligence. By using artificial intelligence, mankind seeks to build better and smarter machines. But researchers were initially unable to program machines to perform more complex tasks that are consistently challenging, except for a few simple tasks, such as finding the shortest path between point A and B. Based on this, a perception was formed that the only possible way to realize this goal is to design machines that can learn from themselves. In this approach, the machine is like a child that learns from itself. Therefore, machine learning emerged as a new capability for computers. Today, this science is used in various areas of technology, and its use has increased so much that people are often unaware of its existence in their daily tools and accessories, [5] and [6].
