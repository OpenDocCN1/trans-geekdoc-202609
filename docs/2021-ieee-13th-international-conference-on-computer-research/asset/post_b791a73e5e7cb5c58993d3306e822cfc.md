
Take BOINC as an example of the traditional C/S framework. Its server has a job generator responsible for generating new jobs, and a scheduler responsible for delivering jobs (Shown as Figure 2). Since all volunteers' task assignment and publisher's task storage need to pass through the task server in large-scale applications, the task server is likely to have performance or network bottlenecks and cause scalability problems and single point of failure problem.

## B. Basic Principles of Blockchain Based Design

In this design, volunteers and publishers exchange data through the blockchain. At the same time, all volunteers and publishers serve as nodes in the blockchain network and support the operation of the blockchain network. Therefore, a decentralized volunteer computing system can be established based on the blockchain. (Shown as Figure 3) Since the task server is no longer used, and thanks to the decentralized nature of the blockchain, the scalability problem and single point of failure caused by the bottleneck of the task server can be solved.

![](img/147d0ed8c8a0094bfeef9a98595d91c8_1_2.png)

Fig. 3. Volunteer computing based on blockchain.

Under this decentralized framework, because there is no centralized server, people cannot use a method like a centralized system to allocate and schedule tasks. Therefore, the publisher needs to split the project into multiple jobs with a suitable amount of computing content and treat each job as a "job block", and then publishes it on the blockchain. Then, volunteers can find unprocessed job on the blockchain for processing, and post a "processing block" on the blockchain to mark that the job is being processed, and other volunteers can use the "processing block" to determine whether the job is being handled by other volunteers. When the volunteer has finished processing the job, a "result block" should be issued to prove that the job has been completed, which should contain the output of the job.

## C. Design of Error Correction Mechanism

Volunteer calculation participants are not responsible for the project (indeed, their identity is unknown), so the results returned by a single volunteer should be considered unreliable and insecure [8]. The wrong result may be due to a variety of reasons, it may be a hardware malfunction, or a malicious attack from a volunteer. Since the frequency of occurrence of the above problems cannot be ignored, volunteer computing requires a verification mechanism to ensure that the job is completed correctly. Therefore, an attribute "Handle count" is introduced for each job block. This is an arbitrary positive integer set by the publisher to indicate how many different volunteers can handle the job, so that the publisher can compare and verify the results of different volunteers.

When a job is completed, the publisher should verify the result blocks published by the volunteers, and publish the accepted volunteer results to a new "completed block" to show that the job has been completed and which results from volunteers have been accepted by the publisher.

## D. Design of Anti-plagiarism Mechanism

As all content published on the blockchain is open to everyone, including the results published by volunteers. If the result is published in its original format, other volunteers can also see the result and can replay the result in their own identity. Since a job block usually allows multiple volunteers to submit results for verification, this allows some fake volunteers to create the illusion that they participated in the computing, even if they did not participate in the computing, but just copied the results of others. Therefore, asymmetric encryption is used here to make the original format of the results only valid for the publisher. Publishers should declare their public keys when publishing job blocks. When volunteers publish results, they should encrypt their own public key together with their result using the publisher's public key, and then write it into the result block to ensure that other volunteers cannot reuse the result. When verifying the result block, the publisher should find the volunteer public key in the processing block referenced by the result block, and check whether the volunteer public key encrypted in the result matches it.

## E. Summary of the Process

In summary, in this kind of system, for a job, a complete process should be:

- The publisher divides a project into several jobs with a suitable amount of computing.
- The publisher packages the job content which may include source code, executable, data, platform restrictions, introduction, etc., and decides the number of volunteers handling the job, and packs this information together with its own public key into a block, then publishes it to the blockchain.
- Volunteers look for the job block released by the publisher on the blockchain and check whether there are enough processing blocks to process the job block. If the processing block does not reach the upper limit of the job, the volunteer will process the job.
- The volunteer selects a job block that can be processed and publishes a processing block, which contains the volunteer's public key and the corresponding job block hash.
- After the volunteer completes a job, the volunteer's own public key together with the processing result will be encrypted using the public key of the job publisher. After that, the encrypted data needs to be packed together with the hash of the corresponding processing block into a result block, and then publish it to the blockchain.
- The publisher collects corresponding result blocks from the blockchain, uses the private key to decrypt the result, and verifies whether the public key in the result matches the public key declared in the corresponding processing block. If the verification is successful, the publisher continues to verify the correctness of the calculation result.
- The publisher lists all accepted result blocks, puts them into the completion block along with the hash of the corresponding job block, and then publishes them to the blockchain to announce the end of the job processing.

## F. Defects and Possible Solutions

Although this design uses the characteristics of the blockchain to effectively solve the problems of centralization and single point of failure, it also introduces some shortcomings. Firstly, if there are multiple volunteers in the standby state, that is, all tasks are currently being processed or have been processed, there are multiple volunteers that are not processing jobs and are waiting for new jobs to process. When a new job block is published, if multiple volunteers want to handle the job at the same time, they will tend to enter a competitive condition. At this time, multiple volunteers want to publish a processing block at the same time. Because the broadcast of the blockchain has a certain delay, it is easy to cause the blockchain to fork. Therefore, each volunteer should be required to process only one job at the same time, otherwise the request to enter the chain of a new processing block should be rejected to reduce the chance of competition, when a new job is released. And a certain blockchain consensus mechanism is needed to maintain the uniqueness of the chain. Secondly, since the key pair can be generated arbitrarily, this system cannot guarantee that each volunteer only has a single volunteer key. If a real volunteer controls multiple volunteer keys and submits the work result in multiple identities, it may affect the verification of the results. Therefore, this design may be more suitable for use in a small area of trust. If large-scale application is required, it may be necessary to establish a centralized or distributed verification mechanism to verify the identity of volunteers to prevent the aforementioned "puppet volunteers".

Anyone can search the blockchain to understand which volunteers participated in a computing for each computing job in history, and the adoption of the results provided by each volunteer. At the same time, the authenticity of history can be guaranteed with the help of the persistent and auditable properties of the blockchain. This paper also raises two possible problems. One is the consensus problem on the chain, and the other is the puppet volunteer problem. And proposed possible solutions to these two problems separately.

In terms of the job content in the job blocks, this paper did not specifically discuss its format. It can be in any format that is not restricted by source code, executables or URLs, or it can be a sandboxed program to achieve cross-platform features. But in the implementation, the packet format should be determined according to the actual situation, and the format should be convenient for volunteers to judge whether their equipment can handle the job.

Although anyone can know the participation of each volunteer through the blockchain, this paper does not discuss how to design rewards for volunteer work. In actual implementation, it is reasonable to consider rewarding volunteers in some way to encourage volunteers to participate in volunteer computing.

## III. CONCLUSION

This paper proposes a blockchain-based volunteer computing system design, which uses the decentralized characteristics of the blockchain to solve the single point of failure. At the same time, because there is no task server as a bottleneck, the scalability problem is solved. It also provides a certain traceability for volunteer computing.
