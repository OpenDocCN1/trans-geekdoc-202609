### [57](#12_Chapter03.xhtml#que3-57). **(A)**
A roundoff error occurs when computers round the final decimal place to different values. For example, `1/3` could be equal to `0.3333333333333` or, depending on the second computer, `0.3333333333333333333333333333`. The two are not equal.

### [58](#12_Chapter03.xhtml#que3-58). **(D)**
Eight bits can hold `2^n` numbers. The largest number that `2^8` bits can hold is `2^8 - 1`: `(2 * 2 * 2 * 2 * 2 * 2 * 2 * 2) - 1 = 255`. Any number greater than 255 will cause an overflow error.

### [59](#12_Chapter03.xhtml#que3-59). **(B), (C)**

If a programmer wants a number outside the range, he or she can use special large integers such as `longs`, but the number should not default to `longs`. A truly infinite integer would take an infinite number of bits to store, regardless of its value.
