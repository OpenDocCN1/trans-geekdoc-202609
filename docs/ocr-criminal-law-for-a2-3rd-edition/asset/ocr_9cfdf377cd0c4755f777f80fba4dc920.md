# Key facts

| Section of Theft Act 1968 | Definition | Comment/Cases |
|---|---|---|
| **S 1** | A person is guilty of theft if he dishonestly appropriates property belonging to another with the intention of permanently depriving the other of it. | Full definition of theft. D is charged under this section. |
| **S 2** | **Dishonesty**<br>1. Not dishonest if D believes:<br>• he has a right in law (s 2(1)(a))<br>• he would have the other’s consent (s 2(1)(b))<br>• the owner cannot be discovered (s 2(1)(c)).<br>2. Can be dishonest even if intends paying for property. | No definition of ‘dishonesty’ in the Act.<br><br>*Ghosh* (1982): two-part test:<br>• Is it dishonest by ordinary standards?<br>• If so, did D know it was dishonest by those standards? |
| **S 3** | **Appropriation**<br>‘any assumption of the rights of an owner’<br>Includes a later assumption of rights. | Held to be assumption of any of the rights of an owner: *Gomez* (1993).<br><br>Given ‘neutral’ meaning, so consent irrelevant: *Lawrence* (1971), *Hinks* (2000). |
| **S 4** | **Property**<br>Includes money and all other property real or personal, including things in action and other intangible property.<br>Land cannot be stolen except by trustee or tenant or by severing property from land.<br>Wild mushrooms, fruit, flowers and foliage cannot be stolen unless done for commercial purpose.<br>Wild animals cannot be stolen unless tamed or in captivity. |  |
| **S 5** | **Belonging to another**<br>Property is regarded as belonging to any person having possession or control or any proprietary right.<br>Property belongs to the other where it is received under an obligation to retain and deal with it in a particular way.<br>Property received by a mistake where there is a legal obligation to make restoration belongs to the other. | Not limited to owner: *Turner (No 2)* (1972) stole own car.<br>*Hall* (1972), *Klineberg and Marsden* (1999).<br><br>*A-G’s Reference (No 1 of 1983)* (1985).<br>Must be a legal obligation: *Gilks* (1972). |
| **S 6** | **Intention to permanently deprive**<br>Treat the thing as his own to dispose of regardless of the other’s rights.<br>Dispose of includes ‘dealing with’ property. | The ‘goodness’ or practical value must have gone from the property: *Lloyd* (1985).<br><br>*DPP v Lavender* (1994). |
