## Conclusion

Make sure you finish with a conclusion. This question asks you to evaluate the accuracy of the statement. So think about whether the reforms have improved the law or if that is in doubt. For this a quick list will help you focus on what has improved and what has not:

- Replacing the various old tests with one of ‘more than merely preparatory’ has made law simpler and easier to apply BUT some cases (e.g. Geddes, Campbell) show this test has problems and is inconsistently applied.
- Do the cases of *Geddes*, *Campbell* show that the law is not effectively preventing criminal behaviour before harm is caused?
- Does the test of ‘more than merely preparatory’ strike a balance between protection of the public and not punishing people for just thinking about an offence?
- The need to show an intent to commit the full offence still exists – does this also strike a balance between protection of the public and not punishing people for just thinking about an offence?
- Recklessness is still not enough for *mens rea*. Attempting the impossible is now within the definition of attempt – this helps with law enforcement.
- The Law Commission has made recommendations for reform which underline the view that the Act has not improved the law adequately in all areas of attempt.

Now finish with a concluding paragraph on the extent to which the Act has succeeded.

## A3.2 Problem questions

On Unit G153 these can also be based on any area or areas of the substantive criminal law studied for the A2 option. For these, knowledge and understanding of the law (AO1) together with identification of the relevant issues and application of the law (AO2) are required. Each question is worth 50 marks, and the allocation of assessment objectives is the same as for essay questions:

- AO1 – maximum 25 marks
- AO2 – maximum 20 marks
- AO3 – maximum 5 marks

As with the essay questions, all problem-style examination questions at the end of chapters in this book are taken from past OCR papers. They can be used as practice for the current specification.

### Examination question

Jin is in a supermarket queue. Jin watches Amy squeeze into the queue ahead of him. Jin is angry and tries to push Amy out of the queue. Amy loses her balance and falls into an elderly couple, Fred and Wanda. Fred has a heart condition and the shock causes him to have a heart attack. Wanda falls; she suffers from brittle bone disease and breaks her hip.

In hospital Fred is put on a life-support machine but dies three hours later. Wanda recovers but before she goes home Doctor Sugar gives her an antibiotic injection. He is busy and does not check whether Wanda has any allergies. Wanda has a massive allergic reaction and dies.

Discuss the criminal liability, if any, of both Jin and Doctor Sugar for the manslaughter of Fred and Wanda.

(OCR G153 January 2010)

- The principle of transferred malice – Jin pushes Amy but it is two other people who die.
- The chain of causation from Jin’s act to the deaths – is this broken?
- The injection given by Doctor Sugar causing Wanda’s death – doctors owe a duty of care to patients so consider gross negligence manslaughter.

### Define/explain/expand

Having identified the issues, now define the law and explain and expand it with case law for each of these issues in turn. Remember that to get to Level 5 it is necessary to have wide citation of cases. Deal with each part and each person separately.

Define unlawful act manslaughter: there must be an unlawful act that is dangerous on an objective test and the act must cause the death. Jin’s push is a battery and so an unlawful act. Would sober and reasonable people inevitably recognise that it must subject V to the risk of some harm (Church)? D must have the required mens rea for the unlawful act.

Explain/expand definition with cases Mitchell, Larkin, Church. Consider the dangerousness. Would the risk of harm to Fred because of his heart condition be obvious to sober and reasonable people? Explain with the cases of Watson and Dawson.

Define the principle of transferred malice.
Explain/expand with cases such as Latimer and Mitchell.
Define the concept of the chain of causation. Include causation in fact and causation in law.
Explain/expand with points such as ‘take your victim as you find him’ Blaue; also explain when the chain of causation may be broken by medical treatment using cases of Smith, Cheshire and Jordan.
Define gross negligence manslaughter – duty of care, breach of that duty which carried a risk of death and death resulting.
Explain/expand the degree of negligence required – Adomako.

### Potential answer

For problem questions it is necessary to:

- Identify the relevant areas of law
- Define the law on those areas
- Explain and expand the law further with cases
- Apply the law.

So don’t forget the IDEA.

### Identify

As there are several issues to look at it is sensible to plan by writing down each one separately. In this question it is necessary to identify the following issues:

- Jin’s pushing of Amy which leads to her falling against Fred and Wanda and their subsequent deaths – clearly not murder (no intention) so consider unlawful act manslaughter.

### Apply

When you have explained all the law you must then apply it to the situations. This is an important aspect for AO2 marks. So state:

- Is Jin liable for the death of Fred? – Jin has done an unlawful act with the intention of doing it, and it can be argued that a reasonable man would see a risk of some harm from the act, the principle of transferred malice applies. The fact that Fred has a heart condition it is likely that the principle of ‘take your victim as you find them’ applies. So Jin can be seen as the factual and legal cause of Fred’s death.
- Alternatively it could be argued that on the basis of the decision in Dawson that risk of harm would not be seen, in which case Jin would be not guilty.
- Is Jin liable for the death of Wanda?
- All the elements of unlawful act manslaughter are present (situation very similar to Mitchell) – the principle of transferred malice would apply as in Mitchell. However the fact that Doctor Sugar gives an antibiotic injection when she has recovered appears to break the chain of causation (Jordan). So Jin is probably not liable.
- Is Doctor Sugar liable for Wanda’s death?
- A doctor owes a duty of care to his patient; he has breached that duty by not checking on allergies; there is a risk of death from this and Wanda has died as a result. So it is very likely that a jury would find the negligence enough for him to be criminally liable.

## A3.3 Dilemma boards/evaluating statements

The final style of question on Unit G153 is known as a dilemma board or evaluating statements. This is a style of question in which students are faced with a scenario plus four statements about it which they have to evaluate. Each dilemma board carries 20 marks. This means that you should spend considerably less time on this than on the other two questions.

For assessment, the marks are all for AO2. This means you must concentrate on analysis and application of the law to the statements.

An example of such a question is given below.

### Examination question

Elsa decides to steal vodka from a shop. She has a gun in her pocket. Outside the shop she pulls out the gun but has changed her mind and returns it to her pocket. Elsa still wants to steal some vodka so she enters the shop but discovers that the shop has no vodka. Angry, Elsa kicks a customer, Reynard, bruising his leg. Elsa runs out of the shop, slamming the glass door so hard it shatters.

Evaluate the accuracy of each of the four statements A, B, C and D individually as they apply to the facts in the above scenario.

**Statement A:** Elsa commits attempted robbery when she gets the gun out of her pocket.

**Statement B:** Elsa does not commit s 9(1)(a) burglary when she enters the shop.

**Statement C:** Elsa commits robbery when she kicks Reynard.
