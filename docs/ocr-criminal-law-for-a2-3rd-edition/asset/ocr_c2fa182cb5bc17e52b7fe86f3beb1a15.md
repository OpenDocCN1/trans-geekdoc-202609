+   (a) D’s acts and omissions in doing or being a party to the killing resulted from D’s loss of self-control;
+   (b) the loss of self-control had a qualifying trigger; and
+   (c) a person of D’s sex and age, with a normal degree of tolerance and self-restraint and in the circumstances of D, might have reacted in the same or in a similar way to D.’

So, the following points are essential for the defence to be successful:

+   - D must have lost self-control
+   - there must be a qualifying trigger (see 7.2.2 below)
+   - a person of the same sex and age would have reacted in the same way as D in the same circumstances.

Note that all cases decided under the old law on provocation are no longer law. However, some are included as they are useful for discussion and comparision purposes.
