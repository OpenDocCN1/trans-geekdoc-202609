# Self-test Questions

+   1. Explain the ‘but for’ testing causation.
+   2. Give a case where D’s conduct was not the factual cause of V’s death.
+   3. What level must D’s contribution be so that it may be considered a legal cause?
+   4. Using a case example, explain the ‘thin-skull principle’.
+   5. Give an example of an intervening act that might break the chain of causation.
+   6. Using a case example, explain when medical treatment might break the chain of causation.
+   7. Give two cases in which medical treatment was held not to break the chain of causation.
+   8. What is the position where a life-support machine is switched off?
+   9. When will V’s own act break the chain of causation?
+   10. Explain two problems in the law of causation.
