
# The Notion of Comprehensible Input

The notion of comprehensible input can be defined into two integrated components, including what sorts of element theoretically constitute input and what types of mechanism practically facilitate comprehension. According to Mystkowska-Wiertelak and Pawlak (2011), the constitution of input is often initially derived from theoretical perspective of non-interventionists as believers in the natural acquisition of the target language in the classroom: in particular, this acquisition is composed of the interrelated systems of lexis, syntax, phonology, and pragmatics and how these systems link language forms with language functions as well as language functions with language uses. Furthermore, the natural acquisition can be explained via the substantial framework of Krashen (1982)’s Monitor Model within which stating that language acquisition occurs when there is a natural pathway for the language information from the comprehensible input to interact with the existent language faculty of human being (Mystkowska-Wiertelak & Pawlak, 2011).

## The Input Hypothesis

Among the proposed hypotheses from Monitor Model, the Input Hypothesis characterizes the notion of comprehensible input from the emphasis of understanding the message in the target language and thus language acquisition takes place when the level of input is beyond our current level of knowledge (Krashen & Terrell, 1983). In the representation of `i+1` as comprehensible input, `i` signifies the current level of learner’s competence while `+1` marks the next stage of development where learners go through the procedure of understanding the meaning rather than the form of the message in the target language-either in the spoken or written form (Krashen & Terrell, 1983; as cited in Lee, 2018).

## Conditional Factors for Comprehensible Input

However, the success in the transmission of an input to be comprehensible rather depends on some conditional factors.

### Fine-tuning and Extra-linguistic Context

The foremost condition is the extent to which the input is finely tuned through the continuous flow of examples in the aim to promote the acquisition of the next structure at the specific stage of language development. The continuous flow of examples is illustrated as the container of unacquired grammar and the transmission of the next structure in this container would rather depend on the extra-linguistic context such as the assistance of visual aids and the reference to familiar topics (Krashen, 1985).

### Internal Language Processor and Affective Filter

The second condition is the role of internal language processor as the device to transform the acquisition of the provided input (Chomsky, 1975). Within this internal process, the device itself operates internal rules for the comprehension of the input and yet this operation is subject to an “affective filter”—an imaginary blockage which prevents the input to become comprehensible before being utilized for language acquisition (Krashen, 1985, p. 81).

## Comprehension and Skill Development

Finally, in the matter between comprehension and skill development, comprehensible input is considered the result of two reverse directions. Following the Comprehension Hypothesis and Skill-Building Hypothesis (Krashen, 2017), the accumulation of comprehensible input is likely to facilitate skill development when the acquirers internally comprehend what they produce; meanwhile, comprehensible input is considered the result of skill development when the process of skill development initiates automaticity in spoken and written language.

Related to the concern of comprehension and skill development is the extent to which a comprehensible input becomes an optimal one. According to Krashen and Mason (2020), the provision of comprehensible input is not sufficient for language acquisition because there is no guarantee for the provided input to be fully transmitted and the process of language acquisition does not require the comprehension of every part of the provided input. Furthermore, the optimal input is thought to be interesting enough for a temporary distraction instead and should be intense in terms of linguistic structure and learning opportunities so that the procedure of acquiring new aspects of language can be gradually processed (Krashen & Mason, 2020).

# Accuracy, Fluency, and Lexis

Having mentioned that comprehensible input `i+1` should be positioned from the current learner’s competence `i` into the attempt for acquiring language at the `+1` level, there are certain conditions which may inhibit the flow of an input in its process of being comprehended by learners, including (a) the fine-tuning of the input with specific assistance, (b) the availability of internal language processor, and (c) the concern between comprehension and skill development. Along the way with reviewing three key terms including accuracy, fluency, and lexis, this section further explores the first two certain conditions (a) & (b) from the distinction between focus on forms and focus on form.

## Focus on Forms vs. Focus on Form

Firstly, the concern of fine-tuning the input with specific assistance can be linked with the form and meaning interface in specific considerations of focus on forms/ focus on form. The focus on forms/focus on form refers to explicit teaching approaches which is respectively distinctive in the emphasis of grammatical forms (form with plural –s) and the emphasis on meaning-based derivation from the acquired structures (Ellis, 2006). Although there remained a concern that the connection between form and meaning may be inadequate toward the development of communicative competence, the attention to grammatical form in teaching instruction is also necessary for the acquisition of the target language in specific teaching context. Fotos (1998) stated that the necessity of explicit instruction on grammatical forms in EFL learning context as the EFL context is limited in opportunities for communicative usage in the real-world context and even in the language classroom. Therefore, the formal instruction on teaching grammatical forms should be modified in the communicative teaching framework so that the promotion of accuracy can be achieved through the interaction with the learning task design as well as the promotion of L2 learner’s understanding of the target form and L2 learner’s awareness to the correct from (Fotos, 1998).
