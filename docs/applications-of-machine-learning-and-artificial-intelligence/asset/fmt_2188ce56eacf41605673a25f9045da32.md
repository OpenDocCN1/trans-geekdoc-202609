
# In this first stage of the ADDIE ID model

In this first stage of the ADDIE ID model, a thorough analysis helped the project manager create a needs analysis survey (see survey questions 1, 2, 3, 8, and 10 in Figure 3), which she designed with `SurveyMonkey`. In the conduct of this survey, she collected essential data from all course instructors at the school locale, by sending them an online survey link. This survey simply demonstrated the technology needs of teachers at the pertinent school locale. She then designed her weekly syllabi based upon the data she derived from this analysis. The data she collected demonstrated that some of the necessary technology tools are already utilized by teachers both inside and outside of the classroom while some others are not. Thus, the data reported by majority of online instructors showed that all teachers needed to be on board as far as their use of cutting-edge technologies in class and in terms of the school administration’s attitude towards and encouragement of their use of innovative, digital tools both inside and outside of the classroom.

## Importance of Instructor Training for Effective Online Teaching

This online instructor training course is crucial for all online instructors teaching at universities, especially for those that have transitioned to teaching with online technology tools with the emergence of the pandemic. By use of social media tools, instructors can gradually build upon their newly acquired knowledge of teaching all skills in the English language. These new technology-assisted teaching skills will be highly congruent with 21st century digital literacies. The teachers’ instructional applications with social media technologies are particularly critical to support their students’ knowledge-building process, because they will initially begin to deliver the content via scaffolding their students’ learning. As these instructors’ students begin to improve their understanding and practice of the subject matter, the instructors will gradually let them learn the subject matter on their own and by use of an array of social media tools. This learning/teaching approach embedded in the initiation phase of this project directly aligns with the constructivist learning pedagogy (Vygotsky, 1978). From an ID viewpoint, another note on the importance of teaching with social networking tools is that the entire life cycle of this online course to be designed for online course instructors will be predominantly guided by the principles of `ADDIE`.

## Gap Analysis: Identifying the Gaps with 3 Strategies

1.  In the process of completing an analysis of the de facto system in an organization and generating results from this analysis, the project management team can use gap analysis-based strategies. The first strategy can help establish target objectives that are specific and clear. This often occurs with a closer look into the organization’s mission statement, strategic goals, and its objectives as the organization learns, grows, and develops.

2.  The second strategy would be to analyze the current state of all business operations and processes across this organization. Gathering relevant pieces of data can be achieved through comprehensive data collection methods such as surveys, interviews, online questionnaires, observations within the organization, brainstorming sessions, and other documented information from within the organization’s repository. The project manager and her team will seek to collect data pertinent to the instructors’ and other staff members’ performance levels, along with the allocation of an array of resources.

3.  With respect to the second gap analysis strategy, it is crucial that the project management team compare the organization’s previously set target goals to those in its current state to see whether or not they were met. During the systems analysis process, the third strategy that a project management team needs to use is preparing a future-oriented and expansive action plan that must outline each particular step the team must take in order to close the gaps they identified. This comprehensive online instructor training project not only provides the project team with a list of specific steps to take for the future state at higher educational institutions, but allow them to achieve the target objectives as they bridge the gaps between this higher educational organization’s current state and future state.

## Content Delivery Methods: The Unit–Module–Topic (UMT) Model for Instructor Training for an Online Course Design Project

This design of this online instructor training course was based on a supporting ID model, `UMT`. The entire design process led the project manager of this course design to consider an array of factors to incorporate into the design. These factors can be listed as the content, the background training of all university instructors, the process through which the students’ learning experiences will take place (i.e., methodology including the type(s) of learning environment and all of the social networking applications), as well as the methods by which to assess instructors’ training/learning experiences. “By following through with this process, the instructors will find that teaching their adult learners with social networking technologies “is an exciting and dynamic experience, one that will be welcomed by both the learners and [the instructors]” (Moore & Kearsley, 2012, p. 152).

## Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

Especially during the initiating phase of the project, it is critical to build a solid team of executive sponsors, subject matter experts (`SMEs`), educational designers, instructional technologists, online course instructors, and school administrators (Hirumi, 2013), all of whom are expected to be on board. Each of these team members will be experts in the learning and teaching field that this project will revolve around.

Each team member’s role and responsibility has been clearly defined for the successful execution of this project and its deliverables. In regards to this, Lynch and Roecker (2007) pontificated that instructional designers are generally “required for the planning and executing phases” (para. 2) of a new project. Nevertheless, during the continuation of the project, some of the team members might be continually needed as far as their roles, responsibilities, and general expertise on computer-assisted education (`CAE`), along with their ability to oversee the benefits and/or risks associated with executing and developing this project. These individuals are to include the project manager, veteran university faculty members (i.e., some of the experienced instructors with tenure), and program supervisors.
