## Gestalt Theory and its Relation to Phenomenological Data Analysis

The underlying principle of Gestalt theory are congruent with the research school of phenomenology in ways that these principles can lead a clear and comprehensive understanding of the basic human experience in regards to a certain phenomenon under investigation. To corroborate such a connection between the Gestaltian way of perceiving things in a research participant’s individual life world—from an experiential perspective—and the methodology of conducting data coding and analysis based on the principles of the phenomenological school of inquiry, Braungardt (2016) provided the following:

> Gestalt theorists argue that when the perceptual system of the human mind forms a gestalt, the whole has a reality of its own, independent of the parts. \[When\] the Gestalt psychologist Kurt Koffka said, “the whole is other than the sum of the parts,” he did not mean that the whole is greater than the sum of its parts. The Gestalt principle is not a principle of addition; it is a theory about the independent existence of the units of meaning that we call a gestalt. From a strictly scientific point of view, this is hard to prove. But in terms of lived experience, we know that an entire visual field may appear to be clear or chaotic. Faces of some people may seem relaxed, others seem tense, tired, or absent-minded. A purely behavioral approach based on hard evidence and objectifiable traits has no language for such qualities, other than the verbal reports themselves. (para. 2)

Because the Gestalt phenomenon is not found in merely a single form in the human perception, but also manifests in memory, decision-making, learning, as well as in other areas of life, then, the characteristics of perceptions, qualities of perceptions include groupings, movements of objects, their shapes, colors, sounds, tones, stresses, rhythms, rhymes, melodies, and other perceptive qualities created by human consciousness (Braungardt, 2016). For this reason, subjects’ lived experiences can appear in varied representations. It was through these multiple forms of representations of the perceived experience that the researcher conducted her entire data analysis process.

## Gestaltian Approach to Data Analysis: Things Manifesting via Data Visualization

During the data coding and analysis stages of this research study, it was pivotal to take on a Gestaltian look at things that manifested via visualization of data. The data analysis method employed in this phenomenological study can provide the reader with a panoramic and holistic understanding of the Gestaltian (Giorgi, Giorgi, & Morley, 2017) way of looking at things that manifest or are given—these things appearing are not necessarily the things that exist. It is these things that helped the researcher of this study to be able to extrapolate specific codes and themes of codes that she derived from deeper meanings, initially by deconstructing the content—or by breaking down the participants’ descriptions both from face-to-face interviews and from the participants’ written LED descriptions—and then by reconstructing them into single meaning units during the conduct of the analysis. This study’s epistemological and ontological premises are in line with how each participant perceived their individual way of experiencing the studied phenomenon (i.e., m-Learning in their English language learning and development as adult second language learmers).

Each individual perception, thought, and feeling stemmed from the manner in which they looked at things as parts and wholes and as abstracta and concreta in their life world. The parts and wholes of perception of a particular phenomenon under inquiry could solely be seen, heard, thought, tasted, touched, and felt by use of five sensory perceptions, which led to the perception of parts as a whole. This way of looking at things explained the underlying principle of the Gestaltian way of perceiving things in the life world.

In this context, it is critical to note that the ways in which all participants lived their individual experiences pertinent to the phenomenon needed to be analyzed initially through sounds (phonemes) and next through visuals. The first type of analysis, which was phonetic coding (via meta transcriptions), was conducted via a special IPA-based phonetic coding system (coding of sounds/vowels) and by use of a specific software program called `NCH Software (WavePad Sound Editor software program)` both in the First Cycle of Coding and in the Second Cycle of Coding. The latter type of data analysis, which was applied by data visualization, was conducted under Pattern Coding in the Second Cycle of Coding, following the initial comprehensive phonetic coding. Each of the themes of the phonetic codes identified was visualized by use of specific data visualization software called `Wordle`. Every visual image represented the feelings, emotions, thoughts, and viewpoints that the partaking adult ESL students expressed during the face-to-face interviews as they described their feelings and perceptions of their experiences related to the phenomenon.

Visualization of data led to a much clearer understanding of the phonetically coded data during analyses, because it allowed for visual representations of the audio data (obtained from participants’ audio recording from the face-to-face interviews) initially coded by use of sounds. In the field of linguistics, in phonetics in particular, these sounds are also called phonemes. This type of data visualization enabled a deeper grasp of what experience each participant lived and how they lived that experience, the two core questions that a phenomenologist should seek in phenomenological inquiry (Moustakas, 1994). Thus, it was of paramount importance to capture how the study participants individually perceived the parts and wholes of that lived experience. Thus, in order to fully understand how every participant saw, thought, felt, and perceived the phenomenon as they personally experienced it, the researcher completed a holistic data visualization process, by generating images of each phonetically coded statement.
