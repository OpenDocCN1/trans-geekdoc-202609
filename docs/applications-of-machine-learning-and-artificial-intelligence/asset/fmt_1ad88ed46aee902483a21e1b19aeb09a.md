# Skin Cancer (Melanoma) Detection Using Deep Learning

Melanoma is an aggressive and dangerous tumor that develops when melanocytes start to grow out of control. (American Cancer Society 2019). estimates for melanoma in the United States for 2020, about 100,350 new melanomas will be diagnosed (about 60,190 in men and 40,160 in women), and about 6,850 people are expected to die of melanoma (about 4,610 men and 2,240 women). Melanoma is much rarer than some other types of skin cancer. But melanoma is more critical because it's much more likely to spread over the body if not identified and treated at an early stage. In the early stages, melanoma is not simply detectable as it is almost indistinguishable from common nevi or benign dysplastic lesions. The difficult detection and the aggressiveness of melanoma produces difficulties even for expert dermatologists, leaving them to choose how to proceed. The usual process for detection involves visual observation of the suspicious region by a dermatologist. Diagnostic accuracy, however, is strongly related to the professional experience of the dermatologist. If melanoma is detected at an early stage, it can be removed entirely from the body with surgery. Hence, early detection is critical for a high rate of survival. Nowadays, automatic detection or diagnosis algorithms for medical purposes are becoming more popular, and researchers use computer vision on dermoscopy images to detect skin cancer.

Digital images of skin cancer have the potential for educating the public and helping health providers in early detection, and directly supporting remote analyses through tele-dermatology, clinical decision support, and automated risk assessment. Deep learning techniques have proved their power in facing such challenging problems. With the help of extensive datasets, these techniques have achieved results comparable with human experts and exceed them in many cases. For example, deep learning techniques rely on the input of large quantities of data and are suited to the analysis of electronic health records (EHRs) to improve diagnosis (Myszczynska et al. 2020). Further, there has been progressing in using deep learning techniques in medical imaging to assist doctors with classification, detection, and segmentation tasks.
