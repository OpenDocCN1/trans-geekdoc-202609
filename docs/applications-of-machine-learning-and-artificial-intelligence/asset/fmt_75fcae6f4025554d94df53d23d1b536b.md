
# Lynch and Roecker (2010) on Project Parameters and Deliverables

Lynch and Roecker (2010) provided that there are parameters and deliverables for a new project’s planning, development, and execution phases. The authors also noted that agile models’ variables and deliverables are clearly articulated in these particular stages of a project at its planning and preparation. According to Lynch and Roecker (2010), “these variables include cost, schedule, requirements, and risks, and as each of these is made more fully known, a go/no-go decision must be made at the end of each phase” (para. 11). Where the planning, development, and execution phases are involved, this e-learning project will draw on the project principles proposed by Lynch and Roecker (2010), who explained that a PMBOLD project “goes beyond the mere delivery of static content. Instead, it includes collaboration, both synchronous and asynchronous, as well as some type of shared learning experience with fellow students” (para. 2) (For change requests and iterative design based on ADDIE and IPECC, see Figures 5 & 6).

These aforementioned change principles will directly align with Hirumi’s (2013) work on e-learning design that focuses on a three-tier pedagogy-technology framework. Similar to this three-level instructional technology framework, this ESL instructor online training project, which will be based upon teachers’ instruction with social networking applications, should be regarded as an action plan. This plan should be considered as one that not only highlights some of the issues that might potentially arise during e-learning interactions (i.e., human-human, human-content, and human-tool interactions), but also eloquently explains the need for integrating prominent theoretical frameworks into practice of e-learning project planning, design, and development. Regarding theoretical frameworks that guide pedagogy and technology training, it is noteworthy that Vygotskian social constructivism (1978) was selected as a major learning theory framework that guided this e-learning project as it incarnated.

# Organizational Change and Leadership

Organizational change is often considered to be a large-scale operation. It requires the type of leadership which is aimed to have a tremendous impact on the values with which all organizational members act, communicate, share, and even react to one another. As such, resistance to change cannot be considered as an independent phenomenon in this change process. In fact, it will be considered as a driving force and a catalyst of change in an organization.

# Communication Management Plan

All successful e-learning projects planned, developed, executed, and evaluated are the products of a collaborative work with all stakeholders involved in each step of the entire project.

Lynch and Roecker (2010) suggested a large network of project team members to run the project starting from the analysis and initiation stages to its conclusion. With this in mind, this online ESL instructor training course design project will follow generic project management principles in all phases of the project (from initiation to planning to design to development to execution to evaluation, and to conclusion).

Besides the primary purpose of executing an effective and efficient online training for faculty at higher educational institutions, it is also the intent that each faculty member and all staff agree to get on board. However, the project managers and team members are cognizant of the fact that this might not often be the case during these particular stages of this technology-assisted instructor training project. Thus, constant, honest, and open communication among all stakeholders involved in this change process is the key to the successful execution and closing of this online instructor training project.

# Facilitation of the Online Course Design

In order to provide all online teacher trainees with ample interaction with the content, the instructor, and with their peers, and to keep them on task, various engaging methods of teaching will be selected. These methods will help facilitate active learning and collaboration among the students and the instructor, while encouraging them to explore their own learning experiences independently, autonomously, which is critical in terms of creating a learning environment that will be student-oriented. Because this is an asynchronous learning/teaching setting, “strategies such as problem-based learning, collaboration, and student-led discussions work well” (Smaldino, Lowther, & Russell, 2011, as cited in Simonson et al., 2012, p. 204). It is imperative in a second/foreign language learning environment supported with asynchronous technologies that the instructor facilitating learning process and encourage her students to take the responsibility and ownership of their own learning processes.

The facilitation of this online course design draws on an exemplary e-learning program that was previously implemented at a research-based university in Sydney, Australia, all members of the staff and faculty learned in time to deal with the project’s extended application and planning periods, set timelines, long-term project development phase, and a learning and evaluation periods (Ward, West, Peat, & Atkinson, 2010). During these phases, faculty, staff, and students commonly experienced and enjoyed all e-learning activities delivered from within this online course design.
