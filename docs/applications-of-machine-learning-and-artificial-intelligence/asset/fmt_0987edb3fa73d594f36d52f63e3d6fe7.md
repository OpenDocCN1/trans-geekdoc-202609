
It was through these originally devised components of transcendental phenomenology (Husserl, 1931) that the researcher of this phenomenological exploration kept in mind that phenomenology rests in encountering things as if for the first time and with the highest level of empathy, as, during her one-on-one interviews with the, she listened to things from them by fresh perceptions. She listened to them as if for the first time and as though her respondents’ given descriptions of their experiences were brand new every time she received a new individual account of that lived experience, or a personal narrative, or a description from them regarding the phenomenon under her study, which was whether or not utilizing mobile learning technologies in the English language learning and development process brought about a possible transformation in all 12 adult ESL learners both epistemologically and ontologically and whether or not they had perceived any changes in their English language learning or in their identity as adult learners and autonomously (adult learning, or andragogy) as they experienced this phenomenon with mobile technologies both inside and outside of the classroom context. Drawing on the components and the findings of this research study, future phenomenologists could listen to their own human subjects’ accounts of their personally lived experiences in such a fresh manner. And every time they do this, they could embrace their partaking respondents’ ideas, or when they listen to their stories in a face-to-face setting, they can successfully gather the pertinent data—and perceiving these data freshly, as if for the first time (Moustakas, 1994).

## KEY TERMS AND DEFINITIONS

**Andragogy**: A term that refers to instruction for adults, or adult learning (Knowles, 1970), which focuses more on the process and less on the content being taught. In andragogical learning, strategies such as case studies, role playing, simulations, and self-evaluation are most useful. Instructors adopt a role of facilitator or resource rather than lecturer or grader. Andragogy makes the following assumptions about the design of learning: 1) adults need to know why they need to learn something; 2) adults need to learn experientially; 3) adults approach learning as problem-solving; and 4) adults learn best when the topic is of immediate value.

**Bracketing**: In phenomenological research, in the process of interpreting the meaning of the phenomenon from the participants’ perspective, the researcher can remove his/her biases by using this method. Bracketing is also called as suspending judgment.

**Bridling**: A data analysis technique in phenomenological inquiry.

**Descriptive Data Coding and Analysis**: It is a phenomenological, psychological data coding and analysis method which allows for an in-depth understanding of a more lucid picture of the consciousness of participants having lived personal experiences related to a particular phenomenon.

**Descriptive Phenomenology**: It focuses on the essence of human experiences, or what humans experienced and how they experienced that phenomenon while they were in the state of intentional consciousness. This leads to phenomenological purity. In this study, phenomenological purity was applied with pure phenomenological attitude towards the phenomenon, and any interpretation by the researcher was minimized, again, by the researcher (Lester, 1999), with the least number of perceptions, judgments, biases, or preconceived ideas about the phenomenon, using the epoché, or bracketing, method.

**Diacritics**: Type of phonetic coding distinct and particular to the English language, as determined by the IPA organization.

**Enunciation**: The act of saying or pronouncing a word or expression in a clear, slow, and definitive manner.

**Epistemological**: A term that refers to knowing or knowledge of things or phenomena.
