
# Features of the “Diagnostic Report” Page

This page includes three important elements (Figure 2). The first is the grades entered by the students. One obvious reason for this is to allow students to check the accuracy of the grades entered. Second, there is a general comment: “Above Average,” “Below Average” or “Bottom 5%.” The general comment is based on the student’s average score and the average score obtained by the previous cohort of students. For example, if the grade entered is better than the average obtained by the previous cohort of students, “Above Average” will be displayed. If the grade is worse than the average, “Below Average” will be displayed. If the grade is among the bottom 5% of students in the previous cohort, “Bottom 5%” will be displayed. This general comment allows students to understand their standing among students in the previous cohort. Third, there is a column on actions to be taken by the student. If students are above average, there will be a general comment asking them to keep enhancing their skills. If and only if students are below average or among the bottom 5% of students, they will be presented with actions they should take. These actions include course-based activities designed especially for the course, materials from the independent learning center selected by the subject leaders, relevant library books and online courses designed by the same unit (i.e., language center). These materials and activities were all selected and suggested by the respective subject leaders to help students who receive a lower grade in certain assessment components. For example, if students achieve a “below average” grade in language, it would be recommended that they complete course-based language activities, specific grammar materials in the independent language learning center and grammar courses designed by the center. Most of these include links so that students can access these resources when they are ready.

**Figure 2.** Screenshot of the “diagnostic report” page

All three courses have their own CDRs (i.e., with different content in the reports). They are accessed by different URLs. The design of all pages for the CDRs of all courses is very similar. The CDR landing pages are identical except for the radio buttons available for the component grades for the respective first assignment in different courses (i.e., the components grades can be different for different assignments). Despite a similar design and layout, the content on the diagnostic report page varies for the CDRs in different courses. The averages are different across courses, and the materials recommended are specific for the course. However, the CDRs look similar across courses, and they provide similar features to students.

# EVALUATION OF COURSE DIAGNOSTIC REPORT

## Methodology

After designing an initial prototype with stakeholders, the CDR was subjected to evaluation by another group of major stakeholders, the students. The following describes the evaluation process of the prototype and presents the results. In general, the CDR was piloted with students taking three courses. After trying the CDR, the students were asked to complete a questionnaire and were invited to participate in a focus group interview.

## Participants

A total of 569 students in their first or second year of university study in the disciplines of business, civil and environmental engineering and health sciences participated in the pilot of the CDR. These students were completing one of the courses in which the CDR was available to them. Due to the data collection arrangements, these students should have completed the first assignment for the course. Depending on the courses they are taking, their English proficiency levels vary. Within the entire sample, 59% of students attained Level 3 or below (equivalent to IELTS 5.48 or below) in English in HKDSE; 29% attained Level 4 (equivalent to IELTS 6.31–6.51); and 12% attained Level 5 or above (equivalent to IELTS 6.81 or above). The project team felt that the sample represented the actual student population.

## Data Collection

The CDR was piloted in the middle of a semester when teaching was ongoing to avoid conflicts with any assessment due dates or examinations during the semester. Students taking the English courses had just received the results of their first assessment with component grades that were needed for the CDR pilot.

## Procedures

In addition to the official ethical clearance obtained from the university, prior permissions from the teachers were obtained by the research team to conduct the research in their classrooms. Thirty-nine class visits were conducted in 23 days. During the class visit, the researcher(s) spent the first 5 minutes introducing the CDR project and the CDR to students. Students were then given 5 to 10 minutes to attempt to use the tool by entering their first assessment component grades, during which a member of the project team assisted students who required further clarification. Students were encouraged to input their actual grades to check their performance on the first assessment in comparison to that of the students who took the same course in previous cohorts. Students were assured that the inputted data would not be collected or stored on the server.

Next, the project team member distributed the questionnaire to all students and invited them to complete it following the CDR trial. Students were reminded that their participation in the research was completely voluntary, that it would not have any effect on their subject grades and that all data collected through the questionnaires were solely for academic research purposes and would be kept or reported anonymously. Students who did not wish to participate were instructed to return a blank questionnaire. A total of 569 completed questionnaires were collected.

To collect more information about students’ perceptions of the tool, the project team member invited one or two groups of one to four volunteers in each class to participate in a 30-minute focus group interview immediately after the completion of the questionnaires. Nine group interviews, involving 28 students, were conducted, in English. Prior to each interview, all interviewees were given further written details about the project and were required to provide signed consent to participate in the interview. Every group or individual was given several minutes of preparation time, and all interviews were conducted in English and were voice-recorded with consent.

## Instruments

**Questionnaire:** The questionnaire contains three parts: Part A includes nine questions using a 5-point scale to indicate agreement; Part B asks for participants’ specifics, including gender, program of study, HKDSE English Language subject results, grades of the first assessment of the English course and other English exam results if available; and Part C asks for participants’ full name and signed consent.

**Interview Guide:** The six interview questions are about student-users’ thoughts about the usefulness and strengths and weaknesses of the CDR, the usefulness of the improvement suggestions provided in the CDR, their behavior when trying the tool, their English learning motivation after using the tool, additional functions to include and additional improvement suggestions to be included in the CDR.
