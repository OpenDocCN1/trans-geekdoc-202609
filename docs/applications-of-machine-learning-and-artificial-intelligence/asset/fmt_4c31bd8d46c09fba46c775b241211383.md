
# Teaching Case 2.1: Coffee House vs. Coffee Shop

Teaching case 2.1 compares coffee house and coffee shop in two varieties of English. The design of grammar teaching activities on the case of coffee shop and coffee house therefore demonstrates the case where teachers can implement the analysis of word pattern from the position of nodes and the relevant descriptive information from daily usage. Teaching activities follow similar procedure on three stages of observation, generalization, and reproduction.

## Observation

The purpose of the observation task is to help Ss recognize the regular existence of the two lexes: coffee house and coffee shop in British and American English. T firstly presents to Ss the extracted patterns of nodes for coffee shop and coffee house from Bank of English (sub-corpus: british and american). T can raise Ss awareness by raising their attention to the position of L1 and develop tentative analysis of how the two items are used in two language contexts.

The extracted patterns of nodes on the position of L1 demonstrate a tendency of coffee shop and coffee house being commonly referents in the United States. The non-existence of coffee shop at the center position and the feature of articles (a/the) in L1 position generates the hypothesis that drinking coffee is rather less common in the United Kingdom.

## Generalization

In the generalization stage, Ss perform Google search for online written articles on the topic of coffee shop and coffee house. Depending on the searched results, they generate classification of their findings on the basis of the following suggested criteria:

1.  Size of the place:
2.  Local naming:

Ss share their findings with other Ss and T will assist Ss to determine which suggested criteria is likely the contributor to the usage of coffee shop and coffee house in real-life context.

## Reproduction

T can conduct another comparative task of how representation of similar concept of where to buy things is varied across British and American varieties of English. Suggested comparative items include the representation of a place for car-fixing in auto body shop (American English) and repair shop (British English) and the representation of a place for buying daily-life products in grocery store (American English) and corner shop (British English).

## Further Suggested Application

The comparison of lexes on place to buy coffee shows how corpus linguistics can be implemented in investigating cultural annotation of language usage across varieties of English. The pattern of language identified in a specific word context denotes the implication of how language usage reveals the reality of object in daily life and its equivalence in another word context. Further similar teaching plans using corpus data can be directed at the investigation of food and animal culture in literary works. Other topics on gender and societal differences on language usage can be directed in combination with text analysis of extracted excerpts and dialogues.

# Teaching Case 2.2: Discount and Concession

## Data Analysis

WordSmith 6.0 and Google search are implemented for data analysis. The purpose is to combine both languaging and googling into the interpretation of language use in sociolinguistic context. In other words, grammatical analysis is expanded into the connection with demographic data and social policy in certain contexts.

## Teaching Procedure

### Observation

The observation stage of grammar teaching can be started with observation of frequent combination of concession and discount in British and American English from corpus data of Bank of English. Ss analyze which part of speech functions as the pre-modifier for the head nouns concession and discount in the british and american sub-corpus. Teaching activities can be further combined with dictionary definitions for explanations of the analyzed patterns.

The extracted patterns of concession and discount in Figure 3 firstly show in American English a major pattern of adjectives being pre-modifiers for the head noun discount. The group of degree adjectives (deep, great, large, substantial) can be seen an addition of information about the degree of “reduction in the usual price of something” (Longman Dictionary of Contemporary English, n.d.). Meanwhile, concession seems to connect with the pre-modifiers embedded with the semantic value of human (adult, student) and this pattern also corresponds to the definition of “a reduction in the usual price of something, made available to students, old people, etc.” (Cambridge Dictionary, n.d.).

### Generalization

Grammar teaching activities in the generalization stage can be developed with the probability of 2x2 word combination of four items: student, senior, discount, concession. T can draw a statistics of hits and search results from the selected corpora resources and Google Search Engine and assists Ss in interpreting these statistics with reference to their background and prior knowledge in other fields of study (e.g. economics, social studies, geography, history).

Statistics from Figure 4 predicts that the student group seem to attain the priority in United Kingdom while the senior citizen group seem to attain the priority in United States from 1993 to 2007. This interpretation can be further linked with demographic settings in these countries within or beyond this period of time.

### Reproduction

Grammar teaching activities in reproduction stage are expected to combine further consolidation of previous identified findings and patterns with an intensive expansion of knowledge from interdisciplinary fields. T can link relevant knowledge from social policy to facilitate discussion about the impact of social structure of population and welfare issues on the emergence of linguistic patterns. The suggested topics include, for instance, education policy in the United Kingdom and national policy for senior citizen in the United States. The reading materials can be electronically retrieved from designated offices such as Department of Education and Office of Retirement and Disability Policy.

## Further Suggested Application

Teaching case 3 demonstrates how sociolinguistics is an interdisciplinary field with corpus linguistics in distinguishing related meanings of synonyms and integrating with demographic data to explain the emergence of linguistic patterns from the collected corpus data. The investigation of synonyms can be conducted in comparative studies in different fields of study such as economics, education, and politics. A further aspect from this teaching case is the expansion of corpus data from specific group of speakers and combines the interpretation with demographic details of speakers in visualizing the significance of the emergent linguistic pattern.
