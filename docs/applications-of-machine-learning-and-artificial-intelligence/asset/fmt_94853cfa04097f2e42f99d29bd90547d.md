## ABSTRACT

In the first cycle of this phenomenological investigation, where the researcher conducted an extensive coding process (i.e., descriptive coding), all of the previous codes were identified under textural descriptions. This first cycle of coding was completed with codes having been formed following an initial deconstruction phase of the raw data given by all participants. Next, a plethora of emerging themes was identified under structural descriptions. The next step comprised the second cycle of coding: pattern coding. Where the second type of coding in the coding phase was involved, the researcher found all participants’ common speech patterns by meta coding. The researcher applied the meta coding technique via IPA-based meta transcriptions. From a linguistic perspective, the researcher employed the type of data analysis that had not previously been exercised in the CALL field and in the realm of phenomenological study design: phonics in data analysis.

## INTRODUCTION

According to Moustakas (1994), the organization and analysis of phenomenological data begins when the researcher studies the transcribed interviews and engages in the methods and procedures of phenomenal analysis. The author provided that these procedures involve horizonalizing the data, meaning that each horizon or statement (verbal or written) pertinent to the topic and question is equally valuable. Working on these horizonalized statements, the researcher can construct meanings or meaning units which he/she then clusters into common categories or themes. During this process, the researcher needs to reduce and abbreviate data or remove redundancies or repetitive statements that overlap. Next, with these clustered meanings and themes, textural descriptions of the participants’ lived experiences are developed.

Moustakas (1994) explicated that by use of these textural descriptions, the phenomenological researcher can then construct structural descriptions and incorporate textures and structures into units of meanings. Finally, the researcher constructs the essences of the phenomenon being studied. The data extensively coded and analyzed in this phenomenological study are presented to the reader, by use of the same approach to deriving categorical meanings and themes. These fundamental methods and procedures of phenomenological data analysis, coupled with additional techniques such as reduction, epoché, bridling (Vagle, 2014), and other methods of analyzing the coded data significantly assisted with the completion of this comprehensive data analysis.

### Developing Codes and Themes of Codes

Phenomenologists analyze data through stories—or by recounting stories. Their data analysis is based on developing codes and themes based on the essences of the meanings derived from the descriptions individually told by each study participant. Thereby, phenomenologists extract thematic meanings elicited from these descriptions during the data analysis phase. According to Englander and Robinson (2009), with openness and by reflecting on an early memory himself/herself, the phenomenological interviewer can demonstrate empathetic abilities toward his/her interviewees. Such reflections could include a lived, persistent meaning of what is regarded as an early emotional memory (Englander, 2007). With a thorough data analysis, a phenomenological study provides researchers with an in-depth understanding of a current issue, problem, or a phenomenon within a specific social context.

### Descriptive Coding and Pattern Coding

During the process of reporting the results and conclusions derived from all partaking respondents of this phenomenological study, which focuses on mobile technology-assisted English as a Second Language (ESL) learning and instruction, two coding techniques were employed: 1) Descriptive Coding and 2) Pattern Coding. These coding techniques were paramount in reporting and presenting all analyses based on the data derived both from the audio recordings and from the written Lived Experience Description (LED) protocols formulated by Van Manen (2001). These LED protocols were completed by each of the 12 study participants (i.e., adult ESL learners) during one-on-one interviews, wherein the questions aimed at an in-depth understanding of the essence of the meanings behind their descriptions related to their personal experiences of whether or not they felt having transformed into more autonomous and developed language learners with the assistance of mobile learning (m-Learning).

The written anecdotes in these LED protocols have significantly helped analyze, code, and interpret the data. The collected data allowed for not only what was hoped to be a set of valid and reliable findings, but also for a solid ground for broader directions for future research¹ pertinent to the phenomenon that each of these 12 participants personally experienced. The data elicited both from the audio interview recordings and those derived from the written protocols that were additionally asked the participants to complete during the interviews led to a thorough analysis and interpretation process, which then substantively helped find the essence of the meanings behind each participant’s orally uttered and written statements in regards to the study’s problem and research questions.

Most importantly, the entire analysis process and eventually this particular presentation of findings related to the study could not have been possible without the implementation of a set of phenomenological data reporting techniques. As far as this research study’s data analyses processes were concerned, Van Manen’s (2007) reflective writing techniques stood amongst all. It was through these specific data analysis techniques that all of the coded, categorized, interpreted, and structured data are currently presented to the reader. A number of recommendations and directions for further exploration pertinent to this phenomenon will also be provided, with the expectation that both the validity and reliability of these reported findings can be consolidated by the followers of this phenomenological study. It is also the anticipation that alternative theoretical frameworks and best practices can be sought in order to further contribute to the technology-supported adult ESL learning and teaching literature.

## BACKGROUND

### Descriptive Phenomenological Data Analysis Method Applied

Husserl and Heidegger believed that the world is described as one specific line among many other worlds and that humans, as conscious beings, see it as one of truths (Sloan & Bowe, 2014). These two pioneers of this unique phenomenological method—but predominantly Husserl—first developed it in 1970, paving the way for what Giorgi (1986) later formulated under specific procedures of descriptive phenomenology. Thus, drawing on both the predecessors’ and contemporary descriptive phenomenologists’ works, particular procedures of descriptive phenomenological data analysis were employed. Application of the fundamental axioms of descriptive phenomenology, which is to rather focus on the essence of human experiences, or what humans experienced and how they experienced that phenomenon while they were in the state of intentional consciousness, led to the use of phenomenological purity. In this study, phenomenological purity was applied with pure phenomenological attitude towards the phenomenon, and any interpretation by the researcher was minimized, again, by the researcher (Lester, 1999), with the least number of perceptions, judgments, biases, or preconceived ideas about the phenomenon, using the epoché, or bracketing, method.
