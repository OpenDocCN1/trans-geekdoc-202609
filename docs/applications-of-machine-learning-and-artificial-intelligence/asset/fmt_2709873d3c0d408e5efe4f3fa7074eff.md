## Family Involvement

To help enforce due dates and monitor students, teachers can ask students’ families for their participation (this is most likely only possible for K-12 students.) Generally, families can monitor their child's progress and make sure that they do their assignments on time. This is especially helpful now that most students join schools from home. On top of that, families can provide moral and emotional support and encouragement to their children. Lastly, families can also make it easier for teachers to communicate with their students and learn about their needs so that the teachers can provide a sufficient online learning environment.

# The Economic Policy

The Economic Policy Institute has created a plan to tackle these problems that have arisen with remote learning. The plan describes “three Rs:” relief, recovery, and rebuilding (Garcia & Weiss, 2020). The idea is to first fund schools during the pandemic to help improve the effectiveness of remote learning (relief). Next, investments in education will be made to help bring those affected by the pandemic up to speed (recovery). Finally, the idea is to build a foundation upon which later school shutdowns can be managed better and help schools understand every aspect of students’ abilities and limitations (rebuilding) (Garcia & Weiss, 2020).

## Relief Stage

Regarding the relief stage, the Economic Policy Institute has provided a checklist of the minimum that needs to be done to help students during the pandemic. They call for schools and community institutions to help feed children and provide more counseling and mental health support. Schools must come up with ways to monitor student needs to not only help those students, but also to be able to provide what students need to learn. Schools must be able to provide technical support, providing both internet access and devices to do schoolwork on to narrow the digital divide and disadvantages of lower quality devices. Care needs to be taken towards those with special-needs and English learners over the pandemic, along with any others that may need more specialized help.

## Recovery Stage

The recovery stage will help affected students get back to their expected rate of academic progress from before the pandemic. Schools should waive performance assessments in favor of diagnostic and curriculum-based exams. This will help both students and schools; the data gathered from diagnostic assessments can be used to evaluate the extent to which this recovery phase needs to be done. Large number of teachers are pressured to leave schools due to the problems that the pandemic has caused, so strategies must be put in place to retain these teachers. Better technological and well-thought-out systems need to be put in place to accommodate both students’ and teachers’ emotional learning and bonds. One of the most important factors to keep in mind for schools and teachers during this recovery phase is that both the quality of learning and the quantity of learning time must be increased to make up for the academic progress lost due to this loss of quality and quantity over the pandemic.

## Rebuilding Phase

As for the rebuilding phase, institutions should take advantage of this opportunity to restructure the education system and reimagine approaches to learning. This is a chance to provide better systems for more effective learning and help improve the accessibility of education to narrow the educational achievement gap across socioeconomic levels and between those of different backgrounds. Schools must start paying more attention to the unique skills of students and nuances of learning. By rewarding the development of cognitive and socioemotional skills, many opportunity gaps can be narrowed, not favoring any one type of student, and going forward with a whole-child approach. By accounting for the impact of poverty and racial/socioeconomic divides, schools can help take the first steps towards a whole-child approach in education.

# Policy Recommendations for Educational Institutions

To prevent the COVID-19 Pandemic from becoming a multi-generational disaster in education, new policies are urgently needed. The biggest international campaign to support countries facing the educational challenges caused by COVID-19 is the Save Our Future program, powered by The LEGO Foundation. This campaign is guided by some of the biggest humanitarian organizations, such as the United Nations International Children's Emergency Fund (UNICEF), The United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO), The United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR), The World Food Programme (WFP), Association for the Development of Education in Africa (ADEA), and Education Above All. All of the policies proposed in this paper are based on the guidelines that were created by the United Nations and the education community (United Nations Sustainable Development Group, 2020). These recommended educational policies can be divided into multiple categories: mitigating the transmission of the virus while reopening the schools, precise allocation of educational funds to reduce the impact of the virus, strengthening the resilience of education for all areas while keeping the education quality the same, restructuring the classical education systems while increasing the positive change in teaching and learning.

## Mitigating Virus Transmission

The most important first step for reopening the schools is mitigating the transmission of the virus. To return to the state of education before the pandemic, first the spread of the virus must be limited. Institutions need to provide the necessary safety measures to ensure the physical and mental health of their students. This could include creating more handwashing and hand-sanitizer stations, providing a nurse at each school who can conduct COVID-19 testing, instituting smaller class sizes or alternating schedules to prevent the crowding, etc. Institutions need to listen to the concerns of all students no matter their age and grade. It will also be safer for institutions to go a step beyond what the local health officials are recommending instead of following the bare minimum safety recommendations.
