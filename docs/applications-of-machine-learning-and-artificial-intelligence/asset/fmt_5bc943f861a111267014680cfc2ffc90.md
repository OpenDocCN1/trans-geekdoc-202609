# Mustafa Duran

Mustafa Duran is currently an instructor at North American University, where he teaches Introduction to Computer Science, Systems Programming, Operating Systems, and Data Structures courses. Also, he has so far taught core Java for the QA Automation Engineering Bootcamp for 3 terms. Professor Duran earned his Bachelor’s degree in Computer Education from Marmara University in Istanbul, Turkey, in 1999. After college, he continued his career as an instructor at Fatih University in Istanbul, Turkey. He then earned his Master’s degree in Computer Science from the same institution in 2011. Duran’s Master’s thesis was published in the format of an academic research book by the Lambert Publishing company. Following his master’s studies, Professor Duran started his PhD studies at Yildiz Technical University in Istanbul, Turkey, in 2012. During this time, he successfully completed the following courses: Image Processing, Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning, in addition to Iris Segmentation, CNN News Classifications, Video Tracking, and more. He moved to the U.S.A. in 2018, and is married with two amazing children. Professor Duran has lived in Richmond, TX, since 2019. Among his hobbies are cycling, seeing new places, and travelling. He is also highly interested in mechanics and fixing things in his daily life. Finally, he very much enjoys spending his leisure time with his family, especially on holidays and by going out on family vacations.

# Dennis Foung

# Tze-Li Hsu

Tze-Li Hsu received his doctoral degree in Sociology at Florida State University in 2014, and he is a tenure track faculty in the department of Sociology at Sam Houston State University since 2015. As an assistant profession of Sociology, he teaches various courses at the undergraduate/graduate level, including Social Statistics, Introduction to Ethnic Studies, and Sociology of Aging. He is interested in the intersection of mental health, aging, gender difference, and marital status in aging and the life course perspective. He has advanced statistical training including structural equation modeling, latent growth curve analysis and managed multiple large-scale projects throughout his time in graduate school. His scholarly work can be seen in Sociological Spectrum and Asian Population Studies. His research examines the association between marital status and mental health, with special attention to variations across age groups and to dimensions of psychological well-being among the elderly, using data from the Midlife and Development in the United States (MIDUS). Also, he is interested in the potential mediating factors in the socioeconomic status and well-being association data from the Health and Retirement Study (HRS). His current agenda focuses on this topic, drawing on interdisciplinary theories, advanced statistical methods, and a focus on age and the broader dimensions of mental health and well-being for the elderly. His research on these topics has led to several papers published, under review or revision and has won Sociological Spectrum 2013 Outstanding Paper Award. He has frequently presented his research findings at national conferences, meetings, and workshops.

# Qingzhong Liu

Qingzhong Liu is currently an Associate Professor at Sam Houston State University. His research interest includes digital forensics, cybersecurity, multimedia computing and computer vision, bioinformatics, computational intelligence, and computing applications. He was the recipient of the Fraud Impact Award at Great Houston in 2015 and the SHSU Excellence in Scholarly and Creative Activity Award in 2017. He has been serving on the Technical Program Committee of the ACM Workshop on Information Hiding and Multimedia Security since 2013, and on the Technical Program Committee and one of area chairs for the ACM Multimedia Conference since 2019. In 2020, Stanford University ranked him among the world’s top 2% scientists in Artificial Intelligence and Image Processing. As a sole inventor, he has three patents in multimedia forensics. His study has been funded by the US Army, NSF, NIJ, and NIH.
