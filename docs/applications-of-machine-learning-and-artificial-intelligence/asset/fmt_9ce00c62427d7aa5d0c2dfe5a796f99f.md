# Data Analysis

**WordSmith 6.0** was chosen as the tool to analyze text data extracted from the corpora. The following features are of primary focus when using **WordSmith 6.0**: the first five words on the left (L5 to L1) and the right (R5 to R1) of the node (i.e. key word of investigation), and the cluster (i.e. the patterns of the node). Structural analysis for the text data was also conducted to explain from a lexicogrammatical approach how arrangement of words in one sentence encoded its grammatical relations. Findings from both **WordSmith** and structural analysis aimed to quantify the length of an action in British and American “moment” expressions: `V+ momentarily`, `V+ for a moment`, and `V+ in a moment`.

# Teaching Procedure

On the concern of languaging, this section presents the teaching procedure of moment expressions in three phases: observation, generalization, and reproduction (Gavioli, 2001). In particular, students initially draw on what they have already known to perceive form/meaning connections; and subsequently, the teacher develop instructional activities to raise learner consciousness of the new constructed form/meaning connections. A summary of key observations will be synthesized after the completion of three stated phases.

## Observation

**Teacher (T)** designs warm up activities to introduce to students (**Ss**) moment expressions: `momentarily`, `for a moment`, and `in a moment`. **T** can show some examples of the following structures `V+ momentarily`, `V+ for a moment`, and `V+ in a moment` from the two varieties British and American English and have **Ss** predict the length of the action indicated by the main verbs.

After that, **T** shows to **Ss** the list of verbs which `momentarily` functions as pre-modifier and draws **Ss** attention to comparing the frequency of verbs associated with `momentarily`. Following after the demonstration of L1 and R1 positions of the node `momentarily`, **Ss** discuss the significance of how `momentarily` is used in sentence structure. Across the two varieties of English, the main verbs following `momentarily` are in passive voice and past tense. It is also noted the passive form of the verb `distract` in past tense was a significant pattern which is associated with `momentarily` in British English.

## Generalization

The interpretation task is drawn from the significant cluster `was momentarily distracted` from the patterns of `momentarily` (Figure 1). **T** performs the search for examples on the selected formulaic sequence of `was momentarily distracted`. From these interpretations, **Ss** will quantify the length of action in the equivalent formulaic sequence of `in a moment` and `for a moment`.

The following examples show `in a moment` as a synonymous expression for `momentarily` in American English to because there is no delay between two actions. Meanwhile, as being identified with a delay between two subsequent actions, `for a moment` is considered a synonym of `momentarily` in British English.

**(american)**: “A 17th century drawing by Rembrandt was snatched from a private art display at a California luxury hotel while a curator `was momentarily distracted`, officials said Monday” (Dawn, 2011)

**(british)**: “The peasant quietly came in and sat down in front of the money. When the assistant `was momentarily distracted` by a fit of coughing he took out his single rupee and put it on the counter opposite the mass of rupees. Then he waited for his money to attract money” (Leggett, 2000)

## Reproduction

### Activity 1: Defining the length of the action

Using the following quotation, **T** raises student awareness to 3 synonymous expressions: `momentarily`, `for a moment`, and `in a moment`.

> An English friend of mine says that he nearly had a heart attack on a flight in the United States when the American pilot announced that the plane would be airborne “momentarily.” In British English, the language my friend speaks, “momentarily” means “for a moment,” and he thought the pilot was suggesting an imminent crash soon after takeoff. In American English, however, “momentarily” means “in a moment,” and the pilot was merely appeasing the impatient passengers (Tharoor, 2003)

Alternatively, **T** can ask **Ss** to interpret the length of the action `be in the air` in the two sentences. Suggested answers are in the brackets:

*   **●** The plane would be in the air `for a moment`. (British English, short action)
*   **●** The plane would be in the air `in a moment`. (American English, long action)

### Activity 2: Checking dictionary

**T** can have **Ss** check online or paper dictionaries of British and American English (e.g. **Oxford**, **Cambridge**, **Macmillian**, **Longman** dictionaries, etc.) of the moment expressions: `momentarily`, `for a moment`, and `in a moment`. **Ss** interpret the definitions and examples from these resources to quantify the length of action indicated by the main verbs. Depending on the level of **Ss**, **T** can set the task of interpreting examples from other online resources such as **Google search** and online newspapers for **Ss** to explore the aspect of length of the action in these three expressions in British and American English.

# Further Suggested Application

This activity shows how corpus data can be used to explore the emphasis of adverbial phrases in certain time frame. Apart from the focus of moment expression on measuring length of time for the collocated actions in verb, there are some other topics worth investigating such as the emphasis on adverbs of frequency (e.g. `often`, `frequently`, `usually`, `occasionally`) on verb collocation in the time frame of present simple. On the perfectual aspect of verb tense, **T** can collect data of adverbs indicating the perfect tense and guide **Ss** into an analysis of the aspect of recently completed action or the continuous aspect of action from past until the present. Meanwhile, for advanced **Ss**, the time frame of future can be analyzed on a variety of adverb usages and verb tenses (present simple, present simple continuous, `be going to`, and future simple).
