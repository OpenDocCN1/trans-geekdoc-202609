# IoT and IIo Powered by Blockchain

# PRIVACY LEAKAGE

There are many protection layers built into blockchain technologies to prevent unauthorized access to transaction-based reports maintained in blockchains. Bitcoin transactions are made using IP addresses rather than actual customer identities, which ensures personal confidentiality. Even though these protection measures are inconsistent and insufficient, one-time accounts (OTAs) can be created by Bitcoin to help users maintain their anonymity. For instance, it has been found that consumer pseudonyms are regularly broken by inferring from learning about the various transactions connected to a single general user. In addition, the entire blockchain’s transaction data space can still have an impact on the possibility of confidentiality, as noted.

# SECURITY VULNERABILITY

IIoT can increase security through encryption and signature creation by blockchains. IIoT continues to work actively on protection against the weaknesses of both blockchain and IoT systems. Building wireless networks in industrial contexts is becoming more popular since wireless communication organizations can be measured and practical. Security breaches like jamming, submersive overwhelming, and recurrent attacks are caused by the open wireless channel. Additionally, due to the resource restraints of IIo nodes, conventional extreme weighted encryption methods might not be applicable in the IIo. In dispersed locations, it can be difficult to keep an eye on the keys as well.

# RESOURCE CONSTRAINTS

Numerous IoT nodes with constrained resources are present. RFID tags, and smart meters, for instance, have been constrained link ability, constrained storage, and decreased computational power. Additionally, the decentralized consensus techniques employed by blockchains typically consume more energy and need more computational power. Additionally, it has been proven that Bitcoin’s PoW uses a large power expenditure. Therefore, the consensus technique, which necessitates a large power expenditure, may not be practical for low-energy IoT nodes. On the other hand, the sheer volume of blockchain data makes it impractical to implement blockchains completely across the IoT. For instance, the size of blockchainbased Bitcoin has crossed 185 GB by the end of September 2018. An IoT node cannot fully satisfy the entire blockchain.
