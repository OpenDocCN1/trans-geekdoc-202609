# INTRODUCTION

Recently, the trends and technology advancements of the Internet of Things (IoT) have made it possible for automation, data collection, and analytics in numerous industries. Communication between physical devices is made possible via the IoT, for example, using sensors for the collection and exchange of data with other devices over the internet. The hub connects various devices, gathers real-time data, analyzes, and shares it locally. The applications of IoT include smart water and smart healthcare-level monitoring, smart solar panels, smart home, smart waste management, and smart agriculture.

The Internet of Things makes it possible for the connectivity of inventory materials, over the Internet; information can be delivered or received. IoT has an impact on a variety of technologies, including embedded systems, artificial intelligence sensors, and real-time analysis. Smart devices can gather information and transmit it to perform specific, everyday life tasks. Smart cities, networked healthcare systems, intelligent transportation systems, and other IoT applications are accessible. IoT implementation in the medical sector is centered on a variety of devices, AI, diagnostic tools, and high-tech imaging equipment [1].

After the data has been connected to the control systems and collected from the sensors near or on patients, it is transmitted to the health monitoring unit. The cloud is used to store data, which helps manage the amount of data while preserving security. Since it is challenging to encrypt data from minimum-resource devices, security is a vital aspect of IoT. When data is sent to the cloud, there is a chance its integrity and confidentiality will be compromised. Its distributed nature, the cloud is the best option for storing health information that is simple for clinicians to gain access to while providing care for patients who reside far away. IoT and cloud use real-time processing, which increases complexity [2].
