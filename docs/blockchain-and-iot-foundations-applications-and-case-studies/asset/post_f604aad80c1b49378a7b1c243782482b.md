## INTRODUCTION

The Internet of Things (IoT) is an effective internet and managerial setting that works together to create a world that is intelligently interconnected [1]. IoT users can broadcast the data that they place on the server where it can be intelligently processed. At present, IoT is being used in many contexts, such as corporations, industries, environmental engineering, mobile devices, and governance. IoT also helps pay the transportation, healthcare, agriculture, energy, and environmental management industries. IoT supports many commercial initiatives in the fields of services, manufacturing, logistics, and food production. Academics and businesses have paid a lot of attention to Industrial IoT (IIoT), which could be very important in the upcoming conversion of industrial schemes. IoT plays a vital role in Industry 4.0, and it is also one of the most important topics in academics and politics. The major difference between IoT and IIoT is that whereas IoT is frequently applied in applications like smart factories and smart manufacturing, IIoT environments frequently feature a variety of automation equipment and industrial devices.

IoT provides connectivity to industrial systems through the help of drives, sensing devices, a pervasive network, and processing power. Implementing IoT is intended to increase manufacturing efficiency and business productivity, lessen device downtime, and raise the caliber of the final output. Specifically, IoT features the following characteristics: decentralization of IoT systems, a variety of IoT-based applications, schemes, heterogeneity of IoT-based information, and network strain [2].
