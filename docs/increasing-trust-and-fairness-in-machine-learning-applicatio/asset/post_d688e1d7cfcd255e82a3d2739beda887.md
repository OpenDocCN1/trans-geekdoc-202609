
## 2.5 Acceptance of ML models

The introduction of a Project Initiation Document (PID) at the organisation is one of the requirements for the organisation's project interpretability. Every new machine learning model-PID also includes sections on bias and discrimination, explainability, and the ethical concerns involved. It must be accepted by the Data Privacy Officer, Compliance and Risk departments before any step of developing a new model or updating an existing model is taken in turn covered by a Record of Processing Activity (ROPA) on data used that is reviewed by the Compliance department. Internal specific guidelines and development methods must be approved by Finance.

Furthermore, the organisation is interested in using automated systems to quantify bias and discrimination as well as to gain insight into the decision making process. Furthermore, the MLX1 system was developed in a way to determine whether a model is biased towards certain demographic groups. Our MLX2 system was designed to support a generalised approach to including interpretability and bias in the Project Initiation Document. This enables the organisation to streamline internal processes when concerning the acceptance of new models.

Furthermore, it describes the development of the systems, as well as the demonstrations of their capabilities. Lastly, we outline the methods for validation of both.

### 3.1. Logical design

The system for local interpretability involves generating explanations for applications that have been flagged as high risk. In Fig. 1 we see the different ways for an application after it has been classified. If an application is classified as high risk then it is passed to the reviewer for review, after which the explanation is presented. A reviewer first interprets the explanation before they would normally, to ensure the model decision is verified. This ensures that the model decision is not influenced by the reviewer after they have made their own decision. The other path follows the normal assessment by a reviewer, if the application has not been judged as a high risk. Based on their decision, the application proceeds or stops. If a risk is identified by a reviewer, the mortgage application is passed onto the anti-fraud department by the reviewer, who do in more research into the application and the decision. If the fraud is proven, it is returned to the organisation. However, resolution of suspicion by the anti-fraud officer is valid to reject the application at first, if the applicant is rejected by the requester. The decision is subject to an appeal, supporting the fraud suspicion but not conclusively proving it.

The MLX1 simply replaces the current method for generating the explanation, which in the current situation is using simple decision rules. It also gives better insight into the decision made by the model, identifying those that support the model (or no risk) indication. Given that the reviewers are expected to spend 5 minutes evaluating the model explanation, can be taken to explain the structure in such a way that this time can limit is still.

Internal processes at the organisation need to start new AI projects from a providing system global interpretability and bias detection. MLX2 was designed to help the organisation to ensure that models are free of bias, and to provide an overview of the decision making process.

Currently, the results of the ROPA on all data used by the model are used to ensure that the model is free, but the identification of bias is still difficult. By identifying and quantifying bias, the PID has been helped by the data scientist, that has been approved by Compliance, Legal and Risk departments before the model is built. Any elements of the PID could lead to unclear how bias is identified and quantified, and the results of this in the PID could streamline the process for AI projects in all future cases.

The MLX1 and MLX2 offer the following two: Firstly, MLX1 allows for local interpretability, giving the opportunity to gain mortgage reviewers insights into the decision making of individual mortgage applications that are affected by local interpretability and bias detection, enabling the system to use and integrate with existing models for bias and gain overall insights into their behaviour.

In light of this, we decided to use SHAP (Lundberg & Lee 2017) for local interpretability, because it is able to identify the contribution of the result of a simple local case is beneficial to the human reviewers working with the outcome of the model. The SHAP method can be used to show which features have a big or small influence on the outcome of a case gives insights into the model behaviour. SHAP is an aggregate, kernel-based explanation for values. SHAP is a more efficient, model-specific explanation for tree-based models.

Where we found that the running time of the Kernel was able to produce local explanations for all cases in test was so slow that TreeSHAP was able to produce explanations in a reasonable time. TreeSHAP has time complexity $O(T2^L)$ (Lundberg et al. 2020).

In this case we found that the running time of the Kernel was able to produce local explanations for all cases in test was so slow that TreeSHAP was able to produce explanations in a reasonable time. TreeSHAP is much faster. As important as SHAP is we have to run once per batch, or per each day. We wanted to use SHAP and TreeSHAP because they did not produce different importance rankings. To do this, we compared the features that were ranked in the top 5, as these are the features that would be used in the explanation of our MLX system. We found, on that, the 5 features produced by both methods contained 4 of the same. In practice, this functional difference has no impact on the use of the system.
