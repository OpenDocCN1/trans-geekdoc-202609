
## Device-Free Localization Using Extreme Learning Machine with DTW-Based Feature Extraction

Jianqiang Xue, Zhenyue Gao, Jianxing Zhang, Wendong Xiao*  
School of Automation and Electrical Engineering  
University of Science and Technology Beijing  
Beijing, China  
e-mail: wdxiao@ustb.edu.cn

**Abstract—** In recent years, WiFi CSI based device-free localization has attracted widespread attention. Most of the previous work used Received Signal Strength Indication (RSSI), and the acquisition of Channel State Information (CSI) significantly improved the localization accuracy based on WiFi. In this paper, we propose a device-free indoor localization method based on CSI. First, we use CSI Tools to collect CSI and use some specific filters to filter out the noise. Then, we use Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) to extract the differences between the two data segments as fingerprints. Finally, Extreme Learning Machine (ELM) is used for modelling and calculating the location of the target. We set up a system in the laboratory to verify the method’s performance, which confirmed the effectiveness of the method proposed in this paper.

**Keywords—** device-free localization; channel state information; extreme learning machine; dynamic time warping

## I. INTRODUCTION

In recent years, wireless network technology has developed rapidly, and people have begun to demand effective and accurate service applications. Target localization is one of them. Presently, outdoor localization technology has matured, and a variety of service applications have been derived, providing great convenience to people's lives. Outdoor localization technology mainly relies on satellite localization technology such as the Global Positioning System (GPS), which can estimate the target's location information in real-time. However, due to environmental factors such as multipath effects, shadows and fading [1], this localization technology is challenging to work in indoor situations. Therefore, obtaining a good target localization effect in an indoor environment is still a problem to be solved urgently.

Although indoor localization has attracted much attention, the immaturity of technology is still a big problem currently facing. Unlike outdoor localization systems such as GPS and base station localization, indoor localization systems still do not have a unified standard organization to formulate unified technical standards and specifications. The current mainstream indoor localization technical solutions are developed under the agreements and solutions defined by each enterprise, resulting in a variety of indoor localization technologies, like the infrared [2], ultrasonic [3], camera [4], ultra-wideband [5] and wireless local area network. Camera-based system can achieve high accuracy, but it requires a good lighting environment and will cause privacy issues; ultrasonic-based system is affected by noise and has limited coverage; infrared-based system requires professional infrastructure; visible-light-based system can only work in visible environment. Due to the popularity of WiFi devices in mobile terminals and the development of indoor environments, WiFi-based indoor localization technologies have received more attention.

WiFi based indoor localization technology analyses the characteristics of the radio signal received by the mobile terminal in the wireless local area network, and estimates the position of the target according to the corresponding localization model. At present, most indoor WiFi localization systems use Received Signal Strength Indication (RSSI) or Channel State Information (CSI) as the received signal. CSI is a research hotspot in recent years. Dynamic-Music [6] proposed a localization method combined with the Multiple Signal Classification Algorithm (MUSIC) algorithm, which uses the CSI phase to detect the human body reflection signal and further calculates the Angle of Arrival (AoA) and Time of Arrival (ToA) to estimate location information. IndoTrack [7] estimates the Doppler velocity from the CSI signal, and further estimates the position and velocity information of the target. Widar [8] uses CSI and locates the target from a geometric angle, and at the same time estimates the target's velocity estimate. Haibin Bian [9] researched on WiFi CSI indoor localization methods, combined CSI information and location fingerprint methods, and used CSI data information as location fingerprint information based on the spatial and frequency characteristics of CSI data, and proposed clustering based on CSI weighted space-frequency characteristics. The algorithm clusters fingerprint data, completes matching localization, removes part of the separated cluster points and inappropriate nearest neighbors in the database through secondary correction and finally realizes WiFi based indoor localization. Fuseloc [10] uses multiple features extracted from the channel state information to perform the first information fusion based on indoor localization.

In this paper, we propose to use Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) to extract signal features and employ Extreme Learning Machine (ELM) to model the relation between fingerprints and target points. Specifically, the workflow of this method includes data collection, preprocessing, feature extraction and relationship modelling. The main contributions of this research are:

1. Use the DTW method to extract signal features;
2. Use ELM to model device-free localization;
3. Build a system and compare the effects of this method with other methods.

The rest of this article is structured as follows. Section II gives a brief introduction to the relevant content, and Section III describes the system design. Section IV introduces the experimental verification in detail. Section V gives the conclusion.

## II. PRELIMINARIES

### A. Channel State Information

In the past WiFi based indoor localization research, most are based on the RSSI. The RSSI is the superimposed energy of multiple wireless signals, which will attenuate as the distance increases, which is very unstable. With the application of OFDM technology in WiFi, the channel state information of WiFi can be extracted. The CSI describes the attenuation of wireless signals in the propagation process, and records the amplitude and phase information of each subcarrier in the frequency domain [11]. It has the advantages of stability, fine-grained, and rich information.

In a wireless local area network, the received data vector can be expressed as

\(Y = HX + W\), (1)

where X is the transmitted data vector, H is the CSI matrix, and W is the noise vector. H can be calculated as

\(H = Y/X\), (2)

H is the CFR sampling information of the channel frequency response in the frequency domain, and CSI tools make it possible to obtain a version of the CFR sampling with 30 samples [12]. Using an IEEE 802.11a/g/n compatible wireless card, a set of CSI is extracted from each packet received, with each CSI representing the amplitude and phase of an orthogonal frequency division multiplexed subcarrier [13]. CSI can be expressed as

\(H(f_k) = \|H(f_k)\| e^{j\angle H(f_k)}\), (3)

where \(H(f_k)\) is the CSI of the kth subcarrier with frequency \(f_k\), \(\|H(f_k)\|\) and \(\angle H(f_k)\) represent the amplitude and phase of the kth subcarrier, respectively.

### B. Extreme Learning Machine

ELM is a single hidden layer feedforward network with a three-layer structure: input layer, hidden layer and output layer [14], as shown in Fig. 1. The most important feature of ELM is that the parameters of the hidden layer are randomly initialised, and only the output weights need to be calculated by least squares estimation [15]. Therefore the learning speed is hundreds or thousands of times faster than most other machine learning methods and the generalisation ability of ELM is also excellent.

![](img/bc94f492e7e471f80902b1c5ae1db01e_1_0.png)

Denoting the number of hidden cells in the feedforward network as \(L_{hn}\), the output function of the ELM can be expressed as

\(f(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{L_{hn}} \beta_i g_i(x) = g(x)\beta = T\), (4)

where x denotes the input to the ELM, i.e. the features of the sample, T is the label of the data, g denotes the activation function and β denotes the output weights. We denote the input weights as w and the bias as b and g(x) can be expressed as

\(g(x) = [g(x; w_1, b_1), \ldots, g(x; w_{L_{hn}}, b_{L_{hn}})]\), (5)

Further, Equation (4) can be expressed as

\(G\beta = T\), (6)

of which,

\(G = [g(x_1); \ldots; g(x_{N_s})] = [g_1(x_1), \ldots, g_{L_{hn}}(x_1); \ldots; g_1(x_{N_s}), \ldots, g_{L_{hn}}(x_{N_s})]\), (7)

where \(N_s\) indicates the number of samples. In addition,

\(T = [t_1, \ldots, t_{N_s}]^T\), (8)

Finally, the estimate of the output weights can be obtained by the following formula

\(\tilde{\beta} = G^\dagger T\), (9)
