## Controlled ovarian stimulation, fertilization and embryo culture

The patients underwent controlled ovarian stimulation with a long gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) agonist protocol or a GnRH antagonist protocol according to each patient's ovarian response and medical history of ART treatment. Human chorionic gonadotropin (HCG; Ovidrel, Merck Serono, Rome, Italy) was administered when at least three dominant preovulatory follicles with mean diameter >18 mm were measured ultrasonographically. Oocyte retrieval was performed via the vagina under ultrasonographic guidance at 36 h after HCG administration.

IVF or intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) insemination was performed according to the patient's condition. IVF was performed 38–39 h after HCG administration at a concentration of approximately 10,000 spermatozoa/50-μl droplet. Oocytes were denuded of cumulus cells 4–6 h after IVF insemination to evaluate extrusion of the second polar body, and the oocytes showing a second polar body were transferred to EmbryoScope+ for culture. Alternatively, oocyte denudation was performed 38–39 h after HCG administration, and ICSI was performed 2 h later. After microinjection, all oocytes were cultured individually in the EmbryoScope+ time-lapse incubator under 6% CO2 and 5% O2 at 37°C. Embryo culture was conducted using pre-equilibrated G-1 culture medium (Vitrolife, Gothenburg, Sweden) until 66–68 h after fertilization.

Conventional morphological assessment was implemented with a static observation on day 3 (68 h) after insemination using the images recorded by the EmbryoViewer software (Vitrolife). Conventional morphological assessments of embryos were primarily conducted according to the Istanbul consensus report (ALPHA Scientists In Reproductive Medicine, ESHRE Special Interest Group Embryology, 2011), and the chief indicators included blastomere number, degree of fragmentation, blastomere evenness and multinucleation. Morphological assessment was conducted by a team of two embryologists with internal quality assurance schemes to confirm consensus in grading. Embryos were assigned the following grades: A (embryos with even blastomeres, <10% cytoplasmic fragmentation and no multinucleation); B (embryos with even or uneven blastomeres, and cytoplasmic fragmentation <25%); C (embryos with even or uneven blastomeres, and cytoplasmic fragmentation between 25% and 50%); and D (embryos with even or uneven blastomeres, and cytoplasmic fragmentation >50%) (ALPHA Scientists In Reproductive Medicine, 2011). Grade D embryos were not transferred during the study period. Grade A embryos reflected the highest priority for transfer, followed by Grade B and Grade C. For embryos with the same grade, those with eight or more blastomeres had highest priority for transfer compared with those with fewer than eight blastomeres. Transferred embryos were divided into six groups based on their morphological grade and number of blastomeres: A+ (grade A and eight blastomeres or more), A (grade A and fewer than eight blastomeres), B+ (grade B and eight blastomeres or more), B (grade B and fewer than eight blastomeres), C+ (grade C and eight blastomeres or more) and C (grade C and fewer than eight blastomeres).

The iDAScore AI model was developed using deep learning and a neural network that was trained to analyse sequences of time-lapse images (Bernsten et al., 2022). The only inputs to the model were the images from the time-lapse sequences, and the outputs were numerical scores from 1.0 to 9.9 that correlated with the likelihood of an FHB. Therefore, iDAScore did not encompass any human-annotated data for training. The images of transferred embryos with KID were thus scored retrospectively using iDAScore Version 2.0.

## Fresh embryo transfer

During this study, patients received two embryos on day 3 of a fresh cycle, unless there was only one transferable embryo or the patient met the indications for SET. SET indications were: scarred uterus; history of cervical conization; cervical incompetence; history of surgery for severe intrauterine adhesion; uterine malformation; history of twin pregnancy; history of obstetric complications; history of chronic disease; or women of short stature, underweight or overweight. After embryo transfer, the luteal phase was supported by a combination of oestrogen and progesterone daily until 12 weeks of pregnancy. The maternal serum β-HCG level was measured 14 days after embryo transfer as an indicator of pregnancy. Implantation was defined as an intrauterine gestational sac with a FHB noted on transvaginal ultrasound 4 weeks after embryo transfer.

## Statistical analysis

Continuous quantitative variables are expressed as mean ± SD, and categorical data are expressed as percentages. Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to assess the distribution of data. Student's t-test was used for continuous data and χ²-test was used for categorical data. The ranking performances of iDAScore and conventional morphological assessment for predicting FHB were calculated by applying an ROC analysis of cluster data (multipredictor comparisons) (Obuchowski, 1997); the calculation of AUC values using iDAScore was based on the continuous value. Tests for significant differences between AUC values were conducted using the paired DeLong's two-tailed test (DeLong et al., 1988). P < 0.05 was considered to indicate significance for all statistical tests, and all analyses were executed using R (http://www.r-project.org) and EmpowerStats (www.empowerstats.com), X&Y Solutions, Inc., Boston, MA, USA).

## RESULTS

### Participant characteristics

In total, 7786 embryo transfers were analysed in 4328 cycles, comprising 870 SET cycles and 3458 DET cycles (TABLE 1). Mean maternal age and the number of previous oocyte pick-up stages were significantly lower in DET cycles than SET cycles (P < 0.001 and P = 0.019, respectively). Furthermore, the ratio of tubal factor was significantly higher in DET cycles than in SET cycles (P < 0.001), whereas combination factor and ovulation disorder ratios were significantly lower in DET cycles than in SET cycles (P < 0.001 and P < 0.001, respectively). There was no significant difference in the mean number of previous embryo transfer cycles and the mean number of previous miscarriages between SET and DET cycles.

### FHB rate of iDAScore groups stratified by morphological grade

The overall FHB rate was 40.1% for all morphological grade groups, which rose with the increase of iDAScore to 25.91% in the 2.0–3.9 group, 43.88% in the 4.0–5.9 group, and 48.34% in the 6.0–8.0 group (TABLE 3). Furthermore, for subgroups of morphological grades A+, B+, B, C+ and C, the FHB rates with an iDAScore of 2.0–3.9 were lower than the rates in the higher iDAScore groups (TABLE 3) (A+ iDAScore 0.0–5.9 P = 0.015, all others P < 0.001, see Table S1, online supplementary material). Additionally, for the A+, B+, B and C groups, the FHB rates in the groups with an iDAScore of 6.0–8.0 were higher than those in all other iDAScore groups. All + groups (i.e. embryos with eight blastomeres or more at time of observation) showed a higher FHB rate than the non-+ groups (i.e. embryos with fewer than eight blastomeres at time of observation).
