
## External validation of a model for selecting day 3 embryos for transfer based upon deep learning and time-lapse imaging

### KEY MESSAGE
Day 3 embryo selection using iDAScore Version 2.0 is more predictive of implantation compared with conventional morphological assessment. A fully automated evaluation system may reduce the time taken for embryo selection, eliminate subjectivity, and serve as a decision support system to assist embryologists in embryo selection.

### ABSTRACT
**Research question:** Could objective embryo assessment using iDAScore Version 2.0 perform as well as conventional morphological assessment?

**Design:** A retrospective cohort study of fresh day 3 embryo transfer cycles was conducted at a large reproductive medicine centre. In total, 7786 embryos from 4328 cycles with known implantation data were cultured in a time-lapse incubator and included in the study. Fetal heartbeat (FHB) rate was analysed retrospectively using iDAScore Version 2.0 and conventional morphological assessment associated with the transferred embryos. The pregnancy-prediction performance of the two assessment methods was compared using area under the curve (AUC) values for predicting FHB.

**Results:** AUC values were significantly higher for iDAScore compared with morphological assessment for all cycles (0.62 versus 0.60; $P = 0.005$), single-embryo transfer cycles (0.63 versus 0.60; $P = 0.043$) and double-embryo transfer cycles (0.61 versus 0.59; $P = 0.012$). For the age subgroups, AUC values were significantly higher for iDAScore compared with morphological assessment in the <35 years subgroup (0.62 versus 0.60; $P = 0.009$); however, no significant difference was found in the $\ge$35 years subgroup. In terms of the number of blastomeres, AUC values were significantly higher for iDAScore compared with morphological assessment for both the <8c subgroup (0.67 versus 0.56; $P < 0.001$) and the $\ge$8c subgroup (0.58 versus 0.55; $P = 0.012$).

**Conclusions:** iDAScore Version 2.0 performed as well as, or better than, conventional morphological assessment in fresh day 3 embryo transfer cycles. iDAScore Version 2.0 may therefore constitute a promising tool for selecting embryos with the highest likelihood of implantation.

### INTRODUCTION
It remains a challenge to optimize assisted reproductive technology (ART) to ensure the selection of embryos with the highest implantation potential. At present, the most commonly used embryo selection method is based upon conventional morphological assessment. For day 3 transfer, embryonic evaluation is based on the Istanbul consensus report (ALPHA Scientists In Reproductive Medicine, ESHRE Special Interest Group Embryology, 2011); this includes the number of blastomeres, degree of fragmentation, blastomere evenness and multinucleation. However, the principal limitation of this method is the subjective influence of assessments made by different observers (Adamson et al., 2016).

Compared with conventional IVF technology, time-lapse technology (TLT) can record total embryonic development continuously, including division timepoints from fertilization and cleavage stages to blastocyst formation. The latter technique provides an improved basis with which to evaluate cleavage patterns, pronuclear movement and other details that cannot be identified using conventional embryo assessment. It thus generates more information that can be applied to embryo selection (Basile et al., 2015; Liu et al., 2020). Although some authors have postulated that the combination of TLT and conventional morphological assessment will improve embryo selection (Coticchio et al., 2018), its clinical value remains controversial (Goodman et al., 2016; Liu et al., 2016).

With the recent development of TLT, attempts have been made to analyse large image datasets using a more powerful computer to identify parameters that may be related to embryonic developmental ability. However, the major differences in culture conditions among individual IVF laboratories hinder construction and wide use of the powerful algorithms involved (Kallas et al., 2022).

Advances in machine learning have increased the availability of electronic medical data and computational processing power, thus facilitating the development of novel artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted methodologies. Such methods involve deep learning, and employ artificial neural networks to analyse the images captured by time-lapse methods to search for patterns related to pregnancy outcomes, potentially providing a more accurate, consistent and objective method to select the most viable embryos (Lundin et al., 2020; Tran et al., 2019). iDAScore AI software is integrated directly into the EmbryoScope+ system (Vitrolife, Göteborg, Sweden), but iDAScore Version 1.0 was trained solely on day 5 or day 6 blastocyst transfer. The original dataset consisted of 115,832 embryos, of which 14,644 were blastocysts transferred with known implantation data (KID) (Bernsen et al., 2022). iDAScore Version 2.0 includes data from day 2 and day 3 transfers (Lassen et al., 2022). This more recent dataset consisted of 181,428 embryos, of which 33,687 were embryos with KID. The network architecture of iDAScore Version 2.0 is identical to Version 1.0 but with a slightly modified training procedure (Lassen et al., 2022). The pregnancy-prediction performance of iDAScore Version 1.0 was confirmed in a large cohort study comprising vitrified-warmed blastocyst transfers (Ueno et al., 2021), and the validation results showed that the program performed as well as, or better than, conventional morphological assessment. Since the performance of iDAScore Version 2.0 has not been validated by independent external datasets, its use with cleavage-stage embryo transfers needs to be externally validated against conventional morphological assessments, such as the morphological grading system based on the Istanbul consensus report (ALPHA Scientists In Reproductive Medicine, ESHRE Special Interest Group Embryology, 2011).

This study compared pregnancy-prediction performance after fresh day 3 embryo transfer using the area under the curve (AUC) from receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis of the two embryo assessment methods: iDAScore Version 2.0 and conventional morphological assessment according to the Istanbul consensus report (ALPHA Scientists In Reproductive Medicine, ESHRE Special Interest Group Embryology, 2011). Additionally, pregnancy-prediction performance of the two embryo assessment methods was compared using AUC values for predicting fetal heartbeat (FHB) rate for all cycle groups and each subgroup (single-embryo transfer (SET), double-embryo transfer (DET), age <35 years, age ≥35 years, and number of blastomeres <8c and ≥8c). To the best of the authors’ knowledge, this is the first external validation of an embryo selection model based on deep learning with respect to cleavage-stage transfer.

### MATERIALS AND METHODS

### Study population and design
This retrospective cohort study was performed at the IVF clinic of Chongqing Health Centre for Women and Children from January 2020 to June 2022. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Chongqing Health Centre for Women and Children (Approval No. 2022-RGI-11, date of approval 10 September 2022).

The inclusion criteria for patients were: (i) fresh autologous oocyte retrieval with day 3 embryo transfer; and (ii) embryos cultured in a time-lapse system (EmbryoScope+). The exclusion criteria were: (i) embryos with blurred or missing photomicrographs during incubation; and (ii) cycles with one FHB in DET cycles. In total, 7786 embryos were included, of which 870 were SET and 6916 were DET. The selection of embryos for transfer was based on conventional morphological assessment. The transferred embryos were divided into six groups based on their morphological grade and number of blastomeres: A+, A, B+, B, C+ and C. The iDAScore values of the transferred embryos were evaluated retrospectively using iDAScore Version 2.0 (Lassen et al., 2022). The iDAScore model outputs numerical scores ranging from 1.0 to 9.9, which are correlated with the likelihood of an FHB. The score ranges from 1.0 to 9.9 for blastocyst transfer and from 2.0 to 8.0 for day 3 transfer. For this study, the iDAScore values of the embryos were grouped into score intervals of 1 (2.0–2.9, 3.0–3.9, 4.0–4.9, 5.0–5.9, 6.0–6.9 and 7.0–8.0) or 2 (2.0–3.9, 4.0–5.9 and 6.0–8.0). Implantation was defined as an intrauterine gestational sac with an FHB noted on transvaginal ultrasonography. AUC values were generated for ranking FHB rates with the use of two embryo assessment methods: iDAScore as an automated AI model, and day 3 embryo morphological assessment established by the Istanbul consensus report (ALPHA Scientists In Reproductive Medicine, ESHRE Special Interest Group Embryology, 2011) as a conventional morphological grading method.
