AUC values for iDAScore were thus significantly greater than those for conventional morphological assessment in both the <8c (P < 0.001) and ≥8c groups (P = 0.012).

## DISCUSSION

This retrospective study showed that iDAScore Version 2.0 is able to predict FHB as well as, or better than, conventional morphological assessment. For transferred embryos with the same morphological grade, the FHB rate increased with the increase in iDAScore. This suggests that day 3 embryo selection based on iDAScore may achieve a higher FHB rate, and is safe and robust compared with conventional morphological assessment.

A significant difference in the quality of transferred embryos was found between SET and DET cycles. The majority of transferred embryos in SET cycles were of lower morphological quality compared with DET cycles. This may be related to the in-house procedure for SET allocation in the study clinic. Although there were no differences overall between SET and DET cycles in the observed FHB rate for embryos with KID within different iDAScore groups, it should be noted that the above analysis was based on embryos with KID, and the actual FHB rates were lower when DET cycles with only one FHB were included. However, the model performance for embryos with KID did not depend upon whether one or two embryos were selected for transfer. Hence, the model could also be used for patient counselling to determine how many embryos should be transferred.

However, for elderly patients, the number of embryos available for transfer is usually limited, and embryos with higher developmental potential can be selected more easily based on morphological assessment. In addition, day 3 embryo morphological grade was associated with euploidy in elderly patients (Moayeri et al., 2008). Therefore, there is a possibility that there was no significant difference in FHB prediction performance between iDAScore and conventional morphological assessment in patients aged ≥35 years. Furthermore, although cleavage-stage embryo morphological grade was associated with a higher blastocyst formation rate (Liu et al., 2022), the pregnancy rate derived from high-quality blastocysts was independent of their morphological grade at cleavage stage (Herbemont et al., 2017; Zilberberg et al., 2021). Therefore, the predictive value of conventional morphological assessment for identifying cleavage-stage embryos with higher implantation potential remains limited.

Previous studies on day 5+ transfers have shown that iDAScore Version 1.0 performed as well as, or better than, blastocyst morphological assessment after a single vitrified blastocyst transfer (Ueno et al., 2021), and that iDAScore Version 1.0 was positively correlated with the live birth rate and negatively correlated with the abortion rate (Ueno et al., 2022).

Although the authors in the aforementioned studies used iDAScore Version 1.0, which was independently validated in the vitrified blastocyst transfer cycles, the results were concordant with the current findings, showing that iDAScore performed better than conventional morphological assessment in pregnancy prediction in fresh day 3 embryo transfer cycles (as also observed with iDAScore Version 2.0). However, the AUC value of 0.62 for fresh day 3 embryo transfer found in this study could not be compared directly with previous studies for iDAScore; for example, the AUC value of 0.70 reported for Kato Ladies Clinic (Ueno et al., 2021) was based on day 5 and day 6 blastocyst transfers. Nevertheless, the lower AUC value should not be considered a decline in predictive performance for iDAScore Versions 1.0 and 2.0, but rather as the result of differences in the specific populations used for validation (Kragh et al., 2021). In addition, a lower AUC value for cleavage-stage transfer compared with blastocyst transfer was expected due to the availability of less information for day 3 embryos compared with embryos after extended incubation. In addition, it should be noted that, regarding internal validation data, iDAScore Version 2.0 exhibited an AUC value of 0.62 for fresh day 3 transfers (Lassen et al., 2022).

The subgroup analysis by maternal age revealed that FHB prediction performance using iDAScore was significantly higher than that using conventional morphological assessment in women aged <35 years, but did not differ in women aged ≥35 years. This suggests that the selection of embryos using iDAScore Version 2.0 was likely to be more beneficial in younger patients. A previous study showed that the best embryos morphologically had a 44% euploidy rate, while the worst embryos morphologically had a 30% euploidy rate in patients aged <35 years (Munné et al., 2007). In addition, day 3 embryo morphology predicted euploidy in women of advanced maternal age but not in younger patients, who may have other factors responsible for embryo dysmorphism (Moayeri et al., 2008). Thus, morphological assessment has reduced ability to select euploid embryos in younger patients, and several good-quality embryos are usually obtained in a single oocyte retrieval cycle for these younger patients. The present study also showed that embryos with higher iDAScores had higher implantation potential among embryos with similar morphological grades. This result suggests that performance of iDAScore for the prediction of FHB may be less dependent on embryo morphology than conventional morphological assessment in younger patients. The subgroup analysis based on the number of blastomeres revealed that FHB prediction performance of iDAScore was significantly higher compared with that of conventional morphological assessment in the <8c and ≥8c groups. The only exception was for subgroup A; however, it is postulated that the small sample size may have contributed to this difference for morphological subgroup A, with only 39 cases in the iDAScore 2.0–3.9 group, 62 cases in the 4.0–5.9 group, and 44 cases in the 6.0–8.0 group. Previous studies have shown that the number of blastomeres is an effective predictor of blastocyst formation rate and IVF success rate after transfer of cleavage-stage embryos (Kong et al., 2016; Tian et al., 2022). In the present study, conventional morphological assessment comprised a static evaluation on day 3 (68 h) after insemination. Previous studies showed that an increased blastomere number on day 3 was correlated with a higher blastocyst formation rate (Yu et al., 2018) and associated with a higher live birth rate (Tian et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2020).
