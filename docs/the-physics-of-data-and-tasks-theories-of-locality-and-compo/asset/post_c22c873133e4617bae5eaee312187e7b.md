
## 3-3 HIERARCHICAL KERNELS AND THEIR SPECTRA

The NTK and RFK of deep CNNs have been derived previously in [Aro+19]. In Section B.2 we report the functional forms of these kernels in the case of hierarchical CNNs. These kernels inherit the hierarchical structure of the architecture and their operations can be visualized via the tree graph of Figure 2. In this case, the leaves represent products between the corresponding elements of two inputs $x$ and $y$, i.e., $x_{1Y}$ and $xy_{1}$, and the root kernel output $\mathcal{K}(x,y)$. The output can be built by layer by following the same recipe for each node: first, the outputs of the previous layer that are connected to the present node, then a nonlinear function that depends on the activation function of the network. In particular, for each couple of inputs $x$ and $y$ on the multipphere $M^{Ps-1}$, hierarchical kernels depend on $s$-dimensional patches of $x$ and $y$ via the $p$ products. As a comparison, Betti and Bachi [BB21] showed that the NTK and RFK of a fully-connected network of any depth depend on the full dot product $x^\top y$, whereas of a shallow CNN can be written as the sum of $p$ kernels, each depending on only one of the patch products [FCW21].

```
$(T_K f)(x) := \int_{S-1} \mathcal{K}(x,y)f(y)dp(y).
```

with $dp(x)$ denoting the uniform distribution of input points on the multipphere. This spectrum of this operator coincides via Mercer’s theorem [Merosg], an alternative representation of the kernel $\mathcal{K}(x,y)$ and a basis for the space of functions that the kernel can approximate. The asymptotic decay of the eigenvalues, in particular, is crucial for the generalization properties of the kernel, as it will be clarified in Section 3.4. Since the input space is a product of $s$-dimensional unit spheres and the kernel depends on the $p$ scalar products between patches of $x$ and $y$, the eigenfunctions of $T_K$ are products of spherical harmonics on the patches (see Section B.1 for definitions and the relevant background). For the sake of clarity, we limit the main paper to the case $s=2$, where each patch $x$ is entirely defined by an angle $\theta$, the multipphere $M^p$ reduces to the $p$-dimensional torus and the eigenfunctions $e^{ik\theta}$, where $\theta:=(\theta_1, \dots, \theta_p)$ and label $k=(k_1, \dots, k_p)$. In this case, the eigenvalues coincide with the $p$-dimensional Fourier transform of the kernel $\mathcal{K}(\cos\theta_1, \dots, \cos\theta_p)$ and the large-$k$ asymptotics are controlled by the nonanalyticities of the kernel [Wi63]. The general case with patches of arbitrary dimension is presented in the appendix.

**Theorem 3.3.1** (Spectrum of hierarchical kernels).
