
## 34 LOCALITY DEFEATS THE CURSE OF DIMENSIONALITY

Theorem 2.4.1 is proven in Section A.4 and extended to the scenario of local teachers and students in Section A.5. Below, we provide an intuitive proof for the simpler nonoverlapping patch case.

We begin by calculating the variance of the coefficients of the target function in the student eigenbasis. By indexing the coefficients with the $s$-dimensional wavevectors $k$,

```
$$\mathbb{E}[|c_k|^2] = \int_{[0,1]^d} d^x \phi_k(x) \int_{[0,1]^d} d^y \phi_k(y) \mathbb{E}[f^*(x)f^*(y)] = \int_{[0,1]^d} d^x \phi_k(x) \int_{[0,1]^d} d^y \phi_k(y)K_T(x,y). \quad (43)$$
```

When the teacher and students share the same filter size ($s = t$) they share the same eigenfunctions. Using the eigenvalue equation for the teacher kernel we have $\mathbb{E}[|c_k|^2] \sim k^{-(a+t)}$ by ordering eigenvalues by increasing wavelet magnitude $k$, with multiplicity $k^{s-1}$ from all the wavevectors having the same modulus, we

```
$B(P) = \sum_{|k|>p^{1/s}} k^{-(a+t)} \sim \int_{p^{1/s}}^{\infty} k^{-(a+t)}k^{s-1} dk \sim P^{-\dots}. \quad (44)$
```

When the teacher size $t$ is reduced, some coefficients $\mathbb{E}[|c_k|^2]$ vanish. Specifically, as the target function becomes a composition of $t$-dimensional components, the only non-zero coefficients are found for $k$'s which lie in $t$-dimensional subspaces of the $s$-dimensional Fourier space. These subspaces correspond to wavevectors having at most of $t$ consecutive non-vanishing components. In other words, $\mathbb{E}[|c_k|^2]$ is finite only if $k$ is $t$-dimensional and the integral on the right-hand side of Equation 44 becomes $t$-dimensional, this

```
$B(P) = \int_{p^{1/s}}^{\infty} k^{-(a+t)}k^{s-1} dk \sim P^{-\dots}. \quad (45)$
```

Finally, if the teacher patches are not contained in the student ones, the target function cannot be represented in the student eigenbasis. Therefore, the generalization error does not decay to zero but instead approaches a finite positive limit as $P \to \infty$.

## 2.5 EMPIRICAL LEARNING CURVES FOR RIDGLESS REGRESSION

We numerically evaluate the asymptotic behavior of learning curves within our teacher-student framework. We simulate different combinations of convolutional and local teachers and students with overlapping patches and Laplacian constituent kernels defined by $c(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j) = e^{-\|\mathbf{x}_i-\mathbf{x}_j||}$. To test the robustness of our predictions to different data distributions, we consider data uniformly generated in the hypercube $[0,1]^d$ (Figure 1) or on a $d$-dimensional hypersphere (Section A.7). Figure 1 presents learning curves for convolutional (left) and local (right panels) students learning a convolutional function. Additional results for the case of a local teacher are included in Section A.7, and confirm the same qualitative trends.

We refer to the six panels of Figure 1. Panels A and B show that, under the assumptions of Theorem 2.4.1, with $\hat{a}_t=a_t=1$, our prediction $\beta=1/s$ holds independently of the embedding dimension $d$. Moreover, fixing the dimension $d$ and the teacher filter size $t_t$, the generalization errors of a convolutional and a local student with the same filter size differ only by a multiplicative constant independent of $P$. The shift-invariant nature of the convolutional student only results in a pre-asymptotic correction to our estimate of the generalization error $B(P)$. Panels C and D display learning curves for different values of $s$ and $t$. When the size of the filters matches the input dimension, the curse of dimensionality is smaller, equal to, or larger than $t$. When $s<t$, the student kernel cannot represent the target function, and the error does not increase by increasing $P$.

All empirical results are in excellent agreement with the theoretical predictions. Additional experimental details, as well as results using the Neural Tangent Kernel of one-hidden-layer fully-connected network as the student kernel, are reported in Section A.7. Notably, despite the lack of translational invariance in that setting, our theoretical predictions still hold.

## 2.6 ASYMPOTICS OF LEARNING CURVES WITH RIDGE

Theorem 2.6.1. *Let us consider a positive-definite kernel $K$ with eigenvalues $\Lambda_\rho \to \infty$, $\Phi_P$ learning a (random) target function $f^*$ with ridge $\lambda$ from $P$ observations. Let us denote by $D_T(\Lambda)$ the reduced density of kernel eigenvalues, and also assume that*

- i) For any $p$ of indices $\rho_1, \dots, \rho_p$, the vector $(\Phi_P(x_1), \dots, \Phi_P(x_p))$ is a Gaussian random vector;
- ii) The target function in the kernel basis with coefficients $c_\rho$ and $D_T(\Lambda) \sim \Lambda^{-(1+r)}, c^2(\Lambda) \sim \Lambda^q$ asymptotically for small $\Lambda$ and $r < q < r+2$;

Then the following equivalence holds in the limit $P \to \infty$ and $\lambda \to 0$ with $1/\sqrt{\lambda} \to 0$:

```
$$\mathcal(\lambda, P) \sim \sum_{\rho: \Lambda_\rho < \lambda} \mathbb{E}[|c_\rho|^2] = \int c^2(\Lambda)D_T(\Lambda)d\Lambda,$$ $(48)$$
```

The following follows from Jacot et al. [Jac2b].

**Corollary 2.6.1.1.** *Performing kernel ridge regression in a teacher-student scenario with smoothness exponents $\alpha_t$ (teacher) and $\alpha_s$ (student), with ridge $\lambda \sim P^{-\gamma}$ and $0 < \gamma < 1/2$, under the joint hypotheses of Theorem 2.4.1 and Theorem 2.6.1, the exponent governing the asymptotic scaling of the generalization error with $P$ is given by:*

```
$$\beta = \frac{\gamma \alpha_t}{\alpha_s + s},$ $(49)$$
```
