The fraction of errors measures how many of the generated data are not compatible with the RHM rules. We observe that for $P < 4,096$, the fraction of errors decreases only in correspondence with memorization: the generated data are valid according to the grammar rules, but they are copies of the training set. For $P > 4,096$, instead, the fraction of errors decreases before the onset of memorization: the diffusion model is generating valid data that do not belong to the training set, and it is therefore generalizing. In Section F.4, we show that the generated data reports the correct statistics of the RHM rules, therefore learning the true data distribution. As a reference, Figure 25 reports the time $	au=P^*$ as a dashed line. We observe that the generalizing models ($P=4,096$ and $P=16,384$) achieve a fraction of errors $<15\%$ for $	au > 	au^*$. Therefore, these models present a dynamical phase $	au^* < 	au_{	ext{mem}}$ where they achieve nearly perfect generalization before starting to memorize. This phase becomes longer with increasing $P$.
