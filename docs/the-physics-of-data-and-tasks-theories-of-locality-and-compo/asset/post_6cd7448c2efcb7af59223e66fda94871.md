### 1.2.2 Infinite-width networks

One tool is the infinite-width limit of neural networks. As the number of neurons per layer grows, the network's behavior simplifies and, in certain regimes, becomes analytically tractable.

**INFINITE-WIDTH AT INITIALIZATION** When the weights of a network as in Equation 1 are initialized with i.i.d. ```

1. The performance of kernels is determined by their spectra.

```
$(T_K f)(\mathbf{x})=\int \mathcal{K}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}')f(\mathbf{x}')' \quad (10)$
```

Given $P$ elements, only the components aligned with the top $O(P)$ are effectively learned [BCP20; SGW20]. By Wiet and Maal [BM19] has analyzed the spectrum of the NTK for two-layer fully-connected networks, showing that eigenvalues decay with the frequency of the corresponding eigenfunctions. This explains why networks exhibit a preference for learning low-frequency (smooth) functions. Moreover, the same spectral property persists in deeper fully-connected networks, suggesting that in this regime, depth alone does not provide benefits to generalization [Ato19; Ato20].
