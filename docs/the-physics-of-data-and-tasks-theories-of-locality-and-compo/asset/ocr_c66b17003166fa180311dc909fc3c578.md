# HIERARCHICAL MODELS OF NATURAL DATA

Generative models of data have a long history of describing the structure of language and image data. In linguistics, formal grammars describe the syntactic of a language through a hierarchical tree [Rs97]. Similar ideas have been explored to decompose visual scenes hierarchically into objects, and primitives by [ZM+07] have been formalized in pattern theory to practical algorithms for semantic segmentation and scene understanding, as in e.g., [Go: Sis+07; LSF9]. Recent works propose hierarchical decomposition of images, in latent variants are wavelet coefficients at different scales [Mar+22; Kad+23a]. In this case, the graph is not tree-like [Kad+23a] – a conclusion that could stem from the specific choice of latent variables.

# HIERARCHICAL MODELS IN MACHINE LEARNING THEORY

More recently, generative models of data received attention in the context of machine learning theory. In supervised learning, deep networks can represent hierarchical tasks much more efficiently than these works learning from information theory view [Pog+17b] [SH20]. For hierarchical models of data, relations between the input data and the task are critical for learning [Mos16; SSSS17; MSS18; MSS20] and representations learned by neural networks with gradient descent reflect the hidden variables of such models both in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) [Cag+24] and transformers [AZL23]. In this chapter, we use these hierarchical generative models of data to study the denoising dynamics of diffusion models theoretically.
