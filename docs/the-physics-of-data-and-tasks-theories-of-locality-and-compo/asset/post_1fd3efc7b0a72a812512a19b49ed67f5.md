
## A.3 MERCER'S DECOMPOSITION OF CONVOLUTIONAL AND LOCAL KERNELS

In general,

```
$\int_{[0,1]^d} d^x d^y K^{LC}(x,y) e^{\mathbf{k}_l^T x} e^{\mathbf{k}_k^T y} = \sum_{i=1}^d \lambda_i \int_{[0,1]^d} d^x d^y \lambda_q e^{\mathbf{q}_i^T (x-y)} e^{\mathbf{k}_k^T y}$
= \frac{1}{2} \sum_{q \in P_l} \lambda_q \delta(\mathbf{k}_l, \mathbf{q}) + \sum_{i \in P_l} \delta(\mathbf{k}_l, \mathbf{0})
+ \sum_{i \in P_i} \epsilon^q e^{\mathbf{q}_i^T x} \delta(\mathbf{k}_l^{(s)}, \mathbf{0}) \delta(\mathbf{k}_{s-o+1}^{(o)}, \mathbf{q}_i) \delta(\mathbf{q}_{o+1}^{(s-o)}, \mathbf{0})$
+ \sum_{i \in P_i} \epsilon^q e^{\mathbf{q}_i^T x} \delta(\mathbf{k}_l^{(s)}, \mathbf{0}) \delta(\mathbf{k}_{s-o+1}^{(o)}, \mathbf{0}) \quad (155)
```

For $k \in \mathcal{F}^u$, with $u=1, \dots, s$, the deltas which set the first component of $k$ to $0$ are satisfied if

```
$\int_{[0,1]^d} d^x d^y K^{LC}(x,y) e^{\mathbf{k}_l^T x} e^{\mathbf{k}_k^T y} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{q \in P_l} \lambda_q \delta(\mathbf{k}_l, \mathbf{q}) e^{\mathbf{k}_k^T y} + \sum_{q} \epsilon^q e^{\mathbf{q}_i^T x} \delta(\mathbf{k}_l^{(s)}, \mathbf{0}) \delta(\mathbf{k}_{s-o+1}^{(o)}, \mathbf{0}) \quad (156)
```

The second term in brackets vanishes for $k \in \mathcal{F}^s$ and the eigenvalue equation is satisfied with $\lambda_k = \Lambda_k/d$. For $\mathbf{k} = \mathbf{k}^{(u)}$ with $u < s$, $\delta(\mathbf{k}^{(s-u)}, \mathbf{0}) = 0$ for any $o \geq u$. As a result of the remainder, the RHS of Equation 156 becomes a sum over all $q$'s which are $s-u+1$ (including $k^{(u)}$ itself). The patch $\mathbf{x}_l$ multiplied by $q$ in the exponent is also a shift of $\mathbf{x}_l$, thus all the factors $e^{\mathbf{q}_i^T \mathbf{x}_l}$ in the sum coincide with $e^{\mathbf{k}^{(u)T} \mathbf{x}_l}$. As $\lambda_q$ depends on the modulus of $q$, all these terms correspond to the same eigenvalue, $\lambda_k$, so that

```
$\int_{[0,1]^d} d^x d^y K^{LC}(x,y) e^{\mathbf{k}_k^T y} = \frac{s-u+1}{d} \Lambda_{k^{(u)}} e^{\mathbf{k}^{(u)T} \mathbf{x}_l} \quad (157)
```

We prove the expansion of Kernel 149

```
$K^{LC}(x,y) = \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in P_l} C(x,y) = \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in P_l} \lambda_k \phi_k(\mathbf{x}_i) \phi_k(\mathbf{y}_i) \quad (158)
```

As in the proof of eigendecomposition of convolutional kernels, all $k$ having $s-u$ vanishing components can be written as shifts of $k^{(u)} \in \mathcal{F}^u$, with the last $s-u$ components vanishing. The shift of $k$ does not affect $\lambda_k$ nor the product $\phi_k(\mathbf{x}_i)\phi_k(\mathbf{y}_i)$, after summing over $i$ leading to degeneracy of eigenvalues which is removed by restricting the sum over $k$ to $\mathcal{F}^u, u \leq s$, and rescaling the remaining eigenvalues $\lambda_k$ with a factor of $(s-u+1)/d$, leading to Equation 149.

## APPENDIX: LOCALITY DEFEATS THE CURSE OF DIMENSIONALITY

## A.4 PROOF OF THEOREM 2.4.1

**Theorem A.1 (Theorem in main text).** Let $K_T$ be a $d$-dimensional convolutional kernel with translationally-invariant $t$-dimensional constituent and nonanalyticity at the origin controlled by the exponent $a_t > 0$. Let $K_S$ be a $d$-dimensional convolutional or local student kernel with translationally-invariant $s$-dimensional constituent, and with nonanalyticity at the origin controlled by the exponent $a_s > 0$. Assume, in addition, that if kernels have nonoverlapping patches then $s \ge 1$ is an integer multiple of $t$; and are uniformly distributed on a $d$-dimensional torus. Then, the following asymptotic equivalence holds in the limit $P \to \infty$,

$B(P) \sim P^{-\beta}, \quad \beta = a_s/t.$

Proof. For the sake of clarity, we start with the proof in the nonoverlapping patches case, and extend it to the overlapping patches case. Since $K_T$ and $K_S$ have translationally-invariant constituent kernels and data are uniformly distributed on a $d$-dimensional torus, the kernels can be diagonalized in Fourier space. Let us start by considering a convolutional student: because of the constituent kernel's isotropy, the Fourier coefficients $\Lambda_k^{(s)}$ of $K_S$ depend on $|k|$. Notice the superscript indicating the dimensionality of the constituents. In particular, $\Lambda_k^{(s)}$ is a decreasing function of $k$ and, for large $k$, $\Lambda_k \sim k^{-(s+1)}$. Then, $B(P)$

```
$B(P) = \sum_{|k|<k(P)} \mathbb{E}[|c_k|^2],$
```

where $k(P)$ is defined as the wavevector modulus of the $P$-th eigenvalue and $\mathbb{E}[|c_k|^2]$ denotes the variance of the target coefficients in the student eigenbasis. $k(P)$ is such that there are exactly $P$ eigenvalues with $k \le k(P)$,

```
$P = \sum_{|k|<k(P)} 1 \sim \frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d} \theta(k, k(P)) = \frac{\pi^{s/2}}{(2\pi)^s \Gamma(s/2+1)} k(P)^s,$
```

i.e., $k(P) \sim P^{1/s}$.

By denoting the eigenfunctions of the student with $\Phi_k^{(s)}$, the superscript (s) indicating the dimensionality of the constituent plane waves,

```
$\mathbb{E}[|c_k|^2] = \int_{[0,1]^d} d^dx \Phi_k^{(s)}(\mathbf{x}) \int_{[0,1]^d} d^dy \Phi_k^{(s)}(\mathbf{y}) \mathbb{E}[f^*(\mathbf{x})f^*(\mathbf{y})]$
```

```
= \int_{[0,1]^d} d^dx \Phi_k^{(s)}(\mathbf{x}) \int_{[0,1]^d} d^dy \Phi_k^{(s)}(\mathbf{y}) K_T(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).
```

## A.4 PROOF OF THEOREM 2.4.1

Decomposing the teacher kernel $\mathcal{}_T$ into its eigenvalues $\Lambda_q^{(t)}$ and eigenfunctions $\Phi_q^{(t)}(y)$,

```
$\mathbb{E}[|c_k|^2] = \int_{[0,1]^d} d^d x \Phi_k^{(t)}(x) \int_{[0,1]^d} d^d y \Phi_q^{(t)}(y) \left( \Lambda_0^{(t)} + \sum_{q} \Lambda_q^{(t)} \sum_{i \in P_q} \Phi_q^{(t)}(\mathbf{x}_i) \right) \quad (163)
```

The $q=0$ mode of the teacher can give $c_k=0$ only because it does not enter the sum. Once the term with $q=0$ is removed, we consider the integral:

```
$\mathcal{I}_k = \int_{[0,1]^d} d^dy \sum_{i \in P_q} \Phi_q^{(t)}(\mathbf{x}_i) \Phi_q^{(t)}(\mathbf{y}_i) \quad (164)
```

```
$= \left( \frac{s}{d} \right) \sum_{q} \Lambda_q^{(t)} \sum_{i \in P_q} \Phi_q^{(t)}(\mathbf{x}_i) \Phi_q^{(t)}(\mathbf{y}_i) \quad (165)
```

As the patches overlap, $s/d$ is replaced by $s$. The remaining eigenvalues are rescaled with a factor of $(s-u+1)/d$. Since $K_T$ and $K_S$ have translationally-invariant constituent kernels, $K_T$ and $K_S$ have the same symmetry. If $K_T$ is local, then $K_S$ is also local, and $K_S$ is a shift of $K_T$. The eigenfunctions $\Phi_k^{(s)}$ are the same for both $K_T$ and $K_S$. The shift of $k$ does not affect $\lambda_k$ nor the product $\phi_k(\mathbf{x}_i)\phi_k(\mathbf{y}_i)$ because the patches are uniform on the torus. Thus, the scaling of $k_c$ remains unchanged.
