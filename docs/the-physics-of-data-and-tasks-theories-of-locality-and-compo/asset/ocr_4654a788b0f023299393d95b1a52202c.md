Our objective is to compute the learning curve $eta$ achieved by a student performing kernel regression. Specifically, the student kernel embodies a prior on the true function described by the teacher in Equation 20, albeit different from the teacher in terms of filter size ($s$) and smoothness ($a_s$). This model includes infinite-width-one-hidden-layer neural networks in the lazy training regime as a special case [JGH18; Du+19; Lee+19; Aro19].

In this chapter, we analyze a teacher-student model, where the teacher is a Gaussian field with covariance $K_T(x, x')$, possessing a filter size $t$ and a smoothness exponent $a_t > 0$. Kernel regression is implemented by a student with corresponding parameters $s$ and $a_s > 0$. Our main contributions are as follows.

Using recent findings based on the method from statistical physics for generalization in kernel methods [BCP20; CBF21; Lou+21a], we derive the learning curve exponent. We establish that $eta = a_s/s$ when $t \leq s$ and $a_s \leq 2(a_t + s)$. Although this result is not rigorous in general, it is for Gaussian fields when data are sampled from a lattice [GW20], and it corresponds to a provable lower bound on the error when teacher and student are matched [MW81]. We systematically verify our theoretical predictions through numerical experiments performing ridge regression across various filter sizes $t, s$, and input dimensions $d$. Furthermore, leveraging the recent work of Jacob et al. [Le+23] and a Gaussian assumption, we obtain a rigorous estimate of $eta$ for ridge regression when the ridge parameter decreases with the size of the training set. Crucially, the exponent depends only on $s$ and is independent of $d$, explicitly demonstrating that using local filters on compositional data allows circumventing the curse of dimensionality.
