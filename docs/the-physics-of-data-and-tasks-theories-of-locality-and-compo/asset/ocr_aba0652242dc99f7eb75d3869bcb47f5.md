# APPENDIX: LOCALITY DEFEATS THE CURSE OF DIMENSIONALITY

The authors of [BCP20; CBP21] found a heuristic expression for the average of the mean error over realizations of the training set $\{\hat{f}_x\}$, based on the replica method of statistical physics, reads

```
\mathcal{E}(\lambda, P) = \partial_{\lambda} \left( \frac{k_{\lambda}(P)}{P} \right) \sum_{P} (\lambda_{ho} + k_{\lambda}(P))^2 |c_{\rho}|^2, \quad 102
```

where (P) satisfies

```
\frac{k_{\lambda}(P)}{P} = \lambda + \frac{1}{P} \sum_{\rho} \frac{\lambda_{ho} k_{\lambda}(P)/P}{\lambda_{ho} + k_{\lambda}(P)/P}, \quad 103
```

In short, the replica method works as follows [MPV87b]: first one defines an energy function $E(f)$ as the argument of the in Equation 97, then to attribute a probability distribution $P(f) = Z^{-\beta E(f)}$, with $Z$ normalization constant and $\beta=0$. As $\beta \to \infty$, the probability distribution $P(f)$ centers around the solution of the minimization problem of Equation 97, i.e., one can replace $f$ in the right-hand side of Equation 101 with an average over $eta$, then perform the limit $\beta \to \infty$ after the calculation to recover the generalization error. The simplification comes from the fact that $f$ is replaced with its eigendecomposition, the energy function $E(f)$ becomes a quadratic function of the coefficients $c_{ho}$. Then, under the assumption the first and second moments of the eigenfunctions $\phi_{ho}(x)$ w.r.t. x, all averages in Equation 101 reduce to Gaussian integrals. Mathematically, $k_{\lambda}(P)/P$ is related to the Stieltjes transform [PB20] of the Gram matrix $K_{p}/P$ in the large-$P$ limit. Intuitively, it plays the role of a threshold: the mathematical contribution to the error to 0 for $\rho$ such that $\lambda_{ho} \gg k_{\lambda}(P)/P$, and $\mathbb{E}[|c_{ho}|^2]$ for $\rho$ such that $\lambda_{ho} \ll k_{\lambda}(P)/P$. This is equivalent to saying that the algorithm predictor $f(x)$ captures only the eigenfunctions having eigenvalue larger than $k_{\lambda}(P)/P$ (see also [jac+2a; jac+2b]). This intuitive picture can actually be exploited to explore the learning curve $\beta$ from the asymptotic behavior of Equation 102 and Equation 103 in the ridgeless $\lambda \to 0$.
