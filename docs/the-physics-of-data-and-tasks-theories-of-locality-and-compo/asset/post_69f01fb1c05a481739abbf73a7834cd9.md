$$\Delta^{m/2} f_1^2 = \int_0^{\pi} d\theta \sin^{d-2}(\theta) f_1(\theta) \Delta^m f_1(\theta). \quad (240)$$

# B.4 GENERALIZATION BOUNDS FOR KERNEL REGRESSION AND SPATIAL ADAPTIVITY

This appendix provides an introduction to classical generalization bounds for kernel regression and extends Corollary 3.4 to patches on hypersphere.

# B.4.1 Classical generalization bounds

RADEMACHER BOUND. Consider the regression setting detailed in Section 3.4 of text. First, assume that the true function $f^*$ belongs to the RKHS $H$ of the kernel $K$. Then, without assumptions on $K$, we have the following dimension-free bound on the excess risk, based on Rademacher complexity [Bac21],[ie22]:

$$E(\lambda, P) - E(f^*) \le C ||f^*||_H \sqrt{\frac{Tr(T_K)}{P}} \quad (248)$$

where $T_K$ is the integral operator associated to $K$. For hierarchical kernel, having a target with more power in local sectors can result in a smaller $f^*||_H$, hence a smaller excess risk. However, this gain is only a constant factor in terms of sample complexity and, importantly, being in the RKHS requires an order of smoothness which is of the dimension, which is a very restrictive assumption in high-dimensional settings.

SOURCE-CAPACITY. The previous result can be extended by including more details about the kernel and target function. In particular, Proposition 7.2 in [Bac21] states that $f^*$ in closure of $\mathcal{H}$, regularization $\lambda \le 1$ and $P \ge \frac{\sigma^2}{\lambda}(1+\log(1/\lambda))$, has

$$\mathcal{E}(\lambda, P) - \mathcal{E}(f^*) \le 16\frac{\sigma^2}{P}\text{Tr}\left((T_K + \lambda I)^{-1}T_K\right) + 16\inf_{f}{\f-f^*\|_{\ell_2}^2 + \lambda\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2} \frac{5}{p^2}\|f^*\|_{L_{\infty}}^2 \quad (249)$$

where $\sigma^2$ bounds the conditional variance of the labels, i.e., $\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim P}[(y-f^*(x))^2 \mid x] \le \sigma^2$.

Then, let us consider the following standard assumptions in the kernel literature [CDV07]:

$$\text{Capacity:} T_K^{1/a} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\lambda_k)^{1/a} < +\infty.$$
$$\text{source:} \|T_K^{1/2}\|^2 = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\lambda_k)^{-1}(f_k)_{\ell_2}^2 < +\infty. \quad (250)$$

In short, the first assumption characterizes the 'size' of the RKHS (the larger $a$, the smaller number of functions in the RKHS), while the second assumption characterizes the regularity of the target function relative to the kernel (when $r=1, f^* \in \mathcal{H}$; when $r<1, f^*$ is less smooth; when $r>1, f^*$ is smoother). Combining these assumptions with Equation 249, one gets

$$\mathcal{E}(\lambda, P) - \mathcal{E}(f^*) \le 16\frac{\sigma^2}{P}C_1\lambda^{-1/a} + 16C_2\lambda' \frac{5}{p^2}\|f^*\|_{L_{\infty}}. \quad (251)$$

Optimizing for $\lambda$ leads to

$$\lambda_p = \frac{C_1\sigma^2}{a r C_2 P^{\frac{1}{ar+1}}}, \quad (252)$$

and the bounds become

$$\mathcal{E}(\lambda, P) - \mathcal{E}(f^*) \le C_2^2(\frac{\sigma^2}{P})^{\frac{a}{ar+1}} + \frac{5}{p^2}\|f^*\|_{L_{\infty}}. \quad (253)$$

Finally, when $r > (a-1)/\alpha, P \ge \frac{\sigma^2}{\alpha}(1+\log(1/\lambda_P))$ is always satisfied for large $P$.
