Alternatively, $P_{k,s}$ is given explicitly as a function of $t=x^	op y \in [-1,+1]$ via the Rodrigues formula [AH12].

```
$P_{k,s}(t) = \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^k \frac{\Gamma(\left(\frac{s-1}{2}\right))}{\Gamma(k+\frac{s+1}{2})} (1-t^2)^{\frac{s-1}{2}} \frac{d^k}{dt^k} (1-t^2)^{k+\frac{s-3}{2}} . \quad (189)$
```

Legendre polynomials are orthogonal on $[-1,+1]$ with respect to the measure density $(1-t^2)^{(s-3)/2}$, which is the probability density of the scalar product between two points on $S^{s-1}$.

```
$\int_{-1}^{+1} dt \, (1-t^2)^{\frac{s-3}{2}} P_{k,s}(t) P_{k',s}(t) = \frac{|S^{s-1}|}{|S^{s-2}|} \delta_{k,k'} . \quad (190)$
```

With $[S^{s-1}]$ to up, $x,y \in S^{s-1}$, functions of $x$ or $y$ can be expressed as a sum of projections on $x^	op y$ onto the orthogonal harmonics $\{Y_{k,l}\}_l$, whereas functions of $x^	op y$ can be expressed as a sum of projections on the Legendre polynomials $\{P_k(x^	op y)\}$. The two expansions are equalated in the Funk-Hecke formula [AH12].

```
$\int_{S^{s-1}} d\tau(y) \ f(x^top y) Y_{k,l}(y) = Y_{k,l}(x) \int_{-1}^{+1} \frac{|S^{s-2}|}{|S^{s-1}|} dt \ (1-t^2)^{\frac{s-1}{2}} f(t) P_k(t) . \quad (191)$
```

If $f$ has continuous derivatives up to the $k$-th order in $[-1,+1]$, one can use Rodrigues' formula in the right-hand side of Funk-Hecke formula and get, after $k$ integrations by parts,

```
$\int_{S^{s-1}} d\tau(y) \ f(x^top y) Y_{k,l}(y) \n= Y_{k,l}(x) \frac{|S^{s-2}|}{|S^{s-1}|} \frac{\Gamma(\left(\frac{s-1}{2}\right))}{\2\Gamma(k+\frac{s+1}{2})} \int_{-1}^{+1} dt \ f^{(k)}(t) (1-t^2)^{k+\frac{s-3}{2}} , $
```

$f^{(k)}(t)$ denoting the $k$-th order derivative of $f$ in $t$. This trick also applies to functions which are not $k$ times differentiable at $\pm1$, provided the boundary terms due to integration by parts vanish.
