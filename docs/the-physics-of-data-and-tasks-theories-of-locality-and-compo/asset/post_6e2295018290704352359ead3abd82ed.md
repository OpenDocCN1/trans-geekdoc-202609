
Alternatively, $P_{k,s}$ is given explicitly as a function of $t=x^\top y \in [-1,+1]$ via the Rodrigues formula [AH12].

```
$P_{k,s}(t) = \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^k \frac{\Gamma(\left(\frac{s-1}{2}\right))}{\Gamma(k+\frac{s+1}{2})} (1-t^2)^{\frac{s-1}{2}} \frac{d^k}{dt^k} (1-t^2)^{k+\frac{s-3}{2}} \quad (189)$
```

Legendre polynomials are orthogonal on $[-1,+1]$ with respect to the measure density $(1-t^2)^{(s-3)/2}$, which is the probability density of the scalar product between two points on $S^{s-1}$.

```
$\int_{-1}^{+1} dt \, (1-t^2)^{\frac{s-3}{2}} P_{k,s}(t) P_{k',s}(t) = \frac{|S^{s-1}|}{|S^{s-2}|} \delta_{k,k'} \quad (190)$
```

With $[S^{s-1}]$ up, $x,y \in S^{s-1}$, functions of $x$ or $y$ can be expressed as a sum of projections on $x^\top y$ onto the orthogonal harmonics $\{Y_{k,l}\}_l$, whereas functions of $x^\top y$ can be expressed as a sum of projections on the Legendre polynomials $\{P_k(x^\top y)\}$. The two expansions are equalated in the Funk-Hecke formula [AH12].

```
$\int_{S^{s-1}} d\tau(y) f(x^\top y) Y_{k,l}(y) = Y_{k,l}(x) \int_{-1}^{+1} \frac{|S^{s-2}|}{|S^{s-1}|} dt \ (1-t^2)^{\frac{s-1}{2}} f(t) P_k(t) \quad (191)$
```

If $f$ has continuous derivatives up to the $k$-th order in $[-1,+1]$, one can use Rodrigues' formula in the right-hand side of Funk-Hecke formula and get, after $k$ integrations by parts,

```
$\int_{S^{s-1}} d\tau(y) f(x^\top y) Y_{k,l}(y) = Y_{k,l}(x) \frac{|S^{s-2}|}{|S^{s-1}|} \frac{\Gamma(\left(\frac{s-1}{2}\right))}{\Gamma(k+\frac{s+1}{2})} \int_{-1}^{+1} dt f^{(k)}(t) (1-t^2)^{k+\frac{s-3}{2}}$
```

$f^{(k)}(t)$ denoting the $k$-th order derivative of $f$ in $t$. This trick also applies to functions which are not $k$ times differentiable at $\pm1$, provided the boundary terms due to integration by parts vanish.

## B.1.1 Dot-product kernels on the sphere

Dot-product kernels are kernels that depend on the two inputs $x$ and $y$ via their scalar product $x^\top y$. When relations lie on the unit sphere $S^{s-1}$, one can use the machinery in the previous section to arrive immediately at the Mercer's decomposition of the kernel [SOWoo].

```
$$\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{x}^\top\mathbf{y}) = \sum_{k\ge 0} \left( N_{\Lambda_k} \int_{-1}^{1} (1-t^2)^{\frac{s-3}{2}} dt \right) \mathcal{K}(t) P_{k,s}(\mathbf{x}) P_{k,s}(\mathbf{y})$$
$$= \sum_{k\ge 0} \left( |S^{s-2}| \int_{-1}^{1} (1-t^2)^{\frac{s-3}{2}} dt \right) N_{\Lambda_k} Y_{k,\ell}(\mathbf{x}) Y_{k,\ell}(\mathbf{y})$$
$$=: \sum_{k\ge 0} \sum_{\ell=1}^{\Lambda_k} Y_{k,\ell}(\mathbf{x}) Y_{k,\ell}(\mathbf{y}). \quad (193)$$
```

In the first line, we have decomposed $\mathcal{K}$ into projections onto the Legendre polynomials, the second line follows immediately from the addition formula, and the third is just a definition of the eigenvalues $\Lambda_k$. Notice that the eigenfunctions of the kernel are orthonormal spherical harmonics and the eigenvalues are degenerate with respect to the index $\ell$. The Reproducing Hilbert Space (RKHS) of $\mathcal{K}$ can be characterized as,

```
$$\mathcal{H} := \{ f: S^{s-1} \to R \text{ s.t. } ||f||_{\mathcal{H}}^2 := \sum_{k\ge 0,\ell\neq 0} \frac{N_{\Lambda_k}}{\Lambda_k} (f, Y_{k,\ell})^2 <+\infty \ \quad (194)$$
```

## B.1.2 Multi-dot-product kernels on the multi-sphere

Mercer's decomposition of dot-product kernels extends naturally to the case considered in this paper, where the input space is the Cartesian product of $p s$-dimensional unit sphere,

```
$$\mathbb{M}^PS^{s-1} = \{ \mathbf{x} = (\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_p) |, \mathbf{x}_i \in S^{s-1} \forall i = 1, \dots, p \} = \times S^{s-1} \quad (195)$$
```

which we refer to as the multi-sphere product between functions on $\mathbb{M}^PS^{s-1}$ by direct extension of Equation 186, one can immediately find a set of orthonormal polynomials by taking products of spherical harmonics. With the multi-index notation $\mathbf{k} = (k_1, \dots, k_p)$, $\mathbf{\ell} = (\ell_1, \dots, \ell_p)$, we have

```
$$Y_{k,\ell}(\mathbf{x}) = \prod_{i=1}^p Y_{k_i,\ell_i}(\mathbf{x}_i), k_i \ge 0, \ell_i = 1,\dots,N_{k_i,s} = \frac{2k_i + s - 2}{k_i} \frac{s + k_i - 3}{k_i - 1} \quad (196)$$
```

These product spherical harmonics $Y_{k,\ell}(\mathbf{x})$ span the space of square-integrable functions on $\mathbb{M}^PS^{s-1}$. Furthermore, as each harmonic is an eigenfunction of the Laplace-Beltrami operator, $Y_{k,\ell}$ is an eigenfunction of the sum of Laplace-Beltrami operators on the $p$ unit spheres,

```
$\Delta_{p}\hat{Y}_{k,\ell} := \left(\sum_{i=1}^{p} \Delta_{i}\right)\hat{Y}_{k,\ell} = \left(\sum_{i=1}^{p} ((-1)^{k_{i}}(k_{i}+s-2))\right)\hat{Y}_{k,\ell} \quad (197)$
```

We can characterize the differentiability of functions of $\mathbb{M}^PS^{s-1}$ via $L^2$ norm of some power of $\Delta_p$. Similarly, we can consider products of Legendre polynomials on $[-1,1]^p$ (see [AH12]). Then, any function $f$ on $S^{s-1} \times S^{s-1} \times \dots \times S^{s-1}$ which depends only on the scalar product can be written as a sum of projections on Legendre polynomials:

```
$f(x^\top y) = \sum_{k \ge 0} \sum_{\ell=1}^{\Lambda_k} c_{k,\ell} Y_{k,\ell}(x) Y_{k,\ell}(y) \quad (198)$
```

Following [AH12], when fixing one of the arguments of $f$, $f$ becomes a function on $S^{s-1} \times S^{s-1} \times \dots \times S^{s-1}$. The two expansions are related by the generalized Funk-Hecke formula:

```
$\int_{S^{s-1}} d\tau(y) f(x^\top y) Y_{k,l}(y) = Y_{k,l}(x) \int_{-1}^{+1} \frac{|S^{s-2}|}{|S^{s-1}|} dt \ (1-t^2)^{\frac{s-1}{2}} f(t) P_k(t) \quad (191)$
```

The eigenfunctions of the kernel are orthonormal spherical harmonics and the eigenvalues are degenerate with respect to the index $\ell$. The Reproducing Hilbert Space (RKHS) of $\mathcal{K}$ can be characterized as,

```
$$\mathcal{H} := \{ f: S^{s-1} \to R \text{ s.t. } ||f||_{\mathcal{H}}^2 := \sum_{k\ge 0,\ell\neq 0} \frac{N_{\Lambda_k}}{\Lambda_k} (f, Y_{k,\ell})^2 <+\infty \ \quad (194)$$
```
