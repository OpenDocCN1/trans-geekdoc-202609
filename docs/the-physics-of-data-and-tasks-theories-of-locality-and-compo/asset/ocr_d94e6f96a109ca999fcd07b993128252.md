# APPENDIX: LOCALITY DEFEATS THE CURSE OF DIMENSIONALITY

# A-4 PROOF OF THEOREM 2.4.1

Theorem A.4.1 (Theorem 2.4.1 in the main text). Let $K_T$ be a d-dimensional convolutional kernel with a translationally-invariant t-dimensional constituent and leading nonanalyticity at the origin controlled by the exponent $a_t > 0$. Let $K_S$ be a d-dimensional convolutional or local student kernel with a translationally-invariant $s$-dimensional constituent, and with nonanalyticity at the origin controlled by the exponent $a_s > 0$. Assume, in addition, that if kernels have nonoverlapping patches then $s \ge 1$ is an integer multiple of $t$; and that are uniformly distributed on a d-dimensional torus. Then, the following asymptotic equivalence holds in the limit $P \to \infty$,

```
$B(P) \sim P^{-\beta}, \quad \beta = a_s/t.$
```

Proof. For the sake of clarity, we start with the proof in the nonoverlapping-patches case, and extend it to the overlapping-patches case. Since $K_T$ and $K_S$ have translationally-invariant-constituent kernels and data are uniformly distributed on a d-dimensional torus, the kernels can be diagonalized in Fourier space. Let us start by considering a convolutional student: because of the constituent kernel's isotropy, the Fourier coefficients $\Lambda_k^{(s)}$ of $K_S$ depend on $|k|$. Notice the superscript indicating the dimensionality of the student constituents. In particular, $\Lambda_k^{(s)}$ is a decreasing function of $k$ and, for large, $\Lambda_k \sim k^{-(s+s)}$. Then, $B(P)$

```
$B(P) = \sum_{(|k|<k(P))} \mathbb{E}[|c_k|^2],$
```

where $k(P)$ is defined as the wavevector modulus of the P-th eigenvalue and $\mathbb{E}[|c_k|^2]$ denotes the variance of the target coefficients in the student eigenbasis. $k(P)$ is such that there are exactly P eigenvalues with $k \le k(P)$,

```
$P = \sum_{(|k|<k(P))} 1 \sim \frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d} \theta(k, P(P)-k) = \frac{\pi^{s/2}}{(2\pi)^s \Gamma(s/2+1)} k_T(P)^s$,
```

i.e., $k_T(P) \sim P^{1/s}$.

By denoting the eigenfunctions of the student with $\Phi_k^{(s)}$, the superscript (s) indicating the dimensionality of the constituent plane waves,

```
$\mathbb{E}[|c_k|^2] = \int_{[0,1]^d} d^dx \Phi_k^{(s)}(\mathbf{x}) \int_{[0,1]^d} d^dy \Phi_k^{(s)}(\mathbf{y}) \mathbb{E}[f^*(\mathbf{x})f^*(\mathbf{y})]$
```

```
= \int_{[0,1]^d} d^dx \Phi_k^{(s)}(\mathbf{x}) \int_{[0,1]^d} d^dy \Phi_k^{(s)}(\mathbf{y}) K_T(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).
```
