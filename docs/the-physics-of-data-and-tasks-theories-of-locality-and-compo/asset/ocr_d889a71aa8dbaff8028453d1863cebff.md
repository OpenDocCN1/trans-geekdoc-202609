# 88 A THEORY OF CREATIVITY AND COMPOSITIONALITY

We argue that the grammar rules can be deduced iteratively by clustering, in wordzace, sequences of tokens based on the statistics of their context. For each level, we analytically derive the corresponding sample complexity:We show these sample complexities match the number of data required by the diffusion model to generate data that follow the grammar rules up to the corresponding level. Since hierarchical clustering procedure requires a number of samples that is polynomial in the size of the token sequence, this mechanism allows the model to learn a high-dimensional distribution while avoiding the curse of dimensionality. Beyond simple PCGs, we predict that diffusion models trained on limited samples that is locally coherent (i.e., satisfying local rules), but not globally, with a coherence length growing with the training time/number of samples. We confirm this prediction in diffusion models trained on OpenWebText and ImageNet.

We conclude by discussing how the principle we forward to build a hierarchy of latent variables generalizes the renormalization group used in physics, where coarse-grained variables are obtained by simple pooling operations.

Kanto and Ganguli [KG24] studied how equivariant diffusion models can compose images by combining local patches in the dataset.
