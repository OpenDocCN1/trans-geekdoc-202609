**Assumption 6.3.1.** The U-Net learns to generate data that is consistent with the rules at level $\ell$ when the correlations between a visible token and a tuple of latents at level $\ell-2$ become detectable from the training data.

Hence, in what follows, we compute the number of samples required to decode these correlations.

LOCAL CONSTRAINTS The first step in the learning process is to recognize the valid $s$-tuples generated by the RHM at the visible level. Since these tuples lock internal structure, they can only be remembered. Each tuple can learn $vm$ possible configurations corresponding to $v$ symbols for the first-level latents and $m$ representations (synonyms) for each of them. Thus, the sample complexity required to learn the local scales as $P_1 \sim vm$.

FIRST-LEVEL LATENTS Once the local constraints are learned, the network can refine its estimate of $x_1$ by utilizing correlations with the neighboring tuples $x_{1+2}, \dots, x_{(s-1)2}$. The sample complexity required to decode the correlations between $x_1$ and $x_{s+12}$ was computed in Cagnetta and Wyart [CW24] and corresponds to

```
$P_2 = (1 - m/v^{s-1})^{-1} vm^2. \quad (84)$
```

For $P \gg P_2$, after learning the first-level rules, the network can collapse the $(s^2-s)$-dimensional space of neighboring tuples into the corresponding first-level latents $h_1^{(1)}$.

SECOND-LATENTS Having built the first-level latent representation, the model can leverage correlations between $s$-tuples of first-level latents $h_i^{(1)}$ and the first token to learn the rules at the second level, improving the denoising task. These correlations can be computed by studying the statistics of the token-latent tuples

```
$C^{(3)}(\mu, v) = \mathbf{P}[x_1 = \mu, \mathbf{h}_{s+12}^{(1)} = v] - \mathbf{P}[x_1 = \mu]\mathbf{h}_{s+12}^{(1)} = v]. \quad (85)$
```
