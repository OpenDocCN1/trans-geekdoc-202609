# A.3 MERCER'S DECOMPOSITION OF CONVOLUTION AND LOCAL KERNELS

proving that $\Lambda_0$ and $\Phi_0$ satisfy the eigenproblem. For $k 
eq 0$,

```
$\int_{[0,1]^d} d^d y \, K^{	ext{CN}}(\mathbf{x}, y) \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} e^{i k \cdot y} \right)^2 = \frac{1}{d^{5/2}} \sum_{i,j=1}^d \int_{[0,1]^d} d^d y \ \lambda_{ij} e^{i q \cdot (\mathbf{x}-y)} e^{i k \cdot y} \\n \frac{1}{d^{5/2}} \sum_{i=1}^d \lambda_{ik}^{(s)} e^{i q \cdot y} = \delta(k, q) + \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_{j,+}} \delta((s-o), 0) \delta(q_{o-1}^{(s)}, k_i) \delta(k_{o+1}^{(s)}, 0) \\n + \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_{l,-}} \delta((k-s), 0) \delta(q_i, q_{o-1}^{(s)}) \delta(q_{o+1}^{(s)}, 0) \\quad (145)
```

When $k \in \mathcal{F}^u$, the deltas from the eigenproblem with $j \in \mathcal{P}_{j,+}$ vanish, showing that the eigenproblem is satisfied with $\Lambda_k = \lambda_k/d$ and $\Phi_k(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_j \Phi_j^{(s)} e^{i k \cdot \mathbf{x}}$. When $u < s$, as the last $s-u$ components of $k$ vanish, there are several $q$'s satisfying the relation in the bracket. For $k=l$, if the $l=j$ term, then there are $s-u$ $q$'s such that $\delta(k_i^{(s)}, 0) \delta(q_{o-1}^{(s)}, k_i) \delta(k_{o+1}^{(s)}, 0) = 1$. These are all the $q$'s having a $u$-dimensional patch equal to $k^{(u)}$ and all the other elements set to zero, thus there are $(s-u+1)$ such $q$'s. Moreover, $\lambda_q$ depends only on the modulus of $q$, all these $q$'s result in the same value, and in the same eigenfunction $e^{i q \cdot \mathbf{x}} / \sqrt{d}$, over other patches.

```
$\int_{[0,1]^d} d^d y \, K^{	ext{CN}}(\mathbf{x}, y) \Phi_k(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{s-u+1}{d} \Lambda_{k^{(u)}} \Phi_k^{(u)}(\mathbf{x}) = \Lambda_{k^{(u)}} \Phi_{k^{(u)}}(\mathbf{x}). \quad (146)
```

Finally, we prove the expansion of the kernel in Equation 138,

```
$K^{	ext{CN}}(\mathbf{x}, y) = \frac{1}{d^2} \sum_{i,j \in \mathcal{P}} C(\mathbf{x}, y) \\quad (147) \\n = \frac{1}{d} \Lambda_k^{(u)} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \sum \Phi_k(\mathbf{x}) \right) \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \sum \Phi_k(y) \right). \quad (148)
```

The terms on the RHS of Equation 147 are equal to those components of Equation 138 for $k \in \mathcal{F}^u$. All the $k$ having vanishing components can be written as $k^{(u)} \in \mathcal{F}^u$, which has the last $s-u$ components vanishing. But a shift of $k$ does not affect $\Lambda_k$ nor $\sum_i e^{ik \cdot \mathbf{s}}$, leading to a degeneracy of eigenvalues $k$ which can be obtained from a shift of $k^{(u)} \in \mathcal{F}^u$. Such degeneracy is removed by restricting the sum over $k$ to the sets $\mathcal{F}^u, u \le s$, of wavevectors with non-vanishing extremal components, and rescaling the remaining eigenvalues with a factor of $(s-u+1)/d$, so that Equation 138 is obtained.
