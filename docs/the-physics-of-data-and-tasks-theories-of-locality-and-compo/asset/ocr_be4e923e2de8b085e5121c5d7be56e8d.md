# 184 APPENDIX: THE ROLE OF DEPTH AND SPATIAL ADAPTIVITY

$s_2 = d$ and $s_1 = p$ (the number of s-patches). Eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of $T_k$ can be organized into 2 sectors associated with the hidden layers of the kernel/network.

+   i. The first sector consists of $s_1$-local eigenfunctions, which are functions of a single patch $\mathbf{x}_i$ for $i=1, \dots, p$. The labels $\ell$ of local eigenfunctions are such that the $k_i$ with $i \neq l$ are zero (the eigenfunction is outside of $\mathbf{x}_i$). The corresponding eigenvalue is degenerate with respect to the location of patch: we call it $\Lambda_{k_i}^{(1)}$.

When $k_i \to \infty$,

```
$$\Lambda_{k_i}^{(1)} = C_{s_1} k_i^{-2^{-(s_1-1)}} + o\left(k_i^{-2^{-(s_1-1)}ight) \quad (229)$
```

with $v_{NTK} = 1/2, v_{RFK} = 3/2, C_{s_1}$ can take two distinct positive values depending on the parity of $k_i$;

+   ii. The second sector consists of global eigenfunctions, which are functions of the whole input $x$. The labels $k, \ell$ of global eigenfunctions are such that at least two of $k_i$ are non-zero. We call the corresponding eigenvalue $\Lambda_k^{(2)}$. When $|k| \to \infty$, for fixed non-zero angles $k/|k|$,

```
$$\Lambda_k^{(2)} = C_{s_2} k^{-2^{-(s_2-1)}} + o\left(k^{-2^{-(s_2-1)}}\right) \quad (230)$
```

With $C_{s_2}$ as a positive function.
A hierarchical RFK/NTK is a multi-product kernel, therefore its eigenfunctions are products of spherical harmonics $\tilde{Y}_k(x) = \prod_{i=1}^p Y_{k_i}(\mathbf{x}_i)$ and are given by Equation 201,

```
$$\Lambda_k = \prod_{i=1}^p \left( \frac{|S^{|k_i|-1}|}{|S^{s_i-1}|} \right) \int dt_i (1-t_i^2)^{-\frac{|k_i|-1}{2}} P_{k_i}(t_i) K(\{t_i\) . \quad (231)$
```

The proof follows the following strategy: first, we show that the infinitely differentiable $K$ results in eigenvalues which decay faster than any polynomial of the degrees $k_i$. We then show that the decay is controlled by the most significant term of the singular expansion and finally compute such by relating it to the number of derivatives of the kernel having a finite l2 norm.
When $K$ is infinitely differentiable in $[-1,1]^p$, we can use Plancherel's formula Equation 189 for each $P_{k_i,\sigma}(t_i)$ and we get to obtain

```
$$\Lambda_k = \prod_{i=1}^p \left( \frac{|S^{|k_i|-1}|}{|S^{s_i-1}|} \right) \left( \frac{1}{2} \right) \frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{|k_i|+1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(k_i + \frac{s_i-1}{2}\right)} \int_{-1}^1 dt_i K^{(k_i)}(t_i) \left( \prod_{i=1}^p (1-t_i^2)^{k_i+\frac{s_i-1}{2}} \right) \quad \quad (232)$
```
