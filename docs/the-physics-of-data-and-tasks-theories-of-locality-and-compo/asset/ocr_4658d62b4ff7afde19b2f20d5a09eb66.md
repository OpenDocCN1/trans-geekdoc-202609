# 2.1 RELATED WORK

Several recent studies have focused on the role of compositional structure in data. When structure is hierarchical, deep convolutional networks have been shown to possess significantly greater power than shallow architectures [Pog170; Pbl20; Dez20]. From a training perspective, Malach and Shalev-Shwartz [MSS21] demonstrated convolutional and locally-connected networks can achieve target generalization errors in polynomial time functions that depend on consecutive bits of a d-dimensional input, with s=O(log d). Conversely, fully-connected networks do not share this advantage.

Bietti [Bie21] explored the impact of locality in the architecture through the lens of kernel methods, using deep convolutional kernels introduced by Mairal [Mai6] and Bietti and Mairal [BM19], and characterized their Associated Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS). However, for isotropic kernels, this membership performance constraints on smoothness might become increasingly restrictive as the dimensionality d grows. By contrast, for kernels exhibiting locality, smoothness constraints depend on the filter size s, rather than the dimensionality d [Bie21]. Additionally, Mei et al. [MMM21] recently established that weight sharing, without locality, provides only a modest improvement in the generalization performance of shift-invariant kernels.

In contrast to the above studies, in this chapter, we specifically consider nontrivial learning curve events, within a framework where the locality and shift-invariance properties of the kernel do not necessarily match the target of function class being learned. Notably, in our setup, the target functions typically do not belong to the RKHS of the kernel. Technically, our finding that student's filter size s determines the learning curve exponent—rather than the teacher's filter size t—reflects the inability of kernels to capture data anisotropy, i.e., dependencies limited to subset of input coordinates, both in worst-case [Bac17] and typical scenarios involving Gaussian fields [PSW21a].
