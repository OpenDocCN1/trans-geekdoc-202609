# 7 RACE BETWEEN MEMORIZATION AND GENERALIZATION

The previous chapter investigated how diffusion models achieve generalization, learning to capture the underlying structure of data training. However, instead of learning to approximate the data distribution, a model can simply store and reproduce the specific training examples it has seen. In, since the score function is learned from the empirical training distribution, minimizing the training loss often leads the model to reproduce the training data itself—a phenomenon known as memorization [Car+23; Som+22]. This phenomenon is observed in practical settings, as models trained on sensitive privacy and copyright concerns, or proprietary data may inadvertently regenerate content, exposing sensitive information or violating intellectual property rights [Wua+22; HP+23].

Despite the empirical success of diffusion models, the mechanisms underlying their ability to generalize remain poorly understood. The prevailing view—rooted in classical learning theory—is that generalization depends on underparameterization [Yoo+23; Zha+23; Kad+23]: only models that lack the capacity to memorize their training data are expected to generalize. In this work, we go beyond this by demonstrating that even overparametri diffusion models exhibit generalization during training before they memorizing the data. We systematically investigate this phenomenon, showing that generalization and memorization are not mutually exclusive but unfold as distinct phases of training. We measure generalization and metrics and systematically vary the training set size, showing that generalization improves boldly, before the onset of memorization.
