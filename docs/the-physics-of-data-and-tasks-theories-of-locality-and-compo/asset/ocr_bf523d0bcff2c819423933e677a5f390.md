# APPENDIX: LOCALITY DEFEATS THE CURSE OF DIMENSIONALITY

$$\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}, \Phi_{\mathbf{q}} = \frac{1}{d} \int_{[0,1]^d} d^d x \left( \sum_{i=1}^d \Phi_{\mathbf{k}}(x_i) \right) \left( \sum_{j=1}^d \Phi_{\mathbf{q}}(x_j) \right)$$
$$\$$= \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}} \int d^d x_i e^{k_i x_i} \int d^d x_j e^{-q_j x_j} + \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \int d^d x_i e^{(k-q)_i x_i}$$
$$\$$+ \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}} \left( \int d^d (x_i-x_j) e^{(x_i-x_j)^+} x_i} \right) \left( \int d^d x_{i,j} e^{(k_{i,j}-q_{i,j})^+ x_{i,j}} \right)$$
$$\$\times \int d^d (x_i-x_j) e^{(q_j-x_i)^+ x_j}$$
$$\$$+ \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}} \{i \neq j, k \neq q} \delta(k,0) + \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \delta(k,q)$$
$$\$$+ \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \delta(k^{(s-o)}) \delta(k_{s-o+1}^{(i)}) \delta(q_{o+1}^{(o)}, 0)$$
$$\$$+ \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \delta(k^{(s-o)}) \delta(k_i^{(o)}, q_{o-1}^{(o)}) \delta(k_{i+1}^{(s-o)}, 0) \eqno(141)$

with $\delta(k,q)$ denoting the multidimensional Kronecker delta. For fixed $i$, the three terms on the RHS correspond to $j$ to $i$'s such that $x_j$ does not overlap with $x_i$: $j=i$ and to $j$'s such that $x_j$ overlaps with $x_i$. We recall that, in patch notation, $k^{(s-o)}$ denotes the subsequence of $k$ formed with the first $s-o$ components and $k^{(o)}$ the subsequence with the last components.

By taking $k \in \mathcal{F}^s$ and $q \in \mathcal{F}^u$, as $k_i, k_j \neq 0$ and $q_i, q_j \neq 0$, Equation 141 implies

$$\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}, \Phi_{\mathbf{q}} = \delta(k,q). \eqno(142)$

In addition, by taking $k \in \mathcal{F}^u$ and $q \in \mathcal{F}^u$ with $u < s$,

$$\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}, \Phi_{\mathbf{q}}^{(i)}} = 0 \quad u < s. \eqno(143)$

The $\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}^s$ with $k \in \mathcal{F}^s$ are orthonormal with each other and orthogonal to all $\Phi_{\mathbf{q}}^u$ with $u < s$. Similarly, by taking $k \in \mathcal{F}^u$ with $u < s$ and $q \in \mathcal{F}^v$ with $v \leq u$, orthogonality is proven to be $\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{(j)}$. The zero-th eigenfunction $\Phi^{(0)x1$ is also orthogonal to all other eigenfunctions by Equation 141 with $k=0$ and trivially normalized to 1.
