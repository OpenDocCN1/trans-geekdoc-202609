### A.1 SPECTRAL BIAS IN KERNEL REGRESSION

In this appendix, we provide additional details about the derivation of Equation 28 within the framework of [BCP20; CBP21]. Let us begin by recalling the definition of the kernel ridge regression estimator $\hat{f}$

$$\hat{f} = \arg\min_{f \in \mathcal{H}} \left\{ \frac{1}{P} \sum_{v=1}^{P} (f(x_v) - f^*(x_v))^2 + \lambda ||f||_{\mathcal{H}}^2 \right\} (97)$$

where $\mathcal{H}$ denotes the Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) of the kernel $\mathcal{K}(x,y)$. After utilizing the Mercer's decomposition of the kernel,

$$\mathcal{K}(x,y) = \sum_{\rho=1}^{\infty} \lambda_{\rho} \phi_{\rho}(x)\phi_{\rho}(y), \quad \int (d^d y) \mathcal{K}(x,y)\phi_{\rho}(y) = \lambda_{\rho}\phi_{\rho}(x). (98)$$

The RKHS can be characterized as a subspace of the functions lying in the span of the kernel eigenbasis,

$$\mathcal{H} = \left\{ f = \sum_{\rho=1}^{\infty} a_{\rho}\phi_{\rho}(x) \left. \ \sum_{\rho=1}^{\infty} \frac{|a_{\rho}|^2}{\lambda_{\rho}} < \infty \right. \right\} (99)$$

In other words, the RKHS contains functions having a finite norm $||f||_{\mathcal{H}}$ with respect to the following inner product,

$$f(x) = \sum_{\rho=1}^{\infty} a_{\rho}\phi_{\rho}(x), \quad f'(x) = \sum_{\rho=1}^{\infty} a_{\rho}\phi_{\rho}(x), \quad (f,f')_{\mathcal{H}} = \sum_{\rho=1}^{\infty} \frac{a_{\rho}a_{\rho}'}{\lambda_{\rho}}. (100)$$

Given any target function $f^*$ lying in the span of the kernel eigenbasis, the mean-squared generalization error of the kernel ridge regression estimator

$$\mathcal{E}(\lambda, \{x_v\}) = \int (d^d x) (f(x) - f^*(x))^2 = \sum_{\rho=1}^{\infty} |a_{\rho}(\lambda, \{x_v\}) - c_{\rho}|^2. (101)$$

With $c_{\rho}$ denoting the $\rho$-th coefficient of the target $f^*$ and $a_{\rho}$ of the estimator, $f$ depends on the ridge $\lambda$ and on the training set $\{x_v\}_{v=1,...,P}$. Notice that, as $f$ belongs to $\mathcal{H}$ by definition, $\sum_{\rho}|a_{\rho}|^2/\lambda_{\rho} < \infty$, whereas the $c_{\rho}$ are free to take any value.

The authors of [BCP20; CBP21] found a heuristic expression for the average of the mean error over realizations of the training set $\{\hat{f}_x\}$, based on the replica method of statistical physics, reads

$$\mathcal{E}(\lambda, P) = \partial_{\lambda} \left( \frac{k_{\lambda}(P)}{P} \right) \sum_{P} (\lambda_{\rho} + k_{\lambda}(P))^2 |c_{\rho}|^2, \quad 102$$

where $(P)$ satisfies

$$\frac{k_{\lambda}(P)}{P} = \lambda + \frac{1}{P} \sum_{\rho} \frac{\lambda_{\rho} k_{\lambda}(P)/P}{\lambda_{\rho} + k_{\lambda}(P)/P}, \quad 103$$

In short, the replica method works as follows [MPV87b]: first one defines an energy function $E(f)$ as the argument of the in Equation 97, then to attribute a probability distribution $P(f) = Z^{-\beta E(f)}$, with $Z$ normalization constant and $\beta=0$. As $\beta \to \infty$, the probability distribution $P(f)$ centers around the solution of the minimization problem of Equation 97, i.e., one can replace $f$ in the right-hand side of Equation 101 with an average over $\beta$, then perform the limit $\beta \to \infty$ after the calculation to recover the generalization error. The simplification comes from the fact that $f$ is replaced with its eigendecomposition, the energy function $E(f)$ becomes a quadratic function of the coefficients $c_{\rho}$. Then, under the assumption the first and second moments of the eigenfunctions $\phi_{\rho}(x)$ w.r.t $x$, all averages in Equation 101 reduce to Gaussian integrals. Mathematically, $k_{\lambda}(P)/P$ is related to the Stieltjes transform [PB20] of the Gram matrix $K_{p}/P$ in the large-$P$ limit. Intuitively, it plays the role of a threshold: the mathematical contribution to the error to 0 for $\rho$ such that $\lambda_{\rho} \gg k_{\lambda}(P)/P$, and $\mathbb{E}[|c_{\rho}|^2]$ for $\rho$ such that $\lambda_{\rho} \ll k_{\lambda}(P)/P$. This is equivalent to saying that the algorithm predictor $f(x)$ captures only the eigenfunctions having eigenvalue larger than $k_{\lambda}(P)/P$ (see also [jac+2a; jac+2b]). This intuitive picture can actually be exploited to explore the learning curve $\beta$ from the asymptotic behavior of Equation 102 and Equation 103 in the ridgeless $\lambda \to 0$. Following, we assume that both the kernel and the target function have a power-law spectrum, in which $\lambda_{\rho} \sim \rho^{-a}$ and $\mathbb{E}[|c_{\rho}|^2] \sim \rho^{-b}$, with $2a > b-1$. First, we approximate the sum over modes in Equation 103 with an integral using the Euler-Macurin formula. Then we substitute the eigenvalues inside the integral with their asymptotic limit Notice that the risk considered in [BCP20; CBP21] differs from Equation 97 by a factor of 1/P in front of the sum. $\lambda_P = A\rho^{-a}$, $\kappa_0(P)/P \to 0$ as $P \to \infty$, both these operations result in an error that is asymptotically independent of $P$. Namely,

$$\frac{\kappa_0(P)}{P} = \frac{\kappa_0(1)}{P} \int_0^{\infty} \frac{d\rho}{A\rho^{-a} + \kappa_0(\rho)/\rho} + \mathcal{O}(1) \quad 104$$

$$\frac{\kappa_0(P)}{P} = \frac{\kappa_0(1)}{P} \left( \frac{\kappa_0(\rho)}{\rho} \right) \int_0^{\infty} \frac{d\sigma}{\sigma^{a-1} A^2}{1 + \sigma} + \mathcal{O}(1)$$

where on the side we changed the variable by $\sigma = \kappa_0(\rho)/P$. Since the integral in $\sigma$ is finite and independent of $P$, we find $\kappa_0(P)/P = O(P^{-a})$. Similarly, we find that the mode-independent prefactor $\kappa_0(P)/P|_{\lambda=0} = \mathcal{O}(1)$. As a result we work with some $P$-independent prefactors

$$\mathcal{E}(P) \sim \sum_{\rho} \frac{\rho^{-2a}}{(A\rho^{-a} + P\rho^{-a})^2} |c_{\rho}|^2. \quad 105$$

Following the intuitive argument about the threshold $\kappa_0(P)/P \sim P^{-a}$, it is convenient to split the sum in Equation 105 into sectors where $\lambda_{\rho} \gg \kappa_0(P)/P$, $\lambda_{\rho} \sim \kappa_0(P)/P$, and $\lambda_{\rho} \ll \kappa_0(P)$, i.e.,

$$\mathcal{E}(P) \sim \sum_{\rho \ll P} \frac{\rho^{-2a}}{(A\rho^{-a})^2} |c_{\rho}|^2 + \sum_{\rho \sim P} |c_{\rho}|^2 + \sum_{\rho \gg P} |c_{\rho}|^2. \quad 106$$

Finally, Equation 28 is obtained by noting that, under our assumptions on decay of $\mathcal{E}|c_{\rho}|^2$ with $\rho$, the contribution of the sum over $\rho \ll P$ is subleading in $P$ whereas the other two sums can be gathered.

### A.2 NTKS OF CONVOLUTIONAL AND LOCALLY-CONNECTED NETWORKS

We begin this section by reviewing the computation of NTK of a one-hidden-layer fully-connected [CO9].

**Definition A.2.1 (one-hidden-layer FCN).** A one-hidden-layer fully-connected network with $H$ neurons is defined as follows,

$$f^{FCN}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{H}} \sum_{h=1}^{H} a_h \sigma(\mathbf{w}_h^T\mathbf{x} + b_h), \quad 107$$

where $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the input, $H$ is the width, $\sigma$ is nonlinear activation function, $\{\mathbf{w}_h \in \mathbb{R}^d\}_{h=1}^H$ and $\{\mathbf{b}_h \in \mathbb{R}\}_{h=1}^H$ are the network's parameters. Inserting Equation 107 into obtains

$$K^{FC}_{NTK,N}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};\theta) = \frac{1}{H} \sum_{h=1}^{H} \sigma'(\mathbf{w}_h^T\mathbf{x} + b_h)\sigma'(\mathbf{w}_h^T\mathbf{y} + b_h) + a_h^2\sigma'^2(\mathbf{w}_h^T\mathbf{x} + b_h)\sigma'(\mathbf{w}_h^T\mathbf{y} + b_h), \quad 108$$

where $\sigma'$ denotes the derivative of $\sigma$ with respect to its argument. If all the parameters are initialized independently from a standard Normal distribution, $K_{NTK,N}^{FC}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}; \boldsymbol{\theta})$ is a random-feature kernel that in the $H \to \infty$ limit converges to

$$K_{NTK}^{FC}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{w}}[\sigma'(\mathbf{x}^T\mathbf{w})\sigma'(\mathbf{y}^T\mathbf{w})] + \mathbb{E}_a[a^2]\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{w}}[\sigma'(\mathbf{x}^T\mathbf{w})\sigma'(\mathbf{y}^T\mathbf{w}) + (\mathbf{x}^T\mathbf{y}+1)]$$

When $\sigma$ is the ReLU activation function, the expectations can be computed exactly using techniques from the literature of arc-cosine kernels [Cs09b]

$$K_{NTK}^{FC}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\mathbf{x}\|^2+1}\sqrt{\mathbf{y}\|^2+1}\sin(\varphi)(\pi-\varphi)\cos(\varphi) + \frac{1}{2\pi}(\mathbf{x}^T\mathbf{y}+1)(\pi-\varphi),$$

with $\varphi$ denoting the angle

$$\varphi = \arccos\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}^T\mathbf{y}+1}{\sqrt{\mathbf{x}\|^2+1}\sqrt{\mathbf{y}\|^2+1}}\right)$$

Notice that, in Section 2.3, for ReLU networks $K_{NTK,x}^{FC}(\mathbf{x})$ displays a cusp at $\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}$.
