Proof. In this we use of results derived from [[Jac20b]]. Our setup for kernel ridge regression corresponds to what the authors of [[Jac20b]] call the *classical setting*. Let us introduce the integral operator $T_K$ defined by

```
$$(T_K f)(x) = \int p(d^d y) K(x,y) f(y). \quad (176)$
```

The trace $Tr[T_K]$ coincides with the sum of $K$'s eigenvalues and is finite by hypothesis. We define the following definition of the generalization error $\mathcal{R}(\lambda, P)$,

```
$$\mathcal{R}(\lambda, P) = \partial_{\lambda}(\lambda) \int p(d^d x) (f^* - (\mathcal{A}_theta f^*))^2, \quad (177)$
```

where $\theta$ is the signal capture threshold [[Jac20b]] and $\mathcal{A}_theta = T_K(T_K + \theta)^{-1}$ is a reconstruction operator [[Jac20b]]. The target function can be represented in the kernel basis by hypothesis (with coefficients $c_p$) and $T_K$ has the kernel basis by hypothesis and eigenfunctions of the kernel definition. Hence,

```
$$\mathcal{R}(\lambda, P) = \partial_{\lambda}(\lambda) \sum_{\rho=1}^{\infty} (\Lambda_{\rho} + \theta(\lambda))^2 |c_{\ho}|^2 = \int_0^{\Lambda} \partial_{\lambda}(\Lambda) d\Lambda D_T(\Lambda)^2 \frac{\theta(\lambda)^2}{(\Lambda + \theta(\lambda))^2}, \quad (178)$
```

where $D_T$ is the reduction of variance Equation 46. We now derive the asymptotics of $\mathcal{R}(\lambda, P)$ in the joint $P \to \infty$ and $\lambda \to 0$ limit, then relate the asymptotics of $\mathcal{R}$ to those of $\mathcal{E}(\lambda, P)$ via a theorem proved in [[Jac20b]].

Proposition 3 of [[Jac20b]] shows that for any $\lambda > 0$, $\partial_{\lambda}(\lambda) \to 1$ and $\theta(\lambda) \to \lambda$ with corrections of order $1/N$. Thus, we focus on the following,

```
$$\int_0^{\infty} d\Lambda D_T(\Lambda) \lambda^2(\Lambda) \frac{\Lambda^2}{(\Lambda + \lambda)^2}. \quad (179)$
```

The functions $D_T(\Lambda)$ and $c^2(\Lambda)$ can be replaced with the small-A eigenvalues $\Lambda^{-(1+r)}$ and $\Lambda^q$ over the whole range of the integral above the assumptions $q > r$ and $q \leq r+2$. In practice, there should be an upper cut-off of the integral coinciding with the largest eigenvalue $\Lambda_1$, but the assumption $q \leq r+2$ causes this part of the spectrum to be irrelevant for the asymptotics of the error.
