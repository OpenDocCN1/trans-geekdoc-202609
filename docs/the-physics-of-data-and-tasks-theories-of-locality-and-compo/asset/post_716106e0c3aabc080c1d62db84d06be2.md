
## A.3 MERCER'S DECOMPOSITION OF CONVOLUTIONAL AND LOCAL KERNELS

- Two patches $x_i$ and $x_j$ overlap if $x_i \cap x_j \neq \emptyset$. The overlap $x_{ij} \equiv x_i \cap x_j$ is an $o$-dimensional patch of $x$, with $o=|x_i \cap x_j|$

- Let $\mathcal{P}$ denote the set of patches associated with a given kernel/architecture. We denote with $\mathcal{P}_i$ the set of patches which overlap with $x_i$, i.e.,

```$\mathcal{P}_i = \{i-s+1, \dots, i+s-1\} = \{\mathcal{P}_{-i}, i, \mathcal{P}_{+i}\};$
```

- Given two overlapping patches $x_i$ and $x_j$ with $o$-dimensional overlap, the union $x_{ij} \equiv x_i \cup x_j$ and differences $x_{i\setminus j} \equiv x_i \setminus x_j$ and $x_{j\setminus i} \equiv x_j \setminus x_i$ are all patches of $x$, with differences $2s-o$, $s-o$ and $s-0$, respectively.

We also use the following notation for denoting subspaces of the $k$-space $\approx \mathbb{Z}^s$

```$\mathcal{F}^u = \{k=2\pi n \mid n \in \mathbb{Z}^s; n_v \neq 0 \forall v < u \leq s\}$. (137)
```

$\mathcal{F}^s$ is the set of all wavevectors $k$ having nonzero extremal components $k_l$. $\mathcal{F}^u$ is formed by first considering only wavevectors having the last $s-u$ wavevectors equal to zero, then asking the result $u$-dimensional wavevectors to have nonvanishing extremal components. Practically, $\mathcal{F}^u$ contains wavevectors which can be explicitly specified by the first $u$-dimensional patch $\mathbf{k}^{(u)}=(k_1, \dots, k_u)$ but not the first $(u-1)$-dimensional one. Notice that, in order to compare $K_l$ in different $\mathcal{F}^s$, we have introduced an index $u$ denoting the dimensionality of the patch.

**Lemma A-3.1** (Spectra of overlapping kernels). Let $K^{CN}$ be a convolutional kernel defined as in Equation 34a, with $\mathcal{P}=\{1,\dots,d\}$ and constituent kernel $C$ satisfying assumptions i), ii) above. Then, $K^{CN}$ admits the Mercer's decomposition,

```$K^{CN}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}) = \Lambda_0 + \sum_{u=1}^d (\sum_{k \in \mathcal{F}^u} \Lambda_k \phi_k(\mathbf{x})\phi_k(\mathbf{y})) \quad (138)
```

with eigenfunctions

```$$\Phi_0(\mathbf{x})=1, \quad \Phi_k(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{l=1}^d \phi_k(\mathbf{x}) \forall k \neq 0, \quad (139)$$
```

and eigenvalues

```$$\Lambda_0=\Lambda_0, \quad \Lambda_k=\frac{s-u+1}{d}\Lambda_k \forall k \in \mathcal{F}^u \text{ with } u \leq s. \quad (140)$$
```

$$\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}, \Phi_{\mathbf{q}} = \frac{1}{d} \int_{[0,1]^d} d^d x \left( \sum_{i=1}^d \Phi_{\mathbf{k}}(x_i) \right) \left( \sum_{j=1}^d \Phi_{\mathbf{q}}(x_j) \right)$$
$$= \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}} \int d^d x_i e^{k_i x_i} \int d^d x_j e^{-q_j x_j} + \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \int d^d x_i e^{(k-q)_i x_i}$$
$$+ \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}} \left( \int d^d (x_i-x_j) e^{(x_i-x_j)^+} x_i} \right) \left( \int d^d x_{i,j} e^{(k_{i,j}-q_{i,j})^+ x_{i,j}} \right)$$
$$ \times \int d^d (x_i-x_j) e^{(q_j-x_i)^+ x_j}$$
$$+ \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}} \{i \neq j, k \neq q\} \delta(k,0) + \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \delta(k,q)$$
$$+ \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \delta(k^{(s-o)}) \delta(k_{s-o+1}^{(i)}) \delta(q_{o+1}^{(o)}, 0)$$
$$+ \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \delta(k^{(s-o)}) \delta(k_i^{(o)}, q_{o-1}^{(o)}) \delta(k_{i+1}^{(s-o)}, 0) \eqno(141)$$

with $\delta(k,q)$ denoting the multidimensional Kronecker delta. For fixed $i$, the three terms on the RHS correspond to $j$ to $i$'s such that $x_j$ does not overlap with $x_i$: $j=i$ and to $j$'s such that $x_j$ overlaps with $x_i$. We recall that, in patch notation, $k^{(s-o)}$ denotes the subsequence of $k$ formed with the first $s-o$ components and $k^{(o)}$ the subsequence with the last components. By taking $k \in \mathcal{F}^s$ and $q \in \mathcal{F}^u$, as $k_i, k_j \neq 0$ and $q_i, q_j \neq 0$, Equation 141 implies

$$\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}, \Phi_{\mathbf{q}} = \delta(k,q). \eqno(142)$$

In addition, by taking $k \in \mathcal{F}^u$ and $q \in \mathcal{F}^u$ with $u < s$,

$$\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}, \Phi_{\mathbf{q}}^{(i)} = 0 \quad u < s. \eqno(143)$$

The $\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}^s$ with $k \in \mathcal{F}^s$ are orthonormal with each other and orthogonal to $\Phi_{\mathbf{q}}^u$ with $u < s$. Similarly, by taking $k \in \mathcal{F}^u$ and $q \in \mathcal{F}^v$ with $v \leq u$, orthogonality is proven to be $\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{(j)}$. The zero-th eigenfunction $\Phi^{(0)}$ is also orthogonal to all other eigenfunctions by Equation 141 with $k=0$ and trivially normalized to 1.

proving that $\Lambda_0$ and $\Phi_0$ satisfy the eigenproblem. For $k \neq 0$,

```$\int_{[0,1]^d} d^d y K^{CN}(\mathbf{x}, y) \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} e^{ik \cdot y} \right)^2 = \frac{1}{d^{5/2}} \sum_{i,j=1}^d \int_{[0,1]^d} d^d y \lambda_{ij} e^{ik(\mathbf{x}-y)} e^{ik \cdot y}$$
$$= \frac{1}{d^{5/2}} \sum_{i,j=1}^d \lambda_{ik}^{(s)} e^{ik \cdot y} = \delta(k, q) + \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_{j,+}} \delta(s-o, 0) \delta(q_{o-1}^{(s)}, k_i) \delta(k_{o+1}^{(s)}, 0)$$
$$+ \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_{j,-}} \delta(k-s, 0) \delta(q_i, q_{o-1}^{(s)}) \delta(q_{o+1}^{(s)}, 0) \eqno(145)$$
```

When $k \in \mathcal{F}^u$, the deltas from the eigenproblem with $j \in \mathcal{P}_{j,+}$ vanish, showing that the eigenproblem is satisfied with $\Lambda_k = \lambda_k/d$ and $\Phi_k(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_j \Phi_j^{(s)} e^{ik \cdot \mathbf{x}}$. When $u < s$, as the last $s-u$ components of $k$ vanish, there are several $q$'s satisfying the relation in the bracket. For $k=l$, if $l=j$, then there are $s-u$ $q$'s such that $\delta(k_i^{(s)}, 0) \delta(q_{o-1}^{(s)}, k_i) \delta(k_{o+1}^{(s)}, 0) = 1$. These are all the $q$'s having a $u$-dimensional patch equal to $k^{(u)}$ and all the other elements set to zero, there are $(s-u+1)$ such $q$'s. Moreover, $\lambda_q$ depends only on the modulus of $q$, all these $q$'s result in the same value, and in the same eigenfunction $e^{ik \cdot \mathbf{x}}/\sqrt{d}$, over patches.

```$\int_{[0,1]^d} d^d y K^{CN}(\mathbf{x}, y) \Phi_k(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{s-u+1}{d} \Lambda_{k^{(u)}} \Phi_k^{(u)}(\mathbf{x}) = \Lambda_{k^{(u)}} \Phi_{k^{(u)}}(\mathbf{x}). \quad (146)$$
```

Finally, we prove the expansion of the kernel in Equation 138,

```$$K^{CN}(\mathbf x, y) = \frac{1}{d^2} \sum_{i,j \in \mathcal{P}} C(\mathbf{x}, y) \quad (147)$$
$$= \frac{1}{d} \Lambda_k^{(u)} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \sum \Phi_k(\mathbf{x}) \right) \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \sum \Phi_k(y) \right). \quad (148)$$
```

The terms on the RHS of Equation 147 are equal to those components of Equation 138 for $k \in \mathcal{F}^u$. All the $k$ having vanishing components can be written as $k^{(u)} \in \mathcal{F}^u$, which has the last $s-u$ components vanishing. But a shift of $k$ does not affect $\Lambda_k$ nor $\sum_i e^{ik \cdot \mathbf{s}}$, leading to a degeneracy of eigenvalues $k \in$ $k$ which can be obtained from a shift of $k^{(u)} \in \mathcal{F}^u$. Such degeneracy is removed by restricting the sum over $k$ to the sets $\mathcal{F}^u, u \le s$, of wavevectors with non-vanishing extremal components, and rescaling the remaining eigenvalues with a factor of $(s-u+1)/d$, so that Equation 138 is obtained.

## APPENDIX: LOCALITY DEFEATS THE CURSE OF DIMENSIONALITY

**Lemma A-3.2** (Spectra of overlapping local kernels). Let $K^{LC}$ be a local kernel defined as in Equation 34b, with $\mathcal{P}=\{1,\dots,d\}$ and constituent kernel $C$ satisfying assumptions $i, ii$ above. Then, $K^{LC}$ admits the following Mercer's decomposition,

```$K^{LC}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) = \Lambda_0 + \sum_{u=1}^s \left( \sum_{k \in \mathcal{F}^u} \sum_{i=1}^d \Lambda_{k,ij} \Phi_{k,i}(\mathbf{x}) \Phi_{k,j}(\mathbf{y}) \right) \quad (149)$
```

with eigenfunctions

```$$\Phi_0(\mathbf x)=1, \Phi_{k,i}(\mathbf{x})=\phi_k(\mathbf{x}) \forall k \in \mathcal{F}^u, u \le s, \quad (150)$$
```

and eigenvalues

```$$\Lambda_0=\Lambda_0, \quad \Lambda_{k,ij}=\frac{s-u+1}{d}\lambda_k \forall k \in \mathcal{F}^u, u \le s. \quad (151)$$
```

Proof. We start by proving the orthonormality of the eigenfunctions. The scalar product $\Phi_{kj},\Phi_{qj}$ depends on the relation between the $i$-th and $j$-th patches,

```$$\int_{[0,1]^d} d^d x \phi_k(\mathbf x) \phi_q(\mathbf x)$$
$$= \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}} \int d^d x_i e^{k_i x_i} \int d^d x_j e^{-q_j x_j} + \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \int d^d x_i e^{(k-q)_i x_i}$$
$$+ \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}} \left( \int d^d (x_i-x_j) e^{(x_i-x_j)^+} x_i} \right) \left( \int d^d x_{i,j} e^{(k_{i,j}-q_{i,j})^+ x_{i,j}} \right)$$
$$ \times \int d^d (x_i-x_j) e^{(q_j-x_i)^+ x_j}$$
$$+ \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}} \{i \neq j, k \neq q\} \delta(k,0) + \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \delta(k,q)$$
$$+ \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \delta(k^{(s-o)}) \delta(k_{s-o+1}^{(i)}) \delta(q_{o+1}^{(o)}, 0)$$
$$+ \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \delta(k^{(s-o)}) \delta(k_i^{(o)}, q_{o-1}^{(o)}) \delta(k_{i+1}^{(s-o)}, 0) \eqno(152)$$
```

Clearly, $(\Phi_0, \Phi_0)$ and setting one of $\mathbf k$ and $\mathbf q$ in Equation 152 yields orthogonality between $\Phi_0$ and $\Phi_{ki}$ for all $k \neq 0$ and $i=1,\dots,d$. For any $\mathbf k$ and $\mathbf q \neq 0$, Equation 152 implies

```$$\Phi_{kj},\Phi_{qj} = \delta(k,q)\delta_{i,j}. \eqno(153)$$
```

unless $k^{(i)} \in \mathcal{F}^u$ and $\mathbf q$ is a patch of $k^{(i)}$. But such a $\mathbf q$ would have $q_1=0$ and there is no eigenfunction $\Phi_q$ with $q_1=0$, apart from $\Phi_0$. Hence, orthonormality is proven.

We prove that eigenfunctions from Equation 139 and eigenvalues from $K^{CN}$. For $k=0$,

```$$\int_{[0,1]^d} d^d y K^{CN}(\mathbf x, y) = \frac{1}{d^2} \sum_{i,j=1}^d \int_{[0,1]^d} d^d y \lambda_k e^{k(x_i-y_j)} = \Lambda_0. \eqno(154)$$
```
