Geifman et al. [Gei+22] derived bounds on the eigenvalue spectra
de deep CNNs, but considered only filters of size in one
first layer and did not address generalization. In contrast, we allow for
general filters and provide tight estimates of the asymmetric-
behavior of eigenvalues, which allow us to predict generalization.

The work of Xiao [Xia22] is the closest to us, as also investigates
t spectral bias of deep CNNs in the kernel regime. However, con-
siders a different asymmetric regime, where both the input
the number of training samples tend to infinity. Importantly, it
does characterize the asymmetric decay of the generalization error
with the training set, a central focus of our work.

Finally, Paccolat et al. [PSW21b], Malach and Shalev-Shwartz
[MSS21], and Abbe et al. [AAM22] studied settings where the target

works show complex separation results between models
operating in the kernel regime and those in the feature regime –
where parameters undergo significant changes during training. In
respect, our results demonstrate that even in the kernel regime,
deep CNNs achieve near-optimal performance when the target func-
