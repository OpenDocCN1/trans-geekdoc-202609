# WHEN PARTALLY GENERALIZING, DIFFUSION MODELS LEARN THE SAME SCORE FUNCTION

Even achieving perfect generalization, diffusion models gradually improve their generalization during training – by capturing the structure of the underlying data distribution. In the RHM case, this corresponds to the lowest levels of the grammar. As a consequence, the score function that is learned during training *before memorization* is the same *independently of the sampling of the training set*. In Figure 26 (b), we train two diffusion models in the same setting as Figure 26 (a) with two disjoint training sets. The difference in their outputs – i.e., the components of the learned score during training by computing their Hellinger distance, defined as $H(p,q)=2^{-1/2}\sum_{i}(\sqrt{p_i}-\sqrt{q_i})^2$, with $p=(p_i)_{i\in[n]}$ and $q=(q_i)_{i\in[n]}$ two discrete probability distributions; this distance is averaged over the tokens and the sampling of the diffusion trajectories from 1024 test data. We observed that the distance between the output functions of the two models, i.e., the learned scores determine the generative process – remains stable during training and only jumps to higher values when the models start memorizing their respective training sets. Therefore, the two diffusion models learn very similar score functions when their generalization is gradually improving, before they overfit their empirical scores.

# A RACE BETWEEN MEMORIZATION AND GENERALIZATION

[Yoo+23; Kad+23b; Gu+25] interpret memorization as an overfitting phenomenon driven by the large capacity of overparameterized networks. [Kad+23b] shows that underized models on disjoint training sets learn the same score function, therefore generalizing by sampling the same target distribution; contrast, overparameterized models memorize their respective data. [LDQ24; WV24] find that during their initial phases, overparameterized diffusion models have an inductive bias towards learning a Gaussian approximation of data. This process achieves a primitive form of partial generalization by capturing some data's low-dimensional structure before the model begins to fully memorize the training points. Our results extend this viewpoint to later training stages and higher-order data statistics. Additionally, we quantify the timescale at which models transform from generalizing to memorizing.

# THEORY OF DIFFUSION

Diffusion models Under mild assumptions on the data distribution, diffusion models achieve a sample complexity scaling exponentially with data dimension [BMR20; OAS23]. The sampling and memorization process has been studied for Gaussian mixtures and linear manifolds using the empirical score function [Bir+24; Amb23; Ach+24; Ach+25; LC24]. Learning the empirical score function was studied in [Cu+23; SCK3; HRX24]. The memorization-generalization trade-off in terms of model capacity with random features was studied in [GVM2]. Generalization bounds for early-stopped features learning simple functions were developed in [Li+23]. [BM23; Amb23; Bir+24] show for Gaussian mixtures the existence of a characteristic noise level during the diffusion process where the single modes merge into one. In [Bir+24], another noise scale is identified, corresponding to short diffusion times, where the backward process collapses into the single data points, associated with memorization. [KG24] studies generalization in diffusion models through the inductive bias of translational equivariance and locality. [Fav+25] show that UNet diffusion models sequentially learn different levels of the grammatical rules, with a sample complexity polynomial in data.
