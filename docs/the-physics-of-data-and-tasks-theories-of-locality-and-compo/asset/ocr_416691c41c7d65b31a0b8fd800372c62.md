# THE ROLE OF DEPTH AND SPATIAL ADAPTIVITY

More specifically, we investigate the generalization properties of deep CNNs with non-overlapping patches and no pooling (defined in Figure 2 for an illustration). These networks are trained on a target function $f^*$ via empirical minimization of the mean squared loss. We consider the infinite-width limit of the networks (Section 3.3), where the parameters change infinitesimally over training, thus the network coincides with the corresponding Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK). Thus, generalization is fully characterized by the spectrum of the integral operator of the kernel: in simple terms, the projections on the eigenfunctions with larger eigenvalues can be learned — to error with fewer training points (e.g., Bach [Bac21]).

SPECTRA (Theorem 3.3.1). Due to the network topology, hidden neurons in each layer depend only on the scale of the input referred to as the neuron's receptive field (highlighted by colored boxes in Figure 2, left panel). We show that the NTK eigenfunctions of a CNN of depth $L+1$ can be organized into sectors indexed by the layer index $l=1,...,L$ (Theorem 3.3.1). Each sector consists of eigenfunctions depending only on the receptive fields of the neurons of the hidden layer. By $d_{eff}(l)$ the scale of the receptive fields of neurons in the $l$-th layer, the eigenfunctions of the $l$-th sector are functions of $d_{eff}$ variables. We characterize the asymptotic decay of the eigenfunctions in the polynomial degree of the corresponding eigenfunctions (Theorem 3.3.1), and show this decay is governed by $d_{eff}(l)$. Consequently, the eigenfunctions with the largest eigenvalues — the easiest to learn — are those that are supported on small, localized subsets of the input and have low polynomial degree. This spectral structure is our main technical contribution, and all of our conclusions follow from it.

SPATIAL ADAPTIVITY (Corollary 3.4.0.1). We leverage the spectral characterization to prove that deep CNNs can adapt to the scale of the target function (Section 3.4). By applying rigorous bounds from the theory of kernel ridge regression [CDV7] (reviewed in the first paragraph of Section 3.4), we prove that when learning with the kernel of a CNN and optimal regularization, the error depends on the effective dimensionality of the target. In particular, if $f^*$ depends only on a localized group of $d_{eff}$ input variables, then the generalization error $\mathcal{E}(P) \sim P^{-eta}$ with $eta \ge \mathcal{O}(1/d_{eff})$ (Corollary 3.4.0.1, see Figure 2 for a typical representation). This is consistent with non-rigorous results from the replica method for the ridgeless case [BCP20; Lou+21b].
