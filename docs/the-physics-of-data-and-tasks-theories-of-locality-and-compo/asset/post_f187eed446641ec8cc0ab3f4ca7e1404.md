## SPECTRAL BIAS ANSATZ
The above is actually tight in the noisy setting, for instance when having labels $y_i = f^*(x_i) + \xi_i$ with $\xi_i$ Gaussian. In a noiseless problem where $y_i = f^*(x_i)$, one expects to find the best performances in the ridgeless limit $\lambda \to 0$, so that the rate of Equation 60 is only an upper bound. In the ridgeless case - where the correspondence between kernel methods and infinitely wide networks actually holds - there are unfortunately no rigorous results to provide a derivation of the error decay based on a spectral bias. Consider the projections of the target $f^*$ onto the eigenfunctions of the kernel. If the decay of $f^*$ is sufficiently slow, one can (recall that both $\lambda$ and $E(f^*)$ vanish in this setting)

$$E(P) \sim \sum_{k \text{ s.t. } \Lambda_k < \Lambda(P)} |f_k|^2, \quad (61)$$

with $\Lambda(P)$ the value of the $P$-th largest eigenvalue of the kernel. This result can be derived using the replica method of statistical physics (see Cenatar et al. [**CBP21**], Loureiro et al. [**Lou21b**], and Tomasini et al. [**TSW22**] and Section B.5) or by using only the features of the problem, namely the asymptotic decay of $\Lambda_k$ and $|f_k|^2$ - see also Cui et al.[**Cui+21**]. For instance, the capacity condition depends only on the kernel spectrum: $a \ge 1$ is finite $\text{Tr}(T_k)$ [**SSB+02**]; the specific value is determined by the decay of the ordered eigenvalues with their rank, which in turn depends on the scaling of $\Lambda_k$ with $k$. Similarly, the power-law decay of the ordered eigenvalues with the rank determines the scaling of the $P$-th largest eigenvalue, $\Lambda(P) \sim P^{-a}$.

## 3.5 EXAMPLES AND EXPERIMENTS

The source condition characterizes the regularity of the target function relative to the kernel and depends explicitly on the decay of $|f^*|^2$ with $k$, as does the right-hand side of Equation 61. This condition was built by [Bac21] to prove that kernel methods are adaptive to the smoothness of the target function: projections onto smoother eigenfunctions display a faster decay with $k$, allowing to choose a larger $r$ and leading to better generalization performance. The following Corollary Theorem 3.3.1 (proof and extension to $s \ge 2$) is presented in Section B.4, Corollary B.4.0.1 which shows that, the spectrum can be partitioned in Theorem 3.3.1, hierarchical kernels display adaptivity to targets which depend only on a subset of the input variables. Specific examples of bounds are considered in Section 3.5.

**Corollary 3.4.0.1.** (Adaptivity to spatial structure). Let $T_k$ be the kernel of a hierarchical deep CNN in Theorem 3.3.1 with $s=2$: the capacity exponent $a$ is controlled by the largest sector of the spectrum, i.e.,

$$\text{Tr}(T_k^*/a) < \infty \Rightarrow a < 1+2v/d_{eff}(L);\quad (62)$$

(ii) the source exponent $r$ is controlled by the structure of the target function $f^*$, i.e., if there is $l \le L$ such that $f^*$ depends only on some meta-patch $x_{l+1\to L+1}$, then only the first $l$ of the spectrum contribute to the source condition, i.e., $|T_k^r f^*|^2_H$ reads

$$\sum_{i=1}^{l} \sum_{k_{i+1}\to L+1}} \left( \Lambda_{k_{i+1}\to L+1}} \right)^{-r} |f^*_{k_{i+1\to L+1}}|^2;\quad (63)$$

The same holds if $f^*$ is a linear combination of such functions. As a result, $d_{eff}(L)$ is large and $a \to 1$, the decay of the error is controlled by the effective dimensionality of the target $d_{eff}(L)$.

### SOURCE-CAPACITY BOUND
Consider a target function $f^*$ which only depends on meta-patch $x_{l+1\to L+1}$ as in Corollary 3.4.0.1. Combining the source equation (Equation 63) with the asymptotic scaling of eigenvalues (Equation 55), we get

$$||T_k^r f^*|^2_H < \infty \Leftrightarrow \sum_k |f^*|_k^{(2+v+d_{eff}(l))} < \infty;\quad (64)$$

where $v = 1/2$ for the NTK and $k$ denotes meta-patch $x_{l+1\to L+1}$ without the subscript to $k$. Since the eigenvalues depend on the norm of $k$, Equation 64 is equivalent to a finite-norm condition for all the derivatives of $f^*$ up to order $m < r(2v + d_{eff}(L))$. As a result, if $f^*$ has finite norm to the $m$-th, the source can be tuned to $r = 2m/(2v+d_{eff}(L))$, inversely proportional to the effective dimensionality of $f^*$. Since the exponent on $f^*$ is an increasing function of $r$, the smaller the effective dimensionality of $f^*$, the faster decay of the error - here hierarchical kernels are adaptive to the spatial structure of $f^*$. In particular, the following generalization bounds

**Corollary 3.5.0.1** (generalization bound for hierarchical kernels). Let $K$ be the kernel of a deep CNN with $s=2$. Let $f^*$ be a function depending only on meta-patch $x_{i-1:i+1}$ or a linear combination of such functions. Furthermore, assume $f^*$ has finite-norm derivatives up to order $m$, $||\Delta^m/f^*|| < \infty$. Then, there exists a constant $C' > 0$ such that optimal-regularized regression with $K$ satisfies $\mathcal{E}(\lambda, p) - E(f^*) \leq C' p^{-\beta}$

$$\beta = \frac{2m(2v+d_{eff}(L))}{2v+d_{eff}(L)^2} \quad (65)$$

As an illustration, let us consider the case $p_L = p = d/2$ (the number of two-dimensional patches). Remarkably, when $p \gg 1$, $f^*$ depends only on a finite-dimensional meta-patch (or a sum of such functions) the exponent $\beta$ in Equation 65 converges to the finite value $2m/(2v+d_{eff}(L))$. In contrast, using a fully-connected kernel to learn the same target results in $\beta = 2m/(2m+p)$ - vanishing as $1/p$ when $p \gg 1$, is caused by dimensionality.

### RATES FROM BIAS VARIANCE
The same picture when estimating the decay of the error from Equation 61. $\Lambda(p) \sim p^{-a}$, where $\sum_k ||k|^{2m}|f|^2 < \infty$ implies $||f^*|^2 \leq ||k|^{2m-d_{eff}(L)}|$ for a target supported on a $d_{eff}(L)$-dimensional meta-patch. Plugging such results in Equation 66 we obtain (details in Section B.6.1)

$$\mathcal{E}(p) \sim p^{-\beta} \text{ with } \beta = \frac{2m}{2v+d_{eff}(L)} \quad (66)$$

Again, with $p=1$ and $d_{eff}(L)=p$, the exponent remains finite for $p \gg 1$. We recover the result of Chapter 2 with a shallow local kernel if the target is supported on $s$-dimensional features. These results show that hierarchical kernels play significantly better with the approximation-estimation trade-off than shallow local kernels, as they are able to approximate global functions of the input without being restricted when the target function has a local structure.
