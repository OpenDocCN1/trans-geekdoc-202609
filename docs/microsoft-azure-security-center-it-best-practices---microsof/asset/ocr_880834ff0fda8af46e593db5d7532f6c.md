Now this isn't meant to doom-and-gloom, nor a sky-falling admonishment not to use the Cloud. Cloud Computing in all its forms—LaaS, SaaS, microservices, and more—can provide significant advantages to any organization. Specific to the security of an organization, I will even say that Cloud Computing has the potential to make you more secure than you ever had a chance to be in your on-premises environment. Skeptical? Read on.

Industry analysts (of course cloud vendors) have long lauded the advantages of scale-up and scale-out for your company's servers, services, and applications. But what about taking scale-up and scale-out capability, and pointing it towards the problem of cybersecurity? And what if we could use scale-up and scale-out, not just to secure your infrastructure, but to increase the security of every Azure customer at large? Enter the Microsoft Intelligent Security Graph (See https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/intelligence).

By applying the massive compute, storage, and machine-learning capabilities of Azure to solve the problem of “How can we better secure our customers?” Microsoft has developed a Security Intelligence service based on security signals that we receive from myriad sources—Office 365, Microsoft Account (formerly Windows Live ID), the Digital Crimes Unit, and the Azure cloud platform itself. By analyzing the traffic from these sources—450 authentication events per month and 400 billion emails—Microsoft can identify and respond to both existing and emerging threats originating from all over the Internet and protect our customers in an rapid fashion.

Azure Security Center is not the only Microsoft service that relies on the Security Graph, but the advantages that ASC receives from the Graph are numerous—we have accurate data concerning new and emerging botnets, Command & Control networks, and new forms of malware that attackers are using to target our customers. And what the Graph learns from one customer, it will feed into ASC so that we can use this learning to protect all our customers.

Imagine having an army of security analysts working day and night to research emerging cybersecurity threats and identify actual instances of malicious behaviors targeting your Azure resources so that you can respond and protect your critical assets in a rapid manner. With ASC you're not imagining it—you have that capability built right into the service with no additional work needed on your part.
