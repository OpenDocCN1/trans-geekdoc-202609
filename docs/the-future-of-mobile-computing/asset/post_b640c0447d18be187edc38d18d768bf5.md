
## Internet of Things for Smart Cities

The concept of smart cities is a product of the pervasive digitalization that has been made possible by the supporting technology that has made it feasible for this to happen. The efforts that are being conducted to construct smart cities are centred upon the Internet of Things (IoT), which is fundamental to the concept. According to projections, the internet will have over 75 billion connected devices by the year 2025. This will result in an increase in the number of apps that are being created. When it comes to smart cities, the IoT makes it feasible for sensors to collect and send the city status data to a central cloud. This information contains information on the city’s present condition. This information may then be mined or analysed with the intention of obtaining patterns and making decisions according to those patterns.

## Built-in Workflow

### Mobile Computing for Quick Responses Close to Home

Mobile computing is the initial gate through which data enters the workflow at the beginning of the process. Only lightweight preparatory tasks, such as data cleansing and reorganization, may be carried out at the mobile computing stage. This is because mobile computers, in general, have a limited capacity, and because of limits such as battery life, they are unable to function properly. On the other hand, in-situ monitoring, when combined with a basic comprehension of the data, has the potential to cut down on time delay for tasks that do not need a great deal of calculation but rather merely the capacity to make straightforward judgments. When an unexpected amount of heat is detected, for instance, alerts that have been set up on a mobile device with a temperature threshold that is limited may be activated in a responsive manner.

### Edge Computing Prepares Data and Controls Microcontrollers

In addition to the collection of data at the edge and the transmission of the raw data to the cloud, as is the case with mobile computing, computing at the edge offers additional possibilities for greater data preparation. These capabilities are in addition to the fact that the data is collected at the edge.

It may take a considerable amount of time to upload everything in its raw form to the cloud due to the growing volume of data, and the tremendous load that is imposed upon the central cluster may cause it to surpass the limit of the computing capabilities available. These observations provide observations with a resolution of seconds. These observations are supplied by the data. Through the collection of data from networks consisting of hundreds of sensors, this study keeps track of local temperatures as well as other environmental characteristics. The high-velocity data transfers that take place inside the network, on the other hand, have the potential to generate traffic congestion owing to the restricted bandwidth that is available.

### Cloud Computing for Processing and Analyzing Huge Amounts of Data

The data that is transmitted from sensors is sent via the edges to the center for improved management. This is made possible by the fact that the virtual storage system is capable of expanding itself automatically. Both mobile and edge devices are unable to process the data that the cloud is able to manage, and the cloud is also able to speed up the processing beyond what a standalone server is capable of accomplishing (Goundar et al., 2021). This is because the cloud has a large computational capacity.

One of the most important problems is the heterogeneity of the data, and the enormous amount of labor involved in cleaning the data needs a substantial amount of processing capacity. According to Botta et al. (2014), the cloud, which is a centralized computing resource pool, provides adequate capacity for any job of this kind. The amount of time spent constructing the model and the amount of human error that occurs during the process of transferring the experimental setting are disadvantages.

## Conclusion

The tremendous reduction in the size of sensing and communication tools in cities has made it possible to conceive of the possibility of creating innovative technical networks for smart city applications. Therefore, the development of smart cities has become feasible as a result of this. The goal of urban computing is to enhance the operational intelligence of smart city services while simultaneously bringing about changes to the lives of city residents and the environs in which they live. This is the objective of urban computing.

With the intention of analyzing, highlighting, and reporting on the most recent initiatives that have been made in the area of urban computing that are aimed toward the smart city paradigm, the objective of this article was to research, highlight, and report on such endeavors. We highlighted four primary use cases of urban computing systems; these use cases were given within the architecture of intelligent cities. In addition to that, a number of possible challenges that may arise in the course of future research were identified. In conclusion, we come to the awareness that although urban computing has the potential to give various benefits in smart cities, the confluence of these paradigms leads to the formation of specific recent studies issues that will need to be solved in the future.
