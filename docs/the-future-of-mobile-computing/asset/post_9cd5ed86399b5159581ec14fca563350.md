## Remote Patient Monitoring

A medical procedure known as remote patient monitoring (RPM) enables medical professionals to keep an eye on patients’ health away from conventional healthcare facilities. In order to gather patient data and send it to healthcare professionals for analysis and decision-making, it entails the use of linked devices, sensors, and mobile applications. By putting computational power and data processing closer to the patient’s location, edge computing is essential for improving the effectiveness and efficiency of remote patient monitoring (Vora J et al., 2017). Here is a detailed explanation of how edge computing is beneficial for remote patient monitoring.

Edge computing facilitates the processing and analysis of patient data at the edge devices, such as wearable technology or home monitoring systems. As a result, healthcare professionals can get quick insights into a patient’s health status and use that information to spot possible problems, monitor trends, and take prompt action. Real-time data analysis is crucial in urgent circumstances, such as identifying irregularities in vital signs or responding to emergencies. With edge computing, patient data can be processed locally instead of being sent to a central cloud server. To cut down on latency and enable faster reaction times, it is instead handled locally at the edge devices (Goundar et al., 2021). As a result, healthcare providers can get timely notifications and take appropriate action in time-sensitive circumstances.

Edge computing makes it possible to continuously monitor the health status of patients. A complete picture of the patient’s health status throughout time is provided by connected devices and sensors, which collect data in real-time. With the use of continuous monitoring, medical personnel can spot small changes in a patient’s health and take early action to avoid complications or hospital stays. Even when network access is spotty, edge computing ensures that patient data is continuously analysed and examined. Edge computing handles privacy and security issues with remote patient monitoring. Sensitive data can be retained inside the boundaries of the edge devices and the patient’s home network by processing patient data locally, lowering the risk of data breaches. By using this strategy, privacy laws are respected, and patient health information is protected. Additionally, edge computing lessens the need to send sensitive data over open networks, lowering the risk of intrusion.

Network bandwidth may be strained when sending a lot of patient data to a central cloud server for processing. Edge computing addresses this problem by processing data locally and sending only pertinent or summarised data to the cloud or healthcare providers. This data transmission optimisation increases system performance by reducing network congestion, cutting costs, and lowering costs. Edge computing enables offline capabilities for remote patient monitoring in circumstances where network connectivity is constrained or sporadic. Even when connectivity is briefly disrupted, edge devices may locally store and analyse data. The gathered data may be synchronised with the main system after the connection has been restored. With these offline capabilities, vital patient data is protected from loss and can be easily included into the whole monitoring process.

Scalable deployments are made possible by edge computing as more patients use remote patient monitoring. In order to ensure effective data processing and analysis without taxing centralised systems, edge devices can handle the increasing volume of data supplied by numerous patients. This scalability makes it possible to expand remote patient monitoring to a bigger population without sacrificing the standard of care or system performance. Patients can participate in remote patient monitoring and receive personalised care thanks to edge computing. Edge devices assess patient-specific data in real time and offer individualised insights and recommendations by processing data locally. Improved patient participation, proactive interventions, and treatment plans that are specific to each patient are made possible. Patients are able to actively manage their own treatment, receive rapid feedback, and monitor their progress.

Real-time anomaly or deviation identification in healthcare is made possible by edge computing. Healthcare professionals can quickly detect anomalies by examining patient data at edge devices and receiving alerts or messages when they do. This enables early action and prompt medical support. The development of illnesses or consequences might be stopped with this preventive approach. Edge computing gives patients the power to actively manage their own healthcare. Patients can track their progress, check their health problems, and make well-informed decisions if they have access to real-time health data via wearable technology or mobile applications. Better health outcomes and higher patient satisfaction may result from this elevated engagement and self-management.

### Energy Efficiency
Local data processing at the edge minimises the requirement for data transmission to the cloud, resulting in less energy use. The irrigation system’s environmental impact is diminished thanks to its energy efficiency. Based on real-time sensor data, edge-controlled systems continuously monitor and modify irrigation parameters. This flexibility makes it possible to quickly alter irrigation decisions in response to shifting soil moisture levels, weather patterns, or crop circumstances. Integration with cloud-based platforms enables extra analysis, monitoring, and long-term data storage while data processing and decision-making are done at the edge. The creation of long-term water management strategies and complete data insights are made possible through cloud integration. To accommodate various field sizes, crop varieties, and farming techniques, edge-controlled irrigation systems are easily scaled and customized. The modular design of edge devices enables adaptable deployment to changing agricultural requirements.

Edge-controlled systems lower the amount of physical labour needed and operating costs related to water use by automating and improving irrigation procedures. Farmers gain from this cost-effectiveness, which also encourages wise resource use. Edge-controlled irrigation encourages effective water management strategies, which is in line with the objectives of sustainable agriculture. These technologies assist long-term agricultural profitability by maximising water use, reducing waste, and increasing crop output. To produce data-driven insights, edge computing enables edge-controlled irrigation systems. These systems can analyse data at the edge to find trends, patterns, and correlations that can help with irrigation strategy optimisation. Long-term planning, predictive analytics, and continual practice improvement of water management techniques can all benefit from these findings.

Even in scenarios where there is little network access, edge-controlled watering systems provide resilience and operational continuity. In the event of network outages or disturbances, these systems’ local processing capabilities allow them to carry on and make irrigation decisions on their own.
