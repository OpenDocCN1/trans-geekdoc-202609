# Edge Gaming

Edge computing provides exciting opportunities in the gaming industry. By leveraging edge servers, game developers can offload computational tasks, reduce latency, and enhance the multiplayer gaming experience (Spinelli et al., 2022). Edge computing enables real-time rendering, fast content delivery, and seamless gameplay across devices. Edge gaming also supports cloud gaming services, where games are streamed and processed at the edge, reducing the need for powerful gaming hardware on the user’s end.

# Autonomous Vehicles

Edge computing is instrumental in enabling autonomous vehicles to make real-time decisions. By processing sensor data locally, edge computing reduces response times and ensures the safety and efficiency of autonomous driving systems (McEnroe et al., 2022, Qiu et al., 2022).

# Telecommunications

Edge computing is being adopted in the telecommunications industry to support low-latency applications such as online gaming, remote surgery, high-frequency trading, and video conferencing and telepresence (Song et al., 2022). Edge computing and 5G/6G are closely intertwined and complementary technologies that have the potential to revolutionize the way data is processed, transmitted, and utilized in the digital era.

# Precision Agriculture

Edge devices can collect a wealth of agricultural data regarding soil conditions, weather patterns, crop growth, and livestock health. This data is processed locally at the edge, allowing for real-time analysis and actionable insights. By applying advanced analytics and machine learning algorithms, farmers can make informed decisions on irrigation, fertilization, pesticide application, and disease management, optimizing resource usage, reducing waste, and maximizing crop yields (O’Grady et al., 2019).
