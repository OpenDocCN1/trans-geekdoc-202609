# How Words Are Classified by EmoLex into Emotional Dimensions

EmoLex uses lexical analysis and manual annotation to classify words and phrases into emotional dimensions. Every word or phrase in the lexicon has an emotional label that is carefully assigned by lexicographers and linguistic experts based on the term’s associations and connotations with particular emotions. Words that are frequently linked to a specific feeling, like “joy” for happiness or “anger” for impatience, are labelled appropriately.

These affective descriptors usually fit into a few categories, such as:

+   - Joy, love, and happiness are positive feelings.
+   - Negative feelings include dread, grief, and anger.
+   - Words that don’t strongly express anyone feeling are considered neutral.

EmoLex utilises this methodical classification to offer an organised depiction of the affective significance of vocabulary in written communication, facilitating the numerical evaluation of affective manifestations.

# The Function of Sentiment Analysis
and NLP’s Emotion Lexicons

In the field of NLP, emotion lexicons such as EmoLex are crucial, especially when it comes to sentiment and emotional analysis. These lexicons allow text to be categorised into different emotional dimensions by acting as useful references for sentiment analysis models and algorithms.

The following succinctly describes the function of emotion lexicons in NLP:

Sentiment analysis models with labelled emotional features are made possible by emotion lexicons. Sentiment analysis algorithms can classify text as positive, negative, or neutral by assigning sentiment scores based on word matches in the text to the emotional labels in the lexicon.

+   - Emotion Detection: Beyond sentiment, emotion lexicons enable the identification of certain emotions conveyed in text, for as identifying depressive or fearful sentiments in conversations about mental health.
