## Applications of Edge Computing

This section explores various domains where edge computing finds practical applications and showcases its potential benefits.

### Video Analytics and Surveillance

Edge computing offers significant advantages in video analytics and surveillance applications. Edge computing reduces latency and bandwidth requirements by processing video data near video sources (Chen et al., 2019). This enables real-time video analysis, object detection, facial recognition, and event detection. Edge computing in video surveillance allows for immediate response to security threats, proactive monitoring, and efficient utilization of network resources.

### Smart Cities

Edge computing is a key enabler of smart city initiatives. Sensors, cameras and IoT devices may be installed at various corners of the city to collect real-time data. By deploying edge devices and servers throughout the city, edge computing facilitates real-time data analytics, and decision-making at the network edge. Edge computing supports various smart city applications such as quality lifestyle, sustainable energy generation, smart water management, environmental monitoring, public safety (Zhou et al., 2022). ### Edge Gaming

Edge computing provides exciting opportunities in the gaming industry. By leveraging edge servers, game developers can offload computational tasks, reduce latency, and enhance the multiplayer gaming experience (Spinelli et al., 2022). Edge computing enables real-time rendering, fast content delivery, and seamless gameplay across devices. Edge gaming also supports cloud gaming services, where games are streamed and processed at the edge, reducing the need for powerful gaming hardware on the user’s end.

### Autonomous Vehicles

Edge computing is instrumental in enabling autonomous vehicles to make real-time decisions. By processing sensor data locally, edge computing reduces response times and ensures the safety and efficiency of autonomous driving systems (McEnroe et al., 2022, Qiu et al., 2022).

### Telecommunications

Edge computing is being adopted in the telecommunications industry to support low-latency applications such as online gaming, remote surgery, high-frequency trading, and video conferencing and telepresence (Song et al., 2022). Edge computing and 5G/6G are closely intertwined and complementary technologies that have the potential to revolutionize the way data is processed, transmitted, and utilized in the digital era.

### Precision Agriculture

Edge devices can collect a wealth of agricultural data regarding soil conditions, weather patterns, crop growth, and livestock health. This data is processed locally at the edge, allowing for real-time analysis and actionable insights. By applying advanced analytics and machine learning algorithms, farmers can make informed decisions on irrigation, fertilization, pesticide application, and disease management, optimizing resource usage, reducing waste, and maximizing crop yields (O’Grady et al., 2019).

Edge computing also offers significant benefits in livestock management. By deploying edge devices equipped with sensors and wearables, farmers can continuously monitor the health, behavior, and location of individual animals. This enables early detection of diseases, identification of potential breeding issues, and timely intervention in case of emergencies. With edge computing, livestock farmers can improve animal welfare, optimize feeding regimes, and enhance overall productivity.

## Summary

The challenges posed by data-intensive and time-sensitive applications are the reason for the evolution of edge computing. Its foundational concepts, including proximity, decentralization, heterogeneity, and edge analytics, enable low-latency processing, optimized bandwidth usage, and enhanced privacy and security. By adhering to key principles such as latency reduction, bandwidth optimization, resilience, and data privacy, edge computing offers a scalable and flexible computing model with diverse applications across various domains.

## Implications of Edge Computing on Society

This section explores the various implications that edge computing has on society, encompassing areas such as connectivity, privacy, sustainability, healthcare, and smart cities. We may better prepare for the societal changes brought about by understanding the implications of the widespread adoption of edge computing.

One of the primary implications of edge computing on society is the provision of enhanced connectivity and ubiquitous services. This enhances user experiences, enables seamless interactions with digital services, and fosters the growth of emerging technologies. Improved connectivity empowers individuals, businesses, and communities to access and utilize advanced applications and services, bridging the digital divide and promoting digital inclusivity (Shanthakumar et al., 2023).

With the proliferation of edge devices and the collection of vast amounts of personal and sensitive data at the edge, privacy and data protection become critical concerns. Edge computing raises critical questions about data ownership, consent, and security as it moves computing capabilities closer to data sources. Safeguarding privacy in edge computing environments requires robust privacy-preserving mechanisms, secure data transmission protocols, and transparent data governance frameworks to protect individuals’ rights and ensure compliance with privacy regulations (Mogilevskaia et al., 2022).

Edge computing has the potential to contribute to environmental sustainability (Arroba et al., 2023). Localized processing at the edge also enables energy-efficient operations, as edge devices can be optimized to operate at lower power levels, prolonging battery life and reducing overall energy consumption. These sustainable practices align with global efforts to reduce carbon footprints and promote eco-friendly technologies (Li et al., 2018).

The implications of edge computing on society are far-reaching, encompassing enhanced connectivity, privacy challenges, environmental sustainability, healthcare transformation, and the development of smart cities. These implications highlight the transformative power of edge computing in various domains, impacting individuals, businesses, and communities. Understanding and addressing these implications are crucial to harness the full potential of edge computing while ensuring privacy, security, sustainability, and societal well-being.
