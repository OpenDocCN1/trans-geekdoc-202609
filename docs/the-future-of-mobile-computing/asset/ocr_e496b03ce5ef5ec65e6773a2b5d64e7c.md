The capacity of edge computing to solve the problems associated with network connectivity and bandwidth constraints is another important advantage of the technology (Varghese B et al., 2016). Only pertinent and useful data is delivered to the cloud or data centre by processing and analysing data at the edge, reducing the amount of data that must cross the network. Edge computing is excellent for situations with sporadic or weak network access since it not only optimises bandwidth utilisation but also lessens the dependence on continual high-speed connectivity.

Additionally, edge computing enables improved data protection and privacy. Businesses can have more control over their data and lower the danger of sensitive data being compromised or exposed during transmission by processing and storing it locally at the edge. In fields like healthcare and finance, where data privacy and regulatory compliance are crucial, this is especially significant.

An important technical development with broad consequences in many fields is edge computing. Its significance comes from its ability to deal with important concerns including data processing, latency, bandwidth, privacy, and real-time decision-making (Khan WZ et al., 2019). # Reduced Latency

By bringing computation closer to the data source, edge computing cuts down on the amount of time data must travel to and from a central cloud server. This decreased latency makes it possible to evaluate data in real-time and make decisions more quickly, which is essential for applications like autonomous vehicles, industrial automation, and remote monitoring that call for quick responses (Stankovski S et al., 2020).
