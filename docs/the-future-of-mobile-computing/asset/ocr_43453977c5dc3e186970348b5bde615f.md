# Mobile Sensors and IoT in farming

A sensor is an apparatus that detects a physical quantity and converts it into a signal that can be read and used by other apparatuses. These days, a huge range of sensors are accessible, and many well-known smartphones already have a number of sensors installed, including gyroscopes, barometers, accelerometers, light sensors, temperature sensors, and GPS.

Smart phones are equipped with three different types of sensors: environmental, motion, and location sensors. Motion sensors, which make up the first category are monitor rotational and acceleration forces. This includes the ambient air temperature measured by a thermometer, the pressure measured by a barometer, and the light level measured by a photometer. The third type of sensors, known as position sensors, measures the actual position of the gadget. These sensors include orientation sensors, GPS, and magnetometers.

# IoT in Farming

Smart Agriculture is the common term used to denote the application of IoT solutions in agriculture. Farmers may collect machine and environmental measurements using Internet of Things (IoT) sensors, as specified in Figure 2 will enable them to improve nearly every aspect of their operations, from livestock to crops, and make more informed decisions. Numerous parts of agriculture could be transformed by technologies (Padhy et al., 2022) and the Internet of Things. Specifically, there are four ways that IoT (Farooq et al., 2022) might enhance agriculture:

+   - Many data points gathered by intelligent agricultural sensors.
+   - Reduced manufacturing hazards and improved internal process control.
+   - Increased quantity and quality of the products.
+   - Smaller environmental impact.
