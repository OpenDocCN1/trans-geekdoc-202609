+   - Augmented Reality Experiences: In the subject of mixed reality, digital overlays enhance the real world (augmented reality). This category includes applications like interactive holograms, virtual information displays, and real-time data visualisations (X. Xia et al., 2019). Users are fully submerged in an improved reality where virtual items harmoniously integrate with their surroundings.
+   - Virtual Reality Immersions: The term Mixed Reality falls under the same umbrella as Virtual Reality. In some cases, customers find themselves momentarily cut off from the outside world and fully engrossed in a virtual environment generated by computers (R. S. Kothari et al., 2021). This category includes interactive environments, simulations, and virtual reality tours that all contribute to improving the sense of presence and teamwork.
+   - Immediate Teamwork and Communication: The ability of mixed reality to facilitate real-time communication and cooperation is one of its challenges. Without being physically present, users can collaborate in the same space, engage with shared virtual items, and create mixed reality experiences. To sum up, investigating the different elements and developments of Mixed Reality offers a wide range of experiences that seamlessly combine the virtual and real worlds. As a relatively new and developing discipline, mixed reality has the power to drastically alter how we see and interact with the environment. It integrates fully immersive virtual environments with augmented interfaces that enhance the real world.
+   - Spatial Computing and Interaction: The goal of mixed reality is to realise the idea of spatial computing, in which a system receives inputs and reacts instantly to the outside environment. Accurate mapping of the environment becomes essential to allow digital objects to react dynamically to changes in the physical world. The same techniques that are used to interact with real-world items can also be used to interact and manipulate virtual ones.
+   - Wearable Technology and Headsets: Wearable technology and helmets with sensors and cameras are frequently used in mixed reality applications. Using these gadgets allows users to smoothly move between the actual and virtual worlds, which in turn makes mixed reality interactions more immersive (Goundar et al., 2021). Microsoft Hollowness and Magic Leap are two of the best examples in this field.
