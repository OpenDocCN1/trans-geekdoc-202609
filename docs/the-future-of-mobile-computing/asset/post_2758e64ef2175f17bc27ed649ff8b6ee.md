
Synchronised data can be sent to central systems for additional analysis or historical records after connectivity has been restored.

## Conclusion

Edge computing is a game-changing technology that has enormous advantages in a number of industries, to sum up. It revolutionises industrial automation by allowing for real-time data processing, localised control, and increased operational effectiveness. With regard to cybersecurity, edge computing offers a distributed and pro-active strategy to protect critical data and reduce online threats. Real-time analysis and individualised care are made possible via remote patient monitoring, which improves patient outcomes and lowers costs. Edge computing also enhances irrigation water management, supporting resource conservation and sustainable practices. Overall, edge computing improves efficiency, security, and response times across a variety of industries, spurring innovation and meeting changing needs in our interconnected world. Edge computing also offers scalability and flexibility, allowing for seamless system expansion and reconfiguration. By reducing infrastructural and data transport dependencies, it also helps with cost optimization. Edge computing is paving the way for cutting-edge technology, wise decision-making, and a more connected and effective future as it is incorporated into a variety of industries.

## Resource Constraints

Benefits for farmers include guaranteed markets for their produce, reduced production risks, knowledge transfer about cutting-edge farming technology, and access to essential agricultural inputs, frequently on credit. Additionally, they exert control over the production system to ensure that it complies with relevant standards for market access. Notwithstanding the evident advantages, several limitations in the connections between farms and agribusinesses have been noted in the cases examined. There are two types of constraints: internal and external.

### Internal Constraints

**Lack of Business Management Skills**

Inadequate production planning and marketing expertise, particularly among small-scale processors, leads to an inability to consider the inputs required for the scheduled processing of raw material quantities. During the harvest season, agribusiness entities need to be particularly adept at financial planning.

**Raw Material Procurement**

During harvest season, there are frequently surpluses of unprocessed farm produce, and setting up continuous processing activities is challenging due to a lack of pre-processing and storage facilities. Agribusiness and farmers have different socioeconomic backgrounds, which makes it challenging to build lasting business partnerships. For many processors, farmers are just a source of raw materials; they have no financial or other incentive to assist or develop them.

**Quality Constraints**

Farmers frequently don’t know that processors have precise requirements when it comes to crop varieties, quality standards, production volumes, and delivery timeliness. Farmers who lack knowledge and expertise may also harvest crops too soon, handle them poorly after harvest, and fail to separate crops into different quality grades.

### External Constraints

**Adverse Macroeconomic Conditions**

Business development is generally hampered by inconsistent and opaque business rules and regulations. Any economic development is stifled in nations with high tax rates, inefficient tax administration, and high levels of corruption. The depreciation raises the price of imported inputs. In the majority of agriculturally based nations, declining government spending on agriculture is more common than unusual.
