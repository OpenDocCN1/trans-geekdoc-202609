
Many edge devices are resource-constrained and powered by batteries or renewable energy sources. Optimizing energy consumption and extending battery life in edge devices is critical to ensure their continuous operation and reduce environmental impact. Edge AI and machine learning: Deploying artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) (Moustafa, 2021) models at the edge can enhance real-time decision-making and reduce dependence on cloud resources. However, running sophisticated AI/ML algorithms (Hamdan S et al., 2020) on resource-constrained edge devices poses computational challenges and requires efficient model optimization and deployment techniques.

Continuous and continued research and innovation will play a crucial role in overcoming these issues and unlocking the full potential of edge computing (Pan J and McElhannon 2020).

## Future Trends

Edge computing has a bright future (Sonone et al., 2020), and a number of trends are anticipated to influence its advancement. The following are some major future trends for edge computing: Integration of 5G: The introduction of 5G networks, which offer extremely low latency and high bandwidth connectivity, will completely transform edge computing. With 5G integration, real-time data processing and analysis at the edge will be possible, supporting augmented reality, smart cities, and driverless cars, among other applications. The future scenarios and opportunities are specified in Table 1.

- Edge AI and Machine Learning: Edge computing will witness an increased integration of AI and machine learning capabilities. By deploying AI models (Chen CM et al., 2022) directly on edge devices, real-time decision-making for data processing can be achieved without relying heavily on cloud resources. This trend will enable a wide range of applications, including real-time video analytics, predictive maintenance, and personalized user experiences.
- Edge-to-Cloud Continuum: The future will see a more centralized cloud systems and edge devices work together and integrate seamlessly (Deshpande S and Kulkarni N, 2021). The edge-to-cloud continuum will allow intelligent workload distribution, where data processing and storage can be dynamically allocated between the edge and the cloud based on factors such as latency, bandwidth, and resource availability.
- Edge Security: As the number of connected edge devices increases, security will remain a critical concern. Future edge computing solutions will focus on enhancing security measures, including device authentication, encryption, secure bootstrapping, and real-time threat detection. Additionally, privacy-enhancing technologies, such as federated learning, will be employed to protect user data while enabling data-driven insights.
- Edge-as-a-Service (EaaS): The concept of Edge-as-a-Service will gain prominence, where organizations can access and utilize edge computing resources and capabilities on-demand, similar to the cloud computing (Perez J et al., 2022) model. This will enable businesses to leverage edge infrastructure without the need for significant upfront investments, facilitating rapid deployment and scalability.
- Federated Edge Computing: Federated learning and computing models will gain traction, enabling collaboration and knowledge sharing between multiple edge devices while maintaining data privacy. Federated edge computing allows distributed learning across multiple edge nodes, facilitating better model accuracy and efficiency.
- Industry-specific Edge Solutions: Edge computing will continue to be adopted across various industries, including healthcare, manufacturing, transportation, and retail. Industry-specific solutions will emerge, tailored to address the unique requirements and challenges of each sector. These solutions will improve operational efficiency and open up new business opportunities by providing optimised processing, analytics, and decision-making capabilities at the edge.
- Edge Management and Orchestration: As edge deployments grow in scale and complexity, efficient management and orchestration systems (Cui W, 2020) will become crucial. Future trends will focus on developing comprehensive management frameworks that enable centralized control, monitoring, and automation of edge resources, ensuring seamless integration, and optimizing resource allocation.

These trends highlight the ongoing evolution and maturation of edge computing, driven by advancements in connectivity, AI, security, and industry-specific requirements. As technology continues to advance, edge computing will play a pivotal role in enabling innovative applications and transforming various industries.
