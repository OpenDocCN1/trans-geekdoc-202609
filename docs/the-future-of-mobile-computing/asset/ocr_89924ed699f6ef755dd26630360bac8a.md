# Market Constraints

Low demand for processed goods is a result of consumers in developing nations having limited purchasing power. As a result, agribusiness’s profitability is constrained. Agribusiness development is further hampered by the lack of large enough processing companies to sustain local producers or suppliers of ingredients, packaging, and processing equipment.

# Limited Availability of Inputs

The agricultural and business sectors are not very profitable due to high raw material costs in both farming and processing. Since local intermediary goods suppliers cannot compete with imports in terms of both price and quality, imports are frequently the source of inputs needed to add value to primary products.

# Environmental Impact

Agriculture has had a huge environmental impact, which is why we need to evaluate it and work to make it as sustainable as we can for future generations. Agriculture encompasses the raising of animals, the cultivation of plants, and any afferent activity. Since humans have lived there for centuries, many animals have evolved to live on farms as a natural consequence. Across the world, agricultural practices vary according to the species that are available and the environmental conditions.

Depending on the environment we have positive impact and negative impact on agriculture.

Positive Effects:

+   - Reduce carbon dioxide (CO2) levels in the atmosphere by storing carbon (C) in soils.
+   - Improve air quality

Negative Effects:

Negative effects of agriculture include water pollution and soil erosion. Through the atmospheric release of greenhouse gases, it can also exacerbate climate change.

+   - Changes to the hydrological cycle.
+   - Soil quality loss
