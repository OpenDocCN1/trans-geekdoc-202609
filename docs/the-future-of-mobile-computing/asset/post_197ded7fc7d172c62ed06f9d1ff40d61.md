## The Role of Wireless Charging in Sustainability

Because wireless charging solves a number of the financial and environmental issues that come with using conventional charging techniques, it contributes significantly to sustainability. The following are a few ways that wireless charging supports sustainability:

- Energy Efficiency: Systems for wireless (Zhou et al., 2019) charging can be made to be extremely effective, reducing the amount of energy lost during charging. In order to promote sustainable behaviours and lower overall energy usage, this efficiency is essential.
- Reduced E-Waste: By eliminating the need for throwaway wires and connectors, wireless charging reduces the amount of electronic garbage (e-waste) that is produced.
- Increased Product Lifespan: Device failure can frequently be attributed to physical connector wear and tear, such as that seen in USB ports. By doing away with the necessity for physical connections, wireless charging may extend the life of electronics and lower the frequency of replacements.
- Clean Energy Integration: A more sustainable charging ecosystem is facilitated by wireless charging infrastructure that is fueled by clean, renewable energy sources.
- Flexibility and Convenience: There is greater freedom in where and how gadgets can be charged when using wireless charging.

## Green Mobile Networks and Connectivity

### Energy-Efficient Cellular Infrastructure

In the telecommunications sector, energy-efficient cellular infrastructure is essential since it minimises operational expenses and the impact on the environment while meeting the growing demand for mobile connection (Ismail et al., 2014; Antonopoulos et al., 2015).

- Network Optimization: Load Balancing and Dynamic Resource Allocation
- Green Base Stations: Renewable Energy Sources and Energy Storage
- Small Cells and HetNets (Heterogeneous Networks): Deploy microcells in densely populated areas to reduce stress on large macrocells and improve energy efficiency
- Energy-Efficient Antennas: Smart Antennas and Massive MIMO
- Energy-Efficient Protocols: LTE-A and 5G Standards and Network Function Virtualization (NFV) and Software-Defined Networking (SDN).
- Energy-Efficient Hardware: Low-Power Components and Energy Harvesting
- Public Awareness and Education: Educate consumers about how mobile phones impact the environment and promote responsible use.

Combining these technologies can help create a more secure and energy-efficient cellular infrastructure.

- Energy Efficiency: Low Power Modes and Dynamic Scaling
- Data Compression: Efficient Compression Algorithms
- Minimize Round Trips: HTTP/2 and HTTP/3
- Content Delivery Networks (CDNs): Edge Computing
- Caching: Client-Side and Server-Side Caching
- Quality of Service (QoS): Adaptive Bitrate Streaming
- Protocol Optimization: TCP Optimization
- Efficient Routing: Routing Algorithms
- Network Virtualization: Network Function Virtualization (NFV)
