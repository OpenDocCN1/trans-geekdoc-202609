## Heterogeneity

Combining models from diverse frameworks and languages creates challenges in data exchange, format conversion, and ensuring compatibility across the entire pipeline.

## Resource Optimization

Efficiently allocating resources for multiple models with varying computational demands requires balancing performance and cost.

## Absence of Prompt Conversation

In a face-to-face setting, communication occurs quickly and makes it easier for students to get answers and clarify confusing concepts. In an e-learning environment, communication is often asynchronous, which means there is a gap between teachers and students. In these spaces, misunderstandings can easily arise; Sometimes problems can be shelved before we can solve them.

## Responses Not Received in Timely Manner

Providing feedback is one of the most important and valuable ways for teachers to interact with students. When feedback is delayed or delayed for weeks because of the online format, students may feel confused or uncertain about your expectations, their progress, and their performance in the classroom.

## Contextual Understanding

The system needs to consider the context and nuances of online learning. Certain behaviors or interactions may seem suspicious but could be legitimate in an online educational setting. Overcoming these challenges requires a thoughtful approach, involving collaboration between educators, students, technologists, and policymakers. Open communication and transparency are vital in gaining stakeholders’ trust and ensuring the system’s successful integration into online learning environments. Clearly communicating the system’s capabilities, limitations, and ethical considerations is crucial. Openness builds trust, mitigates concerns, and allows for ongoing evaluation and refinement of the AI-based detection tools.
