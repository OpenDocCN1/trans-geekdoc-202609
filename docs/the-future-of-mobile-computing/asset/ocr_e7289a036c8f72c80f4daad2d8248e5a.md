Safeguarding privacy in edge computing environments requires robust privacy-preserving mechanisms, secure data transmission protocols, and transparent data governance frameworks to protect individuals’ rights and ensure compliance with privacy regulations (Mogilevskaia et al., 2022).

Edge computing has the potential to contribute to environmental sustainability (Arroba et al., 2023). Localized processing at the edge also enables energy-efficient operations, as edge devices can be optimized to operate at lower power levels, prolonging battery life and reducing overall energy consumption. These sustainable practices align with global efforts to reduce carbon footprints and promote eco-friendly technologies (Li et al., 2018).

The implications of edge computing on society are far-reaching, encompassing enhanced connectivity, privacy challenges, environmental sustainability, healthcare transformation, and the development of smart cities. These implications highlight the transformative power of edge computing in various domains, impacting individuals, businesses, and communities. Understanding and addressing these implications are crucial to harness the full potential of edge computing while ensuring privacy, security, sustainability, and societal well-being.
