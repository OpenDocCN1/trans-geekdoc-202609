## Energy-Efficient Hardware Designs

## Advances in Low-Power Processors for Mobile Devices

The development of low-voltage electronic devices is important for the electricity generation of more powerful and energy-efficient electronic devices, smartphones, tablets and other devices (Lee et al., 2021). The ongoing need for mobile devices to have greater power, better performance, and longer battery life is what is driving these advancements.

Process Technology Advancements: Shrinking Process Nodes and FinFET Technology.

- Advanced Power Management: Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling (DVFS) and Adaptive Voltage Scaling (AVS)
- Low-Power Memory Technologies: LPDDR (Low Power Double Rate Data) memory, Developments in low-power memory technology, including LPDDR, can save energy by lowering the amount of power used by the device’s memory subsystem.
- AI-Based Power Management: AI-Driven Power Optimization, AI algorithms are used to forecast and dynamically control power use. The device’s power profile can be modified by these algorithms in response to user activity and consumption patterns.
- Energy-Efficient Display Technologies: OLED and AMOLED Displays: Mobile devices can use less energy overall by utilizing energy-saving devices like OLED and AMOLED.
- Sensor Hub Integration: Dedicated sensor processing: Some mobile CPUs are equipped with a dedicated sensor hub, which efficiently handles sensor data and frees up the primary processor.

## Energy-Efficient Displays and Batteries

Sensors and automation capabilities are built into energy-efficient batteries and displays (Piguet and Christian, 2018), enabling homes to maximize energy use and drastically lower their carbon footprint while also extending the life of their devices, improving user experiences, and addressing environmental concerns.

Energy-Efficient displays include OLED Technology, Power-Efficient Backlighting, Low-Power Display Technologies, Adaptive Brightness and Refresh Rate. Lithium-Ion Batteries, Solid-State Batteries, Advanced Battery Management Systems (BMS), Optimized Power Consumption, Fast Charging Technologies can be used as energy efficient batteries. Ultimately, information science, engineering, and design are all involved in the complex process of creating energy-efficient batteries and displays.

## Kinetic and Piezoelectric Charging Technologies

Two advanced methods of converting electricity into electrical energy (Jiju et al., 2014) are kinetic charging and piezoelectric charging. Now let’s look at these one by one. The process of converting mechanical energy or kinetic energy into electrical energy is called kinetic energy harvesting. These systems are often used by devices that can record movement (such as the movements of the human body or other systems) and convert it into electricity. Also, to convert kinetic energy into electricity, kinetic energy harvesters (Malla et al., 2016) usually use electromagnetic induction, piezoelectric materials or other means. Fitness monitors, technology and some consumer electronics often use kinetic energy to charge products. The main advantage of kinetic charging is that it produces electricity from an external source and the limitation is kinetic charging frequently produces very little energy compared to other charging techniques

The foundation of piezoelectric charging is the piezoelectric effect (Elmannai et al., 2023), the capacity of some materials to produce an electric charge when mechanical stress is applied. Quartz, Rochelle salt, and lead zirconate titanate (PZT) are examples of common piezoelectric materials. These substances were picked because they can produce a voltage under mechanical stress.
