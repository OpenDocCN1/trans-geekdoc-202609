## GPS Technology in Agriculture

GPS technology has completely transformed the agricultural landscape, revolutionizing traditional farming practices. One of its key applications empowers farmers with accurate positioning information for their tractors, planters, and other machinery. This allows for optimal allocation of resources, such as water, fertilizers, and pesticides, by targeting specific areas in need. Moreover, the integration of GPS technology in Variable Rate Technology (VRT) has further enhanced efficiency in farming.

- Auto-Steer Systems: Tractors and other farm equipment can precisely follow predefined routes thanks to Auto-Steer Systems, which use GPS technology.
- Soil Health Management: GPS devices reduce crop damage and soil erosion by better monitoring variations in soil health and revolving machinery at the proper angles and locations.
- Yield monitoring: During harvest, crop yield in the field is tracked and observed using GPS. In the future, these data will be useful for making well-informed decisions and assessing the effectiveness of implementation activities.
- Tracking equipment: GPS enhances fleet management by assisting farmers in tracking the location and movement of farm equipment.

## Challenges and Future Prospects

- **Limited network usage:** Many rural areas lack robust mobile networks, making it difficult for farmers to access smart agricultural solutions (Yigezu Wendimu et al., 2021, Kheya et al., 2023) Limited connectivity prevents the real-time use of crops, weather and smart technologies.
- **Low bandwidth:** Even with coverage, rural areas can suffer from low bandwidth, affecting data speeds and reliability. Slow data transfer can hinder smart agricultural systems, delaying decision-making and response times.
- **Material differences:** Rural areas often lack the necessary infrastructure for robust and stable connectivity, including 4G/5G towers and reliable internet service. Inadequate infrastructure may limit the use of advanced mobile solutions for precision agriculture and data-driven decision making. Government and telecommunication companies can invest in network expansion to reach remote rural areas. The improved network and reliability can provide easy connectivity and data transfer for smart agricultural applications.
