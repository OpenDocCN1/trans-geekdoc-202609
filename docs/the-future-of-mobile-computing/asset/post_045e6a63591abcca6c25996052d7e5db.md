## Biometric Authentication for Enhanced User Privacy and Security

The method of confirming a person’s identity using their distinct bodily traits or behavioral patterns is known as biometric authentication. Although there are many security and privacy benefits to biometric authentication, it is important to take into account any possible hazards, such as biometric spoofing or false positives. Strong security protocols, ongoing biometric technology development, and adherence to industry standards may all work together to reduce these dangers and provide an environment that is safe and respectful of users’ privacy when it comes to authentication in Figure 4 (Rui et al., 2018).

Distinctive and innate qualities: Face, Iris, Fingerprints, etc. The Biometric identifiers are unique to each individual and difficult to forge. They include fingerprints, iris patterns, and facial features. This uniqueness adds an additional layer of security to authentication processes.

Less Dependency on Passwords: Login without the Need for Passwords. By using biometric authentication, ordinary passwords which are prone to loss or unauthorized access—become less necessary (Ratha et al., 2001). Because they are no longer necessary to memorize complex passwords, users may now authenticate more quickly and securely.

Improved User Experience: Speed and Convenience. Using biometric authentication methods like fingerprint or face recognition, users can access their devices or accounts with ease and speed. This enhances the entire user experience without compromising security.

Two-Factor Authentication (2FA): Several Security Layers. Several authentication factors, including passwords or PINs, can be linked with biometrics to give a multi-layered security strategy. An additional layer of protection is added with this two-factor authentication (2FA).

Biometric Data Protection: Secure Storage. Biometric data is typically stored on devices in secure enclaves, making it challenging for hackers to access or replicate. To further protect the data they store, some biometric systems additionally encrypt it.
