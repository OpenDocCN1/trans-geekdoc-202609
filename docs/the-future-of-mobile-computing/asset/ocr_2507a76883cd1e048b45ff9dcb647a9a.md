Applications for edge computing can be found in many fields and industries. Smart Cities: Real-time monitoring and control of multiple urban systems, such as energy grids, waste management, and traffic management, are made possible by edge computing. Faster reaction times are made possible in urgent healthcare situations by edge computing. Manufacturing: In manufacturing facilities, edge computing facilitates process optimization, real-time quality control, and predictive maintenance. Retail: Real-time analytics for customer behavior analysis, inventory management, and personalized shopping experiences are made possible by edge computing. Edge computing is positioned to play a key role in enabling faster, more efficient, and decentralized data processing, ushering in a new era of intelligent and responsive applications, as technology develops, and the number of connected devices keeps growing.

Cloud computing and edge computing are two distinct paradigms in the field of computing that serve different purposes and address different needs. In terms of Infrastructure Location, the cloud computing infrastructure is centralized and located in data centers, typically operated by third-party service providers. Through the internet, users can access resources and services where the computing infrastructure is dispersed and located closer to the user or data source.
