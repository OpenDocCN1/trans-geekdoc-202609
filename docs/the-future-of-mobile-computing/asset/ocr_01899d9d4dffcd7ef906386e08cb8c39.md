+   - **Fairness and Bias:** In particular, biases that can lead to unfair and discriminatory results are present in machine learning algorithms, which are used in artificial intelligence. The algorithms themselves, or the data they have been trained with, may be to blame for these biases. When a machine learning algorithm is applied to an application, it may disproportionately favor some set of applicants because of their age, gender or socioeconomic status. When using biased machine learning algorithms in mobile applications, they can have a significant impact on social networking, financial services and criminal justice. Inequitable outcomes may lead to an increase of current social disparities and undermine public confidence in such frameworks.
+   - **Transparency and Accountability:** Artificial intelligence plays a major role in addressing transparency and accountability issues. Complicated machine learning algorithms often make it difficult to understand how decisions are made and what information is being handled, which creates distrust and a sense of manipulation. The decentralised structure of mobile computing, making it difficult to identify and hold people accountable for the outputs of ML applications, is also hindering efforts in addressing ethical issues.
+   - **Addressing Ethical Concerns:** The following actions can be taken to address ethical concerns about machine learning (ML) in mobile environments: gathering only the records that are required; giving clear explanations for data usage and decision-making; getting user consent and granting them control over their data; putting bias detection and mitigation strategies into practice; setting up accountability mechanisms and guaranteeing auditability; and educating the public about the moral ramifications of ML in mobile environments. By taking into account those criteria, developers, legislators and consumers can cooperate to ensure that machine learning is ethically and responsibly used within an urban environment (La Polla et al., 2013).
