+   - Collaboration: Augmented reality users can relate to both virtual and physical objects with ease thanks to voice instructions, touch gestures on devices, and real-world movements. Users interact with virtual reality primarily using motion controllers or gestures within the scene to ensure they are completely submerged in it.
+   - Entanglement: Unlike virtual reality, which achieves total isolation from the actual world, augmented reality (AR) maintains a symbiotic relationship with it, making for a less immersive experience. Because it immerses users completely, virtual reality (VR) gives them a strong sense of presence in the virtual environment.
+   - Use cases: Real-time data conception, gaming edges, and navigation apps are all areas where augmented reality is commonly employed to enhance in-person experiences. On the other hand, virtual reality is widely used in training scenarios, games, simulations, and virtual tours since these settings benefit from a completely immersive and skilful environment.
+   - Immersion: Augmented reality (AR) provides a less immersive experience since it maintains an interdependent connection with the real world, unlike virtual reality (VR), which achieves complete separation from the physical creation (H. Adams et al., 2022). Virtual reality (VR) provides users with a powerful sense of presence in the virtual world due to its ability to totally immerse them.

In conclusion, the degree of immersion and the manner in which each technology integrates with the outside world are what set AR apart from VR. Virtual reality (VR) immerses viewers in a meticulously designed virtual environment, providing an entirely immersive experience, whereas augmented reality (AR) skillfully blends virtual aspects into the actual world. The particular use case, the expected degree of immersion, and the amount of user interaction will determine which mix of these technologies works best.
