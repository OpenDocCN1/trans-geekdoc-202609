### Foldable Displays

A type of display technology called “foldable displays” can be bent or folded without damaging the display or negatively impacting its performance. Typically, these screens are employed in electronic devices like laptops, tablets, and smartphones to give users a more adaptable and varied experience (Kingslin et al., 2023). OLED (Organic Light Emitting Diode) technology, which is flexible and does not require a backlight like LCD (Liquid Crystal Display) screens do, is the key technology that makes foldable displays possible. OLEDs are composed of organic compounds that emit light when an electric current is applied (Back et al., 2023).

The following are a few products related to foldable displays:

- Samsung Galaxy Fold and Z Fold Series
- Huawei Mate X
- Motorola Razr
- Microsoft Surface Duo
- Developments in Foldable Technology

The advantage of foldable displays is that their unfolded screen area is larger than their folded profile (Huang et al., 2021). There are still problems to be fixed, like ensuring the foldable components are sturdy and long-lasting. In the future, foldable display technology might develop and expand much further.

### In-Display Fingerprint Scanners and Improved Device Security

Under-display or in-screen fingerprint scanners, sometimes referred to as in-display fingerprint scanners, are a type of biometric authentication technology that is built into the display of a device, like laptops or smartphones (Yeh et al., 2023). The introduction of this technology aims to improve device security in a more seamless and practical manner, as shown in Figure 2.

- **Biometric Authentication: Fingerprint Recognition.** In-display fingerprint scanners use each user’s unique fingerprint patterns to authenticate and unlock a device. This biometric authentication solution is more convenient and often faster than typing in a password or PIN (Ni et al., 2023).
- **Seamless Integration: Integration into the Display.** Unlike traditional fingerprint scanners, which are often located on a device’s front or back, in-display fingerprint scanners are embedded beneath the screen of a smartphone. This design enables more efficient and aesthetically pleasing design.
