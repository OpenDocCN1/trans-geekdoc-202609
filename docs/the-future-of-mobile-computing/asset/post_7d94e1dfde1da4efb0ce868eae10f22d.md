
## Market Constraints
Low demand for processed goods is a result of consumers in developing nations having limited purchasing power. As a result, agribusiness's profitability is constrained. Agribusiness development is further hampered by the lack of large enough processing companies to sustain local producers or suppliers of ingredients, packaging, and processing equipment.

## Limited Availability of Inputs
The agricultural and business sectors are not very profitable due to high raw material costs in both farming and processing. Since local intermediary goods suppliers cannot compete with imports in terms of both price and quality, imports are frequently the source of inputs needed to add value to primary products.

## Environmental Impact
Agriculture has had a huge environmental impact, which is why we need to evaluate it and work to make it as sustainable as we can for future generations. Agriculture encompasses the raising of animals, the cultivation of plants, and any ancillary activity. Since humans have lived there for centuries, many animals have evolved to live on farms as a natural consequence. Across the world, agricultural practices vary according to the species that are available and the environmental conditions.

Depending on the environment we have positive impact and negative impact on agriculture.

Positive Effects:
- Reduce carbon dioxide (CO2) levels in the atmosphere by storing carbon (C) in soils.
- Improve air quality

Negative Effects:
Negative effects of agriculture include water pollution and soil erosion. Through the atmospheric release of greenhouse gases, it can also exacerbate climate change.
- Changes to the hydrological cycle.
- Soil quality loss

Around the world, war, environmental “fickleness,” and other natural or man-made factors all interfere with agriculture. Air pollution, deforestation, and the use of fertilizers and pesticides are just a few ways that agriculture affects the environment. Additionally, agriculture can benefit the environment by lowering air pollution and giving some wild species a habitat rich in resources. An extreme illustration of the consequences of unsustainable agriculture is the Aral Sea. They caused a whole landlocked sea to evaporate.

## Technological Gaps in Traditional Farming
Significant technological gaps frequently exist in traditional farming methods, impeding their productivity, sustainability, and efficiency. The limited application of sophisticated machinery and smart agriculture (Abbasi et al., 2022) technologies is one obvious gap. This results in inefficient resource utilization in addition to adding to the workload. Another technological shortcoming in traditional farming is the lack of access to real-time data and information. Using sensors, satellite imagery, and weather forecasts, data-driven decision-making is essential to modern agriculture.

## Mobile Sensors and IoT in farming
A sensor is an apparatus that detects a physical quantity and converts it into a signal that can be read and used by other apparatuses. These days, a huge range of sensors are accessible, and many well-known smartphones already have a number of sensors installed, including gyroscopes, barometers, accelerometers, light sensors, temperature sensors, and GPS.

Smartphones are equipped with three different types of sensors: environmental, motion, and location sensors. Motion sensors, which make up the first category, monitor rotational and acceleration forces. This includes the ambient air temperature measured by a thermometer, the pressure measured by a barometer, and the light level measured by a photometer. The third type of sensors, known as position sensors, measure the actual position of the gadget. These sensors include orientation sensors, GPS, and magnetometers.
