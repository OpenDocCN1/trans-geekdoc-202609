# CONCLUSION

In summary, we developed a Ti3C2Tx based multivariable sensor for selective VOC detection. The snesor was fabricated by depositing a thin film of Ti3C2Tx on the surface of an IDE. Eight representative parameters of the proposed sensor were selected as VOC characteristics and the multi-parameters sensing responses create a unique fingerprint for each VOC. The multivariable sensor showed a high data dimensionality in PCA. Correct rates of 90.9% and 90% for the identification was achieved for oxygenated VOCs and VOCs mixtures in LDA, respectively. The ethanol concentration estimation accuracy is up to 93.4% based on the proposed sensor. The high level of identification and concentration prediction truly shows the potential of MXene based multivariable sensors for detection of VOCs of interest in the presence of known and unknown interferences. We anticipate that the strategy to solve the cross-sensitivity of gas sensors of this work could be easily adapted to other gas sensors and implemented in a range of emerging applications, including agriculture, pollution monitoring, food quality assurance, and medical diagnosis.
