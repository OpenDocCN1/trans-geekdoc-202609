```
\hat{μ} = arg_{μ∈SO(3)} max \prod_{i=1}^{n} \frac{1}{\sqrt{(2π)^3}} exp\left( -\frac{1}{2}\|μ(x_i)\|^2\right) .
```

After taking the logarithm of the above formula, we can get the mean of the maximum likelihood estimate:

```
\hat{μ} = arg_{μ∈SO(3)} max \sum_{i=1}^{n} \|Φ (\log (μ^{-1}x_i))\|^2
```

where ||Φ (log (μ^{-1}x_i))|| is the Riemannian distance from μ to x. Thus, the maximum likelihood estimate of the mean is also the point at which the square of the geodesic distance is the smallest of all the sample points, i.e., the inner mean value of SO(3).

Similar to SO(2), we assume that the variance is sufficiently small, i.e., λ ≲ 2π, where λ is the eigenvalue of Σ. Using the same assumption as in the previous section, the maximum likelihood estimate of the approximate variance is obtained:

```
\widehat{\sum} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} Φ (\log (\hat{μ}^{-1}x_i)) Φ (\log (\hat{μ}^{-1}x_i))^T .
```
