When sampling $n$-dimensional images, the coordinates of the sample points $(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n)$ are rational numbers (satisfying the sampling theorem). In other words, we map the points $(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n)$ of the $n$-dimensional space to the $n$-1-dimensional space. The corresponding points are constructed

```
$$egin{equation*}\begin{pmatrix} i_1 & i_2 & \ldots & i_n \\ j_1 & j_2 & \ldots & j_n \end{pmatrix}\end{equation*}
```

These points are mapped to

```
$$egin{equation*}\begin{pmatrix} \frac{i_1}{j_1} + \lambda \cdot \frac{i_n}{j_n} \cdot \cos \alpha, & \frac{i_2}{j_2} + \lambda \cdot \frac{i_n}{j_n} \cdot \sin \alpha, & \ldots, & \frac{i_{n-1}}{j_{n-1}} \end{pmatrix}\end{equation*}
```

where $\lambda > 0$ is the scaling factor and $\alpha$ satisfies the condition that $\tan \alpha$ is an irrational number, which ensures that the map is a single mapping (different points in the $n$-dimensional space are also mapped to different points in the $n$-1-dimensional space). In this way, an $n$-dimensional image can be reconstructed in the $n$-1-dimensional space. The selection of the above $\alpha$ ensures that the mapping is a single mapping. The reason is as follows: if two different points

```
$$egin{equation*}\begin{pmatrix} \frac{i_1}{j_1}, & \frac{i_2}{j_2}, & \ldots, & \frac{i_n}{j_n} \end{pmatrix} \quad \text{and} \quad \begin{pmatrix} \frac{p_1}{q_1}, & \frac{p_2}{q_2}, & \ldots, & \frac{p_n}{q_n} \end{pmatrix}\end{equation*}
```

are mapped to the same point in the $n$-1-dimensional space, according to the definition of the mapping, for $2 < k < n$, we have

```
$$egin{equation*}\frac{i_k}{j_k} = \frac{p_n}{q_n}\end{equation*}
```

and

```
$$egin{equation*}\frac{i_1}{j_1} + \lambda \cdot \frac{i_n}{j_n} \cdot \cos \alpha = \frac{p_1}{q_1} + \lambda \cdot \frac{p_n}{q_n} \cdot \cos \alpha \\\frac{i_2}{j_2} + \lambda \cdot \frac{i_n}{j_n} \cdot \sin \alpha = \frac{p_2}{q_2} + \lambda \cdot \frac{p_n}{q_n} \cdot \sin \alpha\end{equation*}
```

Then, explain the case for $k = 1, 2$; if $i_1/j_1 \neq p_1/q_1$, then we have

```
$$egin{equation*}\tan \alpha = \frac{\frac{i_2}{j_2} - \frac{p_2}{q_2}}{\frac{i_1}{j_1} - \frac{p_1}{q_1}}\end{equation*}
```

This is contradictory to $\tan \alpha$, so $i_1/j_1 = p_1/q_1$, which also explains $i_2/j_2 = p_2/q_2$, so that points $(i_1/j_1, i_2/j_2, \dots, i_n/j_n)$ and $(p_1/q_1, p_2/q_2, \dots, p_n/q_n)$ are certain.

```
$$egin{equation*}f(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n) = F(x_1 + \lambda x_n \cos \alpha, x_2 + \lambda x_n \sin \alpha, x_3 \dots, x_{n-1})\end{equation*}
```
