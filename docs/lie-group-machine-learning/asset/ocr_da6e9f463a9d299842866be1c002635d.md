# **Algorithm 14.10**: Procedure Homology boundary division

**Input**: The number of variables; $\theta$: boundary threshold;

**Output**: The boundary of the datum;

Initialise:

+   - for $i=0,1\ldots q$
  	* $\sum \lambda_{i} = 1$;
  			(14.9)
- for $i=0,1\ldots q$
  	* we choose a set of $a^{i}$ that can compose another set;
  		* e.g., $e^{1}=a^{1}-a^{0}, e^{2}=a^{2}-a^{0}, \ldots, e^{q}=a^{q}-a^{0}$. (14.10)
- A set of $e^{i}$ must be independent of linearity;
- Begin
  	* $x_{q}=g_{1}s_{1}^{q}+g_{2}s_{2}^{q}+\cdots g_{aq}s_{aq}^{q}$; $y_{q}=h_{1}s_{1}^{q}+h_{2}s_{2}^{q}+\ldots h_{aq}s_{aq}^{q}$;
  	* $Y=\lambda_{0}e^{0}+\lambda_{1}e^{1}+\ldots \lambda_{q}e^{q}$; in this expression, $y$ must satisfy the conditions of (14.1) and (14.10);
- then
  	* $\partial s^{q}=\sum_{i=0}^{q}(-1)a^{0} \ldots a^{i} \ldots a^{q}$ (14.11)
	* $f(y)=f(\sum_{i=0}^{q} \lambda_{i} e^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{q} \lambda_{i} f(e^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{q} \lambda_{i} \nu(e^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{q} \lambda_{i} a^{i}=x$ (14.12)
- This expression defines the line expansion.
- It can maintain the inflexibility of the centre of gravity
- End Procedure HBDA

# **Theorem 14.2**. The required space complexity of the HBDA algorithm is O(n3), while the space complexity is O(n2).

> Proof. It is assumed that a set of points satisfying the requirements of the algorithm is selected in Euclidean space. Let $g^{q}=\left(a^{0}, a^{1}, \ldots a^{q}\right)$ and $\mathbb{I}^{q}=\left(b^{0}, b^{1}, \ldots b^{q}\right)$ be two-dimension simplices in Euclidean space $E^{m}$ and $E^{n}$ that satisfy $v: a^{i} \rightarrow b^{i}, i=0,1, \ldots q$ is the natural one-to-one correspondence of their vertices.

According to (14.10), the time complexity of the first step of initialisation is O(n), and the space complexity is O (n).

According to the second step of initialisation, we can determine that the time com-plexity is O(n) and the space complexity is O(n).
