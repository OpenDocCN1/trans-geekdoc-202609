### Theorem 1.7. Two complex semisimple Lie algebras have isomorphism if and only if their Dynkin diagrams are the same.

Proof. Let the Dynkin diagrams of the complex semisimple Lie algebras $\mathbf{g}, \mathbf{g}^{\prime}$ be the same, i.e., $\mathbf{g}, \mathbf{g}^{\prime}$ have Cartan sub-algebras $\eta, \eta^{\prime}$; root systems $\Delta, \Delta^{\prime}$; and simple root systems $\Pi=\left\{a_{1}, a_{2}, \ldots, a_{n}\right\}, \Pi^{\prime}=\left\{a_{1}^{\prime}, a_{2}^{\prime}, \ldots, a_{n}^{\prime}\right\}$, respectively. Further, there is a mapping of $\Pi$ to $\Pi^{\prime}$ satisfying

$$
\varphi\left(a_{i}\right)=a_{i}^{\prime}, 1 \leq \mathrm{i} \leq n, \quad (1.18)
$$
$$
\frac{2\left(\alpha_{i}, \alpha_{j}\right)}{\left(\alpha_{i}, \alpha_{i}\right)}=\frac{2\left(\alpha_{i}^{\prime}, \alpha_{j}^{\prime}\right)}{\left(\alpha_{i}^{\prime}, \alpha_{i}^{\prime}\right)}, 1 \leq \mathrm{i}, j \leq n. \quad (1.19)
$$

Since $\Pi, \Pi^{\prime}$ are the bases of $\eta_{R}, \eta_{R}^{\prime}$, respectively, $\Phi$ can be exploited for the linear isomorphism of $\eta_{R}$ to $\eta_{R}^{\prime}$. Let $W, W^{\prime}$ be the Weyl groups of $\mathbf{g}, \mathbf{g}^{\prime}$, respectively; hence, from (1.18), we know that $r_{a_{i}}, r_{a_{i}}^{\prime}$ have the same matrix under the base of $\Pi, \Pi^{\prime}$, i.e.,

$$
M_{\left[\alpha_{1}, \alpha_{2}, \ldots, \alpha_{n}\right] r_{a_{i}}} = M_{\left[\alpha_{1}^{\prime}, \alpha_{2}^{\prime}, \ldots, \alpha_{n}^{\prime}\right] r_{a_{i}}^{\prime}}, \quad (1.20)
$$

while $\left\{r_{a_{i}}\mid 1 \leq i \leq n\right\}$, $\left\{r_{a_{i}}^{\prime} \mid 1 \leq i \leq n\right\}$ are generated groups of $\mathbf{W}, \mathbf{W}^{\prime}$, respectively; hence, there is an isomorphism mapping $\Phi^{*}$ of $\mathbf{W}$ to $\mathbf{W}^{\prime}$

$$
\varphi_{*}(w)=\varphi w \varphi^{-1}=w^{\prime}, \quad \forall w \in W \quad (1.21)
$$

Suppose that $\alpha \in \Delta$, and there exists $w \in W, \alpha_i \in \Pi_i$, such that $w(\alpha) = \alpha_i$; hence,

$$
\varphi(\alpha) = \varphi(w^{-1}(\alpha_i)) = \varphi w^{-1} \varphi^{-1}(\varphi(\alpha_i)) = (w^\prime)^{-1}\alpha_i^\prime \in \Delta^\prime
$$

and $\varphi(\Delta) \subseteq \Delta^\prime$. Similarly, $\varphi^{-1}(\Delta^\prime) \subseteq \Delta$, so $\varphi(\Delta) = \Delta^\prime$.

By Theorem 1.6, we know that $\Phi$ can be extended for $\mathfrak{g}$ to $\mathfrak{g}^\prime$ of Lie algebra isomorphism, i.e., isomorphism of $\mathfrak{g}$ and $\mathfrak{g}^\prime$.

By contrast, let $\Phi$ be an isomorphism of complex semisimple Lie algebras from $\mathfrak{g}$ to $\mathfrak{g}^\prime$; then,

$$
(\Phi(x), \Phi(y))^\prime = (x, y), \forall(x, y) \in \mathfrak{g}. \quad \tag{1.22}
$$

Let $\eta$ be a Cartan sub-algebra of $\mathfrak{g}$, where $\Delta$, $\Pi$ are the root system and simple root system, respectively; then, $\mathfrak{g} = \eta + \Sigma_{\alpha \in \Delta} \mathfrak{g}_\alpha$. Here, $\eta^\prime = \Phi(\eta)$ is a Cartan sub-algebra of $\mathfrak{g}^\prime$, and $\mathfrak{g}^\prime = \eta^\prime + \Sigma_{\alpha^\prime \in \Delta^\prime} \mathfrak{g}_\alpha^{\prime}$.

For $h \in \eta$, $e_\alpha \in \mathfrak{g}_\alpha$, we have

$$
\varphi([h, e_\alpha]) = (\alpha, h)\varphi(e_\alpha),
$$
$$
[\varphi(h), \varphi(e_\alpha)] = \varphi([h, e_\alpha]).
$$

Hence, $\Phi(\mathfrak{g}_\alpha) = \mathfrak{g}_{\Phi(\alpha)}$, $\varphi(\Delta) = \Delta^\prime$. Further, $\varphi(\Pi) = \Pi^\prime$ is a simple root system of $\Phi(\eta_R)$ of a certain order, and from (1.21), we know that the Dynkin diagrams of $\Pi$ and $\Pi^\prime$ are the same.
