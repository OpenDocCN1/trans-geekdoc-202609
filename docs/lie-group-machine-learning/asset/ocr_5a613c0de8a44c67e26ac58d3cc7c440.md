# Theorem 17.6. A linear transformation of symplectic space $S$ is a necessary and sufficient condition for symplectic transformation. It is

$$S^{T} J S=J .$$

Proof. In the symplectic space $(R^{2n}, \omega)$, there is a matrix of a symplectic transform $S$ on an octyl group $\{e_1, e_2, \ldots, e_n, e_{n+1}, \ldots, e_{2n}\}$, $(R^{2n}, \omega)$ is denoted by $S$. Then,

$$e_{i} \text { and } Y=\sum_{j=1}^{2n} y^{j} e_{j} \ .$$

From $\omega(S X, S Y)=\omega(X, Y)$,

$$\left(x^{1}, \ldots, x^{2 n}\right) S^{t} J S\left(\begin{array}{c} y^{1} \\ \vdots \\ y^{2 n} \end{array}\right)=\left(x^{1}, \ldots, x^{2 n}\right) J\left(\begin{array}{c} y^{1} \\ \vdots \\ y^{2 n} \end{array}\right)$$

where $J=\left(\begin{array}{cc} 0 & I \\ -I & 0 \end{array}\right)$ is the matrix representation of the symplectic form $\omega$ under $\{e_1, e_2, \ldots, e_n, e_{n+1}, \ldots, e_{2n}\}$, and $T$ is the transpose of the matrix. Hence,

$$S^{T} J S=J$$

is the full table condition of the symplectic transformation.

End if.
