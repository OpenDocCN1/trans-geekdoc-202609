
Assume that *n* samples (individuals) are selected; *r* variables are selected for each sample, and the observed value $x_{i,j}$ of the *j* th variable of the *i* th sample is measured, with $y_i$ representing the class marking in which $x_i$ is. Then, the observed value and the corresponding tag value of *n* samples of *r* variables can be expressed as follows:

$$
G = \begin{pmatrix} x_{1,1} & \cdots & x_{1,r}, y_1 \\ \ldots & & \\ x_{l,1} & \cdots & x_{l,r}, y_l \\ x_{l+1,1} & \cdots & x_{l+1,r}, y_{l+1} \\ \cdots & & \\ x_{n,1} & \cdots & x_{n,r}, y_{n} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} X_1 \\ X_2 \\ \ldots \\ X_n \end{pmatrix} = (X_1, \ X_2, \ \cdots, \ X_n)^T .
$$

Equation (8.24) constitutes a sample data matrix in which $y_1$ to $y_l$ are the markers whose observed values are determined corresponding to $x_1$ to $x_l$. The markers from $x_{l+1}$ to $x_n$ are not determined and the corresponding $y_{l+1}$ to $y_n$ values are unknown, i.e., $l \ll n$. The unlabelled data accounts for a large proportion of the entire sample data, where the observed values of the *r* variables of the *i* th sample are vectors:

$$
X_i = (x_{i,1}, x_{i,2}, x_{i,3}, \ldots, x_{i,r}, y_i)
$$

$x_{i,j}(i = 1, \ldots, n; j = 1, \ldots, r)$ is the observed data of the *j* th index of the *i* th sample.

## 8.4.2 Semi-supervised learning algorithm based on the parameter Lie group

### 8.4.2.1 Matrix generators of the parameter Lie group

Assume that *G* is a Lie group with *r* continuous parameters $a^{\mu}$($\mu = 1, \ldots, r$) and *g* is its element, so that *r* parameters can be selected, which makes its unit element correspond to the origin of the parameter space; i.e., $e = g(0, \ldots, 0)$. This is because of the analytic nature of the Lie group:

$$
g(0, \ldots, a^{\mu}, \ldots, 0) \approx g(0, \ldots, 0) + ia^{\mu}X_{\mu}(0, \ldots, 0) \ (\text{do not sum } \mu)
$$

which gives an infinitesimally small element. The first-order approximation of $a^{\mu}(\mu = 1, \ldots, r)$ can only be calculated, where *i* is the unit imaginary number. From (8.26), $X_{\mu}$ can be obtained as follows:

$$
X_{\mu} = \lim_{a^{\mu} \to 0} \frac{g(0, \ldots, a^{\mu}, \ldots, 0) - g(0, \ldots, 0)}{ia^{\mu}} .
$$

The entire nature of the parameter Lie group can be obtained from $X_{\mu}(1 \le \mu \le r)$ defined by the unit element annexed with the quantity *r*, and $X_{\mu}$ is the generator of the parameter Lie group. If there are *r* continuous parameters in the Lie group, there are *r* generators [43].

Using the multiplication rules of the group from a generator continuously, the group elements with finite distance from the unit element can be obtained. To get the element $g(0, \dots, a^{\mu}, \dots, 0)$, let $a^{\mu} = Na^{\mu}$, where $N$ is a large positive integer and $a^{\mu}$ is a small one. Hence,

$$
g(0, \dots, a^{\mu}, \dots, 0) = (g(0, \dots, \frac{a^{\mu}}{N}, \dots, 0))^{N} = (e + i\frac{a^{\mu}}{N}X_{\mu})^{N} \text{(do not sum }\mu\text{)} = \left(e + i\frac{a^{\mu}}{N}X_{\mu}\right)^{N}
$$

Let $a^{\mu}\rightarrow 0, N\rightarrow \infty$, and use the algebraic identity

$$
\lim_{N\to\infty}\left(1+\frac{g}{N}\right)^{N} = \exp g.
$$

Obtain

$$
g(0, \dots, a^{\mu}, \dots, 0) = \exp(ia^{\mu}X_{\mu}) \text{(do not sum }\mu\text{)}
$$

which is an accurate result.

For the general element of parameter Lie group $G$, use the above results. Then, we can get

$$
g(a^{1}, \dots, a^{r}) = \exp(ia^{\mu}X_{\mu}).
$$

The infinitesimal element that is near the unit element of $G$, i.e., the infinitesimal transformation, will constitute a transformation group. Hence, let the infinitesimal element be

$$
g_{\lambda} = g(0, \dots, a^{\lambda}, \dots, 0) = e + ia^{\lambda}X_{\lambda} \text{(do not sum }\lambda\text{)} 
$$
$$
g_{\nu} = g(0, \dots, a^{\nu}, \dots, 0) = e + ia^{\nu}X_{\nu} \text{(do not sum }\nu\text{)} .
$$

After calculating the first-order approximation of $a^{\mu}(\mu = 1,\dots, r)$, we have

$$
g_{\lambda}g_{\nu} = g_{\nu}g_{\lambda} = e + i\left(a^{\lambda}X_{\lambda} + a^{\nu}X_{\nu}\right) \text{(do not sum }\lambda,\nu\text{)}.
$$

Here, (8.34) still gives the infinitesimal element of $G$.

**Theorem 8.1.** *In the parameter space ($r$ dimension) of Lie group $G$, the group element that is in the unit element neighbourhood can be generated by the following infinitesimal generators (where $a_{\rho}(\rho = 1,\dots, r)$ is a group element):*

$$
X^{(\beta)} = V_{\beta\rho}(a)\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{\rho}} = \left(\frac{\partial\phi_{\rho}(a, b)}{\partial b_{\beta}}\right)_{b=0}\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{\rho}}
$$
$$
(a = (a_{1}, a_{2}, \dots, a_{r}))
$$

*where $\phi_{i}(a, b)\equiv a_{i} + da_{i}$ is the transformation function of group parameter space (as shown in Fig 8.8), and the parameter space is an $r$-dimensional linear space. Group element $A(a)$ is just like $F(x)$, which is a function of $n$-dimensional linear space.*

**Fig. 8.8**: The change of parameter vector in parameter space.

Proof. Note that $\phi(a, 0) = a, \phi(a, \delta a) \equiv a + da$. As shown in Fig. 8.8, there are two paths from $a \rightarrow a' + da'$. One is from $a$ to $a'$ or $a' + da'$ directly, i.e.,

$$
\phi(a, b + db) = a' + da' \ . \quad (8.36)
$$

The other one passes $a'$ and then arrives at $a' + da'$. Let $a' = \phi(a, b)$. Hence, $a' + da' = \phi(a' + \delta b)$.

Apply Taylor expansion to (8.36), and write it as a component formula:

$$
\begin{align*} 
\phi_{\rho}(a', \delta b) &= \phi_{\rho}(a', 0) + \sum_{\sigma=1}^{r} \left[ \frac{\partial \phi_{\rho}(a, b)}{\partial b_{\sigma}} \right]_{b=0} \delta b_{\sigma} \\ 
&= \phi_{\rho}(a', 0) + \sum_{\sigma=1}^{r} V_{\rho\sigma}(a) \delta b_{\sigma} 
\end{align*}
$$

i.e.,

$$
da_{\rho} = \sum_{\sigma} V_{\rho\sigma}(a) \delta b_{\sigma} (\rho = 1, 2, \ldots, r)
$$

$\forall A(a) \in G$ can be viewed as the function of $r$ variables in parameter space. Further, $a \rightarrow a + da$ can induce the transformation $A(a) \rightarrow A(a) + dA(a)$ in $A(a)$.

In addition,

$$
\begin{align*} 
dA(a) &= \sum_{\rho=1}^{r} \frac{\partial A(a)}{\partial a_{\rho}} da_{\rho} \\ 
&= \sum_{\rho=1}^{r} \sum_{\sigma=1}^{r} V_{\rho\sigma}(a) \delta b_{\sigma} \frac{\partial A(a)}{\partial a_{\rho}} \\ 
&= \sum_{\rho, \sigma} \delta a^{\rho} V_{\rho\sigma}(a) \frac{\partial A(a)}{\partial a_{\sigma}} \ . 
\end{align*}
$$

Thus, the structure of the generator of the Lie group and infinitesimal operator are naturally the same. $\Box$

### 8.4.2.2 Algorithm description

Using the above theoretical analysis, we describe the semi-supervised learning algorithm based on the parameter Lie group (SSLA-PLG), as shown in Algorithm 8.5:

### Algorithm 8.5: SSLA-PLG Algorithm flow

//Algorithm function: use Lie group method for semi-supervised learning //

**Input:** Sample set $X=\left\langle x_{1}, x_{2}, \ldots, x_{l}, x_{l+1}, \ldots, x_{l+k} \in \mathcal{R}^{D}\right\rangle$, where $x_{1}, x_{2}, \ldots, x_{l}$ is the labelled data, i.e., there exists the set $L=\{(x_{1}, y_{1}), (x_{2}, y_{2}), \ldots, (x_{l}, y_{l})\}$, where $x_{l+1}, x_{l+2}, \ldots, x_{l+k}$ are unlabelled data ($l<<k$).
**Output:** Label $y$ of data $x$
**Process:**

**Step 1:** According to the input sample set $X=\left\langle x_{1}, x_{2}, \ldots, x_{l}, x_{l+1}, \ldots, x_{l+k} \in \mathcal{R}^{D}\right\rangle$, represent it with a parameter separately. Determine the corresponding learning system isomorphism (state) parameter Lie group and make full use of the marked data, representing the object to be learned with the parameters of Lie group. The object is required to meet the structure of the parameter Lie group.

**Step 2:** According to the algebraic structure of the parameter Lie group, the group method is used for marking.
Based on the marked data as the base point, the definition of the element generated by the parameter Lie group is

$$
\chi_{y} = \lim _{\alpha^{\mu} \to 0} \frac{g(0, \ldots, \alpha^{\mu}, \ldots, 0)-g(0, \ldots, 0)}{i \alpha^{\mu}}
$$

which calculates the generator.
Determine other unlabelled data according to the group operation rule, and if the unlabelled data can be represented by the matrix generator, the category is marked.

**Step 3:** According to the geometric structure of the parameter Lie group, the analytic manifold is used for marking.
Generate the field $U_{i}(1<i<l)$ of all labelled sample points $x_{i}$;
Analyse the neighbourhood information $U_{i}(1<i<l)$ of each labelled sample point $x_{i}$. Select each constant inner product $\mathbf{g}_{i, j}(x_{i})$ under the function of self-isomorphism on the unit point in the sample set. Calculate the value of $\mathbf{g}_{i, j}(x_{i})$;
Put the value of $\mathbf{g}_{i, j}(x_{i})$ into the following formula:

$$
d(x_{i}, x_{j})=\sqrt{\sum_{i, j=1}^{n} \mathbf{g}_{i, j}\left(x_{i}\right)\left(x_{i j}-x_{j j}\right)\left(x_{i j}-x_{j j}\right)}
$$

Calculate the distance between $x_{i}$ and $x_{j}$ in $U_{i}(1<i<l)$.
Determine the smallest value $d$ of each point $x_{i}$ corresponding to $x_{j}$, and mark $x_{j}$ as the marker that is the same as $x_{i}$.

**Step 4:** Check whether the data is marked; if not, return to Step2 and repeat. Otherwise, go to the next step, i.e., data classification. Complete the data reduction and structural learning steps.
