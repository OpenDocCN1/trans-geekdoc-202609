**Theorem 6.2.** *If $N$ is a connected Lie group, then there exists a single-connected Lie group $M$ that is the coverage group of $N$.*

*Proof.* From the theoretical knowledge of the topological coverage space, where $N$ is a connected manifold, a single-connected manifold $M$ is the coverage space of $N$; $P: M \to N$ is the covering mapping.

For a road $\mathbf{x}$: $[0, 1] \to N$ with $e_N$ as the starting point, $\mathbf{x}(0) = e_N$, the road class $[\mathbf{x}]$ to which $\mathbf{x}$ belongs is a point in $M$ and covers the mapping $P[\mathbf{x}] = \mathbf{x}(1)$.

For two roads $\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}$ in $N$ with $e_N$ as the starting point, we define $\mathbf{z}$ as $\mathbf{z}$: $[0, 1] \to N$, $\mathbf{z}(t) = \mathbf{x}(t)\mathbf{y}(t)$. We define multiplication of points in $M$ as $[\mathbf{x}][\mathbf{y}] = [\mathbf{z}]$ or $[\mathbf{x}][\mathbf{y}] = [\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}]$. This definition is significant because when $[\mathbf{x}^*] = [\mathbf{x}]$ and $[\mathbf{y}^*] = [\mathbf{y}]$, $H_1$ is the fixed-point homotopy of the road $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{x}^*$, and $H_2$ is fixed-point homotopy of the road $\mathbf{y}$ and $\mathbf{y}^*$. We define $H(s, t) = H_1(s, t)H_2(s, t)$, where $H$ is fixed-point homotopy of the road $\mathbf{w}\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{x}^*\mathbf{y}^*$, i.e., $[\mathbf{w}\mathbf{x}] = [\mathbf{x}^*\mathbf{y}^*]$. Further, $[e_N]($road class of constant road $e_N$ in $N) = e_M$ is the unit of $M$. In $M$, the inverse of point $[\mathbf{x}]$ $[\mathbf{x}]^{-1} = [\mathbf{x}^{-1}]$, where $\mathbf{x}^{-1}(t) = (\mathbf{x}(t))^{-1}$.

After defining the multiplication, $M$ is obviously a group. The covering mapping $P([\mathbf{x}][\mathbf{y}]) = P[\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}] = \mathbf{x}(1)\mathbf{y}(1) = P[\mathbf{x}]P[\mathbf{y}]$ is a group of homomorphisms.

Since $M$ is present in the open nuclei $V^*$ of the nucleus $P(V^*)$ in $N$, $M$ can be given a differential, and $M$ becomes a Lie group. Further, $M$ and $N$ are local differential isomorphism. Thus, $\Lambda(M) = \Lambda(N)$. $\square$
