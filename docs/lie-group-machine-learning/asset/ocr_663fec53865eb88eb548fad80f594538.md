$$(q^n - 1)(q^{n} - q) \cdot \cdot (q^n - q^{m-1})$$
possible choices. However, a definite $m$-dimensional subspace can have different bases.

According to the above reasoning, we can see that an $m$-dimensional subspace has $(q^{n} - 1)(q^{n} - q) \cdot \cdot (q^{n} - q^{m-1})$ different bases.

Thus, the number of $m$-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}$ is equal to
$$\frac{(q^{n} - 1)(q^{n} - q) \cdot \cdot (q^{n} - q^{m-1})}{(q^{m} - 1)(q^{m} - q) \cdot \cdot (q^{m} - q^{m-1})} = \frac{(q^{n} - 1)(q^{n-1} - 1) \cdot \cdot (q^{n-(m-1)} - 1)}{(q^{m} - 1)(q^{m-1} - 1) \cdot \cdot (q - 1)} \text{ .}$$
It is proven that the number of $m$-dimensional subspaces is equivalent to the number of $m$ orbits that can be generated (i.e., the number of operators used). In the course of learning, to distinguish each subspace, it is marked with specific real values.

**Definition 2.15.** Let $m$ and $n$ be nonnegative integers. Import tokens
