The above equation is the maximum likelihood equation of the mean of $\mathrm{SO}(2)$ under a Gaussian distribution. Hence, we have $\hat{\mu} = 1/n \sum_{i=1}^{n} \theta_i$. Because of the diversity of $\theta_i$ representations, such as $\theta_i$ can belong to the interval $[0, 2\pi)$ or belong to $[-\pi, \pi)$, which makes the formula about $\hat{\mu}$ ambiguous, we need to limit $\theta_i$ to one of the intervals in a specific problem.

For the derivation of the maximum likelihood estimate of variance, consider the following log likelihood function:

```
$$l(\sigma; \hat{\mu}, \theta_1, \ldots, \theta_n) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \log \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\pi \sigma} + \log \left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty} \exp \left(-\frac{(\theta_i - \hat{\mu} - 2k\pi)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)\right)\right)$$
```
