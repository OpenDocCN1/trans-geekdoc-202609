
## 6.3.3 Applications

In this section, we validate our multiply connected covering model through an important Lie group. The SO(4) group is an orthogonal transformation group of four-dimensional real space. It is a compact Lie group, and the Lorentz group is very close, but the parameters of the real terms are different. The SO(4) group is a typical multi–connected Lie group. By studying the relationship between the SO(4) group and its covering group, we can verify our idea of multiply connected algorithm design.

We know that the SO(4) group is a double-connected Lie group. First, the SO(4) group is expressed by the SO(4) group itself, and the relationship between the SO(4) group and the two SU(2) groups is established. Further, the group elements of the SO(4) group are grouped as two separate sets of SU(2), specifically determining the 1:2 homomorphic relationship. According to this correspondence, we can use the corresponding parameters of the SU(2) group to find the unequal irreducible analytic form of the SO(4) group.

The SO(4) group itself has six generators. Making appropriate combinations of these generators, such as:

$$
T_{1}^{(+)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(T_{23} \pm T_{14}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & \mp 1 \\ 0 & 0 & -i & 0 \\ 0 & i & 0 & 0 \\ \pm i & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}
$$

and its 1,2,3 cycle. The above equation can be expressed as a direct product form of two-dimensional matrices:

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_{1}^{(+)}=\frac{1}{2} \sigma_{2} \times \sigma_{1}, \quad & T_{2}^{(+)}=\frac{-1}{2} \sigma_{2} \times \sigma_{3}, \quad & T_{3}^{(+)}=\frac{1}{2} I_{2} \times \sigma_{2} \\
T_{1}^{(-)}=\frac{-1}{2} \sigma_{1} \times \sigma_{2}, \quad & T_{2}^{(-)}=\frac{-1}{2} \sigma_{2} \times I_{2}, \quad & T_{3}^{(-)}=\frac{1}{2} \sigma_{3} \times \sigma_{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

They are divided into two sets of generators to satisfy the SU(2) group commutation relations:

$$
\left[T_{a}^{(+)}, T_{b}^{(+)}\right]=i \sum_{c=1}^{3} \epsilon_{abc} T_{c}^{(+)}, \quad \left[T_{a}^{(+)}, T_{b}^{(-)}\right]=0.
$$

After the above formulas are transformed by $N$, the decomposition can be seen more clearly:

$$
N^{-1} T_{a}^{(+)} N=(\sigma_{a} / 2) \times I_{2}, \quad N^{-1} T_{a}^{(-)} N=I_{2} \times\left(\sigma_{a} / 2\right),
$$

where

$$
N=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ -i & 0 & 0 & -i \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & i & -i & 0 \end{pmatrix} .
$$

Thus, any element of the SO(4) group may be expressed as:

$$
\begin{align*} R &= \exp \left(-i \sum_{a < b}^{4} w_{a b} T_{a b}\right) \\
&= \exp \left\{-i \sum_{a = 1}^{3} \left(w_a^{(+)} T_a^{(+)} + w_a^{(-)} T_a^{(-)}\right)\right\} \\
&= \exp \left\{-i w^{(+)} \hat{n}^{(+)} \cdot \vec{T}^{(+)}\right\} \exp \left\{-i w^{(-)} \hat{n}^{(-)} \cdot \vec{T}^{(-)}\right\} \\
&= N \left\{u(\hat{n}^{(+)}, w^{(+)}) \times u(\hat{n}^{(-)}, w^{(-)})\right\} N^{-1}
\end{align*}
$$

where

$$
\begin{align*} \mathbf{w}_{1}^{(\pm)} &= w_{23} \pm w_{14} = w^{(\pm)} \mathbf{n}_{1}^{(\pm)}, \\
\mathbf{w}_{2}^{(\pm)} &= w_{31} \pm w_{24} = w^{(\pm)} \mathbf{n}_{2}^{(\pm)}, \quad w^{(\pm)} = \left\{\sum_{a = 1}^{3} \left(w_a^{(\pm)}\right)^2\right\}^{1/2} \\
\mathbf{w}_{3}^{(\pm)} &= w_{12} \pm w_{34} = w^{(\pm)} \mathbf{n}_{3}^{(\pm)}.
\end{align*}
$$

It can be seen that the matrix is clearly expressed as the product of two two-dimensional unitary matrices $u$. If the signs of the two $A$ matrices are simultaneously changed, the $B$ matrix remains unchanged. Thus, the preceding formula gives a two-to-two correspondence between the SO(4) group element and two SU(2) group direct product elements, and this correspondence relationship remains constant for the group element product. Thus, $\mathrm{SO}(4) \sim \mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{SU}(2)\prime$. The parameters of the two SU(2) groups can be chosen as the group parameters of the SO(4) group to make the parameters of the group space and the group elements have a one-to-one relationship. In determining the group space of SO(4), the group space of one subgroup can be reduced by half; i.e., the range of the SO(4) group parameters is as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& 0 \leqslant w^{(+)} \leqslant 2 \pi, \quad 0 \leqslant w^{(-)} \leqslant \pi, \\
& 0 \leqslant \theta^{(+)} \leqslant \pi, \quad -\pi \leqslant \varphi^{(-)} \leqslant \pi
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\theta^{(\pm)}$ and $\varphi^{(\pm)}$ are the polar and azimuthal angles in the $\hat{n}^{(\pm)}$ direction. Since the group space of one subgroup is reduced, it is similar to the group space of the SO(3) group. The points at both ends of the diameter of the group space correspond to the same group of elements, which determines that the group space of the SO(4) group is double-connected.

Thus, the covering group of the SO(4) group can be obtained as the group $\mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{SU}(2)\prime$, i.e., the group space of the double-connected SO(4) group is divided into two pieces, and each covering component is an SU(2) group. The direct product of two SU(2) groups is the covering group of the whole SO(4) group. After selecting this set of parameters, the invariant volume element (density function of the integral) on the SO(4) group is:

$$
\begin{align*} dR &= \frac{1}{8 \pi^{4}} \sin^{2}\left(w^{(+)} / 2\right) \sin^{2}\left(w^{(-)} / 2\right) \sin \theta^{(+)} \sin \theta^{(-)} \\
& \quad \times d w^{(+)} d w^{(-)} d \theta^{(+)} d \theta^{(-)} d \varphi^{(+)} d \varphi^{(-)}.
\end{align*}
$$

The unequal irreducible representation of the SO(4) group can be expressed as two direct products of the SU(2) group unequal irreducible representations, denoted as $D^{jk}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
D^{jk}(\mathrm{SO}(4)) &= D^j(\mathrm{SU}(2)) \times D^k(\mathrm{SU}(2)^\prime), \\
D^{jk}(\hat{n}^{(+)}, W^{(+)}; \hat{n}^{(-)}, W^{(-)}) &= D^j(\hat{n}^{(+)}, W^{(+)}) \times D^k(\hat{n}^{(-)}, W^{(-)}),
\end{aligned}
$$

where $D^{jk}$ is $(2j+1)(2k+1)$-dimensional. Its row (column) indicator is co-marked with two letters, and its generator can be represented by the SU(2) group corresponding to the generation element:

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_a^{jk(+)} &= I_a^j \times I_{2k+1}, \quad I_a^{jk(-)} = I_{2j+1} \times I_a^k, \\
I_{ab}^{jk} &= \sum_{c=1}^3 \varepsilon_{abc} (I_c^{jk(+)} + I_c^{jk(-)}) = \sum_{c=1}^3 \varepsilon_{abc} (I_c^j \times I_{2k+1} + I_{2j+1} \times I_c^k) \\
I_{a}^{jk} &= I_{a}^{jk(+)} - I_{a}^{jk(-)} = I_{a}^j \times I_{2k+1} - I_{2j+1} \times I_{a}^k.
\end{aligned}
$$

The nature and representation of the multiply connected Lie group can be obtained by its covering group. In practical problems, the problem of multi-connected Lie groups is often difficult to solve directly. According to the multi-connected covering algorithm in this paper, we can find the covering group, so that the problem of the connected group is transformed into the representation of the covering group, and the problem is thus solved.

The main idea of this section is to verify the relationship between the multiply connected Lie group and its covering group. By analyzing the SO(4) group, a typical multiply connected Lie group, it can be decomposed into the direct product of two SU(2) groups. Since the SO(4) group is a four-dimensional orthogonal transformation group with $\det(A) = 1$, for the covering group of the SO(4) group, its covering factor takes the unit element. In practical problems, we often need to analyze the data set to determine its covering factor.

## 6.4 Application of the covering algorithm in molecular docking

### 6.4.1 Introduction to the molecular docking algorithm

Molecular docking is the process of recognition through geometric matching and energy matching between two or more molecules. Molecular docking is very important in drug design. In the process involving drug molecules producing pharmacological reactions, the drug molecules and target enzymes combine with each other. First, we need two molecules to approach the appropriate orientation, so that their necessary parts can mutually fit and then interact with each other. Through appropriate conformational adjustment, the process results in a stable complex conformation. The relative position and orientation of the two molecules in the complex are determined by molecular docking, and the optimal binding pattern between the molecules is found.
