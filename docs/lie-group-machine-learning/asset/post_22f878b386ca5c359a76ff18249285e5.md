$$D s_{\alpha}=\sum_{\begin{matrix}1 \leq i \leq m \\ 1 \leq \beta \leq q\end{matrix}} \Gamma_{\alpha i}^{\beta} d u^{i} \otimes s_{\beta}$$

where $\Gamma_{\alpha i}^{\beta}$ is a smooth function on $U$.

Note that

$$\omega_{\alpha}^{\beta}=\sum_{1 \leq i \leq m} \Gamma_{\alpha i}^{\beta} d u^{i} \text{ . }$$

Then,

$$D s_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta=1}^{q} \omega_{\alpha}^{\beta} \otimes s_{\beta} \text{ . }\hspace{5em}\text{(17.15)}$$

Furthermore, a matrix notation is introduced so that the computation is simplified. We use $S$ to represent the column matrices of the local frame field, and use $\omega$ to represent the matrix consisting of $\omega_{\alpha}^{\beta}$, i.e.,

$$S=\begin{pmatrix}s_{1} \\ \vdots \\ s_{q}\end{pmatrix}\text{, }\quad \omega=\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{1}^{1} & \cdots & \omega_{1}^{q} \\ \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ \omega_{q}^{1} & \cdots & \omega_{q}^{q}\end{pmatrix}\text{, }$$

Then, (17.15) can be denoted by $DS=\omega \otimes S$. The matrix $\omega$ is called a connection matrix, which depends on the selection of the local frame field.

$$A=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}^{1} & \cdots & a_{1}^{q} \\ \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ a_{q}^{1} & \cdots & a_{q}^{q}\end{pmatrix}\text{ . }$$

Here, $\det A \neq 0$.

Let the connection $D$ indicate that the square of the local frame field $S^{\prime}$ is $\omega^{\prime}$. Then, from the connection condition, we obtain

$$\begin{aligned}D S^{\prime} &= d A \otimes S + A \cdot D S \\
&= (d A + A \cdot \omega) \otimes S \\
&= (d A \cdot A^{-1} + A \cdot \omega \cdot A^{-1}) \otimes S^{\prime}\end{aligned}$$

Thus,

$$\omega^{\prime}=d A \cdot A^{-1} + A \cdot \omega \cdot A^{-1}\hspace{5em}\text{(17.16)}$$

## **Theorem 17.8.** *On any vector bundle, the connection always exists.*

Proof. Take a coordinate cover $\{U_{a}\}$ of $M$. According to the vector bundles in the local ordinary structure, it can be assumed that each $U_{a}$ has a local frame field $S_{a}$. According to the local structure of the connection, it is necessary to construct a $q \times q$ matrix $\omega_\alpha$ on each $U_\alpha$, so that they can be adapted to the transformation law, when the local frame field is changed. This completes the proof. In the connection calculation, we need to take $q$ smooth cross sections $s_\alpha$ $(1 \leq \alpha \leq q)$ on the neighbourhood $U$ on $E$, so that they are linearly independent everywhere. This $q$ cross section is called a local frame field of $E$ on $U$. In the concrete calculation, the principal component of each neighbourhood is calculated, and the local frame field can be regarded as taking a $q$ cross section on $E$. □
