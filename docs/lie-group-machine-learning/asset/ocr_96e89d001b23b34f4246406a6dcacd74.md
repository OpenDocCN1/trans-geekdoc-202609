**Theorem 2.9.** Let \(1 \leq m \leq n - 1\). For any \(X \in \mathrm{L}_0(m, n)\), define

\begin{equation} r(X)= \begin{cases} \dim X, & \text{if } X \neq \mathbb{F}_q^n \\ m+1, & \text{if } X=\mathbb{F}_q^n \end{cases} \tag{2.21} \end{equation}

Then, \(r: \mathrm{L}_0(m, n) \to \mathbb{N}\) is the *rank function* of *lattice* \(\mathrm{L}_0(m, n)\).

**Theorem 2.10.** Let \(1 \leq m \leq n - 1\). For any \(X \in \mathrm{L}_0(m, n)\), define

\begin{equation} r'(X)= \begin{cases} m+1 - \dim X, & \text{if } X \neq \mathbb{F}_q^n \\ 0, & \text{if } X=\mathbb{F}_q^n \end{cases} \tag{2.22} \end{equation}

Then, \(r': \mathrm{L}_R(m, n) \to \mathbb{N}\) is the rank function of lattice \(\mathrm{L}_R(m, n)\).

Now, the *characteristic polynomial* of \(\mathrm{L}_R(m, n)\) is given.

**Theorem 2.11.** Let \(n > m \geq 0\). Then,

\begin{equation} \chi\left(\mathrm{L}_R(m, n), t\right)= \sum_{k=m+1}^{n} N(k, n) g_k(t) . \end{equation}

where

\begin{equation} N(k, n)=|\mathbb{M}\left(k, n\right)|=\left[\begin{array}{l} n \\ k \end{array}\right]_q= \frac{\prod_{i=n-k+1}^{n}\left(q^i - 1\right)}{\prod_{i-1}^{n}\left(q^i - 1\right)} \tag{2.23} \end{equation}

is the number of \(k\)-dimensional subspaces in \(\mathbb{F}_q^n\) and \(g_n(x)\) is the Gaussian polynomial.

*Proof.* Mark \(V=\mathbb{F}_q^n\), \(L=\mathrm{L}_R(m, n)\), and \(L_0=\mathrm{L}_R\left(\mathbb{F}_q^n\right)\). For \(P \in L\), specify

\begin{equation} \mathbf{L}^P=\{Q \in \mathbf{L} \mid Q < P\}=\{Q \in \mathbf{L} \mid Q \geq P\} \end{equation}

and

\begin{equation} \mathbf{L}_0^P=\{Q \in \mathrm{L}_0 \mid Q < P\}=\{Q \in \mathrm{L}_0 \mid Q \geq P\} \ . \end{equation}

Obviously, \(\mathbf{L}^V=\mathbf{L}\). For \(P \in \mathbf{L}\), \(P \neq V\), according to Theorem 2.8, there is \(\mathbf{L}^P=\mathbf{L}_0^P\). Take any \(P \in \mathbf{L}_0\), where \(\mathbf{L}\left(\mathbf{X}_0^P, t\right)=g_{\dim P(q)}(t)\). Further, \(r': \mathrm{L}_R(m, n) \rightarrow \mathbb{N}\) defined by (2.22) is the rank function of \(\mathbf{L}=\mathrm{L}_R(m, n)\), i.e., there is a rank function \(r'\) on \(\mathbf{L}^V\), and \(\mathbf{L}^V\) is the lattice with the maximum \(\{0\}\) and the minimum \(\mathbb{F}_q^n\). Hence,

\begin{equation} \chi\left(\mathbf{L}^V, t\right)=\sum_{P \in \mathbf{L}^V} \mu(V, P) t^{r'(0) - r'(P)} \ . \end{equation}

Think of \(t\) as a given number. Here, \(f(x)\) on the left-hand side of the formula \(f(x) = \sum_{x \leq y} g(y)\mu(x, y)\) is taken as \(f(V)=\chi\left(\mathbf{L}^V, t\right)\), and the right-hand side \(g(y)\) is taken as \(g(P)=t^{r'(0) - r'(P)} = t^{m+1 - r'(P)}\). Then,

\begin{equation} t^{m+1}=g(V)=\sum_{P \in \mathbf{L}^V} \chi\left(\mathbf{L}^P, t\right)=\sum_{P \in \mathbf{L}} \chi\left(\mathbf{L}^P, t\right) \ . \end{equation}
