
If
$$\left[\overline{E}^{i}, \overline{E}_{k}^{i}\right]=\sum_{(p, q)} C_{(i, j)(k, l)}^{(p, q)} \overline{E}_{p}^{q} \quad(8.11)$$
the structural constants of Lie group GL(n) are
$$C_{(i, j)(k, l)}^{(p, q)}=\delta_{k}^{p} \delta_{q}^{l} \delta_{l}^{q}-\delta_{l}^{p} \delta_{k}^{q} \delta_{q}^{j} \quad(8.12)$$

Note that (8.10) is limited to the unit element $I$, and the multiplication table of the brackets in the tangent space $T_{I}(GL(n))$ is obtained:
$$\left[E_{i}^{j}, E_{k}^{l}\right]=\delta_{k}^{j} E_{i}^{l}-\delta_{i}^{l} E_{k}^{j}$$

Assume that $A, B \in M(n, n) \subset T_{I}(GL(n))$, and set
$$A=\sum_{i, j=1}^{n} A_{i}^{j} E_{i}^{j}, \quad B=\sum_{i, j=1}^{n} B_{i}^{j} E_{i}^{j} .$$

Then,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left[A, B\right] & =\sum_{i, j, k, l=1}^{n} A_{i}^{j} B_{k}^{l}\left[E_{i}^{j}, E_{k}^{l}\right] \\
& =\sum_{i, j, k, l=1}^{n} A_{i}^{j} B_{k}^{l}\left(\delta_{k}^{j} E_{i}^{l}-\delta_{i}^{l} E_{k}^{j}\right) \\
& =\sum_{i, j, k=1}^{n}\left(A_{i}^{k} B_{k}^{l}-B_{k}^{i} A_{k}^{l}\right) E_{i}^{j} \quad(8.13) \\
& =A \cdot B-B \cdot A
\end{aligned}
$$

The ‘·’ in (8.13) represents matrix multiplication. The set $M(n, n)$ of $n \times n$ real matrices is the tangent space at the unit element of Lie group GL(n). When it is the Lie algebra of Lie group GL(n), the parentheses $[A, B]$ of any two elements $A, B \in M(n, n)$ represent its exchange $A \cdot B-B \cdot A$. The Lie algebra of Lie group GL(n) is usually denoted by $gl(n)$.

The general linear group GL(n) can be viewed as the automorphism group of n-dimensional vector space. Assume that $V$ is n-dimensional vector space and GL(V) represents the automorphism set of vector space $V$. The automorphism of $V$ is the non-degenerate linear transformation from $V$ to itself. A base $\left\{\delta_{i}\right\}$ in $V$ is selected, and the automorphism $\alpha \in GL(V)$ accords the matrix $A=\left(a_{i}^{j}\right)$ under the base $\left\{\delta_{i}\right\}$, i.e.,
$$\alpha\left(\delta_{i}\right)=a_{i}^{j} \delta_{j}$$

Because $\alpha$ is non-degenerate, det $A \neq 0$, and the matrix of the inverse mapping $\alpha^{-1}$ of $\alpha$ under the base $\left\{\delta_{i}\right\}$ is $A^{-1}$.

If $\beta$ is another automorphism of $V$, $\alpha \circ \beta$ is also the automorphism of $V$. Thus, the combination of GL(V) for mapping constitutes a group, and the unit element of the group is the constant mapping from the vector space to itself.

Assume that the matrix $\boldsymbol{\beta}$ under the base $\{\delta_i\}$ is $B = (b^j_i)$. Then,
$$\alpha \circ \beta (\delta_i) = \alpha \left( \sum_j b^j_i \delta_j \right) = \sum_j b^j_i \alpha(\delta_j) = \sum_{j, k} b^j_i a^k_j \delta_k \ .$$

Thus, the matrix $\alpha \circ \beta$ under the base $\{\delta_i\}$ is
$$
\begin{bmatrix} 
a^1_j b^1_i & & a^1_j b^1_h \\ 
... & ... & ... \\ 
a^n_j b^n_i & & a^n_j b^n_h
\end{bmatrix} = 
\begin{bmatrix} 
a^1_i & ... & a^1_n \\ 
... & ... & ... \\ 
a^n_i & ... & a^n_n
\end{bmatrix} \cdot 
\begin{bmatrix} 
b^1_i & ... & b^1_n \\ 
... & ... & ... \\ 
b^n_i & ... & b^n_n
\end{bmatrix} = A \cdot B \ .
$$

Hence, if a base $\{\delta_i\}$ in $V$ is selected, let $\alpha \in \mathrm{GL}(V)$ accord its matrix $A \in \mathrm{GL}(n)$ under the base $\{\delta_i\}$. Then, the mapping is one-to-one and is the automorphism from group GL(V) to group GL(n). Thus, GL(V) is an $n^2$-dimensional Lie group. Its smooth structure is induced from GL(n).

## 8.3.2 Semi-supervised learning algorithm based on the linear Lie group

### 8.3.2.1 Operator generators of the linear Lie group

According to the above definition of GL($n$), using the right movement as an example, from the local point of view, the definition of the whole infinitesimal small right shift of Lie group is $g$, and $g$ is closed on the commutator and thus constitutes the Lie algebra, such that G is generated locally with the move around the left and right of the current element $g$, which can be moved by the current element $g$. The operation is generated locally. First, we introduce the following agreement: $f$ is the function defined on $G$, $g \in G$, and in $G$, we use

$$f^{\varepsilon}(x) = f(gx) \ . \tag{8.14}$$

Defining the function $f^{\varepsilon}$: from $(f^{\varepsilon})^h(x) = f^{\varepsilon}(hx) = f(g hx) = f^{gh}(x)$, infer that there is a relationship

$$(f^{\varepsilon})^h = f^{\varepsilon h} \ . \tag{8.15}$$

**Definition 8.9.** Take a cut vector $Z$ at the unit element $e$ of Lie group $G$, and use

$$X_{x}f = Z f^{X}(x \in G) \ . \tag{8.16}$$

to define the infinitesimal transformation $X$, which is called the whole infinitesimal right movement of $G$, denoted by $\mathfrak{g}$. Now, determine the explicit representation of $X$ defined in (8.14). The multiplication of the nearest neighbour of unit element $e$ of $G$ in a local coordinate system is

$$(xy)^{\mu} = \phi^{\mu}(x, y, u = 1, 2, \dots, r) \ .$$

which requires the synthesis function $\phi^{\mu}$ to be parsed. When $x$ is close to $e$, then $f^{x}(y)=f(x y)$ is meaningful to the other point $y$ of the $e$ neighbourhood, and

$$X_{x} f=Z f^{x}=\lambda^{\mu} \frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\mu}}. \quad (8.17)$$

In (8.17), $Z=\lambda^{\mu} \partial / \partial y^{\mu}$. If $x y=z$, (8.12) becomes

$$X_{x} f=\lambda^{\mu} \frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{v}}\left.\frac{\partial \phi^{v}}{\partial y^{\mu}}\right|_{y=e}. \quad (8.18)$$

To visually see that this is an infinitesimal rightward move, let $y^{\mu}=e^{\mu}+\lambda^{\mu} \delta_{t}$, and omit the item $\delta_{t}$, which is higher than one. The mapping $x \mapsto x y$ can be regarded as an infinitesimal rightward move. The corresponding change of $f$ is

$$\delta f=f(x)-f(x)=\left.\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{v}} \frac{\partial \phi^{v}(x, y)}{\partial y^{\mu}}\right|_{y=e} \lambda^{\mu} \delta_{t}. \quad (8.19)$$

Comparing (8.18) with (8.19), we can see that $X f$ represents the following: when you move right along the given direction $\lambda^{\mu}$, it corresponds to a change in the value of the unit of parameter $t$. To write (4.18) in a more convenient form, set

$$\psi_{\mu}^{\nu}=\left.\frac{\partial \phi^{\nu}}{\partial y^{\mu}}\right|_{y=e}(\mu, \nu=1, \ldots, r). \quad (8.20)$$

The function $\psi_{\mu}^{\nu}$ defined as such is the transform function of the Lie group. Using (8.20), we can write

$$X_{x} f=\lambda^{\mu} \psi_{\mu}^{\nu}(x) \frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{\nu}}$$

such that

$$X_{x}=\lambda^{\mu} \psi_{\mu}^{\nu}(x) \frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\nu}}(\mu, \nu=1, \ldots, r). \quad (8.21)$$

Here, (8.21) is the infinitesimal rightward movement defined by $Z=\lambda^{\mu} \partial / \partial y^{\mu}$, and it can be seen that the infinitesimal rightward motion is the inverse vector field. From the infinitesimal right move (8.21), we know that

$$X_{\mu}(x)=\left.\psi_{\mu}^{\nu}(x) \frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\mu}}\right|_{y=e} \frac{\partial \phi^{\nu}(x, y)}{\partial y^{\mu}} \frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\nu}} \quad (8.22)$$

is the generator of the Lie group. This generator is given in the form of an operator, called the Lie generation operator [43].

Similarly, we can define

$$X_{\mu}(y)=\left.\psi_{\mu}^{\nu}(y) \frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}\right|_{x=e} \frac{\partial \phi^{\nu}(x, y)}{\partial x^{\mu}} \frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\nu}} \quad (8.23)$$

as the operator generating element corresponding to the infinitesimal leftward motion of the Lie group. When the synthetic function of the Lie group is given, it is easy to find the operator generating element of the Lie group.

From the viewpoint of the Lie group, (8.22) and (8.23) can be regarded as the generators of the Lie group, but from the viewpoint of Lie algebra, (8.22) and (8.23) are the basis vectors of Lie algebra.

### 8.3.2.2 Algorithm description

Using the above theoretical analysis, the semi-supervised learning algorithm based on linear Lie group is described in Algorithm 8.4:

**Algorithm 8.4: SSIA-GL(n) algorithm flow**

- **algorithm function:** Semi-supervised learning with linear Lie group method.
- **Input:** Sample set $X = (x_{1}, x_{2}, \dots, x_{l}, x_{l+1}, \dots, x_{l+k} \in \mathbb{R}^{n})$, where $x_{1}, x_{2}, \dots, x_{l}$ are labelled data, and there exists a set $L = \{(x_{1},y_{1}), (x_{2},y_{2}), \dots, (x_{l},y_{l})\}$, $x_{l+1}, x_{l+2}, \dots, x_{l+k}$ are unlabelled data ($l \ll k$).
- **Output:** The label $y$ of data $x$

**Steps:**

1.  **Step 1:** According to the input sample set $X = (x_{1}, x_{2}, \dots, x_{l}, x_{l+1}, \dots, x_{l+k} \in \mathbb{R}^{n})$, represented by a matrix, find the automorphism of the GL(n) linear Lie group of the corresponding learning system using labelled data fully, representing the object that is to be studied as a linear Lie group, requiring the learning object to satisfy the linear Lie group structure.
2.  **Step 2:** According to the algebra structure of Lie group, the group method is used for labelling. Based on the marked data as the base point, find its operator generator (or the base vector of the corresponding algebra) through its infinitesimal right shift
    $$X_{\mu}(x)=\psi_{\mu}^{x}(x) \frac{\partial}{\partial x^{v}}=\left.\frac{\partial \delta^{V}(x, y)}{\partial y^{v}}\right|_{y=\epsilon} \frac{\partial}{\partial x^{v}} \ ;$$
3.  Determine other unlabelled data according to the group operation rules. If the unmarked data can be used to generate the meta-representation of the operator, it is marked by the category.
4.  **Step 3:** According to the geometric structure of the linear Lie group, the analytic manifold is used for marking.
5.  A constant inner product under the action of the automorphism is taken in the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ of $X$:
    $$\langle Ad(g) x_{1}, Ad(g) x_{2}\rangle=\langle x_{1}, x_{2}\rangle, \quad x_{1}, x_{2} \in \mathfrak{g}, g \in G$$
6.  Take a set of standard orthogonal bases $\{X_i; 1\le i \le l + k\}$, $\langle X_i, X_j \rangle = \delta_{ij}$. The left shift expands $\{X_i; 1 \le i \le l + k\}$ into the left constant vector field of $G$, which is recorded as $X_i$.
    Thus, the Riemann space structure is established:
    In the Riemann space structure, the unmarked data is marked in each $T_x G$ space, with the marked data as the base point and the left (or right) translation linear transformation $dL_i$ (or $dr_x$) of $X$. The tangent space $T_x G$ is mapped to the marked sample point $x_i$.
7.  **Step 4:** Check whether all the data is marked. If not, then return to Step 2 and repeat; otherwise, go to the next step of Lie group machine learning, which is to classify the data. Complete data reduction and structural learning.
