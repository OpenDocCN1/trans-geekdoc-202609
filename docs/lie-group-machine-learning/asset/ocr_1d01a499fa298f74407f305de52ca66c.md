If
$$\left[\overline{E}^{i}, \overline{E}_{k}^{i}\right]=\sum_{(p, q)} C_{(i, j)(k, l)}^{(p, q)} \overline{E}_{p}^{q} \quad(8.11)$$

the structural constants of Lie group GL(n) are
$$C_{(i, j)(k, l)}^{(p, q)}=\delta_{k}^{p} \delta_{q}^{l} \delta_{l}^{q}-\delta_{l}^{p} \delta_{k}^{q} \delta_{q}^{j} \quad(8.12)$$

Note that (8.10) is limited to the unit element I, and the multiplication table of the brackets in the tangent space $T_{I}(GL(n))$ is obtained:
$$\left[E_{i}^{j}, E_{k}^{l}\right]=\delta_{k}^{j} E_{i}^{l}-\delta_{i}^{l} E_{k}^{j}$$

Assume that $A, B \in M(n, n) \subset T_{I}(GL(n))$, and set
$$A=\sum_{i, j=1}^{n} A_{i}^{j} E_{i}^{j}, \quad B=\sum_{i, j=1}^{n} B_{i}^{j} E_{i}^{j} .$$

Then,
$\begin{aligned}\left[A, B\right] & =\sum_{i, j, k, l=1}^{n} A_{i}^{j} B_{k}^{l}\left[E_{i}^{j}, E_{k}^{l}\right] \\ & =\sum_{i, j, k, l=1}^{n} A_{i}^{j} B_{k}^{l}\left(\delta_{k}^{j} E_{i}^{l}-\delta_{i}^{l} E_{k}^{j}\right) \\ & =\sum_{i, j, k=1}^{n}\left(A_{i}^{k} B_{k}^{l}-B_{k}^{i} A_{k}^{l}\right) E_{i}^{j} \quad(8.13) \\ & =A \cdot B-B \cdot A\end{aligned}$

The ‘·’ in (8.13) represents matrix multiplication. The set $M(n, n)$ of $n \times n$ real matrices is the tangent space at the unit element of Lie group GL(n). When it is the Lie algebra of Lie group GL(n), the parentheses $[A, B]$ of any two elements $A, B \in M(n, n)$ represent its exchange $A \cdot B-B \cdot A$. The Lie algebra of Lie group GL(n) is usually denoted by $gl(n)$.

The general linear group GL(n) can be viewed as the automorphism group of n-dimensional vector space. Assume that $V$ is n-dimensional vector space and GL(V) represents the automorphism set of vector space $V$. The automorphism of $V$ is the non-degenerate linear transformation from $V$ to itself. A base $\left\{\delta_{i}\right\}$ in $V$ is selected, and the automorphism $\alpha \in GL(V)$ accords the matrix $A=\left(a_{i}^{j}\right)$ under the base $\left\{\delta_{i}\right\}$, i.e.,
$$\alpha\left(\delta_{i}\right)=a_{i}^{j} \delta_{j}$$

Because $\alpha$ is non-degenerate, det $A \neq 0$, and the matrix of the inverse mapping $\alpha^{-1}$ of $\alpha$ under the base $\left\{\delta_{i}\right\}$ is $A^{-1}$.

If $\beta$ is another automorphism of $V$, $\alpha \circ \beta$ is also the automorphism of $V$. Thus, the combination of GL(V) for mapping constitutes a group, and the unit element of the group is the constant mapping from the vector space to itself.
