**Theorem 1.1** (Cartan–Weyl). *A complex number of G indicates that the highest weight of multiplicity of* $\Lambda_\phi$ *must be 1, and its characteristic function* $X_\phi$ *can be expressed with the highest formula as follows:*

$$X_\phi(\exp H) = \frac{\sum_{\sigma \in W} sign(\sigma) e^{2\pi i(\sigma(\Lambda_\phi + \delta), H)}}{\sum_{\sigma \in W} sign(\sigma) e^{2\pi i(\sigma(\delta), H)}}, \quad H \in \mathfrak{h} \text{ .}$$

*Thus, the necessary and sufficient condition for the equivalence of* $\phi_1$ *and* $\phi_2$ *is that the two complex irreducible terms of* G *are their highest weights, i.e.,* $\Lambda_{\phi_1}$ *is the same as* $\Lambda_{\phi_2}$*.*

*Proof.* From the above analysis, we know that to prove

$$X_\phi(\exp H) \cdot Q(\exp H) = m(\Lambda_\phi) \cdot \sum_{\sigma \in W} sign(\sigma \cdot e^{2\pi i(\sigma(\delta), H)}$$

+ there may be ‘lower’ items, $m(\Lambda_\phi) = 1$, and there cannot be any ‘lower’ item. The proof is as follows: $\ln( \int_G f(g) dg = 1/|W| \int_T f(t) |Q(t)|^2 dt)$, let $f(g) = |\chi_\phi(g)|^2$, $t = \exp H$, $H \in \mathfrak{h}$. From theorem,

$$1 = \int_G |\chi_t(g)|^2 dg = \frac{1}{|W|} \int_T \chi_\phi(t) |Q(t)|^2 dt$$

$$= \frac{1}{|W|} \left| \int_T m(\Lambda_\phi) \sum_{\sigma \in W} sign(\sigma) e^{2xi(\sigma(\Lambda_\phi + \delta), H) + \cdots} \right|^2 dt \text{ .}$$

From the point of view of $L_2(T)$, the expansion of the product of the right-hand side of the above equation is the expansion of $X_\phi(t) \cdot Q(t)$ for the standard orthogonal base $\{e^{2xi(\sigma(\Lambda_\phi + \delta), H)}\}$. Hence, the above formula can be written as

$$l = |\chi_\phi(g)|^2_{L_2(G)} = \frac{1}{|W|} |\chi_\phi(t) Q(t)|^2_{L_2(T)}$$

$$= \frac{1}{|W|} \{|m(\Lambda_\phi)|^2 \cdot |W| + |\text{may be a lower order}|^2 \} \text{ .}$$

However, the above equation is only possible if $m(\Lambda_\phi) = 1$ and there is no longer a lower order, which proves that $m(\Lambda_\phi) = 1$ and the formula in the theorem holds.

Note: It is easy to see that the above-mentioned highest weight $\Lambda_\phi$ must meet the following conditions

$$\frac{2(\Lambda, ai)}{(ai, ai)} = qi \quad q_i \text{ are non-negative integers, } \quad a \in \prod$$

If the connected Lie group G is simply connected, it can be proved that for any given non-negative term $\{q; i = 1, \ldots, k\}$, by the formula

$$\frac{2(\Lambda, ai)}{(ai, ai)} = qi \quad (i = 1, \ldots, k)$$

only the definite $\Lambda$ must be the highest weight of a complex irreducible representation of G. Thus, there exists a complex irreducible $\phi : G \to \text{GL}(V)$ such that $\Lambda = \Lambda_\phi$. \quad \square$
