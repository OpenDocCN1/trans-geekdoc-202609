and the inner mean of the total samples is

```
$$\vec{\mu} = \operatorname{argmin} \sum_{i=1}^{c} \sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}} d\left(y, y_{i j}\right) \quad .$$
```

In the above formula, $c$ represents the number of sample categories, $n_{i}$ represents the number of samples in the *i*-th category, and $y_{i j}$ represents the new sample after the $j$-th sample in the *i*-th category.

For multiple categories, the divergence between classes after projection is defined as

```
$$\begin{aligned} \hat{S}_{b} & =\sum_{i=1}^{c} n_{i} \cdot d^{2}\left(\vec{\mu}_{i}, \vec{\mu}\right) \\\n& =\sum_{i=1}^{c} n_{i} \cdot\left\|\log \left(\vec{\mu}^{-1} \vec{\mu}_{i}\right)\right\|^{2} \quad . \end{aligned}$$
```

The interclass divergence of a class of samples after projection is defined as $\hat{S}_{i}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}} d^{2}\left(y_{i j}, \vec{\mu}_{i}\right)$. Then, the sum of all $\hat{S}_{i}^{2}$ is defined as the divergence of the total samples:

```
$$\begin{aligned} \hat{S}_{w} & =\sum_{i=1}^{c} \hat{S}_{i}^{2} \\\n& =\sum_{i=1}^{c} \sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}} d^{2}\left(y_{i j}, \vec{\mu}_{i}\right) \quad . \end{aligned}$$
```

Substituting (9.28) to (9.31) into (9.27) and approximating by the first-order BCH formula, the following form is obtained:

```
$$J(v) \approx \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{c} n_{i} \cdot\left\|\log \left(\vec{\mu}_{i}\right)-\log \left(\vec{\mu}\right)\right\|^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{c} \sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\left\|\log \left(y_{i j}\right)-\log \left(\vec{\mu}_{i}\right)\right\|^{2}} \quad .$$
```

Correspondingly, the problem of the Lie–Fisher algorithm is reduced to finding a geodesic $H_{v}$, satisfying the following condition:

```
$$v^{*}=\operatorname{argmax} J(v) \quad .$$
```

# 9.4.3 Lie–Fisher discriminant analysis

The process of Lie–Fisher discriminant analysis is to find a classification geodesic on the Lie group manifold. After all the samples are projected onto the geodesic line, the set of projection points of the sample has the maximum ratio between intra-class divergence and interclass divergence.
