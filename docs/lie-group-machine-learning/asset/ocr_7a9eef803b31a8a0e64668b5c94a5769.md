**4.2.2.1 Symmetric linear transformation learning algorithm for quantum group**

On the two-dimensional quantum hyperplane, it is assumed that the learning problem in the learning space is represented by ‘coordinates’ $(x,y)$, where $x$ represents an instance in the instance space in the learning space and $y$ represents an example in the sample space, satisfying the exchange relation

$$xy = qyx \quad (q \in C) \qquad (4.1)$$

where $q$ is the adjustment factor and $C$ is the set of adjustment factors. In addition, there is a related covariant differential calculation of $(x,y)$, i.e., the ‘form’ $(\xi, \eta)$, which satisfies

$$\xi\eta = -q^{-1}\eta\xi \ , \quad \xi^2 = 0 \ , \quad \eta^2 = 0 \ . \qquad (4.2)$$

When $q=1$, from (4.1), it can be seen that $x$ and $y$ are symmetric, and from (4.2), it can be seen that $\xi$ and $\eta$ are symmetric. Further, $(\xi,\eta)$ can be interpreted as the differential of $(x,y)$, i.e., to reduce the number of examples in the learning space processing. Let

$$\varphi = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}, \quad \psi = d\varphi = \begin{pmatrix} \xi \\ \eta \end{pmatrix}. \qquad (4.3)$$

The ‘coordinates’ $(x,y)$ on the quantum hyperplane are linearly transformed with the ‘form’ $(\xi,\eta)$ at the same time,

$$\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \alpha & \beta \\ \gamma & \delta \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}, \quad \begin{pmatrix} \xi' \\ \eta' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \alpha & \beta \\ \gamma & \delta \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} \xi \\ \eta \end{pmatrix} \qquad (4.4)$$

where $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)$ and $(x,y,\xi,\eta)$ are interchangeable, and if the requirements of $(x',y')$ and $(\xi',\eta')$ are satisfied with the same relationship between $(x,y)$ and $(\xi,\eta)$, then $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)$ must have the following relationship

$$\alpha\beta = q\beta\alpha \ , \quad \alpha\gamma = q\gamma\alpha \ , \quad \beta\gamma = q\gamma\beta \ , \quad \gamma\delta = q\delta\gamma \ , \quad \beta\delta = q\delta\beta \qquad (4.5)$$

$$\alpha\delta - \delta\alpha = (q-q^{-1})\beta\gamma \ .$$

Let algebra

$$A = C \langle \alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta \rangle / \sim \ . \qquad (4.6)$$

It is the associative algebra generated by $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)$, and it satisfies (4.5), i.e.,

$$T = (T_{ij}^{i}) = \begin{pmatrix} \alpha & \beta \\ \gamma & \delta \end{pmatrix} \in GL_q(2) \ . \qquad (4.7)$$

The above transformation will form a quantum super-symmetric linear transformation and yield the quantum group $GL_q(2)$, whose centre is

$$c = \det_q T = \alpha \delta - q\beta\gamma = \delta\alpha - q^{-1}\beta\gamma \ . \qquad (4.8)$$

If $\det_q T=1$ is required, it is called quantum group $SL_q(2)$.
