X, Y, and Z axes has the following matrix form on $R^{3}$

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \cos \varphi & -\sin \varphi \\ 0 & \sin \varphi & \cos \varphi \end{bmatrix} \text{, } \begin{bmatrix} \cos \theta & 0 & \sin \theta \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ -\sin \theta & 0 & \cos \theta \end{bmatrix} \text{, } \begin{bmatrix} \cos \psi & -\sin \psi & 0 \\ \sin \psi & \cos \psi & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

where $\varphi, \theta$, and $\psi$ represent the rotation angles of the X, Y, and Z axes, respectively, corresponding to $\varphi$, $\theta$, and $\psi$ in Fig. 4.1, and the corresponding infinitesimal generation is

$$ e_{1} = \begin{bmatrix} q & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \text{, } \quad e_{2} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & q & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \text{, } \quad e_{3} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & q \end{bmatrix} \text{.} $$

The translation subgroup has no representation of the matrix on $R^{3}$, but is defined in the vector space of the differential function on $R^{3}$, expressed as a displacement operator

$$ \begin{split} T_{x}(\xi) &: f(X, Y, Z) \rightarrow f(X + \xi, Y, Z) \\ T_{y}(\eta) &: f(X, Y, Z) \rightarrow f(X, Y + \eta, Z) \\ T_{z}(\zeta) &: f(X, Y, Z) \rightarrow f(X, Y, Z + \zeta) \end{split} $$

where $\xi$, $\eta$, and $\zeta$ represent the translational directions on the X, Y, and Z axes, respectively, and the related infinitesimal generators are denoted as partial differential operators

$$ e_{4} = \frac{\partial}{\partial X} \text{, } \quad e_{5} = \frac{\partial}{\partial Y} \text{, } \quad e_{6} = \frac{\partial}{\partial Z} \text{.} $$

With these six infinitesimal generators, the infinitesimal transforms e of any three-dimensional quantum group can be expressed as

$$ e = \sum_{i=1}^{6} \nu_{i}e_{i} \quad \text{(4.14)} $$

where $\nu_{i} (i = 1, \dots, 6)$ is a coefficient of $e_{i}$.
