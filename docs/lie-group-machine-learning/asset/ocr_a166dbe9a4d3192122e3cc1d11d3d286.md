(5)  Return

$$\hat{f}(x_{q}) \leftarrow \arg \max _{c \in C} \sum_{i=1}^{K} \delta\left(c, f\left(x_{i}\right)\right)$$

where $\hat{f}\left(x_{q}\right)$ is the estimation of the class $f\left(x_{q}\right)$ of the sample $x_{q}$. An improvement of the FMKNN algorithm is the weighted contribution to the $K$ nearest neighbours, and the larger values are assigned to the nearest neighbours (the closer the sample distance, the higher the similarity, and the greater the contribution). It is usually weighted by the reciprocal of the distance from each nearest neighbour:

```
$$\begin{gathered}\hat{f}\left(x_{q}\right) \leftarrow \arg \max _{c \in C} \sum_{i=1}^{K} w_{i} \delta\left(c, f\left(x_{i}\right)\right), \\ w_{i}=\frac{1}{d_{F i n s l e r}\left(x_{i}, x_{q}\right)} .\end{gathered} \tag{13.21}$$
```

After replacing step (5) of the FMKNN algorithm with (13.21), we can reduce the influence of noise data on the performance of the algorithm, to improve the robustness of the algorithm.
