
## 9.2.1 Gaussian distribution of $R^+$

For the Lie group $R^+$ composed by a positive real number in multiplication, the local coordinates are given by the logarithm, and the solution of the thermal equation is a logarithmic density function:

$p(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma x}\exp\left(-\frac{(\log(x)-\log(\mu))^2}{2\sigma^2}\right).$ (9.6)

Given the independent and identical logarithmic distribution of the sample $x_1, \dots, x_n \in R^+$, the maximum likelihood estimates of their mean and variance are

$\hat{\mu} = \left(\prod_{i=1}^{n} x_i\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}$ , $\quad \hat{\sigma}^2 = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\log(x_i)-\log(\hat{\mu})\right)^2$

where $\hat{\mu}$ is the geometric mean, i.e., the point where the sum of squares of the distance of the geodesic in $R$ is the smallest, i.e., the smallest point of $\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(\mu^{-1}x_i)^2$.

### 9.2.2 Gaussian distribution of SO(2)

The parameter of SO(2) is the rotation angle $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$. Since SO(2) is isomorphic to $S^1$ through the mapping $\theta \mapsto e^{i\theta}$, the mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma$ of the Gaussian distribution on SO(2) are given by

$p(\theta) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{(\theta-\mu-2k\pi)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$. (9.7)

The above equation is the solution to the thermal equation in the $[-\pi, \pi]$ cycle, also known as the ‘wrapped Gaussian’, as shown in Figure 9.4.

Now, we derive the maximum likelihood estimates of the mean and variance. Given the independent set of samples $\theta_i \in [0, 2\pi), i = 1, \dots, n$ distributed in the same form as in (9.7), we assume that $\sigma = 1$. Then, the maximum likelihood estimate for the mean is:

$\hat{\mu} = \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\mu \in [0, 2\pi)} \prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{(\theta_i-\mu-2k\pi)^2}{2}\right)$ (9.8)

Note that the two exponential functions are even functions, and their derivative functions are singular; for fixed $k$, we have

$\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\exp\left(-\frac{(\theta_i-\mu-2k\pi)^2}{2}\right) = -\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\exp\left(-\frac{(\theta_i-\mu+2k\pi)^2}{2}\right)$

Hence, when the form of (9.8) is simply reduced to $k = 0$, the maximisation problem becomes

$\hat{\mu} = \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\mu \in [0, 2\pi)} \prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{(\theta_i-\mu)^2}{2}\right)$. (9.9)

The above equation is the maximum likelihood equation of the mean of $\mathrm{SO}(2)$ under a Gaussian distribution. Hence, we have $\hat{\mu} = 1/n \sum_{i=1}^{n} \theta_i$. Because of the diversity of $\theta_i$ representations, such as $\theta_i$ can belong to the interval $[0, 2\pi)$ or belong to $[-\pi, \pi)$, which makes the formula about $\hat{\mu}$ ambiguous, we need to limit $\theta_i$ to one of the intervals in a specific problem.

For the derivation of the maximum likelihood estimate of variance, consider the following log likelihood function:

$l(\sigma; \hat{\mu}, \theta_1, \ldots, \theta_n) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \log \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\pi \sigma} + \log \left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty} \exp \left(-\frac{(\theta_i - \hat{\mu} - 2k\pi)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)\right)\right)$

The following is obtained by the differential $\sigma$:

$\frac{\partial \mathrm{l}}{\partial \sigma} = -\frac{n}{\sigma} + \frac{1}{\sigma^3} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left( \theta_i - \tilde{\mu} \right)^2 + \frac{1}{\sigma^3} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left( \frac{\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty} \left(2 k \pi \right)^2 \exp \left(-\frac{\left(\theta_i - \tilde{\mu} - 2 k \pi \right)^2}{2 \sigma^2} \right)}{\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty} \exp \left(-\frac{\left(\theta_i - \tilde{\mu} - 2 k \pi \right)^2}{2 \sigma^2} \right)} \right)$

The third term on the right-hand side of the above equation converges, but it does not produce a closed form solution. According to [52], we can see the wrapped Gaussian density function, i.e., this is consistent with the angle constraints of the image processing problem when $\sigma^2 \leq 2 \pi$ approximates the sum term of $k=0$ in (9.7). Hence, we keep only the term $k=0$. We then have

$\frac{\partial \mathrm{l}}{\partial \sigma} = -\frac{n}{\sigma} + \frac{1}{\sigma^3} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left( \theta_i - \tilde{\mu} \right)^2.$

Let the above equation be equal to 0; solving $\sigma$, we can get the maximum likelihood estimate of the approximate variance:

$\hat{\sigma}^2 = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left( \theta_i - \tilde{\mu} \right)^2$ (9.10)

### 9.2.3 Gaussian distribution of SO(3)

Similar to SO(2), we use a logarithmic mapping on S3 to define a wrapped Gaussian distribution with mean $\mu$. It should be noted that the density function is not necessarily a solution to the thermal equation on SO(3). Let $\mu(x) = \Phi(\log(\mu^{-1}x)) \in R^3$, where $\Phi: \mathrm{SO}(3) \to R^3$, $\Phi(A_{ij}) = \nu$ form a standard isomorphism. Let $\bar{\mu}(x) = \mu(x)/\|\mu(x)\|$; according to (9.13), the wrapped Gaussian density function of SO(3) is

$p(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{(2 \pi)^3 |\Sigma|}} \sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty} \exp \left(-\frac{1}{2} \left( \mu(x) - 2 k \pi \bar{\mu}(x) \right)^\mathrm{T} \Sigma^{-1} \left( \mu(x) - 2 k \pi \bar{\mu}(x) \right) \right)$ (9.11)

The covariance structure is defined as the square form on Lie algebra SO(3), represented as a $3 \times 3$ matrix $\Sigma$.

Given the independent set of samples $x_1, \ldots, x_n \in \mathrm{SO}(3)$ distributed in the same form as in (9.11), the maximum likelihood estimates for the mean and variance are derived below. For the mean, without loss of generality, it can be assumed that the covariance is a unit matrix. The joint density is given by the density

$p(\mu; x_1, \ldots, x_n) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} \frac{1}{\sqrt{(2 \pi)^3}} \sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty} \exp \left(-\frac{1}{2} \| \mu(x_i) - 2 k \pi \bar{\mu}(x_i) \|^2 \right)$ (9.12)

Note that the geodesic of SO(3) is isomorphic to SO(2), and the density function (9.11) constrains a geodesic to the wrapped Gaussian on SO(2). It is now possible to use the same assumptions as in the previous section to denote the derivative function of the elimination on the sum formula corresponding term. Maximising p (μ; x1, . . . , xn) is equivalent to finding \hat{μ} in the following formula:

$\hat{\mu} = \arg_{\mu\in\mathrm{SO}(3)} \max \prod_{i=1}^{n} \frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^3}} \exp\left( -\frac{1}{2}\|\mu(x_i)\|^2\right)$

After taking the logarithm of the above formula, we can get the mean of the maximum likelihood estimate:

$\hat{\mu} = \arg_{\mu\in\mathrm{SO}(3)} \max \sum_{i=1}^{n} \|\Phi (\log (\mu^{-1}x_i))\|^2$

where $\|\Phi (\log (\mu^{-1}x_i))\|$ is the Riemannian distance from $\mu$ to $x$. Thus, the maximum likelihood estimate of the mean is also the point at which the square of the geodesic distance is the smallest of all the sample points, i.e., the inner mean value of SO(3).

Similar to SO(2), we assume that the variance is sufficiently small, i.e., $\lambda \lesssim 2\pi$, where $\lambda$ is the eigenvalue of $\Sigma$. Using the same assumption as in the previous section, the maximum likelihood estimate of the approximate variance is obtained:

$\widehat{\sum} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \Phi (\log (\hat{\mu}^{-1}x_i)) \Phi (\log (\hat{\mu}^{-1}x_i))^T$
