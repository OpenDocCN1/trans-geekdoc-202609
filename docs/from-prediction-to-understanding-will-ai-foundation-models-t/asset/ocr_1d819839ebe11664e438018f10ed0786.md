Early studies suggest that units such as ‘cells’ in large vision models, ‘generers’ in early layers, and more recently, a ‘Golden Gate bridge’ (a distinct pattern learned for information) in LLMs. This line of parallel works reveals findings in neuroscience, where ‘concept cells’ have revealed motor lobe—neurons that respond selectively to the Sydney Opera House or to individuals such as Jenkins.

Many models, however, have diverse representations. Many LLMs reduce linear operations in high-dimensional configuration spaces. Researchers now show that models are grouped together (see See and Pavlov for reference). For example, given a neutral prompt with the word ‘good’ without any context, the model could respond with ‘bad’ by adding a vector in the ‘antonym’ direction or to produce the Spanish translation (‘bueno’) by adding a vector in the ‘Spanish’ direction to align across contexts, suggesting that semantic relations are anchored as geometric pattern.
