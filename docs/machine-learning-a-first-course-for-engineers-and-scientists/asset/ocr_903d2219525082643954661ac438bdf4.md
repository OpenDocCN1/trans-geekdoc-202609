# 1.3 Further reading

There are now quite a few extensive textbooks available on the topic of machine learning which introduce the area in different ways compared to what we do in this book. We will only mention a few here. The book of Hastie et al. 2009 introduce the area of statistical learning in a mathematically solid and accessible manner. A few years later the authors released a different version of their book (James et al. 2013) which is mathematically lighter, conveying the main ideas in an even more accessible manner. These books do not venture neither into the world of Bayesian methods nor the world of neural networks. There are several other complementary books doing exactly that, see e.g. Bishop (2006) and Murphy (2021). MacKay (2003) provides a rather early dealing interesting and useful connections to information theory. It is very much worth looking into. The book by Shale-Shwartz and Ben-David (2014) provides an introduction with a clear focus on the underpinning machine learning—with mathematics. It is a perfect book for those of us readers would like to deepen their understanding of the theoretical background of the area. We also mention the work of Elfron and Hastie (2016), where the authors take a constructive historical approach to the development of the area covering the revolution in data analysis. With Smith (2017), Contemporary approaches to the mathematics of machine learning are provided by Strang (2019) and Deisenroth et al. (2019).

For a full account of the work on automatic diagnosis of heart abnormalities, Ribeiro et al. 2020 and for a general introduction to use of machine learning—particular deep learning—in medicine we point to Topol (2019). The application of kernel ridge regression to elaspolite crystals was borrowed from Faber et al. (2016). Other applications of machine learning in materials science are reviewed in the collection edited by Schütt et al. (2020). The London air pollution study was proved by Hamelj et al. (2019) where the authors introduce interesting and useful developments of the Gaussian process model that we explain in Chapter 9. When it comes to semantic segmentation, the ground-breaking work of Long et al. 2015 has received a massive interest. The two main base lines for the current development in semantic segmentation are Zhao et al. (2017) and L.-C. Chen et al. (2017). A thorough introduction to the mathematics of soccer is provided in the book by D. Sumter (2016) and a starting point to recent ideas on how to assess the impact of player actions is given in Decroos et al. (2019).
