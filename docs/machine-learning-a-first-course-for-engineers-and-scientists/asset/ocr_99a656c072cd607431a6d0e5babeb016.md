Each ECG in the dataset comes with an output sorting into classes—no abnormalities, atrial fibrillation, right bundle branch block, etc.—to the status of the heart. In Chapter 6 we introduce deep learning models and their training algorithms.

Evaluating how a model will perform in practice is not straightforward. The study was to ask three different candidates with experience in electrocardiography to examine and classify 827 records from distinct patients. This dataset was then evaluated by the algorithm, 40 year cardiologists, 23 year emergency residents, and 50 year medical students. The average performance was compared. The result was that the algorithm achieved better or the same result compared to the human performance on classifying six types of abnormalities.

Before we move on, let us pause and reflect on the example introduced above. In fact, many concepts to machine learning can be recognized in this image.
As mentioned, the first cornerstone of learning is the data. Taking a closer look at what data actually is, we note that it differs in several ways. First, we have the training data which is to learn the model. Each training data point consists of both the ECG signal, which we refer to as the input, and labels corresponding to the heart condition seen in this signal, which we refer to as the output. To train the latter had to be manually assigned by domain experts (or possibly auxiliary examination). Training a model from labeled data points is therefore referred to as supervised learning. The think of learning being supervised by the domain expert, and the learning objective is to obtain a computer program that mimics the learning done by the expert. Second, we have the (unlabeled) ECG signals that will be fed to the program when it is used “in production”. It is important to remember that the ultimate goal of the model is to obtain accurate predictions in this second phase. We say that the made by the model generalize beyond the training data. How to learn models that are capable of generalizing, and how to evaluate to what extent they do, is a central theoretical question throughout this book (see in particular Chapter 4).
