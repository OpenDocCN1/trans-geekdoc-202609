We now give some more details on the variance reduction that happens in (7.1), which is the entire point of bagging. We average on regression, but the intuition works also for classification.

First make a basic observation regarding random variables, namely that averaging reduces variance. To formalize this, let $z_1, \dots, z_B$ be a of identically distributed (but possibly dependent) random variables with mean $\mathbb{E}[z_b] = \mu$ and variance $	ext{Var}[z_b] = \sigma^2$ for $b=1, \dots, B$. Furthermore, assume that the average correlation between any pair of $B$ variables is $ho$. Then, computing the mean and the variance of the average of $B$ these variables we get

> $\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B z_bight] = \mu.\quad (7.2a)$

> $	ext{Var}\left[\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B z_bight] = \frac{1-ho}{B}\sigma^2+ho\sigma^2.\quad (7.2b)$

The first equation (7.2a) tells us that the mean is unaltered by averaging a number of identically distributed random variables. The second equation (7.2b) tells us that the variance is reduced by averaging if the correlation $ho < 1$. The first term in the variance (7.2b) can be arbitrarily small by increasing $B$, whereas the second term is only determined by the correlation $ho$ and variance $\sigma^2$. To make a connection between bagging and (7.2), consider the predictions $\hat{y}^{(b)}(\mathbf{x}_*)$ from the base models as random variables. All base models, and their predictions, are identically distributed but correlated. By averaging them from the same data $\mathcal{T}$ (via the bootstrap), we decrease the variance, according to (7.2b). If we only use $B$ large, the achieved variance reduction is limited by the correlation $ho$. Experience has shown that if $B$ is small enough, the computational complexity of bagging (compared to using only one base model) pays off in terms of decreased variance. To summarize, averaging the identically distributed predictions from several base models each with a low bias, the bias remains low (according to (7.2a)) and the variance is reduced (according to (7.2b)).
