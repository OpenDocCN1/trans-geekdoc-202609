If elements of $f$, let us say the ones in $f_1$, are observed, the conditional distribution for $f_2$ given the observation of $f_1$ is, by Theorem 9 in Appendix 9.A,

> $p(f_2|f_1) = \mathcal{N}(f_2; \mu_2 + \Sigma_{21}\Sigma_1^{-1}(f_1 - \mu_1), \Sigma_{22} - \Sigma_{21}\Sigma_1^{-1}\Sigma_{12})$.

The conditional distribution is nothing but another Gaussian distribution with closed-form expressions for the mean and covariance.
Figure 9.3 shows a 2-dimensional example where $f_1$ is a scalar $f_1$, and $f_2$ is a scalar $f_2$. For this model we choose the prior mean and variances for $f_1$ and $f_2$ are the same, and $\Sigma_1 = \Sigma_2$. We also assume a positive correlation between $f_1$ and $f_2$ before any observations have been received, respecting the smoothness assumption we have about the underlying random function $f(x)$.
