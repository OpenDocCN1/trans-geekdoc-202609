This table follows Figure 4.3 well. The generalization gap (difference between $E_{new}$ and $\hat{E}_{train}$) is positive and increases with model complexity (decreasing $k$ in k-NN), $\hat{E}_{train}$ decreases with model complexity. Among these values for $k$, $E_{new}$ has its minimum for $k=20$. This suggests that k-NN with $k=2$ suffers from overfitting for this problem, whereas $k=70$ is a case of underfitting.

We have so far concerned about the relationship between the generalization gap and the model complexity. Another very important aspect is the size of the training dataset, $n$. We can in general expect that the more training data, the smaller the generalization gap. On the other hand, $\hat{E}_{train}$ typically increases as n increases, since most models are unable to fit all training data points if there are too many of them. A typical behavior of $\hat{E}_{train}$ and $\hat{E}_{new}$ is sketched in Figure 4.6.

# **Reducing $E_{new}$ in practice**

Our goal is to achieve a small error “in production”, that is, a small $E_{new}$. To achieve that, according to the decomposition $E_{new} = \hat{E}_{train} + generalization gap$, we need to have $\hat{E}_{train}$ as well as the generalization gap small. Let us draw some conclusions from what we have seen so far.

+   - The new error $E_{new}$ will not on average be smaller than the training error $\hat E_{train}$. Thus, if $\hat{E}_{train}$ is much bigger than $E_{new}$ you need for your model to be successful at the application hand, you do not need to waste time implementing cross-validation for estimating $E_{new}$. Instead, you should re-think the problem and method you are using.
- The generalization gap and $E_{new}$ typically decreases as $n$ increases. Thus, if possible, the size of the training data may help a lot with reducing $E_{new}$.

Making the model more flexible decreases $\hat E_{train}$, but often increases the generalization gap. Making the model less flexible, on the other hand, typically decreases the generalization gap but increases $\hat{E}_{train}$.
