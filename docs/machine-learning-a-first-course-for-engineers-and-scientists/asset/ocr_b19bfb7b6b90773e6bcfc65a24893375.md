The effect of bagging in the random forest; see Chapter 7) is, however, clearly that it reduces the variance compared to the regression tree with no noise. Another instance of where bias and variance increase, we illustrate some of these cases in detail in Figure 4.12. We plot some different regression models. The dashed line is the true $f_0(x)$, the dotted lines represent different models $\hat{f}(x)$ learned from different training datasets $\mathcal{T}$, and the solid blue line their mean $ar{f}(x)$. In these figures, bias is the difference between the dashed line and solid blue lines, whereas variance is the spread of the dotted lines around the solid blue. The variance appears to be roughly the same for the first three models, somewhat smaller for the first order polynomial, whereas the variance is clearly smaller for the higher order polynomials. This is compared to Figure 4.11.

![](bbox=[0.148, 0.613, 0.852, 0.843])

Comparing the second order polynomial with little ($\lambda = 0.1$) and heavy ($\lambda = 1000$) is clear that regularization reduces variance, but also increases bias. Furthermore, the random forest has a smaller variance than the regression tree, but without any noticeable change in the solid line $f(x)$, and hence no change in bias.
