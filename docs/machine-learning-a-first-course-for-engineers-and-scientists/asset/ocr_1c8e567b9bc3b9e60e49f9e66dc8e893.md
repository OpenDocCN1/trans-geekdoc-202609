# 7.3 Boosting and AdaBoost

Example 7.5: Boosting illustration

We consider a binary classification problem with a two-dimensional input $x=[x_1, x_2]$. The training data consists of $n=10$ data points, 5 from each of the two classes. We use a decision stump, a classification tree of depth one, as simple (weak) base classifier. A decision stump amounts to selecting one of the variables, $x_1$ or $x_2$, and split the input space into two halves, in order to minimize the training error. The first panel in Figure 7.7 shows the training data, illustrated by red and blue dots for the two classes. The panel below that in Figure 7.7, the colored regions show the decision boundary for a decision stump $h^{(1)}(x)$ trained on this.

The model $\hat{h}^{(1)}(x)$ incorrectly classifies three data points (red dots falling in the blue region), which are encircled in the region. To improve the performance of the classifier we want to find a model that can distinguish these three points from the class. To emphasize we assign higher weights $w_j^{(2)}$ to the data, which are shown in the upper panel of Figure 7.7 (larger radius indicate higher weight). The points correctly classified by $\hat{h}^{(1)}(x)$ are down-weighted, the three points misclassified by $\hat{h}^{(1)}(x)$ are up-weighted. We train another decision stump, $\hat{h}^{(2)}(x)$, and train a third decision stump $\hat{h}^{(3)}(x)$ by finding the weighted misclassification error $\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^n w_j^{(2)}[\hat{h}^{(2)}(x_j)
eq y_j]$, resulting in the decision boundary in the lower panel. This procedure is repeated for a third iteration: we update the weights for the hits and misses of $\hat{h}^{(2)}(x)$ and train a third decision stump $\hat{h}^{(3)}(x)$ shown in the lower panel in Figure 7.7.

The final classifier $\hat{y}_{boost}(x)$, in the bottom panel of Figure 7.7, is obtained as a weighted majority vote of the three decision stumps. Note that its decision boundary is nonlinear, the decision boundary for each member is linear. This illustrates the concept of turning an ensemble of three weak (high-bias) models into a (low-bias) model.
