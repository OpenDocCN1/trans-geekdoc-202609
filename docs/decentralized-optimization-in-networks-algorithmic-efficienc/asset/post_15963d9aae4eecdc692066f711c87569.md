
where $\tilde{\varepsilon}_{i,\theta}^t = \sum_{j=1}^n w_{ij}(\epsilon_{j,\theta}^t - \epsilon_{i,\theta}^t)$ and $\tilde{\varepsilon}_{i,z}^t = \sum_{j=1}^n w_{ij}(\epsilon_{j,z}^t - \epsilon_{i,z}^t)$.
Define $\mathbf{x}^t = [x_1^t, \dots, x_n^t]^\top$, $\mathbf{y}^t = [y_1^t, \dots, y_n^t]^\top$, $\hat{\mathbf{y}}^t = [\hat{y}_1^t, \dots, \hat{y}_n^t]^\top$, $\boldsymbol{\theta}^t = [\theta_1^t, \dots, \theta_n^t]^\top$, $\mathbf{z}^t = [z_1^t, \dots, z_n^t]^\top$, $\tilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_\theta^t = [\tilde{\xi}_{1,\theta}^t, \dots, \tilde{\xi}_{n,\theta}^t]^\top$, $\tilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_z^t = [\tilde{\xi}_{1,z}^t, \dots, \tilde{\xi}_{n,z}^t]^\top$, $\nabla\mathbf{q}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^t) = [\nabla q_1(\theta_1^t), \dots, \nabla q_n(\theta_n^t)]^\top$. Subsequently, we can represent Algorithm 1 in vector notation, as depicted here:

$$
\mathbf{x}^{t+1} = \arg \min_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X}} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{V}} f_i(x_i) + (\theta^t)^\top (\mathbf{x}^t - c), \quad \text{(2.12a)}
$$

$$
\mathbf{y}^{t+1} = \mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}^t + \tilde{\xi}_\theta^t - D_\alpha \mathbf{z}^t, \quad \text{(2.12b)}
$$

$$
\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{t+1} = \mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}^t + \tilde{\xi}_\theta^t - s D_\alpha \mathbf{z}^t, \quad \text{(2.12c)}
$$

$$
\theta^{t+1} = \mathbf{y}^{t+1} + \beta (\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{t+1} - \mathbf{y}^t), \quad \text{(2.12d)}
$$

$$
\mathbf{z}^{t+1} = \mathbf{W}\mathbf{z}^t + \tilde{\xi}_z^t - \mathbf{x}^{t+1} + \mathbf{x}^t, \quad \text{(2.12e)}
$$

where \(D_\alpha = \text{diag}(\alpha) \otimes \mathbf{I}_n\) and \(\alpha = [\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n]^\top\).

**Remark 2.3.** It is worthy of mention that the ET-PDA algorithm builds upon the foundation of [44], thereby inheriting some convergence analysis aspects from that work. Nevertheless, this chapter stands apart from [44] in several respects: firstly, the scope of problems and applications addressed vary significantly; secondly, [44] primarily targets unconstrained convex optimization problems, whereas our work tackles constrained problems; lastly, ET-PDA incorporates an event-triggered mechanism that mitigates Zeno-like behavior, thus contributing to a reduction in communication overhead.

## 2.4.1 Supplemental results

To validate the convergence of ET-PDA, we shall present several pivotal findings, whose justifications are detailed in the references [17,47,48].

**Lemma 2.1.** _([17]) Subject to the stipulations of Assumptions 2.1 and 2.2, the conjugate representation \(f_i^*\) of \(f_i\) adheres to the subsequent characteristics:_

- (a) The conjugate function \(f_i^*\) exhibits strong convexity, characterized by a constant factor of \(1/L_i\).
- (b) The conjugate function \(f_i^*\) is differentiable, and its derivative possesses Lipschitz continuity with a constant factor of \(1/\mu_i\).

This lemma demonstrates that the dual function \(d_i\) in (2.4) possesses both strong concavity with a parameter of \(1/L_i\) and a Lipschitz continuous derivative characterized by a parameter of \(1/\mu_i\).

One can deduce from (2.6) that

\[
\nabla q_i(\theta_i^t) = -x_i^t + c_i. \tag{2.13}
\]

At this point, we can express (2.12d) and (2.12e) in the following manner:

\[
\theta^{t+1} = \mathbf{W}\theta^t + \tilde{\xi}_\theta^t - D_\alpha \mathbf{z}^t + \beta(\mathbf{W}\theta^t - \mathbf{W}\theta^{t-1}) + \beta(\tilde{\xi}_\theta^t - \tilde{\xi}_\theta^{t-1}) - s D_\alpha \beta(\mathbf{z}^t - \mathbf{z}^{t-1}), \tag{2.14a}

\[
\mathbf{z}^{t+1} = \mathbf{W}\mathbf{z}^t + \tilde{\xi}_z^t - \mathbf{x}^{t+1} + c + \mathbf{x}^t - c = \mathbf{W}\mathbf{z}^t + \tilde{\xi}_z^t + \nabla \mathbf{q}(\theta^{t+1}) - \nabla \mathbf{q}(\theta^t). \tag{2.14b}
\]

Furthermore, there exist certain auxiliary variables that serve to facilitate the subsequent proofs, as outlined below:

\[
\mathbf{J} = \frac{1}{n}(\mathbf{1}_n \mathbf{1}_n^\mathrm{T}) \otimes \mathbf{I}_n, \overline{\theta}^t = \frac{1}{n}(\mathbf{1}_n^\mathrm{T} \otimes \mathbf{I}_n)\theta^t, \overline{\mathbf{z}}^t = \frac{1}{n}(\mathbf{1}_n^\mathrm{T} \otimes \mathbf{I}_n)\mathbf{z}^t, \frac{1}{n}(\mathbf{1}_n^\mathrm{T} \otimes \mathbf{I}_n)\tilde{\xi}_\theta^t = 0, \frac{1}{n}(\mathbf{1}_n^\mathrm{T} \otimes \mathbf{I}_n)\tilde{\xi}_z^t = 0.
\]

**Lemma 2.2.** _([47,48]) The subsequent results hold:_

- (a) \(\|\mathbf{W}\theta - \mathbf{J}\theta\| \leqslant \sigma \|\theta - \mathbf{J}\theta\|\), for \(0 < \sigma < 1\), \(\theta \in \mathbb{R}^n\).

- (b) \((\mathbf{1}_n^\mathrm{T} \otimes \mathbf{I}_n)\mathbf{z}^t = (\mathbf{1}_n^\mathrm{T} \otimes \mathbf{I}_n)\nabla \mathbf{q}(\theta^t), t > 0\).

Lemma 2.2(a) encapsulates the averaging characteristic of the consensus weight matrix, a trait that will be repeatedly harnessed in the forthcoming proofs. On the other hand, Lemma 2.2(b) indicates that the mean value of \(z_i^t\) maintains the averaged state of the local gradients.

**Remark 2.4.** Pursuant to Assumption 2.2, a dual optimal solution \(\theta^*\) must exist for the problem (2.5). The optimal vector pair \(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}^*, \theta^*)\) is perceived as the stationary point, or saddle point, of the Lagrangian function \(\mathcal{L}\), that is,

\[
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}^*, \theta) \leqslant \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}^*, \theta^*) \leqslant \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}, \theta^*),
\]

where \(\forall \mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X}, \theta \in \mathbb{R}\). Regarding Lemma 2.2(a), considering that the spectral norm of the deviation from the average matrix, denoted as \(\sigma = \|\mathbf{W} - \frac{\mathbf{1}_n \mathbf{1}_n^\mathrm{T}}{n}\|\), is less than 1, we can infer that \(\|\mathbf{W}\theta - \mathbf{J}\theta\| = \|(\mathbf{W} - \mathbf{J})(\theta - \mathbf{J}\theta)\| \leqslant \sigma \|\theta - \mathbf{J}\theta\|\).

## 2.4.2 Supporting results

To assess the performance of ET-PDA, we monitor the evolution of the following five metrics: (i) Network consensus error: \(\|\theta^{t+1} - \mathbf{J}\theta^{t+1}\|\); (ii) Optimality gap \(\|\mathbf{J}\theta^{t+1} - \mathbf{1}_n \otimes \theta^*\|\); (iii) State differences: \(\|\theta^{t+1} - \theta^t\|\); (iv) Gradient tracking error: \(\|\mathbf{z}^{t+1} - \mathbf{J}\mathbf{z}^{t+1}\|\); (v) Gradient tracking differences: \(\|\mathbf{z}^{t+1} - \mathbf{z}^t\|\).

As these five metrics gradually converge to zero in a linear fashion, they guarantee that the sequence \(\{\theta^t\}\) converges linearly towards the optimal solution of the problem (2.5). Lemmas 2.4–2.8 sequentially demonstrate the bounds on the historical values of these five metrics, and we construct a set of linear inequalities based on these bounds, enabling us to investigate the spectral characteristics of their respective coefficient matrices.

To streamline the entire proof process, we initially determine an upper limit for \(\|\mathbf{z}^t\|\), and replace \(\hat{g}\) with the reciprocal of \(\check{\mu}\), denoted as \(1/\check{\mu}\).

**Lemma 2.3.** Given the stipulated conditions in Assumptions 2.1 and 2.2, the subsequent inequality remains valid for all positive values of t:

\[
\|\mathbf{z}^t\| \leq \hat{g}\sqrt{n}\|\boldsymbol{\theta}^t - \mathbf{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^t\| + \hat{g}\sqrt{n}\|\mathbf{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^t - \mathbf{1}_n \otimes \theta^*\| + \|\mathbf{z}^t - \mathbf{J}\mathbf{z}^t\|.
\]

Proof. The norm of \(\|\mathbf{z}^t\|\) can be disentangled into the subsequent set of inequalities:

\[
\|\mathbf{z}^t\| = \|\mathbf{z}^t - \mathbf{J}\mathbf{z}^t + \mathbf{J}\mathbf{z}^t\| \leq \|\mathbf{z}^t - \mathbf{J}\mathbf{z}^t\| + \|\mathbf{J}\mathbf{z}^t\|. \tag{2.15}
\]

Subsequently, we impose limitations on the second term in (2.15).

\[
\|\mathbf{J}\mathbf{z}^t\| = \|\frac{1}{n} (\mathbf{1}_n \otimes \mathbf{I}_n)(\mathbf{1}_n^\mathrm{T} \otimes \mathbf{I}_n) \nabla\mathbf{q}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^t)\|
= \|\sum_{i=1}^n \nabla q_i(\theta_i^t) - \sum_{i=1}^n \nabla q_i(\theta^*)\|
\leq \hat{g}\sqrt{n}\|\boldsymbol{\theta}^t - \mathbf{1}_n \otimes \theta^*\|
\leq \hat{g}\sqrt{n}\|\boldsymbol{\theta}^t - \mathbf{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^t\| + \hat{g}\sqrt{n}\|\mathbf{J}\boldsymbol{\theta}^t - \mathbf{1}_n \otimes \theta^*\|.
\]

Based on Lemma 2.2(b), the initial equivalence in the above inequality is derived. Then, we obtain Lemma 2.3.
