## Privacy-preserving decentralized dual averaging push algorithms with correlated perturbations

### 7.1 Introduction

In recent years, advancements in communication and sensing technologies within networked control systems have naturally led to a variety of optimization problems across networks [1,2]. Traditional methods typically rely on central intelligent devices for storage, communication, and computation, which are costly, lack robustness in new environments, and are vulnerable to single points of failure [3]. Decentralized approaches, which minimize long-distance communication, offer advantages such as enhanced robustness, reduced communication needs, and improved privacy over centralized methods. They achieve this by breaking down large-scale problems into smaller subproblems, applicable in areas like large-scale data analysis [4,5], wireless sensor networks [6,7], and information control [8,9].

#### 7.1.1 Literature review

Recently, numerous decentralized algorithms have been developed to effectively solve optimization problems [10–16]. Algorithms that only use gradient information are frequently reported due to their scalability and performance [1,2]. Early works on decentralized gradient descent (DGD) [10,11] and their extensions address various problem factors and targets [12–16], demonstrating their effectiveness in decentralized optimization over networks [10–16]. For instance, [14] introduced a decentralized primal-dual algorithm achieving optimal allocation in integrated energy systems, while [15] explored fixed-time decentralized optimal resource management for microgrids. Further, decentralized dual averaging methods have been extensively studied [17–22]. The work in [17] first presented a decentralized dual averaging algorithm for constrained optimization over undirected networks, inspiring many efficient decentralized approaches for acceleration [18,19], online learning [20], and improved efficiency [21,22].

In practical scenarios, communication networks among nodes are often unidirectional, leading to unbalanced and directed communication networks [23,24]. Consequently, several decentralized dual averaging approaches have been proposed using effective strategies like surplus-based [25] and push-sum [26–28] algorithms. For example, [25] proposed a decentralized surplus-plus-based dual averaging algorithm for robust constrained optimization over directed networks, and [26] introduced a push-sum decentralized dual averaging algorithm with similar convergence properties. However, when networks vary over time, the convergence analysis of these algorithms may not hold. To address this, [4,5] proposed effective algorithms for different practical optimization problems over time-varying directed networks. Furthermore, [27] presented a decentralized dual averaging push algorithm suitable for time-varying directed networks, and [28] developed a similar algorithm for online constrained optimization over such networks.

In many real-world applications, not all nodes are normal, and communication often occurs in imperfect environments [29,30]. Decentralized approaches require nodes to share their state information with neighbors, which poses risks if normal nodes contain sensitive information [31]. Malicious nodes can capture transmitted information and infer private data, leading to privacy leaks [31]. With the rise in privacy leakage incidents, there is an urgent need to protect the privacy of nodes in decentralized networks [32–37]. Existing privacy-protected decentralized approaches fall into two categories: encryption-based [32,33] and non-encryption-based [30,34–37,41,42]. While encryption effectively protects privacy, it is computationally complex and costly in communication, making it unsuitable for resource-limited networks. Differential privacy, a non-encryption scheme, has gained attention for its strong mathematical foundation, provable security, and ease of implementation [34–37,41,42]. However, differential privacy may reduce optimization accuracy. To achieve accurate convergence while protecting privacy, some researchers have devised correlated perturbation schemes [30,38–40]. For instance, [39] introduced zero-sum correlation perturbations in DGD to protect privacy, and [40] developed a privacy-protected decentralized dual averaging algorithm for time-varying undirected networks using edge-based correlated perturbations. However, these networks were undirected.

Given the above context, it is evident that there are few privacy-protected decentralized algorithms dedicated to handling general constrained optimization problems over time-varying directed networks accurately. Therefore the main motivation of this study is to develop a privacy-protected decentralized dual averaging algorithm with edge-based correlated perturbations for time-varying directed networks, ensuring accurate convergence while effectively protecting the privacy of normal nodes.

#### 7.1.2 Main contributions

This chapter aims to solve a privacy-sensitive decentralized constrained optimization problem over time-varying directed networks by proposing an effective privacy-protected decentralized algorithm, suitable for practical applications and adaptable to various conditions. Compared to existing works, our contributions are summarized as follows:

- (i) We develop and analyze a privacy-protected decentralized dual averaging push algorithm for general constrained optimization over time-varying directed networks. By using edge-based correlated perturbations, our algorithm surpasses differential-privacy-based decentralized algorithms [36,37] in convergence accuracy. Unlike related algorithms in [39,40] that use edge-based perturbations, our approach also leverages the push-sum averaging protocol to handle imbalances in time-varying directed networks. Additionally, compared to node-based perturbation methods [35,43], our algorithm offers more design flexibility with edge-based perturbations.

- (ii) We provide a rigorous analysis of the convergence and privacy protection of our algorithm. It is demonstrated that our algorithm achieves the same \(\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{K})\) convergence rate (where \(K\) is the number of iterations) as those without privacy protection [25–28], under more relaxed initial conditions. This makes our approach more general than existing methods [25,26,28], which require zero initial conditions. Our algorithm thus does not necessitate the coordination of initial state estimates among network nodes, enhancing robustness and flexibility.

- (iii) Our algorithm ensures that malicious nodes cannot deduce private information from their observations, thereby protecting node privacy. To the best of our knowledge, this is a novel decentralized algorithm that not only safeguards the privacy of normal nodes but also accurately solves a generally constrained optimization problem over time-varying directed networks under weaker initial conditions. Numerical examples further corroborate the performance of our algorithm.
