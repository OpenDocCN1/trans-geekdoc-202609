In addition to the above studies, blockchain-based decentralized algorithms [53,54] and peer-to-peer decentralized schemes [55,56] have been effectively applied to smart grids. Notably, Hassan et al. [53] examined blockchain integration in smart grids, highlighting fundamentals, challenges, and solutions. Tushar et al. [55] provided a comprehensive review of decentralized peer-to-peer energy trading innovations, contributing to future smart grid development. Concurrently, numerous related works employing traditional search algorithms [57,58] and computational intelligence algorithms (including metaheuristic algorithms [59,60] and decentralized reinforcement learning algorithms [64,65]) to tackle EDP have emerged. For example, Park et al. [57] introduced a new algorithm using a modified PSO mechanism to address EDP with nonsmooth cost functions. An adaptive robust hybrid optimization algorithm combining two metaheuristic approaches was developed in [60] for nonconvex EDP cost functions. Subsequently, Dai et al. [64] proposed a novel decentralized reinforcement learning algorithm for dynamic EDP with inaccessible cost functions.

When applying decentralized methods to EDP in real smart grids, the communication network among generators is a critical aspect to consider. Information transmission is often asymmetric in practice, resulting in unbalanced and directed communication networks. Yang et al. [23] proposed a decentralized algorithm using column-stochastic matrices for EDP with communication delays, employing the push-sum average consensus to mitigate directed network imbalance. Li et al. [24] adopted the gradient rescaling technique and presented a decentralized algorithm based on row-stochastic matrices for EDP. Numerous studies have focused on decentralized algorithms for EDP achieving various objectives such as linear convergence [25,26] and accelerated convergence [27] using row- or column-stochastic matrices. Zhao et al. [28] introduced a decentralized algorithm utilizing both row- and column-stochastic matrices, addressing conservatism in related works [23,24] through eigenvector estimation.

In previously mentioned decentralized algorithms [13–16,18–28], direct information transmission among generators in the communication network leads to severe privacy leakage issues [29], allowing external eavesdroppers to effectively attack the power grid. For instance, eavesdroppers can infer and reveal local cost function variables (power generation), designing optimal attack schemes to increase total costs and disrupt the grid [30]. Differential privacy approaches have been widely and successfully applied in optimization for privacy protection in decentralized optimization [31–33]. However, this method compromises solution optimality, balancing optimality and privacy levels [31–33]. Recently, several privacy-protected algorithms incorporating homomorphic encryption into decentralized optimization have been proposed [34,35]. Unfortunately, these decentralized homomorphic encryption algorithms require more information transmission time and are only suitable for undirected networks.
