lim_{t \to \infty}[\alpha_i^t||b|| + ||\phi_i^t - \phi_i^t||] and converges to 0 almost surely (a.s.). Additionally, a direct consequence of (1.17) is that:

$$
p_i^t = \sum_{j=1}^n W_{ij}^t p_j^{t-1} + \epsilon_i^t, \quad i \in \{i^t, j^t\},\\
p_i^t = \sum_{j=1}^n W_{ij}^t p_j^{t-1}, \quad i \notin \{i^t, j^t\},
$$

where $\epsilon_i^t = -\alpha_i^t b - [f_i(h_i^t)]_+ \xi_i^t / ||\xi_i^t||^2$, for $i \in \{i^t, j^t\}$. By taking the conditional expectation with respect to $\mathcal{F}^{t-1}$, we obtain that for all $i$ in the set $\mathcal{V}$,

$$
\mathbb{E}_{t-1}[p_i^t] = \sum_{j=1}^n \bar{W}_{ij} p_j^{t-1} + \eta_i \mathbb{E}_{t-1}[\epsilon_i^t].
$$

Since $\lim_{t \to \infty} ||h_i^t - \phi_i^t|| = 0$ a.s. and $\phi_i^t \in \Xi_0$, we can obtain that $\lim_{t \to \infty} [f_i(h_i^t)]_+ = 0$ a.s.. Consequently, $\lim_{t \to \infty} ||\epsilon_i^t|| = 0$ a.s. considering the fact that $\lim_{t \to \infty} \alpha_i^t = 0$ a.s.. Based on this result and Lemma 1.4, it follows that $\lim_{t \to \infty} ||p_i^t - \bar{p}^t|| = 0$ a.s. for all $i \in \mathcal{V}$. Then, utilizing (1.34a) and the row-stochasticity of $\bar{W}$, we can infer that the sequence $\{||\bar{p}^t - p^*||^2\}$ is convergent a.s.. Noting that $||p_i^t - \phi_i^t||^2 \le ||h_i^t - \phi_i^t||^2 - ||[f_i(h_i^t)]_+||^2 / ||\xi_i^t||^2 \le ||h_i^t - \phi_i^t||^2$ (see the proof of Lemma 1.5) and given that $\lim_{t \to \infty} ||h_i^t - \phi_i^t|| = 0$ a.s., we further obtain that

$$
\lim_{t \to \infty} ||\bar{P}^t - \tilde{\phi}^t|| \le \lim_{t \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i ||p_i^t - \phi_i^t|| = 0 \text{ a.s.},
$$

which indicates that $\{||\bar{p}^t - p^*||^2\}$ converges a.s. as well. Recalling the fact that $\lim_{t \to \infty} \inf b^T \phi^t = b^T p^* \text{ a.s.}$, we can infer that there exists a subsequence $\{\bar{\phi}^{t_k}\}$ that converges a.s. to an optimal point $p_0^* \in \Xi^*$. Consequently, it is evident that $\lim_{k \to \infty} ||\bar{\phi}^{t_k} - p_0^*|| = 0$. Since the sequence $\{||\bar{p}^t - p_0^*||^2\}$ is a.s. convergent, we conclude that $\lim_{t \to \infty} \bar{p}^t = p_0^* \text{ a.s.}$. Furthermore, we note that the inequality $||p_i^t - p_0^*|| \le ||p_i^t - \bar{p}^t|| + ||\bar{p}^t - \tilde{\phi}^t|| + ||\tilde{\phi}^t - p_0^*||$ holds, where all the three terms $||p_i^t - \bar{p}^t||$, $||\bar{p}^t - \tilde{\phi}^t||$, and $||\tilde{\phi}^t - p_0^*||$ converge to zero a.s.. Therefore there exists a point $p_0^* \in \Xi^*$ such that $\lim_{t \to \infty} ||p_i^t - p_0^*|| = 0 \text{ a.s.}$ for $i \in \mathcal{V}$.

Secondly, by considering the update rule in (1.11) and leveraging the strong duality between the primal problem (1.1) and the dual problem (1.3), we can deduce that $\lim_{t \to \infty} x_i^t = x_i^*$ almost surely (a.s.) for all $i \in \mathcal{V}$. This completes the proof. $\square$
