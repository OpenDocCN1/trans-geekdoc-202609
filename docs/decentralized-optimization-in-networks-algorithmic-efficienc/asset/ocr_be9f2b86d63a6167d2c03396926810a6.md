Although previous algorithms tackled constrained problems using the projection operator, they exhibit limitations in computational efficiency, convergence speed, and precise convergence. In a separate study [26], the decentralized constrained optimization problem was explored. When employing a small constant step-size, the algorithm achieved linear convergence to the precise optimal solution, thanks to the gradient tracking method. However, it still suffered from relatively low computational efficiency due to the necessity of computing local batch gradients.

Significant advancements have been made in the realm of decentralized optimization, with notable results such as DGD [27] and its numerous refined algorithms that address constraints [28,29]. These methods have been effectively applied to intricate networks [3,30] and have undergone acceleration [31,32], thereby illustrating the proficiency of decentralized strategies in addressing large-scale problems. However, compared to centralized algorithms, these decentralized methods still face a trade-off between convergence speed and achieving precise convergence. In recent years, numerous techniques rooted in gradient tracking have emerged to address the precision quandary and sluggish convergence associated with decentralized algorithms [33–42]. Specifically, the authors of [33] deliberated on power system problems within undirected networks, while Qu and Li [35] introduced a novel gradient estimation method. The works in [34,36] contemplated time-varying and directed graphs, the authors of [37] explored uncoordinated step-sizes for localized optimization, and Shi et al. [38] examined a synchronous gradient-based algorithm. However, these methods exhibit reduced computational efficiency per iteration due to the necessity of computing local batch gradients. To enhance computational efficiency, decentralized gradient algorithms incorporating the variance-reduction technique have surfaced, including GT-SAGA and GT-SVRG [19], [20], etc. Broadly speaking, the variance-reduction technique mitigates errors stemming from the stochastic gradient algorithm, whereas gradient tracking addresses discrepancies arising from inadequate consensus among nodes. Notably, the variance-reduction technique also elevates computational efficiency.

Based on the information mentioned earlier, it is evident that the computational efficiency of [26] is somewhat limited due to the requirement of calculating local batch gradients. Additionally, it is well-known that the variance-reduction method can significantly enhance computational efficiency. With the aim of achieving greater computational efficiency, our intention is to integrate the variance-reduction technique into [26], thereby presenting an algorithm that boasts superior computational efficiency and precise linear convergence.

Recently, multi-objective optimization problems have captured the interest of scholars concentrating on the creation of decentralized optimization algorithms. The authors of [43] introduced a decentralized optimization algorithm grounded in switching topology to address such multi-objective problems. However, it is worth mentioning that this study emphasizes decentralized optimization issues, not the multi-objective ones.
