# 2.2.1 Notation

Bolded letters are used to denote n-dimensional column vectors. **1**_n_ is the all-1 column vector, **I**_n_ is the n x n unit matrix. **1**_n^T_ and **W**^T are the transpositions of **1**_n_ and **W**. For any vector **v**, we indicate its ith element by _v<sub>i</sub>_. \(\hat{v}\) and \(\check{v}\) are the largest and smallest of these elements, respectively, and diag(**v**) is the diagonal matrix of the vector **v**. \(\rho(\mathbf{W})\) is expressed as the spectral radius of the matrix **W** and \(\otimes\) represents the Kronecker product that will be performed between matrices or vectors. Let \(||\cdot||\) be the Euclidean norm for vectors and matrices. _f_* is the conjugate function of _f_, i.e., _f_*(\(\theta\)) = sup\(_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X}}\theta^T\mathbf{x}\) – _f_(**x**).

To denote n-dimensional column vectors, we employ boldface notation. Specifically, **1**_n_ represents the column vector of all ones, while **I**_n_ stands for the n x n identity matrix. The transposes of **1**_n_ and **W** are denoted by **1**_n^T_ and **W**^T respectively. Given any vector **v**, we refer to its ith element as _v<sub>i</sub>_. Among these elements, \(\hat{v}\) and \(\check{v}\) represent the maximum and minimum values, respectively. Furthermore, diag(**v**) represents the diagonal matrix constructed from **v**. The spectral radius of the matrix **W** is denoted by \(\rho(\mathbf{W})\), while \(\otimes\) signifies the Kronecker product applicable to matrices or vectors. We define \(||\cdot||\) as the Euclidean norm for both vectors and matrices. Additionally, _f_* is the conjugate function of _f_, which is given by _f_*(\(\theta\)) = sup\(_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X}}\theta^T\mathbf{x}\) – _f_(**x**).

| _f<sub>i</sub>_ | the local cost function of generator _i_ |

| _d_ | the dual function of EDP |
| _d<sub>i</sub>_ | the local dual function of generator _i_ |
