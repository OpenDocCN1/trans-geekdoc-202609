
## Event-triggered decentralized gradient tracking algorithms for stochastic optimization in networks

### 4.1 Introduction

The field of decentralized optimization [1–4] holds considerable importance in various domains, including machine learning [1–4], signal processing [5], privacy preservation [6], and resource allocation [7–9]. Recently, the advent of large-scale data computation has sparked a growing fascination with decentralized optimization techniques. Unlike centralized methods [10–13], where a solitary agent handles all data storage and processing, decentralized approaches hinge on local interactions among neighboring agents to refresh estimations. This implies that each network agent interacts solely with its neighbors, only utilizing its local cost function for estimator updates. This method circumvents the need for extensive storage and computation by a single entity. As a result, decentralized methods can achieve similar results with reduced costs in a multi-agent network environment. Furthermore, agents do not share raw data; instead, they only exchange estimators, thus bolstering information security. Nevertheless, a significant challenge in decentralized optimization lies in the fact that much of the existing research [14–17] relies on real-time communication, which often results in inefficient data transmission. Hence, this chapter aims to enhance communication efficiency within the decentralized optimization.

### 4.1.1 Literature review

Over the past few decades, decentralized optimization has emerged as a notable field of inquiry, leading to the introduction of numerous decentralized optimization techniques [18–24]. Among these methods, decentralized gradient descent (DGD) [19,20,25] stands out as the most widely recognized approach. DGD, with its decaying step-size, is capable of achieving convergence towards the precise global optimum. During each iteration of the DGD process, individual agents employ the gradient descent algorithm to determine estimators, subsequently sharing these findings with adjacent agents. This decentralized communication and collaboration among agents facilitate the resolution of the optimization challenge, eliminating the need for a centralized authority.

In recent times, a gradient tracking approach has been introduced that uses prior knowledge to accurately approximate the average gradient. Thanks to its efficiency and simplicity in implementation, the gradient tracking approach found applications in various settings [15,21,25–27]. Specifically, Qu and Li [26] adopted gradient tracking in their DGD method to harness the function’s smoothness, achieving a convergence rate comparable to the centralized method [28]. Nedić et al. [15] merged a decentralized inexact gradient approach with gradient tracking, resulting in a method named DIGing that excels in undirected networks. They further adapted DIGing for directed networks, yielding Push-DIGing, which converges to the global minimum with just a column-stochastic matrix. To tackle nonconvex decentralized constrained optimization problems in directed networks, Scutari and Sun [27] incorporated successive convex approximation techniques into a push-sum method [29]. Indeed, many optimization techniques for directed networks rely on the push-sum framework. However, its usage often leads to additional costs in communication and computation. To circumvent these extra expenses, Xin and Khan [21] devised a method that integrates an inexact gradient approach with a gradient estimation technique, enabling convergence to the global minimum without relying on push-sum. Chen and Sayed [25], on the other hand, introduced adaptive diffusion strategies to decentralized optimization, facilitating real-time agent communication, mitigating gradient noise effects, and quantifying noise through an optimization process. Quantized decentralized optimization has also garnered significant attention due to its communication efficiency [30–32]. Notably, Xiong et al. [30] presented a novel quantized decentralized gradient tracking method called Q-DGT, which effectively achieves its objectives. Xin and Khan [33] demonstrated that most gradient tracking methods are related to the AB/pull-pull method [21,34]. Additionally, they enhanced the AB/pull-pull method by incorporating a momentum term, resulting in accelerated performance even when dealing with ill-conditioned cost functions.

Decentralized stochastic optimization stands as a notable advancement in the field of decentralized optimization. It falls into two categories: one where samples are derived by combining true gradients with noise, and another where samples are randomly selected from the entire dataset. A popular technique to preserve computational integrity involves blending the true gradient with stochastic noise [14,16,17,35,36]. Xin et al. [17] introduced a stochastic gradient variant of the AB, termed S-AB, demonstrating its ability to converge toward the vicinity of the global minimum with a constant step-size. Pu and Nedić [16] presented a distributed stochastic gradient tracking approach, which can be seen as a specialized form of S-AB utilizing doubly stochastic weight matrices. Furthermore, Zhao and Liu [14] delved deeper into the convergence attributes of S-AB when employed with a diminishing step-size, highlighting its excellent performance in terms of convergence precision and speed. In another development, Qureshi et al. [36] put forward S-ADDOPT, a decentralized stochastic optimization technique that can be considered a stochastic gradient adaptation of ADD-OPT [37]. Notably, S-ADDOPT exhibits strong convergence qualities and is influenced by the underlying network structure.

It is important to note that the aforementioned techniques rely on continuous communication. This means that agents are constantly engaging in communication, resulting in significant communication overhead. Additionally, maintaining real-time communication in a real-world network can be challenging due to variations in computational capabilities and data flow among agents. In fact, the effectiveness of decentralized optimization algorithms is predominantly influenced by communication efficiency rather than computational efficiency, as information exchange typically takes longer than computation in a multi-agent network. Therefore finding a method to circumvent continuous communication is crucial. The emergence of the event-triggering mechanism presents an innovative approach to facilitating information exchange between agents. The fundamental concept behind this mechanism is that agents only transmit updates when the discrepancy between the current and previously transmitted updates surpasses a certain threshold. Recent studies have introduced several decentralized event-triggered strategies [38–41]. For instance, Liu et al. [39] proposed distributed event-triggered techniques to address constrained optimization challenges. Gao et al. [38] enhanced communication efficiency by integrating an event-triggering mechanism into an established decentralized optimization approach [15], demonstrating impressive convergence rates. Meanwhile, Li et al. [41] devised an event-triggered method with a consistent step-size for undirected networks, capable of converging to the global minimum under standard assumptions.

### 4.1.2 Main contributions

In this chapter, we focus on enhancing the efficiency of communication for the decentralized stochastic gradient tracking method. Inspired by [14,38], our aim is to introduce an algorithm that optimizes communication by implementing an event-triggering system for the method described in [14]. The key contributions presented in this chapter include:

- (1) We propose a unique, decentralized stochastic gradient tracking algorithm enhanced with an event-triggering system, designated as ET-DSGT. This innovation integrates an event-based triggering mechanism into the decentralized stochastic gradient tracking approach. Utilizing a diminishing step-size, ET-DSGT is capable of achieving convergence to the precise global optimum at a rate of O(1/t).

- (2) Unlike time-triggered methods referenced in [15–17,26], ET-DSGT employs an event-based triggering system to prevent consecutive real-time communication, thereby enhancing communication effectiveness. Furthermore, we demonstrate that for every agent, the duration between consecutive trigger events exceeds the iteration interval.

- (3) Distinct from [16,17,36], we leverage the inherent characteristics of stochastic noise to derive connections between particular norms governed by the doubly stochastic weight matrix constraint. Utilizing these connections, we provide a comprehensive convergence analysis.
