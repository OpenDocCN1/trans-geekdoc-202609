- **1. Initialization:** Each node $i$ initializes $x_i^0 \in \mathbb{R}$, $y_i^0 \in \mathbb{R}$, $k_i^0 \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $v_i^0 = y_i^0$, and $u_i^0 = k_i^0$.
- **2.** for $t = 0, 1, 2, \dots$ do
- **3. Generating the random variable:** Generate a Bernoulli random variable $h_i^t$ independently and identically.
- **4. Consensus on the dual variable:** Update two intermediate state variables $v_i^t, u_i^t$ as follows:

\(\left\{\begin{array}{ l } h_i^t = 1: v_i^t = \sum_{j=1}^n \hat{w}_{ij}^t y_j^t, u_i^t = \sum_{j=1}^n \hat{w}_{ij}^t k_j^t, \\ h_i^t = 0: v_i^t = y_i^t, u_i^t = k_i^t, \end{array}\right. \quad (1.41)\)

where $\hat{w}_{ij}^t$ is the weight from node $j$ to node $i$ at time $t$.

- **5. Optimization on the primal variable:** Update the local primal variable $x_i^t$ as follows:

\(x_i^{t+1} = \arg\min_{x_i \in \Omega_i} C_i(x_i) - v_i^t(x_i - r_i).\)

- **6. Optimization on the dual variable:** Update $y_i^t, k_i^t$ according to the following:

\(\left\{\begin{array}{ l } h_i^t = 1: \\ \quad z_i^t = u_i^t - \alpha_i^t 1_n, \\ \quad y_i^{t+1} = v_i^t - \frac{[J_i(v_i^t) - \xi_i^t] z_{i+}^t}{1+||a_i^t||^2} a_i^t, \\ \quad k_i^{t+1} = z_i^t + \frac{[J_i(v_i^t) - \xi_i^t] z_{i+}^t}{1+||a_i^t||^2} e_i, \\ h_i^t = 0: y_i^{t+1} = y_i^t, k_i^{t+1} = k_i^t, \end{array}\right. \quad (1.42)\)

where $\alpha_i^t$ is a step-size and the vector $a_i^t \in \partial J_i(v_i^t)$.

- **7. end for**

and $\hat{w}_{ii}^t = 1 - \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{in}}, j \neq i} \hat{w}_{ij}^t > \rho, \forall i \in \mathcal{V}$, where $0 < \rho < 1$. Here, it is evident that each matrix $\hat{W}^t, t \ge 0$, possesses row-stochastic properties. To enhance readability and simplify the notation, the algorithms (1.41) and (1.42) designed to solve Eq. (1.16) can be presented as two cases by

\(p_i^{t+1} = h_i^t - \frac{[f_i(h_i^t)]_+ \xi_i^t}{||\xi_i^t||^2}, \quad h_i^t = 1, \quad (1.43a)\)

## 1.5.2 Convergence analysis for Algorithm 2

In this subsection, we delve into examining the convergence properties of Algorithm 2. To lay the foundation for our subsequent analysis, we introduce a crucial lemma that serves as a pivotal element in supporting our primary conclusions.

**Lemma 1.6.** [32] Under the validity of Assumption 1.3, we consider the weighted matrix $\hat{W}^t$. For any $t$ greater than or equal to $s$, we define the product matrix $\hat{W}^{t:s}$ as the consecutive multiplication of matrices from $\hat{W}^{t-1}$ to $\hat{W}^s$, with the convention that $\hat{W}^{s:s}$ is the identity matrix $I_n$. The entries of $\hat{W}^{t:s}$ are denoted by $\hat{w}_{ij}^{t:s}$. Furthermore, there exists a normalized vector $\pi^t$, satisfying the condition $(\pi^t)^T 1_n = 1$, such that $\forall t \ge 0$,

- (i) There exist constants $H > 0$ and $0 < \lambda < 1$ such that the absolute difference between $\hat{w}_{ij}^{t:s}$ and $\pi_j^s$ is bounded by $H(\lambda)^{t-s}$ for any pair of indices $i, j$ belonging to the set $\mathcal{V}$ and for all $t$ greater than or equal to $s$.
- (ii) There is a constant $\varrho \ge (\rho)^{(n-1)B}$ such that $\pi_i^t \ge \varrho$ for any $i \in \mathcal{V}$ and $t \ge 0$.
- (iii) $(\pi^{t'})^T = (\pi^{t+1})^T \hat{W}^t$.

In the subsequent lemma, we establish a comparable relationship akin to Lemma 1.5 presented in Section 1.4. This relationship serves as a foundation for deriving our primary conclusion in the forthcoming analysis.

**Lemma 1.7.** Assuming the validity of Assumptions 1.1 and 1.3, we can assert that for any given $\epsilon$ satisfying $0 < \epsilon < 1/2$, there exists a threshold $\hat{t} = \hat{t}(\epsilon, n)$ (sufficiently large) such that for all time indices $t$ greater than or equal to $\hat{t}$, any element $v$ belonging to the set $S_0$, and any node $i$ from the set $\mathcal{V}$,

\[
\sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^{t+1} \mathbb{E}_t \left[ || p_i^{t+1} - v ||^2 \right]
\le (1 + \alpha_3 / t^{3/2-\epsilon}) \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^t || p_i^t - v ||^2 - \hat{\eta}_{\min} \beta \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^{t+1} || \phi_i^t - \phi_i^t ||^2
- \frac{2}{t} b^T (\bar{\phi}^t - v) + \left[ \frac{\alpha_3}{t^{3/2-\epsilon}} + \alpha_2(1 + 4v + \tau) / t^2 \right] ||b||^2,
\]

where $\bar{\phi}^t = \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^{t+1} \phi_i^t$, $\tau > 1$, $v > \delta\tau D^2 / 4(\tau-1)$, and $\beta = (\tau-1)/(\delta\tau D^2) - 1/4v > 0$ for certain $\delta > 0$.

**Proof.** (i) $h_i^t = 1$: Drawing upon the proof of Lemma 1.5, we can deduce that for any node $i \in \mathcal{V}$,

\[
||p_i^{t+1} - v||^2 + \beta||\phi_i^t - \phi_i^t||^2
\le (1 + \frac{\alpha_3}{t^{3/2-\epsilon}}) \sum_{j=1}^n \hat{w}_{ij}^t ||p_j^t - v||^2 - \frac{2}{t\eta_i} b^T(\phi_i^t - v)
+ \frac{\alpha_3||b||^2}{t^{3/2-\epsilon}} + \frac{\alpha_2(1 + 4v + \tau)||b||^2}{t^2}, \quad (1.44)
\]

where $\beta$ is defined as $\beta = \frac{\tau-1}{\delta\tau D^2} - \frac{1}{4v}$, where $\tau$ and $v$ are adjustable factors. Here, we make the assumption that $v > \frac{\delta\tau D^2}{4(\tau-1)}$ and $\tau > 1$, both of which ensure that $\beta > 0$.

(ii) $h_i^t = 0$: For any $i \in \mathcal{V}$, it derives from (1.43b) that for any $v \in \Xi_0$,

\[
||p_i^{t+1} - v||^2 = ||\sum_{j=1}^n \hat{w}_{ij}^t p_j^t - v||^2
\le (1 + \frac{\alpha_3}{t^{3/2-\epsilon}}) \sum_{j=1}^n \hat{w}_{ij}^t ||p_j^t - v||^2. \quad (1.45)
\]

By integrating the outcomes of Eqs. (1.44) and (1.45) and applying conditional expectation with respect to $\mathcal{F}^t$, we arrive at the following conclusion:

\[
\mathbb{E}_t [||p_i^{t+1} - v||^2]
\le (1 + \frac{\alpha_3}{t^{3/2-\epsilon}}) \sum_{j=1}^n \hat{w}_{ij}^t ||p_j^t - v||^2 - \frac{2}{t} b^T(\phi_i^t - v) + \frac{\alpha_3||b||^2}{t^{3/2-\epsilon}}
- \eta_i \beta ||\phi_i^t - \phi_i^t||^2 + \frac{\alpha_2(1 + 4v + \tau)||b||^2}{t^2}. \quad (1.46)
\]

By multiplying $\pi_i^{t+1}$ on both sides of Eq. (1.46) and summing the resulting expressions for all $i$ ranging from 1 to $n$, we obtain the following relation:

\[
\sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^{t+1} \mathbb{E}_t [||p_i^{t+1} - v||^2]
\le (1 + \frac{\alpha_3}{t^{3/2-\epsilon}}) \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^{t+1} \sum_{j=1}^n \hat{w}_{ij}^t ||p_j^t - v||^2
- \hat{\eta}_{\min}\beta \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^{t+1} ||\phi_i^t - \phi_i^t||^2 - \frac{2}{t} \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^{t+1} b^T(\phi_i^t - v)
+ \left[ \frac{\alpha_3}{t^{3/2-\epsilon}} + \frac{\alpha_2(1 + 4v + \tau)}{t^2} \right] ||b||^2. \quad (1.47)
\]

By leveraging the identity $(\pi^t)^\mathrm{T} = (\pi^{t+1})^\mathrm{T} \hat{W}^t$ as stated in Lemma 1.6 (iii), it suffices to demonstrate that

\[
\sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^{t+1} \sum_{j=1}^n \hat{w}_{ij}^t || p_j^t - v ||^2 = \sum_{j=1}^n || p_j^t - v ||^2 \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^{t+1} \hat{w}_{ij}^t
= \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^t || p_i^t - v ||^2 . \quad (1.48)
\]

Let $\tilde{\phi}^t$ denote the weighted sum of $z^t$ using the probabilities $\pi^{t+1}$: $\tilde{\phi}^t = (\pi^{t+1})^\mathrm{T} z^t = \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^{t+1} \phi_i^t$. Then, we can establish the relationship $\sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^{t+1} b^\mathrm{T} (\phi_i^t - v) = b^\mathrm{T} (\tilde{\phi}^t - v)$. By combining this result with Eqs. (1.47) and (1.48), we can directly deduce the conclusion stated in Lemma 1.7, thereby completing the proof. $\square$

Moving forward, we derive the key outcomes of Algorithm 2 by utilizing the findings presented in Lemma 1.7.

**Theorem 1.2.** Given Algorithm 2, under the validity of Assumptions 1.1 and 1.3, for any $\varepsilon$ satisfying $0 < \varepsilon < 1/2$, there exists a threshold value $\hat{t} = \hat{t}(\varepsilon, n)$ (sufficiently large) such that, for all $t \ge \hat{t}$ and $i \in \mathcal{V}$, the probabilities $p_i^t$ converge almost surely to $p^*$ and the variables $x_i^t$ converge almost surely to $x_i^*$.

_Proof._ By setting $v$ equal to $p^*$ in Lemma 1.7, we immediately obtain the following result:

\[
\sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^{t+1} \mathbb{E}_t \left[ || p_i^{t+1} - p^* ||^2 \right]
\le (1 + \frac{\alpha_3}{t^{3/2-\varepsilon}}) \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^t || p_i^t - p^* ||^2 - \hat{\eta}_{min}\beta \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^{t+1} || \phi_i^t - \phi_i^t ||^2
- \frac{2}{t} b^\mathrm{T} (\tilde{\phi}^t - p^*) + \left[ \frac{\alpha_3}{t^{3/2-\varepsilon}} + \alpha_2(1+4v+\tau) / t^2 \right] ||b||^2 . \quad (1.49)
\]

Based on the proofs of Theorem 1.1 and the individual terms in Eq. (1.49), it is evident that all the prerequisites of Lemma 1.3 are met. Consequently, applying Lemma 1.3 yields the conclusion that

\[
\left\{ \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^t || p_i^t - p^* ||^2 \right\} \text{ converges a.s. as } t \to \infty, \quad (1.50a)
\]
\[
\sum_{t=0}^\infty -\frac{2}{t} b^\mathrm{T} (\tilde{\phi}^t - p^*) < \infty \quad a.s. , \quad (1.50b)
\]
\[
\sum_{t=0}^\infty \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i^{t+1} || \phi_i^t - \phi_i^t ||^2 < \infty \quad a.s. . \quad (1.50c)
