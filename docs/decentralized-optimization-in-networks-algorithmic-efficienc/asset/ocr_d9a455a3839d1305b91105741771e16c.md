$y_{i,k}$ to the dual variable $z_{i,k}$ transmitted in the network, *i.e.*,

$$\tilde{z}_{i,k} = z_{i,k} + Y_{i,k},$$ (5.6)

where $Y_{i,k}$ follows a Laplace distribution denoted as $\mathcal{L}(0, b_k)$ with the parameter $b_k$. Subsequently, node $i$ forwards $A_{ji}\tilde{z}_{i,k}$ and $A_{ji}y_{i,k}$ to each node $j$ belonging to its outbound neighbor set $\mathcal{N}_i^{\text{out}}$. Upon receiving $A_{ij}y_{j,k}$ and $A_{ij}\tilde{z}_{j,k}$ from nodes $j$ in its inbound neighbor set $\mathcal{N}_i^{\text{in}}$, node $i$ integrates this newly acquired data and updates its dual variable $z_{i,k+1}$ by incorporating a cumulative rescaled subgradient, specifically $g_{i,k}/\gamma_{ii,k}$:

$$z_{i,k+1} = \sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij}\tilde{z}_{j,k} + \frac{g_{i,k}}{y_{ii,k}},$$ (5.7)

where $g_{i,k}$ belongs to the subgradient of $f_{i,k}$ at $x_{i,k}$, while $y_{ii,k}$ represents the $i$th component of $y_{i,k}$. This estimated variable addresses the imbalance inherent in directed networks and undergoes an update through a consensus process as outlined below:

$$y_{i,k+1} = \sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij}y_{j,k}.$$ (5.8)

Employing the operator $\Pi_\Omega^h(\cdot)$, the primal variable $x_{i,k+1}$ is ultimately derived,

$$x_{i,k+1} = \Pi_\Omega^h(z_{i,k+1}, \alpha_k),$$ (5.9)

where $\alpha_k$ is a positive and non-increasing step-size. Algorithm 1 provides an outline of the pseudo-code described above.

**Remark 5.2.** The strategy of rescaling gradients is widely used to alleviate the imbalance problem that occurs in directed networks. Numerous empirical studies [23,32,47,50] have shown the effectiveness and practicality of this approach. Our proposed decentralized algorithm integrates the gradient rescaling technique into the decentralized online dual averaging method, making it efficient for addressing general online constrained optimization challenges in directed networks.

**Remark 5.3.** Compared to the zero-sum correlated perturbation approach mentioned in references [45,55], the differential privacy method we are examining possesses distinct characteristics: (i) At every time instant $k$, the differential privacy approach merely necessitates that each node independently produces random noise, eliminating the need for nodes to synchronize their perturbations to maintain a network balance (zero-sum), as required by the zero-sum correlated perturbation technique [45,55]. (ii) While the differential privacy approach does influence the algorithm’s convergence, it is crucial to remember that when employing a decentralized online algorithm for online learning tasks, convergence is only achieved to the proximity of the optimal solution due to fluctuating cost functions. Furthermore, differential privacy boasts a more comprehensive theoretical analysis. This justifies the preference for differential privacy methods in the realm of online learning.
