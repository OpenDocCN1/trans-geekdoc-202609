### 6.5.3 Decentralized targets tracking

In this example, our primary focus revolves around a dynamic target tracking challenge, inspired by the work presented in [29], where a collaborative effort among sensors is essential to monitor several moving entities. Recognizing the inherent nature of real-world scenarios, wherein sensors exchange information in a unidirectional fashion, the ensuing communication network architecture is inherently asymmetric. These attributes align perfectly with the operational context of Algorithm 1. Consider a network comprising 5 interconnected nodes, characterized by its temporal and directional information flow. Specifically, each target, denoted as \( z_{l,t}^* = [z_{l,t}^{*,(1)}, z_{l,t}^{*,(2)}] \in \mathbb{R}^2, l \in \{1, 2, 3\} \), is portrayed in the following manner:

```
\( z_{l,t}^* = \begin{bmatrix} z_{l,t}^{*,(1)} \\ z_{l,t}^{*,(2)} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} z_{l,t}^{*,(1)} \\ \dot{z}_{l,t}^{*,(1)} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} v_l \sin(\gamma_l t + \rho_l) \\ \gamma_l v_l \cos(\gamma_l t + \rho_l) \end{bmatrix}, \)
```

where \( z_{l,t}^{*,(1)} \) and \( \dot{z}_{l,t}^{*,(1)} \) denote the location and speed of target \( l \) at time \( t \) respectively. \( v_l \) signifies the amplitude, \( \gamma_l \) indicates the angular frequency and \( \rho_l \) designates the phase of target \( l \). At instant \( t \), each node utilizes \( \phi_t^i = \psi^i z_t^* \) as an indicator to document its advancements in the tracking process, where \( z_t^* = [(z_{1,t}^*)^T, (z_{2,t}^*)^T, (z_{3,t}^*)^T]^T \) and \( \psi^i \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 6} \) denotes a randomly devised measurement vector. The nodes collectively strive to diminish the universal cost function \( f_t(x) = \sum_{i=1}^5 ||\psi^i x - \phi_t^i|| / 2 \) under the constraint \( x \in [-2, 2]^6 \) across the temporal span \( T \), aiming to effectively track dynamic targets.

In our simulations, the stochastic variables, denoted as \( v_l \) and \( \rho_l \), for \( l \in \{1, 2, 3\} \), adhere to uniform distributions spanning \( [0, 2] \) and \( [0, \pi] \), respectively. We establish a sampling rate of 100 Hz. Analogous to the prior instance, we evaluate the efficacy of Algorithm 1 with \( \alpha_t = \frac{1}{4\sqrt{t+1}} \) and \( \delta = d/\sqrt{T} \) as parameters. Subsequently, the results are illustrated in Figs. 6.9 and 6.10. Fig. 6.9 reveals that node 1 (randomly selected) is capable of partially tracking three dynamic targets, and underscores the detrimental influence of the \( b_t \) value on tracking accuracy. Furthermore, Fig. 6.10 emphasizes that the individual average regret, \( \bar{R}_{\text{avg}}^j(T) \), \( j \in \{1, 2, \cdots, 5\} \), converges asymptotically at a sublinear pace over the duration \( T \), which aligns with the theoretical findings presented in Theorem 6.1. Here, \( \bar{R}_{\text{avg}}^j(T) = \bar{R}_T^j / T \).

## 6.6 Conclusion

In this chapter, we have crafted an efficient zeroth-order decentralized dual averaging methodology, termed ZO-DDA, tailored for optimizing privacy-sensitive online constrained optimization tasks across time-evolving directed networks. Instead of relying on precise subgradient computations, we harness a zeroth-order oracle. ZO-DDA employs specially crafted row-stochastic matrices to rescale the zeroth-order oracle with an auxiliary parameter, thereby mitigating the imbalance stemming from the dynamic directed network topology. Additionally, to uphold differential privacy, ZO-DDA incorporates perturbations infused with Laplace noise. Our theoretical investigations establish that ZO-DDA, under reasonable assumptions, attains a desired sublinear regret while safeguarding the differential privacy of each node’s loss function. We uncover the intricate balance between privacy preservation and optimization precision inherent in our algorithm. Through extensive simulations, we validate the efficacy of ZO-DDA. Looking ahead, we aim to enhance the communication and computational efficiency of our approach. Moreover, extending our findings to decentralized online optimization frameworks encompassing generalized (inequality or equality) constraints presents an intriguing direction for future research.
