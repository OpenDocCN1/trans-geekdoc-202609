# Privacy-preserving decentralized dual averaging push algorithms with correlated perturbations

# 7.1 Introduction

In recent years, advancements in communication and sensing technologies within networked control systems have naturally led to a variety of optimization problems across networks [1,2]. Traditional methods typically rely on central intelligent devices for storage, communication, and computation, which are costly, lack robustness in new environments, and are vulnerable to single points of failure [3]. Decentralized approaches, which minimize long-distance communication, offer advantages such as enhanced robustness, reduced communication needs, and improved privacy over centralized methods. They achieve this by breaking down large-scale problems into smaller subproblems, applicable in areas like large-scale data analysis [4,5], wireless sensor networks [6,7], and information control [8,9].

# 7.1.1 Literature review

Recently, numerous decentralized algorithms have been developed to effectively solve optimization problems [10–16]. Algorithms that only use gradient information are frequently reported due to their scalability and performance [1,2]. Early works on decentralized gradient descent (DGD) [10,11] and their extensions address various problem factors and targets [12–16], demonstrating their effectiveness in decentralized optimization over networks [10–16]. For instance, [14] introduced a decentralized primal-dual algorithm achieving optimal allocation in integrated energy systems, while [15] explored fixed-time decentralized optimal resource management for microgrids. Further, decentralized dual averaging methods have been extensively studied [17–22]. The work in [17] first presented a decentralized dual averaging algorithm for constrained optimization over undirected networks, inspiring many efficient decentralized approaches for acceleration [18,19], online learning [20], and improved efficiency [21,22].
