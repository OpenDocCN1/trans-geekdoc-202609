In this experiment, we utilized the COVTYPE [54], MNIST [55], and BREAST [54] datasets to assess the convergence speed and processing efficiency of the P-DVR algorithm. It is important to mention that we standardized all three datasets and randomly picked two categories of data, specifically class -1 and class 1, from each dataset. Regarding the amount of data chosen for training and testing, we drew 8000 and 100 entries from the COVTYPE dataset, respectively; 1000 and 100 entries from the MNIST dataset, respectively; and 440 and 100 entries from the BREAST dataset, respectively. It is worth clarifying that the reason for selecting varying data volumes across datasets stems from practical considerations: the limited memory capacity of our experimental equipment precluded loading excessive amounts of high-dimensional data, such as the MNIST dataset, or due to the inherently small data size within certain datasets, as in the case of the BREAST dataset.

We conducted simulations with 20 nodes, evenly distributing the aforementioned datasets among them. We set the parameter $\alpha$ to 0.01 and established the closed convex set constraint as $\Omega_0 = [-1, 5]^p$ (where $p$ represents the dataset’s dimension). The step-size was carefully adjusted to ensure optimal performance. Utilizing Metropolis rules, we generated a doubly stochastic matrix linked to an undirected geometric graph. For evaluation, we calculated the squared error using the formula $\|x_k - 1_m x^{*\top}\|^2 / \|x_0 - 1_m x^{*\top}\|^2$, where $x^* \in \mathbb{R}^p$ determined via a centralized algorithm, and $x_k \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times p}$ signifies the decision variable during the $k$th network iteration. Figs. 3.2, 3.3, and 3.4 present our numerical simulation results, confirming the P-DVR algorithm’s linear convergence speed, processing efficiency, and precision.
