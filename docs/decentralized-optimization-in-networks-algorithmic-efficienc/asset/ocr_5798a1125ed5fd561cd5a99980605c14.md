# 5.3 Algorithm construction

In this section, we propose a decentralized algorithm for online optimization, tailored to address the problem described in (5.1).

# 5.3.1 A general dual averaging approach

Initially, we introduce the conventional dual averaging method outlined in [25]. Define the projection operator $\Pi_\Omega^h(v, \alpha)$ as follows:

```
$$ \Pi_\Omega^h(v, \alpha) = \arg \min_{u \in \Omega} \left\{ \langle v, u \rangle + \frac{h(u)}{\alpha} \right\}, $$ (5.4)
```

where $\alpha > 0$, $v$ belongs to $\mathcal{R}^m$, and assuming $h(u)$ is a proximal function exhibiting 1-strongly convex characteristics, satisfying the conditions $h(0) = 0$ and $h(u) > 0$ for all $u = u \in \Omega$ [25,29,49]. Then, the dual averaging method can be employed to resolve the problem stated in (5.1) over a shared constraint set denoted as $\Omega$ (as described in [25]). Specifically, for every $i$ belonging to the set $\mathcal{V}$, the algorithm proceeds as follows:

```
$$
z_{i, k+1} = z_{i, k} + g_{i, k},
$$
$$
x_{i, k+1} = \Pi_\Omega^h(z_{i, k+1}, \alpha_k),
$$ (5.5)
```

where $g_{i, k}$ belongs to the subgradient of $f_i$ at $x_{i, k}$, while $\alpha_k$ represents a non-increasing step-size greater than zero. The update $x_{i, k+1}$ is obtained by projecting $z_{i, k+1}$ onto the constraint set denoted as $\Omega$. Through the introduction of a dual variable, $z_{i, t}$, and the flexible selection of the proximal function $h$ within the projection operator $\Pi_\Omega^h$, the dual averaging method emerges as an efficient strategy for addressing nonsmooth optimization problems with constraints, as referenced in [25]. Nevertheless, it is important to observe that these methodologies are inadequate for resolving the privacy-sensitive decentralized online optimization challenges associated with directed networks, which constitute the focus of our study.
