This is the primary motivation for the next subsection.

> **Remark 7.2.** The push-sum protocol is the most commonly used method to address the imbalance caused by time-varying directed networks and has been proven to be efficient and practical in numerous studies, such as [11,26–28,31,36]. The decentralized dual averaging push algorithm integrates the push-sum protocol into the decentralized dual averaging method, making it effective for solving general constrained optimization problems over time-varying directed networks.

## 7.3.2 Privacy-protected decentralized dual averaging push algorithm

It is important to note that not all nodes in the network are benign; some may be malicious. While adhering to the update rules, malicious nodes may intentionally gather information sent by normal nodes and collude to access local subgradients containing critical data about the local cost functions of normal nodes. Additionally, frequent information transmission among nodes can lead to privacy exposure. Therefore our aim is to ensure all nodes in the network can accurately agree on the optimal solution while safeguarding the private information of normal nodes.

Our algorithm operates as follows. At time \( k \), each node \( i \) maintains three local variables: a primal variable \( x_i(k) \in \Omega \), a state variable \( h_i(k) \in \mathbb{R}^d \), and an auxiliary variable \( q_i(k) \in \mathbb{R} \), initialized with \( x_i(1) \in \Omega \), \( h_i(1) \in \mathbb{R}^d \), and \( q_i(1) = 1 \). At time \( k \ge 1 \), each node \( i \) first utilizes a correlated perturbation mechanism inspired by [39,40] to generate a series of correlated random noises \(\{\tau_{ji}(k)\}_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{out}}(k)}\) and calculates the noise \(\tau_{ii}(k)\) as follows:

\(\tau_{ii}(k) = - \frac{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{out}}(k)} P_{ji}(k) \tau_{ji}(k)}{P_{ii}(k)}.\) (7.3)

Then, the state variable \(h_i(k)\) is perturbed by the correlated random noises \(\tau_{ji}(k)\), \(j \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{out}}(k)\), i.e.,

\(\tilde{h}_{ji}(k) = h_i(k) + \tau_{ji}(k).\) (7.4)

Node \(i\) then transmits \(P_{ji}(k)\tilde{h}_{ji}(k)\) and \(P_{ji}(k)q_i(k)\) to its out-neighbors \(j \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{out}}(k)\). Upon receiving \(P_{ij}(k)\tilde{h}_{ij}(k)\) and \(P_{ij}(k)q_j(k)\) from its in-neighbors \(j \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{in}}(k)\), node \(i\) aggregates the information and updates its state variable \(h_i(k+1)\) (by accumulating the subgradient \(g_i(k) \in \partial f_i(x_i(k))\)) and auxiliary variable \(q_i(k+1)\) as follows:

\(h_i(k+1) = \sum_{j=1}^v P_{ij}(k)\tilde{h}_{ij}(k) + g_i(k),\) (7.5a)
\(q_i(k+1) = \sum_{j=1}^v P_{ij}(k)q_j(k).\) (7.5b)

Using the projection operator \(\Pi_\Omega^\varphi(\cdot)\), the primal variable \(x_i(k+1)\) is obtained. Finally, each node \(i\) calculates its local average \(\hat{x}_i(k)\) (local estimated optimal solution). The detailed update process, at time \(k \ge 1\), is as follows:

\(x_i(k+1) = \Pi_\Omega^\varphi(\frac{h_i(k+1)}{q_i(k+1)}, \gamma(k)),\) (7.6)

where \(\gamma(k) > 0\) is a decreasing step-size specified in the following section. We summarize the above in Algorithm 1.

The noises in the network comply with the following condition:

\(\sum_{j=1}^v P_{ji}(k)\tau_{ji}(k) = 0,\) (7.7)

where \(\tau_{ji}(k)\) equals 0 if node \(j\) is not part of the set \(\mathcal{N}_i^{\text{out}}(k) \cup i\). Due to the edge-based and locally balanced nature of the added noises, as expressed in (7.7), nodes in the network are not required to coordinate in generating the noises, offering more flexibility in noise design compared to node-based methods in [35,43]. Additionally, the noises \(\tau_{ji}(k)\), for \(j \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{in}}(k)\), can be chosen such that

\(\tau_{ji}(k) \in [-\eta\delta_i/d, \eta\delta_i/d]^d, \forall i \in \mathcal{V},\)

**Remark 7.3.** Algorithm 1 employs edge-based correlated perturbations and the push-sum averaging protocol to safeguard the privacy of normal nodes and address the imbalance of time-varying directed networks. From (7.4) and (7.5a), it follows that malicious nodes will receive inaccurate information, preventing them from deducing the local subgradients of normal nodes. Moreover, the condition derived from (7.7) is essential for ensuring the accurate convergence of Algorithm 1.

**Remark 7.4.** Recently, privacy protection has garnered significant attention, becoming a key area of research due to the need for decentralized processing of big data. However, existing privacy-protected decentralized algorithms still have room for improvement. For instance, homomorphic encryption-based decentralized algorithms [32,33] entail high computational complexity, differentially private decentralized algorithms [12,34–37,43] fail to ensure precise convergence, and correlated noise-based decentralized algorithms [39,40] require undirected or balanced networks. In contrast to [12,32–37,39,40,43], Algorithm 1 boasts three notable features: (i) Privacy protection through edge-based correlated perturbations; (ii) Suitability for time-varying directed networks via push-sum averaging protocols; (iii) Accurate convergence under weaker initial conditions, as ensured by condition (7.7).
