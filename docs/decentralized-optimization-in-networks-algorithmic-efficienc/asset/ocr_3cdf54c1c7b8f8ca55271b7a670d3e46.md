# ### 6.5.1 Dynamic sparse signal recovery

Specifically, our objective revolves around addressing the challenge of recovering dynamic sparse signals, focusing on estimating a sparse parameter in a decentralized fashion. The central mission of all sensors is to collaboratively identify the vector \(x^*\) for \(x\) across the period \(T\), in a manner that minimizes the ensuing global cost function:

\(f(x) = \sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{i=1}^n ||z_t^i - P_t^i x||^2 + \rho ||x||_1,\)

where \(x \in \Omega, P_t^i \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times m}\) represents the observation matrix of sensor \(i\) at time \(t\), \(\rho\) denotes a fixed regularization coefficient, and \(z_t^i \in \mathbb{R}^d\) signifies the measurement obtained as \(z_t^i = P_t^i \varpi_t + \varrho_t^i\) with \(\varpi_t \in \mathbb{R}^m\) being the dynamic sparse signal of interest and \(\varrho_t^i \in \mathbb{R}^d\) represents the noise component. For this illustration, we establish \(n = 20\), \(d = 2\), \(m = 2\), and set the regularization parameter as \(\rho = \frac{1}{40}\).
