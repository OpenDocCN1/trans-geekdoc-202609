**Remark 8.1.** Problem (8.1) represents a general EDP in smart grids, extensively studied in recent works [23–28]. As with these studies, this chapter does not address network loss and node voltage constraints but focuses on designing new algorithms to achieve specific goals. Additionally, it assumes that each generator or load device is considered individually in the economic dispatch of smart grids. Therefore solving problem (8.1) involves optimizing the power generated by each generator to minimize the total generation cost while meeting the total load demand and generation capacity.

# **8.2.2 System model**

In this chapter, each generator is assigned to a bus within the bus system of smart grids. The communication among these generators (buses) is modeled as an unbalanced directed network, denoted as \(\mathcal{G} = \{\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}\}\), where \(\mathcal{V} = \{1, 2, \dots, m\}\) represents the set of vertices (generators) and \(\mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{V} \times \mathcal{V}\) represents the set of edges. An edge \((j, i) \in \mathcal{E}\) indicates that generator \(j\) can directly transmit data to generator \(i\), making \(i\) an out-neighbor of \(j\), and vice versa, \(j\) an in-neighbor of \(i\). We define \(\mathcal{N}_i^{\text{out}} = \{j \in \mathcal{V} | (i, j) \in \mathcal{E}\}\) and \(\mathcal{N}_i^{\text{in}} = \{j \in \mathcal{V} | (j, i) \in \mathcal{E}\}\). If there exists an \(i\) such that \(\mathcal{N}_i^{\text{out}} \neq \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{in}}\), then the network \(\mathcal{G}\) is considered unbalanced. Strong connectivity in a directed network implies that there are directed paths connecting any pair of generators. To facilitate communication among generators, we adopt the following assumption regarding the communication network (system model) [24,32,40].

**Assumption 8.2.** The directed network \(\mathcal{G}\) is strongly connected.

This assumption ensures that each generator can directly or indirectly influence all other generators in the network. Although this assumption is more restrictive than the uniformly and strongly connected assumption, it remains broadly applicable [24, 32,37].

**Remark 8.2.** In real-world economic dispatch scenarios of smart grids, data exchange between neighboring generators often occurs in less-than-perfect environments, posing privacy risks. Therefore it is critical to safeguard the privacy of generators in decentralized networks. Current privacy-preserving techniques, such as differential privacy [31–33] and homomorphic encryption [34,35], significantly impact the performance (in terms of convergence and computational cost) of decentralized economic dispatch algorithms. Furthermore, not all transmitted information necessitates feedback, i.e., the network is directed, and practical solutions must accommodate this scenario. Consequently, this chapter aims for the proposed algorithm to achieve three primary objectives under accurate convergence: (i) ensuring privacy protection; (ii) enhancing computational efficiency; (iii) applicability to an unbalanced directed network.

For problem (8.1), a generator's private information includes \(J_i(p_i)\), \(d_i\), \(p_i^{\min}\), and \(p_i^{\max}\), which are inaccessible to other generators. The ultimate goal of this chapter is to enable generators to interact with their respective neighbors over an unbalanced directed network to collaboratively and efficiently solve problem (8.1) without disclosing private information.
