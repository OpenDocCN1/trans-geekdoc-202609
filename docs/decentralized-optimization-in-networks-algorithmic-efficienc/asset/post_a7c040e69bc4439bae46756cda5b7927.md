## Differentially private decentralized dual averaging algorithms for online optimization in directed networks

### 5.1 Introduction

In recent years, there has been a rapid advancement in networked control systems and potential communication/sensing technologies, sparking a surge of interest in diverse network optimization challenges, as referenced in [1,2]. Traditionally, optimization problems are tackled using centralized smart devices to handle storage, communication, and computation tasks. However, these methods can be costly, inflexible to new scenarios, and prone to single points of failure, as noted in [3]. The decentralized network approach offers a parallel computing framework designed to minimize the collective sum of local loss functions distributed across a network of nodes, eliminating the need for a central coordinator, as described in [4]. This collaborative minimization method, relying on local communication and computation, significantly cuts down communication overhead on the parameter server. It finds widespread applications in areas such as wireless sensor networks [5,6], information control [7,8], large-scale data analysis [9,10], online learning [11,12], etc.

### 5.1.1 Literature review

Numerous real-world applications of decentralized optimization often encounter significant uncertainty, as referenced in [13]. One example is the consideration of online forecasting and estimation challenges in wireless networks, where sensor readings fluctuate due to interference and noise inherent in sensor networks. These issues can be addressed through an online optimization framework, where the functions linked to each node may evolve over time. This variation is only recognized by the nodes in retrospect, as mentioned in [14]. The decentralized aspect allows for the conversion of a large-scale problem into multiple smaller ones, while simultaneously maintaining inherent resilience against environmental uncertainties and communication failures. This makes decentralized online methods highly suitable for extensive networks.

Numerous methods exist, including prior works rooted in decentralized gradient descent, as cited in [15,16], along with their diverse extensions for handling constraints [17,18], applications in intricate networks [19,20], achieving efficiency [21,22], ensuring privacy [23,24]. These methods underscored the feasibility of approaching online optimization challenges through a decentralized network-based approach, as evidenced in [15–24].

Although previous works on online optimization problems have garnered attention, decentralized dual averaging methods have also undergone comprehensive examination. Additionally, when addressing real-world online optimization challenges, the network facilitating communication among nodes emerges as a critical aspect deserving careful consideration. Typically, information transfer tends to be unidirectional, resulting in an asymmetry within the communication network, as referenced in [26,27]. In light of this observation, various decentralized dual averaging techniques have emerged, grounded in several proven strategies, such as the push-sum method [28–30] and the surplus-based strategy [31]. In addition to these techniques aimed at addressing information transmission imbalances, the subgradient rescaling strategy, coupled with a consensus step for eigenvector estimation, proved beneficial [23,32]. This strategy ensured the incorporation of row-stochastic weights into the algorithms, simplifying decentralized implementation. Nevertheless, this particular strategy remained unexplored in the context of decentralized dual averaging methods, constituting the focal point of this chapter.

In recent times, due to the significant growth in data size, its widespread distribution, and rapid fluctuations, the ability to communicate and process data in real-time (or online) has become critically important to swiftly address user needs. However, given the inherent fragility of communication networks, there exists a risk that malevolent nodes could intercept information transmissions between nodes, leading to a high likelihood of information breaches [33,34]. Furthermore, handling vast quantities of data inherently poses a threat to user privacy. Consequently, it is imperative to prioritize the protection of sensitive node information within decentralized networks [35,36]. When it comes to preserving privacy in decentralized optimization contexts, data encryption emerges as a viable solution. It has been utilized to maintain the confidentiality of critical information in multiple applications [37–39]. Nevertheless, the associated expenses are understandably undesirable for networks with limited resources [35,36]. Differential privacy, as a compelling alternative, has garnered considerable attention owing to its rigorous mathematical foundation, proven security attributes, and ease of implementation, as detailed in [40–43]. The fundamental concept behind the differential privacy approach involves the introduction of noise, potentially elevating privacy levels albeit at the expense of compromising optimization precision. In this regard, research conducted in [44] investigated a differentially private decentralized online dual averaging algorithm by introducing Laplacian noise into the transmitted data. Subsequently, the study presented in [45] introduced a decentralized dual averaging algorithm that incorporates a correlated perturbation strategy to preserve node privacy.
