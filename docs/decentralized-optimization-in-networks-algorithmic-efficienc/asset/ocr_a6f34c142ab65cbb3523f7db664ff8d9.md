# Differentially private decentralized dual averaging algorithms for online optimization in directed networks

# 5.1 Introduction

In recent years, there has been a rapid advancement in networked control systems and potential communication/sensing technologies, sparking a surge of interest in diverse network optimization challenges, as referenced in [1,2]. Traditionally, optimization problems are tackled using centralized smart devices to handle storage, communication, and computation tasks. However, these methods can be costly, inflexible to new scenarios, and prone to single points of failure, as noted in [3]. The decentralized network approach offers a parallel computing framework designed to minimize the collective sum of local loss functions distributed across a network of nodes, eliminating the need for a central coordinator, as described in [4]. This collaborative minimization method, relying on local communication and computation, significantly cuts down communication overhead on the parameter server. It finds widespread applications in areas such as wireless sensor networks [5,6], information control [7,8], large-scale data analysis [9,10], online learning [11,12], etc.

# 5.1.1 Literature review

Numerous real-world applications of decentralized optimization often encounter significant uncertainty, as referenced in [13]. One example is the consideration of online forecasting and estimation challenges in wireless networks, where sensor readings fluctuate due to interference and noise inherent in sensor networks. These issues can be addressed through an online optimization framework, where the functions linked to each node may evolve over time. This variation is only recognized by the nodes in retrospect, as mentioned in [14]. The decentralized aspect allows for the conversion of a large-scale problem into multiple smaller ones, while simultaneously maintaining inherent resilience against environmental uncertainties and communication failures. This makes decentralized online methods highly suitable for extensive networks.
