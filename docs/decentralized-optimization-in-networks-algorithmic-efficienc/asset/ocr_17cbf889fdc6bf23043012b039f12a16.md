The assessment of online optimization algorithms’ efficacy inherently necessitates the consideration of regret as a pivotal measure. Herein, we introduce a traditional, individualized notion of regret of node \( j \in \{1, \cdots, n\} \), as detailed in [13]:

\( R_T^j = \sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{i=1}^n f_t^i(x_t^j) - \sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{i=1}^n f_t^i(x^*), \)

where \( x_t^j, j \in \{1, \cdots, n\} \) represents the localized variable corresponding to \( x \) at time \( t \) and the optimal decision over time horizon \( T \) is denoted by \( x^* = \arg\min_{x \in \Omega} \sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{i=1}^n f_t^i(x) \). Our current investigation involves incorporating random perturbations into the actual states of nodes to safeguard the privacy of participating entities. Consequently, attaining a precise, deterministic form of regret akin to the conventional regret defined in (6.2) becomes impractical. Instead, we resort to a regret metric that quantifies the discrepancy between the expected cost incurred by node \( j \)’s decisions \( x_t^j \) and the optimal cost across \( T \), as outlined in [10,25]:

\( \bar{R}_T^j = \mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{i=1}^n f_t^i(x_t^j)] - \mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{i=1}^n f_t^i(x^*)]. \)

The current focal point revolves around devising a potent decentralized dual averaging algorithm of the zeroth-order nature, ensuring that not merely does the regret expressed in (6.3) exhibit a sublinear trend concerning \( T \), specifically, satisfying the condition \( \lim_{T \to \infty} \bar{R}_T^j / T = 0, j \in \mathcal{V} \), but also guarantees robust safeguarding of the privacy rights of the participating nodes.

**Remark 6.1.** There exist two compelling motivations for contemplating bandit feedback. Firstly, numerous methods are devoid of gradient information accessibility, as highlighted in [30]. Secondly, calculating the function’s value proves significantly more straightforward than estimating its gradient, as evidenced by [32]. Fundamentally, bandit-based online optimization constitutes a gradient-free (or zeroth-order) approach to tackling online optimization challenges.

# 6.2.2 Communication network

The interaction between nodes can be envisioned as a dynamically evolving directed network, labeled \( \mathcal{G}_t = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}_t) \), comprising a set of nodes \( \mathcal{V} = \{1, 2, \dots, n\} \) and a time-dependent set of edges \( \mathcal{E}_t \subseteq \mathcal{V} \times \mathcal{V} \) for all \( t \ge 1 \).
