## Event-triggered decentralized accelerated algorithms for economic dispatch in networks

## 2.1 Introduction

As society and technology rapidly progress, several domains, including computing, communication, and control, are confronting the growing challenge of ever-increasing data volumes and computational demands [1,2]. Traditional centralized approaches may struggle to cope with such intricate and expansive problems due to their inherent limitations. In response, distributed algorithms have emerged as a viable solution. These algorithms have garnered considerable attention in various real-world applications, such as smart grids [3,4], sensor networks [5,6], and machine learning [7,8], owing to their capacity to facilitate collaborative and coordinated problem-solving among multiple entities. Recognizing the benefits of decentralized algorithms in terms of robustness, adaptability, and scalability, researchers have explored their potential in addressing the economic dispatch problem (EDP) [9,10].

### 2.1.1 Literature review

The Economic Dispatch Problem (EDP) holds a fundamental and crucial position in the grid economy, aiming to facilitate generators in adhering to their generation capacity limitations while minimizing overall generation costs and fulfilling the total generation demand. Currently, numerous studies have delved into decentralized algorithms for addressing the EDP [11–16]. Zhang and Chow [11] introduced an algorithm known as ICC, facilitating a decentralized approach to EDP. However, a significant limitation emerged: maintaining the supply–demand equilibrium necessitated a leader, whose selection significantly influenced the convergence rate. Subsequently, Yang et al. [12] devised methods to eliminate the reliance on a leader. Additionally, Hug et al. [13] presented a “consensus + innovations” strategy, where the mismatch between demand and supply served as the innovative term for each generator’s consensus variable. The references [14–16] tackled EDP from diverse perspectives. Nevertheless, the aforementioned algorithms [7,11–16] rely on decaying step-sizes to attain optimal solutions, thereby resulting in a sluggish convergence.

To enhance the efficiency of processing the Economic Dispatch Problem (EDP), novel algorithms with fixed step-sizes have been formulated [17–20]. Doan and Olshevsky [17] proposed a fully decentralized method that demonstrated linear convergence towards the optimal economic dispatch, provided a constant step-size was selected within a predefined maximum limit. Subsequently, Zhang et al. [18] presented a decentralized Lagrangian algorithm employing a fixed step-size. Unlike the approach in [17], the upper bound in [18] is solely dependent on the cost functions and the network topology of the generators, rather than the total number of generators. As research on EDP progresses, relevant studies are broadening to cover areas such as electric vehicles [21,22], privacy safeguarding [23,24], communication latency [25,26], transmission losses [27], wind power generation [28,29], and numerous other facets. Notably, the heavy-ball method and the Nesterov momentum term are widely adopted gradient-based acceleration strategies [30–33]. To tackle the Economic Dispatch Problem (EDP), researchers have opted to integrate solutions within smart grids. Wu et al. [34] introduced the FDLFDM algorithm, which leveraged the Nesterov momentum term to expedite convergence towards the optimal economic allocation. Notably, all the referenced studies [30–34] solely employed a single momentum term. However, Lü et al. [35] introduced the innovative D-DLM algorithm, which incorporated two distinct momentum terms. In contrast to algorithms lacking momentum terms, D-DLM empowered generators to garner more information from neighboring entities, resulting in enhanced convergence results.

Nowadays, numerous studies have employed time-triggered approaches to address practical challenges [36–38]. Nevertheless, practical implementation reveals that implementing a time-triggered scheme amplifies the communication load and computational demands. It is crucial to note that generators’ communication resources are significantly depleted during actual interactions, thus this mechanism might not be ideal for addressing the Economic Dispatch Problem (EDP). To address this, various decentralized event-triggered methods have emerged in recent literature [39–43], aiming to restrict communication between generators to specific sampling instances, thereby minimizing losses and cost savings. Dong et al. [39], for instance, introduced an event-triggered algorithm with dynamic constant step-sizes, effectively conserving communication resources and alleviating network strain. Subsequently, Ji et al. [40] proposed a distinct event-triggered strategy, integrating it into the decentralized gradient tracking algorithm to achieve similar benefits. Li et al. [41], on the other hand, presented a novel method leveraging asynchronous communication from a multi-energy system perspective. The Zeno-like behavior, which was not addressed in [39,40], was subsequently disregarded in [2,3,41,42]. However, these studies overlooked the momentum terms associated with accelerated convergence. Zhang et al. [43] addressed this gap by introducing an algorithm called ET-DAPDA, which incorporated two momentum term variants. This algorithm not only reduced the number of interactions but also achieved an accelerated linear convergence rate.
