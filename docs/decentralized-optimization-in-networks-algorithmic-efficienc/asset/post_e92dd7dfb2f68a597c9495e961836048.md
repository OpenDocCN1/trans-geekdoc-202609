## Differentially private decentralized zeroth-order algorithms for online optimization in dynamic networks

### 6.1 Introduction

In recent times, the realm of potential communication and sensing technologies within networked control systems has undergone swift advancements, inevitably presenting a myriad of optimization challenges across networks [1,2]. Traditional methodologies for tackling these issues often rely heavily on centralized intelligent systems for storage, communication, and computational processes, leading to high costs, diminished adaptability to novel environments, and heightened vulnerability to single-point failures [3]. By contrast, decentralized algorithms that mitigate the need for long-distance communication, offer several advantages over centralized algorithms, primarily due to their ability to partition extensive problems into manageable subsets. The decentralized algorithms boast enhanced robustness, reduced communication overhead, and improved privacy, thereby enabling their widespread adoption in diverse applications, including large-scale data analytics [4,5], information control systems [6,7], smart grid management [8,9], and online learning frameworks [10,11], among numerous others.

### 6.1.1 Literature review

Numerous real-world instances of decentralized optimization, nonetheless, are characterized by profound uncertainty [12], a prime example being the handling of online forecasting and estimation challenges within wireless networks. These challenges can be effectively addressed within the online optimization framework, where the functionalities associated with individual nodes are subject to temporal fluctuations, with such variations only discernible by the nodes in retrospect [13]. A diverse array of efficient methodologies exists for tackling online optimization problems, encompassing the seminal contributions centered on decentralized gradient descent algorithms [14,15], alongside their multifaceted extensions aimed at resolving constraints [16,17], adapting to intricate network topologies [10,13], enhancing efficiency [18,19], and safeguarding privacy [21], among others.

Expanding upon the existing research on online optimization, methodologies grounded in decentralized dual averaging have garnered significant attention. Specifically, the study presented in [22] introduced a decentralized dual averaging algorithm tailored for tackling online constrained optimization challenges over undirected network topologies. Nonetheless, in practical online optimization scenarios, information exchange is often unidirectional, leading to unbalanced and directed communication networks [23,24,44,45,48]. Recognizing this limitation, numerous decentralized dual averaging techniques have emerged, each designed to address the imbalance inherent in directed networks. These methods leverage diverse strategies, including the push-sum mechanism [27,40,48] and the surplus-based method [28], subgradient rescaling [44,45], among others. More recently, research has addressed the even more intricate scenario where directed networks undergo temporal variations [25]. For instance, the authors of [25] developed a decentralized dual averaging push method specifically for solving online constrained optimization problems in the context of time-varying directed networks. Beyond these strategies, the work in [29] presented an innovative approach by constructing row-stochastic matrices and subsequently rescaling the zeroth-order oracle, demonstrating its efficacy in mitigating the imbalance of time-varying directed networks. However, it is important to note that this particular strategy does not intersect with the realm of decentralized dual averaging methods, which forms the cornerstone of the present investigation.

Moreover, the aforementioned methods necessitate learning the comprehensive loss function or its subgradients, which are unattainable in certain real-world applications, for example, online source localization routing in data networks [30] and adversarial black-box machine learning [49], among others. This underscores the importance of implementing zeroth-order methodologies that approximate the gradient of a loss function solely based on its function value [32–34]. An array of decentralized zeroth-order algorithms have been explored in the literature [35,41], wherein instead of directly computing the true gradient, these algorithms leverage function evaluations to devise gradient estimators. Recently, the realm of decentralized zeroth-order methods has expanded into the online domain [26,29–31]. Notably, the authors in [26] introduced a push-sum based decentralized online method employing zeroth-order techniques (a one-point gradient estimator approach), achieving anticipated sublinear regrets over time-varying directed networks. Similarly, the method presented in [29] attained the desired results utilizing zeroth-order approaches (adopting a two-point gradient estimator approach). Nevertheless, it is crucial to highlight that zeroth-order methods are not incorporated within the framework of decentralized dual averaging methods, which forms the crux of the present investigation.

In numerous practical contexts, communication frequently occurs in less-than-ideal environments [36,37]. Malicious entities within the network can intercept information exchanged between nodes and deduce sensitive details about participating nodes, posing a significant risk of privacy compromise [38,39,42]. Given the surge in privacy breaches recently, safeguarding the privacy of every node in decentralized networks has become a pressing concern [36–39,42]. Differential privacy, an alluring solution, has garnered substantial interest due to its rigorous mathematical underpinnings, provable security properties, and straightforward implementation [43]. Essentially, differential privacy involves introducing controlled noise to transmitted data, striking a balance between preserving privacy and maintaining optimization precision. In light of this, researchers in [20] introduced a differentially private decentralized primal method tailored to address a privacy-sensitive online constrained optimization problem over directed networks. Subsequently, the study in [39] presented a differentially private decentralized online dual averaging method for time-varying directed networks.

### 6.1.2 Main contributions

In this chapter, we concentrate on tackling a privacy-centric decentralized online constrained optimization challenge within the framework of time-varying directed networks. To this end, we devise an efficacious decentralized algorithm, dubbed ZO-DDA, that comprehensively addresses the aforementioned concerns and enhances its applicability in practical scenarios. In comparison to prior research, the salient contributions of our work can be summarized as follows:

- (i) A proficient zeroth-order decentralized dual averaging methodology, termed ZO-DDA, is introduced to address the privacy-sensitive online constrained optimization task within the context of dynamically evolving directed networks. The anticipated sublinear regret of ZO-DDA is rigorously established, demonstrating parity with non-privacy-focused counterparts as reported in [25,29–31]. Furthermore, the algorithm’s capacity for safeguarding privacy is explicitly demonstrated. Additionally, an insight into the delicate balance between optimization precision and privacy preservation is unveiled.

- (ii) The implementation of ZO-DDA leveraging zeroth-order techniques, specifically the two-point gradient approximation, presents a more viable solution than the one proposed in [40] for situations where direct access to the first-order derivatives of the loss function is limited. Furthermore, distinct from the algorithms discussed in [25,27,40], ZO-DDA, by employing tailored row-stochastic matrices, ingeniously adjusts the zeroth-order information source with an auxiliary parameter. This approach addresses the imbalance arising from the dynamic nature of directed networks from a novel perspective [29], a feature hitherto unexplored in existing decentralized dual averaging frameworks.

- (iii) Drawing inspiration from the methodologies outlined in [20,29], yet distinctively, ZO-DDA not only embraces the dual averaging approach, offering a fresh and effective lens for tackling decentralized online constrained optimization challenges, but also incorporates measures to safeguard the privacy of network participants. This privacy-preserving aspect renders ZO-DDA especially suited for applications where confidentiality is paramount, such as military operations and medical care. Furthermore, ZO-DDA permits dual variables to commence with arbitrary initial states, thereby broadening its applicability beyond the constraints of existing algorithms in [25,27,28,39] that necessitate zero initial conditions.
