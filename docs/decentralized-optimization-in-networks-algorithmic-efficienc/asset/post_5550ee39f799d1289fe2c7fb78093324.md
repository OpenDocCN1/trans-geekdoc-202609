Significant advancements have been made in the realm of decentralized optimization, with notable results such as DGD [27] and its numerous refined algorithms that address constraints [28,29]. These methods have been effectively applied to intricate networks [3,30] and have undergone acceleration [31,32], thereby illustrating the proficiency of decentralized strategies in addressing large-scale problems. However, compared to centralized algorithms, these decentralized methods still face a trade-off between convergence speed and achieving precise convergence. In recent years, numerous techniques rooted in gradient tracking have emerged to address the precision quandary and sluggish convergence associated with decentralized algorithms [33–42]. Specifically, the authors of [33] deliberated on power system problems within undirected networks, while Qu and Li [35] introduced a novel gradient estimation method. The works in [34,36] contemplated time-varying and directed graphs, the authors of [37] explored uncoordinated step-sizes for localized optimization, and Shi et al. [38] examined a synchronous gradient-based algorithm. However, these methods exhibit reduced computational efficiency per iteration due to the necessity of computing local batch gradients. To enhance computational efficiency, decentralized gradient algorithms incorporating the variance-reduction technique have surfaced, including GT-SAGA and GT-SVRG [19], [20], etc. Broadly speaking, the variance-reduction technique mitigates errors stemming from the stochastic gradient algorithm, whereas gradient tracking addresses discrepancies arising from inadequate consensus among nodes. Notably, the variance-reduction technique also elevates computational efficiency.

Based on the information mentioned earlier, it is evident that the computational efficiency of [26] is somewhat limited due to the requirement of calculating local batch gradients. Additionally, it is well-known that the variance-reduction method can significantly enhance computational efficiency. With the aim of achieving greater computational efficiency, our intention is to integrate the variance-reduction technique into [26], thereby presenting an algorithm that boasts superior computational efficiency and precise linear convergence.

Recently, multi-objective optimization problems have captured the interest of scholars concentrating on the creation of decentralized optimization algorithms. The authors of [43] introduced a decentralized optimization algorithm grounded in switching topology to address such multi-objective problems. However, it is worth mentioning that this study emphasizes decentralized optimization issues, not the multi-objective ones.

### 3.1.2 Main contributions

In [26], the authors presented a decentralized algorithm, GT-CDGD, boasting a linear convergence rate, tailored for addressing decentralized optimization problems constrained within a closed convex set. Aiming to achieve elevated computational efficiency when dealing with extensive datasets, we leverage the powerful computational efficiency of the variance-reduction method to devise an innovative algorithm, namely P-DVR. This advanced algorithm offers both a linear convergence rate and remarkable computational efficiency. Our specific contributions are outlined below:

- (i) A novel and innovative algorithm, termed as P-DVR, is introduced for addressing decentralized constrained optimization challenges involving closed convex set constraints. This P-DVR algorithm, which incorporates projected decentralized variance-reduction techniques, is specifically designed to tackle constrained optimization issues. Given a small enough constant step-size, the P-DVR demonstrates linear convergence towards the optimal solution, particularly when dealing with strongly convex and smooth local functions.
- (ii) Distinct from the method presented in [26], known as GT-CDGD, the P-DVR algorithm possesses superior computational efficiency due to its incorporation of a variance-reduction method. In contrast to the GT-CDGD method, which lacks this variance-reduction technique, the P-DVR algorithm can theoretically boost computational efficiency per iteration by a factor of $n$, where $n$ represents the number of data items stored in each node.
- (iii) Through numerical analysis, we validate the linear convergence of the P-DVR algorithm, exceptional computational efficiency, and overall efficacy by applying it to solve two distinct problems: a logistic regression-driven binary classification problem and an energy-based problem of acoustic source localization.

## 3.2 Problem formulation

In this chapter, a decentralized optimization problem can be characterized as

$$
\min f(x), \quad \text{s.t. } x \in \Omega_0, \tag{3.1}
$$

where $f(x) : \mathbb{R}^p \to \mathbb{R}$ denotes the global function, which is expressed as a finite-sum of local functions $f^i(x) : \mathbb{R}^p \to \mathbb{R}$, specifically, $f(x) = \frac{1}{m} \sum_{i=1}^m f^i(x)$. Here, $\Omega_0 \subseteq \mathbb{R}^p$ signifies a closed convex set. Additionally, $m$ stands for the total count of nodes, while $p$ indicates the data dimension. Each local function $f^i(x)$ can be further decomposed as $f^i(x) = \frac{1}{n^i} \sum_{j=1}^{n^i} f^{i,j}(x)$, where $f^{i,j}(x) : \mathbb{R}^p \to \mathbb{R}$ represents an individual component of $f^i(x)$ corresponding to the $j$th datum on the $i$th node, and $n^i$ is the number of data assigned to node $i$.

Broadly speaking, addressing optimization problems through a decentralized lens necessitates the establishment of a communication network among nodes for information exchange. Consider a collection of nodes represented by $\mathcal{V} = \{1, ..., m\}$ and a set of connections indicated by $\mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{V} \times \mathcal{V}$. It is straightforward to fashion a communication network represented by an undirected, interconnected graph $\mathcal{G}$. If the criteria $(i, j) \in \mathcal{E}, i, j = 1, ..., m$, and $i \neq j$ are met, i.e., indicating the presence of a link between node $i$ and node $j$, then these nodes can engage in mutual information sharing; otherwise, communication is not possible. Associated with graph $\mathcal{G}$ is a doubly stochastic matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times m}$ that assigns significance to the data transmitted within the network. Denote $a^{ij}$ as the matrix element of $A$ positioned at row $i$ and column $j$. When $a^{ij} > 0$, it signifies that node $i$ and node $j$ can exchange information, whereas $a^{ij} = 0$ means information cannot be transferred between them. Furthermore, for the doubly stochastic matrix $A$, it is guaranteed that $A 1_m = 1_m$ and $1_m^\text{T} A = 1_m^\text{T}$ are consistently valid.

Next, we establish the subsequent assumptions and definitions to articulate the problem we intend to investigate.

**Assumption 3.1.** For every $i$ belonging to the set $\{1, ..., m\}$, each local function $f^i$ fulfills the criteria of being $\beta$-smooth and $\lambda$-strongly convex, with $\beta$ and $\lambda$ both being greater than zero.

It should be noted that Assumption 3.1 ensures that there exists a unique optimal solution, denoted as $x^*$ for the problem given in (3.1). Additionally, the global function $f(x) : \mathbb{R}^p \to \mathbb{R}$ possesses properties of being both $\beta$-smooth and $\lambda$-strongly convex, i.e.,

\(f(\nu) \ge f(\mu) + \nabla f(\mu)^\text{T}(\nu - \mu) + \frac{\lambda}{2}\|\nu - \mu\|^2,\)
\(\quad (3.2)\)

\(\|\nabla f(\nu) - \nabla f(\mu)\| \le \beta\|\nu - \mu\|,\)
\(\quad (3.3)\)

where $\nu, \mu \in \mathbb{R}^p$ are arbitrary vectors.

**Assumption 3.2.** The structure of the communication network connecting various nodes is represented by an undirected, interconnected graph denoted as $\mathcal{G}$. Assuming that $A$ is a basic and doubly stochastic matrix linked to the network $\mathcal{G}$, and let $\sigma_2$ represent the second greatest singular value of $A$, it follows that $\sigma_2 = \|A - \frac{1}{m} 1_m 1_m^\text{T}\|$, which is less than 1.
