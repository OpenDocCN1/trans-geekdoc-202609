**Remark 8.5** (Effects of Conditional Noises). From the definition of \(b^t\) and Corollary 8.1, we infer that the added conditional noise \(\varepsilon_i^t\) to the state \(y_i^t\) slightly reduces the convergence rate but does not impact the algorithm's precision in solving the EDP. The bound of the noises, \(\hat{\varepsilon}^t\), is inversely related to the algorithm's convergence rate. This tradeoff is acceptable since we achieve a significant objective (privacy protection) at a minor cost (slower convergence rate), as confirmed by simulations.

**Remark 8.6** (Impact of the Random Sleep Strategy). Based on the definition of \(\phi_i^l\) and the conclusions from Corollary 8.1, it is evident that the likelihood \(\mathbb{P}(h_i^t = 1) = \hat{\eta}_i^l\) produced by the random sleep strategy directly influences the algorithm's convergence speed. As \(\hat{\eta}_i\) decreases (potentially leading to fewer gradient updates), the algorithm's convergence rate slows accordingly. This aligns with the common understanding that reducing the frequency of optimization steps generally slows convergence. However, it is important to emphasize that the random sleep strategy (8.7) allows generator \(i\) to perform gradient updates at time \(t\) only with a certain probability \(\eta_i\); otherwise, only local computations are executed.
