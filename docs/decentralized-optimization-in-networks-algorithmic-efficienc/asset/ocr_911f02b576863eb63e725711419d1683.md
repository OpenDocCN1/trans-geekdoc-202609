The notion of sensitivity holds a crucial position in determining the optimal amount of noise necessary to ensure a designated level of privacy safeguarding. Definition 6.3 additionally suggests that, given an equivalent level of privacy protection, sensitivity scales directly with the intensity of noise. Thus by moderating sensitivity, we can fine-tune the extent of random noise, thereby ensuring differential privacy is upheld.

# ## 6.3.1 Dual averaging approach

Before unveiling our proposed algorithm, let us first delve into the canonical dual averaging technique introduced in [22]. We shall define the projection mapping \(\Pi_{\Omega}^{\phi}(q, \alpha)\) as

```
\(\Pi_{\Omega}^{\phi}(q, \alpha) = \arg \min_{u \in \Omega} \{ \langle q, u \rangle + \frac{\phi(u)}{\alpha} \},\) (6.5)
```

where \(\alpha\) is strictly positive, \(q \in \mathbb{R}^m\), and \(\phi(u)\) is taken to be a proximal function endowed with 1-strongly convexity, satisfying the conditions \(\phi(0) = 0\), \(\phi(u) > 0\), \(u \in \Omega\). The function \(\phi(u)\), which incorporates a certain norm denoted as \(||\cdot||\), plays a pivotal role in determining the projection operator \(\Pi_{\Omega}^{\phi}(q, \alpha)\) (refer to [46] for further details). Subsequently, for each node \(i \in \mathcal{V}\), we employ the classical decentralized dual averaging methodology, as outlined in [22].

```
\(\left\{\begin{array}{l} q_{t+1}^i = \sum_{j=1}^n p^{ij} q_t^j + g_t^i, \\ x_{t+1}^i = \Pi_{\Omega}^{\phi}(q_{t+1}^i, \alpha_t), \end{array}\right.\)
```

where \(g_t^i \in \partial f_t^i(x_t^i)\), \(\alpha_t > 0\) is a non-increasing step-size and \(P = [p^{ij}] \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}\) is a doubly stochastic matrix, i.e., \(\sum_{i=1}^n p^{ij} = \sum_{j=1}^n p^{ij} = 1\), \(\forall i, j \in \mathcal{V}\). The primal variable \(x_{t+1}^i\) is acquired through the projection of the dual variable \(q_{t+1}^i\) onto the feasible set \(\Omega\). By leveraging the dual variable \(q_t^i\) and providing flexibility in selecting the proximal function \(\phi\) within the projection operator \(\Pi_{\Omega}^{\phi}\), the dual averaging methodology presents an efficient framework for tackling nonsmooth optimization problems with constraints [22].
