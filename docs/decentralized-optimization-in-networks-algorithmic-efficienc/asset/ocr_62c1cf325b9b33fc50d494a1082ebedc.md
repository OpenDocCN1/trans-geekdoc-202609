# Variance-reduced decentralized projection algorithms for constrained optimization in networks

# ### 3.1 Introduction

The exploration of decentralized optimization techniques aimed at resolving extensive, constrained optimization problems has garnered growing interest, driven by heightened demand in various fields, including resource allocation [1], acoustic source localization [2], economic dispatch problems [3,4], signal processing [5–8], and machine learning [9,10]. The advent of massive datasets poses significant challenges to the computational power and storage capabilities of current computing systems. In light of these challenges, it is imperative to devise a decentralized algorithm that exhibits elevated computational efficiency, rapid convergence, and precise solutions. The utilization of projection operators to manage constraints within closed convex sets [11–16] and variance-reduction methods to ensure accurate results [17–22] are invaluable tools in crafting the desired algorithmic solution.

# #### 3.1.1 Literature review

Decentralized constrained optimization problems have garnered significant attention in both practical applications and scientific inquiries. The scholars referenced in [3] introduced a decentralized method for power management, tailored to address the constraints inherent in the Economic Dispatch Problem (EDP). Similarly, Maros and Jaldén [4] presented a linear adaptation of the PANDA algorithm, specifically designed to tackle the constrained EDP. Furthermore, the study documented in [23] offered an algorithm that preserves privacy while resolving the constraints associated with the acoustic source localization problem. Moreover, several other investigations [11–16,24,25] delved into constrained issues and devised corresponding algorithms. To elaborate, the researchers of [11] contemplated problems characterized by decentralized objective functions and constraints, while those of [12] established a straightforward decentralized continuous-time control law incorporating a constraint. The work in [13] incorporated the projection operator to manage constraints, and the work in [14] examined the subgradient projection algorithm within a constrained framework.
