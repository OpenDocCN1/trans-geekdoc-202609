# Asynchronous decentralized algorithms for resource allocation in directed networks

# 1.1 Introduction

Decentralized optimization has garnered significant interest in recent years due to its extensive applicability across various network engineering systems, spanning power grids [1,2], sensor networks [3,4], and edge computing networks [5]. Many optimization challenges encountered in these systems often manifest as decentralized resource allocation quandaries, such as economic dispatch in smart grids [1,2,6,7], computational resource assignment in edge computing [8], and channel allocation in wireless communication [9]. Traditional centralized approaches to resolving such problems typically necessitate a central entity to gather crucial information from all nodes, which proves costly, susceptible to single points of failure, and lacking resilience in new environments. In contrast, decentralized methods circumvent the need for long-distance communication, thus effectively mitigating the drawbacks of centralized approaches while offering enhanced robustness, quicker computation, reduced communication overhead, and bolstered privacy protection [10–13].

# 1.1.1 Literature review

Decentralized optimization techniques, which primarily rely on gradient information, constitute a significant portion of the decentralized resource allocation strategies documented in the research literature [14–21]. Notably, the work presented in [14] introduced a decentralized algorithm without a central coordinator, representing a groundbreaking approach for decentralized implementations. However, this initial algorithm lacks the consideration of local constraints, which are crucial in practical applications, such as limiting the capacity of generators in economic dispatch problems.
