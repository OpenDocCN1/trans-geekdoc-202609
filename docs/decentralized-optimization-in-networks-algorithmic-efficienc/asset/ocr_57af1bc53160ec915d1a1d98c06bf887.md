Given that \(x^*\) represents the optimal solution set for the problem (2.2), it follows that the sum of differences between the optimal variables and constants is zero, *i.e.*, \(\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i^* - c_i) = 0\). Furthermore, when both \(\hat{\alpha}\) and \(\beta\) adhere to the prerequisites of Theorem 2.1, we can deduce that \(\theta_i^t\) converges to \(\theta^*\). Consequently, as \(t\) approaches infinity, the right-hand side of (2.25) tends towards zero, indicating that the sequence \(\{x^t\}\) converges to \(x^*\) at a rate of \(O((\rho(G_{\hat{\alpha}, \beta}) / \beta)^t)\). This completes the proof of Theorem 2.2.

**Remark 2.6.** Employing the bounds for \(\hat{\alpha}\) and \(\beta\) stipulated in Theorem 2.1, we present in Theorem 2.2 the convergence of the sequence \(\{x^t\}\) towards \(x^*\). Notably, the parameters \(\gamma_1\), \(\gamma_2\), \(\gamma_3\), \(\gamma_4\), and \(\gamma_5\) mentioned in Theorem 2.1 are adjustable, and their specific values are determined by the network topology and the associated cost function.

**Theorem 2.3.** *Assuming the prerequisites of Assumptions 2.1–2.3 are met, and provided that* \(\hat{\alpha}\) *and* \(\beta\) *are chosen in accordance with the requirements of Theorem 2.1, it is observed that when* \(E > (3Q_1 + 2Q_1 L)(1 + (\kappa_1)^2) / (\kappa_1)^2\)*, then it holds that* \(t_i^{k(i,t)+1} - t_i^{k(i,t)} \ggg 2\)*, for all* \(i \in \mathcal{V}\) *and for any* \(t > 0\).

**Proof.** Drawing upon the measurement errors denoted by (2.9) and the constraint \(\|\mathbf{v}^t\| \leqslant Q_1(\kappa_4)^t\) outlined in Theorem 2.1, we can infer that

```
\begin{aligned}
\|\epsilon_{i,o}^t\| + \|\epsilon_{i,z}^t\| \\
= \|\theta_i^{k(i,t)} - \theta_i^t\| + \|z_i^{k(i,t)} - z_i^t\| \\
\leqslant \|\theta_i^{k(i,t)} - \bar{\theta}_i^{k(i,t)}\| + \|\bar{\theta}_i^{k(i,t)} - \bar{\theta}^t\| + \|\bar{\theta}^t - \bar{\theta}_i^t\| \\
+ \|z_i^{k(i,t)} - \bar{z}_i^{k(i,t)}\| + \|\bar{z}_i^{k(i,t)} - \bar{z}^t\| + \|\bar{z}^t - z_i^t\| \\
\leqslant (3Q_1 + 2Q_1 L)(\kappa_4)^{t_i^{k(i,t)}} + (3Q_1 + 2Q_1 L)(\kappa_4)^t.
\end{aligned}
(2.26)
```

Revisiting the triggering condition (2.8), we subsequently arrive at

```
E(\kappa_1)t_i^{k(i,t)+1} \leqslant (3Q_1 + 2Q_1 L)(\kappa_1)^{t_i^{k(i,t)+1}} + (3Q_1 + 2Q_1 L)(\kappa_1)^{t_i^{k(i,t)}}.
(2.27)
```

Given that \(\kappa_1\) is less than \(\kappa_4\), the following inequality emerges:

```
t_i^{k(i,t)+1} - t_i^{k(i,t)} \geqslant \ln \frac{(3Q_1 + 2Q_1 L)}{E - (3Q_1 + 2Q_1 L)} / \ln \kappa_1.
(2.28)
```

Provided that \(E\) falls within the specified range in Theorem 2.3, it is postulated that the interval between \(t_i^{k(i,t)+1}\) and \(t_i^{k(i,t)}\) is not less than 2. The proof of Theorem 2.3 is thus finalized.

This shift not only validates the feasibility of Event-Triggered Predictive Data Assimilation (ET-PDA) but also significantly diminishes communication and computational overheads per iteration, thereby enhancing overall execution efficiency.
