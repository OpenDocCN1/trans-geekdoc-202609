

$$
\begin{aligned}
&- \eta_i \beta \mathbb{E}_{t-1} \left[ \| \phi_i^t - \bar{\phi}^t \|^2 \right] - \frac{2}{t} \mathbb{E}_{t-1} \left[ b^T (\phi_i^t - v) \right] \\
&+ \eta_i \left[ \frac{\alpha_3 \| b \|^2}{t^{3/2-\epsilon}} + \frac{\alpha_2(1+4v+\tau) \| b \|^2}{t^2} \right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.30}
$$

To demonstrate the desired result, it suffices to multiply both sides of (1.30) by $\pi_i$ and then aggregate these multiplied expressions by summing them for all $i$ from 1 to $n$. Then, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \mathbb{E}_{t-1} \left[ \| p_i^t - v \|^2 \right] \\
\le (1 + \frac{\alpha_3}{t^{3/2-\epsilon}}) \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \mathbb{E}_{t-1} \left[ \sum_{j=1}^n W_{ij}^t \| p_j^{t-1} - v \|^2 \right] \\
- \eta_{\min} \beta \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}_{t-1} \left[ \pi_i \| \phi_i^t - \bar{\phi}^t \|^2 \right] \\
- \frac{2}{t} \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}_{t-1} \left[ \pi_i b^T (\phi_i^t - v) \right] \\
+ \left[ \frac{\alpha_3}{t^{3/2-\epsilon}} + \frac{\alpha_2(1+4v+\tau)}{t^2} \right] \| b \|^2,
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.31}
$$

where we use $\eta_{\min} \le \eta_i \le 1$ with $\eta_{\min} = \min_{i \in \mathcal{V}} \{\eta_i\}$. By utilizing $\pi^T \bar{W} = \pi^T$, it yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \mathbb{E}_{t-1} \left[ \sum_{j=1}^n W_{ij}^t \| p_j^{t-1} - v \|^2 \right] \\
= \sum_{j=1}^n \| p_j^{t-1} - v \|^2 \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \mathbb{E}_{t-1} \left[ W_{ij}^t \right] \\
= \sum_{j=1}^n \| p_j^{t-1} - v \|^2 \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \bar{W}_{ij} = \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \| p_i^{t-1} - v \|^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.32}
$$

Define $\bar{\phi}^t = \pi^T \phi^t = \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \phi_i^t$. Then, it is straightforward to show that

$$\sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i b^T (\phi_i^t - v) = b^T (\bar{\phi}^t - v).$$

Therefore by combining (1.31) and (1.32), we can deduce the conclusion stated in Lemma 1.5, thus completing the proof. $\square$

Let \( p^* \in \mathbb{R}^{n+1} \) denote an optimal solution of the problem (1.16). In the subsequent analysis, we will leverage Lemma 1.5 to derive the convergence properties of Algorithm 1.

## Theorem 1.1.

Assuming that Assumptions 1.1 and 1.2 are valid. If \( 0 < \epsilon < 1/2 \), there exists a threshold \( \hat{t} = \hat{t}(\epsilon, n) \) (large enough) such that for all \( t \ge \hat{t}, i \in \mathcal{V} \), \( \lim_{t \to \infty} p_i^t = p^* \) a.s., and \( \lim_{t \to \infty} x_i^t = x_i^* \) a.s..

**Proof.** Revisiting Lemma 1.5 and setting \( v = p^* \), we obtain that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \mathbb{E}_{t-1} \left[ \| p_i^t - p^* \|^2 \right] \\
\le (1 + \frac{\alpha_3}{t^{3/2-\epsilon}}) \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \| p_i^{t-1} - p^* \|^2 - \frac{2}{t} \mathbb{E}_{t-1} \left[ b^T(\bar{\phi}^t - p^*) \right] \\
- \eta_{\min} \beta \mathbb{E}_{t-1} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \| \phi_i^t - \bar{\phi}^t \|^2 \right] \\
+ (\frac{\alpha_3}{t^{3/2-\epsilon}} + \frac{\alpha_2}{t^2}(1 + 4v + \tau))\|b\|^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.33}
$$

Observing that \( \epsilon \) falls within the range \( 0 < \epsilon < 1/2 \), it follows that \( 1 < (3/2 - \epsilon) < 3/2 \). This inequality enables us to deduce that the infinite series \( \sum_{t=0}^\infty \frac{\alpha_2}{t^{3/2-\epsilon}} \) converges, and similarly, the series \( \sum_{t=0}^\infty (\frac{\alpha_3}{t^{3/2-\epsilon}} + \frac{\alpha_2}{t^2}(1 + 4v + \tau))\|b\|^2 \) also converges. Given that \( \phi_i^t \) belongs to the set \( \Xi_0 \), we have the positive quantity \( b^T(\bar{\phi}^t - p^*) > 0 \). Since we have already established that \( \beta \) is positive, all the prerequisites of Lemma 1.3 are met. Therefore applying Lemma 1.3 shows that

$$
\left\{ \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \| p_i^t - p^* \|^2 \right\} \text{ converges } a.s. \text{ as } t \to \infty, \quad (1.34a)
$$

$$
\sum_{t=0}^\infty -\frac{2}{t} b^T (\bar{\phi}^t - p^*) < \infty \quad a.s., \quad (1.34b)
$$

$$
\sum_{t=0}^\infty \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \| \phi_i^t - \bar{\phi}^t \|^2 < \infty \quad a.s.. \quad (1.34c)
$$

Due to \( \sum_{t=0}^\infty (2/t) = \infty \), it is deduced from (1.34b) that

$$
\lim_{t \to \infty} \inf b^T \bar{\phi}^t = b^T p^* \quad a.s.
$$

From (1.34c), we can infer that the limit \( \lim_{t \to \infty} \| \phi_i^t - \bar{\phi}^t \| \) converges to 0 almost surely (a.s.). This finding supports the argument that the condition \( \lim_{t \to \infty} \| h_i^t - \phi_i^t \| \) is bounded above by \( \lim_{t \to \infty} \| h_i^t - \phi_i^t \| + \| \phi_i^t - \bar{\phi}^t \| \), which in turn equals

$$
\lim_{t \to \infty}[\alpha_i^t\|b\| + \|\phi_i^t - \bar{\phi}^t\|]
$$

and converges to 0 almost surely (a.s.). Additionally, a direct consequence of (1.17) is that:

$$
p_i^t = \sum_{j=1}^n W_{ij}^t p_j^{t-1} + \epsilon_i^t, \quad i \in \{i^t, j^t\},\\
p_i^t = \sum_{j=1}^n W_{ij}^t p_j^{t-1}, \quad i \notin \{i^t, j^t\},
$$

where $\epsilon_i^t = -\alpha_i^t b - [f_i(h_i^t)]_+ \xi_i^t / \|\xi_i^t\|^2$, for $i \in \{i^t, j^t\}$. By taking the conditional expectation with respect to $\mathcal{F}^{t-1}$, we obtain that for all $i$ in the set $\mathcal{V}$,

$$
\mathbb{E}_{t-1}[p_i^t] = \sum_{j=1}^n \bar{W}_{ij} p_j^{t-1} + \eta_i \mathbb{E}_{t-1}[\epsilon_i^t].
$$

Since $\lim_{t \to \infty} \|h_i^t - \phi_i^t\| = 0$ a.s. and $\phi_i^t \in \Xi_0$, we can obtain that $\lim_{t \to \infty} [f_i(h_i^t)]_+ = 0$ a.s.. Consequently, $\lim_{t \to \infty} \|\epsilon_i^t\| = 0$ a.s. considering the fact that $\lim_{t \to \infty} \alpha_i^t = 0$ a.s.. Based on this result and Lemma 1.4, it follows that $\lim_{t \to \infty} \|p_i^t - \bar{p}^t\| = 0$ a.s. for all $i \in \mathcal{V}$. Then, utilizing (1.34a) and the row-stochasticity of $\bar{W}$, we can infer that the sequence $\{\|\bar{p}^t - p^*\|^2\}$ is convergent a.s.. Noting that $\|p_i^t - \phi_i^t\|^2 \le \|h_i^t - \phi_i^t\|^2 - \|[f_i(h_i^t)]_+\|^2 / \|\xi_i^t\|^2 \le \|h_i^t - \phi_i^t\|^2$ (see the proof of Lemma 1.5) and given that $\lim_{t \to \infty} \|h_i^t - \phi_i^t\| = 0$ a.s., we further obtain that

$$
\lim_{t \to \infty} \|\bar{p}^t - \bar{\phi}^t\| \le \lim_{t \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \|p_i^t - \phi_i^t\| = 0 \text{ a.s.},
$$

which indicates that $\{\|\bar{p}^t - p^*\|^2\}$ converges a.s. as well. Recalling the fact that $\lim_{t \to \infty} \inf b^T \bar{\phi}^t = b^T p^* \text{ a.s.}$, we can infer that there exists a subsequence $\{\bar{\phi}^{t_k}\}$ that converges a.s. to an optimal point $p_0^* \in \Xi^*$. Consequently, it is evident that $\lim_{k \to \infty} \|\bar{\phi}^{t_k} - p_0^*\| = 0$. Since the sequence $\{\|\bar{p}^t - p_0^*\|^2\}$ is a.s. convergent, we conclude that $\lim_{t \to \infty} \bar{p}^t = p_0^* \text{ a.s.}$. Furthermore, we note that the inequality $\|p_i^t - p_0^*\| \le \|p_i^t - \bar{p}^t\| + \|\bar{p}^t - \bar{\phi}^t\| + \|\bar{\phi}^t - p_0^*\|$ holds, where all the three terms $\|p_i^t - \bar{p}^t\|$, $\|\bar{p}^t - \bar{\phi}^t\|$, and $\|\bar{\phi}^t - p_0^*\|$ converge to zero a.s.. Therefore there exists a point $p_0^* \in \Xi^*$ such that $\lim_{t \to \infty} \|p_i^t - p_0^*\| = 0 \text{ a.s.}$ for $i \in \mathcal{V}$.

Secondly, by considering the update rule in (1.11) and leveraging the strong duality between the primal problem (1.1) and the dual problem (1.3), we can deduce that $\lim_{t \to \infty} x_i^t = x_i^*$ almost surely (a.s.) for all $i \in \mathcal{V}$. This completes the proof. $\square$

Let $\hat{\phi}^t$ be defined as $\hat{\phi}^t = (1/\gamma^t) \sum_{s=1}^t (\bar{\phi}^s / s)$ for all $i \in \mathcal{V}$, where $\gamma^t = \sum_{s=1}^t (1/s)$. Leveraging the proven optimality, we proceed to present the error-bound analysis in the following corollary.

## Corollary 1.1.

If the conditions stated in Theorem 1.1 are met, then it follows that \(\mathbb{E}[b^T(\hat{\phi}^t - p^*)] \le \Gamma^t/\gamma^t\), where \(\Gamma^t = \exp(\sum_{s=1}^t q^s)(\mathbb{E}[\sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \|p_i^1 - p^*\|^2] + \mathbb{E}[\sum_{s=1}^t c^s]) < \infty\).

**Proof.** By taking the total expectation of (1.33), we arrive at the conclusion that

$$
\sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \mathbb{E}_{t-1} \left[ \|p_i^t - p^*\|^2 \right] - (1 + q^{t-1}) \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \|p_i^{t-1} - p^*\|^2
\le - \frac{2}{t} \mathbb{E}_{t-1} \left[ b^T (\bar{\phi}^t - p^*) \right] + c^t, \quad (1.35)
$$

where \(q^{t-1} = \frac{\alpha_2}{t^{3/2-\epsilon}}\) and \(c^t = (\frac{\alpha_3}{t^{3/2-\epsilon}} + \frac{\alpha_2}{t^2}(1+4v+\tau))\|b\|^2\). Denote \(\psi^{t+1,s} = (1+q^t)\psi^{t,s}\) and \(\psi^{s,s} = 1\) for any \(t \ge s \ge 1\). By recursively applying the expectation from (1.35), we obtain the following result:

$$
\mathbb{E} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \|p_i^t - p^*\|^2 \right] - \psi^{t,1} \mathbb{E} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \|p_i^1 - p^*\|^2 \right]
\le \sum_{s=1}^t \psi^{t,s} \left( - \frac{2}{s} \mathbb{E} \left[ b^T (\bar{\phi}^s - p^*) \right] + \mathbb{E}[c^s] \right). \quad (1.36)
$$

Given that \(\psi^{t,s} \ge 1\) and \(\psi^{t,s} \le \psi^{t,1}\) for any \(t \ge s \ge 1\), we can derive the following conclusion from (1.36):

$$
\mathbb{E} \left[ \sum_{s=1}^t \frac{b^T (\bar{\phi}^s - p^*)}{s} \right]
\le \psi^{t,1} \left( \mathbb{E} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \|p_i^1 - p^*\|^2 \right] + \mathbb{E} \left[ \sum_{s=1}^t c^s \right] \right). \quad (1.37)
$$

Dividing by \(\gamma^t\), we obtain

$$
\mathbb{E} \left[ b^T (\hat{\phi}^t - p^*) \right]
\le \frac{\psi^{t,1}}{\gamma^t} \left( \mathbb{E} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \|p_i^1 - p^*\|^2 \right] + \mathbb{E} \left[ \sum_{s=1}^t c^s \right] \right). \quad (1.38)
$$

Utilizing the fact that \(\exp(y) \ge 1 + y\) for all \(y \ge 0\), we have \(\psi^{t,1} \le \exp(\sum_{s=1}^t q^s)\). Combining this with (1.38), we arrive at the conclusions stated in Corollary 1.1. Specifically, \(\Gamma^t\) is defined as \(\exp(\sum_{s=1}^t q^s)(\mathbb{E}[\sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \|p_i^1 - p^*\|^2] + \mathbb{E}[\sum_{s=1}^t c^s]) < \infty\), which is guaranteed by Lemma 1.2. This completes the proof.
