
## 5.3 Algorithm construction

In this section, we propose a decentralized algorithm for online optimization, tailored to address the problem described in (5.1).

### 5.3.1 A general dual averaging approach

Initially, we introduce the conventional dual averaging method outlined in [25]. Define the projection operator $\Pi_\Omega^h(v, \alpha)$ as follows:

$$ \Pi_\Omega^h(v, \alpha) = \arg \min_{u \in \Omega} \left\{ \langle v, u \rangle + \frac{h(u)}{\alpha} \right\}, $$ (5.4)

where $\alpha > 0$, $v$ belongs to $\mathcal{R}^m$, and assuming $h(u)$ is a proximal function exhibiting 1-strongly convex characteristics, satisfying the conditions $h(0) = 0$ and $h(u) > 0$ for all $u \in \Omega$ [25,29,49]. Then, the dual averaging method can be employed to resolve the problem stated in (5.1) over a shared constraint set denoted as $\Omega$ (as described in [25]). Specifically, for every $i$ belonging to the set $\mathcal{V}$, the algorithm proceeds as follows:

$$
z_{i, k+1} = z_{i, k} + g_{i, k},
$$
$$
x_{i, k+1} = \Pi_\Omega^h(z_{i, k+1}, \alpha_k),
$$ (5.5)

where $g_{i, k}$ belongs to the subgradient of $f_i$ at $x_{i, k}$, while $\alpha_k$ represents a non-increasing step-size greater than zero. The update $x_{i, k+1}$ is obtained by projecting $z_{i, k+1}$ onto the constraint set denoted as $\Omega$. Through the introduction of a dual variable, $z_{i,k}$, and the flexible selection of the proximal function $h$ within the projection operator $\Pi_\Omega^h$, the dual averaging method emerges as an efficient strategy for addressing nonsmooth optimization problems with constraints, as referenced in [25]. Nevertheless, it is important to observe that these methodologies are inadequate for resolving the privacy-sensitive decentralized online optimization challenges associated with directed networks, which constitute the focus of our study.

### 5.3.2 A decentralized online optimization algorithm

At time $k \in \{1, \cdots, K\}$, in order to safeguard sensitive data, node $i$ initially generates a random noise vector denoted as $y_{i, k}$ that follows a Laplace distribution, and adds $y_{i,k}$ to the dual variable $z_{i,k}$ transmitted in the network, *i.e.*,

$$\tilde{z}_{i,k} = z_{i,k} + Y_{i,k},$$ (5.6)

where $Y_{i,k}$ follows a Laplace distribution denoted as $\mathcal{L}(0, b_k)$ with the parameter $b_k$. Subsequently, node $i$ forwards $A_{ji}\tilde{z}_{i,k}$ and $A_{ji}y_{i,k}$ to each node $j$ belonging to its outbound neighbor set $\mathcal{N}_i^{\text{out}}$. Upon receiving $A_{ij}y_{j,k}$ and $A_{ij}\tilde{z}_{j,k}$ from nodes $j$ in its inbound neighbor set $\mathcal{N}_i^{\text{in}}$, node $i$ integrates this newly acquired data and updates its dual variable $z_{i,k+1}$ by incorporating a cumulative rescaled subgradient, specifically $g_{i,k}/\gamma_{ii,k}$:

$$z_{i,k+1} = \sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij}\tilde{z}_{j,k} + \frac{g_{i,k}}{y_{ii,k}},$$ (5.7)

where $g_{i,k}$ belongs to the subgradient of $f_{i,k}$ at $x_{i,k}$, while $y_{ii,k}$ represents the $i$th component of $y_{i,k}$. This estimated variable addresses the imbalance inherent in directed networks and undergoes an update through a consensus process as outlined below:

$$y_{i,k+1} = \sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij}y_{j,k}.$$ (5.8)

Employing the operator $\Pi_\Omega^h(\cdot)$, the primal variable $x_{i,k+1}$ is ultimately derived,

$$x_{i,k+1} = \Pi_\Omega^h(z_{i,k+1}, \alpha_k),$$ (5.9)

where $\alpha_k$ is a positive and non-increasing step-size. Algorithm 1 provides an outline of the pseudo-code described above.

**Remark 5.2.** The strategy of rescaling gradients is widely used to alleviate the imbalance problem that occurs in directed networks. Numerous empirical studies [23,32,47,50] have shown the effectiveness and practicality of this approach. Our proposed decentralized algorithm integrates the gradient rescaling technique into the decentralized online dual averaging method, making it efficient for addressing general online constrained optimization challenges in directed networks.

**Remark 5.3.** Compared to the zero-sum correlated perturbation approach mentioned in references [45,55], the differential privacy method we are examining possesses distinct characteristics: (i) At every time instant $k$, the differential privacy approach merely necessitates that each node independently produces random noise, eliminating the need for nodes to synchronize their perturbations to maintain a network balance (zero-sum), as required by the zero-sum correlated perturbation technique [45,55]. (ii) While the differential privacy approach does influence the algorithm’s convergence, it is crucial to remember that when employing a decentralized online algorithm for online learning tasks, convergence is only achieved to the proximity of the optimal solution due to fluctuating cost functions. Furthermore, differential privacy boasts a more comprehensive theoretical analysis. This justifies the preference for differential privacy methods in the realm of online learning.

**Algorithm 1** An effective differentially private decentralized dual averaging algorithm – from the viewpoint of \( i \in \mathcal{V} \).
1: Require: \( \alpha_0 > 0, z_{i,1} \in \mathbb{R}^m, x_{i,1} = \Pi_\Omega^h(z_{i,1}, \alpha_0) \), and \( y_{i,1} = e_i \in \mathbb{R}^n \).
2: Set \( k = 1 \).
3: while \( k \le K \) do
4: Generate noise \( \gamma_{i,k} \sim \mathcal{L}(0, b_k) \).
5: Perturb \( z_{i,k} \) by \( \gamma_{i,k} \) and obtain \( \tilde{z}_{i,k} \) via
   \( \tilde{z}_{i,k} = z_{i,k} + \gamma_{i,k} \).
6: Send \( A_{ji}y_{i,k} \) and \( A_{ji}\tilde{z}_{i,k} \) to node \( j \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{out}} \); Receive \( A_{ij}y_{j,k} \) and \( A_{ij}\tilde{z}_{j,k} \) from node \( j \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{in}} \)
7: Update \( x_{i,k+1}, z_{i,k+1}, \) and \( y_{i,k+1} \) via
   \( z_{i,k+1} = \sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij}\tilde{z}_{j,k} + \frac{g_{i,k}}{\gamma_{i,k}}, \\)
   \( y_{i,k+1} = \sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij}y_{j,k}, \\)
   \( x_{i,k+1} = \Pi_\Omega^h(z_{i,k+1}, \alpha_k) \).
8: Set \( k \leftarrow k + 1 \).
9: Return: the primal variable \( \{x_{i,k}\}_{k=1}^K \).

## 5.4 Convergence guarantees

In this section, we present the theoretical guarantees of Algorithm 1. Initially, we incorporate previously documented research to ease the examination of convergence. Subsequently, we infer the convergence outcomes of Algorithm 1 by utilizing the relevant findings. Ultimately, we conduct a thorough examination of the differential privacy aspect of Algorithm 1.

### 5.4.1 Auxiliary results

In this subsection, we provide three supporting results linked to the directed network denoted as \( \mathcal{G} \), the projection operator defined as \( \Pi_\Omega^h(\cdot) \), and the conjugate-type function \( \mathcal{H}_\Omega^h(v, \alpha) \) described below.

**Lemma 5.1.** [32] Based on Assumptions 5.1 and 5.2, a sequence of stochastic vectors exists, denoted as \( \pi = [\pi_1, ..., \pi_n]^T \), which fulfills the conditions that

- a) \( \pi^T A = \pi^T, \pi^T 1_n = 1 \);
- b) there are \( 0 < \varpi < 1, C > 0 \) and \( \theta > 0 \) satisfying that \( |[A^k]_{ij} - \pi_j| \le C \varpi^k, |y_{ii,k} - \pi_i| \le C \varpi^k, \text{and } \theta^{-1} \le y_{ii,k} \le 1, \forall i, j \in \mathcal{V}, k \ge 1 \).

### 5.4.2 Main results

In this subsection, to effectively demonstrate the performance of Algorithm 1, we initially present a vital lemma regarding the maximum limit of the consensus error $\mathbb{E}[\max_{i \in \mathcal{V}} \|z_{i,k} - \bar{z}_k\|]$, which plays a substantial role in shaping our primary findings. Subsequently, we establish our main outcome by determining the upper bound of regret.

By employing the additional vector $\bar{z}_k = \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i z_{i,k}$, one obtains

$$
\bar{z}_{k+1} = \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i z_{i,k+1} = \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i (\sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij} (z_{j,k} + y_{j,k}) + \frac{g_{i,k}}{y_{ii,k}})
= \bar{z}_k + \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i y_{i,k} + \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i \frac{g_{i,k}}{y_{ii,k}},
\tag{5.10}
$$

**Lemma 5.2.** [49] Considering the definition of $\Pi_{\Omega}^h(\cdot)$ given in Eq. (5.4), for arbitrary vectors $v, u \in \mathbb{R}^m$ and a positive step-size $\alpha$, the following holds true:

$$
\|\Pi_{\Omega}^h(v, \alpha) - \Pi_{\Omega}^h(u, \alpha)\| \le \alpha \|v - u\|.
$$

Define $\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^h(v, \alpha)$ as a conjugate-type function applicable to any vector $v$ belonging to $\mathbb{R}^m$ and a positive step-size $\alpha > 0$, in which

$$
\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^h(v, \alpha) = \sup_{u \in \Omega} \left\{ \langle v, u \rangle - \frac{1}{\alpha} h(u) \right\}.
$$

Based on the aforementioned definition, we move forward to establish a crucial lemma that will validate our primary results.

**Lemma 5.3.** [54,55] Bearing in mind the definitions of $\Pi_{\Omega}^h(\cdot)$ and $\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^h(\cdot)$, for any vectors $v$ and $u$ contained in $\mathbb{R}^m$ and step-size $\alpha > 0$, the following apply:
a) $\nabla \mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^h(-v, \alpha) = \Pi_{\Omega}^h(v, \alpha)$ and $\|\nabla \mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^h(-v, \alpha) - \nabla \mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^h(-u, \alpha)\| = \|\Pi_{\Omega}^h(v, \alpha) - \Pi_{\Omega}^h(u, \alpha)\| \le \alpha \|v - u\|$;
b) $\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^h(-v - u, \alpha) \le \mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^h(-v, \alpha) - \langle \nabla \mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^h(-v, \alpha), u \rangle + (\alpha/2)\|u\|^2$.

**Remark 5.4.** Based on the findings presented in [56], it can be deduced that the dual of the Euclidean norm remains the Euclidean norm itself. During our research, we intended to use both the specified norm and its dual for theoretical validations. However, for the sake of simplicity, we consistently applied the Euclidean norm in our subsequent theoretical explorations. Nevertheless, similar validations can be obtained for various other norms. Furthermore, numerous studies [28,29,44,55] have adopted the Euclidean norm for theoretical examinations. Despite the fact that utilizing a general norm and its dual would have been more suitable, this variation did not alter the ultimate theoretical results.
