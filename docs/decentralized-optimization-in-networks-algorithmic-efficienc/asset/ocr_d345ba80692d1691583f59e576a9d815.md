garding the executions $\mathcal{B}(\hat{U}'_t)$ and $\mathcal{B}(U_t)$ are identical, implying $\tilde{q}^i_t \equiv \hat{q}^i_t$. Building upon the rationale presented in Proposition 1 of [29], it suffices to establish that

```
\prod_{i=1}^n \prod_{y=1}^m \frac{[\tilde{q}^i_t(y) - q^i_t(y)]}{[\hat{q}^i_t(y) - q^i_t(y)]} \le \prod_{i=1}^n \prod_{y=1}^m \exp(\frac{|q^i_t(y) - \hat{q}^i_t(y)|}{b_t}) = \exp(\frac{||\hat{q}_t - q_t||_1}{b_t}) \le \exp(\frac{\Delta_t}{b_t}) = e^{\epsilon_t}. \quad (6.38)
```

In addition, one yields that

```
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{B}(U) \in S] = \prod^T_{t=1} \mathbb{P}[\mathcal{B}(U_t) \in S]. \quad (6.39)
```

Thus it deduces that

```
\prod^T_{t=1} \mathbb{P}[\mathcal{B}(U_t) \in S] \le \prod^T_{t=1} \exp(\frac{\Delta_t}{b_t}) \cdot \mathbb{P}[\mathcal{B}(\hat{U}_t) \in S], \quad (6.40)
```

which based on $\hat{\epsilon} = \sum^T_{t=1} (\Delta_t / b_t)$ derives that

```
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{B}(U) \in S] \le \exp(\hat{\epsilon}) \cdot \mathbb{P}[\mathcal{B}(\hat{U}) \in S].
```

According to Theorem 6.2, it becomes evident that the parameter $b_t$ and the sensitivity $\Delta_t$ play pivotal roles in determining the privacy level. Specifically, $\Delta_t$ exhibits a direct proportionality with the privacy level, whereas $b_t$ inversely correlates with it at a given time $t$. Lemma 6.5 underscores the intricacy of adjusting $\Delta_t$, thus necessitating a meticulous selection of $b_t$ to ensure the desired $\epsilon_t$-differential privacy. Furthermore, Theorem 6.2 clarifies that the cumulative privacy budget $\hat{\epsilon}$ is the summation of $\epsilon_t$ across $T$ iterations. This intuitively suggests that as compared to a solitary iteration at time $T$, performing $T$ iterations reveals more sensitive information, thereby amplifying the risk of private data exposure.
