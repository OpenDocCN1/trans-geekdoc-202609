Proof. Based on the definitions of $y'_t$ and $\xi_t$, it follows that $y_t = y'_t + \xi_t$. From this, we can infer that
\[\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}[||y_t||^2] \leq 4\mathbb{E}[||y'_t - 1_N \otimes \bar{y}'_t||^2] + 4\mathbb{E}[||1_N \otimes (\bar{y}'_t - \frac{1}{N}\nabla f(\bar{x}_t))||^2] \\
+ 4\mathbb{E}[||\frac{1}{N} \otimes \nabla f(\bar{x}_t)||^2] + 4\mathbb{E}[||\xi_t||^2] \\
\leq 4\mathbb{E}[||y'_t - 1_N \otimes \bar{y}'_t||^2] + 4L^2\mathbb{E}[||x_t - 1_N \otimes \bar{x}_t||^2] \\
+ \frac{4L^2}{N}\mathbb{E}[||\bar{x}_t - x^*||^2] + \frac{8c_w N}{(1 - \tau_w)^2}(\frac{C\pi^2}{6} + U_1),
\end{aligned}\]
where the second inequality relies on $\bar{y}'_t$, which is defined as $\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \nabla f^i(x_t^i)$, as well as the Lipschitz continuity property of $\nabla f^i(\cdot)$ and (4.13). Then, the proof has been fully established.
