
+ \frac{1}{\gamma(k)}\varphi(x^*) + \langle \bar{h}(1), x^* \rangle
= \varphi^\dagger(-\bar{h}(k+1), \gamma(k)) + \frac{1}{\gamma(k)}\varphi(x^*) + \langle \bar{h}(1), x^* \rangle. \quad (7.21)

Furthermore, we can derive

\varphi^\dagger(-\bar{h}(s+1), \gamma(s))
\le \varphi^\dagger(-\bar{h}(s+1), \gamma(s-1))
= \varphi^\dagger(-\bar{h}(s) - \hat{g}(s), \gamma(s-1))
\le \varphi^\dagger(-\bar{h}(s), \gamma(s-1)) - \langle \hat{g}(s), \varsigma(s) \rangle + \frac{1}{2}\gamma(s-1)\|\hat{g}(s)\|^2, \quad (7.22)

where the inequality holds from [17, Lemma 2]. Then, (7.22) further indicates that

\langle \hat{g}(s), \varsigma(s) \rangle \le \varphi^\dagger(-\bar{h}(s), \gamma(s-1)) - \varphi^\dagger(-\bar{h}(s+1), \gamma(s))
+ \frac{1}{2}\gamma(s-1)\|\hat{g}(s)\|^2. \quad (7.23)

Summing both sides of (7.23) over \(s\) from 1 to \(k\) and rearranging the obtained inequality, we obtain

\sum_{s=1}^k \langle \hat{g}(s), \varsigma(s) \rangle \le \varphi_{\gamma(0)}^\dagger(-\bar{h}(1)) - \varphi_{\gamma(k)}^\dagger(-\bar{h}(k+1))
+ \frac{1}{2} \sum_{s=1}^k \gamma(s-1) \|\hat{g}(s)\|^2. \quad (7.24)

Combining (7.21) and (7.24), we finally obtain

\sum_{s=1}^k \langle \hat{g}(s), \varsigma(s) - x^* \rangle \le \frac{1}{2} \sum_{s=1}^k \gamma(s-1) \|\hat{g}(s)\|^2 + \frac{1}{\gamma(k)} \varphi(x^*)
+ \varphi^\dagger(-\bar{h}(1), \gamma(0)) + \langle \bar{h}(1), x^* \rangle,

which proves the desired result.

**Remark 7.6.** Lemma 7.4(b), which holds under any initial conditions for the dual variable \(h_i\), is essential for the convergence analysis of Algorithm 1. It is more general than results assuming zero initial conditions for \(h_i\) as presented in [25,26,28].

## 7.4.3 Main results

In this subsection, we establish the convergence of Algorithm 1 based on the previous results.

**Theorem 7.1.** Under Assumptions 7.1 and 7.2. Let $\{\hat{x}_i(k)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}$ be generated by Algorithm 1 with $\gamma(k) = \gamma(0)/\sqrt{k+1}, \gamma(0) > 0$. Then, we have

$f(\hat{x}_i(k)) - f(x^*) \le \frac{\Theta_1}{\sqrt{k}} + \frac{\Theta_2}{k},$

where $x^* = \arg \min_{x \in \Omega} f(x), \Theta_1 = 6vL\Theta\gamma(0) + vL^2\gamma(0) + \frac{v}{\gamma(0)}\varphi(x^*)$ and $\Theta_2 = v\varphi^\dagger(-\bar{h}(1), \gamma(0)) + v\langle\bar{h}(1), x^*\rangle$ are positive constants.

_Proof._ According to Jensen's inequality for convex functions, we have

$f(\hat{x}_i(k)) - f(x^*)
\le \frac{1}{k} \sum_{s=1}^k (f(x_i(s)) - f(x^*))
= \frac{1}{k} \sum_{s=1}^k \sum_{j=1}^v (f_j(x_i(s)) - f_j(x_j(s)) + f_j(x_j(s)) - f_j(x^*))
= O_1 + O_2,$

where

$O_1 = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{s=1}^k \sum_{j=1}^v (f_j(x_i(s)) - f_j(x_j(s))),$
$O_2 = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{s=1}^k \sum_{j=1}^v (f_j(x_j(s)) - f_j(x^*)).$

Based on Assumption 7.1 and Lemma 7.1, we obtain

$O_1 \le \frac{1}{k} \sum_{s=1}^k \sum_{j=1}^v L \| x_i(s) - x_j(s) \|
\le \frac{1}{k} \sum_{s=1}^k vL\gamma(s-1) \| \frac{h_i(s)}{q_i(s)} - \bar{h}(s) \|
+ \frac{1}{k} \sum_{s=1}^k \sum_{j=1}^v L\gamma(s-1) \| \bar{h}(s) - \frac{h_j(s)}{q_j(s)} \|.$

Then, by the subgradient inequality and the definition of $\{\varsigma(k)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}$ given by (7.19), one has that

$O_2 \le \frac{1}{k} \sum_{s=1}^k \sum_{j=1}^v \langle g_j(s), x_j(s) - \varsigma(s) \rangle + \frac{1}{k} \sum_{s=1}^k \sum_{j=1}^v \langle g_j(s), \varsigma(s) - x^* \rangle,$

which, in the light of Lemma 7.1, Assumption 7.1, and Lemma 7.4(b), further indicates that

\(\frac{1}{k} \sum_{s=1}^k \sum_{j=1}^v \langle g_j(s), x_j(s) - \varsigma(s) \rangle\)
\(\le \frac{1}{k} \sum_{s=1}^k \sum_{j=1}^v L \| \Pi_\Omega^\varphi(\frac{h_j(s)}{q_j(s)}, \gamma(s-1)) - \Pi_\Omega^\varphi(\bar{h}(s), \gamma(s-1)) \|\)
\(\le \frac{1}{k} \sum_{s=1}^k \sum_{j=1}^v L \gamma(s-1) \| \frac{h_j(s)}{q_j(s)} - \bar{h}(s) \|\)

and

\(\frac{1}{k} \sum_{s=1}^k \sum_{j=1}^v \langle g_j(s), \varsigma(s) - x^* \rangle\)
\(= \frac{v}{k} \sum_{s=1}^k \langle \frac{1}{v} \sum_{j=1}^v g_j(s), \varsigma(s) - x^* \rangle\)
\(\le \frac{v \varphi^\dagger(-\bar{h}(1), \gamma(0)) + v \langle\bar{h}(1), x^*\rangle}{k} + \frac{v L^2}{2k} \sum_{s=1}^k \gamma(s-1)\)
\(+ \frac{v \varphi(x^*)}{\gamma(k) k}.\)

Finally, combining with (7.26)-(7.29), we can obtain that

\(f(\hat{x}_i(k)) - f(x^*)\)
\(\le \frac{1}{k} \sum_{s=1}^k v L \gamma(s-1) \| \frac{h_i(s)}{q_i(s)} - \bar{h}(s) \| + \frac{v L^2}{2k} \sum_{s=1}^k \gamma(s-1)\)
\(+ \frac{2}{k} \sum_{s=1}^k \sum_{j=1}^v L \gamma(s-1) \| \bar{h}(s) - \frac{h_j(s)}{q_j(s)} \|\)
\(+ \frac{v \varphi(x^*)}{\gamma(k) k} + \frac{v \varphi^\dagger(-\bar{h}(1), \gamma(0)) + v \langle\bar{h}(1), x^*\rangle}{k}.\)

Since \(\gamma(k) = \frac{\gamma(0)}{\sqrt{k+1}}\), one has

\(\sum_{s=1}^k \gamma(s-1) \le \gamma(0)(1 + \int_1^{k+1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{s}} ds) \le 2 \gamma(0) \sqrt{k+1}.\)

Thus it is drawn from Lemma 7.3 that

\( f(\hat{x}_i(k)) - f(x^*) \)
\(\le \frac{3vL\Theta}{k} \sum_{s=1}^k \gamma(s-1) + \frac{vL^2}{2k} \sum_{s=1}^k \gamma(s-1) \)
\( + \frac{v\varphi(x^*)}{\gamma(k)k} + \frac{v\varphi^\dagger(-\bar{h}(1), \gamma(0)) + v\langle\bar{h}(1), x^*\rangle}{k} \)
\(\le \frac{(6vL\Theta\gamma(0) + vL^2\gamma(0))}{\sqrt{k}} + \frac{v\varphi(x^*)}{\sqrt{k}\gamma(0)} \)
\( + \frac{v\varphi^\dagger(-\bar{h}(1), \gamma(0)) + v\langle\bar{h}(1), x^*\rangle}{k}, \quad (7.31) \)

which deduces the desired result.

**Remark 7.7.** Based on Theorem 7.1 and the definition of \(\Theta_1\), we infer that the introduction of correlated perturbations to our algorithm results in a slight reduction in convergence speed compared to a similar algorithm that does not account for privacy protection [27]. The parameter \(\delta\) (the upper bound of the perturbations) is inversely related to the convergence performance. However, this tradeoff is justified since we achieve the significant goal of privacy protection with only a minor decrease in convergence rate compared to [27]. Furthermore, Theorem 7.1 shows that under less stringent initial conditions, our algorithm can still attain the \(\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{K})\) convergence rate similar to the one without privacy measures [27]. Additionally, our approach matches the accuracy of the convergence in [27] without incurring high computational complexity and communication costs, highlighting the benefits of our edge-based correlated perturbations strategy over other privacy-preserving methods like homomorphic encryption [32,33] and differential privacy [36,37].

**Remark 7.8.** It is possible to further enhance Algorithm 1 by improving its communication or computation efficiency. In machine learning, each node calculates a local full gradient for all its samples, leading to significant computational costs when the sample size is large. Therefore employing unbiased stochastic gradients (variance-reduction techniques) [50,51] instead of traditional deterministic gradients can significantly reduce the computational burden on nodes without affecting algorithm convergence. Inspired by recent works [2,3,42,46], we can also incorporate an event-triggering strategy into Algorithm 1, allowing each node to decide online when to broadcast its current estimate to its out-neighbors by testing a specific triggering condition, thereby reducing communication load and improving efficiency. These improvements will be explored in future research.

## 7.5 Analysis of privacy protection

This section focuses on demonstrating how Algorithm 1 ensures privacy protection through the edge-based correlated perturbations strategy. We begin with some preliminaries, followed by the main results regarding the privacy-protection properties of Algorithm 1.

### 7.5.1 Preliminaries

Due to the integration of edge-based correlated perturbations in Algorithm 1, the information obtained by malicious nodes about their in-neighbors is inaccurate. Nonetheless, normal nodes can still receive accurate information from their in-neighbors and use it to update their variables. Malicious nodes, however, might collaborate to infer private information about the sender. Therefore it is essential to ensure that malicious nodes cannot distinguish between real and fake subgradients of normal nodes using the edge-based correlated perturbations strategy in Algorithm 1.

We define $\mathcal{D}_H$ and $\mathcal{D}_E$ as the sets of normal and malicious nodes, respectively. Additionally, we make the following assumption regarding the capabilities of malicious nodes.

**Assumption 7.3.** Malicious nodes have full knowledge of the adjacency matrix and all transmitted information from their in-neighbors. Specifically, node $j \in \mathcal{D}_E$ can observe $h_j(k), \tau_{ij}(k), \tilde{h}_{ji}(k), i \in \mathcal{N}_j^{\text{in}}(k)$, and $P(k)$ for all $k \ge 1$.

Assumption 7.3 allows malicious nodes to possess comprehensive network information, which is more realistic than the assumptions in the existing literature [31,36]. We define the observations of nodes $j \in \mathcal{D}_E$ as $S(\{h_i(1), g_i(k), \tau_{ji}(k)\}_{i,j \in \mathcal{V}, k \ge 1}) = \{ \tilde{h}_{ji}(k) \}_{j \in \mathcal{D}_E, k \ge 1}$ about an output of Algorithm 1, which depend on the input $\{h_i(1), g_i(k), \tau_{ji}(k)\}_{i,j \in \mathcal{V}, k \ge 1}$. The following definition describes the privacy protection of Algorithm 1.

**Definition 7.1.** If there exist $\hat{h}_i(1) \in \mathbb{R}^d$, $\{\hat{g}_i(k) : \hat{g}_i(k) \neq g_i(k)\}$, and $\{\hat{\tau}_{ij}(k) : \hat{\tau}_{ij}(k) = \tau_{ij}(k)\}$ for all $j \in \mathcal{D}_E$ such that $S(\{\hat{h}_i(1), \hat{g}_i(k), \hat{\tau}_{ji}(k)\}_{i,j \in \mathcal{V}} = S(\{h_i(1), g_i(k), \tau_{ji}(k)\}_{i,j \in \mathcal{V}})$ for all $k \ge 1$, then the subgradients $g_i(k)$, $i \in \mathcal{D}_H$, are protected.

According to Definition 7.1, if malicious nodes obtain the same observations from both the actual subgradients $\{g_i(k)\}$ and fake subgradients $\{\hat{g}_i(k)\}$, they cannot distinguish between $g_i(k)$ and $\hat{g}_i(k)$ for any $i \in \mathcal{D}_H$. Thus the privacy of normal nodes is safeguarded.

### 7.5.2 Main results

The following theorem demonstrates the privacy-protection capability of Algorithm 1.

**Theorem 7.2.** Suppose Assumption 7.3 holds. Algorithm 1 can protect the private local subgradients $g_i(k)$ for all $i \in \mathcal{D}_H$.
