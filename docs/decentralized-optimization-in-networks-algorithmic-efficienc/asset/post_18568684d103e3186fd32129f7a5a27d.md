## Asynchronous decentralized algorithms for resource allocation in directed networks

### 1.1 Introduction

Decentralized optimization has garnered significant interest in recent years due to its extensive applicability across various network engineering systems, spanning power grids [1,2], sensor networks [3,4], and edge computing networks [5]. Many optimization challenges encountered in these systems often manifest as decentralized resource allocation quandaries, such as economic dispatch in smart grids [1,2,6,7], computational resource assignment in edge computing [8], and channel allocation in wireless communication [9]. Traditional centralized approaches to resolving such problems typically necessitate a central entity to gather crucial information from all nodes, which proves costly, susceptible to single points of failure, and lacking resilience in new environments. In contrast, decentralized methods circumvent the need for long-distance communication, thus effectively mitigating the drawbacks of centralized approaches while offering enhanced robustness, quicker computation, reduced communication overhead, and bolstered privacy protection [10–13].

### 1.1.1 Literature review

Decentralized optimization techniques, which primarily rely on gradient information, constitute a significant portion of the decentralized resource allocation strategies documented in the research literature [14–21]. Notably, the work presented in [14] introduced a decentralized algorithm without a central coordinator, representing a groundbreaking approach for decentralized implementations. However, this initial algorithm lacks the consideration of local constraints, which are crucial in practical applications, such as limiting the capacity of generators in economic dispatch problems. To address this limitation, a diverse range of decentralized methods have been proposed, including the decentralized bisection method [15], dual gradient algorithms [16], consensus-based optimization [17,18], primal-dual gradient approaches [19,20], and dual splitting algorithms [21]. These techniques aim to incorporate local constraints effectively. Additionally, several efficient methods have been introduced to tackle diverse factors in resource allocation, such as privacy considerations [22], communication efficiency [1], and balancing supply and demand [23]. More recently, decentralized game-theoretic frameworks [4,43,44] have garnered attention in addressing the resource allocation challenge. Specifically, the authors in [43] presented an enhanced and flexible game-theoretic framework known as the MSUPC-CP algorithm, aimed at efficient decentralized power control. In this framework, the decision-making process of users acting as players occurs asynchronously and in a decentralized fashion. Subsequently, the authors in [4,44] introduced two novel decentralized algorithms to efficiently compute the two-variable Nash equilibrium points of optimization problems stemming from two distinct real-world resource allocation scenarios.

When decentralized methods are utilized to address real-world decentralized resource allocation issues, the communication network among the nodes stands as a critical component that merits thorough consideration. In actual applications, information exchange tends to be asymmetric, leading to the development of unbalanced and directed communication networks [28,31]. Unlike traditional methods for managing directed networks, such as the push-sum average consensus technique [28] and the gradient rescaling approach [31], researchers in [24,25] employed the epigraph strategy to tackle the imbalance resulting from directed networks from a fresh perspective. It is worth noting that in [24,25], synchronous decentralized algorithms are examined for general optimization problems, where all nodes update concurrently. This synchronization relies on a central timing reference that nodes consult, enabling them to synchronize global parameters (e.g., step-size), but this significantly constrains their applicability. Furthermore, in practical scenarios, some nodes may remain inactive at certain intervals. To facilitate asynchronous operations among nodes, numerous protocols have been introduced, such as gossip-based protocols [26,27], random sleep protocols [29], and broadcast-based protocols [30,32].

Upon reviewing the current landscape, we recognize the scarcity of asynchronous algorithms that are specifically designed to handle decentralized resource allocation problems in directed networks. Motivated by this realization, the principal aim of this chapter is to explore a decentralized asynchronous algorithm that aims to uncover the optimal solution for decentralized resource allocation issues in directed networks.

### 1.1.2 Main contributions

In this chapter, we examine a comprehensive resource allocation scenario that encompasses diverse real-world applications in directed networks. Rather than addressing the original problem directly, we endeavor to solve its dual counterpart in a fully decentralized and asynchronous framework. To this end, we introduce a novel decentralized asynchronous algorithm grounded on the gossip communication protocol and epigraph methodology, rendering it suitable for practical applications and exhibiting flexibility. In comparison to existing research, the distinguishing features of our current work are summarized as follows:

- (i) A novel decentralized asynchronous methodology is put forth to tackle the resource allocation dilemma in directed networks. Unlike the methods presented in [22,28,31], our algorithm employs an asynchronous updating mechanism based on the gossip communication protocol, effectively enabling optimal resource allocation.
- (ii) Distinct from [7,8,22,28,31], we introduce the epigraph approach to address the imbalance stemming from directed networks in the dual-related challenge from an unconventional angle. This novel strategy remains unexplored in the current approaches for resolving resource allocation issues over directed networks.
- (iii) The proposed algorithm incorporates varied step-sizes for each node, thereby achieving a fully decentralized operation that surpasses existing approaches [19,21,22,33]. Additionally, it facilitates the utilization of solely row-stochastic matrices, which are more straightforward to construct than column-stochastic matrices, as noted in [19,20,22,28].
- (iv) An in-depth analysis of the convergence attributes of the proposed algorithm provides fresh insights into the gossip-based communication paradigm and the epigraph approach for solving resource allocation challenges in directed networks. Moreover, when addressing time-varying directed networks, we delve into a pertinent decentralized asynchronous algorithm that is grounded on the random sleep protocol.

In addition, it is also worth noting that the proposed decentralized asynchronous algorithms enjoy the following three appealing features as compared to the existing game-theoretic approaches [4,43,44] that deal with the decentralized resource allocation problem: (i) Applicable to the problem with coupled equality constraints (consensus protocols); (ii) Suitable for unbalanced directed networks (epigraph strategies); and (iii) Robust to real network environments (gossip communication protocols and random sleep protocols).

### 1.1.3 Organization and notation

Organization: In Section 1.2, we delineate the challenge of resource allocation and present its corresponding dual problem. Progressing to Section 1.3, we introduce a novel decentralized asynchronous algorithm. In Section 1.4, we undertake a thorough convergence analysis of the proposed algorithm. Section 1.5 delves into a related decentralized synchronous algorithm, taking into account time-varying directed networks. The simulations in Section 1.6 demonstrate the remarkable performance of our proposed algorithms. Finally, in Section 1.7, we summarize our findings and discuss potential avenues for future expansion.
