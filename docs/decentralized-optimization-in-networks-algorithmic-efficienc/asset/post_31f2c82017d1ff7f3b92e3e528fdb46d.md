
Proof. We introduce the proof of Theorem 7.2, which primarily uses mathematical induction [40]. Malicious nodes $j \in \mathcal{D}_E$ possess two observations
$$
\{ \tilde{h}_{ji}(k) \}_{j \in \mathcal{D}_E, k \ge 1} = S(\{(h_i(1), g_i(k), \tau_{ji}(k))_{i,j \in \mathcal{V}, k \ge 1}\} \quad \text{and}
$$
$$
\{ \hat{\tilde{h}}_{ji}(k) \}_{j \in \mathcal{D}_E, k \ge 1} = S(\{(\hat{h}_i(1), \hat{g}_i(k), \hat{\tau}_{ji}(k))_{i,j \in \mathcal{V}, k \ge 1}\})
$$
that are generated by Algorithm 1, where the fake subgradients $\hat{g}_i(k)$ are not equal to $g_i(k)$, $i \in \mathcal{D}_H$.

When $k=1$, obviously, $\hat{\tilde{h}}_{ji}(1) = \tilde{h}_{ji}(1)$, $j \in \mathcal{D}_E$ holds for certain $\hat{h}_i(1)$ and $\hat{\tau}_{ji}(k)$. When $k > 1$, suppose that $\hat{\tilde{h}}_{ji}(k) = \tilde{h}_{ji}(k)$, $j \in \mathcal{D}_E$ holds. Then, $\hat{\tilde{h}}_{ji}(k+1) = \tilde{h}_{ji}(k+1)$, $j \in \mathcal{D}_E$ needs to be shown to hold. In this way, we can obtain that $\hat{\tilde{h}}_{ji}(k) = \tilde{h}_{ji}(k)$, $j \in \mathcal{D}_E$ holds, for any $k \ge 1$. Then, it follows from (7.4) and (7.5a) that for a certain malicious node $j \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{out}}(k) \cap \mathcal{D}_E$,

$$
\tilde{h}_{ji}(k+1) = \sum_{l \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{in}}(k) \cup i} P_{il}(k) \tilde{h}_{li}(k) + g_i(k) + \tau_{ji}(k+1)
$$
$$
\hat{\tilde{h}}_{ji}(k+1) = \sum_{l \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{in}}(k) \cup i} P_{il}(k) \hat{\tilde{h}}_{li}(k) + \hat{g}_i(k) + \hat{\tau}_{ji}(k+1)
$$

From here, we continue the proof by utilizing the contradiction. First, we assume that $\hat{\tilde{h}}_{ji}(k+1) = \tilde{h}_{ji}(k+1)$ holds for $j \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{out}}(k) \cap \mathcal{D}_E$. Thus one yields that

$$
\sum_{l \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{in}}(k) \cup i} P_{il}(k) \tilde{h}_{li}(k) + g_i(k) + \tau_{ji}(k+1) = \sum_{l \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{in}}(k) \cup i} P_{il}(k) \hat{\tilde{h}}_{li}(k) + \hat{g}_i(k) + \hat{\tau}_{ji}(k+1). \quad (7.32)
$$

According to Definition 7.1, we know that $\tau_{il}(k) = \hat{\tau}_{il}(k)$, $\forall l \in \mathcal{D}_E$. Since $\hat{\tilde{h}}_{ji}(k+1) = \tilde{h}_{ji}(k+1)$, we conclude that $\hat{\tilde{h}}_{ji}(k) = \tilde{h}_{ji}(k)$, $j \in \mathcal{D}_E$ holds, for any $k \ge 1$. In addition, we default that $g_l(k) = \hat{g}_l(k)$ holds for $l \in \mathcal{D}_E$. Based on the above, one has $\sum_{l \in (\mathcal{N}_i^{\text{in}}(k) \cap \mathcal{D}_E) \cup \{i\}} P_{il}(k) (\tilde{h}_{il}(k) - \hat{\tilde{h}}_{il}(k)) = 0$, which combined with (7.32) indicates that

$$
\sum_{l \in (\mathcal{N}_i^{\text{in}}(k) \cap \mathcal{D}_H) \cup \{i\}} P_{il}(k) (\tilde{h}_{il}(k) - \hat{\tilde{h}}_{il}(k)) = \hat{g}_i(k) - g_i(k) + \hat{\tau}_{ji}(k+1) - \tau_{ji}(k+1). \quad (7.33)
$$

Also, we obtain from (7.7) that

$$
\sum_{l \in (\mathcal{N}_i^{\text{out}}(k) \cap \mathcal{D}_H) \cup \{i\}} P_{il}(k) (\tilde{h}_{li}(k) - \hat{\tilde{h}}_{li}(k)) = 0. \quad (7.34)
$$

At time \( k \), we consider a directed (sub)network \(\mathcal{G}_H(k) = \{\mathcal{D}_H, \mathcal{E}_H(k)\}\) consisting of the nodes set \(\mathcal{D}_H = \{1, 2, ..., v'\}\). Edge \((j, i) \in \mathcal{E}_H(k)\), \(i, j \in \mathcal{D}_H\) denotes that at time \( k \), node \( j \) delivers messages to node \( i \). Assume that the network \(\mathcal{G}_H(k)\) possesses \( v_e \) edges at time \( k \). If and only if both the \( s \)th edge \((j, i) \in \mathcal{E}_H(k)\) with \( s \in \{1, ..., v_e\}\) and \( j > i \) (or \( i > j \)) are established at time \( k \), then we set \([A_+(k)]_{js} = 1\) (or \([A_-(k)]_{js} = 1\)), otherwise, both \([A_+(k)]_{js}\) and \([A_-(k)]_{js}\) are equal to 0, where \([A_+(k)]_{js}\) represents the \( s \)th edge for the \( j \)th node of the incidence-like matrix \(A_+(k) \in \mathbb{R}^{v' \times v_e}\) associated with the network \(\mathcal{G}_H(k)\) at time \( k \) and \([A_-(k)]_{js}\) is defined similarly.

Combining (7.33) and (7.34), we obtain a linear equation that is expressed in the form of a matrix as follows:

$$
(\theta(k) \otimes I_d)[h^T(k) \quad \tau^T(k+1)]^T = [g^T(k) \quad 0^T]^T, \quad (7.35)
$$

where \(\theta(k) = \begin{bmatrix} A_-(k) & A_+(k) & I_{v'} & I_{v'} \\ A_+(k) & A_-(k) & I_{v'} & 0 \end{bmatrix}\), and \( h(k) \) is the column vector comprised of \(P_{il}(\tilde{h}_{il}(k) - \hat{\tilde{h}}_{il}(k))\); \(\tau(k+1)\) and \( g(k) \) are comprised of \(\tau_{ji}(k+1) - \hat{\tilde{\tau}}_{ji}(k+1)\) and \(\hat{\tilde{g}}_i(k) - g_i(k)\), \(i \in \mathcal{D}_H\), respectively. From the form of matrix \(\theta(k)\), it is apparent that rank\((\theta(k))\) = rank\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix} I_{v'} & I_{v'} \\ I_{v'} & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right) = 2v'\) holds. According to the property of the Kronecker product, we further deduce that rank\((\theta(k) \otimes I_d) = 2dv'\), which states clearly that the coefficient matrix of Eq. (7.35) has full row rank. Then, at least one solution in Eq. (7.35) is the given \(\hat{\tilde{g}}_i(k)\) for all \( k \ge 1 \), which indicates that the existence of \(\{\hat{h}_i(1), \hat{g}_i(k), \hat{\tau}_{ji}(k)\}_{i, j \in \mathcal{V}, k \ge 1}\) satisfying that \(\hat{\tilde{h}}_{ji}(k+1) = \tilde{h}_{ji}(k+1)\) holds for \( j \in \mathcal{D}_E \).

Consequently, the process of the mathematical induction is complete, which further means that there exist \(\{\hat{h}_i(1), \hat{g}_i(k), \hat{\tau}_{ji}(k)\}_{i, j \in \mathcal{V}, k \ge 1}\) such that two observations of the malicious nodes are the same, and thus the privacy protection is achieved. This completes the proof. \(\square\)

### 7.6.1 Decentralized constrained sparse logistic regression problems

Here, we address a decentralized constrained sparse logistic regression problem incorporating both \( l_1 \)-norm and \( l_2 \)-norm regularized logistic cost functions, expressed as:

$$
\min_{x \in \Omega} \sum_{i=1}^v \sum_{j=1}^{n_i} \ln[1 + \exp(-b_{ij} e_{ij}^T x)] + \rho_1 ||x||_1 + \rho_2 ||x||^2,

where the parameters used are detailed in Section 7.2.3. In our simulations, we utilized the breast cancer dataset from the UCI Machine Learning Repository [45] to assess Algorithm 1’s performance, with \(\sum_{i=1}^v n_i = 200\) (randomly distributed across local nodes) and \(d = 9\). The regularization parameters are set to \(\rho_1 = 0.5, \rho_2 = 0.5\), and the domain \(\Omega = [-5, 5]^d\). The upper limit of the noise \(\tau_{ji}(k), j \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{out}}(k)\), is \(\delta = 30\). Initial conditions follow Algorithm 1. For network configurations, a strongly connected directed network \(\mathcal{G}\) with \(v = 10\) nodes was randomly generated. Each network edge was sampled with a 30% probability at each time \(k\) to create a time-varying network \(\mathcal{G}(k)\), ensuring \(B\)-strong connectivity. The Yalmip toolbox in Matlab was employed to obtain the problem’s optimal solution. Results are described in three segments, using the residual metric \((1/v)(\sum_{i=1}^v ||\hat{x}_i^k - x^*|| / ||\hat{x}_i^1 - x^*||)\) for presentation.
