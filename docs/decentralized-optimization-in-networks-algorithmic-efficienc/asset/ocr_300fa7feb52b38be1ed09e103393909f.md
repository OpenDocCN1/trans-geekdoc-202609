In recent times, a gradient tracking approach has been introduced that uses prior
knowledge to accurately approximate the average gradient. Thanks to its efficiency
and simplicity in implementation, the gradient tracking approach found applica-
tions in various settings [15,21,25–27]. Specifically, Qu and Li [26] adopted gradi-
ent tracking in their DGD method to harness the function’s smoothness, achieving
a convergence rate comparable to the centralized method [28]. Nedić et al. [15]
merged a decentralized inexact gradient approach with gradient tracking, resulting
in a method named DIGing that excels in undirected networks. They further adapted
DIGing for directed networks, yielding Push-DIGing, which converges to the global
minimum with just a column-stochastic matrix. To tackle nonconvex decentralized
constrained optimization problems in directed networks, Scutari and Sun [27] incor-
porated successive convex approximation techniques into a push-sum method [29].
Indeed, many optimization techniques for directed networks rely on the push-sum
framework. However, its usage often leads to additional costs in communication
and computation. To circumvent these extra expenses, Xin and Khan [21] devised
a method that integrates an inexact gradient approach with a gradient estimation
technique, enabling convergence to the global minimum without relying on push-
sum. Chen and Sayed [25] on the other hand, introduced adaptive diffusion strategies
to decentralized optimization, facilitating real-time agent communication, mitigat-
ing gradient noise effects, and quantifying noise through an optimization process.
Quantized decentralized optimization has also garnered significant attention due to
its communication efficiency [30–32]. Notably, Xiong et al. [30] presented a novel
quantized decentralized gradient tracking method called Q-DGT, which effectively
achieves its objectives. Xin and Khan [33] demonstrated that most gradient tracking
methods are related to the AB/pull-pull method [21,34]. Additionally, they enhanced
the AB/pull-pull method by incorporating a momentum term, resulting in accel-
erated performance even when dealing with ill-conditioned cost functions.

Decentralized stochastic optimization stands as a notable advancement in the
field of decentralized optimization. It falls into two categories: one where samples
are derived by combining true gradients with noise, and another where samples
are randomly selected from the entire dataset. A popular technique to preserve
computational integrity involves blending the true gradient with stochastic noise
[14,16,17,35,36]. Xin et al. [17] introduced a stochastic gradient variant of the
AB, termed S-AB, demonstrating its ability to converge toward the vicinity of
the global minimum with a constant step-size. Pu and Nedić [16] presented a dis-
tributed stochastic gradient tracking approach, which can be seen as a specialized
form of S-AB utilizing doubly stochastic weight matrices. Furthermore, Zhao and
Liu [14] delved deeper into the convergence attributes of S-AB when employed with
a diminishing step-size, highlighting its excellent performance in terms of con-
vergence precision and speed. In another development, Qureshi et al. [36] put forward
S-ADDOPT, a decentralized stochastic optimization technique that can be considered
a stochastic gradient adaptation of ADD-OPT [37]. Notably, S-ADDOPT exhibits
strong convergence qualities and is influenced by the underlying network structure.
