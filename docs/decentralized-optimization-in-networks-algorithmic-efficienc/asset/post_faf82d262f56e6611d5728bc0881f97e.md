
To overcome these challenges while maintaining privacy protection for EDP over directed networks, an effective method was proposed in [36] by adding conditional noise to a decentralized algorithm based on column-stochastic matrices. However, this algorithm does not consider computational efficiency, and column-stochastic matrices are harder to achieve in a decentralized manner compared to row-stochastic matrices [24,27,37,38]. The emergence of the random sleep strategy offers a new perspective for designing decentralized algorithms to improve computational efficiency. Numerous works on decentralized random sleep methods over networks exist, such as [39–41]. However, no current decentralized random sleep method simultaneously addresses EDP over directed networks and ensures privacy protection. Therefore it is imperative to develop a privacy-protected decentralized random sleep algorithm to solve EDP over directed networks.

### 8.1.2 Main contributions

This chapter's primary objective is to design an innovative decentralized algorithm to tackle the general EDP, achieving certain goals that current methods do not address. The main contributions of this study are summarized as follows:

- (i) A new privacy-preserving decentralized random sleep algorithm is developed for the EDP over an unbalanced directed network. This algorithm successfully meets three key objectives with precise convergence: (i) safeguarding privacy; (ii) enhancing computational efficiency; (iii) applicability to unbalanced directed networks.

- (ii) While most existing privacy-preserving decentralized methods based on differential privacy mechanisms compromise on optimality [31–33], our proposed algorithm protects sensitive information by incorporating conditional noise into the state exchange and ensures convergence to the optimal EDP solution in an unbalanced directed network.

- (iii) The proposed algorithm uniquely integrates the random sleep strategy into the decentralized optimization method for the EDP in smart grids, achieving optimal dispatch and reducing computational load while improving efficiency compared to existing methods [23–25,36].

- (iv) Utilizing only a row-stochastic weight matrix, the algorithm allows generators to locally assign weights to information received from their in-neighbors, making it easier to construct compared to the column-stochastic weight matrices used in other studies [23,25,26,36]. Additionally, it supports nonuniform step-sizes for all generators, enabling fully decentralized implementation. The error bound and privacy properties of the algorithm are also thoroughly analyzed.

## 8.2 Problem description and algorithm construction

This section outlines the EDP and introduces the problem transformation. Subsequently, a privacy-protected decentralized random sleep algorithm is proposed.

### 8.2.1 Problem description

This study considers a network of \(m\) generators collaboratively solving the following EDP in smart grids:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\min_{p \in \mathbb{R}^m} J(p) &= \sum_{i=1}^m J_i(p_i), \\
\text{s.t. } \sum_{i=1}^m p_i &= \sum_{i=1}^m d_i = D, \\
p_i^{\min} &\le p_i \le p_i^{\max}, \quad \forall i = 1, \dots, m,
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.1}
\]

where \(J_i : \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\), \(p=[p_1, p_2, ..., p_m]^{\mathrm{T}} \in \mathbb{R}^m\) with \(p_i \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\), \(D \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\), and \(d_i \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\). Define \(P_i=\{p^{\dagger} \in \mathbb{R} | p_i^{\min} \le p_i^{\dagger} \le p_i^{\max}\}\) and \(P=P_1 \times \dots \times P_m\). To ensure the feasibility of problem (8.1) and the reliable operation of smart grids, the total power demand satisfies \(\sum_{i=1}^m p_i^{\min} \le D \le \sum_{i=1}^m p_i^{\max}\). Let \(p^*=[p_1^*, p_2^*, ..., p_m^*]^{\mathrm{T}} \in \mathbb{R}^m\) be the optimal dispatch to (8.1).

Similar to many EDP studies [21,23,24,36], this chapter considers general convex cost functions that satisfy the following assumption.

**Assumption 8.1.** The function \(J_i : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}, i \in \mathcal{V}\), is strictly convex and continuously differentiable.

Assumption 8.1 implies that the EDP (8.1) is a convex problem with a unique optimal solution. The cost function considered here is similar to those in the literature [13,19,36], such as quadratic functions used for privacy analysis. Thus Assumption 8.1 is easily met.

### 8.2.2 System model

In this chapter, each generator is assigned to a bus within the bus system of smart grids. The communication among these generators (buses) is modeled as an unbalanced directed network, denoted as \(\mathcal{G} = \{\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}\}\), where \(\mathcal{V} = \{1, 2, \dots, m\}\) represents the set of vertices (generators) and \(\mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{V} \times \mathcal{V}\) represents the set of edges. An edge \((j, i) \in \mathcal{E}\) indicates that generator \(j\) can directly transmit data to generator \(i\), making \(i\) an out-neighbor of \(j\), and vice versa, \(j\) an in-neighbor of \(i\). We define \(\mathcal{N}_i^{\text{out}} = \{j \in \mathcal{V} | (i, j) \in \mathcal{E}\}\) and \(\mathcal{N}_i^{\text{in}} = \{j \in \mathcal{V} | (j, i) \in \mathcal{E}\}\). If there exists an \(i\) such that \(\mathcal{N}_i^{\text{out}} \neq \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{in}}\), then the network \(\mathcal{G}\) is considered unbalanced. Strong connectivity in a directed network implies that there are directed paths connecting any pair of generators. To facilitate communication among generators, we adopt the following assumption regarding the communication network (system model) [24,32,40].

**Assumption 8.2.** The directed network \(\mathcal{G}\) is strongly connected.

This assumption ensures that each generator can directly or indirectly influence all other generators in the network. Although this assumption is more restrictive than the uniformly and strongly connected assumption, it remains broadly applicable [24, 32,37].

**Remark 8.2.** In real-world economic dispatch scenarios of smart grids, data exchange between neighboring generators often occurs in less-than-perfect environments, posing privacy risks. Therefore it is critical to safeguard the privacy of generators in decentralized networks. Current privacy-preserving techniques, such as differential privacy [31–33] and homomorphic encryption [34,35], significantly impact the performance (in terms of convergence and computational cost) of decentralized economic dispatch algorithms. Furthermore, not all transmitted information necessitates feedback, i.e., the network is directed, and practical solutions must accommodate this scenario. Consequently, this chapter aims for the proposed algorithm to achieve three primary objectives under accurate convergence: (i) ensuring privacy protection; (ii) enhancing computational efficiency; (iii) applicability to an unbalanced directed network.

For problem (8.1), a generator's private information includes \(J_i(p_i)\), \(d_i\), \(p_i^{\min}\), and \(p_i^{\max}\), which are inaccessible to other generators. The ultimate goal of this chapter is to enable generators to interact with their respective neighbors over an unbalanced directed network to collaboratively and efficiently solve problem (8.1) without disclosing private information.
