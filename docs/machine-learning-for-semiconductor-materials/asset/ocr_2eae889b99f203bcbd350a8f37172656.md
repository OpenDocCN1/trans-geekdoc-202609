Notably, when biomolecules are present within the cavity at low doping levels, the threshold voltage changes by more than ~400%, indicating substantial sensitivity, crucial for biosensing applications.

While achieving high percentage sensitivity at low doping levels is beneficial, the device's conductivity reduces under such conditions, imposing limitations on using low doping in BioFETs. Thus, a balance between sensitivity and conductivity must be struck through optimized doping levels to achieve optimal performance. Additionally, the presence of charged biomolecules affects the value of the threshold voltage. Positively charged biomolecules attract electrons from the substrate, creating a channel at a lower gate voltage and reducing the threshold voltage compared to neutral biomolecules. Conversely, negatively charged biomolecules increase the threshold voltage. Therefore, the sensitivity of the threshold voltage exhibits a similar trend for charged biomolecules as observed with neutral biomolecules. Understanding these dynamics is crucial for tailoring BioFET designs to specific biomolecular interactions and optimizing their performance in diverse biological and environmental applications.
