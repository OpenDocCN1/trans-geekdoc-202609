# ABSTRACT

A transformer-based method was firstly developed to predict the hourly PM₂₅ concentration at 12 monitoring stations in Beijing. Convolutional neural network short-term memory-attention mechanism (CNN-LSTM attention) were introduced to compare with Transformer models. Experiments on long time series and future prediction were conducted to evaluate the model performance and generalization ability over a period of time. The results, namely the mean variance score (EVS), R² absolute error, mean square error (MSE), and R values of Transformer were 12%, 6%, and 30% higher than those of the CNN-LSTM attention, respectively. The results show that the Transformer outperformed the CNN-LSTM model in terms of prediction accuracy. The capture-turn attention mechanism identifies patterns affected by abruptly meteorological conditions and trends with significant seasonal changes, in autumn and winter when the pollution situation is more complex. Transformer has obvious advantages in overcoming the independence problem of learning, providing a new method for the long-term prediction of air quality.

# 1. Introduction

China's air quality has greatly improved since the implementation of the Air Pollution Prevention and Control Plan in 2013, PM₂₅ concentrations in some cities still exceed the limits recommended by the World Health Organization (10 μg/m³). China still faces challenges in improving air quality in autumn and winter when pollution concentration in northern China sometimes exceeds the recommended limit (Guo et al., 2014). Regional pollution, which is mainly driven by PM₂₅, has been observed extensively in China (Zheng et al., 2008; Zhou et al., 2016). Air quality monitoring stations are densely distributed in urban areas and can provide the public with real-time air quality data, but the public needs a more accurate air quality prediction. The key is to use monitoring data from one or more stations to

predict pollutant concentrations (Xu et al., 2021). Statistical and deterministic methods used in air quality prediction. Methods such as the multiple linear regression (Vloshchinski et al., 2011) and autoregressive moving average (ARMA) (Kumar and Jain, 2010) were used in short-term air quality prediction. However, the linear models cannot address the complex nonlinear effects of atmospheric pollutants and meteorological factors on pollutant concentrations. Furthermore, the photochemical models approach only focuses on individual pollutants that are directly measurable and is computationally intensive.
