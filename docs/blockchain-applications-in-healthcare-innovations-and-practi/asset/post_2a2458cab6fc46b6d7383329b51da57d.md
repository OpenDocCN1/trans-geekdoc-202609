

However complicated it may sound to build new applications, platforms and functionalities on the new-age technology called Blockchain using concepts further next-gen such as smart contracts, it is indeed quite straightforward to implement certain use cases, at least in the shape of a PoC (proof-of-concept). Below are some of the implementations and deployments being carried out by multiple organizations across the globe, either as PoCs, SaaS (software-as-a-service)-based offerings or fully-fledged deployments connected to legacy systems.

## 10.4.1. Transparency in supply chain

A significant challenge in the healthcare sector is to confirm the authenticity of medical devices. Blockchain becomes the centerpiece while companies try to streamline their supply chains to allow themselves, as well as customers, to track items throughout the chain. This results in complete transparency across the value chain.

This has been a big reason to worry about the fact that counterfeit drugs cause tens of thousands of serious ailments and even deaths every year.

Main benefits of Blockchain in such use cases:

- Compliance: it is imperative to be able to record and report elements in a supply chain. Combining supply chain data in a single system helps optimize compliance.

- Customer trust: it is important for medical device and drug manufacturers to be able to build trust with their customers. Doing so is increasingly possible by tracking the products using a Blockchain, which includes third-party integrations with manufacturers, distributors, etc.

## 10.4.2. Electronic health records on the Blockchain

Health systems across the globe face the issue of information being restricted to data silos, thus leaving patients, as well as hospitals, with an incomplete picture of health records forming their medical history.

A valid solution would be to put these EHRs (Electronic Health Records) on a Blockchain-based system, which will eventually give a complete picture regarding a patient – depending on the kind of access privileges. Such access, as well as data additions, can be managed using hashes inserted into the Blockchain, and the patient can provide approval for sharing such data typically by signing these transactions on the Blockchain network.

In such a case, every change to the records and instance of patients’ approval to share their medical record is committed as a transaction on the Blockchain.

Blockchain-enabled EHRs have many benefits. Health insurers can receive validated confirmation of health services directly and instantly from the patient without the expense of an intermediary. As an example, Medicalchain is developing a platform on which other digital health solutions can be built, which include virtual counselling services and medical data exchange, enabling patients to sell their anonymized medical records and data in exchange for MED tokens to help them develop digital health applications.

## 10.4.3. Use of smart contracts for insurance and billing in supply chain management

Many providers offer Blockchain-based systems that allow various healthcare players, such as pharmaceutical companies, wholesalers, insurers and healthcare providers, to verify their corporate identity, log contract data and track transactions of goods and services, and payment processing data for these goods and services. By sharing digital contracts between manufacturers, retailers and healthcare organizations, instead of each player having their own version of contracts, they can significantly reduce disputes over quality checks, chargebacks, claims and other issues.

## 10.4.4. Verification of medical personnel’s identity cards

Verifying background information has been an important cause across domains. When it comes to healthcare, institutes need to be certain about the experience of a medical professional.

US-based company ProCredEx has developed such a system for verifying medical certificates using the R3 Corda Blockchain protocol.

The main advantages of the Blockchain system in such a use case are:

- faster approval from health institutes and hospitals during the recruitment process;

- transparency and security for partners and vendors, for example, organizations outsourcing representatives, or in new virtual care models to inform patients about the experiences of medical staff.

## 10.4.5. IoT security for remote patient monitoring

What we can also see all around us these days is the use of smart devices to monitor our health – fitness bands, weight trackers or even a ring that can monitor your health, sleep and activity patterns, such as the Oura ring. Talking about the medical devices at large, hospitals and institutes have started employing multiple IoT-based devices to track the health of a patient constantly. This may include the elderly who need constant supervision, even while discharged from hospitals.

In such scenarios, the security protocols followed by these devices become a high priority, and the use of Blockchain to successfully trace any intrusions is a good starting point. Additionally, permissioned Blockchains would ensure that only approved nodes (devices) can interact with the system.

## 10.5. Building smart contracts on the Ethereum Blockchain

Ethereum Blockchain is the second-largest cryptocurrency available on the market, after Bitcoin. The evolution of Ethereum had brought significantly great success since its establishment in 2015. The invention of a Russian Programmer conveniently lead to new decentralized applications to be developed. For the development of decentralized applications, Ethereum is the best Blockchain platform recommended. To create your contracts, you can reliably use the Ethereum Blockchain platform. Ethereum is a distributed infrastructure that potentially assists in accomplishing projects. Smart contracts can be created, developed and deployed using Ethereum Blockchain (NTU 2020). You can create smart contracts on the Ethereum platform, so let us understand the execution procedure involved in execution in detail.

### 10.5.1. Ethereum virtual machine (EVM)

EVM is a virtual machine acting as a runtime environment for building smart contracts on the Ethereum platform. EVM is sandboxed from the host operating system. Each EVM runs when any single contract is executed. Smart contracts prefer the Ethereum platform for deployment because of its higher abstraction layer. It is the global supercomputer that gears up the execution of every action. EVM does not make any delayed Internet calls or pass on any random number as it is solely a virtual machine. EVM prefers programming language coding compared to assembly language (NTU 2020; SitePen 2020). Some components take part while implementing the smart contracts on the platform. Each component is explained below in detail.

Before that, we have mentioned a summary of the working of smart contracts on Ethereum. Smart contracts that need to be developed on Ethereum prefer to use code in the solidity programming language. Once the program is written, it is compiled into bytecode because EVM understands bytecode easily. Then, this bytecode is deployed in the Ethereum network transaction. Ethereum depicts the value of a token called Ether (Yuan et al. 2018). Ethereum supplies operations including computation and data transfer with a fixed price. Also, only the desired amount of gas is consumed that is needed for the execution of the operation. The transaction contains a term named gas limit that means to define maximum gas consumption. After the contract calls the function, it requires the lower limit of gas to be specified. There are some conditions applicable during execution (Yuan et al. 2018). If the function executes normally, then it uses the corresponding gas and returns the leftover gas (Yuan et al. 2018). This is the standard procedure while deploying the smart contract on Ethereum.

### 10.5.2. Gas

Gas is a measuring unit present inside EVM to allocate particular fees for each transaction within smart contracts. Every action of computation needs a certain amount of gas. The more the computation is, the more gas is used. Now, to program on Ethereum, a specific programming language called Solidity is needed.

### 10.5.3. Solidity

Solidity is the programming language defined for smart contracts developed on Ethereum. On the top layer of Ethereum, smart contracts are developed using Solidity, which is a simple object-oriented programming that uses class or language. Solidity does not have a difficult syntax that is tough to understand (SitePen 2020). This programming language is influenced via popular languages that developers prefer. These are C++, Python, JavaScript. It can perform several arbitrary computation activities like raising tickets, sending and gaining tokens, and core operations.
