We are well aware that the EHRs have been playing an important role in the e-healthcare system where the exchange of a patient’s medical records by means of a portal is assigned by medical professionals, but there are security issues due to the lack of integrity and confidentiality. The paper by Benil and Jasper (2020) has proposed the Elliptical Curve Certificate less Aggregate Cryptography Signature scheme (EC-ACS), for securing the EHRs by verifying and reviewing the information in the Medical Server Cloud (MSC) by use of the Blockchain technology. For sharing and storing the data in the medical cloud storage, Elliptical Curve Cryptography (ECC) is used for encrypting the medical data and Certificate less Aggregate Signature (CAS) for generating a digital signature. Security and privacy are promised by this scheme, and it also safeguards confidential information from malicious activities in the cloud health storage.

The privacy of sensitive medical data can be compromised when they are shared beyond the scope of the institutions. A Blockchain-based solution has been proposed in Hussein et al. (2018) to resolve the challenges that are related to access control and handling the data. To improve security, the system is equipped with discrete wavelet transform and to upgrade the queuing optimization technique, a genetic algorithm is deployed. Verifying the user becomes fast with the use of this cryptographic key generator as it improves the immunity and the access control of the system. This design allows accountability as the number of users are known and the Blockchain keeps a record of the user’s actions. The sharing of the data is allowed only when cryptographic keys and identities are known.

In the paper by Nguyen et al. (2020), an analysis of the patient’s data that has been encrypted using Blockchain technology is presented. Nowadays, because of advancements occurring in technology, data sharing has become a challenge; on the one hand, the compatibility can be compromised by using heterogeneous data, while on the other hand, the distinctive use of the medical terminologies has limited data comprehension. We have seen that authorized users and centralized data storage have been targets for attackers, and the establishment of the health records over a data-sharing network can be troublesome. For data discovery and access, the concord mechanism is used to substantiate the proof of word and inter-operability. “The approach offered by the paper gives a centralized trust-based source for network consensus and compute consensus proof of semantic and structural interoperability”.

In the paper by Dagher et al. (2018), a Blockchain-assumed framework has been alleged for an interoperable, secure and efficient siege to data by authorized users while preserving the patient’s information. This framework was proposed since despite increasing the security of EHRs and the efforts made by cities all over the globe to form a “smart city infrastructure”, a person’s personal information is at the risk of getting leaked.
