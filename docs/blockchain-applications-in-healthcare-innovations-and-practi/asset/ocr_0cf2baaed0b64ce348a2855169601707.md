We studied the distribution of the 354 articles by publication year, language, place of origin, journal and yearly average number of citations in the fourth phase of evaluation. The articles’ nations of origin were investigated by determining the nationalities of the organizations to which the authors belonged. If an article had two writers working for organizations in two distinct nations, the country of origin was determined to be two countries. The top 10 articles were determined using the yearly average number of citations of the 464 articles. By adjusting for the effects of time after publication, the yearly average number of citations can more effectively represent the degree of influence of each piece on other research than the overall number of citations. For example, an article published in 2016 is more likely to have been referenced than one published in 2019. As a result, we used the yearly average number of citations to rank the 354 papers.
