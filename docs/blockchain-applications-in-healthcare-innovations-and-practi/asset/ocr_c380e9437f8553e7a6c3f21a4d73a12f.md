Decentralization: in a typical centralized transaction system, each action must be verified by a single authorized organization. As a result, decentralization necessitates confidence, which is the most pressing problem, as well as raising durability, availability and failover, for which the decentralized P2P Blockchain framework may be a better fit (Bhushan et al. 2020). In the Blockchain network, a transaction may be made by any two peers (P2P) without the need for permission from a central agent. By using a variety of consensus procedures, Blockchain can help to alleviate confidence concerns. Furthermore, it will have lower processing costs (including production and operating costs) and eliminate bottlenecks in the core server's performance. Blockchain, on the contrary, has some tradeoffs in several ways. In PoW instances, such as Bitcoin and Ethereum, infrastructure and electricity expenses are in greater magnitude, but throughput is frequently orders of magnitude worse.

Persistence: Blockchain provides a platform for analyzing facts, enabling suppliers and consumers to establish that the data is credible and unaltered.
