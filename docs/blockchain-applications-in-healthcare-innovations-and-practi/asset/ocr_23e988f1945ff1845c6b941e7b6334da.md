It is possible to provide data to a third party, i.e. HP2 in this case, but what we need to understand here is that data sharing is not manual via email or physical documents. Instead, we here consider a scenario where an IT-enabled system at HP2 will connect with systems at HP1 and after proper validation, it will access the data. However, in this whole process, there are lot of security challenges such as unauthorized access, elevation of privileges, man in the middle attacks, data leakage, etc. Security controls need to be implemented, some at the perimeter level, and some at the code level too to remediate different security threats, but this will make the system too complex. The consortium Blockchain is the solution here, meaning that there will be a network of hospitals. Every hospital will have their own private Blockchains, and at the same time, a permissioned Blockchain will be created which will be managed by authorized hospitals of the network, this Blockchain is also known as the consortium Blockchain. All hospitals can access the consortium Blockchain using a search index and can look for patient records. Figure 7.9 provides a visualization of this example. It is important to understand that Figure 7.9 is high level; moreover, the low-level architecture will provide the workings of the consensus mechanism, Blockchain-based sharing, the workings of the search index and most importantly validation of requests.
