Given the amount of transactions (sharing of information), including various healthcare objects and the costs that incur in Hospital Maintenance Systems (HIE), using Blockchain technologies that democratically sustain in various public ledgers are recorded with various novel and inspiring prospects in the healthcare industry. The Blockchain technology has the ability to build an electronic environment in which people perform various business transactions (information sharing) through a disseminated community instead of a central authority. This could have a big impact on how transparent the system is and the functions of each institution (Dias et al. 2018; Ravi Kumar et al. 2019). By improving exchange of information along with coordination with various healthcare groups, Blockchain can also help individuals become more self-sufficient in sharing their medical records with clinicians. Based on the scale, most of the mainstream HIE servers are coordinated by huge enterprises or institutions of healthcare. Centralized administration may introduce privacy and security issues due to the abuse of authority, such as ancillary use of medical records and their unapproved access, or even hacker attacks. Blockchain technology, on the contrary, may support a range of characteristics such as decentralization.

Most of the studies looking at the consequences of Blockchain-based distributed ledger technology for performing healthcare practices have generated a lot of interest (Dai et al. 2020; Feng et al. 2020). However, little quantitative study has been done on HIE’s use of Blockchain technology. Patients’ opinions for enabling the deployment of Blockchain-based HIE networks are unknown and it is unclear whether patients (HIE stakeholders) will support its use. As a result, most of the research needed to evaluate the principal implementation of Blockchain technology over the healthcare industry is from the patient’s point of view. This chapter findings can help healthcare organizations or communities and politicians clearly understand the use of Blockchain technology in recognizing various incorporated benefits and risks in healthcare practices (Blum et al. 1988; Crameri et al. 2020).
