Khan et al. (2012) presented various characteristics of CBSD life-cycle models and proposed a new model called an improved model for reuse. This new model encompasses both component-based and software development processes. In this paper, the authors provided a comparative analysis between existing models with their proposed model and discussed their pros and cons. Garg and Jha (2019) compared various component-based life-cycle models and discussed a new proposed model that makes the component selection procedure easier. This proposed model provides the best-fit mechanism for the selection of any existing components. It contains three types of repositories, namely a black repository, white repository and gray repository. To construct any software, a developer can go to each repository in parallel and select appropriate components. Kaushik and Dulawat (2012) and Kumar and Bhatia (2014) discussed comparative analysis between the traditional life-cycle model and CBSD life-cycle models and described their strengths and weaknesses. According to the authors, the traditional approach only has limited requirements with limited features, but the component-based approach requirement changes frequently and supports design flexibility and reusability. According to the authors, component reusability is an emerging topic of the software engineering community. Reusability saves time and effort and reduces the development time and cost of software.

CBSE proposes an attractive approach that can be used to build any software application. This approach supports flexibility of design, changes requirements rapidly, improves quality and performance, and easily detects a fault in implementation (Nautiyal et al. 2012). Hence, due to these features, people get a better-quality product within time and budget. Due to the above facility, many IT industries are adopting this technology to develop healthcare software. In many healthcare applications, component-based approach is treated as a service-oriented approach that handles the complex environment software and gives a better analysis of the patient record (Chu 2005). Healthcare applications always insist on adopting best practices and software development, a process model that helps to improve the quality of care and fast recovery rate from the disease. Healthcare IT solutions help to improve interoperability between the doctor and patient, reduce the overall costs of healthcare delivery and increase the accessibility of patient records according to their requirement.
