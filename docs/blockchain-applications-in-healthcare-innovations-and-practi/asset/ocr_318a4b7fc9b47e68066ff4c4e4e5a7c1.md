+   - Timestamp dependence: the miners calculate the date and time for the transaction generated. They may change the timestamp by a few seconds if other miners approve the transaction they provided. The problem comes from the fact that certain smart contracts use a time as a triggering event, such as a payment system, and thus an adversary might be altered.

+   - Mishandled exceptions: once a contract communicates with another, the present operation pauses for the call to complete before proceeding. The caller may or may not be notified of this exception. To verify that the call was completed, the responder should expressly check the callee's callback function. Nevertheless, if the client refuses to properly examine the callback function, it may lead to potential dangers.

+   - Re-entrancy vulnerability: once a contract communicates with another, the present operation pauses for the call to complete before proceeding. Also, because the fallback method enables an intruder to re-enter the caller's method, an intruder may take advantage of the sender's initial point to conduct repeated calls, culminating in loops of invocations that get many reimbursements and drain the balance. One of the most well-known re-entry vulnerabilities is the Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (DAO) attack (CoinDesk n.d.).

Some security analysis tools are being developed to address contract issues. Since many contract problems are caused by a lexical mismatch among developers' knowledge of the underlying executing meanings smart contracts, Luu et al. (2016) developed Oyente, a symbolic processing tool, to identify possible security bugs in Ethereum smart contracts. Oyente identified 8,833 Ethereum smart contracts as susceptible out of 19366, including the DAO bug. Its analysis is divided into two steps: first, it symbolically examines the dependency structure of the contract in order to elicit precise linguistic metadata from the program. After this, it analyses violation and different activities to see if they capture enough criteria to determine if a property is valid or not. Many flaws, like transaction reordering, repetitive calls, unsafe code patterns and so on, can be analyzed by Securify. Manticore (GitHub n.d.) seems to be another regular expression technique for evaluating binary and smart contracts. It is capable of capturing a program execution trail for each produced input.
