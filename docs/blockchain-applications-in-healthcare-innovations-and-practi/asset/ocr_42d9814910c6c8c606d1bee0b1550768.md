# 2.6.2. Privacy and legal issues

There are two types of smart contract privacy concerns: 1) contract data privacy; 2) reliable data feeds privacy. Not only events, but also contract-related metadata (especially for content on the public Blockchain), including such bytecode, activation arguments and so on, are now publicly accessible. As a result, it is a significant task to keep essential functions/methods hidden, use cryptography and prevent data that should not have been made public from being revealed. The contracts layer is primarily responsible for smart contract legal concerns.

In addition to the issues described above, there are numerous other issues based on the architecture and contract layers. Poor smart contract design mechanisms result in an increase in contract running expenses and minimizes running efficiency on the operations layer. Malevolent intelligent agents on the intelligence layer may profit from their malicious conduct and so on.

# 2.6.3. Immutability issue

The immutability property is a key aspect of smart contracts. Indeed, once a smart contract is launched, no one can modify the code. However, the disadvantage of the immutability notion in smart contracts is that in the event of any flaws in the code, the immutability characteristic of a smart contract prohibits them from being corrected. Similarly, if circumstances change (e.g. the parties have mutually decided to modify the conditions of their commercial transaction, or if the legislation changes, etc.), there is no simple solution to modify a smart contract. To overcome the immutability issue, lengthy and perhaps expensive evaluations of the smart contract by experts prior to its implementation in a Blockchain are necessary.
