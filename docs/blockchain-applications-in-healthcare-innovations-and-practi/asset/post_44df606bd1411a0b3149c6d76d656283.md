## 10.3. Smart contracts

In 1996, a smart contract was defined as a digital contract structured in the form of lines of code and executed in the same way. It is a set of protocols formed between parties to fulfill the desired promises. The smart contract executes the contract, ensuring no malicious contents as well as unpredictable circumstances. It reduces the usage of third parties, which results in low transactional cost, and lowers potential collusions and countered risk. Smart contracts came into existence due to Blockchain technologies (Wang et al. 2019). They are in the form of general-purpose programming. These lines of code and the agreements are stored over distributed and decentralized technology termed as Blockchain.

The speciality of smart contracts is that they are reliable and trustworthy, and permit a faster flow of procedure without any involvement or interference from third parties, or the legal systems. Smart contracts are stored over Blockchain technology that runs on preferred conditions (Wang et al. 2019). Smart contracts are solely focused on automating the execution of the agreement without any loss of time or certain involvement. Users develop their code and form a Turing complete program on the Blockchain network.

Smart contracts are developed on the Ethereum platform. The execution of text on Ethereum consists of the heap, stack and a determined memory over the chain. Once the development of the contract is accomplished, the next step is to deploy the contract. After the deployment of the contract, the contents of the contract cannot be changed or altered, even by the contract creator. The execution of a contract requires a set of operations and specified functions to be carried out. Once the set of functions is carried out, then the program can be finished by the gas depletion. In the next section, we explain the term gas in detail.

### 10.3.1. How smart contracts work

Smart contracts follow a stipulated model and are entirely executed on the Blockchain. A contract is transformed into lines of code that are then executed under certain conditions. A wide range of networks execute the actions over the set of protocols that are verified and validated. The actions related to smart contracts are conducted and executed. These actions involve vehicle registration, notifying the users, raising a ticket and transferring funds to relevant parties. These chunks of actions, once executed, are updated on Blockchain, and mark the transaction as completed. To dive deeper, a smart contract is more than a general script or piece of code that is stored on a decentralized Blockchain network. It maintains transparency between the two parties. Importantly, smart contracts contain terms and conditions in the form of code that helps participants to clearly understand the contract signing digitally, without any enforcement of laws or any legal mechanism (Kemmoe et al. 2016).

Smart contracts are surrounded by a cryptographic protection. The presence of cryptographic protection confirms that the contents of contracts cannot be altered at any cost. It follows a strict security path ensuring the backup of contracts. Smart contracts have multiple benefits, which are as follows.

### 10.3.2. Benefits of smart contracts

- 1) Speed: smart contracts are a digital contract which means that the tasks are automated once the action is executed. It speeds up the procedure because it reduces paperwork (IBM n.d.; Infosys n.d.; Investopedia 2022).

- 2) Efficiency: smart contracts are effective as well as efficient. This is because they are packaged in a secure environment and do not involve hectic legal procedures. The best part is that there is no need for physical presence at the time of signing the agreement (IBM n.d.; Infosys n.d.; Investopedia 2022).

- 3) Accuracy: smart contracts maintain the accuracy of the contents, ensuring that the same task is performed without any misconception or errors, whatever the action (IBM n.d.; Infosys n.d.; Investopedia 2022).

- 4) Trusted and transparent: people usually avoid digital techniques because of insecurity and lack of trust. But this is not the case with smart contracts. The strict cryptographic protection locked the contract into a safe network where there is no possibility of threat or alteration. The contracts are visible and accessible to parties connected with the network (IBM n.d.; Infosys n.d.; Investopedia 2022).

- 5) Security: security is the strength of a smart contract based on Blockchain technology. Firstly, any contracts contents stored over Blockchain networks are encrypted. The hacker cannot attack the network. If the hacker attacks the network, it will have to change the entire chain of records as the contents are stored over the distributed ledger (Investopedia 2022).

- 6) Cost-effective: smart contracts are an automated procedure that executes actions without the usage of paper. This reduced the cost spent on paperwork as well as its fees for overall procedures (IBM n.d.; Infosys n.d.; Investopedia 2022).

### 10.3.3. Roles of smart contracts

Smart contracts may change to deal with various activities like transactions or any other legal work. For enterprises, organizations and institutions, it may be difficult to deal with so many contracts, store them in files and then act accordingly. Smart contracts play a vital role in various sectors, providing them with the flexibility to independently handle contracts without any burden of storage, or spending time as well as money on paperwork (Salmerón-Manzano and Manzano-Agugliaro 2019).

Smart contracts have made it feasible for businesses and trade to anonymously identify the parties without any intervention of a middleman. Smart contracts have reduced the cost usually required for traditional contracts without affecting authenticity and integrity (Salmerón-Manzano and Manzano-Agugliaro 2019). Therefore, smart contracts are useful in various sectors, such as travel companies, supply chain deals, logistics deals and many others. Smart contracts enable features that help both parties to easily carry out their work in an isolated secured environment.

## 10.4. Use of smart contracts in healthcare, patient monitoring, and other use cases

By now, we are well aware of how critical smart contracts can come to be when certain functionalities are concerned – and many of these completely deal with decentralization as the core concept (or for the matter of absence of centralization) for certain processes, procedures and use cases. The concept of smart contracts, though first introduced by Ethereum, which also marked the onset of Blockchain technology’s second generation, actually helped shape the development of similar technologies across other platforms and products. Currently, there are many public as well as consortium Blockchain implementations that provide support for writing and deploying smart contracts, and many times it is in a different language than what Ethereum supports. This has given rise to many developers being able to write smart contracts in languages that they knew before they started in the domain of Blockchain technology – such as Java, JavaScript, Rust, etc.

As more and more engineers become equipped to write smart contracts, the total number of use cases that can see the usage of smart contracts is increasingly growing. When such a turnout happens before and during an ongoing pandemic, it brings out several use cases that are concerned with the domain of healthcare. This can specifically encompass patient monitoring, vaccination drives, vial information, supply chain management of medical equipment, vaccines, medicines and electronic health records (EHRs), further securing protected health data/information (PHI) and regulated access to patient information, as well as ailment data. As healthcare is a massive industry in itself, the use cases that it encompasses usually see a lot of complexity. The same can be said about the deployments trailed on the back of smart contract technology.
