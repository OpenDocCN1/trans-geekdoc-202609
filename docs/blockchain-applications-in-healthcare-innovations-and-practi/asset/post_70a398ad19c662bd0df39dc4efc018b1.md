
## 1.5. Information distribution in Blockchain

For the analysis of data distribution and accountability with the rise of the IoT and 5G, an increasing amount of critical data are being gathered, particularly for the electric management system, whose general steady operation depends on the security of data sharing and transmission. Firdaus et al. (2018) have investigated the security issues along with their solutions like the InterPlanetary File System (Dey et al. 2017) for privacy in data transfer architecture for smart wearable systems in order to provide a balance of performance and resource optimization. Researchers proposed an associated CCTV data protection system as well as a private protecting video encoding and storing technology.

Every user in the proposed model has the ability to communicate and receive data from others. The information is not immediately communicated between users (e.g. A and B), but rather through the following three processes:

- 1) Sensitive data is initially encoded and saved in IPFS via user A. Everybody can receive the encoded material based on a hash function, and only the person who possesses the keys may read it.

- 2) If A wishes to send data to B, they create a record in the Blockchain indicating that data D has been sent to B. The document also includes an access to data D that is encoded by a distributed chain of blocks that keeps track of B’s public key, allowing only B to get the key and retrieve information D to A.

- 3) The consensus algorithm is used to ensure that the information transfer records are non-repudiable. To improve the system, a practical Byzantine fault tolerance (PBFT) consensus algorithm is adopted. Unlike the commonly used PoW or PoS consensus protocols, PBFT can operate without the need for a token or money, which is also not required inside the electrical data management situation.

## 1.6. The growing anticipation of Blockchain

The Blockchain is an immutable ledger and is shared by peers, with records of transactions that are always appended in a successive manner. The distributed decentralization is more beneficial to be implemented in healthcare applications, as it is free from the centralized authority. Both concepts of distributed ledger technology and Blockchain are frequently used interchangeably. The overall type of technology is distributed ledger, and Blockchain is a specific form with more technical details. Both involve the concept of a ledger, which is a file that records who owns what. According to the research findings in Dey et al. (2017), there are four key characteristics of a distributed ledger: i) payment data that is shared by network members, ii) consensus-based updates, iii) time-stamped records with cryptographic signatures and iv) a tamper-proof record with all transactions. Blockchain gives the ability to update database entries in a sequential manner using chained cryptography hash-linked blocks (every step performs a hash of the previous block, thus joining transaction blocks through an unbreakable string, hence the term "Blockchain"). The two forms of Blockchains are public and private Blockchains.

Blockchains that are open to the public can be used by anyone such as Bitcoin and Ethereum, and no one knows who they are. Private Blockchains, which are used by governments and industry consortiums, are similar to a corporate intranet in that users are known and credentialed. One of the most crucial motivations of adopting ICT may be cyber security. Recent data breaches – the Equifax credit scandal where an estimated 145.5 million Americans’ personal information was exposed as a result of the incident and also the identities, email and passcodes of 2.5 billion Yahoo user profiles were stolen – highlight the immediate necessity of improved security measures. Blockchain systems are a tempting target for thieves, but decentralized storage of records backed with cryptographic signatures over Blockchain technology could significantly increase network security. Decentralized solutions are supposed to provide increased user control and permissioning of private data. Distributed ledgers are referred to as "trustless" because they do away with the need to rely on any third institution, such as Equifax or Yahoo, in the midst of private information exchanges. Blockchains are smart networks with no third-party intermediaries that can authenticate and transmit value. A sophisticated protocol that identifies, verifies, confirms and distributes transactions inside the network incorporates intelligence straight into the network’s processes.

In the healthcare domain, the immutability property that does not allow the Blockchain users to alter or modify any of the records is already appended to the Blockchain. The Blockchain ensures the validity as well as the integrity of the patient records. However, the smart contracts, which are also supported by the Blockchain, are the rules that control the flow of the patient’s record. According to European rules, the GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation) refuses to process any personal or sensitive data of any patient unless there is explicit consent. Thus, as per the growing anticipation, the Blockchain can be associated with the development of an EMR-based clinical management system in compliance with GDPR. As a result, a trust-free system emerges in which human counterparts and organizations do not need to be known or authorized. Rather, the computationally intelligent network is trusted, which could aid in the development of the next digital solutions.

### 1.6.1. Challenges faced by Blockchain

The Blockchain in healthcare addresses multiple issues including data security, care regularity issues, coordination and interoperability, since this industry has a variety of data inputs on a regular basis such as medical records, test results, prescriptions and much more. Thus, the potential benefits are as follows.

### Scalability

The first and most important issue is adoption and scalability. Although the transaction network is able to process hundreds of transactions per second without fail, there might be an unusual lag in processing.

### Interoperability

The second major issue that must be solved is interoperability, which is frequently cited as one of the main reasons why these technologies are yet to be implemented. Because they are unable to send and receive data from some other Blockchain-based systems, many Blockchains create silos and do not interact with other peer groups.

To address this, a number of projects have been established to address the issue. For example, the Smart Bridges design is used to bridge the communication gap between Ark’s networks. The technology aims to provide worldwide compatibility and global communication and transmission.

### Consumption of energy

The proof-of-work technique is used by the technology to verify transactions and ensure that they are trustworthy enough to be added to a system. This technique necessitates a substantial amount of computer power in order to solve complicated mathematical riddles, process, validate and more importantly, protect the entire system.

To solve this problem, Ethereum’s co-founder came up with the solution of changing proof-of-work to proof-of-stake. Participants do not have to answer complex puzzles when this approach is used, which saves gobs of energy.

### Talent deficit

The requirement of experts is growing steadily, yet finding high-quality people is typically considered as a significant obstacle when considering the digital technology deployment.

Many corporate executives, organizations and policymakers can undoubtedly benefit from this technology. Nonetheless, there are a few setbacks that are contributing to the technology’s sluggish acceptance. Similarly, as of 2019, the global market for cryptocurrency developers has increased by more than 517% over the previous year. Despite its enormous gains, the majority of people regard Bitcoin as just a well-known field. Despite the increased demand for Blockchain developers, there is a severe shortage.

### Is standardization still lacking?

This is just a question that has yet to be answered: What standardization does Blockchain adhere to? Despite the numerous networks which exist, there really is no uniform standard. Because of a lack of standardization, concerns like compatibility, increased prices and complicated procedures arise, rendering widespread adoption unfeasible. Due to the lack of a standard version of Blockchain technology, it is going to act as just an obstacle to new companies and users. Aside from the issues listed above, the opposing problems for large-scale deployments are expenses, privacy terms and security issues.

When working with healthcare data, records are frequently mismatched or duplicated. But in Blockchain processing, the full data set, not just the initial key, is encrypted to a logbook.
