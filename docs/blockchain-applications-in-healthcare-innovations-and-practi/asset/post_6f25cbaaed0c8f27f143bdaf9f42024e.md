## 5.4. Electronic health records using Blockchain

Over the last decade, medical practitioners, clinics and medical products have also encouraged a growth in the digitalization of medical health records, since digitalization of this data provides for easier access and cooperation, as well as a basis for safer and faster making decisions. In the area of healthcare, the most common Blockchain solutions are currently in the field of EHR. As a result of life experiences that isolate them from one provider’s data to another, patients’ data is distributed among different organizations, and they lose simple access to previous data. A prototype known as “MedRec” (Azaria et al. 2016) makes use of unique Blockchain features to manage authentication, anonymity, honesty and data sharing. It is based on a decentralized system for the management of records that claims to provide patients with a detailed, permanent history as well as convenient access to their healthcare details from a variety of providers and treatment facilities. For MedRec, there is no reason to maintain patient records or wait for an adjustment time. It creates a Blockchain archive and notifies the client, who is entirely liable for the record’s movement. The label ensures that a perfect copy of the record was obtained. Xia et al. (2017) developed MeDShare, a secure and reliable Blockchain architecture for the exchange of medical data between unknown individuals.

EHRs often include highly sensitive and critical patient data that is exchanged on a regular basis by doctors, radiologists, hospital employees, chemists and scientists in order to give correct diagnosis and treatment. The collection and distribution of this very personal patient information to various entities can endanger the patient’s care, posing major threats to the patient’s health and the capacity to maintain an up-to-date patient record. Consequently, maintaining an up-to-date patient’s history has become vital to ensure effective care. To overcome these challenges, a Blockchain-based network for keeping, maintaining and transferring electronic medical information for cancer patients should be developed. To view, handle and archive secure patient data, they used permissioned Blockchain technology. These proposed solutions will be used to incorporate Blockchain technologies in healthcare practices for maintaining the safety and confidentiality of information of patient and background. Perhaps the explanation for the rapid adoption of Blockchain technologies in medicine around the world is a clear promise to patients that using this technology can make their medical data permanent and unaltered. Any access or modification initiative may be easily branded and identified through the Blockchain. This is valuable not only for medical integrity, but it also flags some fraudulent activities, such as wholesale bribery or record adulteration. Furthermore, posting and reviewing authorized medicinal service records would be much easier. When a patient visits, the rest of the patient’s suppliers are likely to notice it right away. Pharmaceutical errors, hypersensitivities and pharmaceutical solutions may be seamlessly related across all Blockchain records using suitable patient-caring algorithms, removing the need for time-consuming prescriptions agreement forms. As a result, the application of Blockchain technology would improve treatment accessibility, medical record management, quick clinical proof verification, greater monitoring and more effective healthcare management.

Al-Karaki et al. (2019) have presented a DASS-CARE that is a Blockchain-based system that enables open connectivity to health centers, particularly medical information, that is scalable and secure. The proposed system would significantly simplify the process of real-time access and notifications without jeopardizing patient data protection, integrity or confidentiality.

Vora et al. (2019) developed a system based on Blockchain for EHR storage and maintenance that is both effective and safe. The suggested system also enables customers, providers and other organizations to connect to medical data in a secure and convenient manner while maintaining patient confidentiality.

Dagher et al. (2018) developed a system based on Blockchain for patients, insurers and third parties to offer secure, accessible and dependable connectivity to health data while protecting patients’ personal information.

## 5.5. Quantitative analysis

Quantitative analysis is concerned with describing and comprehending things statistically and quantitatively. The purpose of quantitative analysis is to comprehend the data collected for the phenomenon via the use of numerical variables and analytics. Quantitative analysis includes statistical and analytical approaches of analysis.

We examined the Scopus database for research published up until November 1, 2021. Studies on Blockchain-based electronic health records (EHRs) have been published not just in medical publications, but also in journals from other fields such as IT, law, engineering, economics and business administration. As a result, given its comprehensive coverage of journals in both the scientific and social sciences, we chose the Scopus database as the source of papers for this study.

We employed four processes to conduct a comprehensive quantitative review: discovering possible research, removing unnecessary research, verifying important studies and evaluating selected articles.

In the first phase of selecting suitable research, we conducted a preliminary search for papers that had a term like “electronic health record” in their titles, abstracts or keywords. This phase yielded a total of 70,616 prospective investigations. In a separate search, we limited these studies to those that had key phrases like “Blockchain electronic health records” in their titles, abstracts or keywords. This resulted in a total of 500 prospective research.

In the second phase, a filter is applied to remove the articles in press which yields the 484 documents out of 500 documents. The Scopus database also shows the articles in press which means that the articles are not published yet online but are in the production stage.

In the third phase of verifying the significance of the 484 available studies, we probed their document types, titles, abstracts and keywords, and filtered out 464 studies that satisfied the set of criteria of our search with the publication in English language alone. This stage approved 464 articles in total.

In the fourth phase of analyzing the 464 articles, we looked at how they were distributed by publication year, language, location of origination, journal and yearly average number of citations. The nations of origin of the publications were studied by identifying the countries of the organization to which the authors belonged. If an article had two writers working for organizations in two distinct nations, the country of origin was determined to be two countries. The top 10 articles were determined using the yearly average number of citations of the 464 articles. When corrected for the effects of time after publication, the yearly average number of citations can more precisely indicate the amount of influence of each item on other research than the overall number of citations. A 2016 article, for instance, is more likely to be mentioned than one produced in 2019. As a result, we ranked the 464 publications using the yearly average number of citations.
