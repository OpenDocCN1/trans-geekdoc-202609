Blockchain technology. Blockchain may operate in a decentralized environment by combining many basic technologies such as cryptographic hashing, digital signatures and a distributed consensus process. As a result, Blockchain has the potential to significantly reduce costs while also increasing efficiency. Furthermore, as the quantity of provider user information and vulnerable devices rises, the deployment of secure, reliable and verified services is critical. Unfortunately, trust is becoming increasingly rare as the amount of information thefts at monolithic technology companies rises, exposing huge amounts of private data. Centralization, a fundamental flaw in many software architectures, provides an uneven relationship among users and suppliers, making the personalized security of electronic information impossible while offering a single point of failure for hackers to attack.

Blockchain is a decentralized, irreversible transaction ledger in which every transaction is inextricably linked to the one before it. Working in untrusted distributed environments may well be safeguarded by both the ledger of information and distributed agreement on the authenticity of the transaction, as the Blockchain technology’s fundamental purpose. Furthermore, transactions incorporate operational code, allowing software services to be provided between untrusted users. Since it was initially used in cryptocurrencies, interest in Blockchain technology has grown in both industry and academia, as they assess the applicability and functioning of the underlying Blockchain technologies.

To enable Blockchain decentralization, nodes involved in transactions and block generation should be able to verify the validity of new updated blocks to the chain. In this scenario, the nodes should reach a consensus since there is no trustworthy centralized method to make such a judgment. Various consensus techniques have been used to achieve such an accord (Katal et al. 2021). These versions must all work towards the same goal: identifying whether transactions inside the Blockchain are legitimate by verifying the activity of each new block introduced to the chain. The differences are in who can add blocks and at what rate, as well as the issue used to reach agreement. Consensus is commonly used to describe “mining”, or the solution of a complicated problem that is easily verifiable but difficult to overturn.

The method by which a miner contributes additional blocks to the network is known as mining. Each block in a chain has its own distinct nonce and hash, and connects to the hash of a block before it is in the network, making the mining of a block challenging, particularly in big chains. Miners use sophisticated programs to tackle the extremely difficult mathematics problem of creating a valid hash. Making changes to any prior block in the chain involves re-mining not only the altered block, but also all following blocks. Hence, it is so difficult to manipulate Blockchains. Consider it “math safety”, because finding golden nonces requires a considerable amount of effort and computational power.
