## 5.6. Proposed framework for the EHRs using Blockchain

This segment introduces the proposed solution for EHR sharing, which is built on the Blockchain network. For block transactions in the network, various methods and configurations are used. This section describes the Blockchain-based EHR system architecture. The proposed scheme has six members. The entities involved are: patients, doctors, administrative staff, insurance company, lab technicians and pharmacies.

- Administrative staff: the administrative staff is responsible for registering the users into the Blockchain. They register the users on the Blockchain and provide permissions to the users.
- Doctor: the doctor is responsible for adding the medical details of the patient to the Blockchain. The doctor is also authorized to view the details of the patient.
- Patient: the patient is responsible to add the previous medical details. The patient is also authorized to insert the new medical record.
- Lab technician: the lab technician is responsible to add the test reports of the patient. They cannot access the patient’s medical details.
- Pharmacy: the pharmacy owner is responsible for delivering the required medicines to the patient. He is able to view the prescription uploaded by the doctor. The pharmacy owner cannot access the medical details of the patient.
- Insurance company: the insurance company is responsible for settling the hospital bills of the patient. The insurance company can only view the hospital bills of the patient uploaded by the administrative staff of the hospital.

### 5.6.1. System workflow

Figure 5.6 shows the workings of the proposed model.

Firstly, the administrative staff registers the patient on the Blockchain by the registration smart contract. Once the patient is registered, the administrative staff registers the particular doctor and grants permission via the smart contract. After the registration process is completed, the doctor provides the treatment to the patient and continuously updates the records on the Blockchain network. The doctor is not able to delete the particular record on the Blockchain. Once the initial medication is done, the doctor recommends lab tests to the patient. The patient conveys the lab information to the administrative staff so that only an authorized lab technician is able to update the record on the Blockchain. Once the test is done, the authorized lab technician is able to upload the reports that can be accessed by the authorized doctor. Once the treatment is done, the doctor will upload the prescription on the Blockchain network. The patient conveys the pharmacy owner details to the administrative staff so that only authorized persons get the prescription details. The healthcare store proprietor obtains the medication history and brings the medications to the healthcare center. Once all the treatment is done, the administrative staff uploads the bill on the Blockchain network and it is not modifiable by the patient. The patient conveys all the details about the insurance to the administrative staff so that the legitimate insurance company can view the bills of the patient and process the claim. The insurance company is not authorized to view the medical details of the patient. In this way, the claim process will be settled without any fraud.

## 5.7. Issues in Blockchain-based EHRs

The EHR Blockchain literature has described interoperability, anonymity and authorization (access control) as major issues and challenges.

Interoperability issues between insurer and healthcare networks contribute to the difficulty of effectively exchanging data (Monrat et al. 2019). Health reports are scattered rather than coherent due to less organized management of data and sharing. Many studies have raised questions about data interoperability and fragmentation.

Since EHR data, including photographs, can be quite vast in size, scalability is a major challenge when transmitting EHR data through Blockchain. Because of a Blockchain network’s distributed replicated existence, saving and duplicating EHR data on the network for redistribution is difficult due to the massive data volume.

The current EHRs have very few patient management permissions, and patients are usually unable to freely exchange their data with researchers or providers. Despite all of medicine’s advancements, multiple EHR mechanisms are unable to interact efficiently.

## 5.8. Case studies

### 5.8.1. MedRec

MedRec is a framework that promotes patient agency by providing direct and open access to medical experience, therefore bridging a societal need with a technological enabler. To extend the banking model, financial systems may have a variety of currency storage facilities, such as one for each network of providers. The issue is that medical files are not interchangeable; each one has a person’s signature. There is no such thing as a like-for-like exchange of money. Although competition and variety may lower market pricing, they typically risk creating a cascade of unsuitable or inaccessible exchange and control obstacles. To circumvent arbitrary middlemen, developers have developed a distributed access and validation approach that leverages the Blockchain.

Members of a proprietary, P2P network share data control and viewership permits, which are reflected in the block content in MedRec. Smart contracts, which are made possible by Blockchain technology, assist the users in automating and monitoring certain state transitions. The developers use smart contracts on the Ethereum Blockchain to monitor the relationship of provider and patient, which link a medical record to accessing privileges and information retrieval directions for implementation on the Blockchain. Patients can consent to the sharing of information across providers. In all situations, the individual collecting new data receives an automatic update and has the opportunity to examine the suggested input before accepting or rejecting it. This allows users to stay up to date on the progress of their papers. MedRec prioritizes accessibility by offering a contract that gathers changes any of a recipient’s client interactions, as well as a central source of contact for looking for health record updates.
