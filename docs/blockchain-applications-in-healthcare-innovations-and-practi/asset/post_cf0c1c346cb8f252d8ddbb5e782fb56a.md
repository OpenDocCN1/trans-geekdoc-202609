## An Optimistic Approach to Share Private Health Records Using Blockchain Technology

These days, the amount of digital health data generated is increasing. Legacy systems with confidentiality and security problems are used to store and share them. Insurance data, banking information and Electronic Health Records (EHRs) are all examples of sensitive data or documents that must be exchanged digitally. Because EHRs include vital information and are frequently exchanged among several parties, including hospitals, patients and government bodies, they are both critical and sensitive. Private clinics or pharmacies maintain this information to be accurate, up to date, secret and made accessible to authorized users. In many circumstances, the transaction takes place between anonymous and unreliable people. As a result, implementing the data exchange using a fair non-repudiation protocol is considerably critical. Blockchain is no longer exclusively associated with cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin. Blockchain is now widely used in applications that require immutable and traceable data, as well as a cryptographic and distributed ledger and is most often used in secure transactions. In a distributed ledger, records are maintained in a linked sequence of blocks, known as a Blockchain. The proposed system connects with the methods such as on-chain and off-chain communication patterns to allow patients and healthcare professionals to share personal health records. A scheme that emphasizes the importance of maintaining a true fair-exchange policy while transferring medical records without the involvement of a trusted third party is proposed. This distributed technology has a lot of potential in new applications over dissimilar fields such as healthcare and stakeholders.

### 6.1. Introduction

Blockchain, according to the current study, has the potential to dislocate trusted business models that are mostly used in present day healthcare systems that perform information sharing (Ethereum n.d.; Esposito et al. 2018). Given the amount of transactions (sharing of information), including various healthcare objects and the costs that incur in Hospital Maintenance Systems (HIE), using Blockchain technologies that democratically sustain in various public ledgers are recorded with various novel and inspiring prospects in the healthcare industry. The Blockchain technology has the ability to build an electronic environment in which people perform various business transactions (information sharing) through a disseminated community instead of a central authority. This could have a big impact on how transparent the system is and the functions of each institution (Dias et al. 2018; Ravi Kumar et al. 2019). By improving exchange of information along with coordination with various healthcare groups, Blockchain can also help individuals become more self-sufficient in sharing their medical records with clinicians. Based on the scale, most of the mainstream HIE servers are coordinated by huge enterprises or institutions of healthcare. Centralized administration may introduce privacy and security issues due to the abuse of authority, such as ancillary use of medical records and their unapproved access, or even hacker attacks. Blockchain technology, on the contrary, may support a range of characteristics such as decentralization.

Most of the studies looking at the consequences of Blockchain-based distributed ledger technology for performing healthcare practices have generated a lot of interest (Dai et al. 2020; Feng et al. 2020). However, little quantitative study has been done on HIE’s use of Blockchain technology. Patients’ opinions for enabling the deployment of Blockchain-based HIE networks are unknown and it is unclear whether patients (HIE stakeholders) will support its use. As a result, most of the research needed to evaluate the principal implementation of Blockchain technology over the healthcare industry is from the patient’s point of view. This chapter findings can help healthcare organizations or communities and politicians clearly understand the use of Blockchain technology in recognizing various incorporated benefits and risks in healthcare practices (Blum et al. 1988; Crameri et al. 2020).

### 6.2. Related work

In Blockchain-based EHR systems, most of the steps involved in improvising the privacy protection of complex health records as privacy preserving methods can be divided into cryptographic and non-cryptographic categories, with encryption or anonymization and access control mechanisms as examples (Praveen and Jayanth Babu 2019).

Encryption schemes, for instance, Public Key Encryption (PKE), Symmetric Key Encryption (SKE), secure Multi Party Computation (MPC) and others are rather widespread. In the proposed system, a Blockchain based in a healthcare network comprises a public key encryption technique, such as Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC). This is used to perform the encryption of private data over a secure channel, which corresponds to the data generated from various sensors that will be uploaded in a Blockchain network by means of a pair of exclusive private and public keys, in order to ensure the privacy and security of biometric data (Daemen and Rijmen 2002; Dai et al. 2020).

Al Omar et al. (2017) advocated that data should be encrypted using a symmetric key technique with a threshold encryption scheme before being uploaded to cloud services. The process of Shamir's Secret Sharing (SSS) divides the symmetric key into many shares that are further distributed to distinct key keepers. The process of decrypting the encrypted text can be performed when the data requestor accepts adequate key segments. Data leaking may not arise when certain key keepers negotiate the (minimal threshold) value. Smart phone is a Healthcare Data Gateway (HDG) that is built on Blockchain, and uses MPC technology by enabling the system that calculates the data that is encrypted directly by performing over the private Blockchain cloud, to attain the concluding results instead of illuminating original data (Azaria et al. 2016; Ahsan et al. 2020).

Changing encryption keys is a common practice in healthcare systems and is a common method to avoid malicious, statistical attacks. But it incurs enhanced storing and managing costs with a large number of historical keys, which should be stored properly in sequence to perform decryption of some historical data in near future. This process leads to an increase in storage costs, specifically because of partial computational means and storage procedures to solve problems such as: web developed trivial backup and recovery key management systems that are implemented as a body of sensor networks (BSNs) to protect the privacy of the sensor data from a person by reducing secret key storage expenses. Without storing any encryption keys, fuzzy vault technology is used to generate, backup and recover keys, with BSNs performing the key recovery of the sensor data that is encrypted using symmetric encryption technology (AES or 3DES) (Blum et al. 1988; Rama et al. 2017). Hence, the attacker will have a hard time decrypting it without a symmetric key.
