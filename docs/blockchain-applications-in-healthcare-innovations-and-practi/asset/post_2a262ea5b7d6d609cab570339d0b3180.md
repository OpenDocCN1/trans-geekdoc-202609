- Decentralization: with the help of digital signature, it is clear that if there is any alteration with the block, then the digital signature will become illegal and the peers in the network will be informed that manipulation has occurred. This whole process becomes more efficient with decentralization as there will be no single authority in Blockchain during decentralization and the system becomes fairer.

The beauty of decentralization is that no node can manipulate ledger but have a duplicate copy of ledger with them, hence, to make any changes in any transaction in the ledger a consensus is required from all of the nodes. Let us take another example, consider there is a hospital HP1 which has its own private Blockchain for the patients being treated there. HP1 store its patients' data in the private Blockchain. Now consider the scenario where a patient is referred to another hospital and say HP2 and HP2 want to access the patient data to see the history of the patient before starting any treatment. It is possible to provide data to a third party, i.e. HP2 in this case, but what we need to understand here is that data sharing is not manual via email or physical documents. Instead, we here consider a scenario where an IT-enabled system at HP2 will connect with systems at HP1 and after proper validation, it will access the data. However, in this whole process, there are lot of security challenges such as unauthorized access, elevation of privileges, man in the middle attacks, data leakage, etc. Security controls need to be implemented, some at the perimeter level, and some at the code level too to remediate different security threats, but this will make the system too complex. The consortium Blockchain is the solution here, meaning that there will be a network of hospitals. Every hospital will have their own private Blockchains, and at the same time, a permissioned Blockchain will be created which will be managed by authorized hospitals of the network, this Blockchain is also known as the consortium Blockchain. All hospitals can access the consortium Blockchain using a search index and can look for patient records. Figure 7.9 provides a visualization of this example. ## 7.4. Regulatory compliance requirements

With the increase in size of data along with emerging technologies such as IoT, cloud, big data, etc., the instances of data breach have also increased. The impact of any data breach is very high when it involves sensitive data like health records. PHI (protected health information) suffers from a greater risk. In the last decade, there have been many cases of PHI breaches either by hospital electronic health record management systems or by insurance service providers, or by mobile healthcare applications such as online doctor consultations, online medicine buying apps, etc. Hence to strengthen medical data privacy and security governance, many countries have come up with regulations like HIPAA, HITECH, etc., even GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation) is not specifically for medical data, but it covers general data which means that any medical data breach in EU is also in the scope of GDPR (Jin et al. 2019).
