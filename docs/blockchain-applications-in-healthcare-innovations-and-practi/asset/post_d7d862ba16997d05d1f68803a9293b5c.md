## 3.2. Literature review

In the paper by Tripathi et al. (2019), the problem related to the medical sector has been discussed, which mainly includes security concerns and how Blockchain technology can significantly help in dealing with such issues. With the flood of medical records using EMR (Electronic Medical Records) and ICT, Blockchain is being used in many medical organizations. The paper discusses Blockchain technology and also the SHS-Smart Healthcare System and the challenges faced by it, further proposing a Blockchain-based SHS framework for analyzing and finding a solution to security and integrity issues.

The paper by A. Islam and Shin (2020) proposes a scheme in which an unmanned vehicle (UAV) collects healthcare data and stores it in the nearest server. Firstly, a relation is established by the UAV with the body sensor hives (BSHs) through a token and afterwards a shared key is generated with BSHs that further establishes a low-power secure communication. Once all the data is collected by UAV, it decrypts all of the encrypted data (shared key encryption is done) and works out a two-phase implementation. And thus, after validation, the UAV sends the data to the server which stores it in the Blockchain network. An even analysis is presented in the paper to show the practicability and the feasibility of the proposed scheme.

A smart healthcare business model observes the customers at the heart of the business, with the evolution of Blockchain technology. The paper by Gul et al. (2020) established that the suggested model can analyze the customer’s level and then provide prizes, as per the regulations that have been set up by the participating organization. From the Internet of Medical Things, the model fetches data and then applies any related script in which the rewarding system exits. The model focuses on the customer and the business while relating technology to manage all of the stakeholders involved.

The paper by Ferrer-Gomila et al. (2019) provides an operational hybrid platform using a consensus mechanism that breaks the problem of partaking the sensitive information of the digital forensic medical evidence. The mechanism records the transparent history of the case and prevents the modification of the data by unauthorized users. Firstly, the digital evidence is encrypted before storing them in an online file storage system that is present in the Blockchain network. A dynamic agreement tool is applied to entertain the data in the Blockchain. Cryptography ensures secured access to digital data stored on various Blockchain nodes.

The complex nature of the modern healthcare system has made them costlier. The cost of the healthcare system can be reduced by befitting the application of insurance companies, amending record management and by using Blockchain technology as discussed in Tanwar et al. (2020). Breakthroughs in Blockchain technology have guided and helped to evolve transactions involving insurance billings, smart contracts and medical records. Using Blockchain in healthcare has many advantages, one of them being the capability to reform the interoperability of the data (healthcare and prescription), device tracking, etc. To prescribe the medication, it is important to know the patient’s medical history, and Blockchain has helped to improve the medical sector framework in this case. Here, many solutions are explored to expand the boundaries of the healthcare system using Blockchain. To liquidate the Hyper-Ledger-based EHR sharing in the system that employs chain code, for correcting data accessibility among healthcare providers, helping in the stimulation of the environment, the Access Control Policy Algorithm is examined. The proposed scheme uses Blockchain technology to improve efficiency and security instead of the traditional system that uses a client-server architecture.

The paper by Abd-alrazaq et al. (2020) discusses the Covid-19 situation currently in the world and how we can use Blockchain technology to mitigate the challenges that are being faced due to the virus. The paper shows various properties of the Blockchain that are generally used in the healthcare sector and the applications that can be used to help government authorities to face the challenges due to the virus. In the fight against the Covid-19 virus, Blockchain technologies are expected to play a significant role.

The paper by Huang et al. (2020) offers a privacy-preserving scheme based on Blockchain technology to ensure safe sharing of the data between the patient and the medical research institution. This scheme not only accomplishes data availability but also consistency between the patient and the institution. A zero-knowledge proof is provided to make sure that the patients’ medical information meets up to the requirements of the research institution, and further a proxy re-encryption technology is used by the institution to decrypt the intermediate cypher text. This scheme can also implement a distributed consensus based on the PBFT algorithm for the transaction purpose between the patient and the institution.

When talking about the traditional medical management systems, certain drawbacks can be addressed by the advancement in cloud-assisted electronic health systems. But at the same time, ensuring the integrity of the electronic health records (EHR) along with guaranteeing the privacy of the EHR while sharing is not easy. Thus, the paper by Huang et al. (2020) proposes a Blockchain-based eHealth system BCES to check the manipulation of the healthcare records. Every handling of the data consumers together with every outsourcing control of the hospitals, in the intended system, will be penned in a pattern of a transaction in the Blockchain in the form of storage that will ensure traceability. Also, the proxy re-encrypted system that is used to gain access control of the medical data and the auditors will recognize any sort of behavior or activity that can threaten the integrity of EHRs. This scheme, due to its security analysis and performance evaluation, has stood out to be secure and efficient.

The paper by Negi et al. (2021) presents a healthcare data managing system that focuses on the patient (patient-centric) using the Blockchain technology as storage to help with maintaining privacy. In today’s world, cyber-attacks have become a common thing especially in the cloud. The data stored on the cloud has always been an attraction to them, and the healthcare data has not been an exception to this. Attacks on healthcare data can be very damaging to organizations in the medical sector. Decentralization which is enabled by the peer-to-peer network makes the storing and running of the healthcare as well as the data in the cloud possible. The Blockchain, because of its decentralized character, guarantees accountability and integrity. Cryptographic functions encrypt patients’ data and ensuring pseudo-anonymity. The cost efficiency of the smart contracts and data processing strategies are employed.

A Blockchain-based architecture is a potential solution to a lot of problems related to the contemporary e-Healthcare networks, discussed by Choudhury et al. (2021). In the modern e-healthcare systems, human recognition activity (HRA) can be said to be one of the most challenging tasks when it comes to monitoring patients who are affected by either disabilities or mental illness and require assistance, and protecting several distinct devices that are connected to the Internet – Internet of Things – is a major issue. Also, numerous activities that are performed by the human being at a time in the diverse healthcare environment decrease the quality of those activities.
