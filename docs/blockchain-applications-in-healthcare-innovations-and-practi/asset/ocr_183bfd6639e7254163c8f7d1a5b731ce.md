# ## 1.5. Information distribution in Blockchain

For the analysis of data distribution and accountability with the rise of the IoT and 5G, an increasing amount of critical data are being gathered, particularly for the electric management system, whose general steady operation depends on the security of data sharing and transmission. Firdaus et al. (2018) have investigated the security issues along with their solutions like the inter-planetary system (Dey et al. 2017) for privacy in data transfer architecture for smart wearable systems in order to provide a balance of performance and resource optimization. Researchers proposed an associated cctv data protection system as well as a private protecting video encoding and storing technology.

Every user in the proposed model has the ability to communicate and receive data from others. The information is not immediately communicated between users (e.g A and B), but rather through the following three processes:

+   1) Sensitive data is initially encoded and saved in IPFS via user A. Everybody can receive the encoded material based on a hash function, and only the person who possesses the keys may read it.

+   2) If A wishes to send data to B, they create a record in the Blockchain indicating that data D has been sent to B. The document also includes an access to data D that is encoded by a distributed chain of blocks that keeps track of B’s public key, allowing only B to get the key and retrieve information D to A.

+   3) The compound annual is used to ensure that the information transfer records are non-repudiable. To improve the system, a practical Byzantine fault tolerance (PBFT) compound annual is adopted. Unlike the commonly used PoW or PoS consensus protocols, PBFT can operate without the need for a token or money, which is also not required inside the electrical data management situation.
