
MedRec’s block information represents ownership rights and viewing privileges maintained by users of a private, P2P system. Scientists use Ethereum smart contracts to register patient interactions by associating a health file with reading rights and data retrieval commands (essentially data pointers) for implementation on other systems. To ensure data integrity, the investigators upload a cryptographic hash of the item to the Blockchain to avoid manipulation. Consumers can authorize the sharing of records among doctors, and suppliers can create a new record linked to a particular client. The researchers validate identity using public key cryptography and a DNS-like solution to link a genuine and widely accepted kind of ID to the individual’s Ethereum address.

### 5.8.2. AI-based solution for EHRs

An Artificial Intelligence (AI)-enabled and Blockchain-driven EHR management platform has immense potential to support trustworthy, secure and resilient EHR storage services. This sort of EHR system would also make it simpler for academics, physicians and officials to obtain data for research, perform analysis and make sound decisions.

Mistry et al. (2021) presented MedBlock, an AI-enabled and Blockchain-powered secure EHR solution for safeguarding the privacy of Covid-19 positive patients. It involves a number of organizations, including physicians, staff workers, public officials, hospitals and consumers. Data transfer in the MedBlock is allowed by using the Ethereum network only if it fits the parameters of the smart contract. MedBlock also ensures that EHRs are not manipulated with or used for malicious reasons. Since information transmission of Covid-19 patients was crucial in the pandemic, the proposed system used a 6G network channel of communication, which reduced information transmission latency (10–100 sec) and reliability (99.99999%). The layers of MedBlock are as follows: (i) data collection and aggregation, (ii) AI, (iii) Blockchain and (iv) accessibility. The data collected and aggregated by the preceding layer is sent into the AI layer. The AI makes decisions based on the data provided. The Artificial Neural Network (ANN) receives the patient's Covid-19 laboratory and CT-Scan results. It functions as a binary classifier based on the input parameters. ### 5.8.3. Improving medical record keeping with Blockchain

phrOS (phrOS – Healthcare Blockchain Operating System) was recently launched by Taipei Medical University Hospital and Digital Treasury Corporation (DTCO) (see DTCO n.d.). It attempts to promote openness among medical organizations by storing all of a patient’s medical records on a Blockchain. It contains photos as well as numerous pieces of information on a patient’s condition. A smartphone app allows clinicians and patients to view the information. It also improves the security of medical data by using Decentralized Ledger Technology (DLT). The open framework allows for autonomous and secure solutions, making smart health a reality. It generates an anonymized digital identity for patients to aid in patient verification and identification, as well as patient data recording and querying, covering in treatment and medication planning. Smart contracts can be used in clinical trials for protocol changes, trial enrollment delays and so on. Patient-authorized claims, including timestamps and medical records, as well as all authorized files, have digital signatures that can be evaluated at any time without the need for third-party services to resolve conflicts.

## 5.9. Conclusion

The usage of Blockchain in application areas is critical in today’s healthcare business. It might lead to autonomous data collection and authentication procedures, as well as trustworthy and integrated information from several sources that is long-lasting, tamper-resistant and safe, with a lower risk of theft. In this chapter, the framework for EHR based on Blockchain is proposed. The primary purpose is to apply Blockchain technology to EHRs by addressing its key properties in order to provide tamper-proof, distributed, irrevocable, transparent, reliable, and open to scrutiny remote medical care. Furthermore, the chapter discussed the potential benefits of Blockchain technology in medical systems. It also includes a discussion of the current Blockchain-based electronic health records that doctors use to store health data. The chapter finishes with a list of challenges that must be solved in order to improve the electronic health record system through the use of upcoming Blockchain-based networks.
