## Patient Data Privacy Using Blockchain

With rapid transformation in the healthcare industry, the enablement of Information Technology and digitalization of healthcare services has increased the use of electronic health records (EHRs) exponentially. On one side of a coin this has many advantages like enhancing patient care, more efficient record management, research advancement, etc., but the other side of the coin warns us about the complexity in maintaining anonymity of patient data and being compliant to the privacy laws and regulations. The interest of hackers is already moving towards the healthcare industry, and there have been so many events in past few years where patient data in large amounts has been breached and misused. Healthcare entities are trying to protect patient data; still, there are so many breaches, the actual reason is that most of the controls are towards data security and there are many times when we may believe that security controls also safeguard privacy, but it is not true, and this generates the gap. Multiple data privacy laws and some laws/standards specific to healthcare data like Health Information Technology for Economic and Clinical Health (HITECH) Act of 2009, Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 (HIPAA), General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), etc. provide various security and data privacy requirements which all healthcare entities need to comply with. These laws also provide various rights to the patients like consent, right to control, right to dispose, etc. Therefore, now the real question is how to implement data privacy controls which will maintain patient data anonymization, at the same time as making sure that healthcare entities comply with privacy laws and regulations and that all rights of the patients towards their data are also preserved: thus, the answer is Blockchain. The major features of Blockchain, i.e. decentralization, transparency and security make it a perfect fit for privacy protection. With the help of Blockchain, an environment can be created in which all patients can review their data before it becomes part of the records, permissioned Blockchain can solve the issue of patient data access and control, consensus algorithms and smart contracts will prevent patient data tampering and unauthorized access. It is known that bitcoins are pseudonymous, but with privacy preserving protocols for the Blockchain, it is possible to achieve anonymity. Similarly, one of the best things that Blockchain can provide for patient data privacy is its role as a data controller. The data controller and data processor are two important roles defined in multiple laws defining data protection responsibilities, hence the implementation of Blockchain for data privacy can compensate the responsibilities of the data controller with better transparency. With the integration of Blockchain in healthcare services or in creating healthcare applications, we can achieve Privacy by Design.

### 7.1. Introduction

Well-being is the establishment of cheerful life, and the current generation has been the luckiest beneficiary of extraordinary advances in clinical innovation. With each innovation, more pieces of information become accessible to translate the issues that plague our prosperity. This information is highly sensitive as it is the Electronic Health Record (EHR). EHR is the sensitive private information of the patients which is usually shared between patients, clinics/hospitals, pharmaceuticals, health insurance companies, test laboratories, etc. The complex part is to track this data down with multiple entities storing and maintaining data. Healthcare interoperability is fixated on the trading of information, a wide accessible interoperability brings new difficulties and prerequisites with respect to security, anonymity and administration (Magyar 2018; Guimarães et al. 2020; Huang and Lee 2020).

There are four main concerned areas that address the challenges in EHR management (Engelhardt 2017; Rejeb et al. 2021):

- a) Patient-centric: usually patients interact with a variety of healthcare entities and provide their data to multiple EHR management tools. This procedure increases the agreement that all data at different entities shall be available to the patient all of the time, so that the patient can take control of their information and access it at anytime.

- b) Data privacy: EHR being sensitive personal data demands privacy and accessible only by authorized parties at appropriate times. This is still a rather complex area since there are many laws and regulations which mandate different principles of data privacy to be in place in the form of privacy controls by the entities which collect, process or transmit healthcare data.

- c) Data integrity: data integrity is all about data completeness, consistency and accuracy. By ensuring that complete health information is available to the right people at the right time, patient-centric information exchange should give patients more control and better outcomes.

- d) Cost: the health expenditure around the globe has increased in the past few decades; still, there is a crisis in healthcare costs. There have been many cases where lots of money has been put into EHR but has gone to waste due to there being less acceptance by people of a certain age group, especially for countries with an aging population. There have also been other factors like security issues, user friendliness and comfortability in using EHR, etc.

### 7.2. Threat modeling – digitalization in the healthcare industry

Digitalization in the healthcare industry is not only about digitalizing the core infrastructure, making healthcare instruments IT enabled or increasing automation/robotics in medical treatments/surgeries (Sharma et al. 2021), but also how this digitalization can be extended to the end-user is the major chunk of automation; however, extending digitalization to the end-user comes at its own cost, this cost is not only monetary but also balancing the fact that the end-user wants it to be as user-friendly, robust and reliable as possible, and at the same time the services/products need to be secured by its design (Singh and Talapula 2019). This means that while in digitalizing we cannot rely on the end-user for security, because that is the most vulnerable end, the services and products shall be developed with security by design principle, i.e. foundationally secure.

Around a decade back, digitalization from an end-user perspective was completely dependent on web applications which were accessible via computers, laptops, etc., but with recent advancements in smartphone technology, where smart mobile devices are available at low costs, everything has changed. This now means that for the end-user, dependency on hardware requirements like a computer or laptop has vanished which has potentially increased the number of end-users who are willing to use healthcare applications via their mobile phones.

These mobile phones are smart because they not only provide end-users an application with a user-friendly interface to access their health records, chat over voice and video with doctors but they are also able to sense changes in temperature, human body measurements, etc. (Papageorgiou et al. 2018). Due to the advance processing capabilities of the smart phones, mobile applications not only provide a platform to store health records, but also have access to live locations and sensitive data like photographs, videos, contacts, etc.

Hence while using health applications, sensitive data is at stake, due to which all health application service providers need to follow many legal regulations and requirements to ensure data privacy and security. Therefore, the actual challenge is providing digitalized healthcare services and implementing security and privacy controls which are also aligned or suffice all legal requirements.
