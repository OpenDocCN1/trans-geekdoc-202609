# 11.2. Benefits of using Blockchain in the healthcare industry

The healthcare industry may be significantly impacted by Blockchain. With regard to patient data transparency and future development execution, this technology could help medical specialists and other industry professionals (Dagher et al. 2018). A variety of Blockchain setups can also be specifically tailored to serve various use cases in the healthcare sector. Among other things, this list includes the secure administration of electronic health records (EHRs), drug traceability, patient consent management, data security in clinical preliminary studies (Benchoufi et al. 2017) and support via micropayments. The contact and costs of existing delegates can be reduced thanks to the healthcare prospects of Blockchain. To improve access to and segment the value of care, the environmental stakeholders have teamed up with innovation. Faster access to clinical records leads to longer-lasting improvements in health.

+   – The ability to fully secure each patient’s EHR. The management of numerous records and reports that are categorized and subject to strict regulations is a common practice in the healthcare industry. One such statute is the 1996 Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA). Most often, these entries are stored in a single database, which can cause interoperability and security problems (as was the case in the first section) (Cai and Zhu 2016; Kiyomoto et al. 2017).

+   – Ease to trace the patient’s health by tracking their records. Here, we use smart contracts innovation to achieve constant, consistent traceability with pop-up messages to limit human mediation and subsequent undesired postponements.

+   – At the moment of transmission, maintain the security of medical data. The development of systems to ensure security and protection is necessary for Blockchain innovation, just like for other types of innovation that can hold data. Because Blockchain data is stored publicly and shared by all members, the majority of cryptographic operations anticipate keys to enable those cryptographic processes; this necessitates some level of encryption/access control.
