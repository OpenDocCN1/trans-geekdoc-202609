## 6.3. Blockchain-based EHR system

Further important aims are defined in the deployment of a secure Blockchain created on EHR schemes, with the requirements of a novel version for protected EHR systems and various features of the Blockchain stated earlier:

- Confidentiality: individual data will be used in a private manner and only authorized persons will have access to the required information.

- Security (CIA): in terms of confidentiality, integrity and availability:
  - 1) Confidentiality: data can only be accessed by authorized individuals.
  - 2) Integrity: when the data is in transit it must be accurate and must not be tampered with by the unauthorized party.
  - 3) Availability: the information and resources that are to be accessed by lawful users cannot be improperly denied.

- Auditability: a crucial aspect of security. For example, audit logs primarily contain statistical evidence of who has access to the EHR (PHR) for the purpose and time stamping performed by any process throughout the life-cycle.

- Accountability: a person or an organization will be assessed and held accountable for their actions.

- Authenticity: the capacity to verify requestor identities before granting entry to complex data.

- Anonymity: for privacy, entities take no apparent identity as absolute anonymity is difficult to achieve. Pseudo anonymy is more widespread, with the use of actual identities. Prevailing Blockchain grounded research in the field of healthcare involves the following essential features in order to meet the above goals.

- Data archiving: the Blockchain is a considerably reliable ledger database, which is used to accumulate a diversity of private healthcare information by securing the storage and guaranteeing data privacy.

- Collaboration on data: in most of the prevailing healthcare systems, almost all of the package contributors are typically the principal data custodians. The aspect of self-sovereignty provides ownership of healthcare data to every user who wants to share personal data is widespread today, hence it is very important to attain safe data sharing across various enterprises and domains.

- Audit of data when conflicts emerge: the audit logs can be used as documentation to hold requestors who are accountable for their interactions with EHRs. A wide range of systems tend to use Blockchain and smart contracts that keep track by recording all of the transactions with auditability of the Blockchain ledger that is accessible at any time.

- Manage your identity: in the system, the legitimacy of each user identity has to be ensured to protect system security and avoid malicious attacks, which makes it possible for legitimate users to make further appropriate requests.

The architecture being implemented in Blockchain is a peer-to-peer (P2P) architecture that consists of distinct nodes made up of various network participants, where each of the associates in the network stores a duplicate copy of the Blockchain and provides for cooperative procedures to further validate and certify all of the digital transactions in the network.

## 6.3.4. Shared ledger

Various members in the distributed network will tend to record all of the digital transactions in a shared ledger by implementing various algorithms for evaluating and verifying the proposed transactions. In the process for every novel transaction is supplementary over the collective ledger when the majority of the network participants accept the transaction which is valid. Modifications in the collective ledger are replicated over all the duplicates of Blockchain within an acceptable amount of time in most of the situations that occur. By definition, every transaction is immutable and combined with the ledger, which cannot be further modified or withdrawn, as every member of the network has a widespread and clear model of the Blockchain and no specific person has the ability to damage or modify the data in this technology.

## 6.3.5. Digital transactions

In a Blockchain, any kind of information or digital quality can be recorded, and the network that outfits the Blockchain defines the kind of information controlled in the transaction to ensure the validity and correctness of data that is encrypted and numerically contracted. Transactions are organized into distinct blocks, where each of the blocks comprises a cryptographic hash that is prior to the block in the Blockchain, as blocks are added in a linear as well as chronological order.
