Blockchain consensus algorithms are divided into two types: proof-based consensus algorithms and voting-based consensus algorithms (Katal et al. 2021a).

### 5.2.1.1. Proof-based consensus algorithms

The basic idea of evidence-based consensus algorithms says that the node with the greatest proof prevails among numerous nodes joining the network and is given approval in the network to add a new node and get the payment. Many varieties of proof-based consensus algorithms have been applied, including those based on Proof of Work (PoW), Proof of Stake (PoS) or a mixture of the two, as well as a variety of different versions.

### 5.2.1.2. Voting-based consensus algorithms

In order to execute the voting-based consensus algorithm, the nodes within the checking network must be customizable. This allows them to exchange messages more easily. This is the most significant distinction from proof-based consensus algorithms, in which nodes are often able to enter and leave the verifying network. Furthermore, with a voting-based consensus mechanism, all nodes in the network must approve the operations or blocks at the same time, in contrast to maintaining the ledger. They will consult with others before determining whether or not to add their suggested blocks to their chain. Using voting-based consensus algorithms is somewhat close to using standard distributed system fault tolerance methods (Andreev et al. 2018). As a result, voting-based consensus should be built to withstand the following scenarios where any nodes have failed or any nodes have been subverted as well as crashed.

### 5.2.2. Types of Blockchain

The two basic types of Blockchains are private and public Blockchains. Nevertheless, there are various variations, including consortium and hybrid Blockchains. Every Blockchain is made up of nodes that are linked together via a peer-to-peer (P2P) mechanism. Each system node has an up-to-date version of the shared ledger. Each node may confirm operations, transmit and receive data and construct blocks. Firstly, the user initiates the transaction in the network. When the transaction is initiated, the process for the creation of blocks starts which includes the previous block’s hash, ownership and the modification history. A nonce creates the cryptographic hash when the first block of a chain is created. The content in the blocks is encrypted until it is mined and is inextricably tied to the nonce and hash. When a block is formed, it is sent to all of the platform's peers for approval. Miners use complex algorithms to solve the extremely difficult mathematical problem of producing an approved hash using a nonce. Because the nonce is only 32 bits long and the hash is 256 bits long, there are roughly 4 billion nonce-hash permutations to mine before finding the proper one. Miners are believed to have uncovered the “golden nonce” when this happens, and the block is included in the chain. When a block is mined correctly, the update is authorized by all servers on the platform, and the miner is rewarded economically. Following acceptance, a current chain is modified with the newly created block. After being added to the chain, the update is disseminated around the system and the action is completed. - **Public Blockchain**: it is a distributed ledger scheme with no permissions. Everyone who has an Internet connectivity can join the Blockchain network. A user (also known as a node) who is a member of the public Blockchain is allowed to get the data, validate transactions and mine for the next block. The sharing of cryptocurrency is one of the most popular applications of shared Blockchains. The majority of the time, public Blockchain is safe, if users adhere to safety protocols. However, if consumers do not adhere to the security protocols, it can be dangerous. Bitcoin, Ethereum and Litecoin are some of the most well-known examples (Andreev et al. 2018; Aggarwal et al. 2019).

- **Private Blockchain**: it is a permissioned Blockchain that can only be used within a closed network. Mostly, they are used inside an association or business where only a few people are involved. Protection, authorizations and usability are only a few of the essential resources that are under the jurisdiction of a governing entity. Any basic operations (such as polling, supply chain management and asset ownership) may be performed using a private Blockchain. Multichain and Hyperledger projects are the examples of private Blockchains (i.e. fabric and sawtooth) (Aggarwal et al. 2019).

- **Consortium Blockchain**: it is a somewhat decentralized Blockchain network operated by a number of entities. This sort of Blockchain enables several organizations to collaborate and serve as the regulator for mining and knowledge exchange. Energy Web Foundation and R3 are two examples of this kind of Blockchain (Aggarwal et al. 2019).

- **Hybrid Blockchain**: it is a hybrid Blockchain network that incorporates private and public Blockchain platforms. In this case, the functionality of all Blockchains is used. Subscribers on the hybrid network may keep records of who has access to which Blockchain information. Just a few of the Blockchain’s selected documents are allowed to be made available, while the others are kept private in the private network. Users should, however, publish this on the public Blockchain for authentication. Public Blockchains enhance hashing and include additional nodes in the validation process. This improves the Blockchain network’s visibility and trust. The hybrid Blockchain “Dragonchain” is an example (Aggarwal et al. 2019).

### 5.2.3. Smart contracts

Szabo coined the word “smart contract” in the mid-1990s, defining it as “a collection of guarantees, defined in electronic format, including rules under which the parties deliver on trust” (Jani 2020). It is a part of Blockchain-based software that helps to enable, implement and monitor contractual arrangements. Its primary objective is to carry out the provisions of an agreement as soon as possible unless certain criteria are satisfied. As a result, smart contracts provide lower settlement costs than conventional schemes, which count on a trustworthy third party to implement and process the terms of a contract. In the literature, several common meanings of a smart contract have been explored. Code that is registered, checked and implemented on a Blockchain is what smart contract code refers to. Smart legal contracts will almost certainly be a hybrid between smart contract code and conventional legal vocabulary. Consider the case of a product seller who enters into a smart legal deal with a manufacturer. The payment terms should be written in code and executed immediately when the product is delivered. However, the manufacturer is likely to insist on an indemnity clause in the deal, in which the seller promises to indemnify the retailer from lawsuits arising from a faulty product. It would be pointless to have this clause in code because it is not self-executing; rather, it remains to be read and implemented by a judge in the event of a lawsuit.

- **Decentralization**: in a typical centralized transaction system, each action must be verified by a single authorized organization. As a result, decentralization necessitates confidence, which is the most pressing problem, as well as raising durability, availability and failover, for which the decentralized P2P Blockchain framework may be a better fit (Bhushan et al. 2020). In the Blockchain network, a transaction may be made by any two peers (P2P) without the need for permission from a central agent. By using a variety of consensus procedures, Blockchain can help to alleviate confidence concerns. Furthermore, it will have lower processing costs (including production and operating costs) and eliminate bottlenecks in the core server's performance. Blockchain, on the contrary, has some tradeoffs in several ways. In PoW instances, such as Bitcoin and Ethereum, infrastructure and electricity expenses are in greater magnitude, but throughput is frequently orders of magnitude worse.

- **Persistence**: Blockchain provides a platform for analyzing facts, enabling suppliers and consumers to establish that the data is credible and unaltered.
