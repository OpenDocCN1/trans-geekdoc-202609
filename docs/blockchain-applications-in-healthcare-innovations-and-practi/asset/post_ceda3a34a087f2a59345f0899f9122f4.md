The paper by Benil and Jasper (2020) has proposed the Elliptical Curve Certificate less Aggregate Cryptography Signature scheme (EC-ACS), for securing the EHRs by verifying and reviewing the information in the Medical Server Cloud (MSC) by use of the Blockchain technology. For sharing and storing the data in the medical cloud storage, Elliptical Curve Cryptography (ECC) is used for encrypting the medical data and Certificate less Aggregate Signature (CAS) for generating a digital signature. Security and privacy are promised by this scheme, and it also safeguards confidential information from malicious activities in the cloud health storage.

The privacy of sensitive medical data can be compromised when they are shared beyond the scope of the institutions. A Blockchain-based solution has been proposed in Hussein et al. (2018) to resolve the challenges that are related to access control and handling the data. To improve security, the system is equipped with discrete wavelet transform and to upgrade the queuing optimization technique, a genetic algorithm is deployed. Verifying the user becomes fast with the use of this cryptographic key generator as it improves the immunity and the access control of the system. This design allows accountability as the number of users are known and the Blockchain keeps a record of the user’s actions. The sharing of the data is allowed only when cryptographic keys and identities are known.

In the paper by Nguyen et al. (2020), an analysis of the patient’s data that has been encrypted using Blockchain technology is presented. Nowadays, because of advancements occurring in technology, data sharing has become a challenge; on the one hand, the compatibility can be compromised by using heterogeneous data, while on the other hand, the distinctive use of the medical terminologies has limited data comprehension. We have seen that authorized users and centralized data storage have been targets for attackers, and the establishment of the health records over a data-sharing network can be troublesome. For data discovery and access, the concord mechanism is used to substantiate the proof of work and inter-operability. “The approach offered by the paper gives a centralized trust-based source for network consensus and compute consensus proof of semantic and structural interoperability”.

In the paper by Dagher et al. (2018), a Blockchain-assumed framework has been alleged for an interoperable, secure and efficient siege to data by authorized users while preserving the patient’s information. This framework was proposed since despite increasing the security of EHRs and the efforts made by cities all over the globe to form a “smart city infrastructure”, a person’s personal information is at the risk of getting leaked. Earlier, the solutions which were provided caused the information to be out of the reach of the patients. The data management systems that are present struggle to balance between data confidentiality and the necessity for the patient and provider to interact with the data regularly. Advanced cryptographic techniques for likewise security, Ancile uses smart contracts in an Ethereum-based Blockchain, for access control and data complication. The paper examines how Ancile would cater to the diverse needs of the patients, professionals and third parties and how this framework could address the longstanding privacy and security problems.

The paper by Chen et al. (2019) talks about a Blockchain-based encryption scheme for EHRs. Leakage of data in EHRs can result in the patient’s information being jeopardized and since most of these records once uploaded do not change, the Blockchain technology can potentially be used to enable data sharing. In the Blockchain network, we have the index of the EHR stored in the form of complex logical expressions for the user to use these expressions and find the index later. Also, the user has complete control over to whom their EHRs are visible because only the index is migrated to the Blockchain to enable propagation. The performance scheme is estimated in two aspects, one for pulling the document IDs from the EHRs and the second for yielding the transactions in the smart contracts in the Ethereum.

### 3.3.1.1. Security

We need to have a proper technology or network to protect and secure the records, and at present, the methods which are being used are not effective. “Data security, integrity and immutability” are the main features of Blockchain technology. Blockchain by the use of a secure decentralized ledger can offer security as it stores the data in multiple computers instead of a single computer. By having the data distributed over the entire Blockchain network, we have the same data stored in many computers. Now we cannot have any third party or malicious actor changing the information or data as the data has to be changed in all the computers over the network. Also, data security and integrity becomes the primary concern when applied to smart devices and cloud infrastructure. By providing “data provenance, auditing and control for cloud-based server”, Blockchain technology can allow safe data sharing among the devices and over the Internet that deposits the medical data, which is shared by using the smart contracts and access protocol implementations.
