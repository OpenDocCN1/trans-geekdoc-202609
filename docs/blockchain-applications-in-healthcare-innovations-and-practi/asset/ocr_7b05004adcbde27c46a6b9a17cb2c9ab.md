Blockchain gives the ability to update database entries in a sequential manner using chained cryptography hash-linked blocks (every step performs a hash of the previous block, thus joining transaction blocks through an unbreakable string, hence the term “Blockchain”). The two forms of Blockchains are public and private Blockchains.

Blockchains that are open to the public, can be used by anyone such as Bitcoin and Ethereum, and no one knows who they are. Private Blockchains, which are used by governments and industry consortiums, are similar to a corporate intranet in that users are known and credentialed. One of the most crucial motivations of adopting ICT may be cyber security. Recent data breaches – the Equifax credit scandal where an estimated 145.5 million Americans’ personal information was exposed as a result of the incident and also the identities, email and passcodes of 2.5 billion Yahoo user profiles were stolen – highlight the immediate necessity improved security measures. Blockchain systems are a tempting target for thieves, but decentralized store records backed with cryptographic signatures over Blockchain technology could significantly increase network security. Decentralized solutions are supposed to provide increased user control and permissioning of private data. Distributed ledgers are referred to as “trustless” because they do away with the need to rely on any third institution, such as Equifax or Yahoo, inside the midst of private information exchanges. Blockchains are smart networks with no third-party intermediates that can authenticate and transmit value. A sophisticated protocol that identifies, verifies, confirms and distributes transactions inside the network incorporates intelligence straight into the network’s processes.

In the healthcare domain, the immutability property that does not allow the Blockchain users to alter or modify any of the records is already appended to the Blockchain. The Blockchain ensures the validity as well as the integrity of the patient records. However, the smart contracts, which are also supported by the Blockchain, are the rules that controls the flow of the patient’s record. According to European rules, the GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation) refuses to process any personal or sensitive data of any patient unless there is explicit consent. Thus, as per the growing anticipation, the Blockchain can be associated with the development of an EMR-based clinical management system in compliance with GDPR. As a result, a trust-free system emerges in which human counterparts and organizations do not need to be known or authorized. Rather, the computationally intelligent network is trusted, which could aid in the development of the next digital solutions.
