# Blockchain in markets

Blockchain technology has the potential to streamline and simplify the entire market procedure, as well as to create a business assets life-cycle where all entities involved in the transaction have access to the information. Within that situation, a system will save a lot of money on infrastructures, increase transparency, speed up processing cycles, enable easy access to data or even eliminate certain intermediaries like dealers. Nasdaq was one of the first financial service companies to develop a Blockchain-enabled product. Private firms use Nasdaq Linq to handle their company's shares, and it leverages cryptocurrencies – power capitalization table.

# Blockchain for performing cross-border activities

Blockchain has the potential to enhance pass transactions through accelerating and decelerating the process, cutting expenses and removing unnecessary steps, several of the traditional intermediaries. At the same time, it may reduce the cost of money remittances. Until now, remittance costs have ranged from 5 to 20%. Blockchain is a technology making it possible to cut prices by 2 percentage points to 3 percentage points of the entire value and to provide secured, actual cross-border payments. Using a smart phone app to make real-time foreign payments in the United Kingdom, Santander was the very first firm to embrace Blockchain technology. The solution, which makes use of Ripple's technology, is the creation of a bitcoin Ripple payment system and exchange network. Blockchain is also being used to improve digital identification, where the users are able to select how they represent themselves or with whom their identification is disclosed after their identity online is shifted to a Blockchain-enabled infrastructure and thus can still register their identities on a Blockchain; however, when they require it, customers do not need to re-register with each network operator, as long as those suppliers also are connected to the Blockchain.

This Blockchain application is frequently used as an illustration of understanding requirements in which a digitized single source of identifiable details could allow more frictionless bank accounts, reduced resources and expenses, while retaining privacy.
