## 2.3. Quantitative analysis

Quantitative research is based on scientific inquiry and relies on data that has been observed or measured to answer questions about the sample population. The findings of quantitative research explain what is and is not relevant to, or impacts, a certain population. It also provides answers to inquiries about the frequency of a phenomenon or the extent to which it affects the sample, and also provides useful data for product design, such as information on user demands, behavior patterns and use cases.

We examined the Scopus database for research published until November 1, 2021. Studies on Blockchain smart contracts have been published not only in medical publications, but also in journals from other fields such as IT, law, engineering, economics and business administration. As a result, given its comprehensive coverage of journals in both the scientific and social sciences, we chose the Scopus database as the source of papers for this study.

We employed four processes to conduct a comprehensive quantitative review: discovering possible research, screening out irrelevant studies, confirming relevant studies and assessing chosen papers.

In the first phase of selecting suitable research, we conducted a preliminary search for papers that had a term like “Blockchain smart contract” in their titles, abstracts or keywords. This phase yielded a total of 5,873 prospective studies. In a separate search, we limited these studies to those that had key phrases like “Blockchain smart contract healthcare” in their titles, abstracts or keywords. This resulted in a total of 373 prospective studies.

In the second phase, a filter was applied to remove the articles in press, which yielded the 356 documents out of 373 documents. The Scopus database also shows articles in press, which means that the articles are not yet published online but are in the production stage.

We investigated the types of document, titles, abstracts and keywords in the third phase of validation, and filtered out 354 studies that fulfilled the criterion of our search for documents published in the English language alone.

We studied the distribution of the 354 articles by publication year, language, place of origin, journal and yearly average number of citations in the fourth phase of evaluation. The articles’ nations of origin were investigated by determining the nationalities of the organizations to which the authors belonged. If an article had two writers working for organizations in two distinct nations, the country of origin was determined to be two countries. The top 10 articles were determined using the yearly average number of citations of the 354 articles. By adjusting for the effects of time after publication, the yearly average number of citations can more effectively represent the degree of influence of each piece on other research than the overall number of citations. For example, an article published in 2016 is more likely to have been referenced than one published in 2019. As a result, we used the yearly average number of citations to rank the 354 papers.

## 2.4. Role of smart contracts in healthcare

### 2.4.1. Health Insurance

Smart contracts possess the ability to remove various inconsistencies in the current setup and may be used on a regular basis in medical insurance. Whenever users use smart contracts to acquire coverage, all policy details are recorded in the user identity quickly. This content is again recorded on the Blockchain.

They may reduce the burden involved with completing paperwork in claim processes. If an insuree has to undertake a clinical procedure covered by the medical policy, the SC will be activated instantly. Therefore, the money through the insurance organization’s wallet will be transferred to the medical facility. This mechanism avoids any inefficiencies and inconvenience, while still enabling the correct reimbursement for health services. As a result, all activities between participants will be accelerated, and the operation would be completed on time.

### 2.4.2. Healthcare

SCs allow data and documents to be recorded on a Blockchain system. This means that if a person has to move through one clinic to the next, they can do it quickly, without needing to complete multiple forms. On the Blockchain network, records may then be seen by the patient’s selected physician.

A number of records containing health information are used by health facilities and medical agencies. These, nevertheless, may be too limited to enable the worldwide distribution of possibly existing information. This data can take a while to get the rightful owner and could be hacked if smart contracts and Blockchain are not used.

If patient details are saved in an SC and maintained on the Blockchain, these records can be accessed by the clinics and academic institutions all over the world. With sufficient acceptance, a user can enter into any clinic in the world for medication, and the organization would have quick accessibility to personal records provided they presented their secret key.

### 2.4.3. Telemedicine

Telehealth is a fast-growing healthcare profession. It allows clinicians and caregivers to connect with their clients using digital equipment such as cell phones as well as other IoT devices. Its major function is to provide treatment for the chronically sick. Practitioners can use videoconferencing to track treatment adherence and obtain actual information on their clients’ health. These technology advancements help to improve patient results by enhancing connectivity and lowering operational costs.

Telemedicine has downsides due to the fact that the procedures entailed are a frequent target for cyber criminals. SCs protect the security and confidentiality of health records and other important patient documentation. To exchange and maintain data, SCs may be applied on a wide level and saved on the Blockchain. They could also help with record upkeep and make sure that critical medical details are kept secure and openly.
