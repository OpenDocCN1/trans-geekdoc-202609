# QA SYSTEM USING DL 211

Demand of industry boosted the research in QA system from 2004. Since then, QA system started to address multidomains. Question classifier was introduced and answering agent started to work in the specific domain determined by the classifier. Corporate giants became interested to employ QA system as per industry requirement. IBM came with a QA project "Watson" which is very successful now. Initially QA systems were built in English language. A group of scientists, academics, engineers, students devoted themselves to find algorithms to improve the performance of the automatic QA system. Stanford university built "Stanford Question Answering Dataset (SQUAD)\ with the help of volunteers. SQUAD [9] which is open dataset available online, contains millions of questions, answers and answer-repository to give the researchers a readily available experimental setup to test the performance of their algorithms in English QA. It would be very contextual to mention two of the earliest static QA system LUNAR [10] and BASEBALL [11] which were built in the decade of 1960s and 1970s and were dedicated to answer geological data of Apollo Moon and famous baseball league of USA respectively. SQUAD eased the work of creating and collecting dataset for the researchers who are working in English language and many of them have tested and published the performance of their QA system. Recently, Albert [12] achieved significant performance with 92.215 F1 score. The retro-reader [13] built on top of Albert has achieved 92.978 F1.

As article 344(1) and 355, the eighth schedule of the constitution of India has 22 scheduled languages which have been given status of official languages and are used by a considerably large population. They are Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Kashmiri, Konkani, Malayalam, Manipuri, Marathi, Oriya, Punjabi, Sanskrit, Sindhi, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu, Santhali, Mithili, and Dogri. In this section, recent research to develop QA systems in these scheduled languages, except Bengali and Bangla, are explored. Bengali QA systems are already discussed in the starting of this survey.

In Hindi, [17–29] are the current research on the QA system. Nanda and her group [17] used feature extraction and Naive Bayes classification to determine the category of the question. Knowledge base is used to retrieve the sentence containing the answer. Devi and Dua [18] applied KNN method for question classification and nine similarity functions to retrieve the answer. Smith Waterman similarity gave the best result and handled both misspelled words and multiphase words.
