# 196 CONVERGENCE OF DL IN CYBER-IoT SYSTEMS AND SECURITY

Beck et al. [25] have used a deep learning network to predict fruitful drugs on coronavirus from existing commercial target medicines. The proposed deep learning-based target interaction model is named molecule former-drug target interaction (MT-DTI). The authors based on their model results analysis claimed that atazanavir, which is an antireviral medication used to treat and prevent the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), is the most effective compound for corona virus. Upon identification of various effective agents like Kaletra by the proposed model, authors also have proposed a list of drugs for ascertain effective treatment strategies for SARS-CoV-2.

In Patankar [26], deep-learning-models for the drug solution of COVID-19 viruses have been proposed. A predictive LSTM model has been used in the proposed models in which the proposed models screen 310k drugs from the ZINC database for the identification of effective compounds. All the predicted drugs predicted by the proposed model claimed to have better binding properties in docking simulations relative to the medically approved drugs for COVID-19 like remdesivir, favipiviravir, and galidesivir. Additionally, the 310k ZINC database molecules with their predicted IC50s were used to train a SemiSupervised Variational Auto Encoder (SSVAE) model. The proposed model was used to generate 10 new molecules from the latent space to demonstrate its utility. Among the new molecules generated by the SSVAE model, the most stable new molecule had binding energy lower than the comparison group of prior drugs. These molecules could potentially be good drug candidates for the SARS CoV and COVID-19. Their experimental results conclude that deep learning-based models could be useful in screening existing compound and generating new molecules to find drugs for COVID-19.
