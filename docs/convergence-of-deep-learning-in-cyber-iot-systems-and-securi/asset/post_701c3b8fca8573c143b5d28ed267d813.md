## 1.2 Literature Survey

The authors have proposed a method by using HAN to detect fake news in Okano et al. [1]. This model has been evaluated in Brazilian and Portuguese fake news corpus, which is named as Fake.Br. Removing or keeping stop words and varying the word embeddings showed slightly less accuracy. The authors have proposed a modern approach to detect fake news, which achieves high accuracy in Zhang et al. [2]. To group news articles in clusters, it has a topic-based classification mechanism and extract events it event extraction mechanism. It compares their events with legitimate events to find the credibility of news. H. Karimi, P. Roy, S. Babe-Sadiya, and J. Tang have introduced a framework MMFD which combines extracted features, multisource fusion and degrees of the fakeness of articles into a standalone model in Karimi et al. [3] and in Wang et al. [4] The authors have proposed a new framework EANN. This framework is made of three elements, a feature extractor which extracts textual and visual features from posts, a detector which learns the differentiable representations and event discriminator to distinguish between fake and not fake news.

In Yang et al. [5], the authors have proposed a network which is named TI-CNN. The method proposes the latent and explicit features to a single space. This model can be trained and tested with both the image and text data at the same time. H. Ahmed, I. Traore, and S. Saad have proposed a method which uses an analysis technique called N-Gram with some techniques of artificial intelligence in Ahmed et al. [6]. After experimenting with many of feature extraction techniques and classification techniques they found that TF-IDF feature extraction technique and LSVM classification technique gives the best performance. The authors have used an automated detector which is based on the Artificial Intelligence technique deep learning and a 3HAN to achieve speed and accuracy in Singhan et al. [7]. The network consists of three tiers, one tier for words, one tier for sentences and one tier for news vector. This method provides an easy to understand using the values given to the components of the used article.

In Ruchansky et al. [8], the authors have proposed a method CSI, which consists of three tiers, for capturing, for scoring and integrating. The first module uses RNN to capture the user activity, the second module learns the characteristics of the source. Then, by integrating both modules, the model can classify whether the article is not or fake. W. Wang has proposed a benchmark dataset consists of manually labeled short stories of various contexts, ‘LIAR’ [9]. The dataset yielded an improved result of deep learning model. M. Gran and V. Mes [10] have proposed an elementary approach for fake news detection using Naive Bayes technique. The software system was tested against dataset created using Facebook posts and achieved a decent result. The authors have introduced a model that uses a hybrid approach, which is a combination of linguistic cue with behavioural data in [11].

## 4.3 Proposed Work

We have tried and tested many classification techniques like Logistic Regression, K-Nearest Neighbors, Naive Bayes and many more. Naive Bayes stands out in the list in terms of accuracy and simplicity of approach.

### Mathematical Explanation of Naive Bayes Classifier

We consider given news report fake or not in basis of calculating the conditional probability that report contains some certain word. The formula is stated in equation 4.1.

$$P(F|W) = \frac{P(W|F).P(F)}{P(W|F).P(F) + P(W|T).P(T)}$$

where:
- P(F|W): conditional probability of a report is fake given the word W occurs in the report
- P(W|F): conditional probability of searching W word in news reports which are fake
- P(F): probability of news report is fake
- P(W|T): conditional probability of searching W word in non-fake reports
- P(T): probability of news report is non-fake

First, we consider that $ P(F|W) $ values for all the words of the news report are available to us. Second, we combine the probability to get certainty that news report is fake. The expressions are presented in equations 4.2, 4.3, and 4.4.

$$a1 = P(F|W_1).P(F|W_2)\dots P(F|W_n)$$

$$a2 = (1-P(F|W_1)).(1-P(F|W_2))\dots (1-P(F|W_n))$$

$$a = \frac{a_1}{a_1 + a_2}$$

where:
- n: Count of words in the report
- a1: Product value of the probability that a news report is fake which contains a certain word for every word in the report
- a2: Same as it using complement probability
- P(F|W_n): Conditional probability that a report is fake given the word W_n occurs in it
- a: The overall probability of news report be fake

Now assuming there is a train set which contains many reports marked as real or fake, so calculate the conditional probability of searching a certain word in training set. We can now describe the probability of searching a certain word in fake reports as a ratio of the fake article which consists of this to the total no. of articles. Similarly, we can describe the probability of searching certain words in true articles.

### 4.3.3 Explanation of Approach

We have used the News Headline and News Body attributes to train our current model. Those articles where the type of article was not mentioned clearly are ignored. Also, the articles which are broken or not complete, are also ignored.

At first, we have stemmed every word of the articles and ignored all the stop words. Stemming is a process to get the root form of the derived words. This technique is necessary to handle similar words (like 'sleep' and 'sleeping') as same words and this may increase accuracy. For finding the Gemma of the English words we have used the NLTK [15] stem methods and for Bangla words, we have used Bangla_stemmer [16] tool. Function words, like articles and prepositions, which do not contribute to the meaning of the sentences, are called Words. These words hardly affect the performance of the classifier and have been removed. Then, we have prepared the global vector using the bag-of-words [17] method.
