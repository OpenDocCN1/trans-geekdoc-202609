# 8.2.1 Review of Face Recognition for Unmasked Faces

Recently compressive (CS)–based technique is used widely in different recognition problems. The CS theory–based sparse representation–based classification (SRC) technique for FR was first implemented in Wright et al. [3]. In Wright et al. [3], test images are represented as a sparse linear combination of the training images and feature extraction steps are based on eigenface and Laplacian images. Their experiment shows that for the large dimension of feature vectors this technique is insensitive to types of features. Nagesh et. [4] proposed an expression-invariant FR technique based on CS theory. Different images of same subjects are considered as an ensemble of correlated signals. The changes of face due to expression variations are sparse in respect to the whole face images. Here training images of any individual person is represented by only two type of feature images, first one is assumed the holistic features and another which captures the expressions of all training images. Fang et al. [5] presented an infrared FR method by integrating the CS theory and Principal Component Analysis [6]. Their experiment shows that this method is robust under the variation of illumination, shadow and facial expression, and provides higher recognition rate.

In the last 5 years, FR-based automated attendance systems have been presented [7–12], and implemented successfully. In some cases, existing techniques have been modified or combined with another technique to improve the accuracy in presence of different challenges [13–16]. Different challenges for efficient FR systems are pose variation, illumination differences, occlusion, and expression variation [17, 18]. Krishna et al. [19] presented an automated FR system to cope with the illumination variation. To overcome the drawback of pose variation Yogesh et al. [20] proposed an efficient FR technique by integrating local binary pattern (LBP), dual cross pattern (DCP), and support vector machine (SVM).
