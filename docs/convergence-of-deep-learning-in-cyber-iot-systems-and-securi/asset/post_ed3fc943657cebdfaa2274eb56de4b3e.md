### 1.2.1 Oral Cancer

Oral cancer [OC] among various types of cancer is the imminent problem nowadays in the Indian population, it is the sixth cancer reported worldwide in population and is found in both sexes. 200,000 new cases are reported annually worldwide, two-thirds of which occur in developing countries including India with a high mortality rate [11]. OC is increasing rapidly in India due to lack of hygiene, alcohol consumption, tobacco chewing, betul smoking. OC can be defined as a malignant neoplasm in the oral cavity, most frequently occurring oral cancer in India with its premalignant conditions like oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), subcutaneous fibrosis (OSF), erythrophelia (OE), leukopenia (OLK), lichen planus (OLP) [12].

OC can be defined as uncontrolled cell growth along lesions in the oral cavity basically starts from any tissue of the mouth and invites any other neighboring areas in the mouth. The most common presenting features are prolonged ulceration, which does not heal, referred to, the ear, difficulty with speaking, and opening the mouth or chewing and ultimately leads to death. Most people are diagnosed with premalignant lesions like OLK and OE white red patches covering the oral cavity [13]. These lesions are further subdivided according to their types, homogeneous (flat, thin), nonhomogenous (speckled). ### 1.3 Primary Concepts

### 1.3.1 Transmission Efficiency

Most current IoT systems send data to the cloud via hypertext protocol (HTTP) and use the message queuing telemetry protocol (MQTT) conventional transport protocols [21, 22]. A unique payload in the HTTP method is sent by every device that delivers data to the cloud. Like a normal web server, the IoT platform gets these data and saves it in a database. As a consequence of this method of communication, IoT devices are constrained by bandwidth, latency, and battery consumption. Consequently, the establishment of a new cloud connection and an HTTP request with various headers, it is necessary to provide a payload for each identified location, which is usually in XML or JSON format. Using this method, which relies on HTTP requests, entails a significant cost for delivering tiny and frequent payloads, as sensor readings, which are often only a few bytes. Firewire, cloud-based infrastructure for IoT platforms sponsored by the European Union and the European Commission, has this approach in several iterations [23, 24]. Other open and standard solutions will benefit greatly from this approach. MQTT is a telemetry system that enables a publish-subscribe-based messaging system that allows communication between servers and devices. HTTP-based solutions, as described in Yasumoto et al. [21], are considerably less efficient than they need more bandwidth and higher latency. AmazonWeb Services, for example, is using this protocol as the foundation for the Internet of Things offerings [21]. The aforementioned companies, as well as others like Kaa, Carriots, and Ubidots [29], have been integrating MQTT into their products in recent years.
