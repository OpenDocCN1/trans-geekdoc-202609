## 2 LITERATURE WORK

Zhou et al. (2020) emphasized the global prevalence of diabetes as a pervasive and persistent ailment, posing substantial risks due to potential complications. Early detection of diabetes holds paramount importance for timely intervention and halting disease progression. Their method not only forecasts future diabetes occurrences but also identifies the specific diabetes type, crucial for tailoring individualized treatment plans. The approach transforms the challenge into a classification task, leveraging a model within the layers of a deep neural network, enhanced by dropout regularization to mitigate overfitting. With meticulous parameter tuning and binary cross-entropy loss, the proposed DLPD (Deep Learning for Anticipating Diabetes) model achieves exceptional accuracy. Notably, it attains a training accuracy of 94.02174% for the diabetes type dataset and an impressive 99.4112% for the Pima Indians diabetes dataset, affirming the efficacy of the proposed deep learning model.
