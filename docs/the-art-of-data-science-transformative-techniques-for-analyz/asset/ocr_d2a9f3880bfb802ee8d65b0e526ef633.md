Bayesian methods also shine in the domain of Bayesian model averaging,
where multiple models are considered, and their predictions are combined
based on their posterior probabilities. This ensemble approach leverages
the strengths of individual models while acknowledging the uncertainty
associated with model selection. Bayesian model averaging is especially
helpful when dealing with model uncertainty or when numerous models
offer complimentary insights into the underlying facts.

Finding the ideal parameter configuration for a particular system or
process is the goal of the emerging area of Bayesian optimization,
highlighting the importance of Bayesian approaches. Bayesian
optimization leverages probabilistic surrogate models to model the
underlying objective function and iteratively selects new configurations to
explore based on the uncertainty in the current model. This adaptive
approach is precious in scenarios such as hyperparameter tuning in
machine learning models, where the goal is to identify the configuration
that maximizes performance while minimizing the number of evaluations.

Despite their benefits, Bayesian techniques can be computationally
demanding, especially when no workable analytical answers exist.
Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods, such as the Metropolis-
Hastings algorithm and Gibbs sampling, and more recent advances like
Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, offer avenues for sampling from complex
posterior distributions. Furthermore, variational inference offers a
different optimization-based method for effectively approximating
posterior distributions. Despite their computing demands, these techniques
