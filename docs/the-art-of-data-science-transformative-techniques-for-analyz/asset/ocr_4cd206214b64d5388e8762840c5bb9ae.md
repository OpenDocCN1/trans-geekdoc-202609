An essential machine learning component is classifying, categorizing, or labeling instances according to predetermined criteria. The primary goal is to develop a model to learn patterns from labeled training data and generalize these patterns to classify new, unseen instances accurately. Standard classification techniques include k-nearest neighbors, logistic regression, decision trees, and support vector machines.

Decision trees are a flexible and easily interpreted categorization method that shows a sequence of binary choices arranged tree-like. Every leaf node corresponds to a projected class, and every decision node represents a test on a particular feature. Decision trees are commonly utilized in industries like banking, marketing, and healthcare because they are excellent at defining complicated decision boundaries. Ensemble techniques like random forests and gradient boosting substantially improve the predictive accuracy of decision trees by fusing several weak learners into a robust model.

Robust classifiers known as support vector machines (SVMs) seek to identify the ideal hyperplane in a high dimensional space for dividing several classes. SVMs work well with datasets with intricate structures or when the decision boundary is nonlinear. Their uses span a variety of fields, including bioinformatics, finance, and picture and text classification. A simple yet powerful classification algorithm called K-nearest neighbors (KNN) gives an instance a class label based on the majority class of its k-nearest neighbors in the feature space.
