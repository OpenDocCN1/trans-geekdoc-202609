In exploratory data analysis, problem definition takes on a different dimension. In this case, the objective can be to look for patterns, trends, or outliers in the data without considering the conclusion beforehand. Formulating such a problem involves defining the scope of exploration, selecting relevant variables for analysis, and determining the appropriate visualization and statistical techniques to gain insights.

Defining and formulating problems is iterative, as data scientists discover when navigating the intricate world of analysis. It is not a linear process but a dynamic one, where insights gained during the exploration phase inform refinements in problem definition and vice versa. The accuracy of the formulation improves in tandem with a deeper understanding of the problem, generating a feedback loop that amplifies the analytical journey's efficacy.

A crucial aspect of problem definition and formulation is integrating domain expertise. Armed with technical skills in statistics, machine learning, and programming, data scientists collaborate with domain experts who bring a nuanced understanding of the subject matter. Domain specialists provide invaluable insights into the issue's complexities, the significance of particular variables, and the contextual subtleties that may escape a strictly data-centric viewpoint. This partnership is mutually beneficial.

The synergy between data scientists and domain experts is particularly evident in cases where the problem involves predicting outcomes in complex systems, such as healthcare, finance, or environmental science.
