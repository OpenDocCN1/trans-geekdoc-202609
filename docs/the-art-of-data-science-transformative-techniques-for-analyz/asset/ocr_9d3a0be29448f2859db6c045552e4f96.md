Conversely, recall—sometimes called sensitivity or actual positive rate—measures the model's capacity to capture every positive case. Recollection is a vital metric in contexts where missing positive instances is more costly than false positives. Finding the right balance between memory and precision depends on the particular goals and limitations of the given forecasting task.

Combining these two metrics into a single number representing the trade-off between precision and memory is the F1 score, a harmonic mean of precision and recall. This statistic is convenient when there is a conflict between reducing false positives and false negatives. The F1 score objectively evaluates a model's overall performance and is particularly pertinent in binary classification tasks. However, variants like macro and micro averages may be used to increase the F1 score's applicability in multiclass classification scenarios.

Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves and the area under the ROC curve (AUC-ROC) are prevalent in evaluating the performance of models, especially in binary classification settings. The ROC curve shows the trade-off between the actual positive rate (sensitivity) and the false positive rate at various threshold levels. The area under the curve is quantified using AUC-ROC, which yields a single-value statistic from 0 to 1. A higher AUC-ROC score indicates better distinguishing between positive and negative examples. ROC curves are valuable tools for analyzing how a model's specificity and sensitivity vary at different decision thresholds, providing information about the model's resilience.
