Virtual worlds are ideally suited to this approach as they allow multiple people to explore an item or location at the same time, sharing notes and approaching the product on their own terms – rather than that of the designer or builder (Anderson et al., 2014). Examples of such approaches include City4.0/Curiosity – which was created using Unity (Hunter et al., 2022), Tirol Town in Brazil – which was created in Minecraft (de Andrade et al., 2020) and the Virtual Library of Birmingham (see Figure 3.3) – which was created in Second Life (Hand, 2011)*. These models need not just be passive representations. Users can be given design choices to try out, and the environment can contain 3D post-it notes to allow users to annotate (and comment and vote up/down) particular issues or can try and navigate a building or use a device when impaired by some disability.
