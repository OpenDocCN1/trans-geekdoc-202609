The present value or intrinsic value calculated from Equation 4.6 is quite sensitive to the estimates used by the investor in the equation. Relatively small variations in the inputs can change the estimated price by large percentage amounts. This is illustrated in Examples 4.2, 4.3 and 4.4.

# Example 4.2

For Nadia Corporation assume the discount rate used, k, is 16% instead of 15%, with the other variables held constant:

$1(1.07)
0.16 - 0.07

P = $11.89

In this example, a 1 percentage point rise in k results in an 11.14% decrease in price, from today's price of $13.38 to $11.89.

# Example 4.3

Assume that for Nadia Corporation the growth rate, g, is 6% instead of 7%, with the other variables held constant:

$1(1.06)
0.15 - 0.06 = $11.77

In this example, a 1 percentage point decline in g results in a 12% decrease in price, from $13.38 to $11.77.

# Example 4.4

Assume that for Nadia Corporation the discount rate rises to 16% and the growth rate declines to 4%:

$1(1.04)
0.16 - 0.04 = $8.67

In this example, the price declines from today's price of $13.38 to $8.67, a 35% change.
