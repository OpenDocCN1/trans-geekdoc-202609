Once this was recognized, the solution to the equation was easily derived.

In the case of a European call option with no cash payments on the underlying asset and with a certain, continuously compounded interest rate, Black and Scholes demonstrated that the value of a call option is an explicit function of the first five factors mentioned above. The Black–Scholes model is shown in Equation 10.10:

C = P N(d₁) - E e^{-rt} N(d₂)
10.10

where:

+   - C = the price of a call option
+   - P = the current price of the underlying asset
+   - E = the exercise price
+   - e = the base of natural logarithms
+   - r = the continuously compounded interest rate
+   - t = the remaining life of the call option

N(d₁) and N(d₂) are the cumulative probabilities from the normal distribution of getting the values d₁ and d₂, where d₁ and d₂ are as follows:

d₁ = [ln(P/E) + (r + 0.5 σ²) t] / [σ √t]
d₂ = d₁ - σ √t

where σ = the standard deviation of the continuously compounded rate of return on the underlying asset.

The term e^{-rt} is the present value of $1 received t periods from the present. It is the continuously compounded equivalent of what we have called d, the present value of $1.

To understand the Black–Scholes model better, consider the case where N(d₁) and N(d₂) are both equal to 1. This is equivalent to assuming complete certainty. Then the model becomes Equation 10.11
