| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1954–58 | Formula Translation, or Fortran, the first high-level programming language, is developed by Jim Backus and others at IBM. |
| 1956 | The first transistorized computer is developed at MIT. |
| 1957 | Light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation, known as laser, springs from the research of American Gordon Gould.<br>Sputnik, the first satellite, was launched by the Soviet Union on 4 October. It spurred a revolution of science education in the US. |
| 1959 | Jack Kilby and Robert Noyce of Texas Instruments develop the integrated circuit. Noyce goes on to found Intel Corp. |
| 1963 | The first minicomputer, the PDP-8, is developed at Digital Equipment Corp.<br>Douglas Engelbart of Stanford patents the mouse; he also develops the word processor. |
| 1968 | UNIX is developed at Bell Laboratories by Ken Thompson and Dennis Ritchie, ushering in the era of computing languages that aren’t tied to a single computer brand. |
| 1969 | ARPANET, a four-computer network, is developed by the US Department of Defense, and forms the basis of what is now the Internet. |
| 1971 | Intel introduces the 4004 chip, the first microprocessor. |
| 1972 | Bill Gates drops out of Harvard. With Paul Allen he starts Traf-O-Data, which makes traffic-counting systems. The company later becomes Microsoft Corp.<br>Ray Tomlinson modifies an e-mail program he created in 1971 for ARPANET where it becomes a big hit. The @ sign was chosen from the punctuation keys on Tomlinson’s Model 33 teletype for its ‘at’ meaning.<br>Atari releases ‘Pong’, the first video game. |
| 1973 | Vinton Cerf and Bob Kahn present the basic Internet ideas at the University of Sussex. |
| 1974 | The Altair, the first popular personal computer, is developed by Edward Roberts of Micro Instrumentation Telemetry Systems and sold as a kit. |
