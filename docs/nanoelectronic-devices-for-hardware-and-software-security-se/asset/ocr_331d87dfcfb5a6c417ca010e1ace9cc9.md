# 1.11 CHALLENGES IN JLTFET AND DLTFET

These JLTFETs and DLTs have shown the required tunneling phenomenon and features for TFET devices. These devices can solve the problems of TFTs with junctions and their fabrication. But they are not free from the inherent problems of TFTs. As all devices work in a similar way, they also face similar challenges. The challenges with these JLTFET and DLTFET are of the same nature as TFTs. Both JLTFETs and DLTFETs have low Igs, like the conventional TFT. This problem of low Igs hampers their ability to couple with MOSFETs. Both JLTFETs and DLTFETs suffer from the problem of variability, which needs to be worked upon [52][53]. This phenomenon of variability leads to negative conduction in these devices.

# 1.11.1 Low ON-State Current

Many techniques have been proposed for the improvement of ON-state behavior of JLTFETs and DLTFETs, which are plasma-based TFTs. The aim of our work is for the ON-state improvement of JLTFETs and DLTFETs only. The techniques for chemically doped TFTs are not mentioned here, although the strategy for ON-state improvement comes for both chemically doped and plasma-based TFTs, as they are both functionally. The use of a dual material gate with lower work function electrode at the source to narrow the tunnel barrier in ON-state has been proposed [54]–56]. The use of hydroelectric with a high-k dielectric at the source also decreases the tunnel barrier in ON-state by enlarging the field due to high-k dielectric [57][58]. The conventional dielectric materials can be replaced by ferroelectric dielectric materials, which show negative capacitance. This negative capacitance dielectric boosted the performance of JLTFETs [59]. The application of low band-gap materials in heterojunction JLTFETs and DLTFETs, which narrow the tunnel barrier, has JLTFETs formed on an n' body, have further enhanced their performance [60]–62]. In these charge plasma-based JLTFETs there is an unavoidable spacer between gate and p/source electrodes. The presence of separate electrodes for channel and source regions rises due to this spacer, which widens the tunnel barrier in ON-state [18][50]. This spacer can be eliminated by the use of a p-type body, which enhances the device performance [55]. The formation of more abrupt tunneling junctions can also be achieved by the use of a metal layer in the oxide region in the middle source and gate electrodes. This also narrows the tunnel barrier in ON-state, thereby improving the device performance. [53][63][64].
