### 10.4.1 Effect of Dielectric Materials

This section presents the effect of dielectric materials near the tunnel junction on DC, and HCE performance is presented at $V_{DS}$ = 1 V of HF-FTED. Here, we considered $L_G$ = 30 nm ($L_{p-k}=L_{SiO_2}$ = 15 nm).

The effects of dielectric material near the source region in linear and log scale on transfer characteristics are shown in Figures 10.2a and b. When $SiO_2$ is taken as dielectric material, it will act as a conventional FTET. It is seen that ON current increases with ambipolar change of the dielectric material near the source region changed from $SiO_2$ to $La_2O_3$. This is because ON-state is extracted by tunnel junction, which is covered by high-k dielectric material, and the ambipolar behavior is driven by drain channel junction. On the other hand, the presence of high-k gate material degrades the OFF current of HF-FTED by an increase in tunneling rate as dielectric material is changed from $SiO_2$ to $La_2O_3$.

In order to elaborate the behavior of transfer characteristics, we have plotted the BTBT rate and energy band of HF-FTED, taking dielectric material as the parameter, as shown in Figures 10.3 and 10.4. The higher value of the BTBT rate indicates enhancement in the ON current. It is observed from Figure 10.3 that the tunneling rate at source/channel junction increases as the dielectric constant of gate material increases, and thus ON current of the device is improved. Likewise, it is observed from Figure 10.4 that the bandgap between the conduction ($E_c$) and valence ($E_v$) reduces as gate dielectric constant increases. This reduction in energy band leads to enhancement in tunneling rate, and thus, ON current is improved.

The switching ratio ($I_{ON}/I_{OFF}$) and threshold voltage ($V_T$) as a function of gate dielectric material is summarized in Figure 10.5. It is visualized that $I_{ON}$ of HF-FTED reduces as the dielectric material is changed, and this is due to significant degradation in OFF current compared to improvement in ON current. As the gate dielectric constant, the ON current increases, and thus less bias is required to change channel in HF-FTED, which indicates lower $V_T$ as the gate dielectric constant increases.

The $I_{ON/AND}$ ratio at $V_{DS}$ = 1 V as a function of dielectric material is summarized in Figure 10.6. It is understood that the $I_{ON/AND}$ ratio improves the dielectric constant of gate dielectric near the source junction, and this is due to increase in ON current.

The effect of dielectric materials near the source region on the horizontal field and impact ionization rate are presented in Figures 10.7a and b, respectively. The influence of drain bias ($V_{DS}$) in linear and log scale on transfer characteristic for HF-FTED is shown in Figures 10.8a and b. It is perceived that $I_{ON}$ of HF-FTED is increased with significant changes in OFF current as $V_{DS}$ changes from 0.5 to 1.2 V. The log scale in Figure 10.8b $I_{ON/AND}$ is degraded significantly as $V_{DS}$ is increased from 0.5 to 1.2 V. This behavior of drain current can be better explained from Figure 10.9, where we have shown BTBT behavior for variation $V_{DS}$.

### 10.4.3 Effect of Length of High-k Gate Dielectric

Here, the DC and HCE performance for the variation in $L_{h-k}$ is highlighted in HF-FTED. We have considered $V_{DS}=1$ V, $L_{SiO2}=15$ nm, and $HfO_3$ as the gate dielectric near the source region.
The effect of $L_{h-k}$ on the transfer character and log scale is portrayed in Figures 10.14a and b, respectively. In this regard, we have considered $L_{h-k}=5, 10, 15$, and 20 nm. It is seen that ON current of HF-FTED is highest at $L_{h-k}=15$ nm and $L_{h-k}=10$ nm. However, the lower value of OFF current and ambipolar current are obtained at $L_{h-k}=10$ nm in HF-FTED.
