This chapter would provide a generic overview on this emerging THz technology, inherent challenges associated with it, and potential applications. It will also deal with various building blocks that are the backbone of establishing a communication link at the THz frequency band. The fundamental limitation of the THz band is extreme atmospheric attenuation, which has surged the development of high gain antennas over the last few years. The present chapter also deals with development of high gain antennas for THz applications. A comprehensive look on the national status of present THz research and activities along with future directions is also presented here. THz frequency range covers the submillimeter regime, fabrication of the small devices and device components calls for many sophisticated technologies; (i) CNC (computer numerical control) milling and (ii) silicon micromachining. Currently, THz systems are predominantly manufactured by CNC milling. In recent years, CNC has achieved a high level of precision and reliability, however, it lacks the ability to manufacture at volume, which makes the overall process time consuming as well as costly. In contrast, silicon micromachining technology is useful for volume production, and at the same it offers fabrication precision on the order of microns or even below (depending upon the technology node and type of lithography process used). Moreover, the micromining process yields a good surface profile with the surface roughness of finished product in the range of nanometers (reducing losses associated with surface roughness at THz). Furthermore, this process is also capable of fabricating high-aspect ratio geometries that, using any other fabrication technology, are hardly possible to fabricate. Due to these inherent advantages of Si-micromachining, many physical, inertial, biological sensors, microphones, and/microwave devices are being fabricated with this technology. The present chapter, as will be elaborated in the next sections, deals with the design and development of various passive components and high gain antennas using silicon micromachining technology for THz applications. The passive components and antennas are designed and optimized using Ansys HFSS suite. The finalized designs are under fabrication using the well-established DRI (deep reactive ion etching) micromachining technique at Semi-Conductor Laboratory (SCL), Chandigarh, India.
