The extended back gate in double JLT widened the tunnel barrier at drain-channel interface and increased the barrier height at source-channel interface in the OFF-state [25]. For nanowire JLTs, many device architectures, such as core-shell nanowire, doping, and nanowire dot, were proposed to mitigate this GIDL by Sahay et al. [22]–[29]. For nanowire JFET, the dual-gate material (work function) gate was proposed to widen the tunnel barrier at the drain channel interface in the OFF-state [24]. A dual metal stack gate was also proposed to counter the effect of GIDL in sub-10 nm nanowire JLT [30]. All these techniques either (a) widened the tunnel barrier, (b) lowered the electric field at the drain-channel interface, or (c) increased the source-channel barrier height in the OFF-state. This led to reduced BTBT, in turn suppressed GIDL. The gate itself spacer design guidelines and diameter difference in nanowire JLT and nanowire JFET for GIDL were also successfully explained in [31][32]. The proposal for raised source/drain extensions to reduce the effect of GIDL by inhibiting parasitic BJT action in DLT on buried oxide (BOX) was proposed by Sindhu et al. [23].

# 1.5 MOTIVATION BEHIND THE WORK PRESENTED ON JLT AND DLT

Therefore, the objectives presented in this chapter are listed here.

+   - One of the objectives behind this chapter is to highlight the problem of GIDL in these nanoscaled, junctionless, and dopeless devices.
- The suppression of short channel effects for nanosaled JLT and DLT is another important problem that has been brought forward in this chapter.
- There have many techniques proposed for nanowire JFETs, but for a more robust technological perspective, we need less complex fabrication process. For novel JLTs and DLTs, single and double gate architectures have been fabricated till now. So we have tried to propose ideas for single gate and double gate architectures. These ideas could be extended to nanowire architectures by suitable fabrication steps.

# 1.6 SIMULATION ENVIRONMENT OF JLT AND DLT

The 2D simulations were implemented by Silvac Atlas [33]. The quantum confinement model proposed by Hansch [34] is activated, the device thickness is less than 10 nm. To model the phenomenon of tunneling in OFF-state or low gate voltages, a nonlocal band-to-band tunneling (BTBT) model based on the Wentzel-Kramillouin method is activated. The nonlocal BTBT is sufficiently calibrated and is already used in the literature [35]. The tunneling masses 0.10 m, for electrons and 0.17 m, for holes were used according to [35] for JLT. Fermi-Dirac statistics were involved in the intrinsic carrier concentration needed for Shockley-Hall (SRH) series. The Lombardi model of mobility, and Auger recombination were incorporated into model mobility and lifetime phenomena. As the doping is higher in a JLT (~10⁹), a band gap narrowing (BGN) is also invoked to model high doping effects. The setup selection calibration was done by replacing the transfer functions of DG-JLT for $V_{DS}=0.02$ V, $T_{SI}=10$ nm, $w=12.5$ nm and work function difference ($\phi_{MS}$) of 1.12 eV.
