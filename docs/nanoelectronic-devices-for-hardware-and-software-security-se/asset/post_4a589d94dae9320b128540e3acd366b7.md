## 10.1 INTRODUCTION

To follow Moore's law, the consistent scaling down of the nanoscale MOSFETs leads to a degradation in power consumption and various short channel performance effects [1–2]. It is very difficult to maintain the switching ratio ($I_{ON}/I_{OFF}$) with the scale of supply voltage, and also, the subthreshold swing (SS) of MOSFET is not scalable by 60 mV/dec at 300K [3]. To overcome these barriers, researchers have proposed a device that operates on band-to-band tunneling (BTBT), which is popularly identified as TFET (TFET) [4–5]. It is promising from a low power perspective due to SS (≤60 mV/dec) as well as low value of leakage current. However, one major limitation of it is the lower value of ON current. The performance of TFET is further enhanced by various techniques like gate engineering, structural engineering, dielectric engineering, and many architectures proposed are double gate (DG), single gate (TG), dual gate (DMG), circular gate (CG), gate-all-around (GAA). In terms of channel engineering, materials used as channel to improve the performance of TFET [15–16]. Researchers have proposed hetero gate TFET (HG-TFET), and this device has improved SS and ON current compared to TFET with SiO₂ as gate dielectric [17]. A HG-TFET with back gate is reported, and the subthreshold characteristic of this device is not affected by the scaling of the dimensions [18]. In this paper, we consider three different gate dielectric materials (Al₂O₃, Hf₂, and La₂O₃) and investigate the effects of these dielectric materials on drain current characteristics, switching performance ($I_{ON}/I_{OFF}$), and VT. Further, we have investigated the hot carrier effect, taking drain bias as parameter in HG-TFET.

## 10.2 LITERATURE SURVEY

The first p–i–n structure, which is popularly known as TFET, was proposed at Brown University, and the effectiveness for spectroscopy was advised [19]. Banerjee have reported TFET structure considering p-type channel in i-region after the gate-source overlap, which introduced tunneling. The performance of line tunneling improves SS and switching behavior of TFET. Reddick from Cambridge reported experimentally that TFET resolves the issue of down-scaling dimension as well as punch-through [21]. Zhang investigated [6] that SS of TFETs can be scaled down to less than 60 mV/dec., S. H. Kim fabricated a heterojunction TFET with Ge source having switching ratio in the order of 10³, and Sandow investigated the behavior of an ultra-thin SOI TFET for the variation in channel length, thickness of gate oxide, and doping in source/drain. They concluded that electrical parameters are strong function of doping concentration, with less dependence on gate length. The BTBT rate in TFET is enhanced by increasing the number of gate-like DG, TG, TFETs [7], which leads to enhanced ON current. Saurabh and Kumar have reported the electrical parameters of DMG TFET, where lower and higher material is placed near the source and drain regions. The DMG TFET boosts drain current, short channel, RF and analog performance compared to conventional TFET. ON current of TFET is improved by introducing tunneling along horizontal [23–24].

A compact analytical model for SOI TFET is reported in [25]. The parameters as threshold voltage, channel length, gate capacitance, drain current, SS transconductance, and output conductance have been modeled analytically. For surface potential and transfer characteristic in a particular gate (CG)-TFET is reported, and this model is validated by the TCAD simulator [14]. Likewise, the electrical parameters for triple material GAA TFET is modeled analytically, and it shows agreement with the simulator [13]. An analytical model for drain current in TFET considering SiGe layer source and channel region is presented in the literature [26]. This model is verified with a simulator for different molecule fraction, gate bias, and dielectric constant.

The ON current ($I_{ON}$) and SS of a TFET are enhanced using a high-k as a gate dielectric material, with the expense of degradation in leakage and ambipolar current [27]. K. Boucart [28] reported that the tunneling mechanism in a double gate TFET is improved by using high-k dielectrics at the tunnel junction. The electrical parameters of a gate dielectric TFET (HG-TFET) is highlighted in literature, and this device has SS and ON current compared to TFETs [17]. A HG-TFET with back gate is reported, and the subthreshold characteristic of this device is not affected by the scaling of the dimensions [18]. The research reported that ON current of TFET is not a function of back gate bias. The DC, RF/analog and linearity performance of junctionless HG-JT (JL HG-TFET) due to interface trap is compared with JL TFET [29]. Results reveal that HG engineering in TFET increases the transconductance and provides better linearity and lower distortion compared to JL TFET. The RF and linearity performance of hetero BOX BOX (HDB TFET) was investigated through a TCAD simulator, and results concludes that it has superior characteristics in transconductance, cut-off frequency, and distortion parameters [30].

S. Moerkaet al. have reported the HCE in TFET, and results show a large field at the tunnel junction, carrier heating [31]. In high-k based TFET was investigated experimentally in the presence of trap charge, and the transfer characteristic degraded compared to TFET with SiO2 as gate dielectric [32]. A dual functional n-TFET and p-type impact ionization FETs (p-IFETs) was designed using the ATLAS simulator [33]. The hot carrier injection (HCI) stress in TFET is studied in the drain region having $p/n$ junction [34], and analysis reveals that the device degrades due to the presence of an interfacial layer at the side.
