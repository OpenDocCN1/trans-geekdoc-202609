```
$I_{BTBT} \sim T_i \sim \exp(-4\lambda(2m^*)^{1/2} E_g^{3/2})/(3h(\Delta\phi + E_g)) \quad (1.6)$
```

Where $T_i$ is the band-to-band tunneling, $\lambda$ is tunnel barrier width, $m^*$ is the effective mass, $E_g$ is the band gap of silicon, $\Delta\phi$ is the range of energy across which tunneling takes, and $h$ is the Planck's constant. The three basic requirements for tunneling of electrons from available states are the (1) availability of states where electrons can tunnel, (2) a suitably narrow energy barrier, and (3) conservation of momentum. Due to this tunneling phenomenon, it is possible for TFET to realize $SS \le 60$ mV/dec for low gate voltages at room temperatures. The SS for TFET is given by equation (1.7) [48].

```
$SS = \ln(10) \frac{dV_{off}}{dV_{GS}} + \frac{F + b \ dF}{F^2} dV_{GS}} \quad (1.7)$
```

Equation 1.7 shows that SS for TFET does not have a unique value but is a function of $V_{GS}$. SS has the lowest value at the lowest $V_{GS}$ and increases with $V_{GS}$. The different values of SS are explained in Figure 1.18d. Point SS is the lowest value of the subthreshold slope at the $I_D-V_{GS}$ curve, and average SS is taken from where the drain current starts to increase from lowest value to the threshold voltage. ### 1.8.2 Advantages

The salient features of TFET are listed here.

- Low SS offered by TFET is the most distinguished advantage of these devices. This can significantly reduce the power consumption of the chip and can provide a boost in low-power applications.
- OFF-current produced by TFET can lessen the loss of leakage current incurred in modern devices. The OFF-current in TFET is due to leakage of p–i–n diode, which is significantly lower than the OFF-current in MOSFETs.
- TFET does not follow the same scaling rules as MOSFET, where the electric field must be constant in the device [49]. TFET offers better scalability than MOSFET, as the ON-state current does not depend on TFET length.

### 1.8.3 Drawbacks

There are some drawbacks or issues in these TFET devices, which are listed here.

- TFET shows ambipolar behavior, which means it can also conduct in the reverse direction or negative gate voltages. For negative gate voltages, the tunnel barrier between the drain and channel gets lowered, which facilitates the electrons tunneling through, shown in Figure 1.19. This is an inherent problem with TFETs.
- Apart from the benefits, TFET has low ON-current (~$10^{-6}$ A) compared to MOSFET (~$10^{-3}$ A). Many techniques, such as heterructure TFET, strain engineered TFET, high-k dielectrics, and carbon-based TFET [49], have been proposed, but there is still room for improvement.

- TFET also has two junctions, that is, drain-channel and source-channel junctions. A narrower tunnel barrier is required for larger ON-current in N-TFET. For a narrow tunnel barrier, the source doping should be high (~10¹²⁰ cm⁻³). Therefore, TFET is also affected from the problem of random dopant fluctuations and the fabrication of ultra-steep junctions at nanoscaled devices. The movement of impurity dopant atoms from drain and source regions to channel is another cause of concern.

## 1.9 JUNCTIONLESS TFET

As explained earlier, the presence of junctions in nanoscale TFETs are problematic. Their fabrication and consequences are a cause of concern. The idea of junctionless TFETs was proposed by Ghosh et al. in 2014 [50]. The junctionless TFET can be a very good alternative to continue the scaling trend in TFET. The working of JLTFET is explained in the next section.

### 1.9.1 Working

The structure of JLTFET is shown in Figure 1.20a. In N-JLTFET, the body is composed of highly doped (10¹⁰ cm⁻³) n-type material. The p-type source region is realized by an unbiased auxiliary gate of high work function, it is 5.93 eV, shown in Figure 1.20a. Due to the high work function of the auxiliary gate, the accumulation of holes takes under the auxiliary gate. The intrinsic channel region is generated by a gate (L_g = 20 nm) of work function 4.3 eV.

The gate and auxiliary gate are separated by a L_s = 5 nm. The drain and source contacts are on the sides. The carrier concentration in OFF-state is shown in Figure 1.20b, which shows the required barrier in p–i–n structure of TFET. When a positive voltage is applied, the carrier concentration in the channel is changed (Figure 1.20b). On the application of positive gate voltage, the barrier amid the channel and source region gets lowered, as shown in Figure 1.20c. The tunneling of electrons takes place between the valence band and the conduction band of the channel region. The I_D–V_GS curve of JLTFET for spacer thickness L_s = 5 nm between the gate and auxiliary gate is shown in Figure 1.20d. The I_D–V_GS curve of JLTFET shows the drawback of conventional TFET with chemical junctions shown in Figure 1.20e. Thus, JLTFET can provide the required tunneling phenomenon of TFET.

## 1.11 CHALLENGES IN JLTFET AND DLTFET

These JLTFETs and DLTs have shown the required tunneling phenomenon and features for TFET devices. These devices can solve the problems of TFTs with junctions and their fabrication. But they are not free from the inherent problems of TFTs. As all devices work in a similar way, they also face similar challenges. The challenges with these JLTFET and DLTFET are of the same nature as TFTs. Both JLTFETs and DLTFETs have low $I_{gs}$, like the conventional TFT. This problem of low $I_{gs}$ hampers their ability to couple with MOSFETs. Both JLTFETs and DLTFETs suffer from the problem of variability, which needs to be worked upon [52][53]. This phenomenon of variability leads to negative conduction in these devices.

### 1.11.1 Low ON-State Current

Many techniques have been proposed for the improvement of ON-state behavior of JLTFETs and DLTFETs, which are plasma-based TFTs. The aim of our work is for the ON-state improvement of plasma-based TFETs only. The techniques for chemically doped TFTs are not mentioned here, although the strategy for ON-state improvement comes for both chemically doped and plasma-based TFTs. The use of a dual material gate with lower work function electrode at the source to narrow the tunnel barrier in ON-state has been proposed [54]–56]. The use of hydroelectric with a high-k dielectric at the source also decreases the tunnel barrier in ON-state by enlarging the field due to high-k dielectric [57][58]. The conventional dielectric materials can be replaced by ferroelectric dielectric materials, which show negative capacitance. This negative capacitance dielectric boosted the performance of JLTFETs [59]. The application of low band-gap materials in heterojunction JLTFETs and DLTFETs, which narrow the tunnel barrier, has JLTFETs formed on an n' body, have further enhanced their performance [60]–62]. In these charge plasma-based JLTFETs there is an unavoidable spacer between gate and p/source electrodes. The presence of separate electrodes for channel and source regions rises due to this spacer, which widens the tunnel barrier in ON-state [18][50]. This spacer can be eliminated by the use of a p-type body, which enhances the device performance [55]. The formation of more abrupt tunneling junctions can also be achieved by the use of a metal layer in the oxide region in the middle source and gate electrodes. This also narrows the tunnel barrier in ON-state, thereby improving the device performance. [53][63][64].
