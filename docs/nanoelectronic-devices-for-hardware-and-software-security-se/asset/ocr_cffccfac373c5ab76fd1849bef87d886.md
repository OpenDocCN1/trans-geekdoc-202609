# 4.3 TAILORING THE ELECTRONIC/MAGNETIC PROPERTIES OF SILICENE AND GERMANENE

From the studies, it is evident that graphene, silicene and germanane are also predicted to have a semimetallic nature [65]. Thus, for any plausible switching device application, it is imperative to ensure the semiconducting behavior of these materials. Along the lines of graphene, researchers have focused on several ideas pertaining to the realization of semiconducting properties in these materials. Including the application of an external electric field, saturation of dangling bond through a different functionalization group, and introducing external domains. Apart from these, one-dimensional confinement of these materials (creating nanoribbons (GNRs), silicene and germanane also possess interesting and useful properties to them. Like graphene nan from a sheet in a particular direction, they are zigzag and armchair. It is revealed through these nanostructures have highly susceptible edges that can undergo reconstruction or functionalization that can modulate the properties of these nanostructures. Literature also suggests that these structures can play a vital role in modulating the properties of these nanostructures. Below are some crucial landmarks from the literature that suggest how key properties of silicene and germanane nanoribbons can be modulated for varied use of these materials.

Ding and Ni [89] have investigated the electronic and magnetic properties of silicon nanoribbons. Based on the first-principles calculation, they showed that armchair nanoribbons can exhibit a metallic or a semiconducting behavior based on the width of the nanoribbons. However, based on the energy state calculated, they demanded zigzag nanoribbons exhibit a magnetic ground state (ferromagnetic).
