Owing to its wide bandgap and the indirect tunneling mechanism, silicon attributes to low tunneling current in TFETs. As the silicon-based TFETs fail to meet the required ON-current for advanced technologies, another method of improving tunneling in TFET is to use a smaller bandgap material. This include using germanium, III-V semiconductors, carbon nanomaterials, and [28–31].nGermanium (Ge) is chemically identical to silicon (Si) material and is compatible with the current CMOS processes to a significant degree. In recent decades, the higher visibility and carrier mobility of germanium in contrast to silicon has drawn the interest of researchers. German possesses a bandgap of 0.66 eV, which makes it a suitable candidate for improving the TFET performances. $I_{ON}$ can be increased–2,700 times by implementing germanium in the channel of TFET instead of silicon-based method [32]. A silicon-germanium (SiGe) alloy in which the mole fraction is carefully estimated is also, as the effective bandgap in SiGe can be smaller than that of Si. Another way is to implement a heterojunction TFET in which germanium is employed at the channel while silicon is used at the channel region. The electron affinity differs from each other (4.0 eV for germanium and 4.05 eV for silicon), they form a strained (type-II) heterojunction. The energy band showing the junction profile for ON-state and OFF-state of the germanium/silicon heterojunction n-type TFET is shown in Figure 2.4.

In Figure 2.4, the effective energy barrier for tunneling is calculated to be 0.61 eV that, $E_g(Ge) - \Delta E_c$, which is somewhat lower than pure germanium. Furthermore, as mentioned before, the process of incorporating germanium in silicon technology is no issue, since in modern CMOS technologies, germanium is used to facilitate cause improved mobility in p-type transistors [33]. The mathematical expression for the tunneling barrier of type-II heterojunctions over the materials $x$ and $y$ is given by:

```
$E_{g,eff} = E_{g,x} - \Delta E_c = E_{g,y} - \Delta E_v \quad$ (2.28)
```

In strained silicon and germanium of 2% and -2.7%, respectively given on 42% of a silicon-germanium buffer can attain an effective bandgap of 122 million-electron volt. Apart from type-II heterojunctions, III-V materials such as indium arsenide (InAs), have a bandgap of 0.36 eV, in $In_{0.73}Ga_{As}$ and a bandgap of 0.74 eV for $In_{0.53}Ga_{0.47}As$ can be used to further enhance the performances of the TFET, depending on its application. These III-V material-based TFETs have shown great performance in both $I_{ON}$ and linear output, which have been proven theoretically and practically [34], [35]. Equation 2.28 can also be used to find other strained hetero-junctionunctions for TFETs applications.
