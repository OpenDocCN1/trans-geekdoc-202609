# 14.4 TYPES OF SEMICONDUCTOR PHOTODECTORS

A semiconductor photodetector is a device that absorbs the incident, that is, incoming photons, and converts it into electrical signals. This conversion mechanism is outlined in previous sections. To understand the development of photodetectors, it is essential to elaborate upon the major types of semiconductor photodetectors.

# 14.4.1 The Semiconductor p–n Single-Junction and p–i–n Dual-Junction Photodetectors

The semiconductor p–n junction and p–i–n junction are the semiconductor photodetectors without an internal gain mechanism, where a single electron-biased hole-pair (EHP) is generated per absorbed photon.

Figure 14.5a shows a reverse-biased highly p-doped (p*) and lightly n-doped (n*) p*-n-single-junction photodetector with diffusion and depletion regions. The depletion layer is created by negatively charged ions in the p-type material and positively charged donor atoms in the n-type material when carriers are swept to their majority under the built-in $E_{field}$. Therefore the width of depletion region depends upon the doping levels for reverse bias voltage (i.e., the lower the doping, the wider depletion region [9]). Thus, carriers are produced in both the diffusion and depletion regions. In the depletion region, the carriers are separated and drifted under the built-in $E_{field}$ while outside this region, the holes diffuse near the depletion region to be collected. Most of the incident photons are absorbed in the depletion region, the width of depletion region should be made as large as possible by reducing the doping in n-region.

The homojunction photodetector suffer from low quantum efficiency due to grading profile. For homojunction, the photodetectors with heterojunction are preferred, as they exhibit better quantum efficiency due to a more efficient $E_{field}$ [32], [34].
