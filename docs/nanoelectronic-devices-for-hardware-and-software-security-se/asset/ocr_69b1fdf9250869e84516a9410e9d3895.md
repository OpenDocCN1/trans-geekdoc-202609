Similar to graphene, in order to further modulate the bandgap, silicene and germanane can be further stripped off into 1D nanoribrows with definite edge states. Unlike two, nanoribrows (NR) are confined in two directions and are allowed to grow only along a third direction. Due to such confinement, these materials exhibit different and unique electronic properties compared to 2D form. Based on the edge states, the nomenclature follows armchair silicene/germanane nanoribrow (ASiNR/AGenR) and zigzag silicene/germanane nanoribrow (ZSiNR/ZGenR), as shown in Figure 4.1.

Similar to graphene nanoribrows, [50], these structures also have unique width-dependent electronic and magnetic properties based on the direction of cutting, the 2D sheet armchair or the zigzag edge states can be produced. There is a 30° cutting difference between ASiNR/AGenR and ZSiNR/ZGenR, respectively. In armchair, the edges are attached with only the two nearest neighboring atoms. Therefore, an additional unsaturated chemical bond (the dangling bond) is associated with each edge of the Si/Ge atom at both edges. The presence of these bonds at the ribbon edges provides a convenient way to alter the electronic properties and the stability of NRs through termination or the chemical edge modification [45, 51–53]. Further, like GNRs in the recent past, research pertaining to substitution within this SiNR/GeNRs has led to exhibition of some exceptional properties [54–57]. Further, these materials (specifically the ones having zero bandgaps) can also have prospective applications such as nanoscale interconnects [58–60]. Spintronics is another field in which precise utilization of the spin degree of freedom of an electron, rather than its electronic charge, serves as the fundamental quality for the flow of information [61] and being looked at as a promising low power alternative.
