# # 7.7.3 Optical PUFs

An optical PUF, which also has been called a one-way physical function, consists of a translucent substance is doped with particles that emit light. A spontaneous and unusual speckle pattern can appear as a laser beam shines upon the material. Placing the particles dispersing light is an unregulated mechanism, and the contact between the particles and the laser is very complicated. It is also tough to reproduce the optical PUF so that there would be an identical pattern configuration.

Pappu et al. [2] used a bubble-filled transparent epoxy wafer and a laser beam through, resulting in a pattern of interference reflection. Here the aim was to establish a one-way mechanism by practical means instead of neural theory. A two-dimensional spatter shape was created under lighting at a given viewpoint from intractable interference with an inhomogeneous transparent plastic token, captured by a charging-coupled device camera (Figure 7.14).
