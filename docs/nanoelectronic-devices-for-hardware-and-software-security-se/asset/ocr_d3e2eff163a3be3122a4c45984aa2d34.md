# 16.2.1 Electronic Properties of Graphene

The evolution of graphene makes scientists bring a rigorous approach in the development of electronic devices for various sensor applications. Nonlinear short channel effects cause major issues in the electron community. It was identified that 2D materials like graphene do not have short channel effects, even when the length of the channel is very small. Consequently, the 2D materials are more efficient than the Si material. The structure of graphene also has a basic reason for its attractive property. The three sp hybridized orbitals are distributed symmetrically with the nearest carbon atoms at an angle of 120°and form the sigma bond, as shown in Figure 16.2a, b, and c. This sigma bond makes the strongest material. Near the Dirac point, graphene shows a linear dispersion relation. The conduction band and the valence band met at the Dirac point where the energy band is symmetric and the band density at the Fermi level is zero. Graphene is one atom thick and massless; hence the motion of an electron is comparable to light.ed has an interesting feature that it does not have backscattering like a carbon nanotube. It increases carrier mobility in graphene. Therefore, high mobility is applicable for pristine samples to reduce the scattering centers. Hence, carrier transport in the material is completely ballistic. High mobility and high velocity in graphene are promised for high frequency digital and analog circuits. Hence, graphene became a promising material for all the FET to apply in any applications.
