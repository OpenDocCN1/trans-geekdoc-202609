
## 15.4.2 Three Terminal Junction (TTJ)

Similar to the ballistic rectifier, another device called the TTJ based on ballistic effect was proposed by H. Q. Xu in 2001, with two input terminals (left and right) and one output terminal [52]. In TTJ, one of the output terminals of FTBR has been omitted to study rectification effects. Also, theoretical formulation relates the output current at the central terminal with the transmission probability of all three terminals, which can further depend on electrochemical potentials. The device can be realized in symmetric and asymmetric configurations, and output has been predicted to be varying quadratically with the applied input voltage [52].

In another experiment, the rectified output of GaAs/AlGaAs heterostructure-based YBJ was studied for different gate voltages at 4.2 K [62]. The TTJ was fabricated on a high mobility InGaAs/InP heterostructure, as shown in Figure 15.8a [57], which proved the rectified output and coefficients up to 200 K, which was further validated by mathematical modeling [52]. The rectified output of the device increased with the decrease in gate voltage and demonstrated a good correlation with the quadratic curve fit, as demonstrated in Figure 15.8 [57].

The TTJ has operating capability up to high frequency (10 GHz) and room temperature [82]. The parasitic capacitance limits the high frequency output of TTJ, which can be further improved ten times by separating the junctions to decrease capacitance. The TTJ has been demonstrated up to 1 GHz with the second harmonic generation up to 300 GHz [58].

Furthermore, the TTJ can also be combined with devices of its class such as FET to rectify rectified response [82], [87]. The more complex digital operations, for half-adder, have been demonstrated using rectified effects in YBJ [59], [60]. In addition, TTJ can also be worked as a diode and triode depending upon the voltage applied between the input terminals [61]. The digital operation such as the SR latch has been demonstrated in TTJ, utilizing two TTJs and in-plane gate terminals to measure the output. The single step lithography process was used to design the latch with the CMOS devices [75].

As to the conventional PN diode, the mentioned applications can work up to THz frequencies due to the ballistic nature of the device. The TBJ demonstrates a nonlinear rectification characteristic, but the device geometry also showed rectification for long branches exhibiting diffusive transport. Under low bias conditions, the device works in the parabolic region contrast to the linear region for high biases [56]. The high frequency response of YBJ up to 40 GHz was demonstrated using YBJ, with minimized input impedance [63]. The carbon nanotube (CNT) has also been used to design the TBJ to generate rectified effects [65].

The high frequency RF response of YBJ has been simulated at room temperature using the ATLAS simulator. The device demonstrates the decrease in the rectification with the increase in frequency from 2 to 20 GHz [53]. The Hf to DC response of YBJ was also demonstrated up to 50 GHz at 77 K [66]. The degradation at higher frequencies was also due to interconnect feeding losses. The high frequency response of YBJ was theoretically reported up to 94 GHz by Bednarz et al. [85]. Two YJs were connected to minimize the impedance of the complete device. Furthermore, the transistor characteristic of TJJ was used [64], where the center output terminal was used as a gate. The current and voltage gain of 60 and -30 with a cutoff of 4 GHz was reported for signal processing and sensing application [64].

The nonlinear rectification of TJJ has also been observed for larger size with low mobility materials. The TJJ with Schottky gates was used to control the two input nodes. The wraps controlled the nonlinearity in the output and changed the curvature of rectified response for the transistor-like characteristics [74], [76]. The In-based yjs were also utilized as TJJ, showing a tunable nonlinear electrical characteristic with frequency mixing and phase detection operation [71].

The NOR gate device has been produced using three TJJs [83]; one of the TJJs was used as a load, and the other was also used as the input to the circuit. Other applications such as frequency mixing and phase detection have been demonstrated [73]. The high frequency mixing operation is useful in the modulation process, where the carrier signal and message signal can be mixed to transmit the modulated signal [85]. Further, a phase detection process is quite useful in telecommunications, where it works as an essential part of the phase locked loop. In [85], double TJJ has been operated up to 90 GHz practically up to THz frequency. The TH response of a TJJ is limited due to the presence of intrinsic impedance [72].

The physical dimensions, temperature, and mean free path of charge carriers largely affect the rectification of TJJ. Ballistic effects are responsible for rectification in small size TJJs, as the device dimensions increase when compared to the mean free path, the shifting from ballistic to quasi-ballistic or diffusive region does not affect the rectifier output due to its novel geometry [70].

The high mobility InAS/AlGaSb was further utilized for the TJJ to work at 20 and 77 K. Due to a large mean free path (430 nm to 1.5 um from 300 to 77 K), the device generated the rectified nonlinear response [86]. Furthermore, the transistor-like characteristic was generated from TJJ with maximum conductance of 100 µA/V and gain up to 1.5 GHz.

The fabrication facilities for nano-devices are before real fabrication; therefore, it is better to use a simulation for designing and optimization of the nanostructures before real fabrication. III-V material based structure such as TJJ and YBJ in although FTBR has been simulated using Monte-Carlo and semiclassical methods [53], [78], [79], [85]. Monte Carlo considers the classical nature of the device rather than the quantum behavior [77], [79]. These devices were simulated in 2d by performing the separate simulation for front view and top view. The front view of the device contains all the layers of the heterostructure, which is used to evaluate the carrier density and mobility of electrons, and the top view contains the air-channel (air-InGaAs) combination of the device. For compensating the effect of other layers, a net background doping and surface charge density have been assigned to the air-channel region. The simulated result confirms the TH response of TJJ and FTBR at temperatures [79], [85], [89]. However, to consider the real device structure, there is a need to consider the 3D geometry of the nano-rectifiers [79], [89].
