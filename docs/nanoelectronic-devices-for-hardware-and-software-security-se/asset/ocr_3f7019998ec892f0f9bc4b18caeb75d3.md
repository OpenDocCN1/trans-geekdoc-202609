Here, two signals scatter down similar paths to the end of the chain, providing an electrical pulse as an activating signal. The signal propagates through a stage via the crossed paths if the input to the challenge is set to one. Then it uses direct routes. Though the cumulative delays for natural paths and crossed paths are identical, the propagation gap on one of the tracks can be longer or shorter due to chip limitations. Therefore, problems arise in varying delays in propagation at the last stage outputs.

An arbiter PUF’s primary physical protection is predicated on the assumption that an attacker cannot calculate the internal details inside the Arbiter PUF stages without breaking the PUF itself, modifying its challenge-response conduct. The intruder can only attempt attacks from an infinite space in this scenario and observe the respective answers.

For the authentication scenario, attractive candidates are the arbiter PUF circuits because their comparatively broad CRP are not safe from machine learning (ML) attacks and demonstrated vulnerability to such attacks. The arbiter PUF, based on the XOR logic function, was implemented as a defense to inhibit the efficacy of ML attacks. The XOR arbiter PUF has k parallel arbiter chains, each with an N stage and an arbiter. See Figure 7.11. XOR-ing the responses of all individual arbiter chains produces the combined response. Two linear arbiter PUFs can be XOR-mixed to ensure improved statistical properties of an arbiter PUF. [30]
