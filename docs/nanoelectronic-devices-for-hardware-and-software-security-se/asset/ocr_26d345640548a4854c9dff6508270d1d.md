# 14.5.1.2 Photosensitivity and Linear Dynamic Range

The photosensitivity is defined as the ratio of the photocurrent density $J_{light}$ at a desired wavelength to the dark current density $J_{dark}$. The generated $J_{light}$ and photosensitivity of the device must be larger to achieve high-performance photodetector.
Linear dynamic range (LDR) defines the input illumination intensity range within which the photocurrent of a photodetector varies linearly with the intensity and is approximated in a logarithmic scale as [66]:

```
$LDR(dB) = 20\log(\frac{J_{light}}{J_{dark}})$
```

A high value of the LDR is preferable to detect both weak and strong light.

# 14.5.1.3 Photoswitch Time

The photoswitch time measures the response speed of a photodetector and is characterized by the rise time and fall time. The rise time (or response time, $	au_r$) and fall time (or recovery time, $	au_f$) are measured at 10% and 90% of maximum $J_{light}$ and are strongly influenced by the charge transport and collection mechanism during the photost. A rapid photoswitch response, that is, smaller values of rise and fall times, is desirable for the high-performance photodetector.

# 14.5.2 Optical Parameters

# 14.5.2.1 Spectral Photoresponse

For any photodetectors, they can only respond to a specific wavelength range. When designing a photodetector, the first consideration is choosing the proper photosensitive material that can respond to the photo signal. To detect the photo signal, the bandgap of the selected material should be smaller or equal to the incident photon energy.
