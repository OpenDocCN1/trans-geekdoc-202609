The Synopsis technology-aided design simulator (TCAD) is utilized in performing these simulations [51]. The physics models activated are as follows: Fermi-Dirac statistics, bandgap narrowing with the Old-Slobook model, Mosetti-model, Shockley-Read recombination, and a dynamic nonlocal BTBT model where the values of coefficients are properly calibrated with experimental data [44]. Here the parameters of silicon are calibrated as $A=3.29 \times 10^{12}/cm^2$ sec and $B=23.8 \times 10^9$ V/cm and germanium parameters are $A=1.67 \times 10^{12}/cm^2$ sec and $B=6.55 \times 10^9$ V/cm, respectively.

It is observed that the channel under-cut length plays a vital role in optimizing the TFT performance in terms of its subthreshold slope, which is a crucial device parameter. However, the ON-state current is not affected by varying the channel under-cut length but it has a major effect on $I_{OFF}$. This leads to a well-improved subthreshold slope and a lower $I_{OFF}$ at a channel under-cut length of 15 nm. As a drive voltage is applied in the OFF-state, the channel/drain potential is reduced. Accordingly, as the channel under-cut length increases, the influence of the drain region is reduced. This decreases the electron BTB generation rate in the OFF-state. Hence, an improvement in $I_{OFF}$ from $2.40 \times 10^{-10} A/mu m$ to $2.77 \times 10^{-14} A/mu m$ is achieved for a channel under-cut length from 0 to 15 nm, respectively. This leads to a huge increment in $I_{OFF}$ up to four orders of magnitude.
