# 9.2 DESIGN OF SOA–MZI-BASED COMMUNICATION LINK

The block diagram of the proposed system is given in Figure 9.1. It consists of a transmitter, all-optical XOR gate, and receiver. An XOR operation is achieved with the XPM technique [28]. Choice of operating wavelength and source defines the wavelength of pump and the probe signal at 10 Gbps is 1552.52 nm and 1558 nm, respectively. Two data signals are generated by a pseudorandom generator, and 80 ps delay is used to generate other data input. Mach–Zehnder modulator is used to modulate the data and carrier signals. Modulated output is transmitted through an XOR gate to generate encrypted output. Message security is enhanced with encryption, which uses right key in both encryption and decryption process. In encryption, XOR logic is widely used because of its advantages such as computational speed, in particular, hardware feasibility, and commutative and associative property. An all-optical XOR gate is the basic block of the encryption process in the proposed system, and it is used for secure data transmission in support of data hiding when no particular security is required. To achieve all-optical XOR operations, SOA-MZI configuration is used by varying the carrier intensity of the pump signal. Optimized phase shift of π occurs when the lower probe signal and high frequency pulse signal travels along the upper and lower arms of the SOA-MZI configuration, which in turn leads to XOR operation.

The Boolean of XOR logic gives 1 at the output when nonuniform input data combines at SOA (combination of A=0, B=1, and A=1, B=0). On the other hand, when both inputs are the same, (combination of A=1, B=1, and A=0, B=0), logic output turns into 0; 1 logic is represented by the presence of optical pulses, and 0 logic is represented by an absence of optical pulse at the receiver output.
