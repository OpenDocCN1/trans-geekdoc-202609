To gauge their performances toward interconnect applications, a two-probe analysis has been performed to calculate transmission channel ($N_{ch}$), kinetic inductance ($L_K$), and quantum capacitance ($C_Q$) for considered structures. The quantum resistance can then be modeled by the equation shown below [58]:

```
$R_Q = rac{h}{G_0} \frac{1}{2q^2 N_{ch}} \quad (4.2)$
```

Here, $R_Q$ is defined as the quantum resistance for small dimensions, $N_{ch}$ defines the number inversely with the transmission channels. Equation 4.2 highlights that quantum resistance varies inversely with the number of transmitting channels, $N_{ch}$. To comment on the value of $N_{ch}$ transmission eigenvalues are then calculate. Transmission eigen represents the probability (or the possibility) with which the carrier (electron wave) can pass through the central region; higher probability indicates less scattering and a more smooth flow of carriers. Transmission mode/$N_s$ around the Fermi level is then calculated, and the results are tabulated in Tabulated Table 4.2. Tabulated values show that only O-H-AGenR-H-H and O-H-AGenR-O exhibit non-zero values of $N_{ch}$ ($N_{ch}=1$ and 2, respectively). Similar analysis can be performed for the zigzag couplers, the tabulated values suggest that $R_Q$ will be more or less than the value of $N_s$ and is the same for all the three cases. However, a detailed analysis of the eigen spectrum shows that due to $c$-symmetry (which is at the edges of) case. O-ZGenR-O, the channel tends to offer smooth flow of carrier (or less scattering) in this case. It is mentioned here that this symmetry is a strong function of bias voltage, and it collapses with the increase in bias voltage and inductance. $L_K = \frac{h}{4q^2 v_f} N_{ch} \quad (4.3)$
