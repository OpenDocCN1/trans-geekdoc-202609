## 2.6 LATERAL AND VERTICAL TFETs

Over the past years, researchers have developed novel TFET architectures to improve performance [38–41]. As the TFET operating mechanism is based on band-to-band tunneling (BTBT), depending on the design of the TFET structure, there are two distinct types of tunneling modes that can be employed. The first one is lateral tunneling, where, due to the application of a positive bias to the gate, the band starts to bend, and the electrons are injected from the source region into the gate region, primarily in a direction parallel to the semiconductor/gate dielectric interface. Lateral tunneling is sometimes stated as point tunneling [42] which is the fundamental BTBT mechanism and operating mode for a typical conventional TFET. Even though there is no definition of vertical TFET, the convention adopted here is that a device is termed a vertical tunnel FET if the electron BTBT direction is predominantly associated with the direction of the electric field of the gate. The source region should be overlapped by the gate. As the direction of gate electric field and the electron tunneling are in the same direction or alignment,

vertical tunneling is also referred to as line tunneling. In a vertical-based TFET, the structural dimensions and arrangements play a crucial role determining the tunneling path length. The depletion width can also be lowered effectively. The gate source overlap region can also determine the cross-section area of the tunneling junction. Due to this, line tunneling TFETs can attain a higher ON current of 2-3 orders of magnitude and a steeper subthreshold slope than tunnel TFETs. Another advantage is that Vertical TFETs are also more prone to leakage currents compared to Lateral TFETs [43], [44]. However, TFET based on lateral tunneling gives an advantage when it comes to fabrication of the device. Compared to the lateral CMOS process, a flow as well as a inverse ion implantation for the source region and drain region is the only additional step necessary. However, considering the effect of physical parameters on electrical characteristics, the designing window decreases substantially. Another way of designing the TFET is to implement a structure that employs both line and point tunneling. This can offer such high ON current by partially overlapping the gate and the source region. Through the configuration, the optimization of the overlap region should be precise. The basic TFET structures employing line tunneling, point tunneling, and both + line tunneling with the transfer characteristics are depicted in Figure 2.7 [45]. It should be noted that the central point of the current curve on the transfer characteristic can be shifted to the left by using a lower metal gate work function. The reduction of gate work function enhances the tunneling of carriers in the source/channel region and reduces the tunneling in the drain/channel region.

## 2.7 VARIOUS VERTICAL TFET ARCHITECTURES

In this section, various TFETs utilizing vertical tunneling or both vertical and lateral tunneling are presented. The device structures are designed and optimized to give the optimum TFET performance using different methods, including the techniques discussed in the previous sections.

### 2.7.1 Germanium Source with Overlap TFET

A germanium TFET employing both line and point tunneling is shown in Figure 2.8a. A gate sidewall spacer of silicon nitride (Si$_4$) of 8 nm is used. An oxide thickness of 3 nm is used. The thickness of the silicon body is 70 nm, while the germanium source, which the gate region overlaps, is of 21 nm. In the On-state, an inversion surface layered electrons can be tunneled through the junction to the inverted layer of the germanium source to supply electrons and pass through the silicon channel region. The gate overlaps the source region, and this region is not sufficiently doped to allow the region to be inverted for acceptable gate voltages. In addition, appropriate functionality of transistor ensures the silicon body gate inversion threshold voltage is less than that of TBTBT, whose drain voltage could be coupled to the germanium source potential, splitting quasi-Fermi levels to activate TBTBT. At this point, the device possesses to an off-state leakage current of 0.1 pA/µm for gate bias of 0.5 V and an ON current of 0.4 µA/µm. Hence an ON/OFF current ratio of the order $10^6$ at a low voltage operation of 0.5 V is achieved for the germanium source with overlap TFET [46].

### 2.7.3 Heterojunction TFET with T-Shaped Gate

The heterojunction TFET with a T-shaped gate (HTG-TFET) employs the recess path at the substrate to change point tunneling into line perpendicular to the p- silicon region that enhances the tunneling region and $I_{ON}$. The structure uses two source – source and source2 – maximize the tunneling, the region drain is situated at the bottom of the transistor to minimize the gate to drain capacitances. As the metal gate looks like the letter T, the TFET is named after it. The T-shaped gate region is overlapped with n+ pocket in both directions, vertically and laterally. a silicon germanium (SiGe) alloy is implemented in the pocket region, as opposed to silicon.
