# 2.6 LATERAL AND VERTICAL TFETs

Over the past years, researchers have developed novel TFET architectures to improve performance [38–41]. As the TFET operating mechanism is based on band-to-band tunneling (BTBT), depending on the design of the TFET structure, there are two distinct types of tunneling modes that can be employed. The first one is lateral tunneling, where, due to the application of a positive bias to the gate, the band starts to bend, and the electrons are injected from the source region into the gate region, primarily in a direction parallel to the semiconductor/gate dielectric interface. Lateral tunneling is sometimes stated as point tunneling [42] which is the fundamental BTBT mechanism and operating mode for a typical conventional TFET. Even though there is no definition of vertical TFET, the convention adopted here is that a device is termed a vertical tunnel FET if the electron BTBT direction is predominantly associated with the direction of the electric field of the gate.
