# 15.4 BACKGROUND

a number nanoelectronic devices are available with dimensions smaller than the mean free path of charge carriers. The ballistic transport of charge carriers is dominant in the active region of the device. The ballistic transport governs the elastic travel of carriers within the channel region of the device without losing any energy. In contrast, scattering takes places only at the device boundary, which results in heat dissipation through the electrical contacts [141]. During the last two decades, ballistic transport has been well explored in nanoelectronic devices at room temperature and high frequency.

The phase coherence in a four terminal conductor was conducted by Büttiker in 1986 [142]. The electrochemical potential and transmission probability and resistance between leads of the conductor have been used to form the current in the output leads. In previous studies, which the voltage drop has been seen over a distance comparable to the mean free path of carriers without considering the phase coherence, [14], four terminal resistance has been developed to relate the symmetry between voltage and current at the input and output leads. Similarly, experiments have been performed on QPC to study the quantum phenomenon [30]. The QPC shows the conductance plateaus at lower temperature due to comparable mean free path, and with the increase of temperature and magnetic field, the quantized conductance behavior includes Hirayama. Hirayama and Tarucha in 1993 reported the room temperature ballistic transport in the four terminal device from AlGaAs/InGaAs/GaAs materials in Figure 15.5 [143]. The bending resistance of junction-junction has the negative characteristic obtained in response to the applied field at different temperatures ranging from 1.5 K to 290 K. The result clearly demonstrates higher carrier mobility at low temperature (6.5 × 10 cm²/Vs as compared to below 35 K) (7.8 × 10³ cm²/Vs vs above 70 K), which suggests that the mean free path is more abundant at lower temperatures.
