# 14.5.3 Noise

Noise refers to spontaneous fluctuations in the current or voltage passing through the semiconductor devices. the shot (quantum) and Johnson-Nyquist (thermal) noise [69] are major sources of noise in the photodetector. Shot noise occurs due to the discrete behavior of charge carriers that contribute to current flow in the devices. On the other hand, thermal noise occurs due to the random thermal motion of the charge carriers. It is also known as white noise due to the excess of the same level at all frequencies [65]. Therefore, the shot ($i_i$) and Johnson-Nyquist ($i_j$) noise currents are estimated by [64].

```
$i_i = \sqrt{2q(J_{\text{light}}+J_{\text{dark}})\Delta f}$ (14.9)
```

```
$i_j = \sqrt{\frac{4KT}{(RA)_{\text{NET}}}}$ (14.10)
```

The total noise current ($i_n$) [69] and signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) [9], [64] are given by

```
$i_n = i_i + i_j$ (14.11)
```

```
$SNR = 10\log \left( \frac{1(Q_{\text{ext}}P_n/\lambda/1.24)}{i_n} \right) dB$ (14.12)
```

In general an ideal high-performance photodetector should possess low dark current density, high sensitivity, high quantum efficiency, high detection speed, high selectivity, high responsiveness, low noise equivalent power, standby power consumption, low noise level, and applied voltage-bias requirements [15].
