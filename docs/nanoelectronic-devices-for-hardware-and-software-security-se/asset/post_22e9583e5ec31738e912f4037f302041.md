## 7.6.6 Nanotechnology-Based PUF

Today, almost all designs of PUF concentrate on the utilization of random fluctuations of process parameters inherent to CMOS technology. Substantial improvement has been made recently in nanoelectronics devices [32], particularly for nano-elements like CNFETs and resistive RAM, spin-torque magnetic random-access memory (STT-MRAM), etc. These innovations are evolving for memory construction, using other storage mechanisms, such as magnetism and resistance, of maintaining the load on the capacitor, such as flash memory, dynamic RAM, and static RAM [31, 33].

## 7.7 A DETAILED STUDY OF SOME POPULAR PUFS

After understanding the various attack scenarios and gone through a handful of PUF designs, we are ready to get deeper. We provide a detailed overview of some popular PUF designs.

### 7.7.1 Arbiter PUFs

A VLSI (very large scale integration) design-based APUF [4] was introduced in 2002 (Figure 7.9). The PUF design elects fabrication randomness in random time delays and interconnects for the gate transistors. This PUF design is uncompressed and possesses an extensive CRP space. However, the APUF is vulnerable to multiple simulation attacks, unlike its counterpart, because of its linear additive structure, the optical PUF. Here an intruder used documented CRP to find an incorrect CRP behavior model [34]. While the new APUF variants [35, 36] can withstand numerous modeling threads, the PUF security race is unlikely to end. In other words, there might be other modeling methods of attack that could degrade or bypass the protection of the new proposed APF variants.

Lim et al. [37] implemented PUFs that use a distinctive structure and, additionally, an arbiter to differentiate the gap in the delay among the directions. The specific design of the PUF system makes it possible for these PUFs to be more resilient to surrounding conditions. The same has also implemented in FPGA. The APUF is the primary type of delay-based PUF but requires careful circuit layout. For example, manual tuning of delay wires is needed when APUF is implemented on field-programmable gate array (FPGA) platforms to minimize response.

### 7.7.3 Optical PUFs

An optical PUF, which is also called a one-way physical function, consists of a material doped with particles. Pappu et al. [2] used a translucent epoxy to create a 2D spatter pattern which was captured by camera. The angle is treated like the optical PUF’s question (input), while the speckle configuration is treated as the solution (output).
