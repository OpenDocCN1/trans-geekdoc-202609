Among photonic devices, photodetector holds a critical position and found a variety of consumer and scientific applications. The major issue of a photodetector is its compatibility with CMOS technology to provide low power dissipation, good noise immunity, high performance, and better reliability. To attain high-performance and CMOS-compatible technology, a suitable semiconductor material must be selected. The semiconductor materials used in a photodetector define many of its critical properties. Both the light wavelength to which it responds and the noise level are crucial parameters that depend on the material used in the photodetector. The wavelength sensitivity of various materials happens when only photons having enough energy to excite an electron across the energy bandgap of the semiconductor material will generate significant energy in producing the current from the photodetector.

Si-based photodetectors [9], [16–18] show their better performance in the ultraviolet (UV) and visible spectral regime with sufficient speed, low dark current, moderate conductivity, and long-term stability. The use of a Si-based photodetector is commercially available and is restricted to the first-generation spectrum due to an indirect energy bandgap of Si (−1.12 eV), which gives a response above 1,100 nm. Therefore, in the spectral regime, 1,100 to 1,600 nm (second-generation systems) research is dedicated to semiconductor materials with narrow bandgaps. This motivates the integration of Si with Ge [10], III–V [11], [16], [19], and II–VI [12] compounds that provide a better response in the wider spectral regime. The performance of such devices has been further enhanced in recent years, and a selection of photodetectors is now commercially.

In addition to the fabrication of sophisticated photodetector architectures developed from III–V conductor materials at 1,300 and 1,550 nm wavelengths, material systems are investigation for longer wavelengths optimized for infrared (IR) transmission (700 to 30,000 nm). In terms of material properties, mercury cadmium telluride (Hg; HgCd,Te) is one of the most favorable materials for IR photodetection due to its adjustable bandgap, high photon absorption, high lattice matching, and moderate permittivity that confines small device capacitance and a low thermal expansion coefficient to give device stability [15], [26]. Other semiconductor materials, namely heterojunction photodetector types and phototransistor, can also be developed from III–V alloy semiconductor materials. Particularly the photoconduct photodetector is suitable as a potential photodetector in the spectral regime of 1,100 to 16,000 nm.
