### 14.1 Introduction

Over the past decades, nanotechnology has significantly influenced the way the miniaturization of integrated circuits (ICs) has opened up opportunities. This was predicted by R. Feynman, who stated "There is plenty of room at the bottom" [1], and by G. Moore, who stated "In two years, the number of transistors doubles" [2].

Conversely, the field of nanotechnology has grown exponentially since the 1980s. The rapid development of nanotechnology has become a crucial reason for the industry. High-performance products have become a significant condition for many industries. Because of its interdisciplinary character, nanotechnology finds its wide use in the areas of life sciences, medicine, information technology, chemistry, materials, energy, environmental engineering, space, data storage, and automotive [3], [4].

Long-distance communication can be carried out in the 1,300–1,550 nm spectral region, which corresponds to the least optical loss for silica doped optical fibers [5]. Up to now, various optical devices such as optical modulators [6], light-emitting sources [6], and other components [7] have been developed in this spectral regime.

Although much effort has been made in all-optical fiber communication systems, in optical communication, system signals are not processed in the optical form and require efficient conversion to electrical signals to be processed by electronic circuits. The theory of optoelectronic and photonic devices first began in the late 1980s, and has since become one of the most intensive research areas driven by the rapid growth of communications [8]. Among photonic devices, a photodetector is a receiver unit which is employed at the end of the path to convert an optical signal into an electrical signal. The quality of the conversion will greatly affect the performance of the system. Photodetectors hold a critical position in photonic devices, have a wide variety of consumer and scientific applications, and a dominant role in the growth of modern physics.
