To overcome these barriers, researchers have proposed a device that operates on the band-to-band tunneling (BTBT) principle, which is popularly identified as a TFET (TFET) [4–5]. It is promising from a low power perspective due to thermal SS (≤60 mV/dec) as well as low value of leakage current [6]. However, one major limitation of it is the lower value of the ON current. The performance of TFET is further enhanced by various techniques like gate engineering, structural engineering, dielectric engineering, and many. The various architectures proposed are double gate (DG), single gate (TG), dual material gate (DMG), circular gate (CG), heterojunction, gate-all-around (GAA), dielectric gate, and undergate TFETs [7–14]. In terms of channel engineering, the materials used as channel material to improve the performance of TFET [15–16]. Researchers have proposed a hetero-heterate dielectric TFET (HG-TFET), and this device has improved SS and ON current compared to TFETs with SiO₂ as a gate dielectric [17]. Mitra have included a back gate in HG-TFET, and the effectiveness of back gate electronics characteristics is highlighted [18].

# 10.2 LITERATURE SURVEY

The first p–i–n structure, which is popularly known as TFET, was proposed in 1978 at Brown University, and the effectiveness of TFET for spectroscopy was advised [19]. Banerjee el have reported a TFET structure considering p-type channel in i-region after the gate [20], and they have considered gate-source overlap in this structure, which introduced a line tunneling. The performance of line tunneling improves the SS and switching behavior of TFET. Reddick from Cambridge reported experimentally that TFET resolves the issue of the down-scaling dimension of MOSFET as well as punch-through [21].

Zhang el [6] investigated that SS of TFETs can be scaled down to less than 60 mV/ dec., and thus S. H. Kim fabricated a heterojunction TFET with Ge source having a switching ratio in the order of 10³, and device Sandow el investigated the behavior of an ultra-thin SOI TFET for the variation in channel length, thickness of gate oxide, and specification of doping in source/drain [22]. They have concluded that electrical parameters are strong function thickness and doping concentration, with less dependence on gate length. The BTBT rate in TFET is enhanced by increasing the number of gate-like DG, TG, TFETs [7], which leads to enhanced ON current. Saurabh and Kumar have reported the electrical parameters of DMG TFET, where lower and higher function material is placed near the source and drain regions, [9]. The DMG TFET boosts drain current, short channel, RF and analog performance compared to conventional TFET. ON current of TFET is improved by introducing line tunneling along horizontal tunneling [23–24].

A compact analytical model for SOI TFET is reported in [25].
