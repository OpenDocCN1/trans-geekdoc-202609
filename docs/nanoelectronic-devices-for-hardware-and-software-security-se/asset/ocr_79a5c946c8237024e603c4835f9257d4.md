# 3.1.4 Gate Dielectric Methods

# 3.1.4.1 High-k Dielectric-Material Gate Dielectric

The high-k dielectric constant material is used to significantly reduce the leakage current and gate tunneling with low gate oxide thickness. In most of the semiconductor fabrication industry, a substitute to the SiO2 will be replaced by a material with a high-k dielectric constant like HfO₂, with the value of k=21. The combination of low dielectric k with high dielectric k stacking is a famous method that easily scales the device without actually influencing the device performance. The equivalent oxide thickness (EOT) can be calculated by using equation 3.1. This will calculate the actual thickness of oxide for low dielectric (t_{ot1}) with high-k dielectric (t_{ot2}) [24].

```
EOT = t_{ot1} + \frac{\epsilon_{r1}}{\epsilon_{r2}}t_{ot2} (3.1)
```

Where $\epsilon_{r1}$ is absolute permittivity for SiO2 and $\epsilon_{r2}$ is absolute permittivity for another high-k-dielectric constant.

# 3.1.4.2 Metal Gate

High-k dielectric stacking is an efficient method of reducing the leakage current; loss of mobility takes place to a large degree with the use of a high-k dielectric. The metal gate is established for providing protection against mobility degradation. Work function variation occurs due to the inconsistency of different gate dielectric alignments in the band [25].

# 3.1.4.3 Multiple Gates

Multiple gates are an effective technique used for THE reduction of SCE for a small scale dimension. It provides good control over the channel, which prevents the device form SCE and improves the device's ability [26]. Among all structures, cylindrical structure that surround the channel will provide a great pathway from charge carriers. Due to introduction of the tri-gate MOSFETs, fabrication is now feasible up to 22 nm. It is surrounded by three sides to provide high controllability over the channel at very low dimensions. This will also improve the drain current to suppression of the leakage current.
