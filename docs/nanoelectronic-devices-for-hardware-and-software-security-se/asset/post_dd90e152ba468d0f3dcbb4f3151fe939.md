## 11.10 ENERGY DISSATION

The Hamiltonian matrix is used to calculate total energy and power in an array of QCA cells. Hence, energy dissipation of a QCA cell in one cycle, $T$, is derived with respect to Hamiltonian and coherence vectors as described in equation 11.3.

$$E_{diss} = -\frac{h}{2} \int_{-T}^{T} \dot{\Gamma} \cdot \frac{d\vec{\lambda}}{dt} dt$$

This tool estimates the dissipated energy of whole circuit for each input combinations under non-adiabatic switching. The upper bound power dissipation is shown in equation 11.4.

$$P_{diss} = \frac{E_{diss}}{T} \left( \tanh\left(\frac{h\Gamma_+}{k_B T}\right) - \tanh\left(\frac{h\Gamma_-}{k_B T}\right) \right)$$

In Table 11.7 and Table 11.8, we compare the average energy dissipation, average switching dissipation, and average energy dissipation of priority encoder structure and the proposed priority encoder; the same for the 4 to 2 priority encoder and the proposed 4 to 2 encoder. It shows various dissipation levels, and from the results it is concluded that both priority encoder and the 4 to 2 priority encoder dissipate less energy as with previous structures.

## 11.11 CONCLUSION

Two inferences can be taken from those simulations. First, using the aforementioned universal gate logic, the number of cells and area needed to construct different encoder circuits is drastically reduced without compromising the effectiveness. The circuits therefore get better compared with other designs, as seen in Tables 11.7 and 11.8. The potential energy of the encoder circuits was measured using standard methods for various input combinations and is rounded off to their nearest possible values.
