## 14.5.3 Noise

Noise refers to spontaneous fluctuations in the current or voltage passing through the semiconductor devices. The shot (quantum) and Johnson-Nyquist (thermal) noise [69] are major sources of noise in the photodetector. Shot noise occurs due to the discrete behavior of charge carriers that contribute to current flow in the devices. On the other hand, thermal noise occurs due to the random thermal motion of the charge carriers. It is also known as white noise due to the excess of the same level at all frequencies [65]. Therefore, the shot ($i_i$) and Johnson-Nyquist ($i_j$) noise currents are estimated by [64].

$i_i = \sqrt{2q(J_{\text{light}}+J_{\text{dark}})\Delta f}$ (14.9)

$i_j = \sqrt{\frac{4KT}{(RA)_{\text{NET}}}}$ (14.10)

The total noise current ($i_n$) [69] and signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) [9], [64] are given by

$i_n = i_i + i_j$ (14.11)

$SNR = 10\log \left( \frac{1(Q_{\text{ext}}P_n/\lambda/1.24)}{i_n} \right) dB$ (14.12)

In general an ideal high-performance photodetector should possess low dark current density, high sensitivity, high quantum efficiency, high detection speed, high selectivity, high responsiveness, low noise equivalent power, standby power consumption, low noise level, and applied voltage-bias requirements [15].

## 14.6 APPLICATIONS

The development in photodetectors has been largely focused on pursuing miniaturized photodetectors with low leakage current, low noise, short response time, high uniformity, large bandwidth, and high gain-bandwidth [15], [31]. Photetection is possible due to the photoelectric effect in photosensitive materials and large-scale fabrication and integration technologies [70]. Photodetectors in the different spectral regimes have gained significant research interest due to their several applications in life, the military, industry, and agriculture, etc.

Depending on the applications, different spectral regimes are frequently used for photodetection, as shown in Figure 14.10 [34]. UV photodetectors found their potential applications in environmental monitoring, space science, military investigation, sterilization, territory intrusions, whereas visible photodetectors have been used for video imaging, displays, industrial safety, optoelectronic storage, etc. [31], [71]. The IR photodetectors have applications in various fields, such as thermal and biomedical pollution detection,

As stated earlier, the tunable optoelectronic properties of HgCdTe semiconductor material makes a promising choice for the development of broadband photodetectors [21], [53], [59], [75]. Several HgCdTe-based high performance IR photodetectors with different device architectures, such as p-n [22], p-i-n [74], and dual-band IR photodetector [23] are being reported at cryogenic temperatures. ## 14.7 CONCLUSION

In summary, this chapter presented an introduction to photodetectors, their various photodetection mechanisms, different types, materials used, characteristics, and their various applications. Photodetectors are the optoelectronic component used to detect incident radiations. Depending on the amount of light absorbed by photosensitive material, photodetectors can find their potential applications in UV, visible, and IR spectral regimes. In terms of material properties, HgCdTe is one of the most widely used material for military-related applications. The operation of HgCdTe based photodetectors is limited to cryogenic temperatures. To deal with such an issue, the heterojunction of graphene with HgCdTe will be the prominent solution to the production of high-performance photodetectors at near-room temperature.
