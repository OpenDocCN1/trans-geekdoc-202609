# 14.4.2 Avalanche Photodetector

Another major type of photodetector with an internal gain mechanism is an avalanche photodetector (APD), as shown in Figure 14.6a, and has a more refined architecture than the p–i–n photodetector to produce an extremely high electric field region (~10 V/cm). Therefore, in the depletion region where the photons are absorbed and the carrier pairs generated, a high electric field region exists where electrons and holes can attain enough energy to newly generated carriers. This phenomenon is recognized as impact ionization or carrier multiplication (CM) and is the process that results in avalanche breakdown in reverse-biased diodes. Normally, this needs a higher reverse bias voltage so that the newly generated carriers via impact ionization can produce more carriers with similar phenomenon, as shown in Figure 14.6b [9]. More than 10⁴ of multiplication can be achieved in APDs. Thus, APDs internally multiply the primary photocurrent before entering the input circuit of the amplifier. As the photocopy is multiplied before facing the thermal noise associated with the receiver circuit, the sensitivity of the receiver increases.

A commonly used architecture for attaining carrier multiplication with slight excess noise is composed of a high resistive, that is, likely p+-type doped (p*) material deposited on a p* substrate. A p*-type ion implant or is then made in the high resistive material, followed by n*-layer. The i-region is intrinsic material that is made with p-type doping. This configuration is termed p*–i–n* reach through the avalanche photodetector (RAPD) and is shown in Figure 14.6a. The term reach-through arises from the photodetector operation.
