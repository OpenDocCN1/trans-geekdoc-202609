# 13.5 SILICON MICROMACHINING-BASED PASSIVE COMPONENTS FOR TRANSCEIVER MODULES AT THZ FREQUENCY

The term 'micromachining' was coined in the 1980s. It describes the process of selective removal of substrate material, either by means of wet chemicals or by the dry etching method. Historically, this micromachining method was applied on silicon substrate to realize various physical or inertial sensors (pressure sensors, gyroscopes, accelerometers, etc.). Currently, this field is not limited to the world of sensors. Rather, it is expanding its domain to RF, microwave, or even THz-frequency range. The feature of precision lithography with batch facility makes silicon micromachining a unique process for high frequency devices. Moreover, the repeatability of the process is quite promising in comparison to existing methods in the market. Initially, waveguide structures are developed for THZ passive devices, which act as a base-line architecture for all passive building blocks.

In this chapter, we discuss about two specific waveguide structures, namely WR-2.2 and WR-3.4. The nomenclatures of the waveguide are defined by the EIA (Electronic Industries Alliance). As per the EIA-standard, WR-3.4 has a cross-sectional dimension of $864 \times 432$ $\mu m^2$, which is suitable for 220–330 GHz applications, whereas WR-2.2 has a standard of $560 \times 280$ $\mu m^2$, which is best for the 325 to 550 GHz band. This work comprises design and simulation of several passive components based upon rectangular waveguide structures.
