# 3.1.5.3 Subthreshold Swings in TFET

The characteristic of the device below threshold voltage ($V_{TH}$) is known as subthreshold slope of the device. The subthreshold slope ($SS$) is defined as the minimum potential of gate-source voltage required to lower the subthreshold current by a factor of 10. It is defined as the minimum amount of the gate voltage required for lowering the subthreshold current by a factor of 10.

The subthreshold swing is defined as follows:

```
$SS = \frac{\partial\log I_D}{\partial\log V_G}^{-1} \qquad (3.4)$
```

The unit of the subthreshold swing is defined as mV/decade.

In the case of MOSFET, the slope is independent of gate biasing voltage ($V_{GS}$) below the threshold voltage.

```
$SS = \frac{\partial\log V_G}{\partial\varphi_s} \frac{\partial\varphi_s}{\partial\log I_D} = \left( 1 + \frac{C_{DEF}}{C_{OX}} \frac{kT}{\ln10} \right) \qquad (3.5)$
```

Where

```
$\frac{kT}{q} \ln10 \geq 60\text{ mV / Decade}$
```

At $T=300\text{ k}$ and $C_{DEF}$ capacitance at the depletion region and $C_{OX}$ is oxide capacitance, $\varphi_s$ is the surface potential. At the equilibrium condition, the Boltzmann mobile charge carriers' distribution at the source region explain that a MOSFET can switch very steeply from OFF-to ON-state and vice versa.

The TFET has an electron-band tunneling process in which the charge carrier injection from the source side, in contrast to the MOSFET, which has thermionic emission, so that the TFET can achieve a sub-$60\text{ mV/decade}$ subthreshold swing. Wang, Hilsbeck et al. reported that, through a theoretical analysis, showed that the subthreshold swing of TFETs can be diminished to under $60\text{ mV/decade}[36].

Bhuwka et al. achieved the subthreshold swing with a value of $44\text{ mV/decade}$ in the vertical structure of TFET (Figure 3.8). On carbon nanotube (CNT) technology, Appenzel et al. implemented the tunneling device and novel method for making doping strategic (DS) junctions in the strained-Si nanowire TFETs [39]. Both n-type and p-type FETs demonstrated strong ON currents. For n-type FETs, a minimum of $30\text{ mV}$ was achieved at $300\text{ K}$.

In a recently published paper, a subthreshold slope has been reported less than $60\text{ mV/decade}[40]$. However the TFET reported a very low SS value, the MOSFET had a better ON/OFF ratio. This thanks to a low ON current output from TFET characteristics. The low current is due to the disparity between the TFET and the MOSFET carrier injection mechanisms.
