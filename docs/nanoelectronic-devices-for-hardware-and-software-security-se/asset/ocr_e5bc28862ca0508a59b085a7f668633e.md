# 4.6 CONCLUSION AND OUTLOOK

In a nutshell, it can be stated that functionalization and substitutional doping leads to significant changes in the electronic and transport properties of SINR and GeNRs, projecting them as a suitable for interconnect applications. The results presented in the chapter systematically highlight the thermodynamic stability (based on calculation of $E_g$ and $E_{eff}$), electronic properties (based on calculation of bandgap), and finally $R_0, L_c, C_0$ (as performance parameters for interconnect application). Based on these calculations, it is proved that with the increase in the O-atom concentration at the edge of nanoribbons, their performance for interconnect improves. However, in case of substitutional doping, the performance depends mainly on the size of the doping. Further, in each case, pertaining to the impact of the bias voltage has also been carried out. Consideration of the effect of the bias voltage on performance of the interconnect plays a pivotal role in determining the reliability and the security and integrity of the interconnect used in the hardware.

From the future perspective, similar studies can be conducted through various metallic interfaces and functionalizations to gauge the performance of SINR and GeNR interconnects. It is also noteworthy that the reliability or security interconnects can also be proved from other dimensions, such as study of defected SINRs/GeNRs or use of metal contacts rather than the extension of the same material as electrodes. Systematic probe of such possibilities will lead to low power and secure nanoscale interconnect for next generation hardware.
