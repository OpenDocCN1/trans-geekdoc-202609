### 13.1 Introduction
Submillimeter wave (mmW) or terahertz (THz) research has gained unprecedented interest and momentum over the last years due to high demands for data-rate at THz. THz rays (T-rays) are non-ionizing radiation, which means it does trigger harmful chemical reaction in human tissue. With this attractive feature, THz technology is becoming attractive in medical applications, too. This chapter would provide a generic overview on this technology, inherent challenges associated with it, and potential. The fundamental limitation of THz band is extreme atmospheric attenuation, which has surged the development of high gain over the last years. The present chapter also deals with national status of THz research and activities. THz frequency covers the submillimeter regime, fabrication of small devices calls for many technologies; (i) CNC (computer numerical) milling and (ii) silicon micromachining. Currently, THz systems are predominantly manufactured by CNC milling. In recent years, CNC has achieved a high level of precision and reliability, however, it lacks the ability to manufacture at volume, which makes the overall process time consuming as well as costly. In contrast, silicon micromachining is useful for volume production, and at the same it offers fabrication precision on the order of microns or even below (depending upon the technology node and type of lithography process used). Moreover, the micromining process yields a good surface profile with the surface roughness of finished product in the range of nanometers (reducing losses associated with surface roughness at THz). Furthermore, this process is also capable of fabricating high-aspect ratio geometries that, using any fabrication technology, are hardly possible. The present chapter, as will be elaborated in the next sections, deals with the design and development of various passive components and high gain antennas using silicon micromining technology for THz applications. The passive components and antennas are designed and optimized using Ansys HFSS suite. The finalized designs are under fabrication using the well-established DRI (deep reactive ion etching) micromining technique at Semi-Conductor Laboratory (SCL), Chandigarh, India.
