From boundary conditions, that is, $U = E_G/2$ when $x=0$, which is accompanied by the consistent electric field, the coefficient factor $c$ is determined. The definitive expression for potential barrier is as:

```
$$(E_X-U) = -2\left| \frac{E_G}{2}^2 - (q\varepsilon x)^2}{E_G} \right| \quad (2.5)$
```

To consider tunneling in 3D semiconductors, the entire momentum of the incident electron is to be considered, which includes the momentum in the tunneling path as well as perpendicular to the tunneling direction. The energy related to the transversal momentum is compensated by adding the term $E_T$ in equation 2.5 [14].

```
$$(E_X-U) = -2\left| \frac{E_G}{2}^2 - (q\varepsilon x)^2}{E_G} \right| + E_T \quad (2.6)$
```

Therefore, the tunneling probability is expressed as:

```
$$(T(E_X, E_T) \approx \exp-2\int_{x_1}^{x_2} \sqrt{\frac{2m^*}{h^2}\left(\frac{E_G}{2}^2 - (q\varepsilon x)^2}{E_G} + E_T\right)} dx \quad (2.7)$
```

It should be mentioned that the integral limit changes from $x_1$ and $x_2$ to $x_1'$ and $x_2'$ with the addition of $E_T$, as the transverse momentum causes an extra damping of the wave function and thereby alters the conventional tunneling positions, which results in an enhanced effective tunnel barrier width, that is, $x_1'$ and $x_2'$. The new tunneling positions could be calculated using:
