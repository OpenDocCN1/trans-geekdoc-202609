# 310 Nanoelectronic Devices for Security

The study on ballistic rectifier has been further extended by considering the tunable rectification using gate control Fermi [93], [94]. The rectification output reversed in the positive direction with the increase of gate voltage. The rectification was quasi-classical for wide channel region rectifier as compared to quantum rectification for a narrow channel region rectifier. The temperature-dependent reversal of rectification characteristics has been explained in [95], considering the effect of variation in the channel conductance. The electrons were accumulated in the narrow or wide-centrosymmetric regions, which fluctuates the conductance of the device.

Similarly, the rectification effects have been studied for non-centymmetric ballistic rectifier for branch angles (30°, 45°, 60°, and 90°) by input and output terminals. The decrease in angle increases the rectifier output while at the upper terminal is independent of change in angle. In addition, variation in the gate voltage made the opposite effect on overall rectification output, and output terminal voltage], [104].

The rectification in InAs/GaSb heterructure-based FTBR has been studied [96], [102], as shown in Figure 15.7. The magneto-resistance property of ballistic rectifier demonstrated the Shubnikov-Hass oscillations at magnetic field above ±IT [96]. In addition, the bending resistance was Shubharized, which represents the electron focusing peak at the positive or negative side of the weak magnetic field and again depends on input configuration [102]. The proposed heterructure can be used to extend the research at higher temperatures [96].

The electro-thermal phenomenon of the ballistic rectifier has been studied by Matthews et al. [97]. Instead of applying a voltage between input terminals, thermal effects have been used to get the rectifier output. The InP/GaInAs heterructure was used to fabricate such a device. A temperature difference has been applied across the input terminals, which generated the thermal voltage at the output terminals. An output reversal similar to the previous discussion has also been observed in the thermal study of the ballistic rectifier. This work suggests the possibility of designing the electronic rectifiers and thermoelectric energy converters [97]. Furthermore, the electro-thermal phenomenon has been studied in cross-junction in a QPC was used in the output terminal to create a potential barrier [99].

The device performance is typically affected by the noise. The low flicker noise of the rectifier has been studied and formulated with the channel and antidot dimensions. The zero intrinsic threshold property of the rectifier eliminates the low frequency flicker noise. In addition, the speed of the device has been checked using the noise equivalent power (NEP) and responsivity characteristic of the rectifier at different frequencies [98].
