For an n-type TFET, a positive source voltage ($V_{gs}$) is applied to the gate, which induces a band bending at the channel, resulting in tunneling of electrons from $E_s$ of the source to $E_c$ of the channel region. The electrons that tunneled the channel region are swept to the drain terminals due to the positive biasing at the drain. At this time, the transistor is considered to be in the ON-state, when the gate is biased and no tunneling occurs from the source to the channel region, it is considered as OFF-state, as depicted in Figure 2.2. ```

### 2.3 PHYSICS IN BAND-TO-BAND TUNNELING

The band-to-band tunneling (BTBT) is based on the phenomena of quantum mechanics in which the electrons tunneled through the forbidden energy gap of the semiconductor. From a physics point of view, BTBT is the snippings of the covalent bonds created among semiconductor atoms. Here, the band-to-band tunneling generation rate ($G_{BTBT}$) will be derived mathematically for a better understanding of how the BTBT mechanism attributes to a steeper subthreshold slope. It will start with the establishment of the general tunneling concept, with the presumptions for the derivations. The tunneling probability ($T$), current density ($J$) and generation rate ($G$) will then be determined with the help of the Wentzel–Kramers–Brillouin (WKB) approximation.

### 2.3 Presumptions Made for Derivation

The first articulation on the tunneling probability was determined by Kane using time-dependent disruption hypothesis in addition to Fermi's golden rule [16], [17]. However, the section would rather utilize a rearrangement with a more intuitive way of dealing with a similar undertaking dependent on the WKB estimation [18]. The evaluation is done in accordance with tunneling of electrons through a reverse biased pn junction as shown in Figure 2.3.

- 1. Presume a close bandgap semiconductor such that the momentum impact due to phonon scatterings could be neglected. In this manner, a simple analytical approach for $G_{BTBT}$ can be achieved.

### 2.3.2 Evaluation of the Imaginary Dispersion Relation

According to the Wentzel–Kramers–Brillouin concept, the tunneling probability ($T$) is given by [18]:

```
$T \simeq \exp\left[-2\int_{x_1}^{x_2} k(x)dx\right] \quad (2.2)$
```

where $x_1$ and $x_2$ are classical turning points. To determine equation 2.2, the imaginary wave vector distribution correlation $k(x)$ should be established. In accordance with the generalized $E-k$ relation, we have [18]:

```
$k(E_s) = \sqrt{\frac{2m^*}{\hbar^2}(E_s-U)} \quad (2.3)
```

where $E_s$ denotes the energy of electron while $U$ represents the potential barrier height. Initiation of the ($E_i$) accompanied by the transverse momentum alters the classical turning points into $x_1$ as well as $x_2$.

From boundary conditions, that is, $U = E_G/2$ when $x=0$, which is accompanied by consistent electric field, the coefficient $c$ is determined. The definitive expression for potential barrier is as:

```
$(E_X-U) = c\left[\frac{E_G}{2\varepsilon}\right] \quad (2.4)$
```

To consider tunneling in 3D semiconductors, the entire momentum of the incident electron is be considered, which includes the momentum in the tunneling path as well as perpendicular to the tunneling direction. The energy related to transversal momentum is compensated by adding the term $E_T$ in equation 2.5.

```
$(E_X-U) = -2\left| \frac{E_G}{2}^2 - (q\varepsilon x)^2}{E_G} \right| + E_T \quad (2.5)$
```

Therefore, the tunneling probability is expressed as:

```
$T(E_X, E_T) \approx \exp-2\int_{x_1}^{x_2} \sqrt{\frac{2m^*}{\hbar^2}\left(\frac{E_G}{2}^2 - (q\varepsilon x)^2}{E_G} + E_T\right)} dx \quad (2.7)$
```

It should be mentioned that the integral limit changes from $x_1$ and $x_2$ to $x_1'$ and $x_2'$ with the addition of $E_T$, as the transverse momentum causes an extra damping of the wave function and thereby alters the conventional tunneling positions, which results in an enhanced effective tunnel barrier width, that is, $x_1'$ and $x_2'$. ```
