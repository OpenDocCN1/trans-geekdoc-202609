# 5.4.2 NEURAL NETWORK

Neural networks have presented analysis and prediction of FoG conditions in PD patients. Zeng et al. [31] employed radial basis function network to analyze the gait dynamics of 93 PD patients and 73 healthy controls, achieving 96.39% classification accuracy, 96.77% sensitivity, and 95.89% specificity. Sun et al. [32] recommended an acronym recognition algorithm that detects the FoG region using a spatial attention network and 3D CNN. Camps et al. [33] developed an eight-layered CNN for FoG detection using a single waist-worn accelerometer, achieving high sensitivity and specificity in tasks involving activities performed by PD patients. Tahir et al. [34] introduced a non-invasive WiFi sensing system to detect FoG symptoms in PD patients. Their system effectively distinguished FoG from activities like sitting-standing, walking, and voluntary movements, using multi-resolution-frequency scales. They achieved remarkable results with 97% sensitivity and 100% specificity in FoG detection through a deep-level CNN trained on the scalogram. Xia et al. [35] introduced a CNN-based FoG detection system. Their study included patient-independent and patient-dependent experiments, with the most favorable outcomes observed in patient-dependent tests. Neural networks have the potential to transform the analysis and prediction of FoG conditions. The ability of these neural networks to capture spatial and temporal relationships in gait data depends upon the integration of multi-modal information to provide real-time predictions of FoG conditions in Parkinson's patients.

# 5.5 CLINICAL APPLICATIONS

The application of machine learning methods for predicting FoG in PD patients holds significant promise, as evidenced by recent clinical trials. These cutting-edge computational approaches provide invaluable insights that have the potential to transform patient care and treatment strategies. This section investigates the practical implications and revolutionary potential of machine learning in predicting FoG, using findings from recent clinical trials to highlight breakthroughs in patient-centered treatment as presented in Figure 5.6.

# 5.5.1 EARLY DIAGNOSIS AND MONITORING

Algorithms based on features extracted from sensor data, such as accelerometers and gyroscopes, can aid in the identification of subtle changes in gait, allowing for early intervention. For disease management, continuous monitoring of FoG condition is essential. Both patients and healthcare providers can benefit from real-time alerts from machine learning algorithms, which can help track the progression of FoG and adjust treatment plans accordingly [27–29, 32, 33, 36–38].

# 5.5.2 PERSONALIZED TREATMENT PLANS

Machine learning models can analyze patient-specific data and personalize treatment plans. Healthcare providers can provide more personalized care by identifying the specific triggers or predictors of FoG in each patient. The combination of patient data and algorithmic insights enables the optimization of medication schedules and therapy regimens, lowering the frequency and severity of FoG [34–36, 38].
