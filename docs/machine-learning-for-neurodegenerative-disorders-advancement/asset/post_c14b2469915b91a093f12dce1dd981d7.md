# Machine Learning Methods for Predicting Freezing of Gait in Parkinson’s Patients Insights from Recent Clinical Trials

Ishan Ay and Chandrasekhar Azad
National Institute of Technology Jamshedpur, Jamshedpur, India

## 5.1 INTRODUCTION

Parkinson's disease (PD) is a progressive neurological disorder characterized by non-motor and motor symptoms that have a significant impact on patients' well-being. Freezing of gait (FoG) is one of the most distressing problems faced by an individual, characterized by a sudden and temporary inability to initiate or sustain movement [1]. Early detection of FoG could help Parkinson's patients prevent fatal injury due to imbalances caused by FoG [2, 3]. Although researchers are working on accurately predicting and managing FoG in PD patients, several challenges still need to be tackled [4].

Machine learning has emerged as a transformative frontier in the healthcare domain; it has the capacity to analyze diverse datasets, uncovering intricate patterns and relationships beyond traditional statistical methods. Recent advances in wearable sensor technology, widespread adoption of electronic health records, and the accumulation of clinical trial data have paved the way for the use of machine learning algorithms in predicting and managing medical conditions such as FoG in PD. These algorithms can process multifaceted data, including gait patterns, physiological signals, medical records, and demographic information, allowing the creation of predictive models tailored to individual patients. Machine learning has seen unprecedented growth and innovation in recent years, spanning novel applications across a wide range of domains. Computer systems are rooted in the principle of training (to learn) and making data-driven decisions without programming. Machine learning's capacity to analyze extensive datasets is quite helpful in identifying patterns and generating predictions, which makes it an invaluable tool in predictive analytics [5, 6]. In recent years, researchers have utilized advanced computational techniques with abundant patient data to develop predictive models. Therefore, machine learning has the potential to transform healthcare for PD by predicting FoG in advance, allowing personal-interventions to prevent injury, reduce the risk of falls or injuries. This shift from proactive care is a promising aspect of machine learning in healthcare [7]. This study aims to understand the design of highly accurate and clinically relevant models by integrating gait data, clinical variables, and medical history, contributing to a better understanding of FoG and enabling its early detection in PD.
