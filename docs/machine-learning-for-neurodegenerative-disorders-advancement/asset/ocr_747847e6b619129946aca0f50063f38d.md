# 4.3.1 DATA COLLECTION AND STANDARDIZATION

In the domain of drug discovery, the role of data is paramount, as they serve as the foundation for all subsequent analyses and decision-making processes. The advent of AI in this field has underscored the critical importance of data collection and standardization. AI algorithms, particularly those based on ML and deep learning, require extensive, well-curated datasets to function effectively. These datasets can include a wide range of information, from molecular structure and biological activity to patient demographic and clinical trial outcome. The data needs to be comprehensive, accurate, and representative of the real-world scenarios in which the drugs will be used [91]. Given the diverse sources from which data can be collected, including academic research, pharmaceutical databases, and clinical trials, the challenge lies in harmonizing this information into a coherent and usable format.

To address this challenge, significant advancements have been made in developing automated pipelines for data collection. Pipelines are designed to extract relevant information from various sources and compile it into centralized databases [13]. For instance, data on existing drugs, including their chemical structures, mechanisms of action, and side effects, can be gleaned from extensive drug databases. Similarly, insights can be drawn from the academic literature, where latest research is published [92]. Clinical trials offer another source of data, providing detailed information on patient responses to drugs, adverse effects, and efficacy [93]. The ability to aggregate this diversity into a single, accessible repository is a critical step for drug discovery. However, the more collection of data is not sufficient; the data needs to be standardized.

Data standardization is a process that involves converting disparate data types into a unified format, making it easier for AI to analyze and derive meaningful insights. This is particularly important given the wide range of data formats and structures used in the field of drug discovery [94]. For example, molecular structures can be represented as Simplified Molecular Input Entry System (SMILES) strings or 3D molecular models. Demographic data and clinical outcomes, on the other hand, require a different approach to categorization and quantification. Standardization ensures that all types of data are transformed into a format that algorithms can use for consistent and accurate processing [95]. This involves not only the homogenization of data formats but also the normalization of values, the handling of missing data, and the resolution of inconsistencies [96].

The effective collection and standardization of data is vital in creating robust models capable of making accurate predictions and analyses in drug discovery. By streamlining these processes, researchers and scientists can fetch high-quality AI algorithm data, leading to more reliable and actionable insights. This, in turn, accelerates the drug discovery process, enhances the precision of predictions regarding drug safety and efficacy, and ultimately contributes to the development of more effective and personalized therapeutic solutions. The integration of advanced data collection and standardization methods represents a significant advancement in the evolution of AI applications within pharmaceutical research, setting the stage for more innovative and impactful discoveries [97].
