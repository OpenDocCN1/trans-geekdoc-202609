# 2.2 NEUROIMAGING MODALITIES AND THEIR CHALLENGES

# 2.2.1 MAGNETIC RESONANCE IMAGING

Neuroimaging modalities play a crucial role in unraveling the complexities of the brain, offering diverse perspectives on its structure and function. Among these modalities, MRI stands out as a non-invasive and versatile technique used in both clinical and research settings. MRI employs powerful magnetic fields and radiofrequency pulses to generate detailed images of the brain's anatomy with exceptional spatial resolution. Despite its strengths, MRI is not without challenges. Issues such as motion artifacts, susceptibility to metal implants, and acquisition times can impact the quality of images, compromising diagnostic accuracy. Technological advancements, such as the development of fast imaging techniques and motion correction algorithms, aim to address these challenges and enhance the efficacy of MRI in neuroimaging [9].

# 2.2.2 FUNCTIONAL MRI

Neuroimaging modalities play a pivotal role in advancing our understanding of brain function, with fMRI standing as a key technique in this endeavor. fMRI allows researchers to map and visualize brain activity by detecting changes in blood flow associated with neural activity. This non-invasive modality provides valuable insights into functional connectivity, localization of brain functions, and the study of various cognitive processes. However, fMRI is not without its challenges. Issues such as susceptibility to motion, low temporal resolution, and the reliance on hemodynamic responses present constraints that researchers must ensure the accuracy and reliability of fMRI data. Ongoing research focuses on mitigating these challenges through the development of advanced data analysis methods, motion correction techniques, and the integration of multimodal imaging approaches [10].
