# 10.1 INTRODUCTION

Drowsiness detection has been the focus of a number of methods, including visual features and biological signals. On the one hand, video-based drowsiness detection applications are effective and resilient to changes in light and noise. However, compared to visual features, biological signals are the most accurate predictors of drowsiness. Thus, monitoring serves as a means of increasing security for the benefit of human well-being. As a matter of fact, this subject is increasingly significant in healthcare applications to enhance medical assessment. The stage of transition between waking and sleep, known as drowsiness, is when the decline in attentiveness is seen. This phase, which precedes the sleep state, is marked by a near lack of reflexes and, as is incompatible with tasks that demand high levels of alertness. The primary characteristics of this state are heaviness in noise, behavioral abnormalities, decreased reflex, and difficulties maintaining the head in the frontal position within the field of view.

Brain signals are one type of physiological marker that can monitor behavior, manners, and mental health. The use of brain signals in brain-computer interfaces has been initiated since electroencephalography (EEG) was developed to record brain signals without the need for complex setups. The technology for EEG recording has evolved dramatically. Signals can be recorded using dry electrodes. The systems additionally include software for removing artifacts and noise. EEG signals are one of the best indicators of drowsiness and alertness variance, according to a number of research on brain signals and drowsiness detection. It is possible to study the EEG signal temporal and spatial domains. The identification of tiredness using EEG involves the application of signal processing techniques, feature extraction methods, and classification approaches. Important feature extraction techniques that have demonstrated excellent efficiency in numerous research is in this state.
