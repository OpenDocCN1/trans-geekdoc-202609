# 2.8.2 TOOLS FOR INTERPRETABILITY

Tools for interpretability in ML are crucial for unraveling the decisions by complex models, especially in domains like neuroimaging, where transparency is essential. Various tools have been developed to enhance the interpretability of ML models, offering insights into feature importance, model behavior, and decision rationale. Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAPs), introduced by [57], is a prominent tool that employs game theory to assign a Shapley value, quantifying its contribution to the model's output. Local interpretable model-agnostic explanations (LIMEs), proposed by [58], generates locally faithful explanations by approximating a black box model behavior around a specific instance. Gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM) visualizes the regions of an image that contributed to a deep learning model's decision, aiding in understanding the focus areas. Additionally, tools like TensorBoard and Yellowbrick provide visualization neural network architectures and model performance, respectively. These tools empower researchers and practitioners to comprehend, validate, and correct the decisions made by ML models, fostering trust and facilitating adoption in critical applications.

# 2.8.2.1 Shapley Additive Explanations

Interpretability is a critical aspect of ML, especially in the context of neuroimaging, where understanding model decisions is essential. SHAPs have emerged as a powerful tool for enhancing the interpretability of complex ML models. Proposed by [57] SHAP values are based on cooperative game theory, specifically Shapley values, and provide a consistent and fair way to allocate contributions of each feature to the model's output. SHAP quantify the impact of individual features on predictions, offering a nuanced understanding of the model's decision-making process. In neuroimaging studies, SHAPs have been applied to evaluate the contributions of specific brain regions or features to diagnostic predictions, shedding light on the factors influencing the model's outcomes [59]. The versatility of SHAPs extends to various ML models, making it a valuable tool for interpretability in neuroimaging applications. Researchers and practitioners can leverage SHAPs to gain insights into the relevance of different neuroimaging features and enhance the transparency and trustworthiness of ML models.

# 2.8.2.2 LIME

Interpretable ML models are essential for understanding model predictions, particularly in the complex domain of neuroimaging. LIMEs, introduced by [58], stand out as a valuable tool for achieving interpretability by generating locally faithful explanations for black-box models. LIMEs operate by perturbing instances in the dataset, creating a new dataset of similar instances, and then training a simple model on this perturbed dataset to approximate the behavior of the original complex model. This local surrogate model provides insights into the decision boundaries around a specific data point, aiding in the interpretation of individual predictions. In neuroimaging studies, LIMEs have been applied to elucidate the rationale behind the predictions of ML models, offering insights into the importance of specific brain regions or features for a given task [60].
