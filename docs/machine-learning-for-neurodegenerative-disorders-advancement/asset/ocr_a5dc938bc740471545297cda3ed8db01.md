Serious traffic accidents can result from drivers operating their vehicles when fatigued. This research proposes a CNN-based wearable EEG technique for wearable EEG data collection, vehicle driver drowsiness, and the early warning technique. First, in the simulation scenario of both awake and sleeping, the EEG signals are monitored and collected using a wearable computer interface (BCI). Second, the Inception module and the MobileNet module are used to train neural networks that classify EEG inputs [24]. Finally, if the driver of the car is seen to be sleepy, the early warning strategy module will activate and sound an alert. EEG data from a simulated driving task was used to test the method. Based on samples from a one-second time window, the results indicate that utilizing a neural network with the Inception module achieved 95.59% classification accuracy, while a modified AlexNet module yielded 94.68%. The suggested tiredness detection method's viability is demonstrated by the simulation and test results.

One of the primary causes of accidents is tired drivers. In real environments, single-mode drowsiness detection techniques have poor anti-interferences and low accuracy. In this study, we suggest an optimal approach that combines motion vehicle data with EEG for driving fatigue identification.

Analysis is done on the relationship between the EEG signal and the motion data of the vehicle. For the EEG, the technique detects the frequency band ratio of the four rhythm waves, measured by $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\theta$, and $\delta$. Calculations are made for the sample entropy and standard deviation based based on driving acceleration, speed, and wheel angle. Every indicator of weariness is subjected to a Pearson correlation analysis [25]. To achieve the fusion drowsiness detection, the connection with the strongest association is chosen and fed into a support vector machine. By means of experimental, we managed to obtain an average accuracy of 92.37%, which surpasses the 2.53% and the 8.78% classification accuracy by EEG driving fatigue detection and vehicle motion information-based driving fatigue detection. Correlation analysis-based multi-source information for driving tiredness detection can enhance system performance.

Moreover, there is still disagreement in the literature regarding the characteristics of the EEG indicator that are most useful in identifying sleepiness.
