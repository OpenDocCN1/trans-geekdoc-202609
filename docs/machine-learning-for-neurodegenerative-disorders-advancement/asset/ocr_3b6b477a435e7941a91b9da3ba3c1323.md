# 8.10 CONCLUSION

In conclusion, this study addresses the complexities associated with ASD diagnosis by introducing a novel approach that merges the capabilities of voting and AdaBoost science models. Given the diverse challenges within the ASD spectrum, accurate and early identification is crucial for comprehensive support. The combination of voting and AdaBoost achieves an impressive accuracy of 98.60% in prediction, demonstrating the potential of this innovative method. Evaluation of the model encompasses a thorough analysis of performance metrics, including accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and kappa coefficient. From K-fold cross-validation in training and testing phases we observed the robustness and effectiveness of the proposed model.

Furthermore, the primary objective of this research extends beyond predicting the presence of autism; it aims to enhance accuracy in gauging the severity of autism among children within the specified group. Recognizing varying degrees of autism is essential for tailoring interventions and support strategies to meet the specific needs of individual children on the spectrum. This proposed approach holds promise for improving the quality of life for individuals on autism spectrum through timely intervention and support.

Suggestions for future research directions include the scalability and real-time applicability of the VO-ADA model in clinical settings. Investigating the addition of additional data sources, such as genetic markers or behavioral observations, could further enhance the predictive capabilities. Longitudinal studies tracking the progression of ASD symptoms over time would would provide valuable insights into the long-term effectiveness of intervention strategies by the VO-ADA model. Overall, continued research in this area has the potential to significantly impact the early detection and management of ASDs, improving outcomes for individuals and their families.
