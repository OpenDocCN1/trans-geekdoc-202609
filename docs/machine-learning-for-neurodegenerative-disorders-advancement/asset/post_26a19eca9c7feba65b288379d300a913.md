In k-fold validation, the dataset is divided into k subsets, and the model is trained and tested k times, each time using a different subset as the testing set and remaining k-1 subsets as the training set. This technique helps ensure the model's performance is robust and not overly dependent on any particular subset of the data.

Progressing further, the analysis is assessed through the application of various performance metrics, which are critical for evaluating the model's accuracy and reliability. These metrics typically include the following:

- i. **Accuracy**: The proportion of correct predictions made by the model out of all predictions.
- ii. **Precision**: The ability of the model to correctly identify only those individuals who have ASD.
- iii. **Recall (sensitivity)**: The ability of the model to identify all individuals who have ASD.
- iv. **F1-Score**: The harmonic mean of precision and recall, providing a balance between the two.
- v. **Kappa coefficient**: A statistical measure that accounts for the possibility of the agreement occurring by chance, providing a more nuanced assessment of the model's accuracy.

These metrics offer a comprehensive evaluation of the model's performance, allowing for a detailed understanding of its strengths and potential areas for improvement.

Finally, the trained models specifically using the VO-DA algorithms are employed to predict whether an individual is likely to have ASD. By using these advanced ensemble methods, the proposed VO-DA model aims to maximize the predictive accuracy and reliability of ASD diagnoses.
