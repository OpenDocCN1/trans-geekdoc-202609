An AD filter, also known as Perona-Malik diffusion [6], filters the artifacts of the image in an anisotropic fashion, diffusing the pixels along the four cardinal directions, smoothing the image and diffusing the edges more sharply compared to the previous filters discussed. AD may remove noise from digital photographs without causing edge blur. One can solve the AD equations with a constant diffusion coefficient to produce the heat equation, which is the same as Gaussian blurring. In the ensuing formulae, when the diffusion coefficient is chosen as an edge-avoiding function, as in Perona-Malik, promote diffusion (and hence smoothing) inside areas of smoother image intensity and sharp-en it across strong edges. This keeps noise at bay while maintaining the image's edges.

In edge detection methods, AD can be used similarly to reduce noise. By employing an edge-seeking detection coefficient for a predefined number of repetitions, the image can expand towards a piecewise constant image, with the boundaries between the constant being recognized as edges.
