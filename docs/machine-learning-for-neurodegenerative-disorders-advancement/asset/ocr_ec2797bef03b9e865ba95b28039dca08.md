In parallel, logistic regression emerges as another cornerstone in the machine-learning arsenal for Alzheimer's detection. Similar to SVMs, logistic regression undergoes fine-tuning procedures, typically involving application of L2 penalty and varying regularization parameters to refine model behavior. This iterative process aids in enhancing the model's accuracy and robustness, enabling more reliable classifications of AD cases. Logistic regression, with its simplicity and interpretability, provides a complementary approach to SVMs, offering insights into the relationships between predictor variables and the likelihood of AD occurrence. Through meticulous parameter adjustment and validation techniques, logistic regression contributes to the iterative refinement of ML models, ensuring their efficacy in AD detection and diagnosis [21].

Decision trees are foundational components of ML algorithms, widely applied in various domains, including AD detection. In this context, decision trees are initially trained without tuning, allowing the algorithm to create a tree structure based on features extracted from the dataset. Subsequently, grid search techniques are employed to optimize parameters such as tree depth and evaluation criterion, which may include the Gini index or entropy. Decision trees offer interpretability but are susceptible to overfitting, requiring pruning and parameter optimization to enhance generalization and model robustness. Despite these limitations, decision trees provide valuable insights into the salient features contributing to AD diagnosis, in the development of effective diagnostic models [22].

Random forests represent an ensemble learning technique designed to address the limitations of individual decision trees. The aggregating predictions from multiple decision trees improve performance and stability. Each decision tree in the ensemble is trained independently, and the final prediction is determined through voting or averaging mechanisms. This approach mitigates overfitting and variance issues enhances robustness of the model. Random forests incorporate grid search and cross-validation techniques to handle large and high-dimensional datasets, random forests are well suited for AD detection tasks, where identifying patterns in complex data is crucial for accurate diagnosis. In conclusion, random forests offer a powerful framework for developing reliable and effective models for AD diagnosis, facilitating early detection and intervention to improve patient outcomes.

Deep learning architectures represent cutting-edge methodologies for analyzing complex datasets, including those pertaining to AD detection. DL frameworks, constructed by multiple layers of interconnected neural networks, facilitate the extraction of intricate patterns and relationships within heterogeneous data modalities. In the context of AD research, DL architectures offer a powerful framework for processing diverse datasets encompassing neuroimaging scans, scan genetic markers, and clinical records. These architectures leverage convolutional, pooling, fully connected, and normalization layers to learn hierarchical representations of data, enabling effective classification and diagnostic inference [23].

CNNs have been extensively applied in AD research, with various architectures including LeNet-5, VGG19, and custom-designed models tailored to neuroimaging datasets. LeNet-5, originally developed for handwritten digit recognition, has been repurposed for binary classification tasks involving AD and healthy controls. VGG19, a deeper CNN architecture comprising multiple convolutional layers, exhibits superior performance in tasks requiring classification among multiple groups.
