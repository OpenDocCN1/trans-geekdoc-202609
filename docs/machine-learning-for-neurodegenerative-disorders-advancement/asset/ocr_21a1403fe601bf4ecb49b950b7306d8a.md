# 6.5.2 STRENGTHS AND WEAKNESSES

The study has a number of strong points, most notably its literature analysis that was methodically carried out using the PRISMA methodology and insightful information about the use of DL and ML approaches for AD diagnosis [4, 32]. It offers a comprehensive analysis of the pathology that underlies AD, clarifying the interactions between tau pathology, amyloid plaques, neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, genetic predisposition, and environmental variables. A thorough analysis of the AI architectures used to detect AD also contributes to better understanding of the field by outlining the advantages and disadvantages of various models, offering suggestions for further research, and benchmarking tables that compare various detection techniques. The paper, however, has a few flaws. It is notable for lacking explanation of the examined models’ clinical validity and practicality. The title’s reference to the bias estimate is not fully explained, which decreases openness. Furthermore, the publication ignores contributions from other domains like neuroimaging and biomarker research because its scope is restricted to ML and DL techniques. Expanding the breadth of discussion on specific themes and addressing data availability and concerns could improve the paper’s overall impact and field contribution.

# 6.5.3 RECOMMENDATIONS

Several important recommendations are made to direct future research and clinical practice in the identification and management of AD in light of the knowledge gained from the evaluation and analysis. First and foremost, further validation research is desperately needed in order to assess the efficacy, dependability, and practicality of ML and DL algorithms in the diagnosis of AD. To guarantee the stability and generality of diagnostic models across various contexts and demographics, validation studies should include a variety of patient groups, including people with demographic traits, illness stages, and comorbidities [33].

Moreover, longitudinal research that integrates multimodal data—neuroimaging, genetic biomarkers, fluid, and clinical evaluations—can offer important insights into the course of a disease and aid in the general improvement of diagnostic algorithms.

By following these suggestions, scientists and medical professionals help create new approaches to diagnosis and treatment that tackle the many problems that AD presents. The field can progress toward more precise, timely, and individualized methods of AD detection and care via teamwork and multidisciplinary research, improving the lives of those impacted by this debilitating disorder.
