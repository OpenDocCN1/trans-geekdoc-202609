# An Enhanced Technique for Predicting Autism Spectrum Disorder

Voting involves aggregating predictions from various individual models (like decision trees, support vector machines, or logistic regression) and making a final decision based on the majority vote or average prediction. This method reduces the likelihood of errors that might arise from any single model's bias or variance, thus improving the overall accuracy and robustness of the predictions.

AdaBoost (adaptive boosting), on the other hand, works by sequentially applying a learning algorithm to different weighted versions of the data. In each iteration, the model focuses on the data points that were misclassified in the previous round, continuously improving its accuracy. This adaptive nature of AdaBoost allows it to excel in recognizing complex patterns within the data, which is particularly valuable in diagnosing ASD, where symptoms and presentations can vary widely across individuals.

These advanced algorithms can be applied to vast datasets that include genetic, neuroimaging data, and clinical histories. By analyzing comprehensive datasets and observations, models can identify subtle and complex patterns associated with ASD that may not be apparent through conventional observation. For example, certain genetic markers or combinations of behavioral traits might correlate with specific subsets of ASD, enabling more tailored and personalized diagnosis. The application of data science in ASD prediction not only enhances the accuracy of but also accelerates the diagnostic process. Traditional methods of diagnosing ASD are often time-consuming, involving lengthy assessments and observations that can span months or years. In contrast, data-driven models can analyze extensive datasets and provide diagnostic insights in a fraction of the time, making it possible to identify ASD at earlier stages. Early diagnosis is crucial because it allows the timely initiation of interventions that can improve outcomes and quality of life for individuals with ASD.

Moreover, the insights gained from data science can lead to a more nuanced understanding of the ASD spectrum. By identifying patterns and subgroups within the ASD population, these models can help refine the definition of the spectrum. This could lead to more precise categorization of ASD subtypes, facilitating more tailored and effective therapeutic interventions. For example, individuals with specific genetic profiles or behavioral patterns might benefit from customized therapeutic approaches that address their needs. In improving diagnostic accuracy and speed, data science has the potential to contribute to the development of new strategies. By analyzing treatment outcomes in large populations, data-driven models can identify which interventions are most effective for specific subgroups of individuals with ASD. This personalized approach to therapy could lead to more effective treatments that are tailored to the individual's unique profile, rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.

# 8.1.4 OBJECTIVE OF THE RESEARCH

The objective of this research is to enhance the prediction of ASD by combining the strengths of the vote and AdaBoost (VO-ADA) models. The main aims include integrating these models to create a more effective ASD prediction model, comparing its performance with traditional models to demonstrate its superiority, and improving overall accuracy and reliability. The research also seeks to evaluate, contribute new insights to the ASD prediction field, and assess the practical applicability of the combined approach in real-world scenarios. Ultimately, the goal is to provide a more dependable and accurate model for early ASD diagnosis and intervention.
