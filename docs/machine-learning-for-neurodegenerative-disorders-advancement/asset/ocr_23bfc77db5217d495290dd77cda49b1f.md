# 1.3.1.5 Computational Neuroscience

Computational neuroscience employs mathematical models and simulations to capture the complex dynamics of the brain. Cognitive neuroscience bridges psychology and neuroscience, studying the neural basis of cognitive functions such as perception, memory, language, and decision-making. It aims to understand how mental models are implemented in the brain and how neural activity correlates with cognitive tasks (Volmonté, 2023). Techniques corresponding to functional MRI, EEG, and neuropsychological testing are used to investigate the brain regions and networks involved in various functions. This field provides insights into how the brain enables behavior and cognition (Choi et al., 2023). Computational models help predict neural responses, and simulate brain functions, shedding light on mechanisms that might be challenging to investigate solely through experiments.

# 1.3.1.6 Neurodevelopment and Neurogenetics

Computational neuroscience uses mathematical models, theoretical analysis, and computer simulations to understand the principles of nervous system. It aims to create models that can explain neural behavior and how neurons and neural networks process information. This field integrates data from molecular, cellular, and neuroscience to develop computational frameworks that map brain activity (Ellwood, 2023). Applications include the development of artificial neural networks and the study of neural coding and information processing. Neurodevelopmental neuroscience focus on the formation and maturation of the nervous system, involving embryonic brain development, stem cell differentiation, and the establishment of connections between neurons (Kawai et al., 2023). Neurogenetics focuses on the genetic factors contributing to neurological disorders, brain structure, and functional variations.

# 1.3.1.7 Neurobiology of Disease

The neurobiology of disease investigates the biological mechanisms underlying neurological and psychiatric disorders. It seeks to identify the cause and molecular changes that lead to conditions like Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases, epilepsy, and depression. Researchers use advanced imaging to study disease pathology and potential therapeutic strategies. This field bridges neuroscience and clinical research, aiming to translate findings into treatments. The pathological mechanisms underlying neurological disorders such as Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, epilepsy, and schizophrenia have explored (Ellwood, 2023). Researchers have investigated how molecular, cellular, and network disruptions contribute to disease progression, aiming to develop effective treatments and interventions.

# 1.3.1.8 Neuroimaging and Neuroinformatics

Neuroimaging and neuroinformatics utilize the use of advanced imaging technologies and computational tools to study brain structure and function. Neuroimaging techniques, for example, position emission tomography, diffusion tensor imaging, fMRI, and MRI provide detailed details of the brain and allow for the visualization of neural activity. Neuroinformatics develops the development of databases, algorithms, and computational models to analyze and interpret neuroimaging data (Rodrigues et al., 2023). These fields are essential for understanding brain connectivity, mapping brain regions, and how brain structure relates to function. Neuroimaging techniques, including MEG, EEG, and fMRI, allow researchers to visualize and measure brain activity.
