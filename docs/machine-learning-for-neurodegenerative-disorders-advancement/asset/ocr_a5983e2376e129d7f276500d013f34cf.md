# A Novel Machine Learning Model

A newly developed convolutional neural network (CNN) possesses the unique capability to elucidate its decision-making process by highlighting specific local areas within input samples that hold crucial information for classification [10]. Leveraging separable convolutions, this network boosts a streamlined design to process EEG signals in a spatial-temporal sequence. Impressively, the model demonstrates an average accuracy of 78.35% over 11 subjects in leave-one-out drowsiness recognition, surpassing conventional baseline methods, which typically range from 53.4% to 72.68%, and state-of-the-art deep learning techniques, which achieve between 63.90% and 65.78% [6]. Visualization techniques underscore the model's proficiency in identifying biologically explicable features within EEG signals, such as alpha spindles, serving as robust indicators of drowsiness across diverse subjects. Additionally, by using visualization, the method explores the causes of misclassified samples, prompting conversations about possible approaches to improve accuracy [11]. This research delineates a promising trajectory by employing deep learning models uncovering meaningful patterns linked to various mental states within intricate EEG signals.

Driver drowsiness poses a significant risk to road safety, impairing driving performance and attention. Our focus centers on predicting drowsiness using EEG signals, previously shown to detect early signs of drowsiness [12]. To achieve this, we introduced an encoding-decoding framework, leveraging EEG signals to estimate Percentage of Eye Closure (PERCLOS), a widely adopted measure of drowsiness [13]. We established a linear relationship between EEG features and PERCLOS, estimating unknown parameters via the least-square method. Additionally, we defined the state transition of PERCLOS, independent of EEG data, and estimated another set of parameters using the least-square approach. This approach introduced a novel dynamic modeling solution, utilizing EEG signals to estimate the instantaneous level of drowsiness, anchored by PERCLOS as the ground truth. In the decoding phase, a Bayesian filtering solution was employed by 13 drivers, we observed robust and reliable performance in performing PERCLOS, evaluating our framework using a dataset of 18 drivers showcasing an average Root Mean Square Error of 0.117 and an average high probability density of 62.5% [1]. Notably, approximately 73% and 66% of selected delta and theta waves, respectively, indicated an increasing trend as PERCLOS intensified during driving tests. The proposed method offers a robust and real-time solution for drowsiness estimation, paving the way for EEG-based measures to be integrated into drowsiness detection systems.

Driver drowsiness, stemming from extended work hours, sleep deprivation, and fatigue, poses a widespread issue. Fatigued drivers akin to exhibit slower reaction times, diminished attention, awareness, and vehicle control, leading to numerous traffic accidents, some of which are fatal. To avert such accidents, early driver alertness is crucial. Drowsiness prediction varies when focusing solely on facial cues, neglecting other fatigue factors like tilting, blinking, and yawning. This proposed solution leverages Internet of Things (IoT) and advanced neural networks—an enhancement of short-term memory (LSTM), VGNN, InceptionV3, and DenseNet. It addresses these limitations by employing transfer learning, magnifying various signs of drowsiness to bolster accuracy across diverse driving conditions. Upon detecting fatigue, our IoT [14] module triggers a warning, emitting both a message and a sound via a Japanese nano monitoring system. Experimentally, utilizing deep neural networks achieves an impressive accuracy of up to 98% [15]. Importantly, it has been validated in scenarios involving masks and glasses usage, a practical relevance after the COVID-19 pandemic's mask mandates in public spaces.
