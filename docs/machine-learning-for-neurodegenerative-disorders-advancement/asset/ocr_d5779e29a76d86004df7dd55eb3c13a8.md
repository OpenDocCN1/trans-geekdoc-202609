# 5.2 BACKGROUND STUDIES

Freezing of gait is a common condition in the later stages of PD, in which patients experience an involuntary and unexpected condition of immobility with the sensation of being stuck to the ground. These conditions can be triggered by stress, anxiety, complex surroundings, and attempting to multitask. During FoG conditions, people are more prone to stumbling or losing their balance, increasing the risk of falls and injuries. As a result of the failure of falling in public, mobility and independence are reduced, causing isolation and emotional distress, lowering the overall quality of life for people with PD.

It is worth noting the likelihood of experiencing FoG in PD among individuals [8]. FoG does not affect all patients, and when it does, its frequency and severity vary. FoG is determined by a variety of factors, including the stage of PD, the presence of other symptoms, and differences in disease prevalence [9]. However, FoG is more common in advanced stages of PD, although it can appear at any stage.

A clinical assessment is typically required to diagnose PD, which includes a meticulous history review, physical examination to identify motor symptoms, and evaluation of the patient's response to dopaminergic medications. Clinical observation, on the other hand, is critical in the diagnosis of FoG. Healthcare providers meticulously track their patients' gait and motor performance by auditing the frequency of FoG conditions utilizing wearable sensors or other objective measurement tools. The diagnosis phase is critical because it guides the tailoring of treatment strategies for Parkinson's patients [10]. Accurate diagnosis enables healthcare providers to comprehend the unique nature of the FoG, which facilitates early and pre-diagnosis, which is critical for optimizing treatment and improving mobility. The diagnosis of FoG is still a challenging task in PD patients. The application of emerging technologies and artificial intelligence has enabled the development of predictive models that have the potential to effectively manage and reduce the frequency of FoG episodes, improving the quality of life for people living with PD [11].
