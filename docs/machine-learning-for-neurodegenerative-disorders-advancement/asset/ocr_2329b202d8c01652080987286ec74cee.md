# 2.6.2 DEEP LEARNING STRUCTURES

Deep structures represent a class of neural network architectures characterized by their depth and hierarchical organization, enabling the automatic extraction of intricate features from complex datasets. These structures have revolutionized various domains, including neuroimaging, by capturing high-level abstractions in data representation [37]. CNNs are prominent examples of deep learning structures widely applied in image analysis tasks, particularly in neuroimaging studies such as brain segmentation and disease classification [37]. RNNs are another class of deep learning structures designed for sequential data, suitable for tasks involving time-series imaging data, such as MRI analysis over time. The versatility and adaptability of deep learning structures, with their capacity to automatically learn hierarchical representations, make them powerful tools for unraveling complex patterns within neuroimaging datasets [44].

# 2.6.3 TIME-SERIES ANALYSIS IN fMRI

Time-series analysis in fMRI is a crucial aspect of neuroimaging that involves the examination of brain activity over time [10]. fMRI captures the blood oxygen level-dependent (BOLD) signal, providing a temporal record of neural activity. Time-series analysis techniques aim to extract meaningful information from these temporal signals, enabling the investigation of dynamic patterns and connectivity within the brain.

One common approach in time-series analysis is seed-based correlation analysis, where the correlation between this seed and other brain regions is computed [30]. This method provides insights into functional patterns and has widely used to explore resting-state networks. Another technique is ICA, which decomposes fMRI data into spatially and temporally independent components, revealing distinct networks of activated brain regions [45].

Dynamic connectivity analysis goes beyond static connectivity by examining how patterns change over time. This is particularly relevant for understanding the temporal dynamics of brain networks during cognitive tasks or resting. Additionally, graph-based analyses assess the brain's functional connectivity as a complex network, characterizing the organization of nodes (brain regions) and edges (connectivity) over time.

Time-series analysis in fMRI plays a crucial role in unveiling the temporal dynamics of brain function and connectivity. These techniques contribute to our understanding of cognitive processes, neural plasticity, and the altered patterns associated with neurological and psychiatric disorder [46].

# 2.6.3.1 Dynamic Time Warping

Dynamic time warping (DTW) is a powerful technique in time-series analysis that allows for the comparison and alignment of time-varying sequences, even when they exhibit temporal distortions. Originally introduced in the context of speech recognition, DTW has widespread applications in various fields, including neuroscience and neuroimaging. DTW addresses the challenge of comparing time-series data with different patterns, making it particularly relevant for fMRI studies.

In neuroimaging, DTW has been applied to align and fMRI time series, allowing researchers to uncover temporal patterns and synchronize functional activations across different brain regions. For instance, the work by Liao et al. [47] used DTW to align fMRI time series obtained from multiple subjects, facilitating the identification of consistent patterns in brain activity over time, providing valuable insights into functional connectivity and dynamic networks [47].

One of the strengths of DTW is in its flexibility to handle variable temporal lengths and warping, making it well suited for studying dynamic interactions in the brain. Moreover, DTW has been applied to align time-series data in other neuroscientific domains, such as EEG and MEG, showcasing its versatility in analyzing temporal dynamics across different modalities [48].
