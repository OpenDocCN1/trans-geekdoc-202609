## 4.7.3 GOVERNMENTAL AND INSTITUTIONAL SUPPORT

With respect to the crucial role of governmental and institutional support, this support is fundamental for advancing the research, development, and application of AI in this field. Government bodies, research institutions, and healthcare organizations play key roles in providing the necessary infrastructure, funding, regulatory frameworks, and collaborative environments needed for the successful integration and advancement of AI technologies [158]. Governmental support is pivotal in fostering an environment conducive to AI-driven research. This can take the form of funding for initiatives, which are essential for supporting the extensive research and development efforts in drug discovery [159]. For example, initiatives such as the BRAIN Initiative in the United States, which aims to revolutionize our understanding of the human brain, provide significant funding for research that employs AI and ML. These investments can accelerate the development of AI tools and algorithms for analyzing complex neural data, potentially aiding in the discovery of new drugs and therapies for neurodegenerative diseases [160]. Moreover, governments can play a key role in establishing regulatory frameworks that support and guide the ethical and safe use of AI in healthcare. Policies and guidelines that address data privacy, patient safety, and ethical considerations in AI are necessary to build public trust and ensure that AI technologies are used responsibly and effectively in healthcare settings. For instance, the European Union's GDPR provides a framework for handling patient data, which is critical in research where large datasets are utilized [161].

Institutional support is also vital for the advancement of AI in drug discovery. Academic and research institutions foster interdisciplinary collaboration, bringing together experts in AI, neuroscience, pharmacology, and other relevant fields. These collaborations can lead to innovative approaches and solutions in drug discovery and repurposing. For example, partnerships between AI research laboratories and neurology departments can facilitate the sharing of expertise and resources, leading to more effective AI models that can predict disease progression and identify potential drug targets [162]. Additionally, healthcare organizations and hospitals provide valuable data for research, contributing to large-scale databases for testing algorithms. Collaborations with these organizations and AI researchers ensure that the algorithms are developed and tested in real-world settings, increasing their reliability and applicability in clinical environments [146]. This support also has the potential to lead to breakthroughs in treatments and therapies, ultimately improving the lives of patients with neurodegenerative disorders.

## 4.7.4 AI INTERPRETABILITY AND EXPLAINABILITY

As AI systems become more sophisticated and integral to the drug discovery process, understanding how these systems make decisions becomes crucial. The interpretability and explainability of AI models are essential for ensuring trust, transparency, and compliance with regulatory standards in the pharmaceutical industry [163]. AI interpretability refers to the ability to understand and articulate the internal mechanisms of AI models, and repurposing means being able to trace how AI algorithms arrive at their conclusions or predictions about potential drug targets, compound efficacy, or patient responses [13]. For example, if an AI model identifies a new compound as a potential drug for Alzheimer’s disease, researchers and regulators need to understand the basis of this prediction. This might involve analyzing features of the compound model focused and how these relate to known mechanisms of the disease. Ensuring that AI models are interpretable not only aids in validating their predictions but also in refining the models further.

Explainability, on the other hand, is to present the strengths of AI models in understandable terms to end-users who may not have technical expertise. This aspect is crucial for the integration of AI in clinical settings, where healthcare professionals need to make informed decisions based on AI recommendations [151]. For instance, if an AI system suggests a particular drug for treating Parkinson’s disease, clinicians need to understand the rationale behind this suggestion to trust and act on it. Patients also need to be able to explain these decisions transparently and understandably.

One of the challenges in improving AI interpretability is the complexity of deep learning models, which are often considered “black boxes” due to their intricate network of algorithms. Addressing this challenge involves developing methodologies that make these models more transparent and their outputs understandable. Efforts in this direction include the development of visualization tools that illustrate how data flow through a network and impact outcomes and techniques such as feature importance analysis, which identifies which features might significantly affect the model’s predictions [164]. Regulatory requirements also drive the push for greater interpretability and explainability. Regulatory agencies such as the FDA are increasingly focusing on the need for clear explanations of how AI tools arrive at their conclusions, especially when these tools are used in drug development and patient care. These require that AI models are not only effective but also reliable and safe for use in medical decision-making.

## 4.8 CONCLUSIONS

This chapter underscores the transformative impact of AI in drug discovery and repurposing for neurodegenerative disorders. Key findings highlight the role of AI in accelerating the identification of drugs and therapeutic targets through advanced techniques such as molecular docking and predictive modeling. The efficacy of AI in drug repurposing has also been notable, as it allows the efficient identification of existing drugs that could be redirected to treat neurodegenerative diseases. Challenges such as ethical concerns, privacy, and security are crucial, emphasizing the need for responsible AI use in healthcare. Additionally, the computational demands and necessity for extensive clinical validation of AI predictions have been identified as significant hurdles. For future research, encouraging collaborative efforts across various disciplines and enhancing data sharing and integration are recommended to develop comprehensive AI models. Emphasizing the interpretability of AI models is crucial for building trust among healthcare professionals. Investment in specialized computational infrastructure is essential for supporting unbiased research. Developing robust ethical and regulatory frameworks is imperative for addressing privacy and ethical implications in applications. Expanding AI research to cover a broader spectrum of diseases can maximize its impact on healthcare. Sust support from governmental and institutional bodies through funding and policy will be pivotal in this field. Conclusion offers promising avenues for treating neurodegenerative disorders, as it is poised to revolutionize drug discovery and repurposing. The path forward involves not only technological advancements but also a unified and coordinated effort to address computational, ethical, and regulatory challenges. With continued support and collaboration, AI-driven approaches can lead to significant breakthroughs, potentially improving outcomes for patients with these challenging conditions.
