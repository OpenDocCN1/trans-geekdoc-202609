# 11.3.2.3 Proactive Communication

Even as the biophysical research in neurodegenerative disease moves into a new age of game-changing technological advancements such as ML and artificial intelligence [72], one aspect traditionally managed less efficiently is proactive communication. Open and transparent communication with stakeholders cannot be considered merely good but for developing the trust that underpins collaboration and is needed to speed progress toward treatments [72]. Earning trust is crucial to further the future of neurodegenerative research. Transparent communication in research findings, discoveries, and limitations enhances public knowledge [74] and promotes involvement in clinical trials and other studies. Proactive communication, on the other hand, keeps misinformation and fears at bay for new technologies by enabling access to the right information in a way that the public can understand clearly. Dialogue with patients and their families provides researchers with a rich source of lived experiences and priorities that exist among community, and as concerns [75], making it patient-centered. It also promotes open data, methods, and results sharing across institutions and disciplines to help secure communication on ethical issues, risks, and safeguards in research also get realized as it ensures that the studies are conducted ethically [86]. By engaging in open communication and ethical practices between researchers and the public, we may find means of doing better research for on-target results against neurodegenerative diseases [77].
