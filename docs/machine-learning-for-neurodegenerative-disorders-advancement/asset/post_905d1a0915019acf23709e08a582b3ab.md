### ##7.1 INTRODUCTION

The growing older population is the primary demographic characteristics of developed countries, facing the rise of various age-related difficulties. Alzheimer’s disease (AD) [1] is one of the most common disorders and a significant cause of disability and loss of self-sufficiency in the elderly. The worldwide battle against AD faces a significant challenge in the early detection of AD at the pre-clinical or early phases (mild cognitive impairment [MCI]), especially in light of the potential benefits of both secondary prevention and disease-modifying treatment. Early diagnosis also provides AD [2] patients and their carers with the chance to plan and recover the proper to help control symptoms. It is believed that 75% of people living with dementia do not have a diagnosis. Despite this, the diagnostic coverage rate is still inadequate. The development of precise, user-friendly screening instruments for the early diagnosis of AD and MCI is becoming more and popular in this field. With an estimated 50 million people affected by the disease and about 10 million new cases annually globally, the World Health Organization (WHO) declared it a public health emergency.

The process of early disease diagnosis is time-consuming and expensive since it requires extensive data collection, the use of advanced prediction algorithms, the involvement of an experienced physician. Automated systems can be employed in decision support systems because they are less prone to mistakes and more accurate than human assessment. Nowadays, researchers are using machine learning (ML) and deep learning-based classifiers for early AD prediction.

Nasrolahadeh et al. [3] use genetic programming (GP) as data-driven evolutionary computation-based model creating a computer-aided diagnosis system for detecting AD. This approach uses a voting-based system to choose a group of the most discriminant features that result in the maximum accuracy of the final classification.

Yildiz et al. [4] presented a method that favors for majority vote, feature selection (FS), classification, and multilevel feature extraction (FE)—a local feature-extraction method built on the binary tree—extracted features from audio signals. Chi2 and the iterative neighborhood component analysis (INCA) were employed to choose the features, $k$-Nearest neighbor (k-NN) and support vector machine (SVM) were used to classify the desired features, and the iterative majority voting (IMV) approach applied. Ultimately, the results were validated using a novel method called leave one out (LORO) cross-validation (CV).

Xia et al. [5] introduced an approach for diagnosing AD by categorizing the resting-state electroencephalogram (EEG) of AD, MCI, and healthy control (HC). The deep pyramidal neural network (CNN) is the foundation of this model. It is a one-dimensional CNN model proposed for the three-class classification of EEG of AD, MCI, HC.

To help families understand how their loved evolves and take preventive action, Liu et al. [6] developed an approach that uses spectrogram characteristics from speech data to identify AD.

Zeng et al. [7] proposes a framework for diagnosing AD, including principal component analysis, FE-processing of MRI images, and SVMs. To optimize the SVM parameters, a switching particle swarm optimization approach is put forth. ADNI dataset, this model is successfully applied to AD and MCI.

Murthy and Koogaludi [8] aim to put forth the vocal and non-local regions by incorporating innovative structure-based features. Formant attack slope (FAS), pitch, shimmer, formant heights base-to-peak (FH1), peak-to-peak (FH2), formant angles at peak (FA1), valley (FA2). Mel-frequency coefficients (MFCCs) and linear prediction coefficients (LPCCs) are other features that have been considered. Classifiers like random forests (RF), SVM, and artificial neural networks (ANN) have been taken into choice. The pertinent characteristics considering all dimensions; this process is known as genetic algorithm-based feature selection (GAFS).

Zhang et al. [9] present a framework for diagnosing AD, including FE, fuzzy k-NN (Fk-NN) algorithm, and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) image pre-processing. This aspect of the framework is in how it optimizes the parameters of FK-NN by integrating the Sobol sequence and stochastic fractal search (SFS) through an enhanced Harris Hawks optimization (HHO) algorithm. Using the Sobol sequence, the HHO approach improves the overall quality of the starting-population, and the SFS mechanism boosts the algorithm's ability to escape the optimum solution.

Shukla et al. [10] have the positron emission tomography (PET) and MRI modalities to implement result visualization for AD prediction. To ascertain which region is most crucial for the identification of AD in comparison to other subsets, it analyzes the subcortical structures using the ensemble model (EM) and additional ML techniques.

A deep learning-based method for identifying AD is proposed by Janghel and Rathore [11] using the ADNI database of Alzheimer's patients. Before the VGG-16 architecture of the CNN for FE, it to 2d sequencing and image scaling. SVM, linear discriminate, k-means clustering, and decision tree (DT) classifiers are utilized in classification.

Clinical trials, a text-based modification of the audio recordings of Alzheimer's patients, were employed by Khan et al. [12]. An approach to linguistic FS via hybrid swarm intelligence is presented, which uses the PSO, DO, and GWO algorithms to extract a composite feature set. Furthermore, a model based on transfer learning is known as HSI-LFS-BERT is put forth. It combines the BERT algorithm with the HSI-LFS approach. Nowadays, of researchers are interested in applying multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) in the field of healthcare systems.
