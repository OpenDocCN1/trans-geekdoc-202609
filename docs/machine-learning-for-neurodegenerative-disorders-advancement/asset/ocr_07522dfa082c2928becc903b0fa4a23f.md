+   * Speech features: Patients suffering from PD frequently experience speech difficulties, such as reduced voice volume and poorly articulated speech. The extraction of important characteristics from speech data includes pitch, loudness, speech rate, and articulatory precision. Speech impairments can be assessed using this comprehensive evaluation.
* Kinetics features: Kinetic characteristics include joint forces, torques, and center of mass (COP) parameters. These characteristics aid in the evaluation of the biomechanical components of motion and equilibrium, providing useful information about muscular weakness, postural instability, and motor control.
* Frequency and time-domain-domain features: The frequency components of various signals, such as power spectra obtained from accelerometers or electromyography data, can be analyzed to detect motor abnormalities and tremor features in PD. Time-domain features, which are typically extracted using techniques such as wavelet or Fourier transform, provide insights into the temporal characteristics of the signals and aid in the detection of patterns or irregularities.

# ### 5.3.3 MODEL DEVELOPMENT

Model development is a crucial step in which various machine learning techniques are analyzed for designing an efficient diagnostic system for FoG in PD. FoG is a disturbing symptom of PD, which is often assessed objectively. Samà et al. have identified accelerometer-based features that are utilized with machine learning-based classifiers, achieving 91.7% specificity and 87.4% specificity in real-life settings [23]. Moore et al. [24] have validated ambulatory FoG monitors using ankle-mounted sensors with an accuracy of 89% (10% false positives), potentially improving FoG management in PD patients.
Detecting freezing in Parkinson's patients is crucial for fall prevention, and wrist-mounted sensors have a 90% FoG detection rate, which has improved user acceptance over lower limbs. Wrist sensors provide a feasible alternative for FoG detection without compromising accuracy [25]. Tripoliti et al. have detected FoG in Parkinson's patients using body-worn sensors and a four-stage methodology. The evaluation with different sensor configurations revealed a method achieving 98.74% specificity, 81.94% specificity, 98.6% AUC, and 96.11% accuracy with random forests classification [26].
