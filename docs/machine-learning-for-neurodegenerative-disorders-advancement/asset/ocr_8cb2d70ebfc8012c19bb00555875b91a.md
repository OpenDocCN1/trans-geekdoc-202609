# 1.5 NEUROIMAGING AND PATTERN RECOGNITION

Neuroimaging and pattern recognition are intertwined fields that have significantly advanced our understanding of brain function, cognitive processes, and neurological disorders. Neuroimaging involves capturing images of the brain's structure and activity, while pattern recognition uses ML techniques to identify meaningful patterns within these images (Zhang et al., 2023). Together, these findings provide insights into complex neural processes and aid in diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment planning for various neurological conditions. Neuroimaging techniques allow us to visualize the brain's structure, connectivity, and activity. Key techniques include:

+   - Functional magnetic imaging (fMRI): fMRI measures changes in blood flow associated with neural activity (Azad et al., 2023). It provides insights into brain regions activated during specific tasks and helps map functional networks.
- Electroencephalography: EEG records electrical activity at the scalp, reflecting neural firing. It is used to study brain rhythms and cognitive processes and diagnose disorders as epilepsy.
- Magnetencephalography: MEG measures the magnetic fields generated by neural activity. It has high temporal resolution and helps localize brain regions in different tasks (Cascone et al., 2023).
- Structural resonance imaging: sMRI provides detailed images of brain anatomy, allowing the visualization of brain regions and structural abnormalities.
- Diffusion tensor imaging: DTI captures the brain's white matter tracts, enabling the mapping of neural connectivity and understanding of information flow.

Pattern recognition and neuroimaging are powerful tools that allow us to delve into the brain's inner workings. They contribute to neuroscience research and clinical applications, opening new avenues for understanding brain function and addressing neurological challenges (Currey et al., 2023).

+   - Feature extraction: Features are extracted from neuroimaging data, for example, in fMRI or EEG channels. These features represent the data's characteristics and are used as input for pattern recognition algorithms.
- Algorithm selection: Different models are chosen based on the nature of the data and the research question. Standard algorithms include support vector machines, random forests, CNNs, and RNNs (Turrisi et al., 2023).
- Training and classification: The algorithm is trained on labeled data, where the patterns of interest are associated with specific conditions or outcomes. Once trained, the algorithm can classify new data into predefined categories such as healthy vs. diseased states.
