Implementing standardized protocols is challenging: Individual goals must be reconciled with collective benefit, and no undue rigidity should exist [36]. However, the potential benefits to patients and researchers are huge. # 11.3.1.3 Public–Private Partnerships

Eradicating neurodegenerative diseases is a challenge that requires enormous resources, knowledge and skills from different angles. Public–private partnerships bridge the gap between the public sector research infrastructure and private industry development capacity [67]. These partnerships have the potential to speed up research, and bring them quickly into treatment use that can enhance patients' lives [70]. In the future, pharma companies, and governments are advised to use advocacy groups to accelerate research and development of effective treatments in neurodegenerative disorders (Figure 11.7).

The isolated nature of academic research and pharmaceutical development has significantly impeded progress in neurodegenerative disorders [37]. Public-private partnerships offer a promising solution by breaking down silos and promoting collaboration between these sectors. Picture academia bringing the underlying scientific and dense data resources, pharma companies offering drug-development competencies, governments providing funding and ethical frameworks [13], and advocacy groups ensuring the voice of patients is being heard, building toward one goal: accelerating research and development for patients [14]. This context, which is truly game-changing for rare disease research and advocacy with the most potential [30], enables accelerating joining hands-on-translation of bench-to-bedside discovery of cost-efficient, risk-sharing, patient-centered innovation that addresses the actual needs of those living with the disease. PPPs are worth consideration, given their unambiguous ability to expedite progress and inspire despite hardships. Rather is to dismantle the silos and envision a future in which collaboration will allow unprecedented advances against neurodegenerative diseases [13].
