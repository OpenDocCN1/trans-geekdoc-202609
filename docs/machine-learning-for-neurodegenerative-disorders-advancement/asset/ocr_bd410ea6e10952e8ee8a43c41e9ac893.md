# 11.3.2.1 Explainable AI

ML can potentially help how we diagnose, treat, and even prevent neurodegenerative disorders. Despite advances, a major challenge persists: the lack of transparency in many models. Internal mechanisms and decision-making processes often remain hidden, making understanding and trusting their outputs difficult [70]. Without transparency, it is difficult to trust the variables used in a model; interpretability and, clinical adoption are hindered by a burgeoning field known as XAI, aiming to illuminate the black boxes, opening up new avenues for improved and reliable ML applications in neurodegenerative research [72].

One of the most critical challenges in healthcare is that ML models are opaque, which makes it difficult to detect bias and fairness violations [73]. If we do not know how the model arrives at its conclusions, this can make biases in the input data or algorithm itself cause patients from some groups to make worse decisions. Interpretability the limits of graph-based neural networks (GBNs) and is crucial for trust and adoption [74]. The rationale for AI-driven recommendations is to acquire clarity in trusting and implementing decisions in favor of patient care. However, that is where XAI comes to the rescue. XAI transforms how models are understood by exposing the hidden patterns and relationships in data, helping reveal decision paths, promoting trust, and enabling the discovery of scientific knowledge within techniques [75]. This new insight could be used to discover new drug targets, thus improving health and the safe use of AI in one of the most important areas [76].

XAI in neurodegenerative research is where the future shines bright. Overcoming these obstacles and creating the next generation of explanations will demystify black-box models with XAI to ensure transparency and ultimately expedite ELSI, by way of legal, social, and ethical guidelines for those who fight against such formidable diseases [77].
