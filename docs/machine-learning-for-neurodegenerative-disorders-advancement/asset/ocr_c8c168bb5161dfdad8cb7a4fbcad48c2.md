# 4.3.2 TARGET IDENTIFICATION

The identification of appropriate targets, such as specific proteins, RNA molecules, or signaling pathways, is a foundational step in the drug discovery process. The efficacy of a drug largely depends on its ability to interact effectively with its intended target, which is typically a key component in the pathology of a disease [98]. In traditional drug discovery approaches, target identification is often labor intensive and time consuming and relies heavily on experimental biology and a deep understanding of disease mechanisms. However, with the integration of AI technologies, the landscape of drug target identification is undergoing a significant transformation [9].

Machine learning algorithms in AI are particularly adept at extracting vast amounts of genetic and molecular data to predict potential drug targets. These algorithms can analyze complex datasets that include genetic expression profile, protein-protein interaction network information, and information on various aspects of molecular pathways. AI can suggest novel targets that have been overlooked by traditional methods [99]. From genome-wide association studies (GWAS) to genes linked to specific diseases, genes or the proteins could be potential targets for new drugs [100]. In addition to revealing new targets, AI can also evaluate existing targets, perhaps identifying additional misses or suggesting new ways to modify them. This revelation could lead to drug repurposing, where existing drugs are applied to new therapeutic areas [12].

The power of AI target identification lies not only in its ability to analyze data but also in its potential to predict the outcomes of drug-target interactions [101]. For example, sophisticated AI models can model how molecules interact with a target at the atomic level, providing insights into the potential efficiency and specificity of a drug. These predictions can help prioritize which targets to pursue in the early stages of drug development, reducing time and resources required for experimental validation [7]. Moreover, AI can identify targets for complex diseases such as cancer or neurodegenerative disorders, where multiple genes and pathways may be involved [102]. By analyzing data from various sources, including patient-derived data such as genetic profiles and clinical histories, AI models can identify key molecular or targets within these complex networks. These insights offer opportunities for more effective and personalized therapies [103]. As AI technologies continue to evolve and integrate with biomedicine, they have the potential to revolutionize the discovery and development of novel, more effective therapeutic agents.

# 4.3.3 MOLECULAR DOCKING SIMULATIONS

Molecular docking simulations are crucial in the drug discovery process, allowing scientists to predict how small molecules, such as drug candidates, interact with a biological target, typically a protein. The core objective of molecular docking is to determine how well a molecule fits into a receptor binding site, finding the correct key for a lock. This process involves not only identifying the orientation of the molecule (the pose) but also estimating how strongly the molecule binds to the target (the binding affinity) [104]. Traditionally, molecular docking has been a computationally intensive task, often requiring significant time and resources. This process involves scanning vast conformational spaces to predict the optimal binding pose and affinity, which necessitates significant computational power and sophisticated algorithms [105].

The advent of AI-driven approaches, especially deep learning models, has led to transformative changes in molecular docking simulations. These AI models are trained on large datasets of protein-ligand interactions and use this training to predict binding poses and affinities for new drug candidates.
