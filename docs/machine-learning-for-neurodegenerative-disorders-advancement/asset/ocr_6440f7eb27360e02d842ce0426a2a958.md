# 3.1.3 The Development of Natural Language Processing in Biomedical Research

In biomedical research, natural language processing (NLP) has progressed from simple information retrieval in the 1980s to deep learning models in the early 2010s. While machine learning introduced techniques like entity recognition, early systems from constrained text and rule-based approaches. Deep learning and pre-trained models as bidirectional encoder representations from transformers (BERT) were adopted, which further improved NLP's capabilities and made it possible to integrate it with genomics and big data. With advancements in language comprehension, artificial intelligence (AI), and ethical considerations in healthcare, NLP is now used to help literature reviews, clinical data extraction, drug discovery, and customized medicine. The use of NLP in biomedical research has revolutionized how researchers and deal with massive amounts of textual data. NLP's branch focuses on how computers and human language interact to understand, interpret, and produce natural language text. NLP proved to be a crucial technique in biomedical research for gleaning insights from a variety of biomedical literature, clinical notes, electronic health records (EHRs), and other text-based sources. In order to advance scientific understanding and enhance patient care, this overview examines the advancements and applications of NLP in biomedical research. Early attempts to create computer algorithms for information retrieval and language translation laid the foundations for NLP in biological research in the 1960s [8]. The necessity for text analysis like NLP has grown over the past few decades due to the accessibility of electronic databases and the explosive development of scientific literature.

By rapidly mining and aggregating huge volumes of scientific literature, researchers are able to extract percent data and spot patterns. The process of doing a literature review is sped up by automated iteration. The detection and extraction of critical information from unstructured text is made by NLP techniques like named entity recognition (NER) and relation extraction. This is done in biomedical research by pulling information from academic papers and medical records about genes, proteins, diseases, and their relationships. The use of NLP in drug development enables the identification of possible target drugs, the prediction of drug-drug interactions (DDIs), and the repurposing of already-approved medications for novel therapies [9]. NLP speeds up the process of finding new drugs and improves the effectiveness of research by sifting through enormous amounts of literature and databases. Through the extraction of percent from clinical notes and EHRs, NLP plays a significant role in clinical decision support systems. This aids medical professionals in making well-informed decisions for the individualized care of patients, identifying treatments, and diagnosing disorders. NLP is used to combine biomedical ontologies and create knowledge graphs that show the connections between different biological entities. These knowledge give a structured and ordered way to represent, aiding data fusion and research. NLP produce writing that is legible by humans from structured data, making it useful for communicating with patients, creating clinical reports, and summarizing research findings [10].
