Table 4.8, selected from ClinicalTrials.gov, illustrates the novel AI-driven drug discovery and repurposing efforts in Phase III clinical trials for ALS. One such trial, NCT00330681, edaravone (MCI-186), has now completed its Phase III trial. Edaravone sweeps free radicals, which harm damaged cells and exacerbate neurogenerative processes, suggesting that it has a neuroprotective effect on ALS. Another trial, NCT03127266, explores the efficacy and safety of tyrosine kinase inhibitor that targets mast cells and microglia, aiming to reduce neuroinflammation and positively modulate the neuronal environment in ALS patients. Additionally, the motor neuron disease systematic multimodal adaptive randomized trial (ND-SMART), under ID NCT0302870, is currently recruiting and testing a combination of mepentine, trazazone, and amantadine. Each of these drugs has a unique mechanism: mepentine helps prevent neuronal damage by blocking NMDA receptors and regulating glutamate overstimulation; trazazone, known for its antidepressant properties, may also improve mood and sleep by regulating serotonin; and amantadine, initially used as an antiviral, is believed to alleviate ALS symptoms such as muscle stiffness and fatigue, although its mechanism in ALS requires further exploration. These trials through glutamate neurotrans modulation, embody cutting-edge approaches for ALS treatment, demonstrating the potential of repurposed drugs and the significant role of AI in accelerating drug discovery processes.
