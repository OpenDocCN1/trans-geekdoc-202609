Bio-medical image segmentation deals with the representation of the anatomical ROI by partitioning the image into multiple regions. The World Health Organization (WHO) has categorized the tumors of the brain into 120 varieties, depending on their characteristics. MRI brain slices are recorded by scanning the subject number to undergo several stages of processing to extract the suite to be. MRI image processing needs an analysis of the image by the acquisition of the slice for clinical intervention. Imaging detail is made by its segmentation into several classes of pixels. Despite being handled by the framework, the process of acquiring an image involves first reading it and then converting its red, green, and blue (RGB) pixels into a grayscale scale, with all of pixels falling between 0 and 255. Before segmentation, the extracted image has undergone no filtering, as the images are vulnerable to Gaussian noise, speckle noise, and salt pepper noise [8]. Digital image noise removal is the ultimate goal of image scanning. For the most part, all of the image processing in a frame consists of methods for eliminating noise. Algorithms malfunction in a loud setting. Consequently, image filters are applied as a pre-processing approach.

Linear and nonlinear filters are both possible in image processing applications. When compared to linear filtering approaches, non-linear techniques typically yield better results. The reason for this is their ability to preserve a substantial amount of brain tumor size, which is crucial for the identification and diagnosis of tumors.

There is a need for a unique filter to eradicate the noise and make image processing more efficient. The mean or average filter in noise cancellation reduces the intensity variation in the median pixels. It is also an intuitive and simple method to smooth images. However, this filter produces false edges of noise is unable to suppress the Gaussian distribution of noise. Another filter that can be employed to denoise the image is the median filter. The median filter works to preserve the sharp edges. In contrast to the mean filter, the median filter takes only into account distributions in the middle and others information. The Gaussian filter works efficiently in eradicating Gaussian noise by implementing a kernel that blurs the image on calculating the weighted average that moves towards the center pixel values. This is more like a mean filter, smoothing and removing noise. But there occurs a loss of information and noise from an image while maintaining or even improving the edge strength. This is a common challenge in low-level vision.

One way to accomplish these objectives might be use nonlinear AD filtering [9]. The findings pertain to a scale–space analysis of diffusion filters that include nonlinear anisotropic models. It is shown that these models provide benefits over isotropic filters, since they employ an adaptive diffusion tensor rather than a scalar diffusivity.
