For example, an AI model identifies a new compound as a potential drug for Alzheimer’s disease, researchers and regulators need to understand the basis of this prediction. Might involve analyzing features of the compound model focused and how these relate to known mechanisms of the disease. Ensuring that AI models are interpretable not only aids in validating their predictions but also in refining the models further.

Explainability, on the other hand, is to present the strengths of AI models in understandable terms to end-users who may not have technical expertise. This aspect is crucial for the integration of AI in clinical settings, where healthcare professionals need to make informed decisions based on AI recommendations [151]. For instance, if an AI system suggests a particular drug for treating Parkinson’s disease, clinicians need to understand the rationale behind this suggestion to trust and act on it. Patients also need to be able to explain these decisions transparently and understandably.

One of the challenges in improving AI interpretability is the complexity of deep learning models, which are often considered “black boxes” due to their intricate network of algorithms. Addressing this challenge involves developing methodologies that make these models more transparent and their outputs understandable. Efforts in this direction include the development of visualization tools that illustrate how data flow through a network and impact outcomes and techniques such as feature importance analysis, which identifies which might significantly affect the model’s predictions [164]. Regulatory requirements also drive the push for greater interpretability and explainability. Regulatory agencies such as the FDA are increasingly focusing on the need for clear explanations of how AI tools arrive at their conclusions, especially when these tools are used in drug development and patient care. These require that AI models are not only effective but also reliable and safe for use in medical decision-making.
