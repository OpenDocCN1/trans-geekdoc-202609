Neuroimaging methods are often used in conjunction with clinical examinations of behavior and cognitive function to make the diagnosis of AD. There are several obstacles facing conventional approaches, such as diagnosing at a late stage when cognitive impairment is already severe and causing irreparable brain damage. AD is diagnosed with the use of medical imaging modalities such positron emission tomography (PET), computed tomography (CT), and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) [8, 9]. The higher capacity of MRI to see soft tissue structures and identify early biomarkers linked to AD allows it to be the favored method among them. AI has emerged as a significant tool in medical diagnostics, providing answers for the early diagnosis of AD. Neuroimaging data has been extensively analyzed via the use of ML techniques, namely support vector machines (SVMs). Nevertheless, ML models may have trouble testing data and frequently customized features [8]. DL models, on the other hand, handle end-to-end learning by automatically identifying relevant features in unprocessed data and streamlining the diagnostic procedure. Hybrid DL (HDL) and transfer learning (TL) techniques improve diagnostic models' generalizability and performance even further [4, 5].

Numerous studies have demonstrated the genetic components that contribute to the development and progression of AD, highlighting the significance that genetics and genomics play in this condition. Key genes that have been discovered as major risk factors include APP, PSEN1, PSEN2, APOE, and PSEN [3, 10]. Genomic information can reveal a person's vulnerability to AD and provide possible targets [6, 7]. Advances in AI, particularly DL, make it possible to create models that handle multimodal data, merging imaging, and clinical data to increase diagnosis accuracy. AI systems are able to recognize structural alterations in the brain that are associated with genetic abnormalities, offering a stronger foundation for early detection.

Our research aims to create a complete system for AD identification that combines AI with genomic and imaging data. The approach aims to increase diagnosis accuracy and reliability by utilizing the advantages of both genomics and imaging. The system comprises feature extraction using DL models, data preparation processes for genomic and imaging data, and a decision-making module that combines outputs from both sources to produce a final diagnosis.
