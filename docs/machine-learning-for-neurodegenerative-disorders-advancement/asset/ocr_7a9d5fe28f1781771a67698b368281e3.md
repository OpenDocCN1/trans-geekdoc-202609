For example, AI models have been used to identify specific proteins or genetic mutations that are more prevalent in Alzheimer's patients and could be used for diagnosis or to assess the risk of developing this disease. Early detection is crucial in Alzheimer's disease management, as it allows for earlier intervention and potentially more effective treatment [130].

Another critical application of AI in Alzheimer's disease research is target prediction. ML models are particularly adept at processing and interpreting complex biochemical and pharmacological data to predict potential targets for new therapeutics [131]. By analyzing data on other proteins, and genetic factors contribute to Alzheimer's disease pathology, these models can predict which proteins are most likely to contribute to the formation of amyloid plaques or tangles, which are characteristic of Alzheimer's disease. Identifying these targets is a crucial step in developing drugs that could halt or reverse the progression of the disease [17].

Additionally, AI-driven platforms significantly enhance the efficiency of compound screening in drug development for Alzheimer's disease. These platforms can rapidly perform in silico screening of vast libraries of compounds to identify those are most likely to be effective against the identified targets. This process not only accelerates the discovery of potential drug candidates but also reduces the cost and time invested in traditional drug screening methods. AI algorithms can evaluate a compound's structure, predict its interaction with the target, and assess its potential efficacy and safety profile. This approach enables researchers to focus their efforts on the most promising compounds, streamlining the path to clinical trials and eventual drug development [132].
