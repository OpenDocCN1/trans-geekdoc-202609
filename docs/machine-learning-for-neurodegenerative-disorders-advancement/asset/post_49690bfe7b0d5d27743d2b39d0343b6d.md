
# AI-Driven Drug Discovery and Repurposing

Drug repurposing, also known as drug repositioning, offers a more efficient alternative to the traditional drug discovery process. This approach involves testing existing drugs that have already been approved for one disease for their effectiveness against others. The advantage of drug repurposing lies in the fact that these drugs have already passed several key stages of the traditional pipeline, notably safety testing in humans. This approach can significantly reduce the time and cost to bring a drug to a market for a new use [86]. However, drug repurposing is not without its challenges. One major issue is that the pharmacokinetics – how it is absorbed, distributed, metabolized, and excreted in the body – may not be compatible with the new therapeutic use. Additionally, a safety test for one condition may have unforeseen side effects when used to treat a different condition. Furthermore, identifying new diseases often requires an in-depth understanding of the diseases and innovative screening strategies [88].

Despite these challenges, both drug discovery and drug repurposing continue to be vital strategies in the development of new therapies. The evolution of computational tools and informatics is continuously enhancing these processes. In recent years, there has been growing interest in integrating AI and ML techniques into these traditional approaches, aiming to increase their efficiency and success rate [9]. These advancements promise to accelerate the discovery of new drugs and the repurposing of existing drugs, potentially revitalizing the field of pharmaceutical development.

## 4.2.3 ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE FUNDAMENTALS

Artificial intelligence represents a groundbreaking shift in the approach to solving problem and data analysis, marking a significant departure from traditional computational methods. At its core, AI is the development of computer systems that can perform tasks that typically require human intelligence. These tasks include learning, reasoning, problem solving, and language understanding [13]. AI systems are designed to mimic human cognitive functions, allowing them to interpret complex data, learn from data, and make informed decisions based on accumulated knowledge. The essence of AI lies in its ability to process vast amounts of information quickly and efficiently, far surpassing the capacity of human cognition in specific tasks [7].

A crucial subset of AI is machine learning, which allows systems to learn and improve from experience without being explicitly programmed automatically. ML focuses on the development of computer programs that can learn and use from themselves. The process of learning begins with observations or data, such as examples, experience, or instruction. The ML algorithm then looks for patterns in the data and makes decisions in the future based on examples that have been processed. The more the data is exposed to, the more it learns and the more accurate it becomes over time [16]. This ability to learn from data makes ML particularly well suited for applications where programming and explicit rule-based algorithms are challenging or infeasible.

Deep learning, a more advanced subset of ML, involves neural networks with several layers (“deep”). These neural networks are inspired by the structure and function of the brain and are designed to mimic how humans learn and process information. By continuously analyzing data with a logical structure, deep learning algorithms attempt to make conclusions similar to those of humans. They are particularly effective at processing and learning from large amounts of unstructured data. Deep learning is instrumental in driving many recent advancements in AI, including speech recognition, image recognition, and natural language processing (NLP) [10].

In the context of drug discovery, AI, particularly ML and deep learning, holds immense potential. It can drastically expedite various aspects of the drug discovery process [89]. For instance, AI algorithms can predict how different drugs interact with biological targets, identify potential new targets for therapy, and suggest modifications to existing molecular structures to improve their efficacy or reduce side effects. This approach is particularly useful for understanding complex biological systems and disease pathways, where traditional experimental approaches can be time-consuming and costly [90]. By rapidly analyzing large datasets, AI can uncover patterns and relationships that might not be apparent to human researchers, opening up new avenues for therapeutic intervention. The ability of AI to rapidly process and analyze vast amounts of data makes it a powerful tool in the modern pharmaceutical industry, with the potential to significantly shorten the drug development cycle and provide effective treatments to patients quickly.
