RWD can be a useful adjunct to treat neurodegenerative diseases because of the information obtained and in terms of efficiency and cost [31]. RWD is more representative and, consequently, has higher external validity as it captures the heterogeneous realities of patients in practice environments, which makes these more generalizable (concerning sensory perception-related quality) and thus directly relevant for clinicians. This increased generalizability also reduces development costs as non-working interventions can be identified earlier in the RWD screening process, and failed late-stage clinical trials are prevented [60].
