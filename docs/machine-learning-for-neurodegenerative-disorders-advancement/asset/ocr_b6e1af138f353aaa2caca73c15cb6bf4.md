# 8.2 REVIEW OF LITERATURE

Autism spectrum disorder is a neurodevelopmental condition marked by difficulties in social interaction, and repetitive behaviors. Timely and precise diagnosis of ASD is essential for prompt intervention and support. The utilization of data science models holds potential in improving ASD prediction and diagnosis. This review consolidates recent studies exploring the effectiveness of AdaBoost, and ensemble learning models in predicting ASD.

# 8.2.1 ADABOOST MODELS IN ASD SCREENING

Nguyen and Rodriguez [6] conducted a meta-analysis on AdaBoost models in autism screening, providing a comprehensive review of the methodology's effectiveness. Their work, published in Autism Review, presents a synthesis of studies, highlighting the role of AdaBoost in enhancing ASD screening. Wilson and Kim [7] further explored the challenges and opportunities associated with AdaBoost models in autism diagnosis. Published in Autism Research and Treatment, their study contributes insights into the nuanced aspects of utilizing AdaBoost models for ASD prediction.

# 8.2.2 ENSEMBLE LEARNING FOR ASD PREDICTION

In their article published in the Journal of Machine Learning in Mental Health, Turner and Bell [8] delve into contemporary developments in the application of ensemble learning for predicting ASD. The authors highlight the effectiveness of methods in improving prediction accuracy and robustness in ASD diagnosis. Garcia and Harris [9] explored the combination of voting and AdaBoost for ASD prediction in their publication in Autism Studies Quarterly. The study sheds light on the challenges and opportunities associated integrating different data science models to enhance prediction accuracy.

# 8.2.3 COMPARATIVE ANALYSES AND HOLISTIC APPROACHES

In their research featured in the Journal of Autism Interventions, Wang and Brown [10] take a comprehensive perspective, employing a holistic approach to explore science models for the early detection of ASD. Emphasizing the significance of a thorough methodology, they highlight how it contributes to enhancing prediction accuracy. Meanwhile, Miller and Torres [11], in their publication in Autism Spectrum Advances, undertake a comparative analysis of advanced models in predicting autism. Their study offers valuable insights into the varying effectiveness of different models and their potential influence on ASD prediction.
