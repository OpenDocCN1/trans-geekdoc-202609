In this approach, the data points are assigned to a cluster such that the sum of the squared distance between the data points and centroid would be minimum. It is to be understood that less variation within the clusters will lead to more data points within same cluster. Figure 10.1 shows the clustering of data points through the K-Means algorithm.

Implementing the K-Means clustering algorithm in ML poses several challenges. The initial placement of cluster centers, can lead to convergence to suboptimal solutions. Selecting the appropriate value for the number of clusters (K) is a non-trivial task and often require domain expertise or additional validation methods. The algorithm is also susceptible to outliers, which can significantly impact the centroid positions and consequently cluster assignments.

Furthermore, K-means assumes that clusters are spherical and equally sized, making another challenge arises with high-dimensional data, the Euclidean distance metric may become less meaningful, impacting the algorithm's performance. Additionally, K-Means is sensitive to noise and may assign points to the wrong clusters, especially in the presence of overlapping clusters or varying cluster densities. These challenges require careful consideration and often involves alternative clustering algorithms or preprocessing techniques tailored to specific data characteristics.
