# 8.3.2 Working Model

Figure 8.1, the proposed VO-ADA model for predicting ASD is elucidated. The workflow begins with the critical step of loading the dataset, which forms the foundation of the entire analysis. This dataset typically contains a vast array of information, including demographic details, behavioral genetics, genetic markers, and clinical history, all of which are relevant to the diagnosis of ASD. Once the dataset is loaded, the next step is data pre-processing, a crucial phase where the raw data are cleaned and structured to ensure that it is ready for analysis. This involves handling missing values, normalizing data, encoding categorical variables, and removing any irrelevant or redundant features. Pre-processing is essential for maintaining the quality and integrity of the data, thereby setting a solid groundwork for the subsequent steps in the workflow. Following identifying and isolating the most relevant features from the processed data, feature extraction is a key step in machine learning, as it directly improves the model's performance by focusing on the most informative attributes that contribute to the accurate prediction of ASD. The extracted features could include behavioral patterns, genetic markers, or other clinical indicators that have been identified as significant in previous studies or through domain expertise. Once features have been extracted, the dataset undergoes a phase of exploration and validation. During this stage, the dataset is typically split into training and testing sets to facilitate model evaluation.
