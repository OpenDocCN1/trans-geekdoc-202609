Predictive modeling is another significant application of AI in Huntington's disease research. AI models can be trained on clinical data, including genetic information, biomarkers, and patient histories, to predict the onset and progression of the disease. These predictive models can assist clinicians in making informed decisions about patient care, as well as when to start treatment or how to manage disease progression [62]. For instance, an AI model might predict the age of onset of Huntington's disease in an individual based on their genetic makeup, or it could predict the progression rate based on early symptoms. This information is invaluable in planning treatment and care strategies, as well as in counseling patients and their families about what to expect as the disease progresses [140].

AI is also being reused for existing drugs for the treatment of Huntington's disease. The complexity of developing new drugs, repurposing these drugs represents a viable and quicker path to identifying therapeutic options. AI algorithms can scan databases of existing medications to identify that might have beneficial effects on Huntington's disease symptoms or progression. These algorithms analyze drug properties, such as molecular structure and biological activity, and match them against the requirements for modifying Huntington's disease pathology [69]. For example, a drug initially developed for a different neurodegenerative disease, or a different type of disorder might show promise in alleviating some of the symptoms or slowing the progression of Huntington's disease. Identifying such drugs can significantly accelerate the availability of treatments for patients, as these drugs have already been tested for safety and efficacy in humans [141].
