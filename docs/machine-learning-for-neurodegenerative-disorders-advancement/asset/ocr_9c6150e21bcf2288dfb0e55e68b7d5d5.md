# 4.6.2 ETHICAL IMPLICATIONS

Concerning the ethical implications, a primary concern is the bias in the data used to train ML algorithms. If the training data are biased or unrepresentative, the algorithm's output may be skewed, leading to unfair or inaccurate results. This is particularly critical in clinical settings, where decisions can significantly impact patient care. Ensuring diverse and representative data is crucial for mitigating such biases [146]. Another ethical consideration is informed consent, especially in scenarios such as drug repurposing. Patients or their families must be fully informed about the nature of the treatments, including their untested aspects, to adhere to the ethical standards of patient care and autonomy. This aspect is vital in maintaining the trust and safety of patients participating in trials involving AI-recommended treatments [147].

# 4.6.3 COMPUTATIONAL LIMITATIONS

In the context of deep learning and other advanced algorithms, these technologies often require specialized hardware, such as graphics processing units or high-performance computing systems, and substantial computational resources. This requirement poses significant barriers to smaller research institutions or laboratories with limited funding, hindering the widespread adoption and application of AI in various research scenarios [148]. Additionally, the inherent complexity of these algorithms, often involving intricate data structures or the need for large datasets, poses another challenge. Their sophisticated nature requires a high level of expertise for effective implementation and maintenance. This requirement for specialized knowledge and skills amidst a growing skill gap in AI and data science can be a significant hurdle, particularly in smaller or less technologically advanced institutions [149]. These factors combined can significantly impact the broader adoption and effective use of AI in research.

# 4.6.4 VALIDATION AND CLINICAL TRIALS

While AI can effectively predict drug efficacy in silico, these findings need to be validated through preclinical and clinical trials. This process is often lengthy and expensive, posing significant barriers in time and cost to the rapid development and deployment of new treatments [144]. Furthermore, navigating regulatory hurdles, such as compliance with standards and ethical considerations, presents another substantial challenge. Even after successful validation, new or repurposed drugs must undergo a thorough review and approval process by regulatory bodies [103]. Although this review process is essential for ensuring the safety and efficacy of treatments, its complex and time-consuming nature often slows the translation of AI-generated insights into clinically actionable treatments. There is a critical need for a streamlined and efficient pathway from AI research to clinical application.
