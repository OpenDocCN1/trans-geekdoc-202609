# 8.1.1 BACKGROUND ON AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a multifaceted and pervasive neurodevelopmental condition characterized by a wide array of challenges that predominantly affect social interactions, communication abilities, and the presence of repetitive or restrictive behaviors [1]. The term "spectrum" captures the extensive variability in symptoms, skills, and levels of impairment affecting individuals with ASD may. Some individuals display social difficulties, while others experience profound communication barriers and behavioral challenges. One of the hallmark features of ASD is its early onset, typically manifesting in the first few years of life. Parents and caregivers may observe signs such as delayed development, lack of eye contact, or an absence of interest in peer interactions. However, the presentation can be subtle, leading to delays in recognition and diagnosis. This variability necessitates a comprehensive and individualized approach to assessment and intervention [2].

Over the past decades, there has been a notable and steady increase in the prevalence of ASD. Current global estimates suggest that approximately 1 in 54 children are affected by this condition [3]. Several factors contribute to this prevalence, including heightened awareness among healthcare professionals and the public, improved diagnostic criteria, and potential environmental influences. Nonetheless, the exact reasons remain a subject of ongoing research and discussion within the scientific community. The escalating prevalence of ASD accentates the critical importance of accuracy and timely detection is not merely about labeling; it serves as the gateway to intervention strategies that can significantly influence developmental trajectories. Interventions initiated during the early developmental windows, such as behavioral therapies, speech and language therapy, and social skills training, have demonstrated efficacy in mitigating some of the challenges associated with ASD [4]. These interventions can enhance communication abilities, foster social engagement, and reduce maladaptive behaviors, thereby improving the quality of life for individuals with ASD and their families.
