# 1.2.1 MACHINE LEARNING CORE CONCEPTS

ML is a subset of artificial intelligence (AI) that involves the development of algorithms and models that allow computers to learn from data and improve their performance on a specific task without being explicitly programmed (Pavarino et al., 2023). ML enables systems to automatically adapt and improve based on experience, making it particularly useful for tasks that involve complex tasks, large datasets, and where explicit rule-based programming is challenging. # 1.2.1.1 Data

Data are the foundation where devices are built and foster human operations. They include all kinds of information, structured or unstructured; algorithms can process to extract meaningful patterns. Structured data are organized into rows and columns, normally in data sources or spreadsheets, and it is much easier to evaluate. Data are the raw information that is used to train and test ML algorithms. Disorganized information, such as text, pictures, and video clips, which do not have a predefined layout, allowing for more innovative preprocessing methods (Pavarino et al., 2023). High-quality data are essential as they directly influence the efficiency of ML designs. They include raw inputs, historical records, sensor results, and any other relevant details that inform the knowing process. The quality, quantity, and variety of the information considerably the design's capability to generalize and make precise predictions. It can encompass various formats, such as numerical values, video, images, or audio (Choi et al., 2023). In supervised learning, data consists of pairs of input features and their corresponding labels (outputs). In unsupervised learning, the data may consist solely of input features without labeled outcomes.
