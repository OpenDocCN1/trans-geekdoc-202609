## Data preprocessing steps

- Tokenization: The process of dividing a text into smaller parts, such as words or subwords (tokens), to make analysis and processing easier. All content should be converted to ensure case-insensitive processing and prevent information from being duplicated as a result of different word spellings.
- Stop removal: A common preprocessing step in NLP involves eliminating common words that are considered to be of little value in determining the meaning of a text. These words, known as stop words, are often frequent and don't carry significant meaning when removing. Stop words help reduce noise in the data and can improve the efficiency of downstream NLP tasks. It involves getting rid of frequent words like “the” and “is,” which have little meaning and can contaminate data.
- Punctuation removal: Eliminating punctuation, including periods and commas, does not advance the text semantic meaning. Punctuation removal is a common preprocessing step in NLP. The purpose of removing punctuation is to simplify the text data and focus on the meaningful content of the words. Punctuation, as commas, periods, and quotation marks, doesn't always contribute significantly to the semantic meaning of the text and can introduce noise during analysis. Remove punctuation marks from each token or during word. Common punctuation includes commas, periods, exclamation marks, question marks, quotation marks, parentheses, etc.
- Lemmatization and stemming: Taking words to their simplest or forms to handle variants (such as running, ran, and to run). Stemming removes prefixes or suffixes from words to obtain the root or base form, known as the stem. The goal is to reduce words to a common base, even if the result is not a valid. For the word “running,” the stem would be “run.” Stemming might also turn “happily” to “happ”. Lemmatization, on the other hand, reducing words to their base or dictionary form, known as the lemma. The result is always a valid word, and the process considers the word's context to determine the appropriate base form. Example: For the word “running,” the lemma will be “run.” Similarly, “better” would be lemmatized to “good.”
- Corpus creation: A sizable collection of text data used for NLP model training and evaluation. To create reliable NLP models that can handle a variety of linguistic patterns, a high-quality corpus must be built.

## 3.3 EXTRACTION OF INFORMATION FROM LITERATURE REGARDING NEURODEGENERATIVE DISORDERS

Named entity recognition is an NLP task that involves identifying and classifying named entities within unstructured text into predefined categories. In the context of biomedical and healthcare research, NER plays a crucial role in extracting disease entities from vast volumes of scientific literature, clinical notes, and EHRs. This provides an introduction to the significance of NER for identifying disease entities, its applications, and the challenges in the process. The abundance of information on diverse diseases found in the biomedical literature and clinical records makes NER crucial for knowledge extraction and data mining. Researchers and physicians can collect pertinent information for analysis, therapy, and decision-making by identifying disease entities. Finding relevant articles, case studies, and clinical trials for disorders is easier with the use of NER. Disease entity recognition is the cornerstone for biomedical knowledge graphs, which link diseases to related genes, proteins, symptoms, and treatments. By lifting through literature to find disease-drug relations, NER helps to find viable medications for ailments.

### Disease entity challenges in NER

- Ambiguity: Disease names may have various connotations or be synonymous, making it difficult to pinpoint the precise disease entity. Identifying identifying diseases is difficult due to ambiguity in names, which results from synonyms and different implications. Confusion might result from vocabulary differences between sources and terms like “senile dementia” and “Alzheimer’s” that are used interchangeably. To enhance the accuracy of disease diagnosis, NLP systems need to handle this by comprising context, identifying synonyms, and using uniform naming conventions.
- Variability: Many acronyms, misspellings, and abbreviations are frequently used in conversations about diseases, leading to variety in terminology that can make information extraction more difficult. The term “Parkinson’s disease” can be shortened to “PD,” for instance, although it can also be misspelled or go by different abbreviations, as “Parkinson’s” or “P.D.” For NLP systems, it is challenging to reliably recognize and categorize pertinent data due to this heterogeneity. Strong NER models are necessary to handle these differences. In addition to their potential misspellings and abbreviations, disease names must be able to be recognized and standardized by these models. Enhancing the system's performance with like fuzzy matching, adding context-aware algorithms, and training on a variety of datasets

- Novel entities: biological research consistently unearths new diseases and subtypes. The capabilities of current NER systems, which might not have been trained on the latest date, may not keep up with the advent of new diseases. NER models need to be flexible and dynamically incorporate fresh data to meet these problems, whether by colloquial or symbolic language.

## Techniques for identifying disease entities

- Rule-based approaches: Using custom rules and dictionaries to recognize disease entities using patterns, regular expressions, or domain-specific lists. Utilizing conditional random fields (CRFs) and support vector machines (SVMs), which have been trained on labeled data, are supervised learning methods that can be used to identify disease entities.
- Deep learning models: Using neural network topologies for tasks like NER that need sequence labeling, as bidirectional short-term memory (BiLSTM) and transformer-based models.
