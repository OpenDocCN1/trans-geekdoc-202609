# 1.5.1 NEUROIMAGING

Within this subsection, we illustrate the application of ML in neuroimaging.

1.5.1.1 Image Segmentation, Anatomical Labeling, Lesion Detection, and Classification
ML algorithms use CNNs to segment brain regions into different anatomical structures (Poikonen et al., 2023). This approach quantifies volume, identifies specific regions of interest (ROIs), and detects abnormalities, for example, tumors or lesions. Moreover, ML can learn to distinguish normal tissue from abnormal regions in MRI or CT scans (Rodrigues et al., 2023). By training on labeled datasets, these models can accurately identify and classify lesions, which is critical for diagnosis and treatment planning.

1.5.1.2 Brain Registration, Alignment, and Functional MRI (fMRI) Analysis
ML algorithms particularly perform deformable registration methods, align images from different subjects or points to a common reference space (Volonté, 2023). This ensures accuracy across individuals and enables population-level analyses. Furthermore, ML fMRI data in cognitive processing, emotion recognition, and decision-making tasks can identify unique patterns of brain activation associated with specific tasks, allowing researchers to map brain regions in various functions (Moridani et al., 2023).

1.5.1.3 Resting-state Functional Connectivity and Event-related Potential Analysis
ML techniques analyze fMRI data to uncover patterns of synchronized connectivity networks between brain regions (Dieckmann et al., 2023). This study revealed functional connectivity networks and offered insights into the intrinsic organization and cognitive pathways of the brain. ML powers EEG data to detect and analyze identify event-related potentials (ERPs) transient electrical responses in the brain to specific stimuli (Hakim et al., 2023). These responses provide insights into cognitive processes, including sensory perception, attention, and memory. ML powers neurofeedback applications, where users learn to regulate their brain activity by receiving real-time feedback (Góritz et al., 2023). BCIs use ML to decode neural signals, enabling paralyzed individuals to control robotic limbs or communicate through thought.

1.5.1.4 Disease Classification and Prediction
ML models can classify neurological data based on patterns associated with specific neurological disorders (Staka al.). Algorithms process data from sources like medical imaging, genomics, and electronic health records to identify patterns of specific diseases. Classification models, such as support vector machines and deep learning networks, categorize patients into diagnostic categories based on these patterns. Predictive models forecast disease progression or treatment responses by analyzing historical data and identifying early warning signs (Jiang, al., 2023). This approach enhances diagnostic accuracy, enables personalized treatment plans, and facilitates early intervention, improving patient outcomes and healthcare efficiency.

1.5.1.5 Biomarker Discovery and Brain Age Estimation
ML algorithms analyze vast neuroimaging datasets to identify subtle patterns as potential disease biomarkers (Lockhart, 2023). These biomarkers could include specific brain regions, connectivity patterns, or structural changes, contributing to early detection and intervention.
