## 6.3 ALZHEIMER’S DISEASE PATHOPHYOLOGY

Alzheimer’s disease is a neurological disease that causes people to lose their memory and abilities. It is still a challenging issue to solve. Even though the etiology of amyloid is still unknown, researchers have found intriguing answers to the pathological riddle to help explain how illness eventually destroys brain function [11].

Amyloid plaques and tangles are the two main characters at the center of this complex web. Where tangles are caused by the misfolding and clustering of tau protein, amyloid plaques are aberrant protein deposits created by the accumulation of beta-amyloid peptides. These aberrant aggregates act to impair normal neuronal activity and communication inside the brain. Their exact functions are still for discussion. The hippocampus is an important area for memory creation and retrieval, and it is the weakest spot of AD frequently seen. The points of contact between neurons, or synapses, are weakened by the buildup of tangles and amyloid plaques, which impairs signal transmission and causes general memory loss. Following this, brain regions show a steady decline in cognition, language, and even physical capacities due to cause cellular damage [12, 13].

Another possible cause of oxidative stress, is a cause of imbalance between antioxidants and free radicals. Free radicals—chemicals that injure cells—appear to hasten the degeneration of neurons. In AD, the development of tau angles and amyloid plaques, as well as damage to brain, may be linked to chronic inflammation, genetic susceptibility also appears an important component. Apart from these well-known participants, gene such as apolipoprotein E (Apo-E4), may affect how amyloid is processed or how proteins fold, increasing the likelihood of AD. Astrocytes are the primary source of ApoE production under normal physiological conditions. ApoE is engaged via ABC cassette transporters ABC1 and ABCG1. ## 6.4 AI ARCHITECTURE FOR THE DETECTION OF ALZHEIMER'S DISEASE

The AI architecture for AD diagnosis includes both DL and ML techniques. A methodical approach in the ML area includes pre-processing, partitioning, feature extraction, and dataset analysis. The models used include SVM, logistic regression, decision tree, and random forest. Parameter optimization is achieved using grid search and fine-tuning, and performance is assessed through cross-validation. CNNs are used in the DL field. They have layers for convolution, pooling, and normalization, which give spatial transformations invariance. Variants LeNet-5, VGG19, and convolutional autoencoders are used; each is designed for a particular goal, such as compatibility with functional MRI (fMRI) data and binary classification of Alzheimer's disease. RNNs are used in sequential data processing to manage variable-length sequences and non-linear decision boundaries by taking use of temporal patterns. By integrating the aspects of ML and DL approaches, this integrated approach highlights the many procedures and architectures used for AD diagnosis and provides diagnostic robustness and accuracy [8, 18].
