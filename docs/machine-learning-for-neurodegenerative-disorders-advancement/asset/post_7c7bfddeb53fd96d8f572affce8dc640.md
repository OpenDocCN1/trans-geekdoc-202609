## 1.1 INTRODUCTION

The past few decades have witnessed an extraordinary convergence of machine learning (ML) and neuroscience. These two seemingly disparate fields have intertwined, reshaping our understanding of the brain and revolutionizing our capacity to analyze. Significant progress has been made in algorithms, computing power, and data availability. The advent of deep learning, a subset of ML that enables artificial networks inspired by the human brain, has led to breakthroughs in image and speech recognition.

This surge in ML capabilities is partly due to the proliferation of big datasets that enable the training of neural models. ML algorithms become more sophisticated, yet the potential to handle complex, high-dimensional data highly relevant to the datasets produced by neuroscience. Simultaneously, neuroscience has made strides in acquiring and analyzing neural activity. Electroencephalography, MRI, and single-cell recording techniques have illuminated the brain's activity with unprecedented detail. These technologies generate vast amounts of data, helping to elucidate the interplay between neurons and brain regions.

However, the complexity of neural data demands advanced computational tools for analysis, interpretation, and prediction, a niche which ML excels. The synergy between ML and neuroscience is evident in various applications. ML algorithms can decode neural activity, identifying specific cognitive features or behaviors.

The sheer volume of neural data for increasingly sophisticated ML techniques Our understanding of neural activity, connectivity, and functional implications has as algorithms become more adept at spotting subtle patterns from large datasets. ML-driven analyses enable the identification of biomarkers for neurological disorders, potentially leading to earlier diagnosis and more targeted treatments. While both fields benefit from each other's advances, challenges persist. The interpretability of ML models applied to neural data remains a critical concern, as complex models can be complex to decipher. Ethical considerations, for example, data privacy, algorithmic bias, and the potential for misusing neural information, require meticulous attention.
