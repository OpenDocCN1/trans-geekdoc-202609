There is no standard treatment for Parkinson's disease; therapy is tailored to everyone's symptoms. Treatments include medication and surgical therapy. Other supportive therapies, such as physical therapy, occupational therapy, and speech therapy, also play a crucial role in managing this condition [46].

Table 4.2 provides a detailed overview of the primary pharmacological treatments for Parkinson's disease. The most common medications for Parkinson's are aimed at replenishing or mimicking dopamine [47]. Levodopa, the most effective medication for Parkinson's disease, is a natural chemical that passes into the brain and is converted to dopamine [48]. It is often combined with carbidopa, a medication that prevents levodopa from being converted into dopamine outside the brain, thereby enhancing dopaminergic activity [49]. Other pharmacological approaches include monoamine oxidase B (MAO-B) inhibitors, such as selegiline and rasagiline, which inhibit the MAO-B enzyme to prevent dopamine breakdown; these agents are used in the early stages or in combination with levodopa in advanced stages and may disease progression. Dopamine agonists such as ropinirole and pramipexole mimic dopamine by directly stimulating dopamine receptors. These drugs are less effective than levodopa but are associated with fewer long-term complications [51]. Catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT) inhibitors such as enclave and tolcapone are used in combination with levodopa to reduce “off” periods and improve the effect of levodopa [52]. Anticholinergics such as trihexyphenidyl in tremor control by reducing acetylcholine activity and balancing dopamine-acetylcholine levels in the brain, although they are less effective at treating bradykinesia or rigidity [52]. Additionally, amantadine is used for the treatment of dyskia associated with levodopa and provides mild symptomatic relief in the early stages of Parkinson's disease [53].

In advanced cases, deep brain stimulation (DBS), a surgical procedure used to treat severe disabling neurological symptoms, can be considered. DBS involves the implantation of a medical device called to a neurostimulator, which sends electrical impulses to specific areas in the brain.
