## 2.7.2 FUNCTIONAL CONNECTIVITY

Functional connectivity, a key concept in neuroimaging, refers to the spatial associations and temporal dependencies between different brain regions' activities. This approach aims to understand how distinct regions communicate during cognitive tasks or at rest. Functional connectivity is often assessed using techniques like functional fMRI and EEG [10]. In fMRI studies, BOLD signals are recorded over time, and correlations between these signals across brain regions are analyzed to infer functional connectivity. One influential study demonstrated the existence of intrinsic connectivity networks during the resting state, revealing patterns of synchronized activity across specific brain regions.

Functional connectivity has significantly contributed to our understanding of brain organization and its alterations in neurological and psychiatric disorders [52]. Studies employing functional connectivity have investigated diverse topics, such as characterizing resting-state networks and examining changes associated with disorders like Alzheimer's disease. These investigations have provided valuable insights into the brain's dynamic interactions and paved the way for the development of biomarkers and therapeutic strategies in various neurological disorders [53].

## 2.7.3 TOPOLOGICAL ANALYSIS AND CONNECTIVITY DISRUPTIONS

Topological analysis of brain networks involves the examination of their structural or functional organization using graph theory methods. This approach provides insights into the intricate connectivity patterns within the brain and allows for the identification of network properties. Connectivity disruptions, which refer to alterations in the normal patterns of connectivity, have been studied extensively in the context of neurological and psychiatric disorders. Topological analysis has been instrumental in characterizing these disruptions, offering a quantitative framework to understand changes in network organization.

One notable study utilized applied topological analysis to investigate disruptions in brain connectivity associated with psychiatric disorders. The researchers utilized graph theory methods to identify hubs in the brain connectome and found that these hubs were often implicated in the anatomical basis of brain disorders. The study highlighted the importance of understanding not only the presence of disruptions but also their specific impact on the network's topological structure [51].

Topological analysis has also been applied to explore connectivity disruptions in neurological disorders such as Alzheimer's disease. Utilizing graph theory methods has revealed alterations in network properties, including decreased network efficiency and disrupted hub regions, providing valuable insights into the structural changes associated with the disease.

In summary, topological analysis serves as a powerful tool to quantify and interpret connectivity disruptions in brain. By applying graph theory methods, researchers can uncover the network-level changes associated with neurological and psychiatric disorders, advancing our understanding of the underlying pathophysiology [54].

## 2.8 INTERPRETABILITY IN MACHINE LEARNING

### 2.8.1 CHALLENGES OF “BLACK BOX” MODELS

Interpretability in ML, especially concerning neuroimaging analysis, is a critical aspect that has garnered increasing attention. The challenges associated with black box models pose significant hurdles in the decision-making processes of complex algorithms, limiting their applicability in sensitive domains like healthcare [55]. In the context of neuroimaging, where the implications of model predictions directly impact patient outcomes, interpretability becomes paramount. Black box models, such as deep neural networks, often lack transparency, making it difficult to comprehend how specific features contribute to predictions. This lack of interpretability hinders the translation of ML findings into clinically actionable insights. Addressing these challenges is crucial for fostering trust in the models and facilitating their integration into medical practice. Researchers are exploring methods to enhance the interpretability of black box models, such as generating saliency maps or utilizing AI techniques, which provide insights into the features influencing predictions. A balanced approach that considers both predictive accuracy and interpretability is essential for the successful deployment of ML models in neuroimaging applications [56].

## 2.8.2 TOOLS FOR INTERPRETABILITY

Tools for interpretability in ML are crucial for unraveling the decisions by complex models, especially in domains like neuroimaging, where transparency is essential. Various tools have been developed to enhance the interpretability of ML models, offering insights into feature importance, model behavior, and decision rationale. Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAPs), introduced by [57], is a prominent tool that employs game theory to assign a Shapley value, quantifying its contribution to the model's output. Local interpretable model-agnostic explanations (LIMEs), proposed by [58], generates locally faithful explanations by approximating a black box model behavior around a specific instance. Gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM) visualizes the regions of an image that contributed to a deep learning model's decision, aiding in understanding the focus areas. Additionally, tools like TensorBoard and Yellowbrick provide visualization of neural network architectures and model performance, respectively. These tools empower researchers and practitioners to comprehend, validate, and correct the decisions made by ML models, fostering trust and facilitating adoption in critical applications.

### 2.8.2.1 Shapley Additive Explanations

Interpretability is a critical aspect of ML, especially in the context of neuroimaging, where understanding model decisions is essential. SHAPs have emerged as a powerful tool for enhancing the interpretability of complex ML models. Proposed by [57], SHAP values are based on cooperative game theory, specifically Shapley values, and provide a consistent and fair way to allocate contributions of each feature to the model's output. SHAP quantify the impact of individual features on predictions, offering a nuanced understanding of the model's decision-making process. In neuroimaging studies, SHAPs have been applied to evaluate the contributions of specific brain regions or features to diagnostic predictions, shedding light on the factors influencing the model's outcomes [59]. The versatility of SHAPs extends to various ML models, making it a valuable tool for interpretability in neuroimaging applications. Researchers and practitioners can leverage SHAPs to gain insights into the relevance of different neuroimaging features and enhance the transparency and trustworthiness of ML models.

### 2.8.2.2 LIME

Interpretable ML models are essential for understanding model predictions, particularly in the complex domain of neuroimaging. LIMEs, introduced by [58], stand out as a valuable tool for achieving interpretability by generating locally faithful explanations for black-box models. LIMEs operate by perturbing instances in the dataset, creating a new dataset of similar instances, and then training a simple model on this perturbed dataset to approximate the behavior of the original complex model. This local surrogate model provides insights into the decision boundaries around a specific data point, aiding in the interpretation of individual predictions. In neuroimaging studies, LIMEs have been applied to elucidate the rationale behind the predictions of ML models, offering insights into the importance of specific brain regions or features for a given task [60]. By providing locally faithful explanations, LIMEs enhance transparency and trust in ML models applied to neuroimaging data, facilitating their integration into clinical decision-making processes.
