Another promising area is the integration of AI with advanced imaging technologies, such as magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and positron emission tomography (PET) scans. AI algorithms can analyze imaging data to detect early signs of neurodegenerative diseases, often before clinical symptoms become apparent. Early detection of this is crucial for the early identification of treatments and for monitoring its progression [153].

The integration of AI with robotics and automation technologies holds significant promise for drug discovery and repurposing. In drug development laboratories, robotic systems can automate repetitive and time-consuming tasks such as high-throughput screening of compound libraries. AI can enhance the capabilities of these systems, enabling them not to perform tasks more efficiently but also analyze the results of these screenings to identify drug candidates [90]. AI algorithms can analyze the thousands of compounds to identify most effective against specific targets in neurodegenerative diseases [154]. AI can be integrated with technologies such as blockchain and cloud computing to enhance data sharing and security in drug development. Blockchain can provide a secure and transparent platform for sharing clinical trial data, while cloud computing can facilitate the storage and analysis of large datasets. This integration can streamline the drug development process by improving collaboration between researchers and ensuring secure and efficient data management [155].

# 4.7.2 EXPANSION TO OTHER DISEASES

The future of AI in the field of drug discovery and repurposing, particularly for neurodegenerative disorders, holds immense potential for expansion to other disease areas. This expansion is fueled by AI's ability to analyze complex datasets, identify new drug targets, and accelerate the drug development process. The versatility and scalability of AI make it invaluable for addressing a range of diseases beyond neurodegenerative disorders [16]. For instance, the application of AI in oncology has already shown promising results. Cancer is a highly complex and varied disease, and AI can analyze genetic, epigenetic, and proteomic data to identify potential targets for cancer therapy. Moreover, AI can be used to screen existing drug libraries to find compounds that could be repurposed for treating specific types of cancer [87]. For example, a drug developed for Alzheimer's might be effective at targeting certain pathways in brain cancer. This approach not only accelerates the process of finding effective treatments but also reduces the costs associated with drug development [7].

Another area where AI could significantly contribute to disease incidence is infectious disease. The recent COVID-19 pandemic highlighted the need for rapid drug development and repurposing. AI can quickly analyze viral genomes, model their interactions with human proteins, and predict antiviral compounds. During COVID-19, AI was instrumental in identifying existing drugs that could be repurposed to treat the virus, demonstrating the speed and efficiency of AI-driven approaches [156]. Cardiovascular diseases, the leading cause of death, are another area where AI can have a substantial impact. AI algorithms can help identify new therapeutic targets and drugs for heart disease by analyzing patient data, genetic factors, and lifestyle patterns.
