The integration of AI technologies in drug repurposing has markedly improved the efficiency and accuracy of identifying new therapeutic uses for existing drugs. AI algorithms can analyze large datasets containing information about drug structures, targets, and effects on various diseases. AI models can uncover hidden relations and predict new uses for existing drugs by examining these datasets [7]. For example, a drug developed for hypertension by AI could be due to the unintended interactions of the drug with biological pathways involved in neurogeneration [101]. AI-driven analyses can rapidly screen existing drugs for these new applications, making them powerful tools for identifying treatment options, especially for diseases with limited therapies are available.

One notable example of successful drug repurposing was aided by AI identification of sildenafil, originally developed for angina and later repurposed for erectile dysfunction. AI analyses drug-target interactions have also led to the repurposing of cancer drugs for the treatment of neurodegenerative diseases on the basis of their effects on shared molecular pathways [117]. In these cases, AI not only identifies new uses but also helps in understanding the mechanisms of action, which is crucial for the successful application of such drugs in new therapeutic contexts. However, challenges remain in drug repurposing, as predicting the efficacy of drugs in new patient populations and understanding the broader implications of drug interactions. Despite these challenges, the potential of AI in revolutionizing drug repurposing, especially in complex fields like neurodegenerative diseases, is immense and continues as technologies advance.

# ## 4.4.2 Data Sources for Drug Repurposing

AI-driven drug repurposing relies heavily on diverse and comprehensive data sources to identify new applications for existing drugs. The first critical source of data is drug libraries, which contain detailed information about the molecular structures, pharmacodynamics, pharmacokinetics, and known patterns of effects of existing drugs. These libraries are extensive repositories that catalog a wide range of compounds, widely used in experiments or drugs that have failed in clinical trials for their initial indications. By analyzing these data, AI algorithms can identify drugs with properties that might be effective against other diseases. For example, a drug developed to treat cardiovascular diseases might be useful for treating neurodegenerative disorders due to its molecular structure and biological activity. AI can analyze these attributes to uncover novel drug-disease relationships [118].

Another data source for drug repurposing is clinical trial data. This includes detailed records from past and ongoing clinical studies, encompassing data on drug efficacy, side effects, dosing regimens, and patient outcomes. Clinical trial databases are invaluable for understanding how drugs behave in real-world scenarios and for gaining insights into their potential uses. AI algorithms can be used to find patterns and correlations that suggest new therapeutic applications. For instance, a drug that showed limited efficacy for its primary indication in a clinical trial but exhibited positive effects on a secondary condition could be a candidate for repurposing [119].

The biomedical literature represents a vast reservoir of knowledge encompassing findings, expert opinions, and case studies. This literature includes published papers, conference proceedings, and other articles that provide insights into disease mechanisms, drug-disease interactions, and the latest developments in medical research. AI algorithms equipped with natural language processing capabilities can analyze unstructured textual data, extract relevant information, and integrate this information with structured data from other sources. This analysis can reveal connections between drugs and diseases that have not been explicitly studied, offering new avenues for repurposing [69].

Patient databases are also crucial sources of data for drug repurposing, especially for gathering information on off-label drug effects. These databases contain real-world data from health care settings, including patient demographics, treatment histories, outcomes, and adverse effects.
