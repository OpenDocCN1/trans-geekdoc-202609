## 5.2 BACKGROUND STUDIES

Freezing of gait is a common condition in the later stages of PD, in which patients experience an involuntary and unexpected condition of immobility with the sensation of being stuck to the ground. These conditions can be triggered by stress, anxiety, complex surroundings, and attempting to multitask. During FoG conditions, people are more prone to stumbling or losing their balance, increasing the risk of falls and injuries. As a result of the failure of falling in public, mobility and independence are reduced, causing isolation and emotional distress, lowering the overall quality of life for people with PD.

It is worth noting the likelihood of experiencing FoG in PD among individuals [8]. FoG does not affect all patients, and when it does, its frequency and severity vary. FoG is determined by a variety of factors, including the stage of PD, the presence of other symptoms, and differences in disease prevalence [9]. However, FoG is more common in advanced stages of PD, although it can appear at any stage.

A clinical assessment is typically required to diagnose PD, which includes a meticulous history review, physical examination to identify motor symptoms, and evaluation of the patient's response to dopaminergic medications. Clinical observation, on the other hand, is critical in the diagnosis of FoG. Healthcare providers meticulously track their patients' gait and motor performance by auditing the frequency of FoG conditions utilizing wearable sensors or other objective measurement tools. The diagnosis phase is critical because it guides the tailoring of treatment strategies for Parkinson's patients [10]. Accurate diagnosis enables healthcare providers to comprehend the unique nature of the FoG, which facilitates early and pre-diagnosis, which is critical for optimizing treatment and improving mobility. The diagnosis of FoG is still a challenging task. The application of emerging technologies and artificial intelligence has enabled the development of predictive models that effectively manage and reduce the frequency of FoG, improving the quality of life for people with PD [11].

### 5.3.1 Data Pre-processing

Effective data pre-processing is crucial in the examination of FoG in PD, significantly contributing to the development of improved diagnostic systems and studies. Data cleaning includes outlier removal, noise reduction, and handling missing values. Data normalization is used to standardize data so it can be compared across individuals. Furthermore, transformation includes feature engineering to extract relevant variables. Dimensionality reduction with feature selection avoids overfitting. Sliding windows allow for more focused analysis on specific intervals [19, 20]. Together, these pre-processing techniques (Figure 5.3) improve the accuracy and effectiveness of modeling FoG condition in patients.

### 5.3.2 FEATURE EXTRACTION AND FEATURE SELECTION

Feature extraction and selection play a critical role in the analysis of various aspects of health and behavior. In this, we highlight significant characteristics illustrated in Figure 5.4 associated with spatiotemporal analysis, handwriting, kinematics, speed, kinetics, and frequency analysis [21, 22].

- Quantitative spatiotemporal features: These parameters include step length, walking speed, gait pattern, and cadence. The extraction of these features provides valuable insights about progressive symptoms and may induce gait disturbances in individuals.
- Handwriting features: PD frequently manifests as distinct changes in handwriting, such as micrographia, which is characterized by a decrease in letter size and spirals. Feature extraction entails quantifying the dimensions, contour, and uniformity, allowing for objective assessment of motor impairments.
- Kinematics features: The kinematics of bodily movements relating to walking, turning, and hand movements illustrate significant information regarding motor control and balance of patients. These data are essential for monitoring gait-related symptoms and developing targeted interventions.

- Speech features: Patients suffering from PD frequently experience speech difficulties, such as reduced voice volume and poorly articulated speech. The extraction of important characteristics from speech data includes pitch, loudness, speech rate, and articulatory precision. Speech impairments can be assessed using this comprehensive evaluation.
- Kinetics features: Kinetic characteristics include joint forces, torques, and center of mass (COP) parameters. These characteristics aid in the evaluation of biomechanical components of motion and equilibrium, providing useful information about muscular weakness, postural instability, and motor control.
- Frequency and time-domain features: The frequency components of various signals, such as power spectra obtained from accelerometers or electromyography data, can be analyzed to detect motor abnormalities and tremors in PD. Time-domain features, which are typically extracted using techniques such as wavelet or Fourier transform, provide insights into the temporal characteristics of the signals and aid in detection of patterns or irregularities.

### 5.3.3 MODEL DEVELOPMENT

Model development is a crucial step in which various machine learning techniques are analyzed for designing an efficient diagnostic system for FoG in PD. FoG is a disturbing symptom of PD, which is often assessed objectively. Samà et al. have identified accelerometer-based features that are utilized with machine learning-based classifiers, achieving 91.7% specificity and 87.4% specificity in real-life settings [23]. Moore et al. [24] have validated ambulatory FoG monitors using ankle-mounted sensors with an accuracy of 89% (10% false positives), potentially improving FoG management in PD patients.

Detecting freezing in Parkinson's patients is crucial for fall prevention, and wrist-mounted sensors have a 90% FoG detection rate, which has improved user acceptance over lower limbs. Wrist sensors provide a feasible alternative for FoG detection without compromising accuracy [25]. Tripoliti et al. have detected FoG in Parkinson's patients using body-worn sensors and a four-stage methodology. The evaluation with different sensor configurations revealed a method achieving 98.74% specificity, 81.94% specificity, 98.6% AUC, and 96.11% accuracy with random forests classification [26]. Orphanidou et al. have employed learning models to predict FoG in advance, using accelerometer data from eight patients.
