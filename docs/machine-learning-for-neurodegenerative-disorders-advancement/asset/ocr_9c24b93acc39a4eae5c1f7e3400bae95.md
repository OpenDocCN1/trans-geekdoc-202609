# 1.3.2.1 Understanding Neural Mechanisms

Neuroscience seeks to elucidate the functional neural mechanisms that underlie brain function. This investigating involves how neurons encode, transmit, and integrate information through electrochemical signaling. At the molecular level, researchers explore neurotransmitter release, receptor activation, and intracellular signaling pathways that modulate synaptic and neuronal excitability (Zarr & Brown, 2023). Techniques such as patch-clamp electro measure ionic currents across membranes, while molecular biology identifies signaling cascades and gene expression changes (Wu et al., 2023). Through combining these, scientists aim to map both the functional and structural aspects of neural circuits. This knowledge is essential for deciphering how behaviors and complex processes emerge from neural activity and for developing targeted interventions for neurological disorders (Poikonen et al., 2023). Now, researchers gain insights into fundamental processes such as perception, memory formation, and decision-making.

# 1.3.2.2 Mapping Brain Structure

Mapping brain structure involves a detailed analysis of the brain's anatomy and connectivity. Techniques such as MRI provide high-resolution images of brain anatomy, allowing for the visualization of cortical and subcellular structures. Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) maps help white tracts by measuring water diffusion, revealing neural connectivity patterns. Advanced microscopy methods, including electron microscopy, offer insights into cellular and subcellular organization (Wang et al., 2023). Mapping brain projects, such as the Human Connectome Project, integrate these imaging methods to create comprehensive maps of brain connectivity and function. Understanding structure is crucial for linking anatomical abnormalities with behavioral functions and for diagnosing and treating neurodegenerative disorders (Rodriguez et al., 2023). These maps identify specific brain regions responsible for various functions, contributing to our understanding of brain specialization.

# 1.3.2.3 Exploring Neural Development

The study of neural development investigates how the nervous system forms during embryonic stages and matures throughout life. Neural development research investigates the processes that shape the formation and maturation of the nervous system from embryonic stages through adulthood. This includes studying neural progenitor cell differentiation, neuronal migration, and synogenesis (Volonté, 2023). Techniques like in situ hybridization and immunohistochemistry are used to visualize gene expression and protein localization during development. Researchers examine how genetic factors and environmental factors drive developmental processes, using model organisms and advanced imaging to track changes over time (Wu et al., 2023). Insights are vital for understanding normal brain development and the origins of neurodevelopmental disorders such as autism spectrum and intellectual disabilities. It encompasses processes such as neurogenesis (the birth of neurons), neuronal migration (the movement of neurons to their proper locations), and synapt (the formation of synapses) (Straka et al., 2023). Understanding these processes provides insights into how the brain's architecture is established and how disruptions can lead to developmental disorders.

# 1.3.2.4 Studying Neural Plasticity

Neural plasticity refers to the brain's ability to change its structure and function in response to experiences. These include synaptic plasticity (strengthening or weakening of synapses) and cortical remapping (reorganization of brain regions) (Rutkowski et al., 2023). Synaptic plasticity, as long-term potentiation (LTP) and long-term depression (LTD), involves changes in synaptic strength and efficacy. Techniques such as two-photon microscopy and electrophysiological recording enable real-time observation of these dynamic changes in neural circuits (Cakar & Yav, 2023).
