# 4.4.3.3 Network Analysis

Network analysis is a powerful approach that involves studying the relationships and interactions within biological networks, such as protein-protein interaction networks, genetic networks, or metabolic pathways. AI algorithms can identify hidden connections between drugs and diseases by analyzing these networks [16]. For instance, a drug might interact with a network of proteins involved in a neurodegenerative disease, suggesting a potential therapeutic use. Network analysis helps in understanding the complex biological context in which diseases and drugs operate, providing insights that are not apparent when analyzing drugs or diseases.

These algorithms and approaches represent a diverse toolkit that can be employed in the purpose of repurposing drugs for neurodegenerative disorders. Researchers can uncover novel therapies for existing diseases, potentially leading to more effective treatments by leveraging advanced computational methods. The power of AI-driven methods lies in their ability to process and analyze large datasets, identify patterns and relationships, and generate actionable insights, all of which are crucial in the fast-paced and complex world of drug discovery and repurposing.

# 4.5.1 AI IN ALZHEIMER’S DISEASE RESEARCH

Alzheimer’s disease, characterized by memory loss and cognitive decline, presents a significant challenge for healthcare systems and researchers worldwide [17]. The complexity of the disease’s pathology and the need for early diagnosis and effective treatments make it prime candidate for the application of AI technologies, especially ML and deep learning. These technologies have been applied to various aspects of Alzheimer’s disease research, leading to notable advancements and promising developments. The endeavor to create effective treatments for Alzheimer’s disease involves significant challenges in both complexity and financial investment. Research and development costs for new Alzheimer’s treatments are estimated at approximately $5.7 billion, a figure nearly times greater than for developing cancer drugs [1]. The most recent drug got FDA approval in this domain, aducanumab, targets amyloid a monoclonal antibody. Its primary action is to reduce the accumulation of β-amyloid in the brain. Although this treatment is notably expensive, costing an estimated $55,000 annually, and is mainly beneficial for patients in the early stages of Alzheimer’s disease who have an amyloid presence [1]. In repurposing existing diseases has become a promising and more cost-effective strategy. This approach utilizes drugs already approved for other conditions, taking advantage of their well-established safety profiles. This approach reduces the risks associated with drug toxicity and can accelerate-the development of new treatments for Alzheimer’s disease.

One significant area where AI has impactful is the identification of biomarkers for Alzheimer’s disease. Biomarkers are biological indicators often detected in blood or cerebrospinal fluid that can indicate the presence or risk of a disease [15].
