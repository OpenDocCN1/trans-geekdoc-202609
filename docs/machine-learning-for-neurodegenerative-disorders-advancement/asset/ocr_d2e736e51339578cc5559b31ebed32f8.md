# 1.3.2.5 Investigating Neural Disorders

Neuroscience is dedicated to unraveling the causes and mechanisms of neurological and psychiatric disorders. Researchers have studied genetic mutations, abnormal neural circuitry, neurotransmitter imbalances, and other factors contributing to such as Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease- epilepsy, and schizophrenia (Wu et al., 2023). This identifies pathological changes at the cellular and molecular levels, such as protein aggregation, neuroinflammation, and synaptic dysfunction. Researchers use techniques like genomic sequencing, transcriptomics, and animal models to explore cognitive and environmental factors (Schmidgall & Hays, 2023), and biomarker analysis are employed to study prevention and identify therapeutic targets. Insights gained from these studies are crucial for developing diagnostic tools, therapeutic strategies, and interventions to mitigate disease impact and improve patient outcomes (Turrisi et al., 2023). Insights into these mechanisms can aid in developing targeted therapies and interventions.

# 1.3.2.6 Advancing Brain-Machine Interfaces

Brain-machine interfaces (BMIs) enable direct communication between the brain and external devices. Scientists work on decoding neural signals to control prosthetics, movement in paralysis, and even translating thoughts into computer commands (Hakim et al., 2023). This involves recording neural activity through invasive (such as electrodes) or noninvasive (EEG) methods and decoding this activity to control devices as prosthetics or computer systems. Techniques such as signal processing, ML, and neural decoding algorithms are used to interpret neural signals and translate them into actionable commands (Zheng et al., 2023). Advances in BMIs also involve biocompatible materials and improving the precision and usability of interfaces. These technologies hold significant potential for restoring sensory and motor functions and enhancing human capabilities through direct neural control (Zhang et al., 2023), BMIs have transformative potential for individuals with motor impairments and offer new avenues for human-machine interaction.

# 1.3.2.7 Understanding Consciousness and Cognition

Neuroscience explores the neural basis of consciousness and cognition and how the brain generates subjective experiences and complex mental processes. Neuroscience benefits greatly from cross-disciplinary collaboration, integrating insights and methodologies from fields such as biology, engineering, and computer science. Effective efforts enable the synthesis of diverse perspectives, from molecular techniques and computational models to engineering solutions and psychological theories (Jang, 2023). Combining neuroscience with AI can lead to advancements in neural network modeling and BCIs. Cross-disciplinary research fosters innovation by applying new tools and perspectives to complex questions about brain function and behavior. Collaborative projects, such as large-scale brain mapping and interdisciplinary research, enhance the depth and breadth of research, driving progress across multiple domains (Turrisi et al., 2023). Research in this area involves investigating brain regions with awareness, studying the neural correlates of decision-making, and exploring memory systems (Zarr & Brown, 2023). This helps understand what we are conscious and how our cognitive functions arise from neural activity.
