# 4.3.5 IN SILICO SCREENING

In silico screening is a computational technique that has become increasingly important in the field of drug discovery. Computer simulations can be used to evaluate and predict potential drug candidates from vast libraries of chemical compounds. This approach serves as a powerful tool for quickly narrowing the list of potential drugs that need to be tested in the laboratory, saving time and resources [112]. The integration of AI, particularly deep learning algorithms, has significantly enhanced the capabilities of in silico screening. These AI algorithms analyze extensive compound libraries, predict the biological activity of untreated compounds, and prioritize them for further investigation. This process is especially beneficial in the context of neurodegenerative disorders, where the biological targets can be complex and the need for effective treatments is urgent [13].

Deep learning algorithms in AI are particularly adept at identifying patterns and relationships within large datasets. By training on databases of drug-target interactions, these algorithms can predict how untested compounds might interact with a target associated with a neurodegenerative disorder [113]. For example, an AI model could predict which compound is likely to inhibit proteins implicated in Alzheimer's disease. This prediction is based not only on the structure of the compound but also on a multiple of factors, such as their chemical properties, bioavailability, and potential toxicity. The ability of AI to process and analyze a diverse range of data at high speed allows researchers to identify promising compounds that may have been overlooked using traditional screening methods [114].

In addition to identifying new drug candidates, in silico screening can also be used to better understand existing drugs. AI can help elucidate how these drugs interact with their biological targets and predict potential off-target effects. This knowledge is crucial for drug repurposing efforts, where existing drugs are tested for efficacy against different diseases [115]. For instance, a drug approved for treating a certain type of cancer might be predicted by an AI model to help define effects in treating a specific neurodegenerative disorder. By screening known pharmaceutical compounds in silico, researchers can rapidly identify candidates for repurposing trials, significantly accelerating the drug development process. This approach not only streamlines the discovery of new drugs but also unveils novel possibilities for repurposing, expanding the potential arsenal of treatments for challenging diseases [10]. As AI technologies continue to develop, their integration into in silico screening promises to revolutionize the tools available for drug discovery and development, herding a new era of rapid and efficient pharmaceutical innovation.

# 4.4.1 OVERVIEW OF DRUG REPURPOSING

Drug repurposing, often referred to as drug repositioning, is a strategy in pharmaceutical development in which existing drugs already approved for one disease are tested for their effectiveness in treating other conditions. This approach capitalizes on the known safety profiles and pharmacokinetic data of these drugs, potentially shortening the path to clinical application and reducing the development costs significantly compared with new drug. The rationale behind drug repurposing is that many drugs can have multiple biological targets, effective against various diseases not considered during their original development [16]. This strategy is particularly appealing in the field of neurodegenerative disorders, where the discovery of new, effective drugs has been historically challenging due to the complexity of these diseases.

Currently, approximately 30% of new drugs and vaccines approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for treating various diseases are repurposed, demonstrating the significance of drug repurposing in modern pharmacotherapy [8].
