## 11.3.1.2 Standardized Protocols

In the rapidly changing field of neurodegenerative disease research by ML and others, standardized protocols are becoming increasingly important [68]. Together these protocols serve as the glue connecting a diverse universe of data, methodologies, and institutions to guarantee continuity or reliability. Standard protocols promote cooperation and make it easier to compare results from different studies, facilitating progress towards the ultimate objective of conquering these horrible diseases [69]. Consider a global community of researchers investigating the same neurodegenerative disorder, yet their siloed data are incompatible due to variations in methods for collection and analysis or measures used [17]. Collaboration is inhibited and findings are hard to compare or build upon–how do you even start your research if every other lab has a different method for the same thing? The groundbreaking strategy is to unify the efforts and reviews from researchers with standardized protocols so that all can meet on common ground [70].

There are various benefits to this holistic approach. Through the proper establishment of data collection, processing and storage standards, we drastically reduce errors, resulting in more reliable (credible) results [35]. Additionally, standardized protocols allow more straightforward reparation and validation of results, which facilitates the overall level of evidence or research progress [38]. Usingless data sharing and collaboration across institutions, in turn, encourages collaborations and promotes research advances [67]. In this manner, robust and generalizable results are created to develop better-quality patient interventions based on evidence, ultimately (hopefully faster) to clinical processes that translate into an improved set of outcomes [71].

Implementing standardized protocols is challenging: Individual goals must be reconciled with collective benefit, and no undue rigidity should exist [36]. However, the potential benefits to patients and researchers are huge. We can overcome the challenges in neurodegenerative research and save a generation of people struggling with these insidious diseases [40]. While this is a major hurdle, the benefits greatly outweigh it. To achieve the optimal future where standardized protocols enable researchers to reach the full potential of their therapy and neurodegenerative research forward [41], both need a concerted drive from collaborative programs, open communication and committed efforts towards common objectives.

## 11.3.1.3 Public–Private Partnerships

Eradicating neurodegenerative diseases is a challenge that requires enormous resources, knowledge and skills from different angles. Public–private partnerships bridge the gap between the public sector research infrastructure and private industry development capacity [67]. These partnerships have the potential to speed up research, and bring them quickly into treatment use that can enhance patients' lives [70]. In the future, pharma companies and governments are advised to use advocacy groups to accelerate research and development of effective treatments in neurodegenerative disorders (Figure 11.7).

The isolated nature of academic research and pharmaceutical development has significantly impeded progress in neurodegenerative disorders [37]. Public-private partnerships offer a promising solution by breaking down silos and promoting collaboration between these sectors. Picture academia bringing the underlying scientific and dense data resources, pharma companies offering drug-development competencies, governments providing funding and ethical frameworks [13], and advocacy groups ensuring the voice of patients is being heard, building toward one goal: accelerating research and development for patients [14]. This context, which is truly game-changing for rare disease research and advocacy with the most potential [30], enables accelerating joining hands-on-translation of bench-to-bedside discovery of cost-efficient, risk-sharing, patient-centered innovation that addresses the actual needs of those living with the disease. PPPs are worth consideration, given their unambiguous ability to expedite progress and inspire despite hardships. Rather is to dismantle the silos and envision a future in which collaboration will allow unprecedented advances against neurodegenerative diseases [13]. ## 11.3.2 ENHANCING TRANSPARENCY AND TRUST

The need to build transparency in neurodegenerative research is pertinent. Hence, adopting XAI, human-in-the-loop (HITL) design by proactive communication among responsible stakeholders will be crucial. We must trust and know how ML models work, hence confidence in ethical usage of AI.

### 11.3.2.1 Explainable AI

ML can potentially help how we diagnose, treat, and even prevent neurodegenerative disorders. Despite advances, a major challenge persists: the lack of transparency in many models. Internal mechanisms and decision-making processes often remain hidden, making understanding and trusting their outputs difficult [70]. Without transparency, it is difficult to trust the variables used in a model; interpretability and clinical adoption are hindered by a burgeoning field known as XAI, aiming to illuminate the black boxes, opening up new avenues for improved and reliable ML applications in neurodegenerative research.

One of the most critical challenges in healthcare is that ML models are opaque, which makes it difficult to detect bias and fairness violations [73]. If we do not know how the model arrives at its conclusions, this can make biases in the input data or algorithm itself cause patients from some groups to make worse decisions. [74]. The rationale for AI-driven recommendations is to acquire clarity in trusting and implementing decisions in favor of patient care. However, that is where XAI comes to the rescue. XAI transforms how models are understood by exposing the hidden patterns and relationships in data, helping reveal decision paths, promoting trust, and enabling the discovery of scientific knowledge within techniques [75]. This new insight could be used to discover new drug targets, thus improving health and the safe use of AI in one of the most important areas [76].

XAI in neurodegenerative research is where the future shines bright. Overcoming these obstacles and creating the next generation of explanations will demystify black-box models with XAI to ensure transparency and ultimately expedite ELSI, by way of legal, social, and ethical guidelines for those who fight against such formidable diseases [77].
