# 1.3 THE LANDSCAPE OF NEUROSCIENCE

Neuroscience is dynamic, with constant interaction between these levels of analysis. Advancements in technology, including imaging techniques, genetic tools, and computational methods, continue to drive our understanding of the intricacies of the brain (Cakar & Yavuz, 2023).

# 1.3.1 LEVELS OF NEUROSCIENCE

Collaborations across disciplines are essential, as neuroscience requires insights from biology, physics, mathematics, psychology, and computer science to unveil the mysteries of the nervous system.

# 1.3.1.1 Molecular Neuroscience

At the molecular level, neuroscience delves into the intricate biochemical processes that govern neuronal function. Molecular neuroscience focuses on the chemical mechanisms underlying your system function. It examines the roles of molecules such as neurotransmitters, receptors, ion channels, and intracellular signaling proteins in neural activity and synaptic transmission (Kawai et al., 2023). By studying these molecules, researchers aim to understand how neural signals are generated, transmitted, and regulated at a fundamental level. This field involves techniques like molecular cloning, electrophysiology, and imaging to explore how changes at the molecular level can affect neural function and contribute to neurological disorders. Researchers have studied the structure and function of individual molecules, including neurotransmitters, receptors, ion channels, and enzymes (Straka et al., 2023). Understanding how these molecules interact allows insights into signaling, synaptic function, and the molecular basis of neurological disorders.

# 1.3.1.2 Cellular Neuroscience

Cellular neuroscience focuses on individual neurons and their interactions. Cellular neuroscience studies the structure and function of glial cells. It explores how these communicate, process information, and support brain function. This field investigates the properties of different cell types, their electrophysiological characteristics, and how they interact within neural circuits. Techniques such as patch-clamp recording, live-cell imaging, and genetic manipulation are commonly used (Vedadai et al., 2023). Understanding cellular mechanisms helps elucidate how complex behaviors and cognitive processes arise from the activity of individual cells. The morphology, electrophysiology, and connectivity of neurons, as well as the mechanisms underlying synaptic plasticity, which are the ability of synapses to strengthen or weaken over time, have been explored (Wang et al., 2023). This level of analysis unveils the fundamental properties that underlie information processing in the brain.

# 1.3.1.3 Systems Neuroscience

Systems neuroscience examines groups of neurons work together to perform specific functions. It investigates neural circuits and networks, maps connections between brain regions, and uncovers how different brain regions collaborate to process sensory information, control movements, and regulate emotions (Rutkowski et al., 2023). This level of analysis provides insights into behaviors and cognitive functions.

# 1.3.1.4 Cognitive Neuroscience

Cognitive neuroscience bridges the gap between brain activity and complex cognitive processes. Systems neuroscience examines how neural circuits and networks process information and generate behavior. It focuses on understanding the functional organization of the brain, from sensory input to output, and how different brain regions interact to perform complex tasks (Kawai et al., 2023).
