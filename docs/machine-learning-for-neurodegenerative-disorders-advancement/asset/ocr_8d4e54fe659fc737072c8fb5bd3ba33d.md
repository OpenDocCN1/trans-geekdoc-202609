There is still more work to be done in order to solve the riddles surrounding Alzheimer's. Every new discoveries shed light on the complex web of biological mechanisms that underpin this illness. This is due to ongoing research and growing understanding of the disease's pathogenesis. Complex neuropathological alterations that lead to cognitive decline and functional impairment are hallmarks of AD [16, 17]. The buildup of tau neurofibrillary tangles and amyloid-beta plaques in the brain are two of the disease's defining characteristics.

+   1. Amyloid plaques: Extracellular plaques are created when amyloid-beta peptides aggregate abnormally. These plaques impair neuronal function and are linked to synaptic dysfunction and neurotoxicity. Research on the specific processes behind the production and toxicity of amyloid plaques is still continuing.

+   2. Tau protein pathology: One of the hallmark characteristics of AD is the presence of intracellular neurofibrillary tangles of hyperphosphorylated tau, in addition to amyloid plaques. Microtubule stability is upset by tau, which results in neuronal malfunction and eventually cell death.

+   3. Neuroinflammation: The pathophysiology of AD is significantly influenced by chronic neuroinflammation, which is triggered by the activation of astrocytes and microglia. Inflammatory mechanisms damage neurons and accelerate the course of illness.

+   4. Oxidative stress: An imbalance between antioxidant defense systems and oxidative stress causes damage to cells and accelerates the neurodegeneration associated with AD. Reactive oxygen species (ROS) worsen neuronal function by causing lipid peroxidation, protein misfolding, and DNA damage.

+   5. Genetic susceptibility: The risk of AD is influenced by genetic variables, which include mutations in genes such as presenilin 1 (PSEN1), presenilin 2 (PSEN2), and amyloid precursor protein (APP). The genetic nature of the illness is highlighted by these genetic variations, which also contribute to abnormal amyloid processing and tau pathology.
