NLP models find it difficult to effectively analyze and extract useful information from the literature on neurodegenerative disorders because of use of complicated scientific jargon, specific medical terminology, and abbreviations. NLP models normally need a lot of annotated data for training, labeling datasets for neurodegenerative disorders take time and resources [14]. The creation of reliable NLP models tailored to neurodegenerative diseases is hampered by the lack of annotated data. Language ambiguity and words with many meanings (polysemy) are frequent in writing neurodegenerative disorders. The context of usage can greatly affect the intended meaning, making difficult for NLP algorithms to resolve ambiguity. The same neurodegenerative illnesses, symptoms, or biomarkers may be referred to by various researchers and sources using different terminologies. It is challenging to integrate and analyze data when there is a lack of terminology usage. Unstructured text formats, such as journal articles and clinical notes, make up a large portion of the literature on neurodegenerative disorders. To reliably extract pertinent information, NLP models must maintain untreated text.

NLP models must comprehend negation and ambiguity in text because researchers frequently provide contradictory results or convey uncertainty about their conclusions. In automated analysis, false conclusions can result from misinterpreting range or uncertainty. NLP models that have been trained on generic literature do not translate well to neurodegenerative disorders. It takes adaption strategies to properly fine-tune NLP models. Sensitive information may be used in the analysis of neurodegenerative illness, posing issues of ethics and privacy. In NLP research including clinical and patient-related information, data anonymization and adherence to clinical standards are crucial.

NLP models, particularly deep learning-based techniques, are frequently referred to as “black boxes,” making it difficult to decipher their judgments and comprehend their thinking. The term “black box modeling” is commonly used in the context of machine learning and AI, NLP refers to models whose internal workings are not easily interpretable or understandable by humans. The model takes in inputs and produces outputs, but the internal processes or decisions made by the model are not transparent or easily explainable. The characterization of NLP models as “black boxes” arises from the complexity of modern machine learning techniques, particularly deep learning models like RNNs, convolutional neural networks (CNNs), and transformer models like BERT fall into this category. While these models often achieve high levels of performance on various tasks, understanding how they arrive at specific decisions or predictions can be challenging. This lack of interpretability raises concerns, especially in applications where transparency and accountability are crucial, such as healthcare, finance, and legal domains [15].

For understanding model predictions and establishing trust in NLP applications, neurodegenerative NLP techniques are essential. The NLP for neurodegenerative disorders confronts substantial obstacles due to language difficulty, data availability, and the requirement for domain-specific knowledge [16]. To overcome these obstacles, researchers, physicians, and professionals must work together to create specialized NLP models, improve data sharing and standardization, and ensure the ethical and responsible use of NLP technologies. Overcoming these challenges will open the door for more accurate and significant insights from the literature on neurodegenerative disorders, ultimately boosting our understanding of these complicated diseases and enhancing patient care.
