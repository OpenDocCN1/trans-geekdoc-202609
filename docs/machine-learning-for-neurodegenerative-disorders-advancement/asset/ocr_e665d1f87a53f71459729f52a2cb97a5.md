ML models are superior data structures at recognizing patterns and computing complex computations that may lack the intuitive, nuanced understanding and critical skills needed in healthcare [79]. However, although humans have great medical expertise and experience, they have biases, fatigue, and are overwhelmed too! HITL systems tackles these [80]. This is delineated in Figure 11.8, a high-level view of the integration and articulation of human expertise and machines towards neurodegenerative disorders.

Machine learning is revolutionizing healthcare in all areas. With algorithms for image analysis and data interpretation, clinicians can easily identify elusive diseases with improved accuracy, in early-stage cases [80]. Additionally, HITL systems can make risk predictions by monitoring patient data, medical history, and real-time measures, informing appropriate personalized, tailored treatments [81]. It is well worth noting that clinicians maintain the final say in decision-making, so human design. Lately, in discovery ML algorithms can handle high volumes of data to suggest possible drugs and improve clinical trial designs for more efficient advancement toward human with manual oversight ensuring ethical use [82]. This confidence between AI and human acumen paves the way for a new era of healthcare, with more timely diagnoses, accurate treatments, and expedited discoveries in disease control. As illustrated in Table 11.2, several examples highlight the positive impact of human-the-loop design in neurodegenerative research.

The HITL design provides a path to the next generation of naturalistic approaches for human-machine collaboration evolving neurodegenerative research [40]. If we invest in the research, developing ethical frameworks, and utilize user-centered design for this collaboration, then perhaps one day will be possible to realize its full potential and improve diagnosis, treatment, and quality of life for those living with these conditions [50].
