## 1.3.1.5 Computational Neuroscience

Computational neuroscience employs mathematical models and simulations to capture the complex dynamics of the brain. Cognitive neuroscience bridges psychology and neuroscience, studying the neural basis of cognitive functions such as perception, memory, language, and decision-making. It aims to understand how mental models are implemented in the brain and how neural activity correlates with cognitive tasks (Volmonté, 2023). Techniques corresponding to functional MRI, EEG, and neuropsychological testing are used to investigate the brain regions and networks involved in various functions. This field provides insights into how the brain enables behavior and cognition (Choi et al., 2023). Computational models help predict neural responses, and simulate brain functions, shedding light on mechanisms that might be challenging to investigate solely through experiments.

## 1.3.1.6 Neurodevelopment and Neurogenetics

Computational neuroscience uses mathematical models, theoretical analysis, and computer simulations to understand the principles of the nervous system. It aims to create models that can explain neural behavior and how neurons and neural networks process information. This field integrates data from molecular, cellular, and neuroscience to develop computational frameworks that map brain activity (Ellwood, 2023). Applications include the development of artificial neural networks and the study of neural coding and information processing. Neurodevelopmental neuroscience focus on the formation and maturation of the nervous system, involving embryonic brain development, stem cell differentiation, and the establishment of connections between neurons (Kawai et al., 2023). Neurogenetics focuses on the genetic factors contributing to neurological disorders, brain structure, and functional variations.

## 1.3.1.7 Neurobiology of Disease

The neurobiology of disease investigates the biological mechanisms underlying neurological and psychiatric disorders. It seeks to identify the cause and molecular changes that lead to conditions like Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases, epilepsy, and depression. Researchers use advanced imaging to study disease pathology and potential therapeutic strategies. This field bridges neuroscience and clinical research, aiming to translate findings into treatments. The pathological mechanisms underlying neurological disorders such as Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, epilepsy, and schizophrenia have been explored (Ellwood, 2023). Researchers have investigated how molecular, cellular, and network disruptions contribute to disease progression, aiming to develop effective treatments and interventions.

## 1.3.1.8 Neuroimaging and Neuroinformatics

Neuroimaging and neuroinformatics utilize the use of advanced imaging technologies and computational tools to study brain structure and function. Neuroimaging techniques, for example, positron emission tomography, diffusion tensor imaging, fMRI, and MRI provide detailed details of the brain and allow for the visualization of neural activity. Neuroinformatics develops the development of databases, algorithms, and computational models to analyze and interpret neuroimaging data (Rodrigues et al., 2023). These fields are essential for understanding brain connectivity, mapping brain regions, and how brain structure relates to function. Neuroimaging techniques, including MEG, EEG, and fMRI, allow researchers to visualize and measure brain activity.

## 1.3.2.1 Understanding Neural Mechanisms

Neuroscience seeks to elucidate the functional neural mechanisms that underlie brain function. This investigation involves how neurons encode, transmit, and integrate information through electrochemical signaling. At the molecular level, researchers explore neurotransmitter release, receptor activation, and intracellular signaling pathways that modulate synaptic and neuronal excitability (Zarr & Brown, 2023). Techniques such as patch-clamp electro measure ionic currents across membranes, while molecular biology identifies signaling cascades and gene expression changes (Wu et al., 2023). Through combining these, scientists aim to map both the functional and structural aspects of neural circuits. This knowledge is essential for deciphering how behaviors and complex processes emerge from neural activity and for developing targeted interventions for neurological disorders (Poikonen et al., 2023). Now, researchers gain insights into fundamental processes such as perception, memory formation, and decision-making.

## 1.3.2.2 Mapping Brain Structure

Mapping brain structure involves a detailed analysis of the brain's anatomy and connectivity. Techniques such as MRI provide high-resolution images of brain anatomy, allowing for the visualization of cortical and subcellular structures. Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) maps help white tracts by measuring water diffusion, revealing neural connectivity patterns. Advanced microscopy methods, including electron microscopy, offer insights into cellular and subcellular organization (Wang et al., 2023). Mapping brain projects, such as the Human Connectome Project, integrate these imaging methods to create comprehensive maps of brain connectivity and function. Understanding structure is crucial for linking anatomical abnormalities with behavioral functions and for diagnosing and treating neurodegenerative disorders (Rodriguez et al., 2023). These maps identify specific brain regions responsible for various functions, contributing to our understanding of brain specialization.

## 1.3.2.3 Exploring Neural Development

The study of neural development investigates how the nervous system forms during embryonic stages and matures throughout life. Neural development research investigates the processes that shape the formation and maturation of the nervous system from embryonic stages through adulthood. This includes studying neural progenitor cell differentiation, neuronal migration, and synaptogenesis (Volonté, 2023). Techniques like in situ hybridization and immunohistochemistry are used to visualize gene expression and protein localization during development. Researchers examine how genetic factors and environmental factors drive developmental processes, using model organisms and advanced imaging to track changes over time (Wu et al., 2023). Insights are vital for understanding normal brain development and the origins of neurodevelopmental disorders such as autism spectrum and intellectual disabilities. It encompasses processes such as neurogenesis (the birth of neurons), neuronal migration (the movement of neurons to their proper locations), and synaptogenesis (the formation of synapses) (Straka et al., 2023). Understanding these processes provides insights into how the brain's architecture is established and how disruptions can lead to developmental disorders.

## 1.3.2.4 Studying Neural Plasticity

Neural plasticity refers to the brain's ability to change its structure and function in response to experiences. These include synaptic plasticity (strengthening or weakening of synapses) and cortical remapping (reorganization of brain regions) (Rutkowski et al., 2023). Synaptic plasticity, as long-term potentiation (LTP) and long-term depression (LTD), involves changes in synaptic strength and efficacy. Techniques such as two-photon microscopy and electrophysiological recording enable real-time observation of these dynamic changes in neural circuits (Cakar & Yav, 2023).
