### 11.3.2.3 Proactive Communication

Even as the biophysical research in neurodegenerative disease moves into a new age of game-changing technological advancements such as ML and artificial intelligence [72], one aspect traditionally managed less efficiently is proactive communication. Open and transparent communication with stakeholders cannot be considered merely good but for developing the trust that underpins collaboration and is needed to speed progress toward treatments [72]. Earning trust is crucial to further the future of neurodegenerative research. Transparent communication in research findings, discoveries, and limitations enhances public knowledge [74] and promotes involvement in clinical trials and other studies. Proactive communication, on the other hand, keeps misinformation and fears at bay for new technologies by enabling access to the right information in a way that the public can understand clearly. Dialogue with patients and their families provides researchers with a rich source of lived experiences and priorities that exist among community, and as concerns [75], making it patient-centered. It also promotes open data, methods, and results sharing across institutions and disciplines to help secure communication on ethical issues, risks, and safeguards in research also get realized as it ensures that the studies are conducted ethically [86]. By engaging in open communication and ethical practices between researchers and the public, we may find means of doing better research for on-target results against neurodegenerative diseases [77]. ## 11.3.3 ADDRESSING BIAS AND FAIRNESS

Neurodegenerative research needs to tackle bias and fairness. Fairness-aware algorithms, as well as a wide representation of researchers, allow us to make sure that ML models are neutral and just. Another benefit of using the ML model is that it enables a wide range of demographics to be associated with such studies, ensuring they work accurately in practice.

### 11.3.3.1 Fairness-Aware Algorithms

ML, having extended the neurodegenerative research space, requires more serious attention to fairness and equity [75]. This needs to progress from accurate model building alone the movement of fairness-aware algorithms that reduce possible biases, promoting equal opportunity in diagnosis, treatment, and clinical trials [87].

While ML methods have great promise as a tool to inform neurodegenerative research, they may be prone to [79]. This could arise from the data, algorithms, or societal factors. In the absence of diverse datasets, models can perform poorly on some demographics, and it can be inherently that certain characteristics are prioritized in algorithmic selection, leading to disparity for specific populations [80]. Furthermore, as long as systems do not take these intricacies into account, it could aggravate the existing healthcare disparities and perpetuate historical discrimination. Further tackling these biases using data curation, fairness metrics, and de-biasing techniques is necessary to ensure ethical AI in a neurodegenerative research case [15].

In neurodegenerative research using AI, bias is a potential threat, and scientists are fighting back. Additional support is needed for data collection and augmentation oversampling to ensure diverse representation in defining fairness metrics and to monitor bias by [88]. Certain algorithm transparency and explainability techniques provide insights into the decision-making process, while partial de-biasing solutions like fairness constraints are used for better-formulating models during model-building [89]. Together, they enable ethical and equitable AI in neurodegenerative research.

Beyond the technical challenges, this is an ethical imperative in neurodegenerative research [73] and sets worthy for developing fairness-aware algorithms. Prioritizing fairness from deployment, involving stakeholders across diverse domains, and iteratively pivoting approaches will enable us to fully realize the potential of AI as a research tool for clinical care that ensures access to diagnosis, treatment, and ultimately cure these disabling health conditions [80].

### 11.3.3.2 Diverse Neurodegenerative Teams

The fight against neurodegenerative diseases demands cutting-edge technology, innovative approaches, and diverse research teams [59]. By bringing together individuals with varied backgrounds, perspectives, and experiences, we can unlock the full potential of research and accelerate progress toward effective treatments and cures [82].

Diversity research transcends tokenism, unlocking a wealth of benefits. Enhanced activity and problem-solving stem from diverse perspectives, challenging assumptions and innovative solutions [89]. Improved representation and generalizability are achieved by ensuring that research apply to broader populations, minimizing bias and overlooking specific disease presentations [90].
