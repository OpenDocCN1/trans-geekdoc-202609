Biases embedded in data, algorithms, or societal factors can lead to unfair outcomes in AI-driven healthcare for several reasons. In data biases, datasets might underrepresent certain demographics, leading to poorly performing models on these groups. For example, algorithms trained primarily on white patients might miss subtle symptoms in patients of color, while in algorithmic biases, the design of algorithms itself can introduce biases, by prioritizing specific features or using decisions that disproportionately impact groups. Pre-existing societal disparities in healthcare access, socioeconomic factors, and historical discriminatory practices can be amplified by AI systems that fail to account for these issues [48].
