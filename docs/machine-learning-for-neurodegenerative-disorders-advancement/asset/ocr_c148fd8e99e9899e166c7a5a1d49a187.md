# 3.6.1 CONSTRUCTION OF KNOWLEDGE GRAPHS FOR NEURODEGENERATIVE DISORDER RESEARCH

As effective methods for organizing and displaying structured knowledge from many sources, knowledge graphs have recently come to the forefront. Building knowledge graphs is an essential step in integrating and combining data on diseases, genes, proteins, pathways, treatments, and other important entities in the context of neurodegenerative disorder research. The importance of knowledge graphs in the study of neurodegenerative disorders, their uses, and the difficulties associated with the procedure are all introduced in this overview.

# 3.6.1.1 Knowledge Graphs’ Contribution to Research on Neurodegenerative Disorders

The study of neurodegenerative diseases uses a wide range of data sources, such as scholarly publications, medical records, and genetic databases. The smooth integration of these many datasets is made possible by knowledge graphs. Knowledge graphs allow for the discovery of relationships among genes, proteins, pathways, and disease mechanisms by bridging the gap between different disciplines of neurodegenerative disorder research. Information graphs offer a thorough and organized perspective of the existing information, assisting researchers in the generation of new hypotheses and research areas [24]. Genes, proteins, illnesses, medications, and other concepts that pertain to neurodegenerative disorders are examples of entities that are represented. Relationships capture connections and associations, such as how disease pathways or how medications impact progress. The “attributes” refer to pertinent characteristics and traits of entities, including the actions of genes, the composition of proteins, and the symptoms of illnesses.

Pertinent data extracted from scholarly works and research papers in order to update the knowledge graph with the most recent findings are known as biomedical literature: incorporating information from freely accessible and genetic databases, biomedical databases, and genetics. By utilizing biological ontologies, entities and their attributes will be represented uniformly and integrated. Natural Language Processing (NLP) and text mining methods are used to extract information from unstructured text and add it to the knowledge network. The interconnection of entities across diverse data sources is undertaken to consolidate information and mitigate redundancy within the knowledge graph. Utilizing resource description framework (RDF) and SPARQL protocol and RDF query language (SPARQL) to represent and query the knowledge graph are examples of semantic web technologies. A knowledge graph is a structured representation of knowledge that captures relationships among entities, concepts, and facts. It is often organized as a graph database, where nodes represent entities or concepts, and edges represent relationships between them. Utilizing knowledge graph can provide a powerful framework for comparative analysis between different datasets, facilitating a deeper understanding of the relationships discussed concepts. Here’s how a knowledge graph can be presented in the context of different studies.

+   1. Entity extraction: By extracting entities and concepts from each study. These entities could include terms, topics, authors, methodologies, or any relevant information discussed in the studies.

+   2. Relationship identification: Determine relationships between entities based on the content of the study. For example, if studies discuss similar concepts, there could be a “related to” relationship between these concepts.
3. Graph representation: Organize the extracted entities and relationships into a graph structure. This graph can be visualized as a network.

+   4. Attribute annotation: Annotate nodes and edges with attributes that provide additional information. For instance, you might include publication dates, study methodologies, or key findings as attributes to enrich the context.
