# 5.6 CHALLENGES AND FUTURE DIRECTION

Researchers have found many challenges while predicting FoG in PD, such as the need to address data variability within a diverse patient population, the real-time application of predictive models, ensuring model interpretability to gain clinical trust, mitigating data privacy concerns, and dealing with unbalanced datasets. The challenges in FoG research include difficulties in data collection due to the limited availability of senior PD patients for collaboration, as well as the unpredictable nature of FoG events, which makes control difficult [42]. The Daphet dataset [43] (101 participants) offered consistent input, which each contain more than 250 episodes of FoG. The efficacy of FoG detection is limited by a significant time latency of approximately 350-500 ms, which limits its ability to prevent physical harm to PD patients; overcoming this challenge requires advancements in hardware or signal processing speed. The prevalence of unbalanced FoG datasets, with a scarcity of FoG events compared to healthy controls, is a common issue leading to inaccurate predictions. The future research should focus on devising improved strategies to correct this imbalance. Furthermore, FoG prediction as a superior approach which currently relies on unreliable clinical sensors, limiting its applicability in home settings. The potential solutions lie in the integration of physiological signals such as heart rate with motion sensors to improve feasibility in home settings. We would like to explore multi-modal data integration to provide a more comprehensive view of FoG triggers. Future research can also work on the advancements in deep learning techniques for capturing temporal dependencies [45–47]. There is a scope for designing an explainable AI (XAI) system for model transparency and personalized medicine. We can explore expanded telemedicine and remote monitoring capabilities, data augmentation and synthesis, and in-depth studies to track FoG in the future (Figure 5.7). These directions represent a promising path toward improving the care and well-being of PD patients with FoG.
