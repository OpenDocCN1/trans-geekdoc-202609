## 4.6 CHALLENGES

While AI holds immense promise in revolutionizing drug discovery and repurposing for neurodegenerative disorders, there are also significant challenges that researchers and practitioners must consider, as detailed in the following sections.

### 4.6.1 DATA PRIVACY AND SECURITY

Ensuring patient confidentiality is paramount, and mishandling this strict adherence to regulatory frameworks such as the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) in the US (https://aspe.hhs.gov/) or the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) in the European Union (https://gdpr-info.eu/). These regulations establish standards for data privacy and protection, ensuring that patient information is handled securely and ethically. Another major concern is the risk of data breaches. With patient data often transferred and stored across various digital platforms, the risk of unauthorized access is heightened. These breaches not only compromise patient confidentiality but also have ethical and legal repercussions [145]. Therefore, implementing stringent security measures to safeguard data against such breaches is critical in AI-driven healthcare research and applications.

### 4.6.2 ETHICAL IMPLICATIONS

Concerning the ethical implications, a primary concern is the bias in the data used to train ML algorithms. If the training data are biased or unrepresentative, the algorithm's output may be skewed, leading to unfair or inaccurate results. This is particularly critical in clinical settings, where decisions can significantly impact patient care. Ensuring diverse and representative data is crucial for mitigating such biases [146]. Another ethical consideration is informed consent, especially in scenarios such as drug repurposing. Patients or their families must be fully informed about the nature of the treatments, including their untested aspects, to adhere to the ethical standards of patient care and autonomy. This aspect is vital in maintaining the trust and safety of patients participating in trials involving AI-recommended treatments [147].

### 4.6.3 COMPUTATIONAL LIMITATIONS

In the context of deep learning and other advanced algorithms, these technologies often require specialized hardware, such as graphics processing units or high-performance computing systems, and substantial computational resources. This requirement poses significant barriers to smaller research institutions or laboratories with limited funding, hindering the widespread adoption and application of AI in various research scenarios [148]. Additionally, the inherent complexity of these algorithms, often involving intricate data structures or the need for large datasets, poses another challenge. Their sophisticated nature requires a high level of expertise for effective implementation and maintenance. This requirement for specialized knowledge and skills amidst a growing skill gap in AI and data science can be a significant hurdle, particularly in smaller or less technologically advanced institutions [149]. These factors combined can significantly impact the broader adoption and effective use of AI in research.

### 4.6.4 VALIDATION AND CLINICAL TRIALS

While AI can effectively predict drug efficacy in silico, these findings need to be validated through preclinical and clinical trials. This process is often lengthy and expensive, posing significant barriers in time and cost to the rapid development and deployment of new treatments [144]. Furthermore, navigating regulatory hurdles, such as compliance with standards and ethical considerations, presents another substantial challenge. Even after successful validation, new or repurposed drugs must undergo rigorous review. There is a critical need for AI-generated insights to be translated into clinical applications.

## 4.7 FUTURE DIRECTIONS

### 4.7.1 INTEGRATION WITH OTHER TECHNOLOGIES

In author's opinion, the future of AI in drug discovery and repurposing is closely tied to other technologies. One key area where AI can significantly impact is genomics and precision medicine. ML models, particularly deep learning, are capable of analyzing vast datasets to identify genetic variants and associations [152]. Another promising area is the integration of AI with advanced imaging technologies, such as MRI and PET. In drug development laboratories, robotic systems can automate repetitive tasks such as high-throughput screening of compound libraries. AI can enhance the capabilities of these systems and also analyze the results to identify drug candidates [153]. AI can be integrated with technologies such as blockchain and cloud computing to enhance data sharing and security. Blockchain can provide a secure and transparent platform for sharing clinical trial data, while cloud computing can facilitate the storage and analysis of large-scale data [155].

### 4.7.2 EXPANSION TO OTHER DISEASES

The future of AI in the field of drug discovery and repurposing, particularly for neurodegenerative disorders, holds immense potential for expansion to other disease areas. This expansion is fueled by AI's ability to analyze complex datasets, identify new drug targets, and accelerate the drug development process. The versatility and scalability of AI make it invaluable for addressing a range of diseases beyond neurodegenerative disorders [16]. For instance, the application of AI in oncology has already shown promising results. Cancer is a highly complex and varied disease, and AI can analyze genetic, epigenetic, and proteomic data to identify potential targets for cancer therapy. Moreover, AI can be used to screen existing drug libraries to find compounds that could be repurposed for treating specific types of cancer [87]. For example, a drug developed for Alzheimer's might be effective at targeting certain pathways in brain cancer. This approach not only accelerates the process of finding effective treatments but also reduces the costs associated with development.

Another area where AI could significantly contribute is infectious disease. The recent COVID-19 pandemic highlighted the need for rapid drug development and repurposing. AI can quickly analyze viral genomes, model their interactions with human proteins, and predict antiviral compounds. During COVID-19, AI was instrumental in identifying existing drugs that could be repurposed to treat the virus, demonstrating the speed and efficiency of AI-driven approaches. Cardiovascular diseases, the leading cause of death, are another area where AI can have a substantial impact. AI algorithms can help identify new therapeutic targets and drugs for heart disease by analyzing patient data, genetic factors, and lifestyle patterns. Moreover, they can predict patient responses to certain treatments, leading to more personalized and effective therapies [157].
