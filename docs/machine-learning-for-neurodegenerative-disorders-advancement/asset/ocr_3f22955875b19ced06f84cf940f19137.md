# ## 2.1 INTRODUCTION

Neuroimaging is a multidisciplinary field that plays a pivotal role in understanding the structure and function of the human brain. It encompasses a variety of techniques designed to visualize and map the neural activity and anatomy. Advances in neuroimaging have significantly contributed to our understanding of cognitive processes, neurological disorders, and brain development. This article explores the historical evolution and key modalities of neuroimaging, shedding light on the transformative impact it has on neuroscience [1].

The roots of neuroimaging can be traced back to the late 19th century when scientists Santiago Ramon y Cajal pioneered techniques that allowed visualization of individual neurons under the microscope. However, it wasn't until the mid-20th century that advancements in technology paved the way for non-invasive imaging of the living brain.

One of the earliest neuroimaging techniques, electroencephalography (EEG), measured in the 1920s, measures electrical activity in the brain through electrodes placed on the scalp, providing a temporal perspective on brain function. Complementary to EEG, magnetoencephalography (MEG) measures the magnetic fields generated by neuronal activity, offering high temporal resolution [2].
