# #### 3.1.1 A GROWING GLOBAL BURDEN OF NEURODEGENERATIVE DISORDERS

An aging population and rising life expectations are contributing to the growing global burden of neurodegenerative illnesses. Diseases like Parkinson's, amyotrophic sclerosis (ALS), and Alzheimer's are growing more prevalent as people live longer, and they have a major impact on people's cognitive and motor abilities. Healthcare systems are under significant pressure to manage the financial burden of direct care and broader economic implications, including the costs associated with long-term care and caregiving, resulting from this in prevalence. The effects on families and caregivers are also severe, as these illnesses not only lower patients' quality of life but also place a financial and emotional burden on them. To tackle this increasing burden, an integrated strategy is being employed, which includes early diagnosis, finding more potent treatments, and furthering research into the mechanisms behind disease. Raising awareness and providing support to impacted persons and their families also relies heavily on public health policies and efforts. To manage and lessen the effects of neurodegenerative disorders, international cooperation in research and care approaches is crucial. Breakthroughs that could eventually lessen the burden on society overall could lead the advancement of the disease and improve outcomes [1]. Disorders are a collection of long-lasting, progressive diseases characterized by the slow death or degeneration of neurons in the brain or spinal cord. The affected people, their families, and society as a whole are all significantly impacted by these diseases, which include Huntington's disease, Parkinson's (PD), ALS, and Alzheimer's (AD). There are considerable problems for healthcare systems, caregivers, and researchers as chronic conditions become more prevalent due to increasing life expectancy around the world. Worldwide, there are millions of people who suffer from neurodegenerative illnesses, and as the world ages, this is predicted to increase sharply. These illnesses not only have terrible effects on patients, but also present a tremendous strain on healthcare providers and the system as a whole, driving up prices and decreasing output. On the areas of the brain damaged and the particular disease, neurodegenerative disorders have different clinical symptoms. Memory loss, neuromuscular dysfunction, cognitive decline, speech difficulty, and problems performing daily tasks are typical symptoms. These illnesses frequently advance and regress, decreasing function and independence over time.
