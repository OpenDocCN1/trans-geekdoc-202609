# 11.2.1.1 Limited Datasets in Neurodegenerative Disorders

So next time you think of those tiny cells away in your brain, remember that what might be an elegant tango in a healthy roomy turns into a tragic waltz of degeneration – and when the scary stop turns starts doing anything. However, the challenge in understanding this dance and interference appropriately is primarily overseen by a lack of comprehensive dataset [19]. This lack of data haunts the promise that will transform how we attack these diseases.

There are no doubt, serious challenges to obtaining large-scale data on neurodegenerative diseases like Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s. Long-term follow-up studies are costly and incur significant operational challenges [20]. Moreover, the variety of symptoms displayed across individuals presents ethical issues as a result of obtaining information from cognitively impaired people another level of cognitive [21].

These restrictions create a significant barrier for to develop and implement ML models. Low-quality data resulting in poor and inefficient model may cause false diagnosis, leading to improper treatment [21]. On the other hand, limited data can exacerbate existing discrimination [22], yielding models that perform poorly on some demographic groups or represent certain forms of diseases [23]. Lack of disease-specific molecular signatures impedes development and validation in tests (further illustrated by the examples below), so innovative early-stage intervention designs or personalized therapeutic decisions are not optimized, thus improving progress that could be made over decades [24].

Datasets are limited in their scope and represent a major constraint to ML-enabled advances across neurodegenerative disorders [24]. Nevertheless, bringing new solutions and developing cooperative approaches based on ethical principles of data sharing can we flip this scarcity into an excellent place to innovate [25], overcoming data limitations on the path toward a better future of technologies, tailored to patients, and more hopeful outcomes than rather destruction by these devastating disorders [26].
