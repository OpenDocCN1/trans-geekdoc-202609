When dopamine levels plummet, intracellular network factors fail, Lewy bodies, clumps of alpha-synuclein protein, emerge as a hallmark feature, wreaking on remaining neurons. The exact role of these Lewy bodies and alpha-synuclein remains under investigation, they are believed to contribute to dopamine degeneration. Additionally, chronic inflammation and increased oxidative stress, prevalent in Parkinson's, further damage and kill neurons. # 4.2.1.3 Huntington's Disease

Huntington's disease is a hereditary and progressive brain disorder that causes uncontrolled movements, emotional problems, and loss of thinking (cognition) [58]. This rare condition results from a genetic mutation in a single gene [14]. This disease has been the subject of numerous studies from 2011 to 2022. These studies suggest that the worldwide incidence of Huntington's disease is approximately 4.88/100,000 individuals, with a 95% confidence interval ranging from 3.38 to 700 [59]. This disease is characterized by the gradual breakdown of nerve cells in the brain, leading to a decline in motor control, cognitive function, and stability [33]. Huntington's disease can appear at any age [59]. The disease is known for its wide range of symptoms, which can vary greatly among individuals [60]. The most common symptoms include involuntary jerking or writing movements (chorea); muscle problems such as rigidity or muscle contraction (dystonia); or abnormal eye movements; impaired gait, posture, and balance; and difficulty in speech or swallowing [61].
