
## 4.3.5 IN SILICO SCREENING

In silico screening is a computational technique that has become increasingly important in the field of drug discovery. Computer simulations can be used to evaluate and predict potential drug candidates from vast libraries of chemical compounds. This approach serves as a powerful tool for quickly narrowing the list of potential drugs that need to be tested in the laboratory, saving time and resources [112]. The integration of AI, particularly deep learning algorithms, has significantly enhanced the capabilities of in silico screening. These AI algorithms analyze extensive compound libraries, predict the biological activity of untreated compounds, and prioritize them for further investigation. This process is especially beneficial in the context of neurodegenerative disorders, where the biological targets can be complex and the need for effective treatments is urgent [13].

Deep learning algorithms in AI are particularly adept at identifying patterns and relationships within large datasets. By training on databases of drug-target interactions, these algorithms can predict how untested compounds might interact with a target associated with a neurodegenerative disorder [113]. For example, an AI model could predict which compound is likely to inhibit proteins implicated in Alzheimer's disease. This prediction is based not only on the structure of the compound but also on a multiple of factors, such as their chemical properties, bioavailability, and potential toxicity. The ability of AI to process and analyze a diverse range of data at high speed allows researchers to identify promising compounds that may have been overlooked using traditional screening methods [114].

In addition to identifying new drug candidates, in silico screening can also be used to better understand existing drugs. AI can help elucidate how these drugs interact with their biological targets and predict potential off-target effects. This knowledge is crucial for drug repurposing efforts, where existing drugs are tested for efficacy against different diseases [115]. For instance, a drug approved for treating a certain type of cancer might be predicted by an AI model to help define effects in treating a specific neurodegenerative disorder. By screening known pharmaceutical compounds in silico, researchers can rapidly identify candidates for repurposing trials, significantly accelerating the drug development process. This approach not only streamlines the discovery of new drugs but also unveils novel possibilities for repurposing, expanding the potential arsenal of treatments for challenging diseases [10]. As AI technologies continue to develop, their integration into in silico screening promises to revolutionize the tools available for drug discovery and development, herding a new era of rapid and efficient pharmaceutical innovation.

## 4.4 AI-DRIVEN DRUG REPURPOSING

### 4.4.1 OVERVIEW OF DRUG REPURPOSING

Drug repurposing, often referred to as drug repositioning, is a strategy in pharmaceutical development in which existing drugs already approved for one disease are tested for their effectiveness in treating other conditions. This approach capitalizes on the known safety profiles and pharmacokinetic data of these drugs, potentially shortening the path to clinical application and reducing the development costs significantly compared with new drug. The rationale behind drug repurposing is that many drugs can have multiple biological targets, effective against various diseases not considered during their original development [16]. This strategy is particularly appealing in the field of neurodegenerative disorders, where the discovery of new, effective drugs has been historically challenging due to the complexity of these diseases.

Currently, approximately 30% of new drugs and vaccines approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for treating various diseases are repurposed, demonstrating the significance of drug repurposing in modern pharmacotherapy [8]. Drug repurposing, which involves finding new uses for existing drugs, can be approached in two primary ways: drug-centric and disease-centric.

The drug-centric approach focuses on understanding and expanding the potential applications of a known drug, exploring its efficacy in treating conditions other than those for which it was originally approved. This method leverages the existing knowledge about the safety profile and mechanism of drugs [116]. On the other hand, the disease-centric approach starts with specific search for diseases, whether either or discontinued, which might be effective against it. This method is particularly useful for identifying treatments for diseases which effective therapies are lacking. Both of these approaches can be studied and facilitated by experimental methods, such as clinical trials and in silico studies, which computational models and simulations predict a drug's efficacy and safety in different scenarios [12]. These computational methods are particularly valuable for screening large numbers of compounds quickly and effectively, making a crucial component of modern drug repurposing strategies.

The integration of AI technologies in drug repurposing has markedly improved the efficiency and accuracy of identifying new therapeutic uses for existing drugs. AI algorithms can analyze large datasets containing information about drug structures, targets, and effects on various diseases. AI models can uncover hidden relations and predict new uses for existing drugs by examining these datasets [7]. For example, a drug developed for hypertension by AI could be used for other conditions. AI-driven analyses can rapidly screen existing drugs for new applications, making them powerful tools for identifying diseases with limited therapies are available.

One notable example of successful drug repurposing was sildenafil, originally developed for angina and later repurposed for erectile dysfunction. AI-driven analyses have also led to the repurposing of drugs for neurodegenerative diseases [17]. In these cases, AI not only identifies new uses but also helps in understanding the mechanisms of action. Despite challenges, the potential of AI in revolutionizing drug repurposing, especially in complex fields, is immense and continues as technologies advance.

### 4.4.2 Data Sources for Drug Repurposing

AI-driven drug repurposing relies heavily on diverse and comprehensive data sources to identify new applications for existing drugs. The first critical source of data is drug libraries, which contain detailed information about molecular structures, pharmacodynamics, and pharmacokinetics. These libraries are extensive repositories that catalog a wide range of compounds. By analyzing these data, AI algorithms can identify drugs with properties that might be effective against other diseases. For example, a drug developed to treat cardiovascular diseases might be useful for treating neurodegenerative disorders due to its molecular structure and biological activity. AI can analyze these attributes to uncover novel drug-disease relationships [118].

Another data source for drug repurposing is clinical trial data. This includes detailed records from past and ongoing clinical studies, encompassing data on drug efficacy, side effects, dosing regimens, and patient outcomes. Clinical trial databases are invaluable for understanding how drugs behave in real-world scenarios and for gaining insights into their potential uses. AI algorithms can be used to find patterns and correlations that suggest new therapeutic applications. For instance, a drug that showed limited efficacy for its primary indication in a clinical trial but exhibited positive effects on a secondary condition could be a candidate for repurposing [119].

The biomedical literature represents a vast reservoir of knowledge encompassing findings, expert opinions, and case studies. This literature includes published papers, conference proceedings, and other articles that provide insights into disease mechanisms, drug-disease interactions, and the latest developments in medical research. AI algorithms equipped with natural language processing capabilities can analyze unstructured textual data, extract relevant information, and integrate this information with structured data from other sources. This analysis can reveal connections between drugs and diseases that have not been explicitly studied [69].

Patient databases are also crucial sources of data for drug repurposing, especially for gathering information on off-label drug effects. These databases contain real-world data from health care settings, including patient demographics, treatment histories, outcomes, and adverse effects.
