# Key Modalities

# Structural Imaging

Computed tomography (CT): Uses X-rays to create detailed cross-sectional images. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI): Utilizes magnetic fields and radiofrequency pulses to produce high-resolution images of the brain's structure [4].

# Functional Imaging

Positron emission tomography (PET): Measures metabolic activity by detecting radioactive tracers. Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI): Maps changes in blood flow associated with neural activity during specific tasks.

# Temporal Imaging

Electroencephalography (EEG): Records electrical activity in the brain with high temporal resolution. Magnetencephalography (MEG): Measures magnetic fields generated by neuronal activity, providing temporal insights [5].

Neuroimaging, with its diverse array of imaging modalities, generates vast amounts of complex data, offering insights into the structure and function of the brain. As the volume and complexity of neuroimaging data grow, the need for advanced analytical tools becomes imperative. Machine learning (ML), a subset of artificial intelligence, has emerged as a powerful ally in the field of neuroimaging, offering the potential to unlock hidden patterns, enhance accuracy, and streamline the extraction of meaningful information [6]. This article explores the growing need for ML applications in neuroimaging, highlighting key challenges and referencing notable studies in the field.

# Challenges in Neuroimaging

*Data complexity*: The intricate nature of neuroimaging data, whether structural, functional, or temporal, poses challenges in extracting relevant information manually [7]. *Dimensionality*: High-dimensional data, such as functional MRI time series or diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) data, presents challenges for analytical methods. *Inter-subject variability*: Differences in brain anatomy and function necessitate personalized approaches, making it challenging to generalize findings across populations. *Pattern recognition*: Identifying subtle patterns of neurological disorders or cognitive states within large datasets is a complex task that requires advanced computational techniques.

# Role of ML in Neuroimaging

Image segmentation and classification: ML algorithms excel in automating image segmentation and classification tasks, aiding in the identification of regions of interest and the categorization of brain structures.

Disease diagnosis and prediction: ML models contribute to the accurate diagnosis and prediction of neurological disorders, such as Alzheimer's disease and multiple sclerosis, by leveraging patterns hidden within neuroimaging data.
