However, if we take the same example as cyberspace, we find that cybercrime tends to involve a number of intricate connections that makes the investigation more complicated (Broadhurst, Grabosky, Mamoun, & Chron, 2014). Thus, using the same example, if the victim attends their bank to withdraw a sum of money and finds that it has been emptied by an online attack, the victim is likely to be informed to report this matter to action fraud unit and is not likely to even see a police officer. Therefore, the likelihood of any suspect identified arrested and prosecuted is very low.

As shown by the two examples despite the efforts involving the victim, bank and money, the levels of service provided vary vastly. This is compounded by a lack of standard operating procedures for officers to follow in investigating the online example, as well as lack of knowledge as to what data are available and how data can be used. This is further exacerbated by the government's comprehensive spending review and the extensive cuts to police budgets which have resulted in fewer police officers (Millie & Bullock, 2012). Furthermore, any additional officers that the police seek to combat cybercrime will require training not only in the field of cybercrime but the basics of UK law and the traditional police investigation processes. When training is made available, the legislation needs to be in place to support the investigation techniques and the evidence-gathering processes.

Effective cyber investigations are usually undertaken using best practice approaches as evidence-based policing. In an evidence-based policing approach, officers and staff create, review and use the best available evidence to inform and change policies, practices and decisions. Such best practices help in effective and efficient investigations and evidence gathering. For traditional policing, the training and sharing of best practice is managed through the College of Policing. Training for new recruits is controlled through the IPLDP. Additional training for detectives is provided through the Initial Initial Crime Investigators Development Programme and for experienced managers through the Senior Investigating Officers programme (College of Policing, 2015).
