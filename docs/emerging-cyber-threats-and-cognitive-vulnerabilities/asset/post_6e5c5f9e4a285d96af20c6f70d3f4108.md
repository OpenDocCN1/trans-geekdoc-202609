The factual ‘RuNet award’ is a 43-centimetre statue made of bronze and completely covered with gold weighting around 5 kg. The statuette represents a column crowned with a three-dimensional letter designation of the Russian domain zone RU (‘RuNet award’ is sometimes called the ‘Internet Oscar’). On the front side of the sculpture on a special plate is engraved ‘RuNet award’, year and the name of the winner.

To win this nationwide award symbolized a patriotic/heroic act, i.e. it motivates individuals and companies to consider their ideas and knowledge to a common project that promotes the sense of nationalism and political legitimacy of ‘unified information space’. Here the Russian sense of security is established by celebrating the ‘Russian way’ of doing things. Participation in the ritualistic events links people to the community’s worldview and perception of its own history.

The establishment of 'Clean Internet' is related to the law 'On the Protection of Children from Information Harmful to Their Health and Development', but more commonly known as the 'Blacklist Bill' that passed in 2012 (Zakon, 2012). The 'blacklist' is instituted and maintained by Roskomnadzor with the stated purpose of blocking the sites related to child pornography, materials on drug abuse or production and suicide. It includes measures that allow Roskomnadzor to censor individual URLs, domain names and IP addresses. Additionally, the law allows censoring websites that would encourage 'mass riots in unsanctioned events (Soldatov & Borogan, 2015, p. 263–264).

According to their website "Clean Internet — Center for the protection of the rights and legitimate interests of children in the information sphere (Tsentr po zashcite prav i zakonnykh interesov grazhdan v informatsionnoy sfere "Chistyi Internet") is a non-profit Russian organization established by the initiative of the Minkomsvyaz in December 2012. 'Clean Internet' aims to develop mechanisms for self-regulation of RuNet controlling the distribution of illegal content and creating a safe internet environment (positionania internet-sreda).

Within the 'Clean Internet' project a voluntary association 'Safe Internet League' was established (Soldatov & Borogan, 2015, p. 298). According to its website, Safe Internet League is the largest and most reputable Russian organization fighting dangerous web content. It is a voluntary association of citizens devoted to helping law enforcement organizations. Its volunteers monitor the Internet for violations on behalf of law enforcement. The site posts detailed information on MVD Directorate K18 — the MVD component responsible for Internet crime and provides a direct email link for reporting violations. In the league's view, violations include child pornography, pornography accessible to children, promotion of drug and alcohol abuse as well as violent or 'extremist' content. All the joint cleaning projects reflect the Russian turn towards traditional values, creation of ‘morally safe environment’ and the isolation of the Russian ‘unified information space’. ‘Safe Internet League’ and ‘MediaGuard’ are presented as existing without government intervention. They resemble a Soviet subbotnik that means a free labour performed at leisure time for the benefit of society. Besides, ‘MediaGuard’ is opened by United Russia Party and it could be used for politically motivated censorship.
