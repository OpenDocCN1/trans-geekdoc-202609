As cyberattacks become more sophisticated and more collaboration will be needed between institutions (e.g., business, governments, insurance and academia) to combat cyber-based crimes and prevent major cyber incidents such as those that occurred in the recent past (e.g., WannaCry and Petya) (Choo, 2011). Currently, 69% of firms globally confirm that they are collaborating with other firms and institutions for the improvement of their security in an attempt to reduce the risk of future cyberattacks (PricewaterhouseCoopers, 2018).

We suggest that the academic community should address the process of cyber insurance support for SMEs. The governance, risk and compliance processes become more complex and the liabilities for falling a victim of cybercrime (such as with the recent introduction of the GDPR), SMEs are more prone to cyberattacks and more susceptible to their consequences. We propose for the future research agenda to look into formulating a structured governance framework for SMEs. The framework will align their cyber insurance needs to their circumstances, aspirations for growth, small business ecosystems and ultimately promote engagement with other SMEs and government agencies in standing up to cybercrime.
