# 128 6. Avoiding a cyber war: rational motives

The 2015 US Department of Defense Cyber Strategy reveals a change in cybersecurity priorities. One of the main priorities is cyberdeter. Effective deterrence, in traditional military contexts and in cyberspace, is achieved when an adversary is convinced that an attack will not produce the expected damage and that the aggressor will suffer countermeasures. This logic is reflected in the United States' 2015 cybersecurity strategy, which refers primarily to a need to demonstrate the United States' capabilities and its commitment to responding to cyberattacks (DoD CS, 2015). The updated strategy emphasizes a deterrence-by-punishment model. The expansion of the United States' offensive capabilities is also mentioned in the 2015 strategy. The paper notes that cyberattack deterrence is based on a set of actions and principles including declarative defensive positions, red lines, effectively alerting opponents and increasing the United States' access to information (DoD CS, 2015). # China

In China, discussions of digitalization and information as an important aspect of the country's economic development ensued in the 1990s. By 2003, China began to develop its cyberspace security policy. China’s cyberspace policy policy and motives evolved during two distinct time periods: 2003–2012 and 2012–18.
