
# Emerging Cyber Threats and Cognitive Vulnerabilities

Edited by Vladlena Benson and John McAlaney

Humans are adaptive creatures. Daily, we navigate a complex and changing social world. We have developed strategies to process information to allow us to identify what is important and what must be acted upon. Technology fundamentally alters the ways in which we establish trust with others and creates new ways in which this trust can be exploited by malicious actors. Yet it also provides new techniques that can be used to empower individuals and organisations to better protect themselves and their assets. This book explores the opportunities for conflict and cooperation in human interactions that arise in the cybersecurity space.

*Emerging Cyber Threats and Cognitive Vulnerabilities* contributes to this dynamic and fluid topic using contributions from academics and non-academics from a range of disciplines and professional contexts.

## Key Features

- Explains the individual and social factors that predict the actions of actors within the cybersecurity space
- Explores the risks and benefits of information and communication technology for organisations and SMEs
- Discusses the growth of interconnectedness and the conflict this creates between the desire for privacy and the need for security
- Investigates how data can be used to both predict cybersecurity incidents and to provide the evidence need to bring about prosecution of malicious cyber actors
