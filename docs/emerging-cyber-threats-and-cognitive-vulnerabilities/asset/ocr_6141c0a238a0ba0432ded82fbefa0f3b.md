Some researchers do metaanalyses on the information privacy to locate where other researchers focused on or miss (e.g., Bélanger & Crossler, 2011; Smith, Dinev & Xu, 2011). In Smith, Dinev and Xu undertook a metadata analysis of 320 articles and 128 books on information privacy. They classify the literature in two ways, using an ethical-based nomenclature and based on their level of analyses. As a result of this classification, they identify three areas that researcher locates: the first one is the conceptualization of information privacy; second, the relationship between information privacy and other constructs; and third, contextual nature of this. They found that there are many theoretical developments in the body of normative and descriptive studies that have not been addressed in empirical research on privacy. They also found that some analyses receive less attention and researchers should focus on concerns and its outcomes.

Similarly, Belangar and Crossler (2011) performed metadata analyses on 142 journals and 102 conference papers. They assert that information privacy is a multilevel concept, but rarely studied as such. The researchers mainly focus on explaining and predicting theoretical contributions with less attention to the action contributions. The paper also finds that information privacy research has been heavily reliant on student-based and US-centric samples, which results in findings of limited generalizability. Finally, information privacy can be studied at multilevel levels.

Information system researchers have studied information privacy from different perspectives and found different approaches to information privacy. They studied the e-commerce, organisation, behaviour and technical among other perspectives. Furthermore, researchers have tried to secure and protect information privacy through different approaches and methods. Some of them used technical solutions (e.g., Sutano & Tan, 2013), while others tried to change the user's behaviour (e.g., Boss et al., 2015; Gross & Acquisti, 2005, pp. 77–81; Johnston & Warkentin, 2010; Johnston, Warkentin & Siponen, 2015; Siponen & Vance, 2010; Wang, Gupta & Rao, 2015). Furthermore, some researchers have developed companies that and secure users' private information (e.g., Lee, Ahn & Bang, 2011; Smith, 2010).
