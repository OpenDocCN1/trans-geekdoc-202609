### Five-layer model

When conducting TAA in cyber domain, we need to know what to look for and where to look for it. Whether a TAA is based on a traditional model, as presented in US field manual (FM3-005, 1), on a more detailed model proposed by Taylor et al. (2014), or on a cyber-specific one, such as proposed by Suler (2016) (which will be discussed later) or one thing remains: the sources of information. As mentioned earlier, cyber domain is vast, boundless and constantly changing. Therefore, we need a cyber domain-specific framework for data collection and utilization. Libicki’s (2007) five-layer model as presented in Sartenon et al. (2016) provides a useful framework for data collection and utilization.

One important feature of the five-layer model is that communication between humans in the cyber domain will typically pass through all the layers, meaning that communication leaves residue to be collected and analyzed in all layers and provides means of influence. It has to be pointed out that in addition to easily acknowledge means, such as sending direct influence messages, there are multiple, subtle ways of altering one’s interpretation of his or her surroundings. As an example, instead of trying to alter the content in all the relevant web pages a user typically visits, an alternative way could be to gain access to user’s browser to change the search engine’s parameters, distorting results and thus affecting the interpretation of user’s reality.

## Cyber personalities

To understand cyber personalities and their possible use as a TA, we need to understand how individuals present themselves in cyberspace. As Suler (2016) points out, cyberspace is a psychological space; ‘It is an extension of our individual and collective minds. It is a space “out there” where the minds of self converge’. To understand this space and its psychological aspects, Suler has proposed eight dimensions of cyber:

- Identity: includes self-presentation in particular environment
- Social: includes relationships with individuals and groups
- Interactive: entails how well a person can understand, navigate and control a digital environment
- Text: includes all text-based communication and the psychological effects of communicating
- Sensory: ensures that cyberspace activates senses (mainly auditory and visual)
- Reality: ensures how much of the experience is grounded in everyday life and how much of it is mere fantasy (such as presenting oneself via avatar in multiplayer online role game)
- Temporal: is the use of time
- Physical: involves physical existence, including bodily sensations and movements

Through these dimensions, it is possible both to understand and anticipate behaviour of individuals in cyberspace, and they can serve as a useful framework when we have enough information or when we can interact with them. The challenge is that conducting TAA in the cyber domain, it is not often possible to have close interaction with TAs. There are, however, numerous studies on behavioural residue leave behind when they operate in the cyber domain. Studies show various correlations between data collectable from the cyber domain and behaviour. Such data includes, but is not limited to digital pictures, audio and video files, social media posts, social media activity (such as liking, linking or commenting), browsing and data history and topographic analysis of both physical and virtual networks. The idea of a cyber personality in the form we propose in this chapter is to combine this data collected from various sources. In other words, it is an attempt to conceptualize a theoretical model for utilizing data from the five layers of the domain. While single sources of data predict behaviour to a certain extent, combining the data of multiple sources into a single entity can provide a more complete (possibly conflicting) picture and therefore provide information necessary for conducting TAA in cyberspace.
