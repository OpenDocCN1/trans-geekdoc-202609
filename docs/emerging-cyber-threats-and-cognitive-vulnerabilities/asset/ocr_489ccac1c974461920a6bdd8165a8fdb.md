# Myth or reality?

A debate continues to rage that there has not been any catastrophic terrorist attack. Denning (2012) states that for a politically motivated attack to be considered cyberterrorism, it would have to be sufficiently serious to incite terror that caused by violent physical attacks. Furthermore, Weimann (2004) asserts that the potential and the actual damage caused by cyberterrorism have been ignored. Various scenarios have been proposed to depict the potential chaos that could be if critical infrastructure were to be attacked (Squitieri, 2002). Terrorism has metamorphamorph from the traditional form to the cyber form of technology-aided terrorism as cyberterrorism (Samuel & Osman, 2014).

Hacking tools are growing in sophistication and agitated terrorist groups may look for an innovative outlet for their political, religious or philosophical ideals. For cyberterrorists there is no shortage of new tools and technologies that have allowed them to commit their criminal acts virtually anywhere and from anywhere in the world (Subramanian, Liu, & Winterdy, 2016). Denning has stated that the next generation of terrorists are experiencing a digital world in which hacking tools provide for a much more powerful, simpler, easier and accessible environment (Elrom, 2007). Terrorism could turn their focus of attack on critical infrastructure in order to have a far-reaching impact. Already, with the Stuxnet virus and malicious interruptions at power plants, the effects of tampering with core services can be felt.

Already in 2014, President Obama believed that cyber threats to national security and expected cyberterrorism was to grow in the years as 15 years ago was not on the radar screen but will be one of the biggest concerns for whoever is president after him (Gasparino, 2014). In the United States, cybersecurity has become a priority for the government. The Department of Homeland Security has recognized that sophisticated cyberattackers and nation-states could exploit vulnerabilities and go on disrupt, destroy or threaten the delivery of essential services.
