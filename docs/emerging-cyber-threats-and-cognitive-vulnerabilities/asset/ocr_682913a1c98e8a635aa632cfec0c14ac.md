As pointed out in FM3-05-301, for a set of messages to be effective, a TA typically needs to consist of a homogenous group of people with similar conditions and vulnerabilities. There are various ways of building these homogenous groups, such as looking for demographic or geographic features. Centres of gravity (people or small groups that have large degree of power over others) or key communicators or other high-value individuals (HVIs) are also desirable, although such TAs are typically small (FM3-05-301, 5–3–54). These TAs can be most effective, regardless of their size size. In these situations, TAA should be more detailed than what is described in FM3-05-301. To understand and influence it is possible to gather far detailed information than it is from larger TAs, which are always more general in nature. When dealing with HVIs or small groups, the requirements for information can be very detailed. Taylor, Furnham, and Breeze (2014) have proposed six key dimensions for understanding and influencing a single individual. These elements are (1) culture, clan; (2) biography, family; (3) intelligence; (4) personality; (5) dark side of personality and (6) motivation. Most of the information in these dimensions can be extracted from the cyber domain.

Once initial TAs have been selected, a more detailed scrutiny is applied, beginning with the conditions that the TA has to cope with. Conditions, according to FM3, are the life-affecting elements over which the TA has little or no control. In an ideal TAA, common conditions within the group lead to similar needs. These unfulfilled or perceived needs are manifested as vulnerabilities, as the TA's desired to fulfil, alleviate or eliminate the needs as motivation to change behaviour. A successful TAA provides the most effective means of satisfying the TAs in ways that allow the psychological operation's objectives to be reached (FM3-05-301, 5–4–9).

The TAA desired is built for a military purpose and requires the TAs to be approved by an appropriate command structure before executing the influence activities. Thus, a requirement of identifying the members of the TA is built into this process. This, in turn, is required to ensure only legal targets are engaged by the military to streamline the influence activities with other, typically kinetic activities. A feedback loop is also included in the process, in the process of setting impact indicators that are monitored to find out whether or not the objectives are being achieved (FM3-05-301, 5–18).
