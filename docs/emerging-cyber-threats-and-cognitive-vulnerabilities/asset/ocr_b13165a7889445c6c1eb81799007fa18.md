Johnston et al. (2015) also used fear appeal framework and made an enhanced fear appeal rhetorical framework to motivate people compliance with information security policy and procedures. They argued that fear appeal and PMT have two problems when alluring security. First, the fear appeal has been used to grasp the individuals an existing threat without concern for behaviour mechanism. PMT assumes that all threats are personally related to the recipient. Thus, they made an enhanced fear appeal rhetorical framework where they added the elements of fear appeal to elements of formal and informal forms of sanction severity, certainty and clarity. They conducted a survey and interviews on 559 employees of Finnish government, and found that using the enhanced fear appeal rhetorical framework will provide a significant positive influence on compliance intention. In another work, these studies limited their focus primarily to the construct of PMT, thereby ignoring other determinants of behaviour that employees who mostly have rules and policies to follow. Finally, they suggested a technical solution to change the behaviour considering the variety of the context.

Siponen and Vance (2010) reviewed 174 ethical decision-making and surveyed 790 employees using neutralization theory. Their results suggested that practitioners should work to counteract employees' use of neutralization techniques. The neutralization theory, according to Sykes and Matza (1957), stated that law-abiding citizens and criminals or rule breakers in the norms and values of society. Sykes and Matza (1975) suggested five techniques of neutralization: denial of responsibility, denial of injury, denial of the victim, condemnation of the condemners and appeal to higher loyalties. This study applied to a specific context and culture where it cannot be generalized. It also failed to show the cause of noncompliance to the policy.
