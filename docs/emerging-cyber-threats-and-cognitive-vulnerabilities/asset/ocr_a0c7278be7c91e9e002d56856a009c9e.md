Law enforcement may use the same tools as advertisers or marketers, but these tools are traditionally intended for marketing purposes rather than the needs of law enforcement agencies. It has been noted within PPDR authorities that the use of location-based services will become a more common tool in the field of crime prevention. As communication technology evolves, people's living environment develops. Development of intelligent cities brings new kinds of opportunities to develop services from a safety environment perspective.

If a citizen walks to the geofenced area and receives a mobile advertisement message, the citizen might be motivated to look at and take action on the text message based on: if permitted someone to send such a message (opt-in); if they trust a company producing or selling a product or service (brand trust); if the product or service is relevant to the customer's current needs and wants; if the customer likes the price that is quoted on the message; if the customer is financially in a position to buy such a product or service last, if the customer is in the right mood to buy a product (Kini & Suomi, 2018).

If a citizen does not know the purpose of using privacy data, usage is ethically untenable. Therefore, it is important for a citizen to be aware of how she can be treated as a customer in marketing, but also as a potential threat to the functions of CI.

According to Sheng et al. (2006) customers' privacy concerns vary depending on their purpose or context for using the technology. Personalization has major implications in emergency situations, for example at the site of an accident where appropriate services need to be delivered to the right person and place. The effect of personalization on perceived benefits is greater in emergency than non-emergency contexts.
