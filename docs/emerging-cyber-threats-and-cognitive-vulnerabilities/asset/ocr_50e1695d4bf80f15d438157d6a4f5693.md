# Five-layer model

When conducting TAA in cyber domain, we need to know what to look for and where to look for it. Whether a TAA is based on a traditional model, as presented in US field manual (FM3-005, 1), on a more detailed model proposed by Taylor et al. (2014), or on a cyber-specific one, such as proposed by Suler (2016) (which will be discussed later) or one thing remains: the sources of information. As mentioned earlier, cyber domain is vast, boundless and constantly changing. Therefore, we need a cyber domain-specific framework for data collection and utilization. Libicki’s (2007) five-layer model as presented in Sartenon et al. (2016) provides a useful framework for data collection and utilization.

One important feature of the five-layer model is that communication between humans in the cyber domain will typically pass through all the layers, meaning that communication leaves residue to be collected and analyzed in all layers and provides means of influence. It has to be pointed out that in addition to easily acknowledge means, such as sending direct influence messages, there are multiple, subtle ways of altering one’s interpretation of his or her surroundings. As an example, instead of trying to alter the content in all the relevant web pages a user typically visits, an alternative way could be to gain access to user’s browser to change the search engine’s parameters, distorting results and thus affecting the interpretation of user’s reality.
