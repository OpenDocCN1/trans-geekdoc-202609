

# Preface

On May 10, 2017, my student Dr. Zhao Guanghui and I discussed issues related to the development of intelligent transportation at AIMS in Hangzhou. Subsequently, he planned to write a book on transportation big data. I told him that the University of Michigan has a world-leading intelligent transportation laboratory. They spent years trying to optimize a few traffic lights next to the University of Michigan. Afterwards, they came to a large city in China and began managing traffic lights using big data, conducting transportation big data experiments. With the help of traditional cameras and induction loops, traffic lights were switched manually every 90 seconds. By analyzing congestion data sent to the monitoring center, AI was used to adjust the traffic lights. Two months later, peak-hour congestion in the city was alleviated by 20%. In the future, more cities around the world will optimize traffic lights through big data, which will help maximize the utilization of limited road resources and save a tremendous amount of time and transportation resources. Furthermore, China has 150 million to 200 million private cars. These vehicles sit idle in parking lots approximately 95% of the time each day. They occupy vast amounts of space, waste resources, and cause congestion. With the help of big data, these idle resources can be mobilized to serve people in need of rides. What a tremendous contribution this practice will make!

After reading this manuscript co-authored by Dr. Zhao Guanghui and Professor Zhu Gusheng, I am deeply convinced that big data technology has driven the development of transportation in China. The examples covered in the book include the collection of transportation big data sources, the application of transportation big data platforms, pain points, cases, and business models in transportation logistics, public transportation, and traffic safety, operational experience, as well as the current status, strategies, and actions of transportation big data in other countries. Dr. Zhao proposes that transportation big data has changed the travel mode of Chinese society, shifting from the past "finding a car" to the current "dispatching a car to find a person." By analyzing data from rainy days, sunny days, peak hours, off-peak hours, weekdays, and holidays, it is possible to predict future travel conditions and passenger flow at specific locations under these circumstances. The accuracy rate has now reached 85%–90%. Decisions can be made 15 minutes in advance to dispatch vehicles to designated locations. With 15-minute advance dispatching, route planning, and positioning, ride-hailing services have brought tremendous convenience to people, which was previously unimaginable. Now people have taken it for granted.

The effort to match passengers and vehicles is not the only application. Thanks to big data, the effort to match people and goods has also undergone tremendous changes. At 24:00 on November 11, 2017, Tmall's single-day sales reached 168.2 billion yuan. Of this, online shopping contributed 90%, with buyers and sellers covering 222 countries and regions. Before "Double 11," sellers used big data to predict that express parcel volume would grow by more than 30% compared to 2016. Big data predicted that parcel volume on "Double 11" would reach 1.4 billion, with over 1.7 million couriers participating in logistics. By enhancing collaboration and data sharing to improve logistics services, electronic waybills covered over 80% of the entire industry. This was the first time big data prediction covered more than half of the industry's express parcels, improving efficiency through the connection of big data and social collaboration. Based on the upgrade of the logistics early warning radar, the big data platform has been comprehensively upgraded from time-period prediction to real-time prediction, covering over 50% of China's express parcels. Algorithm optimization has enabled the application of more dimensions of big data. As a result, real-time prediction of daily parcel delivery volume has become possible, with an accuracy rate exceeding 90%. This data can help express partners deploy capacity, and enterprises can prepare capacity in advance based on predicted data. Consequently, efforts increased by 4,000 temporary workers and 5,240 trucks, and aircraft lease tonnage was increased in advance. "Big data order routing" can automatically calculate delivery routes based on waybill information and assign parcels to the outlets nearest to buyers. Over 50% of parcels reach buyers faster through big data order routing. Sellers use big data to predict best-selling products and manage inventory in warehouses nationwide, bringing parcels as close to buyers as possible. By using big data to predict parcel volume, goods can be delivered to buyers earlier. The model has shifted from "buyers waiting for parcels" to "parcels finding buyers."
