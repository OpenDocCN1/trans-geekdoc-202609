

# Case Study of China's Shared Bicycle Success

In contrast, shared bicycles in China have been far more successful. Represented by Mobike and OFO, domestic shared bicycles were once China's dockless shared bicycle travel platforms. They created the "dockless bike-sharing" model, dedicated to solving urban travel problems. Users only need to scan the QR code on the bike body via the WeChat official account or App, or directly enter the corresponding license plate number to obtain the unlock password, to unlock and ride, available for pick-up and use anytime, anywhere, or share their own bicycles to the OFO sharing platform to obtain lifetime free usage rights for all OFO yellow bikes, exchanging one for N. Since going online in June 2015, OFO has deployed 10 million shared bicycles, providing over 4 billion rides for more than 200 million users in over 250 cities across 20 countries worldwide. From October to November 2018, OFO was listed in multiple enforcement cases by courts including the Beijing No.1 Intermediate People's Court and the Beijing Haidian District People's Court, involving enforcement targets exceeding the limit of 53.6 million yuan. Currently, the OFO shared bicycle brand has exited the Chinese market. OFO developed rapidly; within just over a year since the birth of shared bicycles, the active user base reached as high as 70 million, with relatively high user stickiness. Particularly in short-distance travel demand, the usage frequency of shared bicycles is very high.

## Technical Implementation and Big Data Application of Shared Bicycles

By combining user location information with bicycle location information, shared bicycles can reasonably schedule bicycle distribution locations, allowing users to quickly find bicycles. When a user scans the code to unlock a bicycle with a smartphone, the smart lock authenticates the user information, and then unlocks via the signal connection between the smart lock and the bicycle operation backend. Data such as user riding trajectories, trips, and time during the entire riding process can be viewed in the shared bicycle backend. These backend big data are not only used by bicycle operating companies for scheduling bicycles to meet user needs but also for precision marketing.

According to the "2017 Shared Bicycle and Urban Development White Paper" released by Mobike, the cumulative riding mileage of shared bicycles in China exceeded 2.5 billion kilometers, equivalent to approximately 3,300 round trips from the Earth to the Moon. Riding has replaced private car travel to a certain extent, bringing considerable "green effects." The birth of shared bicycles has doubled cycling travel volume. In cities, shared bicycles have become a good alternative to public transport (buses, subways) and private cars. To a certain extent, the use of shared bicycles has reduced dependence on private cars; after the emergence of shared bicycles, the proportion of private car travel decreased by about 3.2%.

Shared bicycles are only a microcosm of the sharing economy in the transportation field. With the emergence of shared cars, shared motorcycles, and shared buses, these sharing modes affect big data transportation in the following ways:

(a) **Revitalizing existing transportation resources and improving resource utilization rates.** For example, shared cars like Baojia Car Rental revitalize private cars and improve the resource utilization rate of the whole society (see Figure [4.1](#ch04.xhtml#fig4.1)).

(b) **Improving travel efficiency.** For example, shared bicycles solve the "last kilometer of travel" problem, changing the previous situation of illegal taxis in short-distance travel, allowing everyone to travel freely, thereby alleviating traffic congestion.

(c) **Data analysis makes transportation smarter and more targeted.** Under the sharing economy, users have a strong purpose in using transportation tools, coupled with precise solutions from big data mining, eliminating the blindness of past travel and alleviating traffic congestion.

(d) **The sharing economy model makes urban transportation smarter and more environmentally friendly.**

# 4.2 Big Data Transportation Feature Two: Full Utilization of Resources

The high empty-seat rate of cars in transportation has always been a headache for traffic managers. A five-seat car, when fully occupied with five people sharing one car, is unobjectionable. If five people each drive a car, four extra cars will be added to the road, which will inevitably aggravate congestion, and exhaust emissions will increase pollution.

The model of five people sharing one car is the hitchhiking (shunfengche) or carpooling (pinche) model. Didi Chuxing has long provided hitchhiking and carpooling services. When passengers choose carpooling, one Didi driver can pick up and drop off multiple passengers to different destinations simultaneously, thereby reducing the number of vehicles used for travel while ensuring normal passenger travel, significantly alleviating congestion.

According to statistics, during the 2017 Spring Festival travel rush, Didi Hitchhiking transported 4.2 million passenger trips. Converted based on existing railway capacity, this was equivalent to adding 1,909 conventional slow trains or 5,874 8-car EMU trains, which was 4 times the number of passengers transported by trains during the same period last year. A total of 2.8 million car owners participated in empty seat sharing. Even on New Year's Eve (22:00 on Jan 27 to 6:00 the next day), 70,000 people still embarked on their journey home for reunion. During the Spring Festival travel rush (Jan 28 - Feb 1), cross-city hitchhiking was mainly for short-distance home returns, visiting relatives, and short-distance tours around surrounding cities. ^([2](#ch04.xhtml#fn2))

From the perspective of passenger structure, people from Wenzhou prefer to travel alone, with 66.3% of Wenzhou people returning alone via hitchhiking; people from Guangzhou prefer to travel in groups, with 52.11% of Guangzhou people choosing to return via group hitchhiking.

Under the carpooling and hitchhiking models, idle resources are utilized to the maximum extent, becoming an effective way to relieve traffic pressure. Most importantly, the government and enterprises have obtained real first-hand data on people's travel through hitchhiking and carpooling. The government formulates congestion relief plans and decisions based on this, and enterprise services are more precise.

In addition to carpooling for small cars, other modes of transportation have also attempted carpooling. For example, in October 2017, the Xi'an Railway Bureau launched train carpooling through crowdfunding. Passengers participating in the crowdfunding boarded the train on time at the agreed time, and the train adjusted its original departure time to a time convenient for passengers, avoiding the embarrassment of passengers having no cars or buses after arriving at their destination late at night. This is another form of carpooling, presented in a crowdsourcing manner. If it develops maturely, multiple special trains can be customized through carpooling to avoid empty runs (see Figure [4.2](#ch04.xhtml#fig4.2)).

Of course, large buses like Dudu Bus also have carpooling and hitchhiking models. Regardless of the model, the data precipitated by the platform is the most real and valuable. It is precisely with the support of these data that big data transportation becomes more efficient and intelligent.
