
Autonomous driving technology is the most watched new transportation revolution in recent years. Currently, most automotive enterprises worldwide have begun R&D on autonomous driving technology, and China's Baidu has successfully launched its own autonomous vehicles, marking a critical step forward for autonomous driving.

Compared with traditional driving modes, autonomous driving technology relies entirely on big data operations. It is a high-end artificial intelligence capable of real-time road condition judgment based on massive map data. Compared to manual driving, autonomous driving technology will play an ultimate role in routing, driving modes, and emergency avoidance, maximizing the benefits of big data. Therefore, future autonomous driving technology will gradually replace traditional driving modes, and big data will become the primary "natural person" of autonomous driving.

### 11.1.3 Connected Vehicles (Internet of Vehicles)

Compared with vehicle-road coordination technology and autonomous driving technology, connected vehicles have seen widespread application. The characteristic of connected vehicles is implanting internet capabilities into traditional driving modes, such as real-time voice broadcasting of road conditions, voice association between mobile phones and vehicles in critical road conditions, rapid payment upon entering highways, and intelligent parking payment. Many brand vehicles have integrated mobile payment, which is an effective attempt at internet technology.

For example, vehicle health checks are an effective application of big data via the internet. Through big data, connected vehicles collect usage data from all users, analyze vehicle pros and cons, maintenance cycles, and tire pressure, and send this information to owners via onboard screens or mobile Apps. Thus, owners will fully understand vehicle conditions and perform timely maintenance, avoiding sudden vehicle breakdowns on the road. When vehicles are in healthy condition, traffic accidents will also be significantly reduced.

### 11.1.4 Rapid Promotion of ETC Technology

Currently, ETC technology has been actively and effectively promoted in many provinces. It is also a manifestation of personalized services in the big data transportation industry. ETC, or Electronic Toll Collection System, is currently the most advanced toll collection method in the world. After a vehicle installs an ETC card, it can utilize microwave dedicated short-range communication between microwave antennas at ETC lanes before highway toll stations, and use computer networking technology for backend settlement processing with banks. Vehicles can complete payment without waiting, greatly alleviating the queuing and payment problem at highway entrances/exits.

Undoubtedly, ETC technology can be used for traffic optimization, applicable not only at highway toll stations but also in scenarios suitable for payment. In the future, ETC will further play the role of "traffic resource optimization," deeply integrating with bus cards and electronic traffic management cards to achieve the goal of "three cards in one." This will further form the "Transportation + Big Data" model. One card can meet multiple needs such as driving, riding, and payment, greatly improving traffic efficiency and alleviating traffic congestion and resource waste issues.

### 11.1.5 Deep Integration with Individuals

So-called personalized services must be combined with individuals to highlight "non-mass-market" characteristics, thereby conforming to the definition of personalization. The exploration of personalized services in the big data transportation industry must be precise down to the individual. With the accumulation of big data, we can analyze travelers' daily travel habits, shopping habits, and living habits to make precise suggestions and judgments. Even big data can provide unique travel modes by analyzing travelers' personalities, physical conditions, and environments. For example, for the elderly, big data will quickly push "sleeper" travel plans based on their physical conditions and living habits; for young people keen on exploration, big data will push "flight + car rental" combinations. Such personalized services can not only provide unique experiences for travelers but also efficiently utilize travel time, optimize various transportation modes, and achieve effective utilization of traffic resources.

### 11.1.6 Ride-Hailing "Going Rural"

Compared with cities, rural areas have fewer traffic problems, but with the continuous development of urbanization, rural traffic issues are becoming increasingly prominent. In cities, ride-hailing has become mainstream. It can effectively optimize traffic resources, allowing empty-running vehicles to quickly find passengers through big data information transmission. The traffic optimization effect is very significant. In the future, this taxi model will gradually extend to rural areas.

For example, Guizhou Province has begun trials. Guizhou launched the "Tongcuncun App" for rural areas, allowing villagers to call nearby taxis and buses through it. Simultaneously, the App displays detailed departure times, stops, and seat information, and villagers can purchase tickets directly through the system. This "Tongcuncun" rural version of the ride-hailing model has attracted widespread attention from the capital market, and the big data transportation industry has begun to penetrate rural areas.

In addition to the above, the big data transportation industry will further solve travel traffic problems from a content perspective, such as precise recommendation pushes for personal car purchases. As the big data transportation industry becomes increasingly personalized, resources can effectively complement each other, road conditions can be effectively optimized, thereby creating a new transportation operation system.

### 11.2.1 Cross-Border Integration Becomes the General Trend

Residents' travel is highly dependent on taxis. However, the number of taxis in most cities is limited, making it difficult to balance supply and demand. Especially during peak hours, taxis are nowhere to be found, and even some areas see taxis privately raising prices during peak hours. This not only leads to poor user experience but also seriously interferes with normal traffic development.

Through big data analysis, we can see a phenomenon in the transportation field: the automobile is the entrance to service. If the automobile problem is not solved, the traffic problem cannot be solved.
