
# 12.1.4 Impact of Unmanned Vehicles on the Automobile Industry

It is because of the unprecedented impact that unmanned vehicles have had that they are causing a huge change in the automobile industry. Related studies show that with the advent of unmanned driving automobiles, highway safety will be greatly enhanced, and air pollution and traffic congestion will be effectively alleviated.

## 12.1.4.1 Enhancing Highway Safety

Today, highway accidents are a major problem troubling the world. According to statistics, in the United States, about 35,000 people die in car accidents each year; in China, about 260,000 people die in car accidents each year; and in Japan, about 4,000 people die in highway accidents each year. According to the World Health Organization, the number of people killed in highway accidents worldwide each year is as high as 1.24 million.

According to statistics, the annual loss due to fatal car accidents is up to $260 billion, the loss due to injuries caused by car accidents is up to $356 billion, and the annual loss due to highway accidents is about $625 billion. According to a survey by the RAND Corporation, among various factors causing fatal traffic accidents, drunken driving accounts for 39%. In this regard, the widespread application of unmanned driving vehicles will effectively alter this situation and reduce or even avoid car accident casualties. In addition, according to data released by the transportation department, 60% of all traffic accidents in China are caused by collisions between pedestrians, bicycles, electric bicycles, and cars and trucks. Human error is responsible for about 94% of all motor vehicle accidents in the United States.

Research by the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety in the United States shows that with the installation of automatic safety devices (collision braking system, collision warning system, lane departure warning, blind spot detection, etc.), cars will be safer on the highway, and highway accident fatalities can be reduced by 31%—that is, about 11,000 people will be prevented from being lost in traffic accidents per year.

## 12.1.4.2 Relief of Traffic Congestion

Traffic congestion is an epidemic in major cities. In the United States, each driver wastes about 40 hours a year stranded on the road due to traffic congestion, and the cost of traffic congestion is about $121 billion. In major cities such as Moscow and Rio de Janeiro, the time wasted in traffic congestion is even worse, with each driver wasting more than 100 hours per year in traffic jams. In China, the number of cities with more than 1 million cars is about 35, and the number of cities with more than 2 million cars is 10; about 75% of roads in urban areas of large cities are congested. The number of private vehicles has now reached 126 million, a 15% increase from the previous year.

Donald Shoup's study found that 30% of traffic congestion in urban areas of large cities is caused by drivers circling back and forth looking for parking spaces. This is the very factor that causes traffic congestion and air pollution, as 30% of carbon dioxide emissions come from cars, and carbon dioxide is considered responsible for climate change. In addition, it is estimated that 23–45% of traffic congestion occurs at road intersections. Because traffic lights and stop signs are stationary and time intervals are set in advance, they cannot take traffic flow in all directions into account, making it difficult for them to play a real role in regulating traffic flow.

As unmanned vehicles account for an increasing proportion of traffic flow, in-vehicle sensors will be combined with intelligent transportation systems to optimize traffic flow at road intersections. The time intervals of traffic lights will be automatically adjusted according to traffic flow in each direction, so that the efficiency of vehicle traffic flow at road intersections will be improved to provide an effective solution to traffic congestion problems.

## 12.1.4.3 Reducing Air Pollution

Cars are a very important contributor to air pollution. According to a study by RAND (James M. Anderson, Nidhi Kalra, Karlyn D. Stanley, Paul Sorensen, Constantine Samaras, Tobi A. Oluwatola, et al. [www.rand.org/t/RR443-2](http://www.rand.org/t/RR443-2)), unmanned driving technology may improve fuel efficiency by 4% to 10% through smoother acceleration and deceleration compared with manual driving. According to surveys, smog in industrial areas is closely related to the number of cars, and some studies have proved that exhaust emissions from cars forced to stop at red lights or in traffic jams are 40% higher than those emitted during normal driving. The increase in the number of unmanned vehicles can alleviate air pollution to a large extent.

Unmanned driving vehicle sharing systems may help achieve energy savings. Researchers at the University of Texas at Austin conducted a study of carbon monoxide, sulfur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, volatile organic compounds, fine particulate matter, and greenhouse gases and found that unmanned car-sharing systems can save energy and reduce emissions of many types of pollutants.

Uber found that 30% of passengers in Los Angeles and 50% of passengers in San Francisco choose to carpool. Globally, about 20% of passengers choose to carpool. Whether unmanned or conventional cars, the more passengers choose to carpool, the better the environmental benefits and the more traffic congestion will be alleviated. Air quality is effectively improved because carpooling changes the pattern of one-person-one-car rides.

# 12.2 Global Distribution: Competing for the High Ground of Unmanned Driving

## 12.2.1 Competitive Pattern of the Global Unmanned Driving Industry

The unmanned driving automobile is a type of smart car, also known as the wheeled mobile robot, which makes unmanned driving a reality by relying mainly on the smart driver in the car.

In simple terms, unmanned driving automobiles make automatic driving of the vehicle a reality on the basis of high-precision maps, supplemented by data collected by onboard sensing devices, successfully working with the help of intelligent algorithms to identify, and operations to make decisions to control the vehicle. As can be seen through the working principles of unmanned driving vehicles, unmanned driving technology is an integration of technological achievements in various fields such as artificial intelligence, vehicle braking, environmental recognition, etc., bringing new development opportunities for the automotive, smart chips, sensors, and map navigation markets.

### 12.2.1.1 A Look at the Unmanned Driving Industry from California Road Test Qualifications

Currently, California is the first in the world to pass unmanned driving legislation and is the premier test site for unmanned driving automobiles in the world. The U.S. Highway Traffic Safety Administration, which oversees U.S. auto safety, is headquartered in California. Because of its openness, authority, and inclusiveness, California has become the world's leading test site for unmanned driving cars.

The Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) in California issues California Driverless Road Test Permits for unmanned driving automobile companies, which are granted to test unmanned driving automobiles on specific public roads. In September 2014, the DMV issued the first driverless road test permits (see [Figure 12.1](#ch12.xhtml#fig12.1)). To date, 45 companies have been granted driverless road test permits worldwide, and these companies cover a diverse group of enterprises including traditional car companies, parts suppliers, technology giants, and startups.
