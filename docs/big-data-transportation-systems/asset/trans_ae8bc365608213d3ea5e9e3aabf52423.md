

### 10.5.2 Upgrading the Big Data Traffic Management Model

Five basic management models constitute the foundation of big data traffic management. On this basis, big data traffic management is upgraded to enable big data to play a more effective role. Targeting China's current traffic conditions, the upgrade direction of big data traffic management should focus on the following points:

(1) **Cross-regional management systems**. Traffic departments in various regions possess a certain degree of administrative autonomy, leading to poor information flow between regions. This problem is particularly prominent between provinces and cities. Therefore, big data traffic should break such information barriers and achieve resource sharing to optimize traffic conditions from a macro perspective.

(2) **Fully leveraging aggregation advantages**. Because traffic departments at all levels implement an administrative regional autonomy model, and different traffic themes are managed by different departments, data is often overly dispersed, making it difficult to improve efficiency. The introduction of big data management models aims to break information barriers between departments, such as data from bus companies, subway companies, taxi companies, and bike-sharing companies, so that stations can learn from each other during establishment, thereby improving efficiency. This is not only an optimization of traffic management but also an optimization of cooperation models between different departments, thereby effectively improving administrative efficiency.

(3) **Optimizing resource allocation**. Insufficient resource allocation is a shortcoming of traditional traffic management systems. Some departments have sufficient staffing and complete monitoring equipment, even with idle resources; while other departments, due to a lack of manpower and financial resources, cannot effectively carry out traffic management work and fully utilize resources. With the help of big data aggregation, resources can be effectively centralized to compensate for uneven distribution.

It can be seen that the upgrade of the big data management model lies in "resource sharing and departmental collaboration." Only by establishing a sound management system and carrying out comprehensive organizational optimization can big data further play its role and improve traffic management efficiency.

### 10.5.3 The Hangzhou Model: Comprehensive Utilization of Big Data

Currently, the big data traffic management model in Hangzhou, Zhejiang Province, has gradually become clear, forming a new "One Center, Three Systems" management model, which effectively integrates the city's traffic departments. "One Center, Three Systems" refers to the Traffic Command Center, the Traffic Management Information System, the Traffic Control System, and the Traffic Engineering Information System.

This management model enables traffic management departments in various urban districts to collaborate effectively, with the Traffic Police Detachment becoming the main force of ground traffic services. Based on platform information, a graded early warning and intervention mechanism is implemented on main roads and intersections. At the same time, the digital service rooms of traffic police squadrons carry out online patrol duties. Through big data applications, traffic problems are detected at computer terminals, effectively expanding the coverage of road management. When problems erupt in a concentrated manner, joint operations are launched immediately, with the traffic police detachment, urban management, urban construction, and other departments responding quickly to solve road traffic problems, greatly alleviating traffic congestion and ensuring road traffic safety and smooth flow.

The Hangzhou big data traffic management model also actively introduces third-party enterprise forces. Data from Didi, Baidu Maps, etc., are also imported into the Hangzhou Big Data Traffic Platform and released by the Hangzhou Traffic Police Department. For example, with the help of third-party enterprise big data statistics, the Hangzhou Traffic Management Department found that as of the end of February 2017, the number of female drivers in Hangzhou reached 1.3964 million, accounting for 36.3% of the total number of motor vehicle drivers, which can provide data support for the formulation of traffic regulations. Parking lot construction, drainage planning, and route planning can take the female group into consideration, making them more in line with Hangzhou's traffic characteristics.

In December 2017, another major big data traffic event occurred in Hangzhou. On December 2, 23 big data enterprises established the Zhejiang Traffic Big Data Maker Space, featuring "Internet + Traffic," to serve traffic in Hangzhou and even the whole of Zhejiang Province. Among them were not only institutions such as the Zhejiang Provincial Traffic Information Center, the Zhejiang Provincial Highway Bureau Settlement Center, the Zhejiang Provincial Tourism Information Center, and the Zhejiang Expressway Information Engineering Technology Co., Ltd., but also Baidu, Huawei, Alibaba Cloud, Prologis, Beijing E-Hualu, Wangshen, and the three major operators, jointly constructing a complete new big data management model. In the future, the big data management model will inevitably present a collaborative posture of "departmental institutions + third-party enterprises," effectively integrating forces from all parties to find the key to urban traffic development.

### 10.6 Big Data Traffic Credit Model

The operation of traffic big data also requires credit support. This is because, whether it is data collection, screening, analysis, or release, the grassroots masses are often at the receiving end. If information frequently deviates, it will inevitably lead to public distrust of big data traffic, affecting the operation of big data.
