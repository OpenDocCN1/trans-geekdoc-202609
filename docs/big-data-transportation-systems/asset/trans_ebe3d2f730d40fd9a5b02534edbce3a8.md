
Autonomous vehicles are equipped with onboard sensors to collect environmental data around the vehicle. Onboard sensors mainly consist of cameras, Global Positioning Systems (GPS), LiDAR, and other components. Meanwhile, the intelligent processing module in autonomous vehicles can effectively control operating parameters such as steering and speed parameters to ensure safe driving.

Specifically, the intelligent processing module consists of a decision-making module, a control module, and other parts. It can acquire various types of data, including video image data collected by cameras, point cloud data collected by LiDAR, position data collected by GPS, steering or speed data output by the decision-making module, and execution result data output by the control module. The intelligent processing module is used to control the operation of the autonomous vehicle.

In many regions, traffic capacity is very limited due to factors such as the number of lanes. The realization of autonomous driving by autonomous vehicles can shorten vehicle following distances, ensure driving safety, reduce traffic accidents, improve road capacity, and enhance network capacity.

### 12.3.6.1 Analysis of Influencing Factors

There are many factors affecting highway capacity. Three factors are selected for analysis: the number of routes, the proportion of autonomous vehicles among all traveling vehicles, and the peak hour capacity of the road, to obtain their corresponding relationships.

Autonomous vehicles do not require drivers; if a road emergency occurs, the vehicle reaction time approaches zero. According to data, the average speed of autonomous vehicles is far faster than that of human drivers. Therefore, the higher the proportion of autonomous vehicles in the traffic flow, the stronger the road capacity. In addition, the more routes a road has, the greater its capacity. Besides these two major factors, the presence of intersections also significantly affects road capacity; the more intersections, the worse the road capacity.

Traffic flow is affected not only by road capacity but also by other factors such as geographical location, network density, road service level, regional vehicle ownership, and travel time. These five factors will be analyzed in detail. The more prosperous the area where the road is located, the higher the network density, the higher the road service level, the greater the regional vehicle ownership, and the larger the traffic flow during peak travel periods.

### 12.3.6.2 Analysis of the Effects of Introducing Autonomous Vehicles on Different Roads

Regions with different network densities have different approaches to introducing autonomous vehicles. First, road capacity is affected by multiple factors. If road conditions do not fundamentally change, road capacity will be significantly affected as more and more autonomous vehicles hit the road. To gain a deeper understanding of this impact, relevant institutions have conducted research with the following results.

First, on free-flowing roads, when the proportion of autonomous vehicles is below 54%, the growth rate of road capacity will continuously slow down as the proportion continues to increase; when the proportion exceeds 54%, the growth rate of road capacity will accelerate as the proportion continues to increase. However, considering the manufacturing cost of autonomous vehicles, such roads are not suitable for large-scale deployment of autonomous vehicles.

Second, on generally smooth roads, the impact of autonomous vehicles on road capacity is greatest when the proportion reaches 40%. As the proportion continues to increase, this impact gradually weakens. Therefore, autonomous vehicles can be introduced on such roads to alleviate traffic pressure.

Finally, on roads prone to severe congestion, road capacity is strongest when the proportion of autonomous vehicles reaches 45%. As the proportion continues to increase, road capacity actually decreases. The reason for this phenomenon is that the maximum road capacity limits traffic flow growth. For such road sections, traffic authorities can set up dedicated lanes for autonomous vehicles to alleviate traffic pressure.

Comparing congested road sections with actual maps reveals that the city center has the largest traffic flow, especially at intersections of arterial roads and surrounding dense roads. Therefore, opening dedicated lanes for autonomous vehicles in this area can solve traffic congestion problems.

### 12.4 Intelligent Traffic Management Model Based on Autonomous Driving

### 12.4.1 Deep Integration of Free Travel and Sharing Modes

For autonomous vehicles to replace human-driven vehicles, it is necessary not only to break down barriers between technologies such as the Internet of Things, big data, cloud computing, sensors, and artificial intelligence, but also to achieve breakthroughs in management aspects such as urban planning adjustments, traffic rule improvements, and regulatory system construction. From the development practices of multiple industries, technology and management complement each other; only with their coordinated development can autonomous vehicles be widely promoted and popularized on a large scale.

Therefore, when exploring traffic management models matching autonomous vehicles, one cannot wait until autonomous driving technology is fully mature before exploring. Lagging planning, construction, and regulation will impose numerous restrictions on technological development. For example, setting high thresholds for autonomous vehicles to operate on roads will prevent them from conducting road tests, posing a major obstacle to the development of autonomous driving technology.

Many countries worldwide have now realized the important value of intelligent traffic management models. For instance, in August 2014, the Singapore government established the Committee on Autonomous Road Transport for Singapore (CARTS) to provide guidance and assistance for the development of autonomous vehicles in four dimensions: freight transport, fixed routes, point-to-point transport, and public facility operations.

(1) **Freight Transport**: Completing cargo transportation and delivery through automated truck platoons.

(2) **Fixed Routes**: Providing transport services on intercity and intra-city public transit routes.

(3) **Point-to-Point Transport**: Meeting point-to-point and short-distance travel needs through shared vehicles.

(4) **Public Facility Operations**: Providing public services such as plant irrigation, garbage collection, and road sweeping.

In December 2017, the Beijing Municipal Commission of Transport, jointly with the Beijing Municipal Public Security Bureau Traffic Management Bureau and the Beijing Municipal Economy and Information Technology Bureau, issued two guiding documents: the "Guiding Opinions on Accelerating the Road Testing of Autonomous Vehicles in Beijing" and the "Implementation Rules for the Management of Road Testing of Autonomous Vehicles in Beijing." This will undoubtedly have a very positive impact on the further development of autonomous vehicle technology and management models.
