

# Safety and Development of Autonomous Vehicles

When the driver and the vehicle vie for control (traffic accidents), traffic authorities struggle to determine liability. If drivers are required to stay alert and be ready to switch to manual mode while the vehicle is in motion, one might as well adopt manual driving directly. This "human or vehicle" design renders autonomous driving meaningless, increases the driver's psychological burden, and dashes the desire for free and comfortable driving.

Essentially, the "human or vehicle" design is an irresponsible manifestation regarding autonomous driving safety. If there is a "fallback" of manual driving, relevant personnel will not focus on autonomous driving safety issues; unified standards for the design and manufacture of autonomous vehicles cannot be established in the short term, and user safety cannot be effectively guaranteed.

On May 7, 2016, a Tesla with the `Autopilot` driver-assistance system engaged collided with a white tractor-trailer; the `Autopilot` roof was completely destroyed, and the driver was killed instantly. Tesla explained: the white truck was not recognized against the bright sky background, and from the `Autopilot`'s perspective, the side of the trailer appeared suspended in mid-air, causing the system to misjudge and fail to activate automatic emergency braking, resulting in the collision. Furthermore, because the impact occurred at the `Autopilot`'s windshield area, the crash safety system failed, leading to the driver's instant death.

The accident caused a sensation, as Tesla had claimed a fatal accident occurs every 94 million miles in the US, while the `Autopilot` system had logged 130 million miles. Tesla emphasized that autonomous driving technology is continuously evolving and that `Autopilot` is not perfect. Who exactly should be held responsible? Some blame Tesla, some blame the negligent driver, and others blame the sensor supplier `Mobileye`. This perception of autonomous vehicle accidents recalls the "War of Currents" between Tesla and Edison.

In 1880, Tesla invented the world's first AC generator, while Edison invented the electric chair to publicize the dangers of AC power. After fierce competition, Tesla's AC system prevailed, making outstanding contributions to the development of human civilization. All new things have both pros and cons; there are no absolute advantages or disadvantages. Facing the new phenomenon of autonomous vehicles, we should grasp the development trend and not completely deny it due to a single accident.

## How to Solve Autonomous Vehicle Safety Issues?

To reduce traffic accidents during autonomous vehicle operation, manufacturers should establish a strict system of regular factory maintenance for vehicles and an emergency response service system for operation. They should focus on establishing a "dual-system" control mode to address the risk of vehicle network compromise (e.g., hacking, virus damage). In the "dual-system," the primary system refers to the networked open system, and the backup system refers to an independent closed-loop system. Once the driver detects that the primary system has been compromised, they can immediately switch to the backup system to ensure normal vehicle operation and safe passenger evacuation, followed by repair.

If the primary system is damaged but the driver fails to notice (e.g., code tampering causes the vehicle to hit an obstacle instead of avoiding it), the driver may not have time to switch systems, leading to an accident. Therefore, manufacturers must install protective software on autonomous vehicles to enable automatic fault detection and virus scanning/removal, and they must assume responsibility for accidents caused by insufficient protection, preventing manufacturers from shirking responsibility and harming user rights. Additionally, manufacturers should ensure that internal and external vehicle interfaces are difficult to disassemble and tamper with, preventing human sabotage and effectively enhancing safety performance.

Furthermore, if traffic authorities mandate that autonomous vehicles must be switchable to manual mode at any time, bus and taxi companies—which are the most suitable candidates for introducing autonomous vehicles—will completely lose the "labor-free" advantage. The reason is: without full-time drivers, companies would first have to verify users' licenses, alcohol consumption, drug use, etc.; the process becomes not simpler but more complicated, and the original convenience of autonomous vehicles risks becoming an illusion.

In summary, traffic authorities should not enact regulations requiring "autonomous vehicles to be switchable to manual mode at any time"; otherwise, it will lead to vague industry standards, unclear responsibilities, and a chaotic, awkward situation. If manual assistance is accepted to avoid accidents, autonomous driving has a major loophole. To achieve the ultimate goal of autonomous driving development, traffic management departments must reform the existing traffic system, clarifying the responsibilities of autonomous vehicle manufacturers, system software providers, bus operating companies, and insurance companies, so that users can ride with peace of mind. Recently, the US federal government and some states have affirmed the legality of autonomous driving; the NHTSA determined that `Google`'s autonomous vehicle is controlled by the automated driving system, not manually by the owner. That is, the old rule of "must be equipped with a steering wheel and brakes" no longer applies.

In the future, autonomous vehicles will no longer be equipped with steering wheels, pedals, instrument clusters, control levers, rearview mirrors, and other devices. Instead, they will feature panoramic windows, 3D screens, spacious open cabins, mobile communication and office equipment, etc. At that time, the car will no longer be a "connector" of life but an extension of living space; people's time and energy will be completely liberated to engage in more meaningful and valuable affairs.
