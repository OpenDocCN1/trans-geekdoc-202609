If we additionally know that $u$ is piecewise smooth, for instance as in the solutions of convex scalar conservation laws (Holden and Risebro 2015), then one can use the results of Petersen and Voigtlaender (2018) to obtain the following result (De Ryck et al. 2024c, Lemma 3.4).

# **Lemma 4.17.**

Let $m,n\in\mathbb{N}$, $1\le q<\infty$ and let $u:[0,1]\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}$ be a function that is piecewise $C^m$-smooth and with a $C^n$-smooth discontinuity surface. Then there is a tanh neural network $\hat u$ with at most three hidden layers and $O(\epsilon^{-2/m+\epsilon^{-q/n}})$ neurons such that

$$
\|u-\hat u\|_{L^q([0,1]\times[0,T])}\le \epsilon. \tag{4.13}
$$

Finally, De Ryck et al. (2024c) found that we have to consider test functions $\varphi$ that might grow as $|\varphi|_{W^{1,p}}\sim\epsilon^{-3(1+2(p-1)/p)}$. Consequently, we will need to use Lemma 4.16 with $\epsilon\leftarrow\epsilon^{4+6(p-1)/p}$, leading to the following corollary.

# **Corollary 4.18.**

Assume the setting of Lemma 4.17, assume that $mq>2n$, and let $p\in[1,\infty]$ be such that $1/p+1/q=1$. There is a fixed-depth tanh neural network $\hat u$ with size $O(\epsilon^{-(4q+6)n})$ such that

$$
\max_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\sup_{\varphi\in\Phi_\epsilon}\mathcal{R}(\hat u,\varphi,c)+\lambda_s\|\mathcal{R}_s[\hat u]\|_{L^2(\partial D\times[0,T])}^2+\lambda_t\|\mathcal{R}_t[\hat u]\|_{L^2(D)}^2\le\epsilon. \tag{4.14}
$$

where

$$
\overline{\Phi}_\epsilon=\{\varphi:|\varphi|_{W^{1,p}}=O(\epsilon^{-3(1+2(p-1)/p)})\}.
$$

Hence we have answered Question 1 in the affirmative for scalar conservation laws.

**Proof.** We find that $\hat u$ will need to have a size of $O(\epsilon^{-(4q+6)/p})$ for it to hold that $\max_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\sup_{\varphi\in\Phi_\epsilon}\mathcal{R}(\hat u,\varphi,c)\le\epsilon/3$, where we used that $q=p/(p-1)$. Since in the proof of Lemma 4.17 the network $\hat u$ is constructed as an approximation of piecewise Taylor polynomials, the spatial and temporal boundary residuals ($\mathcal{R}_s$ and $\mathcal{R}_t$) are automatically minimized as well, given that Taylor polynomials provide approximations in the $C^0$-norm.
