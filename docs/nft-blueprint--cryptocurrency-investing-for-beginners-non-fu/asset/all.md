What is a Non-Fungible Token?

 

## 1.1 Definition of NFTs

The abbreviation NFT stands for non-fungible tokens, on German: non-replaceable token. NFT are certificates for unique digital objects. You certify that you own a specific file. Fungible in itself means that a thing can be replaced by something equivalent. For example, it does not matter whether you have 20 dollars in your wallet as a single note, four 5-dollar notes or 20 1-dollar notes.

However, objects that are unique by certain properties are not fungible. Such objects can exist either physically or digitally. For physical objects, such as a painting or car, it is easy to set a value. This is measured by the uniqueness of the work of art and how much people are willing to pay for it.

For digital objects, this is more complicated. After all, despite copyright and usage rights, every image, song and video can be copied and redistributed at the click of a mouse. It is difficult to determine the exact value or owner of a file. This is where the idea of NFT starts: The digital tokens contain information about a particular file that is both unique and verifiable. The blockchain technology is used to verify the authenticity of the files.

### NFTs and Blockchain

Blockchain is basically a kind of database in which transactions of digital goods, such as cryptocurrencies, are stored in blocks and distributed on many computers worldwide. For example, Bitcoin is on a blockchain. Its architecture makes the blockchain tamper-proof.

Unlike cryptocurrencies, NFT in a blockchain does not represent an interchangeable asset that is transferred from one person to the next, but a concrete asset – like a unique digital work of art that is linked to an owner and a value. Simply put, by storing the NFT in the blockchain, digital goods become things that really belong to you.

Currently, the open Ethereum blockchain is the most important for NFT trading. Purchases of virtual goods are therefore usually processed in Ether, the second highest endowed cryptocurrency after Bitcoin.

###  

### What is the difference between fungible and not fungible?

To understand what makes NFT so special, you first need to understand the difference between fungible and not fungible. If something is fungible, it means that something is homogeneously interchangeable. Examples of this from the real world include banknotes or precious metals: one gram of pure gold is worth as much as another gram of pure gold. And if you give someone a ten-dollar note, it wouldn't matter if they didn't give back exactly the same note.

All this changes when something is not fungible. Although two items may look identical at first glance, both have unique information or features that make them irreplaceable or non-interchangeable.

An example of a non-fungible good could be a plane ticket. Airline tickets are the same at first glance, but each ticket contains a different passenger name, a different destination and a different seat number. Therefore, there could also be serious consequences if you exchange a plane ticket for another. The same is true in the digital space with NFTs. Another example would be Internet domains, as each domain can only exist once.

Non-fungible tokens can limit things in digital space and present them uniquely. Many other tokens or cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin or Ether are fungible. If you send someone an ether and get an ether back for it, you wouldn't notice any difference.

The same applies to tokens: Most tokens so far are based on Ethereum's ERC-20 standard. For the sake of simplicity, one can imagine that each of these tokens represents a 10-dollar note. If you send this token to someone and get another one back a week later, it's the same as the other.

All of this changes with non-fungible tokens. Currently, most NFT on the Ethereum blockchain use the so-called ERC-721 standard. Tokens that use this standard can be compared to Pokémon or Yu-Gi-Oh collectible cards. Each token contains unique information and a different level of rarity.

There is also another serious difference to consider. Fungible tokens are divisible, which means it is possible to send or own a fraction of Bitcoin or another ERC-20 token. Similar to cash, where you can pay with a ten-dollar note and receive change.

Non-fungible tokens cannot be shared and must be purchased or sold in their entirety. As with collectible cards, where no one would think of buying half a card.

Here are the unique properties of something fungible, like cash or cryptocurrencies, that are different from NFTs.

### Criticism of NFT

But there are also headwinds – and not only because of the large crypto price fluctuations. One of the criticisms concerns possible copyright violations. It becomes problematic, for example, if it is not verified whether a seller on the blockchain really owns the rights. In addition, the owner owns the crypto object only on the blockchain on which it was purchased. In most cases, this is the Ethereum blockchain. In theory, however, any object on other blockchains that support NFT could be reoffered.

NFT are also energy consuming. Since a single transaction of the Ethereum blockchain requires the computing power of many computers, the power consumption is very high. NFT consist not only of a transaction, but of a variety of them, depending on the number of bids at an auction. As a result, the energy demand increases. The digital artist Memo Akten analyzed the energy expenditure of a single NFT-sale. On average, a sale consumes 340 kilowatt hours. By comparison, an ordinary Ethereum transaction accounts for about one-tenth of that.

 

 

## 1.2 The History of NFTs and Important Milestones 

Just as DeFi (Decentralized Finance) protocols mimic banks' financial products, NFTs mimic the world of art galleries, shops, and museums. But without expensive and risky intermediaries.  
This is possible with Smart Contracts (dApps) that were originally born on Ethereum. This type of programmable blockchain can serve a variety of decentralized applications. In other words, NFT marketplaces are dApps that enable the entire NFT economy by connecting an NFT creator directly to a buyer. This greatly facilitates the entry of both parties and thus removes the usual barriers to entry.

NFTs themselves are a type of token - ERC-721 - that is non-divisible or not fungible, hence the name. Its value is based on the assurance of blockchain technology that no NFT can be falsified. In addition, the entire transaction history is traceable and verifiable as a fully digital asset, be it as graphics, music, video, animation, gaming asset, etc.

As we can see from Google Trends, global interest in NFT has increased enormously since February. With more and more people appreciating digital assets over physical ones, this NFT trend was almost predictable. With the following top 11 of the most expensive NFTs, we're immersing ourselves in this new world.

Now that we have explained what NFT is, we can dive into the extensive history of these assets.

### 2012–2013: Coloured Coins

It can be argued that Colour coins are the first NFT's to exist. Coloured Coins consist of the small values of a BTC often as small as 1 Satoshi. Colour Coins can be used to represent a large number of assets and can have multiple use cases, including:

●Estate

●Coupons

●Ability to extract your own cryptocurrency

●Issuing shares of a company

●Subscriptions

●Access tokens

●Digital collections

Coloured Coins were an example of a big leap in Bitcoin's capabilities. The downside was that it could only represent certain values if everyone agreed on their own value. Bitcoin's coding language was never designed to enable this type of behaviour within its own network, so Colour Currencies were only as powerful as its weakest participants. For example, 3 people agree that 100 Colour Coins represent 100 company shares. If a participant decides that they no longer sync Coloured Coins to represent company shares, the whole system crashes.

The first mention of Coloured Coins appears to come from a blog post by Yoni Assia in early 2012 titled "bitcoin 2.X (aka Colour Bitcoin) – first features". In his post, he discusses Coloured Coins, but makes no reference to the various assets or their use cases. Instead, he claims that Coloured coins are unique and identifiable from normal bitcoin transactions, since they are bitcoins that are part of the "Genesis process". The potential of these new assets was apparently not explored until December 4, 2012, when Meni Rosenfeld published an article titled "Overview of Coloured Coins". A few months later, in 2013, another article titled "Coloured Coins – BitcoinX" was published. 

The flaws of Coloured Coins were obvious; the system worked best in a permissible environment, which means that in some cases it is better to simply use a database. However, Colour coins opened the door for more experiments and laid most of the foundations for NFT's. The enormous potential of putting real-world assets in distributed ledgers was clear, but implementation required a more flexible blockchain.

### June 2017 – Cryptopunks

It wasn't until the Ethereum Blockchain that NFTs really picked up speed. The first Ethereum NFTs appeared as Crypto Punks in summer 2017. Crypto Punks are 10,000 unique digital characters created by [Larvalabs.](https://www.larvalabs.com/cryptopunks) Your digital property records are stored in the form of unique tokens on the Ethereum blockchain. Each Crypto Punk has individual optical properties, so that no one is the same. Crypto punks inspired the digital art market and were the forerunners for later standards. Due to the limited offer and the cult status of early users, they are already considered digital antiques. They are still tradable and interoperable with most NFT applications on Ethereum.

As the trade of rare peps in Ethereum increased rapidly, two "creative technologists" decided to create their own NFT projects with a slight twist. John Watkinson and Matt Hall realized that they could create unique characters created on the Ethereum blockchain. Characters will be limited to 10,000 and the two characters will not be the same. In reference to the Cypherpunks who experimented with bitcoin's pioneers in the 1990s, they named their project Cryptopunks.

Surprisingly, Watkinson and Hall chose to allow anyone with an Ethereum wallet to request Cryptopunk for free. All 10,000 Cryptopunks were quickly requested, launching a thriving secondary market where people bought and sold them. Interestingly enough, Cryptopunks does not follow the ERC721 standard as it has not yet been invented, but due to its limitations they were not entirely ERC20. Therefore, Cryptopunks can best be described as ERC721 and ERC20 hybrid.

### October 2017 – Birth of CryptoKitties

With CryptoKitties, NFT's have reached the mainstream. CryptoKitties is a blockchain-based virtual game that allows players to adopt, grow, and trade virtual cats. Cats on the blockchain!

Although Crypto Punks were officially the first NFTs on the Ethereum blockchain, most of the CryptoKitties are still in the memory. At the end of 2017, they became the first mainstream application of NFTs.

2017 was already a year of hype around cryptocurrencies, driven by the ICO bubble on the Ethereum platform. In a short time, countless new ICO projects started and each of them had new and supposedly scarce tokens on offer. This triggered a veritable gold-digger mood. More and more new cryptocurrencies on the Ethereum blockchain saw the light of the digital world. It wasn't long before the network became increasingly congested, with significantly increased transaction fees as a result. However, one of the reasons for the high utilization was, among other things, the creation and trading with CryptoKitties.

CryptoKitties are digital images of comic cats created by Dapper Labs for a blockchain computer game. Each cat is unique and therefore only present once on the blockchain. The special thing about them is that in the on-chain computer game, new unique CryptoKitties can be bred by clubs of different cats. These new cats could then be auctioned or sold on the open market.

 

The CryptoKitties Bubble

Breeding has attracted many speculators. The idea was simple: you buy some rare CryptoKitties, breed some new, even rarer cats with them, sell them at high prices and then the whole thing again from the front. At peak times in 2017, the trading volume was approximately 5000 ETH and sales of CryptoKitties achieved prices of more than 100,000 US dollars.

It was almost absurd to pay such high prices for digital cats. But the incredible story behind it, combined with the great hype around cryptocurrencies, brought many new buyers into the market in a short time. Ethereum's network capacity at the time was not up to the rush, resulting in horrendous fees and hours of waiting for the trading and breeding of CryptoKitties.

This mixture of hype, speculation and viral story eventually ended up in a bubble that also attracted attention in the press. But, as might be expected, the bubble burst soon after. In mid-December 2017, demand for CryptoKitties fell at once and prices plummeted. The entire crypto bubble began to burst shortly after, and a multi-year bear market was launched.

CryptoKitties are great examples of NFT collectibles. CryptoKitties are digital cats on the Ethereum blockchain. The idea behind this NFT is to enable buyers to nurture these kitties and reproduce, just as they would in real life. Within a few weeks of launching in 2017, about $20 million was spent on them. But the collectibles market is not limited in any way, as it extends in different directions. Another excellent example of the usefulness of NFTs involves art collecting. Experts can verify and authenticate paintings and sculptures before creating a non-fungible token for each piece of art.

This incredible project was apparently featured on every news station from CoinDesk to CNN. Perhaps this was because the game blocked and slowed down the Ethereum blockchain, or people made crazy profits in trading. Some virtual cats even sold for over $100,000.

## 2.1. Standardization

Crypto developers have over the years created common, reusable token standards on public blockchains. These are the ones that are going to basic principles such as ownership, transfer and simple access controls.

These policies or protocols are similar to how the HTTP protocol sends a request between 2 devices when images are displayed in JPEG or PNG formats between two different computers.

Blockchains add primitive elements and a layer that allows them to store their metadata, providing developers with a whole new set of state information on which they can develop applications. This area is called blockchain standards (which support stateful data storage and logical operations).

The token for Ethereum is the virtual storage or representation of any asset on Ethereum or other blockchains.

ERC-721 is a standard that explains how to create unique tokens on the Ethereum blockchain. Unlike tokens created with the ERC-20 standard, each token created with ERC-721 is unique.

Non-fungible tokens created with the ERC-721 standard are unique, unlike other tokens. Since each ERC-721 token is different from each other and is not of the same value, it is more difficult to exchange tokens with each other.

Coins such as Bitcoin and Ethereum, as well as fiat currencies such as USD are fungible, meaning that each unit is equivalent. Therefore, each unit of such cryptocurrencies and fiat currencies has the same value and can be exchanged directly with each other.

In addition, fungible coins and tokens can be divided into small units such as Bitcoin 0.5 bitcoin and 0.0002 bitcoin, for example, while a non-fungible token is a single unit and cannot be divided into small units.

The ERC-721 standard defines the minimum interface that a smart contract must perform to allow functions such as managing and swapping unique tokens, while not requiring or restricting the addition of additional functions for token metadata.

### ▪ERC-721 vs ERC-1155

ERC-1155 is a multiple token standard. Although the ERC-721 was an instant success and was used to create probably millions of NFT, it also had some limitations. For example, it was expensive for deploying large databases of items.

Erc-1155 multi-token standard created by Witek Radomski, Andrew Cooke, Philippe Castonguay, James Therien, Eric Binet, Ronan Sandford on June 17, 2018. They are also called semi-fungible tokens.

In short, the ERC-1155 enables an infinite number of both interchangeable and unchangeable tokens to be traded in a single smart contract. It is designed to avoid bottlenecks in future transfers by allowing the management of fungible, semi-fungible and non-fungible tokens,

Thus, different types of tokens are collected in only one transaction instead of a lot of transactions, allowing different types of items to be transferred faster, cheaper and lighter (within the scope of the data size area covered within the block). To overcome these shortcomings, Vitek Radomsky decided to create his standard - ERC-1155. This means that the ERC-1155 is an advanced and better version of the ERC-721, offering many new features to create NFTS.

For example, with the new ERC-1155 standard, you can use both an infinite number of non-fungible and fungible assets in one deployed smart contract. ### Blockchain Forking

Hard Fork and Soft Fork are important components of blockchain technology. Bitcoin's blockchain is at the limit of its capacity. A new block is created approximately every ten minutes, carrying a large amount of data on countless transactions. The blocks can be used to verify that a transaction is valid. These blocks may be at most one megabyte in size on Bitcoin's blockchain. To circumvent this restriction, a Hard Fork or a Soft Fork are possible. What are the advantages and disadvantages of a hard fork and a soft fork?

Basic Problems of Blockchain

A subsequent change of the blockchain is not possible. Adding new blocks is also an act of strength that can only be accomplished with the computing power of various computers. However, once data has been stored in the blockchain, the information can be considered correct. On average, a new block is produced every ten minutes within this system. In order to remain stable and balanced, the difficulty of calculating blocks increases regularly. When the limit of possible transactions is reached, there may be sharp price drops. With Bitcoin, this has already happened, which is why an enlargement of the blocks is planned.

What happens to a Hard Fork?

Unlike Soft Fork, a hard fork is a way to make changes to the blockchain that is not compatible with the old blocks. Old clients do not understand this change in the log and, if in doubt, discard the individual transactions or entire blocks. The prime example of a hard fork is a change in block size. A block larger than one megabyte on Bitcoin's blockchain is considered faulty by the current clients and is therefore discarded. Proponents of the new block size cannot simply send the large blocks to all clients but must ensure that they are accepted. This is only possible if enough participants can be found to use the appropriately adapted mining software.

Changing the size of the protocol is technically relatively easy to perform. Rather, the problem lies in convincing a large part of the community of the new block size so that it is accepted in its implementation. In the case of the Hard Fork, the new system is introduced after a specific block, which has been defined in advance. This creates two different blockchains: the first blockchain continues the old system and refuses to accept the changed blocks. The second blockchain, on the other hand, only accepts the new blocks and no longer allows the old ones. The two blockchains share a common past but go their separate ways after the split.

 

Possible Consequences of a Hard Fork

A hard fork is a difficult undertaking because it needs the support of the community. In the absence of miner support, the Hard Fork has failed because no transactions are forwarded without it. A failure would also be a lack of user support: in this scenario, all users, exchanges and traders use the old version, which would no longer make the miners' reward system profitable. Accordingly, it is in the mind of the miners that many users are behind the new system. With a large minority of miners, the "scoured" coins would only be exchanged within the small circle of proponents in fiat currencies, which would reduce demand and the price of the coins generated.

The worst-case scenario would be if both protocols are actively used and therefore run side by side. This would, for example, create a second currency besides Bitcoin. Such a case has already occurred twice, once Bitcoin Cash and once Bitcoin Gold has been created, which exist parallel to Bitcoin. In order to avoid secession, there must be consensus among both miners and users. However, achieving this in practice is difficult, as all blockchain participants are mindful of their own economic advantage and hope that Hard Fork will give them an advantage over other market participants.

What is a Soft Fork?

With a soft fork, the risk of separation is much lower than with a hard fork. The soft fork approach is often used to up-source the blockchain. Two well-known and successful soft forks of Bitcoin's blockchain are BIP 66, which has revised the validation of signatures, or P2SH, which changed the formatting of wallet addresses. The Soft Fork rejects all blocks that still have the old properties. However, this only works with Soft Fork if the community and miners are behind the change. Dissent would otherwise lead to secession, i.e., a second cryptocurrency.

### How do Smart Contracts Work?

Simply put, smart contracts work like vending machines and work hard. In smart contracts, you can think of a required amount of cryptocurrency, escrow, right to host, id or account as the amount you have. All rules and penalties are not only predefined by smart contracts, but also enforced by them.

A smart contract can work on its own, but it can also work with other smart contracts. They can be set up in a way that they depend on each other. For example, the successful completion of a particular smart contract can trigger the start of someone else, and so on. Theoretically, all systems and organizations can resort to completely intelligent contracts. To some extent, this is already applied in various cryptocurrency systems where all laws are predefined and therefore the network can function spontaneously and independently.

It starts with predefining the contract: At this point, all the terms of engagement are established by all stakeholders. This could be the specific currency that will be used for making payments, the variable interest rate, or the currency rate. Also, the terms for execution are set while predefining the contract, and this could be the date, time, or several other criteria.

✔Events: This is the point where events (such as the information received and the kick-off of the transaction) instantly trigger the implementation of the contract.

✔Execution and delivery: What determine the movement of value are the terms of the contract, and this is based on the fulfilment of the set conditions.

✔Settlement of contract: This is the final stage of the process, and it can take place in two ways. The first is off-chain assets, which means physical assets such as fiat and stocks. In this case, the changes to accounts on the distributed ledger will have to match the off-chain settlement instructions. The second type of settlement of contract is on-chain, which has to do with digital assets. The accounts are automatically settled in the case of virtual assets like digital currency. To get the particular product you want, all you need to do is deposit the cryptocurrency required. The entire process is automated, so if a buyer or seller fails to fulfil their end of the bargain, the transactions will not be completed. The Ethereum smart contract will instantly cancel the transaction in the spirit of if you don’t comply.

### Objects of Smart Contracts

Essentially, each smart contract has three integral parts, also called objects. The first is those who are signatories of the smart contract.

The second object is the subject of the agreement. This can only be an object that exists in the environment of the smart contract. Alternatively, smart contracts should have unhindered and direct access to the object. Although smart contracts were first discussed in 1996, it was this particular object that stopped their development. This issue was[ only partially resolved after the first ](https://www.coinkolik.com/kripto-para-nedir/)cryptocurrency appeared in 2009.

Finally, any smart contract must include certain terms and conditions. These terms need to be mathematically fully defined and a programming language suitable for a specific smart contract environment should be used. This includes the terms expected of all participating parties and all rules, rewards and penalties for such terms.

Smart contracts need to work within a specific appropriate environment for them to exist and function properly. First of all, the environment needs to support the use of public key encryption, which allows them to log on to the process using unique, especially generated encryption codes. This is the exact system used by the absolute majority of existing cryptocurrencies.

Second, an open and decentralized database is required, which all parties to the contract can fully trust and are fully automated. Moreover, the entire environment needs to be separated from the centre for the smart contract to be implemented. Blockchain, especially Ethereum Blockchain, are excellent environments for smart contracts.

Finally, the source of the digital data used by the smart contract must be fully reliable. This requires the use of root SSL security certificates, HTTPS, and other secure connection protocols that are already widely used and automatically applied in most of today's software.

What are Ricardian Conventions? Second Generation Smart Contracts!

A Ricardian Convention is a legal contract first initiated in 1995 by Ian Grigg, a well-known programmer. The concept is now part of the blockchain. Here's the basic definition:

"It is a kind of digital document agreed between the two parties, containing the terms of the interaction between the parties."

What makes it unique is because it’s signed, verified, and encrypted. Even if it is a digital document, it is in easy text that is easy for people to read (not just for lawyers). It is a unique legal contract that can be read for computer programs and also for people.

Simply put, it has two parts or serves two purposes. First, it is an easy-to-read legal contract between two or more parties. Your lawyer can easily understand and even you can read and understand the basic terms of the contract.

The latter is also a contract that can be read by the machine. With blockchain platforms, these agreements can now be easily collected, signed, and saved on the blockchain.

Ian Grigg: The Man Behind Ricardian Contracts

### What is a DAPP?

A DAPP is nothing more than a special form of an app. These are based on a blockchain, are open source and decentralized and must be able to generate their own tokens. Such a token is, for example, the Bitcoin, the DAPP behind it, is the Bitcoin network. The big advantage is that no company controls all functions, but that the open-source community continues to improve the DAPP.

There are three different types of DAPPs. On the one hand, there are Type 1 DAPPs that operate their own blockchain, such as the Bitcoin network. Now there are other DAPPs that rely on the blockchain of this Bitcoin network. Such DAPPs are called Type2. The third type is again based on type 2 DAPPs. Microsoft Azure, for example, is a Type2 DAPP that is based on the Ethereum blockchain. The company offers an infrastructure that is used by Type 3 DAPPs, in this case, for example Absolut or Heineken.

So, you can see that even if the Type1 DAPPs are decentralized and based on a blockchain, companies with Type2 or Type3 DAPPs can benefit from this. This creates entire networks, which explains why the systems behind the cryptocurrencies are becoming more and more important.

Apps and DAPPs – The Differences

Apps and DAPPs may seem relatively similar to the newcomer at first glance. Nevertheless, there are glaring differences, which will be discussed in more detail here. Apps are mostly released by companies that have full control over all the features of the respective app. For example, as a user, you can have no influence on google changing its YouTube app so that adblockers no longer work.

DAPPs, on the other hand, are open source. This means that the source code is visible to everyone and can therefore be changed at will. If you want to redesign a DAPP according to your own wishes and bring the necessary know-how with you, you can change the source code and create your own DAPP variant.

Another difference is that DAPPs are always based on a blockchain and are stored in decentralised locations. Therefore, it is almost impossible to hack a DAPP, since one would have to attack the entire blockchain behind it, including all networked computers, at the same time in order to change the structure of the DAPP. An app, on the other hand, is usually supported by the respective company's own servers. Here you would "only" have to hack the one datacenter to bring the respective app under his control.

In summary, it can be said that DAPPs are both safer and freer in their development, as no company controls them centrally. Apps, on the other hand, are clearly owned by a company that has full control. Also, the source code is not publicly visible here, so you do not get to see the structure of the respective app without further ado.

How are DAPPs Defined?

The community has agreed on a clear definition of the DAPP. First, DAPPs are not issued and maintained by a single provider. A DAPP runs distributed and decentralized over a peer-to-peer network. In addition, four criteria must be met that an application can be called a DAPP. It first must be the source code of a DAPP open source. Furthermore, it must be based on a blockchain, have cryptographic encryption and be able to generate its own tokens. Only if all these criteria are met can we speak of a DAPP.

●Open Source

The fact that a DAPP is open source does not mean that every programmer can simply decide for himself what the DAPP should look like after its own changes in the future. Rather, the community decides which modifications are accepted and which are not. Nevertheless, the source code remains visible to everyone, so that every developer knows exactly what the current state of affairs is. The fact that a DAPP is open source ensures continuous development. No individual administrator or even a company sets the future direction, but the further development of the DAPP is controlled by the entire developer community together.

Of course, as a programmer you also have the possibility to easily redesign the respective source code according to your own needs and thus get a very personal DAPP. Of course, you should master the underlying programming language and understand the subtleties of the source code. Whether your own changes will actually be applied, however, will then be decided by the entirety of the developers.

●Blockchain

The second criterion that every DAPP must meet is that it is based on a blockchain. The blockchain makes the DAPP decentralized and is stored on many different computers at the same time. As a result, it becomes almost impossible that a DAPP can be deleted or that criminals can hack into the DAPP. Well-known blockchains are, for example, the Bitcoin network or the Ethereum network. Here, all data is stored on the users' PCs and not, as for example with apps, centrally on special servers. Such data includes the source code, all transactions of the cryptocurrencies and other information necessary for the smooth running of the DAPP. The blockchain is always the heart of a DAPP. Type1 DAPPs have their own blockchain, while Type2 and Type3 DAPPs are based on it. Each block is completed and encrypted after a certain period of time.

●Cryptographic Encryption

The cryptographic encryption of each block is used to obtain and verify all information in a certain period of time. If you look at the Bitcoin network, for example, enormous computing power is spent here to verify and store all transactions. So-called Bitcoin miners take over this computing power and in return receive rewards in the form of Bitcoin and also the transaction fees. If a block is completed, this is done by generating a hash. A hash is a kind of checksum that ensures that all transactions and information made in the previous block are authentic and traceable.

However, individual trades will be difficult to identify in retrospect, as it is very time-consuming to make the previous information visible again from a hash. The cryptographic encryption thus serves both to ensure the authenticity of all information, as well as the anonymization of the individual users. Therefore, your own data is much more secure if you use a DAPP than if you make it available to the providers of apps.

●Token generation

The last criterion that must be met by a DAPP is token generation. With the Bitcoin network, such a token is simply the Bitcoin. The miners who provide their computing power to create hashes must be rewarded so that they want to fulfil this task. This is done using a reward in Bitcoin once a hash is found. Investors who speculate with cryptocurrencies know how high the Bitcoin price is today. So, each token has an equivalent value in real currency.

The token generation only serves to make it worthwhile for the miners to provide their computing power. Hash generation is important for any blockchain, as it would no longer work without it. As a result, token generation ensures that it remains attractive for miners to keep the blockchain running. If all these four criteria are met, one can speak of a DAPP.

This often gives the impression that NFTs are a completely new phenomenon. But the history of blockchain-based NFTs can already boast several years. The first NFT experiments were already running in 2012 with coloured coins on the Bitcoin blockchain.

Ethereum is currently the largest smart contract blockchain. Even with NFTs, Ethereum has clearly been ahead since 2017 until today. Whether crypto punks or CryptoKitties, the first Jack Dorsey tweet or the Beeple collage, all of them as well as most marketplaces are part of the huge NFT world on Ethereum.

### Token Standards

All tokens on Ethereum, whether cryptocurrencies or NFTs, can be assigned to unique standards. Standards are an important building block for how tokens work. Only with suitable standards are the essential characteristics such as ownership, access and transferability guaranteed. Standards are also essential for software development. In this way, the basic functionalities and behaviours are clearly clear to all those involved.

The three most common Ethereum token standards are ERC20 for Fungible Tokens (exchangeable tokens such as cryptocurrencies) and ERC721, as well as ERC1155 for Non-Fungible Tokens (non-exchangeable tokens = NFTs).

The ERC20 standard assigns amounts to addresses. Thus, per Ethereum address, the exact number of cryptocurrencies held can always be recognized.

With the birth of CryptoKitties, the important NFT standard ERC721 was created, on which most NFTs are based to this day. ERC1155 is another NFT standard developed by Enjin. ERC1155 is a hybrid of exchangeable and non-interchangeable tokens. ERC721 maps unique IDs to owners, while ERC1155 has a nested mapping of IDs to owners to sets.

NFT Premiere on Ethereum: Crypto Punks

It was only with the Ethereum blockchain that NFTs really picked up speed. The first Ethereum NFTs appeared as Crypto Punks in the summer of 2017.

Crypto Punks are 10,000 unique digital characters created by [Larva Labs.](https://www.larvalabs.com/cryptopunks) Their digital proofs of ownership are stored in the form of unique tokens on the Ethereum blockchain. Each Crypto Punk is characterized by individual optical characteristics, so that no one is the same as the other. Crypto punks have inspired the digital art market and were the forerunner for later standards. Due to the limited offer and the cult status among early users, they are already considered digital antiques. They are still tradable and interoperable with most NFT applications on Ethereum.

2017: Birth of CryptoKitties

Even though Crypto Punks were officially the first NFTs on the Ethereum blockchain, the CryptoKitties are still in the memory of most people. At the end of 2017, they became the first mainstream application of NFTs.

2017 was a year with a lot of hype around cryptocurrencies, anyway, driven by the ICO bubble on the Ethereum platform. In a short time, countless new ICO projects started and each of them had new and supposedly scarce tokens on offer. This triggered a veritable gold digger mood. More and more new cryptocurrencies on the Ethereum blockchain saw the light of day in the digital world. It wasn't long before there was an increase in network congestion, with significantly increased transaction fees as a result. But one of the reasons for the high utilization was, among other things, the creation and trading with CryptoKitties. [CryptoKitties](https://www.cryptokitties.co/) are digital images of comic cats created by Dapper Labs for a blockchain computer game. Each cat is unique and thus only present once on the blockchain. The special thing about them is that in the on-chain computer game, new unique CryptoKitties can be bred by clubs of different cats. These new cats could then be auctioned off or sold on the open market.

The CryptoKitties Bubble

Breeding attracted many speculators. The idea was simple – you buy a few rare CryptoKitties, breed a few new, even rarer cats with them, sell them at high prices and then do the whole thing all over again. At its peak in 2017, the trading volume was around 5000 ETH and sales of CryptoKitties achieved prices of over $100,000.

It was almost absurd to pay such high prices for digital cats. But the incredible story behind it in combination with the great hype about cryptocurrencies brought many new buyers into the market in a short time. Ethereum's network capacity at the time could not cope with this onslaught, so that trading and breeding CryptoKitties incurred horrendous fees and hours of waiting.

This mixture of hype, speculation and viral story eventually resulted in a bubble that also received appropriate attention in the press. But as expected, the bubble burst soon after. In mid-December 2017, the demand for CryptoKitties suddenly declined and prices tumbled. The entire crypto bubble began to burst shortly thereafter, and a multi-year bear market was ushered in.

2018 – 2020: Developments during ongoing bear market

After this hype, interest and media coverage of NFTs flattened. But that didn't mean that exciting NFT concepts weren't developed afterwards. On the contrary, CryptoKitties had laid the foundation for new ideas.

In the years from 2018 to 2020, many new courses were set for blockchain-based NFTs, without which no new onslaught would probably have been possible in 2021. Numerous blockchain projects have taken advantage of the crypto winter to drive new interesting NFT applications.

### NFTs as Digital Art

This is how the art market discovered NFTs for itself. The property of a clear proof of ownership and the possibility to display this transparently are almost ideal for digital art. Some artists began experimenting with NFTs. Platforms for the trade in digital art such as [OpenSea](https://opensea.io/), [SuperRare](https://superrare.co/), [MakersPlace](https://makersplace.com/) or [Known Origin](https://knownorigin.io/) were founded and continuously expanded. So-called minting platforms such as [Digital Art](https://digitalartchain.com/), [Mintbase](https://mintbase.io/) and [Mintable](https://mintable.app/) integrated solutions to make it easy and fast to create NFTs for anyone without programming knowledge.

Gaming and virtual worlds

The gaming industry around blockchain-based virtual reality also recognized the great potential of NFTs at an early stage. First and foremost are the Decentraland and Cryptovoxels projects, which enable the purchase and ownership of virtual land in the form of NFTs. In these virtual game worlds, new digital ecosystems with virtual cities and landscapes are created.

Another pioneer in the field of blockchain-based virtual reality is [Enjin.](https://enjin.io/) This project allows gaming utensils in the form of NFTs to be easily transferred from one game to another.

Domain names

In addition to art and gaming, the domain name market has also adapted NFTs at an early stage to provide domains similar to ".com" with decentralized blockchain technology. The best-known service provider Ethereum Name Service was founded in May 2017 and connected to the most common NFT marketplaces in 2019. The number of registered “.eth" domains has grown steadily.

Another well-known service provider is Unstoppable Domains, which has issued the domain “. crypto", among other things.

Between 2018 and 2020, many important foundations were laid for future NFT mass applications. In the meantime, NFTs have penetrated into numerous other sectors of the economy. These include sports, film, music, leisure, finance and collectibles. These new NFT ecosystems will be discussed again later.

### Disadvantages of Ethereum for NFTs

Even though Ethereum is the largest NFT platform, it is not always the first choice for every type of NFT. Ethereum is still struggling with the challenge of increasing capacity limits. For this reason, the creation and trading of NFTs sometimes incur extremely high fees. With a very valuable NFT like the Beeple artwork, $100 in fees may be insignificant. But for many smaller artists who want to sell their works for $100 to $200, such large amounts are a clear obstacle.

Against this background, several alternative blockchains have stepped up to cleverly close this market gap. Depending on the NFT use case, there is also a suitable NFT blockchain that promises faster and more cost-effective trading of NFTs.

The problems with current blockchains

Three problems occur again and again with known blockchains:

●Cost to users

●Duration of transactions

●User experience for beginners & developers

And to solve these problems, the blockchains need to bet on better scalability. This is done either at the expense of the developers, as programming is much more difficult. Or by abandoning decentralization, as for example with Libra or EOS. Both solutions are suboptimal.

 

## 6.2. [FLOW](https://www.onflow.org/): The Blockchain Built for NFTs

When CryptoKitties, brought the Ethereum blockchain to a standstill in late 2017 due to an immense overload. Developer Dapper Labs learned first-hand that blockchains of the current generation were not built for this demand.

Instead of simply finding another home for the decentralized app or wait until the Ethereum scaling solutions are mature. Dapper has decided to create a blockchain that the company and other developers can rely on.

The result is Flow, a block chain specifically designed to support things like NFT collectibles and large-scale crypto games. Flow is a blockchain designed for comprehensive scaling without using sharding techniques that enable fast and cost-effective transactions that make sense for dApps like NFT marketplaces and crypto-infused video games.

As mentioned earlier, Flow comes from Dapper Labs, who decided to solve the problem of blockchain congestion directly by developing one provided for games and other interactive experiences. Dapper now uses Flow for all of its own projects, including NBA Top Shot, but is also open to other developers.

### How does Flow work?

Flow uses a pledge verification model where validators must deploy a certain number of FLOW tokens to participate in the network.

However, the way validation works is unique among blockchains in that Flow splits validation tasks into four separate node types: consensus, verification, execution, and capture. All four node types participate in the validation of each transaction.

According to Dapper, splitting tasks makes processing transactions more efficient than competing blockchains. This is an alternative option for sharding or distributing a blockchain's storage and compute requirements across numerous nodes. Flow does not use sharding. In this way, according to Dapper, Flow keeps transactions atomic, consistent, isolated and permanent (ACID) and allows developers to build on each other's work.

Flow is also updatable smart contracts that allow smart contracts to be deployed in beta and then expanded or repaired before they are completed and made immutable.

Dapper Labs convinces with a diverse network. Well-known investors joined early and co-financed the development of the Flow Blockchain. These include the Warner Music Group and star investor Andreessen Horowitz. At the same time, they are already working intensively with the American basketball league NBA. The UFC or the game developer Ubisoft are also among the partners, who also include Coinbase and Samsung.

#### High scalability

Among experts, the programming language is considered to be particularly developer friendly. As a result, more and more developers are turning to the Flow Blockchain and working on optimization. At the same time, the commands in the network are broken down into individual tasks, which can then be processed in parallel. This increases the efficiency of the computing capacity; the network becomes more scalable.

Users of the Dapper Wallet can use both Ethereum and Flow and get access to both networks. Dapper's current users will be the first to gain access to the now released Flow network and be able to test applications and use the latest applications there.

#### SpoCKs technique instead of consensus

Flow uses the new cryptographic approach Specialized Proof of Confidential Knowledge (SpoCKs). This technique was developed by the Flow team and published in a special technical documentation. With SpoCK, any number of developers of a third observation instance can prove that they each have access to the same confidential knowledge. There is no information about confidential content in the evidence and they act interactively.

Each SpoCKs is individually and specifically tailored to the developer and cannot be copied or forged by any other developer on the network. Basically, the technique asks the execution and verification nodes to prove their knowledge. This knowledge can only be obtained through the execution of all transactions assigned to them. And only when this has been clarified, they receive the reward.

#### Smart Contracts with ACID Guarantee

A single common status for all smart contracts ensures that each transaction complies with the following guarantees:

1.  Atomicity = atomicity stands for seclusion in transactions

1.  Consistency = Consistency means that a transaction leaves a persistent database state

1.  Isolation = isolation prevents or restricts transactions from affecting each other during execution

1.  Durability = durability guarantees the permanent storage of transactions in the database

In German also referred to as ACID properties, these four criteria are considered prerequisites for the reliability of systems.

This makes it possible for each blockchain developer to build on each other's code and therefore develop completely new products and services at a rapid pace. Technically, this is composability.

The composability approach is similar to an open-source community where developers can quickly pick up on innovations because they can ultimately access a wider range of possibilities. A platform takes up the aspect of composability (composability/compatibility) when the existing resources are used as building blocks and can be programmed in higher-order applications.

Compared to the competition, the Flow Blockchain is hardly decentralized. 20% of the tokens belong to Dapper Labs, while the development team comes up with almost the same number of tokens. In addition, there is a nearly thirty percent reserve for the further expansion of the flow ecosystem. Dapper Labs promises the long-term HODLing of tokens. However, if these are sold, this is likely to depress the price. However, due to the holding promise, the Flow Token could be less volatile than the crypto competition in the long term.

### Is Flow the better alternative to Ethereum?

Flow’s developers want to make the blockchain accessible to the mainstream. You shouldn't have to have in-depth expertise to use the innovative blockchain technology. At the same time, Dapper Labs and its partners are an experienced contact person who can improve the blockchain in a targeted manner. At the same time, this ensures easy access for business partners from the mainstream. Finally, they can contact the responsible start-up directly to sound out a cooperation.

Currently, Flow is a good deal ahead of its biggest competitor Ethereum in terms of scalability. However, the question arises whether Ethereum will get a grip on the current problems with the upgrade to version 2.0 and the introduction of Proof of Stake. If so, it will be harder for Flow to catch up with the top dog. However, if there are still problems with Ethereum, Flow is a serious alternative in the competition for the blockchain for the mainstream.

## 6.3. NFTs and Binance Smart Chain

You've probably heard of Binance Chain, the home of the BNB currency. Binance Chain is optimized for ultrafast trading. To achieve this, he has had to make concessions on some issues – one of which is that he is not as flexible in terms of programmability as other blockchains.

Binance Smart Chain, a new blockchain with a fully equipped environment for developing high-performance decentralized applications, aims to change that. Built for cross-chain compatibility with Binance Chain so users can benefit from the best features of both worlds.

Binance Chain was launched by Binance in April 2019. Its main goal is to support fast and decentralized (or non-escrow-based) trading. Unsurprisingly, the largest decentralized application on Binance Chain (DApp) is Binance DEX, one of the most user-friendly decentralized exchanges. Binance Chain can be used through the web interface at binance.org or through its local integration with Trust Wallet.

However, due to the internal limitations of blockchain systems, the chain does not have much flexibility – smart contracts in a system optimized for fast trading – can significantly clog the network. Do you remember CryptoKitties? This application has made the Ethereum blockchain stop at the height of its popularity.

Scalability remains one of the most difficult obstacles to overcome in blockchain development. And that's where the Binance Smart Chain comes in.

What is Binance Smart Chain?

Binance Smart Chain (BSC) can be best defined as a blockchain that runs parallel to Binance Chain. Unlike Binance Chain, BSC has intelligent contract functions and compatibility with the Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM). The purpose of the design here is to incorporate smart contracts into the ecosystem while maintaining the high efficiency of Binance chain as it is.

Most fundamentally, these two blockchains work side by side. It is also important to note that BSC is not a layer two or non-chain scalability solution. BSC is an independent blockchain that can continue to work even if Binance Chain remains offline. However, both chains are quite similar in design.

BSC is available to support the rich world of Ethereum tools and DApps because it is EVM compatible. In theory, this feature makes it easy for developers to move their projects from Ethereum to here. For users, this means that applications such as MetaMask can be configured to work with BSC. Indeed, to do this, it is enough to change several settings. To learn what to do, you can read the MetaMask Usage guide for Binance Smart Chain.

How does Binance Smart Chain work?

Consensus

Binance Smart Chain reaches block times of approximately 3 seconds with a Proof of Stake reconciliation algorithm. The algorithm used is Proof of Staked Authority (PoSA), where participants stake their BNB to be verifiers. If users make a valid block suggestion, they will charge transaction fees for transactions within that block.

Since BNB is not inflationary, it should be taken into account that, unlike many protocols, there is no block allowance of newly issued BNBs. Instead, the BNB supply decreases over time as the Binance team performs coin burns at regular intervals.

Cross-chain compatibility

Binance Smart Chain is designed as a complementary system to the independent but existing Binance Chain. Dual chain architecture was used to allow users to seamlessly transfer assets from one blockchain to another. In this way, both trading can be done quickly in Binance Chain and strong decentralized applications can be built on BSC. With this interoperability, users have access to a broad ecosystem that can serve many uses.

## 6.4. NFTs and TRON Blockchain

TRON Blockchain introduced a new TRC-721 standard for non-fungible tokens (NFT). This will empower developers.

The TRC-721 is a set of standard interfaces to be used to publish NFT on the TRON network and is fully compatible with the basic standard ERC-721 used in the Ethereum blockchain to create NFT.

According to the description, NFT's can be used on the TRON blockchain with the help of the TronLink plugin for Chrome. Users also have to have a minimum balance of TRX 350 (about $18.3 at current prices) in their account to publish NFT's. On the other hand, it can customize the name and symbols of tokens during trading.

TRON's NFT standard aims to provide new capabilities for applications built on blockchain. In the long run, it is also projected to increase the pressure on Ethereum from rival NFT platforms such as Flow. NFT standards are often used in various areas where blockchain is applied. However, most of the NFT is based on Ethereum (ERC-721 standard and its improvements).

The number of decentralized financial projects (DeFi) interested in integration with NFT to improve their ecosystems is growing steadily. Therefore, the Tron developers believe that the TRC-721 standard will open up many possibilities for developers who are building new blockchain-based applications.

Each TRC-721-based token has a unique ID that can be used to move real elements digitally without compromising their value. Tokens created based on the TRC-721 standard represent all digital types of tangible assets in the real world. Individuals can own and manage data associated with a specific material object or document. This protects industries that are vulnerable to fraud, plagiarism or counterfeiting.

The developers can apply TRC-721 more effectively to their products and applications thanks to Tron's efficient infrastructure. The TRC-721 standard will find various applications in the future that will strengthen the crypto-economy and make blockchain technology more unique.

TRON claims to combine low transaction fees with instant payment convenience. According to the Tron Foundation, the protocol has been operating "without any major losses" for almost three years, despite being the target of a malicious attack in November 2020 that resulted in a temporary halt to production of new blocks.

Breaking The NFT Myths

 

## Who creates NFT and where to buy it?

On various online marketplaces such as [OpenSea](https://opensea.io/) or [Rarible](https://rarible.com/) you can buy and sell NFT, they are stored in a digital wallet - similar to what is known from cryptocurrencies. With the right feeling you can make real money with it: The initially free CryptoPunks, actually only pixelated portraits to collect, currently go for the equivalent of around 50,000, - € over the virtual counter. You don't have to sell NFT as unique pieces, for each work you can create any number of tokens - with a corresponding effect on the market value, of course.

Anyone can create and sell NFT themselves. Provided you have a corresponding digital work and find a customer. On a trading platform, you upload files such as MP3, GIF or PNG, pay a small fee (in the form of a cryptocurrency) and can then store your NFT in the wallet. Because the tokens are then part of the blockchain, you don't have to sell them on the same platform where you created them. There is no obligation to sell at all, anyone can become a collector of their own works of art with NFT.

## How are NFT's produced?

NFT's are manufactured according to the ERC-721 token standard prepared by Ethereum developers. There are platforms that make it possible to perform the transaction easily so that users do not choke on the technical world of blockchain. All you need to produce NFT is a digital cryptocurrency wallet. After uploading the file to be produced as NFT to the IPFS system through these platforms, the parameters of the NFT that cannot be changed are determined. After determining how many NFTs will be produced, its price, how much share will have to be paid on its next sale, NFT is produced by paying the transaction fee of the blockchain network. Upon completion of the transaction, NFT's are transferred to the cryptocurrency wallet and recorded on the entire blockchain where the production is carried out.

## Is NFT an investment tool?

NFT's are different from other cryptocurrencies by design and their value is determined by the person or investor who produces the work rather than the checkboard. If the subject of investment is interpreted through digital art, it is not so different from investing in physical art. Similarly, parameters such as how rare that asset is, age and cosmetics determine the value of an asset purchased as a collection piece.

## Are NFTs different from cryptocurrencies?

"Non-Fungible Token" means digital asset (token) that cannot be divided and can never be produced again. Unlike cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin and Ether, which are "fungibles", NFT's are unique digital assets. To illustrate with an example, today you can buy and buy Bitcoin or Ether by breaking it into pieces and exchange the same cryptocurrency in its own unit. However, NFT, a private digital entity, cannot be traded with another token because it is unique. Its internal value is determined by its manufacturer or investor.

## What connects NFT to Ethereum?

Most NFT are currently stored as part of the Ethereum blockchain. Ethereum, like Bitcoin, is one of the cryptocurrencies and offers space in its blockchain for additional information on which NFT depends. Theoretically, however, other blockchains can also be used for NFT. At the cryptocurrency Tron, corresponding tokens with the TRC-721 standard were already presented in December last year – probably also in response to the boom of ERC-721, the Ethereum protocol for NFT.

## And what does NFT have to do with art?

Some in the art industry are already celebrating NFT as a game-changer, in fact the associated digitization of the art market opens a few rusty doors. On the one hand, more and more works of art are being created in virtual space, be they memes, graphics or objects in computer games. NFT allows a clear determination of ownership claims and enables new models of monetization. At the same time, the art market has a general demand problem and the hype is just right: When Beeple's 69-million-dollar work was sold, for example, almost exclusively new customers were in the auction house.

NFT also opens up new investment opportunities in art objects. Owners can sell percentage shares in works via NFT without having to give up their own ownership. Museums could also develop new forms of financing: shares in works by great artists could be sold proportionately with NFT, while the affected object remains in place. Owners can also use NFT to set royalties for their digital works and then receive a share of the sale price each time they resell.

## What impact will NFTs have?

Considering the current uses of NFT, it can be said that it solves intellectual property problems first. While it is very easy to copy a work digitally; The NFT solves this problem by revealing who owns the rights to the work. Because they are a programmable digital asset, artists' share of the next sale can also be encoded inside the NFT. Thus, in the event of a sale, the relevant amount is automatically transferred to the artist's wallet. The digital nature of the NFTs allow users of all types to buy them from all over the world. As the customer base expands thanks to digitalization, the number of people who can reach the work and want to invest, and therefore the demand increases, as demand increases, the price rises. As demand increases, so does supply. More NFTs at different price levels are coming to market. This increases accessibility for investors and art lovers and expands the ecosystem by enabling more works to be sold.

## Can you copy the digital object that makes NFTs?

Sure, that's possible. Digital goods such as images, videos or memes can be easily copied by anyone. Even if the owner, copyright or the uniqueness of such files are documented and proven by NFT, this can be done with a simple right click. However, NFT gives the owner something that cannot be copied: the ownership and all associated claims to the digital work. As a practical example: Anyone can order an art print of the Mona Lisa or put it on a copier at home, but the original still hangs only once in the Louvre in Paris.

## How can I be sure of the authenticity of NFT?

In the cryptocurrency ecosystem, people's reputation is highly valued. Artists who produce NFT and want to put it up for sale go through a stream of verified profiles used by NFT marketplace apps to prove that their work is original. If you have the information of the wallet address of the artist concerned, which is shared publicly for the purpose of receiving payment, you have the chance to confirm the originality of the work. In addition, by tracking the movements of the NFT from the blockchain monitoring application, the authenticity of both the artist and his works can be ensured.

## Are NFTs theft-proof?

Yes and no. With NFT, simple "piracy", just like with common cryptocurrencies, is virtually impossible. This is ensured by the Ethereum blockchain. Information about buyers and sellers or the date of purchase are stored transparently for each transaction in the blockchain, comparable to the documentation in the account management. Owners, uniqueness and authenticity are thus virtually set in stone. In order to fraudulently manipulate part of the blockchain, an evildoer would have to hold at least 51% of the computing power in the mining network. In the industry, this scenario is considered quite a super-GAU, but the practical risk for it is minimal.

However, NFT can be stolen in the same way as Bitcoin or Ethereum: by a potential thief gaining access to the wallet in which the tokens are stored. So, copying and pasting is not enough, you need criminal energy.
