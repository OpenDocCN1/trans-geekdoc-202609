
# Bitcoin and Decentralized Finance

Bitcoin is considered to be the first cryptocurrency and was able to convince by the concept of decentralization, among other things. The most important feature is that not a single instance can determine the direction of the project. On the contrary, everyone can contribute to the success of the project and the Community will vote on proposals. This reduces the risk of damage by an individual (Single Point of Failure).

Decentralized finances also follow this principle, creating new opportunities. To manage a project collaboratively, all actions must be communicated transparently. This requires clear and unambiguous conditions on which participants can vote. The requirements are defined in a smart contract, whereby a retroactive change can only take place by arrangement.

Participants can usually vote in the form of utility tokens. This creates a demand for these tokens and the price is subject to the laws of the market. So the more successful and innovative a project is, the higher the demand for their tokens and the higher the price can rise.

## Central Finance (CeFi)

Central finances describe the previous financial business, whereby a central authority must always serve as an intermediary of trust. This can be a bank or another institution that demands a commission of the transaction in return. As a result, customers have a direct contact person who is legally obliged to keep their promise. Thus, in case of doubt, they must also be liable for their actions.

Gradually, established financial institutions have also recognized the potential of blockchain technology and are offering their own products. The development of these services is carried out exclusively by the company and its employees. Unlike Decentralized Finance, CeFi is not an opportunity for investors to participate in the project.

However, CeFi applications also have significant potential and offer many benefits. Laws and regulations also play a major role in this, which ensure greater security. An example of a successful provider of CeFi products is London-based Nexo. Customers can make instant loans based on cryptocurrencies and thus earn a passive income. All deposits are insured and the risk to investors is low as a result.

## What it Takes to Consider an App as "DeFi"

DeFi does not represent any cryptocurrency or value. DeFi represents a trend such as "Open Banking" or "Open Source Code". Projects or platforms based on central blockchains can market themselves as DeFi applications. They can access financial instruments offered on DeFi, such as earning interest or borrowing. However, the fact that they offer these services certainly does not guarantee that they belong to the DeFi area or that they are safe. Central Finance = CeFi (Celsius, Nexo, BlockFi).

In order for an application to be considered "DeFi", you should ideally be able to answer "yes" to most or all of the following questions:

1.  Does the app offer a financial service, as you can see in traditional markets? (Clearing, interest, debt, funds, etc.)
2.  Do I need a custody-free wallet to connect to the app? (MetaMask, Argent, Trust, etc.)
3.  Do I have 100% control of my funds using this wallet? (Personal Finance)
4.  Do I avoid contact with any intermediary person/institution by trading only through smart contracts when connecting to the application?
5.  Can I use the application without having to perform KYC (Know Your Customer) procedures?
6.  Is there no regional limit for use?
7.  Is there no minimum to participate?

## The Difference Between dApps and Centralized Applications

"Smart contracts" are programs that run on the blockchain and can be executed automatically when certain conditions are met. In the world of decentralized Finance, these smart contracts enable developers to develop functionality that is far more advanced than simply sending and purchasing cryptocurrency. These programs are now decentralized applications, that is, programs that we call dApps.

The biggest differences of dApps from centralized apps are:

1.  Always have your funds under your control and ask you for permission through a custody-free wallet to invest in any application.
2.  The fact that the application (platform/protocol) is working on a completely decentralized blockchain, that is, it is not controlled by any centralized organization. In this example, the administrative tasks that we call managers in traditional finance are transferred to smart contracts, that is, to code.
3.  All transactions are finalized, that is, they cannot be changed by anyone, and can be transparently followed by anyone.

Decentralized Apps (dApps), that is, applications in the world of DeFi, allow us users to invest in financial instruments (earning interest, lending, insurance) that are completely under our control and without the need for any brokerages.

This ecosystem has been developed because the financial system based on banks, which we are accustomed to, is open to human errors and corruption. Financial democracy and freedom are the most popular uses of DeFi, which provides the financial world with advantages such as eliminating intermediaries and central system risks, as well as greatly reducing costs.

### How Do Smart Contracts Work?

Simply put, smart contracts work like vending machines. In smart contracts, you can think of a required amount of cryptocurrency, escrow, right to host, ID, or account as the amount you have. All rules and penalties are not only predefined by smart contracts, but also enforced by them.

A smart contract can work on its own, but it can also work with other smart contracts. They can be set up in a way that they depend on each other. For example, the successful completion of a particular smart contract can trigger the start of another, and so on. Theoretically, all systems and organizations can resort to completely intelligent contracts. To some extent, this is already applied in various cryptocurrency systems where all laws are predefined and therefore the network can function spontaneously and independently.

It starts with predefining the contract: At this point, all the terms of engagement are established by all stakeholders. This could be the specific currency that will be used for making payments, the variable interest rate, or the currency rate. Also, the terms for execution are set while predefining the contract, and this could be the date, time, or several other criteria.

- **Events**: This is the point where events (such as the information received and the kick-off of the transaction) instantly trigger the implementation of the contract.
- **Execution and delivery**: What determines the movement of value are the terms of the contract, and this is based on the fulfilment of the set conditions.
- **Settlement of contract**: This is the final stage of the process, and it can take place in two ways. The first is off-chain assets, which means physical assets such as fiat and stocks. In this case, the changes to accounts on the distributed ledger will have to match the off-chain settlement instructions. The second type of settlement of contract is on-chain, which has to do with digital assets. The accounts are automatically settled in the case of virtual assets like digital currency. To get the particular product you want, all you need to do is deposit the cryptocurrency required. The entire process is automated, so if a buyer or seller fails to fulfil their end of the bargain, the transactions will not be completed. The Ethereum smart contract will instantly cancel the transaction in the spirit of "if you don't comply."
