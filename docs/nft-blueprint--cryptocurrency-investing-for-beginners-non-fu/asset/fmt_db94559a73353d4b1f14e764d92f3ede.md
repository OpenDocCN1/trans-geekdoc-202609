
# Spells of Genesis: Pioneers of Blockchain Gaming and ICOs

Spells of Genesis game creators were not only pioneers but also among the first to launch an ICO to release in-game assets to a blockchain through Counterparty. So early, in fact, ICOs were later called crowdfunding. Spells of Genesis financed the development by launching a token called `BitCrystals`, which is used as an in-game currency.

## August 2016 – More Trading Cards on the Other Side

In August 2016, Counterparty teamed up with the popular trading card game Force of Will to launch its cards on the Counterparty platform. Force of Will was the 4th card game by sales in North America; only behind Pokemon, Yu-Gi-Oh and Magic. Their entry into the ecosystem pointed to the value of putting such assets in a blockchain.

## October 2016 – Rare Pepe on the Other Side

In October 2016, people began to issue "rare pepes" as assets on the Counterparty platform. Rare Pepe is a type of meme featuring a frog character. These have a huge fan base. There is even a directory called the Rare Pepe Wallet (often referred to as an index).

As if being on a Bitcoin blockchain wasn't enough, the Rare Pepe Wallet has "experts" who certify that pepe memes are rare. Oddity aside, this example shows the point at which people want unique digital elements. Today, Counterparty has a large number of projects built on its platform, many of which include NFT-like assets. Check out the various projects on Counterparty here.

## March 2017 – Rare Pepes on Ethereum

With Ethereum coming to the fore in early 2017, memes began trading there. In March 2017, it was announced that a project called Peperium was "a decentralized meme market and trading card game (TCG)" that allows everyone to create memes that live forever in IPFS and Ethereum. Similar to Counterparty, Peperium also had a token associated with it (ticker symbol `RARE`), which is used to pay meme creation and listing fees.

## June 2017 – CryptoPunks

It wasn't until the Ethereum Blockchain that NFTs really picked up speed. The first Ethereum NFTs appeared as CryptoPunks in summer 2017. CryptoPunks are 10,000 unique digital characters created by [Larva Labs](https://www.larvalabs.com/cryptopunks). Ownership records are stored in the form of unique tokens on the Ethereum blockchain. Each Crypto Punk has individual attributes, so that no two are the same. CryptoPunks inspired the digital art market and were the forerunners for later standards. Due to the limited supply and the cult status of early users, they are already considered digital antiques. They are still tradable and interoperable with most NFT applications on Ethereum.

As the trade of rare pepes on Ethereum increased rapidly, two "creative technologists" decided to create their own NFT project with a slight twist. John Watkinson and Matt Hall realized that they could create unique characters generated on the Ethereum blockchain. Characters would be limited to 10,000 and no two characters would be the same. In reference to the Cypherpunks who experimented with bitcoin's precursors in the 1990s, they named their project CryptoPunks.

Surprisingly, Watkinson and Hall chose to allow anyone with an Ethereum wallet to claim a CryptoPunk for free. All 10,000 CryptoPunks were quickly claimed, launching a thriving secondary market where people bought and sold them. Interestingly enough, CryptoPunks does not follow the ERC721 standard as it had not yet been invented, but due to its implementation they were not entirely ERC20 either. Therefore, CryptoPunks can best be described as an ERC721 and ERC20 hybrid.

## October 2017 – Birth of CryptoKitties

With CryptoKitties, NFTs reached the mainstream. CryptoKitties is a blockchain-based virtual game that allows players to adopt, breed, and trade virtual cats. Cats on the blockchain!

Although CryptoPunks were officially the first NFTs on the Ethereum blockchain, CryptoKitties are often credited with bringing NFTs to mainstream attention. At the end of 2017, they became the first mainstream application of NFTs.

2017 was already a year of hype around cryptocurrencies, driven by the ICO bubble on the Ethereum platform. In a short time, countless new ICO projects started and each of them had new and supposedly scarce tokens on offer. This triggered a veritable gold-rush mood. More and more new tokens on the Ethereum blockchain saw the light of the digital world. It wasn't long before the network became increasingly congested, with significantly increased transaction fees as a result. However, one of the reasons for the high utilization was, among other things, the creation and trading of CryptoKitties.

CryptoKitties are digital images of cartoon cats created by Dapper Labs for a blockchain computer game. Each cat is unique and therefore only present once on the blockchain. The special thing about them is that in the on-chain computer game, new unique CryptoKitties can be bred by pairing different cats. These new cats could then be auctioned or sold on the open market.

### The CryptoKitties Bubble

Breeding attracted many speculators. The idea was simple: you buy some rare CryptoKitties, breed some new, even rarer cats with them, sell them at high prices, and then repeat the process. At peak times in 2017, the trading volume was approximately 5,000 ETH and sales of CryptoKitties achieved prices of more than 100,000 US dollars.

It was almost absurd to pay such high prices for digital cats. But the incredible story behind it, combined with the great hype around cryptocurrencies, brought many new buyers into the market in a short time. Ethereum's network capacity at the time was not up to the rush, resulting in horrendous fees and hours of waiting for the trading and breeding of CryptoKitties.

This mixture of hype, speculation, and viral story eventually ended up in a bubble that also attracted attention in the press. But, as might be expected, the bubble burst soon after. In mid-December 2017, demand for CryptoKitties fell at once and prices plummeted. The entire crypto bubble began to burst shortly after, and a multi-year bear market ensued.

CryptoKitties are great examples of NFT collectibles. CryptoKitties are digital cats on the Ethereum blockchain. The idea behind this NFT is to enable buyers to nurture these kitties and reproduce, just as they would in real life. Within a few weeks of launching in 2017, about $20 million was spent on them. But the collectibles market is not limited in any way, as it extends in different directions. Another excellent example of the usefulness of NFTs involves art collecting. Experts can verify and authenticate paintings and sculptures before creating a non-fungible token for each piece of art.

This incredible project was apparently featured on every news station from CoinDesk to CNN. Perhaps this was because the game clogged and slowed down the Ethereum blockchain, or people made crazy profits in trading. Some virtual cats even sold for over $100,000.

## 2018–2019 – NFT Cambrian Explosion

2018 and 2019 saw tremendous growth in the NFT ecosystem. There are now more than 100 projects within the space and more in the works. The NFT markets, led by OpenSea and SuperRare, are growing. Trading volumes are small compared to other crypto markets, but they are growing rapidly and have come a long way. As Web3 wallets like MetaMask continue to evolve, it becomes easier to get involved in the NFT ecosystem. Dapper Labs recently launched a Dapper wallet that doesn't require gas payments (via meta-transactions). In addition, there are now websites such as nonfungible.com and nftcryptonews.com that track NFT market metrics, game guides, and provide general information about the area.
