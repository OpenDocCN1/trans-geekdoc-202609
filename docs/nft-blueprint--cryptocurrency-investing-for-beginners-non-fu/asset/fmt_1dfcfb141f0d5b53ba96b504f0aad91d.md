
# NFTs: Collectibles, Gaming, Tickets, Identity, and the Future of Art

A good example of collectibles on the blockchain via non-fungible tokens has already been mentioned with the CryptoKitties. Of course, the market for collectibles is much larger and does not only include funny cats, which nevertheless serve as a prime example for this sector.

An NFT can be useful for all collectibles. Especially in cases where it is about authenticity, not only about the ownership, in valuable paintings or sculptures. These could be verified and authenticated by experts before creating an NFT whose possession simultaneously reflects the ownership and authenticity of a collectible. Thus, two central problems of the art world, namely the falsifying of valuable works and the fraudulent trade in stolen pieces, can be curbed.

## Online Games

In many online games, the characters can collect in-game currencies, often in the form of skins (clothes, weapons) and exchange them with other players. If you were to make these in-game currencies into an NFT, players could also exchange and trade them in the real world.

In this way, completely new economies for fictional goods can emerge. An example of this is the Decentraland project, which interactively creates a world of its own from which players can buy a piece of land in the form of an NFT. However, we are still at a very early stage here. One can assume that online games in particular will devote more and more time to non-fungible tokens and their integration into game concepts in the coming years.

## Tickets

Tickets are another area that will probably be the first to be really influenced by NFTs. The trade in tickets, both online and offline, has flourished, especially since the introduction of the Internet and corresponding trading platforms. One problem is often fake tickets that are used to defraud buyers. With the involvement of NFTs in this sector, these problems could be avoided.

## Identity and Certification

It gets really interesting when we shift the focus from real goods to more abstract things, such as the control of identities with the help of NFTs. Whether birth certificate, passport or driver's license – as an NFT you could communicate more efficiently with authorities that need personal data. Of course, this is only about ensuring the correct use of personal data, not about the trade in driving licences, which is, of course, not possible.

## Sports

Traditionally, the sports industry has been leading when it comes to collectable items – especially trading cards. And when it comes to NFTs, the industry hasn't been left behind either. The top two NFT projects in sports are NBA Top Shot and Sorare.

### What are NFTs for sports fans?

Apart from digital works of art, there have long been NFTs for sports fans. The French company Sorare, for example, has transferred the principle of trading cards to the Internet. If you register on the platform, you can buy the cards there, of which there are only a limited number. With your cards you can then participate in leagues and win money or other trading cards there, which can later be used or sold again.

Of course, many fans are still wondering: What are NFTs and is this new trend really gaining ground? The figures show that so far it is indeed a niche market. At Sorare, at the end of March, only just under 14,000 users worldwide had decided to buy at least one card with their own money. Within a month, however, this number had more than doubled. The growth rates of the company, which has only been usable at all since the beginning of 2019, are enormous. In addition, the huge number of football fans worldwide means that there is huge potential.

This is also underlined by the revenues that the NBA already generates with NFTs. Although these are still quite small compared to the revenues from TV or advertising contracts, growth is currently rapid here as well.

And yet another aspect suggests that NFTs in the sports field are not a typical bubble. The way fans want to see sports is changing. Especially with younger fans, entire games are no longer as in demand as the summary of the highlights. Statistics are also playing an increasingly important role – a trend that can be seen in virtually all popular television sports. It is therefore important for the managers to find new ways to interact with the fans.

## Why NFTs are the Future of the Art World?

There's a real excitement about NFTs. "The ground-breaking events of recent weeks are finally evidence of advances in digital art," says experts. Crypto art is the artistic expression of what is possible today. "A natural development for artists interested in new technologies thematically or as a tool." The aesthetics of technology are its own – reminiscent of avatars and games.

Artists who allowed the digital world to flow into their work have been part of contemporary art since the late 1960s. It all started with the drawings that the first computers created – they were still huge machines back then. Pioneers of computer art were artists such as Manfred Mohr, Frieder Nake and Vera Molnár. The first computer animations were created immediately afterwards.

Digital art consists of mixed work, such as interactive installations that include a software or audio component. If videos are digitally generated, video setups are part of it. Video art with cameras is a separate section. A parallel universe has opened up. There are a lot of amazing artists in this area. One name to remember is Yucef Merhi. One of his works, The Poetic Clock, is a machine that turns time into lyrical poetry and produces 86,400 different poems every day.

### NFTs and Art

"EVERYDAYS: THE FIRST 5000 DAYS" is the name of the work of the digital artist Beeple, which was able to achieve a proud 69 million US dollars at an auction of the prestigious auction house Christie's in London. The special feature of the auction is the fact that the image does not exist physically but is only available digitally. From May 1, 2007, the creator created a digital image every day with reference to his or all of our lives – for 5,000 days. The compilation of all these pictures results in the now auctioned work of art.

But not only the auction of a purely digital product is something special for Christie's. Founded in 1766, the historic auction house also dared to use the crypto parquet with the Beeple auction. With a resolution of 21,069 x 21,069 pixels and around 319 megabytes, the masterpiece in Jpeg format was linked by the artist with a so-called Non-Fungible Token, NFT for short. With this token, the new owner can prove his ownership rights to the digital creation with the blockchain. As the new owner, he is now known on the Ethereum network.

The masterpiece "EVERYDAYS: THE FIRST 5000 DAYS" by Beeple is a picture from the "EVERYDAYS" series. The artist has already been able to sell 21 works of art from this series via Nifty Gateway, the NFT art trading platform of the Winklevoss brothers. He generated proceeds of $3.5 million.

The work of art was created in more than 13 years, the artist had taken a digital picture every day. His Instagram account is followed by around 2 million accounts, which confirms that the public's interest in digital art is very high.

The 5,000 individual images made the artist stone-rich, and he apparently got a taste for selling digital art with NFTs. This was followed by "The Complete MF Collection", which consisted of 20 works and was sold for 3.5 million US dollars.
