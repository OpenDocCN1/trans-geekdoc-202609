
The second object is the subject of the agreement. This can only be an object that exists in the environment of the smart contract. Alternatively, smart contracts should have unhindered and direct access to the object. Although smart contracts were first discussed in 1996, it was this particular object that stopped their development. This issue was only partially resolved after the first [cryptocurrency](https://www.coinkolik.com/kripto-para-nedir/) appeared in 2009.

Finally, any smart contract must include certain terms and conditions. These terms need to be mathematically fully defined and a programming language suitable for a specific smart contract environment should be used. This includes the terms expected of all participating parties and all rules, rewards and penalties for such terms.

Smart contracts need to work within a specific appropriate environment for them to exist and function properly. First of all, the environment needs to support the use of public key encryption, which allows them to log on to the process using unique, especially generated encryption codes. This is the exact system used by the absolute majority of existing cryptocurrencies.

Second, an open and decentralized database is required, which all parties to the contract can fully trust and are fully automated. Moreover, the entire environment needs to be separated from the centre for the smart contract to be implemented. Blockchain, especially Ethereum Blockchain, are excellent environments for smart contracts.

Finally, the source of the digital data used by the smart contract must be fully reliable. This requires the use of root SSL security certificates, HTTPS, and other secure connection protocols that are already widely used and automatically applied in most of today's software.

## Advantages of Smart Contracts

Smart contracts provide:

- **Autonomy** – You are the one who makes the contract; it is not necessary to rely on an intermediary, lawyer or other mediator to approve it. By the way, this also creates the danger of manipulation by a third party, since execution is automatically managed by the network, not by one or more, possibly biased person, which may be wrong.
- **Trust** – Your documents are encrypted in a shared ledger. There's no way anyone's going to say they lost.
- **Backup** – Imagine that your bank has lost your savings account. On the blockchain, each of your friends has your back. Your documents are duplicated over and over again.
- **Security** – Cryptography used to encrypt websites keeps your documents secure. There's no hacking. In fact, you have to be an abnormally intelligent hacker to crack the code and infiltrate it.
- **Speed** – You would often have to spend time and paper processing documents manually. Smart contracts use software code to automate jobs, which saves time in a variety of business processes.
- **Savings** – Smart contracts save you from an intermediary and mediator. For example, you have to pay the notary to witness your transaction, but thanks to smart contracts you don't need a notary.
- **Accuracy** – Automated contracts are not only fast and inexpensive, but also accurate in that they prevent errors caused by filling out form stacks manually.
- **Guaranteed outcome** – One of the reasons most people involved in transactions go to court is because one or more persons have failed to abide by the rules and terms of the agreement. But smart contracts can significantly lower or even eliminate the need for litigation. Parties will always be committed to operating by the rules of the underlying code when using self-executing contracts.

### What are Ricardian Conventions? Second Generation Smart Contracts!

A Ricardian Convention is a legal contract first initiated in 1995 by Ian Grigg, a well-known programmer. The concept is now part of the blockchain. Here's the basic definition:

> "It is a kind of digital document agreed between the two parties, containing the terms of the interaction between the parties."

What makes it unique is because it's signed, verified, and encrypted. Even if it is a digital document, it is in easy text that is easy for people to read (not just for lawyers). It is a unique legal contract that can be read for computer programs and also for people.

Simply put, it has two parts or serves two purposes. First, it is an easy-to-read legal contract between two or more parties. Your lawyer can easily understand and even you can read and understand the basic terms of the contract.

The latter is also a contract that can be read by the machine. With blockchain platforms, these agreements can now be easily collected, signed, and saved on the blockchain.

### Ricardian Conventions: Historical Context

While Ricardian Conventions is something new for blockchain, it's a thirty-year-old concept. The Ricardian convention is not the only modern term for blockchain introduced in the 1990s. Many recent ideas, such as Smart Contracts and Proof of Business, also appeared at the same time, but were later implemented when blockchain became mainstream.

### Ian Grigg: The Man Behind Ricardian Contracts

Ricardian Contracts were first developed in 1995 as part of the Ricardo Payment System. Ian Grigg, the man behind this new legal document, is considered one of the pioneers of financial encryption. Interestingly, he was still a student when he developed the Ricardian Conventions. That's why people called it the Ian Grigg Ricardian Contract.

In 1998, he published his detailed work on Ricardian Conventions under the name "Financial Encryption in 7 Layers". In his published article, he described the Ricardian Conventions with the following words:

> "A digital contract between two or more spouses, mutually and cryptographically signed and verified, defining the conditions of interaction. The important thing is that it can be read by both man and machine and is digitally signed."

Anyone who can read his work can understand that his time is way above his time. After all, he developed an interesting concept for quantifying a legal contract or any financial instrument or entity. However, in the 90s, there was no suitable technology for applying ideas according to their values. But this limitation no longer exists after the advent of blockchain technology.
