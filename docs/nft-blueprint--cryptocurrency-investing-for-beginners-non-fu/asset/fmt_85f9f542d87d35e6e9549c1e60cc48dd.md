
# Changing the Size of the Protocol

Changing the size of the protocol is technically relatively easy to perform. Rather, the problem lies in convincing a large part of the community of the new block size so that it is accepted in its implementation. In the case of the Hard Fork, the new system is introduced after a specific block, which has been defined in advance. This creates two different blockchains: the first blockchain continues the old system and refuses to accept the changed blocks. The second blockchain, on the other hand, only accepts the new blocks and no longer allows the old ones. The two blockchains share a common past but go their separate ways after the split.

## Possible Consequences of a Hard Fork

A hard fork is a difficult undertaking because it needs the support of the community. In the absence of miner support, the Hard Fork has failed because no transactions are forwarded without it. A failure would also be a lack of user support: in this scenario, all users, exchanges and traders use the old version, which would no longer make the miners' reward system profitable. Accordingly, it is in the mind of the miners that many users are behind the new system. With a large minority of miners, the "scoured" coins would only be exchanged within the small circle of proponents in fiat currencies, which would reduce demand and the price of the coins generated.

The worst-case scenario would be if both protocols are actively used and therefore run side by side. This would, for example, create a second currency besides Bitcoin. Such a case has already occurred twice, once Bitcoin Cash and once Bitcoin Gold has been created, which exist parallel to Bitcoin. In order to avoid secession, there must be consensus among both miners and users. However, achieving this in practice is difficult, as all blockchain participants are mindful of their own economic advantage and hope that Hard Fork will give them an advantage over other market participants.

## Disadvantages of Hard Fork

- Possible price fluctuations
- Uncertainty among investors
- Economic losses

## What is a Soft Fork?

With a soft fork, the risk of separation is much lower than with a hard fork. The soft fork approach is often used to up-source the blockchain. Two well-known and successful soft forks of Bitcoin's blockchain are BIP 66, which has revised the validation of signatures, or P2SH, which changed the formatting of wallet addresses. The Soft Fork rejects all blocks that still have the old properties. However, this only works with Soft Fork if the community and miners are behind the change. Dissent would otherwise lead to secession, i.e., a second cryptocurrency.

## What happens if you spin off the blockchain?

Bitcoin has already been split twice in the past year. The fear of the investors was strongly felt before the first split: there were enormous price fluctuations. However, investors' fears were unfounded, as the spin-off of Bitcoin and Bitcoin Cash led to a profit for all market participants. During the division, the new currency was made available to all investors 1:1. So if a user had two Bitcoin in their portfolio, they will be given the same amount in the new currency. However, the future of Bitcoin will remain exciting even after the two hard forks: The next Hard Fork is due in November 2017, but this should not lead to the split of the blockchains, as the expansion is supported by all major miners and users.

## Supply Chain Management and Blockchain Technology

The processes involved in managing supply chains (which involve the creation and distribution of goods) are extremely complex. In fact, depending on the product, it can go through multiple geographic locations, hundreds of stages, involve several individuals and entities, and produce lots of invoices and payments.

All of this takes several months to complete. The increasing interest in blockchain technology in the supply chain and logistics industry is a result of the complexity and lack of transparency in traditional supply chains. To clearly understand the solutions distributed ledger technology can offer, it's crucial to look at the current issues in the industry and how most of the unique attributes of blockchain can help.

### Challenges with Supply Chain

Supply chains were quite simple a hundred years ago, or even further back, and this is because commerce was just local. But over the years, it has grown more complex despite several innovations like the shift to hauling freight by truck instead of by rail. Another factor that led to a significant change in supply chain management was the emergence of the personal computer in the ‘80s.

As soon as manufacturing became globalized (a significant portion of it taking place in China), the complexity of supply chains increased even more. Globalization is one of the challenges of supply chain management since most companies now have to run their supply chain through several countries to procure various parts of their products.

This is further affected by the issue of corruption, since those running the supply chain are compelled to trust all the participants to execute their jobs fairly. Of course, in some cases, humans are not trustworthy. Customers now find it hard to estimate the value of the products they purchase due to a lack of transparency in the existing systems. There is also the difficulty in investigating supply chains that are suspected of engaging in illegal or unethical practices.

### So, How Can Blockchain Influence Supply Chain?

Apart from the most popular use of blockchain technology in Bitcoin and cryptocurrency, one of the reasons blockchain is here to stay is that it has several applications. Remember, one of the features of blockchain technology is transparency, and the fact that all transactions are recorded on a block that is stored on several computers in different locations makes it an excellent solution to supply chain challenges.

The secure and decentralized nature of blockchain makes it scalable and efficient. Since blockchain is capable of pairing with the Internet of Things as well as mobile device-based technology, this combination can deliver a system for real-time monitoring of deliveries. Examples of blockchain projects that are focused on supply chain management include:

1.  **VeChain**: This supply chain management-based blockchain project has offered excellent opportunities to transform the supply chain management lifecycle in various ways. The project has many use cases that cut across industries like healthcare, logistics, cold-chain logistics, agriculture, automobiles, liquor, and several others.

1.  **Waltonchain**: This is actually the first crypto project focused on supply chain management and combines the best of two worlds – the digital and physical – to deliver an efficient system.
