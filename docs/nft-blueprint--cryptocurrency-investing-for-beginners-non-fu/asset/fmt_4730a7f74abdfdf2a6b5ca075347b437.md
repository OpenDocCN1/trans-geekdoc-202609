# Session 6: NFTs and Popular Blockchain Integrations

For NFT minting, you have different blockchains to choose from. The `Ethereum` network is the first major blockchain to enable the creation of NFTs as we know them today. Later, other blockchains were added, including `Binance Smart Chain`, `Polkadot`, `Tron`, and `Tezos`.

Most NFTs today are either on `Ethereum` or the `BSC`. However, the gas charges on `Ethereum` make the creation and handling of NFTs relatively expensive.

## 6.1. NFTs and Ethereum

When it comes to NFTs, `Ethereum` dwarfs almost every other blockchain network. NFTs have become much more present in the media in recent months. Headlines such as Jack Dorsey's $2.9 million NFT tweet or the sale of the Beeple NFT for $69 million bring NFTs closer to a much wider audience.

This often gives the impression that NFTs are a completely new phenomenon. But the history of blockchain-based NFTs can already boast several years. The first NFT experiments were already running in 2012 with coloured coins on the `Bitcoin` blockchain.

`Ethereum` is currently the largest smart contract blockchain. Even with NFTs, `Ethereum` has clearly been ahead since 2017 until today. Whether crypto punks or `CryptoKitties`, the first Jack Dorsey tweet or the Beeple collage, all of them as well as most marketplaces are part of the huge NFT world on `Ethereum`.

### Token Standards

All tokens on `Ethereum`, whether cryptocurrencies or NFTs, can be assigned to unique standards. Standards are an important building block for how tokens work. Only with suitable standards are the essential characteristics such as ownership, access, and transferability guaranteed. Standards are also essential for software development. In this way, the basic functionalities and behaviours are clearly clear to all those involved.

The three most common `Ethereum` token standards are `ERC20` for Fungible Tokens (exchangeable tokens such as cryptocurrencies) and `ERC721`, as well as `ERC1155` for Non-Fungible Tokens (non-exchangeable tokens = NFTs).

The `ERC20` standard assigns amounts to addresses. Thus, per `Ethereum` address, the exact number of cryptocurrencies held can always be recognized.

With the birth of `CryptoKitties`, the important NFT standard `ERC721` was created, on which most NFTs are based to this day. `ERC1155` is another NFT standard developed by `Enjin`. `ERC1155` is a hybrid of exchangeable and non-interchangeable tokens. `ERC721` maps unique IDs to owners, while `ERC1155` has a nested mapping of IDs to owners to sets.

### NFT Premiere on Ethereum: Crypto Punks

It was only with the `Ethereum` blockchain that NFTs really picked up speed. The first `Ethereum` NFTs appeared as `Crypto Punks` in the summer of 2017.

`Crypto Punks` are 10,000 unique digital characters created by [Larva Labs.](https://www.larvalabs.com/cryptopunks) Their digital proofs of ownership are stored in the form of unique tokens on the `Ethereum` blockchain. Each `Crypto Punk` is characterized by individual optical characteristics, so that no one is the same as the other. `Crypto Punks` have inspired the digital art market and were the forerunner for later standards. Due to the limited offer and the cult status among early users, they are already considered digital antiques. They are still tradable and interoperable with most NFT applications on `Ethereum`.

#### 2017: Birth of CryptoKitties

Even though `Crypto Punks` were officially the first NFTs on the `Ethereum` blockchain, the `CryptoKitties` are still in the memory of most people. At the end of 2017, they became the first mainstream application of NFTs.

2017 was a year with a lot of hype around cryptocurrencies, anyway, driven by the ICO bubble on the `Ethereum` platform. In a short time, countless new ICO projects started and each of them had new and supposedly scarce tokens on offer. This triggered a veritable gold digger mood. More and more new cryptocurrencies on the `Ethereum` blockchain saw the light of day in the digital world. It wasn't long before there was an increase in network congestion, with significantly increased transaction fees as a result. But one of the reasons for the high utilization was, among other things, the creation and trading with `CryptoKitties`. [CryptoKitties](https://www.cryptokitties.co/) are digital images of comic cats created by `Dapper Labs` for a blockchain computer game. Each cat is unique and thus only present once on the blockchain. The special thing about them is that in the on-chain computer game, new unique `CryptoKitties` can be bred by clubs of different cats. These new cats could then be auctioned off or sold on the open market.

#### The CryptoKitties Bubble

Breeding attracted many speculators. The idea was simple – you buy a few rare `CryptoKitties`, breed a few new, even rarer cats with them, sell them at high prices and then do the whole thing all over again. At its peak in 2017, the trading volume was around 5000 `ETH` and sales of `CryptoKitties` achieved prices of over $100,000.

It was almost absurd to pay such high prices for digital cats. But the incredible story behind it in combination with the great hype about cryptocurrencies brought many new buyers into the market in a short time. `Ethereum`'s network capacity at the time could not cope with this onslaught, so that trading and breeding `CryptoKitties` incurred horrendous fees and hours of waiting.

This mixture of hype, speculation and viral story eventually resulted in a bubble that also received appropriate attention in the press. But as expected, the bubble burst soon after. In mid-December 2017, the demand for `CryptoKitties` suddenly declined and prices tumbled. The entire crypto bubble began to burst shortly thereafter, and a multi-year bear market was ushered in.

#### 2018 – 2020: Developments during ongoing bear market

After this hype, interest and media coverage of NFTs flattened. But that didn't mean that exciting NFT concepts weren't developed afterwards. On the contrary, `CryptoKitties` had laid the foundation for new ideas.
