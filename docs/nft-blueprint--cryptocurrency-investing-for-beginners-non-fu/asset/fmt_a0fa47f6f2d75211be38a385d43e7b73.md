# 2.4. Liquidity

Thanks to the growing number of NFT marketplaces, the liquidity in NFTs is increasing. However, given the fact that NFTs are, by design, rare and unique, it means that the liquidity for a specific NFT will always be low. That’s because NFT creators artificially maintain their NFTs in limited supply. NFT markets will create liquidity pools based on auction, almost as in DeFi liquidity pools, and in return for the value here, individuals will be able to buy fungible tokens by donating NFT tokens, and vice versa.

Suppose you go to a sporting event or there's a rock band you love, you want to buy tickets to his concert. As well as being unique in terms of the characteristics of tickets purchased for both occasions, NFT's are unique, meaning they cannot be exchanged for each other.

Each NFT is different from one another, which is perhaps the most significant characteristic of NFTs. As we've mentioned above, NFTs cannot be interchanged with one another due to their uniqueness. No one NFT can be made other than the same smart contract with the same token number. This means that a manufactured token is single and solitary and cannot be produced or copied any other way. This is similar to the fact that the Guernica painting, which Picasso drew with his own hands, is priceless, although there are thousands of copies. This is confirmed by blockchain and can be seen by everyone.

# 2.5. Immutability and Provable Scarcity

NFTS are indestructible thanks to the immutable nature of blockchain technology and smart contracts, NFTs cannot be altered, duplicated, or destroyed. This means that whoever buys the NFT owns it and not the creator of the NFT. If the owner sells the NFT, they cannot falsify the ownership records since this is impossible with blockchain technology. This property of NFTs is what makes their potential in the music industry invaluable. We will discuss this in a later section.

They are resistant to subsequent change and precisely determinable of its supply during creation. The identity of the NFT owners are permanent records on the blockchain network. When NFT changes hands, the subsequent owners can be verified. This ensures that digital artwork such as video clips and music can be traced back to their original owners. It makes authentication much easier and efficient since third parties won't be needed for the process.

More so, NFT is produced with royalty if the manufacturer wishes to do so. This copyright also means that the buyer has all commercial rights of the product, such as promotion and advertising, through the relevant blockchain. This means that it will also increase the commercial value of all kinds of products.

Smart contracts allow NFT creators to set fixed limits on the release of the unique token and implement persistent features that cannot be changed after NFT's are released. Two ERC-721 tokens of the same type cannot be exchanged. This process is possible in theory, but when done in practice, they cannot completely replace each other. Because their metadata won't be the same. The difference between ERC-721 tokens introduced in 2017 from ERC-20 tokens is that they cannot be used as cryptocurrencies and therefore cannot be exchanged for another cryptocurrency on an exchange. The ERC-721 is mostly used as a collection.

However, it's not the first time that for example, a developer can keep the supply of more common items endless while requiring the creation of only a certain number of specific rare items programmatically. The fact that developers over time cannot change the characteristics of products and the number of predetermined supplies thanks to the blockchain's "immutable" feature is especially suitable for items such as original artworks.

# 2.6. Rarity

NFTs being rare shouldn't be confused with scarcity which is one of the primary features of most cryptos. NFTs are rare in the sense that they are unique tokens tied to rare items such as music. More so, they are also scarce, which is where they derive their value from. Although NFT developers can create as many NFTs as they wish, they often limit the number of tokens.

NFT's are rare because they are unique and non-copyable. In addition to the knowledge that rare things are generally valuable, a very limited number of artists and producers have NFT, especially at this time. This is a good opportunity, especially in a constantly evolving virtual world where we cannot easily predict what will happen. If we make a very simple assumption that the values of the products offered for sale in the first place will be lower than in the future, it may also mean that we may have products that will be sold much more expensively in the future.

NFT's have no lifespan and are very available for purchase and sale so that they can be retained for years. In other words, turning a product, you don't want to end your life into an NFT can also mean attributing a partial infinity to it.

Like traditional digital assets, NFT's are fully programmable. For example, in CryptoKitties, the ancestor of NFT, you were able to grow and mature digital cats through design mechanisms such as feeding them baked cookies. Most of today's NFT's have more complex mechanics such as forging, processing, cashing in, random creation. In my opinion, the Design area of NFT's is infinite in the possibilities. There are various platforms where you can create NFTs.

They use metadata to enable objects or entities to gain properties from an external location outside the contract.

These are coded data in `JSON` format that enables digitally stored assets to be presented to us visually on platforms and gain acquisition through these elements and gain the decisive features of erc-721 NFT.

`JSON` is not stored in the Ethereum agreement. Because it is very expensive in cost. Instead, it is stored in type `URI` (Universal Resource Identifier) String, which indicates where the user's token metadata can find the `JSON` description.

This information is drawn through the `API`, and we are provided with information about the features of products on NFT platforms.
