While the stances of governments, financial regulators, and banks around the world on cryptocurrencies range from being extremely prudent to carefully accepting, the technology behind it – Blockchain and smart contracts – is widely accepted and implemented at all levels.

For example, recently, the Depository Trust and Clearing Corporation (DTCC) and four major banks -- Bank of America Merrill Lynch, Citi, Credit Suisse, and J.P. Morgan -- successfully processed blockchain's credit default swap developed by Axoni using smart contracts. According to the press release, the smart contract provided a new level of transparency for partners and regulators, with information such as individual trade details and equivalent risk measurements.

It's happening everywhere. A consortium of 61 Japanese and South Korean banks are testing Ripple's Blockchain and smart contracts to provide cross-border money transfers between the two countries. The new system will be in place this year. Sberbank, a bank controlled by the Russian government, is testing Ethereum's Blockchain and the smart contracts it provides, even in Russia, a country known as a place where there is no more hacking.

The tests began by joining the Enterprise Ethereum Alliance, an organization of more than 100 businesses, including top players such as Cisco, BP, ING, Microsoft, etc. The Alliance aims to develop a fine-tuned Blockchain for business use where smart contracts needed for specific companies can be developed and implemented.

As smart contracts are developed alongside cryptocurrencies, they are mostly implemented in the world of finance and banking. Nevertheless, this technology can be used by governments around the world to make the voting system more accessible and transparent. Supply chains can be used both to track goods and to automate all related tasks and payments. Real estate, healthcare, taxes, insurance, and countless other industries can benefit from the implementation of smart contracts and the benefits they offer.

## Management

Blockchain not only provides a single ledger as a source of trust but also shaves off potential bottlenecks in communication and workflow thanks to accuracy, transparency, and automated systems. In general, business operations are often delayed while awaiting approval and resolving internal and external issues. A blockchain ledger will sort it out. It also eliminates inconsistencies that often arise during independent processing, which can lead to costly litigation and settlement delays.

In 2015, Depository Trust & Clearing Corp. (DTCC) used a blockchain ledger to process 1.5 quadrillion pounds of securities representing 345 million transactions.

## Supply Chain

Smart contracts work under the if-then premise. In the words of Jeff Garzik: "Most often, supply chains are blocked by paper-based systems; here the forms have to go through numerous channels for approval, which increases the exposure to loss and fraud. Blockchain overrides this by providing a secure, accessible digital version to all parties in the chain and automates tasks and payments." Barclays Corporate Bank uses smart contracts to record property changes and automatically transfers payments to other financial institutions upon arrival.

## Cars

There's no doubt we're moving from dignified human vertebrates to super-intelligent robots. Imagine a future where everything is almost automated. Google comes with smartphones, stylish glasses, and even smart cars. This is where smart contracts work. An example of this is that smart contracts can factor into a kind of "oracle" game that can determine who is at fault in an accident. Self-driving or self-parked vehicles with sensors, as well as countless other variables, serve as good examples. Using smart contracts, an auto insurance company can charge customers different fees based on where and under what conditions they operate their vehicles.

## Real Estate

You can earn more money with smart contracts. Normally, if you want to rent a house to someone, you need to pay an intermediary vendor, such as sahibinden.com or a newspaper, to advertise it. Not only that, but you may also need to pay a realtor. A digital ledger reduces your costs. All you have to do is pay in bitcoin and encode your contract in the ledger. Everyone sees it, and you manage to execute it automatically. Brokers, real estate agents, hard-pressed lenders, and anyone associated with real estate can profit.

## Health Care

Personal health records can only be encoded and stored in a blockchain with a private key that will provide access to specific individuals. The same strategy can be used to ensure that research is conducted (safely and confidentially) under HIPAA law. Surgery invoices can be stored in a blockchain and automatically sent to insurance providers as a delivery document. The ledger can also be used for general health management, such as drug inspection, compliance, testing results, and health supplies administration.

## Problematic Parts

Smart contracts aren't perfect. What happens if a bug enters the code? Or how will governments regulate those contracts? Or how will governments accrue taxes on these smart contract transactions? At this point, let's consider the rent example given above.

What if I send the wrong code, or send the correct code, but my apartment goes public without my consent before the rental date arrives? If this was a traditional contract, I could have cancelled it in court, but blockchain is a different matter. The contract, no matter what, is carried out.

The list of challenges continues. Experts are trying to solve them, but these critical issues are hesitating potential adopters.

## The Future of Smart Contracts

Part of the future of smart contracts lies in solving the problems mentioned above. For example, some lawyers who think that smart contracts will enter our daily lives in the future are dedicated to investigating these concerns.

Actually, when it comes to smart contracts, we're entering a sci-fi field. Some suggest that smart contracts can affect changes in certain industries, such as the law. In this case, lawyers will move to producing standard smart contract templates by writing traditional contracts, similar to the standardized traditional contracts you will find in LegalZoom. Other sectors, such as commercial buyers, credit companies, and accountants, can implement smart contracts for tasks such as real-time audits and risk assessments. Others foresee smart contracts being converted into a hybrid of paper and digital content, where contracts are verified via blockchain and backed by physical copy.
