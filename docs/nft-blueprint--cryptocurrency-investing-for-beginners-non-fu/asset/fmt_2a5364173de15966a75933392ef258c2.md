## 3.1 Basics of Blockchain

The blockchain technology is undoubtedly the most impactful technological innovation of the past decade.

Blockchain is an approach developed to create data records in digital environments using encryption science (cryptology). There may be different blockchain applications, but their common feature is that the data cannot be deleted or modified retroactively. A copy of the data is stored in decentralized distributed structures, and data ownership and control within this distributed structure is also secured with a password key. With this system in our hands, we can now start shaping the data on it as we wish. The entire blockchain realm is actually made up of platforms, products, services and services built on this basic logic.

The first person to accomplish the work described above is Satoshi Nakamoto, who remains a mystery. Nakamoto developed the blockchain philosophy with the Bitcoin network he designed and subsequently positioned data records called bitcoin as a financial instrument on this system.

You can think of the Bitcoin network as the first wheel discovered in history to become a tool. The wheel (blockchain) and its components were discovered, but it was the first time Nakamoto had converted it into a useful vehicle. However, the concept of blockchain emerged and was accepted because the method of how to save data is expressed with 'chainblocks'.

As a result of the development of technology, we no longer have to save data to centralized systems. It is possible to store each dataset by making and distributing any number of copies we want. The size of the data is no longer an obstacle to copying and distributing to many locations because we have very high-speed communication networks. In fact, this offers us the first stage necessary for entry into the blockchain world.

Keep in mind that a blockchain or blockchain is an immutable, sequential chain of records called blocks. They can contain transactions, files, or any data you want. But the important thing is that they are chained together by hash methods.

### Historical answer to the question of what is blockchain: The Stalemate of Byzantine Generals

Above, we tried to explain blockchain technology with technical concepts. The concept of blockchain can also be explained through a historical problem: the Byzantine Generals' Stalemate.

Competing with each other, the 4 Generals set out to conquer a city surrounded by strong walls during the Byzantine Empire. Each of the 4 generals commands 4 different armies. When each army approaches the city, it stops at 4 different points and begins preparing attacks. The inhabitants of the city are not yet aware of the danger that is approaching them. The Byzantines must attack the city at the same time to leave the siege as the victor. But there is an important problem that will determine the fate of the war. This is a communication problem.

Generals don't want the success of others because they're competing with each other. That's why the commander of the 1st Army thinks, "When I attack, if the other side doesn't attack, I'll be defeated." Other military commanders feel the same way.

The 1st army commander can solve the problem of transmission by sending a messenger to other armies, but the messenger can be persuaded by other army generals and forced into treason. Or the besieged city dwellers can decipher their plans by capturing the messenger. Therefore, byzantine generals need a mechanism by which they can make decisions at the same time and others can control their decisions. In addition, this mechanism should not be interfered with from the outside. This is only possible with Blockchain technology.

Data transfer on a communication network created with blockchain technology is only possible with confirmation from each node. In addition, blockchain networks are protected by near-impossible-to-break cryptography. At least 51 percent of the nodes need to be checked for the network to be manipulated.
