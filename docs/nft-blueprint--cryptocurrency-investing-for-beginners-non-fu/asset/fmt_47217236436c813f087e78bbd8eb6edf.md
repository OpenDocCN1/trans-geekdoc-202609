Use cases for NFT are virtually endless. In fact, non-fungible tokens can serve as the foundation for a new digital economy based on blockchain technology. ## 1.3 What Are the Biggest Applications for NFTs Today?

We will cover the application of NFTs in detail later on in this book. Although the origin of NFTs can be traced back to 2012, their explosion into the mainstream was not until early 2021. This is in part, thanks to the rise of DeFi and the several potential applications of the NFTs. One of the great challenges of 2020 was explaining to our grandparents how to deal with social platforms, now we need to find a way to explain another increasingly popular topic: NFTs and other "crypto-phenomena" that also affect brands and the world of marketing - after they have shaken the world of finance.

But let's take a step back to defining NFT: Non-Fungible Tokens. Non-fungible essentially means that it is something unique - in short, the digital equivalent of a work of art. NFT refers to any type of content that can be digitized: videos, music, GIFs, 3D objects, images, games, text, memes or even a tweet, you remember?

This digital content is collected in the blockchain, which – by its very nature – allows anyone to see who owns the token (or show possessions, e.g., like when you invite someone to see your art collection if you have one).

In fact, there are many marketplaces, and it is important to choose the right marketplace to find or sell certain objects. There are, for example, some very exclusive ones that can only be accessed by people who have been invited to sell their works, then there are "open ones" where everyone can try to sell their digital content. Then there are those where the works sold are selected by the curators of the platform - just like in the world of "traditional art."

Some of the most famous marketplaces are `SuperRare`, `Nifty` and `OpenSea`.

It is clear that, as with any phenomenon, there are some brands for which the relationship is more natural, and others for which the connection with this world may seem less direct at first glance. Here are three scenarios that could be very interesting and which, in our opinion, represent a not to be underestimated opportunity for brands.

### Sports: The First Mover

Sports brands were among the first - and fastest - to enter the world of the NFT.

The NBA has shown in the past that it is able to recognize and anticipate various trends, and this time it hasn't taken long: Through a special platform - `NBAtopshot` - collectors have spent more than 330 million dollars to buy short clips with the best actions of their favorite players.

Every person has unique characteristics – from appearance to our educational qualifications and our medical history. Non-fungible tokens make it possible to tokenize this identity. Thus, all the data that exists about a person can be presented in the form of an NFT and people can return control over their data. The uniqueness of each NFT is what the sports industry is trying to capitalise on.

Nike has also jumped on the NFT bandwagon quickly, tapping the hyperactive Sneakerhead community with `CryptoKicks`. This is essentially the ability to link each shoe to a unique digital identity (so the authenticity of sneakers can be verified via the blockchain - an increasingly hot topic given the value of the limited-edition sneaker market).

### Collectibles

As you've seen with the `CryptoKitties`, the Fantasy Football Game `Sorare` or `NBA Top Shot` from Dapper Labs, NFTs are already being used to create completely new types of collectibles. `Sorare`, for example, allows you to purchase tokenized versions of your favorite players. The principle is very similar to the Panini collectible images that many still know from their childhood. The same principle is now applied in the digital world, in the form of Digital Collectibles, which digitally represent the possession of a collectible card.

### Music and Art

In the digital world, it is often a nightmare for artists to protect their copyrights and make money. Through NFTs, someone can buy a work of art and present it in a virtual space, with the blockchain proving ownership. Artists can thus protect their copyright and secure a larger share of the profits generated by the sale. In addition, it is possible to configure an NFT to create a recurring source of income where you can contribute to every subsequent sale of the NFT. Recently, some NFTs have already been sold for several hundred thousand dollars and many expect the art NFT sector to grow enormously in the future.
