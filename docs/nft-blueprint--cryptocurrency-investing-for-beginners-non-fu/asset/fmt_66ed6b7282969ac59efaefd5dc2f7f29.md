
# ERC-721 vs ERC-20 vs ERC-1155

There is another key difference between the `ERC-721` and `ERC-20` tokens. `ERC-20` tokens are divisible, just as you can divide one $10 banknote into two five-dollar bills. However, `ERC-721` tokens are not divisible. Can anyone in their right mind share a ticket or a baseball card? No, just like with NFTs.

The `ERC-721` is currently the most commonly used standard for digital art and blockchain gaming. Hundreds of Dapps have since used it, and more than a million unique tokens have been created.

## ERC-721 vs ERC-1155

`ERC-1155` is a multi-token standard. Although the `ERC-721` was an instant success and was used to create millions of NFTs, it also had some limitations. For example, it was expensive for deploying large databases of items.

The `ERC-1155` multi-token standard was created by Witek Radomski, Andrew Cooke, Philippe Castonguay, James Therien, Eric Binet, and Ronan Sandford on June 17, 2018. They are also called semi-fungible tokens.

In short, the `ERC-1155` enables an infinite number of both fungible and non-fungible tokens to be traded in a single smart contract. It is designed to avoid bottlenecks in future transfers by allowing the management of fungible, semi-fungible, and non-fungible tokens.

Thus, different types of tokens are collected in only one transaction instead of many transactions, allowing different types of items to be transferred faster, cheaper, and lighter (within the scope of the data size area covered within the block). For example, this is an example of `ERC-1155` when you buy individual items with `ERC-20` and `ERC-721` on in-game purchases on a blockchain and NFT gaming platform, but instead of buying them on a single standard in a single `ERC-1155` transaction.

To overcome these shortcomings, Witek Radomski decided to create his standard - `ERC-1155`. This means that the `ERC-1155` is an advanced and improved version of the `ERC-721`, offering many new features to create NFTs.

For example, with the new `ERC-1155` standard, you can use both an infinite number of non-fungible and fungible assets in one deployed smart contract. According to Witek Radomski, it is also easy to work with the blockchain network. He also argues that this new set of standards is good for the blockchain-based gaming industry and is also a good option for creating tokens for all forms of ownership, whether digital or physical.

## 2.2. Interoperability

## 2.3. Tradability

Note that NFTs cannot be traded in the form of 0.50 BTC or 0.333 ETH, as in Bitcoin or other cryptocurrencies, and can be sold or bought at a rate of 1:1 because an NFT product is not divisible in reciprocal transfer transactions. You cannot exchange one NFT for another. Whether they are collectibles, gaming, or any other type of NFT, they can't be interchanged. With cryptos, you can always exchange one crypto for its equivalent in another crypto – for example, you can exchange 1 BTC for its equivalent in ETH or any other crypto.

However, there are several marketplaces where you can buy and sell NFTs. Remember that NFTs cannot be divided into smaller constituent units. Unlike cryptos or fiat currencies, NFTs only exist as one whole item.

NFTs also have the quality of variability (in terms of value, popularity, and temporality). Think of a band. We can also see that they make a lot of noise in certain periods but can lose their popularity over time. The same applies to NFTs. For example, the value of an item can be 0.1 ETH in 2021, compared to 670 ETH in 2024.
