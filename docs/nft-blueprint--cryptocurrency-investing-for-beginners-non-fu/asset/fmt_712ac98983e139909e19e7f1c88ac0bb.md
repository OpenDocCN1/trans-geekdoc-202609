
# Beeple's Success and the Rise of Crypto Art

Beeple's success is probably unique – at least for now – but there are many artists who achieve great results by selling crypto art: the artist couple **Hackatao**, one of NFT's pioneers in Italy, or **DotPigeon**, whose virtual exhibition on Nifty sold out within a week.

Among bands, **Kings of Leon** were the first to distribute an album as an NFT, with three different tokens giving access to exclusive assets (from the special edition of the album to the possibility of having lifetime seats in the front row at their concerts).

## Gaming

The sale of rare weapons or skins is currently prohibited in popular games such as **World of Warcraft**, **Fortnite**, **CS:GO**, or **League of Legends**. It is also not possible to use items or skins from one game in another. With NFTs, it would be possible to transfer objects and clearly assign property rights, solving some of the biggest annoyances faced by enthusiastic gamers.

These are probably the most fertile markets, as buying virtual products is the norm for users in video games—for example, skins (including trademarked ones) in **Fortnite**, or fashion brands selling clothes for virtual avatars within games, like **Gucci** in **Tennis Clash**.

An excellent example of NFTs in these markets is German footballer **Mesut Özil**, who, together with **Boss Logic** and **Genies**, developed a clothing line for virtual avatars; the proceeds from the sale of these assets will be donated to **BigShoe**, a non-profit organisation supporting children living in poverty.

For brands, it is important to understand what kind of market and action can best be combined with a campaign associated with this world: for some brands, this is very natural, while for others it may require a less conventional approach to use NFTs.

By "approach," we mean both the type of content to be created (e.g., a 3D object, a video, or generative art) and the type of platform through which the works are auctioned, depending on the audience you want to reach and the message you want to convey.

## Assets

The NFT virtual asset market is large. On platforms like **Decentraland** and **Cryptovoxels**, it is possible to buy plots of virtual land. These plots have unique properties, just like real-world plots. Within virtual worlds, these plots are already being traded for tens of thousands of dollars. In addition, the site **Unstoppable Domains** has tokenized website domain names. Each domain name can thus be converted into an NFT, which can be traded freely by anyone.

This area of NFTs is still at an early stage, but it is possible to tokenize real assets such as works of art or record contracts as NFTs. NFTs are used to prove that someone owns a work of art (proportionately) or to settle claims from royalties.

Anything that happens in these weeks will undoubtedly lead to the birth of a new generation of NFT-related platforms: marketplaces will increasingly resemble art galleries where digital artworks can be exhibited and sold in the same way as traditional ones. Art is art, whether digital or physical.

The same goes for clothes worn on virtual catwalks by avatars that can be seen digitally and bought by people who want to dress up their digital counterparts.

One thing to keep in mind, however, is that NFTs can have a big impact on the carbon footprint (the production of cryptocurrency requires a large amount of energy, which clearly affects the level of CO2 produced), so it is important that brands are aware of this and are informed about how to act responsibly.

# Session 2: Decoding a Few Concepts to Understand NFTs Better

## 2.1 Standardization

Crypto developers have over the years created common, reusable token standards on public blockchains. These standards establish basic principles such as ownership, transfer, and simple access controls.

These policies or protocols are similar to how the `HTTP` protocol sends a request between two devices when images are displayed in `JPEG` or `PNG` formats between two different computers.

Blockchains add primitive elements and a layer that allows them to store their metadata, providing developers with a whole new set of state information on which they can develop applications. This area is called **blockchain standards** (which support stateful data storage and logical operations).

The token for Ethereum is the virtual storage or representation of any asset on Ethereum or other blockchains.

`ERC-721` is a standard that explains how to create unique tokens on the Ethereum blockchain. Unlike tokens created with the `ERC-20` standard, each token created with `ERC-721` is unique.

Non-fungible tokens created with the `ERC-721` standard are unique, unlike other tokens. Since each `ERC-721` token is different from every other and is not of the same value, it is more difficult to exchange tokens with each other.

Coins such as `Bitcoin` and `Ethereum`, as well as fiat currencies such as `USD`, are fungible, meaning that each unit is equivalent. Therefore, each unit of such cryptocurrencies and fiat currencies has the same value and can be exchanged directly with each other.

In addition, fungible coins and tokens can be divided into small units (e.g., `0.5 bitcoin` and `0.0002 bitcoin`), while a non-fungible token is a single unit and cannot be divided into smaller units.

The `ERC-721` standard defines the minimum interface that a smart contract must implement to allow functions such as managing and swapping unique tokens, while not requiring or restricting the addition of extra functions for token metadata.

**CryptoKitties**, a game in which unique cats are bred and sold, is the most well-known project created with non-fungible tokens using the `ERC-721` standard.

Currently, non-fungible tokens are used in many projects such as **Decentraland** and **EnjinCoin**.

The uses of non-fungible tokens are not limited to the gaming industry; they offer many different uses, such as the tokenization of real assets like artwork and real estate.

Another popular blockchain-based game played with `ERC-721` tokens is **Blockchain Cuties**.

To browse the trading or games of non-fungible tokens, check out **OpenSea**, the largest marketplace for digital items on blockchain.

### What is the ERC-721?

This is the non-fungible token standard. Currently, most NFTs are implemented on top of the Ethereum platform as `ERC-721` tokens. You can find other tokens under the NFT umbrella with different standards and other protocols and blockchains (e.g., on `NEO` or `EOS`), but `ERC-721` is currently the most popular NFT standard. The `ERC-721` has a set of standard features and attributes that define it in the form of a smart contract. You need to follow these attributes and features to own, trade, and manage `ERC-721` tokens. Simply put, `ERC-721` provides a standard for creating and exchanging NFTs. Each `ERC-721` token is unique in that it does not belong to `ERC-20` tokens.

**General definition:** A type of token that provides a standard for NFTs. In other words, such tokens are unique and may have a different value than another token in the same smart contract. `ERC-721` tokens can differ according to their age, rarity, and even visual appearance.

**Technical definition:** `ERC-721` (Ethereum Request for Comments 721) is a non-fungible token standard that implements an API for tokens on smart contracts.

When the `ERC-721` feature was first conceived before Ether was born, it was intended to be added to the world of `Bitcoin` in terms of certification of real estate and assets other than `Bitcoin`, but it was not successful or stable, so it came to life with `Ethereum`.
