# Session 7: Breaking The NFT Myths

## Who creates NFT and where to buy it?

On various online marketplaces such as [OpenSea](https://opensea.io/) or [Rarible](https://rarible.com/) you can buy and sell NFT; they are stored in a digital wallet—similar to what is known from cryptocurrencies. With the right feeling you can make real money with it: The initially free CryptoPunks, actually only pixelated portraits to collect, currently go for the equivalent of around 50,000 € over the virtual counter. You don't have to sell NFT as unique pieces; for each work you can create any number of tokens—with a corresponding effect on the market value, of course.

Anyone can create and sell NFT themselves. Provided you have a corresponding digital work and find a customer. On a trading platform, you upload files such as `MP3`, `GIF` or `PNG`, pay a small fee (in the form of a cryptocurrency) and can then store your NFT in the wallet. Because the tokens are then part of the blockchain, you don't have to sell them on the same platform where you created them. There is no obligation to sell at all; anyone can become a collector of their own works of art with NFT.

## How are NFTs produced?

NFTs are manufactured according to the `ERC-721` token standard prepared by Ethereum developers. There are platforms that make it possible to perform the transaction easily so that users do not choke on the technical world of blockchain. All you need to produce NFT is a digital cryptocurrency wallet. After uploading the file to be produced as NFT to the `IPFS` system through these platforms, the parameters of the NFT that cannot be changed are determined. After determining how many NFTs will be produced, its price, how much share will have to be paid on its next sale, NFT is produced by paying the transaction fee of the blockchain network. Upon completion of the transaction, NFTs are transferred to the cryptocurrency wallet and recorded on the entire blockchain where the production is carried out.

## Is NFT an investment tool?

NFTs are different from other cryptocurrencies by design and their value is determined by the person or investor who produces the work rather than the checkboard. If the subject of investment is interpreted through digital art, it is not so different from investing in physical art. Similarly, parameters such as how rare that asset is, age and cosmetics determine the value of an asset purchased as a collection piece.

## Are NFTs different from cryptocurrencies?

"Non-Fungible Token" means digital asset (token) that cannot be divided and can never be produced again. Unlike cryptocurrencies like `Bitcoin` and `Ether`, which are "fungibles", NFTs are unique digital assets. To illustrate with an example, today you can buy and buy `Bitcoin` or `Ether` by breaking it into pieces and exchange the same cryptocurrency in its own unit. However, NFT, a private digital entity, cannot be traded with another token because it is unique. Its internal value is determined by its manufacturer or investor.

## What connects NFT to Ethereum?

Most NFTs are currently stored as part of the Ethereum blockchain. Ethereum, like `Bitcoin`, is one of the cryptocurrencies and offers space in its blockchain for additional information on which NFT depends. Theoretically, however, other blockchains can also be used for NFT. At the cryptocurrency Tron, corresponding tokens with the `TRC-721` standard were already presented in December last year—probably also in response to the boom of `ERC-721`, the Ethereum protocol for NFT.

## And what does NFT have to do with art?

Some in the art industry are already celebrating NFT as a game-changer; in fact the associated digitization of the art market opens a few rusty doors. On the one hand, more and more works of art are being created in virtual space, be they memes, graphics or objects in computer games. NFT allows a clear determination of ownership claims and enables new models of monetization. At the same time, the art market has a general demand problem and the hype is just right: When Beeple's 69-million-dollar work was sold, for example, almost exclusively new customers were in the auction house.

NFT also opens up new investment opportunities in art objects. Owners can sell percentage shares in works via NFT without having to give up their own ownership. Museums could also develop new forms of financing: shares in works by great artists could be sold proportionately with NFT, while the affected object remains in place. Owners can also use NFT to set royalties for their digital works and then receive a share of the sale price each time they resell.

## What impact will NFTs have?

Considering the current uses of NFT, it can be said that it solves intellectual property problems first. While it is very easy to copy a work digitally; The NFT solves this problem by revealing who owns the rights to the work. Because they are a programmable digital asset, artists' share of the next sale can also be encoded inside the NFT. Thus, in the event of a sale, the relevant amount is automatically transferred to the artist's wallet. The digital nature of the NFTs allow users of all types to buy them from all over the world. As the customer base expands thanks to digitalization, the number of people who can reach the work and want to invest, and therefore the demand increases, as demand increases, the price rises. As demand increases, so does supply. More NFTs at different price levels are coming to market. This increases accessibility for investors and art lovers and expands the ecosystem by enabling more works to be sold.

## Can you copy the digital object that makes NFTs?

Sure, that's possible. Digital goods such as images, videos or memes can be easily copied by anyone. Even if the owner, copyright or the uniqueness of such files are documented and proven by NFT, this can be done with a simple right click. However, NFT gives the owner something that cannot be copied: the ownership and all associated claims to the digital work. As a practical example: Anyone can order an art print of the Mona Lisa or put it on a copier at home, but the original still hangs only once in the Louvre in Paris.
