# 3.7. GC-MS and LC-MS analysis

A total of 25 peaks were found in the GC-MS chromatogram (Fig. 5) of the optimized extract of S. swarm. The phytocompounds were identified by comparing their retention, retention times, peak percentages and mass spectral fragmentation patterns to those of compounds listed in the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). Among the 25 peaks, eleven of bioactive compounds (based on the active nucleus of the structure) were identified. The major phytochemical compounds were alpha-beta-sitosterol, campferol gallicoside, quercetin, kaempferol, rutin, vanillic, methyl alcohol, d-rhamnopyranoside, 3-ethylthio-1-propane, ethyl p-methoxycinnamate, (4-methylphenyl) acetate, vanillin, and cis-vaccic acid. # 4. Conclusions

The objective of the research was successfully achieved in this study and effectively extracted the parameters for the highest yield of total polyphenols, total flavonoid compounds and their antioxidant potentials from S. swarm. Using methanol was identified as a suitable solvent for highest yield of phytochemical compounds. The statistical technique of RSM based on face-central composite design, coupled with ANFIS and machine learning were effective in identifying the optimal parameters. In the independent experiment, parameters like methanol concentration, ultrasound intensity, temperature, and particle size significantly influenced the yield of phytochemical compounds. The linear parameters, and their interactions as well as quadratic terms also predicted the highest yield of phytochemicals. The high R^2 value (0.97) showed the reliability of the result. The optimized condition was obtained at 65% methanol, 600 mL of ultrasound intensity, temperature at 45 C for 15 min, and particle size of 0.5 mm, which resulted in the highest yield of flavonoids (g, 75.22% of DDPHC, 84.12% of ABTSC, and 85.02% of H2O2Osc). The optimized flavonoid values were well predicted with RSM, ANFIS and machine learning models. Furthermore, 12 1 bioactive phytochemicals were identified from the optimized extract of S. swarm. Through GC-MS and LC-MS respectively, RSM combined with ANFIS and machine learning models can be applied for designing and effective extraction of bioactive phytochemicals from plant sources on an industrial scale.
