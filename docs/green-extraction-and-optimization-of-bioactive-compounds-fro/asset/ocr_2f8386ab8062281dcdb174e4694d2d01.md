Biologically important compounds based on phenolic flavonoids and their free radical scavenging potential were harvested from Solanum swartz through ultrasound-aided solvent extraction (UAE) method. The free-centered central design (FCCD) in response surface methodology was applied to optimize the extraction parameters, and adaptive-fuzzy artificial intelligence system (ANFIS), as well as machine learning algorithm models were used to predict the yield of bioactive phytochemicals. The effect of extraction parameters, methanol concentration (X₁: 55-66% in v/w), ultrasound-intensity (X₂: 50-60 cm²), temperature (X₃: 30-45 °C), time (X₄: 15-25 min) and particle size (X₅: 0.5-1.5 mm) for each level (+2.3) were studied for optimized yields of total phenolic (TP; y₁), total flavonoid compounds (y₂), and their free radical scavenging abilities (%DPPH% (y₃), %ABTS% (y₄), and %ORAC% (y₅₅). The optimal condition was achieved at X₁: 65%, X₂: 40 cm², X₄: 25 min, and X₅: 1.5 mm, where the highest content of gallic acid (GAE) was 5.38 mg/g (y₇); y₈ = 75.22%, and y₉ = 85.02% was obtained. Most of the experimental results were well matched with the predictions of the RSM model. Further, ANFIS and random forest machine learning algorithm were utilised to compare and validate the optimized extraction parameters. Also, GC-MS and LC-MS were used to investigate the presence of various bioactive phytochemicals in the extract.
