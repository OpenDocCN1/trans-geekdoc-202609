## 2. Experimental section

### 2.1. Solanum tor Sw. Fruits

The fruits of S. torvam Sw. were collected from shelves of the Western Ghats in near Shenbagathoppu Hills (at longitude: 7.6225; 9.5225); Srivilliputh, Virunagagar, Tamil Nadu, India. The surface of the fruits was washed with water and dried at shade for two weeks. The dried fruits were ground into fine powder using mesh grinder (Greenree Elects Ltd., Chennai, India). The powder was then screened to vary particle size powders (0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.5 mm) using several sieves (Mesh No. 35, 25, 18, 16, and 2). The samples were stored under air conditioner until the start of the experiment (moisture content: 10 ± 2%).

### 2.2. Chemicals and reagents

Solvent colorimetric reagents, ABTS, DPPH were supplied by Sigma Aldrich, Bengaluru, India. Methanol, Folin-Ciocalteu reagent, sodium carbonate, sodium hydride, aluminum chloride, hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) were purchased from HiMedia Pt. Ltd., Mumbai, India. All other chemicals and reagents were of analytical or equivalent grade and obtained from Merck and SRL, Ltd., India.

### 2.3. Ultrasound-assisted solvent extraction

Using an ultrasound-assisted solvent extraction (UAE), the bioactive phytochemicals were extracted from the fine powder of S. torvam Sw. The experimental apparatus had an ultrasonic water bath (temperature precision of ±1.0 °C, 220 V, and continuous at 20 kHz intensity ultrasonic processor) including a jacketed reactor with 25 ml capacity (capacity assessed by AC Pharma, India). The concentration of phytochemicals were measured using a Shimadzu UV-1800 series UV-Visible spectrophotometer using UV Probe 262 software from Japan.

### 2.4. Preliminary experiments for identification of ideal solvent system

The preliminary experiments were conducted to identify the ideal solvent system based on the concentration of TF, TP and DPPH* using S. torvam Sw. The solvents such as methanol, ethanol, ethyl acetate, diethyl ether, chloroform, and n-hexane were used for the preliminary experiments. The extraction was carried out in each solvent system (20 ml of 70% v/w in water) with 2 g of powdered S. torvam Sw. with the extraction conditions of particle size: 0.5 mm, ultrasound intensity: 60 W, and temperature: 65 °C.

### 2.5. Selection of process parameters

The five extraction parameters (ordered parameters) were selected as solvent concentration (55-66% v/w in water), ultrasound intensity (50-60 W cm⁻2), temperature (30-45 °C), and time (1.5-25 min) were selected for the yield of bioactive phytochemicals based on the dependent parameters, such as total phenols (TP), total flavonoids (TF), 2,2'-2,1'-diphenylhydrazyl (DPPH*), 2,2'-bis(3-sulfonopropyl)-6-ionic acid (ABTS), and hydrogen peroxide (H2O₂) measuring antioxidant ability of S. torvam Sw. Further in comparison the optimal solvent was chosen as the best solvent based on the results of preliminary experimental studies (Table 1). ### 2.7. Determination of total polyphenols (TPF)

Spectrophotometric analysis was used to determine the total polyphenol content using the Folin-Ciocalteu reagent (Medina, 2011). Briefly, 5 ml of 10 diluted Folin-Ciocalteu reagent was mixed with 0.2 ml of each extract, individually. 2 ml of 7.5% sodium carbonate (Na$_2$CO$_3$) was added to the mixture for 5 minutes, and the mixture was then left in dark for an hour. After turn results were measured by measuring the absorbance at 765 nm using the spectrophotometer. The results were expressed as mg of gallic acid per gram of sample using gallic acid as the reference.

### 2.8. Determination of total flavonoids (TF)

One ml of each sample was mixed with 0.3 ml of 5% sodium methanol, and the mixture was incubated for 5 min under dark conditions. The reaction was stirred vigorously for 6 minutes at room temperature and then, 0.3 ml of 10% sodium hydroxide was added. The absorbance was measured at 510 nm using a UV-visible spectrophotometer. The results were expressed as mg of rutin.

The antioxidant capacity of the extracts was evaluated using three assays: DPPH, ABTS, and H$_2$O$_2$. Each assay was performed according to standard protocols.
