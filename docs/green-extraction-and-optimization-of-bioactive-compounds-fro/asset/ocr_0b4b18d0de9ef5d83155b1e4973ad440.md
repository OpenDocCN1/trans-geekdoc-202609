Use of natural, particularly bioactive compounds from plants enhance the area of drug discovery. Naturally, plant provide a wide range of secondary metabolites in the phenolic compounds in their tissues to protect themselves against various pathogens, pathogens, and diseases (Ganes et 2018). Recently, bioactive compounds from plants gained more attention due to their therapeutic potential (Chandrasekhar, 2020).

Several research works have shown excessive levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS), reactive nitrogen species (RNS) and DNA damage are responsible for various cellular functions (Veller et al., 2013; Kalimathu et al., 2021; Giringding et al., 2016). In reality, free radicals play a dual role with both benefit (at low concentrations) and toxic effects (at high concentrations)(Poprac et al., 2017). Plants with secondary metabolites may act as antioxidants to neutralize the level of free radicals generated in the human body (Rajkumar et al., 2022). The secondary metabolites of polyphenolic compounds are attractive because of their biological properties antioxidant activities and the suppression of reactive oxygen species. Antioxidants have been shown to inhibit oxidative stress and prevent the consumption of oxidant, sparing the target molecules that may be damaged. Particularly phytochemical compounds help to prevent, or inhibit various health complications including cancer (Tungmunthim et al., 2018). In fruits, vegetables, leaves, and grains, more than 300 phytochemicals have been identified and quantified (Igbal et al., 2015). Capsaicin, flavonoids, kaempferol, quercetin, niacin, ellagic acid, curcumin, are some of the well-known bioactive compounds from plants.

Solanum swertum, as Turkey berry, is a small plant found in many countries and around 5 leaves and branch (Goussaut et al., 2005; Saha and Dutta, 2017). The fruits and leaves are abundant in bioactive compounds and exhibit antioxidant, neuroprotective, anti-microbial, anti-inflammatory, antiseptic and anticancer activities (Saha et al., 2022). The plant also contains various compounds such as solanine, 3-β-D-glucopyranoside, 2-o-β-D-glucopyranosyl(1→2)-β-L-rhamnopyranosyloxy3-β-D-glucopyraninoside, 6-o-β-D-glucopyranosyl(1→6)-β-L-rhamnopyranosyloxy)-3-β-D-glucopyraninoside, isquercetin, rutin, 6-o-β-D-glucopyranoside, 6-o-β-D-rhamnopyranosyl(1→6)-3-β-D-glucopyraninoside, isquercetin, kaempferol and quercetin (Ismail and Tuar, 2023). The extraction of bioactive compounds from plants is a very important source in the isolation of bioactive phytochemicals. In plant tissues these bioactive compounds are present in various locations and therefore the selection of an ideal extraction technique is essential for the recovery of these bioactive compounds from plant sources. Some extraction techniques such as conventional, blending, dection/distillation, stir bar probe ultrasonic extraction, pressurless ultrasound solvent extraction, microwave-assisted extraction, ultrasound-assisted carbon dioxide supercritical extraction, were formulated in laboratories and to study the properties of compounds (Choi and Verrier, 2001). Some of these extraction techniques require more amount of solvents, longer time, require high energy, and may damage the thermo-sensitive compounds, which are driven by research results. For effective extraction, the interaction between solvent and ultrasound waves (ultrasound assisted extraction (UAE)) has been used to enhance the yield of bioactive compounds from plants. The heat generated by the solvent system to enhance the solubility of bioactive compounds from plants. The heat could potentially destroy the thermosensitive compounds. This is the major disadvantage of UAE for thermosensitive compounds (Selvari et al., 2022). Another effective extraction technique compared is ultrasound-assisted solvent extraction (UAE) (Kuan et al., 2020). The positive aspects of UAE increase yield, shorten extraction time, requiring lower temperature and solvent consumption compared to other methods. By increasing mass transport, ultrasonic vibrations speed up the release of reactive bubbles in the solvent and create damage to plant cells through high pressure pressures generated during ultrasound cavitation (Alessandro et al., 2012). While Supercritical extraction is better among all the three, ultrasound-assisted-extraction technique is not suitable for thermosensitive compounds.

The objective of the present study is the maximal extraction of bioactive compounds from S. swertum, while also considering the extraction parameters such as solvent concentration, particle size, temperature, pulse cycle, and mode/pH, etc., would significantly impact the yield of phytochemicals and their antioxidant potentials. To achieve the highest yield, a combination of these extraction parameters must be optimized. Response surface methodology (RSM), as an efficient statistical method, was used in this study to enhance the condition to increase the yield of bioactive compounds. Furthermore experimental design-based methodology has been employed to address the factors affecting aqueous-ultrasound solvent extraction (Yolmdeh et al., 2015). One of the statistical methods and techniques that is frequently used for the optimization of efficient extraction parameters is RSM (Aydar, 2018). RSM was used in the chemical, food, and pharmaceutical industry to enhance efficiency. The advantages of RSM are in the use of fewer experimental parameters to obtain more results (Sanjaka et al., 2019). The central composite design (CCD) and the Box-Behnken design (BBD) are the designs used frequently for the optimization of RSM to achieve the optimal conditions for bioactive compounds from plant sources (Xi et al., 2016). Additionally, ANFIS (artificial neural-fuzzy inference system) and machine learning approaches are implemented and used to investigate the interaction of the variables and the nonlinear effects compared with the RSM output (Sarayari et al., 2017). In order to assess the experimental data, the RSM and ANFIS prediction models were constructed and Machine learning is used to tailor the most effective extraction parameters (Fan et al., 2017).
