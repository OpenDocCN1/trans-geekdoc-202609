# 6.5.4 Observing disease development

Accurate segmentation is also important in assessing the dynamics of a disease and efficiency of a therapy over a period of time. The problem is that in most cases we are able to obtain only a single snapshot of the disease process, while segmentation allows for serial observation of diseases and in consequence, frequent and accurate evaluation of the anatomical changes and pathological processes. This data is necessary for making wise decisions regarding patient care, changing the treatment strategies, and evaluating the effectiveness of actually used treatments.

# 6.5.5 Advancements in multiomics integration

Its major component is an attempt to combine imaging data with multiomics data, including genomics, proteomics, etc. This is due to the fact that accurate segmentation allows us to relate molecular signatures to imaging characteristics and, as a result, acquire a deeper understanding of disease mechanisms. This integration paves the way to discovery of new drugs and biomarkers, which in turn are used to develop tailor-made treatment plans based on the bio signatures of patients.

# 6.5.6 Opportunities and threats

The applicable capabilities of accurate segmentation contributing to the development of personalized medication are promising, nevertheless a series of barriers have to be solved. Maintaining the external validity of segmentations as well as the reliability of the used algorithms when applying them to different patient groups and imaging techniques is important to translate the developed software into clinical practice. Evolving concerns with data privacy and protection, model interpretability, and fairness and bias in model development are significant to overcome the barriers of acceptance with healthcare providers and users/patients.

It is anticipated that continuous improvement of superior segmentation algorithms together with the trend of new large-scaled annotated imaging databases would facilitate the future promotion of personalized medical system. Hence the importance of accurate segmentation in delivering a true vision of personalized healthcare still remains, because the segmentation provides more detailed and specific information of the relevant patient’s anatomy and pathology.
