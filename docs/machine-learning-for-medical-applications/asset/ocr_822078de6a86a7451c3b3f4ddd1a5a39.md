# 5.4.2 Excitation wavelengths

Due to the confinement in dimensions QDs exhibit a distinct fluorescence emission when excited by particular wavelengths of light. Generally, excitation wavelength is absorbed by the QD to move electrons to a high energy level. The selection of the excitation wavelength depends on the QD material implemented and the size of the QDs. For example, for CdSe QDs, UV to visible light is necessary for excitation, while for PbS QDs, near-infrared excitation is needed. The excitation spectrum of QDs is typically broad involving excitation at different wavelengths and is helpful in various imaging modes.

# 5.4.3 Emission wavelengths

When QDs are excited, they glow at certain wavelengths dependent on the energy difference in GaAs/AlAs quantum well system. Emission wavelength depends on the size of the QD, its core materials, and any additional surface coatings. In general, QDs with a smaller size radiate blue to green light while those with larger size radiate red to near-IR light. This tunability allows targeting of QDs exhibiting specific emission characteristics for various imaging uses. Fluorescence emission spectra are normally sharp and distinct, which minimize spectral congestions and improve the dexterity of multihued imaging.

# 5.4.4 Spectral range and resolution

QDs have broad spectral range with sharp emission peaks making it easier for imaging to be more sensitive and have high resolution. The convenience to change the wavelength of emitted light in this system in a narrow range offers the possibility of distinguishing the channels in multiplexed assays. The reduced FWHM of the emission peaks lowers the noise level and enhances the reliability of signal measures. This property is useful in applications where fine detail is needed to be identified.

# 5.4.5 Influence of surface coatings

It is therefore possible to modify the surface of QDs and use surface coatings to alter the excitation and emission wavelengths. These surface changes alter the electronic structure of the QD, thus invoking changes of wavelength in excitation and emission. For instance, the binding of specific ligands modifies the energy states of QDs as well as their fluor optical properties. Some applications require ideal optical properties of the surface that is why layering of the surface coatings carefully performed.
