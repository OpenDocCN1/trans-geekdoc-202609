Fusion in multimodal networks can occur at three primary levels: early fusion, intermediate fusion, and late fusion. Early fusion performs the data fusion directly on the raw signals from different modalities so that the network can take integrated inputs. Intermediate fusion combines the features extracted from each modality and hence, allows the network to learn intermodal relationship while preserving the intramodal characteristics. Figure 15 illustrates how late fusion enables simple and relatively unrestricted architectural solutions by combining the outputs of the models that were trained independently. Recent developments like attention mechanism and graph-based architectures add to the extent that they distinguish the contribution of each modality.

The well-known approach to multimodal fusion was based on hybrid neural architectures. For example, CNNs work on graphics data, while RNNs work on sequence data. To this end, the modality-specific models are connected at fusion layers to learn synchronous representations. In fusion frameworks, VAEs and GANs have also been used in order to generate and reconstruct the multimodal network, which in turn aids in improving the detection performance in a complex scenario.

## 7 Conclusion

The chapter presents a comprehensive discussion on the state-of-the-art ML models and their combinations for effective anomaly detection in scanned visual information. By combining supervised learning, unsupervised learning, and RL methodologies, the work adequately handles some of the perennial challenges associated with anomaly detection, such as diminishing ability in handling high-dimensionality data and imbalanced datasets. The reporting of hybrid models, which involve CNNs, RNNs, GANs, and VAEs, exhibits how the application of these frameworks increases the accuracy of anomaly detection and reduces the computations required. Notable developed areas are feature extraction techniques, reliable ensemble methods, and the utilization of multiform data for purposes of improved anomaly detection. It also covers issues and concerns related to the implementation of the suggested methods, including scalability, interpretability, and flexibility across domains of interest, particularly in diagnostic medicine, digital security, and manufacturing. These methodologies enhance the results and enable the framework to be practical as evidenced by high recall and precision rates for different types of fine-grained anomalies.
