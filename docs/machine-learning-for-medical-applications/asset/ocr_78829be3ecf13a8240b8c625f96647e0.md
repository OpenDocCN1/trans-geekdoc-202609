Another characteristic feature of RL is the dual approach that involves exploration of the environment as well as exploiting the learned information. Exploration is very important to enable the model to search for new strategies that not have been identified in the data set, especially when searching for new and quite rare anomalies; while in exploitation, learned policies are used to gain the maximum detection rate of miscellaneous objects. In anomaly detection tasks, this balance is important because it makes the system capable of producing good results for new anomalous patterns without being impacted by previous patterns of anomalies. To keep the exploration at an acceptable level other methods like epsilon-greedy strategies policies are used, which also makes RL-based systems more capable in more complex and nonstationary environments.

The approach of RL deepens anomaly detection subject to various application areas, among which are network security, industry monitoring, and healthcare diagnostics. For example, in cybersecurity, the RL agents can learn different strategies for protection from new threats since the policies can be easily updated. Thus, the RL-based anomaly detection in industrial systems can adapt to equipment deterioration operating settings, which attempt to hint at failures before they worsen. Similarly in the context of healthcare, RL can be used to detect signals of variation of patient data streams in real time and manage variability for each and every patient.

# 5.2 Exploring deep Q-learning in anomaly detection tasks

DQL, an improved form of Q-learning integrated with deep NNs, has been used in anomaly detection because of its ability to learn in high dimensional data spaces. Original Q-learning approaches are based on the use of tabular representations of Q-values, which cannot be applied to large datasets. DQL resolves this issue by training the deep NNs to approximate these Q-values to design anomaly detection in variants of environments including time series analysis, image sorting, and cyber security. With experience replay and TD learning, DQL is capable of finding good solutions to real-world anomaly detection problems.
