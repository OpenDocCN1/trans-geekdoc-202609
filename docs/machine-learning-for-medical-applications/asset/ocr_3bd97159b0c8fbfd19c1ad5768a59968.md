# 2.3.5 Bioprinters outlined: challenges of bioprinting (2003)

Since bioprinting technologies involve the creation of biological tissues and structures, 3D printing has become one of the significant breakthroughs in the field. The first steps in bioprinting addressed this approach by creating scaffold structures infused with cells for tissue engineering and regenerative medicine purposes. As it enables the development of structures that are both biological and functional concurrently with tissues, this development revolutionizes the medical implant industry.

# 2.3.6 Multimaterial and color printing, multimaterial printing, advanced color printing (2009)

The versatility and functionality of AM technology were improved by integrating additional features into the color and multimaterial printing technologies of 3D printers. It has become possible to design and create components that are both multicolor and multifunctional due to the availability of new technologies for the combination of different colors and materials. From this development, the range of applications was increased to such areas as individual prosthetic devices and medical implants.

# 2.3.7 High-resolution 3D printing, which was released to the public in 2014

The precision and detail that are achievable with additive manufacturing have been enhanced by the new high-resolution 3D printing technologies. The fidelity of implants and prosthetics has increased because of the formation of intricate and realistic structures due to the enhancement of the printing accuracies. Achieving this mark has been crucial to meeting the high requirements for the applications of the technology in medicine.

These milestones are major steps forward in 3D printing technology: all of them contribute to the improvement of additive manufacturing and expand its applications. From the early inventiveness to convert abstract designs into tangible models, 3D printing has caused a revolutionary impact on numerous fields, especially in the field of biological customization, medicine, and implants.
