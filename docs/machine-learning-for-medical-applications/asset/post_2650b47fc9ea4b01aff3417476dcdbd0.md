## 2.4 Parameters affecting nanofiber morphology and properties

Among them, the types of nanofibers and their shape and features are mainly influenced by the properties of electrospun polymer solutions. Control factors consist of polymer concentration that influences the viscosity of the solution with consequences on the fiber diameter and uniformity. Softer fibers are derived from higher polymer concentration while thinner and more nonsymmetrical fibers are derived from lower polymer concentrations. During the electrospinning process, the formation of the fibers as well as their solidification are affected by the type of solvent and the rate of evaporation. In order to have stable jets and continuous fibers deposition on the collector, it is necessary to fine-tune the solution’s viscosity and surface tension properties.

Managing the production of nanofibers needs regulation of electrospinning factors, each of which includes the applied voltage, flow rate, and distance from the spinneret to the collector. Control of the electrostatic force that expels the polymer solution out of the nozzle depends on the voltage applied, as it affects the orientation and diameters of the fibers. Although finer fibers are produced at higher voltages, there is a likelihood of bead formation and fiber instability. The rates of extrusion and deposition affect the diameter and density of the fibers, and are controlled by the flow rate of the polymer solution. Often, longer lengths lead to thinner fibers and almost of equal diameter. The minimum distance required from the spinneret to the collector influences the stretching as well as solidification of the fiber.

In this process, various environmental conditions, including temperature and humidity, play a major role in controlling the electrospinning process and the properties of the nanofibers. The rate of evaporation of the solvent is largely influenced by temperature, which in turn determines the morphology and dimensions of the fibers formed. Generally, increased temperature accelerates solvent evaporation, thereby encouraging the formation of thinner fibers. However, excessively rapid evaporation may lead to structural defects in the fibers. Relative humidity also significantly impacts the rheological behavior of the polymer solution and the overall fabrication of nanofiber webs. High humidity tends to result in beaded fibers due to insufficient solvent evaporation and moisture-induced swelling, whereas low humidity conditions typically produce more uniform, yarn-like fibers that are prone to breakage due to brittleness.

The shape of the collector also has paramount influence on the orientation and disposition of electrospun nanofiber. Because collectors can come in various forms, for example as flat plates and rotating drums, certain collector orientations can affect the flow of the deposited fibers and their orientation. For instance, rotating collectors can cause fiber stretching and alignment and hence result in enhanced mechanical properties of the fibers. The position of the collector, relative to the spinneret, also influences fiber orientation and morphology, as also the transverse and longitudinal distances at which the collector is positioned with respect to the spinneret. One of the key requirements to obtain uniform fiber deposits and reduce defects is the uniformity of the collector surfaces.

Post-spinning treatments, which are also able to change the properties of the electrospun nanofibers, include heat treatment, chemical cross-linkage, and plasma treatment. Heat treatment helps in enhancing the crystalline structure of the polymers and residual solvents to enhance the strength and stability of the fibers. Covalent bonding can also be made to occur between polymer chains through a process called chemical cross-linking; this enhances the utility of the fiber and its life span. Plasma modification enhances the biocompatibility of the nanofibers’ bioactive molecules to attach on their surface. Since it is essential to attain specific fiber characteristics without a doubt on their properties, such treatments should be controlled.

## 2.5 Advances in electrospinning techniques

The technique of electrospinning has been developed since its discovery and new technologies are implemented to increase the accuracy in creating the nanofiber matrix. Newer methods in electrospinning have been defined to overcome these drawbacks of electrospinning, including poor control over fiber properties, scalability, and ability to create structures of multiple fibers. These developments are to increase the performance, multifunctionality, and variety of applications of electrospun nanofibers in order to meet the increasing needs of different sectors such as biomedical engineering, electronics, environmental engineering, and others.

Three significant progresses have been made in electrospinning. Identification of more than one needle electrospinning system: Seven-point probes system allows the creation of several nanofiber streams from one system. This technique improves throughput and lowers processing time, which makes it very ideal for business with massive operations. Multineedle electrospinning also enables the use of more than one type of polymer, as well as the formation of nanofibers of different dimensions and sizes within a single step, making the created materials significantly more useful.

Increased surface area allows for greater incorporation of pharmacological agents into the fibers, making them particularly effective in drug delivery applications. In such systems, efficient drug encapsulation and sustained, controlled release are critical for therapeutic success. The extensive surface area of nanofibers supports gradual drug diffusion, which helps minimize adverse side effects while enhancing the overall therapeutic efficacy of the treatment. Growth factors, peptides, and other therapeutic substances can be more easily incorporated and interacted with because of the large surface area of electrospun nanofibers. The uniform distribution and efficient loading of bioactive compounds throughout the nanofibers are made possible by this large surface area. Under such conditions, in several biomedical uses, including in wound healing, where bioactive chemicals can promote tissue repair and regeneration, this attribute enhances the efficacy of the nanofiber systems.

Owing to the high S/V ratio, nanofibers produced through electrospinning are suitable for improving sensitivity and detecting ability in sensor and diagnostic fields. It helps to target analytes more successfully due to the higher density of functional sites, which is made possible by the larger surface area. This characteristic is very helpful for the creation of biosensors and diagnostic tools, where quick detection and great sensitivity are necessary for precise and fast findings.

## 3.2 Mechanical properties

The mechanical properties of electrospun nanofibers have significant influence on the performance of different applications. Of most importance is biomedical employment due to the fact that the material is strong and flexible as well as long lasting. The size of the nanofiber, type of polymer, and the electrospinning parameters influence the mentioned characteristics. Understanding and controlling these mechanical properties are critical for tailoring nanofibers for the specific applications that include protective clothing, medical dressings, and beds for tissue regeneration.

Tensile strength is one of the mechanical properties that characterizes the strength of electrospun nanofibers to hold stretch without failure. Both the inherent strength of the polymer as well as the radius of the fiber proves to have a profound effect on this property. For this reason, in comparison to wider fibers possessing lesser SA/V ratio, increased resistance to deformation prevents the fibers from bending, and slimmer fibers possess greater tensile strengths. The last essential characteristic to consider is the ability of fibers, in other words, the elasticity of fibers to recover their initial form after deformation.
