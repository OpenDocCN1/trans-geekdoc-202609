## 4 Current applications of 3D printing in medicine

### 4.1 Customized implants and prosthetics

This technology has many applications in the medical field, but prosthetics and customized implants are among the most significant. This method takes advantage of the inherent characteristics of additive manufacturing to fabricate unique medical devices that can address patients' functional and anatomical needs.

Customizing implants for individual anatomies: The use of 3D printing in creating customized implants that fit the structure of a patient is one of the most significant applications of this technology. Imaging techniques, which essentially consist of taking multiple digital scans of a patient's body, include MRI and CT scans. These models help in defining implants that fit the patient in a very efficient manner as far as functional performance and implant outcomes are concerned. Although custom implants are in demand in various procedures, more emphasis is placed on reconstructive procedures, especially orthopedic and craniofacial ones, where standard implants are not suitable.

Two application areas that have seen significant use of 3D printing in the medical field include prosthetics and custom implants. This method takes advantage of the opportunities that have been afforded by additive manufacturing, whereby intricate medical devices and implants can be modeled to fit the individual functional and physical needs of each client.

Customizing implants for individual anatomies: Probably the most recognized application of 3D printing is the crafting of specialized implants that fit the specific body structure of a patient. Conventional and specialized imaging analysis techniques, including MRI and CT, make 3D detailed reconstructions of the human body.

Complex and functional designs: This invention also offers the benefit of a high level of design freedom for implants and prosthetics, which is commonly not achievable via conventional fabrication technologies. Interior designs, open cellular substrates, and geometries can be incorporated into implants and prostheses with the aim of improving function and biocompatibility. For instance, interconnected porosity can enhance bone-to-implant contact and osseointegration, and surface topologies can meet certain functional demands.

Reduction in manufacturing time and costs: In manufacturing customized implants and prosthetics, 3D printing becomes a better option in terms of manufacturing time and cost. These aspects mean that assisted design-for-manufacturing engineering creates an opportunity to produce and deliver specific devices within short periods and promptly intervene when necessary. Furthermore, the additive manufacturing process reduces the wastage of materials and brings down the cost of manufacturing, thus making custom solutions affordable.

Enhancements in surgical outcomes: Specifically, modified implants have been shown to enhance surgical results, such as better orientation, fewer complications, and shorter time to rebuild than standard surgical applications. For example, in patient-specific implants, where the instruments have the possibility to fit more accurately, they are often more effective in joint replacement surgeries, providing better joint function. Likewise, there are individualized authentic cranial plates that can replace complicated cranial damage with increased accuracy to minimize esthetic and functional consequences.

### 4.2 3D printing in surgical planning and simulation

This way, 3D printing has advanced surgical planning and simulation, offering great value for better surgeries and patient treatment. This has added precision, customization, and visualization in operations when 3D models of patients are used in planning and simulation before the operation.

Preoperative visualization: In the past, the employment of 3D printing for planning surgeries involved constructing detailed, bio-anatomic models from medical imaging data. These models have the added advantage of providing surgeons with a clear and accurate picture of a patient’s anatomy, made through processes such as SLA and FDM. Coordination of detailed technical operations necessitates a more comprehensive appreciation of complicated topographic features that can be gained only through manipulative and physical observation of these models.

Custom surgical instruments and tools: Surgical instruments and tools can be designed and printed through 3D printing in a way that fits specific surgeries. It is possible to make these instruments with specific dimensions that correspond to the peculiarities of the patient’s body, which helps to improve the accuracy of the manipulation and ease of use during the operation. Such fast prototyping also allows for the development of new instrument designs and the constant improvement of surgical tools that enhance the quality of surgical operations.

Simulation and training: The use of 3D print models is common in simulations of surgeries and training exercises. It should be noted that these models give surgeons real-life experience, affording them practice on human figures before surgically operating on human beings. Technological advancements in 3D printers can be embraced in simulation to provide better feedback in learning logistics since the models approximate real anatomy.

Preoperative planning and decision-making: Tactile physical models that are developed through 3D printing also enable surgeons to make preliminary assessments regarding several strategies and methods of operation before a surgery begins. Doctors using these models can therefore have an estimate of what to expect in the surgical operations, the risks incurred, and the best approach for each patient. This integrated planning tends to minimize intraoperative unpredictability concerning the operative site and other related domains, which enhances surgical productivity.

Patient communication and education: 3D models were further used as an informative tool and were beneficial in explaining the condition and the planned surgery to the patient. Cranelike and doll-like models help the patient understand their situation and the planned surgery, its purpose, and possible results. Greater patient understanding boosts the patient enrollment process and is the essence of informed consent and patient satisfaction.

One of the most significant advancements in medical practice is the utilization of 3D printing for surgical planning and simulation. In this case, 3D printing benefits surgery by offering accurate, individualized models, instruments, and exercises in training. Through the proper use of planning and simulation methods, the use of 3D printing technology improves patient care and treatment, as demonstrated in the transmission of the surgical methodology.

### 4.3 Bioprinting

When specifically focused on engineering biological tissues and organ architectures, bioprinting was a visionary application of 3D printing systems in medical science. In this revolutionary technique, cells and biomaterials are accurately placed, organizing active constructions that work like true tissues and organs. The tissue engineering, regenerative medicine, and personalized medicine industries all experience comprehensive transformations attributable to the advancement and utilization of bioprinting technology.

Principles of bioprinting: Similar to conventional 3D printing, it involves the layering of materials, but the fundamental material is living cells. It refers to the layering of bioinks, which are composed of cells mixed with a suitable material to create structures that are 3D in nature. These structures are designed to mimic the structural and functional features of real tissues. Different techniques involved in the fabrication of a tissue construct through the print head are used to deliver the desired resolution and accuracy, which include extrusion, laser, and inkjet technologies.
