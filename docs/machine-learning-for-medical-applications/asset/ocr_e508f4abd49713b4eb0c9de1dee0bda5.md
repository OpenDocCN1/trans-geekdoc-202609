Several optimization techniques based on machine learning are highly valuable in scaffold design including surrogate modeling, reinforcement learning, and Bayesian optimization. These techniques utilize predictive models to guide the search toward optimal solutions. Two commonly used surrogate modeling approaches are artificial neural networks and Gaussian process regression, which evaluate objective functions and direct optimization using historical data. Reinforcement learning has been adopted in robotic and adaptive manufacturing processes, allowing systems to build scaffolds by learning optimal strategies through environmental interaction. Scaffold design also benefits from Bayesian optimization, which aids in parameter tuning and improved design outcomes. These methods incorporate probability-based mechanisms to manage the exploration–exploitation trade-off, enabling efficient searches for near-optimal solutions.

A wide range of global optimization techniques are also employed in scaffold design. These techniques systematically explore the solution space to identify the most effective design strategies. By combining the strengths of model-based and global optimization approaches, researchers can navigate large and complex design spaces and discover near-optimal solutions for developing innovative scaffolds in tissue engineering and regenerative medicine.

# 5.2 Gradient-based optimization methods

Optimization algorithms often used in scaffold design as gradient based methods are popular because of their efficient work for various differentiable and continuous objective functions. The optimization goal of these techniques is to search for the vector in the search space that makes the objective function as large as possible, subject to a convergence criterion, by moving the design variables in the steepest direction of the objective function.

Gradient descent is one of the simplest, depending on the gradients optimization methods. By mean of gradient descent, the design variables are adjusted by the gradient magnitude, while the direction of the descent of the objective function is determined by the negative gradient. In the gradient descent methods, not only is the method used to go gradually toward the aim of the objective function, but even the actual direction that is being taken is the gradient one. In each iteration, the design variables are modified in order to achieve a lower objective function.

The conjugate gradient descent is therefore an optimization that aids gradient descent to converge by providing information from prior steps.
