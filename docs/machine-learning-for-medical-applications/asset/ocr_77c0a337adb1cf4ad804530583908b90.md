# Machine learning application in tissue engineering: scaffold design

# Abstract:

One of the major advancements in tissue engineering and regenerative medicine is the involvement of predictive modeling into the construct process of the scaffold. The present study reflects on the use of predictive modeling approaches to predict scaffold qualities and enhance scaffold production environment. A range of details were collected, which covered fabrication parameters and the respective properties of obtained scaffolds, including but not limited to printing rate, temperature, layer thickness, composite material type, porosity, pore size, mechanical properties, and degradation rate. The dataset was compiled and supervised machine learning models were developed using regression approaches in order to predict scaffold properties resulting from fabrication parameters. In order to compare the results, some of the most popular methods of regression were employed, including neural networks, decision trees, random forests, and linear regression. The input data was preprocessed by feature engineering to arrive at relevant features, and features selections method were employed to establish what characteristics were most valuable for the prediction of scaffold attributes. The performance of the machine learning models was measured using how well-fitted were the quantitative results based on coefficient of determination, mean absolute error (MAE), and mean squared error (MSE). The results indicated that scaffold characteristics are rather well-predicted using predictive modeling, and such models as random forest and neural network are the most effective ones. The ability to predict the scaffold characteristics for a new scaffold design and to optimize fabrication parameters were achieved by incorporating these machine learning models into scaffold production. This method is beneficial in generating scaffolds with specific properties that are required to be utilized in tissue engineering in a shorter time span and at a lower cost with a less complex process of scaffold fabrication.
