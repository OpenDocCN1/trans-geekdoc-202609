# 5.4 Strategies for removing noise from experimental data

The general approach to disentanglement of noise from experimental data lies in identifying its sources. Randomness in experiment results is caused by several sources, such as errors arising from measurements, and changes in the environment in experimental conditions. This knowledge makes it possible to apply specific measures that help to reduce the effects of these sources. Other interventions include noise arising from inaccuracies in the instruments, contamination of samples, and variability in the observation techniques. The proper identification and characterization of these sources are also important to allowing the application of suitable noise reduction methods.

Data filtering techniques are basic tools when it comes to eliminating noise and improving clarity in experimental data. One of the techniques is low-pass filters, which remove high levels of noise from the signal while retaining the original form. Others that can be used with a view to removing random fluctuations include moving average filters and Gaussian filters. Also, it is possible to employ technical realization based on the application of DSP methods, such as Fourier transforms, to filter out periodic noise. As a result, the means for filtering should be selected depending on the explored characteristics of noise and data.
