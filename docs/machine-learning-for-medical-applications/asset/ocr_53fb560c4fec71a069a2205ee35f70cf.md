# 4.5 Functionalized and surface-coated quantum dots

Surface-functionalized and surface-coated QDs are a breakthrough in nanotechnology particularly in areas of application where tagged nanotechnological reagents require improved stability, specificity and performance. This section introduces functionalization and surface coating of QDs, the techniques used, and their importance in their various applications.

In the present article, we will discuss the various surface functionalization techniques to improve its performance.

# Ligand exchange:

Ligand exchange is a popular method of providing the syntactical change of the QDs surface chemistry. This process entails the substitution of the initial stabilizing ligands by others that should improve solubility, biocompatibility, and the target substances. In this way, researchers can modify the surface ligands of the QDs to meet the requirement for its applications including selective cells in medical imaging.

# Covalent bonding:

Covalent chemistries are used to functionalize the QD surface because the functional groups are grafted onto the core. This approach enables the formation of a robust and irreversible linkage of biomolecules including peptides and antibodies. Specificity and stability of QDs in the biological environment is enhanced using covalent functionalization process that makes the QD relevant in imaging as well as diagnostics.

# Electrostatic and van der Waals interactions:

Coulombic and non-Coulombic forces are employed for chemical tailoring of QDs as chemisorbed charged into the QD surface. This method creates the possibility of connecting other biological molecules that can modify the action of the QD. This approach is less stable than the covalent bonding, thus it forms a weaker interaction and finally, loses functional groups easily.
