**Applications and importance:**
High quantum yield is especially critical in instances where low level of signal is needed to be detected and was needed. For example, in biological imaging, QDs with high quantum yields have a stronger signal that can increase the visibility of the targets to be imaged and hence higher accuracy. The high ability to use energy in fluorophores adds efficiency and sensitivity to detect specific and actual analytes in diagnostic assays.

# 5.3.2 Photoluminescence efficiency

**Definition and measurement:**
Another quantity closely related to photoluminescence efficiency is quantum yield, which states the ratio of emitted to absorbed photons out of all the incident light occurring at the QD. It is measured in percent, calculated on a per-QD basis, and quantifies the efficiency of how well a QD transfers the absorbed energy in the form of heat to emitted light. Large photoluminescence efficiency suggests the extent to which the energy absorbed by QDs can be used to produce luminescence.

**Comparison with quantum yield:**
Although the quantum yield was closer to the ratio of emitted and absorbed photons, the photoluminescence efficiency gives more general indication concerning the light conversion efficiency. While these two metrics are closely connected, they have their variables, which affect their outcome. Quantum yield is the common parameter used to determine the performance of QDs in a particular application, but overall photoluminescence efficacy is the best parameter that identifies the efficacy of the QDs in converting the absorbed photons into emitted fluorescence.

# 5.3.3 Nonradiative processes

**Types of nonradiative processes:**
Nonradiative processes are the processes that cause energy that has been absorbed to be dissipated without an accompanying output of fluorescence. These processes are internal conversion, vibrational relaxation, and surface trapping. Nonradiative losses greatly decrease the quantum yield and the photoluminescence efficiency of the QDs.

**Impact on performance:**
Reducing other unwanted effects is essential in order to achieve the high quantum yield and efficient photoluminescence of QDs. Methods for surface passivation include coating with a layer that is protective and functionalization with stabilizing ligands, so that energy loss through non-emissive mechanisms was minimized. Optimal passivation brings improvements to the general performance of QDs, achieving higher fluorescence intensity and better stability.
