# 5.3 Ultrasound imaging

A type of diagnostic imaging, sonography, also referred to as ultrasound imaging is a common method of diagnosing illnesses that involves the use of high frequency sound to produce images of internal organs. For this reason, the real-time imaging, and mobility and nonionizing radiation of this imaging technique are highly valued.

# 5.3.1 Principles of ultrasound imaging

The principles of transmission and reflection of sound waves form the fundamental basis of ultrasound imaging. High-frequency sound waves are emitted from the transducer head, travel through body tissues, and are reflected back at the interfaces where different tissue types meet. These reflected waves are then captured by the transducer, converted into electrical signals, and processed to generate accurate images of internal structures. # 5.3.2 Tables in clinical application

Ultrasound imaging is applied in different fields of medicine because of its flexibility and noninvasive technique. It is employed for probing soft tissues, the liver, kidney and heart as also for blood flow analysis and for identification of such diseases as tumor and cystic. In obstetrics, ultrasound is utilized in assessing fetal growth, in diagnosing pregnancy complications, and in planning antenatal management. Also, musculoskeletal ultrasound is used to analyze joint, muscle, and tendon consistency pictures that would be useful in sports medicine and orthopedics.

# 5.3.3 Advantages of ultrasound imaging

Real-time imaging allows for functional evaluation of organs and their movements, which was the advantage of using ultrasound imaging. The method was also of particular worth in terms of safety since it does not employ ionizing radiation, making this method most suitable for regular scans/v scans and on vulnerable groups such as children and sometimes pregnant women. In some cases, equipment used in ultrasound was cheaper and more transportable than other imaging modalities such as CT thus facilitating their use in clinical practice.
