It is found that there are several trade-offs to be made among these various parameters to achieve the optimum balance necessary to influence the diameter of the fibers, their porosity, and surface characteristics, all of which affect the performance of nanofibers in their biomedical applications. For instance, adjusting the electrospinning parameters produce fibers with smooth and nonbeaded surfaces of different degrees of porosity and surface irregularity, depending on the need in application, including drug delivery. Accordingly, the electrospinning process provides high versatility in generating polymeric nanofibers, with the desired characteristics, to satisfy certain functional demands.

For biomedical applications, precise control of fiber morphology is deemed essential because the size, porosity, and roughness of nanofibers are among the factors known to affect issues such as cell attachment, growth, and drug elution. For example, the nanofibers used for tissue engineering should have certain mechanical characterization and surface topologies that favor cell–scaffold and subsequent tissue interactions. Likewise, in drug delivery applications, the porosity and surface areas of the nanofibers should be manipulated in order to let the nanofibers deliver the drug to the site of action in the controlled release process for creating therapeutic efficacy.

# 2.3 Types of polymers used in electrospinning for biomedical applications

The suitability of polymers for electrospinning plays a crucial role in the outcome of nanofibers, especially in biomedical applications. The two categories of polymers widely used in electrospinning are synthetic and natural, each having distinctive features to satisfy medical needs. Important elements, including mechanical strength, biocompatibility, biodegradability, and the capacity to include bioactive substances, are all influenced by the polymer selection, and are critical for medical applications such as drug administration, tissue engineering, and wound healing. Specifically, the way that polymer chemistry and electrospinning interact affects fiber form and functioning greatly.

Because natural polymers have inherent biological activity and great biocompatibility, they are frequently employed in electrospinning. These biologically produced polymers are ideal for uses where intimate contact with live tissues is necessary. As collagen enhances cell adhesion and proliferation, it is widely used in fabricating tissue engineering scaffolds. This matrix is largely composed of collagen.
