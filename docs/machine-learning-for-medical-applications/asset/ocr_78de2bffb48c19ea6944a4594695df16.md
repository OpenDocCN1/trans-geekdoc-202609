Another challenge was variations in the shape of surrounding structures of any human organ. Lesion and tumor detection means the detection and segmentation of lesions. This task was essential for cancer staging and assessment of the response to the treatment in patients with cancer. The shape, size of the lesions, and blurred margins pose several difficulties. Vessel segmentation aims at delineation and extraction of the vessels in the medical imaging techniques employing CT and MRI angiography. Successful vessel segmentation was crucial for the identification of vascular pathology and planning interventions; nevertheless, in addition to being tortuous, thin blood vessels are relatively indistinct from other tissues and artifacts. Finally, functional region segmentation was used to segment regions of interest including active brain during cognitive tasks that demonstrate metabolic activity. This task was important in fMRI and PET; variability in functional activity and their relationship with anatomy makes segmentation difficult.

# 2.4.1 Challenges in segmentation

Several factors affect the medical image segmentation. Some of the problems highlighted include variability in imaging modalities. A variety of imaging techniques such as MRI, computerized axial tomography, and ultrasound generate images with dissimilar resolutions, contrast levels, and distortions. Such intra-observer variability has highlighted the need to adopt modality-specific previously used segmentation techniques to obtain accurate segmentation across different imaging modalities. Other factors that are also somewhat challenging include image quality and the artifacts, which occur in the acquired images above. There are many factors that have a negative impact on the segmentation process, which include: noise, blurring, and artifacts that are common in medical images crowding the boundaries. Such imperfection requires that the techniques to be employed should also be strong enough to work with such deficits and give accurate results. There was patients’ variability: each patient was unique in some ways such as organ size and organ shape, which means that the segmentation could also be affected. These variations then must be reflected in the models to ensure that the targets fit all categories of patients. Another drawback was in the class imbalance, unique to in rare lesions. This was because, on average, prejudiced models that do well on the most often seen classes but badly on the least seen classes are developed due to the fact that few, if any, annotated examples of such classes are available for training of the model. And last, computational complexity was an issue because advanced segmentation methods, especially using DL algorithms, require a long time and much computation. Current technological advances in medical imaging data especially in large-scale data necessitate efficient algorithms and resource management strategies.
