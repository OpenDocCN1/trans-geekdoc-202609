
Patch-based approaches gained popularity initially due to their straightforward implementation and computational feasibility, especially when hardware resources were limited.

### 2.5.1 Challenges in patch-based segmentation

Due to their simplicity, patch-based CNNs face several challenges. One significant limitation is the lack of context captured by individual patches. Since patches are processed independently, the model struggles to maintain coherence across neighboring regions, leading to inconsistencies in the segmentation results. The fixed-size patches can lead to issues when encountering objects that span multiple patches. This problem necessitates careful handling of patch boundaries and often requires post-processing steps to merge the outputs from adjacent patches seamlessly.

### 2.5.2 Advantages of patch-based approaches

Patch-based CNNs were used initially because they have a lot of advantages. A significant benefit was a relative requirement of lesser computation as compared to the full-image methods. A characteristic of patch-based CNNs was that they can save on memory and computational power because they operate from square one at smaller fields than the complete image; this makes such networks ideal for systems with restricted memory and power. In addition, the patch-based approach allows to simultaneously apply data augmentation techniques such as flipping and cropping that can enhance the model generalization and its ability to be less sensitive to data variations. Patch-based CNNs were reasonable to use for the first attempts at picture segmentation because of these characteristics.

### 2.5.3 Comparisons with fully convolutional networks

Compared to FCNs, patch-based CNNs exhibit both strengths and weaknesses. FCNs, by processing the entire image in one pass, can leverage global context and produce more coherent segmentation maps. In contrast, patch-based approaches struggle with integrating information across the entire image, potentially leading to less accurate segmentations. Patch-based CNNs are often simpler to implement and can be effective for specific applications where local context was sufficient. The comparison highlights the trade-offs between processing efficiency and segmentation quality, paving the way for hybrid approaches that combine elements of both methods.

### 2.5.4 Applications and developments

Patch-based CNNs have found applications in various domains, including medical imaging, where they are used for tasks such as tumor detection and organ segmentation. In these contexts, the patch-based approach enables the analysis of small regions, which is helpful, especially when high point annotations are needed. The advancements in recent years have attempted to solve some of the problems associated with the use of patch-based approaches, including overlapping patches and multiscale analysis. These innovations are intended to enhance the stability of segmentation results and provide a more accurate reflection of the objective data, while still maintaining the computational advantages of the patch-based approaches.

## 3 U-Net and variants for biomedical image segmentation

### 3.1 Introduction to U-Net architecture

A specific deep learning architecture called U-Net was created with biomedical picture segmentation in mind. U-Net was created to solve the difficulties of precisely segmenting medical pictures, and because of its capacity to generate segmentation maps that are correct even with little annotated data, it has grown to be a mainstay in the area. The architecture was distinguished by its unique U-shaped form, which comprises an expanding path (decoder) and a contracting path (encoder), as first shown by Ronneberger et al. in 2015. Because of its nature, the model was able to accurately segment complex structures in medical imaging by capturing both contextual and spatial information.

### 3.1.1 Contracting path: encoder

The encoder, which is also referred to as the contracting path of U-Net, is in charge of gathering the context and removing features from the input picture. It is made up of max-pooling procedures after a sequence of convolutional layers. The network can learn hierarchical representations of the picture data, thanks to these convolutional layers, which gradually decrease the image’s spatial dimensions, while deepening the feature maps. The encoder’s job is to extract the contextual data and the high-level characteristics needed to distinguish different structures in the picture.

### 3.1.2 Expansive path: decoder

The high-level feature maps that the encoder generates are used by the expansive path, also known as the decoder, to rebuild the picture. It is composed of convolutional layers that serve to improve the spatial resolution of the feature maps, after upsampling procedures. By concatenating feature maps from the corresponding levels of the contracted route, skip connections let the decoder to integrate information from the encoder. In order to maintain fine-grained details and guarantee that the segmentation output maintains the spatial accuracy of the source picture, these skip connections are necessary.

### 3.1.3 Skip connections and their importance

The usage of skip connections, which connect the encoder and decoder routes, is a distinguishing characteristic of U-Net. These linkages assist in retaining spatial information that could otherwise be lost during the downsampling process by facilitating the movement of high-resolution characteristics from the contracting channel to the expanding path. Concatenating the feature maps from the encoder and decoder allows the U-Net to generate more accurate and detailed segmentation maps. This makes it especially useful for jobs that need high-resolution outputs, such as defining tiny structures.

### 3.1.4 Applications and impact

The paper does not discuss any limitations. U-Net has shown outstanding performance in a wide spectrum of biomedical imaging applications, including organ, tumor, and other structures segmentation from MRI, CT, microscopy, and many other images. Some common types of architecture have been developed that have a great deal of flexibility and can be elaborated, depending on their particular circumstances. The impact of U-Net extends beyond medical imaging, influencing the development of segmentation models in other domains and setting a precedent for the design of encoder-decoder architectures in deep learning.

### 3.2 Extensions and improvements of U-Net

U-Net, originally developed for biomedical image segmentation, has served as the foundation for numerous extensions and enhancements aimed at improving its performance and adaptability. Its encoder-decoder structure, characterized by symmetric skip connections, enables effective retention of spatial information while achieving high segmentation precision. Researchers have since proposed improvements to the original U-Net to address specific challenges such as limited training data, computational efficiency, and variability in imaging modalities. These enhancements often focus on boosting segmentation accuracy, reducing computational overhead, and increasing robustness across diverse clinical contexts.

### 3.2.1 U-Net++: nested U-Net architecture

One significant advancement was U-Net++, which introduces nested skip pathways to improve feature propagation and segmentation accuracy. U-Net++ builds on the original U-Net by adding additional convolutional blocks within the skip pathways, creating a more refined feature extraction process. These nested skip pathways help capture more detailed contextual information and enable better feature reusability. The introduction of deep supervision and improved feature fusion techniques in U-Net++ results in enhanced performance, particularly in complex biomedical imaging tasks where fine details are crucial.
