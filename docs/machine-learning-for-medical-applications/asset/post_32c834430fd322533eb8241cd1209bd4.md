## 6.3 Case study: predictive modeling for scaffold degradation kinetics

Knowledge of degradation rates for the scaffolds was useful in tissue engineering to control the delivery of the bioactive molecules encapsulated in the scaffolds, at the interconnection of the scaffolds with the tissue, and for mechanical strength during tissue repair. Scaffold degradation kinetics can be predicted effectively using mathematical models and based on the parameters of scaffold composition, morphology, and environmental conditions.

Researchers have recently compiled a dataset comprising the information on scaffold degradation kinetics. The features for the dataset were scaffold composition, which included polymer type, cross-linking density, and scaffold morphology, including pore size, surface area, and degradation environment, which includes the pH, temperature, and degradation time. The degradation behavior, which includes degradation characteristics of the scaffolds and the degradation mechanism, was determined through experimental methods such as weight loss analysis, scanning electron microscopy, and spectroscopic analysis.

In the analysis of the given dataset, the standard approach of regression models as a part of supervised machine learning was employed to construct the model of degradation kinetics of the scaffold. To determine the most suitable model for the prediction of scaffolds’ degradation kinetics, various methods of regression: support vector, polynomial, and linear regression were employed.

The input data was preprocessed with the help of feature engineering paradigms so as to find useful features that captured the associations of the scaffold features with the degradation rates. To reduce the sparsity in the input space that results from a large number of predictor genes and to determine the most critical characteristics for modeling scaffold degradation kinetics, various feature selection methods such as correlation analysis and recursive feature elimination were performed.

Each of the machine learning models was also evaluated by the coefficient of determination (R-squared), MSE, and MAE. Their generalization abilities were checked, the models were tested against overfitting through techniques such as LOOCV and k-fold validation. The results of the study confirmed that the use of machine learning model could estimate scaffold degradation kinetics with reasonable accuracy. MARS analysis identified support vector regression as the most appropriate model for the assessment of scaffold degradation kinetics, when compared to other regression models to test the generality of both the models and to ensure that none was prone to overfitting.

The results of this study indicated that the interactions of scaffold cells could be marginally approximated by the machine learning model. The results for both training and prediction suggested that random forest and support vector machine algorithms were the most effective models to predict scaffold–cell interactions.

This way, researchers have pointed out that they had been able to increase certain cell responses by acting on the scaffolds features, as learned by the machine learning models for new scaffold predictability on cells. In the context of tissue engineering applications, this method complemented approaches that enabled the creation of scaffolds with specific attributes for promoting the required cell behavior and tissue development.

## 6.5 Case study: Integrating predictive modeling into scaffold fabrication processes

The incorporation of the newly developed predictive modeling componentry into fabricating scaffolds is a milestone in the tissue engineering and regenerative medicine. Several predictive modeling approaches present an effective platform to improve scaffold fabrication and to estimate scaffold properties in order to design scaffolds with a specific performance profile.

In a study done recently by researchers, scaffold fabrication was combined with predictive modeling to determine the best fabrication parameters and scaffold properties. A body of data regarding the fabrication parameters of scaffold, including printing rate, temperature, layer thickness, and type of material used, was collected. Additional characteristics of scaffold included are porosity of the scaffold, pore size, mechanical properties of the scaffold, and degradation rate.

Supervised machine learning models that belong to the regression family were developed on the dataset in a bid to predict scaffold features from fabrication parameters. Before deciding which method of regression would be suitable for prediction of scaffold features, a comparison of several models, including neural networks, decision trees, random forest, and linear regression, was made.

The input data was preprocessed by applying feature engineering in an attempt to assess and derive suitable features that describe the inherent relationships between scaffold qualities and the manufacturing parameters. To reduce the input space dimensionality as well as determine the optimal features for predicting scaffold qualities, both principal component analysis and recursive feature removal were used.

The machine learning model performances were evaluated based on MSE, MAE, and coefficient of determination (R-squared). The ability of the models to generalize and the aspect of ensuring that the models were not overfitting were measured by the application of cross-validation such as the LOOCV and k-fold cross-validation.

The outcomes of the study showed that predictive modeling is capable of predicting scaffold properties from fabrication inputs with reasonable precision. In this work, a random forest and neural network algorithms proved to be the most appropriate approach for predicting scaffold properties to offer higher accuracy and generalization. To do this, this approach made it possible to minimize scaffold fabrication costs and time, as well as advance the creation of scaffolds with suitable properties for tissue engineering applications.

## 7 Conclusion

Tissue engineering and regenerative medicine could benefit immensely from the efficient incorporation of the predictive modeling practice in scaffold fabrication processes “The feasibility study revealed the ability of this method to predict scaffold characteristics with reasonable accuracy and optimize scaffold fabrication conditions using machine learning algorithms.” In fact, through the course of this research, it was shown how accurately various regression techniques, such as random forest and neural network, predict scaffold qualities, based on the manufacturing variables. The identified valuable features represent the inherent relations between scaffold characteristics and manufacturing conditions through feature engineering and feature selection. The integration of the use of this predictive modeling for the various scaffold manufacturing processes was seen to have highly positive implications for the faster development of scaffolds that are specifically intended for certain tissue engineering applications. The former becomes possible due to the use of predictive modeling that allows researchers to iteratively create new scaffolds, possessing the required characteristics, as well as to save money on expensive materials and time on experiments. This method helps in easily constructing low numbers of scaffolds and relates to the idea of using a number of scaffold designs in different tissue engineering applications. Further research is necessary in the future to enhance the generality of the identified machine learning models as well as to expand their recognitive abilities to predict regarding scaffold construction. Improving the reliability of scaffold construction by prediction modeling techniques implies that more research effort is needed in feature selection, model optimization, and assessments. Optimization algorithms for designing relevant fabrication processes as well as multiscale modeling methods could further improve scaffold design and fabrication methods. Scaffold design and fabrication methods can be enhanced by using machine learning by the researchers who work on the project and in the long run, the resulting scaffold that is produced would have better qualities for tissue regeneration and repair.
