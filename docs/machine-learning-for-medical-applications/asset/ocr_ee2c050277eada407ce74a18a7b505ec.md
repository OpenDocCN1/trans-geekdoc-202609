# 2.5 Evolution of standards and protocols

Standard and protocol development in MIP have played a crucial role in the advancement of this field and in achieving synergy across different imaging systems. The increased use of standards has ensured standardization of data transfer, which, together with increased image quality and improved flow of clinical processes, is critical in diagnosis and management.

# 2.5.1 Beginning of DICOM standard

A major milestone in the evolution of medical imaging occurred in 1985, when the American College of Radiology (ACR) and the National Electrical Manufacturers Association (NEMA) introduced the DICOM standards. DICOM provided a unified framework for the digital archiving and transmission of medical images, ensuring compatibility across various imaging equipment and healthcare networks. With imaging data now commonly stored in DICOM format, integration into Electronic Health Records (EHRs) and Picture Archiving and Communication Systems (PACS) has improved the efficiency and consistency of medical imaging workflows.

# 2.5.2 The development of image quality assurance protocols

With the improvement of the medical imaging technologies, there were growing needs for developing sound image quality assurance. The aim of these guidelines was to ensure that imaging equipment consistently delivers excellent image quality, which is mandatory to aid diagnosis and treatment planning in surgeries performed at the hospital. Numerous guidelines and measures for picture quality have already been set and developed by various organizations such as RSNA and AAPM. Some of these are protocols for calibration and testing of imaging equipment's performance, as well as protocols for imaging equipment maintenance. These quality assurance measures that have been put into practice have helped make medical imaging more accurate and credible to enhance diagnostic processes.
