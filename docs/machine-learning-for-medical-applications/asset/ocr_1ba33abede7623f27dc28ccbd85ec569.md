# 4.4 Recurrent neural networks for sequential anomaly detection in visual data

The detection of anomalies in sequential pictorial information such as a series of images is not easy due to the temporal relations and intricate patterns that are imbedded in the sequential data. Data can then be sequential in which its structure changes over time indicating that the frames are in a developmental state. RNNs have recently emerged as leading solutions for working on sequential data as they can learn temporal relations and other contextual features. Because sequence information over time can be learned, such as learning the normal sequence of events, RNNs can identify events that are out of the ordinary, so together with LSTM it is excellent for activities like video surveillance, activity recognition, and anomaly detection in time-series images.

RNNs are supposed to deal with sequential inputs: take an additional hidden state, which contains the information about previous inputs and affects the current input prediction. This ability to pass on temporal information was the reason that RNNs have the capacity to comprehend long term relationships; something that cannot be done by feedforward NNs. In the case of anomaly detection, RNNs are used to train normal sequences in order to acquire normal temporal patterns of the data. With regards to normal data, the program learns the temporal pattern and whenever it is presented with an anomalous input, the program highlights the difference from the learnt pattern. The utility of the model is based on its ability to assess not only the frames themselves, but the relations between them in terms of temporal dynamics.

Gated recurrent units (GRUs) and long short-term memory (LSTM) networks’ outputs are two RNN types that were created to solve some of the issues with conventional RNNs, such as the vanishing gradient issue seen in Figure 9. LSTMs and GRUs employ additional control gates to enable the networks to capture long dependency information and discourage an otherwise known forget-algorithm in processing long sequence data. These architectures are especially useful in identifying abnormalities in the visual data if the temporal organization of the sequences is important. For instance, in video surveillance, LSTMs can learn the sequences of movements in the object over time and if something is irregular, including a motion in a way that is not normal, LSTMs detect them as abnormal.

The first benefit of utilizing RNNs in perception-based sequential anomaly detection is that temporal reoccurring characteristics can be learned among two consecutive frames in visual data. This temporal context becomes especially important in such cases as the detection of a subject’s odd behavior in security camera footage of equipment in some manufacturing lines. Here, what is considered normal is clear; in other words, normal means that there is an established sequence, and anything that altered the sequence is abnormal. For example, in video-based anomaly detection, a human walking pattern in specific direction can be learnt by an RNN as a sequence.
