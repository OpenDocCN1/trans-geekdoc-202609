The other pattern was the semisupervised anomaly detection that uses a graph where nodes are the data points with the edges forming the connections in between. Semisupervised graph-based anomaly detection methods centers on the labeled nodes to spread the labels through the graph, so that strong similarity between a labeled node and an unlabeled node can be used in order to label the unlabeled node as an anomalous node. These methods are especially beneficial in identifying outliers in large intricate systems and data spaces of high dimensions because relationships between the data points provide insights when the single attributes do not.

Similarly, autoencoder and variational autoencoder (VAE) are being implemented in semisupervised anomaly detections. These models that are trained to extract compressed representations of the input data can be trained with a limited set of labeled data that are normalized. A trained autoencoder reconstructs the input data afterward, and the difference in the two data sets was considered as a measure of anomaly. Due to the fact that these models are normally trained on normal data, high reconstruction errors imply anomalous data. She also indicated that the model can be further fine-tuned with other unlabeled data in order to enhance the model's exquisite ability to identify further deeper and concealed anomalies.

An application of ensemble learning to the semisupervised learning approaches also enhances detection since multiple models are trained on different splits of the labeled and unlabeled datasets and the joint result was used in detecting malicious patterns. For instance, bagging and boosting strategies are beneficial for semisupervised anomaly detection methods, since due to the model decentralization, the influence of overfitting is reduced. Ensemble methods can combine various opinions on what should be considered as normal behavior and then use these opinions to assess deviant behaviors more skillfully.

Despite the seemingly high efficiency of the approaches of semisupervised learning, the latter is not without its drawbacks. One of the challenges was to prevent the pseudo-labeled data from introducing noise that arise from wrong labeling that definitely affects the performance of the model in terms of detection. Also, the choice of model of semisupervised learning depends on the nature of the data set and the particular problem of anomaly detection. Nevertheless, the use of both labeled and unlabeled data in a semisupervised framework offers a practical solution to the critical issue of limited labeled data that was commonplace in anomaly detection applications.

Semisupervised learning in the context of anomaly detection also shows promise and the current investigation focuses on increasing the stability and effectiveness of the models. Further evolutions concern the enhancement of the algorithms themselves used for pseudo-labeling, the domain adaptation of the learning process with additional domain knowledge, and the generalization of the concept of semisupervised learning to more high-powered, multidimensional features.
