# 4.3 AI’s impact on sensitivity

Diagnostic sensitivity means the extent to which the test can detect patients with the disease. It is very important for early diagnosis and control of the disease in the facility. Low false negative rates are important to ensure that diseases are detected and treated early by reducing the extent of over sensitivity. There are significant improvements of diagnostic sensitivity through the application of AI, commenting most of the drawbacks observed in the traditional diagnostic processes.

This was one area that has directly benefited from AI, as the sensitivity of several diagnostic tests can be analyzed with the application of this technology. For instance, DL algorithms have revolutionized medical imaging by identifying small abnormalities that may not be visible to the human eye. These algorithms outperform previous methods in analyzing high-quality images and detecting early signs of illness. Advanced diagnostic techniques have improved the ability to detect subtle changes in imaging data, thereby increasing the sensitivity of diagnostic tests. This enhanced sensitivity allows for earlier interventions, significantly improving patient outcomes.

AI facilitates greater sensitivity in molecular diagnostics and genomics by enhancing the analysis of complex molecular data. As illustrated in Figure 7, AI-driven pattern recognition algorithms can detect subtle genetic variations, identifying disease-associated genes with higher precision than traditional methods. These models are particularly adept at uncovering rare mutations and low-frequency biomarkers that standard tests often overlook. By improving the sensitivity of genetic screenings, AI enables the early identification of individuals at risk for specific genetic disorders, allowing for timely, personalized medical interventions that can significantly improve patient outcomes.

AI is crucial for increasing the sensitivity by combining disparate data inputs. AI analyzes data on changes in vital signs and other signs received from wearables, patient history, and electronic medical records, more broadly, than a human can do. It allows the identification of disease indicators that are not obvious in data extracted from a single source. Consequently, the diagnostic devices based on AI can operate with higher sensitivity due to better reception of other clinical data and orientation to the likelihood of a sickness.
