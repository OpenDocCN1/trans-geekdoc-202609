
### 3.5.2 Level set methods

Before proceeding any further, let us extend the active contours approach where contours are represented as implicit surfaces by means of level set function. It includes a level set function whereby it transforms from one state to another in capturing the edges of the objects. One of the strengths of level set methods is the interaction with topology changes such as contour merging and splitting, which is important for sharp and dynamic geometry. This flexibility is especially advantageous in medical imaging, for structures are vastly different from one another.

### 3.5.3 Deformable models

Other model-based techniques are deformable models, incorporating a priori beliefs about the shape and appearance of objects to drive the segmentation process. These models deform and fit the observed data by using statistical shape models also known as templates. This deformation was achieved according to the objective function capable of maintaining consistency of the models with the image features. As has already been seen, deformable models are quite useful when the structure in the images has similar shape in different people, such as organs, but needs fine-tuning for appearance and small shape differences.

### 3.5.4 Statistical shape models

Statistical shape models extract information from a training set of examples of the shape we are modeling. These models represent object shapes as distortions from the average shape that is embedded in a lower dimensional shape space. Using the training data, statistical shape models identify the changes that occur in shape onto which new images are mapped for segmentation. It is especially advantageous in linear structures where the geometry is seen to vary little from the main structure, such as with bones, and this approach could potentially improve segmentation results if prior information about within-class shape variation is available.

### 3.5.5 Template matching

Template matching means applying templates of certain anatomic structures and then using them to match and segment images. Usually, it expands the image data to the predetermined templates and utilizes the relevant similarity assessment to make a decision on the selection. Template matching in general can employ the correlation approach, a feature-based approach. Although this method proves efficient in certain registries, its application is rather weak with large templates and when the object has a huge variation in appearance.

These model-based methods give structured and principled solutions to segmentation of medical images by using mathematics and statistics frameworks. Every method of image segmentation has its advantages and disadvantages and the efficiency of each is dependent on the characteristics of the images and objects to be segmented.

Conventional approaches perform poorly due to noise and artifacts on the medical images. The sensitivity learnt in this layer causes problems with segmentation especially in low-quality images with high level of noise.

Few of the classical approaches to segmentation can address the issue of complex structures that have highly variable resemblance to other tissues. ### 4.2.3 Limitations of early machine learning models

As for the drawbacks of the initial methods of image segmentation using machine learning approaches, they include: the complexity of the features used and the necessity for a large amount of preprocessing. Many of these models are specialized in a certain type of image and cannot generalize well to other datasets or, in fact, other modalities. Also, building detailed handcrafted features put further limitations on the models to capture complex patterns and distribution shifts in the data.

### 4.2.4 Migration of problems to deep learning paradigms

The segmentation of images has improved incredibly with the adoption of DL to solve this case of classical machine learning. As DL models, CNNs obtained what we could automatically learn as hierarchical features of unprocessed picture data without the necessity of feature engineering. They also permit the direct extraction of fine-grained patterns and spatial organization from the underlying data, which leads to a better segmentation.

### 4.2.5 Impact and advancements

The introduction of U-Net and FCNs enhanced the segmentation much higher than the previous methods in different medical imaging techniques. These models benefit from end-to-end training, which results in high accuracy and overcoming many of the challenges associated with previous machine learning models. Large scale datasets and diverse training have improved the model's versatility and usability in a clinical environment.

The shift to using DL-based approaches in medical image segmentation in general reveals a growing trend of using high-end computational tools for tackling other challenges in medical imaging. Steady advancements in DL necessarily entail further enhancements in segmentation capability, attribute speed, and versatility as it extends innovation to healthcare and the medical field.
