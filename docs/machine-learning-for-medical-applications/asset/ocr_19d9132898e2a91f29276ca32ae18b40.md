The shell serves multiple roles in enhancing quantum dot (QD) performance. As illustrated in Figure 6, it passivates surface defects, thereby suppressing nonradiative recombination of charge carriers, which in turn improves the photoluminescence efficiency. The shell increases the quantum yield and stability of the QD by providing a protective barrier against environmental degradation. Systematic control over the shell’s composition and thickness is crucial, as these parameters directly impact the QD’s emission wavelength, optical brightness, and long-term stability making them critical design considerations for bioimaging and optoelectronic applications.

# 4.1.2 Drawbacks of nano-core-shell structures

**Enhanced photostability:** The outer shell helps shield the core QDs from various events that lead to cohesion destruction by external processes such as oxidation and degradation, and as a result enhances the photostable nature and stability of the dots. This is especially true in cases where a given application needs to take long images in hostile environments.

**Increased quantum yield:** The shell layer is proved to passivate the surface defects and suppress nonradiative recombination, and thus improve the quantum yield of the QDs. This results in enhanced and more efficient fluorescent light, which is needed for resolution and detection-based applications.

**Tunable optical properties:** The core shell structures can also be designed to some extremely high precise control of the optical characteristics of the material such as emission and wavelength intensity. Researchers have thus been able to find ways of controlling the emission characteristics of QDs with the core and shell materials, as well as the thickness of the shell.
