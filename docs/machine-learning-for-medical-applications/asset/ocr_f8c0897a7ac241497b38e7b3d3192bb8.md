# 3.2.4 Region splitting and merging

Region splitting and merging is a strategy that begins with the whole image as a single region splitting into smaller regions based on a homogeneity criterion. After the splitting at first time, the algorithm fuses neighbors if two areas meet the homogeneity criteria. This approach lies right in the middle between the extreme of very fine subdivisions of the region and the cohesiveness of each segment. The effectiveness of the method depends on the split-and-merge criteria that are applied and has to be sensitive to splitting and merging too much.

# 3.2.5 Active contours (snakes)

Snake contours are a technique where an energy minimization curve is used to outline the object boundaries. The algorithm actively updates a contour using energy functions that include internal energy attributed to continuity of the contour, and external energy associated with gradients of an image. It continuously adjusts the contour according to the object’s shape and size, which is an advantage to the contour because segmentation can be complex in some images. Active contours are particularly suitable for the segmentation of objects with clear boundaries, yet need initial placement of the contour and the tuning of the parameters so as to achieve the best outcome.

These region-based methods are the basis of many of the more conventional image segmentation techniques and provide useful tools with which images can be partitioned on the basis of spatial and intensity properties. Both methods have some advantages and disadvantages and the efficiency of their application varies according to the features of the images and the intended application.
