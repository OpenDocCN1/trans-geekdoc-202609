# 6.4.3 Enhancing reproducibility and consistency

As opposed to manual segmentation techniques automated segmentation is guaranteed to give consistent results across images due to use of specific algorithms and parameters. This makes sure that segmentation outcomes at different regions for various patients and different scanning sessions can be made relatively to allow improved comparisons to be made. Higher reproducibility is useful in follow-up cases where accurate segmentation of the subsequent scans was critical in evaluating the change in dynamics of the disease and effectiveness of the therapy.

A critically important feature of the method aims at minimizing the subjectivity of the interpretation.

Sources of variability in manual segmentation can be attributed to differences in identification of anatomical borders and pathological signatures. This speculation is not founded on the persons’ subjectivity since these automatic methods restrict the necessity of determining boundaries and are trained to identify and demarcate structures according to learned characteristics. This objectivity minimizes variability in segmentation results and ensured that segmentation was less prone to biases from the different human factors and background of practitioners.

# 6.4.4 Supporting the quantitative computation

Quantitative characteristics include volume measurement, lesion area, and other shape features and it is due to the automated segmentation. These metrics are used in measuring and comparing of pathological features in an unbiased manner. In view of these findings, automated segmentation provides accurate quantity data for decision-making and facilitates follow-up and the assessment of treatment outcome and research.

# 6.5 Potential for personalized medicine

The concept of personalized medicine is to direct treatment by organ-specific characteristics of the individual patient. In this case, it is critically important to define segmentation of medical images correctly. It is helpful to obtain accurate and detailed information of the picture segmentation for the later distinction of a patient's anatomy and pathology to guide customized therapy.
