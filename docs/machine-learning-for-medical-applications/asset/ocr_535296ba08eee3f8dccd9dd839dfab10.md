Transparent and efficient reporting, as well as documentation, are essential elements for the verification of scaffold research. The following should be part of the standardized protocols: the way experimental conditions are recorded, the ways data were collected, and how results were recorded. Documentation should include all features of the research, including scantling construction information as well as analysis methodologies. Once again, this means that standards contribute to the overall reliability of research and allow for a more comparative analysis across studies than can be collected otherwise.

# 2.2 Best practices for documenting scaffold properties and environmental conditions

Adopting standardized documentation of scaffold properties and environmental conditions was a basic requirement in order to obtain reproducible and reliable outcomes. Documentation was initially descriptive and quantitative on the characterization of scaffold materials in terms of tensile strength, elasticity, and porosity. Some of these measurements required the use of uniform techniques and equipment for purposes of comparability across different investigations. For example, care was taken to report scaffold properties in units that are familiar to the construction industry through conformance to standards set by ASTM International.

Aside from material properties, the fabrication process of the three-dimensional scaffolds in this study had to be recounted. This includes recording the specific protocols used, such as solvent systems, temperature conditions, and processing times, as these factors can significantly influence scaffold characteristics. Detailed records of these parameters not only aid in the reproducibility of experimental setups but also provide valuable insights into how variations in fabrication techniques impact scaffold performance.

Environmental conditions during experiments and long-term studies must be meticulously documented, as they play a critical role in the performance and stability of biodegradable nanofiber scaffolds. Key factors such as temperature, humidity, and pH can significantly influence the degradation rate and structural integrity of the scaffold materials. To ensure experimental reliability, calibrated sensors should be employed, and environmental parameters should be recorded at regular intervals. Comprehensive documentation should include not only the intended set points but also any deviations from these conditions, along with corrective actions taken.
