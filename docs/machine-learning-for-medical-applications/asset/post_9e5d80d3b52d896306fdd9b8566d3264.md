This approach is superior to the single-modal systems, where data from a single source is analyzed, as such systems do not collect enough information spectrum necessary to produce accurate anomalies [3–10]. Combined presentation of multimodal data facilitates detection of marginal patterns that could not be identified when analyzing each data set separately, thus enhancing the possibility of accurate diagnosis and optimization of workflow. Especially in areas where multiple couplings and interdependences are present, as for instance industrial production, diagnostics, and real-time monitoring, this integrated approach proves to be highly beneficial.

Many approaches have been created to make it easier to integrate and analyze multimodal data in order to find anomalies [11]. Two important methods are decision-level fusion, which integrates the outputs of distinct models trained on several modalities, and feature-level fusion, which combines data features from various modalities before classification [12]. Model-level fusion further extends these methods by combining multiple machine learning models that individually process different modalities [13]. Newer additions have also included deep learning, and graph-based methods that improve the feature of modeling high order relations and interactions in multimodal data. While graph-based approach highlights data correlation using complex graph structures, CNN and RNN models are efficient in feature extraction and fusion of multiple high-dimensional data.

The use of multimodal anomaly detection is across several important areas, while each gains from the capacity to fuse and analyze different types of information [14]. For example, in industrial manufacture, integration of data obtained through visual inspection, in combination with sensors, fare greatly in detecting defects and enhancing manufacturing processes [15]. There are numerous digital cases that show how information obtained from high-definition cameras and machine tools can help decrease the defect rates and improve the product quality across the automotive segment. In the same manner, combining data from the MRI and the CT scans with patient health records in the medical field has resulted in correct diagnoses and good treatment plans. Real-time monitoring systems adopted in smart cities and self-driving cars also owe their ability to recognize anomalous events across multiple modalities in order to improve awareness and increase safety [16].

Multimodal data integration has benefits, but in order to reach its full potential, a number of issues need to be resolved [17]. Given that the quantity and complexity of data rise with the number of modalities and data sources, scalability is a major concern [18]. Managing large-scale data integration requires robust storage solutions and efficient computational resources. Real-time processing is another challenge, particularly in dynamic environments where data needs to be processed and analyzed instantaneously [19]. Additionally, interoperability and standardization of data formats are crucial for ensuring seamless integration across different modalities [20]. Addressing these challenges involves developing advanced algorithms and technologies that can handle the complexities of multimodal data while maintaining performance and accuracy [21].

Looking forward, several promising directions and innovations are anticipated to advance multimodal anomaly detection [22]. Addressing the present issues depend on the creation of scalable computational frameworks and more effective data fusion methods [23]. Real-time data processing capabilities are anticipated to be improved by developments in distributed systems and edge computing [24]. New prospects for enhancing the efficiency and accuracy of anomaly detection arise from the integration of future technologies like artificial intelligence (AI) and Internet of things (IoT) sensors [25]. Research into novel fusion techniques and the application of multimodal anomaly detection in new domains continue to drive progress and expand the potential applications of this technology. As these advancements unfold, multimodal data integration is poised to become an increasingly powerful tool in the quest for more accurate and actionable insights.

## 2 Fundamentals of multimodal data integration

### 2.1 Introduction to multimodal data

The use of multimodal data has become a crucial development in anomaly detection, greatly improving the precision and resilience of detection systems. Multimodal data integration includes merging information from diverse data sources to get a more thorough knowledge of the data environment. This method overcomes the drawbacks of relying on just one modality, by utilizing the advantages of several data kinds, including text, audio, pictures, and sensor measurements. By synthesizing diverse data sources, multimodal systems can capture a richer set of features and patterns, which are crucial for identifying anomalies that missed when relying on a single data type.

The integration of multimodal data presents both promising opportunities and complex challenges, including unforeseen privacy concerns. As illustrated in Figure 1, the combination of complementary information from various modalities such as visual data from cameras and textual or numerical data from sensors enables more granular and accurate analysis of the phenomena under study. For example, correlating image-based observations with sensor readings can significantly enhance the understanding of manufacturing processes and increase the likelihood of defect detection. Effectively handling multimodal data introduces technical challenges such as data synchronization, alignment, and fusion. A critical consideration is ensuring that the integration process preserves the unique value of each modality while producing a cohesive and interpretable output, which serves as a key criterion for successful multimodal system design.
