# 4.3 Dot-in-rod and dot-in-matrix structures

Dot-in-rod (QD) structures and other related structures are essential in modifying the light-emitting and electric features of QDs for purposes of application. Of these structures, the dot-in-rod and dot-in-matrix structures have different uses and applications because of differences in their structure.

# 4.3.1 Dot-in-rod structures

Dot-in-rod QDs refer to a structure where a QD is coated within a semiconductor rod. This structure incorporates elements from the solid core and the rod to improve performance in a range of applications.

Enhanced optical properties: Due to the presence of the rod-shaped matrix around the core QD it is possible to have different absorption and emission characteristics. The nature of the rod structure can improve the emission intensity and photostability of the core QD.

Anisotropic emission: The dot-in-rod structure can demonstrate anisotropic emission characteristics, that is, the direction of the emission changes according to the position of the rod. For applications where directionality of emitted light detection is desired, this can be an advantage.

Improved quantum efficiency: The rod matrix can also work as a confinement layer, preventing nonradiative recombination processes, which will enhance the quantum efficiency of the core QD doped within the matrix.

Complex synthesis: Synthesis of dot-in-rod structures requires extensive construction processes that are difficult to regulate with high accuracy. This makes the detail of its composition somewhat more complex and leads to problems with obtaining the necessary homogeneity and repeatability in mass production.

Size and shape control: It is often difficult to obtain a fine control over size and aspect ratio of the rod matrix and the core QD, which affects the optical properties.

# 4.3.2 Dot-in-matrix structures

Dot-in-matrix QDs are constructed by placing a QD in the middle, encased in a three-dimensional matrix of a different semiconductor material. This structure gives extra flexibility in tuning the optical and electronic features of the quantum points.
