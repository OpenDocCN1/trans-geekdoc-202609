Since collagen and alginate belong to hydrogels, which are close to the extracellular matrices that promote cell attachment and division, they are frequently employed. Recent advancements in material science continuously enhance the properties and biocompatibility of bioinks.

Applications in tissue engineering: bioprinting in tissue engineering: The principal usage of the science was the application for the formation of tissues for research, experimenting with medications, and transplantation. Tissue printing should be applied for the identification of disease sources, testing of new drugs, and development of patient-specific therapy. It has been possible to bio print skin, cartilage, bone, and vascular tissues, but the challenges of anastomosis and tissue revascularization remain a major subject of concern.

Development of organ models: Bioprinting has the potential to be applied to culture customizable organ models for the enhancement of transplantation and regenerative medicinal science. Current work is being developed to print complicated organ structures such as kidneys, livers, and hearts. Although full-fledged organs are still under development, there has been monumental advancement in the creation of organoids, small organs that mimic organs in terms of function and are helpful in understanding the development of the organs and the illnesses that affect them.

Challenges and future directions: There are some challenges that need to be overcome for bioprinting to make the desired breakthrough in the clinic. Some of the critical challenges are maintaining cell survivability through the printing process, approaches toward cell integration and blood supply, and the issue of translation from small-scale manufacturing to large-scale clinical use. New work is aimed at tackling these challenges, investigating new types of biomaterials, and refining the existing methods of bioprinting in order to increase the practicality and integration of bioprinted tissues and organs.

# 4.4 Development of medical instruments and tools

Technological advancement of medical instruments and tools through 3D printing has revolutionized and improved various areas of practice in health care. This has also led to the development of a vast line of medical devices that address different requirements, making medical procedures more effective.

Customized surgical instruments: 3D technology in surgery enables the development of surgical tools to fit specific surgical requirements. Even forms of forceps and clamps for surgeries can therefore be developed and constructed to reflect the measures that the specific surgery demands. It also increases the comfort of the surgeon in performing some complex procedures for successful surgical actions with increased precision.
