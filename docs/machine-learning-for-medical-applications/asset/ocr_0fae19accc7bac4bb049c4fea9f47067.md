The current application of AI has shifted the dimension because it provides a superior analytical capability to the human decision-making process in that field. ML algorithms, on which the functioning of AI is based, are perfect for processing large data, and realizing patterns that doctors cannot immediately notice. For better therapeutic management approaches that can inform clinical practice, such algorithms facilitate the analysis of patient records, including imaging, laboratory, and clinical records. AI systems, for example, can predict the trajectory of an illness based on historical information, which means that doctors can attempt to deliver treatment options with greater accuracy.

Decision-making has been taken to the next level with the help of AI through DL, which is a subcategory of ML that allows for analysis of complex data, such as genetic, as well as other medical imaging information. ML methods in radiology, more specifically DL models, are often more accurate than a human radiologist when it comes to detecting minor abnormalities in images. Covering areas of concern, this capacity not only enhances the accuracy of diagnosis but also early detection of illness.

AI offers more than prognostic diagnostic tools with regard to decisions. AI enables performance of predictive analysis for treatment plan optimization and patient prognosis. The latter, possibly, provide useful insights to systems that directly manage decisions, through the evaluation of tendencies and variability of a patient’s data stream. Some of these insights are as follows: probability of a patient’s readmission and the possibility of a dangerous mix up of medications. Disease predictors are common in healthcare, where people in the industry can use this predictive skill to take action before a consequence occurs, thereby improving patient outcome and reducing adverse outcomes.

Further, interactive, knowledge-driven decision support systems, also referred to as real-time decision support, enhance the decision-making capability of clinicians at the time of treatment. These systems enhance the accuracy of nonclinical health decisions since the data is gathered from different sources, and such algorithms offer a customized therapy plan. This strategy is best illustrated by integrating AI into EHRs; decision support systems alert a physician of a potential problem, categorize a scenario based on best practices, and make decisions based on evidence and data.

That being said, there have been new discoveries that underscore the need for people to recognize that AI was a complement to, rather than a replacement for, human judgment. The concept of utilizing AI as a decision-support tool in healthcare aims to enhance decision-making by providing additional data and insights. This was only useful if AI was integrated into the decision-making process of healthcare professionals. It was suggested that as the applicability of AI technologies expands, become more integral in shaping healthcare decisions, ultimately leading to more advanced methods for improving patient outcomes and the future of patient care.
