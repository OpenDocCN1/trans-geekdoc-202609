# 1 Introduction

Advances in medical industry and incorporation of artificial intelligence (AI) into diagnostics highly enhance the accuracy and efficiency of illness diagnosis [1]. Unfortunately, the sensitivity and specificity of many conventional diagnostic tools are often compromised, which help determine early diagnosis and control of medical conditions [2]. Due to the improved and faster diagnoses, AI technologies that utilize high data analysis and powerful AI algorithms improve these domains [3, 4]. This has been done through the use of AI to work with massive volumes of data, including variables with mutual interconnections that are often complex, often beyond complex analyses for a human being [5].

The results also revealed that the overall diagnostic sensitivity of AI was extremely significant [6, 7]. Traditional analysis and correlation techniques often lack the sensitivity achieved by AI systems, which can detect subtle and complex patterns using advanced algorithms trained on large datasets [8]. This capability means that early indicators of disease are more likely to be detected, increasing the chances of diagnosis at an early stage. This was particularly beneficial for medical conditions such as cancer, cardiovascular diseases, and neurological disorders [9]. Early identification of these illnesses can lead to better treatment outcomes, enable timely medical intervention, and ultimately improve the overall standard of healthcare.

AI improves the accuracy of diagnostic procedures while enhancing speed through automation and real-time data analysis [10]. AI-enabled biosensors allow doctors to access results immediately after evaluating a patient, enabling continuous monitoring of patient health data [11]. This reduces the need for invasive procedures and frequent hospital visits by supporting timely and efficient healthcare delivery [12]. Real-time monitoring powered by AI increases the ability of medical practitioners to intervene promptly when changes in a patient's condition are detected, thereby improving treatment efficiency and patient safety [13].

The integration of AI into diagnostic materials also raises a number of both ethical and legal concerns [14]. That is why aspects such as data privacy, balance of algorithms, and openness must become key issues to consider when ensuring that AI systems are designed and implemented in an ethical manner [15]. Safeguarding mechanisms for patients' information, eliminating biases in the outcome of diagnostic assays, and ensuring that the AI system has good reasons for providing its recommendations are precondition for the ethical use of AI in diagnostic decision processes [16]. To ensure patient safety and foster development of new diagnostic equipment, the emphasis of regulations is shifting more and more toward these aspects [17].

More promisingly, for the future of AI in diagnostics, continued advances in AI technology and its applications are anticipated in the near term [18]. Emerging trends span across areas such as multi-omics data integration, wearable biosensors, and the development of machine learning (ML) algorithms and AI for prognostic models [19, 20].
