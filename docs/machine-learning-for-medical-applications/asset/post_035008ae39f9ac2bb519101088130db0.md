The structural and mechanical characteristics of the electrospun nanofiber scaffolds are the most critical factors in bone tissue engineering. Using electrospinning to develop the nanofiber scaffold, the material must be able to have the mechanical properties of natural bone for support during the regeneration process. Recent improvements in electrospinning promote the design of scaffolds with tunable pore size, fiber diameter, and mechanical characteristics. It means that by varying and optimizing these parameters, researchers fabricate scaffolds not only for cell attachment but also for withstanding physiological loads and the eventual integration with the host bone tissue.

Electrospun nanofiber scaffolds were examined in bone tissue engineering based on animal testing and clinical experiments. These investigations assessed the biocompatibility, biodegradability, and tissue regeneration proficiency of the scaffolds in animal subjects. Positive outcomes in these studies evidence the potential of hosting electrospun nanofibers to foster new bone formation and at integrating with bone tissue. Applications of these scaffolds are used in bone grafts, orthopedic implants, and reconstructive surgeries, where they provide prospective solutions for intricate bone repairing and regeneration.

## 5.3 Use of nanofibers in cartilage and ligament regeneration

The components of the musculoskeletal system that aid in the movement and bearing structure consist of cartilage and ligaments. Severe clinically related effects are observed when these tissues are damaged in any form of degenerative diseases. Often, at that time, the functional integrity of these tissues is not reestablished by conventional therapy. Since electrospun nanofibers have the ability to provide biomimetic environment needed for tissue repair and regeneration, it can be considered as a potential approach for the regeneration of cartilage and ligaments. Due to their unique characteristic such as large surface area and high porosity, nanofiber creates an environment that support cells growth and tissue formation.

Electrospun nanofibers have been used to regenerate cartilage due to the nanofibers’ similarity to the ECM of cartilage. Chondrocytes commit to producing cartilage, which can adhere to and proliferate on the large surface area of the nanoscale fiber of electrospun scaffolds. For example, growth factors, which stimulate chondrocytes activity and the formation of cartilage tissue can be incorporated into nanofiber scaffolds. Improving the functional outcomes of tissue repair, these scaffold constructs design can be further optimized to provide load-bearing mechanical properties approaches to native cartilage.

In ligament repair, electrospun nanofibers are versatile and have high tensile strength to offer a close resemblance to natural ligament tissue. Alignment and orientation of nanofibers during the electrospinning process can be regulated in a manner that assists the aligned manner of orient fibroblast cells, which are ascribed for ligament formation. These aligned nanofiber scaffolds can effectively guide the correct specific alignment and organization of collagen fiber and this is paramount to the mechanical strength and functional rehabilitation of ligaments. Compatible biological signals constituents can be woven into the nanofibers to provide the extra advantage for repair and the successful maturation of functional ligament tissue.

The case for using electrospun nanofibers to support tissue engineering scaffolds is made when one compares them with other conventional scaffold materials. Nutrient delivery and cell attachment, which are critical for the development of the tissues and also for cell sustenance, are enhanced through the high surface-area-to-volume characteristics of the nanofibers. The highly specific physical structure of an interconnected and porous internal design enhances the exchange of waste products and nutrients within the nanofiber scaffolds to promote the growth of a healthy tissue. Scaffolds are based on the nanofiber with specificity of the composition, structure, and mechanical properties, and therefore it can be used for various kinds of regeneration.

In spite of the promising advances made in cartilage and ligament regenerative medicine using electrospun nanofibers, there are many issues to address. Challenges in applications of nanofiber composites include the ability to upscale the nanofiber synthesis, to incorporate nanofiber scaffolds with the surrounding tissues, and to determine the most appropriate stability and degradation periods of the scaffolds. Further studies should involve advanced formulation of nanofiber, and improved ultra-fine fiber fabrication procedures, including the need for extensive investigation of these scaffolds in preclinical and clinical models before endorsement for use in human beings. These could be critical for taking this research forward to find ways on how readymade solutions in tissue repair and regeneration could be applied.

## 5.4 Electrospun nanofibers for nerve tissue repair

Hailed as the most prevalent clinical problem, nerve injury and neurodegenerative diseases are still some of the biggest challenges in regenerative medicine today because the body's CNS has a very poor capacity to regenerate itself. Unfortunately, electrospun nanofibers present a potential strategy to bridge the gap of nerve tissue repair by supplying suitable ECM substitutes for neuron sprouting and regeneration. These nanofibers can be used to repair peripheral nerve injury and stimulate the otherwise scarcely treatable injuries in the CNS.

Electrospun nanofiber scaffolds are designed to possess the required structural and mechanical characteristics of the nerve tissue. Parameters that are important in the design are fiber diameter, fiber alignment, and pore size, all of which determine the responses of the cells and nerve regeneration. For instance, aligned nanofibers are known to direct axonal elongation and orientation in essentially the same way as their natural counterparts. These factors, combined with the ability to manipulate electrospinning parameters and choice of polymers, allow for a tailored nerve repair scaffold microenvironment.

Electrospun nanofibers, in order to have the ability to fix damaged nerve tissue, must be biocompatible. For use in tissues, these nanofibers should facilitate cell attachment, proliferation, and differentiation while not inducing adverse immune response. Peculiarities of the interaction between nanofibers and brain cells are addressed by the functionalization of the nanofibers with adhesion peptides to enhance cell adhesion and promote neurogenesis. It has been found that when stem cells are cultured on nanofiber scaffolds, they could be tempted to differentiate into neurons, and nanofiber scaffolds could support the proliferation of Schwann cells, which are very important in the regeneration of the peripheral nerve.

This review highlights how the integration of growth factors and neurotrophic agents into electrospun nanofibers can considerably improve their performance. Such molecules can be delivered in a controlled fashion for an extended period, enabling the regeneration and repair of nerves. For example, nerve growth factor and brain-derived neurotrophic factor have been effective in spinal axonal outgrowth and neuronal protection. Any factor can be incorporated within the nanofiber linked to the fiber surface. It is therefore possible to develop scaffolds that not only provide the structural framework needed for repair but also participate in the biochemical communication needed for nerve regeneration.

The application of electrospun nanofibers in nerve tissue repair is advancing steadily, with numerous studies and preclinical trials demonstrating their promising potential. These nanofibers offer structural guidance for nerve regeneration and can be tailored to deliver bioactive molecules, supporting functional recovery. Future research must address several critical challenges, including the scalability of nanofiber production, integration with existing surgical techniques, and ensuring long-term biocompatibility. Emerging innovations such as biodegradable nanofibers, advanced surface functionalization methods, and multimodal therapeutic systems are expected to further enhance their clinical applicability. The goal is to translate these technological advancements into practical solutions for treating various nerve injuries and neurological disorders, thereby improving patient outcomes. ## 5.5 Enhancing stem cell differentiation through nanofiber scaffolds

In tissue engineering practice, a major operation involves stem cell differentiation, which means orienting stem cells to form specific cell types for the restoration of damaged tissues. In this technique, electrospun nanofiber scaffolds play an important role because they mimic the mechanism of stem cell adhesion and proliferation/differentiation. Because of such characteristics as the ability to provide a large surface area for a small volume of material and the structural similarity to ECM, nanofibers are ideal for creating scaffolds that can modulate the stem cell response and promote new tissue formation.
