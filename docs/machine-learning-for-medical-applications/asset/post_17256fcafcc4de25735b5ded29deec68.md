### 4.3.4 Technique analysis of advanced imaging for MRI

Generation of a magnetic resonance image involves different methods of image analysis for improving the image quality as well as detecting useful information. Any photograph, before it is considered for analysis or before any analysis is made on it, has to undergo some forms of preprocessing like noise reduction and proofing of artifact that is likely to distort that image or even hinder or distort the results of the analysis. ### 4.3.5 Prospects and problems

However, there are challenges that exist in MRI. However, widespread availability of MRI in the hospitals is still a challenge due to its high cost of equipment in addition to technical infrastructure requirements. Also, it is significantly vulnerable to patient movement and artifacts and thus may distort diagnosis results based on MRI images. Further, some of these problems, such as excessively slow imaging and inability to correct for subject motion, were still being solved by the development of faster imaging techniques and better motion correction methods. Current research developments that can be expected in MRI technology include: the consideration of the possibility of the use of higher magnetic field strengths to produce even sharper images, the consideration of using artificial intelligence in the analysis of the images created, and the addition of functional and molecular imaging, which will give further insight into the causes of diseases and how the body responds to treatment.

MRI has always been an important type of medical imaging offering superior resolution and diagnostic potential. It is envisioned to expand in its application of integration with advanced processing techniques and machine learning algorithms allowing for higher utility in clinical applications with improved, hefty, and detail imaging solution for various clinical conditions.

## 4.4 Ultrasound imaging

Ultrasound imaging known as sonography is one of the most frequently used diagnostic techniques with use of high-frequency sound waves to display real-time images of the internal structure of a body. It is special for its lack of invasiveness as it does not produce ionizing radiation and numerous therapeutic applications. In the subsequent sections, a comprehensive analysis of ultrasonic imaging is carried out with regards to its fundamental principles, applications, image processing techniques, and advancements.

### 4.4.1 Hands-on principles of ultrasound imaging

Some of the important principles including the principles of sound wave and sound wave echoes will be used to explain ultrasonic imaging. A transducer produces ultrasound waves that pass into the body and are usually in a frequency band of 2–18 MHz. The transducer receives echoes that are reflected back to it; these sound waves pass through significant tissues and surfaces in the body. To allow the interior structures to be seen by processing, the echoes are converted to electrical signals following the utilization step. The categorization of tissues depends on the acoustic impedance of tissues, where tissues with different impedances reflected the transmitted sound waves differently. It produces an image with relative brightness and contrast, which corresponds to the density and composition of the given tissue.

### 4.4.2 Sonography application in treating humans

It was often used in many medical domains because ultrasound imaging was safe and has real-time imaging advantage. In obstetrics and gynecology, ultrasound was applied mainly to diagnose the conditions that required prenatal intervention, assess fetal development, and monitor fetal health. Echocardiography was a diagnostic modality used in cardiology practice to assist in treatment planning and management of cardiovascular diseases by giving a precise image of the heart. Abdominal ultrasound can also diagnose diseases such as cysts, tumors, and gallstones besides using it in evaluation of abdominal organs such as the liver, kidneys, and gallbladder, among others. When reviewing the tendons, ligaments, and soft tissues musculoskeletal ultrasonography was beneficial in offering info on inflammation and injury. Also, vascular imaging employs an ultrasonic technique to estimate blood flow and identify irregularities in blood vessels.

### 4.4.3 Techniques used in image processing of ultrasound

In ultrasonic examination, there are many methods used in the process of image production with the intention of improving the quality of the picture and arriving at the correct diagnosis. The basic forms of processing are gain control, which deals with the brightness of the image and the time gain compensation (TGC) that describes the variation of amplitude weighting of the echoes from different depths due to attenuation. Therefore, methods such as spatial filtering and adaptive filtering are applied to the image in order to reduce speckle and improve the quality of the image. Image post-processing techniques, sharpening, and brightness changes are applied to define the different structures of the body and thereby enhance the diagnostic properties of the image. Special procedures include Doppler imaging, which allows for further determination of blood flow and vessel characteristics on the basis of frequency variation of the reflected sound waves as signs of speed and direction of blood flow.

### 4.4.4 Difficulties in analyzing ultrasound pictures

On a positive note, ultrasound imaging has many advantages as a diagnostic tool, although there are disadvantages, which influence aspects of picture quality and diagnostic accuracy. These distortions get in the way of correct interpreting of the underlying anatomical structures because structures appear enhanced, speckled, or overshadowed. Yet another challenge was operator dependence because the quality of the ultrasound picture depends to a large extent on the operator’s skills and knowledge. Generally, the visibility of tiny structures or lesions in deep tissue imaging can be depressed by the effects of resolution limits. In addition, variations in patients’ characteristics and settings of the used equipment also contribute to image consistency issues that affect the credibility of the comparison research.

### 4.4.5 More innovations in ultrasound

The methods using ultrasound have recently undergone several developments that expand the scope of the field and efficiency of diagnosis. Imaging structures in volume were enabled by 3D and 4D ultrasonography, which provide higher accuracy and spatial resolution of the structures. A new technique with the name elastography determines the elasticity and the hardness of the tissue and provides valuable data concerning the characterization of tumors and fibrosis of liver. Microbubble contrast agents enhance CEUS, since ultrasonography excels in blood flow and tissue vascularity display helping in tumor detection and heart function assessment. Furthermore, hand-held and portable ultrasound systems can be manufactured, which means availability and convenience in diagnostics of emergency and anytime situations.

Thus, due to its noninterference, actual time control possibilities, and adaptability concerning various clinical scenarios, ultrasound has remained an essential part of diagnostics. Ultrasound was an indispensable modality of the present day medical practice due to the ongoing advances in hardware technologies and image enhancement techniques that enhance the application along with the accuracy.
