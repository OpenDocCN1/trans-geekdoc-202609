The effectiveness of these imaging techniques was boosted early on by the application of digital image processing in medicine. Digital radiography, which replaced older film-based procedures allowed more than transferring, modifying, and archiving pictures. One of the outcomes of this shift was the invention of picture archiving and communication systems (PACS), which revolutionized how medical pictures are handled in healthcare organizations. According to history, the expansion of a digital medical imaging system has created the foundation for more extended and subsequent image processing and interpretation advancements in the incorporation of AI and machine learning in the medical imaging system.

All these developments can be described as a combined set that revolutionized the diagnostics of the human body by offering clinicians effective and efficient methods to visualize and analyze clients. The innovations of this period raised the framework for the huge developments that are characteristic of imaging processes that have the capability to enhance medical research, diagnosis, and treatment.

# 2.2 The emergence of digital imaging

Another major in medical picture processing is the migration from analog to digital images; it shifted paradigms of how medical pictures are acquired, stored, and reviewed. This process began in the second half of the twentieth century due to a gradual progress in computer technology and with realization of the disadvantages of an analog system.

Analog techniques dominated the early stage of imaging systems, but these systems suffered from problems such as image decaying over time, a lack of ability to duplicate the image, and problems of image processing and enhancing the pictures. Such limitations were offset by the advent of the facility to digitize visible images based on the revolutionary concept of digital imaging as applied to medical imaging.

In the seventies, Sir Godfrey Hounsfield came up with perhaps one of the most important and initial steps towards digital imaging known as CT. CT technology based on current computer algorithms set out X-ray pictures taken from different angles and created cross-sectional images. This was one of the very first practical applications of digital image processing in the medical arena demonstrating that targets have the capability of providing more detailed and in-depth information than conventional methods of X-ray imaging.
