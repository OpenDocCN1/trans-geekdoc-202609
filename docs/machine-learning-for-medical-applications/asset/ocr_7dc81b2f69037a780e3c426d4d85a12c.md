Quasi-Newton methods are another family of gradient-based optimization techniques, wherein the design variables are updated using an estimate of the inverse Hessian. Quasi-Newton techniques support the approximation of the second-order derivative mentioned above with the help of gradients computed in the previous steps rather than by finding the Hessian matrix, which is rather time-consuming in the case of a high-dimensional space. Quasi-Newton techniques though minimize the objective function and estimate the Hessian matrix. They enable convergence toward the minimum by updating the estimate Hessian matrix incrementally, while avoiding the computational burden of computing an accurate Hessian.

The use of gradient information to direct the search for better solutions enables such methods to navigate complex design spaces, find the best design solutions, and facilitate the creation of new scaffolds for tissue engineering and regenerative medicine applications.

# 5.3 Evolutionary algorithms for scaffold optimization

Evolutionary algorithms are a group of optimization techniques based on the genetics and natural selection principles. These methods’ ability to operate in environments where objective functions are nonlinear, and/or discontinuous, multimodal, complex properties, as well as their capability to approach optimality make them widely applied in scaffold design.
