## 2.2 Basic principles of electrospinning

Using electrostatic forces to draw fibers of sizes varying from the micron to nanometer scale, electrospinning is a flexible and popular method for creating nanofibers from polymer solutions. The basic idea of electrospinning is to apply a high-voltage electric field to a melt contained in a syringe that has a needle serving as the spinneret. A Taylor cone is formed at the tip of the needle when the voltage is applied because it charges the liquid droplet's surface. A thin jet of the polymer solution is ejected from the tip of the Taylor cone when the electrostatic repulsive forces are greater than the surface tension of the liquid.

Whipping instability is responsible for the formation of ultrathin fibers from the polymer jet that stretches and evaporates solvent when passing through an electric field. Accordingly, the fibers are then combined on a conducting charged collector which is implemented in different structures such as flat plates, drum, etc. Depending on certain specificities of the electrospinning process, the thickness of the developed nanofibers varies from tens of nanometers to several microns.

Other parameters for controlling the electrospinning process as well as the shape of the fibers that appear include viscosity and concentration, coupled with the conductivity of the polymer solution, the voltage being applied, the distance from the needle tip to the collector, and the rate of flow of the polymer solution. The fibers’ morphology and the rate of solvent evaporation are also significantly influenced by the type of solvent solution used in the electrospinning process. Solvents with lower boiling points typically result in the formation of thinner fibers due to their rapid evaporation, whereas high boiling point solvents tend to produce thicker, often beaded fibers because the solvent may not fully evaporate during the jet’s flight path. Additionally, ambient environmental factors such as humidity and temperature play a crucial role in fiber formation and consistency. It is found that there are several trade-offs to be made among these various parameters to achieve the optimum balance necessary to influence the diameter of the fibers, their porosity, and surface characteristics, all of which affect the performance of nanofibers in their biomedical applications. For instance, adjusting the electrospinning parameters produce fibers with smooth and nonbeaded surfaces of different degrees of porosity and surface irregularity, depending on the need in application, including drug delivery. Accordingly, the electrospinning process provides high versatility in generating polymeric nanofibers, with the desired characteristics, to satisfy certain functional demands.

For biomedical applications, precise control of fiber morphology is deemed essential because the size, porosity, and roughness of nanofibers are among the factors known to affect issues such as cell attachment, growth, and drug elution. For example, the nanofibers used for tissue engineering should have certain mechanical characterization and surface topologies that favor cell–scaffold and subsequent tissue interactions. Likewise, in drug delivery applications, the porosity and surface areas of the nanofibers should be manipulated in order to let the nanofibers deliver the drug to the site of action in the controlled release process for creating therapeutic efficacy.

## 2.3 Types of polymers used in electrospinning for biomedical applications

The suitability of polymers for electrospinning plays a crucial role in the outcome of nanofibers, especially in biomedical applications. The two categories of polymers widely used in electrospinning are synthetic and natural, each having distinctive features to satisfy medical needs. Important elements, including mechanical strength, biocompatibility, biodegradability, and the capacity to include bioactive substances, are all influenced by the polymer selection, and are critical for medical applications such as drug administration, tissue engineering, and wound healing. Specifically, the way that polymer chemistry and electrospinning interact affects fiber form and functioning greatly.

Because natural polymers have inherent biological activity and great biocompatibility, they are frequently employed in electrospinning. These biologically produced polymers are ideal for uses where intimate contact with live tissues is necessary.
