Including computer modeling and computation with experimental analysis helps to improve the work of predictive models. Through tuning simulations, one can enhance the stable numerical models to integrate the actual environment. This integration comprises comparing simulated outcomes with experimental outcomes, such as degradation rates and mechanical tests, in order to refine model parameters. The methodology of successive construction of models to get as close as possible to reality strengthens the relevance of simulation results to experimental findings.

The key benefits of the method include the opportunity to study a broad spectrum of design parameters and operating conditions without a direct need for physical modeling and testing. Experiments can also be used in areas that are hard to model and were almost impossible to set up. There are a few constraints, such as the accuracy of one's own data, and the model may not be very accurate due to some assumptions. These limitations have to be familiar to researchers as well as the use of simulations in combination with experimental calibration to present a full picture of scaffold functionality.

It was anticipated that future developments in computational simulations and modeling would add new value to scaffold research. Most of the areas of application involve the need to model various features of social systems by using algorithms, which become gradually more complex due to the development of numerous possible advancements in artificial intelligence. Enhancing machine learning algorithms with computational models can improve predictive methods by recognizing other intricate data structures that had not been detected earlier. Ongoing developments in simulation techniques and future approaches improve the design, evaluation, and usage of biodegradable scaffolds for tissue regeneration.

Real-world performance data from clinical trials were critical for validating the efficacy and safety of biodegradable scaffolds in practical applications. Unlike controlled laboratory settings, clinical trials provide insights into how scaffolds perform under diverse conditions and across varied patient populations. The advantage of these data is that they provide more concrete insight into the effectiveness and mechanics of scaffolds when implemented in real-life scenarios, offering a more grounded approach to theoretical treatments of scaffold use.

Clinical performance data collection involves several processes that include constructing valid protocols for clinical field trials, choosing valid measures of clinical outcomes, and defining valid methods of data collection.
