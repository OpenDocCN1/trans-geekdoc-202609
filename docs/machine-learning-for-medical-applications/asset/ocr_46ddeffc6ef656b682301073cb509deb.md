# 6 Wound healing applications of electrospun nanofibers

# 6.1 Nanofiber dressings for enhanced healing and moisture retention

Wound healing is a complex process involving multiple stages. It comprise coagulation, inflammation, cell proliferation, and tissue remodeling. Appropriate dressing is important in order to enhance tissue regeneration and to minimize risk factors associated with wound infection. Conventional occlusive dressings are also generally suboptimal in terms of the local environment. They do not provide the environment for the wound to heal, both regarding hydration and antimicrobial status. To overcome these drawbacks, electrospun nanofiber dressings are considered as they offer a powerful tool for improving the wound healing and moisture at the wound site.

Of importance is the ability of the dressing to retain moisture; standard non-electrospun nanofiber dressings are inferior to the latter. Several studies have established that because of their relatively large surface area and porosity, nanofibers retain exudate on wound and ensure that the associated process of healing is enhanced and the pain reduced. Due to the structure of nanofiber dressings, they offer the right amount of moisture, maintaining the wound from drying, and at the same time, avoiding flooding of the wound site with excessive fluids. Any unwanted moisture can also be evaporated in a controlled manner. It is paramount for promoting the appropriate five factors involved in the healing process and to maintain the wound bed for moist balance.

To enhance the capability of the nanofiber dressings to afford protection against infections, the dressings could be functionalized with antimicrobial materials with antimicrobial films. Iodine incorporated into the matrix of nanofiber by itself has shown to possess antibacterial properties of copper not only to prevent growth of bacteria but also decrease the infection rate of the wound. This gives these antimicrobial nanofiber dressings sustained and controlled antimicrobial effect that ensures that the wound environment remains clear of microorganisms and useful for the management of chronic and infected wounds.

One of the factors considered while developing an appropriate dressing of nanofiber is biocompatibility. The materials that are used to identify electrospun nanofibers are typically nonreactive and biocompatible with tissue. The nanofibers have a decisive structural organization corresponding to the ECM that would enhance the regeneration of tissues and movement of cells. Thereby, the dressing is quickly incorporated into the wound bed to promote the body’s healing process without scar formations. This can be useful and effective for wound healing. Some nanofiber dressings can moreover be incorporated with growth factors active substances.
