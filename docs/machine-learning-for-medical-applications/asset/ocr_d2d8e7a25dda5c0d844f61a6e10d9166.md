# 6.2.2 Accuracy in the use of radiation

Radiation therapy is concerned with local control of the disease that is built around the ability to deliver the maximal dose to malignant tissues and minimal doses to the surrounding healthy tissues. Imaging has a unique crisis in tube image processing, which is a significant factor for establishing accurate treatment planning techniques brought in by medical images combined with the treatment planning system. Approaches including image fusion in which information derived from different imaging studies is integrated enable the correct segmentation of tumor margins and structures. This integration aids in the development of three-dimensional radiation treatment plans that describe distribution of the radiation doses, increasing the efficiency of treatment and reducing the risks of the side effects.

# 6.2.3 Organ-specific surgery

MIP provides significant value for surgical planning. Preoperative imaging can inform the surgeon the relative position of the tumor with the internal organs and structures to enable the choice of the right surgical exposure. Tools including the 3D models and virtual surgery enable surgeons to see and practice it before even operating the patient. Such preparation assists in minimizing surgery-associated risks, improving precision, and therefore improving results.

# 6.2.4 Treatment plans for individual patients

Image processing involves using images in medical diagnosis and treatment since it enables the integration of imaging data with the rest of a patient’s data. Machine learning techniques and statistical models derived from the images let experts determine the prognosis to the treatment consequences based on patient-related characteristics. This makes it possible to design specific treatment interventions for clients based on their individual characteristics and illness severity, thus increasing the effectiveness of treatment and reducing side effects.
