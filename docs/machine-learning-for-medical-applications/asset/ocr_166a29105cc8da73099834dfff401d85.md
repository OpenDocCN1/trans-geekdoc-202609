Another important characteristic of electrospun nanofibers that directly influences their mechanical, physical, and biological properties is fiber diameter. Smaller fiber diameters typically result in higher surface-area-to-volume ratios, which enhance drug loading efficiency, facilitate better interaction with biological environments, and improve the mechanical behavior of scaffolds. These nanoscale dimensions are especially advantageous in tissue engineering, as they more closely mimic the architecture of the natural extracellular matrix (ECM), thereby promoting cell adhesion, proliferation, and tissue regeneration. In contrast, larger-diameter fibers are better suited for applications like wound dressings, where mechanical strength, durability, and moisture management are prioritized. The impact of fiber diameter on performance across different biomedical applications is illustrated in Figure 4, which compares nanofiber size variations with their corresponding functional outcomes.

Fiber diameter in electrospinning can be controlled by altering certain factors, namely, polymer concentration, solution viscosity, voltage, and collection distance. Thicker fibers are obtained by raising the polymer concentration, while finer fibers are obtained by raising the voltage by stretching the polymer jet. Another factor is solution viscosity: the higher the viscosity of the solution, the larger the fibers it produces, while the lower viscosity solutions provide for finer fibers. These parameters are effectively controlled in a manner that gave the desired fiber diameter, as well as the ability to orient the nanofibers for certain use.
