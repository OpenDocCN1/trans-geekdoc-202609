# 3.4.5 Watershed algorithm

The watershed algorithm was derived from topographic watersheds and was applied for image sectioning under the notion of “water flow.” It deals with the image as a topological surface and detects the catchment basins and watershed lines through mimicking the water flow from local minima. The largest margin of success is recorded while using the algorithm to segment images that are clearly defined and highly structured. It is noisy and if noise has to be attenuated then smoothing the image is often necessary to get the best solution.

The approaches proposed based on clustering formulate the problem in a flexible way with a variety of tools dedicated to medical image segmentation with their own merits and demerits. Although there are various clustering procedures available in the literature that provide some superior solutions to the abovementioned requirements for different segmentation problems, these techniques in most cases need precise parameters tuning and data preprocessing to get better accuracy of medical images. Current advancements in clustering algorithms have also combined it with newer, advanced methods like DL and this continues to refine segmentation, making it efficient for clinical use.

# 3.5 Model-based methods

Model-based methods for medical image segmentation involve using mathematical and statistical models and aim to draw out structures and regions from images. These approaches provide an organization for data segmentation and take into consideration the a priori knowledge and beliefs about the form and characteristics of the structures of a trainee's body.

# 3.5.1 Active contours and snakes

There are also active contour models, which are called snakes, a model-based technique that utilizes energy to detect edges of an object. The method used is a process of adjusting a contour of the object using an energy function made of internal and external forces. Interactions between structural features keep the contour as smooth as possible, whereas outside forces force the contour towards the object edge. This method was especially efficient in segmenting objects with clear edges but was not so good with objects that were frequently blurred around the edges.
