
## 1 Introduction

Advances in medical industry and incorporation of artificial intelligence (AI) into diagnostics highly enhance the accuracy and efficiency of illness diagnosis [1]. Unfortunately, the sensitivity and specificity of many conventional diagnostic tools are often compromised, which help determine early diagnosis and control of medical conditions [2]. Due to the improved and faster diagnoses, AI technologies that utilize high data analysis and powerful AI algorithms improve these domains [3, 4]. This has been done through the use of AI to work with massive volumes of data, including variables with mutual interconnections that are often complex, often beyond complex analyses for a human being [5].

The results also revealed that the overall diagnostic sensitivity of AI was extremely significant [6, 7]. Traditional analysis and correlation techniques often lack the sensitivity achieved by AI systems, which can detect subtle and complex patterns using advanced algorithms trained on large datasets [8]. This capability means that early indicators of disease are more likely to be detected, increasing the chances of diagnosis at an early stage. This was particularly beneficial for medical conditions such as cancer, cardiovascular diseases, and neurological disorders [9]. Early identification of these illnesses can lead to better treatment outcomes, enable timely medical intervention, and ultimately improve the overall standard of healthcare.

AI improves the accuracy of diagnostic procedures while enhancing speed through automation and real-time data analysis [10]. AI-enabled biosensors allow doctors to access results immediately after evaluating a patient, enabling continuous monitoring of patient health data [11]. This reduces the need for invasive procedures and frequent hospital visits by supporting timely and efficient healthcare delivery [12]. Real-time monitoring powered by AI increases the ability of medical practitioners to intervene promptly when changes in a patient's condition are detected, thereby improving treatment efficiency and patient safety [13].

The integration of AI into diagnostic materials also raises a number of both ethical and legal concerns [14]. That is why aspects such as data privacy, balance of algorithms, and openness must become key issues to consider when ensuring that AI systems are designed and implemented in an ethical manner [15]. Safeguarding mechanisms for patients' information, eliminating biases in the outcome of diagnostic assays, and ensuring that the AI system has good reasons for providing its recommendations are precondition for the ethical use of AI in diagnostic decision processes [16]. To ensure patient safety and foster development of new diagnostic equipment, the emphasis of regulations is shifting more and more toward these aspects [17].

More promisingly, for the future of AI in diagnostics, continued advances in AI technology and its applications are anticipated in the near term [18]. Emerging trends span across areas such as multi-omics data integration, wearable biosensors, and the development of machine learning (ML) algorithms and AI for prognostic models [19, 20]. These advancements hold the potential to revolutionize diagnostic processes, as consistently enhance the specificity, accuracy, selectivity, and depth of diagnostic information

### 2.3 Integration of AI with traditional diagnostics

One significant development that is seen in the medical diagnostic domain is the integration of the AI in the standard diagnostic process. Years back, most of the diagnosis procedures were done by hand and almost all of them relied on the skills, talent, and perception of the doctors to arrive at definite conclusions once results were obtained. Other traditional assessment techniques were histopathology, laboratory assessment, blood test, and MRIs, while imaging like X-ray, CT scan, and MRI though are important in assessing the overall health of the patient. These techniques often faced limitations regarding its sensitivity, specificity, and the ability to handle large amount of data proficiently.

AI has pioneered and revolutionized the enhancement of conventional diagnostic tools through advanced data processing and pattern recognition capabilities. As illustrated in Figure 2, machine learning (ML) techniques applied to medical imaging have significantly improved diagnostic accuracy by detecting anomalies that might be missed by the human eye. Deep learning (DL) models, in particular, trained on vast and diverse datasets, can recognize subtle patterns in images that are often imperceptible to human observers. Radiology has been a major beneficiary of these advancements, as AI systems now assist clinicians by offering diagnostic suggestions and highlighting potential issues based on prior image inputs streamlining the workflow and improving clinical decision-making.

AI determines how data is processed and how results are interpreted in laboratory diagnosis. A lot of test data consists of characters, which are processed and analyzed by automated systems with the help of AI algorithms rather quickly and, in many cases, accurately, than by manual systems. Such systems include AI, which is capable of analyzing the pattern and anomalies in blood analysis for instance, facilitating the profiling of illnesses such as diabetes, heart disease, and virus. In most of the processes, AI reduces the amount of time that needs to be spent on monotonous tasks, thus helping the healthcare workers to focus on the quality of patient care and the state-of-art decisions that are made.

AI is also entering into histopathology as well, which is used for tissue sample analysis. It is capable of examining histology pictures to determine whether the cells appear malignant and sorting them based on biomarkers. This method hastens the diagnosis and treatment planning, since it enhances the diagnostic process, while reducing the amount of time taken for analysis.

Despite all these advancements, challenges are still observed in incorporating AI with traditional diagnosis. AI used for diagnostics bring up several issues, for instance, algorithm bias, the need for good training data samples, and data privacy. Most important changes are related to the workflow, and specific training for the healthcare personnel to use these technologies adequately for getting the maximum result from AI systems.

In general, the use of AI in diagnostics, together with standard tools, has enhanced medical diagnostics with higher accuracy, effectiveness, and potential to deliver more personalized approaches for a patient. This is expected, as with increasing maturity of the underlying AI technologies, their contribution to enhancing traditional diagnostic approaches would grow, and the overall context of the healthcare system would be changed.

## 2.4 Challenges in pre-AI diagnostics

Before the use of AI in diagnostics, conventional diagnostic techniques were subjected to multiple problems, impeding their efficiency and accuracy. One of the primary problems that emerged was that barely any diagnostic tests could be considered sensitive in the early detection of COVID-19. Several traditional techniques, including colorimetric assays and manual microscopy, were limited in their sensitivity because they failed to quantify biomarkers with low concentrations, resulting in increased false-negative rates. Because of such insensitivity, the evaluations were often not timely; this effect could potentially have a deleterious influence on patient outcomes when early interventions are critical.

Another avoidable difficulty in diagnostic accuracy is the natural inter-observer variation in assessments based on manual analysis. Hearing aid and diagnostic tests, in regard to imaging, demand specialist evaluation, which is not perfect and prone to variations due to the difference in interpretation among different individuals. Standard deviations between different operators between different institutions could be catastrophic and thereby compromise the diagnosis of patients, amongst a host of other complications that could arise from variation.
