# 5 Optimization techniques

# 5.1 Overview of optimization algorithms used in scaffold design

Optimization algorithms are especially helpful in scaffold design because they are used to aid in finding solutions to problems that are best described using high dimensions in the design space. These algorithms are used for tuning any of the aspects of scaffold characteristics and functionality, such as material and scaffold architecture and fabrication process variables. Many optimization methods have been incorporated in scaffold design with varying success, flexibility, and suitability to certain design contexts.

Gradient algorithm is one of the optimization methods that determines the direction of optimization for design variable and it is updated until the criterion has been met. There algorithms, namely quasi-Newton, gradient descent, and conjugate gradient descent, are used to optimize many objective functions that are continuous and are function of a single differentiable variable. Shape and material characteristics of a scaffold are especially suitable for these methods, as the objective function can be presented as continuous function of design variables.

Because of their capability in coping with both nonlinearity, discontinuity, and multimodality in the objective function, in addition to the capability of exploring enormously large solution space, the evolutionary algorithms that stem from the principles of natural selection and hereditary features are widely used in scaffold design. Those of the evolutionary type are simulated annealing, genetic algorithms (GAs), differential evolution (DE), and particle swarm optimization. To enhance the fitness of a population of candidate solutions with respect to the goal function, these algorithms, for example, use selection, crossover, mutation, etc. and iteratively build the population. GAs are very useful in searching through large complex design domains and also in cases where the design function has several local optima and is discrete and/or nonlinear.

Due to their ability to effectively and efficiently find near-optimal solutions in large solution spaces, metaheuristic algorithms like the firefly algorithm, ant colony optimization, as well as the artificial bee colony, are applied to scaffold design. These algorithms are inspired by groups of some physical processes.
