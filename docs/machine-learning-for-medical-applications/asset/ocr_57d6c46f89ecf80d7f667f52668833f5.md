Originally developed for video surveillance, where spatiotemporal anomaly detection was a necessity, the CNN-RNN model proved to be successful at detecting irregularities such as movements of abnormal objects or crowds, in the physical environment. For instance, an anomalous spatial behavior, like an unlikely congregation of people in a given location, is characterized from another perspective that is, spatially by CNN, and from another perspective, that is, temporally by RNN. Indeed, both the architectures of the proposed hybrid model can effectively detect these activities not only in their static forms, but also when they change over time. In addition, it is possible to stack both CNN and RNN layers with information computed at different scales, where multiple CNN layers can learn explicit hierarchical spatial representations, while multiple RNN layers can model both long- and short-term temporal dependencies at the same time. Such stacking enables the model to learn consecutive and more complex spatiotemporal patterns enhancing its capability to detect different types of anomalies.

Hybrid CNN-RNNs for anomaly detection in spatiotemporal domains entail a selection of parameters in light of data characteristics. The learning process could be computationally expensive, since the two dimensions, spatial as well as temporal, need to be learned at once. Modern development in parallel computation and GPU integration makes these types of models trainable, even on large volumes of data such as long video sequences data. Other than enhancing training efficiency, model performance similarly further increased by using various methods such as transfer learning where preexisting CNN models are retrained for specific anomaly detection problems. It can also greatly lower the quantity of manually identified abnormal instances necessary in training the model the speed at which the model can generalize to new, unknown anomalies.

As for the evaluation model, we can use more common evaluation indices in anomaly detection, including precision, recall, F1-value, and the value of area under the curve (AUC-ROC). As some of these deviations are often very sparse in many spatiotemporal datasets, accuracy measure has the tendency of being less useful than precision-recall curves. The interpretability of the models was still a question since the architecture that unites CNN and RNN creates a significant number of interactions, which are not immediately clear when explaining why exactly this has been detected. Nevertheless, the recent trends in XAI attempt to offer more interpretability on how the results of such composite models are produced, which could allow users to verify their outputs.
