# 4 Key imaging modalities in medical image processing

# 4.1 X-ray imaging

# 4.1.1 Introduction to mechanisms of X-ray production and detection

The foundation of X-ray imaging was the assumption that the X-ray radiation would be partially absorbed by the body. X-rays are produced from an X-ray tube; the radiation gets into the human body and is absorbed in different degrees by different forms of tissues. A biologic tissue that transmits less X-rays appears as a shade of gray in the resultant image while bones that transmit more X-rays appear white. A detector converts the beam of the X-rays that penetrate the body into either a conventional photographic film or an electronic sensor. This procedure creates a radiographic picture that provides the right information regarding the internal structures of the body and is valuable for diagnosis.

# 4.1.2 Different methods for improving X-ray images

There has been impressive change in the enhancement of images through image processing resulting in enhanced X-ray images. Facilities that entail contrast improvement, edge, and sharpness boosting, as well as noise elimination improve the image quality. Reduction methods in an artifact center on areas such as motion blur and image noise, thus leading to enhanced diagnosis. There is growing ability to use machines in automatic identification and analysis of pathologies for example, fracture or tumor, thus increasing the CI. In addition, CT, which uses plotted imagery to produce several two-dimensional X-ray images to create a three-dimensional image, reveals invisible structures to an extent, using X-rays alone.
