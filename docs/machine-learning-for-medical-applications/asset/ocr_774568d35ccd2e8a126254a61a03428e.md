Better disease staging: In addition to the overall improvement of disease staging the grading system used to determine the stage of the disease has also been enhanced.

In oncology and other medical fields, accurate segmentation plays a crucial role in properly staging and grading diseases. For instance, in cancer treatment, distinguishing between malignant and benign tumors requires precise volumetric segmentation, which informs staging and directly influences treatment planning. # 6.1.2 Refinement of surgical planning

Exact segmentation was crucial in enhancing tactical temporal segmentation, which reveals detailed anatomical information that guides surgical procedures. Before the operation, segmentation analysis allows for the identification of all relevant structures, helping the surgeon better visualize the treatment area. Such detailed information regarding the anatomy of a particular organ reduces the probability of complications associated with surgeries and increases the safety of most surgical operations. Postoperative segmentation plays a key role in assessing the results of the operation and monitoring for potential complications during follow-up care.

# 6.1.3 Radiotherapy treatment assistance

In radiotherapy, the surfaces must be accurately segmented to find the volumes to be treated and the doses of radiation that are to be administrated. When segmenting cancerous tissues from surrounding healthy tissues, tumor contouring guarantees that the dose set for cancer treatment delivers the intended dose of radiation to the tumor without compromising the health of the surrounding, healthy tissues. This adjustment towards careful treatment planning is essential to achieve the highest amount of treatment success and preventing any side effects. Correct separation also facilitates the utilization of the form of radiotherapy, which alters the treatment plan in view of variations in size of the tumor mass during the various phases of therapy.
