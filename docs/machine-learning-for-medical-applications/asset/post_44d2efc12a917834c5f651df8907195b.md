Several optimization techniques based on machine learning are highly valuable in scaffold design including surrogate modeling, reinforcement learning, and Bayesian optimization. These techniques utilize predictive models to guide the search toward optimal solutions. By combining the strengths of model-based and global optimization approaches, researchers can navigate large and complex design spaces and discover near-optimal solutions for developing innovative scaffolds in tissue engineering and regenerative medicine.

## 5.2 Gradient-based optimization methods

Optimization algorithms often used in scaffold design as gradient based methods are popular because of their efficient work for various differentiable and continuous objective functions. The optimization goal of these techniques is to search for the vector in the search space that makes the objective function as large as possible, subject to a convergence criterion, by moving the design variables in the steepest direction of the objective function.

Gradient descent is one of the simplest, depending on the gradients optimization methods. By means of gradient descent, the design variables are adjusted by the gradient magnitude, while the direction of the descent of the objective function is determined by the negative gradient. In the gradient descent methods, not only is the method used to go gradually toward the aim of the objective function, but even the actual direction that is being taken is the gradient one. In each iteration, the design variables are modified in order to achieve a lower objective function.

The conjugate gradient descent is therefore an optimization that aids gradient descent to converge by providing information from prior steps. Gradual descent methods are shown in Figure 6 that is based on the gradient of methods that make iteration on the model parameter to optimize the loss functions. Conjugate gradient descent algorithms employ conjugate directions to eliminate oscillations in updating the design variables, and get to the global minimum of the objective function faster. By the use of conjugate directions, conjugate gradients can descend at a faster rate than some of the differentiation-based algorithms, specifically with regard to ill-conditioned and highly nonlinear problems.

Quasi-Newton methods are another family of gradient-based optimization techniques, wherein the design variables are updated using an estimate of the inverse Hessian. Quasi-Newton techniques support the approximation of the second-order derivative mentioned above with the help of gradients computed in the previous steps rather than by finding the Hessian matrix, which is rather time-consuming in the case of a high-dimensional space. Quasi-Newton techniques though minimize the objective function and estimate the Hessian matrix. They enable convergence toward the minimum by updating the estimated Hessian matrix incrementally, while avoiding the computational burden of computing an accurate Hessian.

The use of gradient information to direct the search for better solutions enables such methods to navigate complex design spaces, find the best design solutions, and facilitate the creation of new scaffolds for tissue engineering and regenerative medicine applications.

## 5.3 Evolutionary algorithms for scaffold optimization

Evolutionary algorithms are a group of optimization techniques based on the genetics and natural selection principles. These methods’ ability to operate in environments where objective functions are nonlinear, and/or discontinuous, multimodal, complex properties, as well as their capability to approach optimality make them widely applied in scaffold design.

Scaffold optimization has been exercised with the help of the most popular evolutionary algorithm known as the GA. The central scheme of GAs is to maintain a population of potential solutions, and then evolve them on the basis of selection, crossover, and mutation operators, and in this respect, GAs mimic the process of natural selection. In Figure 7, we have exemplified the use of evolutionary algorithms on scaffold structures and demonstrated how the algorithms loop through and improve the structures for maximum effectiveness. The “weaker” genes, in terms of fitness, are gradually replaced over many generations, while those that are “stronger”, measured in terms of the objective function, have a higher chance of being selected for reproduction, crossover, and high mutation rates. Due to this, a population of candidate solutions is repeatedly improved in each generation of GAs and eventually come closer to the optimal solution.

## 6.2 Case study: predicting mechanical properties of scaffolds using machine learning

The mechanical behavior of scaffolds in tissue engineering scaffold design ensures that it can bear the physiological load and allow tissue formation. A useful way of forecasting the mechanical properties of scaffolds with reference to their architecture, constitution, and fabrication parameters can be accomplished by employing artificial neural networks.

In a study done by the authors, information about different scaffold-forming materials, along with the related mechanical characteristics, were collected within a dataset. The collected data involved scaffold characteristics (i.e., material type of the scaffold, materials used as reinforcement, and polymers), scaffold design (i.e., pore size and porosity), and process conditions (i.e. printing rate and temperature). The Young's modulus and the compressive strength of the scaffolds were determined using standard mechanical tests.

Supervised machine learning methods were applied in this study to develop regression models on the scaffold dataset with the goal of predicting mechanical properties of the scaffolds. Results of the current experiment showed that to define the best model for predicting the scaffold's mechanical characteristics, support vector regression, decision trees, random forests, and linear regression were used.

The input data was preprocessed and the so-called features representing the underlying relations between scaffold characteristics and mechanical properties were engineered. To reduce the dimension of the input space data and to extract the most useful features for predicting scaffold's mechanical properties, the authors used feature selection techniques, principal component analysis and recursive feature removal techniques.

The efficiency of the machine learning models was evaluated using statistically analytical measurement such as coefficient of determination (R-squared), MSE, and the MAE. The ability of the models to generalize was tested; cross-validation techniques that included leave one out cross-validation and k-fold cross-validation were used to ensure that the models developed were not overfitted.

The outcome of the study showed that machine learning approaches could accurately predict the mechanical properties of the scaffolds with moderate levels of reliability. Comparing various regression models, random forest regression was deemed to be the most suitable approach to predict the scaffold's mechanical properties due to its high accuracy and model generalization.
