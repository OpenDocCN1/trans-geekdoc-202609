## 3.5 Pros and cons of different synthesis methods

The synthesis of QDs involves the following methods, and each of the methods has its own merits and demerits. Learning every one of these methods and their relative advantages and disadvantages is critical to define which technique is better suited depending on the properties and/or applications required for the synthesis of a specific type of QDs.

### 3.5.1 Colloidal synthesis

- Versatility and control: QDs obtained by colloidal synthesis have strong size-, shape-, and composition-dependent properties that can be easily tuned by varying the reaction conditions like temperature, concentration of precursors, and reaction time. This versatility makes it possible to synthesize QDs with specific optical characteristics.
- Scalability: The overall colloidal synthesis process is not too cumbersome and hence makes it possible to synthesize QDs on a large scale for use in different applications.
- Surface functionalization: This approach also permits the alteration of QD surfaces with ligands and coatings, which help stabilize the QDs, as well as improving their solubility and compatibility with biological systems.
- Complexity of reaction conditions: Achieving uniform size and composition with a desired reaction is a matter of strict control of the reaction conditions and can be difficult due to batch-to-batch variability.
- Solvent use: While preparing colloids, it should be noted that organic solvents and stabilizing agents are toxic and thus need to be handled with concern due to their impact on the environment and safety of humans.

### 3.5.2 CVD

- High purity and uniformity: CVD techniques mentioned above create highly pure and uniform QDs, which are crucial for uses where the required optical properties are well defined.
- Scalability: The CVD method is appropriate for mass production where QDs require high quality for commercial use, especially in industrial applications.
- Complex equipment requirements: CVD requires specialized and costly instruments, and hence, start-up expenses are likely to be higher than in the other syntheses.
- Limited size control: Although CVD processes can achieve good coverage and size uniformity, it is easier to lose control over the size of the QD structures to be formed; therefore, CVD is useful only in size-control-related imaging applications.

### 3.5.3 MBE

- Atomic-level precision: MBE gives the best control over the growth and dimensions of QD layers and enables the synthesis of high-quality QDs with appropriate electronic and optical features.
- High uniformity: The method produces exceptionally small and monodisperse QDs, which are desired for reproducible applications.
- High cost and complexity: MBE is often complicated and not cheap, which means that such equipment is not available for all sorts of research fields. The process also requires a vacuum environment and stringent control over the deposition parameters.
- Slow deposition rates: MBE has a low deposition rate compared to other PVD techniques, particularly sputtering, and this makes processing time longer and throughput lower.

### 3.5.4 Hydrothermal and solvothermal synthesis

The most widely used methods among green techniques for the synthesis of metal and metal oxide nanoparticles are the hydrothermal and solvothermal syntheses.

- Simple and cost-effective: Hydrothermal and solvothermal processes are more straightforward, without the need for costly precursors and dangerous conditions to synthesize QDs using water-based solvents.
- Ability to produce various materials: These methods can be used to synthesize most kinds of QD materials, including those that are challenging to synthesize by other methods.
- Limited size control: The two main criteria, size and uniformity, are difficult to control accurately, which in turn causes fluctuations in the optical properties of the QDs.
- Safety concerns: These methods involve high pressure and temperature, resulting in a number of risks; mishaps may be prevented by practicing adequate safety measures and using protective gear.

### 3.5.5 Laser ablation

- High purity: Laser ablation techniques synthesize QDs with good purity because they use a technique that directly removes material from the target in a controlled manner.
- Flexibility in materials: It enables one to synthesize QDs out of any recoverable material, no matter how complex it is when using other methodologies.
- Low yield and scalability: Laser ablation in aprotic solvents offers the disadvantage of relatively small production of QDs when compared to other approaches for mass production.
- Complexity of equipment: The apparatus used for laser ablation is costly and intricate, which makes it somewhat restricted for some uses.

Consequently, depending on the application, each synthesis method for QDs exhibits unique advantages and disadvantages. The selection of the method depends on the features that are required in the QDs, the level of production, and available tools.

## 4 Quantum dot structures and variants

### 4.1 Core-shell quantum dots

One of the most popular functional forms of QDs are core-shell QDs due to the additional layers aimed at modifying their optical and electronic characteristics. These structures comprise a central body surrounded by concentric layers, each performing a key role that enhances the performance of the QDs.

#### 4.1.1 Structure and composition

QDs of the core-shell kind usually have a semiconductor core originally credited with optical properties, enveloped by a shell that not only tunes the core’s properties but also provides protection. The core is typically of a semiconductor material with preferred optical properties such as cadmium selenide or cadmium telluride. The shell is of a different semiconductor material, such as ZnS, which serves to tailor the electronic and optical properties of the core.

The shell serves multiple roles in enhancing quantum dot (QD) performance. Systematic control over the shell’s composition and thickness is crucial, as these parameters directly impact the QD’s emission wavelength, optical brightness, and long-term stability, making them critical design considerations for bioimaging and optoelectronic applications.

#### 4.1.2 Benefits of nano-core-shell structures

- **Enhanced photostability:** The outer shell helps shield the core QDs from external processes that can lead to their destruction, such as oxidation and degradation, and as a result enhances the photostable nature and stability of the dots. This is especially true in cases where a given application needs to acquire images over long periods in hostile environments.
- **Increased quantum yield:** The shell layer has been shown to passivate surface defects and suppress nonradiative recombination, and thus improves the quantum yield of the QDs. This results in enhanced and more efficient fluorescent light, which is needed for resolution- and detection-based applications.
- **Tunable optical properties:** The core-shell structures can also be designed to achieve extremely precise control of the optical characteristics of the material, such as emission wavelength and intensity. Researchers have thus been able to find ways of controlling the emission characteristics of QDs with the core and shell materials, as well as the thickness of the shell.
