# 2.1 Semisupervised learning approaches for anomaly detection

In a variety of real-world applications, first and foremost was the issue of the lack of labeled data, especially when anomalies are the exception and often hard to characterize. Semisupervised learning appears to be one of the best solutions to the problem as it incorporates advantages of both approaches from the group of supervised and unsupervised learning. Semisupervised learning happens when the models are trained with a little annotated data with a greater amount of unmarked data. This approach was a good compromise since it uses the little available labeled data to supervise the process, while at the same time utilizing the large amount of available unlabeled data, thus making it very useful in anomaly detection where labeling is very expensive.

In this method of semisupervised learning the algorithm focuses on learning the nature of the normal data and applying it on the unidentified portion to separate the anomalies. It was especially beneficial when labeled anomaly points are limited, and the model learns more from a big number of unlabeled data that was typical. Semisupervised methods are generally realized by a combination of clustering, classification method, and density estimation in order to differentiate the normal data from the unusual cases.

The most popular method of applying semisupervised anomaly detection was self-training where the first model was trained using a small labeled data set and was then iteratively refined with predictions of the same data from the unlabeled set. In the self-training process, the considered model labels the unlabeled data based on the current learned normal behavior and such pseudo-labeled instances are incorporated into the training data set.
