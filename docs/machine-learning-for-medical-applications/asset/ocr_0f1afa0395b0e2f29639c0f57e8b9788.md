In treatment planning, segmentation offers important data, which can be used in surgery and radiotherapy. Segmentation allows outlining the exact margins of tumors of interest to deliver treatment as effectively as possible, reducing harm to healthy tissues within the vicinity of the tumor. To enhance recovery from surgery with minimal impact of side effects originating from radiation therapy, this precision was important.

Medical image segmentation is useful in evaluating the progression of a particular disease as well as the effectiveness of a given treatment. Clinicians assess tumor size, shape, and treatment response, relying on time-sensitive observations derived from segmental image analysis when making critical decisions about modifying treatment modalities. Figure 2 illustrates this process, highlighting how segmented imaging supports clinical interpretation and decision-making. This capability also facilitates the continuous monitoring of chronic diseases and contributes to the development of personalized therapeutic strategies tailored to the unique needs of each patient.
