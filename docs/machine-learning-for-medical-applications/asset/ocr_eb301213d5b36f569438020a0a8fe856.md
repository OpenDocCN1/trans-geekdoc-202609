Preoperative planning and decision-making: Tactile physical models that are developed through 3D printing also enable surgeons to make preliminary assessments regarding several strategies and methods of operation before a surgery begins. Greater patient understanding boosts the patient enrollment process and is the essence of informed consent and patient satisfaction.

# 4.3 Bioprinting

When specifically focused on engineering biological tissues and organ architectures, bioprinting was a visionary application of 3D printing systems in medical science. In this revolutionary technique, cells and biomaterials are accurately placed, organizing active constructions that work like true tissues and organs. The tissue engineering, regenerative medicine, and personalized medicine industries all experience comprehensive transformations attributable to the advancement and utilization of bioprinting technology.

Principles of bioprinting: Similar to conventional 3D printing, it involves the layering of materials, but the fundamental material is living cells. It refers to the layering of bioinks, which are composed of cells mixed with a suitable material to create structures that are 3D in nature. These structures are designed to mimic the structural and functional features of real tissues. Different techniques involved in the fabrication of a tissue construct through the print head are used to deliver the desired resolution and accuracy, which include extrusion, laser, and inkjet technologies.
