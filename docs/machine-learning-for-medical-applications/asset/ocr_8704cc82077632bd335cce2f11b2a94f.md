This can be literally any ML algorithm; it could be a logistic regression and its task is to provide the best answer based on information provided by base models. Stacking is a very effective approach when used in the problem of anomalies detection as different models are trained on the different features of the data and yield their results through the stacking system in order to make the final prediction. In addition, it works best in applications from which we have to detect various types of anomalies that differ from each other and covers a broad spectrum of characteristics.

Another commonly used ensemble method for anomaly detection is called XGBoost; this approach is selected for its efficiency and ability to generate high predictive accuracy for large datasets. XGBoost, like boosting, constructs a sequence of W T competent learners, but it does this while including a measure of shrinkage, which is used to avoid overfitting and increase the model’s ability to generalize. Specially, XGBoost’s tree-based method is more suitable for high-dimensional and sparse data to detect complex patterns of anomalous features while at the same time efficient in terms of computation. The great freedom that this approach offers is to adapt XGBoost algorithms in various settings of an anomaly detection that can include missing observations.

Use of diversified ensemble methods for anomaly detection is advantageous over conventional single model methods in the following ways: first, increased detection rate since multiple views on source data are less likely to miss significant cases; second, improved resilience in that the influence of noise that led to the deterioration of single models can be reduced. For instance, one particular model that was erroneous in identifying the anomaly is corrected by other models in the group. Third, the same anomaly type can be detected by different models of different type, for example by tree-based models and NNs, which can substantially enhance their performance.

# 3.1 Random forests and gradient boosting for detecting visual anomalies

This becomes a problem because inconsistencies in visual data are difficult to uncover due to the inherent complexity and because images constitute a high-dimensional data set. Nevertheless, random forest and gradient boosting have posed significant results in handling this challenge. Such methods, which combine the result of multiple models, are good approaches to building reliable frameworks for the identification of visual anomalies in generic applications such as medical imaging, industrial defects inspection, and surveillance systems. As such, factors that normally do not appear important due to their low frequencies in the data are captured by these ensemble methods as the strengths of individual classifiers are added together, making it easier to track the often-subtle abnormalities that are missed by a single model.
