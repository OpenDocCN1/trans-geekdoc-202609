Normalization, also referred to as feature scaling, is one of the most important and mandatory steps in which characteristics of the given dataset are transformed into shared range values. This makes certain that each input is equally involved in the predictive modeling process as is particularly important when member characteristics of the dataset have different units. Z-score normalization and Min-Max scaling are common normalizing techniques that are used to normalize the given features.

Another common preprocessing technique employed specifically in scaffold design applications, when addressing the dimensionality curse and improving the computational efficiency, is dimensionality reduction. More data preprocessing in scaffold design, as strategies for improving the accuracy and the efficiency of the computational models that are used, are shown in the Figure 2: normalization, selection, and the reduction of the features. The goal of the methods, including the leading eigenvectors identified in the principal component analysis (PCA) and feature selection, is to determine the relevant characteristics of the corresponding dataset, while maintaining low noise and eventual redundancy. These methods help in making interpretations easier and also in boosting the predictive model performance by reducing the dimensionality of the dataset.

Numerical representations of categorical variables are worked out by machine learning algorithms by using categorical encoding techniques. These involve feature mapping methodologies such as label mapping, where a number is given to each category mapping, where for each feature, variable vectors of 0s and 1s are created. By using such encoding techniques, the categorical variables are encoded and are helpful in encoding exercises when doing predictive modeling tasks.
