# 3 Materials and synthesis methods

# 3.1 Semiconductor materials used (e.g., CdSe, CdTe, and Si)

QDs consist of semiconductor materials that determine their optical and electronics characteristics. The kind of semiconductor has direct implications on the emission wavelength, quantum yield, and overall performance of the QDs. Semiconductor substances: potential materials for QD application are CdSe, CdTe, and Si. Every material has its own benefits and properties to enforce different uses and applications.

# 3.1.1 Cadmium selenide (CdSe)

CdSe is also one of the most popular materials in the field of QDs among all types of semiconductors. CdSe QDs are distinguished by their highly efficient photoluminescence and wide absorption bands. CdSe QDs, which can be synthesized to emit light at a variety of wavelengths across the visible spectrum due to changes in particle size, allow for a great variety of imaging uses. Though the material, which is utilized here is cadmium which was a heavy metal and has environmental and health effects, it has led to more research being conducted to find other materials.

# 3.1.2 Cadmium telluride (CdTe)

The QD materials include CdTe, which is another broadly used semiconductor material. CdTe QDs are characterized by high photostability and the quantum yield, as well as the transition to the emission of light with different wavelengths is achieved with the help of the change in the size of the CdTe QDs. Of CdTe QDs, which can be used in productive applications, special attention is given to those that possess good absorption and emission characteristics for senses. Like in CdSe, the utilization of cadmium in QDs, specifically on CdTe, adds to the environmental and safety concerns, and hence researchers are looking for environmentally friendly replacements.

# 3.1.3 Silicon (Si)

In fact, Silicon QDs are less hazardous compared to the cadmium-related commodities. Si QDs are safe since the substrate material, silicon, is nontoxic and readily available, the former characteristic being of great significance in biomedical image enhancement. Generally, Si QDs have comparatively lower quantum yield and lesser control on photoluminescence emission as compared to CdSe and CdTe QDs.
