# 4 Hybrid deep learning architectures for complex anomaly detection

# 4.1 CNN and RNN hybrid models for spatiotemporal anomaly detection

The analyzed patterns of objects’ states in production, video surveillance, and climate observation, often occur at any point in time and space and are dependent on space and time. Being able to take these relations entails using models, which are capable of handling spatial and temporal data at the same time. CNNs are acclaimed for their capacity to capture spatial features in data, especially image-like data while recurrent NNs (RNNs) are known for their ability to capture sequential features in the data. The integration of these two architectures in a hybrid model has been proven to be an effective solution toward spatiotemporal anomaly detection model by developing benefits of CNN and RNN models toward improvement of detecting changes in anomalies that happen both in space and time.

CNNs are most commonly applied in extracting spatial features from vision data. In the context of anomaly detection, CNNs can indeed discover local pattern and texture of the data that serves as important departure for separating normal and abnormal regions. Figure 7 illustrates how CNNs are used to extract features from individual frames in video data that talk about statically spatial patterns that show irregularity. These fixed-size local patches allow CNNs to emphasize differences in pixel-level intensity, object contour, and spatial layout, all of which are also characteristic of the anomalies. Simple CNNs do not suffice to detect temporal changes because they do not model temporal dynamics by themselves.

LSTMs models are intended to work with sequential data since they carry information on preceding inputs from the previous time through recurrent connections. In the temporal data, especially for anomaly detection where the patterns of behavior change with time, these architectures are excellent in capturing long-range dependencies. For example, in the video surveillance, it detects the motion of objects with time, for instance, appearance of people in some areas is a model that can detect that something is not normal. While CNNs are limited by their inability to supply the temporal context needed for reliable anomaly detection in a spatiotemporal domain, the inherent temporal structure of RNNs makes them capable of offering this context. As powerful as RNNs are, there is a problem that they encounter long-term dependency issues if the temporal information is variable.

To obtain benefits of both representations, CNNs and RNNs can be linked together in a way, wherein CNNs provide the spatial image input to the RNNs to model temporal sequence. This approach enables the model to exploit both CNNs’ strong capability in recognizing small and complex spatial relations, as well as RNNs’ capacity to learn temporal relations.
