An important factor that defines the mechanical properties of the electrospun nanofibers is the diameter of the manufactured nanofibers. Generally, fibers with small diameters tend to have high tensile strength and stiffness but low flexibility and strain, rather than the large-diameter fibers. Other characteristics of the nanofibers and their structure, for instance, the alignment and orientation of the fibers, also have some influence on the mechanical properties. With fibers aligned, composites typically exhibit greater mechanical properties than the same composite with randomly oriented fibers. Such fibers are particularly useful for use in directional applications, for instance in extracellular matrices.

Properties of different polymers and their composites change when nanofibers are produced through electrospinning. For example, although both PLA- and PGA-based polymers are biocompatible, they possess unique mechanical properties that are appropriate for diverse biomedical applications. The use of inorganic nanoparticles, including silica nanotubes, can improve the mechanical properties of nanofiber polymer matrix. Such composites can present higher tensile strength, increased stiffness, and also greater resistance to shape change – all attributes that can make them ideal for use in situations where greater strength and durability are needed.

Mechanical properties of electrospun nanofibers are found to be highly dependent on the environmental conditions such as temperature and humidity. Higher temperature weakens the mechanical strength and preserves elasticity of the polymeric matrix of the nanofibers, while higher humidity changes the moistening and swelling characteristics of the fiber. In order to understand which properties the developed nanofiber materials should possess in order to maintain their performance under the influence of the environmental effects described above, it is essential to study the impact of these factors on the mechanical characteristics of nanofibers.

## 3.3 Porosity and fiber diameter

One essential characteristic of electrospun nanofibers that greatly affects their performance in a variety of applications is their porosity. During the electrospinning process, a network of linked voids is created by solvent evaporation and polymer jet stretching, which gives nanofibers their porous structure. For uses like wound dressings and tissue engineering, where it allows gas and nutrient exchange, the fiber imitates the ECM, and encourages cell infiltration and tissue development. Here, high porosity is essential. Higher porosity usually results in greater drug absorption and regulated release. This means that porosity plays an important role in the capability of the delivery devices to load drugs and release them.

The various parameters that affect the porosity of electrospun nanofibers include polymer concentration, solution viscosity, voltage, and flow rate. Lower concentrations of polymer and higher rates of solvent evaporation are seen to increase porosity due to the enhanced formation of fibers and larger voids. On the other hand, higher levels of polymer concentration and slower evaporation rates give denser fiber structures, presumably because of the smaller pore size. Further, the nature of the solvent and its rate of evaporation have a direct bearing on the porosity of the nanofibers. The values of these parameters could be adjusted to meet the target porosity for a given application, and hence fine-tuning of these parameters is critical.

Another important characteristic of electrospun nanofibers that directly influences their mechanical, physical, and biological properties is fiber diameter. Smaller fiber diameters typically result in higher surface-area-to-volume ratios, which enhance drug loading efficiency, facilitate better interaction with biological environments, and improve the mechanical behavior of scaffolds. These nanoscale dimensions are especially advantageous in tissue engineering, as they more closely mimic the architecture of the natural extracellular matrix (ECM), thereby promoting cell adhesion, proliferation, and tissue regeneration. In contrast, larger-diameter fibers are better suited for applications like wound dressings, where mechanical strength, durability, and moisture management are prioritized. Fiber diameter in electrospinning can be controlled by altering certain factors, namely, polymer concentration, solution viscosity, voltage, and collection distance. Thicker fibers are obtained by raising the polymer concentration, while finer fibers are obtained by raising the voltage by stretching the polymer jet. Another factor is solution viscosity: the higher the viscosity of the solution, the larger the fibers it produces, while lower-viscosity solutions provide finer fibers. These parameters are effectively controlled in a manner that gives the desired fiber diameter, as well as the ability to orient the nanofibers for certain uses.

Since porosity and fiber diameter are shown to influence the total efficiency of the electrospun nanofibers in various applications, the extent of the performance depends on both factors. For instance, in a tissue engineering scaffold, they have to be capable of supporting cell proliferation and the formation of tissues but at the same time also provide mechanical stability and porosity, and the diameter of fibers has to be balanced. Studies have identified that the rate and extent of drug release depend on the fiber diameter and porosity, where higher porosity and smaller diameter fibers provide a higher release efficiency of the drug. Comprehending and refining these attributes are crucial in order to create electrospun nanofibers with customized performance attributes for particular medicinal uses.

## 3.4 Biodegradability and biocompatibility of nanofibers

One key feature of electrospun nanofibers is their biodegradability, especially in clinical applications, where the relevant materials are expected to break down and be metabolized in the body harmlessly. It has been established that the type, chemical composition, and environment control the rate at which the nanofibers degrade. Biodegradable polymers degrade through enzymatic and hydrolytic mechanisms, making PLA, PGA, and PCL suitable materials to fabricate electrospun nanofibers. Polymer type, molecular weight, crystallinity, and the amount and type of additives control the rate of breakdown. These characteristics minimize foreign body responses after a long duration and also phase out surgery to remove the material because the fibers can dissolve themselves at a rate compatible with tissue healing requirements.

Another important characteristic of electrospun nanofibers is their biocompatibility, since these nanofibers are ideally suited for use in tissue engineering and as wound dressings. The ability of a substance to engage in reactions with biological systems and not produce unfavorable reactions is known as biocompatibility. The electrospun nanofibers must favor cell adhesion, proliferation, and differentiation, and on the same note, decrease inflammation and toxicity levels. Nanofiber biocompatibility depends on a number of factors such as fiber diameter, surface chemistry, and the presence of functional groups and active compounds. Given the relationships between nanofibers and biological tissues, in vivo animal studies and in vitro cell culture studies are often employed. The purpose is to ensure that the nanofibers used in medical applications are innocuous, noncytotoxic, and do not elicit an immune response so as to avoid undesirable outcomes.

The biodegradability and biocompatibility of electrospun nanofibers are highly dependent on the choice of the polymer. Biodegradable synthetic polymers include poly(ethylene glycol), PLGA, and PCL, while biocompatible natural polymers include chitosan, silk fibroin, collagen, and more. These polymers frequently aid in tissue regeneration and normal cellular functions.
