# 5.2.2 Brightness

Definition and importance:
Brightness is one of the attributes associated with the fluorescence emission intensity of QDs and is dependent on the quantum yield and excitation efficiency. Since sensitivity and resolution are critical for most practical applications, high brightness is required to guarantee that the emitted signal is strong enough to be distinguished from background noise.

Quantum yield and its role:
Brightness of QDs is attributable to another parameter recognized as the quantum yield. Fluorescence intensity represents the ability to transform the number of photons absorbed to fluorophore emission. High quantum yield QDs are desirable for applications such as multiplexed imaging assays where the signal intensity was a factor.

Size and composition effects:
The standards of emitted light can also be controlled taking into account the size and composition of the QDs. Lower quantum yield values occur when the size of the QDs is increased because larger QDs show additional nonradiative recombination processes. Moreover, the semiconductor materials used and the core-shell structures have an effect on the overall brightness of the QDs. In order to obtain the necessary brightness for applications, extent synthesis parameters were crucial for the compounds.

Comparative advantages:
In most cases, QDs can be brighter than old fashioned fluorescent materials. The analytes are more sensitive and have better signal-to-noise ratios attributed for their capability to fluoresce at certain wavelengths. This increased brightness again shows that QDs offer considerable advantages in transition states, which include early detection diseases and precise cell diagnostics.
