Convolutional layers: As integral components of CNNs, convolutional layers are designed to help learn the spatial hierarchies of features in images in an automatic and adaptive manner. They use kernels to convolve the input data and obtain the feature maps. One can use them to document local characteristics of complex structures, shapes, textures, edges, etc.

Pooling layers: These layers make them useful in reducing the complexity in processing as well as in minimizing the level of overfitting by reducing the size of the feature map. The most commonly used pooling process is known as max pooling, as the most obvious features are retained by selecting the largest value from a prespecified frame within a range of values.

Fully connected layers: These are layers where all the neurons within the layer above are connected to all other neurons. These layers are often applied later in the neural network implementation to introduce characteristics that have been learned by the previous layers and then generate the predicted results based on the characteristics that have been retrieved.

Loss functions: These mark the difference between ideal and real conditions and, as such, by characterizing errors, guide the training procedure. In the aspect of loss functions, mean squared error (MSE) is tied to regression, while cross-entropy loss is tied to classification. As for choosing the loss function, the decision depends on the final problem to be solved.

Optimization algorithms: These algorithms adjust the weights of a neural network in order to reduce the level of loss. Gradient descent is another method studied in the past in which weights are updated in a sequential manner through the gradient of the loss function. There are two forms that boost the model and the rate of convergence: Adam and stochastic gradient descent.

Methods of regularization: The methods to fight overfitting include dropout and L2 regularization, which are strategies for limiting penalties during the training process. Although dropout works during training by arbitrarily turning off some of the neurons to force the network to consider more reliable properties, L2 regularization introduces a term grounded on the square of weights in the loss function.

Hyperparameters: They are related to training parameters, dealing with aspects such as the learning rate and the number of epochs, among others. Extra caution was taken to ensure that the hyperparameters were tuned in a way that gives the best results from the model.

Backpropagation: This is a critical part of the training of neural networks. It is a process of calculating gradients of the loss function with respect to the weights of the neural network and using those gradients to update the weights. This approach consists of two main steps: a situation where forward propagation is used to determine the loss, while backward propagation is used to determine gradients and update the weights.
