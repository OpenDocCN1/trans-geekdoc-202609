The availability of big, labeled datasets to feed into deep learning models became more emphasized as the need for big data with labels increased. Deep learning techniques are less helpful in cases where there is a data deficit because it takes a lot of time and even money to gather and label such datasets in various fields.

Lack of explainability and interpretability: Due to the fact that they are black-box models, deep learning models are often seen as problematic. This lack of clarity about how models arrive at their conclusions and involve their decision-making process makes it difficult to gain insight into some of the models and is especially undesirable in cases where a certain explanation is necessary.

Risk of overtraining: It is common in deep learning models when they are built on very small datasets. While specific regularization strategies can mitigate this danger, it is challenging to guarantee that the models being built will apply well to new data.

Ethical issues: The majority of deep learning algorithms could encourage some form of stereotype, as was illustrated by the prejudice and moral concern hinted at in the previous section. To address these ethical dilemmas, prejudice identification, ways of reducing such biases, as well as the training of equal opportunity-oriented AI must be given profound thought.

As for the origin of neural networks in the domain of computer vision, it can be traced back to the 1980s, which was the start of the whole process of developing new methods in the sphere of image processing and, in particular, image recognition. Initial simple structures aimed at avoiding human thought and making computers recognize what we want, like perceptrons and multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), at the initiation stage depicted how to learn the representation from the imagery data. These models were helpful to illustrate that despite their shortcomings, neural networks were capable of providing good solutions for problems belonging to pattern recognition.
