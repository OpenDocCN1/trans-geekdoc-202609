A number of critical processes are involved in the use of medical imaging and processing (MIP) within the scope of personalized medicine. As illustrated in Figure 12, imaging plays a foundational role in delineating diseases such as identifying tumor types, determining cancer stages, and tracking disease progression. This diagnostic precision is essential for selecting the most suitable treatment methods, determining optimal dosage, and deciding on the appropriate route of administration. Imaging also enables the design of therapeutic interventions that specifically target affected areas while preserving surrounding healthy structures, thereby improving therapeutic efficacy. In surgical contexts, imaging aids in planning and execution by enhancing precision and minimizing risks. Likewise, in radiotherapy, imaging is used to define tumor boundaries and monitor treatment response, ultimately supporting the development of complex treatment plans that limit radiation exposure to healthy tissues.
