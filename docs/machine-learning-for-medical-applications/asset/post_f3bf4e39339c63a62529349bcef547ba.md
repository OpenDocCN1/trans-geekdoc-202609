
Another landmark work was ResNet, introduced by He and colleagues in 2015. To enable the training of much deeper neural networks without suffering from performance degradation, they introduced residual connections. This innovation set new benchmarks across multiple computer vision challenges and made it possible to train networks with hundreds of layers.

These milestones together signify the fast pace of growth of deep learning and prove that the field has the ability to learn and progress enormously. It goes on to show that deep learning enhancements persist in moving forward in their variety of fields, most notably in computer vision, reaffirming its stake as the ongoing revolution for AI.

## 2.3 Key concepts and terminology

### Neural networks
These are computer models developed based on the structure of the human brain, which were critical in the deep learning process. This neural network consists of hidden layers, output layers, and input layers of neurons. Essential concepts for understanding the more advanced techniques and concepts discussed in this chapter, along with their corresponding vocabulary, are illustrated in Figure 2. Such networks steer data through these layers, and every neuron receives inputs, calculates a weighted sum of data, and processes it through an activation function.

### Activation functions
The activation functions make the neural network more nonlinear, which is useful in the identification process exhibited by the system. Standard activation functions are sigmoid that limits the range of its input between 0 and 1, tanh that restricts the input from -1 to 1, and ReLU, where if the input is positive, it is returned as is, otherwise 0. Different functions possess distinct aspects regulating the performance and training of the network.

### Convolutional layers
As integral components of CNNs, convolutional layers are designed to help learn the spatial hierarchies of features in images in an automatic and adaptive manner. They use kernels to convolve the input data and obtain the feature maps. One can use them to detect local characteristics of complex structures, shapes, textures, edges, etc.

### Pooling layers
These layers are useful in reducing the complexity in processing as well as in minimizing the level of overfitting by reducing the size of the feature map. The most commonly used pooling process is known as max pooling, as the most obvious features are retained by selecting the largest value from a prespecified frame within a range of values.

### Fully connected layers
These are layers where all the neurons within the layer above are connected to all other neurons. These layers are often applied later in the neural network implementation to introduce characteristics that have been learned by the previous layers and then generate the predicted results based on the characteristics that have been retrieved.

### Loss functions
These mark the difference between ideal and real conditions and, as such, by characterizing errors, guide the training procedure. In the aspect of loss functions, mean squared error (MSE) is tied to regression, while cross-entropy loss is tied to classification. As for choosing the loss function, the decision depends on the final problem to be solved.

### Optimization algorithms
These algorithms adjust the weights of a neural network in order to reduce the level of loss. Gradient descent is a method studied in the past in which weights are updated in a sequential manner through the gradient of the loss function. There are two forms that boost the model and the rate of convergence: Adam and stochastic gradient descent.

### Methods of regularization
The methods to fight overfitting include dropout and L2 regularization, which are strategies for limiting penalties during the training process. Although dropout works during training by arbitrarily turning off some of the neurons to force the network to consider more reliable properties, L2 regularization introduces a term grounded on the square of weights in the loss function.

### Hyperparameters
They are related to training parameters, dealing with aspects such as the learning rate and the number of epochs, among others. Extra caution was taken to ensure that the hyperparameters were tuned in a way that gives the best results from the model.

### Backpropagation
This is a critical part of the training of neural networks. It is a process of calculating gradients of the loss function with respect to the weights of the neural network and using those gradients to update the weights. This approach consists of two main steps: one where forward propagation is used to determine the loss, while backward propagation is used to determine gradients and update the weights.

## 2.4 Differences between deep learning and traditional machine learning

Deep learning comes under the large family of machine learning, and both are disciplines of AI. The primary areas by which these approaches differ are the structure of the model, data handling, and learning capability.

Most of the traditional artificial neural networks are poorly organized and have fewer layers. Some of the most frequently used models are decision trees, support vector machines, and linear regression models. Most of the time, these models are used when other models do not have fewer interactions between the features, are less clear, and are easier to specify. On the other hand, deep learning models use DNNs, which are comprised of multiple layers that contain nodes. Compared to shallow models, deep learning models have depth by interpreting more intricate structures from complex data streams such as images and spoken language.

Feature engineering, a specific process in classical machine learning, consists of domain experts consciously selecting suitable features from raw data. It is time-consuming and also requires good expertise in the areas of the topics under discussion. However, deep learning applies this in a synthetic manner through a layered structure of the neural network. CNN removes the need for manually engineering features since DNNs can learn low-level features and then build high-level characteristics from these data through multiple layers. Deep learning should especially come in handy for processing high-dimensional data because it can automatically acquire the features needed.

Since most of them rely on features that are built with care to give a summary of the data, traditional machine learning models often produce a high level of accuracy with relatively small datasets. Failure to generalize over extensive and complex datasets if these models are not adjusted accordingly remains a challenge. Deep learning models often require a considerable amount of labeled data in order for them to work effectively. Thanks to the large dataset, deep learning networks obtain high-order representations that make them capable of generalizing well to unseen data. They also have significantly fewer computing demands than the so-called deep learning models. On the other hand, as deep learning models require numerous matrix operations for the training of DNNs, the training process in deep learning models necessitates specific hardware such as GPUs. These models are more computationally intensive. In return for the increased capability and accuracy that deep learning brought to the table, there was a price for it in the computational aspect.

## 2.5 Advantages and limitations of deep learning

### High performance on complex problems
Deep learning models perform very well on complex tasks that require working with large, high-dimensionality datasets. Significant progress has been demonstrated in some fields like speech recognition, picture identification, and natural language processing because of their ability to learn feature extraction from raw data.

### Feature extraction
Most of the described typical machine learning approaches involve feature engineering, but deep learning chooses interesting features on its own as part of the data preprocessing step. Therefore, this capability decreases the amount of time spent on preprocessing and domain knowledge, eliminating time wastage in the development process.

### Scalability and adaptability
Big data-based deep learning models have been adaptive to large data and occur along with advanced computational power. They are flexible, easily tunable, and scale up well with large quantities of data as more data is fed into the system. This scalability of the proposed model affords improvements in model accuracy and reliability over time.

### Flexibility across domains
In fact, deep learning is very versatile and can be applied in almost every field and discipline. Figure 4 summarizes the major strengths and weaknesses of deep learning. Based on the learning algorithm, deep learning can automatically extract features from the raw dataset and show high accuracy in large and complicated tasks. Deep learning has a high computational cost and requires a large amount of data. The reason that deep learning is applied to various types of tasks such as language translation, self-driving automobiles, and image and video analysis.

### Application-based continuous enhancement
Deep learning is an ever-evolving subject under constant research, with new architectures, approaches, and methods being introduced continuously. It continues, which also means improvements for the model and growth in the possibilities of creation and implementation.

### Resource-intensive computation
This is often required for deep learning models since the training of large networks is usually performed with large amounts of data, necessitating large amounts of memory and compute. For small businesses and apps that have limited hardware resources, this high demand could be a significant drawback.
