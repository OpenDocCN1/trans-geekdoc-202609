# 5.3.1 Quantum yield

Definition and measurement:

As illustrated in Figure 9, absolute photoluminescence quantum yield (PL Quantum Yield) is defined as the ratio of emitted photons to absorbed photons in a quantum dot (QD). This metric evaluates the efficiency of fluorescence emission and is typically expressed either as a percentage or on a scale from 0 to 1. A high quantum yield indicates that a substantial proportion of the absorbed photons are re-emitted as fluorescence, resulting in brighter signals. This increased brightness enhances detectability and is especially valuable in applications such as bioimaging, where strong signal clarity is critical.

Influencing factors:

The properties such as size, the type of material, and the chemistry at the surface of the QDs determine their quantum yield. Size of QDs is also an important parameter for determining electronic structure and optical properties – the lower size QDs give higher quantum yield because of the smaller number of nonradiative decay pathways. Material selection and synthesis procedure play a pivotal role in quantum yield since changes in these aspects are related to the fluorescence output. The general properties of surface coatings and functionalization depend on the quantum yield as a result of the modification of environment of the QDs and decrease in nonradiative losses.
