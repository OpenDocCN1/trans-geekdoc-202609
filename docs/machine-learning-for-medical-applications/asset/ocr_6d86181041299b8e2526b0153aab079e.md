This cycle leads to the improvement of the final device to a very precise model that would suit the intended application by meeting all the necessary needs.

In practice, 3D printing strengthens practical applications, optimizes geometric accuracy, helps to reduce tolerances, and combines several functions to increase medical device efficacy and reliability, leading to better patient treatment and more positive clinician outcomes.

With this capability, it provides several important advantages, resulting in improved functionality and the individualized character of medical solutions.

Customization for individual anatomies: Among the key advantages of 3D printing is the ability to build items for medicine that fit the individual, unique anatomy of every patient. Thus, many implants, prosthetics, and surgical tools and equipment can be made to order, solely based on the patient's CT scans. There is better patient satisfaction and clinical outcome experience when equipment is adapted to the needs of a specific patient.
