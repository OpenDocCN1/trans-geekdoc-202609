# 5.3.4 Measurement techniques

Spectroscopic methods:
Most times, quantum yield and photoluminescence efficiency of QDs are determined using spectroscopic methods. These methods include use of a standard calibrated light source to excite the QDs and measuring the emitted fluorescence in order to deduce the ratio of emitted to absorbed photons. Among standard methods, steady-state fluorescence spectroscopy and time-resolved fluorescence spectroscopy are widely used for the analysis of optical characteristics of QDs and their performance parameters.

Standardization and comparisons:
Quantum yields and photoluminescence efficiencies can only be measured with an acceptable degree of repeatability and reliability if the key parameters are fixed and defined and the results are expressed in terms of reference materials. When comparing different QD methods, it is important to do it in the same environment so that the results that are obtained are real and genuine. Standardization of measurement so often assists in rating and comparing the performance of any QD for specific uses.

# 5.4 Excitation and emission wavelengths

QDs have tunable photoluminescence properties that offer themselves well to applications in imaging and sensing. Considering their properties and versatility, the excitation and emission wavelengths of QDs are useful parameters when determining their efficiency and functionality. This section explains reasons for these wavelengths and their practical usage.

# 5.4.1 Wavelength dependence on size

Both excitation and emission wavelengths are size-dependent because of the quantum confinement effects characteristic of QDs. When the size of the QDs is reduced, the energy required for the transition from the valence band to the conduction band is higher in energy, thereby shifting the emission wavelength to the blue region of the spectrum. In contrast, larger QDs have more reward emission at longer wavelengths. This size-tunable property also enables the synthesis of QDs with emission across a broad range of colors needed in multiplexed imaging.
