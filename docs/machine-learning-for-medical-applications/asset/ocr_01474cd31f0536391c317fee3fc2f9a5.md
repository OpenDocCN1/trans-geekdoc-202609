# 6.3.5 Laser ablation

Low yield: Laser ablation can have very low QD yields that often poses a challenge when trying to generate large batches. The low yield still hinders the possibility of applying this method for commercial production for large industrial use.

Equipment and maintenance costs: Laser ablation is found to be expensive and the kind of equipment needed is intricate for the production of the required number of ablated patterns in volume quantities. Also, the usefulness of such equipment is outweighed by the costs of its maintenance as well as its operation.

Optimization of laser parameters: Controlling laser parameters and enhancing the efficiency in ablation lead to higher yield and make the process of scaling up easier to achieve. Another way of helping the model to be more scalable is to look at differing types of ablation and other materials.

# 6.4 Functionalization challenges

Surface modification of QDs is another important parameter that greatly improves its versatility to be used in different application areas like imaging agents, diagnostic tools, biosensors, etc. The functionalization process carries a number of issues that can affect the performance and applicability of QDs as follows.

# 6.4.1 Stability of functional groups

Chemical stability:
The issues involve the stability of the functional groups connected to the body of QDs. Functional groups should not be deleted and should be able to perform their tasks at any given period and under certain environmental conditions and stress. The chemical instability results in the detachment of the functional groups, and in turn affects the performance of the QDs. It is crucial to develop functional groups that would remain practically impenetrable to chemical reactions in the case of real-world use of QDs.
