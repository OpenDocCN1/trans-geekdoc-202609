# 5.4 Electrospun nanofibers for nerve tissue repair

Hailed as the most prevalent clinical problem, nerve injury and neurodegenerative diseases are still some of the biggest challenges in regenerative medicine today because the body's CNS has a very poor capacity to regenerate itself. Unfortunately, electrospun nanofibers present a potential strategy to bridge the gap of nerve tissue repair by supplying suitable ECM substitutes for neuron sprouting and regeneration. These nanofibers can be used to repair peripheral nerve injury and stimulate the otherwise scarcely treatable injuries in the CNS.

Electrospun nanofiber scaffolds are designed to possess the required structural and mechanical characteristics of the nerve tissue. Parameters that are important in the design are fiber diameter, fiber alignment, and pore size, all of which determine the responses of the cells and nerve regeneration. For instance, aligned nanofibers are known to direct axonal elongation and orientation in essentially the same way as their natural counterparts. These factors, combined with the ability to manipulate electrospinning parameters and choice of polymers, allow for a tailored nerve repair scaffold microenvironment.
