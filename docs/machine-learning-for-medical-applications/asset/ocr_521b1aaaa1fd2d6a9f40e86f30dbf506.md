Though significant advancements are driven by deep learning, numerous challenges remain in the field of computer vision. Hyperparameter tuning, for instance, continues to be a complex and computationally intensive process that must be carefully optimized to achieve maximum model performance. Model evaluation also presents its own difficulties, particularly in establishing a comprehensive set of metrics and reliable validation strategies to ensure that these results are robust and generalizable. The training and deployment of deep learning models must account for ethical considerations including bias and fairness. Continued investment was needed in these areas, with key goals being the improvement of the efficiency, interpretability, and fairness of models [24]. The future of computer vision will likely be shaped by ongoing innovations in hardware, software, and algorithm design, as current limitations are addressed and new frontiers are explored. Over the coming years, the capabilities and impact of computer vision are expected to grow substantially through further advancements in deep learning frameworks [25].

# 2 Overview of deep learning

# 2.1 Definition and scope of deep learning

Deep learning is a subset of machine learning that uses multilayered artificial neural networks, also referred to as deep neural networks (DNNs), for modeling and understanding complex patterns in data. Essentially, deep learning aims at implementing hierarchical representation of the data and automatically deriving features from raw data. This enables models to perform actions such as generation, prediction, and even classification with minimal to no intervention from human beings at all.

Deep learning has found widespread application across numerous domains due to its ability to process massive amounts of data and uncover complex correlations within them. The fundamental concepts, key methodologies, and primary application areas of deep learning are illustrated in Figure 1. In the field of computer vision, tasks such as object detection, image segmentation, and classification have been significantly transformed by deep learning approaches. Unlike traditional methods, which often rely on surface-level features extracted from raw data, deep learning algorithms are capable of identifying fine-grained patterns such as the orientation and location of microfeatures across diverse datasets. This was achieved through multiple layers of neural networks that allow for increasingly abstract and accurate representations of input data.
