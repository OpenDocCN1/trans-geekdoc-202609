With the use of DL-based segmentation algorithms in healthcare facilities, a number of problems and questions arise. One issue is determining how these models can be generalized and effective across a wide cross section of patients as well as across different types of imaging technology [7, 19, 20]. Despite that, when applied to different clinical scenarios, the models derived from specific datasets proved to exhibit lower accuracy, and need significant tweaking to adapt. In addition, since physicians need to trust and understand the decision-making of DL models, interpretability was essential for therapeutic use of these models. It was necessary to introduce techniques that would help explain the model in order to increase confidence and trust in such tools [21]. More research and work were needed to make DL-based segmentation tools more effective and fairer in clinical use as well as ensure the compliance with regulations and ethical principles such as data privacy and model bias [22, 23].

# 2 Definition and scope of medical image segmentation

# 2.1 What is medical image segmentation?

Medical image segmentation is the procedure to split a medical picture in parts with appropriate area and/or segments that are directly associated with an organ of the disease. This procedure involves identifying and outlining some aspects of the image like lesion or tumor of clinical interest. The main purpose of segmentation was to decentralize an image's representation so that it could be analyzed and explained for research and therapeutic purposes.

Segmentation can be done in a number of ways, ranging from fully manual to fully automated methods, as well as semi-automated techniques that balance between the two. Manual segmentation, though sometimes cost-efficient, introduces operator bias and may compromise performance consistency. In contrast, automated methods improve both precision and speed by mimicking human cognition through algorithmic logic. Segmentation in medical imaging is important for dealing with a myriad of issues. It allows for quantification of the size of body parts, determination of the stage of a disease, and reconstruction of a surgical operation plan.
