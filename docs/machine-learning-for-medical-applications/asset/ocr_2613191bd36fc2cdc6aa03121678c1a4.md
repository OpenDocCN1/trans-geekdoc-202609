# 4.3.1 Integrating genomics and imaging information

Since genomics data has to be used in combination with medical imaging data, complex data fusion strategies are needed. Genomic information is usually characterized as a vector of genetic markers from the SNP’s data, gene expression profiles, and mutation maps, while medical imaging data is formed by visual representations of the anatomical structures of some organs. Hence, integration methods include the alignment of genomic features with image data on a common biological process. Differentiation strategies that can be used in the context of the present work include feature fusion, where genomic and imaging features are pooled and represented by a set of features, where two models for genomic and imaging data are developed and then combined.

This integration also makes it easier to detect certain abnormalities that cannot be discerned based on one’s naked eyes, hence enhancing early diagnosis and identifying right interventional procedures.

# 4.3.3 Applications and case studies

Several examples show that the integration of genomic data and medical imaging is beneficial. In oncology, as an instance, the harmonization of genomic profiles with tumor imaging can show relationship between certain genetic changes and the nature of tumor, thus improving cancer diagnosis and management. Likewise, in neuroimaging, the results of key neuroimaging predictors of neurodegenerative disease can be integrated with genomic data to determine the corresponding genetic markers of disease progression and pathology. This inventory indicates that integration of this fifth modality with other medical imaging procedures can improve diagnostic performance and foster a greater understanding of disease processes.

Handling and computing complex, diverse, and disparate data has always been a challenge and is one of the problems faced during integration of genomic and imaging data.
