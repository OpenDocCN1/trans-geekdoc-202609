### 3.2.2 Solvothermal synthesis

Solvothermal synthesis includes treatment of the precursor materials by undergoing reaction in a high temperature and pressure solvent solution. This technique enables the fabrication of QDs of different materials and geometries: core-shell and alloyed QDs. Solvothermal method has the advantage that the growth kinetics and morphology of the QDs can be closely controlled to produce high-yield, uniform, and high-quality products. The problem of synthesizing QDs with desired property can be solved by changing the reaction conditions for solvothermal synthesis; the synthesized QDs can be applied in imaging and diagnostic techniques.

### 3.2.3 Hydrothermal synthesis

Hydrothermal synthesis was therefore a sub-category of solvothermal synthesis in which water solutions were used. This technique is characterized by the reaction of precursors under high temperature and high pressure in an aqueous solution. There is a special use of hydrothermal synthesis in the preparation of water-soluble QDs and in production of QDs, which needs biocompatible surface modification. This method allows for size and shape control of the QDs and also for functionalization of the surface of the QDs for conjugation of specific functional groups for targeted imaging undertaken for QD synthesis, making hydrothermal synthesis more appropriate for synthesizing QDs for use in biological systems and for in vivo imaging.

### 3.2.4 Reverse micelle method

The reverse micelle process using a surfactant as a stagnant cage allows the synthesis of QDs at the nanoscale. Micelles are used here for the formation of nanoscale reactant compartments in a nonaqueous media by using surfactants. These precursors are then added into these micelles where they react to form QDs. Compared to the other methods, the reverse micelle method provides a strong access control to the size and monodispersity of the QDs and any application that involves the particle size and the picture will be consistent. This technique is used when synthesizing QDs with multiple surface chemistries for different imaging systems. Altogether, these techniques play a role in enhancing the QD technology and related fields in medical imaging.

## 3.3 Chemical vapor deposition

CVD is another common method of QD synthesis as the technique provides a good opportunity to obtain high-quality nanoparticles with a defined size, shape, and composition. The process in this method includes the condensation of vaporized chemical precursors onto a substrate and subsequent reaction to form solid QDs. The CVD process has certain advantages and particular features that make it appropriate for QD formation.

### 3.3.1 Growth of gallium arsenide as a foundation of chemical vapor deposition

CVD process is initiated by the supply of gaseous precursors into a reaction tube. Such precursors are often represented by metal-organic compounds gases containing the elements necessary for QD formation. On heating, the precursors decompose on the substrate surface to form the QD colloids. Deposited material sticks to the substrate and then is subjected to further treatment for its intended size and characteristics.

### 3.3.2 Chemical vapor deposition (CVD) advantages

The advantages of using CVD with respect to the synthesis of QDs are the following. The first advantage is achieving high purity and uniformity of the subsequently obtained QDs. The CVD chamber provides an enclosed atmosphere where deposition conditions, such as temperature, pressure, and precursor flow can be regulated in order to obtain reproducible QD characteristics. Further, it is also established that CVD can produce QDs of a narrow size distribution and well-defined geometries, which are important for the predetermined optical and electronic characteristics of the compound.

Various types of CVD techniques are employed for QD synthesis, including:

- **Metal-organic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD):** In MOCVD, metal-organic precursors are introduced to grow self-assembled QDs on a substrate. This technique is especially suitable for realizing II-VI and III-V semiconductor QDs including the CdSe and InP materials. This method provides a highly accurate means of controlling QD size and composition since the ratio of metal to organic components can be varied.
- **Hydride vapor phase epitaxy (HVPE):** HVPE uses hydrides such as arsine and phosphine for the reaction with metal precursors to create QDs. This technique is widely applied to the synthesis of high-quality III-V semiconductor QDs. By offering high growth rates and superior crystalline qualities, HVPE is especially well-suited for the mass production of a large number of QDs.
- **MBE:** MBE is a very careful CVD technique in which molecular beams of elemental sources are aimed toward a substrate in a chamber under vacuum. This method enables the controlled epitaxial growth of QDs and is employed to build structures with varying thicknesses on a nanometer scale. MBE is particularly useful in the epitaxy process of QDs with sharp interface and thickness variations.

### 3.3.3 Challenges and considerations

Although CVD is a mature technique in synthesizing QDs, there are several drawbacks and challenges that must be overcome. Another difficulty is the control of process characteristics that allow for the proper deposition on the substrate plane and the required QD characteristics. Fluctuations in the temperature of the system, pressure, and the flow of the precursors also affect the size and the shape of the produced QDs. Further, one potential weakness is the high costs of equipment for maintaining a stable reaction environment. Another concern is to make the operations effectively scalable for large-scale production through CVD methodology.

It is now possible to enumerate the uses of these CVD-synthesized QDs: QDs produced by CVD methods are used in different spheres: medical diagnostics, optoelectronics, and environmental analyses. The characteristics of high quality and narrow size distribution of CVD-synthesized QDs combined with better reliability of imaging provide better resolution and sensitivity to the fluorescence imaging. CVD techniques allow manufacturing QDs with desired optical properties for use in sensors and LEDs.

## 3.4 Molecular beam epitaxy

MBE is a complex method utilized in the synthesis of QDs that provides an innovative means to control the size and composition of the synthesized nanostructures and its structural qualities well. This method is characterized by growing atomic beams on a substrate under ultra-high vacuum and ensures high-quality semiconductor with quite a uniform profile.

### 3.4.1 One of the principles of molecular beam epitaxy

The fundamental idea of MBE practice is the method in which material atoms are deposited on a heated substrate. Employing the ultra-high vacuum, each of the constituent material requires a source for the production of atomic beams. These beams are focused on the substrate where it is condensed to form a thin layer. Growth process is also in-situ and techniques like reflection high-energy electron diffraction are used to get real-time surface morphology and film quality of the resulting films.

The selection of elemental composition and materials in MBE is often guided by practical considerations including thermal conductivity, mechanical stability, and compatibility with integrated systems for purposes such as ventilation, lighting, heating, and sound insulation.
