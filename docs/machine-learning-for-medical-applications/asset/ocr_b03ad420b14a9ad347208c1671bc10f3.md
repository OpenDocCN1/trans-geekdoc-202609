# 5.3.2 Uses of segmentation in disease diagnosis and treatment management

One of the most critical segments of numerous medical applications is segmenting. Tumor segmentation is critical for oncology diagnosis, treatment procedure planning, and assessing reaction to therapy. The separation of cardiac structures was important in the practice of cardiology both for strategy and in the assessment of function. In neurology, tissue segmentation benefits in determination of availability and assessment in disease progression of neurological diseases. Segmentation is also successful in facilitating accurate measuring of anatomical structures that enhance the efficiency in clinical evaluations and therapies.

# 5.3.3 The difficulties and opportunities of medical image segmentation

Some issues that present themselves when carrying out medical image segmentation include: fluctuations in the quality of the images acquired, image noise, and variability in the structures of different patients. There are such methods as advanced filtering, preprocessing, and post-processing, which can decrease these issues. Combining so-called multimodal data, in which data from different imaging modalities are used, also enhances the segmentation performance. Current active research works include the enhancement of better and efficient algorithms and the massive annotated dataset to handle these issues effectively.

The need for MIP is due to the fact that image processing sometimes necessitates that specific features such as a particular region of an image are collected from the complex image data. Segmentation techniques are being constantly refined due to the advances in machine learning and increased algorithmic efficacy; opportunities for diagnostics and therapeutic intervention exist widely across the medical field.

# 5.4 Image registration

One important concept, which was developed in the context of MIP, was image registration, which means matching of several pictures of the scene obtained at different time moments, or made from a different angles, or with the help of different imaging modalities. However, before comparing and analyzing the images, image registration process is designed to align the pictures to the same coordinate system. This procedure was crucial for working through various clinical trials, fine-tuning diagnosis, and fusing the data of multispectral imaging.
