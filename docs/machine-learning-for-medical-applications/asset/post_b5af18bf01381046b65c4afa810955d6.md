Making sure that the injury site is moist is important for healing and for minimizing scarring as well. Nanofibers that are electrospun can absorb exudate and at the same time modulate the moisture contents at the wound site, resulting in cellular migration and proliferation. The multifarious nanostructure of the formed nanofiber matrix provides protective covering to the cells and tissues from toxin attack, while maintaining diffusion-limited transport of oxygen/nutrient supply. Thus, biodegradable nanofibers not only protect wounds from drying up but also help the human skin to heal fairly quickly, independent of external help.

To accelerate tissue regeneration, biodegradable nanofiber scaffolds can be loaded with relevant growth factors, cytokines, and therapeutic agents. The controlled release of these bioactive compounds plays a crucial role in enhancing cellular functions essential to wound healing, such as collagen deposition, angiogenesis, and fibroblast proliferation. The nanofiber matrix acts as both a physical support and a biochemical stimulus, promoting organized tissue repair. Moreover, targeted modulation of the inflammatory response by selectively suppressing excessive inflammation helps prevent fibrosis and reduces the likelihood of scar formation. This localized therapeutic approach creates an optimal microenvironment for tissue regeneration. ## 1 Introduction

Biodegradable scaffolds offer critical support to tissue engineering by providing a short-term mechanical framework for tissue healing [1]. These scaffolds imitate the extracellular matrix, which helps cells to grow, develop, and synthesize new tissue [2, 3]. They gradually atrophy and are replaced by newly formed tissue, thus making surgical intervention less likely and reducing the risk of chronic detrimental effects [4-6]. The successful implementation of biodegradable scaffolds is vital for the advancement of regenerative medicine, as it addresses the need for compatible, resorbable materials that integrate seamlessly with the body's natural processes [7].

Traditional experimental approaches to assessing scaffold degradation involve time-consuming and resource-intensive procedures [8]. These methods often include in vitro studies, animal models, and clinical trials, each providing valuable but limited insights into the long-term behavior of scaffolds [9-12]. Experimental trials can be constrained by factors such as sample size, variability in experimental conditions, and the duration required to observe degradation [13]. These limitations highlight the need for complementary approaches that can offer a more comprehensive understanding of scaffold performance over extended periods [14-18].

Predictive machine learning models offer a transformative approach to overcoming the limitations of traditional methods by providing advanced analytical capabilities [19]. These models utilize algorithms to analyze large datasets, identifying complex patterns and relationships that are not readily apparent through experimental methods alone [20]. Machine learning models can simulate various degradation scenarios, predict long-term behavior, and optimize scaffold design parameters [21-23]. This approach enables researchers to make data-driven decisions and enhance the precision of predictions regarding scaffold stability and functionality.

The efficacy of predictive models was heavily dependent on the quality and integration of diverse data sources [24]. Data collected through longitudinal studies, experimental data collected, and clinical observations are equally useful in the development of the model as the information gathered is compatible. Extensive information from multiple resources provides a more comprehensive understanding of scaffold behavior in terms of composition, degradation, and biological performance. Data integration and analysis tools and methods are critical in feeding this information to ensure that models are built and developed from large and diverse sets of data [25].

This notwithstanding, there are several factors that have not been well addressed in efforts to enhance the performance of biodegradable scaffolds based on modeling. Problems such as data quality, data consistency, and validation requirements have remained as major determinants of the effectiveness of these models to date. Future studies should redirect the effort to solving these problems through improving the methods of data collection and analysis, improving the predictive algorithms in scaffold design, and expanding the applications of machine learning in scaffold design.
