# 3.4.4 Robotics and drone uses

Application of U-net occurs in robotics and drones, where it is used for object detection and scene understanding. For example, U-Net has been used for objects and obstacles in images taken by drones for environmental monitoring, search and rescue operations, and precision agriculture. The details of the characteristics of the scene in practical robotics applications are provided by the U-Net, which helps in object manipulation and navigation. This unveils the robot's prospects in acing the mechanical interplay of operations with the surrounding space in order to accomplish intricate tasks independently.

# 3.5 Challenges in U-Net architectures

Although the U-Net design has become quite popular for its usage in biomedical image segmentation, it faces challenges. Despite the fact that U-Net has the encoder-decoder structure with skip connections and experiments prove its success to gather characteristics that are fine-grained and high-level at the same time, there are several challenges that influence how effectively U-Net works in different cases. It would be important to understand these hurdles toward building improved models and toward raising the level of segmentation.

# 3.5.1 Time and space complexity and time requirements

The main drawback associated with the proposed U-Net is that it has a very high computational cost. The number and depth of convolution layers, as well as the expenditure of feature maps, make the model demand high computational and memory resources, especially when used for high-resolution image data. Such computation becomes costly in terms of resources when applied in environments where there is limited availability of systems, hence calling for optimization solutions to handle such a challenge.

# 3.5.2 Overfitting and generalization issues

U-Net's ability to achieve high accuracy on training data does not always translate to superior performance on unseen data. Overfitting remains a concern, particularly in scenarios with limited annotated datasets, which are common in biomedical imaging.
