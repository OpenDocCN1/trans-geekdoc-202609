To bridge the gap between experimental measurements and computational predictions, multiscale modeling techniques are employed. These methods integrate information across various hierarchical levels from molecular and atomic scales to macroscopic structural dimensions capturing the complex behavior of scaffold materials more accurately. For instance, microstructural imaging data from experimental scaffolds can be fed into computational simulations to enhance the accuracy of predictions related to degradation behavior and mechanical performance. By incorporating detailed experimental observations into simulation inputs, multiscale modeling enhances the fidelity and reliability of computational outcomes. This integrative approach is illustrated in Figure 5, which demonstrates how different scales of material characterization are linked to build comprehensive and predictive scaffold models.

Machine learning plays a crucial role in how experimental and computational data are combined, where patterns and correlations that are not easily recognizable can usually be detected. Supervised learning, which involves the use of labeled experimental data, and unsupervised learning, which examines inherent structures in data, are other ways through which the integration of datasets can be achieved. It is also possible to use machine learning to enhance the prediction of scaffold behavior through pattern extrapolation between experiments and computational simulations.

Both merging and cleaning data are important in that they allow for the creation of one data set from two different data sets. For this reason, tactics like the cross-validation procedure and sensitivity analysis tests are used to ensure the accuracy and stability of integrated data.
