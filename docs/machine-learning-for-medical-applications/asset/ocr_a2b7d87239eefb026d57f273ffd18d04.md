Despite these shortcomings, thresholding was a fundamental step in image segmentation and was useful as a basis for more sophisticated approaches in applications that require simplicity and speed.

# 3.2 Region-based methods

Region-based approaches are also one of the main types of conventional image segmentation techniques, which partition an image based on certain criteria for pixel values and spatial relations within the picture. These techniques are based on the assumption that some attributes such as difference in intensity of texture have to be used to determine within a picture.

# 3.2.1 Area development

One of the simplest techniques used in segmentation was called region growing; this procedure begins by segmenting several seed points from images. These seed points are then enlarged multiple times by the algorithm, in a neighboring pixel that meets several criteria such as texture. When the entire image was separated into sections that meet the criteria, this process was continued. Region growing was particularly effective for partitioning regions that shared similar properties, but most times, were unable to handle clusters that displayed a lot of variation.

# 3.2.2 Region merging

From this we see that region merging techniques work on the principles obtained from segmentation where adjacent regions are combined based on resemblance. Originally, the image was partitioned into an overpartitioned map of small segments that are gradually merged by using features like intensity, texture, and statistical measures. During the merging process segmentation improved and resulted in much larger and more whole areas. Although region merging was useful in grouping small segments into sensible structures, its applicability heavily depends on the initial segmentation and needs a proper tuning of merging criteria in many cases.
