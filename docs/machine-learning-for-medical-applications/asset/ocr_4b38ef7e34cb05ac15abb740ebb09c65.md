# 2.5 Pioneering studies and breakthroughs in medical 3D printing

Specifically, the development of medical 3D printing has been influenced greatly by groundbreaking studies, which set the basis for modern uses in the literature. Several early case studies have been proof-of-concept studies that have established the possibility and versatility of additive manufacturing in the medical field.

Dr. Carl Deckard remains one of the most celebrated pioneers of medical 3D printing from the 1980s, when he invented the SLS technique. This breakthrough enabled people to form concrete structures from powdered materials that are both complex and robust compared to traditional processes. In 3D printing, Deckard created a foundation that would later be useful in the development of complex medical devices and implants produced through the use of this technology.

The second major new day was marked in the early nineties with the introduction of SLA technology by Dr. Chuck Hull. SLA, which was invented by Hull, enabled the right alignment of intersecting photopolymer resins to create complex 3D objects. In the medical field, this technique is quite helpful for creating accurate anatomical models and surgical tools.

At the beginning of the new millennium, researcher Dr. Anthony Atala and his team at the Wake Forest Institute for Regenerative Medicine hit the headlines. By integrating and optimizing 3D printing technology to generate vascularized tissue constructs that biomimicked natural tissues, they achieved an important developmental landmark. This study demonstrated that bioprinting holds great promise for generating tissue and organ models for research and therapy.

Other advancements made in the 2010s include the application and use of 3D printing in crafting customized medical implants. One of the most groundbreaking projects led by Dr. Vladimir P. Zharov in 2014 involved the development of 3D-printed cranial implants for reconstructive surgery of complex skull defects in patients. While advocating for the adoption of 3D printing in surgeries because of its effectiveness in creating custom implants that fit the patient's anatomy, this study showed how surgery positively impacted patient recovery rates.
