Quantum yield:
Fluorescence quantum yield defined as the ratio between the emitted photons and absorbed photons is QD size-dependent. There is evidence that smaller QDs have higher quantum yields, as it is less likely for them to undergo a nonradiative decay. The quantum yield is reduced sharply in very small QDs for increased surface effects and nonradiative recombination. Control of size to achieve high quantum yield together with other favorable characteristics is crucial to its application in imaging and sensing.

Photostability:
This also has implications on the photostability of QDs, or, in other words, their ability to be photobleached. In general, larger QDs are more photostable than QDs of small size. This increased stability is believed to be linked to a relatively lower surface area-to-volume ratio, thereby negating the effects of surface anomalies such as oxidation on the materials. But, the size effect on photostability has to be correlated with other optical properties for service lifetime of the samples in practice.

# 5.5.2 Surface modification effects

Surface coating and ligands:
The optical characteristics of QDs are mainly affected by surface coatings and ligands. Fluorescence intensity and, emission spectra of the aptamer can be impacted based on the type of surface coating used. The use of a coating material can influence one of these characteristics: fluorescence intensity; emission spectrum; photostability. Such organic ligands would improve solubility and cut back on nonradiative losses; silica in inorganic coatings improves the stability of QDs and prevents degradation. This indicates that varieties of coating types and thickness can impact the light incidence and absorption on the optical properties of QDs.

Surface passivation:
It has been discovered that surface passivation is an important step in minimizing surface defects and improving the optical quality of QDs. By creating a suitable ligand, the surface nonradiative recombination of charge carry out can be suppressed, thus increasing the quantum level and photostability. Passivation also plays a critical role in the determination of emission properties because it shields the core of the QD from undesirable environment factors that change its optical characteristics.

Functionalization effects:
The conjugation of biomolecules groups onto the surface of QDs affects their emission properties due to new interactions or/and changes in the surrounding milieu.
