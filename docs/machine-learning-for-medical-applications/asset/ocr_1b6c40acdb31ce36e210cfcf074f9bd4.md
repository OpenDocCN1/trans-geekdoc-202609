Median filtering: Though initially employed as a reliable technique for eradicating salt-and-pepper noise from images, median filtering has lately received a great deal of application in medical images in enhancing image quality without blurring edges.

# 2.3.2 A review of newer image segmentation techniques

Region growing and splitting: Local base on region segmentation approach that includes region growing/merging and region splitting was completed in the 1970s and 1980s. These methods group images according to the similarity measures defined in homogeneity criteria and prove to be useful for drawing structures in medical images.

Active contour models (snakes): Active contour models, or snakes, introduced by Kass et al. in 1988 are one of the most popular techniques for image segmentation by minimizing energy forces and allowing a contour to evolve along object boundaries. It has been widely used in the field of medical image segmentation especially the delineation of anatomical structure.

# 2.3.3 Improved methods of reconstruction

Filtered backprojection (FBP): Of all developments that arose in the course of the progress of CT, the FBP algorithm, which originated in the 1970s, is credited for offering a technique for reconstruction of images from projection data. FBP still continues to be widely used to reconstruct images from the projection data in computerized tomography.

Iterative reconstruction techniques: In the 1990s and 2000s, iterative reconstruction algorithms were introduced to solve the problem of quality of clinical images and artifacts. These methods successively update the estimate of an image and are currently used in clinical trials to improve the accuracy of diagnoses.
