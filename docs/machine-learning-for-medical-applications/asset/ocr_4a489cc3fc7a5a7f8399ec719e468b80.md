# 5.1.1 Color tuning techniques

Size control during synthesis:
One of methods of tuning emission color is through adjustment of QD size during synthesis. By carefully controlling the reaction conditions, for example, temperature of the reaction, concentration of precursors, and time of reaction, it is possible to obtain QDs with required size and in turn conjugate them with the specific emission wavelength. This method can be used independently for simple and direct means of obtaining color across the visible spectrum.

Composition engineering:
Local control can also be applied in QD material whereby modification of the composition of the material can change the emission spectra. For example, altering the proportions of some elements of core can move the emission wavelength. In this approach, the QDs are incorporated in differing compositions in order to attain a set of colloids and fine-tune their performance attributes.

Post-synthesis modification:
Surface passivation treatment is also used on the synthesized QD samples to alter the emission wavelength. For instance, incorporating specific ligands the surrounding surface can cause the emission wavelength to be tuned and improve color stability. Any of these changes can be applied to satisfy specific needs of certain uses of optical characteristics of QDs.

# 5.1.2 Performance metrics

Quantum yield:
Fluence efficiency: Fluence yield is another performance parameter for quantifying the extent of emitted photons to the number of photons absorbed by the photocatalyst. High quantum yield means that majority of the absorbed energy is turned to emitted light. High quantum yield QDs give out brighter and intense emission, which is crucial in cases where sensitivity and resolution are required.

Photostability:
Photostability is measured in terms of the capability of QDs to exhibit the same performance in terms of emission intensity and lifetimes of the excited state for a large number of excitation cycles of the specific light. The high photostability of QDs means that they have a very low level of photobleaching and therefore make reliable and constant optimized imaging over long periods. This property is significant in applications where constant instances of light exposure are necessary.
