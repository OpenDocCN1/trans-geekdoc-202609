# 6.1.1 Toxicity of quantum dots

Chemical composition and potential risks:

QDs typically made from such materials as CdSe, PbS are hazardous if spread in the surroundings in a biological system. Cadmium and lead could be the most worrisome because they are toxic to human health and carry health risks in the long run. The emission of such materials from QDs by degradation is a big threat to human health and the environment.

Cellular and systemic toxicity:

QDs used in biological applications can be toxic in size, coating, and concentration, and in other biological applications. Cellular damage results from the internalization of QDs by cells and it induces an oxidative stress, the formation of reactive edema of cell functions. Another issue for the in vivo application of QDs is the systemic toxicity; QDs are thought to accumulate in organs and thus produce negative impacts. Mitigating and quantifying these toxic impacts are important for the future use of QDs in biomedical applications.

Potential for accumulation and bioaccumulation:

The ability of QDs to accumulate and bioaccumulate in living beings are also other issues of interest. QDs are found to be stable in biological systems and this results in longer circulation and slow, continuous release of hazardous substances. Accumulation in the organs would lead to continued effects and health hazard after a long time. To overcome these risks, it is crucial to make certain that QDs are biocompatible and biodegradable in design.

# 6.1.2 Environmental impact

Environmental release and persistence:

The discharge of QDs into the environment through industrial applications, misuse of QDs, and their degradation can cause environmental pollution. There is a concern of whether the QDs will not degrade in the environment in which they are placed, especially if they are not biodegradable. Here, it is stipulated that persistent discharge of QDs can build up on soils and in water and affect wildlife and the balance of ecosystems.
