# 6.2 Techniques for cross-validating data from different sources

The verification of data obtained from different sources was considered one of the basic approaches to increasing the reliability and accuracy of the obtained research results and outcomes in general, especially within biodegradable scaffold investigations. Therefore, if data from different experimental configurations and clinical studies are compared, inconsistencies can be detected, the results can be verified, and the conclusions made are less likely to be questioned. Cross-validation enables the reduction of biases, verification of data, and enhancement of the overall generalizability of a study's findings.

Comparative analysis can be defined as the regular process of comparing different datasets from different sources in an attempt to check for similarities and differences. This technique involves bringing different formats of data, normalizing processes, and units of measurement into proper comparison. Different datasets hold the same variables and outcomes but are collected from different research studies; by performing the comparison of such items, researchers can identify and compare the outcomes. For example, degradation rates studied at the laboratory level can be compared with the ones presented in clinical research to ensure their conformity.

It was by means of cross-validation of data from various sources that correlation analysis, regression analysis, hypothesis testing, and related tools were used. Simple correlation examines the similarity of variables across various data sets; complex correlation examines consistency in the prediction of a model in diverse data sets. Testing of hypotheses was useful in identifying if the differences observed in the datasets are real or variations at random. All these statistical techniques offer numerical indices of data accuracy and consistency.

Data merging and accretion methods join multiple datasets into one, which serves as a benchmark for the end dataset. Approaches like data fusion, data combining, and data integration are employed for integrating data, which helps in providing a conclusive detection of the validity of data. For instance, superposing the experimental results with computational predictions shows how consistent the models are with the empirical findings. Data fusion techniques also assist in solving conflict and inconsistency since there is always a bigger picture of what is being looked at.

Sensitivity analysis assesses the repercussions of high inputs of data from different sources on the results of predictive and analytical models. To declare the stability and reliability of results, the researchers change the inputs systematically and observe the effects.
