A thin jet of the polymer solution is ejected from the tip of the Taylor cone when the electrostatic repulsive forces are greater than the surface tension of the liquid.

Whipping instability is responsible for the formation of ultrathin fibers from the polymer jet that stretches and evaporates solvent when passing through an electric field. Accordingly, the fibers are then combined on a conducting charged collector which is implemented in different structures such as flat plates, drum, etc. Depending on certain specificities of the electrospinning process, the thickness of the developed nanofibers varies from tens of nanometers to several microns.

Other parameters for controlling the electrospinning process as well as the shape of the fibers that appear include viscosity and concentration, coupled with the conductivity of the polymer solution, the voltage being applied, the distance from the needle tip to the collector, and the rate of flow of the polymer solution. The fibers’ morphology and the rate of solvent evaporation are also significantly influenced by the type of solvent solution used in the electrospinning process. Solvents with lower boiling points typically result in the formation of thinner fibers due to their rapid evaporation, whereas high boiling point solvents tend to produce thicker, often beaded fibers because the solvent may not fully evaporate during the jet’s flight path.
