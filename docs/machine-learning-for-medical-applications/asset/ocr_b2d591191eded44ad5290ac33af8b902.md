# 3.2.3 Challenges in designing image processing algorithm for huge high-dimensional images

The problem arises from the fact that many medical image data come in formats that are difficult to handle, say from imaging techniques that produce multiple slices or contain time-series analysis. The amount of computations that go into the analysis and processing of these voluminous and multifaceted data is considerable and requires high-level algorithms and computing power. Self-similar processing models, utilization of distributed computing and GPUs can be useful in overcoming these kinds of scalability problems, but also introduce problems of their own, especially in implementation and resource management.

# 3.2.4 Ways in which big data affects the amount of computation required

One of the main challenges, at least in the United States, concerns demands on computer properties like memory and storage as well as computational power due to high volumes of imaging data. Software and general organization of large amount of data in image processing activities require efficient methods and high-performance computing system. Large dataset processing can be time-consuming and hence associated with longer processing time and higher operating costs, which are not favorable in health practices and reduce efficiency in medical imaging. Algorithm optimization and resource allocation are two ways to minimize these difficulties in the clinical practice environment.

In today’s world, the need for efficient data compression techniques has long been felt, owing to increasing demand for storage space and shrinkage of storage costs.

Compression assists in reducing the bulk of qualification data with medical imaging known to be sensitive to compression. Compression has been defined as an essential process in medical imaging that can minimize the storage space needed to contain images and enhance the speed with which images can be transmitted across networks without any loss of visual quality. Different mechanisms of data compression terms – lossless compression technique and the lossy compression technique – help in trade-off of extra data and image quality. The refinement of highly compressed data leads to high-quality images and small data volume, which is under development and may have further influence to storage and analysis.

It is crucial to make changes regarding the object of analysis since medical images cannot be effectively analyzed at scale; they are simply too numerous to be handled manually. The strategies that have been put in place include the deployment of scalable storage platform, review of data processing strategies, and highly efficient compression techniques to curb the increasing issue of demand and to provide timely medical imaging services.
