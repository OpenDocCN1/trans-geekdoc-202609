# 4.3 Data fusion strategies for enhancing scaffold stability predictions

Data fusion was the process by which numerical data was compiled from different sources to form a single dataset to make our scaffold stability estimates more accurate. Data fusion applies information from myriad sources – experimental, clinical, and computational – and provides a more integrative picture of scaffold behavior under a variety of conditions. This approach builds on the capabilities of different forms of data, offering a more complex view of how biodegradable scaffolds work and how they degrade.

The data fusion execution entails merging data from a number of sources like laboratory-based experiments, in vivo analysis, and computational models. Most of the data collected in laboratories are in terms of mechanical properties and degradation rates, while clinical data are more about the actual performance and patient characteristics. More importantly, theoretical models can be tested in computational simulations to provide predictions. By integrating such data sources, more comprehensive data are derived, and the creation of better approaches to modeling becomes possible.

In the data fusion process, it was essential to synchronize information collected from various sources to ensure consistency and analytical accuracy.
