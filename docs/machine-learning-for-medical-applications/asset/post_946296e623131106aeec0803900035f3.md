## 6 Wound healing applications of electrospun nanofibers

### 6.1 Nanofiber dressings for enhanced healing and moisture retention

Wound healing is a complex process involving multiple stages. It comprises coagulation, inflammation, cell proliferation, and tissue remodeling. Appropriate dressing is important in order to enhance tissue regeneration and to minimize risk factors associated with wound infection. Conventional occlusive dressings are also generally suboptimal in terms of the local environment. They do not provide the environment for the wound to heal, both regarding hydration and antimicrobial status. To overcome these drawbacks, electrospun nanofiber dressings are considered as they offer a powerful tool for improving the wound healing and moisture at the wound site.

Of importance is the ability of the dressing to retain moisture; standard non-electrospun nanofiber dressings are inferior to the latter. Several studies have established that because of their relatively large surface area and porosity, nanofibers retain exudate on the wound and ensure that the associated process of healing is enhanced and the pain reduced. Due to the structure of nanofiber dressings, they offer the right amount of moisture, maintaining the wound from drying, and at the same time, avoiding flooding of the wound site with excessive fluids. Any unwanted moisture can also be evaporated in a controlled manner. It is paramount for promoting the appropriate five factors involved in the healing process and to maintain the wound bed for moist balance.

To enhance the capability of the nanofiber dressings to afford protection against infections, the dressings could be functionalized with antimicrobial materials with antimicrobial films. Iodine incorporated into the matrix of nanofiber by itself has shown to possess antibacterial properties, and copper not only to prevent growth of bacteria but also decrease the infection rate of the wound. This gives these antimicrobial nanofiber dressings sustained and controlled antimicrobial effect that ensures that the wound environment remains clear of microorganisms and useful for the management of chronic and infected wounds.

One of the factors considered while developing an appropriate dressing of nanofiber is biocompatibility. The materials that are used to fabricate electrospun nanofibers are typically nonreactive and biocompatible with tissue. The nanofibers have a decisive structural organization corresponding to the ECM that would enhance the regeneration of tissues and movement of cells. Thereby, the dressing is quickly incorporated into the wound bed to promote the body’s healing process without scar formations. This can be useful and effective for wound healing. Some nanofiber dressings can moreover be incorporated with growth factors and active substances.

Such nanofiber dressings can be altered to fit specific wound care needs, making them highly flexible in use due to their electrospun nature. The properties of nanofibers, including the fiber size, tensile strength, and biodegradation rate are adjustable in a manner that they can be used to meet different types of wound healing, from acute wound healing to chronic ulcer healing. The possibility of using various functional additives thus allows creating very special types of dressings that have clinical characteristics needed in specific clinical situations. For instance, one can incorporate a uniform nanofiber dressing to deliver drugs slowly into the wound, with a better sticking surface for better positioning on the wound.

### 6.2 Antimicrobial electrospun nanofibers for infection control

Wound infections have always been seen to complicate the treatment process as well as the healing of wounds, as most of them are characterized by worsening outcomes, longer length of stay, and increased cost of treatment. Due to their high surface area, combined with modifiable properties, electrospun nanofibers are a feasible choice in wound care infection control. These advanced fabrics can provide local and long-term antibacterial activity due to the impregnation of antimicrobial agents into the nanofiber structure. This reduces the likelihood of infections and increases the rate of wound healing.

Appliances incorporating antimicrobial electrospun nanofibers employ multipronged strategies to inhibit microbial adhesion and growth. These approaches often involve embedding antimicrobial agents such as silver nanoparticles, antibiotics, and essential oils within the nanofiber scaffold. Silver nanoparticles, for instance, demonstrate strong antimicrobial properties by releasing silver ions that penetrate bacterial cells, rupture the cell membrane, and disrupt essential metabolic activities. Antibiotics can be incorporated to target specific bacterial processes, while essential oils provide broad-spectrum antimicrobial effects. Together, these mechanisms create a synergistic and holistic antimicrobial environment at the wound site, enhancing protection and supporting healing.

The synthesis of antimicrobial electrospun nanofibers uses several methods to incorporate and release antimicrobial agents. Techniques like blending the polymer with other agents during preparation and infusing an antimicrobial agent onto the electrospun fibers are typical. For incorporation of the antimicrobial agents in the nanofiber matrix, antimicrobial agents are incorporated into the polymer solution before electrospinning, to control distribution. Surface modification techniques include extruding antimicrobial agents onto shaped nanofibers, which can provide homogeneous treatments. Such fabrication methods are vital, whereby ultimate antimicrobial potential and sustained release of the active agents are considered.

### 6.4 Nanofibers for chronic wounds and diabetic ulcers

Diabetic ulcers and chronic wounds imply severe medical complications because of their long healing periods and being prone to infections. Various techniques of treatment are required in case of chronic injuries such as pressure ulcers and venous leg ulcers, and diabetic ulcers that are most commonly found in patients with diabetes mellitus, in order to promote healing and prevent adverse outcomes. Special features that are associated with electrospun nanofibers include large surface area, porosity, and capability of being functionalized for specific therapeutic uses, which make it a material of a choice in the wound healing process. Due to these characteristics, dressings derived from nanofibers are very beneficial in the treatment of diabetic ulcers and chronic wounds.

Chronic wound dressings with electrospun nanofiber dressings provide good absorption of moisture, which is essential to achieve the best healing outcomes. The structure of nanofiber mats enable the absorption of exudate and at the same time, maintain moisture to enhance cell migration and tissue regeneration – a property that is well understood in the wound healing process. Also, the nanofibers offer a covering layer from exterior pollution and rubbing influence. This kind of protection assists in preventing the possibility of infections and trauma to the wound while promoting the healing process.

An important component in the maintenance of chronic wounds and diabetic ulcers is infection control. Hydrophilic electrospun nanofibers can additionally be designed with extra infection-combating factors, equivalent to silver nanoparticles, antibiotics, and peptides. The incorporation of these agents into the nanofiber matrix guarantees controlled release mechanisms that has the potential of resisting microbial attack, thereby minimizing infections associated with the wound. This antimicrobial functionality, apart from making it easier and being recommended to maintain a sterile environment also leads to the healing process, since complications that are brought about by infections are avoided.

Due to their exceptional properties, nanofiber dressings can also be used to deliver bioactive compounds to the wound site in an attempt to form a self-assembling nanotissue to speed up the healing process. For more control over cell growth, other proteins such as EGF and FGF can also be included in the nanofiber matrix for enhanced tissue regeneration and cell proliferation. Other items can also be used in order to reduce the inflammation and make the healing environment better. In diabetic and chronic wounds, the encapsulation and slow delivery of these bioactive chemicals from the nanofiber dressings enhance the tissue growth and accelerate the wound’s healing.

The biocompatibility of the electrospun nanofiber dressings is critical to their functionality, especially where treating chronic wounds and diabetic ulcers.
