# 2.4 Parameters affecting nanofiber morphology and properties

Among them, the types of nanofibers and their shape and features are mainly influenced by the properties of electrospun polymer solutions. Control factors consist of polymer concentration that influences the viscosity of the solution with consequences on the fiber diameter and uniformity. Softer fibers are derived from higher polymer concentration while thinner and more nonsymmetrical fibers are derived from lower polymer concentrations. Doing the electrospinning process, the formation of the fibers as well as their solidification are affected by the type of solvent and the rate of evaporation. In order to have stable jets and continuous fibers deposition on the collector, it is necessary to fine tune the solution’s viscosity and surface tension properties.

Managing the production of nanofibers needs regulation of electrospinning factors, each of which includes the applied voltage, flow rate, and distance from the spinneret to the collector. Control of the electrostatic force that expels the polymer solution out of the nozzle depends on the voltage applied, as it affects the orientation and diameters of the fibers. Although, finer fibers are produced at higher voltages, there is a likelihood of bead formation and fiber instability. The rates of extrusion and deposition affect the diameter and density of the fibers, and are controlled by the flow rate of the polymer solution. Often, longer lengths lead to thinner fibers and almost of equal diameter. The minimum distance required from the spinneret to the collector influences the stretching as well as solidification of the fiber.

In this process, various environmental conditions, including temperature and humidity, play a major role in controlling the electrospinning process and the properties of the nanofibers. The rate of evaporation of the solvent is largely influenced by temperature, which in turn determines the morphology and dimensions of the fibers formed. Generally, increased temperature accelerates solvent evaporation, thereby encouraging the formation of thinner fibers. However, excessively rapid evaporation may lead to structural defects in the fibers. Relative humidity also significantly impacts the rheological behavior of the polymer solution and the overall fabrication of nanofiber webs.
