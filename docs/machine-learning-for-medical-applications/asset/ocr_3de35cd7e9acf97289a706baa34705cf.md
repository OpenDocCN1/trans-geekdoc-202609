The continuous growing need for nanostructured materials, especially in biomedical and industrial applications, has also underlined the capacity of electrospinning in the production of fibers of sub-micron and nanometer size. People started realizing that the process can be leveraged to construct fibers that are highly useful due to such characteristics as large external area-to-volume ratios, the ability to set porosity values deliberately, and reasonable mechanical properties. So electrospun technique has the versatility of producing nanofibers from both synthetic and natural polymers, which extends its usability in multifaceted fields such as medicine, filtration, and energy storage.

The requisite advancements in materials science and nanotechnology in the last two decades of the twentieth century and the first two decades of the twenty-first century have also greatly enhanced the growth of electrospinning technology. Scientists began consciously studying the factors that influence the electrospinning process, including the concentration of the polymer solution, the voltage applied, flow rate, and the surrounding conditions. Preliminary findings also established that controlling the fiber diameter, shape, and surface properties became much less of a challenge as a result. Due to narrowing down of the variety of the fiber capabilities, other approaches for electrospinning were developed, such as melt electrospinning and coaxial electrospinning. From these advancements, it became possible to incorporate a number of materials into a single fiber, thus enabling the production of nanofiber composites for usages in tissue engineering, medication dispensing, and other high-quality filtration systems.

Electrospinning has gained significant attention in the medical field due to its ability to produce nanofibrous scaffolds that closely resemble the extracellular matrix (ECM) of natural tissue. Because of these morphological similarities, electrospun nanofibers are highly suitable for applications in tissue engineering and regenerative medicine, as they promote cell attachment, proliferation, and differentiation. Electrospun nanofibers are preferred over other nanofiber fabrication techniques in drug delivery systems due to the versatility of electrospinning in producing fibers with tunable drug release profiles.

Recently, efforts have been made to scale up electrospinning processes for industrial use. These include advancements in nanofiber production efficiency and the incorporation of bioactive components such as growth factors, antimicrobial agents, and other therapeutic molecules directly into the fiber matrix during fabrication.
