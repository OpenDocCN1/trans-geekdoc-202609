Variability in image quality: This variation in image acquisition parameters and image noise also affects the quality of the imaged features. These variations require that good feature extraction techniques consider them in order to deliver accurate results.

High dimensionality: The features obtained from medical images are frequently large in number typically resulting in high dimensions. To deal with this issue, convincing dimensionality reduction techniques such as PCA and feature selection methods are applied to facilitate the analysis in question.

Inter-observer variability: Variability in feature identification – or feature abstraction – known as feature variability is a potential source of variation if recognition is performed by different observers. There is need to ensure that such processes are standardized and that there are routine automated methods in place to minimize this problem.

Computational resources: Many of the enhanced feature extraction and analysis processes demand a significant amount of computation, especially in the case of big data. To overcome these computational requirements, efficient algorithms and GPU hardware footprints are applied.

Every usage of medical image in clinical practice relies on the feature extraction and analysis of the data encapsulated within the image. In this way, derived and characterized features become useful for healthcare professionals that focus on description of diseases, treatments, and patients’ outcomes. This chapter pays attention to the feature extraction process and indicates that the constant development of imaging technologies and computational methods has expanded the potential and versatility of feature extraction in medical image analysis.
