# 5.4.6 Practical considerations

It is possible to select QDs for practical use by determining excitation and emission wavelengths referring to the imaging tool and application necessity. The type of excitation source, for instance, lasers, LEDs, and the choice of the detection system such as filters and detectors should be appropriate for the QDs. Information on the photostability and quantum yield of QDs when used at different wavelengths such as excitation and emission can be considered in the functionality and efficiency of the imaging tasks.

# 5.5 Influence of size and surface modification on optical properties

QDs have tunable photoluminescence properties that depend greatly on the size and the nature of surface coating. Knowledge about these factors is of great importance for tuning QDs more appropriately to a particular task, especially in areas like biomedical imaging and diagnostics, and sensing.

# 5.5.1 Size-dependent optical properties

Emission wavelengths:

The emission wavelength of quantum dots (QDs) is primarily governed by their size due to the quantum confinement effect shown in Figure 10. When the size of QDs decreases, the energy gap between the valence band and conduction band increases, resulting in emission shifting toward the shorter wavelengths (i.e., blue end) of the visible spectrum. Conversely, larger QDs exhibit a smaller bandgap, causing their emission to shift toward longer (red) wavelengths. This intrinsic, size-dependent tunability in emission makes QDs exceptionally valuable in multiplexed imaging systems, allowing researchers to design and utilize QDs that fluoresce in distinct colors from a single material platform.
