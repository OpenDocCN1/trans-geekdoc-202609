## 2.5 Transition to AI-driven diagnostics

The evolution of medical testing and disease diagnosis spans centuries, culminating in the current era of AI-assisted diagnostics. As depicted in Figure 3, earlier diagnostic methodologies largely relied on clinicians’ subjective opinions and estimations, which often led to variability and lower accuracy. Traditional techniques such as colorimetry and immunoanalysis were groundbreaking at the time, providing significant advancements in detecting diseases. Over time, limitations in sensitivity, specificity, and speed became apparent especially as the complexity and volume of cases grew. The demand for more rapid, precise, and objective diagnostic tools has driven the integration of artificial intelligence, marking a pivotal shift in modern clinical diagnostics.

This change in diagnostics is influenced by the development of AI, as it brought a paradigm change in approach. The new capabilities that came with AI technology are reflected in the areas of DL and ML. The opportunity to use and analyze large amounts of data made it possible to use ML algorithms as a new and innovative solution to efficiency and accuracy problems that were encountered when using previous diagnostic methods. This transition was made possible by DL, which was a kind of ML that supports the capability of recognizing complex data such as genetic data as well as medical images.

## 3 Fundamentals of artificial intelligence in healthcare

### 3.1 Overview of AI technologies

AI is an umbrella term for a number of technologies that are currently reshaping several industries in general, and healthcare in particular. The generation of structures and policies by which robots could undertake tasks that normally entail human cognition is the definition of AI. AI is used to enhance the management of patients, the development of treatment plans, and methods of diagnosis in the healthcare setting.

- **ML:** ML, as a branch of AI, is focused on developing algorithms that can analyze and make predictions upon the result of data analysis. Since ML algorithms increase their learning, the performance of the algorithms is better with time. In recent years, many healthcare companies have incorporated the use of ML to analyze large datasets such as electronic health records and medical images, with a view to determining trends and predict outcomes. The methods in use for constructing these models include reinforcement learning, unsupervised learning, and supervised learning, all of which can support the task of illness diagnosis, therapy suggestion, and individualized therapy.

- **DL:** DL is described as a category of ML and concentrates on recognizing and analyzing various types of data by implementing numerous neural networks, which puts the word “deep” into context. Big improvements have been achieved when using DL in fields such as voice and picture recognition. Medical applications are other areas where DL models are applied when identifying the accuracy of medical pictures such as an MRI, CT scan. These models can detect flaws and assist in analyzing diseases for radiologists, while doing it faster and more accurately than human counterparts.

- **Natural language processing (NLP):** NLP, Natural Language Processing, is a subfield of AI focusing on the relationship between people and computers, specifically the manner in which the latter interacted with language. Thanks to it, robots can understand, in a very real sense, the human language and produce it as well. NLP has been applied in the analysis of unstructured datasets in healthcare information to identify relevant information such as from a published work, and from clinical reports. It also supports patient interactions, makes information retrieval easier, and maintains record keeping.

- **Computer vision (CV):** This area of AI focuses on how information gleaned from visuals is presented to machines. Medical images, histopathology slide images, as well as retinal images are analyzed using CV technology. CV contributes to the recognition and categorization of sign of illness by constructing image recognition and object detection algorithms. This enhances the assessment of developments in the overall treatment process and increases the chance of making the right diagnosis.

Predictive analytics is the process of utilizing historical data to forecast future outcomes, employing tools such as statistical modeling and AI-based learning. As shown in Figure 4, in the healthcare domain, this approach plays a vital role in refining care plans, identifying proactive risk factors, and predicting patient outcomes. Predictive models are particularly useful in analyzing patient data to uncover underlying trends and behavioral patterns. These insights enable healthcare providers to deliver more personalized and efficient treatments, ultimately improving clinical decision-making and patient satisfaction.
