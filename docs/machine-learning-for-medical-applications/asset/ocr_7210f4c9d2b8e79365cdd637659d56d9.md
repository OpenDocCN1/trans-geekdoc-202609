Since porosity and fiber diameter are shown to influence the total efficiency of the electrospun nanofibers in various applications, the extent of the performance depend on both factors. For instance, in a tissue engineering scaffold, they have to be capable of supporting cell proliferation and the formation of tissues but at the same time also provide mechanical stability and porosity, and the diameter of fibers have to be balanced. Studies identified that the rate and extent of drug release depend on the fiber diameter and porosity, where higher porosity and smaller diameter fibers provide a higher release efficiency of the drug. Comprehending and refining these attributes are crucial in order to create electrospun nanofibers with customized performance attributes for particular medicinal uses.

# 3.4 Biodegradability and biocompatibility of nanofibers

One key feature of electrospun nanofibers is their biodegradability, especially in clinical applications, where the relevant materials are expected to breakdown and be metabolized in the body harmlessly. It has been established that the type, chemical composition, and environment control the rate at which the nanofibers degrade. Biodegradable polymers degrade through enzymatic and hydrolytic mechanisms, making PLA, PGA, and PCL suitable materials to fabricate electrospun nanofibers. Polymer type, molecular weight, crystallinity, and the amount and type of additives control the rate of breakdown. These characteristics minimize foreign body responses after a long duration and also phase out surgery for removing the material because the fibers can dissolve themselves at a rate compatible with tissue healing requirements.

Another important characteristic of electrospun nanofibers is their biocompatibility since these nanofibers are ideally to be used in tissue engineering as wound dressings. The ability of a substance to engage in reactions with biological systems and not produce unfavorable reactions is known as biocompatibility. The electrospun nanofibers must favor cell adhesion, proliferation, and differentiation, and on the same note, decrease inflammation and toxicity levels. Nanofiber biocompatibility depends on a number of factors such as fiber diameter, surface chemistry, and the presence of functional group active compounds. Given the relationships between nanofibers and biological tissues, in vivo animal studies and in vitro cell culture studies are often employed. The purpose is to ensure that the nanofibers used in medical applications are innocuous, noncytotoxic, and do not elicit an immune response so as to avoid undesirable outputs.

The biodegradability and biocompatibility of electrospun nanofibers are highly dependent on the choice of the polymer. Biodegradable synthetic polymers include poly(ethylene glycol), PLGA, and PCL, while biocompatible natural polymers include chitosan, silk fibroin, collagen, and more. These polymers frequently aid in tissue regeneration and normal cellular functions.
