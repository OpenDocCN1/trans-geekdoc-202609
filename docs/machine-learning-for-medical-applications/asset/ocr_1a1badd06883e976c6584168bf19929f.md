# 6.2.4 Applications and use cases

The swin transformer’s generic structure makes it suitable for various image segmentation methods. For example, the technique is applied well in medical imaging for segmentation of complex structures such as tumors. In remote sensing, its performance in handling satellite images has been useful for the classification of land cover as well as object detection. Reliability and speed of the model also make it viable for use in self-driving cars and robotics, where accurate segmentation of objects is critical.

The swin transformer faces challenges that warrant further exploration. The model’s complexity and memory requirements, although reduced compared to earlier transformers, still pose challenges for deployment in resource-constrained environments. Additionally, while the hierarchical approach improves scalability, optimizing the model for real-time applications remains an ongoing challenge. Future research focus on refining the swin transformer’s efficiency, exploring novel attention mechanisms, and extending its capabilities to even larger and more diverse datasets.

# 6.3 SegFormer

As a model designed to address the limitations of previous transformer approaches, SegFormer combines the strengths of transformers with efficient segmentation techniques. By leveraging the capabilities of self-attention mechanisms, SegFormer aims to provide accurate and contextually rich segmentation results while maintaining computational efficiency.
