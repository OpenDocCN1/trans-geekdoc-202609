In Positron Emission Tomography (PET), gamma rays resulting from annihilation events between positrons and electrons within the body are detected to generate diagnostic images. The process begins with the administration of a radiotracer typically a biologically active compound tagged with a positron-emitting radionuclide. When the radiotracer accumulates in specific organs or tissues, it undergoes positron emission, leading to the release of gamma rays. As shown in Figure 8, these photons are detected by the PET scanner, which reconstructs detailed three-dimensional images reflecting the distribution of the tracer throughout the body. PET is widely used for assessing brain function, diagnosing cancer, evaluating metabolic activity, and monitoring the effectiveness of therapeutic interventions.

### 4.5.1 Clinical uses and value of PET scanning

In cardiology, neurology, and cancer PET imaging is often used. PET scans are instrumental for definition of treatment response as well as the stage of disease and the identification of malignancies in oncology. PET has excellent capacity to respond to changes in metabolism; this means that even before the morphological features of cancerous cells change, PET can spot a tumor. PET is employed in neurology to study metabolism in the brain and to see neurodegenerative illnesses, for example, Alzheimer's. There is limited understanding of the progression of neurological diseases, and brain activity is further measured using PET imaging. In cardiology, PET provides data about metabolism and functionality of the heart and, therefore, the diagnosis of coronary artery disease and the quality of the heart.

Finally, there are principles of SPECT.

SPECT radiotracers are gamma-emitting radiotracers used in getting functional information concerning organs and tissues. SPECT relies on the gamma rays that are directly emitted from the radiotracer, in contrast with PET, which uses the events resulting from positron annihilation. To get several two-dimensional pictures of the patient from different angles, a gamma camera rotates around him/her. These source photographs are then merged to form stereoscopic visualizations and hence three-dimensional visual outlook. SPECT is less sensitive than PET but in the comparative analysis of the maturity of the technologies and the cost of the procedures, SPECT has an upper hand. It is employed in the examination of bone disorders, brain issues, and cardiac performance.

### 4.5.2 SPECT in clinical practice

There are basically three major areas that apply SPECT: oncology, neurological disorders, and cardiovascular diseases. SPECT for cardiology was the method aimed at the determination of myocardial function and perfusion for the diagnosis of CAD and the evaluation of the severity of MI. In neurology, the evaluation and diagnosis of disorders such as epilepsy, dementia, ischemic stroke and its related outcome is done using SPECT, which offers data on the cerebral blood flow. SPECT imaging is slightly less common than PET in oncology but can be employed for tumor location and assessment of the therapy effectiveness. Since SPECT provides dynamic functional images, it is effective in assessing a variety of physiological functions and pathologies.

### 5.1.2 Role of sensors and detectors

Imaging signals also require sensors and detectors to pick up as well as translate signals into images in the digital platform. X-ray and CT imaging utilize detectors that can be categorized as either film or digital; however, digital methods present benefits such as fast image production and flexible image processing. In MRI, detectors are incorporated in the radio frequency coil antenna that receives the signals produced in tissues due to applied magnetic fields. Ultrasound scan as a technology uses piezoelectric transducers, which transform sound waves into electric impulses. The type of sensors and detectors differ with the resolution, sensitivity, and quality of the images collected during the tests.

### 5.1.3 Effectiveness of some parameters of acquisition on image quality

Image acquisition factors such as duration of exposure, radiation dose, and image resolution have a significant effect on picture quality. Examples of quantitative factors that influence the trade-off between radiation dose and image quality in X-ray and CT are tube current, voltage, and slice thickness. Factors influencing contrast and resolution of the images may include field strength, echo time (TE), and repetition time (TR). In ultrasonography, image definition and diagnostic information depend on several factors such as depth of scanning, gain controls, and transducer frequency. It is important to calibrate these values accurately in order to get optimum picture quality at minimum risk to the patients.

### 5.1.4 Methodologies for enhancing the process of image capture

We discuss a range of approaches to make the image acquisition as optimal as possible in terms of image quality and diagnosis. In X-ray and CT imaging, modernization such as modulation of the patient X-ray dose and application of iterative reconstruction algorithms decrease radiation risks while acceptable image quality is provided. In MRI, methods including parallel imaging and the use of advanced pulse sequence can help achieve high-resolution images in shorter amount of time. Ultrasound imaging optimization refers to optimization of probe settings with the use of harmonic imaging.

Enhancing comparability of the images. Histogram modification or matching changes the histogram of an image to the desired one. This is especially helpful in standardizing the image appearance or simply in certain features. These methods assist in tackling problems of interpreting medical images to produce results with better visibility.

## 5.2.4 Multimodal image restoration and enhancement

Fusion is the process of fusing two or more images typically from different imaging modes to form one fused image from the fused component images. This approach improves on the former diagnostic modalities by adding other complementary information, structural from CT scan and functional from PET. Multimodal overlay enhancement techniques are mostly concerned with the overall visibility and contrast of structures within each modality as well as with the fact that all important features identified within one modality are preserved in the fused image. Image fusion in multimodal images methodology includes intensity-based fusion, feature-based fusion, and wavelet-based fusion to give better diagnosis of patient’s status than using only one kind of image.

Since diagnostic applications require a picture of the human body to be detailed, image enhancing methods play an important role of improving the quality of such pictures. Operations such as contrast stretching, noise filtering, edge detection, and histogram evaluation as well as the fusion of picture images can assist in magnifying medical pictures to reveal certain information, making a precise diagnosis easily achievable, and in formulating effectual approaches to treatment. Motivations include enhancement of patient care while making it easier for the continuous changes that occur in medical imaging.

## 5.3 Image segmentation

Another major concept discussed in the MIP is the process of segmentation, the partitioning of an image into even discrete regions or segments that represent structures of interest within the image. It is a procedure that enables one to get relevant information from complex image information by image analysis and interpretation of medical pictures.

The segmentation techniques are historical and recent ones – automatic segmentation technique and manual segmentation technique.

With automatic segmentation methods, computer algorithms are employed to identify and delineate regions of interest (ROIs) without direct human intervention. Techniques such as thresholding, region growing, and clustering particularly the k-means algorithm are commonly used. In thresholding, specific intensity values are selected to separate regions within an image; region growing methods initiate from seed points and expand based on predefined criteria; and clustering algorithms group pixels with similar characteristics into distinct segments.
