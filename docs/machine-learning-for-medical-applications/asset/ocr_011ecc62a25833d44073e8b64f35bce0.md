Peculiarities of the interaction between nanofibers and brain cells are addressed by the functionalization of the nanofibers with adhesion peptides to enhance cell adhesion and promote neurogenesis. It has been found that when stem cell are cultured on nanofiber scaffolds, they could be tempted to differentiate into neurons, and nanofiber scaffolds could support the proliferation of Schwann cells, which are very important in the regeneration of the peripheral nerve.

This review highlights how the integration of growth factors and neurotrophic agents into electrospun nanofibers can considerably improve their performance. Such molecules can be delivered in a controlled fashion for an extended period, enabling the regeneration and repair of nerves. For example, nerve growth factor and brain-derived neurotrophic factor have been effective in spinal axonal out growing and neuronal protection. Any factor can be incorporated within the nanofiber linked to the fiber surface. It is therefore possible to develop scaffolds that not only provide the structural framework needed for repair but also participate in the biochemical communication needed for nerve regeneration.

The application of electrospun nanofibers in nerve tissue repair is advancing steadily, with numerous studies and preclinical trials demonstrating their promising potential. These nanofibers offer structural guidance for nerve regeneration and can be tailored to deliver bioactive molecules, supporting functional recovery. Future research must address several critical challenges, including the scalability of nanofiber production, integration with existing surgical techniques, and ensuring long-term biocompatibility. Emerging innovations such as biodegradable nanofibers, advanced surface functionalization methods, and multimodal therapeutic systems are expected to further enhance their clinical applicability. The goal is to translate these technological advancements into practical solutions for treating various nerve injuries and neurological disorders, thereby improving patient outcomes. # 5.5 Enhancing stem cell differentiation through nanofiber scaffolds

In tissue engineering practice, a major operation involves bisexual cell differentiation, which means orienting stem cells to form specific cell types for the restoration of damaged tissues. In this technique, electrospun nanofiber scaffolds play an important role because they mimic the mechanism of stem cell adhesion and proliferation/differentiation. Because of such characteristics as the ability to provide a large surface area for a small volume of material and the structural similarity to ECM, nanofibers are ideal for creating scaffolds that can modulate the stem cell response and promote new tissue formation.
