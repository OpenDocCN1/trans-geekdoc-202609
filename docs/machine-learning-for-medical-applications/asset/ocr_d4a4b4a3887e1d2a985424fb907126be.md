# 6.2.1 Chemical stability

Degradation and oxidation:

QDs are unique nanoparticles that can undergo chemical decay and oxidation, especially under environmental stress. The core material is usually created with semiconductor compounds; they can oxidize, which eradicates their optic characteristics. As depicted in Figure 11, these perturbations can greatly impact the stability and lifetime characteristics of QDs in applications that require stability. Chemical stability must therefore be enhanced through the incorporation of sound surface formation and high protective layers that prevent the encroachment of the environment into the body of the material.

Surface ligand stability:

The stability of surface ligands is also a serious issue because of rough environments. Capping ligands are used to passivate QDs and improve their solubility and biocompatibility. Dissociation from the QD surface over a period alters its chemistry and the resulting optics. Future investigations for more stable and long-lasting ligands and good surface passivation measures are needed to increase long-term stability.
