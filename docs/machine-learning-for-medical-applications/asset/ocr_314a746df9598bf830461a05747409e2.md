Preoperative simulation and rehearsal: Such a prospect means not only better preparation for surgery but also an opportunity to rehearse on the model of a person printed by a 3D printer. This rehearsal occurs with outstanding complications, and it can facilitate alteration of the surgical plan, hence increasing accuracy and saving time. Also, rehearsals enable the different members of the surgical team to understand the position and location of relevant structures in relation to the planned surgery, making the coordinated activity during the operation more efficient.

Improved communication with the surgical team: Surgical plans become easier to explain when there is a model to refer to, and 3D-printed models enable surgical team members to have a better understanding of the intended surgery. Surgeons and anesthetists, as well as scrub nurses and circulating nurses, use models to enhance the shared mental picture of the upcoming surgery to better co-ordinate their efforts. Such improvements in communication go a long way in making the surgical team more cohesive and better organized.

Enhanced patient education and engagement: Using models printed by a 3D printer, in turn, helps patients comprehend their ailments as well as the operations performed on them. The patient was able to see what was wrong with their body and what was to be done in the operation, and thus was able to be more active in the treatment process. This increase in patient awareness can translate to better interaction and strict adherence to the preoperative directions given, hence making the process a positive one.

Reduced surgical risks and complications: This application of practical models, specifically 3D-printed models, assists in the detection of risks and possible surgical complications before the actual surgery takes place. Such problems imply that surgeons can prepare for potential issues, hope for the best, and act in a manner that reduces the odds of complications. This method of work helps to prevent complications and improve the quality and safety of surgical operations.

# 6 Challenges and limitations of 3D printing in medicine

# 6.1 Material limitations and biocompatibility issues

The use of 3D printing in medicine is still a relatively new concept; the method faces several challenges related to the type of material being used and biocompatibility. These problems have a significant impact on 3D-printed medical equipment including medical devices and implants; hence, it underlines the imperative for further research.
