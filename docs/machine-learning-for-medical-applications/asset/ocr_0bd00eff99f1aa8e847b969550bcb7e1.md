# 3.1.1 Contracting path: encoder

The encoder, which is also referred to as the contracting path of U-Net, is in charge of gathering the context and removing features from the input picture. It is made up of max-pooling procedures after a sequence of convolutional layers. The network can learn hierarchical representations of the picture data, thanks to these convolutional layers, which gradually decrease the image’s spatial dimensions, while deepening the feature maps. The encoder’s job is to extract the contextual data and the high-level characteristics needed to distinguish different structures in the picture.

# 3.1.2 Expansive path: decoder

The high-level feature maps that the encoder generates are used by the expansive path, also known as the decoder, to rebuild the picture. It is composed of convolutional layers that serve to improve the spatial resolution of the feature maps, after upsampling procedures. By concatenating feature maps from the corresponding levels of the contracted route, skip connections let the decoder to integrate information from the encoder. In order to maintain fine-grained details and guarantee that the segmentation output maintains the spatial accuracy of the source picture, these skip connections are necessary.

# 3.1.3 Skip connections and their importance

The usage of skip connections, which connect the encoder and decoder routes, is a distinguishing characteristic of U-Net. These linkages assist in retaining spatial information that could otherwise be lost during the downsampling process by facilitating the movement of high-resolution characteristics from the contracting channel to the expanding path. Concatenating the feature maps from the encoder and decoder allows the U-Net to generate more accurate and detailed segmentation maps. This makes it especially useful for jobs that need high-resolution outputs, such as defining tiny structures.
