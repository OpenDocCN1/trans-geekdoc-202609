# 3 Basic principles of 3D printing

# 3.1 Fundamentals of additive manufacturing processes

3D printing, as additive manufacturing, is a process of constructing a 3D object by depositing material successively. This methodology is in contrast to other, more conventional subtractive manufacturing processes that entail the removal of material from a block of material. Because of the need to produce complex forms and intricate geometries in additive manufacturing, full control of material placement is mandatory.

Additive manufacturing was unarguably constructed on a layer-by-layer concept. The first step that was completed required one to develop a computer-aided design (CAD) to begin the model. Subsequently, we subdivided this model into a number of thin, flat layers in the XY plane. These slices serve the machine as a blueprint for layer-by-layer deposition of the materials in order to create the object. Structures so complex and convoluted that it would be impossible to provisionally design and manufacture by other means could now be built due to this layer-by-layer approach.

Fused filament fabrication is a process of using a heated nozzle to extrude thermoplastic filaments. Sequences of solid material are formed because the substance is accumulated in layers and becomes solid when it cools. Due to the relatively low cost and multifunctionality, this technique is widely used; however, the practical application is generally limited by the mechanical properties of the final parts and the resolution of the extruded layer.

SLA: In the case of SLA, the liquid photopolymer resin that was applied was cured with the expertise of UV light. To solidify the resin surface at matching cross sections of the digital model, a UV laser light source was used. SLA was famous for the high accuracy of the part's resolution and its ability to produce complex, polished components. In particular, it was efficient when accuracy and specific detail of a situation were required.
