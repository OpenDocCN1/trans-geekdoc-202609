
## Deep learning in computer vision

M. Chitra, V. Tejasri, K. Balachandar, Mohit Tiwari, and Manas Ranjan Mohapatra

**Abstract:** Deep learning led to the rebirth of the subject of computer vision and provided a new driving force in the further development of new theoretical concepts and real-life applications. In this chapter, the authors provide an overview of the more significant waves and new propulsive trends in deep learning for computer vision as well as their evolution and impact. This was achieved by presenting the general principles of deep learning, describing the roles of these new architectures, as well as the advancements in the hardware that enable image computing. Applications of deep learning for dynamic computer vision in real-world operations such as augmented reality, self-driven cars, specific shops, security and surveillance, and healthcare are illustrated. This chapter also provides basic information on model evaluation, hyperparameter tuning, and consideration of the importance of the model’s ethical issues. Discussing these components more comprehensively in the framework of the chapter, the authors point to revolutionary features of contemporary deep learning technologies and their prospects as the future of computer vision research and development.

**Keywords:** deep learning, computer vision, real-time systems, hyperparameter tuning, model evaluation, hardware advancements

## 2 Overview of deep learning

### 2.1 Definition and scope of deep learning

Deep learning is a subset of machine learning that uses multilayered artificial neural networks, also referred to as deep neural networks (DNNs), for modeling and understanding complex patterns in data. Essentially, deep learning aims at implementing hierarchical representation of the data and automatically deriving features from raw data. This enables models to perform actions such as generation, prediction, and even classification with minimal to no intervention from human beings at all.

Deep learning has found widespread application across numerous domains due to its ability to process massive amounts of data and uncover complex correlations within them. The fundamental concepts, key methodologies, and primary application areas of deep learning are illustrated in Figure 1. In the field of computer vision, tasks such as object detection, image segmentation, and classification have been significantly transformed by deep learning approaches. Unlike traditional methods, which often rely on surface-level features extracted from raw data, deep learning algorithms are capable of identifying fine-grained patterns such as the orientation and location of microfeatures across diverse datasets. This was achieved through multiple layers of neural networks that allow for increasingly abstract and accurate representations of input data.

Deep learning also consists of a number of architectures and methods like CNNs, GANs (generative adversarial networks), and recurrent neural networks. From processing sequential data and generating new data instances to infer the spatial embeddedness present in a picture, each architectural design has a particular use.

Lastly, the level of depth showed points such as that deep learning requires large datasets and large amounts of computing power, often using GPUs to speed up the training and inference process. The increase in computational capability and the ability of deep learning models to learn features directly from raw data have made deep learning one of the primary frameworks used in current AI, paving the way for speech and voice recognition technologies, natural language processing, robotics, among others.

### 2.2 Historical development and milestones

In this chapter, we outline the three main milestones in the development of deep learning that have significantly impacted AI. In the 1940s and 1950s, while McCulloch and Pitts developed the first mathematical description of a neural network, the principle of artificial neural networks, which was the basis for deep learning, was in place. This early model helped to lay the basis for other more sophisticated improvements of neural networks.

The backpropagation algorithm was introduced by Ronald Williams, David Rumelhart, and Geoffrey Hinton way back in the 1980s. This approach enabled the training of multilayer networks for computing the gradients for neural networks, which regained interest in neural networks. Although there was potential, the absence of big labeled datasets and the fact that computer resources would limit the application of neural networks notwithstanding.

Deep learning experienced a resurgence in the mid-2000s due to advancements in computer power and technology. The work done by Geoffrey Hinton and his team in 2006 on deep belief networks initiated a new phase in the field. By training DBNs, it was demonstrated that higher-order neural networks could be trained quickly without supervision, thus opening the door for more complicated and powerful models.

The transition of deep learning from text to images happened in 2012 with an architecture called AlexNet by Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever, and Geoffrey Hinton. AlexNet surpassed other approaches in the ILSVRC, achieving the increase in picture categorization precision. This achievement showed the potential of deep learning, thus calling for its application and more research on the same.

After AlexNet, several vital improvements concerning deep learning practices can be identified. In 2014, the authors of VGGNet at the Visual Geometry Group, University of Oxford, gave a glimpse of deeper networks designed with a very small convolutional filter size. In 2014, there was the inception of GoogLeNet (Inception), which focused on inception modules to enhance the effectiveness and efficiency of the architecture.
