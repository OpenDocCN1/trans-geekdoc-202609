The best models that are effective in distinguishing minority class can be assigned with the higher weights in a way that influences final decision-making processes more than other models. This approach assists in defining a new method for obtaining more precise anomaly detection since it focuses on the models having a greater probability of identifying rare anomalies.

Another synthesis method includes ensemble reduction, which can be of two types: first, where weak classifiers are rejected from the ensemble in order to enhance efficiency and optimization of the models; second, where high-variance classifiers are reduced from the ensemble, so as to improve efficiency and optimize the models. Specifically, in samples with an uneven distribution of anomalies, ensemble pruning has the useful function of removing models that are inclined to favor the majority class and thus are noncontributory to the identification of anomalies. Some of the bad models can be pruned – those that are not able contribute to the solution but only add extra noise to the solution by being part of the ensemble. This then increases the efficiency of the model in using the less represented class.

Furthermore, the ensemble models can be adapted with a cost-sensitive learning that assigns different costs to using an anomaly classifier and a normal instance classifier. A common problem with imbalanced datasets is that missed classifications of anomalies are costly due to the nature of the problem domain. Using the cost-sensitive approach, both true positive and false negatives are given higher cost in the ensemble framework; hence the models are forced to enhance the detection of anomalies. This adjustment makes the ensemble pay more attention to the minority class, making its capability of identifying imbalanced datasets better.

Other enhancement techniques, which can be used in fitting ensemble models, are also very efficient in cases of imbalance data sets such as AdaBoost and gradient boosting. These techniques have the operationality of training models in an unbounded loop, then emphasizing on those misclassified data of previous rounds. When misclassifying anomaly patterns, samples are trained with higher weights in order to improve on the performance of the ensemble on the particular minority class in anomaly detection. Due to the iterativeness of the boosting approach, the model is capable of learning from mistakes in order to accurately detect the few off apparatuses. Furthermore, boosting methods are immune from significant class imbalance compared to bagging-based ensemble, because boosting methods naturally pay more attention to the samples that are most difficult to classify.
