In frameworks of DQL for anomaly detection based on DQL, the environment is the source of data; states are therefore the features of the data, actions define the decision of whether what data was normal, and reward is given based on the rate of accurate detection. The idea is to find the best policy for the agent, which results in the maximum total reward: with well-detected anomalies and with as few false alarms as possible. Strictly speaking, the deep Q-network is a mode of function approximation to transform state-action pairs to Q-values that can help make decisions. In terms of using DQL for anomaly detection another big advantage was the possibility to analyze high-dimensional input. This capability is most useful in situations like the image-based anomaly detection where each image translates into the high dimensional feature space. DQL applies the CNNs to identify innovative spatial characteristics from the visual data, which minimizes dimensionality and targets the significant anomalous area. In time-series data we use recurrent structures inside DQL that help in capturing temporal structure for improving anomaly detection in the sequential data patterns. These strategies help DQL to detect various and sophisticated patterns in various datasets.

In DQL the touchpoint of reward function design is very important for anomaly detection. Reward function aims at tuning between the true positive detections and false alarms while keeping the model locked on the worthwhile anomalies. Sanctions on false positives and bonuses on correct anomaly detection built an optimal detection policy for the agent. Additional to the simple reward functions that encourage the agent to avoid unknown states, more advanced reward functions that increase when anomalies are encountered, taking into account the number of critical anomalies and the rarity of their occurrence also complement the DQL approach to work with imbalanced datasets. They make sure that the model does not let important kinds of failures pass through but are also not overly protective about less serious kinds of failures.

DQL has been implemented and used successfully in various domains that require an advanced form of anomaly detection functionality. In cybersecurity, DQL models find out existing intrusion within networks through the behavior of incoming and outgoing data packets and self-learning. In the healthcare field, a number of DQL based systems possess the ability to scan medical imaging data and find out anomalies, which are suggestive of diseases, which forms the basis for early diagnostics. In industrial automation, DQL observes sensor data to identify any failure in equipment in operation, reducing downtime. The above applications show the extensibility of DQL in meeting a multiplicity of anomaly detection needs.
