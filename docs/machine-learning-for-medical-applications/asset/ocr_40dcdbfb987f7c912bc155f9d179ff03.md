An important factor that defines the mechanical properties of the electrospun nanofibers is the diameter of the manufactured nanofibers. Generally, fibers with short diameters tend to have high tensile strength and stiffness but low flexibility and strength, rather than the large diameter fibers. Other characteristics of the nanofibers and their structure, for instance, the alignment and orientation of the fibers also have some influence on the mechanical properties. With fibers aligned, composites typically exhibit greater mechanical properties than the same composite with randomly textured fibers. Such fibers are particularly useful for use in directional applications, for instance in extracellular matrices.

Properties of different polymers and their composites change when nanofibers are produced through electrospinning. For example, although both PLA- and PGA-based polymers are biocompatible, they possess unique mechanical properties that are appropriate for diverse biomedical applications. The use of inorganic nanoparticles, including silica nanotubes can improve the mechanical properties of nanofiber polymer matrix. Such composites can present higher tensile strength, increased stiffness, and also greater resistance to shape change – all attributes that can make them ideal for use in situations where greater strength and durability are needed.

Mechanical properties of electrospun nanofibers are found to be highly dependent on the environmental conditions such as temperature and humidity. Greater temperature weakens the mechanical strength and preserves elasticity of polymeric matrix of the nanofibers while greater humidity changes the moistening and swelling characteristics of the fiber. In order to understand which properties the developed nanofibers materials should possess in order to maintain their performance under the influence of the environmental effects described above, it is essential to study the impact of these factors on the mechanical characteristics of nanofibers.

# 3.3 Porosity and fiber diameter

One essential characteristic of electrospun nanofibers that greatly affects their performance in a variety of applications is their porosity. During the electrospinning process, a network of linked voids is created by solvent evaporation and polymer jet stretching, which gives nanofibers their porous structure. For uses like wound dressings and tissue engineering, where it allows gas and nutrient exchange, the fiber imitates the ECM, and encourages cell infiltration and tissue development. Here, high porosity is essential. Higher porosity usually results in greater drug absorption and regulated release. This means that porosity plays an important role on the capability of the delivery devices to load drugs and release them.
