Basically, edge detection algorithms work on changes in pixel intensity and these changes give you the boundary of an image. Such methods usually involve using the differential of intensity of the image in the hope of magnifying drastically different areas. The primary aim is to identify and outline the shape of anatomic structures, which in turn are essential for further characterization.

Several edge detection operators are commonly used, each with its own strengths and limitations:

Sobel operator: Sobel operators are used to open an image up and measure the first derivative of image intensity in the horizontal and vertical directions. It applies convolution kernels to enhance edges and is found to be useful in direction-based edge detection. But it is sensitive to noise and not very effective on areas with weak edges.

Canny edge detector: This is a multistage technique, which involves hysteresis for edge detection of the image in addition to the gradient computation, non-maximum suppression, and Gaussian smoothing. It is excellent in edge detection accuracy and has some level of noise reduction capability. Due to high performance of the Canny detector for any kind of imaging scenario, it is used often.

Prewitt operator: Like in the case of Sobel operator, Prewitt operator also measures the first-order grey level difference and intensity gradient allows the use of different kernels. It is successful in edge detection and can easily be modified for use in different environments. It is too noisy and needs some additional processing to get better edge detection results.

Roberts Cross operator: The operator, Roberts Cross, aims at detecting edges; then its gradient is calculated by the use of two convolution masks. It is very effective in images where the contrast between objects and their backgrounds is high, though it is not very good in determining edges where the contrast is low and images are noisy.

In medical image processing, edge detection methods are employed to locate sharp boundaries like organs’ structure boundary. They are used as a separate method in less complicated methods of segmentation with other methods to improve upon its performance. This is especially applicable in imaging such as X-ray in which the high contrast image can be processed well with edge detection.

Medical image segmentation poses several challenges to edge detection techniques. Noise and artifacts commonly present in medical images can significantly impact edge detection accuracy, increasing the likelihood of false positives and resulting in incomplete or fragmented edge mapping. These methods are particularly ineffective in scenarios where image contrast is low and edge transitions are smooth a condition often encountered in specific pathological cases.
