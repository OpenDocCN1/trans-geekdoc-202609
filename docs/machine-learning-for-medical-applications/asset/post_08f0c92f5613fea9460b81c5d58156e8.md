## 2 Historical background of medical image processing

### 2.1 Early developments in medical imaging

Solutions for the creation of previously inaccessible views of the human body and organ systems that fundamentally revolutionized the approach to diagnostics mark an important stage in the development of medicine. X-ray basis for modern exclusive imaging was pioneered by Wilhelm Röntgen in the year 1895. The technique discovered by Rontgen, which made it possible to examine bones and some internal organs indirectly, without any surgery, quickly found applause in the medical community. The later prospect of capturing pictures of the skeletal system through X-ray enhanced diagnostics became the order of the day in hospitals and clinics across the globe.

The second great advancement following the X-rays was the development of the computed axial tomography (CAT or CT scan) by Godfrey Hounsfield in early 1970s. Using CT scans cross-sectional views of the body were taken to give a detailed and improved picture of internal organs, tissues, and abnormalities. This innovation revolutionized medical diagnostics by making three-dimensional pictures using both computer algorithms and X-rays. Apart from helping in illness identification and diagnosis, this new technology of CT imaging opened a new level of additional imaging advancement.
