## 4 Key imaging modalities in medical image processing

### 4.1 X-ray imaging

### 4.1.1 Introduction to mechanisms of X-ray production and detection

The foundation of X-ray imaging was the assumption that the X-ray radiation would be partially absorbed by the body. X-rays are produced from an X-ray tube; the radiation gets into the human body and is absorbed in different degrees by different forms of tissues. A biologic tissue that transmits fewer X-rays appears as a shade of gray in the resultant image, while bones that transmit more X-rays appear white. A detector converts the beam of the X-rays that penetrate the body into either a conventional photographic film or an electronic sensor. This procedure creates a radiographic picture that provides the right information regarding the internal structures of the body and is valuable for diagnosis.

### 4.1.3 Constraints of X-ray imaging and their resolution

However, there are certain drawbacks that constrain use of X-ray imaging; these are being addressed by modern technology as follows. Some of the issues include high radiation, which is a potential danger, especially when a number of images are taken. Radiation protection strategies and dose-reduction techniques aim to tailor the correct balance between dose rate and diagnostic quality. X-rays also have poor contrast for soft tissues compared to bones; soft tissues also have very homogeneous density. To counter this, there are methods including dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry and the use of contrast agents, which help in the visualization of soft tissues. Also, the interpretation of pictures on an X-ray can be somewhat of a challenge, as this requires knowledge. Machine learning algorithms are being created to assist radiologists in localizing minor manifestations and enhancing the assessment’s accuracy.

### 4.2.2 Recent breakthroughs in image processing: noise reduction and artifact removal

However, CT imaging has some limitations; these are noise and artifacts, which distort image and lead to inaccurate diagnosis. To overcome these difficulties, sophisticated methods of image reconstruction are used. IR method reduces the visibility of random noise and gives better image quality to demonstrate the anatomical details. It is acknowledged that certain artifact correction techniques such as metal artifact reduction and beam-hardening correction are employed to compensate for the impacts of metal implants and other confounding imaging sources. These enhancements improve the analysis of CT images and decrease the rate of re-scans.

### 4.2.3 Methods of increasing the contrast of CT pictures

Sharpening technique is necessary for segmentation of tissues and perception of pathologies in CT scans. Iodine-based contrast agents are given intravenously to outline blood vessels, organs, and tumors for ease of identification. Further techniques for increasing the contrast and detailed view of CT images include image contrast enhancement and histogram equalization. Multiphase imaging techniques and dynamic contrast-enhanced CT contribute further information regarding tissue flow and function in tissue characterization and diagnostic paradigms.

### 4.2.4 Artificial intelligence in automated CT image analysis

CT imaging is becoming increasingly integrated with machine learning and AI to increase the speed and accuracy of diagnosis. One subclass of deep learning algorithms, specifically CNNs, is fed large amounts of data to enable them to learn, detect, and classify anomalies in CT scans on their own. These AI tools enhance the work of a radiologist by identifying potential issue areas, performing measurements autonomously, and providing support for decisions. Through enhancing the capability of analyzing the data obtained from CT images, the use of machine learning in CT imaging is likely to improve specific treatments, quicken the process of diagnosis, and reduce human error.
