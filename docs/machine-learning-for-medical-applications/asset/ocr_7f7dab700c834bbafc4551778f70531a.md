Multimodal anomaly detection has also been used effectively in diagnosing and observing neurological diseases. For example, structural MRI, together with functional MRI and EEG, shows increased metabolic activity in certain areas of the brain concomitant with impaired structural integrity. Research has demonstrated that combining these techniques increases the likelihood of detecting abnormalities related to conditions like epilepsy, Alzheimer's disease, and multiple sclerosis. Structural changes point to disease progression, while functional disruption provides an understanding of the progression of the disordered physiology, which helps clinicians develop more appropriate treatment strategies.

# 4.5.2 The use of PETs and genomic data

PET and genomic data integration in oncology is one of the greatest leaps in the field of personalized approach to patient care. While PET scans give details predisposing toward the metabolic activity level and tumor load, genomic data provides information about the molecular features of tumors. The integration of these approaches helps to provide a more detailed picture about the mechanism of tumor formation and its reactivity to therapy. Examples have been given on how this multimodal approach would point to specific genetic changes that confer drug resistance, thereby proactively improving the strategy for treatment. It also facilitates the determination of individual patient therapies and enhances the outcome prediction possibility.

# 4.5.3 Interdisciplinary work: multiple varieties in cardiology

In cardiology, the multimodal anomaly detection has been used in the analysis of cardiac functions and diseases. For instance, the combination of echocardiography with Cardiac MRI gives a picture of the heart muscles, blood flow patterns, and composition of tissues. This combined approach improves the sensing of such pathologies like valvular heart diseases, cardiomyopathies, congenital diseases of the heart, and blood vessels. Several studies have shown that multimodal analysis enhances diagnostic and treatment assessment, and enhances patient care, and finally care delivery.

The performance indicated from using MDA in medical imaging application is quite encouraging; however, the following challenges are revealed. Some of these challenges are, for example, data integration difficulty, computational requirement, and large annotated data that hinder scalability of any of the mentioned approaches.
