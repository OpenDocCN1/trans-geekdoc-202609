## 2.2 Best practices for documenting scaffold properties and environmental conditions

Adopting standardized documentation of scaffold properties and environmental conditions was a basic requirement in order to obtain reproducible and reliable outcomes. Documentation was initially descriptive and quantitative on the characterization of scaffold materials in terms of tensile strength, elasticity, and porosity. Some of these measurements required the use of uniform techniques and equipment for purposes of comparability across different investigations. For example, care was taken to report scaffold properties in units that are familiar to the construction industry through conformance to standards set by ASTM International.

Aside from material properties, the fabrication process of the three-dimensional scaffolds in this study had to be recounted. This includes recording the specific protocols used, such as solvent systems, temperature conditions, and processing times, as these factors can significantly influence scaffold characteristics. Detailed records of these parameters not only aid in the reproducibility of experimental setups but also provide valuable insights into how variations in fabrication techniques impact scaffold performance.

Environmental conditions during experiments and long-term studies must be meticulously documented, as they play a critical role in the performance and stability of biodegradable nanofiber scaffolds. Key factors such as temperature, humidity, and pH can significantly influence the degradation rate and structural integrity of the scaffold materials. To ensure experimental reliability, calibrated sensors should be employed, and environmental parameters should be recorded at regular intervals. Comprehensive documentation should include not only the intended set points but also any deviations from these conditions, along with corrective actions taken. ## 2.3 Methods for ensuring consistency in degradation rate measurements

To ensure consistency in degradation rate measurements of biodegradable scaffolds, it was essential to implement standardized measurement protocols. This involves defining uniform procedures for preparing and testing scaffolds to minimize variability between different experiments. Particularly recommended are descriptions regarding the choice of scaffold materials, their handling and preparation, the size of the scaffold samples used, and preconditioning steps. Furthermore, environmental factors like temperature and humidity settings in the fluid must also be kept constant and can have their standard protocols throughout the study. Such procedures are followed

to guarantee reliability in measurement as well as reproducibility in other subsequent studies and even different laboratories.

The degradation rate was best determined from instruments that have been well-calibrated and regularly maintained. This means that it was important for equipment that includes mass balances, spectrophotometers, and microscopes, among others, to be calibrated often. Preventive maintenance should be done as recommended by the manufacturer's operating procedures that determine whether an instrument is operating properly. Having a schedule to follow with regard to your instruments and recording the calibration records assists in reducing the chances of getting incorrect measurements, which are usually generated by instruments that have not been calibrated.

Incorporating reference materials and control samples in degradation studies provides a benchmark for consistency. Reference materials with known degradation rates can be used to validate measurement techniques and ensure that the experimental setup is performing as expected. Control samples, which undergo the same treatment conditions as experimental scaffolds but have known properties, allow for the identification of any deviations in measurement results. Regular use of these controls helps in verifying the reliability of degradation rate measurements and identifying any anomalies in the data.

Quality control (QC) procedures are vital for ensuring measurement consistency in scaffold degradation studies. QC measures should include routine checks and validation steps, such as repeating measurements and comparing results across different experiments. Establishing criteria for acceptable ranges of degradation rates and implementing statistical analysis to identify outliers is essential for maintaining data integrity. Comprehensive documentation of QC procedures and results allows for tracking and addressing potential issues promptly.

Ensuring that personnel involved in degradation rate measurements are well-trained and skilled in the protocols is essential for consistency. Training programs should cover the standardized procedures, calibration techniques, and QC practices. Regular refresher courses and assessments help maintain a high level of expertise among researchers. By fostering a consistent level of skill and knowledge, the potential for variability due to human factors is minimized, contributing to more reliable and reproducible degradation rate measurements.

## 2.5 Recommendations for reporting and sharing data in scaffold research

A precise and detailed description of the experimental conditions was critical to making pairs of scaffold studies repeatable and valid. The scaffold materials, the technique used in the fabrication of the scaffold, and the methods employed in the degradation of the scaffold should be elaborated. This encompasses the choice of the polymers to be incorporated and in what proportions, the methods of preparing the nanostructured mats (e.g., electrospinning), and the experimental conditions under which the degradation characteristics of the mats are determined (temperature, pH, humidity, etc.). These conditions are reported over and over, and this makes it possible to replicate the experiments besides making comparisons on the various studies.

The scaffold's properties and its degradation studies involve several quantitative measurements; it was essential to record all experimental data. This should comprise raw data, final statistics, and all activity of data preprocessing that has been conducted. It was essential to attach the experimental and calibration data acquisition procedures, with metadata. Therefore, the documentation that was fully provided increases the research's credibility and enables other scholars to follow and verify the analysis of the results.
