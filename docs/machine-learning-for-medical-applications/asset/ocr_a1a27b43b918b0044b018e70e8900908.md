# 5.1.3 Case studies and applications

Several examples are presented in industrial applications of how visual inspection can work together with sensor information for improved decision-making. For instance, in cars manufacture, visual inspection to check for imperfection in the body of cars and sensors for temperatures and pressures of welding processes can be combined. When all these data sources are incorporated, a manufacturer can be in a position to guarantee the effectiveness of their production process and service of their end products, as well as the efficiency of various tools used in the process. For instance, firms in semiconductor manufacturing industry employ visual sensory means to inspect physical surface imperfections in wafers, and environmental sensors check the levels of humidity and temperature. The cooperation of these data sources assists in identifying problems that affect product quality.

As beneficial as the use of visual inspection, together with the sensory data is, it has its limitation as explained below. Challenges that include synchronization, alignment, and management of high-dimensional data can pose a challenge to the fusion of data. More specifically, dealing with big data and even bigger volume of multimodal data comes due to the computational complexity that cause problems in terms of the needed computing power and storage space. Future advancements in this area probably stress further advancements in data fusion strategies, advancement in real-time computing, and investigation of sophisticated machine learning algorithms for performing better integration and analysis of visual and sensing data.

# 5.2 Quality control systems

Quality assurance procedures are important in manufacturing to ensure that the final products have quality and the quality that has been defined. Multimodal anomaly detection has turned out to be quite effective in quality assurance. It enables manufacturers to possibly identify irregularities that cannot be easily discerned through conventional inspection. The advantages of these systems are that by combining external and internally collected data, such as visual observations and sensor data, together with the process parameters obtained, the systems provide a holistic solution for ensuring product quality and controlling the production processes.

This chapter aims to explore how visual inspection and sensor data can be connected within an automated system.
