Size and surface interactions:

Size and surface modification of materials can complicate the impact on optical properties. For example, ultra-small QDs with much surface functionalization show fluorescence properties that are quite different from those of large QDs with little surface functionalization. In particular, the structure provides information about the relationship between the dimensions of the nanoparticles and their surface roughness, thus suggesting the optimal conditions for size and surface modification to provide the necessary optical properties for specific applications, for example, in high-resolution imaging detectors.

# 5.5.3 Performance metrics for optimization

Fluorescence intensity and resolution:

QD fluorescence intensity is the performance parameter that depends on size and surface modification, whereas concern for resolution and detection and higher intensity are preferred by most of the respondents. To increase the fluorescence resolution and to reduce the background signals, it is very important to control the size and surface chemistry of the microstructures.

Spectral range and separation:

The greatest concern is the broad spectral range and high spectral separation that are important for multiplexed imaging. The emission range depends on the size of QDs and the surface modifications must be optimized to give suitable spectral characteristics of the fluorescent markers and the lack of overlap with other markers.

Long-term stability and reproducibility:

Optical and electronic stability, size distribution, and dispersion are crucial factors influencing the stability and their performance in several applications. Large surfaces and changes to the surface that increase photostability also decrease batch-to-batch variability to yield consistent results.
