# 3.3.3 SLS

SLS employs a laser to fuse powder materials, usually metal, into a solid form. The build platform was coated with a thin layer of powder, and then selective exposure to the laser was used to melt the powder particles as per the prescribed design. As the construction platform descends, the second coat of powder is applied. SLS is valued for allowing the creation of even the most detailed patterns and, at the same time, producing useful elements equipped with a high level of mechanical density and durability, which is ideal for the production of medical implants that must bear the load.

# 3.3.4 DMLS

There exists a subcategory of SLS which focuses on metal materials, and it is referred to as DMLS. In DMLS, a high-intensity laser is utilized to selectively sinter metallic particles to produce thick and dense metallic parts. For uses that need high-strength parts, including orthopedic implants and dental prostheses, the above method is compulsory. Precisely due to the need to modify the mechanical properties and biocompatibility of the implant, DMLS allows for the formation of complex metal shapes as well as the integration of lattice structures.

# 3.3.5 Digital light processing and digital micromirror device (DMD)

SLA looks very similar to DLP, but instead of using a digital light projector to solidify the photopolymer resin, the projector can illuminate the entire layer of the design all at once, while the SLA works in a point-to-point method. This technique is also renowned for the benefit of shorter build time and the provision to produce high-quality prints. DLP is deemed most useful in the generation of intricate medical models and prototypes with precision of surface details and smoothness.
