Making sure that the injury site is moist is important for healing and for minimizing scarring as well. Nanofibers that are electrospun can exudate and at the same time modulate the moisture contents at the wound site, resulting in cellular migration and proliferation. The multifarious nanostructure of the formed nanofiber matrix provides protective covering to the cells and tissues from toxin attack, while maintaining diffusion-limited transport of oxygen/nutrient supply. Thus, biodegradable nanofibers not only protect wounds from drying up but also help the human skin to heal fairly quickly, independent of external help.

To accelerate tissue regeneration, biodegradable nanofiber scaffolds can be loaded with relevant growth factors, cytokines, and therapeutic agents. The controlled release of these bioactive compounds plays a crucial role in enhancing cellular functions essential to wound healing, such as collagen deposition, angiogenesis, and fibroblast proliferation. The nanofiber matrix acts as both a physical support and a biochemical stimulus, promoting organized tissue repair. Moreover, targeted modulation of the inflammatory response by selectively suppressing excessive inflammation helps prevent fibrosis and reduces the likelihood of scar formation. This localized therapeutic approach creates an optimal microenvironment for tissue regeneration. Recent clinical and preclinical searches have shown that biodegradable nanofibers are useful in accelerating scar-free healing. Such studies give evidence of the effectiveness of nanofiber-based wound dressing to enhance results in different kinds of wounds, including acute injuries, chronic ulcers, and mostly surgical operations. Three-dimensional biodegradable nanofibers have been implemented in animal models, and preliminary human studies to show their capacity to aid tissue regeneration and reduce scarring.
