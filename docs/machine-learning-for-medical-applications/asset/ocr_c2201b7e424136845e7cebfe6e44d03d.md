This, coming to the topic of bias-variance trade, was also connected to role of diversity in the context of ensemble-based anomaly detection. Another advantage of diversity is that it decreases both bias and variance on the part of the model as a result of more chances. In particular, if a combination is performed in some way, for example, by averaging the models, then it leads to a decrease in the degree of bias in the detection of anomalies due to the possibility of more accurate identification of the structure of the data extracted from the data samples. On the one hand, the training of multiple models reduces variance since the errors that different models make offset each other. Therefore, overfitting to noise is reduced and the performance of the ensemble generalization to unseen data is enhanced.

The addition of diversity assists in addressing problems of instabilities within the models. In anomaly detection tasks, noise and outliers hinder the performance of the individual model by giving fluctuating results. Nevertheless, as multiple models with differing training data, algorithms, and architecture are included in an ensemble, all these discrepancies can be balanced. The end product is a more stable outcome in terms of prediction, which is not influenced easily by the anomalies of some individual model, thus making overall anomaly detection practical models more robust.
