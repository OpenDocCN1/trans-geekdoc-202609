# 2.3.1 Stereolithography of three-dimensional objects with the aid of light (1986)

Chuck Hull of the University of Utah invented the process known as stereolithography (SLA), which started the present-day 3D printing. SLA was originally conceived to use UV light to cure and solidify one layer of liquid resin at a time in order to create 3D objects. Finally, this discovery paved the way for further development of 3D printing technology and introduced a clear understanding of how the additive manufacturing process can be applied to the formation of complex and highly detailed structures.

# 2.3.2 Patent of the fused deposition modeling (1988)

A significant development in the advancement of technology was realized with Scott Crump’s creation of fused deposition modeling. To create its items, FDM utilizes thermoplastic material that is then laid down in a specific pattern by an integral heated carriage. This technology became popular in both industrial and consumer fields owing to the fact that it brought down the cost of production and development.

# 2.3.3 Select laser sintering development (1989)

Another type of 3DP depends on SLS, invented by Carl Deckard, which fuses powder materials with a laser in a selective manner. SLS made it possible to create parts that are far more durable and useful than the previous parts that could be made using additive manufacturing technology. This development cleared the way for uses referring to accuracy, mechanical strength, and some other aspects.

# 2.3.4 Emerging technique: direct metal laser sintering (1995)

With DMLS, significant progress has been made in the process of printing metal parts with high-strength characteristics. DMLS involves using a laser beam to sinter metal powders, and the final geometry can be complex since it retains the excellent mechanical properties of the base metal. This material application has drastically transformed industries that rely on metal parts such as aerospace, car manufacturing, and implants.
