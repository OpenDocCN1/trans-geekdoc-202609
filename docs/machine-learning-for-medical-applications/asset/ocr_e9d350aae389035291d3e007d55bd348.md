# Size dependence:

For the purpose of quantum confinement effects, the emission spectra of QDs are found to be size-dependent. When the QD size is smaller, then the energy levels are quantized and thus peak emission wavelength experiences a blue shift. On the other hand, large QDs show a red shift. This size-dependent emission can be utilized to fit the color of the emission anywhere between those two extremes for nearly any intended application.

# Core-shell structures:

As illustrated in Figure 8, when a core material is encapsulated with a shell made of a different semiconductor, the resulting core–shell quantum dot (QD) exhibits a shift in its emission spectrum. This shift arises due to alterations in the electronic structure caused by the shell. The shell plays a critical role in enhancing desirable photoluminescence characteristics, such as increasing the quantum yield and improving the color purity of emitted light. By carefully selecting and engineering the core and shell materials, researchers can finely tune the emission wavelength, allowing for precise spectral control suited to specific imaging or sensing applications.

# Surface effects:

The emission spectra are also affected by surface passivation in which the QD is covered by organic. Substrate surface can influence the electronic outline and minimize nonemissive recombination orders, hence increasing luminous quantum yield and stability. Optimization of the surface passivation can also improve the quality of emission spectra as is evident.
