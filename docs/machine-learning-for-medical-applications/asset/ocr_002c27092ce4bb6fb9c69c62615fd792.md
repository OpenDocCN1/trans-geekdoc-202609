# 3.2.2 Solvothermal synthesis

Solvothermal synthesis includes treatment of the precursor materials by undergoing reaction in a high temperature and pressure solvent solution. This technique enables the fabrication of QDs of different materials and geometries: core-shell and alloyed QDs. Solvothermal method has the advantage that the growth kinetics and morphology of the QDs can be closely controlled to produce high-yield, uniform, and high-quality products. The problem of synthesizing QDs with desired property can be solved by changing the reaction conditions for solvothermal synthesis; the synthesized QDs can be applied in imaging and diagnostic techniques.

# 3.2.3 Hydrothermal synthesis

Hydrothermal synthesis was therefore a sub-category of solvothermal synthesis in which water solutions were used. This technique is characterized by the reaction of precursors under high temperature and high pressure in an aqueous solution. There is a special use of hydrothermal synthesis in the preparation of water-soluble QDs and in production of QDs, which needs biocompatible surface modification. This method allows for size and shape control of the QDs and also for functionalization of the surface of the QDs for conjugation of specific functional groups for targeted imaging undertaken for QD synthesis, making hydrothermal synthesis more appropriate for synthesizing QDs for use in biological systems and for in vivo imaging.

# 3.2.4 Reverse micelle method

The reverse micelle process using a surfactant as a stagnant cage allows the synthesis of QDs at the nanoscale. Micelles are used here for the formation of nanoscale reactant compartments in a nonaqueous media by using surfactants. These precursors are then added into these micelles where they react to form QDs. Compared to the other methods, the reverse micelle method provides a strong access control to the size and monodispersity of the QDs and any application that involves the particle size and the picture will be consistent. This technique is used when synthesizing QDs with multiple surface chemistries.
