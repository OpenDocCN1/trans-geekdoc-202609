## 5 Data preprocessing and cleaning

### 5.1 Methods for handling missing or incomplete data in scaffold studies

Dealing with missing and incomplete values starts with determining the degree and types of missing data present. Determining whether missing data were missing completely at random (MCAR) or missing at random (MAR) is important for choosing the right techniques. It becomes the task of researchers to determine whether data were MCAR or MAR. This assessment is useful for selecting the methods for closing the gaps and ensuring that the integrity of the dataset is maintained.

Several procedures for handling missing values that involve estimation of the values with various techniques are called imputation methods. For numerical data, common methods include mean imputation, median imputation, and mode imputation; whereas for categorical data, most frequent category imputation can be employed. Other techniques are more complex and include, for example, multiple imputations, where the missing values are filled several times, creating several datasets, and then the results obtained regarding the variables with missing data are pooled as a measurement of the uncertainty under imputation. Besides that, classical methods that belong to the class of statistical algorithms include multiple imputations based on following the distribution of the neighbors, regression imputations, KNN, and other methods based on artificial intelligence, with the assistance of which missing values can be predicted based on data patterns.

Data augmentation addresses the issue of the availability of large amounts of data, which was missing in different studies. Some methods, like data synthesis, can enrich and improve the quality by adding to the dataset. In essence, augmentation strategies can, to some extent, reduce the damage caused by the lack of complete data and thereby ultimately affect the reliability of forecasts and statistical estimates.

If data was missing to a very large extent and data imputation was not possible, exclusion of data was contemplated. The logic of analysis in such cases was that researchers can remove cases that are unfinished; yet, this process must be done comprehensively to expunge bias. When carrying out data analysis, more assumptions are made, and this is where sensitivity analysis can be used to determine the impact of treatments of missing data on the results of the study. Through conducting the analysis on the results of different handling techniques, authors can gauge the stability of their results as well as mitigate biases from the missing data.

It was important to document the handling process of missing data well for purposes of accountability and documentation. Missing data and its related information should be recorded by researchers; the way and purpose of imputing the missing data, together with the assumptions made, should also be noted down. The documentation of these methods was clear to ascertain the outcomes and to help other scholars check those discoveries.

New approaches to handling missing data are provided by modern trends in data science. For example, the expectation-maximization (EM) algorithm and most of the Bayesian methods are more effective methods of imputing missing values based on statistical models. Such techniques can be especially beneficial when applied to datasets where conventional techniques of imputation fail to provide adequate results. The use of enhanced methods can raise the certainty of findings in scaffold research and development.

It was also noteworthy that by addressing the problem of data incompleteness directly, it was possible to reduce the number of such cases in the first place, for example, due to the optimization of the collection methods. It was possible to minimize the problem of missing data by applying strict data entry procedures, using computer-assisted data collection, and periodically performing validity checks. Consequently, by trying to discover the causes of the lack of completeness in a dataset, the quality of the set was determined to have been improved.
