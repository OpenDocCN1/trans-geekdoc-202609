## 10.4.5.3 Context-Aware Access Control

Recently, Keyes et al. [37] introduced a new access control solution, called the CAAC, based on role and attribute, which can be effectively used in today's dynamic environments. The CAAC system can be used to manage the security of blockchain systems, which in access to the system is provided to the individuals based on role and contextual information [37,39].

The CAAC system involves static and dynamic roles and authorization among different users in dynamic environments. In particular, this type of access control system is beneficial for the healthcare industry. CAAC systems [37,40] can be used to improve data sharing and unauthorized data access based on contextual information [41]. Based on the CAAC state of the art, the following requirements should be designed to implement a blockchain-based CAAC: the relationship-specific data access [42], the situation-specific data sharing [43,44], and the data access according to the imprecise contextual changes [45]. In today's dynamic and pervasive environments, we can integrate both blockchain and CAAC technologies together to implement more advanced access control systems working toward future implementations of security solutions for data sharing between different entities.

## 10.5.1 Open Research Issues

We identify several open research issues that are not well addressed in the literature while considering privacy, security, and access control challenges.

### 10.5.1.1 Data Access Through the Blockchain Technology

The authors in the majority of the research papers surveyed in this study agreed on the fact that blockchain can offer better data access and sharing between different parties. Gordon and Catalini [22] considered the Bitcoin-based blockchain as a stringent mechanism for data sharing, and consensus through smart contracts for restricting the membership. This is also for the healthcare sector, apart from the financial institutions. Whereas, Peterson et al. [10] presented the point of view that it depends on other factors such as the access control system for the system. This is also backed by Vora et al. [31]. On the other hand, Patel [30], Chen et al. [33], Omar et al. [27], and Shen et al. [32] considered blockchain-based systems that provide better security on data access control.

The exploitation of roles, attributes, and contexts in databases have already resulted in data breaches and security vulnerabilities, which are causing huge losses to the companies and governments in today's data-driven environments. Proper access control systems help in regulating the access of data to certain individuals based on different characteristics. Most common characteristics are attributes, roles, and contexts of a situation. The use of the specific characteristic for providing the data based on different scenarios and the access decisions can be based on organizational policies and legislation. The aforementioned three forms of access control systems can be integrated with blockchain technology for secure data sharing (e.g., for data sharing between trusted parties within organization).

### 10.5.1.2 Data Ownership in the Blockchain Technology

Other than access control, blockchain also can provide better privacy and security measures for data owners. Gordon and Catalini [22], Peterson et al. [10], Patel [30], Omar et al. [27], Vora et al. [31], Chen et al. [33], and Shen et al. [32] considered blockchain technology as pivotal data ownership of digital assets. Stephenson and Alex [46] stated that data ownership is more stringent than other options, especially for the consortium and private blockchain-type system. The high level of trust and transparency fused with a high sense of data ownership and data access control can make it an attractive for implementation on a wider scale. However, the limitations associated with blockchain technology can create obstacles in their wider-scale implementation.

Dias et al. [47] confirmed blockchain technology as security in terms of data ownership by stating it provides data ownership to users, who then allow whether information must be shared with others or not.

### 10.5.1.3 Data Sharing and Interoperability the Blockchain Technology

Collectively it can be concluded that blockchain technology provides better security for data sharing. Gordon and Catalini [22] reported that the patient-driven interoperability can be achieved through blockchain technology; however, it can also impose new challenges for privacy and data sharing.

Peterson et al. [10], Patel [30], Omar et al. [27], Vora et al. [31], Chen et al. [33], and Shen et al. [32] considered blockchain technology as a valuable addition in providing advanced security to the data sharing mechanism. In addition to the previously stated arguments, Gaetani et al. [48] also supported arguments that blockchain technology has emerged as a new technology that can provide better data sharing owing to its high transparency.

This might be helpful in tracking down cyberattacks, owing to its ability to provide substantial information for audit. Solanki [49] believed that blockchain provides more secure option in terms of data sharing, owing to the high interaction with RBAC and CAAC systems. Such an integration of blockchain and access control can provide a secure transaction of data and access to specific individuals in an organization.

### 10.5.1.4 Data Breaches in Healthcare

Esposito et al. [24] indicated that data breaches in the healthcare sector are widely observed. This led to the occurrence of different incidents, including disclosure of patient data, loss of credentials, malware, and others. According to Kostova et al. [50], personal information of patients is a valuable asset for healthcare sector and is highly sensitive. According to the Health and Human breach report, approximately 15 million health records have been hacked or accessed in the course of data breaching. According to the reports of the Office of the Australian Information Commissioners, it is identified that over 10 million patients are affected by Australian data breach, in which private information of patients is one of the most affected sectors in the entire country.

Kim et al. [51] claimed that healthcare records of patients are characterized as business record and property of patients; therefore, it is crucial for healthcare providers and organizations to maintain the confidentiality of patient records. Moreover, the process of document, sharing, and storing the healthcare data should be executed with the assurance of authentication and taking other ethical aspects. Although the use of digital platforms and advanced technologies for sharing patient can enhance the integration of advanced technologies in the healthcare sector. However, privacy, confidentiality, security, integrity are some of the issues to be considered when using the blockchain for data sharing.
