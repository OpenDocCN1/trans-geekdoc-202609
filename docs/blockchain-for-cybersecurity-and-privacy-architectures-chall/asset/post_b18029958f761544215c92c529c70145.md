# 1.2 BLOCKCHAIN TECHNOLOGY EXPLAINED

Blockchain technology comprises tamper-proof and tamper-evident ledgers executed without a central repository, as a distributed system, and often without a central authority, such as a government, bank, or a firm. It allows users in a community to store transactions in a shared ledger within community. The transactions cannot be changed when they are published in the blockchain network's normal operation. A new cryptocurrency based on blockchain was created in 2008 by combining it with other computing technologies and concepts. In 2009, BTC became very famous the start of the BTC cryptocurrency, which allowed digital cash to be signed and transferred to a distributed ledger. In Bitcoin, users' digital cash can be digitally signed and transferred to another BTC user. The blockchain announces this transfer publicly to all network users to independently verify the transaction's validity; moreover, a distributed group of users independently manages and maintains the blockchain, and this, with cryptographic mechanisms, creates BT's resilience towards subsequent attacks to modify the ledger by counterfeiting or altering the blocks. Blockchain technology has enabled the development of numerous cryptocurrency systems, such as Ethereum and Bitcoin, and that is why some people tend to restrict BT to cryptocurrency solutions; however, a range of different industry sectors are contemplating BT in their applications [12].

The Nakamoto wallet [13] introduced the concept of electronic cash, and with the launch of BTC in 2009, BT became one of the widely talked-about technologies. Blockchain is a database of blocks that are linked together with a cryptography hash, with replicated information stored in all participants' server. The data in BT database is immutable. It can only be opened by appending new block (data) at the end of the chain by authenticated users (miners) with strong cryptography, as they can add the new block through a competitive mining scheme. Bitcoin is not blockchain. Blockchain is one of the many applications utilizing BT to support the blockchain network, which allows digital cash to be transferred without a distributed ledger. There are many other cryptocurrencies like Ripple (XRP), Ethereum (ETH), Bitcoin Cash (BCH), Litecoin (LTC), and Binance Coin (BNB). In BTC, users' ownership of a BTC can be digitally signed and transferred to another BTC user. There are three main types of BT: private, public or permissionless, and federated consortium blockchain. Both private and consortium blockchains are considered as permissioned; participant management entity is required to grant access rights to trusted and known participants. Examples of private blockchain include Multichain, Monax, and Quorum. A consortium blockchain is controlled by more than one organization. The group of organizations that control the consensus mechanism have predetermined nodes in the network. Examples are Ripple, R3 (banks), and B3 (insurance). In contrast to the previous (two types), public blockchain allows anyone to write or read the data in the blockchain network, without any permission from authority, and the operation is entirely decentralized and anomalous. Some examples are Mono, Ethereum, and Bitcoin. Public blockchain often uses a consensus-based system.

Consensus mechanisms determine which users submit the next block. Consensus mechanisms are designed to allow distrusting users in a blockchain network to coordinate. Many consensus mechanisms have been used in BT, and these proof of work (PoW), proof of stake (PoS), round-robin, proof of authority (PoA) or proof of identity (Po), and proof of elapsed time (PoET).
