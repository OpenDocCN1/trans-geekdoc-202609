# 1.1 INTRODUCTION

Blockchain technology (BT), which is the underlying technology of Bitcoin, has emerged with a number of potential applications. In less than a decade, BT has seen investment by many companies, stimulated the establishment of a range of consortia, and raised US$3.1 billion in total venture capital [1]. The cryptocurrency market created by BT is estimated to reach a total market capitalization of more than US$143 billion [2]. Furthermore, BT has been applied to the public sector and academic institutes [3], and governments have been planning and developing the use of blockchain in the public sector [4]. KPMG [5] reports that in the first half of 2018, investment in blockchain was $14.2 billion. Furthermore, BT has been applied to the public and private sectors, and over 53% of Deloitte's respondents say that BT is a priority for their organizations [6].

Blockchain technology promises a new dimension of conducting business transactions among untrusted entities; it supports verification, identification, authentication, and immutability are guaranteed by cryptography, transparency, and decentralized smart contracts and smart ledgers. Blockchain offers chron chron linked and replicated digital ledgers in a decentralized database and sharing of transactions in an extensive network of untrusted entities. It also provides independent verification guarantees, which eliminate the need to rely on a central authority. Furthermore, given the absence of central authorities, blockchain services are able to provide better security properties for systems that are distributed among different entities and can apply immutability against abuse and supervision if there is a malicious insider.

Given that BT is a cutting-edge technology with many promises, there are concerns about its robustness [7]. There have been many reported cyberattacks, and several cybersecurity vulnerabilities have been identified in blockchain implementations [8].
