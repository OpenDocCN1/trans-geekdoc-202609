These defects raise questions about whether BT can deliver the security guarantees – in practice – that it promises. The growing use of BT as a service delivered by governments or large firms, such as the financial technology industry, has raised users' concerns about its security. Recently, reports have been published about cyber-attacks and cybersecurity vulnerabilities in BT. For instance, 8833 Ethereum smart contracts are vulnerable, and their total balance is 3,068,654 million Ethereum, which is equal to US$30 million [8]. Financial losses are possible because of the vulnerabilities in the smart contracts. For example, an attacker Mt. Go in 2014, the platform for BT trading, stole Bitcoins up to US$40 million, which led to the collapse of Mt. Go. Another example is when a hacker managed to exploit vulnerability and steal Ethereum, which were equal to more than US$60 million in 2016 from the Decentralised Autonomous (DAO), a smart contract in Ethereum blockchain [10]. Taylor et al. [11] showed that researches on BT of cybersecurity are mostly concerned with how blockchain can provide security to current and future systems. There is a paucity of studies on the cybersecurity vulnerabilities of BT. This research raises the following questions:

+   * Is blockchain a proven technology and what does it promise?
* What vulnerabilities are usually exhibited in the BT design?
* How have the theoretical vulnerabilities in blockchain been exploited between 2009 and 2019?

In this chapter, we extend the previous works from different entities and conduct a systematic analysis of identifiable incidents and vulnerabilities reported so far and as establish a direction for the future development of BT. We adopted the methodology to conduct our research:

+   * We started with reviewing news available from sources that specialize in Bitcoin, Ripple, Ethereum, and other digital currencies. The purpose was to collect information about incidents reported in news and logs. Coin-Desk, Coinlegraph, Bitcoin.com, and CCN are some of the sites that we have visited.
* The second stage was to analyze the incidents to look for clues that could lead us to the answers that we have set in the previous questions.
* The third stage was another round of review in search of any new articles and articles that explained those incidents, allowing us to conclude the questions set this research.

The contributions of our study are as follows:

+   * We identified 65 blockchain-related cybersecurity incidents between 2011 and 2019.
* We conducted a detailed analysis of the attack vectors, victims, and damage in each incident and categorized the attack vectors.
