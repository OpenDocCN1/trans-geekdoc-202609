Juan et al. proposed a seven-layered architecture model that supports all communication types in the IoV (Contreras-Castillo, Zeadally, and Guerrero Ibáñez 2017). The layers, from bottom to top, are user interaction, acquisition, processing, communication, management, business, and security layers. The user interaction layer provides a user-vehicle interface to manage notifications and interactions between the driver and the vehicle to reduce driver's distractions. The data acquisition layer allows to gather data for safety, traffic information and infotainment from all sources located on the vehicle, on the street, or on the highway. In this context, several data collection schemes have been proposed (Bali and Kumar 2016; Aaidil et al. 2018; Zhou et al. 2018; Aliquot, and Harous 2019; Senous, Aliquot, and Aliou 2019; Wang et al. 2019). The data-filtering and preprocessing layer analyzes the collected data and performs to avoid the dissemination of irrelevant data and then reduce the network traffic. The communication layer is for intelligently selecting the appropriate network to send the information to heterogeneous and full connectivity, with an optimal quality to the user. The control and management layer is a global coordinator that aims to manage different network service providers. The functions of the business layer include storing, processing, and analyzing all the information received from the other layers. Thus, a decision can be chosen to the best strategy of applying this model in order to improve different data services or to develop new applications. The security layer communicates with all other layers to provide security functions, including authentication, integrity, non-repudiation, confidentiality, and so on. The protocols of this layer should be implemented to mitigate various types of security attacks in IoV, such as denial-of-service (DoS) and Sybil attacks.
