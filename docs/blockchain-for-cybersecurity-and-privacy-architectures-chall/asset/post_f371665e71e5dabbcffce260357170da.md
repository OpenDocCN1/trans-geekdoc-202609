## 13.1 INTRODUCTION

Blockchain technologies provide a secure, decentralized, immutable, transparent, and reliable environment for storage and transfer of digital assets. Smart contracts go one step further and enable computerized transaction protocols that execute terms of a contract (Szabo, 1996) on blockchain, including the automated transfer of digital assets. Autonomous, decentralized, and self-executing features of the smart contracts minimize reliance on lawyers, banks, and other intermediaries, resulting in savings transaction fees and administrative costs (Crosby, Nachiapian, Pattayak, Verma, & Kalyanamaran, 2016; Fanning & Centers, 2016).

One of the main advantages of smart contracts is their capability of automated and secure execution of the contract conditions through a decentralized consensus, with no need for a central authority or a trusted intermediary. Here, smart contracts enable the automated transfer of funds when certain contract conditions are satisfied.
