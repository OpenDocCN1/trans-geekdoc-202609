# 12.4.3.2.5 Selfish Mining

Selfish mining is one of the major attacks in blockchain networks. Selfish mining is where one miner successfully mines a block but does not broadcast to other miners. The miner can keep adding blocks to his secret block, creating a chain. In a blockchain network, the longest chain is considered the correct one. Hence, the blocks of other miners with small chains are invalidated and become orphaned. A selfish miner makes transactions with the chain before they are hidden. Monaco blockchain was attacked in Japan by trying to send it exchanges outside of Japan to exchange it with other coins, before the hidden chains are revealed in the blockchain. The malicious miner had about 57% hash rate or computing power to execute selfish mining. The attack was identified on May 18, 2018, but the authorities stated that the miner had been trying to exploit for about 6 months (Gutteridge 2018).

# 12.4.3.2.6 Fork Problems

A fork in a blockchain when part of a network has different views on transaction history than another part of the network. The fork can occur naturally or can be purposefully introduced. Blockchains were very long and were managed by a few people. Some were split or forked, so that many people could work on small chains. Large cryptocurrencies split or fork their currencies to generate new ones. For instance, Bitcoin underwent a fork on October 24, 2017, to create a new coin Bitcoin. This was created with the aim of allowing more people to mine BTG with less powerful machines. Expert opinion is divided on whether a fork is good or bad for a blockchain (Kharal 2017).
