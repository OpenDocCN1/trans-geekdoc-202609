## 16.3.1 WALLET CREATION

In the proposed system approach, we have considered that each of the stakeholders willing to take part, as any of the roles listed in Table 16.1, must be registered with the blockchain process with their details. The information they need to share while in the registration phase must include at least two identity proof: for example, Indians provide Aadhaar card, voter id, driving license, or passport as proof of identity. Once they are registered and proofs are verified, stakeholders need to apply for wallet assignment process, as shown in Figure 16.3. This wallet assignment process deals with a generation of public-private key combinations, Moreover, each of the stakeholders uses those keys as per the cryptographic applications' requirement. This WiD has a wallet ID WiD to identify the corresponding stakeholder. This WiD also works as pseudonymity of the stakeholder. WiD is generated by applying hash function on the social identity proof. We can use any candidate cryptographic hash function (which we have selected SHA-512) for the process. The private key to protect the privacy of the users, the private key is generated, and mapped to Wi$_{ID}$ information such as name, location, and social identity proofs is all mapped to Wi$_{ID}$ by b bits from the hashed output taking random b bits concatenated with timestamp. The public key is generated by the ring modulo operation on the private keyringing generator.

Private key of User ($U_{PR}$): $\prod_{brand=rand} SHA-512(Social Identity Proof)$
$\in e_{G_1 \times G_1} \rightarrow G_1$

Public key of User ($U_{PB}$): $\tilde{G} \times SHA512(Social Identity Proof) \mod p$

where, $G_1$ is a group of ring elements; $p$ is large prime with condition of $1 < p < b$, and $\tilde{G}$ is the generator of $G_1$.

## 16.3.2 BLOCK GENERATION

Each of the property is considered a block of transactions. For example, a real estate is established with a series of processes such as buying land, raw materials purchase, government dealing, construction, and many more. So, all transactions of processes can be bundled in a single block and are distributed in the RESCHAIN network, as shown in Figure 16.4.

It may happen that all the stakeholders are not part of RESCHAIN at the very beginning. Initiation of a successful blockchain can be done by any of the stakeholders, and other stakeholders can join the network, as applicable. Once blocks are generated, pseudonymity is provided to the block by hash function, which is kept transparent and public. This hash serves two purposes: pseudonymity and integrity of the block data is to be done by the consensus approach.

## 16.3.3 CONSENSUS PARTICIPATION

Once any transaction on the block by any stakeholder is initiated, the initiator broadcasts the request of block $B_{Req}$. Upon receiving the request, the stakeholders (e.g., in Figure 16.5, the existing stakeholders are S1, S2, S3, and S4) of particular block start the verification through the consensus. The pseudonymity is provided to the block by hash function, which is kept transparent and public.
