
## Blockchain Variants

Generally, blockchains are classified into three variants: Public blockchain, private blockchain, and federated blockchain.

## Public Blockchain

If we define a simple way, it is a kind of blockchain ‘for the people, by the people and of the people’. Anyone in the public domain can access the blockchain. The user can participate in reading/writing and auditing the blockchain. The public blockchains are not in the control of a single authority. These types of blockchains are more open and transparent. Since there is no single authority to control the administration of the blockchain, the decisions are taken by various decentralized consensus mechanisms.

## Private Blockchain

As the word makes it self-explanatory, private blockchains are owned by an individual or an organization. The private blockchains are under the control of an individual or an organization. Unlike a public blockchain, in a private blockchain, administrator decides about the access rights and user permissions. In a private blockchain, the consensus is achieved through selective endorsement. This is based on the concept that network participants have gained permission to be there and that the participants involved in a transaction are able to confirm it (Singh and Singh, 2016; Liu, Chen, and Hu, 2017; Taylor et al., 2019).

## Federated/Consortium Blockchain

Basically, a federated blockchain is a group of companies or individuals coming together. It is cost-effective and fast. Federated blockchain looks like a private blockchain but it is not. In a private blockchain, a single organization has control over the blockchain, whereas in a federated blockchain, a consortium of organization has control over the blockchain. It is a permissioned blockchain, where access and permissions are decided by consortium.

## 15.3 BACKGROUND AND LITERATURE REVIEW OF THE EXISTING WORKS

There are very limited research papers, book chapters, and dissertations available on applications of blockchain in the digital forensics domain. Most of them have proposed a model based on blockchain, whereas few papers have discussed a prototype model built on the construct. The research and development on this topic are in an infant stage and are yet to witness a success story.

Lone and Mir (2019) have proposed forensic-chain: a blockchain-digital forensics of custody. The objectives of the authors are to provide a tamperproof chain of custody for digital forensics. A prototype of a blockchain-digital forensics of custody is discussed in the paper.

Bonomi, Casini, and Ciccotelli (2018) have proposed blockchain-based chain of custody (B-C). The work focuses on the implementation of chain of custody. The authors have also performed performance evaluation of Ethereum.

Ryu et al. (2019) have proposed blockchain-based forensics for IoT environment. The IoT environment includes smart grid, smart home, smart applications, smart industry, sensors, etc. The research focuses.

Al-Khateeb, Epiphaniou, and Daly (2019) have briefly discussed the admissibility of digital evidence in a court of law and the importance of chain of custody. The work focuses on forensics readiness using Ethereum.

Li, Qin, and Min (2019) have proposed the evidence identification, preservation, analysis, and presentation recorded in the chain of blocks.
