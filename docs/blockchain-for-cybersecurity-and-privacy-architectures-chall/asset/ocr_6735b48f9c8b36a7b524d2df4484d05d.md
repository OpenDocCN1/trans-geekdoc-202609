The Byzantine Generals Problem can be put in a nutshell how we can make that multiple entities, which are far apart each other, are in full agreement (consensus) before an action is taken?

For instance, assuming there is a general in the Byzantine army who is planning an attack on an enemy city that is surrounded by several other lieutenants led by their own generals. The lieutenants are far in the distance from each other, and they need to have a coordinated attack in order to achieve success, as an uncoordinated attack will likely end in defeat. The general decided to attack at dawn, but they need to be certain that all generals reach consensus and all attack at dawn. When they send messages to others through messengers, it might be possible the messenger is captured and the message gets intercepted, or the enemy sends a fake reply, or the messenger gets killed and no reply is received, or the enemy confirms that all lieutenants reach consensus and attack concurrently? If we map the explained example on computer systems, the lieutenants would be computers on a network and the generals would be program running ledgers, which record transactions and events in the exact order that they happened, and they absolutely for everyone, for that soon as a change occurs on one of the copies of the ledger, all are synchronized with it. In this way, consensus is achieved on an event, without the requirement of any third party as an intermediary for trust.

When the order of transactions is being calculated, there comes a time when consensus is reached ultimately, which is referred to as the BFT. It basically means:

+   We are going to consensus;
We will know when we've come to consensus;
We're never wrong

It is mathematically assured that all individuals reach the same consensus. The BFT has two categories: asynchronous byzantine (aBFT) and partially asynchronous byzantine. The difference is the level of system's presumptions. As aBFT assumes the practical scenario; that is, untrustworthy nodes are always present in the systems. Conversely, partially asynchronous BFT works on a false approach or an ideal-world assumption that evil characters do not exist in the system.
