## 8.4.6.5 Healthcare

The healthcare is a sensitive sector where privacy preserving provide a secure environment for medical data is a major issue. The blockchain can provide for storing and sharing data between providers, payers, and patients in the healthcare systems. The blockchain can be used for healthcare management, such as supervising drugs, regulation compliance, testing results, and pharma supplies.

## 8.4.6.6 Smart Cities

Smart cities are addressing urbanization problems by integrating new technologies. Many projects have been implemented using the blockchain in smart cities, such as the Smart Dubai 2021. This project aims to move all government operations and records on the blockchain by the year of 2021; the goal of this is to provide better services to citizens. Many applications are attached to this project; one, is the smart mobility option, which includes automated traffic signals and autonomous cars as a transportation alternative.

With these kinds of applications, smart cities are tending to be better in terms of efficiency and transparency, which would improve the life of residents of these cities.

## 8.4.7 Blockchain-Based Solutions

The authors in Busysin et al. (2018) introduced a floating genesis block as an attempt to deal with blockchain growth. The main idea is to include not only the hash of the current block (state update) but also the hash of all the system state (blockchain). As the next state can be computed from the previous one only a single state could be stored. Thus, all previous data except block headers could be stored.

In order to avoid a secret chain attack, the authors in Bruce (2017) suggested newly arrived to download the system state from a trusted source. However, this solution could only be used with applications of a limited lifetime. This may conflict with the traceability requirements, such as routing and authentication of transactions.

Sharma and Park (2018) introduced novel hybrid network architecture for smart cities. The proposed architecture takes advantages of both software-defined service and blockchain technologies to deal with high latency, bandwidth, and scalability requirements. In addition, a memory-hardened PoW scheme is implemented to ensure security and privacy. The memory-hardened PoW consists of disk encryption and instantiation parameters for cryptocurrency applications (Coelho, Larroche, and Colin 2017). For an attacker, it is very laborious to elucidate the proper chain elements with their correct path in the Merkle tree.

In Hossain, Hasan, and Zawoad (2017), the authors proposed a digital forensic framework that investigates criminal cases in IoV systems. The main purpose is to collect evidence and store them into an external service that maintains a secure provenance. Evidence is signed and stored in a secure blockchain, where each block contains a set of proof of past stories (PPS) as evidence. This ensures the integrity of the stored evidence and allows investigators to verify the integrity of the evidence and to prevent attackers from manipulating outcomes of an investigation. PPS does not contain any information related to nodes' real identities and interactions, it is published on the Internet, and privacy cannot be violated.

The authors in van Heijden et al. (2017) proposed a blockchain-based solution that provides the accountability of messages and revocations through a distributed ledger called event record. It allows a collaborative and transparent decision through using this ledger, never we trust in any centralized party. Scalability is ensured using a hierarchical consensus by creating clusters by vehicles. The authors employed a permissioned blockchain that backups the transactions. The cars form clusters and propagate the state to the RSUs, which in turn publish their results to misbehave authorities. These transactions are published to a permissionless blockchain for public verifiability and to provide global revocation of misbehaving vehicles. Vehicles participate in clustered communications (permissioned blockchain) to agree on a common state and decide to revoke the corresponding vehicle. The clusters reduce the state and update the vehicles from mining into blockchain. The size and frequency of updates are reduced. Moreover, the authors proposed the algorithm's scalability by using a hierarchical consensus; the authentication is ensured by revoking misbehaving vehicles.

Singh and Shiho (2018) proposed a branch-based blockchain solution for securing intelligent vehicles' communications. The proposed method includes two blockchains and seeks a unique cryptocurrency identified intelligent vehicle trust point (IVTP) to ensure trustworthiness among vehicles. The vehicle that has IVTPs seems to be trustworthy. The local dynamic blockchain (LDB) stores data according to its size. The main blockchain (MB) stores permanently data forwarded by LDB. The MB uses the Merkle tree to keep track of IVTP between vehicles. The authors introduced the LDB branching to split the blockchain into multiple parallel chains, each chain covers a distinct geographical region.

In Sharma (2019), the authors proposed an energy-efficient model for blockchain IoT by controlling the number of transactions required to update ledgers through a distributed cluster. The excessive transactions and energy consumption for ledger updates are many concern in this study. The proposed solution uses distributed clustering based on the stochastic volatility model. The solution of energy-efficient transition was attained by performing optimal offloading and selecting new Cluster Head (CHs), whereas the network's energy consumption goes beyond the limits.

Authors in Li et al. (2018) proposed a privacy-preserving blockchain-based incentive announcement network using CreditCoin. The algorithm is to address the lack of motivation of users and to forward announcement without revealing users' identities. The proposed scheme is based on two phases: announcement and incentive mechanisms. The announcement protocol named echo announcement provides threshold authentication and a certain privacy level to guarantee that anonymous announcements are reliable in CreditCoin. The incentive mechanism works with the echo announcement in which every user in CreditCoin owns a account at several addresses. The account contains points called coins. Users reward traffic announcements from a certain area by paying some coins. The authors prevented the MIM attack by using consensus algorithm (i.e., if an attacker modifies any contents of the transaction, such as the values of coins or addresses, the transaction will be rejected by users through the verification of the consensus phase). The proposed method also resists Dos, message tampering, and Sybil attacks.

In Zhang and Chen (2019), the authors proposed a data security system based on the consortium blockchain (DSSCB). They employed digital signature technique based on bilinear pairing for elliptic curves to ensure reliability and integrity. The emerging consortium blockchain technology is used to provide a decentralized, secure, and reliable database, which is maintained by the entire network. The DSSCB uses smart contract to limit the triggering conditions for selected nodes when storing, transmitting, and allocating data coins to vehicles that participate in the contribution.

Arora and Kumar (2018) introduced an authenticated and secure data transfer algorithm using the blockchain technology to ensure true information communication between the IoV nodes. They used two chains of blocks, one for the authentication and the other for the communications. Their method ensures security through two steps. In the authentication phase, every vehicle needs to register with the authority to prevent any malicious node from joining the network. In the communication phase, the authenticated vehicles exchange data with each other, using digital signature and group key to secure the communications. Security is granted by enabling only authenticated vehicles to participate in the network. These vehicles are provided with a shared symmetric key to encrypt the transferred messages, thus protecting the confidentiality of data.

Zhaojun et al. (2018) proposed a blockchain-based reputation system to establish trust and privacy in VANET. They used two authorities: a local enforcement authority (LEA) that manages the whole system and stores the pair of identity and public key and a certificate authority that issues the certificate for vehicles and revokes the misbehaving ones. They employed three blockchains: chain for recording the exchanged messages and for the issued certificates and the revoked keys. The basic security requirements are granted with PKI, and public keys are used as pseudonyms in communications, without exposing any information about real identities. The authentication mechanism is based on certificates and PKI, which protect against outsiders' attackers and identify the connected vehicles. To establish trust, a reputation system among vehicles, is presented to prevent the distribution of forged messages. It evaluates the score of the vehicle that broadcasts the message and decides to accept or ignore the message.
