# 11.2.1 DSCSA AND FMD POLICIES

Both DSCSA and FMD mandate that the originators of drugs in the supply chain represented by manufacturers and packers are required to provide a unique identifier for each package, and those identifiers are printed as two-dimensional barcode labels and attached to each package and lot [3,4]. Both many similarities in the type of data needed to build the unique identifier. The DSCSA requires the use of the National Drug Code (NDC) which is interchangeable with Global Trade Item Number (GTIN) in the EU FMD. Both require that each label contains the lot number, the expiry date, and a serial number for each package. The aforementioned data is the metadata for each single package.

The FMD regulation takes a step further by introducing the requirement for the dispenser (e.g., a pharmacy) to ensure the verification of each sealable package. The FMD and DSCSA also define the set of business processes that a product could possibly experience throughout the product life cycle by the GSI supply chain rules to include all events from the time of origin until the drug being dispensed, which require that third-party logistics providers (3PLs) to perform verification and decommissioning activities and that in the system with high level of trust in performing those operations.

Highlighting those issues and regulatory requirements, the authors suggest a blockchain-based system that offers a viable solution to simplify the logistical process and enable quality control throughout the supply chain. The next section describes the technology and its fit to supply chain requirements. They suggest a system for supply chain members to authenticate drugs as they move from one entity to another.

The proposed decentralized blockchain-based system allows its members to authenticate products and identify how entities authenticate products each time to build a complete distribution history, which can help in identifying rogue actors and tighten security throughout the supply chain. The authors view that the system is to be used for offering custody relating to the record of organizations, entities, and locations that have physical possession of the drug and tracing of ownership that records the change of drug ownership until the end customer.

# 11.3 BLOCKCHAIN AND DISTRIBUTED LEDGER TECHNOLOGY

Blockchain is defined as a shared distributed digital ledger for transactions. It employs public key cryptography in forming the identity and pseudonymity of all participants and consensus algorithms to maintain the ledger intact and to verify any transaction. The distributed ledger is made by combining many of transactions in a block and their content with the hash value of the previous block to construct the hash value of the new block. This happens for each newly block, except for the genesis block.
