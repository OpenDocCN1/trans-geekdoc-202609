The authors in van Heijden et al. (2017) proposed a blockchain-based solution that provides the accountability of messages and revocations through a distributed ledger called event record. It allows a collaborative and transparent decision through using this ledger, never we trust in any centralized party. Scalability is ensured using a hierarchical consensus by creating clusters by vehicles. The authors employed a permissioned blockchain that backups the transactions. The cars form clusters and propagate the state to the RSUs, which in turn publish their results to misbehave authorities. These transactions are published to a permissionless blockchain for public verifiability and to provide global revocation of misbehaving vehicles. Vehicles participate in clustered communications (permissioned blockchain) to agree on a common state and decide to revoke the corresponding vehicle. The clusters reduce the state and update the vehicles from mining into blockchain. The size and frequency of updates are reduced. Moreover, the authors proposed the algorithm's scalability by using a hierarchical consensus; the authentication is ensured byoking misbehaving vehicles.

Singh and Shiho (2018) proposed a branch-based blockchain solution for securing intelligent vehicles' communications. The proposed method includes two blockchains and seeks a unique cryptocurrency identified intelligent vehicle trust point (IVTP) to ensure trustworthiness among vehicles. The vehicle that has IVTPs seems to be trustworthy. The local dynamic blockchain (LDB) stores data according to its size. The main blockchain (MB) stores permanently data forwarded by LDB. The MB uses the Merkle tree to keep track of IVTP between vehicles. The authors introduced the LDB branching to split the blockchain into multiple parallel chains, each chain covers a distinct geographical region.

In Sharma (2019), the authors proposed an energy-efficient model for blockchain IoT by controlling the number of transactions required to update ledgers through a distributed cluster. The excessive transactions and energy consumption for ledger updates are many concern in this study. The proposed solution uses distributed clustering based on the stochastic volatility model. The solution of energy-efficient transition was attained by performing optimal offloading and selecting new Cluster Head (CHs), whereas the network's energy consumption goes beyond the limits.

Authors in Li et al. (2018) proposed a privacy-preserving blockchain-based incentive announcement network using CreditCoin. The algorithm is to address the lack of motivation of users and to forward announcement without revealing users' identities. The proposed scheme is based on two phases: announcement and incentive mechanisms. The announcement protocol named echo announcement provides threshold authentication and a certain privacy level to guarantee that anonymous announcements are reliable in CreditCoin.
