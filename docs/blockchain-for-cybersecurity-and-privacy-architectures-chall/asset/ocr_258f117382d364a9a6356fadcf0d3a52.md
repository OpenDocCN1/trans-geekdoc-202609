The blockchain technology augmented by the building information model (BIM) provides a powerful tool for keeping the records of the BIM during the design and construction phases (Shou, Wang, & Wang, 2015; Wang, Wang, & Shou, 2017) ensures accuracy of information through immutability and identification of the person making changes with the details of the changes to allow better recording and tracking of the data (Stoglou & Magneron, 2018). Blockchain integrated with BIM may enable addressing challenges such as traceability and data ownership by providing change, as well as provenance tracking (Nawari & Ravandran, 2019). As a result, the environment provided by the blockchain could maximize the efficiency of the collaborative design process and integrated project delivery strategies. In addition, blockchain presents a method to address cybersecurity threats such as age failures, information corruption and disruption of BIM operations by authorized access, and malicious intent of involved participants, which can affect BIM workflow and its connected systems. The proof of delivery, which is secured and transparent via blockchain, can also be used as a contract document management tool.

Blycha (2019) proposed the idea of recording the delivery dates of the materials and performing automated payment of them by smart contracts when they deliver to the predetermined location. Applying the blockchain technology in supply chain phase of the contract projects may significantly increase the performance of the supply chain management (Li, Greenwood, & Kassem, 2018). Pilagos (2019) presented an example in which the weather retrieved from the weather sensor on-site is an input to the smart contract, which enhances compensation of weather events according to the contract conditions.

The immutable blockchain provides an environment in which the compliance information can be stored securely (Icertis, 2019). The certificates related to the materials and site quality checks can be recorded, stored, and shared through the participants within the blockchain system (Penzis, 2018). Hence, blockchain can also be utilized for quality assurance (Wang et al., 2017) for confirming the quality requirements specified in the contract and the specifications.

One of the key application areas of smart contracts for contract management is the assurance of payment for equipment, materials, and services. Hence, smart contracts present a major opportunity to eliminate or reduce payment delays and trust issues in the industry. In the procurement phase, the payment regarding the materials and equipment could be embedded into a smart contract and be automatically transferred when the materials and equipment are delivered to the site; the need for the letter of credit (a document that is provided by the bank to guarantee the payment) could be eliminated (Ahmadsheikharsmar & Sonnez, 2018). Smart contracts with embedded funds are also proposed for the construction phase of the projects for eliminating payment issues and to improve the efficiency of the contract administration process (Cardeira, Wang et al., 2017; Mason 2017; Li & Kassem, 2019).
