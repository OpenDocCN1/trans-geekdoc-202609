# 5.5.6 AUTHENTICATION

The IoT consists of a large number of devices involved in data capturing and transmission from one place to another, without the involvement of human beings in the whole process. However, the critical challenge faced by today's IoT network is the authentication and authorization of devices to transmit data in a secure and trusted manner. For the achievement of such challenges in IoT, Hammi et al. (2018) proposed the blockchain-based decentralized authentication scheme to identify and authenticate the device using the features of blockchain technology. The scheme works in the form of secure zones called bubbles, which are responsible for authenticating the IoT devices for secure communication among them. The smart contracts are utilized to secure the communication rules over miners to authenticate and register the IoT devices in a particular region.

The integration of blockchain technology into IoT has started to resolve the various security and privacy issues. Not only decentralization but other features, such as immutable ledger, transparency, and auditability, have the potentials to come up with new and significant solutions for IoT networks. Jiang et al. (2019) proposed a blockchain-based public key mechanism for clients as an example to make blockchain-integrated IoT work practicable. The thin clients believe as IoT devices are capable of downloading a full copy of the distributed ledger into their storage. In addition, the proposed architecture protects the privacy of thin IoT clients using private information retrieval in the network. In this manner, the decentralized architecture can provide a secure environment to both thin and full nodes for securely exchanging their information.

With the increasing number of devices in the IoT network, the management of such devices is a crucial task. Such a task demands potential efforts from the researcher communities to overcome this by providing meaningful solutions. The integration of blockchain technology with IoT network brings new reforms to the maintenance of network operations by the core features of blockchain. For example, the immutability feature in the blockchain allows the network administrator to manage and configure the devices by resisting against malicious modifications. To implement such an, Jiang et al. (2019) proposed a blockchain-based decentralized architecture, which monitors and the IoT devices using a private blockchain. The architecture allows us to Create, Read, Update, and Delete operations with the utilization of advanced control mechanisms. In the proposed architecture, an updated file related to the configuration for devices is stored at the immutable ledger. In case of any device failure, the file can easily be downloaded from the blockchain database. As a result, the data are updated and record on the database securely.
