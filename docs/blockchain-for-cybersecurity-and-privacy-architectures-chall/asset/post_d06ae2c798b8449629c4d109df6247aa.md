## 9.1 Introduction

Healthcare is an indispensable part of life. Technological advancements have improved the standard of life, providing better healthcare services. The emergence of Internet of Things (IoT) has enabled patients to monitor their own health. Patient data can be categorized into two types: electronic medical record (EMR) and personal health data (PHD). EMR is the digital form of patient’s health history and treatment plans. These records are owned and maintained by healthcare providers. The current storage mechanism of EMR is mainly a centralized database system. PHD is generated by gadgets and healthcare applications such as Fitbit, Apple Watch, Google Fit. This data is generated and maintained by the patients. The enormous amount of data generated from personal health has raised concerns about secure storage capacity of centralized as cloud systems. The current system of exchanging health data between various entities inside the healthcare domain is called health information exchange (HIE). It can be a healthcare provider sharing data with another provider or a physician sharing prescriptions to a pharmacy so that patients can receive medicines. HIE aims at increasing interoperability between different entities. Currently, HIE covers only the exchange of EMR.
