## 16.6 SUMMARY OF THE CHAPTER

Queuing theory is a generally accepted and essential tool for improving decision-making in manufacturing and service organizations. Given increasing globalization and the presence of international competitors in the market, the scenario of these organizations is also changing. The objective of the research was to investigate waiting-line problems in college canteens.

Queues are common phenomena that we encounter regularly in our day-to-day lives. The fundamental qualities of a waiting-line phenomenon are that units arrive, at standard or sporadic intervals of time, at a given point called the service center. The “queuing or waiting lines” may involve either scheduled arrivals or the delivery of an appropriate quantity of services, or both, so as to attain an optimal balance between the cost related to waiting time and idle time.

In a college cafeteria or canteen, students expect a definite level of service; however, the owner of the canteen providing the service attempts to spend as little as possible while delivering the essential service. The present study was carried out to optimize the balance between waiting time and the service rendered in the college canteen.

The data was collected during peak or rush hours. From that data, various parameters, i.e., the average time a student spends in the canteen, the average waiting time of a student in the queue, and the utilization rate, etc., were also observed. It was observed that queuing theory plays a vital role in the decision-making process for various waiting-line problems.

From the study, it was concluded that the owner of the canteen currently has three service providers and has to pay a salary of Rs. 71 per hour to each of them. On the basis of the analysis, it was observed that the owner needs to employ only two service providers in order to optimize costs, while saving Rs. 21 per hour.

The scope of the present research work was limited to one canteen only, but it could be expanded to other canteens and also to other sections of the college, such as the accounts department and broader student services. With the implementation of queuing theory, there will be both time and cost savings. Waiting-line theory is a very easy and user-friendly tool that may help decision-makers formulate appropriate business policies in the future. With this, the business will grow and achieve new milestones in the world.

### 17.1 INTRODUCTION

The Driver Drowsiness Detection and Alerting System refers to in-vehicle systems that monitor driver and/or vehicle behavior. These systems keep an eye on the driver’s performance and alert or stimulate them if they exhibit signs of impairment. They alert drivers when they start to feel sleepy. There are many approaches employed, including eye monitoring, physiological movement, and the lane position of the car [1].

These tools, which come as standard in all cars, can only alert a motorist that he or she is drowsy. It is the driver’s responsibility to take a break and to make sure that the break is long enough to allow them to refocus [2]. The technology is unable to evaluate the quality of the driver’s break. After a pause, however, the system will continue to track the driver’s behavior and notify them once again if fatigue is detected [3]. It is anticipated that the warnings will provide drivers with the motivation they need to take a rest.

Road accidents can happen for a number of reasons, and one of those reasons is drowsy driving. This generally occurs when the driver has not had enough sleep [4]. The body performs less effectively when it is fatigued. Drivers may suffer serious injuries or even die. According to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration’s assessment, in 2013, drowsy driving contributed to 800 fatalities, 40,000 injuries, and 70,000 collisions [5].
