As discussed in earlier works, a variety of convolutional neural network models were created with the aid of deep learning to detect plant diseases [11]. Our algorithm was trained using a dataset made up of 14,956 pictures, separated into 38 classes. After our model has been trained, photographs where diseases have been detected were obtained to carry out several activities such as preprocessing, segmentation, feature extraction, feature fusion to blend all the features and categorize the diseases. For the problem classification, we used cutting-edge machine learning methods like Random Forest, SVM, KNN, and ANN. Confusion matrix was used as the evaluation criteria for the experiment. Numerous computations, including those for accuracy, recall, precision, specificity, and F-Score, were then performed using the confusion matrix [15].

Examining the visible patterns on the leaves of a plant is an important part of plant pathology research [12]. By comparing the color and texture of different parts of the same plant, we can now tell which parts are healthy and which are diseased. The diagnostic process for plant diseases is typically conducted in a controlled laboratory setting. This process takes a long time and is costly. This study used many approaches to achieve the goal, which helped both to save money and diagnose diseases at an early stage.

The pictures of the leaves of three different kinds of Ficus trees—F. benjamina, F. punctata, and F. sumatrana—were selected [16]. In this study, 54 photo samples of leaves were used. Three main processes—image preprocessing, feature extraction, and recognition—were used to build the suggested system. The recognition models used were the artificial neural network (ANN) and the support vector machine (SVM). The proposed approach was shown to have an accuracy of 83.3% when identifying photos of leaves in tests. However, when using the area under the ROC curve (AUC) metric, the ANN model performed marginally better. The classification technique used in this investigation can reliably distinguish between the specified Ficus species.

Foliar samples with 1.016 spectral signatures at four disease stages (T1: healthy, T2: mildly infected, T3: moderately infected, and T4: severely infected) were used to get these results [14]. The visible spectrum, particularly the green end of it, produced most of the effective results. The classification accuracy of ANN was 83.3% in the 540–550 nm range for identifying healthy oil palms and 100% for identifying oil palms infected with Ganoderma at the T2 stage. When comparing fronds 9 and 17, the results revealed that the sensitive frond number modeled by ANN offered the highest accuracy of 100.0% for frond 9. The research found that ANN was effective in predicting the onset of the basal stem rot (BSR) disease in oil palm plants [17–20].
