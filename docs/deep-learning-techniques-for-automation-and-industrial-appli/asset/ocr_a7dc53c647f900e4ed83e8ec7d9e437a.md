# 7.3.2.4 AdaBoost Learning Technique

AdaBoost [10] or adaptive boosting is one group of boosting classifiers proposed by Yoav Freund and Robert Schapire in 1996. The AdaBoost classifier [10] is a combination of poorly performing or less accurate classifiers. They have been combined and their weights have been set. Then, all their sample data are trained every time with the expectation that by doing so, higher accuracy ratings can be gained.

The fundamental concept behind AdaBoost is to establish numerous classifiers and iteratively refine the training data in each iteration to ensure accurate predictions of observations [10]. Artificial intelligence-based calculations are used as a basic and strong classifier if it accepts loads on the set of preparation.

# 7.3.2.5 KLT Tracker

In computer vision, the Kanade–Lucas–Tomasi (KLT) [11] highlight tracker is a way to deal with feature extraction. It is proposed primarily to manage the issue that conventional picture enlistment procedures are commonly expensive. KLT utilizes spatial power data to coordinate the scan for the position that yields the best match. It is quicker than customary systems for inspecting far fewer potential matches between pictures [12].
