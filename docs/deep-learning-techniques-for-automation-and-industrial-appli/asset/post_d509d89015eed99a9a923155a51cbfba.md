However, the essential methodology for plant disease detection in parts of underdeveloped countries remains a process still conducted through visual observations by trained farmers. This requires ongoing professional supervision, which may be prohibitively expensive in agricultural production. Additionally, in certain distant areas, farmers may have to move from place to place to reach experts, making discussions expensive and time-consuming. The goal of leaf detection is to recognize and extract data on the health, growth, and yield potential of plants and crops from photographs of plants and crops using computer vision and machine learning algorithms. Decisions about crop management practices, such as pest control, fertilization, and irrigation, can be made using this knowledge. Large databases of plant and crop photos can be analyzed to find patterns and signs of disease using machine learning and artificial intelligence techniques. This can help with early and accurate disease identification, which can result in quicker, more efficient care of the condition and more effective preventive measures.

Plant and agricultural disease detection has a variety of applications in agriculture, including disease control, yield improvement, and sustainable farming. The need for effective and sustainable food production is driving growth in research and development in the field of disease detection. One of the most effective ways to forecast and classify crop diseases is by collecting photos of crop development and creating a detection model. This can increase the efficacy of disease management and decrease agricultural losses brought on by diseases, while also providing timely and effective early warning and intervention for agricultural output.

However, this technology cannot be fully extended and can only be used in small spaces. Computerized plant disease detection is an important research area because it might be useful in monitoring vast fields of crops, and accordingly, automatically detecting disease symptoms as early as possible. One of the direct challenges to food security is plant disease. Up to USD 250 billion is lost each year due to diseases and insect pests in agriculture worldwide. By 2021, 400 million hectares of China would be afflicted by serious plant diseases and insect infestations. The candidate region-based detection approach has poor real-time performance and significant computational effort, although it has reasonably good accuracy. To ensure quick and effective identification, researchers have devised a single-stage target detection technique [1–3].

Therefore, timely crop disease detection through the plant leaves is essential for agricultural production. Finding a quick, automated, more affordable, and precise strategy to recognize plant infections is in this way vital, according to a commonsense viewpoint.

## 3.2 Literature Review

The research paper “Using Deep Learning for Image-Based Plant Disease Detection” was written by Sharada P. Mohanty and published in 2016 [1]. This research uses CNN, and the results show that random guessing only achieves an accuracy of 2.63% on a dataset with 38 class labels. Each of our experimental configurations includes three visual representations of the picture data, and the accuracies we were able to obtain here on the Plant Village dataset varied from 85.53% to 89.54% [4].

The research paper titled “Machine Learning in Cereal Crop Disease Detection” was written by F. G. Waldamichael, T. G. Debelee, and F. Schwenker and was published in 2022. This research used deep learning techniques. As a result, we received five distinct accuracy results from Agarwal, Siby, and Barman. The references in this paper are Hasan and Tian, and the limitations and future work are to better understand the most recent machine learning applications for cereal crop disease detection. There was also a detailed study of literature consisting of 45 research articles focused on the utilization of deep learning for the identification of major diseases that infect five distinct types of cereal grains, namely wheat, rice, maize, oats, and barley [5, 6].

The research paper titled “Review of Imaging Technologies for Detecting Plant Diseases” was published in 2020, and was authored by N. Sharma and S. Singh. The techniques used in this paper were classification techniques such as CNN, Random Forest, and Decision Classifier. The paper’s main conclusions include a summary of alternative imaging techniques that are effective for early detection of plant diseases [7, 8].

The research paper titled “A Comparative Study of Various Techniques for Crop Disease Detection and Segmentation” was written by A. Khakimov and was published in 2022. The main technique employed was the Content-Based Image Retrieval (CBIR) system, which mainly uses color, shape, and texture as its basic features [9]. An image containing these basic features is set as the query image, and CNN, SVM, and K-means were also used. The paper presents future work on the use of the CBIR technique to retrieve diseased leaf images, which yields better results in image extraction. The comparative study and its results were presented in the form of a table that contains the accuracy of different algorithms.

The authors of this research suggest using the ResTS (Residual Teacher/Student) architecture as a visualization and classification method for identifying plant diseases. ResTS has developed from the similarity-based teacher/student architecture [10]. The convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture, on which ResTS is built, consists of a decoder, two classifiers (teacher and student), and a convolutional neural network. The shared representation between the teacher and the student is utilized as a proxy to visualize the relevant portions of the picture for classification in this architecture, which trains both classifiers as effectively as feasible. Testing has shown that the teacher/student design (F1 score: 0.972) was surpassed by the suggested structure of ResTS (F1 score: 0.991), which would produce more precise representations of the disease's symptoms. To locate and identify diseases in citrus trees, researchers who performed this study using the current ResTS architecture proposed a hybrid technique. The two primary stages of the desired research are (a) finding citrus fruit and leaf lesions, and (b) categorizing citrus diseases. Citrus lesion spots are extracted using an upgraded input picture and a modified weighted segmentation algorithm. Then, by fusing geometrical components, color, and texture, a codebook is produced. To find the best features, a hybrid feature selection strategy is also applied. Principal component analysis (PCA) values, fluctuation, and standard deviation covariance vectors are fundamental parts of this methodology. Once the most suitable qualities have been selected, they are then input into the M-SVM model for analysis, focusing on a range of citrus diseases. This analysis includes testing the proposed technique using a combined dataset from Plant Village, Citrus Pictures Information Base of Plagued with Scale, the Citrus Disease Image Gallery Dataset, and image databases obtained from their respective owners. Anthracnose, black spot, canker, scab, greening, and melanosis are among the citrus diseases that have been detected and categorized using these databases [11, 12]. On the combined dataset, the local dataset, and the citrus disease image gallery dataset, the proposed technique outperformed the existing methods with a classification accuracy of 97%, 89%, and 90.4%, respectively.

In this research, a machine learning-based real-time system to diagnose the type of crop disease based on leaf photos is proposed. A single-shot approach is employed to identify and locate the leaf, and a deep convolutional neural network architecture is proposed to perform the classifications [13]. These models are utilized for real-time in-field plant disease recognition and identification on embedded equipment, specifically the Nvidia Jetson TX1. The obtained results are compared, and the accuracy of the proposed deep convolutional neural network structure and the subsequent disease classification is approximately 96.88%.
