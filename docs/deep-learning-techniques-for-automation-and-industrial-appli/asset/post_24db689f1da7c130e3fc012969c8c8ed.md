Also, they pretrained their network with autoencoders and found better results as compared to not using them. They were not able to surpass the SRM results, and this they believed was due to the lack of availability of GPU. Inspired by this prior work, researchers [16] came up with a CNN network with Gaussian Neuron (GN) activation function which was specific for solving steganalysis problems. The results were only slightly lower than SRM on BOSSbase 1.01 [17] dataset of 10,000 images and were comparable with SRM on ImageNet [18] dataset of millions of images. They also used a preprocessing layer in which the image was convolved with the KV high pass filter of SRM to calculate the residual of the image. The residual image was then fed to the remaining CNN network. In [19], researchers have used deep transfer learning, and their network has learned features of cover and stego images at higher payload, and they have then refined those features for lower payload. Soon after this, in [20], authors again customized CNN with a preprocessing layer containing the fixed KV filter, absolute layer to compute the absolute values of the noise residuals, batch normalization (BN) layer to prevent the network from falling into local minima and tanh activation function. They were able to perform with comparable accuracy to that of SRM + EC on the two steganographic algorithms of the spatial domain. In [21], researchers have further created an ensemble of CNNs to improve the results. With the availability of GPU, instead of using a single SRM filter, researchers have started using 30 SRM filters in their preprocessing layer to help their CNN network converge better. Some researchers [22] have kept these filters fixed while some have made them trainable [23, 24].

In [25], the authors have made use of a deep residual network with skip connections and named it SRNet. They used minimum heuristics for training their network in the field of steganalysis for the first time. They did not make use of SRM filters, but instead used a deeper network with a wide front part for learning noise residuals automatically. They have shown that their network’s performance is better than various other networks for steganalysis in the spatial domain [7, 23] as well as in the JPEG domain [26] for different stego algorithms.

All the above works were conducted on two popular datasets, BOSSbase 1.01 and BOWS2 [27] of grayscale spatial domain images. The ImageNet dataset of colored images in JPEG format was also used in some of the research on the spatial domain by decompressing and converting them to grayscale images. Cogranne et al. [28] published the ALASKA challenge to make steganalysis more realistic for forensic applications. Fifty thousand realistic and diversified images were provided in the JPEG domain, which was embedded with different stego algorithms and embedding rates, making the steganalysis scenario closer to reality. Winners of this challenge, Yousfi et al. [29] trained multiple SRNets [25] for JPEG images. The ALASKA-II challenge [30] was published just after the ALASKA challenge which consisted of 3 × 25,000 images with three different JPEG quality factors. The authors of this challenge also published stego images embedded with different payload and stego algorithms. For this challenge, pretrained networks were highly used by top-runners and thus gave a different perspective on using pretrained networks in the field of steganalysis. Yousfi et al. [31] have used pretrained networks which were already trained on ImageNet dataset for classifying images into 1000 classes. The authors have demonstrated the performance of these networks better than networks trained from scratch. Similarly, Butora et al. [32] trained SRNet on ImageNet dataset for 1,000 class classification problems. This pretrained network was then used for JPEG domain steganalysis. The authors also used the pretrained networks trained on colored images in the JPEG domain to grayscale images in the spatial domain. Yousfi et al. [33] made use of EfficientNet family pretrained on ImageNet dataset. However, the authors proved that these pretrained networks need to be modified to deal with the difficult steganalysis scenario, and thus they themselves used EfficientNet after doing several modifications for ALASKA-II steganalysis. In this way, pretrained networks have been highly used for spatial as well as JPEG steganalysis.

This chapter provides a comparative analysis of different pretrained models for steganalysis and XuNet [20] specifically created for it. With experimental results, it is shown that pretrained methods in their original forms are less effective as compared to the networks created for steganalysis, but there are a number of ways to modify these well-performed and pretrained networks of computer vision for steganalysis. Thus, in the future, several modifications would be done on pretrained networks to make them suitable for the specific needs of steganalysis and to exploit the advantage of their pretraining on millions of images for ImageNet dataset classification.

This work uses XuNet as a baseline as it is the first model giving comparable results to that of SRM with less computational complexity. The images used in this work are grayscale images that are convolved with a high pass filter for XuNet. The developers of XuNet made use of the same high pass filter for the convergence of their network. This operation helps to suppress the content of the image and calculate the noise residuals where very weak stego signals are hidden. After this preprocessing part, the convolved image is passed to the trainable part of the XuNet. Originally, XuNet was trained on 512 by 512 image size, but in this work, it is trained on 256 by 256 image size to make it comparable with other pretrained models. The pretrained models used in this work are VGGNet-16 [34], ResNet-50 [35], and EfficientNet-B0 [36], all initialized with ImageNet weights. The preprocessing layer of fixed SRM filters is not used with these networks as this has to be attached as the initial layer of the pretrained networks which may decrease the performance of these networks. Further, our work wants to observe the performance of pretrained networks without using any preprocessing filter. Fully connected layers of pretrained networks are discarded to finetune the network with a new set of fully connected layers. L2 regularization is used in fully connected layers except the last one. The dropout layer is also used before fully connected layers. Image size is taken as 256 by 256 for all the networks used in this work.

## 8.2 General Framework

### 8.2.1 Dataset

In this work, BOSSbase 1.01 dataset [17] of 10,000 grayscale images are used. These images act as cover images for steganalysis. The images are of size 512 by 512. All images are resized to 256 by 256. These 10,000 images are then converted to stego images using HUGO [11] steganography algorithm at payload 0.8 bpp. A training set constitutes of 6,000 pairs of cover and stego images is used. A validation set of 1,000 pairs and a testing set of 3,000 pairs are used in this work.
