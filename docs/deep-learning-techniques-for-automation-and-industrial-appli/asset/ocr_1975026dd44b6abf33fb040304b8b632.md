Traditional machine learning and image processing methods were initially used to detect crop diseases programmatically from crop photos, but these methods had limitations in terms of the precision and applicability of the findings. Deep learning for plant disease identification has increased prediction accuracy and increased the range of plant species that are thought to have diseases. This article gives a summary of a study that uses deep learning to identify plant diseases and analyzes it in terms of the datasets that were used.

The research paper titled “Harvest Illness Recognition and Observing Framework” was written by L. R. Priya, G. Ignisha Rajathi, and R. Vedhapriyavadhanа. In this paper, K-means clustering techniques were used [17]. There are four models in this review, with a precision rate of 89%. To decide if a leaf is healthy or not, we utilized K-means grouping, an unaided gaining method that breaks down the separation from each distinguished hub. All the required training photos are included in an image acquisition model, which is ultimately resized to 256 by 256 pixels.

The research paper titled “Automated leaf disease diagnosis in different crop species by Visual Characteristics Analysis and One-Class Classifiers” was written by X. E. D. Pantazi and was published in 2019 [13]. The approach employed in this paper was classification, and the classifier utilized is a one-class classifier, according to Moshou, A.A.

The research paper titled “An Overview of the Research on Plant Leave Disease Detection Using Image Processing Techniques” was written by Ms. Kiran R. Gavhale and was published in 2014 [14]. The techniques utilized in this study were fluffy C-implies grouping and brain network planning.

The research paper “Deep Learning Models for Plant Disease Detection and Diagnosis,” written by Konstantinos P. Ferentinos, and published in 2018, made use of deep learning techniques [15]. According to CNN, the model’s accuracy is 99.53%. There are 87,848 total photographs in this model, and we acquired them using an open database. The main drawback of the paper was the prevalence of laboratory images in the database, many of which had characteristics that distinguished them from actual images [17].
