The ResNet architecture also uses a global average pooling layer after the convolutional layers, which averages the feature maps across the spatial dimensions and produces a single vector that can be used for classification. This helps reduce the number of parameters in the network and prevent overfitting [13].

The ResNet architecture's capacity to train extremely deep neural networks without experiencing the vanishing gradient problem is one benefit. This allows for the development of more powerful and expressive models that can capture more complex relationships between features. Additionally, the skip connections in the residual blocks help improve the flow of gradients and reduce the impact of vanishing gradients on the training process.

Another advantage of the ResNet architecture is its performance on a wide range of computer vision tasks, including fruit leaf classification. The pre-trained ResNet models have been shown to achieve high accuracy on a variety of datasets and can be fine-tuned for specific tasks with relatively small amounts of training data.

However, one disadvantage of the ResNet architecture is its computational and memory requirements, which can make it difficult to train on smaller datasets or on devices with limited resources. Additionally, the use of skip connections can introduce additional complexity into the network, which can make it more difficult to understand and interpret.

Despite its limitations, the ResNet architecture is a powerful tool for transfer learning and has been widely used for fruit leaf classification and other computer vision tasks. Its ability to train very deep networks and achieve high performance makes it an attractive option for researchers and practitioners in the field.

Inception is a deep convolutional neural network architecture that Google researchers unveiled in 2014. It was created to deal with the issue of overfitting in deep neural networks and achieved remarkable performance on the ImageNet dataset.

The Inception architecture uses a combination of different convolutional filters at each layer, including 1x1, 3x3, and 5x5 filters, as well as max pooling and average pooling operations shown in Figure 10.4. The idea behind this approach is to capture features at multiple scales and resolutions, which can improve the network's ability to recognize objects of different sizes and shapes.
