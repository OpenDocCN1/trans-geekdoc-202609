
## 8.4 Conclusion and Discussion

The comparative analysis of XuNet and pretrained models used in this work shows that XuNet performed better than the pretrained models. The reasons for this are as follows: (a) XuNet uses a preprocessing step of convolving the image with the K₅ filter of SRM for calculating noise residual from the image, which is affected by the weak stego signal; (b) the dataset size of 12,000 is enough to train a six-layered XuNet network; and (c) due to the small size of the network, it can be trained with a large number of epochs. However, XuNet, when used without the K₅ filter, did not converge. On the contrary, pretrained networks converge without the use of any preprocessing filter. Further in XuNet, researchers have already optimized the hyperparameters for steganalysis, while a lot of hyperparameters need to be optimized when using pretrained models for steganalysis. As this is a preliminary study of using pretrained networks for steganalysis, hyperparameter optimization has not been done properly with established methods like Grid Search, Random Search, and Bayesian Optimization. Again, a dataset size of 12,000 is not enough to train pretrained networks with a large number of layers.

The analysis shows that pretrained networks for steganalysis tend to overfit when the dataset is small. Another reason for overfitting can be because of non-optimization of the various hyperparameters of the pretrained networks for steganalysis. The advantage of using pretrained networks over XuNet is their faster convergence without the use of any preprocessing filter. XuNet, without the preprocessing filter, does not seem to converge even after training it with a large number of epochs, as done in this work. Also, pretrained networks can be used for JPEG steganalysis directly, whereas XuNet, trained for spatial domain, cannot be directly used in JPEG domain.

In the future, to make better use of pretrained networks for steganalysis, the dataset can be extended by including some of the other datasets available or by using image augmentation methods such as translation, flip, and rotation. Also, hyperparameter optimization will be done in a systematic way for the pretrained networks.

This work has used HUGO at higher payload of 0.8 bpp rather than a common payload of 0.4 bpp used by most researchers. At lower payloads, the difference between cover and stego is quite low, so CNN networks are difficult to converge. As this is an initial study of applying deep networks for steganalysis, a higher payload is used to get reasonable results for comparison. In future work, a lower payload dataset shall also be used. For this, deep transfer learning will be used in which a network trained on a higher payload can be further fine-tuned on a lower payload. This kind of transfer learning has already given promising results, as shown by various researchers in this domain.
