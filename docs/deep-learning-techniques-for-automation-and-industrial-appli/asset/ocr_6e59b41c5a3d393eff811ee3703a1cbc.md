If more epochs were used, the training accuracy of these pre-trained models will keep increasing while validation accuracy starts dropping. This is due to a large number of hyperparameters of the pretrained models that need to be optimized in a systematic way for steganalysis. Further, for pretrained models with a large number of layers, the training dataset of 12,000 images is not enough.

XuNet, giving an accuracy of 90%, is trained from scratch on steganalysis with a predefined heuristic of calculating residual from the image, and then applying a trainable network. Our work also trained XuNet without the K₅ filter, but even with a large number of epochs, the model did not converge. Figure 8.8 shows the accuracy curve of XuNet for the last 500 epochs when trained with 1,000 epochs without the K₅ filter, and it is clear that the model does not converge. On the other side, pretrained networks in their basic form and without any preprocessing step are converging and are giving good results with EfficientNet-B0 providing 81.45% testing accuracy. ResNet-50 is providing 74.61% and VGGNet-16 is providing 66.03% accuracy. VGGNet gives the lowest accuracy as it does not contain a BN layer, which has proven beneficial for steganalysis by many researchers. Another benefit of using pretrained networks is that, though they are pretrained on JPEG images, they are also providing good results on grayscale images. On the contrary, if XuNet is applied to JPEG images, it needs a different set of preprocessing filters for making the network converge, whereas these pretrained models have already been trained on JPEG images, so they can be easily applied to JPEG domain steganalysis without the use of preprocessing filters.
