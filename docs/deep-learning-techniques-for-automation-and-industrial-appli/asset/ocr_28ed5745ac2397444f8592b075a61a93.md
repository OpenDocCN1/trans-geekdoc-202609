Logistic regression. For COVID-19 detection, several supervised learning algorithms are available, including logistic regression, SVMs, decision trees, and random forests. Logistic regression is a widely used binary classification algorithm that predicts the likelihood of a patient being infected or not [2]. SVMs are another popular algorithm that identifies the hyperplane that distinguishes infected and non-infected patients in the feature space. Decision trees and random forests are tree-based algorithms that recursively split the feature space based on the most informative features [5].

Deep learning. Supervised deep learning methods can also be utilized for COVID-19 detection [3]. CNNs are highly effective in image-based diagnosis, especially for medical images like chest X-rays or CT scans. CNNs learn to extract hierarchical representations of the input images and identify COVID-19-specific features, enabling them to detect COVID-19 pneumonia accurately. Meanwhile, RNNs are suitable for analyzing time-series data, such as a patient’s vital signs and symptoms. RNNs can model the temporal dependencies in the data and predict the probability of a patient having COVID-19 [6].

Unsupervised learning is a machine learning technique that involves training a model on unlabeled data to discover hidden patterns or structures. In the context of COVID-19 detection, unsupervised learning can be applied to group patients into subgroups based on similarities in their symptoms and vital signs [4]. Unsupervised learning algorithms, such as clustering and autoencoders are commonly used to achieve this objective [7].

Clustering. In the context of COVID-19 detection, unsupervised learning techniques such as clustering are utilized to group patients based on similarities in their symptoms and vital signs [5]. This helps in identifying subgroups of patients with similar disease severity. Another unsupervised learning technique, principal component analysis (PCA), is used to reduce the dimensionality of the data by identifying the principal components that capture the most variation in the data. Additionally, deep learning algorithms like autoencoders can be used for unsupervised learning in COVID-19 detection. They can learn compressed representations of the input data and reconstruct the original data from the compressed representations.
