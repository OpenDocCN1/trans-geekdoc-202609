Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are a prevalent form of neural network utilized for recognizing images and videos. These networks have multiple layers of filters that can be trained to recognize distinct patterns and characteristics in the data. [19] In the realm of COVID-19 detection, CNNs are capable of scrutinizing chest X-rays and CT scans of the lungs to recognize patterns and features that are linked with COVID-19.

Recurrent neural networks (RNNs) are a prevalent form of neural network employed for processing sequence data, including time-series and textual data. These networks have the ability to capture the temporal associations between the data points, enabling them to make predictions based on prior inputs. In the context of COVID-19 detection, RNNs can analyze patient data over time and forecast the likelihood of developing severe symptoms or complications [27].

Deep belief networks (DBNs) are a class of neural networks that have multiple layers of hidden units, which can learn to represent the data hierarchically. These networks are frequently utilized for unsupervised learning, where the model is trained to reconstruct the input data from a compressed representation. In the domain of COVID-19 detection, DBNs can be employed to detect underlying patterns and associations in the data that may not be visible using conventional techniques [28].

Deep learning techniques offer a significant benefit of acquiring intricate representations of data, leading to exceptional performance across various tasks. Nevertheless, training deep learning models can be computationally intensive, necessitating significant amounts of data and computational resources. Moreover, interpreting the representations learned by these models can be challenging since they may not be easily comprehensible to humans.

In summary, deep learning techniques are an asset for detecting COVID-19 disease. Different algorithms have distinct advantages and limitations, and the data quality and quantity are crucial for the models’ effectiveness. Overcoming the challenges associated with utilizing deep learning techniques for COVID-19 disease detection can be achieved through appropriate techniques such as data preprocessing, model selection, and interpretation.
