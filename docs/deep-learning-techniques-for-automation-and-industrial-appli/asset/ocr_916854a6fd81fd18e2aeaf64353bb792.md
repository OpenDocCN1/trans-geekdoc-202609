# 10.1.4.1 Visual Geometry Group

Researchers at the University of Oxford created Visual Geometry Group (VGG), a deep convolutional neural network architecture. It was introduced in the 2014 ImageNet competition, and achieved second place in the classification task with an error rate of 7.3%. VGG is known for its simplicity and effectiveness in image classification tasks, and has been widely used as a pre-trained model for transfer learning. The VGG architecture consists of several layers of convolutional and pooling layers, followed by fully connected layers.

The network receives an RGB image with a 224x224 pixel input. After the convolutional layers, which employ 3x3 filters with stride 1 and padding 1, the max pooling layers, which employ 2x2 filters and stride 2, are used. The VGG-16 architecture has a total of 13 convolutional layers, while the VGG-19 architecture has 16 convolutional layers. The network employs three fully connected layers with 4096 units after the convolutional layers each, followed by a final softmax layer for classification. The VGG architecture has a total of 138 million parameters in the VGG-16 architecture and 143 million parameters in the VGG-19 architecture. One advantage of the VGG architecture is its simplicity and ease of implementation. It has a straightforward structure with small filters and maximum pooling layers, which makes it easy to train and fine-tune. It has also been shown to perform well on a variety of image classification tasks, including fruit leaf classification. However, one disadvantage of the VGG architecture is its high memory and computational requirements. The large number of parameters in the network can make it difficult to train on smaller datasets or on devices with limited resources. Additionally, the architecture does not take into account the spatial relationships between features, which can limit its performance on tasks that require more complex reasoning about object structures and relationships.
