## 4.2 Feature Extraction-Based Word Embedding

Preprocessed data will be used in the word embedding based technique to create word vectors using the PV-DM and PV-DBOW models of paragraph vectors. These vectors will be used as highlights for the next step. The practice of representing text in numerical form is called word embedding. Most early studies on sarcasm detection included one of the word embedding techniques, such as Bag of Words, TF-IDF, and Word2Vec.

### (a) Bag-of-words (BoW) and Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF)

These techniques are used to extract features from text. A vocabulary of every term included in the data was built using the BoW technique. The TF-IDF approach, which implies that the value of a word in a sentence is exactly proportional to the number of times the word occurs in the document, is another way to express textual data in the form of a fraction where term frequency denotes the relevance of a word in the document. Inverse document frequency plays a different role in TF-IDF; a word’s relevance is not always correlated with how often it appears in the text. In the IDF, uncommon words are given greater weight.

### (b) Word2Vec

This strategy is essentially predicated on the idea that there are not any words in the text that are the same in any way. The techniques covered in the preceding paragraph allow Mikolov et al. (2013)’s study to offer word vectors that can capture semantic and grammatical similarities between words. The fundamental model design utilizes the NNLM (neural network language model). Word2Vec comes in two variations.

- **Continuous Bag of Words model:** This model takes a continuous distributed representation of the context, similar to the classic BoW, and then averages the resulting vectors.
- **Skip-gram model:** In this approach, the previous words in the phrases are utilized to predict the subsequent word.
