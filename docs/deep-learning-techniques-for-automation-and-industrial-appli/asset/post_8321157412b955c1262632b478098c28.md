## Abstract

Sarcasm is a sardonic or bitter remark intended to express disrespect or ridicule. Sarcasm is used in the Hindi language for decades, and many Hindi idioms and proverbs use indirect sarcasm, which intentionally commenting on someone like “अन्धों में काना राजा” has the meaning “मूर्ख मण्डली में थोड़ा पढ़ा-लिखा भी विद्वान और ज्ञानी माना जाता है.” [Translation: “A king with partial vision in a kingdom of blind people”] Sentiment categorization is less difficult in comparison to sarcasm detection as we see in the above written idiom that it contains a negative sentiment, but the meaning of what the sentence represents is intended towards less educated people in this case, described that ‘in a group of foolish people, even the one with the meager amount of intelligence is considered the brainiest’. In the present scenario, people on social media platforms like Twitter, Facebook, and WhatsApp succeed in recognizing sarcasm despite interacting with strangers across the world. Sarcasm detection is a challenging task in the natural language processing due to the richness of morphology. Sarcasm detection in the Hindi language tweet is a prime task for natural language processing to avoid misconstruing sarcastic statements as original literal statements. Twitter is one of the most widely used social networking platforms used by people to express thoughts, reviews, comments, compliments, and discussions on current events and to convey information in the form of short texts. The relevant context of the tweets is often specified with the use of # (hashtags), in the context of this research #sarcasm, #sarcastic, #kataksh, #कटाक्ष, etc., will be used to access the sarcastic tweets and then analyze the tweets’ sentiments to detect sarcasm in text by using different machine learning algorithms.

## 5.1 Introduction

Analyzing written languages like Hindi, English is a fascinating subject that has been concentrated by many Natural Language Processing disciplines. These stages advance the utilization of non-literal language gadgets, for example, sarcasm and phony for informative purposes. At social networking platforms, sarcasm detection is a necessary part of Natural Language Processing (NLP) that is useful to find the origin of an opinion for any topic in a form of text [2]. The presence of sarcastic, phony or ‘kataksh’ [In Hindi: कटाक्ष] contents in the corpus makes opinion mining a very challenging task and in general, most sentiment analysis systems do not consider sarcastic or phony sentiments for analysing sentiment. Because of this, a large portion of the current frameworks for sentiment analysis are not efficient in sarcasm detection. Sarcasm is a unique type of emotion that normally alters the direction of the opinion for a given text or social media tweets. Human beings regularly use strong apparent pressure and nonverbal cues, such as eye movement and hand motion, to convey sarcasm in spoken discussions. These apparent and gestural signs are clearly absent while communicating non-verbal sarcasm like social media tweets or posts which are generally in textual form, that makes sarcasm detection a complicated job. Most of the time, humans feel trouble to comprehend sarcasm in text. In general, sarcastic sentences look like positive sentences, the overall significance becomes negative because of the presence of sarcasm.

## 5.2 Literature Review

In this segment we will see all the past research work made in sarcasm detection, their qualities and shortcomings in various situations.

Past research work attempted to recognize sarcastic sentences in English and Hindi tweets by utilizing various ways. The following literature review is classified into three primary categories: without Machine Learning based, manual features with Machine Learning, and word embedding with Machine Learning.

### 5.2.1 Literature Review of Sarcasm Detection Based on Data Analysis Without Machine Learning Algorithms

Manohar and Kulkarni (2017) state that persons use social media platform like Twitter to communicate their personal views about different subjects. Work done by them indicated that sarcasm can be distinguished effectively by utilizing specific corpus methodology. According to them, there is no such need of any type of Machine Learning algorithm; rather, a corpus of action words was made and scoring-based classification is done to distinguish sarcastic sentences. Finally, a comparison is made between input text and stored action words and analysed sentiment, after that; they perform sentiment aggregation over the compared result for identification of the sentence is sarcastic or not. In the sequence of sarcasm detection, Rendalkar and Chandankhede (2018) worked on a scoring-based methodology. Instead of making a stored corpus of action words, in this approach each word in the sentence will be scored and included, after completion of all the words in the sentence, last score will decide the general estimation of the sentence. SentiWordNet tool was used for determining scores. Many systems were utilized to distinguish sarcasm, like word- and emotion-based detection, hybrid sarcasm detection which consolidates both word- and emotion-based methodology, sarcasm detection based on positive and negative circumstances, pattern-based, and interjection-word-beginning-based sarcasm detection.

According to Bharti *et al.* (2015) detecting sarcasm is one of the most challenging tasks in natural language processing. In their work, they created two algorithms to be specific Parsing Based Lexicon Generation Algorithm (PBLGA) and Interjection Word Start Algorithm (IWSA). The two algorithms are structured in an unexpected way; the first one focuses more on sentiment and situational inconsistency, while the second algorithm can distinguish a sarcastic sentence by the immediate presence of the interjection word at the start of the given sentence. Information related to #sarcasm was collected from Twitter in an efficient way. In this approach, all types of algorithms have their own significance under specific circumstances, but it does not work efficiently when sarcastic sentences contain interjection words in the centre of the sentence. The majority of the researchers work on the tweets which are gathered and stored, yet Bharti *et al.* (2016) worked on real time streamed tweets utilizing a Hadoop system. Various methodologies were utilized to recognize sarcasm, for example, PBLGA, IWSA, positive sentiment with antonym pair (PSWAP), tweets reflect to widespread realities, tweets which are negating to time subordinate realities.
