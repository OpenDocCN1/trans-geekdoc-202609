The authors of this research suggest using the ResTS (Residual Teacher/Student) architecture as a visualization and classification method for identifying plant diseases. ResTS has developed from the similarity-based teacher/student architecture [10]. The convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture, on which ResTS is built, consists of a decoder, two classifiers (researcher and student), and a convolutional neural network. The shared representation between the researcher and the student is utilized as a proxy to visualize the relevant portions of the picture for classification in this architecture, which trains both classifiers as effectively as feasible. Testing has shown that the instructor/understudy design (F1 score: 0.972) was surpassed by the suggested structure of ResTS (F1 score: 0.991), which would produce more precise representations of the disease's symptoms. To locate and identify diseases in citrus trees, researchers who performed this study using the current ResTS architecture proposed a hybrid technique. The two primary stages of the desired research are (a) finding citrus fruit and leaf lesions, and (b) categorizing citrus diseases. Citrus lesion spots are extracted using an upgraded input picture and a modified weighted segmentation algorithm. Then, by fusing geometrical components, color, and texture, a codebook is produced. To find the best features, a hybrid feature selection strategy is also applied. Principal component analysis (PCA) values, fluctuation, and standard deviation covariance vectors are fundamental parts of this methodology. Once the most suitable qualities have been selected, they are then input into the M-SVM model for analysis, focusing on a range of citrus diseases. This analysis includes testing the proposed technique using a combined dataset from Plant Town, Citrus Pictures Information Base of Plagued with Scale, the Citrus Disease Image Gallery Dataset, and image databases obtained from their respective owners. Anthracnose, black spot, canker, scab, greening, and melanosis are among the citrus diseases that have been detected and categorized using these databases [11, 12]. On the combined dataset, the local dataset, and the citrus disease image gallery dataset, the proposed technique outperformed the existing methods with a classification accuracy of 97%, 89%, and 90.4%, respectively.
