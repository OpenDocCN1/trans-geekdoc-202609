Winners of this challenge, Yousfi et al. [29] trained multiple SRNets [25] for JPEG images. The ALASKA-II challenge [30] was published just after the ALASKA challenge which consisted of 3*25,000 images with three different JPEG quality factors. The authors of this challenge also published stego images embedded with different payload and stego algorithms. For this challenge, pretrained networks were highly used by top-runners and thus gave a different perspective on using pretrained networks in the field of steganalysis. Yousfi et al. [31] have used pretrained networks which were already trained on ImageNet dataset for classifying images into 1000 classes. The authors have demonstrated the performance of these networks better than networks trained from scratch. Similarly, Butora et al. [32] trained SRNet on ImageNet dataset for 1,000 class classification problems. This pretrained network was then used for JPEG domain steganalysis. The authors also used the pretrained networks trained on colored images in the JPEG domain to grayscale images in the spatial domain. Yousfi et al. [33] made use of EfficientNet family pretrained on ImageNet dataset. However, the authors proved that these pretrained networks need to be modified to deal with the difficult steganalysis scenario, and thus they themselves used EfficientNet after doing several modifications for ALASKA-II steganalysis. In this way, pretrained networks have been highly used for spatial as well as JPEG steganalysis.

This chapter provides a comparative analysis of different pretrained models for steganalysis and XuNet [20] specifically created for it. With experimental results, it is shown that pretrained methods in their original forms are less effective as compared to the networks created for steganalysis, but there are a number of ways to modify these well-performed and pretrained networks of computer vision for steganalysis. Thus, in the future, several modifications would be done on pretrained networks to make them suitable for the specific needs of steganalysis and to exploit the advantage of their pretraining on millions of images for ImageNet dataset classification.

This work uses XuNet as a baseline as it is the first model giving comparable results to that of SRM with less computational complexity. The images used in this work are grayscale images that are convolved with a high pass filter for XuNet. The developers of XuNet made use of the same high pass filter for the convergence of their network.
