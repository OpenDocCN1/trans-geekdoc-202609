# 11.3  Heating and Cooking Emissions

Another factor contributing to the rise in global temperatures is the heat energy lost to the environment through heating and cooking systems. Black carbon is coal that is produced as a result of burning coal and biomass fuels, air pollution from transportation, outdoor fires, and trade [18, 19]. A significant amount of heat and carbon dioxide is also released into the atmosphere by separate electric power and heat-producing systems, which ultimately contribute to GHGs [11, 12].

# 11.4  Automobile Systems Emission

The CO₂ emissions in the global transport economy may also have some effects on climatic changes. The extensive use of hydrogen-powered cars can tend to lower releases from sources of fossil fuels. The application of biofuel and biodiesel in the transportation industry can also cut emissions.

# 11.5  Power Systems Emission

The affordability and capacity to produce vast amounts of power, fossil fuels are being used more frequently in the production and use of electricity in modern society. As a result, fossil power plants are more prevalent today. Electric power generators, known as fossil power plants, use the burning of fossil fuels to produce electricity for human consumption. Petroleum, coal, and natural gas are the three main fossil fuels now in use. According to the consensus view of experts in energy and environmental pollution, the action of fossil fuel power plants emits CO₂, which is a significant source of GHGs.
