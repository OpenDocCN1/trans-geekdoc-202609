## Chili Leaf Classification Using Deep Learning Techniques

Chenchupalli Chathurya*, Diksha Sachdeva and Mamta Arora

*Computer Science and Engineering, Manav Rachna University, Faridabad, India*

## Abstract

Agriculture is of paramount importance in everyone's life. Crops and plants play a huge role in sustaining life, and taking care of crops and plants is both important and difficult. So, to detect diseases in plants, this research paper is formulated to determine whether the plant is healthy or not. The dataset consists of chili plant leaves collected from one of the fields located in Andhra Pradesh, India. Many image classification models as well as transfer learning models have been applied. Moreover, deep learning models like convolutional neural network (CNN) and transfer learning models like InceptionV3 and VGG16 have been applied with and without data augmentation. The most successful model was found to be CNN with data augmentation, which showed an accuracy of 90% and also predicted almost all the testing images correctly. The accuracy of the model can be improved by increasing the size of the dataset.

*Keywords:* CNN models, transfer learning, machine learning, deep learning, AlexNet, InceptionV3, VGG16, leaves

### 2.1 Introduction

Humans are dependent on plants for necessities, from food to clothes. Thus, one needs to take care of plants. Although humans are aware of the distinctness of leaves, there should be a way to figure out a disease before a plant becomes susceptible to it. Detecting the disease in advance and taking proper care of the plant will help save the plant consequently, saving other resources or products that come from plants. Thus, a model has been prepared in which, by detecting patterns on a leaf, one would be able to tell whether the plant has any disease and the desired precaution can be taken. As a result, the study of automatic leaf disease detection in agriculture is an important area for research since it could offer benefits in the observation of wide fields of produce and help to spot disease symptoms as they appear on plant leaves. Using computer vision and machine learning techniques, the researchers proposed a prediction model for plant leaf disease detection and categorization. Preprocessing, segmentation, and extraction of attributes such as shape, color, texture, and veins are done on the raw image of a leaf. The leaf image is classified using a variety of machine-learning classifiers.

### 2.2 Objectives

The main objectives are the following:

- (a) To illustrate a model that can detect and predict a disease in a plant
- (b) To classify whether the leaves are healthy or unhealthy.

### 2.3 Literature Survey

As explored in prior studies [1], various types of convolutional neural network (CNN) models were made to detect plant diseases with the help of deep learning. The database used here is an open database comprising approximately 90,000 images with 58 classes and 25 different types of plant leaves. Different types of CNN model architectures were made for the identification of plant diseases, like AlexNet, AlexNetOWTBn, GoogLeNet, Overfeat, and Visual Geometry Group (VGG) [1]. Although all these models show accuracy greater than 97%, the success rate of VGG turned out to be 99.53%. The improvement here can be made by choosing a variety of training data, such as data from different locations (e.g., fields), and data from different cultivation conditions, and also by making an integrated mobile application [2].

There are two types of image augmentation strategies: the traditional approach and Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) [2]. The second approach, GAN, helped in making synthetic data based on the already existing dataset so that the accuracy could be improved. The models that were used here are AlexNet, VGG19, Inception version 3, DenseNet201, and ResNet50 [3]. However, the models we used couldn’t effectively handle testing images with complex backgrounds, such as cases where leaves appeared in the background of some images. To solve this problem, two deep neural network-based object detection algorithms were used. The first algorithm filters the images with complex backgrounds, and the second algorithm classifies all the remaining images. This trained model achieved an accuracy of 93.67% and it also proved to be successful in the testing of leaf images with complex backgrounds. The accuracy here can be increased by using a varied dataset with images in different phases or leaves in different locations [4].

As seen previously in a study by Arsenovic et al., a convolutional neural network has been developed to detect diseases in plants [3]. The dataset used for this study is the Plant Village dataset; however, the model is specifically applied to the apple and tomato classes. The model used consists of four convolutional layers, each followed by a pooling layer, two dense layers, and, lastly, a sigmoid function to detect whether the plant has the disease or not. The model achieved an accuracy of 88.5% with 45,000 parameters. The model was also run on Tesla’s GPU to calculate the accuracy. Future work can be done by applying transfer learning algorithms like VGG or any other architecture to the aforementioned model for higher accuracy [5, 6].

A hybrid model of a convolutional autoencoder (CAE) and a convolutional neural network was used in a study by Sasubilli et al. [4]. This approach has been done to ensure that minimal parameters are used to classify the leaf. The dataset used here is the Plant Village dataset, but it mainly uses images of leaves of peach plants, with approximately 4,500 images. This hybrid model achieved a training accuracy of 99.35% and a testing accuracy of 98.38% with only 9,914 training parameters. Future work can be done by training the model for disease detection on low computational power systems, or a mobile application can be made to detect the disease [7, 8].

In a study by Kumar et al., the authors themselves collected the images in the dataset [5]. The images captured were of maize plant leaves on a white background using a 13-MP camera. A total of 750 images were captured, out of which 600 were used for training and the remaining 150 for testing. Here, a disease detection system was used to detect the disease, consisting of two modules: one of which consists of a camera and computer to capture and store the images, while the other module is used to detect diseases on the stored images. The disease detection model used here was CNN, which resulted in a training accuracy of 99.5% and a testing accuracy of 86.7%. Future work can be done by using a more varied dataset with more images.
