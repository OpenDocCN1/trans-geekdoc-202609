# 11.2 CO₂ Emissions in Sectors

The current worldwide social and material development has made energy a major sustainable prerequisite. This is because access to energy services is necessary for providing basic human needs, including heat, light, and motive power (e.g., water pumps and transportation). Additionally, the availability of energy services is crucial for business, industry, commerce, and public services, including modern healthcare, education, and communication [7].

Many variables, such as infant mortality, illiteracy, and life expectancy, have a direct correlation with the lack of sufficient energy facilities [8]. The degree of a country’s energy consumption can be used to assess its economic and physical progress [10]. The pursuit of development has resulted in the growth of a number of energy-producing and energy-consuming industries, as well as related environmental pollutants like greenhouse gases (e.g., carbon dioxide, methane, nitrogen oxides, and sulfur oxides) and water vapor. From this perspective, energy has developed into a crucial component of daily human existence. Utilizing renewable energy sources, which are either emission-free or have typically low emissions, is the best alternative approach.
