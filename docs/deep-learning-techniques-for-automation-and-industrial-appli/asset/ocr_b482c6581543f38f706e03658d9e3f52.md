# 6.1 Introduction

The quality of any output optical image device is dependent on its surrounding conditions of weather like mist, haze, dust, and fog.

Such weather conditions directly affect the color and contrast of the scene, resulting in degraded quality. It mainly occurs due to optical phenomena of light absorption and scattering reflected by the object surface. The reflected light from the surface of an object gets attenuated by aerosol particles present in the environment, and it decays by a factor called transmission. Further light is scattered by particles of surroundings, which is affected by the camera distance from the object [1]. Overall, these effects result in a poor quality output, which make it difficult for use in the field of vision systems such as surveillance and satellite. Thus, single-image dehazing is highly recommended as a pre-processing task for any computer vision method to enhance performance in hazy conditions. Single-image dehazing techniques do not carry any external knowledge of the single-input scene. Haze is spatially variant as it is affected by scene depth, having impact on different parts of the scene. Therefore, scene depth is needed in a haze-formation model to produce haze-free output [2].

# 6.2 Formation of a Haze Model

The image captured by the sensor has two parts. The first part is the scene radiance, which consists of light reflected from the object. The second part is environmental illumination, which consists of light reflected from the ground, direct sunlight and diffused skylight [3]. Nayar and Narasimham developed a dichromatic atmospheric scattering model as mentioned in Figure 6.1. This model explained haze formation by the linear combination of air light and transmission value [4].

As a mathematical equation, it can be expressed by:

I(x) = J(x) t(x) + A (1 - t(x))    (6.1)

where J (x) t (x) represents the attenuation model, A(1 – t (x)) represents the degradation model, J (x) is the clear scene, A represents global atmospheric light, t(x) represents transmission value, which denotes quantity of light not scattered and that reaches the sensor, and I (x) is the observed hazy scene. The main aim of dehazing is to restore the J(x) value from I(x).
