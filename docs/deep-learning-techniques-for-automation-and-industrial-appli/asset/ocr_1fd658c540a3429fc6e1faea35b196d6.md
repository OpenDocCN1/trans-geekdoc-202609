As seen previously in a study by Arsenovic et al., a convolutional neural network has been developed to detect diseases in plants [3]. The dataset used for this study is the Plant Village dataset; however, the model is specifically applied to the apple and tomato classes. The model used consists of four convolutional layers, each followed by a pooling layer, two dense layers, and, lastly, a sigmoid function to detect whether the plant has the disease or not. The model achieved an accuracy of 88.5% with 45,000 parameters. The model was also run on Tesla’s GPU to calculate the accuracy. Future work can be done by applying transfer learning algorithms like VGG or any other architecture to the aforementioned model for higher accuracy [5, 6].

A hybrid model of a convolutional autoencoder (CAE) and a convolutional neural network was used in a study by Sasubilli et al. [4]. This approach has been done to ensure that minimal parameters are used to classify the leaf. The dataset used here is the Plant Village dataset, but it mainly uses images of leaves of peach plants, with approximately 4,500 images. This hybrid model achieved a training accuracy of 99.35% and a testing accuracy of 98.38% with only 9,914 training parameters. Future work can be done by training the model for disease detection on low computational power systems, or a mobile application can be made to detect the disease [7, 8].

In a study by Kumar et al., the authors themselves collected the images in the dataset [5]. The images captured were of maize plant leaves on a white background using a 13-MP camera. A total of 750 images were captured, out of which 600 were used for training and the remaining 150 for testing. Here, a disease detection system was used to detect the disease, consisting of two modules: one of which consists of a camera and computer to capture and store the images, while the other module is used to detect diseases on the stored images. The disease detection model used here was CNN, which resulted in a training accuracy of 99.5% and a testing accuracy of 86.7%. Future work can be done by using a more varied dataset with more images.
