The authors of this study propose a deep ensemble neural network-based technique (DENN) for automatically predicting plant diseases. Utilizing transfer learning, the pre-trained models are improved, while data augmentation methods such as feature extraction, rotation, resizing, and translation are used to prevent overfitting [8]. In this study, a full taxonomy of the efficiency of several pre-trained neural networks is offered, along with the exhibition of a weighted ensemble of those models that are pertinent to establishing leaf disease identification. Moreover, the 38 classes from 14 harvests that make up the openly accessible plant town dataset are utilized to assess how great the proposed endeavor is. Execution-wise, DENN outmaneuvers state-of-the-art pre-arranged models including ResNet 50 and 101, InceptionV3, DenseNet 121 and 201, MobileNetV3, and Nast. Overall, execution has been used to show how well the suggested model orchestrates data.

This review paper proposes a mechanized technique for recognizing and sorting plant leaf diseases utilizing a convolutional brain network in view of an optimal versatile organization (OMNCNN). The proposed OMNCNN model uses preprocessing, including extraction, division, and grouping, as its essential functional advances. The afflicted region of the leaf image is located using Kapur’s thresholding-based picture request and two-sided filtering (BF)-based preprocessing. Additionally, the Emperor Penguin Optimizer (EPO) method is used to boost the Mobile Net model’s hyperparameters’ accuracy in detecting plant diseases [14]. In the end, an extreme learning machine (ELM)-based classifier is used to give the applied plant leaf pictures the correct class labels. To demonstrate the OMNCNN model’s improved performance, several simulations were run. The experimental findings show that the OMNCNN model outperforms the state-of-the-art techniques presently in use with a maximum precision of 0.985, a recall of 0.9892, an accuracy of 0.987, and a robustness of 0.987.
