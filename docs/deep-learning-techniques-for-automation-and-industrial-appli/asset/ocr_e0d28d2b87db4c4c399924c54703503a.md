# 12.1.1   Social Media

SM is described as a category of internet-based applications focused on Web 2.0 philosophy and technologies that enable user-generated content to be generated and shared. Social media is a range of online platforms, including traditional media such as newspapers, radio, and TV, as well and popular applications such as Facebook and Twitter that allow users to create, share, and network content for online social interactions. Social media is simple to use and enables people to connect and network on an unparalleled scale at speeds not visible in conventional media. However, many websites spread fake facts. They print misleading news, lies, hoaxes, and conspiracy theories in a cover-up of true news. The primary aim of fake news outlets is to manipulate the public on some (mostly political) topics. Examples of this can be found in Ukraine, the USA, Great Britain, Russia, and many others [12].

While social networking was initially met with great enthusiasm among new generations, all ages in India seemed to accept this new social culture. More and more days spent on social applications being considered a waste of time are long gone, as internet connection rates, as well as smartphones, increase. In the beginning, the usage of social networking was restricted to firms and organizations for communicating with peers, clients, clients, Twitter-managed customers, Facebook pages, or WhatsApp on business cards. Social networking now involves friends, family members, and classmates. SM provides different possibilities, such as content access, videos, social network expansion, the opportunity to share themselves, learn new things and have new experiences, go on a 'quest' online, and connect with friends and families.
