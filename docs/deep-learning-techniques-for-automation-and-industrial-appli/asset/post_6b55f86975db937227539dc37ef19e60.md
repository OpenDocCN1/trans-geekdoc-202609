### Abstract

Images are the main source for all image-processing fields like surveillance, detection, recognition, and satellite. Good visibility of images captured by sensors becomes crucial for all computer vision tasks. Sometimes, the scene quality is degraded by bad weather conditions like haze, fog or smoke; therefore, making it difficult for the computer vision area to obtain actual information. Haze can be removed from a single input scene by using single image dehazing methods. Synthetic hazy images are created by a haze generator. Currently, most image dehazing techniques are applied for synthetic haze. Various single-image dehazing techniques are being developed and tested on real-world scenes captured in hazy environments using cameras. These techniques aim to be practical solutions for removing haze from images. This study focuses on dehazing methods for both synthetic and real datasets totaling 45 hazy scenes. The output qualities of different techniques are measured using different parameters, such as CIEDE2000, Peak Signal to Noise Ratio (PSNR), and Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM).

Keywords: Color attenuation prior, dark channel prior, convolutional neural network, deep learning

### 6.1 Introduction

The quality of any output optical image device is dependent on its surrounding conditions of weather like mist, haze, dust, and fog.

Such weather conditions directly affect the color and contrast of the scene, resulting in degraded quality. It mainly occurs due to optical phenomena of light absorption and scattering reflected by the object surface. The reflected light from the surface of an object gets attenuated by aerosol particles present in the environment, and it decays by a factor called transmission. Further light is scattered by particles of surroundings, which is affected by the camera distance from the object [1]. Overall, these effects result in a poor quality output, which make it difficult for use in the field of vision systems such as surveillance and satellite. Thus, single-image dehazing is highly recommended as a pre-processing task for any computer vision method to enhance performance in hazy conditions. Single-image dehazing techniques do not carry any external knowledge of the single-input scene. Haze is spatially variant as it is affected by scene depth, having impact on different parts of the scene. Therefore, scene depth is needed in a haze-formation model to produce haze-free output [2].

### 6.2 Formation of a Haze Model

The image captured by the sensor has two parts. The first part is the scene radiance, which consists of light reflected from the object. The second part is environmental illumination, which consists of light reflected from the ground, direct sunlight and diffused skylight [3]. Nayar and Narasimhan developed a dichromatic atmospheric scattering model as mentioned in Figure 6.1. This model explained haze formation by the linear combination of air light and transmission value [4].

As a mathematical equation, it can be expressed by:

I(x) = J(x) t(x) + A (1 - t(x))    (6.1)

where J (x) t (x) represents the attenuation model, A(1 – t (x)) represents the degradation model, J (x) is the clear scene, A represents global atmospheric light, t(x) represents transmission value, which denotes quantity of light not scattered and that reaches the sensor, and I (x) is the observed hazy scene. The main aim of dehazing is to restore the J(x) value from I(x).

### 6.3 Different Techniques of Single-Image Dehazing

#### 6.3.1 Contrast Enhancement

A hazy scene has a lower contrast compared to haze-free images due to bad weather [7]. To increase the contrast of the scene histogram, equalization is applied [8]. However, this technique does not consider object depth proportional to haze thickness, which differs locally. To increase the contrast of the hazy scene, fast Fourier transform and dark channel prior (DCP) are applied to estimate the transmission value instead of the bilateral filter [9]. Wavelength compensation techniques are applied to underwater images, which effectively restore the color balance of images and dehazed outputs [10]. Kim et al. proposed an algorithm in which information is preserved and contrast is enhanced by minimizing cost function [11]. Principal component analysis has been used to estimate atmospheric value by extracting the feature of a haze patch [12]. Enhanced output is produced by estimating transmission by supervised learning methods. For nighttime haze, a dual channel prior technique is proposed, which results in dark channel prior and bright channel prior. Initial transmission value is estimated by dark channel prior, while refined transmission value is obtained by bright channel prior [13]. In [14], white balancing has been applied first to retain the natural color followed by adaptive histogram equalization. Finally, information lost due to clipping in histogram equalization is restored by applying a guided filter. In [15], novel techniques based on dark channel and histogram equalization have been proposed. First, haze density is estimated by applying a dark channel. After that, scene contrast is increased through histogram equalization, and lastly color correction is applied at final stage.

#### 6.3.2 Dark Channel Prior

He et al. performed an empirical experiment for haze and haze-free images. It was found that hazy scenes carry dark pixels whose values are nearly zero for any one of the color channel bands [16]. The dark channel is mathematically defined as:

J^dark(x) = min_{y∈Ω(x)} ( min_{c∈{r,g,b}} J^c(y) )   (6.4)

Here, Ω(x) represents a local patch, which is centered at x. c ∈ {r, g, b} is the color channel band. J^c is the color intensity. All pixels in Ω(x) and the minimum value from all color bands are chosen as dark channels [17]. Tomasi et al. proposed a bilateral filter for edges, which can be used to minimize the dark channel [18]. Tang et al. proposed new techniques: Firstly, atmospheric value is estimated by a binary tree algorithm. Secondly, transmission value is estimated by two different parts: the bright and dark regions. Transmission value in the dark region is determined through DCP, and adaptive transmission is applied on the bright region. Lastly, guided filter is applied for both parts [19].
