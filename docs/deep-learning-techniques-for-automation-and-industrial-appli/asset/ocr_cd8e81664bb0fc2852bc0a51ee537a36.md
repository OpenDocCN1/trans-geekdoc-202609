+   - Soil should be of type gravel and sandy as they support high percolation.
+   - Drainage density should be high.
+   - Because of the gentle slope, the residence time of the surface water on the ground is reduced. Hence, water gets a longer time for ground absorption. If the slope surface area is smaller, surface runoff is reduced, improving seepage (or drainage).
+   - Subsurface recharge is mainly determined by surface level flow of water speed and vertical percolation. This in turn depends on slope degree.
+   - Groundwater is abundant at lower elevations.
+   - Topographic wetness index (TWI) index is inversely proportional to groundwater yield.
+   - Slope length value should be low.
+   - Fractures, voids of consolidated rocks, and porosity of unconsolidated sediments are suitable storage options. Faults facilitate groundwater recharge in hard rocks due to fractures, joints, and lineaments. These act as small water carrying channels from the above surface to below surface groundwater.
+   - Weathered rocks and unconsolidated or consolidated sediment rocks improve water retention.
+   - Lithological properties such as grain size, color, and texture determine the electrical resistivity or conductivity, soil porosity, and flow in the aquifer.
+   - Water movement is facilitated by the unconsolidated sediment rocks due to larger gaps.
+   - High lineament density means high secondary porosity, enabling increased percolation, which expedites the faster aquifer recharging.
