## 3.5 SMART BEDS

Smart hospital beds have a remote monitoring system that monitors the patient. Smart hospital beds contain sensors for body temperature, heartbeat, blood oxygen and weight sensors, among others. Those signs are required and important for specialists to watch the health of the patients. This system is found inside the clinic beds and transmits every patient signal to the medical staff, especially in those cases that need medical attention. This significant information is conveyed to the central system of the medical clinic and enables healthcare managers to instantly review and monitor the patient's organs. Furthermore, this system sends alert messages, or flags, to the staff just in the event of any sudden change in the patient's condition during that time. Since a large amount of time is spent on the bed, it is essential for hospitals to monitor the patient's condition while the person is on the bed. Smart beds have offered an effective solution for healthcare providers to continuously monitor patients to provide better care to the patients. Such advanced beds reduce the burden on medical staff responsible for monitoring the patient's condition. Furthermore, smart beds have proven instrumental for healthcare providers to anticipate, detect, and prevent unscheduled bed exits.

Hospital beds are where most patients spend most of their time, and new “smart beds” encourage patients to be careful, while “smart” functions encourage nurses to examine data and improve patient care. The beds interface with EMR systems to send patient data and encourage nurses to monitor metrics such as movement and weight changes. Technology firms are also involved in the smart bed trend, providing useful tools that integrate with the beds to yield even more significant information.[4]
