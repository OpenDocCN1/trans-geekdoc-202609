## 3.4 SMART PILLS

Diagnosing a patient with inflammatory bowel disease such as Crohn's disease (CD) is a process that involves multiple steps. If the results of physical examination, blood tests, and stool tests suggest that a person's symptoms are being caused by inflammation within the gastrointestinal tract, the doctor may refer the patient to a gastroenterologist. Gastroenterologists are physicians specializing in the health of the gastrointestinal system; they are specially trained to perform a special type of diagnostic procedure known as endoscopy. Endoscopic procedures allow the gastroenterologist to examine inside a person's gastrointestinal tract using a special instrument called an “endoscope.” Associated with this instrument is a long, thin tube with a tiny camera and light connected to its tip, which projects images of the gastrointestinal tract onto a screen for analysis. Endoscopes can be inserted into the body through a natural opening, such as the mouth and down the throat, or through the anus. A medical instrument may also be inserted through a small incision (cut) made in the skin during keyhole surgery.

The term “smart pills” refers to electronic devices smaller than traditional ones, designed in the form of pharmaceutical containers yet performing highly advanced functions, such as detection, imaging, and drug delivery. They may incorporate biosensors or imagers, pH or chemical sensors. Once swallowed, they travel through the digestive tract to collect data that is usually difficult to obtain, and they are effectively transmitted by the system. Their design as ingestible sensors distinguishes them from implantable or wearable sensors. Several endoscopy methodologies and a wide variety of endoscopic strategies are likewise performed to investigate or screen for cancer. Traditional rigid endoscopes used for these methods are uncomfortable for patients and may cause internal injury or illness due to reuse on multiple patients.
