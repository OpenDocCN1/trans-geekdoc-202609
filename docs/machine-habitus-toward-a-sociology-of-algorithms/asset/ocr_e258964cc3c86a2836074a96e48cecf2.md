Machine Habitus

The degree of informational asymmetry also depends on the digital literacy of the human in the loop (Burrell 2016). Klawitter and Hargittai concluded their qualitative research on entrepreneurs' understandings of automated systems by arguing that 'algorithms' are very hard to grasp for people [...]. Algorithmic skills remain the domain of a select few users, even the most motivated (2018: 3505). A recent study by Gran and colleagues (2020) has shown that Norwegian Internet users' level of awareness and understanding of platform algorithms are strongly associated to socio-demographic factors, as happens with other digital skills (Hargittai and Micheli 2019); Lutz 2019). More specifically, older, female and lower-educated respondents living outside urban areas were significantly more likely to have low or no awareness of their ubiquitous interactions with artificial agents. Such discrepancies are likely to be even broader in less developed countries. When characterized by a lack of 'algorithmic awareness' on the part of the users (Gran, Booth and Bucher 2020), human—machine interactions take on the asymmetric configuration of a 'technological unconscious' invisibly governing digital experiences (Beer 2009).

Far from being natural or inevitable, informational asymmetries are an in-built characteristic of the deliberately panoptic architecture of for-profit apps and platforms (Rosenblatt and Stark 2016; Ruckenstein and Granroth 2020; Cheney-Lippold 2017). Reducing them one is one of the political rationales behind the open-source code of IAQOS, which has been designed to allow its interlocutors to – see how the AI system thinks in real time (Iacones and Persico 2019). The same intent of creating a horizontal circulation of information between calculative devices and their classified users has driven other projects at the intersection of art, activism and research, such as DataSelf (Hang 2018) – a browser extension simulating Facebook’s predictive surveillance in order to make visible and increase algorithmic awareness – or the free software Dow, defined by one of its creators as ‘a smart digital network appliance [...] that makes it possible to connect objects and people in a friendly, conscious and responsible manner’ (Roio 2018: 54).
