This perspective is widely shared in the social sciences and humanities (e.g., Burr, Cristianini and Ladyman 2018; Esposito 2017; Knorr 2009; Floridi and Sanders 2004; also see Cerulo 2009), and it has been developed in sociology by the STS scholar Donald McKenzie.

Taking inspiration from Knorr Cetina’s work, McKenzie has recently argued that Erving Goffman’s classic theories of social interaction can be extended ‘to situations in which trading algorithms interact with each other rather than human beings’ (2019:41). In the highly digitized domain of financial markets, most of the work is done by autonomous machines, employed to place and match orders, price movements and deceive competing algorithms (D. McKenzie 2018; 2019; O’Neil 2016; Pardo-Guuerra 2010). purely instrumental goals and total lack of consciousness, thousands of agents working for high-frequency trading firms co-produce an ‘interaction order’ that, according to McKenzie, presents communicative strategies and forms of collective organization which resembles the face-to-face situations famously observed by Goffman (1983). Nonhuman interactions are also somewhat shaped by social institutions – as financial regulations – and material arrangements such as the length of the fibres connecting servers, which physically affects the speed of automated interactions and, therefore, their success (D. McKenzie 2018; 2019). These opaque data-based exchanges can potentially have very tangible effects on the social world, as the following example of an algorithm-induced ‘flash crash’ in the stock markets:

> @AP: Breaking: Two Explosions in the White House and Barack Obama injured. This message appeared on Tuesday, 23 April 2013, at 1.07 pm on the official Associated Press Twitter account @AP. It quickly received over 4000 retweets and a number of followers. Within seconds, according to the Reuters data, the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 143.5 points, and nearly $136.5 billion of the Standard & Poor’s 500 Index’s value was wiped out. [...] The tweet was revealed as the result of a malicious hack of the Associated Press Twitter account. The recovery of the markets was rapid.
