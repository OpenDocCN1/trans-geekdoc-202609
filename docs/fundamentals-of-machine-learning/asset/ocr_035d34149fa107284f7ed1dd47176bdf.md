Given a hypotheses space with Wapnic-Chervonencis dimension VC(h), then, with probability $1 - \delta$, the error of the empirical risk compared to the expected risk (true risk) is

```
$|R(h) - \hat{R}(h)| > O \left(\sqrt{\frac{VC}{m} \log \frac{m}{VC}} + \frac{1}{m}\right).$
```

The VC dimension is thereby a measure of how many points can be divided by a member of the hypothesis set for possible label combinations of the point. For example, consider three arbitrary points in two dimensions as shown in Fig. 3.12, and let us consider the hypothesis class of all possible lines in two dimensions. We can always divide the three points under any class membership condition, of which two examples are also shown in the figure. By contrast, it is easy to find examples with four points that cannot be divided by a line in two dimensions. The VC dimension of lines is thus $VC = 3.3$
