A special function of random variables, which is of particular interest as it can approximate many processes in nature. Sums of random variables often occur, or for when calculating averages from measured quantities,

```
$$\bar{X} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i,$ \quad (6.28)
```

and we are interested in the probability density function of the random variables that represents the mean. This function depends of, course, on the specific density of the random variables $X_i$. However, there is an important observation summarized in the **central limit theorem**. This theorem states that the average (normalized sum) of $n$ random variables that are drawn from any distribution with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma$ is approximately normally distributed with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma/n$ for a sufficiently large sample size $n$. The approximation is, in practice, even good for relatively small numbers of added variable. For example, the normalized sum of only seven uniformly distributed pseudo-random numbers is shown in Fig. 6.2 that was produced with the code in Listing 6.1.

![](bbox=[0.298, 0.576, 0.636, 0.735])

**Fig. 6.2** Histogram of 1,000 samples of a random number that is the sum of seven distributed random numbers.
