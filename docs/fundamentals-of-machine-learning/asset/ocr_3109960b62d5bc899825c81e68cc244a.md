Before leaving this area, it is useful to point out some general observations. Artificial neural networks have certainly been one of the first successful methods for nonlinear regression, implementing non-linear hypotheses of the form $h(x; w) = g(w^T x)$. The corresponding mean square loss function,

> $$L \propto (y - g(w^T x))^2$$

is then also a general function of the parameters. Minimizing such function is generally difficult. However, we could consider instead hypotheses that linear in the parameters, $h(x; w) = w^T \phi(x)$, but that the MSE loss is quadratic in the parameters,

> $$L \propto (y - w^T \phi(x))^2$$

The corresponding quadratic optimization problem can be solved much efficiently. This type of thinking has been further developed in support vector machines that we reviewed earlier. I point out here to stress that basic neural networks are not always optimal in the way they are commonly implemented and that variations are possible. Also, an interesting and further issue is how to choose the nonlinear function $\phi$. This brings us back to non-linear support vector machines and deep learning.
