In this section, we elaborate further on MLOps enabling techniques and contemporary tools that AI practitioners can use when executing the AI architecture implementation framework. We begin with MLOps techniques by providing an introduction to DevSecOps. As we mentioned earlier, MLOps evolved from the well-established discipline of DevOps, and security was integrated and encapsulated later under DevSecOps.

### From DevOps to MLOps

The DevOps movement began by formulating a more structured approach to delivering applications and services from the development stage [16]. DevOps became a logical progression from the early days needing agility and speed incorporating CI/CD and monitoring. The Agile methodology was accepted as the guiding principles, as stated in the Agile Manifesto in table 10.5 in chapter 10. Kim et al. described a case when 2011 needed a more structured approach (as offered by DevOps approach) to methodically move from development to operations and scale with the goal of “paying down technical debt” during their rapid growth.

The five I-V&V steps, delineated in the previous section, are well aligned with DevOps and Agile movements breaking complex systems into manageable subsystems and employing rapid prototyping [17]. An important component of the Agile methodology, as a technique for rapid prototyping, is to adopt a product mindset, not a project mindset. Product mindset deals with timelines shorter than a month, with stakeholder feedback on the value and instances. Another very important requirement to make the Agile approach work, is to avoid monolithic architecture where data is in silos [18]. The team must also be stakeholder centric, as espoused in SRLs 1 and 2, and emphasize the importance of people (e.g., customers), process, and technology [19]. The Agile technique will help AI organizations reduce what Cunningham coined as “technical debt” by implementing rapid deployments through flexible, extensible systems [20].

SRL 4 calls for having a development and operational AI ecosystem, depicted in figure 10.4. As part of rapid prototyping, the team must execute measurements to evaluate the performance of AI systems using a set of standards: benchmarks, and metrics. We also want to stress that benchmarks in the context of AI ecosystem shown in figure 10.4 are system-level. As discussed earlier concerning SRL 5, each of the five I-V&V steps must be evaluated on the basis of correctness, scalability, and behavior within and outside the operating envelope; these results must be stored in a well-defined configuration repository [22].

Now let us turn our attention to the “security” part of DevSecOps. Security vulnerabilities can threaten AI systems. Techniques discussed earlier in the book certify protecting data at rest, in motion, and in use. However there are many surfaces accessible to malicious attackers as discussed in chapter 7.

A technique with great potential for AI applications is processing on encrypted data. Researchers have been developing secure multiparty computation (SMPC) and encryption. The latter is still computationally intensive to be practical. Shen et al. have shown the value of SMPC for obfuscating the content of data and enabling information sharing and analysis [23]. As shown by other researchers, SMPC allows computation on a subset of data by individual parties, making access to sensitive and private data very difficult by cryptographic protocols [24, 25]. Also, Ricci and colleagues have established a company called CipherLabs that focuses on the application of SMPC to ML applications, preserving the confidentiality of the data.

Security in the DevSecOps cycle implies that safeguards are in place from the beginning of AI project or service development. Both physical security and cybersecurity are part of the AI architecture implementation framework. The assessment of these key attributes is required as part of SRL 7.

Rajapak et al pointed out that ‘the adoption of DevSecOps in practice is a challenge’ [26]. The authors performed a comprehensive and systematic literature review. They identified twenty-one challenges and thirty-one specific solutions across four main themes: people, practices, tools, and infrastructure. For example, we discuss containerized approaches later in this section. They also identified vulnerabilities affecting the CI/CD cycle due to the need for fast responses. Among the specific solutions recommended, they suggested implementing capabilities in a cloud-centric environment (we see discussions of cloud-native computing in chapter 5 and in the next subsection), using orchestrators to reduce the attackers attack surface. We discuss Kubernetes (K8s) as a type of orchestrator in this section.

Secured AI systems require more than just technical solutions. AI-driven organizations must address people (and the required training), as well as maintaining tight security practices within the organization, including role-based access control (RBAC) measures. Recently, Uber Technologies suffered a major cybersecurity attack by a hacker who gained significant access to the company’s internal system [27]. Security analysts believed that the attacker was able to steal customers’ credentials, providing access to the company’s private network.

The US National Acad Organization completed a detailed report on the future of encryption [28]. Encryption solutions—such as the application of cryptography—will need to evolve rapidly to fend off attacks on AI systems from malicious actors. Furthermore, AI organizations must prepare themselves for when quantum computers break encrypted solutions by making AI systems quantum-safe [29].

## MLOps Building Blocks and Contemporary Tools

Let us set the stage by reviewing what MLOps distinct in the implementation of AI systems. As outlined by Vengiser et al. in reference to the Continuous Delivery Foundation—Special Interest Group MLOps, there is a set of capabilities that differ from traditional software engineering practices [31]:

- MLOps aims to unify the release cycle for ML and software application release.
- MLOps enables automated testing of ML artifacts (e.g., data validation, ML model testing, and ML integration testing).
- MLOps enables the application of agile principles to ML projects.
- MLOps enables supporting ML models and data sets to build these models as first-class citizens within CI/CD systems.
- MLOps reduces technical debt across ML models.
- MLOps must be a language-, framework-, platform-, and infrastructure-agnostic practice.

In bringing CI/CD as a top priority throughout the ML life cycle, the I-V&V process must occur across the end-to-end AI pipeline illustrated in the inner circle of the AI system architecture implementation framework (see figure 10.3), including data conditioning, ML models, human-machine teaming (HMT), modern computing, and robust responsible AI. Monitoring must include an assessment of data drifts, algorithm specification changes, and performance drifts, likely resulting in a need for refreshing the data, retraining the ML models, or both.
