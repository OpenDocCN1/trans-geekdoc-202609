In 2016, the company DeepMind Technologies Limited (which had been acquired by Google two years earlier) demonstrated the ability for a machine to defeat the top Go player, Lee Sedol from South Korea. This major AI accomplishment reinforcement learning gleaned from deep neural networks (DNNs) through reinforcement learning examples of human play. Reinforcement learning requires agents to set goals, policies, and then a network to take actions to achieve the goals constrained by the policies. The AI system was AlphaGo [14] and as reported by Google, it used 1,202 central processing units (CPUs) and 176 graphics processing units (GPUs) in a distributed processing architecture. In 2017, DeepMind introduced a new AI system called AlphaGo Zero [15], which boasted the ability to defeat the previous system, AlphaGo, by self-reinforcement learning instead of relying on a large number of CPUs and GPUs in a distributed computing architecture using a single machine in the Google Cloud with four tensor processing units (TPUs). DeepMind continues to accomplish significant breakthroughs. For example, DeepMind's AlphaFold is a neural network that can predict protein structures with a high degree of accuracy. DeepMind's prediction is significant accomplishment in computational biology, which has vexed researchers for fifty years [17].

Notable researchers are now working on bringing together the strengths of DNNs and symbolic AI to employ the benefits of both. In their Turing Award lecture, Yoshua Beng, Yann LeCun, and Geoffrey Hinton [18] emphasized the need to consider both neural network approaches and rule-based approaches. Their discussion generated great interest in the AI research community through an analogy to the premise in the award-winning book by Daniel Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow. A Nobel laureate, Kahneman defines system 1 as representative of a human's fast thinking. Deep learning encompasses system 1-type reactive inference. Kahneman constructs system 2 to represent a human's slow. Symbolic AI is characterized as performing reasoning, or sequential reasoning, which is closely aligned with system 2. Kahneman emphasizes that systems 1 and 2 can be thought of as "categories of mental processes" [19]. These analogies are very important since researchers predict that in the next several decades, we will see AI systems integrating both approaches under the rubric of neuro-symbolic AI [20–24].
