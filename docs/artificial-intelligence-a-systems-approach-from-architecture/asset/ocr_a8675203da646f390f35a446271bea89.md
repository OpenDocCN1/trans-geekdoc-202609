There are different levels depending on how critical the issue is and difficulty in providing a recommendation. If the chatbot is unable to resolve the issue, the topic is escalated to a human.

On the other hand, searching and discovering insightful content, in high-stakes and critical applications, will require a very careful assessment of the accuracy or validity of the output from the ML stage. These applications will require humans to intervene in the final courses of action. If the recommendation time cycles a relatively long time constant (meaning if the HMT stage operates in hours or on a longer time scale), then the human analysts can make their own judgments on a valid course of action (or recommendation).

We want to illustrate one example of this for the cybersecurity domain. Often, cyberanalysts are undocumented with cyberreports, which contain valuable cybercontent in a sea of other documents that are of limited value (i.e., void of cyberinformation). Figure 6.5 shows a prototype AI system developed at the MIT Lincoln Laboratory that normally takes for forensic cyberanalysts to search and discover malicious discussions in social media by leveraging natural-language ML techniques [23].

The input data on the left of the chart represented surrogate sources of social media, such as Twitter, Reddit, Stack Exchange, and others, consisting of thousands of documents. After performing a simple feature generation as the first step, using a TF/IDF algorithm, through word stemming (e.g., "hacker", "hacking", "hacks"), the output resulted in documents containing approximately 1 percent correlated. This was followed by a logistic regression classifier to separate documents with relevant cybercontent from documents that were void of any cybercontent of interest. This two-step process (i.e., ML stages) resulted in more than 80 percent of documents containing cybermaterial of interest provided to the forensicanalyst, with the ability to discover 90 percent of the cyberdiscussions with less than 1 percent false alarms.

AI as a search-and-discovery tool will continue to be an important technique to augment human analyst capabilities. The volume-velocity-variety of data are continuing to grow at an exponential rate, requiring the use of AI to make the search-and-discovery task manageable.
