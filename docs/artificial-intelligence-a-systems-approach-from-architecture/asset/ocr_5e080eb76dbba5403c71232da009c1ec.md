Let us look at an example of preventing a computing system—defensive stage
2—from operating with counterfeit parts. Koziel et al. automated the process of eval-uating hardware parts by monitoring the power levels of hardware devices [90]. As shown in figure 5.9, their approach consisted of collecting unique signatures of power
traces from an authentic part (the golden part), and correspondingly collecting power
trace signatures from suspected parts. PCA was performed on the collected input data
to reduce the dimensionality to the most dominant subspace components, followed
by a tenfold cross-validation SVM to determine if the suspected part was close enough
to the golden part. Their approach enabled rapid testing of devices before
integrating them into an embedded system. Zhu et al. also implemented a K-nearest
neighbor algorithm to classify anomalies potentially present in a printed circuit board,
identifying malicious attacks [91].
