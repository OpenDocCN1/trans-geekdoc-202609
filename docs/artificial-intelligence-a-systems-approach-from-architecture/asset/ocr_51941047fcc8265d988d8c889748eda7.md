Communication can be split into two major categories—persuasive and instructive. Communication for persuasion involves the use of language and other communication tools to convince the audience to take a specific action, adopt a certain viewpoint, or simply think favorably of you, your idea, or your product [3]. This type of communication often includes elements such as storytelling, emotional appeals, and logical arguments to persuade the audience.

On the other hand, communication for instruction involves the use of language and other communication tools to import knowledge or information to the audience. The goal of this type of communication is to help the audience understand a concept, process, or idea, and such communication may include elements as clear explanations, examples, storytelling, and visuals to support understanding. While both forms of communication overlap in certain situations, the primary difference is in the goal of the communication: persuasion aims to influence the audience to accept a belief or argument, while instruction aims to help the audience understand a topic [4]. Both types of communication can be important in different contexts as a practitioner. but also highlight some heuristic rules for instructive communication. For a comprehensive review of rules and techniques for effective communication, refer to Winston's book Make It Clear: Speak and Write to Persuade and Inform [3].
