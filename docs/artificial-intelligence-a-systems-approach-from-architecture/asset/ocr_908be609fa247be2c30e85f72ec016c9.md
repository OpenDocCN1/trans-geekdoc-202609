These approaches, and additional techniques, are compared and discussed in great detail by Bhatt et al. [50], Mittelstadt et al. [51], and Linardatos [52], including some of their limitations.

Bhatt et al. [50], emphasized that these approaches are focused on local explainability (i.e., relevant to specific model inputs). We lack techniques that are more focused on global explainability. This observation reinforces our approach in this book of looking at AI as a system, from its end-to-end architecture design through its deployment.

The steps discussed in chapter 2, following the AI system implementation framework illustrated in figure 2.2, delineate a more global approach by focusing on stakeholders, subsystem assessments, interplay among architecture building blocks, risk management, and end-to-end system performance. This more global approach also helps in addressing two of the desired technical properties outlined here—namely, having a reliable and verifiable AI system. Ultimately, with domain knowledge must take part in helping explain the AI system output—especially in high-stakes cases—including an assessment of the confidence level versus the consequence of actions, as discussed in chapter 6. For example, a radiologist must help a physician understand ML output to determine if the results of X-rays show the presence of a malignant tumor.

Another useful mitigation technique is using counterfactuals. The employment of counterfactuals can be motivated by the work of Byrne [53], since humans often think about what would happen if inputs or scenario are taken into account. Counterfactuals are inputs to a system or model motivated by asking-if questions. They can be used at the end-to-end level (global explainability) or at the specific target input to a model (local explainability). However, one limitation in the use of counterfactuals is how to select those inputs that represent best and can lead to a more thorough explanation, with the goal of achieving a robust AI system [54].

These are very important protection objectives necessary to make an AI system robust.
