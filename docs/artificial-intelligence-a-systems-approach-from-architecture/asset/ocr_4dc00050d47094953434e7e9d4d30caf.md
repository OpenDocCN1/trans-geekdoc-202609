There is ongoing and very interesting human-machine (robot) research focusing on building mental models that the human knows when and not to rely on intelligent agents (i.e., a human finds an accurate mental model of the AI strengths and weaknesses) [56]. Another exciting area of research is in the human teaching the robot and versa, and the associated lessons, to strengthen the human-machine models, especially in real-world and messy, unstructured environments [57, 58].

However, performance metrics for these research areas are still evolving. One could, for example, assess the effectiveness in meeting the goals and objectives of a task or set of tasks. Some of these metrics include:

+   * Quantitative assessment of confidence level versus consequence of action (as per tiers 1 and 2)
* Time to reach a trustworthy decision by the human operating alone versus the improvement (or lack of) by human-machines as teammates
* Adaptability to real-world scenarios
* Counterfactual analysis of sensitivity of human-machine interactions to data inputs
* Compliance with the FASTEPS principles as the tier 4 metric under the rubric of responsible AI (RAI), discussed further in chapter 8
