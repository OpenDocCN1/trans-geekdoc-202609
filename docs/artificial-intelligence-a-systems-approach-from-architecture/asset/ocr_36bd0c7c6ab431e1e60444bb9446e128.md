+   * Leverage quantum computing: There are two efforts (at the macro-level) that must be attended to now. First, in the near term, we must prepare for the postquantum computing era [120]. ML algorithms, and AI in general, which rely on encryption to protect private and valuable business content, must be resilient to the postquantum computing era. The second emphasis is on continuing to advance quantum computing to improve the ability to model and reduce the computation time of advanced algorithms. One example of such an algorithm is the HHL algorithm (named after its authors, Aram Arrow, Avinatan Hassidim, and Seth Lloyd), which solves a set of linear equations commonly found in linear algebra computations using a quantum computer [121]. Once feasible, the solution will require log (N) computations, with a quantum computer, instead of N required of a classical computer, where N is the size of a sparse matrix A, as part of Ax=b; where x and b are vectors of length N.

There are strategic road maps using classical computing complemented by quantum computing as an approach to take advantage of the of both and, when quantum computing is available, integrate quantum computing into the mainstream [122]. AI researchers at IBM are pursuing multiple hardware architectures, including specialized hardware based on a von Neumann architecture (as discussed earlier in this chapter), analog AI devices, and quantum computing for AI [123].

+   * Scale computing to exploit multimodal data (i.e., text, speech, video): By incorporating several modalities, AI systems can introduce more context, improving reasoning about the environment and providing more insight into the AI decision support cycle (i.e., explainable AI/XAI). However, the computation throughput to ingest multimodal data, perform data conditioning, and process multimodal data through ML algorithms—while maintaining spatial-time coherence—are daunting. There are high-stakes applications where multimodality is very beneficial, including driverless cars, medical diagnosis, autonomous drones, and robotics [75,124,125]. More research is needed to scale computing across multalities.
