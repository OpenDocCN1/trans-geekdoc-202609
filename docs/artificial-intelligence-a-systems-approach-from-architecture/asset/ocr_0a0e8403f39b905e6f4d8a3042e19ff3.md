Great leaders also serve the role of on-the-job teacher to their direct reports by showing and providing learning-by-doing opportunities, as discussed by Finkelstein [39].

Leadership roles and responsibilities that are not well balanced can put a lot of stress on the leader and the team, potentially leading to poor results. It is a complete myth that those at the top got figured out—in fact, one of the common weaknesses of early career leaders is to behave as if they have all the answers. Ancona et al. [40] stressed the importance of accepting weaknesses as leaders to avoid burnout and of relying on their direct reports to amplify the collective strengths of the leadership team.
