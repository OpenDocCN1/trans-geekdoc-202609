## Machine Learning

Recent years have witnessed remarkable progress in machine learning (ML) due to the convergence of big data, advanced algorithms, and modern computing technologies. The proliferation of large volumes of data, known as “big data,” is largely attributed to the prevalence of smart phones, Internet of Things (IoT), and social media daily, which have collectively contributed to over 2.5 million terabytes of data that have been generated daily and over 90 percent of the world’s data that has been generated over the last two years. Along with big data, advancements in modern computing technologies such as graphics processing units (GPUs) and tensor processing technologies (TPUs) have made it possible to efficiently process data and large-scale computations, which is crucial for the intensive task of model training in ML, as will be discussed in chapter 5. Finally, breakthroughs in new algorithms and ML techniques have been key to efficiently and effectively extracting actionable insights from massive volumes and varieties of data in ways that were previously unimaginable.

ML—one of today’s most rapidly growing technical fields—is a branch of artificial intelligence (AI) focused on employing data and algorithms to extract knowledge and insights without explicit instructions. At its core, ML is all about designing algorithms and models that can learn from data without being explicitly programmed.
