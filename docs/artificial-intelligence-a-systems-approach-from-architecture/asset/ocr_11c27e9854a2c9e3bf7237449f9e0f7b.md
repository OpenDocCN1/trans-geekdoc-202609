AI aiding in search-and-discovery tasks will also have an impact on the enterprise level. For example, with advances in computer vision and cameras, managers will be able to predict potential problems by monitoring the factory floor in real time [24].

Another application of search and discovery, at the enterprise level, is using softwareagents to parse a large volume of documents—for example, reducing the workload of humans performing structured and repetitive tasks as part of enterprise resource planning. This type of enterprise application is commonly referred to as “robotic process automation”—it is not, strictly speaking, a robotic function, but the community, advancing enterprise processing using AI, has adopted robotic process automation to refer to functions performed by software agents automating routine functions, enabling humans to dedicate more time to cognitive functions [25, 26].

In 6.3, we address AI as a teammate. Remember that HMT hits on a spectrum. Although AI as a teammate falls between a human and a machine on the spectrum shown in figure 6.2, there are neither fixed nor hard boundaries. This loose separation is driven by the level of complexity with machine intelligence, plus the dependency on human-machine interplay and the level of confidence versus consequence of actions at the output of the HMT stage. As the members of the human-machine team work closer and complement each other, there is an implied higher degree of trust in the results of the ML stage.
