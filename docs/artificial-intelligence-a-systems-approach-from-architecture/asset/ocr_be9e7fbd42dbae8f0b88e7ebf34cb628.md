Advances in the deep learning community have led to the innovation of new network architectures. Different architectures have developed specific use cases by better emulating biological processes for tasks like image and language understanding. As such, new techniques, coupled with the advances in computing technologies and big data, have proved to be highly successful when applied to specific tasks. For example, CNNs are primarily used for image processing, but they also can be used for other types of input such as audio. Composing a complete and comprehensive list of all existing network architectures is practically impossible, as new architectures are being developed all the time, and there is no comprehensive summary of all existing neural network architectures. Liu et al. discuss widely used deep learning architectures and their practical applications, providing a deeper discussion of autoencoders, CNNs, deep belief networks, and restricted Boltzmann machines [35]. While it is by means no comprehensive list, Leijen et al., from the Asimov Institute, also identify and describe some salient neural network architectures. Our objective is to expose the reader to the variety of neural network architectures that exist today and the different functions they serve, not to give an exhaustive list.

One important advancement in neural networks (captured in figure 4.15) is the development of generative adversarial networks (GANs). GANs, introduced by Goodfellow et al. in 2014, are a class of ML frameworks in which two neural networks compete in a zero-sum game [37]. One network, called the "generator," creates new instances of data with the same statistics as the training set, while the other network, called the "discriminator," evaluates the authenticity of these new instances of data. The training objective of the discriminative network is to evaluate the data (i.e., belonging to the training set) or not, while the objective of the generative network is to increase the error rate of the discriminator.
