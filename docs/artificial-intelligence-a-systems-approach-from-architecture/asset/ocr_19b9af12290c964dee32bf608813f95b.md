These examples fall in the category of either AI as a search-and-discovery tool or AI as a teammate (discussed further in sections 6.2 and 6.3, respectively). Representative examples of AI as a teammate are decision support systems [6]. Similarly, there have been impressive robotics applications in manufacturing [7, 8], warehouse automation [9, 10], precision agriculture [11, 12], and robots disinfecting areas potentially contaminated with the COVID-19 virus [13, 14], to name a few. We address these latter classes of AI systems in 6.4.

In the creation of the HMT building block—from the knowledge created at the ML stage—the role of the human machine occupies a spectrum, as shown in figure 6.2. We use this spectrum topology to parse the courses of action insight created between the human and/or the machine. In section 6.2, the HMT courses of action are principally decided by the human. In section 6.3, the discussion centers on the human and the machine operating closer to the middle of the spectrum. The human and machine complement each other in the process of reaching courses of action. In section 6.4, the machine is given autonomy to take a course of action but guided by the human if and when intervention is needed.

A study undertaken by Ransbottom et al. elaborates on the importance of integrating humans and machines within their organization's AI infrastructure, such that humans learn from AI and vice versa. One of their key findings, from research and global survey of more than 3,000 managers, is that only when organizations take this approach they are able to accrue significant financial gains.
