Many organizations fail at successfully transitioning AI prototypes into operations because they do not know how to establish a foundational infrastructure, and they also lack the knowledge of what is required to achieve a successful deployment. As properly pointed out by Ezzoni in the quote at the start of this chapter, AI is a revolutionary tool (in this context, “tool” is to be interpreted broadly), but how we deploy to provide beneficial value is totally up to the cognizant AI-driven operations.

Noreda et al. emphasized the importance of carefully evaluating AI projects, measuring success, embedding responsible AI (RAI), monitoring AI model performance, pursuing continuous improvement, and checking other criteria to increase the chances of success-fully adopting AI capabilities [2]. These are tasks that are part of what we are going to discuss in this chapter and the next. Another valuable reference is by Stoica et al., “Berkeley View of Systems Challenges for AI,” in which they identify a number of research areas that must be addressed for the benefits of AI [3]. They identified, a key challenge with AI systems: the need to “design AI systems that learn continually by interacting with a dynamic environment, while making decisions that are timely, robust, and secure.”

At the macro-level, meeting these challenges demands a careful analysis of stakeholder needs, a well-defined system architecture, a methodical and rigorous development approach, and quantitative and qualitative evaluations at each of the system integration steps in the discipline of AI systems engineering, as discussed in chapter 2. It is useful to repeat the definition used throughout our book, informed by the INCOSE's definition:

> An integrated set of AI architecture elements, subsystems or assemblies that accomplish a defined objective. These elements technologies enablers of AI products (hardware, software, firmware) or AI services, adhering to a set of processes, and using people, information, techniques, infrastructure, and other support elements. The integrated AI system must meet a minimum set of threshold requirements, while attending to FASTEPS issues.

We will refer to this definition throughout this chapter while addressing the AI deployment guidelines.
