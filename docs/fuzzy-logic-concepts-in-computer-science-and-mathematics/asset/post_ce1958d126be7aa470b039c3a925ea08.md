
## 7.3.2 IFS

Atanassov [1] defined IFS as a generalization of FSs:

$$A = \{<x, \phi_{A}(x), \psi_{A}(x)>|x \in X\}$$

where, $\phi_{A}(x), \psi_{A}(x): X \rightarrow [0,1]$ are the membership and nonmembership function of $x \in X$ with $\pi_{A}(x)=1-\phi_{A}(x)-\psi_{A}(x)$ as the intuitionistic index or hesitation index of $A$ in $X$.

## 7.3.3 INTUITIONISTIC FUZZY VARIABLE

For a time variable, $t$, $\zeta(t)=\left(\phi_{\zeta(t)}, \psi_{\zeta(t)}\right)$ is called the intuitionistic fuzzy variable.

In general, If $t = t_{1}, t_{2}, \ldots, t_{p}$, then $\zeta\left(t_{1}\right), \ldots, \zeta\left(t_{p}\right);\left(\phi_{\zeta\left(t_{i}\right)}, \psi_{\zeta\left(t_{i}\right)}\right) \in[0,1]$ be the intuitionistic fuzzy numbers (IFNs) collected at different periods.

The concept was proposed by Xu and Yager [15].

## 7.3.5 DYNAMIC INTUITIONISTIC FUZZY WEIGHTED AVERAGING (DIFWA) OPERATOR

According to Xu and Yager [15], let $\zeta(t_1),\ldots,\zeta(t_q)$ be a collection of IFNs collected at $q$ different periods and $\tau$ be the weight vector of the periods $t_*$, then $DIFWA_{\tau(t)}$ $(\zeta(t_1),... ,\zeta(t_q)) = \tau(t_1)\zeta(t_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus \tau(t_q)\zeta(t_q)$ is called the DIFWA operator.

$$\begin{gathered} DIFWA_{\tau(t)}(\zeta(t_1),...,\zeta(t_q)) \\ = \left(1-\prod_{k=1}^{q}(1-\phi_{\zeta(t_k)})^{\tau(t_k)},\prod_{k=1}^{q}\psi_{\zeta(t_k)}^{\tau(t_k)}, \prod_{k=1}^{q}(1-\phi_{\zeta(t_k)})^{\tau(t_k)}-\prod_{k=1}^{q}\psi_{\zeta(t_k)}^{\tau(t_k)} \right) \end{gathered}$$

where, $\tau(t_k) \geq 0 ; \sum_{k=1}^{q}\tau(t_k) = 1$

In this chapter, information is taken in the form of ITrFN and decision-making is performed with the help of the DIFWA operator.

## 7.4 MAIN CONCEPT

The idea of MCDM has been used by many researchers to discuss real-life situations encountered in day-to-day life. In this chapter, an algorithmic approach of the DIFWA operator proposed by Xu and Yager [15] has been discussed by taking information as ITrFN for the selection of a suitable site for the manufacturing plant under certain attributes. The attributes considered for the decision are given in the form of IFNs. A hypothetical case study has been developed to explain the algorithm. In this study, five factors have been considered that are responsible for the selection of a suitable site for the manufacturing plant and are given in Figure 7.1.

### 7.4.1 ALGORITHM

Following hypotheses have been considered and are given as:

- **I:** Let $\Theta = \{\theta_1,\ldots, \theta_n\}$ be the set of $n$ alternatives.
- **II:** Let $MP = \{MP_1,\ldots, MP_m\}$ be the finite set of attributes articulated in IFNs, with weight vector as $\omega = (\omega_1,\ldots, \omega_m)^T$
  where $\omega_j \geq 0$ and $\sum_{j=1}^{m}\omega_j = 1$

![](img/e9de70cd491e84262133af63cb33d500_114_0.png)

**FIGURE 7.1** Parameters responsible for the selection of suitable site for manufacturing Plant.

- **III:** Let $t_k$ having $q$ periods with weight vector be $\zeta(t)=(\zeta(t_1),...,\zeta(t_q))^T$,
  $$\zeta(t_k)\geq 0;\sum_{k=1}^q \zeta(t_k)=1$$
- **IV:** Let $\mathfrak{R}(t_k) = (r_{ij}(t_k))_{n\times m}$ be an IF decision matrix of the period $t_k$, where $r_{ij}(t_k) = \big(\phi_{r_{ij}}(t_k), \psi_{r_{ij}}(t_k), \pi_{r_{ij}}(t_k)\big); (i=1, ..., n, j=1, ..., m)$ is the attribute value defined in the form of IFN.

**The steps of the algorithm are given as:**

- **Step I:** Using the DIFWA operator, defined in Section 7.3:

  $$r_{ij} = DIFWA_{\zeta(t)} \left( r_{ij}(t_1), ..., r_{ij}(t_q) \right)$$

  $$= \left( 1-\prod_{k=1}^q (1-\phi_{r_{ij}}(t_k))^{\zeta(t_k)}, \prod_{k=1}^q \psi_{r_{ij}}(t_k)^{\zeta(t_k)}, \prod_{k=1}^q (1-\phi_{r_{ij}}(t_k))^{\zeta(t_k)} - \prod_{k=1}^q \psi_{r_{ij}}(t_k)^{\zeta(t_k)} \right)$$

  To aggregate all the IF decision matrices:

  $$\mathfrak{R} = (r_{ij})_{m\times n}$$

  where, $r_{ij} = \big(\phi_{ij}, \psi_{ij}, \pi_{ij}\big)$; $\phi_{ij} = 1-\prod_{k=1}^{q} (1-\phi_{r_{ij}}(t_k))^{\zeta(t_k)}$; $\psi_{ij} = \prod_{k=1}^{q} \psi_{r_{ij}}(t_k)^{\zeta(t_k)}$.

- **Step II:** Let $\hat{\Phi} = (\hat{\Phi}_1,...,\hat{\Phi}_m)^T$ and $\bar{\Phi} = (\bar{\Phi}_1,...,\bar{\Phi}_m)^T$ be the IF positive and ideal solutions, respectively, where $\hat{\Phi}_j$ and $\bar{\Phi}_j$ are the $m$ largest and $m$ smallest IFNs, respectively. Furthermore, let $\theta_i = (r_{i1},...,r_{im})^T$.

- **Step III:** Determine IF positive and negative ideals: IFPIS($\Phi^{+}$) and IFNIS ($\Phi^{-}$) from the alternatives $\theta_{i}$ as:

  $$\delta(\theta_{i}, \Phi_{i}^{+}) = \sum_{j=1}^{m} \omega_{j} \delta\left(r_{ij}, \Phi_{i}^{+}\right) = \sum_{j=1}^{m} \omega_{j}\left(1-\phi_{ij}\right)$$

  $$\delta\left(\theta_{i}, \Phi_{i}^{-}\right) = \sum_{j=1}^{m} \omega_{j} \delta\left(r_{ij}, \Phi_{i}^{-}\right) = \sum_{j=1}^{m} \omega_{j}\left(1-\psi_{ij}\right)$$

  where, $r_{ij} = \left(\phi_{ij}, \psi_{ij}, \pi_{ij}\right)$

- **Step IV:** Calculate the closeness coefficient of each alternative:

  $$C(\theta_{i}) = \frac{\delta(\theta_{i}, \Phi_{i}^{-})}{\delta(\theta_{i}, \Phi_{i}^{+}) + \delta(\theta_{i}, \Phi_{i}^{-})}$$

  Since, $\delta(\theta_{i}, \Phi_{i}^{+}) + \delta(\theta_{i}, \Phi_{i}^{-}) = \sum_{j=1}^{m} \omega_{j}(1+\pi_{ij})$

  $$\therefore \quad C(\theta_{i}) = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{m} \omega_{j}(1-\psi_{ij})}{\sum_{j=1}^{m} \omega_{j}(1+\pi_{ij})}$$

- **Step V:** On the basis of closeness coefficients $C(\theta_{i})$, rank the alternative $\theta_{i}$. Greater the $C(\theta_{i})$, better the alternative.

- **Step VI:** End.
