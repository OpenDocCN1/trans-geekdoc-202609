## 2.4 DESIGN OF CONTROLLER

The initial stage in the construction of the controller is the compensation of the nonlinearity, $f(V_{in}, N_{engine})$, using its inverse function, $\hat{f}^{-1}(V_{in}, N_{engine})$. It can be observed that this technique is applicable as a result of the monotonic nature of the function $f(., .)$. After obtaining the local linear models for various values of $T$ and $N_{engine}$, as well as the function $\hat{f}^{-1}(., .)$, an optimization approach is employed to compute a suboptimal proportional-integral (PI) controller for the given operating point (Figure 2.5). The optimization's cost function is specified as

$$J(K_p, T_i) = \lambda M_p + (1 - \lambda) \int_0^{\infty} t (e^2(t) dt)^{1/2} \quad (2.1)$$

where $K_p$ and $T_i$ represent the proportional gain and integral time of the PI controller, respectively. The value of $\lambda$ is equal to 0.5 and is utilized to quantify the significance of the O.S. in the cost function.

![](img/e9de70cd491e84262133af63cb33d500_46_0.png)

**FIGURE 2.5** Generated parameters $P$ and $T_i$.

The tracking error is denoted as

$$e(t)=P_{\mathrm{ref}}(t)-P_{\mathrm{sec}}(t) \tag{2.2}$$

represents the discrepancy between the preferred value and the secondary value at time $t$. The O.S., $M_p$, characterizes the maximum deviation of the output, $y(t)$. Mathematically, $M_p$ is defined as

$$M_p=\max\{\max\{y(t)-1\}, 0\}. \tag{2.3}$$

The use of the quasi-Newton approach to unconstrained multivariable optimization was demonstrated. The PI controller, which incorporates a collection of local PI controllers, can be mathematically expressed in continuous time as

$$C(s)=K_{p}\left(T,N_{\text {engine }}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{T_{i}\left(T,N_{\text {engine }}\right)s}\right). \tag{2.4}$$

In the context of discrete time, the expression can be written as

$$C(z)=K_{p}\left(T,N_{\text {engine }}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{T_{i}\left(T,N_{\text {engine }}\right)\left(1-z^{-1}\right)}\right). \tag{2.5}$$

By employing this approach, all quantization effects are accounted for, resulting in enhanced precision of the controller. The values of $K_p(T,N_{\text {engine }})$, $T_i(T,N_{\text {engine }})$, and $\hat{f}^{-1}(V_{\text {in }}^{r}, N_{\text {engine }})$ are restricted to certain operating points. A method of interpolation is required in order to facilitate smooth transitions between various operational locations. The utilization of an interpolation approach is required. It has been determined that an effective approach would involve the utilization of an FIS [6–9] to approximate the lookup table.
