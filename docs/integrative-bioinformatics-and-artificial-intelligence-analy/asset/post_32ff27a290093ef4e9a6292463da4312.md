## 2.9. Occurrence of the identified biomarkers in a large cohort of MDD patients from diverse ethnicities

Cross-validation was performed for the potential DEGs between the MDD patients and HCs that were identified with bioinformatics analysis and ML models from the discovery dataset. Independent cross-validation sets were considered on four well-characterized independent datasets (GSE99725, GSE67826, GSE38206, and GSE32280) from different populations with diverse ethnicities (Caucasian, Japanese and Chinese) (Table 1). First, the Limma package under the R software [34] was used to evaluate the DEGs between MDD patients and HCs in each dataset at a significance threshold of p<0.05. Next, all identified datasets including the discovery and the confirmation extremal datasets were combined and only the common genes were considered. ML was re-performed on 80% of the common genes among the entire datasets while the performance was evaluated in the 20% holdout.

## 2.10. Recruitment of MDD patients from the Kazakhstan population for biomarker assessment

The identified MDD biomarker was further assessed in an independent cohort with particular genetic background from the Kazakhstan population. Saliva samples of well-characterized MDD patients and HCs were collected at Al-Farabi Kazakh National University. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Al-Farabi Kazakh National University (IRB-A03 and IRB-A267). Inclusion criteria were diagnosed with MDD by a psychiatrist for the first time (based on ICD-10) and no medication/treatment. Exclusion criteria were volunteers with substance or alcohol abuse and taking antipsychotic medications. All participants after signing the consent form completed the Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology (IDS) [35]. Subjects who showed IDS>20 were interviewed by a psychiatrist for diagnosis. Saliva samples were collected.

## 2.11. mRNA gene expression evaluation using qRT-PCR

The top biomarker NRG1 was selected for quantitative gene expression evaluation between MDD patients and HCs from Kazakhstan population. Saliva samples were stored in RNAlater solution at +4°C until analysis. RNA was extracted using the RNeasy Mini kit (Qiagen, Germany), according to the manufacturer's protocol. RNA purity and quantity were assessed using NanoDrop2000 (Thermo Scientific, US). Total RNA was transcribed using a High Capacity Reverse Transcription Kit (Applied Biosystems, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The primers for NRG1 gene expression evaluation were Forward: 5'-TCGTGAATCAAAGCTACA-3' and Reverse: 5'-ACTCCCATTCACACA-3'. 18S rRNA was used as a housekeeping gene with primers: 5'-TCGTCTCACAAACTAGA-3' and Reverse: 5'-TGACTCAAGGGA-3'. The mRNA expression of the candidate and reference genes were quantified in MDD patients and HCs using SY Green/Master Mix (Thermo Scientific) on the QuantStudio3 system (Applied Biosystems). Amplification for each sample was carried out in two biological and two technical replicates, then the mean Cq value (quantitation cycle) was used to determine the fold expression change in MDD patients compared to the HCs. The relative expression levels of the target gene were calculated using 2^-ΔCt method.

## 3. Results

### 3.1. Identification of the differentially expressed genes among MDD patients

The differential transcriptomic profile across MDD patients and HCs were analyzed using a bioinformatics pipeline, and the result of data distribution before after quality control, normalization and filtration of the GSE9793 dataset are shown in Figure 1A. Comparing MDD patients to HCs, a total of 3294 and 2088 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified, respectively, in batch 1 and batch 2. The total of DEGs was merged into a common expression matrix based on the gene names to include 1446 common genes between 106 MDD patients and 51 HCs.

### 3.2. Enrichment analyses of the DEGs in MDD patients

To explore the molecular signals and biological processes of the identified DEGs in MDD patients compared to HCs, GSEA was performed. It consists of a computational method that identifies whether an a priori set of genes shows statistically significant and coherent differences between groups around several thousand pre-defined datasets including complex molecular mechanisms, immunologic signatures, biological processes, molecular activities and cellular structures. The enrichment analysis showed a total of 3262 and 2186 significant pathways (p<0.05) were identified in batch 1 and batch 2, respectively. The GSEA revealed that the DEGs in MDD patients are mainly enriched in pathways related to immune response, immune effector processes, signaling by cytokines, inflammatory response, proinflammatory and proinflammatory mediators, cellular responses to stimuli, neurodegeneration pathways, cerebellum pathway, and neuroactive ligand-receptor interactions. The details of all significant pathways are listed in Supplementary Table 2.

### 3.3. The most informative MDD-related genes from the activated cellular pathways

To identify the core genes that potentially determine the significant enrichment of the corresponding identified genes, the leading-edge analysis was also performed for all identified significant genes in each subgroup of MDD and HCs. Subsequently, the gene frequency was calculated based on the occurrence of a gene amongst all enriched leading-edge core genes from the significant over-represented gene sets in each batch. Overall, 353 genes resulted based on the merged data of MDD patients compared to HCs (Supplementary Table 3). These identified genes could be the most informative genes that play or control biological roles among the over-enriched pathways in MDD compared to HCs. The functional annotation of these top leading genes in MDD patients further confirms that the immune response-related pathways, the inflammatory responses, and metabolic pathways are the main significantly enriched in MDD compared to HCs (Figure 2). Next, only genes with large gene expression FC>1.5 or <0.5 between MDD patients and HCs were considered. Hence, 28 significantly important genes between MDD patients and HCs were left as shown in Table 2.

### 3.4. Explainable Machine learning models

To identify the most sensitive and potent predictive MDD-related genes, we further evaluated the above-mentioned DEGs between MDD and HCs with another computational method by applying different ML models. The fine-tuned parameters for the best models were presented in Table 3. Among all tested models, the best results were obtained with 3 final models on the merged batches Table 4. The best model is LR as the FS method (number of selected features=157). The final model's score is 0.76±0.11, and ROC-AUC is 0.82±0.12. While, the best model in batch 1 is LR (F1=0.73±0.17, ROC-AUC=0.88±0.13) and in batch 2 is SVM (F1=0.7±0.16, ROC-AUC=0.79±0.15). Additionally, the performance classification of the best-class classifiers trained on the discovery dataset was further evaluated in four well-characterized independent transcriptomics datasets from different populations. To compare the performance of the best model with baseline F1-macro, we built a dummy classifier that predicts the most frequent class label. Due to batch effect and domain shift, the F1-macro score was similar to the baseline in the majority of the datasets (Table 5). Hence, to resolve this, transfer learning was additionally applied to datasets that the 10-fold cross-validation has improved for all compared to the classifiers without pretraining, and in all cases, the F1-macro score was better than the baseline as in Table 5.

### 3.5. Relevant and stable MDD features from the best-built ML models

For the discovery dataset, the performances of all models showed ≥ 75% of accuracy for both and separate batches, consequently, we decided to include features from the best models of the separate and merged data for the downstream analysis. We included the top 20 important features for batch 1, batch 2 and merged batches. The score for each feature was computed as a share of all, which particular feature was selected.
