## 3.6. MDD-related biomarkers emerged from Data Integration

The identified gene lists from ML final models and the top frequent genes from the GSEA on the merged data from batch 1 and batch 2 were intersected to identify the MDD-related biomarkers. Since ML models are based on different principles and therefore reveal different biomarkers, thus, important features from the three best models were extracted to increase the set of overlapping biomarkers from the two approaches. The overlapping between the 86 most important features resulting from ML models and the 28 top leading genes resulting from the GSEA showed 10 common genes (Figure 3) including CEAM8, CLE12B, DEF4A, HCN, NRG1, M4, SERPING1, TCN1 and THBS1.

## 3.7. Mapping of the MDD-related biomarkers across Human brain regions

To get a better understanding of the association between brain structural alterations and gene expression changes in MDD, we assessed the brain-wide gene expression changes of the identified MDD-related biomarkers, using the whole-brain transcriptomic dataset from HBA. The mapping of the 10 MDD-related biomarkers showed significant (p<0.05) related up and down-regulated genes across the brain regions as listed in Table 6. The network analysis showed interconnections between the different involved brain regions suggesting that the evaluated genes are biologically interconnected as a group in different cognitive functions and varied range of behaviors (Figure 4). Thus, the identified MDD-related biomarkers are capable of modeling multivariate associations between brain plasticity and behavior disorders.

## 3.8. Reproducibility of MDD-related biomarkers expression changes across different populations

To evaluate the identified MDD-related biomarkers in another cohort of 42 MDD patients and 57 HCs, independent external transcriptomic datasets were selected different populations with diverse ethnicities (Caucasian, Japanese and Chinese). Transcriptomic analysis was performed for each dataset separately, then identification of DEGs was proceeded for each comparison of MDD patients compared to HCs and also for the follow-up of patients e.g. before/after surgery, remise and treatment. All studied comparisons were summarized in **Table 7**. The 10 MDD-related biomarkers were evaluated across different comparisons to show that all these genes, except *OLFM4* gene, were showing expression changes in at least one subgroup of MDD patients (Table 7). Importantly, NRG1 neuregulin1, was also frequently expressed in three out of four MDD populations.

In the GSE99725 dataset, the *NRG1* was up-regulated in HCs compared to MDD patients (FC=0.07, p=0.04) before and after (FC=0.11, p=0.03) surgery, however it didn't show significant variation (p>0.05) when comparing MDD patients before and after surgery.

Likewise, in the GSE76826 dataset, the NRG1 was down-regulated in HCs to MDD patients before removal and treatment (FC=0.87, p=0.0007) and (FC=0.85, p=0.0005), while, no gene changes were detected among MDD patients before and after removal. Furthermore, in the case of Venlafaxine treatment compared to the GSE32280 dataset, the NRG1 was down-regulated in HCs compared to MDD patients before and after the treatment (FC=0.44, p=0.04) and (FC=1.44, p=0.001), respectively, it was up-regulated when comparing MDD patients before and after Venlafaxine treatment (FC=1.5, p=0.001). Furthermore, the ML on 80% of the entire datasets including the discovery and external datasets along with performance evaluation in the 20% hold showed that the best-classifiers trained on the merry discovery dataset which of the LR best model with LR as the feature selection method showed a 0.58±0.05 F1 macro score on 5-fold cross-validation which was better than baseline with 0.38 ±0.04 F1 macro score.

Importantly, the NRG1 gene was considered among the most performing genes to distinguish between MDD patients and HC groups among the independent populations with diverse ethnicities. These results together, we suggest NRG1 as a potential robust biomarker to differentiate between MDD patients and HCs under different depression-related conditions as well as follow up the MDD patients remise.

## 4. Discussion

In the present study, we applied a mixture of bioinformatics and ML approaches to mine transcriptomics data. The study workflow is summarized in Figure 7. The integrative analysis of bioinformatics and ML identified 10 potent biomarkers including CEAM8, CLE12B, DEFA4, HCN, NRG1, OLFM4, SERPING1, TCN1 and THBS1, that showed the most significant and strong differential expression between MDD patients and HCs.
