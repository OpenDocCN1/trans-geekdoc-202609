# 238 2.9. Occurrence of the identified biomarkers in a large cohort of MDD patients from
239 diverse ethnicities

240 Cross-validation was performed for the potential DEGs between the MDD patients and HCs
241 that were identified with bioinformatics analysis and ML models from the discovery dataset.
242 Independent cross-validation sets were considered on four well-characterized independent
243 datasets (GSE99725, GSE67826, GSE38206, and GSE32280) from different populations with
244 diverse ethnicities (Caucasian, Japanese and Chinese) (Table 1). First, the Limma package
245 under the R software [34] was used to evaluate the DEGs between MDD patients and HCs in
246 each dataset at a significance threshold of p<0.05. Next, all identified datasets
247 including the discovery and the confirmation extremal datasets were combined and only the
248 common genes were considered. ML was re-performed on 80% of the common genes among
249 the entire datasets while the performance was evaluated in the 20% holdout.

# 250 2.10. Recruitment of MDD patients from the Kazakhstan population for
251 biomarker assessment

252 The identified MDD biomarker was further assessed in an independent cohort with
253 particular genetic background from the Kazakhstan population. Saliva samples of 12 well-
254 characterized MDD patients and 8 HCs were collected at Al-Farabi Kazakh National
255 University, Kazakhstan. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Al-Farabi
256 Kazakh National University (IRB-A083 and IRB-A267). Inclusion criteria were diagnosed
257 with MDD by a psychiatrist for the first time (based on ICD-10) and no medication/ treatment.
258 Exclusion criteria were volunteers with substance or alcohol abuse and taking antipsychotic
259 medications/treatment. All participants after signing the consent form completed the Inventory
260 of Depressive Symptomatology (IDS) [35]. Subjects who showed IDS scores>20 were
261 interviewed by a psychiatrist for diagnosis. Saliva samples were collected from all.
