# 3. Results

# 3.1. Identification of the differentially expressed genes among MDD patients

The differential transcriptomic profile across MDD patients and HCs were analyzed using a bioinformatics pipeline, and the result of data distribution before after quality control, normalization and filtration of the GSE98793 dataset are shown in Figure 1A. Comparing MDD patients to HCs, a total of 3294 and 2088 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified, respectively, in batch 1 and batch 2 subgroups. The total of DEGs was merged into a common expression matrix based on the gene names to include 1446 common genes between the 106 MDD patients and 51 HCs.

# 3.2. Enrichment analyses of the DEGs in MDD patients

To explore the molecular signals and biological processes of the identified DEGs in MDD patients compared to HCs, GSEA was performed. It consists of a computational method that identifies whether a priori set of genes shows statistically significant and coherent differences between groups around several thousand pre-defined datasets including complex molecular mechanisms, immunologic signatures, biological processes, molecular activities and cellular structures. The enrichment analysis showed a total of 3262 and 2186 significant pathways (p<0.05) were identified in batch 1 and batch 2, respectively. The GSEA revealed that the DEGs in MDD patients are mainly enriched in pathways related to immune response, immune effector processes, signaling by cytokines, inflammatory response, proinflammatory and profib mediators, cellular responses to stimuli, neurodegeneration pathways, cerebellum pathway, and neuroactive ligand-receptor interactions. The details of all significant pathways are listed in Supplementary Table 2.

# 3.3. The most informative MDD-related genes from the activated cellular pathways

To identify the core genes that potentially determine the significant enrichment of the corresponding identified genes, the leading-edge analysis was also performed for all identified significant genes in each subgroup of MDD and HCs. Subsequently, the gene frequency was calculated based on the occurrence of a gene amongst all enriched leading-edge core genes from the significant over-represented gene sets in each batch. Overall, 353 genes resulted based on the merged data of MDD patients compared to HCs (Supplementary Table 3). These identified genes could be the most informative genes that play or control biological roles among the over-enriched pathways in MDD compared to HCs. The functional annotation of these top leading genes in MDD patients further confirms that the immune response-related pathways, the inflammatory responses, and metabolic pathways are the main significantly enriched in MDD compared to HCs (Figure 2).
