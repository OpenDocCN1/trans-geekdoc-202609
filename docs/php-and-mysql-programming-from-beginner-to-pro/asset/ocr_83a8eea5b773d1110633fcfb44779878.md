# High availability and disaster recovery in MySQL

Maintaining continuous operation and swift recovery capabilities in MySQL databases is essential for critical application infrastructures, where prolonged downtimes can lead to significant operational disruptions. High availability (HA) focuses on reducing system downtimes, ensuring that database services remain accessible during minor failures or maintenance activities. Conversely, disaster recovery (DR) strategies are designed to quickly restore system functions after major disruptions, such as significant hardware failures or natural disasters, minimizing data loss and downtime.

# High Availability Mechanisms for MySQL

Achieving high availability in MySQL involves deploying architectures and solutions that eliminate single failure points, thereby ensuring persistent data accessibility.

+   * Master-Slave Replication: Establishing master-slave replication forms the basis of HA by replicating data from a primary MySQL server (the master) to secondary servers (slaves). This setup enables the distribution of read queries among slave servers, reducing the master's load and providing a fallback option in case the master fails.
