Typically, the nostrils have the lowest brightness values in the approximate area of the nose. If the set of all pixels in the left nostril region is X which contains N (X) pixel points. The centroid of the left nostril region can be expressed as $\left( x_{l}, y_{l} \right)$, where $x_{l} = \left( \frac{\sum_{x \in X} x}{N(X)} \right)$, $y_{l} = \left( \frac{\sum_{y \in X} y}{N(X)} \right)$. Similarly, the centroid of the right nostril region is $\left( x_{r}, y_{r} \right)$. (3)

If the lateral distance between the left and right nostril points is d, it is generally believed that the tip of the nose is located at d/2 above the middle perpendicular of the two nostrils. Let the midpoint of the left and right nostril points be $\left( x_{0}, y_{0} \right)$, where $x_{0} = \left( x_{l} + x_{r} \right) / 2$, $y_{0} = \left( y_{l} + y_{r} \right) / 2$ and then $d = x_{r} - x_{l}$. The coordinates of the center of the nostril are $\left( x_{m}, y_{m} \right)$, where $x_{m} = x_{0}$, $y_{m} = d / 2$. (4)

## 2.2. Geometric Triangular Head Pose Estimation Model

Using Kinect combined with geometric triangles to track the three degrees of freedom parameters of the head direction around the X, Y, and Z axes, the mathematical models are established, respectively. As shown in Figure 5, changes in the rotation angle in the state of head posture deflection such as pitch, yaw, and roll are converted into triangular deformations, which are represented by Pitch, Yaw, and Roll, respectively.

Figure 5. Characteristic triangle model. (a) Face triangle model; (b) feature triangle.

During head position tracking, the geometric triangle is formed by the center of the pupil, and the tip of the nose changes with the doctor’s head and eye movements and presents certain geometric characteristics. According to the geometric features of the face, when the head is facing the camera, the Euler angle is defined as 0, and the left and right eyes and nose form an isosceles triangle AABC. When the head is deflected, the triangle also undergoes a corresponding position shift as the geometric position of the feature points changes, resulting in deformation. By analyzing the geometric features of △ABC, you can determine the current head posture.

According to the coordinates of three characteristic points, namely nose tip coordinates $A(x_1,y_1)$, left eye coordinates $B(x_2,y_2)$, and right eye coordinates $C(x_3,y_3)$, the three side length of △ABC can be obtained as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a & = \sqrt{\left(x_3 - x_2\right)^2 + \left(y_3 - y_2\right)^2} && (3) \\
b & = \sqrt{\left(x_3 - x_1\right)^2 + \left(y_3 - y_1\right)^2} && (4) \\
c & = \sqrt{\left(x_2 - x_1\right)^2 + \left(y_2 - y_1\right)^2} && (5)
\end{aligned}
$$

The three side length $a, b, c$ correspond to $\angle A, \angle B$, and $\angle C$, and their angular sizes are expressed as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\angle A & = \pi - \angle B - \angle C && (6) \\
\angle B & = \arccos\left(\frac{1}{2c} \cdot \left(\frac{1}{a}(c^2 - b^2) + a\right)\right) && (7) \\
\angle C & = \arccos\left(\frac{1}{2b} \cdot \left(a - \frac{1}{a}(c^2 - b^2)\right)\right) && (8)
\end{aligned}
$$

The height corresponding to the side length a is expressed as follows:

$$
h = \frac{2\sqrt{p(p-a)(p-b)(p-c)}}{a} \tag{9}
$$

where, $p = \frac{a+b+c}{2}$. Based on the obtained pixel coordinates of the eyes and nose tip, a facial feature triangle is constructed, and a mathematical model of head posture is established and derived. The doctor’s head posture parameters are estimated based on the changes in the position of the triangle.

In order to accurately study the technology of doctors' head angle of view and orientation tracking, we establish triangular models for the rotation of the head around different coordinate axes to determine the head and angle of view posture.

### (1) Head posture positioning relative to the X-axis (pitch)

The pitch angle is the solution corresponding to the rotation of the doctor's head about the X-axis. When the doctor raises his head or nods, the pitch angle changes to $\alpha$, and for the frontal image in the triangle model, the side length $a$ of the feature triangle changes, but the corresponding height $h$ does not change. Based on prior knowledge of the distribution of facial organs, the positional relationship between the nose and the eyes can be obtained as follows:

$$h' = 0.8a \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad (10)$$

where $h'$ is the change in the height of the triangle when the doctor's head changes, and $a$ is the distance between the eyes after turning the head.

$$a = \arccos\frac{2\sqrt{(p-a)(p-b)(p-c)}}{0.8a^2} \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad (11)$$

When the pitch angle of the doctor's head changes, the angle between the two triangles corresponding to the change will also change. When the doctor raises his head, the corresponding angle $a$ is a negative number; When the doctor lowers his head, the corresponding angle $a$ is positive.

### (2) Head posture positioning relative to the Y-axis (yaw)

The solution of the yaw angle corresponds to the rotation of the doctor's head around the Y-axis. When the doctor's head turns to the left or right, the pitch angle changes marked as $\Psi$, and the shape of the feature triangle changes.

If $y_2 > y_3$, the head is deflected to the left, and the deflection angle $\Psi$ is expressed as follows:

$$\Psi = \arccos(\frac{y_2 - y_1}{c}) - \frac{1}{2} \angle C \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad (12)$$

If $y_2 < y_3$, the head is deflected to the right, and the deflection angle $\Psi$ is expressed as follows:

$$\Psi = \arccos(\frac{y_3 - y_1}{c}) - \frac{1}{2} \angle C \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad (13)$$

When the doctor's head is tilted to the left, the corresponding angle $\Psi$ is positive; When the doctor's head is tilted to the right, the corresponding angle $\Psi$ is negative.

### (3) Head posture positioning relative to the Z-axis (roll)

When the doctor turns his head left or right, the change of the roll angle is set to $\varphi$ as follows:

$$\varphi = \arccos\frac{a}{a'} \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad (14)$$

where $a$ is the distance between the pupils after turning the head, and $a'$ is the distance between the pupils in the forward direction. During head turning analysis, the side length $a$ of the feature triangle changes, but the corresponding height does not change.

$$a' = \frac{h}{0.8} \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad (15)$$

The solution for obtaining the final roll angle is obtained as follows:

$$\varphi = \arccos\frac{0.8a}{h} \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad (16)$$
