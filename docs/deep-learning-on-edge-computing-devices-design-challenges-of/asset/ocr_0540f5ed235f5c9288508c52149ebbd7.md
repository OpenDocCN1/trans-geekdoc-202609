embedding operation as

```
vec(W * X) \doteq \Phi x = y, (9.3)
```

where * is the convolution operation, vec(·) is the vectorization operation, \(\Phi \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}\) is the associated square embedding matrix determined by the kernel matrix **W**, and **y** is the vectorized output of the convolution operation.

Since the kernel matrices in the convolutional neural network model are learned from training samples, the sensing matrix \(\Phi\) is determined by iterative training. Define the column vector \(\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^{2Q+3}\) based on the kernel matrix **W** as \(\mathbf{w}^T = [w_1, w_2, w_3, \mathbf{0}, w_4, w_5, w_6, \mathbf{0}, w_7, w_8, w_9]\), where \(w_1, \dots, w_9\) are the nine elements of the kernel matrix **W**. The embedding matrix associated with the kernel can be written as

```
\(\Phi = \left( \begin{array}{ccccc} \mathbf{w}^T & \mathbf{0} & \cdots & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} \\ 0 & \mathbf{w}^T & \cdots & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} & \cdots & \mathbf{w}^T & 0 \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} & \cdots & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{w}^T \end{array} \right)_{N \times N}, (9.4)\)
```

where **0** and 0 are the row vector of zeros and the scalar zero. The embedding matrix \(\Phi\) is a sparse matrix with 3 × 3 nonzero elements in each row.

It is worth noting that Eqs. (9.3) and (9.1) have the same linear form, but different from the standard compressive sensing, the convolution operation of the convolutional neural network does not reduce the dimension of the data. To compress the image to dimension \(M (M \ll N)\), a *uniform-pooling layer* is inserted after the compressive convolutional layer for dimension reduction. In practice, a single feature map is selected for subsampling, and \(M \times N\) elements are uniformly extracted in a row-by-row fashion with equal probability.
