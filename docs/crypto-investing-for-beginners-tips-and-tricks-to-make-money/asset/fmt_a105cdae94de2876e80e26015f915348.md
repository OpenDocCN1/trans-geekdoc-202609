# Chapter 1 What Is Cryptocurrency?

Unless you are in the know, you are probably wondering what a cryptocurrency is. Sure, you've heard of Bitcoin – who hasn't? You'd likely heard of Ethereum, Litecoin, Ripple, and others but aren't sure what they are.

In simple terms, cryptocurrency is digital money. Traditional currency, like the Euro or U.S dollar, can be digitally transferred, but that's completely different from digital money. Right now, you can use Bitcoin to pay for some goods and services in the same way you use traditional money to make electronic payments. In time, cryptocurrencies will become more mainstream, and more places will accept them.

Cryptocurrency really differs from traditional cash in the technology it is built on. Most people are only concerned with the cash they have, not the technology behind it, but traditional systems have more than their fair share of problems. These include:

*   Wire transfers, credit cards, and other payment systems are outdated
*   Intermediaries cause transactions to be slow and expensive
*   Global equality in the financial world is fast-growing
*   3 billion people do not have access to financial services

The idea behind cryptocurrency is to solve these problems and many more.

## Cryptocurrencies

The money you currently use is backed by the government and held in banks. You need an ATM or internet banking to transfer money to others or get some for yourself. Cryptocurrencies aim to do away with all this by relying on blockchain technology. Blockchain is decentralized, so there are no intermediaries, and no one person is in control. Instead, a network of computers confirms every transaction but more about how the blockchain works in a later chapter. First, let's learn more about cryptocurrencies.

## What Is Money?

For money to be considered valuable, it needs to demonstrate the following:

*   Enough people have money
*   It must be accepted as a payment form
*   It must be trusted by society as valuable now and will remain so

In early times, actual coins and banknotes didn't exist, and people used to trade or barter items. However, when real money came about – those banknotes and coins, even credit cards we rely on today – it brought about real changes in the trust model. Transaction ease is also a major factor, and one of the primary reasons for money being invented was the logistical difficulty in transferring gold bars being countries. Credit cards followed, but these are also based around government-backed currency, known as fiat money. The more interconnected the world becomes the more concern there is about authorities in control and whether they have our interests in mind. Because of that, cryptocurrencies are set to provide a viable alternative.

## A Brief History of Cryptocurrency

We all know that Bitcoin was the first cryptocurrency, created after Satoshi Nakamoto, who remains anonymous to this day, released a white paper in 2008 detailing a "peer-to-peer version of electronic money." Since then, many other cryptocurrencies have been created, some successful, some not. Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies are created in one way only – mining. However, rather than the conventional methods of mining precious metals, ores, and gems, cryptocurrency mining is done by powerful computers solving mathematical riddles. We'll talk in more depth about mining later in the book. In 2011, some people began to notice that Bitcoin was flawed. That led to the creation of altcoins to make Bitcoin's design better – they were faster, more secure, and anonymous, among other things. Litecoin was the first altcoin, but many thousands more have been created since then, and right now, we have thousands to choose from. Later, I'll introduce you to some of the most popular.

## Cryptocurrency Benefits

These are some of the solutions cryptocurrencies aim to solve by being decentralized:

### 1. Easier Transactions

Cryptocurrency transactions are cheaper, easier, and more secure than traditional ones. You can use a phone app, crypto, or exchange wallet to send and receive crypto, and you can even buy some at Bitcoin ATMs, which can then be transferred to your phone app or crypto wallet without needing a bank account.

### 2. More Secure

Cryptocurrencies are heavily based on blockchain and cryptographic technology, ensuring they are more secure. The security is based largely on hash rate – the higher it is, the more computer power is needed for the network to be compromised. Bitcoin has the highest hash rate of all cryptocurrencies. The only real way to lose access to your stored cryptocurrency is to lose your private key, or it is stolen – more about keys later.
