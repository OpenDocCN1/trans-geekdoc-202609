
# Chapter 7: The Different Cryptocurrencies

In 2009, the first digital currency, Bitcoin, was released to much fanfare. At the time, it had little to no competition and reigned supreme, but that would soon change. By 2011, other cryptocurrencies began emerging as the adoption of the underlying technology, the blockchain, began to rise. Today, we have thousands of cryptocurrencies to choose from, and each provides its own unique functions and features. However, most of them are built on the same principles as Bitcoin:

*   No cryptocurrency is regulated, issued, or backed by a bank, government, or any other central authority.
*   They are all created on the blockchain and P2P review.
*   Many crypto coins are encrypted with cryptography.
*   Cryptocurrencies are considered assets and are typically stored in a crypto wallet which can be hardware or software. These allow the crypto owners to manage their coins and trade with them.

As of now, there are upwards of 6-10,000 different coins you can trade in with a total market cap of $2 trillion.

## The Different Types of Crypto

Cryptocurrencies are typically split into two categories:

*   **Coins**, such as Bitcoin and altcoins.
*   **Tokens**, programmable assets on the platform's blockchain.

The three words - crypto, tokens, coins - are used interchangeably, but you should understand the differences between the three so you understand cryptocurrency. Any blockchain currency that isn't Bitcoin is an altcoin - more about those shortly.

### Coins vs. Tokens

Both tokens and coins are forms of cryptocurrency, but they function differently. A crypto coin is a form of currency built into its blockchain. For example, `BTC` (Bitcoin) is built on the Bitcoin blockchain, and `ETH` (Ethereum) is built on the Ethereum blockchain. Tokens are also built on blockchains, but they are not currency. They are programmable assets that allow users to create smart contracts and execute them. Smart contracts are used to establish who owns specific assets not on the blockchain network. Tokens are also used to represent a value of some sort, such as money, electricity, digital assets, etc., and can be received and sent. As an example, the `BAT` (Basic Attention Token) is an Ethereum token used in digital advertising.

### Altcoins

Altcoin is the short form of "alternative to Bitcoin" and is any digital coin other than Bitcoin. Most were created to improve on some parts of Bitcoin, and some examples include Ethereum, Litecoin, USD Coin, Namecoin, and Peercoin. Bitcoin is hard-capped, which means only a set number of coins will ever be in circulation, and other cryptocurrencies are the same. This ensures demand and a rise in perceived value. Most of today's altcoins share the basic framework Bitcoin was built on, along with some of Bitcoin's characteristics. However, they all provide something different. Some do the same as Bitcoin, producing transaction blocks and validating them, but they have a different process to do it. Others offer brand-new features, such as smart contracts.

### Tokens

Typically, tokens are created and distributed via an `ICO` (Initial Coin Offering), which is similar to a traditional stock offering. Tokens may be represented in the following ways:

*   As value tokens, such as Bitcoins.
*   As security tokens, which are much like stocks.
*   As utility tokens, which are for specific use-cases.

All tokens represent a value of some description, but they are not valuable in themselves. In the same way, the paper dollar you have in your wallet may not be worth what it says it is. However, a token can be used to carry out transactions in different things. The primary way a token is different from a coin is that tokens are constructed on blockchains that already exist for digital coins, such as Ethereum or Bitcoin.

## Top 12 Common Cryptocurrencies

Now onto some of the more popular cryptocurrencies by market cap. Market cap is one of the best ways of identifying the higher valued cryptocurrencies in an over-packed market.

> **NOTE** - prices were correct at the time of writing but are subject to change regularly.

### 1. Bitcoin (BTC)

*   **Current Price** - $39,000
*   **Market Cap** - $741 billion

As the forerunner of all the cryptocurrencies we know today, Bitcoin is still the one that everyone talks about when they mention digital currency. It came to life in 2009, and since then, its journey has been one of sharp rises and steep falls. In 2017, it suddenly became even more popular when people started to pay attention and invest in it.

### 2. Ethereum (ETH)

*   **Current Price** - $2584
*   **Market Cap** - $310 billion

Ethereum is a cryptocurrency platform and is the next most popular name in the cryptocurrency world. The currency used on the platform is `ETH` (Ether), and with it, you do different functions. However, what makes Ethereum so popular is the smart contract side of it.

### 3. Tether (USDT)

*   **Current Price** - $1
*   **Market Cap** - $80 billion

Tether is classed as a stablecoin, and its price will always be $1 per coin. All stablecoins are tied to an asset's value and, for Tether, that asset is the U.S. dollar. Tether tends to be a medium that traders use when moving between cryptocurrencies; instead of changing one crypto back into dollars before purchasing another, they use the Tether stablecoin. However, there are some concerns that Tether doesn't have safe backing from reserved dollars but, instead, seems to use short-term unsecured debt.

### 4. Binance Coin (BNB)

*   **Current Price** - $368
*   **Market Cap** - $62 billion

`BNB` is Binance's crypto coin, and it comes from one of the biggest crypto exchanges worldwide. Originally, it was created as a token and was meant to be used as a form of payment for discounted trades, but it can now be used to purchase services and goods and make payments.

### 5. USD Coin (USDC)

*   **Current Price** - $1
*   **Market Cap** - $52 billion

Another stablecoin, `USDC`, is pegged to the U.S. dollar, meaning its value should remain stable. Its founders say the coin has fully reserved assets backing it, those with an equivalent fair value, stored in regulated institutions within the U.S.

### 6. XRP (XRP)

*   **Current Price** - $0.73
*   **Market Cap** - $34 billion

Created in 2012, XRP used to be called Ripple. It provides a payment method in multiple real-world currencies and is popular in making cross-border payments. Payments are facilitated via a trustless mechanism.

### 7. Terra (LUNA)

*   **Current Price** - $102
*   **Market Cap** - $34 billion

The Terra platform uses the `LUNA` currency to backstop stablecoins that are based on the Euro, dollar, and other fiat currencies. Terra ensures that the stablecoin price is stable and offers support for smart contracts.

### 8. Solana (SOL)

*   **Current Price** - $82
*   **Market Cap** - $26 billion

Solana is relatively new, only launching in 2020. Its biggest features are its transaction speeds and its web-scale platform being so robust. The `SOL` is hard-capped at 480 million coins.

### 9. Cardano (ADA)

*   **Current Price** - $0.80
*   **Market Cap** - $25 billion

The Cardano platform uses the `ADA` cryptocurrency and was founded by Charles Hoskinson, one of Ethereum's co-founders. Cardano uses smart contracts to help manage digital identities.

### 10. Avalanche (AVAX)

*   **Current Price** - $72
*   **Market Cap** - $19 billion

Avalanche is one of the fastest and is a low-cost smart contract-based platform. Its focus is primarily on building `DApps` and facilitating developers in building their own custom blockchains. Transactions can be processed in the platform token, `AVAX`.

### 11. Binance USD (BUSD)

*   **Current Price** - $1
*   **Market Cap** - $18 billion

Another stablecoin backed by the US dollar, Binance USD comes from a partnership between the Binance exchange and Paxos. Launched in 2019, `BUSD` is fully regulated by the `NYDFS` (New York Department of Financial Services), and it runs on Ethereum's blockchain.
