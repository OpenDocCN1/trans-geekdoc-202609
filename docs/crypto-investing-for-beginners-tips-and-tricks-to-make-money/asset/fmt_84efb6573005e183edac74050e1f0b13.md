
# IRS Notice 2014-2021 and Crypto Mining Taxation

In the first case, Notice 2014-2021 has been issued by the IRS to address the tax issues surrounding crypto mining. According to the notice, miners must declare an amount of gross income for each crypto reward they get; that amount must be a fair indication of the coin or token value at the time of receipt. If the mining is considered part of a business, the tokens or coins are treated as self-employment income and are subject to the relevant taxes. Similarly, if the miner works for someone else, their crypto payments are seen as wages and are subject to income tax laws, withholding unemployment taxes, Medicare, and social security.

## Is Mining Legal?

Right now, few laws govern cryptos, so, in most countries, it is not clear whether mining is legal or not. Under the FinCEN (Financial Crimes Enforcement Network), miners are classed as "money transmitters," meaning the laws governing that activity may apply to them. For example, cryptocurrency mining is seen as a business in Israel and comes under corporate income tax laws. Elsewhere, there is a lot of regulatory uncertainty, although some countries, such as the USA and Canada, appear to be okay with it. However, other than a few jurisdictions that have made crypto-related activities illegal, few countries stop people from mining.

## Is Cryptocurrency Mining Sustainable?

Most cryptocurrency miners have plenty of curiosity and a desire to learn, two things that are a must for anyone thinking of joining in. New technologies are constantly emerging, and the mining arena is always changing. The miners who get the best rewards are those who study and optimize their methods and strategies to improve continually. However, some advocates for climate change have voiced concerns about the level of fossil fuels crypto mining burns through. Concerns like these have led some communities, like Ethereum, to move from a Proof-of-Work framework to something more sustainable, such as Proof-of-Stake.

# Chapter 9: Strategies for Short-Term Trading

Investing in cryptocurrencies is a high-risk endeavor, perhaps more so than traditional trading. Cryptos are digital assets, a new asset class with a short history, and anyone thinking of investing must be aware that their investments can disappear quickly.

As an investor, you should take time to familiarize yourself with the rules and regulations surrounding tax and cryptocurrency profits. If you choose the most popular short-term strategy, day-trading, crypto sales are subject to short-term capital gains tax. Before we look at some strategies, here are some of the pros and cons of trading in cryptocurrency:

## Pros

*   If you have access to a crypto exchange, you can freely trade in cryptocurrency. Besides verifying who you are and putting money in your account, short-term traders have few barriers to their market access. If you want to own Bitcoin but can't afford the current price, you can buy and sell fractions of it.
*   Crypto markets work 24/7, 365 days a year. There are no limits to buying and selling, and you can trade whenever you want to.
*   Cryptos and blockchain technology are both decentralized, ensuring anonymity and no intermediary fees, keeping the cost of transactions low.

## Cons

*   The ease with which you can start trading means it is also easy for newbies to lose big money as easily as they can gain big profits.
*   The market being open 24/7 isn't always a good thing. It means there are few trading patterns to follow, especially if you choose day-trading. You may find yourself watching things for much longer than you would in the traditional markets to ensure you execute your trade at the right time. Trends are not so easy to spot, and a quick session can turn into an all-nighter.
*   Although FinCEN and other government watchdogs are gradually increasing anti-money laundering and KYC requirements, the crypto space is largely unregulated. That lack of regulation means that traders and exchanges have no protection from hackers and scammers. If your chosen exchange is subject to an attack, unless they have insurance (highly unlikely), your funds are likely gone for good.
*   You are also at risk of wash-sales or pump-and-dump schemes. These happen when people pump the price of a cryptocurrency up artificially in a bid to pull in traders. When the traders earn their profits, they dump the crypto, and the price falls fast.

## Let's Look at Some of the Best Strategies to Get You Started in Short-Term Trading

### Choose Your Platform

While it may be tempting to act like a kid in a sweet shop where crypto trading is concerned, you must take your time and choose the right platform to trade on. Remember that if you are based in the USA, you are limited to domestically regulated platforms. Several factors will determine the platform you choose - asset and exchange liquidity, fees, etc. - you also need to consider volatility because crypt values can spiral up and down without warning. If you are in it for a quick profit, you must be in a position to move fast when needed. What doesn't help is that there are so many differences between the crypto exchanges, so here are the most important things you need to consider:

*   **How Long the Exchange Has Been Active** - the longer it has operated, the more chance it has of being reliable, stable, and credible.
*   **The Cryptos Supported** - this is one of the most valuable tools to a trader; there is little point in joining an exchange only to find that you have no interest in the supported cryptos or it only offers limited support.
*   **The Fees** - these are charged for each trade and are how the exchange earns their money. The fee structures between exchanges will vary - some don't charge any fees for trades of more than $10,000, for example, as an incentive for traders interested in large-scale trades.
*   **Their Deposit Methods** - you need to get money into your exchange account, so the payment methods offered are important. Some will accept wires and bank transfers, but only a few will allow you to fund your account with a credit card, gift card, or PayPal.
*   **Reviews** - look on the crypt communities to find reviews and feedback on exchanges. Most users on these forums will provide honest feedback, be it negative or positive.
*   **Exchange Liquidity** - this covers the ease and speed with which an exchange converts assets with no price effect. The best exchanges have high liquidity, meaning they can buy and sell immediately with no discernible difference between the bid and asking price.

### Choose Your Investments

The exchange is just the first step. Next, you need to decide what you want to invest in. If you opt for day-trading, keep volatility in mind - where a market condition could put buy-and-hold (long-term) investors off, it could be just what a day trader needs to grab a decent profit. New traders are advised to invest in the biggest coins or tokens, but this doesn't always pan out. Critically, you need to research what's available against how much you want to invest and determine if it's worth the risk. You can only do that through careful research of a specific coin or token, how it has behaved in the past and what it's doing now.
