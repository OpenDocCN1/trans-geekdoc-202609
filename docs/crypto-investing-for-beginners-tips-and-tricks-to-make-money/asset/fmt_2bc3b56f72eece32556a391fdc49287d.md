
# Proof-Of-Work

Miners who want their transactions and block added to the blockchain must provide an answer to a specific puzzle, but it isn't easy to provide, which is why so much computing power is needed. However, it is easy for the rest of the network to verify the correct answer in a process known as proof-of-work.

For example, if you manage to guess a padlock's combination, it is proof of the challenge. It isn't easy to start with because you need to go through tons of combinations, but verification is easy - simply enter the code, and if the padlock unlocks, it's the correct one. The same applies to cryptocurrency mining. The miner who solves the problem before any of the others is rewarded. This is a way of incentivizing the miner to continue and providing healthy competition between them.

Some cryptocurrencies use proof-of-work to ensure that the network hasn't been manipulated. However, this concept does have downsides where blockchain technology is concerned. One of the biggest is the sheer amount of electricity and computing power needed to make what amounts to random guesses. Many new cryptocurrencies have shunned proof-of-work in favor of a different concept called proof-of-stake.

# Proof-Of-Stake

The proof-of-stake concept is different from proof-of-work because you must prove ownership of a specific stake or amount of money. That means those with the most amounts of crypto have the most mining power, thus getting rid of the requirement for vast amounts of computing power. It is easy to prove the calculations in this concept, so you have ownership of a percentage of all available cryptos.

This system also differs in that block rewards are not offered. Instead, the "winning" miner is given the transaction fees. This is how the system is many times more effective than proof-of-work.

However, proof-of-stake also has its downsides. You could say that the system rewards those who hoard coins. Each miner can only mine the transaction percentage related to their cryptocurrency stake. For example, if a proof-of-stake miner owns 20% of the total available cryptocurrency, they can mine 10% of the network blocks. This limitation provides nodes with an excellent reason to save rather than spend their coins. It could also lead to the rich continuing to get rich because they can mine a bigger percentage of the network blocks.

# Proof-Of-Importance

The proof-of-importance concept first came about with the NEM platform in support of its cryptocurrency, XEM. It is similar to proof-of-stake in that nodes are only allowed to mine if they hold a specific amount of cryptocurrency. The network then scores all those nodes who meet the requirements, creating a block with a similar proportion to the score.

However, nodes don't score higher just because they have more cryptocurrency. Instead, the score considers all sorts of variables to solve the problem of hoarding seen with proof-of-stake.

The NEM community uses harvesting, which Investopedia defines as "Instead of each miner contributing its mining power in a cumulative manner to a computing node, a harvesting participant simply links his account to an existing supernode and uses that account's computing power to complete blocks on his behalf."

# Putting It All Together

To summarize, this is how cryptocurrency works:

1.  When you use cryptocurrency to purchase something, your network and wallet will check your transactions history to ensure you have sufficient crypto to make the purchase. This requires your public and private keys, which we'll discuss in chapter 6 when we talk about wallets.
2.  The transaction gets encrypted, sent to the network, and added to a queue where it waits to go on the distributed ledger.
3.  The mining process adds the transaction to the ledger. The addresses for the sender and receiver are hash values or IDs tied to a wallet. These are not linked to any particular user ID, allowing the user to remain anonymous.
4.  In proof-of-work cryptocurrencies, a puzzle must be solved by the network miners before the transaction can go on the blockchain. In proof-of-stake, each node's mining power is directly related to the number of coins they hold, rather than actual computing power. This solves the issues raised with Bitcoin, where vast amounts of electricity and expensive computers were required to guess random numbers. Proof-of-information uses different variables to choose which nodes have mining power, which solves the hoarding issue raised by proof-of-stake.

# More Important Cryptocurrency Concepts

Although I will be going into more detail in the next couple of chapters about wallets, exchanges, brokers, and some of the different cryptocurrencies, I will cover some more concepts here that you will likely come across. Some other things ensure cryptocurrency is different from fiat currency.

## Adaptive Scaling

This is one of the major reasons people choose to invest in cryptocurrency, and it means that, as time passes, it gets more difficult to mine a cryptocurrency. It also ensures cryptos are good on small scales and large. This is why cryptocurrencies have a cap on supply, thus creating scarcity, and, as more coins enter public circulation, the mining reward is reduced too. Adaptive scaling ensures that mining difficulty rises and falls based on coin popularity and the blockchain, thus ensuring cryptocurrencies enjoy market longevity.

## Decentralization

The biggest advantage to the blockchain is that the technology is decentralized, meaning no one organization or person controls it. Some say that Ripple, and other similar cryptocurrencies, are not really classed as decentralized, and their reasoning is that they don't follow the mining protocol Bitcoin uses. Ripple doesn't have any miners, ensuring the transaction system is faster and more reliable by using a centralized blockchain. The Ripple developers chose this route because it wants the option of working with some of the larger banks, combining the advantages of blockchain technology and cryptocurrency with those of fiat currency.

There is some debate over whether Ripple and any other cryptocurrency that isn't mined is considered a real cryptocurrency, but that doesn't stop you from investing them, and they can prove to be quite lucrative.

## Harvesting

Harvesting is a method used in place of mining to maintain the network's integrity. The NEM platform designed it to generate XEM, which is their cryptocurrency. Harvesting works in this way:

*   When a transaction is carried out, the first device that sees the transaction and verifies it notifies all nearby nodes of it. This creates an information cascade.
*   This process is how a block is generated. When a miner with 10,000 or more XEM generates a NEM block, they are paid by way of the block's transactions fees.
