
# Things to Consider Before Starting

Up to this point, you've learned what cryptocurrency is and how it works. We've discussed different types of cryptos, looked at how the blockchain works, and even looked at some long and short-term strategies you can use. In the last chapter, we looked at taxes, so you should now know if you are ready to go ahead with investing in crypto. Before you take that final step, I'm giving you a list of things you should consider before you go all-in.

## Don't Get Over-Excited

Starting a new adventure is exciting, no matter what it is, and in some cases, getting a head start can put you ahead of everyone else. However, cryptocurrency investing is no different from any other type of investment. In fact, perhaps more so, it requires strict risk management, discipline, and more than a little patience. Crypto investing should never be seen as a scheme to get rich quick - sure, you've sat with your jaw dropped, watching Bitcoin rise to massive prices in the past, but whether that happens again is a different matter. The crypto bubble has burst, and the chances of those heady heights being reached again are low. Investors know more than they did back in 2017, and their investment decisions are taken with a clearer head and more calculation - so should yours be.

## Know Your Risk Tolerance

Before you dive in, ask yourself these questions:

*   Is cryptocurrency investing the right move for you?
*   How much money should you invest?
*   How much can you afford to lose?
*   Can you stomach the highly volatile nature of the crypto market?
*   Are you patient enough to wait out the dips?

The first step in any investment is knowing your risk tolerance, and it revolves around your ability to handle risk and your willingness to take that risk. Risk tolerance is all about knowing what level of risk you are comfortable with. Given that the crypto market is volatile, not least because of the level of speculation involved, risk tolerance is one of the most important factors in determining your strategies. Let's face it, as we've seen, crypto values can be halved overnight, you could lose your private key, hackers can steal deposits from exchanges, and so on. However, your tolerance should not be a set percentage of the total amount you invest in the market; instead, it must be a percentage of the total amount of money invested in your portfolio. In short, it is how much you can afford to lose without it affecting your day-to-day life and finances.

## Keep Your Crypto Wallet Protected

When you begin investing in cryptocurrency, you should set up a crypto wallet to secure your purchases. There are many types available, and some exchanges even provide their own. However, these are not the most secure - there have been instances of hacking on these exchanges where significant amounts of crypto have been lost. We talked in an earlier chapter about the different types, so make sure you do your homework and choose a secure wallet to keep your crypto secure.

## Choose the Best Broker or Exchange

Brokers and exchanges are popular places to purchase and trade crypto, but they vary wildly. Some offer only a few cryptos, while others offer a much wider range. Some charge low or no transaction fees; others are much higher. Some are more secure than others, some allow you to top up your account using fiat currency, and others will only allow you to use digital money. We discussed brokers and exchanges earlier, so, as with the wallets, do your homework and make sure you get one that fits your needs and circumstances. Sometimes, you may be better having more than one, or an exchange and a broker, again depending on your requirements.

## Decide Whether You Want Long or Short-Term Investing

This will come down to several factors:

*   Your risk tolerance
*   Financial/investment goals
*   Financial situation now
*   How much time do you have?

For example, if you work a full-time job, you won't want to spend the rest of your time worrying about managing a portfolio of short-term investments. You won't have time for a start, and if you spend all your downtime focusing on that, you won't be fit for your main paying job. Long-term investing requires much less attention and is often less risky too. Most people opt for a balance between the two - a couple of short-term investments and several longer-term ones. And don't forget that long-term investments are also subject to lower rates of tax.

## Don't Dive in at the Deep End

In other words, start small. If you are just dipping your toes in and haven't yet drawn up a financial plan, there is little point in throwing a whole heap of money at the market - that's a surefire way to lose everything. Set a limit - perhaps a couple of hundred dollars - and stick to it. Start with just one crypto, too, rather than spreading your money too thin. Also, if this is your first time, don't use all your funds just for crypto; diversify and have a few stock, foreign exchange, or exchange-traded funds too. Eventually, you will get your portfolio just right and know exactly what you need to invest in and how much.

## Know Your Cause and Follow It

Almost every cryptocurrency available today is based on a blockchain application that solves a particular world or social problem. Whether it is providing banking to those who can't get it or are underbanked or helping prevent voter fraud, something will suit what you want. When you invest your money in specific crypto with an appealing cause, you are helping it achieve its goals. This ensures that crypto investing has some meaning to you, and it also helps you choose the cryptos you want to invest in from the many available.

## Consider Mining

Many cryptocurrencies rely on mining to go into circulation. Take Bitcoin, for example; without mining, there wouldn't be a Bitcoin currency or market. Mining requires network nodes to solve complex problems using electricity and powerful computers. If you successfully solve a problem, you earn a crypto reward. However, many people find it too expensive because of the investment required to set up a mining rig and the electricity required to run it. A lot will depend on the crypto's value. If your setup costs and electricity bill are less than what you could make on the crypto, it may be a consideration. Alternatively, you could join a mining pool.
