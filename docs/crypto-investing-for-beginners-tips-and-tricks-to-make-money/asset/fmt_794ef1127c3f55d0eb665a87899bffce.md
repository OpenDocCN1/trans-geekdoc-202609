
# Long-Term Investing

While trading can be short or medium term, investing tends to be mid to long-term. The idea behind it is to place your money into one or more assets and leave it to earn interest or sell it when the price is high. Long-term investors can plan for years ahead and grow their savings in this way, but the cryptocurrency market does not make long-term investing very easy.

With cryptos, long-term investing means investing for at least a year, and it requires far more in-depth knowledge than short-term trading. Because your investments should be held for a minimum of a year, getting a high return involves a certain amount of risk. Those risks are much higher in terms of cryptocurrency, partly because of an unfamiliar and fairly new market. However, as time passes, it becomes better-known, and some of the larger financial institutions are starting to take notice of it. Long-term crypto investment is an excellent opportunity for traditional traders who want some portfolio diversification.

## Long-Term Investing Explained

Long-term investment strategies typically seek profit by holding assets for a minimum of a year but usually more than three. Let's say Company A has a large stake in company B but doesn't have a majority of the voting shares. The stake purchase cost should go on the balance sheet, and the short-term investments should go on the profit/loss sheet. When long-term investments are sold, they would not normally have any relation to the profit/loss but, instead, classed as another type of comprehensive income.

Here's a better example; We have a pair of sisters - Belinda and Jane. Each of them has religiously saved $100 per month towards their long-term investments. After 20 years, they each have $24,000 to invest with an 8% annual return. Belinda began her investments when she was 36 and Jane when she was 446. By the time Belinda reaches 56, she will have $131, 613 and Jane will have $59,295. In this example, the return is double the original investment because Belinda started saving ten years before Jane. This shows how long-term investments can benefit you far more than short-term ones because of the interest accruing every year.

When you hold a long-term investment in the crypto market, it is called a `HODL`. This is an incorrectly spelled version of the word `HOLD` and became popular after the misspelling was posted on Bitcointalk in 2013.

## Why Would You Invest Long-Term in a Volatile Market?

Long-term investments are the most reliable method of maximizing a stable asset's returns. Many traders who don't know how to do proper technical analysis or have little to no knowledge of market behavior tend to rely on long-term investments. It does have a distinct advantage if not requiring daily, weekly, or monthly monitoring - it can simply be left until the time is right. Long-term investing is considered a good way of diversifying a portfolio using emerging markets, like cryptocurrencies, while having short-term investments in stocks and other traditional markets.

## Suitable Crypto Types for Long-Term Investment

You can invest in the crypto market in two ways:

*   `HODL`
*   Active Trading

When investors choose the first way, they typically purchase cryptos such as Bitcoin and hold them for a long period. They forget about the short-term market fluctuations and wait until the price is high enough to maximize profit. Not all cryptos are suitable for this type of investment, but some that got through the crypto crash in 2018 relatively unscathed are considered the best, given their apparent stability and acceptance by investors. Here are five that survived and are considered excellent choices for long-term investment:

*   Bitcoin
*   Ethereum
*   Litecoin
*   Monero
*   XRP (formerly Ripple)

Other cryptos worth long-term investments are those that yield dividends. When you invest for the long term, you don't just have to choose cryptos that will hopefully rise in price; you can also choose those that pay dividends and hopefully rise as well. Some of the best are:

*   Decred (DCR)
*   Komodo (KMD)
*   Navcoin (NAV)
*   Neblio (NEL)
*   Neo (NEO)
*   PIVX (PIVX)
*   Reddcoin (RDD)
*   VeChain (VET)

However, you must be aware that you need a systematic approach to investing in the crypto market, and you must have the right strategy to be successful.

## Investment Strategy

No matter what we want in life, having a plan is critical to ensure success. Long-term investments are no exception to this. Your strategies must include an investment plan covering the following points:

*   Your investment objectives
*   How long you plan to hold your investments
*   Tax implications
*   Entry/exit plan

Your strategy must be well-researched and effective, with a history showing it provides returns. With crypto investing, though, you need a little bit more, including some effective, exclusive strategies. One of the most effective is `HODL`, where investors only hold the asset. Another is called dollar-cost-averaging, where investors allocate the capital amount at regular intervals. This one is ideal for the smaller investors who struggle to get the capital they need in one hit. Success is entirely dependent on how you execute your trades and manage your risks. The maximum output requires the investors to provide a time for when they intend to pull the investment and their risk assessment. In short, a long-term investor must know the length of time they want the investment held and their own personal risk levels.

## How Much Does Long-Term Crypto Investment Cost?

Long-term investments always work out cheaper than short-term trading, but there are some high costs to consider before you dive into the crypto market. Here are some of the costs you need to consider in your plan:

*   **Expense Ratio** - this indicates the operating expenses, such as admin costs. To keep costs down, look for cryptos with low ratios.
*   **Financial Advisory Fees** - this is how much you pay to the professionals involved in the trade. Cryptocurrency advisory companies provide rankings or guidance that can help you find the right opportunity for investing long-term.
*   **Compound Interest** - if you use credit cards or loans to raise your capital, you must factor compound interest into your plan. If you stand to make less from the asset than the interest costs, it's not worth investing in it.
