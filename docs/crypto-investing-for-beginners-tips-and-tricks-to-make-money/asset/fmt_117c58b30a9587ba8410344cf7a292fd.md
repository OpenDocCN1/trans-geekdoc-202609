
# If you are new to investing

If you are new to investing, not just in cryptocurrency but in anything, you will likely find things somewhat overwhelming. It isn't always easy to find the right investment strategy while educating yourself about the crypto sector, so starting with foreign exchange or stocks might be best. Once you are comfortable with the ins and outs of investing, then you could consider moving into cryptocurrencies and growing your portfolio. Forex trading is often the easiest way to start—trading one currency for another, i.e., US dollar for Euro or Japanese Yen.

## 10. Make Sure You Have a Support Group

Most people sit at their computers or phones and trade by themselves, but that's quite lonely, especially when you don't know anyone else who is doing it. You don't have anyone to talk to, and things can get frustrating, especially when everything seems to be against you. Sometimes, you need someone else to tell you when to get out of the market. Technical analysis and thorough research can help you identify entry and exit points, but it can all be a bit too much when you are new to investing. Getting together with others in the same boat as you can be very helpful, and there are plenty of forums like Reddit and Telegram channels where you can connect with other investors and help each other out. You will also find a lot of expert traders on these channels, and they can often point you in the right direction. These forums are also useful for helping you find the right assets to invest in, finding out about exchanges, and others may even ask questions you never even considered—all useful stuff. In fact, I would recommend you join these forums before you start investing, just to get an idea of what help is available and get advice from those in the know.

# Chapter 15 Challenges and Opportunities

Our final chapter introduces you to some of the challenges and opportunities cryptocurrency investing can put your way. There is a good reason why I have put these together, and that is simply because an obstacle doesn't always have to be a bad thing—treat it right, and it could become an opportunity that leads to profit.

## 1. New Cryptocurrencies

The world's first cryptocurrency, Bitcoin, is now nearly 24 years old, but while it is still the most popular one, plenty of alternatives are available. Good or bad, new cryptos are appearing with more and more regularity these days, and that isn't going to change anytime soon. Granted, many of them will fade away to nothing and never be seen again, but a handful are likely to go somewhere, and a few may even get somewhere near Bitcoin and Ethereum. There could even be one right now, waiting to be developed, that could take the world by surprise and displace Bitcoin from the number one spot. You can see this as a challenge and an opportunity. Cryptocurrency investing requires you to keep an open mind and draw up your vision for the future. You need to be able to see through the new cryptos and choose those with promising potential, and you can't do that if you are stuck on only the big cryptos.

## 2. Find the Economic Data

This is seen as an industry-wide challenge because, although plenty of organizations are dedicated to crypto news, it isn't easy to find the real economic data driving the crypto market. The industry's economic system has not been developed, which means the media appears to have free reign in creating greed, fear, and hysteria out of nothing and have nothing solid to back up their claims. You want to steer clear of traps like these, so don't follow a single crypto news organization. Instead, follow several but take everything you read with a pinch of salt. And, as I said earlier in this book, if you hear or read something about a particular crypto, find several other sources that back it up before you take it as gospel.

## 3. Market Regulations

Right now, regulations surrounding cryptocurrency are very new. Although some countries have made large strides ahead, giving their residents good opportunities to get into the market, others haven't. Don't expect things to happen overnight; these things take time. Regulation (or a lack of it) is one of the biggest industry concerns, but it can work in your favor by allowing you to get in when the crypto is at a low price. As more crypto regulation comes in worldwide, those prices are likely to rise, especially when governments and banks accept it as an official financial instrument.

## 4. Hackers

While most people see hacking as a major challenge, you can see it as an opportunity too. Sure, if you get hacked and lose your crypto, it's a massive challenge, but you can take steps to protect yourself from this, i.e., choosing the right exchange, transferring your crypto off the exchange to a secure wallet, and so on. Hacking is unlikely to disappear anytime soon, but the impact on the crypto markets is typically minor, and the resulting negative effects on price are usually only temporary. That's when it becomes an opportunity, though. That's when you can step in and purchase crypto at the lower price, store it in your wallet and wait—the prices will go up again. However, the one thing you should never do is automatically invest in the company that got hacked. Make sure you do your research first and look at the circumstances that led to the hack. If an exchange or crypto was hacked in a way that cannot be reversed, there is little point in pouring money into it. However, it can create a knock-on effect on other cryptos for no specific reason other than sharing the industry. When one crypto falls, it tends to affect others too, so do your research before investing your money.

## 5. Crypto Bubbles

The crypto bubble may have burst in 2017, and the crypto markets are settling down and stabilizing. That doesn't mean there won't be another bubble, though, be it a current crypto or a new one. By doing solid and thorough research and analyzing different cryptos, you can learn how to identify where a bubble might occur, usually when a crypto experiences rapid growth in prices with no explicable reason to back it up—basically, market hype is doing its job. That is when you should do one of two things—sell any crypto you may hold at a higher price or steer clear until everything calms down again. That latter part is where the challenge lies, especially when FOMO kicks in.

## 6. Down Markets

One thing that can knock your self-confidence is when your crypto portfolio is down, and it can easily sour a positive mindset. However, please don't allow yourself to think that it's in any way your fault; the markets are constantly on the move, and they rise and fall all the time. See it as an opportunity to buy other good-performing cryptos at a lower price and build up your portfolio without exceeding your risk tolerance. None of it means that you are bad at investing and don't know how to make a profit. On the other hand, when your portfolio is up and doing well, it doesn't automatically translate to you being fantastic at what you do and a true master at investing—even experienced investors find their portfolios falling at times. The difference is that they have the experience to weather the storm, which you need to learn.
