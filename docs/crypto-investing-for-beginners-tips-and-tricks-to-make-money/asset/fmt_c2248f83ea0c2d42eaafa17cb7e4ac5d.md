
# Are Market Dips Good for Long-Term Investment?

When you "buy the dip," you purchase your crypto when the price has gone down, assuming it will rise again in the future. This requires you to watch the market carefully for a long period to see when the crypto drops to a significantly low price all of a sudden—this is a short-term decline. Anyone interested in crypto knows of the 2018 crash when Bitcoin plummeted to $5933 from $19675. If you were to purchase at the lower price, which many did, it was considered a buy-dip. However, the nature of the financial markets means they dive about all over the place, giving us swing lows and swing highs. Finding the dip is often not easy, so the best method is to look for a bearish pressure as these often bring about several lows. You can also consider analysts' reports to understand what the experts think of the price movement. Those who have been in the business a long time and have expert technical analysis knowledge can better interpret these reports. Lastly, consider using an exchange with a low cost and price reliability as one of the best resources to buy dips. Your success is dependent on whether the crypto in question is likely to rise again in the future. Again, Bitcoin is an excellent example. During the pandemic, its price crashed, and those who opted to purchase at that time are now rolling in potentially large profits. Long-term investment can only be successful and effective if you pick the right crypto to invest in. While it takes knowledge and skill to know what you are doing, there is also a percentage of good luck involved, given market behavior is unpredictable and cryptos are volatile. Perhaps one of the best strategies is a mixture of long-term investments and short-term trading, ensuring you benefit all around. You should also have good technical analysis knowledge to ensure you understand what the market is doing.

# Chapter 11 Limiting Losses While Making Gains

We often hear about cryptocurrency in the news, and many will call it a ruthless market. However, much of this reputation has likely come from investors who took the leap in 2017, when crypto hype was at its highest. Driven by FOMO (fear of missing out) and no small amount of greed, investors threw themselves into the market willy-nilly. Most had no understanding of the cryptocurrency market's fundamentals and set about buying everything they could. Unsurprisingly, a bubble appeared around cryptocurrencies, and Bitcoin reached a historical high of $20,000 by the time 2017 ended. However, that wasn't to last very long, and the bubble burst in 2018, leading to a lengthy bear market, and all those last-minute investors, fueled by greed, paid for it in spades. Things have changed somewhat, and the crypto market is looking pretty bright at the moment. However, while it's easy to say dive in and invest your money, you need to make sure you aren't going to end up with egg on your face, like those earlier investors. To help you, I will give you some tips on maximizing your gains and minimizing your losses.

## Maximize Your Gains

### 1. Only Invest What You Can Afford to Lose

This is the number one rule in any type of investing, be it cryptos, stocks, or any other financial market. Why? There are two reasons for this, especially when investing in cryptos:

*   The cryptocurrency market is one of the most volatile in the world.
*   The cryptocurrency market works 24/7, meaning trading never stops.

One of the first traps many new investors fall into is monitoring prices constantly. All that does is tire you out, mentally and physically, causing stress and a much higher chance of making the wrong move. Worn-out investors are also more sensitive to that FOMO feeling. To not be exposed to that feeling and make poor trading decisions, you should only ever invest as much you can comfortably afford to lose without risking your life and finances. The best way to do that is to create a budget every month. List down all your income and outgoings and how much you have spare each month. Then, decide on a figure to invest in the crypto market and stick to it rigidly.

### 2. Keep Bitcoin as Your Priority Investment

Bitcoin is constantly in the news, mostly because someone is criticizing it, usually someone from the finance world, regulators, or economists. However, some of those attacks also come from the crypto world. That's because every other cryptocurrency competes with Bitcoin, regardless of how much they say they aren't. Ethereum, Bitcoin Cash, Ripple, EOS, or any of the many others are all after one thing—toppling Bitcoin from its place at the top. Never forget this and never forget that Bitcoin still dominates the market, with a 65% market share. Bitcoin is always the first crypto that people think of and is the most decentralized of all cryptocurrencies. This all adds up to Bitcoin being the best investment since it has huge potential for capital appreciation over the long term and offers deep security for any money you invest in it. As such, it should be your investment priority. If you can't afford to buy entire Bitcoins, some exchanges will allow you to purchase and trade in bits of Bitcoins.

### 3. Make Sure Your Crypto Portfolio Is Diversified

While you should make Bitcoin your priority, you should also diversify your portfolio by investing in other cryptocurrencies, especially promising ones. Try for a distribution ratio of 70% Bitcoin and 30% Altcoins. From there, you can do one of two things—increase your Bitcoin share as you see fit, or reduce it and invest in other promising Altcoins. This choice can only be made by assessing your current risk aversion and working out what you can afford to invest. If you think you can comfortably take more risks, reduce Bitcoin to 60% and up your Altcoin investments to 40% of your portfolio, so long as you only choose those with a good chance of increasing in the future. You must remember one important thing—rebalance your Altcoins regularly and, on occasion, your Bitcoin percentage. In this way, you can adapt to economic news and events that impact the market and make better profits.

### 4. Listen to Warren Buffet on Fear and Greed

I don't expect you thought you would see Warren Buffet's name in a chapter about maximizing crypto profit. It's no secret that the financial world's biggest investors don't think much of cryptocurrencies. After all, he once said that "Bitcoin is rat poison squared." How reassuring is it that a man with an $85 billion+ fortune can sometimes be wrong about money! However, that same man also said something incredibly smart, something all investors should take note of—"Be fearful when others are greedy and greedy when others are fearful." Buffet addressed this warning to people investing in traditional markets, but it applies to the cryptocurrency market just as well. No matter what market you invest in, human psychology is the same. This means that when most other investors are in FOMO mode and pushing you to get in there and buy some, be very careful. You risk huge disappointment and some not-so-small losses if you fall for it. Conversely, when many investors are panicking, perhaps because of a news event, and are selling their holdings, be prepared to step out of your comfort zone and put greed to work. This is the time when you can make some excellent investments that are highly likely to pay off handsomely in the future. If in doubt, think back to 2018, when investors chose to purchase Bitcoin at the height of the bubble burst when investors were selling to minimize their losses. Those who held their investments, known as HODLERS, are now raking in huge profits. When the market reached its bottom in December 2018, panicking investors were selling for a little over $3,000 per Bitcoin. Those same Bitcoin are now worth more than ten times that amount.
