
# Minimizing Your Losses

Because Bitcoin and cryptocurrency markets, in general, are so volatile, you must trust in your own instincts when you start investing and trading. To make sure you are in the right position to make the best trades most of the time, you need to be informed, and there is one basic rule for all investors, especially in cryptocurrency - **DYOR - Do Your Own Research**. So, first, you must train yourself in how to do technical analysis. This way, you can analyze the price forecasts for the specific assets you want to invest in, rather than going by someone else's analyses. You also need to take the time to learn about the assets you want to invest in. Read their white papers, trawl the internet and look on social media, particularly Reddit, as they are a mine of useful cryptocurrency information. Because you will make your investment decisions based on your research and no one else's, you should not regret any investment you make. From there, you can move easily to the next trade, not panicking about the previous one's outcome.

Over the years since Bitcoin first came into being, the crypto markets have been up and down like yo-yos, even within the bull and bear trends. While we can honestly say that all dips in the crypto market have thus far been followed by a rise, sometimes a significant one, these declines can cause no end of stress, and most investors, especially newbies, find them hard to navigate. As these are the time when you are likely to take the most losses, especially if you are not careful, the following tips will help you hang on to your value and keep your losses to a minimum.

## 1. Don't Fall For FUD and FOMO

While it is imperative to stay updated with the latest crypto trends and news, you can have too much information. This is truer when the market is in decline, when it's easy to lose your nerve, let your instincts over-run you, and make bad decisions. **FUD** (fear, uncertainty, and doubt) and **FOMO** (fear of missing out) are commonly used terms in crypto investing, and both can strongly influence us to buy and sell at the wrong times. **FUD** typically surrounds negative sentiment, usually caused by adverse news, rumors, or a well-known figurehead expressing some concern over an asset or market. This can cause some traders to sell off their investments because they expect the price to continue falling. This negatively affects the price. On the other hand, **FOMO** is more about traders who get carried away after seeing positive news. They tend to ignore basic signals, in too much of a rush to jump on the bandwagon and shoot for a moon that may be nothing more than a mirage. The future is not predictable, especially in the cryptocurrency markets, and no one person can give you better advice than you researching for yourself and making your own decisions. Please keep one thing in mind - some publishers and influencers gain from causing **FOMO** or **FUD** to send the markets in the direction they want. When you hear or read something about a crypto you are interested in or the markets in general, try to confirm it with several sources, not just one.

## 2. Have Clear Goals, a Diversified Portfolio, and Trade Only What You Can Afford

You can have as much confidence as you like in a crypto asset, but if you invest more than you can afford, you are in for a world of hurt. Nobody wants to get caught up in a rollercoaster of emotions, watching their portfolio prices drop while waiting for them to rise again. Also, any good investor will also have several assets in their portfolio, some short-term and some long-term, and usually a mixture of cryptocurrencies and stocks. Because the markets are so volatile and working 24/7, 365 days a year, investors should ensure they have a trading strategy in place before they put any money down, including stating your entry and exit points. To be fair, you could see every piece of information about crypto, but it only takes one thing - a tweet, a hack, etc. - to send the prices into freefall. This is why planning ahead is critical so you can mitigate your losses in the event of a crash. In the previous chapter, we looked at some strategies you could follow, and **dollar-cost averaging** is a good way to help you trade without using your emotions and without the need to constantly stare at charts. **Dollar-cost averaging** is all about purchasing and selling in small amounts regularly. Don't forget - when you hold volatile assets, you can easily find yourself getting carried away. Any type of trading is high-risk, particularly when the market is in a bear period. Investors should set goals to create a balance between potential gains and potential losses.

## 3. Long-Term Investing and HODLing

There's a saying that goes along the lines of "it isn't a loss until you sell." While this isn't 100% true, there is something to it. If your assets have dropped in value since you purchased them, it's called **unrealized losses**. If you sell them for less than you bought them, they become **realized losses**. Over the years, Bitcoin has been in a near-constant uptrend. Even when the prices have temporarily fallen or a bear market persists, historically, we can see that prices do rise eventually - in the case of cryptocurrency, that rise is normally driven by scarcity and other factors. Scarcity is the one factor that leads many people to believe cryptocurrency prices will continue rising. Allowing for your investment timeframe being long-term, you can see negative movements as temporary and can wait them out. Holding assets for a longer period, known as **HODLING**, is a long-proven strategy, and Bitcoin has definitely shown itself to be the most successful asset in the last 10 years. Don't forget that in some countries, like the USA, holding for long periods can also help you when it comes to taxes, i.e., holding for at least a year can benefit you more than selling early.

## 4. Be Prepared to Take Your Profits or Ride the Dip Out

Perhaps the safest way of avoiding volatility and staying safe during a dip is to convert volatile holdings for stable ones. This way, you can lock your balance in and keep your risks to a minimum. It also reduces the need to be active in your portfolio management and keeps your stress levels down when the market is in a bull period. **USDC** and other stablecoins try to keep their value fixed, and investing in some of these stable assets can keep your exposure to price changes to a minimum when the markets are declining. However, when you sell everything at the same time, it is known as **capitulation**, and it can lead to crypto investors losing big time if the market suddenly shoots up again. This is why you need a plan detailing roughly what profit and loss levels you can be comfortable with BEFORE you are under pressure and need to make decisions. Lots of investors opt to use stable assets in their portfolio, moving in and out of them in a strategy revolving around market withdrawal and a buy-back strategy. In this way, provided they get their timing right, their portfolios gradually increase. However, this is not easy and definitely not for new investors; even experienced investors get their timing wrong.
