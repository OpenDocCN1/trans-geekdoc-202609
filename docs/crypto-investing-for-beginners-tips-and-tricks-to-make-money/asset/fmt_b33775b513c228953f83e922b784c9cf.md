
# Even if the markets are in freefall, you can always find opportunities

Even if the markets are in freefall, you can always find opportunities - you just need to know where to look. Where some might see doom and gloom, keen-eyed investors will see a ray of light where they can get the right asset at the right price and wait for the profits to come rolling in.

## Strategies for Market Dips

### Buy the Dip
One of the most popular ways to do this is to buy the dip. This gets you into the market or helps you raise your current position. Even in downtrends, the market still fluctuates, and prices still go up at times. Provided you have technical analysis skills, you can benefit from using your knowledge to predict those movements, buying the lows and selling the highs.

### Short Selling
Another good strategy during market dips is to short sell, which means betting that the price will fall on a specific asset.

### DeFi Yield Farming and Staking
DeFi yield farming and staking can also level your returns out, ensuring your crypto balance continues to grow when others around you are falling like dominos.

### Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)
And again, the fallback is dollar-cost averaging, especially where you think an asset is definitely going to rise because this strategy works whether the markets rise or fall. In fact, with dollar-cost averaging, you stand to gain more when the market is down.

## Risk Warning
Don't forget that these activities are not for those of a nervous disposition and, done wrong, could lose you a lot of money. They could also lead to you spending more time than you need to watching price charts and getting further stressed out.

# Chapter 12: The IDDA Strategy

![](image_rsrc1NY.jpg)

**IDDA** stands for **Invest Diva Diamond Analysis**. Before you go ahead and execute your newly drawn-up strategy, you need to use some different methods to analyze the markets, and many traders choose just one or two. However, this is a dangerous way to go; IDDA suggests using five points to analyze the markets, and we're going to discuss those now:

![](image_rsrc1PF.jpg)

## Point One - Fundamental Analysis
This is when you use every news story, economic event, gossip, and the financial situation of the crypto you want to invest in and consider potential upcoming events that could affect the market. Fundamental analysts use all this data to determine whether to invest in an asset by analyzing the political, social, and economic forces affecting a crypto's supply and demand. However, although this is all valuable data, it can be overwhelming, but this is what you should be looking at:

*   Company and industry information from sources like `Yahoo Finance`, `Motley Fool`, `NASDAQ`, etc.
*   Economic and current events from sources like `WSJ` and `Barron's financial journals`, `Washington Post`, `New York Times`, `Bloomberg`, `CNBC`, etc.

However, we all know that the internet is full of scams and avoiding false news is imperative. Two of the most common scams are:

*   Those that promise a get-rich-quick scheme (no such thing in most investing, especially not crypto investing)
*   Pump-and-dumps - promoters purchase specific cryptos, pump the price up by creating a lot of hype and then dump it at a high price, thus knocking the price back down again.

You can find plenty of information on avoiding these scams at [`www.sec.gov`](http://www.sec.gov).

## Point Two - Technical Analysis
This requires you to look at the trading charts and analyze the price action history and revolves around attempting to predict what a particular crypto's future might hold. The main difference between this and fundamental analysis can be seen in a shopping example. A fundamental analyst looking for the right shop to buy a wedding present would ask their friends what they think of the products sold in several different shops. Then you would visit each store, look at each product and decide what to buy. A technical analyst would sit and watch each store. They would analyze what each shopper has in their bag, what their facial expressions are before and after they shop, and so on, and they would base their decision on certain patterns. You know the old saying, "history repeats itself" - that is technical analysis in a nutshell. In short, you look for past patterns and use them to create a strategy based on the crypto repeating those price movements. The best way to do this is to look at each cryptocurrency's charts as you can see price movements and trends quite easily.

## Point Three - Market Sentiment Analysis
This revolves around traders' attitudes and emotions regarding a particular crypto and is the study of the bulls and the bears. When traders expect a price to go up, the market sentiment is bullish. Conversely, the sentiment is bearish when they expect the price to go down. All traders have their own intuition or feelings, so purely studying prices won't tell you very much. Their opinions are expressed in whether they take a bearish or bullish position in the market, thus adding to the overall sentiment.

## Point Four - Capital Analysis
This is quite possibly the most important of the five points and is the one most ignored by retail traders. Capital analysis requires you to research your risks, capital, and potential for reward. This is where calculating risk tolerance comes in, one of the most important steps to do before you decide on a trading strategy. You must calculate your available capital, leverage that makes the most sense, and the spread with your chosen broker; otherwise, you will fall prey to unpredictable losses. This kind of thing is also known as risk management. Your trading strategy will depend entirely on how much money is in your account and your capital. When you enter and exit a trade, your broker requires a fee known as the spread - this is the difference between your bid and the ask price. It is not recommended to speculate or trade right before a risk event - if you've done your fundamental analysis, you'll know what's what.

## Point Five - Overall Analysis
This is where you get an overall analysis of the previous four points. You need to know a couple of things:

*   First, are 90% or more of the previous points telling you a trade will be successful?
*   Are you comfortable executing the trade, or does your gut tell you to hold off?

We've all heard the term "women's intuition," and there is much research indicating that women can tell what someone is feeling through a kind of psychic ability. However, where trading is concerned, it doesn't mean you have to bring your wife/mother/sister in to help you because men use their gut feelings too - if you trust yours, it will rarely put you wrong. Instinct or intuition can do two things - it can be your starting point for analysis and a final indicator of whether you should go ahead. The one piece of advice I really can give you here is this - if you are not sure, if something is telling you not to do the trade, don't do it. Never execute trades based purely on intuition, and always make sure you don't confuse it with greed and/or fear. Always use all five points to ensure the trade is right for you.
