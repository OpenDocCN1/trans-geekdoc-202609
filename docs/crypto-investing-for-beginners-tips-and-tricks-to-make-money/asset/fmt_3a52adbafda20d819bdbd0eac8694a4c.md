
# Chapter 5 Exchanges and Brokers

Now you understand more about cryptocurrency, its advantages and disadvantages, and how it and the blockchain work, you're ready to start your investing journey. Most crypto trading and investing occurs online since they are digital assets, although there are limited methods to purchase cryptos using cash.

You should be aware that two primary trading methods are using an exchange or a broker. But you should also be aware that there are differences between them.

## Exchanges vs. Brokers

The first thing to note is that an exchange is very similar to a stock exchange in its processes. Cryptocurrency exchanges are where buyers and sellers come together to trade fiat money and cryptocurrencies. The exchange is an intermediary that charges a fee to facilitate the trade. Two common examples of how cryptocurrency exchanges are used are using fiat money to buy cryptocurrency and trading different cryptos — for example, buying Ether (Ethereum) with BTC (Bitcoin). Every exchange has its own trading pairs, so which one you use is entirely down to you and your needs.

Let's give you an example of how this works. Let's say you are an investor and you put USD onto an exchange. That means you can only use trading pairs related to USD, i.e., USD/ETH, USD/BTC, etc. before you can begin trading in any other altcoin. Crypto exchanges are the commonest method of trading cryptocurrencies, especially where only small amounts of money are used.

A broker mediates between the market and traders. In simple terms, if you use a broker for your crypt trading, you place money into their account. This gives you access to their offered products. You are not limited to trading in the fiat currency or cryptocurrency you deposited and can use any other trading pair. The broker finds the counterparty — the other party in the transaction — and, in some cases, may even serve as the counterparty, executing the trade.

Here's an example to help you understand. You deposit BTC with a broker but choose the Ethereum/XRP pair for leverage trading. You can trade that pair based on how many BTC you deposited. If you want to trade in Ethereum/XRP on an exchange, your BTC must be exchanged for Ether first.

So, what are the primary differences? Brokers are better for those trading in large amounts of money because they have better liquidity and security. Once your money is deposited, you can choose from several opportunities, such as leverage positions, depending on what your chosen broker offers. On the other hand, exchanges make cryptocurrency trading much easier, using the trading pair's order book dictated by the fiat currency or cryptocurrency deposited. You should remember that when you use an exchange, the traded asset is physically delivered, while with a broker, you are only trading an underlying asset.

Another major difference between brokers and exchanges is the target audience. Crypto exchanges tend to be more used by crypto HODLers looking for mid or long-term investments, purchasing a particular cryptocurrency, and then waiting for the price to go up. Brokers tend to be used by speculative investors, who make different trades and use the different tools their broker offers. They are usually looking at short or mid-term profits and use the broker as a mediator to gain them.

## Registration and Verification

Each exchange will have its registration process. Some only require a customer to provide an email address, which they then validate and confirm and ask you to set a password. Others, more so those where fiat currency is handled, require ID verification. This could be a scanned ID document, a video call, a photo of you holding your ID, or it may be a complete KYC process, although this is only when you want access to the upper deposit and withdrawal limits. KYC is a Know Your Customer process used by financial institutions to identify and verify you when you open an account. Binance is the largest exchange, and you can trade without full KYC; however, you can only withdraw 2 BTC per day. Verification can take hours up to days, so be prepared for this and don't expect to open an account and start trading all in the same day — it may not happen.

Brokers also have a fairly easy verification process, but it is critical because official regulations cover brokers. Mostly, you will need to provide a photo ID and proof of where you live, usually a utility bill or rent book. Verification also doesn't take so long with a broker, and you can sign up, deposit, and trade all on the same day.

## Deposits and Withdrawals

Crypto exchanges offer two ways to deposit and withdraw. First is fiat money, and that isn't easy — not all exchanges will accept fiat currency, so you need cryptocurrency to deposit into your account. Also, fiat currencies tend to attract higher fees. Be aware that adding fiat currency to your crypto exchange account from a bank account will attract fees, as will using a debit or credit card. In terms of withdrawals, fees are higher than those charged for deposits. You must also check that your bank will accept crypto exchange transfers — not all do.

The fees tend to be significantly lower if you deposit and withdraw crypto, especially for deposits, because many exchanges will not charge a fee for cryptocurrency deposits. Brokers have more options in place for deposits — they will accept debit and credit cards, bank accounts, e-wallets, and, of course, crypto, and they don't tend to charge deposit fees. Their withdrawal fees tend to be lower, typically between 0% and 3%, whereas some exchanges charge 6%.

## Trading

Exchanges are easy platforms for trading crypto, and most use order books, allowing you to do a limit order, market order, or both. Each exchange's liquidity is different, based on DOM (depth of market). Those with high liquidity usually have a smaller spread between the purchase and sell orders. Most exchanges only offer basic functions, which is usually enough for a newbie or average user. More specialized exchanges provide access to high-leverage instruments, like perpetual swaps or futures. However, the biggest advantage is that exchanges offer a wide range of cryptos for trading purposes.

Brokers offer additional trading instruments, such as CFD (contract for difference) and derivatives. They also provide trading tools to help customers with their trades, such as indicators and moving averages. Some even provide automated robots and strategies to help traders optimize their risk management and improve their trading. In terms of CFD trading, exposure is multiplied by using leverage. For example, if a trader's chosen crypto's CFD leverage ratio is 1:2, and the price shifts 5%, the CFD trader profits or loses 10%, depending on the direction of the shift and the position type chosen by the trader. Simply put, CFD traders can make significant profits quickly but can also lose money very quickly too. Leverage trades are best left to those more experienced.

## Security

Spreads tend to be tighter with brokers than with exchanges and is one of the reasons why margin traders use them. You have undoubtedly heard of hacks on crypto exchanges, such as the Mt. Gox hack in 2014, which saw thousands of Bitcoins stolen. Even the biggest exchanges are targets — Binance was targeted with a KYC hack in 2019 when thousands of customers' verification details were leaked. Crypto exchanges will also be a vulnerable point for hacks and scams, and brokers can also be targeted. However, brokers are regulated and have some financial protection in place for customers, where compensation can be paid should the worst happen. Brokers also tend to be thoroughly audited, so keep this in mind when choosing where to put your money, and look for those regulated by the FCA or SEC. Brokers also hold your money in a traditional bank account, which is also covered by financial regulation. Make your choice based on your requirements but bear in mind that there is another option — you could store your funds in a hardware wallet, like Ledger or Trezor. The reasoning behind this is sound — when you have a wallet, you have a private key and can access your funds whenever you want. If you store your cryptos on the exchange, you don't have that private key — the exchange does. There's a cryptocurrency saying here that is highly relevant — "Not your keys, not your crypto."

## Income

Brokers and exchanges are similar in many ways but also differ in terms of income source. Sure, both charge fees, but they differ in how they approach their clients. Exchanges offer a wide range of trading pairs which attract many traders. Those pairs offer the exchanges a widespread because the depth of market is quite small and liquid. Their fees for withdrawals and trades are usually higher, and sometimes deposit fees are higher too. Brokers have much lower trading and withdrawal fees, but traded amounts are significantly higher, so the fee amounts could be in the same region as those of an exchange.
