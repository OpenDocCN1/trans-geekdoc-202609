# Interference

In networking, the term interference can refer to electromagnetic interference (EMI) or radio frequency interference (RFI), both of which can be disturbing influences on network communication. EMI and RFI are typically caused by electrical or electronic equipment in proximity to the network cables, as light fixtures, wireless phones, or radio equipment. There are natural sources of interference, as well as solar flares.

Measuring the levels of interference on a cable run be part of the network installation process, but it is possible for interference to be added after the cables are in place. When this occurs, cable testers can be used to detect and quantify various types of interference. The only solutions for interference caused by other equipment are to move the cable and the equipment farther apart or replace the cable with shielded twisted pair (STP).

# Decibel (dB) Loss

The strength of a cable is measured in decibels (dB). Decibel loss is a value representing the ratio of a signal's strength to its attenuation at its destination. As noted, all cable types suffer from attenuation to some degree; the decibel loss measurement is a means of quantifying that effect. Decibel values are logarithmic, meaning that the strength decreases exponentially as the decibel level increases. If a 10 dB loss means the strength at the destination is one-tenth of its original strength, then a 20 dB loss means the signal is one-hundredth of its original strength.

# Incorrect Pinout

Inside a twisted pair are eight or more wires, grouped in pairs, with each pair consisting of a signal and a ground wire. At each end of the cable is a connector that plugs into the computer, wall outlet, or patch panel.
