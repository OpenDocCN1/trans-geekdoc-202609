While there are plenty of small-scale DoS attacks, in which an intruder uses one computer to generate traffic and direct it towards the target system, the truly dangerous ones are called denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks. This type of attack involves one intruder, but many computers, referred to as zombies because they are running an application called a bot that can execute instructions at the behest of an attacker, as instructions to bombard a particular target with traffic. DDoS attacks are virtually unlimited in scale and have been known to bring legitimate networks to a standstill.

# Bots

A bot is a software application or routine that operates remotely deployed on a computer. An army of bots obeying a single controller is called a botnet. Many bots are benign and perform useful services, but like any automated system, they can be made to perform malicious attacks.

In a typical attack scenario, the attacker uses viruses or other means to deploy an army of bots on the computers of unsuspecting victims. The bots communicate with a command and control server, which is run by the attacker, who can use it to command the bots to initiate a DDoS attack or perform other malicious acts. # On-Path Attack

Formerly known as man-in-the-middle attack, an on-path attack is a technique in which an intruder intercepts data packets as they travel over the network, reads and perhaps modifies the contents of the packets in some fashion, and then sends the modified packets on their way to their intended destination. There are any number of ways that an attacker can take profitable advantage of this arrangement.
