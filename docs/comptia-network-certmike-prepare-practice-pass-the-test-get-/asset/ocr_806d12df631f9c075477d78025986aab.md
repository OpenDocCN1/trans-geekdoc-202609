Today, asset tagging manufacturers have greatly expanded the functionality of their products by creating tags using radio frequency identification (RFID) chips or other wireless tracking mechanisms. This can enable network administrators to not only identify devices, but actually track their locations within the facility.

# Tamper Detection

Tamper detection is any mechanism that can detect when a particular device, container, or facility has been opened by unauthorized persons. At its simplest, there are detection tapes and stickers that administrators can place on computers or other devices in such a way that when someone opens the device's case, the tape is broken.

Of course, mechanical tamper detection devices like this require someone to see the broken tape, which means regular inspection of network devices. Better than these mechanisms that can generate a signal when they detecting and alert an appropriate administrator. Many servers have switches into their cases that are tripped when someone opens it. The switch is typically connected to the motherboard so that the computer receives the tamper detection signal and can take action on it, such as generating an SNMP trap.

# PREVENTION METHODS

It is important to be able to detect an attack or intrusion when it occurs, but it is far more preferable to prevent those incidents from happening before they occur. While some intrusion methods involve the application of technology or hardware, others are a matter of careful planning and communication, with end users and IT support personnel. # Employee Training

While there are many types of attacks and intrusions that administrators can prevent by applying technology, it is important to remember that end users nearly outnumber IT personnel. They can be the organization's first line of defense, as long as they know what to look for and what to do when a security event occurs. Employee training is the answer to that.

Security training for end users consists largely of things they should look out for that can indicate an incident attack, or even one in progress, such as phishing messages, social engineering contacts, tailating attempts, and other suspicious behaviors. End users should also be informed about security-related policies and the reasons for them, such as mandatory password changes and multifactor authentication.

In the same way, IT personnel—and especially the first-line people—should receive regular training to keep them updated about changing conditions and threats.
