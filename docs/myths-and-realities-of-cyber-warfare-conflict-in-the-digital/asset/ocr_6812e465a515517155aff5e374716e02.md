However, deploying software that is designed to complete excessive bandwidth interrupts all traffic, including those of targets (in this cyber criminals) but also of others attempting to conduct legitimate traffic. Analysts studying the impact of cyberattacks against Estonia in 2007 noted the substantial costs of remedial actions made necessary for the public sector, as well as the financial impact on the nation’s major banks. These were deemed to pale in comparison to the geopolitical toll on both the national level policy makers and the population as a whole “Had the cyberattacks disabled the provision of vital services on a large scale, public trust in the government and digital infrastructure would have been seriously compromised.”18 In the midst of the attacks, course, there was no way to anticipate realistically whether the scale of attacks or selection of targets might shift in exactly this direction.
