The Dawn of Glad Tidings helped trumpet the capture, and in advertising ISIS successes the propaganda campaign helped the terrorist group's victories seem inevitable. The ISIS propaganda cleverly transformed the victories of sympathizers into bot machines because the permissions granted through loading of the app give ISIS the opportunity to add them to the throng of machines that deluded social media with reports of ISIS military success and with signals of brutal irony met to ISIS's unlucky enemies who failed to escape its grasp.³³ Although the app was closed by Twitter within days of the city's capacity, the Dawn of Glad Tidings provided a brief but crucial change for ISIS to flood the global commons with its message in the form of thousands of tweets daily. Panic on the high volume served ISIS to tragic effect, Mosul was not wrestled from their hands until the summer of 2017 after hard fighting.

Termination of the Dawn of Glad Tidings did not bring an end to ISIS's online information operations. Indeed, researchers believe that at least 46,000 Twitter accounts supported ISIS in the months following the app's closure, although not necessarily at one time.³⁵ Brookings Institute researchers believed that a small subset of these accounts was controlled by a cadre of “hyperactive users, numbering between 500 and 2,000, which tweet in concentrated bursts of high volume.”³⁶ This is keeping with patterns described in a Strategic Quarterly report that outlined how a “small core group” coordinates a message that is then magnified by a bot network with accounts that may follow each other as well as the core group of human actors, and that below this cost is unlikely to follow each other's accounts but will follow the accounts of the core group and they will sometimes additionally follow the accounts of the bots above them, perhaps unaware that the bots do not belong to human users who are true believers in the movement.³⁷

Named for the site of a Muslim prophesied battle equivalent to Armageddon, ISIS released a dozen issues that centered on a jihadist theme roughly every month or two.³39 Overlapping messages in different formats or seemingly from different sources is a time-honored tool in propaganda, due to the reinforcing effect it can exert. The ISIS Twitter footprint recovered significantly over the next two years. Twitter, perhaps seeking to emphasize the contrast with its earlier reluctance to become involved in content moderation related to violent extremism, announced in August 2016 that it had suspended 360,000 accounts between mid-2015 and mid-2016 because of their posts courting terrorism.³⁴ By the end of 2017, a total of 600,000 account suspensions had been tallied.⁴⁵ As much as this formidable number of account suspensions reflects Twitter’s efforts to curb violent speech, it indicates the ease with which ISIS sympathizers were able to relaunch new accounts.
