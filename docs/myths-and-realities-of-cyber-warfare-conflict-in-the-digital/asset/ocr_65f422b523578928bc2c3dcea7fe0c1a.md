# Myths and Realities of Cyber Warfare

The prospect of organized and politically motivated cyberattack is fundamental to any coherent concept of a cyber Pearl Harbor. An awareness of the fact that the original Pearl Harbor fit within a larger strategic context—that the attack supported a strategic purpose and was the most prominent among what was actually virtually simultaneous strikes—should also be demanded of sources producing digital equivalents.

The term has been adopted with a degree of gusto. While Richard Siemnon notes that "only a crippling military defeat outweighs controlling of the cyber domain deserves to be labeled a Cyber Pearl Harbor," he has also predicted that "in the future there will be a devastating use of cyber attack against military systems; a true cyber Pearl Harbor."48 Whether or not the prediction is realized, the phrasing does at least indicate an awareness that cyberattack “Pearl Harbor” carries meaningful connotations. Not all authors seem to fully appreciate this: "imagine if Pearl Harbor had been attacked and there had no response from Washington. This is the actual case today" through the "secret war against the United States conducted through cyberattacks.49

Other authors have skithered Pearl Harbor imagery or used it to highlight potential distinctions between the provocative visions and the potential dangers they envision. Ronald Deibert, the founder of the Canadian cybersecurity analysis group Citizen Lab, pointed to fragility in internet infrastructure from threats as a ship's anchor securing undersea communications cables to cascading satellite debris known as the Kessler Syndrome "ending]" global cyberspace as we know it."50 Perhaps partly because this fragility is not vulnerable to intentional actions, Deibert avoided using "Pearl Harbor" allegories. A work describing cyberwar's "potential to make planes fall from the sky or nuclear power plants to melt down" nonetheless need to some length to inserting Pearl Harbor illusions.51 Pearl Harbor can even to extent provide a foil to the prospect of cyber warfare: whereas Japanese planes strafing Ford Island or bombing USS Arizona were marked with Japan's red-sun rumble, attribution "won't be simple" in the wake of a crushing cyberattack, and after we answer the question of who did it, the next is "Is it war?"52
