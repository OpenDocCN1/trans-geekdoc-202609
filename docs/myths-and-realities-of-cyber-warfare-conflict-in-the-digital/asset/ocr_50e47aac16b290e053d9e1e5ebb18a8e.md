When this statement was made over three decades ago, computers were infinitely less ubiquitous and less interconnected than they are today. There is no real way to follow the earlier precept as advice if one wanted to do. Interconnection stands at the center of what modern devices are intended to do. Air-gapping is a standard compromise for protecting devices whose operation is of critical importance whose functionality is not crucially impacted by electronic isolation.

The literature reflects about the degrees of effectiveness of partial air-gapping. It has been suggested that time-sensitive attacks against air defense system might be foiled or at least frustrated by “intermittently air-gapped system.” Cybersecurity writers described how the introduction of an air gap complicates the journey of malware to a target but also obscures the effect of the malware by observation by attackers, who are left to extrapolate or guess target system, the effects, and the defender’s response.60 Personnel at the U.S. Naval College identified cyberattacks in 2006, and as these activities continued unabated, officials extended the air-gap of the Naval War College by disconnecting the facility offline. Similar steps were taken the following year at the Pentagon when cyberattacks triggered the decision to “temporarily disconnect the unclassified network [NIPRNET] from the Internet.”/61

Although temporary or intermittent air-gapping is not an uncommon practice in the face of an identified and episodic cyberattack threat systems that can survive a period of disconnection, it is an impractical option for protecting systems that must always be operating and connected—and periodic air-gapping is of little use in the face of attacks that aim for quasi-random results.
