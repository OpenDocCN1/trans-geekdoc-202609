# FROM SPEECH TO RECRUITMENT

The most infamous groups to leverage the internet and especially social media to support warfighting are the self-proclaimed jihadi entities aiming to impose their interpretations of Islam upon other Muslims and potentially living outside the lands that had been populated by Muslims during the medieval era. Marginalized from traditional modes of communication and propaganda, such groups eagerly adopted internet means for distributing their ideas and connecting with sympathizers. Between 2001 and 2006, a period coinciding with the early phases of the Global War on Terrorism and bracketed by the September 11 attacks and the in insurg violence that precipitated the U.S. surge in Iraq, the number of jihadist websites increased by an estimated 20,000%.44 The emergence of social media opportunities showed jihadist propagandists as early adopters.45

Recruiting extremists poses complex challenges since it involves recruiting like-minded individuals who face prosecution for their intentions and also because it means converting people who would not necessarily favor major aspects of the philosophy of the movement recruiters want them to join. "Groups with extreme ideologies, from the Bolsheviks to the Nazis, are not mass movements, but rather relatively small groups of true believers who take power and impose their worldview on the masses."46 The gulf between the likely ideas of recruits and the tenets of extremist movements illustrates the reasoning assertions that extremists should be small but without rhetorical victories, such as fanatic zealot groups like al-Qaida and ISIS in the Middle East. Commonly known as "Islamists" or "jihads," some argue that using the term (the latter of which refers to holy war) normalizes and lends a degree of credibility to their hate-filled and blood-soaked ideology, which actually opposes the ideas and practices of large numbers of Muslims.47
