Two observations seem to characterize the main threat of the notion that social media makes a crucial information operations battlespace: “In the coming age, conflict will be waged more than[n]ever for the minds of people,” and “pure Cyberwar is dwarfed in the possible results when compared to clever conjectured SOFTWAR and Perception Management.”⁶ Intriguingly, however, these statements were made shortly prior to the polarization of social media. They appeared at the same time that the term “Web 2.0” was first coined in a prediction that social media platforms would transform the way people engaged with the internet. The full content of latter these two quotations emphasizes the impact of television as a mode of perception management relative to the impact of technical malware activities, both statements reflect the focus and concern today regarding a political entity’s ability to shape perceptions in a target state. Social media has both exposure and participation in such activities far more probable, since, “unlike weapon systems, anyone can use a computer”⁶ or connected device.

Despite the possible (but arguably fading) temptation to dismiss the potential of social-media-vector, information operations and despite the ongoing revelation of battlefield realities in the physical domains (times overlooked by the most zealous students of social media’s strategic effects), the impact of social media must be recognized and in context. To the extent that social media is available and used by a targeted society, it is liable to constitute an ideal battleground.
