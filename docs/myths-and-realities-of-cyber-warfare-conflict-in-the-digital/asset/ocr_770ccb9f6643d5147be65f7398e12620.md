Facebook built an admirably robust and user-friendly platform that to date has a base that rivals the total population of the People’s Republic of China and of India combined. The expectation that no one would allow a serious effort to repurpose the platform for a strategic purpose certainly in retrospect appears utopian, even naive. Unfortunately as it may be, the launch and growth of social media giants like Facebook new dimensions of opportunity for communication, and when people communicate, they do not necessarily say only the things that their masters want to hear.

History indicates that new technologies will be used for strategic aims, barring extraordinary and conscious efforts to preclude them. In fact, earlier technologies that had been heralded as bringing connection, advantage, and understanding to humanity have been repeatedly used for voice and political strategy. Radio technology that connect societies was soon to disseminate propaganda. Chemistry that alleviated hunger was quickly repurposed to facilitate explosives in wartime. Even maps and cartography have a long history that is intertwined with warfighting. No one wants to lose a war because the price of defeat can be devastating.
