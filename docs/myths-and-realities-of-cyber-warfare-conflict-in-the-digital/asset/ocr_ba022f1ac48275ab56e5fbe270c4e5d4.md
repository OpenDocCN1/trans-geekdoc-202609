# Myths and Realities of Cyber Warfare

A realistic appraisal of the myth that cyberattacks possess instantaneous speed, therefore, demands an examination of some of the different kinds of hostile activities. While many other forms of attacks exist, further consideration of distributed denial of service (DoS) attacks and of advanced persistent threats (APT) attacks can help shed light on the issue of the presumably instantaneous speed of cyberattacks.

# DISTRIBUTED DENIAL OF SERVICE

Denial of service (DoS) attacks represent possibly the most common of an easily established, swiftly striking cyberattack. Different approaches exist, but the essence of a DoS attack is that attacking devices flood with either deliberately incomplete or spoofed signals. The target is overwhelmed in the first case because it first interprets and reacts to the attack as if it were a series of legitimate incoming encountering some technical hurdle, or it is overwhelmed in the second kind of DoS by the deluge of spoofed signals.¹⁴ Attacks can be distributed (thus DDoS), although they are commonly launched to enumerate political statement, the threat of DoS attacks has also been used by criminals to exert money.¹⁵ This points to a recurring theme, that intersections between crime, espionage, and conflict in the cyber domain are frequent and the distinctions can be either obscure or dubious.
