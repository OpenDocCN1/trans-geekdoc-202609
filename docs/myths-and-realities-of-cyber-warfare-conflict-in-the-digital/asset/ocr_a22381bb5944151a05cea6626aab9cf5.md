# HACKIVISTS

The term “hacktivist” for a hacker-activist is not new, and examples of hacktivists at least to the last days of the Cold War, with computer-savvy members of the antinuclear movement. The mindset of hackers feeds into the traits seen in hackivism. One cybersecurity expert has explained that, for hackers, “there is a shared ethical and moral set of values; there is a shared historic culture of things that they find funny, and other people would not find funny.” Making the computer for the portal for all pursuits, spanning work and socialization, connects with a “transcending of boundaries and applying the discipline of the inocost to your everyday life.”29 If this is the case for hackers, one might suggest that some hacktivists these characteristics are combined with some common sense that hacking should be used to pursue a shared vision of the world that aligns with those idealistic ideas and boundarylessness. Holding these views (which may vary too much to smoothly apply a word “sharing”) is the long pole in the tent for hackivism; considerable variations in actual hacking skills exist among members. For hacktivists, the motivation is the commonality and the expertise is a variable.
