## WESTPHALIAN RESURGENCE FOR THE DIGITAL REALM

Unsurprisingly, chaos triggers conflict. Paul Rosenzwe has aptly noted that “the Westphalian image is one of conflict, rather than cooperation.”73 alluding to the tradition of respect among states for each dealing unmolested with its own internal affairs. This tradition was established with the Peace of Westalia in 1648. It emerged because the alternative of encroaching on each’s internal politics (at least regarding religious confession) sucked Europe into three decades of blood-soaked conflict. Arguably, the 2010s witnessed a resurgence of Westphalian sentiment in response to the perceived shortcomings and failures of an extended post-Cold War era that had been marked by internationalist efforts at cooperation towards establishing an enduring and prosperous commons. Signs of Westphalianism since cyberspace have been identified by scholars Demchak and Peter Dombrowski since 2010,74, and the intervening decade has witnessed continuing sovereignty in cyberspace. that the “process of establishing cyber borders and thus states’ sovereignty will be nonlinear, dangerous, and lengthy.”75

Several factors feed the hunger for cyberspace to take on a more Westphalian flavor. One is the need for security and justifiable concern that security cannot be guaranteed by measures that cannot be verified. An international agreement to prohibit cyberweapons, could be difficult to establish, due to controversy about what constitutes a “weapon” in addition to concerns about how a ban would impact the obsolescence of whatever cybersecurity measures particular states might have already undertaken. Some scholars have attempted to address ambiguity by advocating agreements that would prohibit all cyberweapons except those that would be attributable and only reversible effects.76 To some extent for another, this type of advocacy merely replaces one set of obstacles for the problems of attaining as a comprehensive ban would be replaced by challenges defining what “attributability” entails or what attacks are universally interpreted as having “reversible” effects.

The People’s Republic of China began working to establish a sovereignty-oriented set of regulations about proper electronic communications early. In December 1997, its Ministry of Public Safety banned information that the government deemed to be "incite research" to the country’s laws or the socialist system, that prompted "hatred or . . . harms . unity," that propagates rumors or . . . destroys social order," or advances a broad range of criminal activities. In a globally connected landscape, products are rarely “sourced” in a single country. Instead, they are very likely to be assembled from components that have been built in another country, from materials that may in in turn been imported, and that the assembled product has been programmed with different software designed by different companies that may each have an international presence. The diverse origins of a single product can mean that several entities may have an opportunity to manipulate the functionality or fidelity of the end product.
