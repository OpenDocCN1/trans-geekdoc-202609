# THE PURPOSE OF THE BOOK

This book aims to examine several of the notable clichés—even mythologies—which have taken hold regarding the cyber conflict. Excellent works on war and technology, as Martin van Creveld's classic *Technology and War*, have explained that "the idea that the war is neither self-evident, not necessarily correct, nor ever very old," his suggestion that the concept gained ground in the mid-twentieth century because of World War II.⁸ Other scholars speaking specifically to a conflict waged through the cyber domain contributed significant points to the conversation. Martin Libicki's description of "cybersecurity and system compromise" as "games of competitive learning" harken to classical interpretations of military struggle as a contest between actively interacting forces.
