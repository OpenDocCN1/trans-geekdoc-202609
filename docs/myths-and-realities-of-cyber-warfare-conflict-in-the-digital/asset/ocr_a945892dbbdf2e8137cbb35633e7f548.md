# DEFINING “WAR” NARROWLY

Many authorities on cybersecurity issues prefer to define war more narrowly. Reluctance to engage in acts deemed cyberwar frequently accompanies these views, although some of these scholars go so far as virtually declare the concept of cyber and exploration of systems on one hand (which are deemed legitimate) and the destruction or manipulation of systems on the other (considered illicit).

Hereas hack back involves the defender’s actually tracing and potentially retaliating against an assailant, more passive alternatives exist as well. One time-honored approach is the use of so-called honeypots. Borrowed from the physical domains’, notably during the Cold War, or of interpersonal seduction to gain intelligence against unwitting targets, cyber honeypots attract an intruder to explore red herring elements in a defender’s system. They are maintained specifically to watch for intruders, since to an outsider they may resemble a valuable target but to insiders, they are extraneous or false elements in a system. Cyber honeypots come in several forms, including honeyports designed to “detect network scanning and enumeration attempts,” and honeywords that simulate an administrator’s passwords. Honeypots quickly alert defenders to the presence and activity of an intruder, and this helps defenders work to prevent future intrusions and minimize the impact of future incidents.”^24
