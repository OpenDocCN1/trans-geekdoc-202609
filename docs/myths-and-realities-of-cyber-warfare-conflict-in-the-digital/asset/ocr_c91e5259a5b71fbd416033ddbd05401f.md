This realization seems to explain the intent in conceptualizing the role of resilience in cyber defense. Resilience—the ability to continue despite intrusions—has been suggested as a way to raise the bar for attackers,35 and it is sometimes even implied to serve a different function. As this book’s author has previously illustrated through historical parallels, defenses are built with an eye toward delaying rather than perpetually defeating an attacker; the time that is purchased must be translated into positive actions with to reverse the situation confronting the defender.36

This positive action has been hypothesized as operating successfully despite a “degraded or denied environment." Different researchers and service leaders have suggested that this might entail the application of the electronic warfare concept of the war reserve providing latent capabilities to be unleashed in the event of conflict, or military units determining which components are most vital to prioritize protection so that they can persist and allow units “fighting through the attack” to continue operating.37

Conti and Raymond point out that, far from being “just resident software,” vulnerabilities also appear throughout “firmware, network protocols, security procedures, human users, physical security,” and elsewhere. In beyond software coding, complexity breeds markets, as does a rush to market or to field a technology and aaundiced attitude regarding inconvenient processes to limit errors.38 These factors apply across the spectrum of cybersecurity issues and are applicable points regarding security even broadly.

On the technical side of the cybersecurity equation lie weaknesses that pose the prerequisite gaps in the armor through which an exploit can be used. The fact that software imperfections can be leveraged through technical attack explains the philosophy of blaming the victims for technical cyberattacks, as described in Chapter 3. But “everything electronically typically has a vulnerability in it.
