As a result, authoritative voices like Libicki argue that "operational cyber is tailor-made for surprise and a poor choice for repeated attacks," "because" it is difficult to surprise the same sysadmin twice in the same way." Similarly, Stiennon asserts that "once used, a cyber weapon quickly becomes ineffective for use until the victim discovers the targeted vulnerability and deploys corrections." A cybersecurity panel found that either the use or the diversion of the vulnerabilities has the effect of "rendering the capability useless in the future" because "cyber weapon capabilities generally depend on the exploitation of unknown target vulnerabilities."80

Strictly speaking, it is correct that a sysadmin will be more likely to identify the attack the second it comes. However, applying this invites problematic oversights.
