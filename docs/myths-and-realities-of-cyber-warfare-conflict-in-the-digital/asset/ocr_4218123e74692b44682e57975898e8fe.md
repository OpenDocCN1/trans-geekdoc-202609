# WHERE ESPIONAGE FITS

Where scholar Paul Rosenweig observes that "in cyberspace, the line between intelligence and war is ill-defined, at best," Hayden is even more blunt "the distinctions break down entirely in the cyber domain."55 This complicates the implications of other concepts about espionage, and paint the cyber domain as a dangerous landscape. The Tallinn Manual 2.0, published in 2017 and built on the shoulders of its predecessor, declares that "the International Group of Experts agreed that customary international law does not prohibit espionage per sc." Thus, although the spy may be virtually indistinguishable from the cyber warrior, the former's activities cannot be universally condemned in an abstract sense. The year Tallinn Manual 2.0 appeared, Hayden himself added, "the theft of data is acceptable international practice. It is honorable national practice."56
