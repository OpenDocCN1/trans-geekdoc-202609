Predictions that the LikeWars of tomorrow will be fought by highly intelligent, inscrutable algorithms spewing convincing propaganda and that autonomous systems “become cyber fighters on the future battlefield”⁷¹ rely on contingency for their realization. Rather than beuiled by apparently inexorable tendencies, it is vital to keep in mind that decisions—made in the context of contemporary circumstances and the ways that they are understood—will exert a powerful impact on whether and how the predictions about tomorrow’s wars will become realized.

This book has examined several of the key clichés and mythologies about cyber warfare, including notions about cyberweapons instantaneous speed, having a reach that reduces to irrelevance, inherently cheap and widely available, being limited to a single user, exerting only reversible effects, or representing an offensive superiority. Like generalizations, mythologies often stem from elements of fact. The contradictory aspects of various mythologies, including certainly about cyber conflict, illustrates how complex issues can suffer from over-reduction.

This book also sought to consider how social media has been refashioned for weaponized uses, as well as certain forms have been effectively commandeered for other functions. The human need for connection, by means that promise to help fill that need, creates an environment teeming with potentially valuable data that can be piled, aggregated, and leveraged for monetization. The leveraging of data stands at the core of how such networking services could be provided without overt frees. The flow of data defines the internet, and the model of many internet giants embodies the leveraging of data. It should come as little surprise that lucrative resources also hold potential strategic uses as well.

Martin Libicki’s advice that “it pays to be serious but not desperate”⁷² holds true struggling in the cyber domain. Although the domain is largely defined by its technologies, challenges and dynamics in cyberdomain security are not confined to technical aspects. This is why “security will be ‘solved’ in the foreseeable future”⁷³—or indeed lie beyond that. Assertions that “how we prepare for enemies might help to invent them” carries a degree of truth, in the sense that the actions shape the subsequent context and impact others’ perceptions and how they prioritize their alternative options. Actions within conflict similarly affect ideas defining the appropriate and the acceptable.⁷⁴
