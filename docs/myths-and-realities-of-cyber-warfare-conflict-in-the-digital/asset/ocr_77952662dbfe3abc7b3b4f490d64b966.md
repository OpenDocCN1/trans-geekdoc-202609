Put simply, tankers lacking tanks and redundancy planes vastly less combat power when they become foot-bound infantry; although they are not rendered helpless, their effectiveness is definitely impacted. Libicki is right about encouraging readers not lead to conclusions about cyberwar that are reminiscent of interwar strategic bombing advocates who insisted that airpower could induce fatal industrial paralysis by dropping high explosives on factories. This pitfall can be avoided while acknowledging that attacks will exert a potentially significant impact without being totally devastating.

Greater perspective on the limitations of a cyberattack can help mitigate an excess faith or fear in the power of attack in cyberspace—ideas about defenders' own options and opportunities are valuable tools, developing response plans help put into place to take in response to an event that will occur at the time chosen by the attacker, and infrastructure redundancy will provide a cushion against some attacks and can buy time for a defender to put its contingency plans into action.¹⁵⁵ International cooperation could be another useful, but difficult to orchestrate, tool. Cybersecurity advocates have observed that the NATO partners prioritized cybersecurity threats before witnessing the evidently politically motivated and orchestrated DDoS attacks against Estonia in 2007. Not only did cybersecurity experts provide assistance to Estonia during this high-profile watershed event, but NATO by the following year reevaluation of the importance of cybersecurity; although not considering the cyberattacks to trigger the NATO charter's Article 5 clause was established and the Estonian capital city of Tallinn as new NATO Cyber Security Centre of Excellence was selected as headquarters in a point meant to signal solidarity with the new NATO member that had been subjected to the wave of cyberattacks.¹⁵⁶

For the future, Conti and Raymond predict an ongoing "race for advanced capabilities" which defenders will continue "lag significantly behind" While this seems to predict a future that looks much like the present, they note that future research will lead to an explanation of opportunity areas for defenders who have so far been overlooked. For example, "deception isn't only for the offense,"⁵⁷ and a more conscious use of deception by defenders could help them withstand attacks and gain intelligence about attacking adversaries. This would turn on its head notions that an attacker would have better in-the-moment intelligence about an attack than would the defender. Nor is the concept without precedent, since the development of a honeypot on the Berkeley computer system played a key role in enabling researchers to trace the 1986 Cuckoo's Egg hackers (the first famous case of strategic online espionage) to their origins in East Germany.
