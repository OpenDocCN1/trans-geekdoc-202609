While evidence aligns with views that information operations failed to impress into the election itself, subsequent instability such as the “yellow vest” riots in Paris detract from notions that the United States was uniquely vulnerable among democratic societies. If imposing chaos seems an unlikely policy goal rather than an intermediate step toward establishing more specific policy scenario, perhaps the missing piece involves the sovereignty issue. Journalists experienced in Russian affairs have suggested that a long-standing goal in Moscow has been ensuring continual backdoor access into social media and online communications services, ranging from Facebook and Twitter to Gmail and YouTube.55 One is left to wonder whether seeding discord might be meant as a way to impose chaos, and whether chaotic might be used not only for facilitating short-term gains such as the re-annexation of Crimea but also in the long term confusing democratic countries and capitalist institutions into accepting and embracing ideas that a shift from a multi-stakeholder system into one more in line with Kremlin conceptions of cyber sovereignty.
