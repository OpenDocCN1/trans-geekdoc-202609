# ZERO-DAYS

Most unfamiliar activities in cyberspace use old tricks rather than created malware. The old tricks are tried in a pattern reminiscent of a chain letter: most chain letters may be thrown away, but a handful will be sent forward, and thus scaling the number sent out will increase the chances that some will succeed. Defenses like anti-virus suit for previously identified malware and block these attacks. For an attacker to increase the chances of a particular target falling to an attack, the attack could be developed in a way that skips recognition. Developing new malware costs time and money, and developing new malware that takes advantage of a software vulnerability that has not been identified requires even more time to do—or money to purchase from a researcher who has quietly discovered a previously unknown vulnerability, or "zero-day."
