A common chord among many cybersecurity thinkers is the consequent need for cyber education. As noted in Chapter 4, cyber warriors should not necessarily come from the STEM fields of science, technology, engineering, and mathematics—and a STEM background does not denote a cyber defender or attacker. However, technical understanding is crucial in the next generation of Facebook creators after the training of security-conscious programmers raises concern for authorities such as Yannakorgorgos, who also notes that “most cyberspace users . . have only fleeting knowledge of the underlying technologies” they rely on.24 Suggested solutions include partnerships across sectors so that cybersecurity experts can not only update their skills but also gain fresh vantage points.25 Such concepts depend on foundations of trust and interoperability that underpin the establishment of the cyber world, and yet tend toward hiding the skills of existing specialists, whereas increasing the pool remains an elusive goal. A distressing parallelism of Winston Churchill’s comment after winning the Battle of Britain, Ron Aldbert noted (before many of the tribulations recently witnessed with social media manipulation and ransomware),“never before we have been surrounded by so much technology upon which we depend, and never before we have also known so little about how that technology actually works.”26

# POLLUTION OF THE INFOSPHERE

Triangulating the dangers of an infosphere-turned-battlespace pose challenges. Singer and Brooke argue that “the internet has become a battlefield,” and evidence supports that contention. The exact meaning remains a subject debated by various analysts and experts of cybersecurity and military affairs, however. Many figures depict the more extreme visions of cyber as bygone. While Rid has worked to reduce fears about the potential of cyber warfare, Healey has contrasted the appreciation of advanced cyber intrusions with apparently overblown predictions of cyber warfare. Other cybersecurity experts have noted that although a growing attack surface and lagging security practices have led to increasingly massive data breaches, breach statistics do not prove whether attackers are becoming more talented.27
