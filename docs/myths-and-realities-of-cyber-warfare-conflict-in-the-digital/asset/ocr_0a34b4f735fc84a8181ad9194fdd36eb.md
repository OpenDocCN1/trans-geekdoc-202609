# INSTANTANEOUS SPEED

Perhaps the most important of the temporal mythologies regarding cyberwar deals with time itself. The actions targeting Georgia in 2008, coinciding with the kinetic combat between that country and Russian military units, was for example dubbed by some as “the internet of blitzkrieg.\¹ This in itself is a significant characterization. “Blitzkrieg,” the infamous use of “lighting war” by Nazi forces against Allied powers, evokes such visceral imagery that it has become almost a cliché. In a sense, this illustrates the nature of mythologies. What journalists from neutral nations saw in 1939 and 1940 and called “blitzkrieg” was, for the actual practitioners, something different. Certainly, it was a “quick and lively” approach to campaigning that sought to leverage speed in order to sow confusion within the enemy and facilitate an attacker’s exploitation of opportunities² The instantaneous speed of German advances in World War II was itself an impression,—a methodology.
