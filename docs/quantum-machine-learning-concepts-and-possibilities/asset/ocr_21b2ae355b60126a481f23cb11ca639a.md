# 6.2.11.1 When do barren plateaus occur?

Barren plateaus are not universal, they depend on specific circuit choices and cost functions. Key findings include:

+   - **Global cost functions** (e.g. fidelity between full states) are more susceptible.
- **Deep random circuits** (or those approximating 2-designs) show exponential gradient decay.
- **Unstructured ansatze** with too much expressivity suffer worse than task-specific circuits.
- **Problem-invariant initializations** (e.g. all-zero angles) often land directly in flat regions.

Recent research has formalized these results and identified several conditions under which barren plateaus arise.

# 6.2.11.2 Avoiding barren plateaus

Several strategies have been developed to mitigate this phenomenon:

+   - **Local cost functions**. Observables that depend on a small number of qubits (e.g. local Hamiltonian terms)
- **Layerwise training**. Gradually grow the circuit depth during training. This keeps the parameter landscape smoother in early stages [33].
- **Problem-inspired ansatze**. Architectures developed from the structure of the physical system (e.g. hardware efficiency or QAOA circuits) [34, 35].
- **Parameter initialization schemes**. Break symmetry using random initializations or schemes as identity blocks or shallow pretraining [36].
- **Quantum gradient methods**. Incorporate curvature information to traverse plateaus more effectively.

```
$$\mathcal{L} = \langle Z_{i} Z_{i+1} \rangle.$
```

This prevents averaging over the full state and preserves meaningful gradients [32].

These insights help to co-design circuits and training routines to avoid untrainable regimes, an especially important consideration for scaling to larger quantum systems.

# 6.3 Architectural variants: QCNNs and quantum graph-based models

Variational quantum classifiers can be realized through a variety of circuit architectures, each imposing different inductive biases and capabilities. Two prominent classes of models that extend beyond simple layered ansatze are **quantum convolutional neural networks (QCNNs)** [37] and **quantum graph-based models**, such as **quantum graph neural networks (QGNNs)** [38].
