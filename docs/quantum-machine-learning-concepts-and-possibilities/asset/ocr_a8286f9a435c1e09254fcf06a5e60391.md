#  4.3.2 Evaluating kernels

In practice, the quantum kernel $\kappa(x, x')$ is evaluated using a quantum circuit. Two
common strategies are:

+   * The swap test: A circuit that estimates the overlap $|\langle\phi(x)|\phi(x')angle|^2$ by using an
ancilla qubit to interfere with two quantum states. This requires controlled-swap
gates and multiple copies of the states.
* Direct fidelity estimation: If both states $|\phi(x)angle$ and $|\phi(x')angle$ are prepared on the
device, the fidelity can be estimated by preparing the state $U_\delta(x)^\dagger U_\delta(x')|0angle$ and measuring the probability of obtaining the all-zero state.

After computing pairwise kernel values over a training set, one obtains the
kernel matrix $K$, where $K_{ij} = \kappa(x_i, x_j)$. This matrix is then used as input to a
classical kernel method such as an SVM, yielding a hybrid quantum-classical
learning pipeline.

#  4.3.3 Training with quantum kernels

Once the kernel matrix is computed, the training procedure follows that of a
standard classical kernel method. For instance, in an SVM classifier, the optimiza-
problem involves solving a quadratic,

```
$$\min_{\alpha} \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j} \alpha_i\alpha_j\kappa(x_i, x_j) - \sum_i \alpha_i y_i, \quad (4.14)$
```

subject to the constraints $0 \le \alpha_i \le C$ and $\sum_i \alpha_i y_i = 0$, where $\alpha_i$ are dual variables
and $C$ is a regularization parameter.

Importantly, this is performed on a classical computer. The quantum
device is only used to compute the kernel matrix $K$, which captures the geometry of
data in Hilbert space.

#  4.3.4 Generalization and expressivity

A key question in quantum computation is whether quantum kernels offer any
statistical or computational advantage over classical kernels. Several recent years
have analysed this question through the lens of:

+   * Kernel alignment: Measures how well the quantum kernel matrix aligns with
t ideal kernel separates the data. Poor alignment can result in
underperformance compared to classical kernels.
* Inductive bias and reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS): Every positive-\inite kernel defines an RKHS. The hypothesis class of quantum kernel
methods is constrained by the RKHS associated with $\kappa(x, x')$, which
determines the types of functions that can be learned.
* Hardness of classical approximation: If $\kappa(x, x')$ is difficult to approximate
classically (e.g. related to problems such as random circuit sampling or
instantaneous quantum polynomial-time (IQP) models), the quantum kernel
could provide a separation from classical methods.
