A critical challenge facing expressive quantum models is the onset of barren plateaus. This raises fundamental questions about the tradeoff between expressivity and trainability. Highly expressive models may have sufficient capacity to learn complex distributions, yet may be untrainable without architectural constraints. Research is needed to identify the conditions under which expressive models remain trainable and to design ansatz formats that balance circuit depth, entanglement, and gradient flow.

Beyond classical data, quantum learning theory must also address the task of learning from quantum data, states, and measurements that arise in quantum physics and information processing. Theoretical tools such as tomography and randomized measurement protocols offer pathways to learning in this setting, but more rigorous understanding of the sample complexity, generalization, and trainability of quantum-data layers is required.

Finally, most existing theory focuses on worst-case guarantees. However, practical quantum learning applications are often structured, and real-world data opens opportunities for distribution-dependent analysis, smoothed complexity bounds, and average-case generalization. Developing such theory could bridge the gap between quantum advantage in principle and practice.
