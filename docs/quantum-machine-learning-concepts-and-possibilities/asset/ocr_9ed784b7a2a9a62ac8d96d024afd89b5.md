Together, these challenges represent a significant research frontier. Understanding and overcoming them is to fully realize the potential of variational quantum algorithms in quantum chemistry, combinatorial optimization, and machine learning.

While variational quantum algorithms face a lack of obstacles in their practical deployment, they also offer a broad landscape of opportunities—both in terms of algorithmic innovation and real-world applications. As researchers develop a deeper understanding of the limitations of NISQ hardware and the expressive capabilities of parameterized quantum circuits, new directions continue to emerge that may unlock scalable quantum advantage.

One particular opportunity lies in the development of problem-inspired ansatz designs instead of relying on generic or hardware-efficient structures, problem-specific designs can leverage the symmetry, structure, or prior knowledge of the physical system being simulated. In quantum chemistry, for example, the UCC ansatz is grounded in well-established methods from classical electronic structure theory and can encode meaningful correlations between orbitals. The exact implementation of UCC is often too deep for current circuits, and Trotterized variants offer tractable approximations. In combinatorial optimization, opportunities exist in the incorporation of diabatic terms to improve convergence, as well as modifications to the mixing operators that explicitly enforce constraints. These constraints guide the optimization effectively while preserving feasibility [48, 49].

Beyond fixed ansatzes, adaptive variational algorithms offer a dynamic approach to circuit construction. Methods such as ADAPT-VOE and operator-based expansions start from a minimal ansatz and iteratively add operators based on gradient information or energy heuristics. These adaptive frameworks hold great promise for discovering compact, expressive circuits tailored to the specific instance of problem, potentially avoiding the overhead associated with expressive or randomly initialized circuits. Moreover, by expanding the circuit only when necessary, adaptive approaches mitigate issues related to barren plateaus and overparameterization.

Variational algorithms are also fertile ground for advances in quantum-aware optimization strategies. Unlike traditional machine learning scenarios where computation is efficient variational quantum algorithms must contend with noisy evaluations and stochastic gradients. This has motivated the development of optimization strategies that are robust to quantum noise, such as stochastic shift estimators [50], robust gradient descent [51], and Bayesian optimization [52]. Further opportunities exist in exploring quantum-aware optimizer scheduling, where hyperparameters adapt dynamically to noise or signal strength, and in combining classical surrogate models with quantum evaluations to accelerate convergence.

Another important opportunity lies in the strength of expressivity and entanglement in variational circuits. A growing body of theoretical work seeks to understand the trade-offs between circuit expressivity, trainability, and generalization capacity [53, 54].
