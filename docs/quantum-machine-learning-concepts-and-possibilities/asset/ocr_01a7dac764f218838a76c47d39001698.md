# 7.2.3 Quantum generative adversarial networks

Quantum generative adversarial networks (QGANs) [22] adapt the classical GAN framework [12] into the quantum setting, with a quantum generator and a discriminator that may be classical or quantum. The goal is for the generator to produce quantum states indistinguishable from the target data distribution.

The generator prepares a parameterized quantum state:

```
$|\psi_g(\theta)\rangle = U_g(\theta)|0\rangle^{\otimes n}$
```

which is either:

+   - Measured to generate classical bitstrings, or
- Kept as a quantum state for comparison with quantum data.

The discriminator distinguishes between real and generated data. In a fully quantum QGAN, it is a parameterized observable or variational circuit $U_d(\phi)$ followed by a measurement. The adversarial objective is

```
$\min_{\theta}\max_{\phi}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim P_{data}}[D(x; \phi)]-\mathbb{E}_{x\sim P_{	heta}}[D(x; \phi)]$
```

# 7.2.4 Quantum variational autoencoders

Quantum variational autoencoders (QVAE) [23] extend classical VAEs by using quantum circuits in the encoder and/or decoder networks. The basic idea is to map classical inputs to a distribution over quantum latent variables and decode them to reconstruct or generate new data.

+   - **Encoder:** Maps data $x$ to a parameterized quantum state $|z(\phi_x)\rangle$, possibly by encoding $x$ into gate angles.
- **Decoder:** A parameterized quantum circuit $U_d(\theta)$ maps the latent state back to a distribution over outputs.

The training objective is the evidence lower bound (ELBO):

```
$\mathbb{L}_{ELBO} = \mathbb{E}_{q(z|x)}[\log p(x|z)] - KL[q(z|x) || p(z)]$
```

where $q(z|x)$ and $p(z)$ may be encoded by quantum circuits, and evaluation often requires classical post-processing.
