Variational quantum models (e.g. PQCs or QNNs) are more challenging to analyse directly using classical complexity measures. Instead, generalization bounds are often obtained through algorithmic stability arguments or PAC-Bayesian frameworks applied to the output distributions of quantum measurements. Some bounds rely only on the structure of the QFIM, which governs the circuit's sensitivity to parameters.

This highlights the need for both expressive and trainable architectures when designing quantum learning models.

# 8.3 Positive results from quantum learning

This section highlights provable contexts where quantum learners offer advantages over classical counterparts. These advantages can manifest in reduced complexity, improved computational efficiency, or the ability to realize hypothesis classes that are hard to emulate classically.

# 8.3.1 Query and time complexity speedups

Several results from quantum learning theory establish that, under specific oracle models or problem instances, quantum algorithms can outperform classical ones in terms of query complexity and runtime. These separations are typically shown in idealized models such as access to quantum membership or evaluation results.

# 8.3.1.1 Learning juntas

In the classical setting, learning a $k$-junta (a Boolean function depending on $k \ll n$ out of $n$ variables) requires $\Omega(k \log n)$ queries in the worst case. Quantum algorithms can reduce this to $O(k)$ with Fourier sampling or Grover-type searches [5].
