# 8.2 performance measure: sample complexity and generalization

This investigates how expressive quantum hypothesis translate into learnable models. We develop bounds on sample complexity and generalization error, adapting classical learning-theoretic concepts to quantum settings. We also highlight the subtleties introduced by quantum-data access and quantum circuits, and summarize known results on both upper and lower bounds for quantum learning.

# 8.2.1 PAC and agnostic learning

In the probably correct (PAC) learning model [3, 4], the learner aims to find a hypothesis with lower generalization error using only a limited number of training samples drawn i.i.d. from a fixed distribution. Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a hypothesis class mapping inputs $x \in \mathcal{X}$ to labels $y \in \{0, 1\}$ or real values in regression. The learner has access to a dataset $\mathcal{S} = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^n \sim \mathcal{D}$, and the goal is to find $h \in \mathcal{H}$ such that the population risk $R(h)$ is close to the minimum possible risk with $\mathcal{H}$:

```
$R(h) = \mathbb{E}_{x,y \sim \mathcal{D}} [l(h(x), y)], (8.1)
```

In the agnostic learning model, we do not assume the data labels are consistent with any hypothesis in $\mathcal{H}$. The learner competes with the best possible hypothesis in the class.
Quantum models can be analysed in this framework with minor adjustments. The hypothesis $h_0$ now results from a measurement on a quantum circuit:

```
$h_0(x) = \mathbb{E}[M|U_0 U_{enc}(x)|0], (8.2)
```

where the expectation is taken with respect to the Born rule over repeated shots. The training dataset may be classical (a list of $(x_i, y_i)$) or quantum, consisting of access to pure or mixed quantum states encoding the data-label pairs.
Two quantum-data models are often considered:

+   * **Classical access:** The learner receives $(x_i, y_i)$ drawn i.i.d. from $\mathcal{D}$.
* **Quantum examples:** The learner has access to the quantum superposition stat,

```
$|psi_y\rangle = \sum_{x} \sqrt{p(x, y)}|x, y\rangle, (8.3)
```

possibly along with oracle related operations.

The PAC learning guarantees under both access models often follow similar forms, with dependence on a complexity measure such as VC dimension or effective dimension.
