# 2.2 Dirac notation
Dirac's notation is a formalism used in quantum mechanics to describe quantum states and the time evolution of operators. In Dirac's notation, a vector $v'$ is represented as $|vangle$. It is based on the use of the bra-ket notation, where a ket represents a column vector and a bra is its conjugate transpose. Thus, a qubit is in the superposition of states 0 and 1, can be represented by the vector or state $|\psiangle$. Such state can be represented as the linear combination between $|0angle$ and $|1angle$ in the following way:

```
$|\psiangle = \alpha|0angle + eta|1angle$
```

The coefficients $\alpha$ and $eta$ are complex numbers $(\alpha, eta \in \mathbb{C})$. The bra and ket represent vectors in a two-dimensional Hilbert space where the states $|0angle$ and $|1angle$ form an orthonormal basis. The bra-ket notation allows one to represent inner products, outer products, and other operations on these in a concise and intuitive way. For example, in this orthonormal basis, we can write $|\psiangle$ as

```
$|\psiangle = egin{bmatrix} \alpha \ eta \end{bmatrix}$
```

The physical interpretation of $\alpha$ and $eta$ are the probabilities $|\alpha|^2$ or $|eta|^2$ of finding our state in $|0angle$ or $|1angle$, respectively, after a measurement. Furthermore, these probabilities are normalized to unity. Finally, in Dirac's notation, the bra-ket notation is used to represent quantum states and operators. For example, a quantum state $|\psiangle$ can be represented as a ket and an operator $A$ can be represented as a bra-ket pair $\langle\psi|A|\psiangle$.

# 2.3 Bloch sphere
The Bloch sphere is a graphical representation of the state of a two-level quantum system, such as a qubit. The Bloch sphere is a sphere of unit radius, with the north pole representing the state $|0angle$ and the south pole representing the state $|1angle$. Any state is a point on the surface of the Bloch sphere. For example, the state $|+angle$, which is a superposition of $|0angle$ and $|1angle$, is represented by the point on the equator that is halfway between the north and south poles.
