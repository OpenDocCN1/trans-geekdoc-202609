# 7.6 Open challenges and future directions

Quantum generative modeling presents both exciting prospects and substantial technical hurdles. As a rapidly evolving field of quantum computing, probabilistic modeling, and optimization, it sits at the intersection of quantum computing. This section discusses **open challenges** currently limiting the scalability and practicality of quantum generative models, followed by a summary of **opportunities** for advancing the field, both theoretically and experimentally.

# 7.6.1 Challenges

Training quantum generative models often requires optimizing non-convex, high-dimensional landscapes. In variational settings, in particular with deep or uncertain ansatz, gradients can vanish exponentially with the number of qubits. This limits scalability for circuits initialized randomly or without prior knowledge. Understanding the relationship between circuit architecture, expressivity, and learnability remains an open research area, particularly when targeting hard distributions.

**QGANs** are not immune to the failure modes that classical generative models encounter. For example, QGANs and QBMs can suffer from **mode collapse**, where the model learns to generate only a subset of the target distribution's support. This is exacerbated by limited expressivity in shallow circuits or poor entangling strategies, and by the inability of some training objectives to fully penalize degeneracy.
