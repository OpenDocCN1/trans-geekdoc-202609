
# Be careful searching for machine learning growth strategies in a search engine

Be careful searching for machine learning growth strategies in a search engine. That one is going to follow you around for a while. You are going to get all sorts of advertisements back [2]. During my journey down that search path, I read an estimate that the machine learning market is going to end up around $8.81 billion in 2022 [3]. That estimate seemed interesting enough at first glance. That might have been due to the context of a recent article I read about Google building out something called Model Search, which is essentially an open-source platform for finding machine learning models [4]. That really caught my attention. A ton of use cases exist, and models have been trained for a lot of them. Being able to get going without the cost of training a model yourself could kickstart your effort. I had an idea about it that I’ll share in a couple weeks. It could turn into a project at some point.

## Executing a plan can be very rewarding

Executing a plan can be very rewarding. When that plan is tied to budget-level KPIs with solid ROI, the rewards are even quantifiable. You have to plan to grow based on successful ROI. That happens when spending is tied to budget items geared toward successful outcomes. Part of that is about knowing your KPIs and managing the process along the way, and the other part is about having the fiscal discipline to build ROI models and execute that strategy. Yeah, planned growth is harder than it sounds. You will find that building out a layered plan to get things done involves operationalizing your strategy vision and executing it in a planful way. Some of that is low-hanging fruit for the organization, and other parts are more complex algorithmic use cases [5].

## Getting a really solid list of the use cases where growth could be driving is a challenge worth attempting

Getting a really solid list of the use cases where growth could be driving is a challenge worth attempting. That is more than just engaging in cost avoidance or reducing ongoing operational expenses; it is about finding the places where growth is really possible. That is a core part of understanding the business and ultimately should be very closely tied to how the budget is defined. A solid strategic plan would tie the right investments to the right places going forward. That sounds simple as a sentence but is difficult in practice. The places where growth was possible could change and shift within the year. Some efforts to move forward will be more successful than others. That is why you need the right KPIs to drive and guide evidence of progress or to shine the light on areas that need help from the leadership team to stay on track.

# Substack Week 8: Is the machine learning we need everywhere now?

Published on March 19, 2021

Just as you read that title that the machine learning we need is everywhere now, some of you looked at your Apple Watch. You don’t have to look very far to see some application of machine learning in your daily life. People for years have been making the argument that machine learning is everywhere [1]. Now if machine learning has become that obvious in your daily existence, you can only imagine how deeply it is starting to get ingrained in the business world [2]. Pretty much every vendor, every application, and every underlying technology are investing in some combination of machine learning and artificial intelligence [3]. This creates some interesting situations where you have machine learning models working in unanticipated compounding and confounding ways. Yeah, I’m really proud of that last sentence. I’m invested in the combination of talking about how models can be compounding and confounding. That is probably just a loquacious way of saying either the models have working results or conflicts develop.

## We have reached a point where businesses may not be aware of all the different machine learning elements running in the organization

We have reached a point where businesses may not be aware of all the different machine learning elements running in the organization. They could even be running at cross purposes. A chatbot could be trying to answer simple questions and close out the conversation as quickly as possible with very little friction for the customer. Another element of machine learning could be running to evaluate what options, opportunities, or suggestions the organization has for the customer, and that entire line of conversation might never be engaged. You have to remember that targeted optimization algorithms could be at odds with an engagement strategy. You also could have different parts or programs or different elements of a complex workflow working at very different purposes and outcomes along the way. That type of confounded interdependency will create havoc for your overall machine learning strategy. It is probably evidence of either a failed machine learning strategy or no real strategy at all.

# Substack Week 9: Valuing machine learning use cases based on scale

Published on March 26, 2021

This week we are jumping right into valuing machine learning use cases based on scale. You will find that most of the time scale is everything when it comes to adding value. Being able to quickly scale up your machine learning solution to provide value in a workflow is generally where the benefits begin to accelerate [1]. Very few examples exist in the wild where only a handful of interactions are going to bring the majority of value. Those might be use cases where machine learning might not be ready to handle such a critical part of your business. Sometimes applying machine learning to a use case is not a viable path to success [2]. You have to evaluate and invest in machine learning use cases with high potential ROI. The value derived from the work being undertaken is going to directly line up with ROI.

## Conceptually I have been breaking down the categories of machine learning use cases into three buckets

Conceptually I have been breaking down the categories of machine learning use cases into three buckets:

- **“Things you can call,”** such as external API services
- **“Places you can be,”** or ecosystems where you can build out your footprint, such as `AWS`, `GCP`, `Azure`, and many others that are springing up for machine learning operations (`MLOps`) delivery
- **“Building something yourself,”** such as open-source and self-tooled solutions

That categorization helps me think about where things for the use case are going to happen. It is a tactical rather than a strategic question. Each of the three buckets will receive more coverage later in this series [3]. In terms of API services, you can scale very quickly. You can go out right now and get access to several API machine learning solutions on `AWS`, `Azure`, or `GCP` without any real barriers. The level of friction to enter that external-technology-driven use case is relatively low. Seriously, these solutions are really low-friction options with highly scalable infrastructure on the back end. People are using them all over the place right now [4].
