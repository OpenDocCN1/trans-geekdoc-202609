The Democratization of Wealth

What if all the wealth in the world would be distributed more fair and equitably to all people in every part of the world?

It sounds like a utopian dream but reality is about to happen.

The game changer that will make this happen is blockchain, the underlying technology brought into the limelight by Bitcoin – the first digital currency of the world.

Blockchain is a distributed digital ledger on which transactions are recorded chronologically on a global network of nodes (computers).

All transactions are public, transparent, time-stamped and cannot be reversed or tampered with, and most importantly, the entire network is not owned or controlled by anyone.

Unlike blockchain, almost all data on the internet of today is stored in “data silos”, giant servers owned and controlled by the likes of Microsoft, Google, Facebook, Apple and Amazon.

These tech giants not only store all data, they also sell data to anyone that is interested to use it for any purpose imaginable. Most is for commercial purposes but recent scandals involving Facebook and Cambridge Analytica has cast a dark shadow on the usage of private data for commercial purposes and profits. And in the process, these tech titans have become “untouchable” because the vast data these companies have amassed is not only of commercial value, but of immense strategic importance such as for spying and surveillance.

US federal agencies such as the NSA, CIA and FBI can scour this data and use it to their advantage as well as to the disadvantage of others. Other agencies such as the Pentagon, IRS and SEC also have a vested interest in having global data stored within US territory, only accessible by the US government, inaccessible by any outsiders.

Because of this strategic importance, all American tech giants are virtually immune to prosecution, any privacy breaches are simply fined in the millions or billions, largely uncontested and paid by whichever US tech company happens to break the law.

Google and Facebook are the leaders in data privacy breach fines, having spent billions in fines and lawyers’ fees, but this has not affected their finances at all. After all, Apple, Google and Microsoft are “platinum” members of the coveted “Trillion Dollar Club”.

Crypto Anarchists

In the early 1980’s and throughout the 1990’s, the “cypherpunk” movement started to gain ground and spread across the world.

Sometime in 1992, the first cypherpunks - Eric Hughes, Timothy C. May and John Gilmore - founded a small group at Gilmore’s company Cygnus Solutions in the San Francisco bay area.

Cypherpunks, a new term derived by combining the words “cipher” and “cyberpunk”, are sort of “crypto anarchists” - politely referred to as activists - that are advocating the use of advanced cryptography and privacy-enhancing technologies for social and political change.

Crypto anarchist and cypherpunk Timothy May described in his “Crypto Anarchist Manifesto” how the use of cryptography will allow for the creation of a system in which people will be able to interact directly, with more self-sovereign within an “anarcho-capitalist” society, free from the influence of governments.

To spread the word and get activists involved, a cypherpunk mailing list was started that by 1994 had 700 subscribers. It evolved to a very active forum that included scholar and technical discussions ranging over computer science, cryptography, mathematics, political and philosophical. Occasionally, the forum was subject to a barrage of attacks and personal arguments which made it very lively and contributed to its growth, by 1997 it had reached more than 2000 subscribers.

In the same year, Jim Choate and Igor Chudov set up the “Cypherpunks Distributed Remailer”, a network of independent mailing list nodes intended to eliminate the “SPOF’ (Single Point of Failure), which simply is a part of a system that, if it fails, will stop the entire system from working.

For example, a blackout of the electricity grid or interruption of water supply.

SPOFs are undesirable in any system, be it an industrial system or software application that has the objective to be highly reliable and “available” at all times. As a result, systems can be made more robust by adding a “redundancy” in all potential SPOFs.

Edward Snowden talked about the dangers of SPOFs on the sole repository of information, a/k/a “data silos”, controlled by “Big Tech” and used by governments as well as “Big Brother” in the world of spying, surveillance and citizens’ control.

Centralization is where one entity controls the data on its network and that can result in the dreaded SPOF of the system.

 

Information Asymmetry

In economic terms, information asymmetry is the ultimate key advantage to have; it’s the ticket to vast profits, and for governments, of strategic importance.

In the financial world, information asymmetry and “trust arbitrage” - leveraging the trust factor in money/asset/wealth management as well as in the processing of financial transactions - can yield massive profits.

More importantly, information asymmetry centralizes knowledge and wealth in legacy financial institutions that can overcharge freely and thus enhance their profit margins to the detriment of the customer.

FinTech (financial technology) flips the equation; the customer is the purveyor of his/her own wealth and the financial institution is merely the safe keeper, guardian or custodian of funds, providing financial advice that is coupled with performance metrics.

The challenge for the financial world is to find a balance between centralized control and decentralized consensus, while at all times serving the customer and safeguarding their financial interests.

Banks, brokerages and financial institutions will argue in favor of the “status-quo”, that is, centralized control by government and central banks.

Millennials will prefer to differ, deciding on the right to choose on which platform or channel to grant access to their money and personal financial information.

Disruption and decentralization of the financial markets – using “Autonomous Economic Agents” on DLT/Blockchain protocol - is rapidly gaining ground and advancing regulation will ensure fair-play for all market participants.

Legacy financial systems and networks will inadvertently pave the way for digital disruption at every level and the global financial industry will then witness exponential growth.

The answer to centralization is decentralization, and blockchain will be the conduit.

 

Decentralization – Power to the People

The dynamics that drives blockchain development at a superfast pace is the willingness of an army of coders, developers and computer science volunteers that continuously improve blockchain protocol and the applications layer on top of blockchain.

 

As blockchain is open source and based on consensus, everyone can contribute to the protocol layer and make improvements in a perpetual cycle that has the only objective of making blockchain better, that is, faster and more scalable.

 

In crypto currency, protocol consensus has been challenging at best, with “hard forks” being used to split-up blockchains into different chains and varying block size. In the short history of crypto, the most famous hard forks are the Bitcoin split into “Bitcoin” and “Bitcoin Cash”, followed by another hard fork of “Bitcoin Cash” and “Bitcoin SV” (Satoshi’s Vision). Regrettably but unforeseen, the latter hard fork caused a complete collapse of the crypto market and the recovery was very slow to materialize.

 

Even that the world has entered into the digital realm, power, money and natural resources remain at the source of all conflicts and wars, with crypto the new front of global power and control, aptly called the “crypto fault line”.

 

Assets Tokenization on Blockchain

Any digital asset can be “tokenized” on blockchain, by simply dividing that particular asset into units or fractions of ownership, which then become “tokens” defined by “Ethereum” smart contracts.

To avoid any possible conflict, these smart contracts are coded beforehand to ensure that all parties receive their fair share once the conditions of a smart contract have been executed and fulfilled.

For example, in the music industry, smart contract-based royalties would be paid out to all parties concerned, after a certain pre-agreed quantity of songs or albums have been sold and production expenses deducted. The record label, recording studio, musicians, song writers, distributors, publishers and any other party in the record-producing and distribution chain, would receive their respective payment fairly and within the agreed timeframe as coded into the smart contract.

This ground-breaking and immeasurably fair payments system can be applied to include all kinds of intellectual-, commercial-, technological-, sports- and entertainment rights, revenues and royalties.

Liquid Democracy and Hard Forks

“Through delegation, people with domain-specific knowledge are able to better influence the outcome of decisions, which in turn leads to an overall better governance of the state. Because of this, Liquid Democracy naturally evolves into a Meritocracy, where decisions are mainly made by those who have the kind of knowledge and experience required to make well-informed decisions on issues”.

“Overall, Liquid Democracy holds a lot of potential to not only be the foundation of decision making in virtual communities, but also for local communities and entire governments.”

 

Source: Dominic Schiener on Medium

 

Liquid democracy is a form of “delegative democracy”, whereby an electorate has the option of conferring voting power to delegates, rather than voting directly. In the crypto world, liquid democracy does not necessarily depend on delegated voting, but more on a “weighted” and “transitory” voting system, usually determined by the voter’s token holdings at any given time.

 

 

By combining the advantages of “direct democracy” and “represented democracy”, it creates a powerful voting mechanism for collective decision making in online/virtual communities such as a DAO (Decentralized Autonomous Organization) or governance in relation to an Ethereum smart contract on blockchain.

 

In simple terms, the more tokens held by a governance delegate or consensus voter, the more voting power that delegate or voter has, but always subject to the strict governance and consensus of the pertinent blockchain protocol. The error margin in cryptography is non-existent, the code output is fixed by the input, therefore all coding is conclusive in the same way that math is definitive.

 

Governance or consensus problems only arise when errors in coding - thus “human error” - are made in the first place, then the outcome will be materially different than intended.

 

Ethereum’s the “DAO” – Decentralized Autonomous Organization – was a prime example of coding gone wrong, resulting in $50 million hack. By instigating a controversial “hard fork”, losses were recovered but the concept of DAOs suffered a major setback that has yet to recover.

 

Blockchain is open protocol, this means that everyone can access and improve the protocol in accordance with coding standards that are governed by consensus. This prevents any bad actor or rogue coder from even trying to manipulate or compromise any protocol without the immediate knowledge by the consensus governance.

 

The problem arises when one or more participants decide on opposing coding policy or protocol changes - called “hard forks” in tech parlance – and these can have major implications on the entire chain because it automatically creates two separate chains with differing block sizes, nodes and governing rules.

 

Bitcoin (BTC) takes the crown when it comes to hard forks with 3 splits so far, Bitcoin (BTC), Bitcoin Cash (BCH), Bitcoin Gold (BTG), Bitcoin ABC (Adjustable Blocksize Cap) and Bitcoin “Satoshi’s Vision” (BSV).

Below are the hard forks splitting Bitcoin by date and/or block:

*   [Bitcoin Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitcoin_Cash "Bitcoin Cash") : forked at block 478558 on 1 August 2017, for each bitcoin (BTC), an owner got 1 Bitcoin Cash (BCH)
*   [Bitcoin Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitcoin_Gold "Bitcoin Gold") : forked at block 491407 on 24 October 2017, for each BTC, an owner got 1 Bitcoin Gold (BTG)
*   [Bitcoin SV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitcoin_SV "Bitcoin SV") : forked at block 556766 on 15 November 2018, for each Bitcoin Cash (BCH), an owner got 1 Bitcoin SV (BSV).

 

Source: Wikipedia

 

The most recent Bitcoin split, resulting in Bitcoin ABC and Bitcoin SV was considered by some a “civil war” between two Bitcoin camps, the first led by entrepreneur Roger Ver and Jihan Wu of Bitmain (one of the biggest crypto exchanges in the world) for Bitcoin ABC, and the second camp, led by Craig Steven Wright and billionaire Calvin Ayre for the competing version Bitcoin SV which would increase each block size to 128MB from the standard 1MB.

Blockchain - Advanced Technology but Underdeveloped

 

In spite of blockchain being revolutionary and about to disrupt the entire internet as we know it today, it’s still immature and cumbersome to use.

 

Apart from the crucial importance of having highly-educated data scientists, cryptographers and blockchain experts developing the ultimate blockchain protocol, all blockchain protocols up to now suffer from the same deficiencies that underpin blockchain technology:

 

1 - Speed - there have been many improvements but blockchain transactions are still ultra-slow compared to the current internet speed as well as the ultra-fast transaction speed of legacy financial networks. For example, “high frequency trading” is executed in milliseconds (one thousandth of a second) and some networks even in microseconds (one millionth of a second). By comparison, Bitcoin can handle about 3-4 transactions per second and the Ethereum network has a maximum capacity of 15 transactions per second, however this rapidly being increased with new and advanced protocols.

 

2 - Scalability - blockchain blocks of 1 MB are the norm and changing this capacity to higher has proved very challenging so far. Subjective Theory of Value

 

The idea that an object's value is not inherent of the good’s properties, nor the amount of labor necessary to produce the good, but instead worth more to different people based on how much they desire or need the object. The Subjective Theory of Value places value on how scarce and useful an item is rather than basing the value of the object on how many resources and man hours went into creating it.

This theory was developed in the late 19th century by economists and thinkers of the time, including Carl Menger and Eugen von Boehm-Bawerk.

It will take time and probably high-level decision-making at the UN, IMF and World Bank to agree on a unified and globally accepted definition as well as legal status of cryptocurrencies.

 

At present, the main difference between fiat and electronic money (e-money for short) and digital money (cryptocurrency) is government and central bank control.

 

Whereas crypto currencies are digital only and borderless, national currencies – in digital or physical form – are controlled and confined within the country’s own borders and as such, controlled by its government and central bank (economic and monetary unions such as the Euro and East Caribbean dollar are some examples of the exemption, however these are also controlled by a central bank with the latter being pegged to the US dollar since 1976)

 

This gives governments such as the United States great power and influence, because the mighty US dollar is used in more than half of all global financial transactions (the dark number of illegal or criminal dollar transactions is unknown) and about 3 times the reserve currency held by foreign governments, central banks and financial institutions (an estimated 64% of all known foreign exchange reserves).

Centralized Crypto Currencies

 

The global crypto and blockchain community will be offended by the mere mention of a “centralized” crypto currency.

 

It might sound crazy but it’s certainly something to look into.

 

Being able to use crypto currencies such as Bitcoin for everyday payments has been the objective of many start-ups around the world.

 

The problem is that Bitcoin and other crypto currencies are not yet “stable” enough to be used as a viable currency for everyday payments or purchases.

 

With Bitcoin wildly fluctuating up and down in double-digit percentages, price swings of this magnitude cannot be easily absorbed by your average consumer.

 

Transferred to the real world, buying a cup of coffee with Bitcoin would either mean a huge profit or big loss for the vendor depending on the hour and minute of the day. Conversely, the consumer would either be overcharged or undercharged depending on which part of the day that cup of coffee is consumed.

 

For all intents and purposes, Bitcoin (or Bitcoin Cash for that matter) and most other crypto currencies could not be used as “real money” because of the instability and multi-digit “numerical” characteristic of crypto currencies.

 

In simple terms, if a regular cup of coffee in New York costs about $ 2, how would this be converted into Bitcoin ? At an indicative Bitcoin price of around $ 8,000 the coffee vendor would have to charge BTC 0.00025 per cup and this price would change by the minute. When using a credit card to pay in Bitcoin, the variable price calculation would be automatic however the vendor would have to adjust his coffee cup price as well as the price display continuously. For consumers, prices behind the comma are very unusual to say the least and also not practical in remembering the cost of any item.

 

People are just used to count in pennies and dollars, pounds and pence, cents and Euros or whatever other currency that is used.

 

Consumers also use a currency as a “measure of value”, for example when shopping for food or consumer goods and therefore that currency functions as a “unit of account”. That’s why is just much easier to display prices in multiple digits before a comma and only 2 digits behind.

 

Conversely, it would indeed be very strange and awkward if a cup of coffee would be charged for example at a price of $ 2,14986 and could only be paid for in cash with 2 dollars and 15 cents of real physical money (rounding off to the top).

 

Currently, Bitcoin and almost all other cryptocurrencies do not have the stability and fundamental attributes of government issued currencies and as such are not yet a viable alternative to real money issued by central banks. Crypto currencies, for all intents and purposes, do not qualify as “everyday money” that can be used to buy daily goods and services, unless instantly converted on the spot to fiat money via a crypto app, debit- or credit card.

 

However, some asset-backed cryptocurrencies such as Tether that are pegged to the US dollar or other fiat currencies or precious metals, have witnessed some success and adoption.

 

This has spurred on the current race to develop asset-backed digital currencies or tokens as “stable coins” that are – as the name implies – stable enough to be used as currency for daily transactions and everyday cryptocurrency purchases via a decentralized payment network.

 

Developing state-issued coins or digital currencies could be very beneficial to cryptocurrencies and the entire “crypto-economics” that comes with it.

 

 

Asset-Backed Stable Coins

 

A “stable coin” is a cryptocurrency that is pegged to another stable asset, like gold or the U.S. dollar. It’s a currency that is global, but is not tied to a central bank and has low volatility. This allows for practical usage of using cryptocurrency like paying for things every single day.

Many noble ideas abound, the crypto world is full with asset-backed coins ranging from gold-, diamond- and commodity-backed all the way down to digital-asset backed (music rights) and exotics such as “lemon coins”.

 

 

Cryptocurrencies are native digital assets that are founded on some form of cryptoeconomic model that incorporates incentives & disincentives to create consensus amongst non-related participants in a network.

Security, Scarcity, Incentives, Dis-incentives, Consensus, Utility, Network Effects, Anti-censorship, Decentralization, Ledger Immutability.

These are the attributes of true cryptocurrencies that are built on open, decentralized consensus protocols using blockchain technology.

There is basically no reason to build a crypto currency that is asset backed. It’s useless.

 

Source:Aleksandar Svetski on Medium 2018

(source link: https://hackernoon.com/asset-backed-cryptos)

 

At last count, there are over 4000+ cryptocurrencies traded on 33000+ markets across the globe (Coinmarketcap – Jan 2021) and it appears that newcomers are not in short supply, it’s only a question of cool product design – “shitcoin” must have taken the Grand Prize home – finished off with a touch of flair.

 

It is almost as if there is a branding team on site for when coders decide to mine a new coin with some kind of specialty of sorts.

 

Another crucial point to consider is whether crypto currencies are “legal tender” as this has far-reaching implications in the world of finance and banking as well as the global economy and trade.

 

There are many definitions of the term “legal tender”, however the following is the most descriptive :

 

Money that is legally valid for the payment of debts and

that must be accepted for that purpose when offered.

 

source: Merriam-Webster dictionary

So where do asset-backed cryptocurrencies fit in? Officially, nowhere.

 

Unless a coin or token is accepted (or backed) by a government, it is not “legal tender” but only a far-fetched “legal claim” in accordance with the terms of a smart contract on Ethereum.

 

In other words, in case of any dispute, a claim in relation to a coin or token can only be pursued in a court of law. And that alone is a challenge on many fronts because of a decentralized coin or token being “borderless”, that is, it only exists on top of a blockchain protocol that in all likelihood will be Ethereum. The question then is, which courts or jurisdiction apply.

 

It is perhaps for this exact reason, that Bitcoin and its 4000+ clones are flooding the markets like a tsunami, there’s no redress in court and no central bank control, there also no banks, brokers or intermediaries, just 2 parties to a transaction agreeing upon the fair value of the exchange.

 

Crazy – or even noble - ideas for asset-back cryptos all fail on this premise, there’s no one to be held accountable and if so, there’s no claim to be secured anywhere.

 

Even if for some worldly strange reason someone has a claim against Bitcoin, he could not go to Mt. Gox or any exchange world-wide to claim his/her rights or seize assets to satisfy his claim.

 

“Satoshi Nakamoto – even if he does exist – cannot be held accountable for Bitcoin. It’s the Bitcoin protocol of incentives and disincentives that creates consensus amongst its participants, the Bitcoin miners”.

 

Even if a coin or token is backed by assets, then what is the fair value of that asset ? And who will determine that value ? And what happens when the value of an asset such as gold, oil or real estate/property fluctuates ?

 

The questions are endless, the solutions even lesser.

 

The “Petro” is one example of a very poor attempt by a shaky government to issue a coin on base of the underlying value of its countries most precious resource, that’s oil.

 

Even that the “Petro” is backed by a sovereign government - Venezuela, it flopped on every front and became the outright laughing stock throughout the world and the subject of many discussions by crypto fanatics and academics alike.

 

Coins that are backed by real estate – that is physical property – face even bigger challenges. Who is going to go to court to claim and which courts or jurisdiction is applicable, who will value the property and how will a claim be enforceable and who will be the beneficiaries in the eventual case of a successful sale of the assets ?

 

For commodity-backed cryptocurrencies it gets even more trickier. Some coins claim that everyone single one of its coins is backed by real physical gold and that just sounds great. But from a practical point of view, who will pay out that real gold ? Where can it be collected or where will it be delivered ? And how to prove the rightful ownership is the next problem, after all the coins - which are part of the exchange - are virtual, whereas the gold is physical.

 

At this point, there must be someone or some authority to intermediate and that goes against everything that the decentralized cryptocurrency world stands for.

 

On top of that, there is the possibility that a sudden spike in demand outstrips the supply of a particular commodity (gold, silver, diamonds, etc.) that is part of the asset-backed cryptocurrency.

 

Let’s imagine that an asset-backed cryptocurrency is backed by gold and that that particular cryptocurrency enjoys the same meteoric rise such as Bitcoin, there probably would not be enough physical gold available – at least not in the short term – to satisfy a sudden spike in demand for gold should there be a sudden flight from Bitcoin to the “safe haven” of gold.

 

This actually happened on August 15^(th) , 1971 when President Richard Nixon unilaterally suspended the convertibility of US dollars into gold and by doing so, effectively ended the Bretton-Woods agreement.

 

The world was in shock and in particular the French did not take this lightly, the then French President Charles de Gaulle actually sent a French warship to New York harbor and demanded that the US Federal Reserve hand over its gold reserve in exchange for the US dollar foreign reserves of France.

 

This cannot happen to cryptocurrencies but the problem is real, should there ever be a sudden “run” on a particular commodity of an asset-backed cryptocurrency, then supply could be severely constricted.

So what is PSD2?

 

In simple terms, if a customer has given explicit consent, it will force banks and financial institutions to share customer account information with other licensed financial-services providers through API (Application Programming Interface).

 

The thinking behind PSD2 is to open the banking and financial industry to newcomers and at the same time make it more competitive.

There are many definitions of open banking—it clearly means different things to different people depending on what they have seen or read.

 

With that in mind, we define open banking as the use of APIs (application programming interfaces) to open up consumers’ financial data (with their permission) to third parties, enabling those companies then to create and distribute their own financial products .

 

Four trends have converged to create the conditions for open banking:  

 

1 - Regulation - new regulations, such as PSD2, are ushering in open banking.  

 

2 - Innovation - new technology, particularly smart devices and the shift to instant payments, are opening up new opportunities in financial services.  

3 - Changing consumer expectations - consumers’ increasing desire for frictionless experiences with the always-on, multi-device and 24/7 becoming expected  

 

4 - Competition - competitive pressures including the emergence of new entrants, such the neo-banks, and incumbent banks launching their own API portals  

 

On January 13, 2018, PSD2 has become national law in each of the EU member states and then banks will have to be ready for immediate and proper implementation.  

 

source: AT Kearny - A Brave New World in Financial Services

The Crypto Czar and SEC Powerhouse

The feared and revered Securities and Exchange Commission – shortly known as the SEC – is an independent agency of the United States federal government that is controlled by a commission and its Chairman that is actually independent from the control of the Executive branch which is the President of the United States.

 

This means that while the SEC is constitutionally part of the executive branch, the US President has limited power over the agency and only statutes passed by Congress grant authority and define the purpose of the SEC. This grants the SEC the primary and exclusive responsibility for enforcing securities laws and regulating the securities industry and is therefore the highest authority to propose or amend any and all security laws.

 

This is good and bad news at the same time. The US President cannot direct the policy for the SEC and at the bipartisan Congress level, all proposed laws and statute changes must pass through several committees and sub-committees before reaching Congress and being enacted into law.

 

In relation to ICO and crypto currencies, this becomes problematic because only the SEC can change the definition and criteria of what constitutes a security. At first, this appears to be logical as the SEC is only there to regulate the securities markets and protect investors from bad actors in the securities industry or, in the case of ICOs, the scammers and fraudsters. But this approach puts everyone in a compromising situation. If we follow the Howey test rule, then all past/present/future ICOs are breaking the law and should be shut down. All digital assets – coins, tokens and copyrights – are made to be stored or traded – read “flipped” - for profit and therefore should be classed as “digital securities” and regulated accordingly.

 

Leaving digital assets in the “grey zone” is absolutely and positively the wrong approach and misguides unwitting investors and market participants.

 

From the moment that all digital assets are classified as securities, then exceptions can be made for “utility tokens” that are truly utility and only used in an ecosystem or community for the sole purpose of serving and benefitting its participants . The original pioneer of selling future revenues in the form of royalties was the world-famous rock star David Bowie who back in 1997 issued the infamous “Bowie Bonds”. The entire batch of “Bowie Bonds” were purchased for US$55 million by Prudential Insurance company of America and guaranteed by David Bowie’s record label EMI Record which at the time had signed a $30 million record deal with David Bowie himself. The bonds paid an annual interest rate of 7.9% and were termed on average for 10 years.

By forfeiting ten years of contractual royalties from his record label EMI, David Bowie was able to receive an upfront payment of US$55 million, a financially smart move that allowed him then to buy his songs back from his former manager.

By March 2004, Moody’s Investors Service downgraded the bonds from an A3 to a Baa3 rating - one notch above junk status - prompted by a “lower-than-expected” revenue scenario and because according to Moody’s, EMI Records was an “unknown” record label which unfit designation didn’t make sense at all.

The Bowie Bonds however did not default and were liquidated as originally planned in 2007 after which the rights and royalties were returned back to the late David Bowie. Following the average success of the Bowie Bonds – they didn’t default but were downgraded – celebrity bonds did not take off due to lawsuits, controversies and a general lack of investors’ interest.

Digital securities are equity, debt and assets that are issued and traded using blockchain (DLT) technology, mostly involving “smart contracts” that determine governance and distribution of rights and benefits of a particular digital security, asset or derivative.

 

The crucial difference and determining factor between a security (as it’s defined presently) and a digital security is the governance by consensus , subject to the conditions as determined by the smart contract protocol . Another term used to describe a digital security is a “security token” which involves the issuance of tokens that then become “tradable” on the secondary markets (as main stream adoption and trading on the primary financial markets is still way off).

Bitcoin is actually the first functional example of “digital value” that can be exchanged transparently between two parties without the involvement of any middleman or third party.

 

Smart contracts are created on “irreversible” blockchain using cryptographic code that executes – exactly as specified - the fixed contractual terms and conditions as established by its creators and these digital contracts are also enforceable by law (for example, in relation to property).

The Random Walk theory suggests that changes in stock prices have the same distribution and are independent of each other, therefore, the past movement or trend of a stock price or market cannot be used to predict its future movement. source: Investopedia

Six basic tenets of Dow theory - Summary

**1\. The market has three movements**

**(** 1 **) The "main movement", primary movement or major trend may last from less than a year to several years. It can be bullish or bearish.**

**(** 2 **) The "medium swing", secondary reaction or intermediate reaction may last from ten days to three months and generally retraces from 33% to 66% of the primary price change since the previous medium swing or start of the main movement.**

**(** 3 **) The "short swing" or minor movement varies with opinion from hours to a month or more. The three movements may be simultaneous, for instance, a daily minor movement in a bearish secondary reaction in a bullish primary movement.**

 

**2\. Market Trends have three phases**

Dow theory asserts that major market trends are composed of three phases: an accumulation phase, a public participation (or absorption) phase, and a distribution phase.

 

**3\. The stock market discounts all news**

Stock prices quickly incorporate new information as soon as it becomes available. Once news is released, stock prices will change to reflect this new information.

 

**4\. Stock market averages must confirm each other.**

 

**5\. Trends are confirmed by volume**

Dow believed that volume confirmed price trends.

 

**6\. Trends exist until definitive signals prove that they have ended.**

Dow believed that trends existed despite "market noise".

 

Markets might temporarily move in the direction opposite to the trend, but they will soon resume the prior move.

Impact Investing

 

Impact investments are investments made with the intention to generate positive, measurable social and environmental impact alongside a financial return. Impact investments can be made in both emerging and developed markets, and target a range of returns from below market to market rate, depending on investors' strategic goals.

The growing impact investment market provides capital to address the world’s most pressing challenges in sectors such as sustainable agriculture, renewable energy, conservation, microfinance, and affordable and accessible basic services including housing, healthcare, and education.

source: Global Impact Investing Network

 

Millennials are more conscious and concerned about the environment than their parents, simply because climate change is more noticeable now than ever before.

And for this reason, the younger generation actively seeks out companies to invest in that are “environmentally conscious” and have a lower carbon footprint.

 

Even university endowments have removed “polluting” companies from their investment portfolios simply because it’s not socially acceptable to participate in revenues from companies that are destroying planet earth.

 

And while the global population at large wants to drastically reduce greenhouse gas emissions in accordance with the Kyoto Protocol that came into force in 2005, the United States recently decided to pull out, much to the surprise and detriment of the world.

 

Almost every country in the world – 192 in total – adopted the Kyoto Protocol in their fight against global warming. Regretfully, Canada withdrew on December 2012 and the United States withdrew itself from the Paris Agreement on June 2017, because of concerns that reduced carbon emissions would hurt the US economy.

 

While the current government administration is more concerned about the economy, millennials view life from a different angle.

 

Within the next decade, over $24 trillion in assets will be transferred to the next generation which is the biggest wealth transfer in history. These assets will have to be re-invested and the trend is toward impact investing.

 

According to a report by Fidelity, more than 77% of affluent millennials and over 72% of Generation X indicated that they would investing their fortunes in socially responsible companies which are aligned with their personal values.

 

Although the impact investing industry is nascent, the numbers are staggering. It is estimated that impact investment funds have invested over $228 billion in environmentally conscious companies and compounded growth has been more than 102% annually.

 

It’s estimated that over $140 billion has been invested in 2017 alone and that the total AUM will exceed $4.3 trillion within the next decade.

 

What stands out is that millennials are so environmentally conscious; they are prepared to forego financial gains in order to give preference to the act of “doing good”. In other words, they don’t have high expectations of return, but more of moral conviction to do the right thing before it’s too late.

 

Impact investing is categorized into three main segments: environment, social and governance (ESG). Regardless of the investment strategy applied, most millennials are more concerned about planet earth than their own inheritance and as such, are determined to invest a bigger part of their assets in sustainable investments.

 

Wealth managers have spotted this trend and are actively pursuing millennials, sometimes applying the term “impact investing” as a marketing tool without adopting an intentional and thoughtful approach to actually pursuing sustainable investment opportunities.

 

Industry organizations such as the Global Impact Investing Network (GIIN) are actively trying to establish a common identity in order to attract more investment and at the same time provide tools, resources, research and education to interested parties in order to bring impact investing to the forefront of wealth management.

 

Howard Buffett, grandson of Warren Buffett, teaches at Columbia University and he’s building a course on impact investing. Even that Howard Buffett doesn’t need to work a day in his life, his moral compass overrides the urge to take it easy in life and this is the spirit of millennials, save planet earth before it’s too late.

Wealth in a Tokenized World

 

The coming of age of digital disruption will impact everyone and everything.

*   **Super Artificial Intelligence **

Artificial intelligence, machine learning, robotization, automation and the gig economy will disrupt the global labor market profoundly.

 

Experts estimate that more than 50% of the labor market will be replaced by freelance, automation and robotics, everything from banking to retail, transport through to logistics, and anything that involves intermediates or brokers. And this prospect is actually very discomforting, considering that technology is evolving at such a rapid pace that there will be further disruption down the line.

 

To counter the effects of technology disrupting the labor markets, a global discussion has emerged on the subject of UBI – universal basic income – that will replace all social benefits such as unemployment, pensions, housing and social welfare (as well as other social services) and provide for a minimum for every person to live on.

 

In theory, this sounds great, reminiscent of the good old days of communism, the “collective” taking care of its people. The only fundamental question remains, who will pay for all of this?

 

While experts on both sides of the divide argue the benefits and possibilities, one fact is certain, there is no country in the world at this very moment, that is willing or can provide its citizens with UBI, case closed. Even resource-rich countries like the United States, United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Iran and Russia - not to mention Hong Kong, South Korea, China, Australia, Singapore and Norway (in the form of accumulated sovereign wealth) or banking paradise Switzerland - do not and cannot afford to offer its citizens UBI.

 

Because for the simple reason, that productivity will be stifled significantly and the division of labor would in all likelihood be affected so profoundly and unfairly, that is, the “productive” working and paying for the “non-productive”, it could very well destabilize, and possibly collapse, the government and its economy. If not, government money coffers would be depleted so fast, it could lead to hyperinflation “Venezuelan” style – the primary example of one of the biggest oil producers in the world that cannot even stabilize its economy and provide for the basic needs of food and shelter for its citizens.

 

UBI is just a utopian dream.

 

The only purposeful objective is the empowerment of the people, through an effective redistribution of wealth using blockchain as well as a fair system of inclusion for the unbanked and underserved in the financial systems of the world.

 

One realistic and viable alternative for the “common good” would be UBA – Universal Basic Assets – a sort of modern times “collective” where citizens participate in the ownership and revenues of a public utility such as water, sustainable energy, toll roads and other public infrastructures. Unlike UBI, receivers at the UBA end can re-invest surplus cash and create more wealth and prosperity as opposed to UBI receivers that will only be able to survive on the minimum.

 

Taking it one step further, taxing the digital disruption would yield significantly more and benefit the entire global community. Currently, there is already talk of a proposed “digital tax” on revenues of tech companies, and this could further be expanded and applied across the world. In particular, a high tax on the use of robots could be a great equalizer in the disruption of the labor market.

 

Tokenizing the internet on blockchain would be the ultimate taxation and the perfect UBA concept. Everyone that participates in the “Internet of Value” is entitled to their “fair share” of global tax revenues encoded into smart contract.

 

Perhaps a far-fetched idea for now, but by all means definitely something to consider in the near future.

 

In the end, it would just be better to provide economic incentives and support for all citizens of the world to get educated or skilled, in order to produce products and services that benefit the population at large and not just big tech and the rest of the corporate world.

 

Empowering people is a gargantuan task and daunting challenge but could resolve many of the world’s conflicts by removing the one element that causes it all - money.

 

Simply, because money buys power and control that doesn’t care about equality or equal opportunity.

 

Decentralization will transfer wealth, prosperity and power to the people as opposed to inefficient government, corporate meddling and central bank control.

 

This paradigm shift from centralized control to decentralized consensus will enable the global citizens of the world to participate equitably in the abundance of the world, unrestrained from the interests of government and the corporate world. A global rebalancing of wealth based on fairness of open and free capitalism.

 

Financial independence in an equitable world is the aim of tokenization, where blockchain will lay the foundation for the “Internet of Value”, accessible by all and controlled by no one, paving the way for the Democratization of Wealth.

Crypto Crash Course

 

 

The following terms are the most important Crypto concepts and Tech definitions, in order of importance and relevance, which will give readers a better understanding of the contents and themes in this book.

 

 

Crypto

A cryptocurrency is a digital asset designed to work as a medium of exchange that uses strong cryptography to secure financial transactions, control the creation of additional units, and verify the transfer of assets.

 

 

Cryptography

Cryptography is the science of protecting information by transforming it into a secure format. This process, called encryption, has been used for centuries to prevent handwritten messages from being read by unintended recipients. Today, it is used to protect digital data.

 

 

Blockchain

A blockchain is a growing list of records, called blocks, which are linked using cryptography. Each block contains a cryptographic hash of the previous block, a timestamp, and transaction data. By design, a blockchain is resistant to modification of the data.

 

Blockchain is a ledger of records organized in ‘blocks’ that are linked together by cryptographic validation, sometimes referred to as a “digital storage of consensus truth”.

 

 

Decentralization

Decentralization is the process by which the activities of an organization, particularly those regarding planning and decision making are distributed or delegated away from a central, authoritative location/group

 

FinTech

Financial technology, often shortened to FinTech or fintech, is the new technology and innovation that aims to compete with traditional financial methods in the delivery of financial services. It is an emerging industry that uses technology to improve activities in finance

 

 

Tokenization

Tokenization is the process of converting rights to real world assets into a digital token on a blockchain

 

 

Satoshi Nakamoto

Satoshi Nakamoto is the name used by the unknown person or people who developed bitcoin, authored the bitcoin white paper, and created and deployed bitcoin's original reference implementation. As part of the implementation, they also devised the first blockchain database.

 

 

Vitalik Buterin

Vitaly Dmitriyevich "Vitalik" Buterin is a Russian-Canadian programmer and writer primarily known as a co-founder of Ethereum and as a co-founder of Bitcoin Magazine

 

 

Solidity

Solidity is an object-oriented programming language for writing smart contracts. It is used for implementing smart contracts on various blockchain platforms

 

 

Smart Contract

Auto-executing and legally binding contract coded in blockchain protocol

 

 

TGE

Token Generation Event – another title for an ICO or STO

 

 

ICO – Initial Coin Offering

An Initial Coin Offering ( ICO ) is the cryptocurrency space's rough equivalent to an IPO in the mainstream investment world. ICOs act as fundraisers of sorts; a company looking to create a new coin, app, or service launches an  ICO .

 

 

STO – Security Token Offering

In opposition to tokens offered in an ICO which do not give any rights or obligations, and instead provide access to a specific network, platform or service, tokens offered in an STO are actual financial securities that are backed by something tangible like the assets, profits, or revenue of the company, and which offer legal rights such as voting or revenue distribution.

 

 

DAO

Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (leaderless companies) are decentralised, distributed, resilient, transparent and incentivised applications on blockchain that will prove themselves to the world by remapping the technological landscape

 

 

AML/KYC

Anti-Money Laundering/Know Your Customer – requested during the ID verification process when signing up for a service or access to a platform

 

 

FOMO

“Fear Of Missing Out” on an investment opportunity

 

 

HODL

“Hold On to Dear Life” is commonly used in the crypto sphere meaning to hold on to crypto no matter what happens in the markets. It’s a reference to the early buyers of Bitcoin who in the process of “HODL-ing” their Bitcoins, became wildly rich millionaires - and some even billionaires - like the Winklevoss brothers (of Facebook fame).

 

 

Nodes

A node is a basic unit used in computer science. Nodes are devices or data points on a larger network. Devices such as a personal computer, cell phone, or printer are nodes. When defining nodes on the Internet, a node is anything that has an IP address.

 

 

Hard Fork

A hard fork is a rule change such that the software validating according to the old rules will see the blocks produced according to the new rules as invalid. In case of a hard fork, all nodes meant to work in accordance with the new rules need to upgrade their software.

 

 

Liquid Democracy

Delegative democracy, also known as liquid democracy, is a form of democracy whereby an electorate has the option of vesting voting power in delegates rather than voting directly themselves. The term is a generic description of either already-existing or proposed popular-control apparatuses

 

 

Consensus

Blockchain, the distributed ledger most commonly associated with Bitcoin, also relies on consensus algorithms to reach agreement among nodes. A blockchain can be thought of as a decentralized database that is managed by distributed computers on a peer-to-peer (P2P) network.

 

 

dApps

Decentralized application using blockchain protocol

 

 

Stable Coin

Stablecoins are cryptocurrencies designed to minimize the effects of price volatility. To minimize volatility the value of a stablecoin can be pegged to a currency, or to exchange traded commodities (such as precious or industrial metals). Stablecoins backed by currencies or commodities directly are said to be centralized, whereas those leveraging other cryptocurrencies are referred to as decentralized

 

 

Nick Szabo

Nick Szabo is a computer scientist, legal scholar and cryptographer known for his research in digital contracts and digital currency. He graduated from the University of Washington in 1989 with a degree in computer science. He holds an honorary professorship at the Universidad Francisco Marroquín

 

 

EVM – Ethereum Virtual Machine

Ethereum Virtual Machine can be thought of as quasi-Turing complete machine. Turing completeness refers to a system of data manipulation rules, and is named after Alan Turing, creator of the Turing machine

 

 

P2P – Peer-to-Peer

A  peer \-to- peer  ( P2P ) economy is a decentralized model whereby two individuals interact to buy or sell goods and services directly with each other, without an intermediary third-party, or without the use of a company of business

 

 

Emergency Aid/Stimulus/Relief Packages

Government enacted emergency aid packages are usually a combination of aid, relief and stimulus measures to support citizens as well as businesses and to stimulate a struggling economy, boost economic growth and lead the economy out of economic slowdown or recession.

 

Quantitative Easing

Quantitative easing is a monetary policy whereby a central bank buys government bonds or other financial assets in order to inject money into the economy to expand economic activity. By increasing the money supply, central banks are said to be “printing money” out of nothing, simply increasing the size of their balance sheets to increase the amount of credit available to borrowers. (source: Wikipedia)

 

 

GDP

Gross domestic product is a monetary measure of the market value of all the final goods and services produced in a specific time period (source: Wikipedia)

 

 

Inflation

In economics, inflation is a general rise in price level relative to available goods resulting in a substantial and continuing drop in purchasing power in an economy over a period of time (source: Wikipedia)

 

 

Gig Economy

Refers to the so-called “flexible” labour market, characterized by the prevalence of freelance work on short-term contracts but with no workers’ rights or protection, secured employment, steady income, paid holiday, sick pay, medical coverage or “social safety” net. Tech companies present the gig economy as beneficial for flexible work and working hours, but labour experts, gig workers and independent contractors consider it a form of workers’ exploitation.

 

The gig economy is a novel free market system in which temporary positions are offered and “on demand” companies hire workers or independent contractors for short-term commitments. The term "gig" is actually a slang word used primarily in the music world, it refers to a musician’s job that lasts for only a specified period of time without actually becoming a permanent band member

 

The New Yorker magazine calls the “flexible” gig economy the “on-demand, peer, or platform economy.” In simpler terms, the gig economy consists of companies who engage contract workers for a temporary time period or project-based jobs, instead of hiring them for permanent positions.

 

Source - https://mhrglobal.com/us/en/blog/gig-economy-good-bad-and-future#!

 

 

IoT - Internet of Things

The Internet of things is a system of interrelated computing devices, mechanical and digital machines provided with unique identifiers and the ability to transfer data over a network without requiring human-to-human or human-to-computer interaction (source: Wikipedia).

 

 

Quantum Computing

Refers to the use of quantum phenomena such as “superposition” and “entanglement” to perform computations.

 

Quantum computers perform hyper-fast calculations - billions per second - based on the probability of an object’s state before it’s measured by using “qubits” which results in the possibility to process exponentially more data than regular computers. A relevant and key aspect to quantum computing is that ever since “quantum supremacy” has been achieved by the joint project of NASA and Google (in October 2019), it can be used to crack any password or code in a fraction of time, leading to the need of “quantum resistant”, “quantum safe” or “quantum proof” computers (source: Wikipedia)

 

 

5G

In telecommunications, 5G is the fifth generation technology standard for cellular networks, which cellular phone companies began deploying worldwide in 2019, the planned successor to the 4G networks which provide connectivity to most current cell phones. Like its predecessors, 5G networks are cellular networks, in which the service area is divided into small geographical areas called  cells . All 5G wireless devices in a cell are connected to the Internet and telephone network by radio waves through a local antenna in the cell. The main advantage of the new networks is that they will have greater bandwidth, giving higher download speeds, eventually up to 10 Gigabits per second (Gbit/s). Due to the increased bandwidth, it is expected that the new networks will not just serve cell phones like existing cellular networks, but also be used as general internet service providers for laptops and desktop computers, competing with existing ISPs such as cable internet, and also will make possible new applications in internet of things (IoT) and machine to machine areas (source: Wikipedia).

 

 

6G

Sixth-generation wireless is the successor to upcoming 5G mobile technology, where 6G networks will be able to use higher frequencies than 5G networks and provide substantially higher capacity and much lower latency with hyper-fast speeds of 100 x 100 gigabits per second, making 6G as much as **100 times faster** than 5G. Samsung is already taking the lead, followed by China trying to compete within its own borders (source: Wikipedia)

 

 

The Financial Revolution

Digital disruption of money, finance and economy through financial technology

 

 

Equitable Wealth

The distribution of a nation’s revenues and wealth that is “equitably” divided among its population or on a global scale, the way in which the wealth of the world is divided among nations. An equitable distribution of wealth gives all global citizens a fair opportunity to become financially independent and successful.

 

 

Conscious Capitalism

Socially responsible economic and political philosophy created by John Mackey and Raj Sisodia. Proponents believe businesses should operate ethically by serving the interests of all stakeholders involved, not just corporate management and shareholders (source: Wikipedia)

 

It’s a philosophy based on a simple idea that when practiced consciously, business elevates humanity. 

 

Conscious Capitalism is a way of thinking about capitalism and business that better reflects where we are in the human journey, the state of our world today, and the innate potential of business to make a positive impact on the world. 

Source - https://www.consciouscapitalism.org/credo

 

 

RPA – Robotic Process Automation

The term used for software tools and systems that partially or fully automate human activities that are manual, repetitive and rule-based.

 

 

Cognitive RPA

Refers to using advanced Robotic Process Automation in combination with artificial intelligence and machine learning, enabling Cognitive RPA to make intelligent decisions , thus entirely replacing humans in the RPA process.

 

 

Robotics

Robotics is an  interdisciplinary  research area at the interface of  computer science  and  engineering .  Robotics involves design, construction, operation, and use of  robots . The goal of robotics is to design intelligent machines that can help and assist humans in their day-to-day lives and keep everyone safe. Robotics draws on the achievement of  information engineering ,  computer engineering ,  mechanical engineering ,  electronic engineering  and others.

Robotics develops machines that can substitute for humans and replicate human actions (source: Wikipedia).

 

 

Financial Inclusion

It is defined as the availability and equality of opportunities to access financial services. It refers to a process by which individuals and businesses can access appropriate, affordable, and timely financial products and services which include banking, credit, loan and insurance products (source: Wikipedia)

 

 

Unbanked

The unbanked are adults who do not have their own bank accounts. Along with the underbanked, they may rely on alternative financial services for their financial needs, where these are available (source: Wikipedia)

 

 

Impact Investing

Impact investing refers to investments "made into companies, organizations, and funds with the intention to generate a measurable, beneficial social or environmental impact alongside a financial return." Impact investments provide capital to address social and/or environmental issues.

 

 

XKeyscore

It was first revealed by Edward Snowden in the Guardian newspaper that a secret computer system with sophisticated software was used by the NSA (America’s National Security Agency) for mass surveillance as well as for continuous searching, analyzing and collecting of global internet data and web user interactions on a daily basis.

 

 

Technospheres of Influence

The Sino-American trade war - with both vying for “technological influence” - has brought about the emergence of technological spheres of influence. These technological spheres of influence are geographical areas in which an external power has  privileged   but not  exclusive capability to control   technology and/or where the external actor exerts predominant influence in terms of technology governance.

 

(source: LSE Ideas – Medium article by author Oxford PhD Valentin Weber - “Making Sense of Technological Spheres of Influence” – article link: https://medium.com/@lseideas/making-sense-of-technological-spheres-of-influence-e1fb56581ec6)

 

 

Eurasian Technological Crescent

It’s the combination of Europe, South(east) Asia, and East Asia as the world’s  Technological Crescent , the most prized territory over which great powers are seeking to gain influence. It contains states with important rare earth resources (China, India, Malaysia, Myanmar, Thailand, and Vietnam), which are used to produce high-tech products. It also plays host to the world’s technology supply chain and innovation hubs, spanning from China, Taiwan, South Korea, Japan, Thailand, through to Germany, France, and the UK as well as passing through cyber powers like Iran and Israel.

The technological Crescent’s geopolitical importance also bears more traditional characteristics. It is here that two of the three largest economic, military, and technological political entities — China and the EU — interact. It is more critical of a geographical pivot than the Eurasian hinterland. 

 

(source: LSE Ideas – Medium article by author Oxford PhD Valentin Weber - “Making Sense of Technological Spheres of Influence” – article link: https://medium.com/@lseideas/making-sense-of-technological-spheres-of-influence-e1fb56581ec6)

 

 

FVEY - Five Eyes

International alliance of intelligence agencies comprising US, UK, Canada, Australia and New Zealand that spy and share information between agencies.

 

 

China Social Credit System

China’s national reputation system that is currently being developed by the Chinese Communist Party uses big data to build a “high-trust society” where individuals, businesses and organizations are scored on financial as well as social reputation and behaviour. The system includes a list of “Dishonest Persons Subject to Enforcement” by the Supreme People's Court as "not qualified". This can result in someone that’s on the list of not being able to buy a train or plane ticket, banned from travelling abroad, getting credit or loans or even buying property.

 

China's social credit system has been compared to George Orwell’s 1984, Black Mirror, Big Brother and every other dystopian future scenario imaginable that sci-fi writers can think up.

 

 

Xinomics

Deepfakes

Also known as “synthetic media” in which a person in an existing image or video is replaced with someone else's likeness. While the act of faking content is not really new, deepfakes leverage powerful techniques from machine learning and artificial intelligence to manipulate or generate visual and audio content with a high potential to deceive. The main machine learning methods used to create deepfakes are based on deep learning and involve training generative neural network architectures, such as “autoencoders” or “generative adversarial networks” (GANs)

 

Deepfakes \- a portmanteau of "deep learning" and "fake" - have garnered widespread attention for their uses in celebrity pornographic videos, revenge porn, fake news, hoaxes, and financial fraud which has elicited responses from both industry and government to detect and limit their use (source: Wikipedia).
