# Conclusion

Artificial intelligence is rapidly assuming a crucial role in the elucidation and characterization of tumor immune interactions. Deep learning driven computational algorithms that support the construction of detailed spatial maps appear to be a relatively straightforward way to characterize general tumor immune interactions in the tumor microenvironment, which may be very helpful to predict treatment response and clinical outcomes. Thus, the utilization of advanced digital pathology and deep learning applications will likely grow very quickly over the next few years. This chapter has focused primarily on describing methods behind rapid advances in our ability to generate cancer immune maps and emerging methods that can be used in combination to provide many different types of potentially useful information to deliver precision medicine. Currently, the interpretation of cancer immune dynamics with the complex TME is challenging, but disease site-specific correlations between predictions with digital pathology and deep learning methods, quantitative analyses of mIHC and mIF, clinical outcomes, and treatment response are being actively studied to discover biomarkers and elucidate complex relationships between tumors and immunity in order to help advance our ability to treat cancer. Even though mapping patient-specific tumor-immune interactions are still at an early stage, the crucial next phase of research regarding the quantitative analysis of spatial morphologic and molecular patterns emerging from these methods is beyond fascinating and hopefully clinically applicable. Last but not least, the power of the digital pathology and deep learning methods also lies in the ability to perform these computational analyses of WSIs across thousands to hundreds of thousands of cases in parallel without the need for additional tissue, time, or resources. It is also quite conceivable that analyses of mIF and mIHC in small subsets of site-specific diseases can be used to identify generalizable relationships that can be correlated with more high-throughput standard Pathomics analyses in H&E WSIs in much larger collections in order to gain further insight into tumor biology. Hopefully, these developments can lead to workflows that can be implemented in pathology laboratories as practical clinical decision support tools for routine diagnostic surgical pathology. Thus, we hope the end of this chapter serves as a beginning to help imagine a future where WSIs of incoming tissue samples are analyzed with automated algorithms that perform diagnostic screening, quantify the expression of biomarkers in mIHC panels, provide prognostic information, help identify therapeutic targets, and predict treatment response.
