# Identifying TILs with deep learning

The biological behavior of various types and subtypes of cancer is intrinsically coupled to the characteristics of the tumor microenvironment, which consists of stroma, immune infiltrate, nerves, and lymphovascular vessels [17,31–35,50]. Since these distinct tissue components cohabit the TME with cancer cells, it is widely believed that tumor antigenicity–driven immune responses are likely quite influenced, if not directly dependent, on the type, quantity, and spatial localization of immune cells that are present in the immediate landscape of the TME [17,31,87]. In order to further examine the relationship of immune cells in the TME with cancer cells, the assessment of TILs is gaining importance as a candidate prognostic and predictive biomarker that may be useful to identify and stratify patients with the highest likelihood of responding to immunotherapy treatment options in a variety of cancer types [32–35,88].

Thus, the routine of evaluation of TILs is gaining popularity as a readily available and widely applicable biomarker that needs to be incorporated into diagnostic surgical pathology reports. Even though the implementation of TILs as a routine clinical biomarker has not been achieved yet, the immediate need to incorporate the evaluation of TILs to improve current protocols for immune checkpoint therapy and other forms of immunotherapy is very clear. The evaluation and appropriate characterization of TILs in the TME is also warranted in terms of addressing immunotherapy-related safety issues since serious adverse effects and the lack of long-lasting responses in many patients suggests that the activation of previously tumor-immunosuppressed TILs needs to be carefully controlled [63].

High densities of TILs correlate with favorable clinical outcomes [89], including longer disease-free survival (DFS) or improved overall survival (OS) in multiple cancer types [90]. Moreover, the spatial localization and distribution of TILs in terms of the immune infiltrate in the tumor center and invasive margin have also become important in cancer prognosis [89].
