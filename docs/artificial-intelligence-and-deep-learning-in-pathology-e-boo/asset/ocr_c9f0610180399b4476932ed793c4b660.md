Previous work in several studies have explored three immune biomarkers on a single tissue section in combination with an adjacent section stained with H&E or another biomarker to delineate the tumor [132]. Due to the availability of mIHC, mIF, and the variety of approaches to apply this advanced histologic technique, there have been several recent studies with multiple markers in NSCLC and cancer in other anatomic sites [131–133,135,137–139,145,146]. Novel multispectral approaches that can identify, segment, and evaluate more than three colors at a time have been developed to interpret mIHC within the context of the biological properties of the protein markers of tumor and immune cells by incorporating methods to assign each pixel with a collection of constituent signals from the chromogens in the color mixture [42,135,142,147–149]. This method can usefully address IHC co-expression in subsets of cell types with mIHC and mIF and represents a lot of progress since the maximum number of stains that could be unmixed was limited to three colors previously [42,142,147,149].

A prospective study has also been performed that explored the heterogeneous nature of metastatic melanoma using mIHC correlated with flow cytometry (FACS) to quantify immune subsets in the intratumoral, tumor-associated stromal compartments, and leading invasive edge of the tumor [150]. This study evaluated the association between intratumoral CD8+ T-cells and PDL1 expression in melanoma in combination with FACS to obtain data regarding the differentiation of T-cells and subsets, activation status, and expression of immune checkpoint molecules. mIHC identified significantly higher Treg cells than FACS and showed preferential stromal CD4+ T-cells. This study proposed a model of the immune context of metastatic melanoma with respect to PDL1 expression in melanoma cells and/or macrophages, presence or absence of intratumoral CD8+ TILs, and intratumoral and peritumoral tumor location. This study also provided insight into treatment response with checkpoint inhibitor therapy because PDL1 expression was more prevalent in macrophages in this study [150]. A separate study in metastatic melanoma utilized a novel combination of quantitative IHC, quantitative mIF, and next-generation sequencing for T-cell antigen receptors (TCRs) to show that the proliferation of intratumoral CD8+ TILs was directly correlated with radiographic reduction in tumor size with Pembrolizumab therapy [151]. This study also identified that pretreatment samples had increased numbers of intratumoral and peritumoral cells that expressed CD8, PD-1, and PD-L1, as well as close proximity between PD-1 and PD-L1.
