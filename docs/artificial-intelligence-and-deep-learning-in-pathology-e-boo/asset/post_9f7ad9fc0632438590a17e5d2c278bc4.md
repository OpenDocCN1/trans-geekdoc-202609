Tumor-TIL maps from the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) project have been made publicly available online in a web-based interface that allows the user to interactively navigate and evaluate the predictions of tumor-TIL maps with the corresponding H&E WSIs in 1015 WSIs of the TCGA Breast Cancer (BRCA) collection [128].

Le et al. have also developed a coarse-grained pancreatic cancer detection tool for invasive pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) [129] that identifies image patches with malignant poorly formed glands and invasive nests of tumor cells within the context of normal pancreatic acinar tissue, pancreatic islet cells, stroma, and TILs.

TILs can be subdivided in a data-driven manner with emerging artificial intelligence applications in digital pathology image analysis to specifically identify intratumoral and peritumoral TILs, which can be further subdivided to identify intraepithelial TILs and stromal TILs. To put this into context, if a pathologist or group of pathologists attempted to perform this type of task in multiple fields of view under the microscope for every tissue section for multiple slides per case across hundreds to thousands of glass slides, this would be prohibitive due to the amount of labor, time, and cost and almost impossible to realistically consider given the limited availability of practicing pathologist and intrinsic intra- and interobserver variability.

## Multiplex immunohistochemistry with digital pathology and deep learning

Building on the exciting advances with computational detection of lymphocytes to identify TILs within the histologic landscape of the cancer, a conceptually straightforward approach is to integrate insights from TIL maps and specifically identify the various types and subsets of aforementioned immune cells based on the expression of surface and cellular biomarkers and their in situ spatial distribution in the TME [42, 130–133]. In routine diagnostic surgical pathology, consecutive serial sections of tissues are stained with one marker at a time and interpretation is based on the presence of a marker or a semiquantitative estimation of the number of positive cells of interest. Multiplexed chromogenic immunohistochemistry (mIHC) and immunofluorescence (mIF) have recently emerged to simultaneously detect multiple biomarkers in the same tissue section to perform molecular immune profiling in situ in formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) samples in order to preserve limited tissue in samples [133–141].

However, the manual evaluation and interpretation of the immunohistochemical (IHC) expression of a single marker is highly subjective and time-consuming due to the nuances in relationship between expression and stain intensity, as well as non-specific background staining, heterogeneous expression, subcellular localization, and variability in selecting appropriate ROIs to score [42, 142], which can be heavily influenced by preanalytic variables intrinsic to the various differences in chemical methods, tissue fixation, and histology laboratory processing and staining protocols. Similarly, challenges still remain for computationally aligning or registering serial sections with companion H&E tissue sections, which can vary due to intrinsic architectural and texture differences in each section. There are also drawbacks with generating spatial molecular maps from serial sections that are related to the spatial resolution of molecular IHC biomarkers, as well as challenges associated to variations that are introduced into tissue samples during histologic sectioning and processing for sample preparation. Another overarching challenge that remains as a barrier to the analyses of spatial multiplex IHC and IF in WSIs is the need for training datasets.

Despite these challenges and variables, digital pathology image analysis tools can play a very important role to help pathologists routinely quantify the expression of IHC biomarkers in specific types of cells, differentiate various expression levels, and assess co-expression. A promising direction for mIHC and mIF is exploring unbiased, unsupervised, and semisupervised deep learning methods to perform automatic IHC analysis in order to isolate and detect the expression of individual markers and measure the extent of expression in tumor and immune cells through color deconvolution, optical density, and multispectral separation of chromogens [42,142].

Previous work in several studies has explored three immune biomarkers on a single tissue section in combination with an adjacent section stained with H&E or another biomarker to delineate the tumor [132]. Due to the availability of mIHC, mIF, and the variety of approaches to apply this advanced histologic technique, there have been several recent studies with multiple markers in NSCLC and cancer in other anatomic sites [131–133,135,137–139,145,146]. Novel multispectral approaches that can identify, segment, and evaluate more than three colors at a time have been developed to interpret mIHC within the context of the biological properties of the protein markers of tumor and immune cells by incorporating methods to assign each pixel with a collection of constituent signals from the chromogens in the color mixture [42,135,142,147–149]. This method can usefully address IHC co-expression in subsets of cell types with mIHC and mIF and represents a lot of progress since the maximum number of stains that could be unmixed was limited to three colors previously [42,142,147,149].

A prospective study has also been performed that explored the heterogeneous nature of metastatic melanoma using mIHC correlated with flow cytometry (FACS) to quantify immune subsets in the intratumoral compartment, tumor-associated stromal compartments, and leading invasive edge of the tumor [150]. This study evaluated the association between intratumoral CD8+ T-cells and PD-L1 expression in melanoma in combination with FACS to obtain data regarding the differentiation of T-cells and subsets, activation status, and expression of immune checkpoint molecules. mIHC identified significantly higher Treg cells than FACS and showed preferential stromal CD4+ T-cells. This study proposed a model of the immune context of metastatic melanoma with respect to PD-L1 expression in melanoma cells and/or macrophages, presence or absence of intratumoral CD8+ TILs, and intratumoral and peritumoral tumor location. This study also provided insight into treatment response with checkpoint inhibitor therapy because PD-L1 expression was more prevalent in macrophages in this study [150]. A separate study in metastatic melanoma utilized a novel combination of quantitative IHC, quantitative mIF, and next-generation sequencing for T-cell antigen receptors (TCRs) to show that the proliferation of intratumoral CD8+ TILs was directly correlated with radiographic reduction in tumor size with Pembrolizumab therapy [151]. This study also identified that pretreatment samples had increased numbers of intratumoral and peritumoral cells that expressed CD8, PD-1, and PD-L1, as well as close proximity between PD-1 and PD-L1.
