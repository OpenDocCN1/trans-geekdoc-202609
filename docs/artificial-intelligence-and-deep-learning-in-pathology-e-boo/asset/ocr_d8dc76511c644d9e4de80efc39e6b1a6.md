# Multiplex immunohistochemistry with digital pathology and deep learning

Building on the exciting advances with computational detection of lymphocytes to identify TILs within the histologic landscape of the cancer, a conceptually straightforward approach is to integrate insights from TIL maps and specifically identify the various types and subsets of aforementioned immune cells based on the expression of surface and cellular biomarkers and their in situ spatial distribution in the TME [42, 130–133]. In routine diagnostic surgical pathology, consecutive serial sections of tissues are stained with one marker at a time and interpretation is based on the presence of a marker or a semiquantitative estimation of the number of positive cells of interest. Multiplexed chromogenic immunohistochemistry (mIHC) and immunofluorescence (mIF) have recently emerged to simultaneously detect multiple biomarkers in the same tissue section to perform molecular immune profiling in situ in formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) samples in order to preserve limited tissue in samples [133–141].

However, the manual evaluation and interpretation of the immunohistochemical (IHC) expression of a single marker is highly subjective and time-consuming due to the nuances in relationship between expression and stain intensity, as well as non-specific background staining, heterogeneous expression, subcellular localization, and variability in selecting appropriate ROIs to score [42, 142], which can be heavily influenced by preanalytic variables intrinsic to the various differences in chemical methods, tissue fixation, and histology laboratory processing and staining protocols. Similarly, challenges still remain for computationally aligning or registering serial sections with companion H&E tissue sections, which can vary due to intrinsic architectural and texture differences in each section. There are also drawbacks with generating spatial molecular maps from serial sections that are related to the spatial resolution of molecular IHC biomarkers, as well as challenges associated to variations that are introduced into tissue samples during histologic sectioning and processing for sample preparation. Another overarching challenge that remains as a barrier to the analyses of spatial multiplex IHC and IF in WSIs is the need for training datasets.
