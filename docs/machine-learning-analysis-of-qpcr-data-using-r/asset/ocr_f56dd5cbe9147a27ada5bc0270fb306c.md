The Taq-pol is now engineered to be produced in E. coli (Lawyer et al., 1989). Taq-pol, however, does not have a high fidelity (it introduces eight mismatched nucleotides every million insertions), and high-fidelity temperature-stable DNA-pol have been isolated from other organisms such as the archea Pyrococcus furiosus (Pfu, which introduces one erroneous dNTP every million insertions) (Cline et al., 1996). In addition, Taq-pol can incorporate modified dNTPs; this feature is used to mark the synthesized DNA with, for instance, digoxigenin or biotin.
Another instance where the wide-range of dNTPs accepted by Taq-pol is that of uracil insertion. By replacing Ts with Us, it is possible to make the synthesized DNA susceptible to uracyl N-glycosylase (UNG) treatment: the digested DNA will reduce the risk of carry-over contamination from one reaction to a subsequent one. Several thermo-stable DNA-pol have been isolated from microbes but, in recent years, mutagenesis is used to specifically enhance the characteristics of the enzyme, such as to provide higher processivity or fidelity (Kermekchiev et al., 2003; Raghunathan and Marx, 2019).
