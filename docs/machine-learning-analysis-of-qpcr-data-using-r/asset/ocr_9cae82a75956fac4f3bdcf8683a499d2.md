It is possible to relate the initial loads N₀ of two sample A and B as follows (Kubista et al., 2006):

N₀ᴬ/N₀ᴮ = (1 + E)^{CqA - CqB} = (1 + E)^{ΔCq}.  (2.2)

When the efficiency of amplification of the PCR is 1 (100%), then the base becomes 2^{ΔCq}. In the example, the difference in Cq between the UV-treated and UV-untreated samples is six, the ratio between their load is 2⁶ = 64.

It is possible to relate the quantity of ROI N_t at a given amplification cycle t to the initial amount of target N₀ with the equation (Rutledge and Côté, 2003):

N₀ = N_t (E+1)^{-t}.  (2.3)

Equation 3 can be linearized by converting to logarithms:

log(N₀) = log(N_t) - log(E+1) t =
= - log(E+1) t + log(N_t).  (2.4)

From Equation 3, the slope of the curve is -log(E+1) and the intercept is log(N_t). The curve needed to generate this curve is practically obtained by serially diluting a stock of a nucleic acids measured with an accurate method. Such series goes under the name of standard dilution and the resulting Cqs are used to generate a standard curve, that is a linear regression model. The interval between the lowest and highest dilutions should be within the dynamic range of the assay, that is the minimal and maximal amounts that the assay can accurately measure.
