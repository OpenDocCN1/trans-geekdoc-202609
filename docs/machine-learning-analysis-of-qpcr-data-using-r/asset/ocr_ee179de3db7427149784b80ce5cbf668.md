That is, a drop of 0.1% and 10% in the efficiency can lead to a copy number overestimation of 1 and 224 percent over full efficiency. Conversely, reactions where the efficiency of amplification is above 100% lead to underestimation of the actual copy numbers. Thus, an efficiency of 110% conveys 67% less copy numbers than by considering the reaction fully efficient. To put such shift in quantification in context, let’s consider the following example. The typical limit of detection of HIV VL is about 40 copies per millilitre (c/mL) of blood (Adams et al., 2019). Let’s assume a sample with 35 c/mL and efficiencies of amplification of 90 and 110 percent, respectively. Using the functions PCR and find_cycle previously defined, it is possible to estimate the expected copy numbers based on a threshold of 10^6 light units:

```
d <- 2
K <- 10^13
A1_0 <- 35
A1 <- PCR(A1_0, d, K)
a1 <- which(A1 > 1000000)[1]
cq_1 <- find_cycle(A1, 1:45, a1)
cq_1
> [1] 16.60976
```

Hence, the returned copy numbers are:

```
E <- c(0.990, 1.100)
log_N0 = -((log(E+1)*cq_1))+log(Nt)
N0 <- 10^log_N0
N0
> [1] 243.08398 31.05318
```
