# Figure 1.1. Overview of the amplification reaction.

The PCR selectively amplifies a region of interest (ROI) of a nucleic acid. A. The PCR cycling conditions. A double-stranded DNA (dsDNA) is denatured by increasing the reaction temperature to about 95°C (melting). The thermal-cycler then reduces the reaction’s temperature to about 56°C allowing synthetic oligonucleotides (primers) to anneal to a selected locus within ROI (annealing). The actual annealing temperature depends on the characteristics of the primers ta hand. The temperature is raised again to about 60°C (elongation). This temperature matches the optimum for the DNA-dependent DNA-polymerase (DNA-pol for short) present in the reaction vessel and depends on the chosen enzyme. B. The PCR produces several types of DNA fragments with different abundances. The initial ROI, a dsDNA, is depicted at the top of the panel.
