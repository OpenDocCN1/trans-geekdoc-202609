It can be seen in the insert of Figure 1.2 that Equation 1 models a never-ending amplification. In reality, the reaction will eventually stall. Liu et al. stated, therefore, that their model was valid only for the initial phases of the PCR. As mentioned in the preceding paragraphs, about 1 µM of primers corresponds to about 10^13 oligonucleotides, hence only 10^13 amplicons can be generated; after that, the reaction simply won’t go any further. The same applies for the triphosphate-nucleotides. In addition, the DNA-pol will become inhibited by the byproducts of the amplification, hence it will amplify with less and less efficiency at each cycle. To cut a long story short, the EF at the end of the PCR will show samples with the same quantity of amplicons. This type of PCR is known as ‘end-point PCR’. For simplicity, this trajectory will be simulated by a logistic function, which increases exponentially in the initial phases of the reaction but then goes to saturation in later phases, just like in the PCR.
