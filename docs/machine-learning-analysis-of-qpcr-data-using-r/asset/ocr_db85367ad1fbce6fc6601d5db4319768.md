(cont.) 3′-end of ROI (unless ROI is so long that the DNA-pol cannot cover the whole segment by the time allocated in the elongation phase). The result is a pair of molecules with heterogeneous strand lengths (c and d). In the second cycle of the PCR, the full-length strands of c and d serve as a template for c and d molecules. Instead, the short strands of c and d will generate fragments extending from the 5′-end of the reverse primer to the 3′-end of ROI (b and e), which are still of different lengths but shorter than c and d. In the third PCR cycle, the short strand of b and e will produce fragments extending from the 5′-end of the forward primer to the 3′-end of the reverse primer (a). The a molecule will be a homogeneous dsDNA fragment whose length is known in advance due to the design of the primers and goes under the name of ‘amplicon’. Since the DNA-pol can perfectly duplicate a within the time allocated for the amplification phase, a will multiply under the 2″ kinetics and quickly becoming the most prevalent molecule in the reaction vessel. In contrast, the other fragment will duplicate linearly under the 2″ kinetics.
