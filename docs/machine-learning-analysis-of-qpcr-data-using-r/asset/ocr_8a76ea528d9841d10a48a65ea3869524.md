Graphically, it is easy to track the Cq into the axis of the quantities by interpolation. Mathematically, regression can only map a y value from an x value; thus, a function of Cq is needed to compute the associated quantity. The quantification is given by the formula:

10^{(Cq - I)/s}  (2.7),

which is the inverse of Equation 2.5, and provided the following inputs:

```
> high_load
[1] 203 109.5
> low_load
[1] 4.521494
```

Practically, the low load is issues as ‘below the limit of detection of the assay’. From Equation 2 it can be seen the quantification described so far relies on the assumption that the efficiency of amplification is the same for all reactions. However, in real-life, organic molecules present in the biological sample might affect the DNA-pol reducing the PCR efficiency. For instance, heme from red cells, fat in the blood, and heparin in the sampling vessel are well-known inhibitors of the PCR (Al-Soud et al., 2000).
