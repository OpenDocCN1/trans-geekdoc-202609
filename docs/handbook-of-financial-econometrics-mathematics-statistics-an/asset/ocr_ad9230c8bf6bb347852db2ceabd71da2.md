An alternative model, UTAUT, was developed in 2003 as an attempt to create the most powerful IT adoption model by the integration of eight models, including TAM and IDT. This model operates on four factors: performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence and facilitating conditions (Venkatesh et al., 2003). In 2012, Venkatesh et al. modified this model by adding three new constructs: hedonic motivation, price value and habit. The researchers also hold only three of the four moderating variables: age, gender and experience. This model was called UTAUT2.

Actually, this tends to build the extensions of well-known models (TAM2, TAM3 and UTAUT2) that originated from empirical studies. A wide stream of the literature shows that integrated and extended models seem to be more powerful than basic ones. Thus, all three core basic models (TAM, IDT, UTAUT) were found to be more powerful while combining extra factors or integrated with each other.

For example, Luarn and Lin (2005) extended TAM by adding three new factors, found all additional factors significant and reported a higher explanatory power of the extended model. Later, Wang, Luarn and Lin (2006) respecified and validated this model. Their results supported the previous findings. In addition, new relationships between the existing chains were also found. They reported the significance of security and privacy for mobile banking, and found that knowledge and financial resources were also important. Later, Gu et al. (2009) added new variables to the integrated model, such as determinants of the two core factors of TAM, and trust as the third core construct. Most of the new factors were significant in the model, and the risk-side factor or trust also seemed to be important as an incentive to use mobile banking.

Recently, Hanafizadeh et al. (2014) tested a model with six factors that are not covered in TAM. The empirical data supported all their hypotheses. The authors not only showed the influence of core factors (perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use) on the intention to use mobile banking but also the importance of other factors: social factors such as the need for personal interactions, or economic factors such as the perceived risk.

IDT-based models were also reported to achieve better estimations when such models were extended with new factors or combinations of factors. Chen (2013) added the intermediary variable of attitude which was connected with the brand, as well as the determinants of risk to the IDT-based model.
