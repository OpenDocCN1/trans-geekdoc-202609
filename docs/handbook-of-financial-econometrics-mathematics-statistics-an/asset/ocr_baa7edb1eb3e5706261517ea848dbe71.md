A model such as this is known as a moving average of infinite order and can be expressed by the following equation:

$y_t = \delta + \Theta_1 a_{t-1} + \Theta_2 a_{t-2} + \cdots + \Theta_{\infty} a_{t-\infty} + a_t,\qquad (26.30)$

where $\delta$ = mean of the process; $a_{t-\infty}$ = random shock that occurred $\infty$ periods earlier; and $\Theta_{\infty}$ = parameter that relates the random shock $\infty$ periods earlier to the current value of $y$.

The moving-average model just discussed should not be confused with the moving-average concept previously discussed in this chapter. Previously, the term moving average was used to refer to an arithmetic average of stock prices over a specified number of days. The term moving average was used because the average was continually updated to include the most recent series of data. In time-series analysis, a moving-average process refers to a series generated by a weighted average of past random shocks.

Because it would be impossible to estimate a model of infinite order, in practice it is best to specify a model of finite order. A moving-average process of order $q$ with zero mean would be expressed as follows:

$y_t = \Theta_1 a_{t-1} + \ldots + \Theta_q a_{t-q} + a_t.\qquad (26.31)$

A moving-average process is not the only way to model a stationary time-series. Again, consider a moving-average process of infinite order:

$y_t = \Theta_1 a_{t-1} + \Theta_2 a_{t-2} + \ldots + a_t.\qquad (26.32)$

Equation (26.32) can be rewritten in terms of the error term $a_t$:

$a_t = y_t - \Theta_1 a_{t-1} - \Theta_2 a_{t-2} - \cdots.\qquad (26.33)$

Because equation (26.33) is recursive in nature, it is easy to generate an expression for $a_{t-1}$:

$a_{t-1} = y_{t-1} - \Theta_1 a_{t-2} - \Theta_2 a_{t-3} - \cdots.\qquad (26.34)$

By substituting the expression for $a_{t-1}$ into equation (26.33):
$a_t = y_t - \Theta_1(y_{t-1} - \Theta_1 a_{t-2} - \Theta_2 a_{t-3} - \cdots)$
$- \Theta_2 a_{t-2} - \Theta_3 a_{t-3} - \cdots$
$= y_t - \Theta_1 y_{t-1} + (\Theta_1^2 - \Theta_2) a_{t-2} + (\Theta_1 \Theta_2 - \Theta_3) a_{t-3} + \cdots.\qquad (26.35)$

By generating expressions for $a_{t-2}, a_{t-3}, \ldots$ and substituting them into equation (26.35) $a_t$ can be expressed in terms of past values of $y_t$:

$a_t = y_t + \phi_1 y_{t-1} + \phi_2\, y_{t-2} + \cdots.\qquad (26.36)$
