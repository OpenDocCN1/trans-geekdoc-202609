Social media are increasingly taking up a high share of company information online in the same way that bank relationships provide companies’ financial information. That is, social media and bank relationships both overcome problems of asymmetric information in firm value.

The business press provides equity market participants with fundamental information about firms that is incremental to that provided by other information intermediaries and accounting data and reduces information asymmetries between equity investors (Tetlock *et al.*, 2008; Bushee *et al.*, 2010). Information asymmetry is a condition in which one participant in an exchange possesses more or better information than the other (Akerlof, 1970). A defining feature of equity markets is that investors do not generally have privileged access to the firms’ confidential information as the securities law prohibits unequal access to such information. This feature creates an opportunity for the media to serve as an information intermediary for equity investors seeking information from sources external to firms (Bushee *et al.*, 2010; Bushman *et al.*, 2017).

In general, firms provide information to investors through financial statements and other disclosures such as annual reports; numerous studies have suggested the existence of asymmetric information between firms and investors (Healy and Palepu, 2001). To overcome this asymmetry, investors seek additional information from multiple sources, such as new product announcements, expert reviews, and user-generated content (Chen *et al.*, 2012; Tellis and Johnson, 2007; Tirunillai and Tellis, 2012). However, social media communication between a firm and its consumers provides one such source of information to investors, which can then infer the firm’s level of involvement with its customer base and the firm’s commitment to improving its relationships with customers from its social media efforts.

Social media have opened new ways to communicate, collaborate, share content and advertise online in support of branding, sales, customer service, and product development. In the same way, online investors are largely involved in browsing firm and product webpages and exploring information on search engines and social media platforms. This environment reduces the information asymmetry between firms and investors and widens the application of associated investments. In addition, some website visitors may be current or potential investors who access websites to obtain information that is relevant to investment decision-making (Chung *et al.*, 2014; Luo *et al.*, 2013). Social media content is updated instantly and spreads virally at an unprecedented speed, thus providing first-hand information to investors ahead of any other sources. In this sense, the social media metrics of a firm determine the association between social media and bank relationships and the effect of such association on firm value.

## 66.3 Data and Methodology

### 66.3.1 Data sources and sample

To construct the data, we used a sample comprising publicly listed companies in Taiwan. Of the 882 listed companies, we excluded 33 firms in the financial industry and thus obtained 849 sample companies. The effective original sample comprised 6,636 year-firm observations. The financial data of the Taiwanese listed companies for the period of 2008–2015 were obtained from the TEJ database, from where we obtained all the firm characteristics and Tobin’s Q values. Bank Loan details that reflect bank relationships were obtained from the TEJ bank loan database.

The sample was classified into eight industries in accordance with the industry classifications proposed by the Directorate General of Budget, Accounting, and Statistics (DGBAS) and compiled under the “Standard Industrial Classification of the ROC (Taiwan)”.² Appendix A presents a table comparing all the listed companies by industry group and the standard industrial classification (SIC) of Taiwan.

### 66.3.2 Methodology

To exploit the variation in firm value across social media and bank relationships, this strategy can be generalized to a regression framework. Consider first the difference between social media and bank relationships. Then include the interaction between social media and bank relationships. Also, include the influence of firms’ financial variables to control for exogenous factors that can affect firm value. The baseline regression is specified in equation (66.1) as follows:

#### 66.3.2.1 Firm value

Tobin’s Q ratio has been used extensively as a measure of a firm’s value. The use of Tobin’s Q for measuring firm value is based on fact that the long-term equilibrium market value of a firm must be equal to the replacement value of its assets, giving a Tobin’s Q value close to unity. We adopted the Tobin’s Q developed by Chung and Pruitt (1994), where Tobin’s Q = (MVE + PS + DEBT)/TA. Here, MVE is the product of a firm’s share price and the number of outstanding common stock shares. PS is the liquidating value of the firm’s outstanding preferred stock, DEBT is the value of the firm’s short-term liabilities net of its short-term assets plus the book value of the firm’s long-term debt, and TA is the book value of the total assets of the firm.

#### 66.3.2.2 Social media

Social media include the following social media platforms: Facebook, YouTube, Twitter and other sites such as Google+ and LinkedIn. The data were gathered manually from the companies’ social networks (Facebook, YouTube and Twitter) and then categorized as official and unofficial. Official social networks were those with access links directly provided in company websites; unofficial ones were those with links not provided in company websites and were searched for in social networks.

Available Facebook data were collected through the Facebook Application Programming Interface (API) graph of each firm. We obtained the timestamp of the posts/comments, looked for the year of establishment and counted the number of “likes” in the company pages across different years.

Available YouTube data were obtained by examining the companies’ YouTube activities from the home pages of their official websites or unofficial social media networks. We also considered and recorded the number of views and subscribers of the companies’ YouTube channels.

Available Twitter data were obtained by examining each company’s Twitter activities from the home page of the firm’s official website. We also considered and recorded the number of tweets and followers as the proxy.

We gathered the information regarding the social media platforms used by the sample firms and found that the leading social media used by the companies are Facebook, YouTube, Twitter and Others (Google+ and LinkedIn). These platforms served as the dummy variables. Facebook, YouTube and Twitter as dummy variables were equal to 1 if the firm’s home page provided a link to an official Facebook page, YouTube channel and Twitter account and were equal to 0 otherwise. We added Others as a dummy variable, which was equal to 1 if the firm’s home page showed a different social media icon and equal to 0 otherwise.

#### 66.3.2.3 Bank relationships

Bank relationships in this study include two measures: BankRelationship, which was measured by the logarithm of 1 plus the total number of banks that provided loans to the firm during the fiscal year; and BankDebt_TA, measured by the total amount of bank loans divided by the total assets. These data on bank relationships were obtained from the TEJ bank loan database and calculated accordingly.

#### 66.3.2.4 Control variables

The controls are as follows. Firm age (Age) is measured by the number of years since the establishment of the firm. Firm size (LnSize) is measured by the logarithm of the firm’s book value of total assets. The ratio of financial structures that are considered intangible assets (Intangible) is measured by total goodwill and intangible assets divided by total assets. Financial leverage (Leverage) is measured by the book value of the firm’s total liabilities divided by total assets. Solvency is determined by the ratio of the current assets (CurrentRatio), which is measured by the book value of the firm’s current assets divided by current liabilities. Working capital ratio (NWC_Assets) is measured by the net working capital divided by total assets. Cash flow ratio (OCF_CL) is measured by the operating cash flow divided by current liabilities. The firm’s growth is measured by sales growth rate (SaleGrowth) from (Sales_t — Sales_t-1)/Sales_t-1.

Appendix B provides detailed definition about the construction of the variables. All continuous variables were winsorized at the 1% and 99% levels to limit the influence of outliers.
