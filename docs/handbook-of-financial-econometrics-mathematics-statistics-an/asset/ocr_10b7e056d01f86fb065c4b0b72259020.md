At the optimal point, utilizing the Karush–Kuhn–Tucher optimality conditions, we have the following saddle point equations:
\(\frac{\partial L}{\partial w}=0,\quad \frac{\partial L}{\partial b}=0\quad\text{and}\quad \frac{\partial L}{\partial \xi_i}=0,\)

Combined with (20.2), we obtain the following equations:
\(w = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i y_i \phi(x_i),\quad \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i y_i = 0\quad\text{and}\quad \alpha_i = C - \theta_i\quad i=1,\dots,n.\tag{20.3}\)

By substituting (20.3) into (20.2), and by replacing $(\phi(x_i)\cdot\phi(x_j))$ with kernel functions $K(x_i,x_j)$, we get the dual optimization problem of the original problem:
\[\max\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_i - \frac12\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_i\alpha_j y_i y_j K(x_i,x_j),\]
\[\text{s.t.}\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_i y_i =0,\]
\[0\le\alpha_i\le C,\quad i=1,\dots,n.\tag{20.4}\]

On solving the dual optimization problem, solution $\alpha_i$ determines the parameters of the optimal hyper-plane. This leads to the decision function, expressed as
\[f(x)=\operatorname{sgn}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} y_i\alpha_i K(x_i,x)+b\right),\tag{20.5}\]
where $b = y_i - \sum_{i=1}^{n} y_i\alpha_i K(x_i,x_j)$, for some $j$, $\alpha_i \le C$.

Usually, there is only a small subset of Lagrange multipliers $\alpha_i$ that is greater than zero in a classification problem. Training vectors having non-zero $\alpha_i^{*}$ are called support vectors, that is, the optimal decision hyper-plane depends on the support vectors exclusively. Finally, the decision function could be written as follows:
\[f(x)=\operatorname{sgn}\left(\sum_{i\in SV} y_i\alpha_i^{*}k(x_i,x)+b^{*}\right).\tag{20.6}\]
