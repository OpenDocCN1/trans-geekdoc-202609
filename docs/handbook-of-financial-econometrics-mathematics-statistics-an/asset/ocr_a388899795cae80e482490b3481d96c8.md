In contrast, to avoid the free cash flow problem, a profitable firm may increase its borrowing. The interests and principle payments will reduce the free cash flow and the potential agency problem of the profitable firm. Therefore, based on Jensen’s (1986) agency costs theory, the association between the capital structure and the profitability is positive. In accordance with Titman and Wessels (1988), the indicators of profitability are operating income over sales (OI_S) and operating income over total assets (OI_TA).

# 35.2.1.2 Macroeconomic factors

McDonald (1983) extends Miller’s (1977) theory and investigates the impact of government financial decisions on the capital structure. The equilibrium of McDonald’s (1983) model shows that the corporate debt-to-wealth ratio is negatively related to the government debt-to-wealth ratio. This finding implies that the decrease in federal borrowing would lead to an increase in the firm’s debt–equity ratio.

The previous studies confirm the negative relationship between government leverage and private sector debt (see Greenwood et al., 2010; Greenwood and Vayanos, 2010, 2014; Bansal et al., 2011; Krishnamurthy and Vissing-Jorgensen, 2012; Graham et al., 2015). Bansal et al. (2011) provide an equilibrium model to illustrate the endogenous supply relationship between short-term public debt and private debt. The researchers employ the vector auto-regression (VAR) to perform empirical work and confirm the prediction of their model that an increase in government leverage leads to the decrease in private debt. Krishnamurthy and Vissing-Jorgensen (2012) show the negative correlation between the government leverage and the corporate bond spread, which is the difference of yields on AAA corporate bonds and long maturity Treasury bonds. When the supply of public debt decreases, the wide corporate bond spread implies an increase in the supply of corporate debt. This evidence is consistent with the finding in Greenwood et al. (2010) that the issues of private debts appear to shift in the supply of government debt. Graham et al. (2015) use both macroeconomic factors and firm characteristics to investigate the determinants of capital structure and find that government leverage (debt-to-GDP ratio), which is defined as the ratio of federal debt held by the public to GDP, is an important determinant of the variation in the aggregate leverage, which is defined as the ratio of aggregate total debt to aggregate book value.
