In addition, higher buy-back ratio of stock repurchase is a debt-like signal for the CB issues. This result supports the signaling equilibrium model of Constantinides and Grundy (1989), which provides a debt-rationing rationale as opposed to the equity-rationing rationale for CBs issues in combination with stock repurchase that is linked to arbitrage-related short selling by convertible arbitrageurs (see, e.g., de Jong *et al.*, 2011; Brown *et al.*, 2012).

Finally, firms’ risk-shifting potential cannot deteriorate CBs’ risk-mitigating effect. For riskier firms, higher risk-shifting potential, either higher ratio of discretionary asset or higher free cash flow, decreases the sequential conversion rate. This implies that the CBs’ risk-mitigating effect is maintained for riskier firms.
