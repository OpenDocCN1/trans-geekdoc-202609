# 6.2.4 Combination forecasting model

According to Granger and Ramanathan (1984) and Lin et al. (2015), the combination forecasting model of TAIEX, there are two ways to setup the model. One is simple average way, the other one is minimum error way. The simple average combination forecasting model could be represented as: ln \hat{P}^C = ω₁ ln \hat{P}^{M1} + ω₂ ln \hat{P}^{M2} + ⋯ + ω_k ln \hat{P}^{Mk}, where k is the number of models, and ω₁, ω₂, …, ω_k are the weight of each model, and in this model the weight is calculated by average (ω₁ + ω₂ + ⋯ + ω_k)/k. The minimum error combination forecasting model could be represented as: ln \hat{P}^C = ω₁ ln \hat{P}^{M1} + ω₂ ln \hat{P}^{M2} + ⋯ + ω_k ln \hat{P}^{Mk} + ε, and in this model the weight is calculated by simple regression method.

# 6.3 Empirical Analysis

Let P be the TAIEX, D be the INDU (Dow Jones Industrial Average Index), E be the exchange rate, and C be the consumer price index. In order to eliminate the inconsistency of the data unit, natural logarithms in the series TAIEX, INDU, exchange rate, and consumer price index are taken and expressed as ln P, ln D, ln E, ln C. To establish the prediction model for the TAIEX, the researchers used the time-series data from the Directorate-General of Budget, Accounting and Statistics, Executive Yuan, R.O.C.,³ and Taiwan Economic Journal.⁴ The data periods from January 1991 to January 2012 yielded 258 months of data.
