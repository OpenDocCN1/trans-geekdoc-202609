Table 92.5 and Figures 92.2(a)-92.2(e) present the co-integrating coefficients and their corresponding tracks across various quantiles. We find that future prices have stronger co-integration relationships with gold spot prices at high quantiles. By contrast, futures prices have lower co-integration with gold spot prices at low quantiles. That means the co-integration between gold spot and futures is increasing with quantiles. These findings suggest that when gold spot prices are at low quantiles, the response of spot prices to futures prices is miniscule. However, when spot gold prices are at high quantiles, the responses become considerable.

Moreover, long-term futures prices have lower co-integration than short-term futures prices at the same quantile. For example, Table 92.5 shows that the co-integrations of gold spot and 1-, 2-, 3-, and 6-month futures prices at 0.9 quantiles are associated with the coefficients of 1.0004, 1.0000, 0.9998 and 0.9992, respectively.

For gold futures with maturities of 2- 3-, or 6-month contracts, we find that for low quantile memberships, the Johansen co-integration overestimates the co-integration, whereas for high quantile memberships, this estimate is underestimated. This evidence is more pronounced when the quantiles of gold returns are high, and this pattern applies to all maturities of gold futures contracts, including spot, short- or long-terms futures.
