## 38.4.3 Results analysis

To examine whether the constructed mixture copulas can satisfactorily capture the dependence structure between the three types of risks, the performance of these mixture copulas is compared with seven other major risk integration approaches comprehensively.

### 38.4.3.1 *Effectiveness and efficiency comparison*

In this subsection, all of the five major individual copulas and the three mixture copulas constructed (Section 38.3.2) are used to describe the dependence structure between credit, market and operational risks of Chinese banks. Then, they are compared from the perspectives of both effectiveness and efficiency. Table 38.8 shows their estimated parameters, efficiency comparison results and effectiveness comparison results.

To evaluate the effectiveness of the constructed mixture copulas, one of the most widespread goodness-of-fit tests, the Cramer–von Mises test, is used to examine whether these mixture copulas can accurately describe the dependence structure between the three risk types. The test results of all these approaches are listed in the last column of Table 38.8. In line with other goodness-of-fit tests, the larger the P value is, the better the copula can fit the dependence structure, and the threshold is also 0.05. The comparison of the goodness-of-fit test results uncovers several important and interesting findings.

A notable finding is that none of the individual copulas can pass the goodness-of-fit test, including the t copula. As shown in Table 38.8, P values of the five major copulas, the Gaussian copula, t copula, Gumbel copula, Clayton copula and Frank copula, are 0.00, 0.02, 0.00, 0.00 and 0.01, respectively. All of them are much smaller than 0.05, indicating that it is inappropriate to use these copulas to describe the dependence between the three risk types. Moreover, the P value of the t copula is the largest, which nicely accords with the theoretical analysis in Section 38.3.2 and proves that the t copula is indeed the best choice among the individual copulas. Most interestingly, this finding uncovers an important pitfall. That is, although copula approaches are now widely recognized as the leading approach in risk dependence modeling, they are all strongly rejected by the goodness-of-fit test in this case, including the t copula. The usual choice of the t copula is similar to selecting a general from a lame team. The effectiveness of the copula approach is questionable.

On the contrary, another finding is that all the mixture copulas do pass the goodness-of-fit test. To be precise, P values of the t-Gumbel copula, Gaussian–Gumbel–Clayton copula and Gaussian–Gumbel copula are 0.37, 0.26 and 0.14, respectively, which are all much larger than 0.05. This indicates that all of these three mixture copulas can satisfactorily model the dependence structure and provides strong evidence of the effectiveness of the mixture copula approach. Moreover, the t-Gumbel mixture copula performs the best with the largest P value, which is as high as 0.37, followed by

the Gaussian–Gumbel–Clayton copula with 0.26 and the Gaussian–Gumbel copula with 0.14, which nicely corroborates the analysis in Section 38.3.2 that the t-Gumbel copula is able to capture the overall typical features of risk dependence, the Gaussian–Gumbel–Clayton copula has less ability, and the Gaussian–Gumbel copula has the least.

For a more rounded evaluation of the mixture copulas, computational efficiencies of these approaches are also analyzed. The longer the computational time is, the lower the efficiency is. As mentioned in Section 38.3.1, compared with individual copulas, mixture copulas not only contain the association parameters of all component copulas but also hold extra weight parameters. Therefore, the increase in the number of parameters inevitably leads to additional estimation time. Table 38.8 shows the number of parameters and the corresponding estimation time of each type of copula. All parameters are estimated by the maximum likelihood method with the EM algorithm.

As shown in Table 38.8, the number of parameters of the Gaussian copula, t copula, Gumbel copula, Clayton copula and Frank copula are 3, 4, 1, 1 and 1, which cost estimation time of 0.01, 0.05, 0.13, 0.03 and 0.08 seconds, respectively. All have estimation time much smaller than 1 second. In contrast, the time cost of the three mixture copulas is significantly higher. The t-Gumbel copula comprising two weight parameters and five association parameters, a total of seven parameters, costs 15.42 seconds. Compared with the t-Gumbel copula, there is one more weight parameter for the Gaussian–Gumbel–Clayton copula and a total of eight parameters are needed to be estimated, costing 16.37 s, which is a little longer than for the t-Gumbel copula. The Gaussian–Gumbel copula needs to estimate 2 weight parameters and four association parameters, a total of six parameters, costing 13.18 s, which is less than for the former two mixture copulas.

To sum up, the goodness-of-fit test demonstrates that compared with individual copulas, the capability of the mixture copula to capture dependence between different types of risks is significantly improved. More component copulas can bring more flexibility; however, the computational cost also increases because more parameters are involved. Therefore, the correct way to construct a proper mixture copula is to cover the overall features with the least possible number of copulas.
