
## 4.3.2 Summary statistics

Table 4.1 provides the preliminary statistics of stock, bond and gold prices. As shown in Panel A of Table 4.1, the means of stock, bond, and gold prices over the entire sample period are 974.61, 120.68, and 590.84, respectively. The skewness and kurtosis indicates that these price series are not normally distributed and rather right-skewed. Price variabilities are captured by the coefficients of variation (CV), which equal 0.39, 0.11 and 0.71 for stock, bond and gold prices, respectively. The fluctuations in gold price are much greater than that in the stock and bond market within the entire sample period.

As we divide our sample into three subsample periods shown respectively in Panel B, Panel C and Panel D of Table 4.1, the following implications are observed. The observed price of gold fluctuated around $338.28 per ounce within the period of 1990-2001 and declined to a trough of $254.27 per ounce in early 2000s. Moreover, the average value of gold price during the period of 2008-2013 equals $1284.99 as observed in Panel D of Table 4.1, which is much greater than that during the periods of 1990-2001 and 2002-2007. This reflects the evidence of recent bull market of gold since the early 2000s. On the other side, the coefficient of variation of gold price fluctuated much more since 2002, which is about double of the value in the first sub-period of 1990-2001. The results of coefficient of variation reveals that gold price increased significantly during the last 14 years. In addition, S&P 500 index fluctuated much more within the period of 1990 to 2001 with a coefficient of variation of 0.51, which exceeds triple of the value in the other two sub-periods.

Overall, as measured by coefficients of variation, we can find that the fluctuations in the return of gold are much greater than that in the stock and bond market since the early 2000s. This finding is in line with the result of Jaffe (1989) that gold’s standard deviation is greater than that of stocks and bonds. Even though gold itself has a fair amount of volatility, Jaffe (1989) still finds that the induction of gold can reduce the risk of an already diversified portfolio because of the correlation of gold with other financial assets. However, Tang and Xiong (2012) indicate that the correlation of commodity and other financial return has been increasing because of the financialization of commodity. Therefore, an interesting question arises whether the role played by gold in a diversified portfolio is affected by the financialization of commodity markets.

## 4.3.3 Rolling correlation

In this subsection, the preliminary analysis of correlation relationship among gold, US stock and US bond returns is provided in Figure 4.1. Panel A of Figure 4.1 illustrates one-year rolling correlations among gold, US stock and US bond returns since 1991. While the average correlation between gold and US stock equals 0.03 marked by the dash line in Panel A of Figure 4.1, the feature of the figure shows that correlations vary widely from this average. The correlation estimates vary in the range of −0.26 to 0.35 with a standard deviation of 0.13. Similarly time-variation of gold-bond correlations have also been apparent in Panel A of Figure 4.1. The findings here provide evidences that the short-term correlation relationship among gold, US stock and US bond returns is dynamic rather than static. It is remarkable that a temporarily negative estimate of correlation between gold and stock is usually found in the turmoil periods: such as the Gulf War oil crisis of 1991, the Asian crisis of 1997, the internet bubble of 2000 and the credit crisis of 2008. The finding is consistent with the result of Baur and Lucey (2010) that gold is often regarded as safe haven during the crisis periods. This confirms that the diversification benefits of gold work well in stock portfolio when it is needed most.

## 4.4 Empirical Results

### 4.4.1 Wavelet power spectrum analysis

The variabilities of these three asset returns here are captured by the estimated wavelet power spectrum presented in Figure 4.2. The wavelet spectrum provides a better measure of variance attributed to localized events. Local maxima in the wavelet spectrum provide information on the scales in which important characteristics or coherent events provide a significant contribution. As shown in Panel A of Figure 4.2, the S&P 500 return has the highest short-term (one-year) wavelet spectrum on average while the bond return has the lowest among the three assets. Moreover, the short-term stock market volatility measured by the power spectrum is relatively high during the crisis periods of this study: 1997–1998, 2001–2002, 2008–2009 and 2011. In particular, the relatively low volatility of gold in times of market turmoil is consistent with the fact that gold provides the need for a safe haven for investors. Overall, the short-term power spectrum of three asset returns has increased remarkably over the last decade.

Looking at the long-term wavelet power spectrum as shown in Panel B of Figure 4.2, we observed that the long-term power spectrum of three asset returns has also increased observably over the last decade.
