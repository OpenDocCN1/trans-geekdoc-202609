where

$$\sum_i \equiv \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \sigma_v^2 & \sigma_v^2 & \cdots & \sigma_v^2 \\ \sigma_v^2 & \ddots & \ddots & \vdots \\ \vdots & \ddots & \ddots & \sigma_v^2 \\ \sigma_v^2 & \cdots & \sigma_v^2 & \sigma_\varepsilon^2 + \sigma_v^2 \end{bmatrix}\tag{28.29}$$

is $T \times T$ with $\sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2$ on the principal diagonal and $\sigma_v^2$ elsewhere, and $\mathbf{i}$ is a $T$-vector of 1s.

To obtain the GLS estimates of $\beta$ we need to know $\sigma_\varepsilon^2$ and $\sigma_v^2$, or at least their ratio since GLS estimation requires only that $\Omega$ be consistently specified up to a factor. So to obtain feasible GLS estimates, we need a consistent estimate of this ratio. Fortunately, it is always possible to find a consistent estimate of $\sigma_\varepsilon^2$ by estimating the fixed effects model (28.24) because no matter how $N$ and $T$ may behave as $NT \to \infty$, there are $NT$ residuals. Dividing the sum of squared errors from (28.24), $SSE_{FE}$, by $NT - N - K$ gives an unbiased and consistent estimate of $\sigma_\varepsilon^2$ since the error terms for this model are just the $\varepsilon_{it}$. But the natural estimator of $\sigma_v^2$, the sample variance of the elements of $\hat\gamma$ is not consistent unless $N \to \infty$ (so the RE estimator is problematic for small samples).

If $NT \to \infty$ as $N \to \infty$, $\sigma_v^2$ can be estimated consistently. Start with the regression

$$\mathbf{P_D y} = \mathbf{P_D X}\beta + \text{residuals},\tag{28.30}$$

where $\mathbf{P_D} \equiv \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{M_D}$ and $\mathbf{M_D}$ denotes the projection matrix $\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{D}(\mathbf{D' D})^{-1}\mathbf{D'}$, to obtain the between-groups estimator:

$$\hat\beta_{BG} = (\mathbf{X' P_D X})^{-1} \mathbf{X' P_D y},\tag{28.31}$$

which is based on only $N$ observations since all variables are the same for every observation in each group. It only exists if $N > K$ since the matrix $\mathbf{X' P_D X}$ can have at most rank $N$. It is called the between estimator because it uses only the variation among the group means.

If the restrictions of the random effects model are not satisfied, the $\hat\beta_{BG}$ estimator, if it exists, is in general biased and inconsistent. To see this, unbiasedness and consistency require that the moment condition

$$E\big((\mathbf{P_D X})'_{it}(y_{it} - \mathbf{X}_{it}\beta)\big) = 0,\tag{28.32}$$

where $(\mathbf{P_D X})_{it}$ is the row labeled $it$ of the $NT \times K$ matrix $\mathbf{P_D X}$. Since $y_{it} - \mathbf{X}_{it}\beta = v_i + \varepsilon_{it}$ and $\varepsilon_{it}$ are independent of everything else in (28.32), this condition is equivalent to no correlation between the $v_i$ and elements of the matrix $\mathbf{X}$.
