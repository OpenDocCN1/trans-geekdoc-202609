# Proposition 2: (Hedging with Greeks, specifically rho and phi).

The second proposition focuses on Rho and phi because the risk premium is made up of the difference between continuous risk-free interest rate and dividend yield. Sebehela (2016) explored Greeks including rho and phi in the presence of exchange options in the REIT industry. The results illustrates that the phi call increases as deal approaches the maturity while phi put does the opposite. For the rho during the same period, most of the rho is the put as opposed in the call. Benhamou (2003) stated that Greeks are not only important in risk management and hedging, but also in pricing of products. In non-finance terms, the overall rho (phi) seems to be made up of call and put rhos (phis) as the two rhos (phis) are pulling into different directions. Then, this chapter separates rho and phi for hedging strategies.

# Proposition 2(a): (Rho hedging).

Rho hedging is normally part of other Greeks. Benhamou (2003) explored Malliavin weighting in modeling the Greeks. He argues that the Greeks are normally modeled using finite difference method but when one calculates bumpy parameters and re-prices, two errors emerge; (i) derivative function by means of finite difference, and (ii) the numerical computation. In his modeling, he drives new Greeks formulas based on Malliavin principles.
