Engle (1982) proposes the autoregressive conditional heteroscedasticity (ARCH) model to describe the aforementioned phenomena, particularly in the fluctuation of foreign exchange rates. Diebold et al. (1993) also provide evidence that the monthly stock returns of the NYSE firms have a rather common ARCH effect during the 1939–1983 period. They found that about 25% of NYSE firms have significant ARCH effects at the 10% level. This phenomenon raises the concerns of the test methodology of event studies that assumes homoscedasticity in residual returns. The sample average abnormal return is an efficient estimator of the expected abnormal return in the presence of heteroscedasticity. They therefore suggest that the identification of a well-defined variance structure such as ARCH can be used to improve research design.

Specifically, we develop a time-varying systematic risk model with return to normality by employing the theoretical framework in Bollerlev et al. (1988) who cast the ARCH model into the CAPM.¹ We expect that the market would revise their assessment of systematic risks once the accounting earnings have been announced. We use Kalman filter to estimate how the market revises the systematic risks subsequent to the announcement of accounting earnings. We then examine the cumulative abnormal returns (CARs) estimated from both the conventional as well as the time-varying systematic market models to see if the PEADs can be attributed to the misspecification of CAPM.

Our empirical analysis is based on a sample of US publicly held companies during the five-fiscal year sample period, 2010–2014. Controlling for misspecification and other potential confounding effects, we document that the phenomenon of PEADs are economically insignificant for investment decision making.

This chapter adds to the literature in two ways. First, we provide empirical evidence on how the revision of systematic risk affects the phenomenon of PEADs. Secondly, we provide an alternative approach for reducing the misspecification of CAPM. More specifically, we show that incorporating Kalman filter and ARCH into the conventional CAPM helps reduce the conditional heteroscedasticity subsequent to earnings announcement.

> Bollerlev et al. (1992) cite over 200 articles using ARCH techniques in a wide variety of finance applications. However, this technique has not yet become popular in accounting literature.
