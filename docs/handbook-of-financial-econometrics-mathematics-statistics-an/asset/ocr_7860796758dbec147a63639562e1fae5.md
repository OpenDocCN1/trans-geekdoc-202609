In the literature, two approaches are proposed for improving the Gaussian GARCH model. The first approach applies leptokurtic and skewed non-Gaussian distributions on the log-return innovations in a GARCH-type model (e.g., see Duan, 1999). The second approach, characterized by excess skewness and kurtosis resulting from occasional large price jumps, imposes stochastic jumps on the log-return innovations in a GARCH-type model (e.g., see Jorion, 1988; Duffie et al., 2000; Pan, 2002; Eraker et al., 2003, 2004). In a more advanced GARCH-jump mixture model, namely, the GARJI model, the conditional variance of asset returns is divided into two components to describe moderate and large price movements resulting from normal and extreme news events, respectively (e.g., see Chan and Maheu, 2002; Maheu and McCurdy, 2004). More specifically, two conditionally independent autoregressive processes for the variability corresponding to normal and large price movements, respectively, are used to describe the dynamics of the two components in a discrete-time setting. Despite the explanatory power of the excess skewness and kurtosis provided by the GARCH-jump mixture model, the locally risk-neutral valuation relationship (LRNVR) for asset pricing proposed by Duan (1995), which provides a link between the risk-neutral distribution and the corresponding historical distribution, cannot be obtained under GARCH-jump models even by further restricting investor preferences (e.g., see Duan et al., 2006).

Rather than using two conditionally independent autoregressive processes, this study proposes a GARCH-type model, namely, a VG NGARCH model, which is constructed based on a variance-gamma (VG) process embedded with a generalized autoregressive conditional heteroskedasticity (GARCH) process on its shape parameter that describes the conditional variance of asset log-returns. Being a purely jump process that accounts for jump discontinuities in asset price dynamics due to the news arrivals having different impacts on the magnitudes of price movements (e.g., see Madan, 1998), the VG process has been applied in finance for asset price dynamics. However, as noted by Konikov and Madan (2002), a homogeneous VG model fails to explain volatility clustering phenomenon owning to the feedback effect. To account for the volatility clustering phenomena and the leverage effect, the proposed VG NGARCH model considers the use of a nonlinear asymmetric autoregressive structure on the shape parameter.
