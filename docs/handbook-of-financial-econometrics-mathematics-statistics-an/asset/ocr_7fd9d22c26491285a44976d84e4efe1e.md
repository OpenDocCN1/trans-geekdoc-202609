If we have large sample, $\hat{S}^2$ may be a good estimator as the sample mean and sample covariance matrix are maximum likelihood estimators (MLEs). However, MLEs may not perform well due to sampling errors when we have only a limited sample period, such as in emerging markets, or when we are interested in its time-varying nature.

Despite the importance of the sample maximum squared Sharpe ratio, little efforts are devoted to studying its issues and its improvement. Kan and Smith (2008) find that the sample minimum variance frontier is on average too optimistic. We find that the optimistic bias also affects $\hat{S}^2$ because a large part of error comes from the large upward bias of the sample squared slope of the asymptote to the minimum variance frontier ($\hat{\psi}^2$). In the theoretical literature, MSR is used for establishing a bound for the sum of squared pricing errors in beta pricing equations (Chamberlain and Rothschild, 1983). The significant upward bias of the sample maximum squared Sharpe ratio will lead to a higher probability of not rejecting a false pricing model.

The objective of this chapter is to search for improved estimators of MSR in finite sample. We find that a large part of error in $\hat{S}^2$ comes from the upward bias in $\hat{\psi}^2$. The bias is more significant in the case of the finite sample. By subtracting the bias from $\hat{\psi}^2$ and with some scale adjustment, we can get uniformly minimum variance unbiased estimator (UMVUE) of $\psi^2$, denoted by $\hat{\psi}_u^2$. The upward bias in $\hat{S}^2$ can be greatly reduced when $\hat{\psi}^2$ is replaced by $\hat{\psi}_u^2$. However, the chances of having negative estimates of $\psi^2$ and hence negative $\hat{S}^2$ increase. To show the impact on estimation of $S^2$, we evaluate the probability of getting negative estimates with the parameters derived from US market.$^2$ Our simulation shows that using $\hat{\psi}_u^2$ will result in the chance of having negative estimates for $S^2$ as high as 33% when the sample period is five years. Moreover, in the Japanese market, $\psi^2$ is very low due to long-term sagging economy. When we calculate $\hat{\psi}_u^2$ over rolling five years from 7/1990 to 12/2017, there are 192 negative values out of 271 estimates.
