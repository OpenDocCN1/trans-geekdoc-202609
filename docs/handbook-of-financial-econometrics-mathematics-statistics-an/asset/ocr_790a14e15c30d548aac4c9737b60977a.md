where the larger α is, the heavier the dependence is. Specifically, α = 1 implies the independence copula, and as α approaches ∞, we obtain the perfect dependence copula. The Gumbel copula exhibits greater dependence in the right tail than in the left.

(4) The Clayton copula is presented as

$C_{\text{Clayton}}(u_1, u_2, \dots, u_n|\beta) = \left(\sum_{n=1}^{N} u_n^{-\beta} - N + 1\right)^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}, \quad \beta \in (0,\infty),$ (38A.4)

where the larger β is, the heavier the dependence is. Specifically, as β approaches 0, we obtain the independence copula, whereas as β approaches ∞, we obtain the perfect dependence copula. The Clayton copula exhibits greater dependence in the left tail than in the right.

(5) The Frank copula is presented as

$C_{\text{Frank}}(u_1, u_2, \dots, u_n|\lambda) = -\frac{1}{\lambda} \ln\left(1 + \frac{\prod_{n=1}^{N} (e^{-\lambda u_n} - 1)}{(e^{-\lambda} - 1)^{N-1}}\right), \quad \lambda \neq 0,$ when $N \ge 3, \lambda \in (0,\infty),$ (38A.5)

where the Frank copula displays perfect negative dependence for λ equal to −∞, perfect positive dependence for λ equal to ∞, and independence for λ equal to 0. The Frank copula can only capture symmetric dependence and is insensitive to either left or right tail dependence.
