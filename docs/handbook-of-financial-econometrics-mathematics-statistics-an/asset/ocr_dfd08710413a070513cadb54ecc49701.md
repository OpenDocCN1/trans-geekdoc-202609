In particular, the error introduced in the optimal payout ratio by misspecification of the growth rate as deterministic is identified and shown to relate to its initial stochastic conditions. The model developed in this chapter brings out explicitly the influence of covariance between risks of non-stationary profitability and growth rate on the optimal dividend policy.

Empirical work on examining the mean-reverting process of the optimal growth rate and the importance of the interaction between the profitability risk and the growth risk are confirmed. The partial adjustment regressions show that, on average, firms will partially adjust their growth rate and take five years to make the growth rate reach the optimal target rate. The fixed-effects model with a structural change not only confirms the flexibility and the free cash flow hypotheses proposed by Lee et al. (2011), but also shows the covariance between the profitability and the growth rate is one of the determinants of divided payout. In addition, the probit model shows that the interaction between the profitability risk and the growth rate can affect a firm’s decisions on not only stopping dividend payments but also in not initiating dividend payments. Such an irreversible effect along with the intertemporal behavior of the covariance term may shed some light on the puzzle of disappearing dividends over the last 40 years.

Important work, as mentioned earlier, in determining the optimal equilibrium values of corporate decision variables has been done by Gordon (1962), Lintner (1964), Modigliani and Miller (1961), Miller and Modigliani (1966), DeAngelo et al. (1996), Fama and French (2001), DeAngelo and DeAngelo (2006), and Lee et al. (2011). The need, however, for further research in tracing the optimal time path under uncertainty leading to these equilibrium values and the relevant stability conditions remains, and this chapter is a modest step forward in that direction.
