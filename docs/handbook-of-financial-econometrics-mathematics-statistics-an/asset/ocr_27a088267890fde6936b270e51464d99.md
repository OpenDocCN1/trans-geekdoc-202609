Figure 46.2 depicts this system as a queueing network. Thus, our model consists of two interacting supply chains: one of product and the other of funds. Mathematically, it is a population process of PEMUs circulating directionally among three pools (inventory, receivables and available funds), subject to identity changes. As such, it captures the turning of the “wheel of commerce” in that it describes how money turns into product and product into money. The system starts operations with a capital investment in the treasury of G > 0 PEMUs, a credit line of H ≥ 0 PEMUs, an empty inventory with base stock level S > 0 PEMUs, and no receivables. The reason for requiring a positive G is that borrowing is often subject to covenants that restrict the percentage of debt relative to equity, thereby requiring the borrower to risk some of its own funds. The term available funds refers to the sum of cash on hand in the treasury and residual credit. Furthermore, the quantity A = G + H is the firm’s available funds maximum.
