By (41A.4) and dominated convergence, we have

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}E_x\left[\int_0^{\tilde{\tau}^t}e^{-nu}(L-r)\Phi_n(X_u)\,du\right]=E_x\left[\int_0^{\tilde{\tau}^t}e^{-nu}(L-r)\Phi(X_u)\,du\right]. \tag{41A.5}$$

By (41A.2) and (41A.5), letting $n\to\infty$ for both sides of (41A.1), we get (41.10).

# **Proof of Proposition 41.3.3:**

Fix $x>0$. Since $(L-r)\Phi(x)=0$ by (41.20), we get that (41.22) holds for $k=0$ and, hence, $\Phi\in C^2_{0,b}(\mathbb{R}_{++})$. Since $\Phi$ is continuous at $x$ (Theorem 41.2.1), we obtain, by Theorem 41B.3, that $T^+_{B_+}\Phi$ is differentiable at $x$ and

$$\frac{d}{dx}T^+_{B_+}\Phi(x)=B_+T^+_{B_+}\Phi(x)+\Phi(x)I.$$

Similarly, we have $\frac{d}{dx}T^-_{B_-}\Phi(x)=-B_-T^-_{B_-}\Phi(x)-\Phi(x)I$. A similar argument holds for general $k$.
