As shown by Kreps and Porteus (1978), agent prefers early resolution of uncertainty over temporal lotteries if $V(.,.)$ is convex in its second argument. Alternatively, if $V(.,.)$ is concave in its second argument, agent will prefer late resolution of uncertainty over temporal lotteries.

The aggregator function is further parameterized by $$V_t = \left[(1 - \beta)C_t^{1-1/\sigma} + \beta(E\tilde{V}_{t+1}^{1-\gamma})^{\frac{1-1/\sigma}{1-\gamma}}\right]^{\frac{1}{1-1/\sigma}} = \left\{(1 - \beta)C_t^{(1-\gamma)/\theta} + \beta(E_t\tilde{V}_{t+1}^{1-\gamma})^{1/\theta}\right\}^{\theta/(1-\gamma)}.\tag{12.2}$$ The parameter $\beta$ is the agent's subjective time discount factor and $\gamma$ can be interpreted as the Arrow–Pratt coefficient of relative risk aversion. It can also be shown that $\sigma$ measures the elasticity of intertemporal substitution. In this parameterization, the agent's preference over the timing of the resolution of uncertainty is determined by the parameters $\gamma$ and $\sigma$. For instance, if the agent's coefficient of relative risk aversion ($\gamma$) is greater than the reciprocal of his elasticity of intertemporal substitution ($1/\sigma$), then he prefers early resolution of uncertainty. Conversely, if the reciprocal of the agent's elasticity of intertemporal substitution is larger than his coefficient of relative risk aversion, he prefers late resolution of uncertainty. If $\gamma$ is equal to $1/\sigma$, the agent's utility becomes an isoelastic, von Neumann–Morgenstern utility and he is indifferent to the timing of the resolution of uncertainty.

Furthermore, $\theta$ is defined as $\theta = (1 - \gamma)/[1 - (1/\sigma)]$ according to Giovanini and Weil (1989). There are three special cases that are worth mentioning. First, $\theta \to 0$ when $\gamma \to 1$. Second, $\theta \to \infty$ when $\sigma \to 1$. Third, $\theta = 1$ when $\gamma = 1/\sigma$. In this case, equation (12.2) becomes the von Neumann–Morgenstern expected utility $$V_t = \left[(1 - \beta)E_t\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\beta^{j}C_{t+j}^{1-\gamma}\right]^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma}}.\tag{12.3}$$
