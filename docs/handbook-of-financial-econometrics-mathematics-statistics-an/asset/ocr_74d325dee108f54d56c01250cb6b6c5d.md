(iii) Max(and Min) $\sum_{j=1}^{5} s_{ij}x_j$, $i = 1,\dots,5$ (54.6)
subject to: (54.4)

(iv) Max(and Min) $\sum_{j=1}^{5} c_{ij}x_{ij}$, $i = 1,\dots,5$ (54.7)
subject to: (54.4).

Group (i) of the above LP problems has two types of optimization: one is maximization and the other is minimization. Groups (ii), (iii) and (iv) have six problems, respectively. All of them use the same constraints (4). The total number of problems is eight. We define the objective value of the maximization problems by $\mathbf{U_i}$ as the upper bound of DMs' acceptability for the objective payoff; and the objective value of the minimization problems by $\mathbf{L_i}$ as the lower bound of DMs' acceptability for the objective payoff, $i = 1,\dots,12.$

# **Step 2:** Let $f(x)_i$, $i = 1,\dots,12$, be the objective functions in Step 1. Then, we solve the following linear program:

Max $\beta$ (54.8)
subject to:
$\beta \le (f(x)_i - L_i)/(U_i - L_i)$, $i = 1,\dots,12$, (54.9)
$\beta \ge 0$,
$\sum_{j=1}^{5} b_{ij}x_j \le (c_{1i},\dots,c_{5i})(\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_5)^t$
and $x_j \ge 0$, for $j = 1,\dots,5.$

The resulting values of $x_j$ are the fuzzy optimal solution and the value of $\beta$ is the satisficing level of the solution for the DMs.

The above fuzzy solution method has systematically reduced the complexity of the MCMDM capital budgeting model (54.1) and (54.2) because it transforms the MC$^2$ LP problem into an LP problem. The above two solution steps can be easily implemented by employing available commercial computer software.
