In the first category, for example, Barberis and Huang (2008) employ the decision weights, which overestimate probabilities of extreme events, to explain the skewness effect on the cross sectional returns, i.e., more positively skewed stocks show lower average returns (see Boyer et al., 2009; or Bali et al., 2011). As for the trading behavior of investors, the most important and famous findings extracted from prospect theory must be the disposition effect. The seminal work to examine empirically this effect is conducted by Odean (1998). After that, Barberis and Xiong (2009) and Da Costa et al. (2013) also study the disposition effect based on prospect theory. For the last category, possible issues include how to explain the equity premium puzzle, the predictability and excess volatility of aggregate returns, or ambiguity aversion about distributions.

This chapter focuses on resolving the equity premium puzzle³ by incorporating the value function in prospect theory and the concept of the habit formation into the classical consumption-based asset pricing model. As pointed out in Campbell (2000), there are some major unsettled issues when prospect theory is incorporated into traditional asset pricing models. One issue is the determination and updating of the reference point, and the other is the argument of the choice of the function form for the value function. In this chapter, these two issues are addressed while we incorporate prospect theory into the consumption-based asset pricing model, which is employed by Mehra and Prescott (1985) to demonstrate the existence of the equity premium puzzle.

One of the main innovations of the proposed model is to develop a consumption-based (rather than wealth-based) asset pricing model with prospect theory. In contrast, all previous related studies focus on "wealth-based" prospect theory, such as Benartzi and Thaler (1985) and Barberis et al. (2001). However, we argue that, in a standard intertemporal model, investors derive their utilities from consumption rather than wealth.
