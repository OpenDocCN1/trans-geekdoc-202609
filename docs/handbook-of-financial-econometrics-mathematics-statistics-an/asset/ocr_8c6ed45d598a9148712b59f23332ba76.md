Gaussian copula function is

$$C(u_1, u_2; \varrho) = \Phi(\phi^{-1}(u_1), \phi^{-1}(u_2)),$$
$$= \int_0^{\Phi^{-1}(u_1)} \int_0^{\Phi^{-1}(u_2)} (2\pi)^{-1}(1 - \varrho^2)^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)} \times \exp\left(-\frac{[x_1^2 - 2\varrho x_1 x_2 + x_2^2]}{2(1 - \varrho^2)}\right) dx_1 dx_2,$$

where $\varrho$ is the correlation coefficient between the two variables. Differentiating the function, the bivariate Gaussian copula density function is

$$c(u_1, u_2; \varrho) = (1 - \varrho^2)^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)} \exp\left(-\frac{\varrho^2 \xi_1^2 - 2\varrho \xi_1 \xi_2 + \varrho^2 \xi_2^2}{2(1 - \varrho^2)}\right).$$

Similarly, the Student's $t$ copula distribution cannot be written in a simple closed form. When $n = 2$, the bivariate Student's $t$ copula function is

$$C(u_1, u_2; \varrho) = t_v(t_v^{-1}(u_1), t_v^{-1}(u_2)),$$
$$= \int_0^{\Phi^{-1}(u_1)} \int_0^{\Phi^{-1}(u_2)} (2\pi)^{-1}(1 - \varrho^2)^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)} \times \left[1 + v^{-1}(x_1^2 - 2\varrho x_1 x_2 + x_2^2)\right]^{-\frac{v+2}{2}} dx_1 dx_2.$$

Differentiating the function, the bivariate Student's $t$ copula density function is

$$c(u_1, u_2; \varrho) = K (1 - \varrho^2)^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)} \left[1 + v^{-1} (1 - \varrho^2)^{-1} (\xi_1^2 - 2\varrho \xi_1 \xi_2 + \xi_2^2)\right]^{-\frac{v+2}{2}} \cdot \left[(1 + v^{-1} \xi_1^2)(1 + v^{-1} \xi_2^2)\right]^{\frac{v+1}{2}},$$

where $\xi_1 = t_v^{-1}(u_1)$ and $\xi_2 = t_v^{-1}(u_2)$ are realizations of the Student's $t$ variables, and $K$ is a constant, $K = \Gamma\left(\frac{v}{2}\right) \cdot \Gamma\left(\frac{v+1}{2}\right)^{-2} \cdot \Gamma\left(\frac{v+2}{2}\right).$
