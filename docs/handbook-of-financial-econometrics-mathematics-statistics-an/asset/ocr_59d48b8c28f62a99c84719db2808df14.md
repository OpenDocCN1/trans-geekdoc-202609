H0 : αt = (μt^s − μt^e) = 0 against the two-sided alternative H1 : αt = (μt^s − μt^e) ≠ 0, employs the test statistic

Hotelling’s T² = T αt′ Σt⁻¹ αt = T (μt^s − μt^e)′ Σt⁻¹ (μt^s − μt^e), (47.7)

where Σt⁻¹ is the inverse of the covariance matrix of raw returns. When the null hypothesis is true, the quantity F = (T−n)/(n(T−1)) T² has the F distribution with degrees of freedom n and T − n, where n is the number of portfolios and T is the number of time-series observations. The decision rule is

Accept H0 : μt^s = μt^e if T² ≤ n(T − 1)/(T − n) Fα;n,T−n and reject H0 otherwise.

See Morrison (1967, p. 120).

Interestingly, the Hotelling’s T² test is more closely related to MV portfolio theory than the GRS test. It is stated in terms of the alphas and the covariance matrix of raw returns, α′ Σ⁻¹ α. Then, using the efficient set mathematics and some algebra it can be shown that the sample means tangency portfolio is x_Tan^s = (Σ⁻¹ α + θ2 x_m)/(ι′ Σ⁻¹ α + θ2). And, the difference in the squared Sharpe ratios of the sample means and equilibrium means frontiers is Sh_s² − Sh_e² = α′ Σ⁻¹ α + 2θ2 α′ x_m. In the GRS test and the (Σ, x_m)-compatible means specification, where θ_Int = r̄_ft and θ_Slt = μ^s_mt − r̄_ft, the alpha of the market portfolio is equal to zero, which means that the market portfolio’s sample mean is equal to its equilibrium mean. That is, α_mt = α_t′ x_mt = 0, and μ^s_mt = μ^e_mt.⁷ In this (Σ, x_m)-compatible means case, the difference between the squared Sharpe ratios of the sample-means and equilibrium-means minimum-variance frontiers, both generated from the same n-assets, is

Sh_s² − Sh_e² = α′ Σ⁻¹ α.
