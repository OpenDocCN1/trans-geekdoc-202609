# 124.1.2 Models

# 124.1.2.1 Simplest setting for DID analysis

We can use the below general formulas to demonstrate the simplest form of the DID model, which includes only two groups and two periods (i.e., a 2 × 2 setting):

$Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 dT + \beta_2 dB + \delta_1 dT^* dB + e,$
(124.1)

where $Y$ is the outcome of interest. $dT$ is the dummy variable for the post-treatment period (e.g., one for post-treatment period, zero otherwise), which captures the aggregate changes in $Y$ without the treatment (or policy change). The dummy variable $dB$ captures the possible differences between the treatment and control groups before the treatment is applied (or during the implementation of the policy). The coefficient of interest on $dT^* dB$ (the interaction term), $\delta_1$, captures the effect of the treatment (or the implementation of the policy) on the treated group in the post-treatment period. The estimate for DID can be rewritten as below:

$\hat{\delta}_1 = (\bar{Y}_{T,1} - \bar{Y}_{T,0}) - (\bar{Y}_{C,1} - \bar{Y}_{C,0})$

The term $(\bar{Y}_{T,1} - \bar{Y}_{T,0})$ represents the estimated (average) change in the outcome for the treatment group from pre-treatment to post-treatment period. The term $(\bar{Y}_{C,1} - \bar{Y}_{C,0})$ represents the estimated (average) change in the outcome for the control group from the pre-treatment to post-treatment period. Therefore, the DID estimator, $\hat{\delta}_1$, captures the differences in the post-treatment outcomes between the treated and control groups. Thus, $\hat{\delta}_1$ indicates the estimated average effect of the treatment on the treatment group.

To test whether $\hat{\delta}_1$ is statistically different from zero, we can use a regression analysis to estimate equation (1) above. Then, we find the standard error of $\hat{\delta}_1$ to compute its $t$-statistics. The intercept, $\beta_0$, is the average outcome before the treatment for the control group.
