## 36.4 Conclusion

The assessment of the degree of the interdependence between stock market returns is useful for global investors who wish to diversify their investment internationally. This chapter investigates the co-movement between and within European, American and Asian stock markets by means of the wavelet technique. The wavelet analysis has the advantage of examining the relationship in the time domain and frequency domain simultaneously and in a unified framework. Our data are daily stock returns and cover the period from December 31, 1993 to September 30, 2015 with a total of 5674 observations. We found that: (i) The major world stock markets tend to move together in all investment horizons and in crisis and tranquil periods, and the return linkages increase with time scales, which indicates that the international diversification benefits have largely disappeared across intra and inter economic trading blocs. (ii) The Latin American stock markets are the least integrated regional stock markets and the EU stock markets are the most integrated ones. (iii) A fundamental relationship between stock market returns characterized by a high degree of co-movement at low frequencies across the whole sample, which facilitates the spread of financial crises from one nation to another. (iv) A lead–lag relationship within and between regional stock markets, which is a key determinant for international investors to manage their investment portfolio in the event of sudden changes, and France’s stock market has the potential to lead or lag the other major world stock markets at all wavelet scales. (v) The multiple cross-correlation gets weaker with successive lags and tends to zero for short-term investment horizons while it remains high for long-term investment horizons.
