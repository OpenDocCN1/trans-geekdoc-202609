To investigate the capability of the parameter specification of the bubble in explaining US stock price volatility, Wu (1997) computes the in-sample root mean square error (RMSE) and mean absolute error (MAE) of the fitted log stock price of his bubble model and two alternative models. The first alternative is the simple present-value model, while the second is the present-value model with bubbles exclusively driven by dividends (intrinsic bubbles) as specified in Froot and Obstfeld (1991). Figures 64.2-64.4 plot the percentage prediction errors of the Wu’s (1997) bubble model, the simple present-value model and the model with intrinsic bubbles, respectively. Two alternative models do a poor job in fitting the data while Wu’s (1997) model does a credible job in characterizing the data. This is confirmed by Table 64.1 (Wu (1997), Table V), which shows both RMSE and MAE of Wu’s (1997) model are much smaller than those of the alternative models.
