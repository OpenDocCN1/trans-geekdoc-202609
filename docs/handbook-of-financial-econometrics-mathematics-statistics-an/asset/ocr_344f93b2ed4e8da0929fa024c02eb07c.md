This implies that

$$\tilde d(t)=\{\big[\tilde r(t)-h\big]A(0)e^{ht}+\delta\dot m(t)p(t)\}/m(t),\qquad(96.5)$$

and the mean and variance of the dividends per share can be expressed as follows:

$$E[\tilde d(t)]=\{\big[\mu-h\big]A(0)e^{ht}+\delta\dot m(t)p(t)\}/m(t),$$

$$\operatorname{Var}[\tilde d(t)]=A(0)^2\sigma(t)^2e^{2th}/m^2(t).\qquad(96.6)$$

Also, let us postulate an exponential utility function of the following form³:

$$U[\tilde d(t)]=-e^{\alpha\tilde d(t)},\quad\text{where }\alpha>0.\qquad(96.7)$$

Following the moment generating function, we have

$$E\big(-e^{-\alpha\tilde d(t)}\big)=-e^{-\alpha E[\tilde d(t)]+\frac{\alpha^2}{2}\operatorname{Var}[\tilde d(t)]},\qquad(96.8)$$

where $\bar d(t)$ is the certainty equivalent value of $\tilde d(t)$.$^4$

From equations (96.6) and (96.8), the certainty equivalent dividend stream can be written as

$$\bar d(t)=\frac{(\mu-h)A(0)e^{th}+\delta\dot m(t)p(t)}{m(t)}-\frac{\alpha' A(0)^2\sigma(t)^2e^{2th}}{m(t)^2},\qquad(96.9)$$

where $\alpha'=\alpha/2$. Therefore, taking advantage of exponential utility, we can obtain a risk adjusted dividend stream. Furthermore, $\bar d(t)$ will reduce to the certainty case, if we assume $\sigma(t)^2=0$.

$$\tilde r(t)=a+b\tilde I(t)+\tilde\varepsilon(t),\qquad(96.10)$$
