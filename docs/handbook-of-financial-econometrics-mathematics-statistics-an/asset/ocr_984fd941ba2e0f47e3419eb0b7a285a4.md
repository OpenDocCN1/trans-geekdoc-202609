# Figure 46.3: Equilibrium pmf surfaces for various μ and ν parameters.

Figure 46.3 consists of nine sub-figures, arranged in a 3×3 matrix, each displaying the joint pmf surface as function of S and A of a cash conversion model, with rate parameters taken from the corresponding matrix element in Table 46.1.

Figure 46.3 shows that each pmf surface has a single significant promontory. For each row of this figure (fixed μ and increasing ν), this promontory tends to move in the direction of higher inventory level, n, and more available funds, k. This is consistent with the observation that increasing the payment rate tends to decrease the receivables level. In a similar vein, for each column of the figure (fixed ν and increasing μ), the promontory tends to move in the direction of higher inventory level, n, and less available funds, k. This is also consistent with the observation that increasing the replenishment rate tends to increase the availability of inventory and reduce the availability of funds.

Figure 46.4 consists of nine sub-figures, arranged in a 3×3 matrix, each displaying the three equilibrium marginal pmfs of the corresponding joint pmf in Figure 46.3, given by (cf. Section 46.6.1):

+   - inventory size pmf: π_I(n) = Σ_{k=0}^{A-n} π(n, k, A - n - k) (dashed line);
+   - available funds pmf: π_C(k) = Σ_{n=0}^{min{S, A-k}} π(n, k, A - n - k) (solid line);
+   - receivables level pmf: π_R(j) = Σ_{(n,k)∈{(n',k'):n'+k'=A-j}} π(n, k, A - n - k) (dotted line).
