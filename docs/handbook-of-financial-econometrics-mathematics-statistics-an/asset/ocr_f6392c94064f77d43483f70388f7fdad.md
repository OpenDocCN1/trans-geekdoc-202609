# 24.5.2 Index futures

Constantinides et al. (2011) utilize the stochastic dominance bounds derived by Constantinides and Perrakis (2009), which consider incomplete and imperfect market involved in bid-ask spread and the transaction costs into the derivation of the bounds for American options, as the benchmarks for trading strategy in the market of American options on the S&P 500 index futures. Constantinides et al. (2011) test whether the options on S&P 500 index futures from 1983 to 2006 are profitable by only using the violations of option bounds as signals for trading. Their major findings are as follows: (1) there are significant violations of upper bounds in the bid price of American call options rather than American put options. (2) The trading strategy that selling (buying) options when they violate the upper (lower) bounds is profitable in the market of America options on S&P 500 index future. Without given probability distribution of S&P 500 index futures, the empirical results in Constantinides et al. (2011) are not affected by estimation error and model misspecification problem of the underlying asset’s return.
