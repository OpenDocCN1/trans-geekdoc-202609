As we noted above, from December 2008 through December 2015, the Fed had kept its target for the Fed Funds rate at essentially zero and so a very popular question among people working on research in this area surrounds the performance of dynamic term structure models at the ZLB. After providing an exhaustive review of the above models, we show that, despite its convenience as a choice of methods, consistent with much of the literature, affine term structure models perform poorly near the ZLB when principal components are used as the factors. We come to these conclusions by examining the JSZ normalization and the JLS model. Our results are based on the demonstration that cross-sectional fits and out-of-sample forecasts of the yield curve produced by these models frequently violate the ZLB over the period of our examination spanning January 2000 through December 2017. We also demonstrate that cross-sectional fits produced by JSZ around the ZLB are characterized by especially large volatility compared to other sub-periods. In our study, sub-periods are verified through the usage of a Chow (1960) (hereafter, Chow)-based F-test in determining structural breaks in the relation between the actual bond yield and lagged realizations of each model-fitted yield. Our findings contribute to the growing literature that documents the inability of affine term structure models for producing reasonable model forecasts for the yield curve around the ZLB.
