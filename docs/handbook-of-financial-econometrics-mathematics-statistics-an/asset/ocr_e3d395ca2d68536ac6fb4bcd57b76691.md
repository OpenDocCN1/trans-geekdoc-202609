# 24.5.4 Real options

Since real options are involved in capital budgeting decisions which are distinct characteristics from other options, risk preference of the decision maker is of important factor in pricing real option bounds. Mello and Pyo (2006) and Pyo (2008) employ minimax deviation approach incorporated with risk-averse utility functions to obtain real option bounds in incomplete market. Their numerical results show that their minimax bounds are more efficient and narrower than other two approaches, gain–loss ratio bounds by Bernardo and Ledoit (2000) and good-deal bounds by Cochrane and Saa-Requejo (2000).

# 24.6 Conclusion

In this paper, we provide a comprehensive literature review of option bounds. Option bounds can be generally obtained with fewer assumptions on the investor’s preferences and/or the underlying asset’s price processes. The studies on option bounds are categorized either by their mathematical techniques to derive the bounds or by their different approaches to tighten the bounds. The most important mathematical methods for deriving option bounds include searching for arbitrage conditions, stochastic dominance, linear programming, and semi-parametric and non-parametric methods. The bounds on option prices can be further tightened using a variety of approaches including: imposing restrictions on investor preferences or pricing kernels, the selection risk neutral measure, and consideration of market frictions. We also survey the papers that have applied option bounds to different empirical applications. Although the empirical studies concentrate on the options with underlying assets in equity market, their results conclude with a summary that the violation of no-arbitrage and stochastic dominance approach are a in empirical work. These option bounds have also been shown to be useful in many areas, such as providing qualitative properties of option prices and screening market data for mispricing.
