
## 116.2.2 Over-identified equation case

Consider the following over-identified simultaneous equations system:

y1 = α1 y2 + β1 ξ1 + ν1, (116.18a)
y1 = α2 y2 + β2 ξ2 + β3 ξ3 + ν2. (116.18b)

Suppose that the covariance matrix of true exogenous variables is as follows:

Cov(ξi, ξj) = [ σ11 σ12 σ13 ; σ21 σ22 σ23 ; σ31 σ32 σ33 ].

The reduced forms of model (116.18a) and (116.18b) are

y1 = π11 ξ1 + π21 ξ2 + π31 ξ3 + u1, (116.19a)
y2 = π12 ξ1 + π22 ξ2 + π32 ξ3 + u2. (116.19b)

Then the corresponding reduced form coefficients are as follows:

π11 = -β1 α2 / (α1 - α2), π21 = β2 α1 / (α1 - α2), π31 = β3 α1 / (α1 - α2), u1 = (α1 ν2 - α2 ν1) / (α1 - α2),
π12 = -β1 / (α1 - α2), π22 = β2 / (α1 - α2), π32 = β3 / (α1 - α2), u2 = (ν2 - ν1) / (α1 - α2). (116.20)

From the preceding reduced form coefficients, we obtain

α1 = π21 / π22 = π31 / π32. (116.21)

From this, we know that α1 is over-identified. By employing the relationship between the structural parameters and the reduced form parameters, we have

| π21 π22 |
| π31 π32 | = 0. (116.22)

Assume that there is a random error of measurement in ξ1, x1 = ξ1 + η1, but not in ξ2 and ξ3. Here, η1 is a random variable that has zero mean and constant variance, σ²_η.

### 116.2.2.2 Two-stage least square method

The two-stage least square estimation uses all the exogenous variables in this system as instruments to obtain the predictions of endogenous variables. For example, y2 in the first stage can be estimated by

$$y_2 = \alpha_{12}\xi_1 + \alpha_{22}\xi_2 + \alpha_{32}\xi_3 + v_2.$$ (116.27a)

Since $\xi_1$ is unobserved, we should replace it by $x_1$ to estimate $\alpha_{12}$, $\alpha_{22}$, and $\alpha_{32}$. The observed relationship corresponding to (116.27a) can be written as

$$y_2 = \alpha'_{12}x_1 + \alpha'_{22}\xi_2 + \alpha'_{32}\xi_3 + v'_2,$$ (116.27b)

where $x_1 = \xi_1 + \eta_1$. $\eta_1$ has zero mean and constant variance, $\sigma_\eta^2$. From Eqs. (116.27a) and (116.27b), we can obtain two equivalent expressions for Cov($y_2x_1$). From (116.27a), we have

$$\text{Cov}(y_2x_1) = \text{Cov}[(\alpha_{12}\xi_1 + \alpha_{22}\xi_2 + \alpha_{32}\xi_3 + v_2)(\xi_1 + \eta_1)] = \sum_i \alpha_{i2}\sigma_{i1}.$$ (116.28a)

From (116.27b), we have

$$\text{Cov}(y_2x_1) = \text{Cov}[(\alpha'_{12}(\xi_1 + \eta_1) + \alpha'_{22}\xi_2 + \alpha'_{32}\xi_3 + v'_2)(\xi_1 + \eta_1)]$$

$$= \sum_i \alpha'_{i2}\sigma_{i1} + \sigma_\eta^2.$$ (116.28b)

Similarly, based on two equivalent expressions for Cov($y_2\xi_j$), $j = 2,3$, we can also obtain the following condition:

$$\sum_i \alpha_{i2}\sigma_{ij} = \sum_i \alpha'_{i2}\sigma_{ij}\quad (i = 1,2,3; j = 2,3).$$ (116.29)

From (116.28a), (116.28b) and (116.29), we can solve $\alpha'_{12}$, $\alpha'_{22}$, and $\alpha'_{32}$ in terms of $\alpha_{12}$, $\alpha_{22}$, and $\alpha_{32}$ as follows:

$$\alpha'_{12} = \frac{\alpha_{12}\kappa(1 + c_{1\cdot2}c_{1\cdot3}c_{2\cdot3} + c_{1\cdot3}c_{3\cdot2}c_{2\cdot1} - c_{2\cdot3}c_{3\cdot2} - c_{1\cdot2}c_{2\cdot1} - c_{3\cdot1}c_{1\cdot3})}{1 + \kappa(c_{1\cdot2}c_{1\cdot3}c_{2\cdot3} + c_{1\cdot3}c_{3\cdot2}c_{2\cdot1} - c_{1\cdot2}c_{2\cdot1} - c_{3\cdot1}c_{1\cdot3}) - c_{2\cdot3}c_{3\cdot2}},$$ (116.30a)

$$\alpha'_{22} = \alpha_{22} + \frac{\alpha_{12}(1 - \kappa)(c_{1\cdot2} - c_{1\cdot3}c_{3\cdot2})}{1 + \kappa(c_{1\cdot2}c_{1\cdot3}c_{2\cdot3} + c_{1\cdot3}c_{3\cdot2}c_{2\cdot1} - c_{1\cdot2}c_{2\cdot1} - c_{3\cdot1}c_{1\cdot3}) - c_{2\cdot3}c_{3\cdot2}},$$ (116.30b)

$$\alpha'_{32} = \alpha_{32} + \frac{\alpha_{12}(1 - \kappa)(c_{1\cdot3} - c_{1\cdot2}c_{2\cdot3})}{1 + \kappa(c_{1\cdot2}c_{1\cdot3}c_{2\cdot3} + c_{1\cdot3}c_{3\cdot2}c_{2\cdot1} - c_{1\cdot2}c_{2\cdot1} - c_{3\cdot1}c_{1\cdot3}) - c_{2\cdot3}c_{3\cdot2}}.$$ (116.30c)

Here, κ = σ₁²/(σ_η² + σ₁²), c_{i,j} = σ_{ij}/σ_j². From (116.30a)–(116.30c), we can conclude that the biases in first stage carry over to second stage and the estimates of structural parameters are affected by the measurement error in ξ₁.

## 116.3 Measurement Error Problem in the Dividend and Investment Decisions

This section aims to study the impact of measurement error on the parameter estimates of variables measured without error. Here, single equation as well as simultaneous equations model are investigated. In this section, the direction of the bias due to measurement error is clearly investigated.

### 116.3.1 Asymptotic bias problem

### 116.3.1.1 Asymptotic bias of OLS estimator in the single equation model

Suppose that dividends are the result of a partial adjustment process towards a target ratio. The target pay-out level is assumed to be a fixed proportion of expected earnings as follows:

DIV*_{it} = γ P*_{it}  (116.31)

where DIV*_{it} and P*_{it} are the target dividend and expected earnings of firm i at year t. Here, we assume that changes in dividends are determined by the difference between last year’s dividends and this year’s target dividend as ΔDIV_{it} = α₀ + α(DIV*_{it} − DIV_{it−1}) + ε_{it} = α₀ + α(γ P*_{it} − DIV_{it−1}) + ε_{it},  (116.32)

where ΔDIV_{it} is the changes in dividends of firm i at year t. DIV_{it−1} is the dividend of firm i at year t − 1. We can rewrite equation (116.32) as follows: ΔDIV_{it} = β₀ + β₁ P*_{it} + β₂ DIV_{it−1} + ε_{it}.  (116.33)

Here, α = −β₂ denotes the speed of adjustment of dividend decision. Since the expected earnings is unobservable, using reported accounting earnings as a proxy may result in a measurement error problem (Hsu, Wang and Wu, 1998). We suppose that there is an error of measurement in expected earnings as follows: P_{it} = P*_{it} + ν_{it}.  (116.34)

Assume that the measurement error ν_it is independent of P*_it, DIV_it−1, and ε_it. Carroll et al. (2006), and Chen, Lee and Lee (2015) show the asymptotic biases of traditional OLS estimated coefficients are as follows:

$$\operatorname{plim}\{\hat\beta_1 - \beta_1\} = \left(\frac{-\sigma_\nu^2}{\sigma_{P_t^*|\mathrm{DIV}_{t-1}}^2 + \sigma_\nu^2}\right)\beta_1, \qquad (116.35)$$

$$\operatorname{plim}\{\hat\beta_2 - \beta_2\} = \left(\frac{b_{P_t^*|\mathrm{DIV}_{t-1}}\sigma_\nu^2}{\sigma_{P_t^*|\mathrm{DIV}_{t-1}}^2 + \sigma_\nu^2}\right)\beta_1, \qquad (116.36)$$

where σ²_ν is the variance of measurement error, σ²_{P*_t|DIV_{t-1}} is the variance of P*_t conditional on DIV_{t-1}, and b_{P*|DIV_{t-1}} is the coefficient of DIV_{t-1} in the auxiliary regression of P*_t on DIV_{t-1}. Equation (116.35) shows that the OLS estimate of β̂₁ is downward biased. In addition, equation (116.36) shows that the estimated coefficient of DIV_{t-1} measured without error is also biased, the direction of bias of estimated coefficient of DIV_{t-1} will rely on the dependence of P*_t and DIV_{t-1}. We can conclude that the estimated coefficient of variable measured without error is also biased in general, unless the variable is independent of the variable measured with error.
