Schweikert (2018) applies a quantile co-integration model to gold and silver prices and to prices of the corresponding futures contracts. The results find an asymmetric dependence between silver prices and gold prices that a stronger response of silver prices to gold price changes is observed when silver prices are at a relatively high level and vice versa.

In other words, the value of quantile co-integrating coefficients reflects shocks to the prices received in each period. Under these conditions, the possible prices of gold spot and futures are nonlinearly co-integrated with a time-varying and quantile-dependent co-integration, while those of the conventional co-integration analysis may not be detected. Motivated by these works of literature, this study attempts to answer the short- and long-term dependence on the gold spot and futures by employing a novel quantile co-integrating approach developed by Xiao (2009).

Gold has had its dual role both as a currency and a commodity, although after 1978, the gold standard was no longer obtained, and the monetary role of gold slowly faded. Gold is still regarded as an important reserve currency as the monetary properties of gold are vital in affecting its price in case of a credit bubble or other volatile situation. The major gold markets, including London, Zurich, Hong Kong, Tokyo, and New York, constitute a 24/7 cycle of buying and selling, making gold a powerful hedge tool to quickly respond to broader market conditions.

This study contributes to the literature by modeling the state- and time-dependence of the short- and long-run relationships between gold spot and futures prices, and attempts to explain if the gold spot and futures move together in the long-run. The contributions of this study are in several ways. First, we use the quantile co-integration model to examine the relationships of gold spot price and various maturities of gold futures, which allow the co-integrating coefficients to vary over the conditional distribution of gold spot prices. This approach can avoid the coefficient biases once the variables are non-normally distributed or have extreme values. We find that the co-integration of gold spot and futures contracts within six months increases with the quantiles, indicating that the co-integration at high quantile is greater than those observed at low quantiles. As such, the traditional Johansen co-integration would underestimate the co-integration for high quantile memberships. Second, we also find that at the same quantile, the co-integration of short-run gold futures and the gold spot is greater than those observed in the long-run gold futures.
