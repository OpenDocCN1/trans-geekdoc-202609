## 56.2.2 External forces (work environment)

### 56.2.2.1 Organizational support

The following factor (organizational support) means that the organization provides instruction on the application, formal training, competency development activities and technical support lines for problem-solving ability related to the introduction of new technologies. For example, Sengupta et al. (2015) argue that organizational resources improve employees’ skill set, help to reduce their job stress and contribute to their personal development, while the lack of organizational support negatively impacted effective computer usage or utilization (Lee et al., 2013).

Furthermore, formal training enhances job performance of employees by improving their skill set required to handle customer demands and contributes to their productivity. Also, training programs enhance employees’ behavioral skills and reduce their turnover intentions (Sengupta et al., 2015).

Besides, Walczuch et al. (2007) confirm that technological support acts as a stress reliever and allows employees to channel their efforts to the most rewarding aspects of their tasks and produce premium quality service to their customers.

Finally, according to Lee et al. (2013), top management support, as one form of organizational support, plays a significant role in stimulating the use of the computer technology programs and training by providing required resources and computer systems upgrades. Organizational and top management support enhances employees’ trust of the organization, which allows them to take risks to integrate computer technology into practice.

## 56.2.3 Control variables: Individual differences and differences in organizations and industries

Because the possible effects of individual differences on behaviors should be examined, so we will consider the effects of gender, education, job experience, job title as control variables for their potential influence on job performance of the employee. In addition, we will consider the effects of organizational differences such as age, size, and the type of financial institutions related to the organization.

With reference to gender, it has been stated that men demonstrate higher levels of computer skills and find information technologies easier to use than do women (Morris et al., 2005). However, Elie-Dit-Cosaque et al. (2011) have found that women are more computer-anxious and feel less comfortable with technologies at work than men. Besides, Riquelme and Rios (2010) revealed that the social influence appears to be more appropriate among females than males. Ease of use has a significant influence on female respondents than male, whereas relative advantage has a stronger effect on perception of usefulness on men.

If we talk about education, then a higher level of education was significantly related to lower computer anxiety scores (Elie-Dit-Cosaque et al., 2011). Additionally, Sengupta et al. (2015) showed that highly educated employees report unmet expectations more frequently than those with lower education levels. With respect to age, younger people feel more comfortable with computers than elder persons (Palfrey and Gasser, 2008). With respect to experience with IT, Elie-Dit-Cosaque et al. (2011) revealed no significant influence of this characteristic on computer anxiety.

With reference to work status, researchers have found that supervisors tend to experience more computer anxiety than executives (Elie-Dit-Cosaque et al., 2011). As for firm size, in general, small firms are more labor intensive as compared to larger firms. Moreover, motivation-enhancing practices are significant to maintain employee effort in the labor-intensive context of small firms. Given that each individual employee has a stronger influence on the eventual performance of a small firm in contrast to larger firms, motivation-enhancing practices should contribute to the higher payoff in small firms (Rauch and Hatak, 2016).

As for firm age, Messersmith and Wales (2013) also indicated that investment in training and development activities by young firms allows them to seek more productive levels of progress. Additionally, motivation-enhancing practices help to standardize processes and increase the firm’s reliability, therefore the increasing young firm’s operational efficiency. Older firms, in contrast, face fewer challenges associated with social legitimacy, resource stability, and operational efficiency, and, thus, benefit less strongly from HR-enhancing practices than young firms in terms of firm performance (Rauch and Hatak, 2016).

## 56.2.4 Company performance and labor productivity

We begin a discussion on measures of company performance. For example, Chari et al. (2017), examining large UK firms, used profitability, customer satisfaction and market effectiveness as measures of a company’s performance. Profitability implies return on investments, return on sales and reaching financial goals. Indicators of customer satisfaction are satisfaction, reputation among end users and retaining valued customers. Market effectiveness represents market share growth relative to competitors, growth in sales revenue, acquiring new customers and increasing sales to existing customers.

Compared with Chari et al. (2017), Hu et al. (2017), studying Taiwanese firms in the international trade industry, divided firm performance into two aspects and besides financial performance also included service performance. Financial performance contains return on investment, revenue growth, profit margins and market share as measures. Meanwhile, service performance includes the following indicators: quality and flexibility of process, dependability of delivery process and customer satisfaction.

Finally, in contrast with the above, Rauch and Hatak (2016) coded financial performance along three dimensions: accounting-based measures, growth and perceived performance. Accounting-based measure comprises indicators such as profitability, return on sales, return on assets, and failure. Measures of growth are mainly sales and employment growth. Besides, the authors coded perceived performance when research evaluated performance subjectively by asking the respondents for their performance assessment, for instance, by comparing their own company’s performance level with the performance of their major competitors. Operational performance includes measures of innovation, entrepreneurial orientation, differentiation, and quality of products/services.

## Acceptance of New Technologies by Employees in Financial Industry

Within the scope of our study, we will consider the following dimensions of firm performance: customer satisfaction, managing risk and forecasting losses, company–customer relationships, revenue growth, market share, reduced costs, profit margins and market value.

Next, we focus on dimensions of labor productivity, which is major indicator of business progress (Kaufman, 2015). According to De Clercq et al. (2018), job performance can be measured through the following criteria: adequate fulfillment of assigned duties, performance of responsibilities specified in his/her job description, tasks that are expected of him/her, compliance with formal performance requirements of the job, neglect of aspects of the job he/she has to perform and failure to fulfill essential duties.

Moreover, Zaheer et al. (2010) used commitment and esprit de corps as measures of employee performance. Commitment implies the degree to which employees sacrifice for, identify with and involve themselves in reaching organizational goals. Employee attachment and loyalty are additional dimensions of employee commitment. Esprit de corps refers to teamwork and cohesion in the context of an organization. It means employee attachment to seek common objectives. Effective organizational leadership and behavior lead to pride, commitment and dedication of employees, which are essential determinants of esprit de corps. Finally, Sengupta et al. (2015) coded job performance using three operational definitions: an employee as a top performer, employee’s performance is in the top 10 percent and an employee has been rated consistently as a star performer.

In accordance with the purpose of our study, we will take into account three measures of job performance such as fulfillment of assigned duties, performance of current responsibilities better and performing his/her job faster.

Taking all these into account, we conceptualize a model that identifies the relevant forces that drive employee’s acceptance of new technologies (Figure 56.1). The key hypotheses of our study could be framed as follows:

- (1) There will be a positive relationship between labor productivity and company performance (Riley et al., 2017).
- (2) High personal motivation with regard to use of disruptive technology in general leads to higher labor productivity (Ali et al., 2012).
- (3) Organizational support enhances company performance (Sengupta et al., 2015).
- (4) Greater level of digital skills can lead to higher labor productivity (Rubel et al., 2017).
