This table provides the summary of the DID estimation for a time comparison before and after 2012 between firms that use Facebook and firms that have bank relationships and those without. The model is presented using the following equation:

Tobin's Q = β₀ + β₁ Facebook + β₂ After × Facebook + β₃ Bank Relationship × Facebook
+ β₄ After × Bank Relationship × Facebook + β₅ After 2012
+ β₆ Bank Relationship + β₇ Firms Characteristics + εₜ. (Equation (66.3))

concerns that endogeneity might drive our results. The coefficient estimates for Facebook and After_Facebook are 0.25 and −0.184, which are significant. These results indicate that the net effect of use of FB on average increases Tobin's Q by 6.16% (= (0.25 − 0.184)/1.071) in the post-IPO period.

In this specification, β₁ captures the effect of Facebook on Tobin's Q for firm value before IPO, while β₁ + β₂ represents the net effect of using Facebook for firms in the post-IPO period; here, β₂ captures the marginal effects of the use of Facebook in the post-IPO period. As the Facebook IPO imposed an exogenous shock on the social media usage of firms, β₁ + β₂ + β₃ + β₄ captures all the effects of social media on firm value and the direct effects of post-IPO simultaneously. Hence, one would obtain the clean effect of social media (β₁ + β₃ + β₄) by subtracting the direct effects of post-IPO (β₂) from the combined effect (β₁ + β₂ + β₃ + β₄).
