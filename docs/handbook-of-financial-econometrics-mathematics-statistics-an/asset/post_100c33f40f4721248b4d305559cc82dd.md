## 22.6 Conclusions
This paper extends and reinforces the growing literature on auditor independence and nonaudit services. Economic rents arise because of auditor learning over time or the switching costs imposed on the client (DeAngelo, 1981).

Auditors must therefore weigh the costs of reporting a financial misstatement, which are associated with potential lawsuits and loss of reputation, with the opportunity costs of losing the economic rents that may be accrue from such work. The purpose of our study is to measure the economic bond to investigate whether there is a trade-off point between economic dependence and reputation protection when auditors make their own decisions with regard to compromising their independence. We use the panel threshold regression model (Hansen, 1999) to examine this issue and obtain new evidence to explain the association more accurately. In addition, it is important to examine the association between fees and auditor independence after the Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX) was implemented in 2002 (Li, 2009). We thus examine the impact of SOX on auditor independence by comparing the effects of services provided by auditors during the pre-SOX (2000-2002) and post-SOX (2003-2007) periods. We examine the relation between discretionary accruals which are calculated by the modified-Jones model as a proxy variable for earnings management and auditor fees. The results of this paper suggest that nonaudit services impaired audit quality before SOX, however, nonaudit services are associated with earnings management only when FEERATIO is higher than the threshold value in the post-SOX period. Our results support the notion that economic dependence would cause auditors to jeopardize their independence and report leniently to retain valuable clients (Reynolds and Francis, 2001).

According to the results of our descriptive statistics (Table 22.3), the audit fees increased dramatically, but there was no significant difference in the mean value of the absolute value of discretionary accruals between the two periods (Table 22.2). We expect that auditor independence will be lead to improvements due to the scrutiny of the accounting profession after the implementation of SOX. Unfortunately, since auditor firms are profit-making enterprises, they do not necessarily pursue the integrity at the expense of greater earnings. Although audit fees have become the main source of revenue for auditors in the post-SOX period, nonaudit services still can impair audit quality.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first paper using the panel threshold regression model to identify the relationship between economic dependence and reputation protection. We view this issue in a different light from previous papers, and use more sample years (2000-2007) to demonstrate that there is a structural change between discretionary accruals and nonaudit fees.
