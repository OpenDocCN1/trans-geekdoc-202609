# 72.4.1 Size of test statistics

This section concentrates on the empirical size of the test. For the values of (μ₂, β̄₂, σ̄₂), we consider two cases:

Case 1 : (μ₂, β̄₂, σ̄₂) = (0.0003, 0.4, 0.0099),  and
Case 2 : (μ₂, β̄₂, σ̄₂) = (0.0003, 0.8, 0.0088).

For Case 1, {sₜ} is an independent copy of {rₜ}, and Cₐ¹ᵀ = Cₐ²ᵀ. For Case 2, equation (72.6) and the fact that μ₁ = μ₂, σ₁² = E(rₜ − μ₁)² = σ̄₁² exp(β̄₁²/2) = σ₂² imply Cₐ²ᵀ = Cₐ¹ᵀ + O(1/√T). Hence, the null hypothesis Cₐ¹ᵀ = Cₐ²ᵀ holds asymptotically, as T → ∞.

The empirical frequencies of rejecting the null hypothesis based on 1,000 replications are summarized in Tables 72.1 and 72.2 for Cases 1 and 2, respectively. In these two tables, the rejection rates range from 0.037 to 0.066, indicating the empirical sizes of the test statistic close to their nominal values.
