From equations (96A.1) and (96A.6), we have

$g(t) = \delta \frac{\dot{m}(t)}{m(t)} - k$

$\exp\left(\int g(t)dt\right) = \exp\left(\int \left(\delta \frac{\dot{m}(t)}{m(t)} - k\right) dt\right)$

## Appendix 96B: Derivation of Equation (96.21)

This appendix presents a detailed derivation of equation (96.21). In equation (96.20), the initial value of the firm can be expressed as

$p(0) = \frac{1}{m(0)^{\delta}} \int_{0}^{T} \left\{ (a + b \bar{I} - h) A(0) e^{th} m(t)^{\delta - 1} - \alpha' A(0)^2 \rho(t)^2 \sigma(t)^2 e^{2th} m(t)^{\delta - 2} \right\} e^{-kt} dt. \quad (96.20)$

To maximize firm value, the number of shares outstanding at each point of time should be determined; therefore, the objective function can be written as follows:

$\max_{\{m(t)\}_{t=0}^{T}} p(0). \quad (96B.1)$

Following the Euler-Lagrange condition (see Chiang, 1984), we take first-order conditions on the objective function with respect to $m(t)$, where

t ∈ [0,T] and let such first-order conditions be equal to zero.

$$\frac{1}{m(0)^{\delta}}\Big\{ (\delta - 1)(a + b\bar{I} - h)A(0)e^{th}m(t)^{\delta-2} - \alpha' A(0)^2 \rho(t)^2 \sigma(t)^2 e^{2th} m(t)^{\delta-3} (\delta - 2) \Big\} e^{-kt} dt = 0, \quad (96B.2)$$

where t ∈ [0,T].

To simplify equation (96B.2),

$$\begin{aligned} &(\delta - 1)(a + b\bar{I} - h)A(0)e^{th}m(t)^{\delta-2} \\ &-\alpha' A(0)^2 \rho(t)^2 \sigma(t)^2 e^{2th} m(t)^{\delta-3} (\delta - 2) = 0, \quad (96B.3) \end{aligned}$$

where t ∈ [0,T], which is equation (96.21).

## Appendix 96C: Derivation of Equations (96.28) and (96.29)

This appendix presents a detailed derivation of both equations (96.28) and (96.29). In equation (96.35), the optimal payout ratio with no changes in total risk or systematic risk is

$$[\bar{D}(t)/\bar{x}(t)] = \left(1 - \frac{h}{a + b\bar{I}}\right) \left[ \frac{-k + h e^{(h-k)(T-t)}}{h - k} \right]. \quad (96.35)$$

Considering the finite growth case, if (h − k)(T − t) < 1, then following Maclaurin expansion, the e^{(h-k)(T-t)} can be expressed as

$$\begin{aligned} e^{(h-k)(T-t)} &= 1 + (h - k)(T - t) + \frac{(h - k)^2 (T - t)^2}{2!} + \frac{(h - k)^3 (T - t)^3}{3!} \\ &\approx 1 + (h - k)(T - t). \quad (96C.1) \end{aligned}$$

Therefore, equation (96.31) can be approximately written as

$$[\bar{D}(t)/\bar{x}(t)] \approx \left(1 - \frac{h}{a + b\bar{I}}\right) (1 + h(T - t)),$$

which is equation (96.38).

We further take the partial derivative of equation (96.33) with respect to the growth rate. Then the partial derivative of optimal payout ratio with

respect to the growth rate can be approximately written as

$$\frac{\partial[\bar{D}(t)/\bar{x}(t)]}{\partial h} \approx \left(\frac{(a + b\bar{I})(T - t) - 2h(T - t) - 1}{a + b\bar{I}}\right),$$

which is equation (96.29).

## Appendix 96D: Using Moving Estimates Process to Find the Structural Change Point in Equation (96.36)

To estimate the empirical breakpoint, we first assume the dummy variable associated with risk as $D(g_{i,t} < c \cdot ROA_{i,t})$. We introduce a no structural change model, equation (96D.1), and a structural change model, equation (96D.2). In equation (96D.2), $c$ is a continuous constant variable, ranging from 0.2 to 1.8 ($c \in [0.2,\ 1.8]$). The breakpoint of structural change is at $g_{i,t} = c \cdot ROA_{i,t}$.

$$\ln\left(\frac{payout\ ratio_{i,t}}{1 - (payout\ ratio_{i,t})}\right) = \alpha + \beta_1 Risk_{i,t} + \beta_2 Growth_{i,t} + \beta_3 \ln(Size)_{i,t}$$

$$+ \beta_4 ROA_{i,t} + e_{i,t}, \tag{96D.1}$$

$$\ln\left(\frac{payout\ ratio_{i,t}}{1 - (payout\ ratio_{i,t})}\right) = \alpha + \beta'_1 Risk_{i,t} + \beta'_2 D\left(g_{i,t} < c \cdot ROA_{i,t}\right)$$ 

$$\cdot Risk_i + \beta'_3 Growth_{i,t} + \beta'_4 \ln(Size)_{i,t}$$

$$+ \beta'_5 ROA_{i,t} + e_{i,t}. \tag{96D.2}$$

By using the moving estimates process, we calculate the $F$ statistics for all potential structural change points between $g_{i,t} = 0.2 \times ROA_{i,t}$ and $g_{i,t} = 1.8 \times ROA_{i,t}$. The $F$-test is under the null hypothesis that no structural change exists on the relationship between the payout ratio and the risk, that is, equation (96D.1) is identical to equation (96D.2). Finally, we can locate the breakpoint of the structural change at the point with highest value of $F$-statistics.

From Table 96D.1, the process has a clear peak at $c = 0.93$ when using the beta risk as the independent variable and $c = 0.96$ when using the total risk as the independent variable. Figure 96D.1 also graphically presents a peak $F$-statistics point at $c = 0.93$ (0.96) in terms of beta risk (total risk). Results from the moving estimates process indicate that a structural change on the relationship exists between the payout ratio and risks. The breakpoint of the structural change is at $g_{i,t} = 0.93 \times ROA_{i,t}$ or $g_{i,t} = 0.97 \times ROA_{i,t}$.
