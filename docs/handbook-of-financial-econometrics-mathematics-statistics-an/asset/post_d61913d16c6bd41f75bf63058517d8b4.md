## 46.7.2 Optimal capital structure

Given a cash conversion process with fixed inventory base stock level \(S\) and available funds maximum \(A=G+H\), decision makers often need to determine the source of funds: either from the firm’s capital investment \(G\) or debt with a credit limit \(H\). Different decision makers will use different perspectives in their decision process. For example, an operations manager’s main goal is to maximize the firm’s net profit rate, while investors are more interested in maximizing their rate of return. Intuitively, the optimal allocation of funds to capital investment and credit line depends on the costs of capital and credit, everything else being equal.

Figure 46.5 depicts a 3×3 matrix of plots, each showing the two financial metrics, φ (solid line) and ρ (dashed line), as functions of \(H\) for various financial parameters \(\gamma, \alpha, \beta\) with \(\omega = 1\), \(\eta = 0.005\) and \(\ell = 1\), and the following fixed parameters taken from Figures 46.3(e) and 46.4(e): \(S = 30\), \(A = 50\), \(\lambda = 1\), \(\mu = 1/15\), and \(\nu = 1/20\).
