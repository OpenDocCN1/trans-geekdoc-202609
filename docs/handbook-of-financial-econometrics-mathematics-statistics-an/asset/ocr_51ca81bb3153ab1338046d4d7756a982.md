Therefore, it is not possible for all of the off-diagonal elements to be negative unless the $\delta_{ij}$ are greater than one. This means that the signs of the $\delta_{ij}$ coefficients cannot be predicted with absolute certainty. However, it should be noted that the primary reason most corporations hold liquid assets is to facilitate this type of adjustment process; the role of liquid assets is to give flexibility to the capital budget. Therefore, it is reasonable to expect short-term investment to take up the slack caused by the adjustments of the other components. Going back to the dividend example, we would expect short-term investment to rise to ensure that $\sum_{i=1}^5 \delta_{i1} = 1$. It is then possible that $0 < \delta < 1$, and $\delta_{31}, \delta_{41}, \delta_{51} < 0$. Although this is not the only adjustment process a corporation can choose, it appears to be the most likely one.
