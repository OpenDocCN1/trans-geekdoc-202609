In complete markets the stochastic discount factor $M_{t+1}$ is unique because investors can trade with one another to eliminate any idiosyncratic variation in their marginal utilities. Equation (122.17) is written as the expectation of the product equals the product of expectations plus the covariance

$$E_t[(1 + R_{i,t+1})M_{t+1}] = E_t[(1 + R_{i,t+1})]E_t[M_{t+1}] + Cov_t[R_{i,t+1}, M_{t+1}]. \tag{122.18}$$

Substituting equation (122.18) into equation (122.19) and rearranging produces

$$1 + E_t[R_{i,t+1}] = \frac{1 - Cov_t[R_{i,t+1}, M_{t+1}]}{E_t[M_{t+1}]}. \tag{122.19}$$

Equation (122.19) must hold for any asset, including a riskless asset whose gross simple return is $1 + R_{f,t+1}$. Since the simple riskless return has zero covariance with the stochastic discount factor, that is,

$$1 + R_{f,t+1} = \frac{1}{E_t[M_{t+1}]}, \tag{122.20}$$

equation (122.20) can be used to rewrite equation (122.19) as

$$1 + E_t[R_{i,t+1}] = (1 + R_{f,t+1})(1 - Cov_t[R_{i,t+1}, M_{t+1}]), \tag{122.21}$$

because the intertemporal marginal rate of substitution of the investor, also known as the stochastic discount factor is $M_{k,t+1} = \delta[U'(C_{k,t+1})/U'(C_{k,t})]$. Under the assumption of the form of utility function, we can get the stochastic discount factor from the first-order conditions of maximizing investors' utilities. Then, according to equation (122.21), we can describe any expected excess return on risky assets over the riskless rate.

$$E \sum_{t=1}^{\infty} \delta^t \frac{(C_t - X_t)^{1-\gamma} - 1}{1 - \gamma}, \tag{122.22}$$

where $X_t$ denotes the level of habit and $\delta$ belongs to the time discount factor.
