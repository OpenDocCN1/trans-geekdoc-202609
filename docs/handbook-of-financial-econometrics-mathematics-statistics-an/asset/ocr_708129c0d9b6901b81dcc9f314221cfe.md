Community banks are created to serve a relatively homogeneous group, such as agricultural producers in a region or residents of a particular area. Community bank executives are likely to develop loan policies to protect equity and will benefit from the increased information available by serving a geographically homogeneous set of customers, thereby better evaluating credit risk. Klomp and Haan (2012) account for loan quality, measured as non-performing to total loans, in their study of bank behavior. Nevertheless, increases in non-interest costs associated with generating loans may offset any desire to increase income through any perceived risk of offering loans affected by the Dodd–Frank Act, in particular the perception of increased costs due to regulatory modifications. Alternatively, Naceur and Omran (2011) find increased credit risk has a positive and significant effect on cost efficiency.

Prior research indicates regulation compliance has a heterogeneous effect on financial institution performance. Zywicki (2013) considers compliance costs of DFA “invariant to the size or output of the firm,” thereby affecting small institutions disproportionately. Since most community banks have assets smaller than traditional FDIC measures that denote large banks, this is of particular concern. Klomp and Haan (2012) depart from traditional observations of regulatory compliance costs by asset size when analyzing the effect of regulation based on financial institution risk quantiles and conclude regulations disproportionately affect riskier financial institutions. To the extent that asset risk depends on financial institution capitalization, DFA compliance costs may disproportionately affect community banks because of their relatively small size as observed in the heterogeneity in compliance costs across bank size bands for compliance with SOX (Garneau and Shahid, 2009) or the Patriot Act (Dolar and Shughart, 2012).

# 131.4.1 Empirical methods

A bank may be constrained in its operations by regulatory requirements. A consequence of this constraint should be a change in the income available from a bank’s existing asset portfolio. To that end, a random effects model is proposed to estimate the effect of loan volume and financial institution size on variations in the ratio of non-interest expense to assets or to the sum of net interest and non-interest income.
