β_N^* ≤ β_u [ (∑_{t=0}^{N-1} a_t + b_N/β_u) / (∑_{t=0}^{N-1} a_t + c_N) ] . (114.18)

If b_N/β_u is less than c_N, the same result, β_N^* < β_u, as found by Levhari and Levy, is obtained since β_N^* has an upper bound less than β_u. In addition, β_N^* will be less than −β_u if, for some N, b_N or c_N is negative such that either the numerator or the denominator of equation (114.18) becomes negative. Specifically, the effect of autocorrelation in the market rates of return may decrease the magnitude of β_N^* associated with a defense stock. And, for some N, the N-period systematic risk may become negative. This result helps explain why some estimated N-period systematic risks associated with defensive stocks are negative in Levhari–Levy empirical results. Therefore, the existence of autocorrelation in the market rates of return can be used to interpret the empirical results found by levhari and Levy.
