Whether we classify IPO performance as bubbles, hot and cold markets, or sequential learning or some provide some other form of classification for their performance, there are some key elements about the process of issuance and the subsequent performance that researchers should be cognizant of prior to attempting to gauge this performance. The following subsections of this chapter will identify those areas specifically and provide the reader with some insight on how to contend with these issues, before attempting to classify or identify a cycle.

# 67.3 Hot and Cold IPO Market Performance

Ibbotson et al. (1975) initially identified hot issues markets for IPOs as “periods in which the 1st-month performance (or aftermarket performance) of new issues is abnormally high” (p. 1027). In their initial specification of “hot” and “cold” markets, Ibbotson et al. (1975) used the median market-adjusted (i.e., S&P 500 Index) as a sorting device and based on whether the average IPO performance in any given month was above or below the median performance of all monthly market-adjusted IPO performances in the sample, they were categorized as hot or cold IPOs, respectively. In a comparative study, Ritter (1984) examined data from 1977 to 1982 and illustrated that in the 15 months, which included January 1980–March 1981, the average monthly market-adjusted performance generated by IPOs was 48.4%, he compared this estimate to his entire sample that had an average market-adjusted performance of 16.3%, and categorized the period lasting from January 1980 to March 1981 as the hot market and the 1977–1979 period as the cold market.
