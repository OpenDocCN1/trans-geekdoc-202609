Furthermore, prior studies have examined the quality of financial advisors on firm performance in M&As. While financial advisors can assist targets to negotiate better terms during the process of the transactions, this can then create higher value to target shareholders. Studies, such as Walter et al. (2008) and Schiereck et al. (2009), document that targets hired by more prestigious financial advisors obtain lower returns. McLaughlin (1992) and Chahine and Ismail (2009) report that no significant relationship exists between the quality of financial advisor and target premia. On the contrary, Bowers and Miller (1990) report that targets gain more if either the target or bidder is advised by a first-tier advisor. Allen et al. (2004) find that targets gain more when targets employ their own banks as financial advisors. In a recent paper, Ma (2013) reports that targets obtain higher premiums and abnormal returns when targets hire top-tier investment banks.

With regard to the evidence for bidders, several studies have documented that bidders hired first-tier financial advisors to obtain lower returns (Bowers and Miller, 1990; McLaughlin, 1992; Servaes and Zenner, 1996; Rau and Rodgers, 2002; Hunter and Jagtiani, 2003; Allen et al., 2004; Walter et al., 2008). McLaughlin (1992) argues that high-reputable financial advisors tend to engage in more complex deals and then require higher premia. This may reduce the gains to acquiring firms. In a recent paper, Song et al. (2013) find that bidders obtain lower deal premiums when bidders hire boutique advisors. Chang et al. (2016) report that acquirers pay lower takeover premiums when acquirers employ targets’ ex-advisors. Chuang (2017) finds that bidders obtain higher announcement returns when hiring low-reputation financial advisors. However, Rau (2000) reports that bidders obtain higher gains when employing first-tier investment banks in tender offers. Golubov et al. (2012) similarly find that bidders in public acquisitions obtain higher gains when hiring top-tier advisors. Bi and Wang (2018) report that bidders that hire top-tier financial advisors can help improve the operating performance and reduce bid premiums. In sum, a number of existing literatures have examined the quality of financial advisors on the influence of deal outcomes in M&As. However, prior studies report mixed results and none of prior studies explores the impact of domestic and foreign advisors on deal outcomes in M&As. Thus, it is unknown as to whether firms hired by high-reputable financial advisors can create higher value their shareholders and whether domestic advisors can outperform their foreign counterparts.
