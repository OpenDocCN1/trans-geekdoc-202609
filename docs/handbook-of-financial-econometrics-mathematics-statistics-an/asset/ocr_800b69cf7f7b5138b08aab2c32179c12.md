```
$$\Delta\text{LnGold}_{futures,t}=\alpha+\eta\hat{e}_{t-1}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{i}\Delta\text{LnGold}_{futures,t-i}
+\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{i}\Delta\text{LnGold}_{spot,t-j}+e_{t},\tag{92.3}$$
```

where Δ denotes the first difference of variables, and N and M are the lag lengths. The variable \hat{e}_{t-1} is the lag-1 variable of the estimated error-correction terms given from equation (92.1), and e_t denotes a residual. In our empirical work, we set N and M the same and choose them with Schwarz information criteria (SIC) in vector auto-regression (VAR) models.

# 92.2.2.2 Quantile co-integration

The traditional co-integration model describes the following equation:

```
$$y_{t}=\alpha+\beta'_{t}x_{t}+\mu_{t}\tag{92.4}$$
```

where y_t and x_t are integrated with order 1(I(1)) and μ_t is stationary in level. We followed Xiao (2009) who applies the method proposed by Saikkonen (1991) to decompose μ_t into the lead-lag terms $\sum_{j=-K}^{K}\Delta x_{t-j}$ and a pure innovation component ε_t in order to deal with endogeneity in the traditional co-integration model given below.

```
$$y_{t}=\alpha+\beta'_{t}x_{t}+\sum_{j=-K}^{K}\Delta x'_{t-j}\Pi_{j}+\varepsilon_{t}.\tag{92.5}$$
```

If the τth quantile of ε_t is Q_ε(τ), let Ω_t = σ {x_t, Δx_{t-j}, ∀j}, the τth quantile of y_t is given by

```
$$Q_{y_t}(τ|Ω_t)=α+β(τ)'x_t+\sum_{j=-K}^{K}\Delta x'_{t-j}\Pi_j+F_{ε}^{-1}(τ),\tag{92.6}$$
```

where F_ε(.) is the cumulative distribution function of ε_t. Let Z_t be the vector of regressions consisting of (1, x_t) and (Δx'_{t-j}, j = -K, ..., K), θ = (α, β'_t, Π'_{-K}, ..., Π'_K)', hence, we arrive at

```
$$θ(τ)=(α(τ),β(τ)',Π'_{-K},...,Π'_K)',\tag{92.7}$$
```
