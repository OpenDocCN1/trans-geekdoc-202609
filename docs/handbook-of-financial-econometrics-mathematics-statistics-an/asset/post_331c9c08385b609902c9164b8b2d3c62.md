## 61.2.2.3 The model of Joslin, Le, and Singleton (2013a)

Joslin et al. (2013a) (hereafter, JLS) develop a Gaussian macro PCDTSM (GMPCDTSM) that is very similar in nature to the JSZ normalization of PCDTSM. The key difference is that this model includes macro variables in its specification. The GMPCDTSM was developed by the above mentioned authors using the framework of JSZ and Ang and Piazzesi (2003). Moreover, many additional key insights are provided in Joslin et al. (2013b). So, the key advances were made by Ang and Piazzesi (2003) and JSZ and Joslin et al. The factors would include the three latent factors described above. But, in our implementation we focus on the yield curve with macro factors in DRA and add three key measures of our macroeconomy to our vector of factors; capacity utilization (CU_t), inflation (INFL_t), and the fed funds rate (FFR_t), with data on all three measures for the US economy being collected from the Federal Reserve Economic Database (FRED). Assuming that the yields still load only on the three latent factors, we assume that the matrix in the abovementioned measurement error equation is N × 6 with the three right most columns assumed to be zeros. Thus, the structure of the model remains parsimonious and estimation via a Kalman filtering algorithm is simple in principle.7 We assume that as many yields as factors are measured with error and include the six-month, one-year, two-year, five-year, seven-year, and 10-year in our estimation. We also follow the authors and assume that ε_t and η_t are orthogonal to each other and the initial state from which we begin the filtering algorithm. We also assume that these two error processes follow Gaussian white noise dynamics with zero mean and diagonal covariance structure. This means that we have 55 parameters to estimate in our state space formulation (1 for Γ, 6 for μ, 36 for A, 6 for factor covariance matrix, 6 for measurement error covariance matrix). So, although this model has a parsimonious structure, due to the fact that it requires 55 parameters to estimate, it is challenging to estimate this model. Fortunately, the authors provide, in Table 7 of their paper (Ang and Piazzesi, 2003), their parameter estimates and note that they initialize λ in the filtering algorithm at the value specified in Diebold and Li (2006) which is 0.0609. Using these initial conditions greatly shortened the computation time and resulted in remarkably small cross-sectional fitting errors for the six examined yields with varying maturities measured in months; 7.0466 basis points for the bond yield with a six-month maturity, 5.9957 basis points for the one-year bond yield, 5.8202 for the two-year bond yield, 6.6047 basis points for the five-year bond yield, 7.2423 basis points for the bond yield with a seven-year maturity, and 17.3465 basis points for the 10-year bond yield. These errors are also quite close to those reported by DRA.8 We present a plot of the model fitted dynamics over the sample period under examination implied by the DRA model in Figure 61.1.

Estimation of a yields-macro model with 55 parameters is quite a daunting task without suitable initial conditions. Recent work by JLS attempts to address this challenge by developing a new family of GMPCDTSM in which most parameters can be estimated via linear regressions and when a researcher desires to employ a Kalman filtering algorithm for estimation suitable initial conditions for a full estimation (such as the one described above) can be obtained from linear regressions. DRA do not use PCA to extract factors, and so there is no orthogonality restrictions on the factors. But, as we note below usage of PCA (which does restrict the analysis by assuming orthogonality) allows for simple and convenient estimation of factors, avoiding the usage of computational burdensome optimization algorithms that are associated with numerous local optima.

We begin our discussion of the JLS model with the supposition that M macroeconomic variables GMPCDTSM as risk factors and that the short rate $r_t$ is an affine function of $M$ and an additional $L$ pricing factors $P_t^L$, so that

$$r_t = \rho_0 + \rho_{1M} M_t + \rho_{1P} P_t^L = \rho_0 + \rho_1 Z_t. \tag{61.49}$$

In equation (61.49), $Z_t = (M_t^T, P_t^{L T})^T$. In our implementation of this model, we take $M = 2$ and $L = 3$. The risk factors $Z_t$ are assumed to follow a Gaussian VAR$^{\mathbb{Q}}$(1) process:

$$Z_t = \kappa_{0Z}^{\mathbb{Q}} + \kappa_{1Z}^{\mathbb{Q}} Z_{t-1} + \sqrt{\Sigma_Z} \varepsilon_t^{\mathbb{Q}}. \tag{61.50}$$

In equation (61.50) above, $\varepsilon_t^{\mathbb{Q}} \sim N(0, I)$. The yield portfolios $P_t$ can be expressed as

$$P_t = A_{TS} + B_{TS} Z_t. \tag{61.51}$$

In equation (61.51), the loadings $(A_{TS}, B_{TS})$ are known functions of $(\kappa_{0Z}^{\mathbb{Q}}, \kappa_{1Z}^{\mathbb{Q}}, \rho_0, \rho_1, \Sigma_Z)$ analogous to JSZ. To obtain the canonical form of the normalization, JLS note that $M$ macro factors and $L$ latent factors (these are obtained from PCA) distinctly contribute to the pricing of the bonds. Therefore, all bond yields can be expressed as affine functions of $P_t^N$ where $N = M + L$. In our implementation of this model since we employ two macro factors and three latent factors, $N = 5$. A key implication of this is that the macro factors must be spanned by $P_t^N$

$$M_t = \gamma_0 + \gamma_1 P_t^N. \tag{61.52}$$

Based upon this relation, the authors apply the following rotation to adopt the form of JSZ:

$$Z_{t\,5\times1} = \begin{pmatrix} M_{t\,2\times1} \\ P^L_{t\,3\times1} \end{pmatrix}_{5\times1} = \begin{pmatrix} \gamma_{0\,2\times1} \\ 0_{3\times1} \end{pmatrix}_{5\times1} + \begin{pmatrix} I_{L\,3\times3} & \gamma_{1\,2\times5} \\ 0_{3\times2} \end{pmatrix}_{5\times5} P^N_{t\,5\times1}. \tag{61.53}$$
