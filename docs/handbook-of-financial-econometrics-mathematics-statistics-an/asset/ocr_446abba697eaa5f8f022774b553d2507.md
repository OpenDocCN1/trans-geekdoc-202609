# 91.2.2 Stale Pricing

This section analyzes the nature of stale or smoothed pricing embedded in hedge fund returns and examines the literature on stale and smoothed pricing. Stale pricing occurs as a result of managers not fully integrating the impact of a market or another systemic factor on the returns of the fund. The reason why this is an important issue to contend with in the literature on hedge funds and hedge fund performance is that due to the relatively opaque nature of hedge funds and the manager’s ability to conceal their underlying holdings and positions, they have an opportunity to manipulate the returns associated with their underlying holdings to improve the perceived performance results of their funds if the underlying assets are priced intermittently. The works of Asness et al. (2001), Bollen and Pool (2008, 2009), Cumming and Dai (2010), Cassar and Gerakos (2001), Agarwal et al. (2011, 2013a), Jorion and Schwartz (2014), Aragon and Nanda (2017), and Smith and Mutmaz (2017) are discussed to explore stale pricing within the context of hedge fund performance.

Asness et al. (2001) introduced a very interesting and important point that practitioners and academics should understand when attempting to interpret the traditional metrics that are used in professional practice to assess the performance of hedge funds compared against standard benchmarks. Asness et al. (2001) contend that when evaluating hedge fund performance, researchers should consider both the current betas associated with the market’s influence on hedge fund performance and a lagged beta to attempt to understand the true systemic risk that investors are taking when investing in these products.
