The regression results concerning sentiment effect on risk-neutral skewness under different market conditions are provided in Panels 2A and 2B of Table 42.4. Panel 2A-1 reports a significantly positive relation between the investor sentiment proxy and the pre-crisis risk-neutral skewness separately derived from call and put option prices. After controlling for rational predictors, Panel 2A-2 indicates that, for a bearish change in investors’ irrational sentiment, call and put option traders tend to expect a negative jump in underlying asset prices during the pre-crisis period. Alternatively, the significant influence of the consumer sentiment proxy disappears in the presence of rational variables, implying that both call and put option traders’ beliefs about the magnitude and direction of jumps in underlying asset prices are related to consumers’ rational sentiment. Furthermore, the estimated coefficient and t-statistic of the sentiment proxies in the risk-neutral skewness derived from calls are larger than those in the risk-neutral skewness from puts, implying that investor and consumer sentiment effects are stronger on call option traders’ expectations about jumps in underlying asset prices. In Panels 2B-1 and 2B-2, during the crisis period, call and put option traders’ expectations about price jumps are still positively influenced by investors’ irrational sentiment. Compared with the crisis risk-neutral skewness from puts, the influence of investors’ irrational sentiment on the crisis risk-neutral skewness from calls is greater as well. On the other hand, we find different impacts of the consumer sentiment components on risk-neutral skewness separately derived from call and put option prices. Consumers’ rational sentiment still significantly influence call option traders’ expectations about price jumps, while consumers’ irrational sentiment becomes important in explaining put option traders’ expectations in the crisis period.
