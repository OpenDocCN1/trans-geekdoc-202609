# Abstract

Based upon Ritchken (1985), Levy (1985), Lo (1987), Zhang (1994), Jackwerth and Rubinstein (1996), and others, this chapter discusses the alternative method to determine option bound in terms of the first two moments of distribution. This approach includes stochastic dominance method and linear programming method, then we discuss semi-parametric method and non-parametric method for option-bound determination. Finally, we incorporate both skewness and kurtosis explicitly through extending Zhang (1994) to provide bounds for the prices of the expected payoffs for options, given the first two moments and skewness and kurtosis.

# 7.1 Introduction

This chapter will first review and compare parametric, semi-parametric, and non-parametric approaches for option bound determination, then it will investigate how a parametric method incorporates both skewness and kurtosis explicitly through extending Zhang (1994) to provide bounds for the prices of and the expected payoff for options, given the first two moments and skewness and kurtosis. The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 7.2 discusses parametric and mathematical methods to derive option bounds, while Section 7.3 discusses semi-parametric and non-parametric methods.

Section 7.4 discusses upper and lower bounds of expected payoffs in terms of the first four moments. Then Section 7.5 translates the bounds of the expected payoffs of options into bounds for option prices through discounting the bounds of the expected payoffs at the lower bound of the expected rate of return of options. In this section, we use Grundy’s (1991) lower bounds of the expected rates of return of options instead of the risk neutrality valuation relationship used by Lo (1987). In order to test the effectiveness of our bounds, in Section 7.6, we consider the application of the risk-neutral valuation relationship in our problem to compare our bounds with those of others.
