Proof. Similar to the proof of Theorem 46.4, we consider the following cases:

Case 1: A = 1. Then clearly H* = 0 and G* = A − H* = 1.

Case 2: A > 1. In this case, we search for an integer H in the range {0, …, B} that maximizes ρ(A−H, H). To this end, consider three sub-cases.

# Case 2.1:

π_C(0) ≥ [λ[1 − π_I(0)]ω − η Σ_{n=1}^S n π_I(n)] / (β A) − α/β.

Then by Lemma 46.7, Δρ(A−H, H) is strictly decreasing in H with Δρ(A−1,1) ≤ 0 by equation (46.33), implying H* = 0 and G* = A.

# Case 2.2:

π_C(0) < [λ[1 − π_I(0)]ω − η Σ_{n=1}^S n π_I(n)] / (β A) − α/β

and there exists a minimal H′ ∈ {1, …, B}, such that Δρ(A − H′, H′) ≤ 0. Then H* = H′ − 1 by Lemma 46.7.

# Case 2.3:

π_C(0) < [λ[1 − π_I(0)]ω − η Σ_{n=1}^S n π_I(n)] / (β A) − α/β

and there does not exist H′ ∈ {1, …, B}, such that Δρ(A − H′, H′) ≤ 0. Then, the maximum is obtained at the boundary where H* = B.

Finally, the first line of equation (46.34) follows by combining Cases 1 and 2.1, while the second line of equation (46.34) follows by combining Cases 2.2 and 2.3.

Theorem 46.5 implies that if A ≥ λ ω / α, then the firm’s rate of return is maximized by allocating all available funds to its own capital investment and none to credit (i.e., H* = 0 and G* = A).
