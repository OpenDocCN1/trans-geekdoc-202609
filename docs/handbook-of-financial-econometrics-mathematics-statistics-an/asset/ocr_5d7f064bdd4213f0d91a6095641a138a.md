# 53.3.2 Research methodology

We begin with a test of ARCH effect on daily stock returns, using simplified Lagrange multiplier. A number of studies find evidence of heteroscedasticity in the market model and subsequent related models, but most of them focus on unconditional and cross-sectional heteroscedasticity. For instance, Diebold et al. (1993) focus on conditional volatility movements of stock returns. They used Engle's (1982) autoregressive conditional heteroscedasticity. (ARCH) model to study the temporal behavior of residual return variance in the market model. Their empirical results indicate that the ARCH effect is a rather common phenomenon for monthly returns for NYSE firms. However, it could be premature to suggest that the daily returns used to examine the phenomenon of the post-earnings-announcement also exhibit the ARCH effect. We employ the simplified Lagrange multiplier test proposed by Engle (1982) to examine whether the daily returns also exhibit ARCH effect. The test statistic is $T * R^2$; where $T$ is the number of observations and $R^2$ is the coefficient of determination of regressing the OLS squared residuals on a constant and a $k$-lag OLS squared residuals. This statistic has an asymptotic Chi-square distribution with $k$-degree of freedom under the null hypothesis.

We proceed to the examination of systematic risks around earnings announcement. We estimate systematic risk by regressing the daily stock returns against the equally weighted market indices. We use Chow test (Chow, 1960) to examine whether the systematic risks have been changed subsequent to the announcement of earnings. A rejection of the null hypothesis would provide us with preliminary information that the conventional market model, which states that the systematic risk is constant prior and subsequent to earnings announcement, may be misspecified in terms of estimating abnormal returns.
