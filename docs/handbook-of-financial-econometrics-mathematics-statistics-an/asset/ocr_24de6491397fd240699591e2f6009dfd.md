The mixture copula is the convex combination of several individual copulas and is also called mixed copula or M-copula in some studies (Turgutlu and Ucer, 2010). The function of mixture copula can be formulated as in equation (38.1).

```
$$C_{mix}(u_1, u_2, ..., u_n | \theta) = \sum_{k=1}^{s} w_k C_k(u_1, u_2, ..., u_n | \theta_k), \qquad (38.1)$$
```

where {C_k(u_1, u_2, ..., u_n | θ_k)} is a sequence of known copulas with unknown parameters {θ_k} and {w_k}_{k=1}^s are the weights satisfying 0 ≤ w_k ≤ 1 and Σ_{k=1}^s w_k = 1 for k = 1, ..., s. Denote the entire parameters in the mixed copula as θ, that is, θ = (θ_1, ..., θ_s, w_1, ..., w_s)^T. The mixture copula facilitates separation of the concepts of degree of dependence and structure of dependence, embodied in two different groups of parameters, i.e., association parameters and weight parameters (Hu, 2006). The association parameters, denoted as (θ_1, ..., θ_s)^T, are the parameters that control the degree of dependence in each copula function, while weight parameters, denoted as (w_1, ..., w_s)^T, are the shape parameters reflecting the credence we should assign the corresponding copula. For example, if the mth copula is the most appropriate structure for the data, then we would expect that w_m is close to 1 (Cai et al., 2008).

The biggest advantage of using a mixture copula model is that it can nest different copula shapes, i.e., mixed copulas can capture different patterns of dependence structure. Therefore, it is more flexible to model the dependence and can deliver better descriptions of dependence structure than an individual copula (Hu, 2006; Cai et al., 2008). In other words, a mixture copula can integrate the dependence capturing abilities of its component copulas. For example, a mixture copula that includes Gaussian and Gumbel copulas can improve a single Gaussian dependence structure by allowing for possible right tail dependence (Cai et al., 2008). In general, a mixed copula model is able to generate dependence structure that does not belong to one particular existing copula family. By carefully choosing component copulas in the mixture, a model that is simple yet flexible enough to generate most dependence patterns can be constructed (Chen and Tu, 2013).
