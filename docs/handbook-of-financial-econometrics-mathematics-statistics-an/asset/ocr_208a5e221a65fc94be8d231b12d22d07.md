However, the successful adaptation of disruptive technologies depends on both job environment (Walczuch et al., 2007) and personal attitudes of employees (Elie-Dit-Cosaque et al., 2011). This chapter aims at examining employees’ attitudes and acceptance of the new technologies in the Russian financial institutions. It may depend on both internal and external factors of technology adoption (Elie-Dit-Cosaque et al., 2011) and fills the gap in the literature by providing an empirical evidence of employees opinions about the impact caused by disruptive technologies on their productivity and firm performance (Gai et al., 2018).

Online survey was used to collect the data. The link to the survey was sent to the members of CFA Russia, CFA exam candidates as well as to students and alumni of HSE Banking Institute. In total, about 2000 professionals were invited to fill the questionnaire. The final sample includes 148 valid responses. Most of the respondents were employed in financial services (58%), especially in commercial banks (53%).

Using structural equation modeling (SEM), we test the hypotheses concerning the positive relationship between labor productivity and company performance (Riley et al., 2017), personal motivation and labor productivity (Ali et al., 2012), organizational support and company performance (Sengupta et al., 2015), digital skills and labor productivity (Rubel et al., 2017). Our results have shown that all hypotheses were supported by the data. Both labor productivity and organizational support have a strong positive impact on company performance related to disruptive technologies. The labor productivity is driven by both motivation to use disruptive technologies and digital skills. However, motivation affects labor performance stronger than digital skills. More motivated employees will be better prepared to use disruptive technologies and perceive higher improvements of productivity. Higher digital skills lead to higher labor performance.
