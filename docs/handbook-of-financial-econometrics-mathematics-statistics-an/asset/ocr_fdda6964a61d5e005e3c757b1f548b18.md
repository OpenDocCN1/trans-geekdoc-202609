Let $x_4 = a(s)$ stands for the implicit function determined by $P(x_4)=0$ and take derivative to both sides of $P(x_4)=0$ with respect to $s$ yields

$\displaystyle\frac{\partial\alpha(s)}{\partial s}=\frac{3s^2}{P'(x_4)}\left[\phi-\left(\frac{x_4}{1-s}\right)^4\right].\qquad (7A.13)$

Letting the right hand side of equation (7A.13) equal zero yields

$\alpha(s) = (1-s)\phi^{1/4}.\qquad (7A.14)$

Taking derivative to both sides of equation (7A.13) using equation (7A.14) yields

$\displaystyle\frac{\partial^2\alpha(s)}{\partial s^2}=\frac{12s^2\phi}{(1-s)P'(x_4)}.\qquad (7A.15)$

Equation (7A.15) implies that the necessary and sufficient condition for $x_4 = \alpha(s)$ to have a maximum ($\bar R_{4C2}$ reaches maximum simultaneously as $x_4 = \alpha(s)$ reaches maximum) is $\phi > 0$. For positively skewed asset prices, $\kappa > 0$, and $\kappa > 0$ is sufficient for $\phi > 0$ but not necessary. When $\phi > 0$, the negativeness of equation (7A.15) shows that $x_4 = \alpha(s)$ reaches its maximum at the $s$ that is determined by equation (7A.14). Equation (7A.14) is a function of $s = F(K)$, it determines $s$ as a function of $\phi$, therefore, the optimal $s = F(K)$ which yields the maximum $x_4$ can be written as $F(K) = G(\mu, \delta, \eta, K)$.
