(ii) The correlation coefficient is defined as

$$\rho\left(\frac{\bar{R} - R_f}{S}, S\right) = \frac{d(R_f - \mu)_6}{\sigma_{sp}\sigma_s},\tag{69.9}$$

where

$$\sigma_{sp}^2 = \operatorname{Var}\left(\frac{\bar{R} - R_f}{S}\right)$$ 

$$= \frac{\Gamma((n - 3)/2)}{2\Gamma((n - 1)/2)} + (\mu - R_f)^2\left(\frac{n}{2\sigma^2}\right)$$ 

$$\times \left\{ \frac{\Gamma((n - 3)/2)}{\Gamma((n - 1)/2)} - \left[\frac{\Gamma((n - 2)/2)}{\Gamma((n - 1)/2)}\right]^2 \right\},\tag{69.10}$$ 

$$\sigma_s^2 = \operatorname{Var}(S) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n} \left\{ (n - 1) - 2\left[\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}\right]^2 \right\}.\tag{69.11}$$

Observing equations (69.5) and (69.9), one can see that the estimated Sharpe’s measure is uncorrelated with the estimated risk measure only when the risk-free rate of interest is equal to the mean rate, $n$, of the return on the market portfolio or when the sample size $n$ is infinite. The risk-free rate is generally not equal to the mean rate of return on the market portfolio and the sample size used in empirical work is generally finite. Therefore, the estimated Sharpe’s measure is in general correlated with the estimated risk measure $(S)$. If the risk-free rate is less than the mean rate of return on the market portfolio, the estimated Sharpe’s measure is negatively correlated with the estimated risk measure. Conversely, the estimated Sharpe’s measure and the estimated risk measure will be positively correlated if the risk-free rate is greater than the mean rate of return on the market portfolio.$^7$

The quantity $d$ defined in (69.6) is a decreasing function of the sample size $n$.$^8$ For example, the value of $d$ is 0.010 for a sample size of 50 and 0.005 for a sample size of 100. Thus, the covariance defined in (69.5) gets smaller as the sample size is increased. This implies that the bias (indicated by nonzero covariance) associated with the estimated Sharpe’s measure can be reduced by using a larger sample size. Hence, to reduce the bias in the empirical research, a large sample of the rates of return should be used to estimate the Sharpe measure.
