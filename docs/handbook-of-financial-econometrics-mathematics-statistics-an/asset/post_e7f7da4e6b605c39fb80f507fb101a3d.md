If the λ_t follows a Markov chain regime-switching process with transition probabilities p_ij = Prob[λ_t = j|λ_{t-1}= i], the above state-space model becomes state-space model with regime switching.

Chen and Liu (2000) propose a special sequential Monte Carlo method, the mixture Kalman filter (MKF), which uses a random mixture of the Gaussian distributions to approximate a target distribution. The MKF can be used to filter and estimate the state-space model with regime switching. We introduce the algorithm of the MKF in the following part.

Let y_{1:t} = (y_1,...,y_t) be the observation and λ_{1:t} = (λ_1,...,λ_t) be the regime indicator up to time t. We observe that p(x_t|y_{1:t}) = ∫ p(x_t|λ_{1:t}, y_{1:t})p(λ_{1:t}|y_{1:t})dλ_{1:t}, where p(x_t|λ_{1:t}, y_{1:t}) ~ N{μ_t(λ_{1:t}), Σ_t(λ_{1:t})}, in which, {μ_t(λ_{1:t}), Σ_t(λ_{1:t})} can be obtained by running the Kalman filter with given trajectory λ_{1:t}. The main idea of the MKF is to use a weighted Monte Carlo sample of the indicators,

$$S_t = \{ (λ_{1:t}^{(1)}, w_t^{(1)}), ..., (λ_{1:t}^{(m)}, w_t^{(m)}) \}$$

to represent the distribution p(λ_{1:t}|y_{1:t}). In detail, we sample λ_{1:t}^{(j)}, j = 1,...,N from a pre-selected trial distribution q(λ_{1:t}|y_{1:t}, x_{1:t}) and assign w_t^{(j)} = p(λ_{1:t}|y_{1:t})/q(λ_{1:t}|y_{1:t}, x_{1:t}). Following an importance sampling logic, the mixture of Gaussian distribution

$$\frac{1}{W_t} \sum_{j=1}^N w_t^{(j)} N\{ μ_t(λ_{1:t}^{(j)}), Σ_t(λ_{1:t}^{(j)}) \}$$

where W_t = ∑_{j=1}^N w_t^{(j)}, approximates the target distribution p(x_t|y_{1:t}). For any integrable function h(·), we can estimate the quantity of interest E{h(x_t)|y_{1:t}} as

$$\hat{E}\{h(x_t)|y_{1:t}\} = \frac{1}{W_t} \sum_{j=1}^N w_t^{(j)} \int h(x) \phi\{x; μ_t(λ_{1:t}^{(j)}), Σ_t(λ_{1:t}^{(j)})\} dx,$$

where φ is the Gaussian density function.

Set KF_t^{(j)} = (μ_t(λ_{1:t}^{(j)}), Σ_t(λ_{1:t}^{(j)})), which records the posterior mean and covariance matrix of x_t, conditional on y_{1:t} and a given trajectory λ_{1:t}^{(j)}. This can be obtained by the Kalman filter. Then the MKF updating scheme consists of recursive applications of the following steps. For j = 1,...,N:

- 1. Generate $\lambda_{t+1}^{(j)}$ from its predictive distribution $p(\lambda_{t+1}|\lambda_{1:t}^{(j)},\mathrm{KF}_t^{(j)},y_{t+1})$;
- 2. Obtain $\mathrm{KF}_{t+1}^{(j)}$ by a one-step Kalman filter, conditional on {$\mathrm{KF}_t^{(j)}$, $z_{t+1}$, $\lambda_{t+1}^{(j)}$},
  $P_{t+1}=H_{\lambda_{t+1}}\Sigma_t H'_{\lambda_{t+1}}+W_{\lambda_{t+1}}W'_{\lambda_{t+1}}$
  $S_{t+1}=GP_{t+1}G'+VV'$, 
  $\mu_{t+1}=H_{\lambda_{t+1}}\mu_t+P_{t+1}G'S_{t+1}^{-1}(z_{t+1}-GH_{\lambda_{t+1}}\mu_t-Dg_{t+1})$
  $\Sigma_{t+1}=P_{t+1}-P_{t+1}G'S_{t+1}^{-1}GP_{t+1}$; and
- 3. Update the new weight as $w_{t+1}^{(j)}=w_t^{(j)}\times u_{t+1}^{(j)}$, where
  $u_{t+1}^{(j)}=\frac{p(\lambda_{1:t}^{(j)},\lambda_{t+1}^{(j)}|y_{1:t+1})}{p(\lambda_{1:t}^{(j)}|y_{1:t})p(\lambda_{t+1}^{(j)}|\lambda_{1:t}^{(j)},\mathrm{KF}_t^{(j)},y_{t+1})}\propto p(y_{t+1}|\mathrm{KF}_t^{(j)})$ 
  $=\sum_{i\in I}p(y_{t+1}|\Lambda_{t+1}=i,\mathrm{KF}_t^{(j)})p(\Lambda_{t+1}=i|\lambda_{1:t}^{(j)})$, 
- 4. (resampling-rejuvenation) if the coefficient of variance of the $w_{t+1}$ exceeds a threshold value, resample a new set of $\mathrm{KF}_{t+1}$ from {$\mathrm{KF}_t^{(1)},...,\mathrm{KF}_t^{(m)}$} with probability proportional to the weights $w_{t+1}^{(j)}$.

where $I$ is the set of possible values of $\Lambda_t$. Specifically, the MKF updating step 3 can be implemented in the following steps:

- (3.1) for each $\Lambda_{t+1}=i$, $i\in I$, run the Kalman filter to obtain $v_i^{(j)}\propto p(y_{t+1}|\Lambda_{t+1}=i,\mathrm{KF}_t^{(j)})p(\Lambda_{t+1}=i|\lambda_{1:t}^{(j)})$, where $p(\Lambda_{t+1}=i|\lambda_{1:t}^{(j)})$ is the prior transition probability for the indicator and $p(y_{t+1}|\Lambda_{t+1}=i,KF_t^{(j)})$ is a by-product of the Kalman filter $p(y_{t+1}|y_{1:t},\lambda_{1:t},\lambda_{t+1})\sim N(GH_{\lambda_{t+1}}\mu_t+Dg_{t+1},S_{t+1})$;
- (3.2) sample a $\lambda_{t+1}^{(j)}$ from the set $I$, with probability proportional to $v_i^{(j)}$;
- (3.3) let $\mathrm{KF}_{t+1}^{(j)}$ be the one with $\Lambda_{t+1}=\lambda_{t+1}^{(j)}$; and
- (3.4) the new weight is $w_{t+1}^{(j)}=w_t^{(j)}\sum_{i\in I}v_i^{(j)}$.

## 64.5.3 Application

Currently there is only one asset pricing study applying the MKF in estimating the state-space model with regime switching. Chang, Chen, Wang and Wu (2018) uses the MKF to detect periodically collapsing rational bubbles in stock prices. They express the stock-dividend-bubble system in a log-linearized Gaussian state-space model with Markov switching. A two-regime specification includes surviving and collapsing regimes, in which a bubble grows at a speed greater or smaller than the asset’s required rate of return, while at the same time the rational bubble condition is imposed. They apply the MKF strategy to estimate the model for both simulated observations and real US stock market indices. The methodology can fit the bubble process better and identify most of bubble collapsing episodes in history. They also discuss the feasibility and superiority of a three-regime specification.

## 64.6 Conclusion

In this chapter, we provide a brief summary of applications of filtering methods in estimating asset pricing models. We review the filtering methods for four types of state-space models, including linear Gaussian state-space model, nonlinear Gaussian state-space model, nonlinear non-Gaussian state-space model, and state-space model with regime switching. These models cover most of the asset pricing models ranging from discrete to continuous models and from structural to non-structural models. These methods can be helpful for finance researchers to estimate a rich set of asset pricing models.
