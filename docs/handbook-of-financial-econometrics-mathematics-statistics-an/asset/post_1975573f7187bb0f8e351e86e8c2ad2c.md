## 42.4 Sentiment Effect on Call and Put Option Prices

### 42.4.1 Rational and irrational sentiment effects on call and put option prices

Table 42.3 summarizes the significant differences in the shape of risk-neutral distributions separately derived from TAIEX call and put option prices. The previous literature shows that sentiment is related to changes in option prices. To find a potential explanation concerning the diversion between call and put option prices, we examine whether a sentiment effect difference exists between call and put option prices. The sentiment proxies adopted in this chapter are an investor sentiment proxy (LongShort_Futures) and a consumer sentiment proxy (CCI). The relation between sentiment proxies and pre-crisis (crisis) risk-neutral moments is analyzed by running the following regression with daily time-series observations:

Momentₜ = α₀ + α₁ Momentₜ₋₁ + α₂ IXₜ + εₜ, (42.2)

where Moment represents the risk-neutral confidence interval, skewness, and kurtosis and IX denotes the investor sentiment or consumer sentiment proxy.

The investor (consumer) sentiment proxy is correlated with investors’ (consumers’) irrational sentiment, which is the aggregate error in investors’ (consumers’) beliefs regarding underlying asset prices (general economic prospects). Moreover, the investor (consumer) sentiment proxy may reflect investors’ (consumers’) rational sentiment, which is the rational updating of investors’ (consumers’) expectations as new information appears. Based on the work of Baker and Wurgler (2006), we decompose the sentiment proxies to study the influence of irrational and rational sentiment on risk-neutral moments. Each sentiment proxy is regressed on the rational predictors of the underlying asset return and indicators of investment opportunities, analyzed by running regression (42.3). In this chapter, the rational control variables include an industry production index (IP), the difference in yield between 10-year Treasury bonds and the 90-day repurchase agreement rate (Yield), the 30-day repurchase agreement rate (RP), the TAIEX dividend yield (DY), and difference in returns between the TAIEX and a one month repurchase agreement (Risk Premium).13 The risk-neutral moment is then regressed on the residual of the sentiment proxy ε*_t, shown in regression (42.4)

IX_t = β0 + β1 IP_t + β2 Yield_t + β3 RP_t + β4 DY_t
    + β5 Risk Premium_t + ε*_t,          (42.3)
Moment_t = α'_0 + α'_1 Moment_{t-1} + α'_2 ε*_t + ε'_t.   (42.4)

Panels 1A and 1B in Table 42.4 report results of sentiment effect on the risk-neutral confidence intervals separately extracted from call and put option prices during the pre-crisis and crisis periods, respectively. In Panel 1A-1, both sentiment proxies are negatively related to the pre-crisis risk-neutral confidence intervals from calls and puts; moreover, the influence of the consumer sentiment proxy is significant. Panel 1A-2 shows the results concerning sentiment effect on the pre-crisis risk-neutral confidence interval in the presence of rational control variables. We find that the relation between the residual of the consumer sentiment proxy and the pre-crisis risk-neutral confidence intervals becomes insignificant. The implication is that the significant influence of the consumer sentiment proxy shown in Panel 1A-1 is driven by a rational sentiment component.
