Equations (114.3a) and (114.3b) show that the return of long period can be presented by the summation of rates of return of every individual subperiod which are disjoint. And this property is called additivity. For example, if $r_t$ and $r_{Mt}$ represent monthly rates of returns and $T/n = 3$, then $R_T$ and $R_{MT}$ will represent the quarterly rates of return.

To simplify the analysis, the market model deviation from the mean in terms of monthly rates of return is defined as follows¹:

$$Y_t = \beta X_t + u_t, \quad t = 1,2,\dots,N \tag{114.4}$$

where $Y_t = r_t - \bar{r}_t$, $X_t = r_{Mt} - \bar{r}_{Mt}$, $\beta$ is scalar parameter, and $U_t$ is a non-autocorrelated error term with $E(u_t)=0$ and $E(u_t^2)=\sigma^2$ for all $t$.

Following Zeller and Montimarquette (1971) and the definitions defined in (114.3), the market model without intercept in terms of $N$-period rates of return is given as follows:

$$\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{Y} = \boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{X}\beta + \boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{U} \tag{114.5a}$$

where $\boldsymbol{Y}' = (Y_1, Y_2, \dots, Y_{(J\times N)})$, $\boldsymbol{X}' = (X_1, X_2, \dots, X_{(J\times N)})$, $\boldsymbol{U}' = (u_1, u_2, \dots, u_{(J\times N)})$, and $\boldsymbol{A}$ is a $J \times (J \times N)$ matrix of the form

$$\boldsymbol{A} = \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{I}' & \boldsymbol{o}' & \dots & \boldsymbol{o}' \\ \boldsymbol{o}' & \boldsymbol{I}' & \dots & \boldsymbol{o}' \\ \dots & \dots & \dots & \dots \\ \boldsymbol{o}' & \boldsymbol{o}' & \dots & \boldsymbol{I}' \end{bmatrix},$$

and $\boldsymbol{I}' = (1,1,\dots,1)$ and $\boldsymbol{o}' = (0,0,\dots,0)$ are row vectors of $1 \times N$.

In addition, we specify

$$\boldsymbol{A}_j'\boldsymbol{Y} = \beta \boldsymbol{A}_j'\boldsymbol{X} + \boldsymbol{A}_j'\boldsymbol{U} \tag{114.5b}$$

where $\boldsymbol{A}_j'$ is the $j$th row of $\boldsymbol{A}$.

Given the assumptions made about the elements of $\boldsymbol{U}$ in connection with (114.4), we have $E(\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{U}) = 0$, and the $J \times J$ covariance matrix for $\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{U}$ in (4) is

$$E(\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{U}\boldsymbol{U}'\boldsymbol{A}') = \sigma^2 \begin{bmatrix} N & & \\ & N & \\ & & \ddots & \\ & & & N \end{bmatrix}. \tag{114.6}$$
