## 56.2 Literature Review

Organizations who consider employee like a human capital and as an asset to be invested in and developed can expect higher performance (Riley et al., 2017). Voslobodan (2012) revealed that employee performance significantly contributes to the growth of the firm performance. Employee with higher productivity provides a competitive advantage on the market and the service delivery will be done before competitors.

Moreover, through a survey of Indian software organizations, Mathew et al. (2012) also confirmed the impact of job performance on the company’s growth. The authors argued that employee work outcomes such as satisfaction at work, productivity and quality of work are a consequence of organizational culture. Furthermore, satisfaction and productivity at work lead to firm profitability and growth while quality of work is presented as a contributor to organizational innovation.

In recent decades, job performance and the quality of service have often depended on how the high-tech product is adapted. This aspect may depend on many internal and external forces, and on organizational and individual characteristics.

Internal forces (or forces stemming from the person) contain, for instance, preferences, traditions, and expectations, which modify attention and judgement. As internal factors affecting the efficiency of the employees in financial organizations, we will consider personal innovativeness, ambitions, psychological barriers (personal factors), digital skills, and motivation in order to investigate preferences, norms and expectations, which modify adaptation strategies. The greater levels of digital skills allow employees complete tasks faster and generally feel more comfortable and confident with the advanced technologies. The practice of digital skills can finally promote the competitiveness of employees (Rubel et al., 2017). After all, motivation improves the performance of employees and leads to organizational goals (Ali et al., 2012).

External forces (or forces stemming from the environment) can be found in the context in which an individual acts and makes decisions, and these factors can alter individual perceptions. As external forces influencing the job performance of the employees in financial organizations, we will consider organizational support in order to examine how external conditions can alter individual perceptions. Lee et al. (2013) indicate that the lack of organizational support negatively influence on effective computer usage or utilization. Moreover, formal training enhances the job performance of employees by improving their skill set required to handle customer demands and reduce their turnover intentions (Sengupta et al., 2015).

Further, relying on several empirical studies, we will consider each factor and conceptualize a model that identifies the relevant forces that drive employee’s acceptance of disruptive technologies.

### 56.2.1 Internal forces

#### 56.2.1.1 Personal innovativeness

The concept (innovativeness) means that a person has a tendency to be the first to use the latest technologies. Personal innovativeness with technologies is related to the extension of positive perceptions about the advantage of decision support systems, greater computer ambitions, and convictions such as ease of use, compatibility, usefulness and intention to use new IT (Elie-Dit-Cosaque et al., 2011).

However, Walczuch et al. (2007) discovered that personal innovativeness is negatively related to perceived usefulness, using the empirical data from 810 employees of a multisite financial service provider. The authors explain that in the result obtained innovative persons are more critical towards technology since they expect all technology to fulfill the highest demands.

Meanwhile, Kamaludin and Purba (2015) conducted the study that aims to find out the technology readiness of the employees in using IT in their workplaces. The empirical data was collected from 250 employees at the bank GHB, located in South Jakarta. Analyses revealed that there is a strong willingness of the employees to be always ready in adopting the novel technology in banking institutions.

#### 56.2.1.2 Ambitions

The following construct (ambitions) implicates the judgment of one’s ability to use the advanced technology. Ambitions are an essential motivational variable, affecting effort persistence and motivation. In addition, being ambitious has a positive impact on intention to adopt new technologies (Nasri and Charfeddine, 2012).

It is important to note that the statement above is reflected in the previous empirical studies. Lee et al. (2013) applied the technology acceptance model to examine the employees’ attitudes and acceptance of e-learning systems in organizations and revealed that the computer’s ambitions influence the new technology adoption, IT ease of use perceptions, IT usage and behavioral intention to use IT. The authors showed that employees with high computer ambitions tended to have higher expectations to use computers to carry out their work activities.

#### 56.2.1.3 Psychological barriers

The following factor (psychological barriers) implies that an employee feels discomfort, anxiety, nervousness and a sense of being overwhelmed. Walczuch et al. (2007) revealed that discomfort has a negative effect on perceived ease of use and no impact on perceived usefulness. Insecurity has a negative effect on perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use. In this case, (insecurity) suggests that an employee does not trust the new technology for security and privacy reasons.

Furthermore, computer anxiety is negatively linked with IT adoption, IT use, end-user satisfaction, productivity and general individual performance in the workplace with computers. This fact may lead to computer avoidance (Elie-Dit-Cosaque et al., 2011). Thus, people who are anxious about computers are less likely than others to improve confidence in their ability to adopt the new technology that they use over time. In addition, anxiety reduces perceived computer ambitions and tends to jeopardize one’s self-esteem and confidence in performing a behavior. The authors also showed that increasing autonomy, offering organizational support, reducing work overload, and innovativeness with the new technology can together reduce computer anxiety.

Finally, Chen (2013) in his own study divided the sample into two behavioral segments (frequent/infrequent users) in accordance with usage frequency. The author revealed that frequent users are concerned more with psychological risk, as infrequent users emphasize financial, performance, time, and privacy risks.

#### 56.2.1.4 Digital skills

The following construct (skills) means how an employee can handle the new technology. Apart from introducing and maintaining the novel technology, employees must also develop personal skills to adopt the high-tech product effectively. The success of the new technology adoption depends mainly on users’ behavior, namely their readiness to accept, adopt, and use IT. Effective technology adaptation and practice of digital skills and special knowledge can finally promote competitiveness of employees. Furthermore, technology adaptation behaviors may influence job performance of employees and their level of job satisfaction (Rubel et al., 2017).

In addition, Elie-Dit-Cosaque et al. (2011) conducted a survey in France with IT users enrolled in professional training programs. Researchers came to the conclusion that people with greater experience of working with technologies often demonstrate greater levels of digital skills, which allow them to complete tasks better and generally feel more comfortable and confident with the advanced technologies.

#### 56.2.1.5 Motivation

The final internal force (motivation) indicates an employee’s confidence in using the new technology for the purpose of personal and organizational development. Research shows that motivated persons play an important role in the success of the organizations. Motivation is a driving force from an organizational point of view because it transforms human resources into action. It improves the efficiency level of the employees and leads to perform organizational goals (Ali et al., 2012).

Besides, Strizhova (2017) using multidimensional scaling obtained empirical scales of the motivational space of work activity of employees, focused on career development: “Personal Priority”, “Social importance” and “Perceptivity”. The results of the research scale are actual factors of motivation for participants of the personnel reserve in the financial organizations.
