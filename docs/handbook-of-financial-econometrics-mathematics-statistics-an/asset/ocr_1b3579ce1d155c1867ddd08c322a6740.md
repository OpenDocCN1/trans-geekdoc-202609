In addition, the government created the Securities and Futures Investors Protection Center along with a guarantee fund, and in addition established a system for trading in Taiwanese government bonds. The seventh pillar of Financial Reform was the enactment of new laws that related to asset securitization — both financial assets and real estate. This allowed Taiwanese banks to finally securitize and sell their loans, and in turn increased substantially the amount of domestic bank lending. The eight pillar of Financial Reform was the establishment of risk-based capital requirements for insurance companies and many other financial institutions. The intention of this was to help stabilize and ensure the solvency of Taiwanese financial institutions. The ninth and final pillar of Financial Reform was an overhaul of banking and finance laws to permit more stringent and harsher penalties for perpetrators of financial crimes. As a result of these measures, the Taiwanese banking system was substantially different pre-reform relative to post-reform. It is of interest to examine if and how the impact of foreign banking relationships on firm performance might have been affected by the sweeping reforms.

# 25.3 Data

The data employed are drawn from three separate sources: the *Taiwan Economic Journal* (TEJ) financial reports database, the TEJ lending information database, and the TEJ ownership structure database. Our sample covers a 15 year period from 1991 to 2005. The observations are comprised exclusively of non-financial firms listed on the Taiwan Stock Exchange (TSE). We exclude financial firms because their asset and liability structures differ substantially from those of the non-financial firms. We also eliminate those firms with obvious errors in their financial reports, such as firms with negative debt or negative sales. We also exclude firms with missing data in relation to short-term and long-term debt or other required variables during our sample period. The final total is 8,877 effective year-firm observations from a potential starting figure of 9,690 observations.

For each firm in the sample, the TEJ provides an income statement, balance sheet and information pertaining to borrowing and lending as of the end of each fiscal year.
