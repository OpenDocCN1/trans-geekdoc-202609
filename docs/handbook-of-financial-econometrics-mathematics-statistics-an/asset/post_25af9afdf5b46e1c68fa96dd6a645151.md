## 7.6 The Effectiveness of the Bounds

In order to test the effectiveness of our bounds, we compare in this section, our bounds with the option prices of Cox and Ross (1976) and Schroder (1989) in the general Constant Elasticity Volatility (CEV) model. Lo (1987) argues that bounds of option prices exist in general using the bounds of the expected payoffs and the Risk Neutral Valuation Relationship (RNVR) and points out the empirical difficulty in using the RNVR to obtain bounds of option prices. In this section, we use the general CEV model to obtain the bounds of the risk-neutral distribution, then discount these bounds at the risk-neutral rate of return and obtain the bounds for option prices.

The use of the RNVR is problematic in the two-moment case as Lo points out, because it requires the same information to obtain the two-moment-bounds when the Black–Scholes prices can also be obtained, thus the two-moment-bounds are not very useful under the RNVR. However, in the four-moment case, the use of the RNVR provides valid bounds as results of the first four moments whereas neither the Black–Scholes model nor the Cox–Ross model considers skewness and Kurtosis explicitly. In other words, when the first four moments are considered explicitly, whereas the Black–Scholes and the Cox–Ross models fail to hold, these bounds are still valid even under RNVR. In the general CEV model, the stock price is assumed to be governed by the diffusion process6
