where, now

$$c_1=\frac{e^{-\frac{T}{2}\sigma^2(\beta-1/2)^2}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,2^{\frac{T+3}{4}}\left(\frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{T-1}\right)^{\frac{T-1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{T-1}{2}\right)}.$$

The MVU estimator of $\beta$, denoted $\hat{\beta}$ is

$$\hat{\beta}=\frac{T-3}{T-1}\frac{\bar{Y}}{S^2}+\frac{1}{2}. \tag{65.12}$$

The fact that $\hat{\beta}$ is the MVU estimator is straightforward to prove. It is well known that $\bar{Y}$ and $S^2$ are complete, sufficient statistic for $\mu$ and $\sigma$, and therefore by the Rao–Blackwell theorem, an unbiased estimator of $\beta$ that is a function of $\bar{Y}$ and $S^2$ is MVU estimator of $\beta$ (Hogg and Craig, 1978). It is also well known that the expected value of $\bar{Y}$ is $\mu$, and the expected value of $1/S^2$ is $(1/\sigma^2)[(T-1)/(T-3)]$, and therefore, the expected value of $[(T-3)/(T-1)](\bar{Y}/S^2)$ is $\mu/\sigma^2=\beta-1/2$, from which (65.12) follows.

The mean of $\hat{\beta}$ is $\beta$ and the variance of $\hat{\beta}$ is

$$\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\beta})=\frac{T-3}{(T-5)\sigma^2}\left(\frac{1}{T}+\frac{2\mu^2}{(T-3)\sigma^2}\right), \tag{65.13}$$

which is always less than the variance of the biased estimator $\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{ML}}$.

The exact sampling distribution of $\hat{\beta}$ is obtained from (65.10) or (65.11) by observing that $\hat{\beta}=[(T-3)/(T-1)]U+1/2$ is a linear transform of the statistic $U$. Thus, the exact sampling distribution of $\hat{\beta}$, in terms of the three parameters $T$, $\sigma$ and $\beta$ is the pdf, say

$$\begin{aligned} f(\hat{\beta})={}&\frac{c_2}{v}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\Gamma\left(\frac{2r+T+1}{4}\right)\\ &\times\frac{1}{r!}\left(\frac{2T\sigma^2(\beta-1/2)(\hat{\beta}-1/2)\left(\frac{T-1}{T-3}\right)-(T-1)}{\sqrt{2T}\sigma(\hat{\beta}-1/2)\left(\frac{T-1}{T-3}\right)}\right)^r,\\ &-\infty<\hat{\beta}<\infty,\ \sigma>0,\ T>3,\ -\infty<\beta<\infty \tag{65.14} \end{aligned}$$
