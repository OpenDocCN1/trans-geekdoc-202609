Gârleanu et al. (2009) modeled demand–pressure effects on option prices. The crux of their study is competitive pricing of options for risk-averse dealers when risk is imperfect hedgeable. In Gârleanu et al. (2009), dealers can arbitrarily trade option contracts at discrete time. The agents that are covered in this study are end-users. According to them, the demand–pressure should entice option prices and should lead to optimal hedges having positive payoff. They estimate that larger dealer positions should lead to profits and/or losses varying between $100 million and –$100 million with a cumulative amount approximately $800 million over a six-year sample. The model for demand pressure is discrete-time infinite-horizon economy. Prices of securities are endogenously determined. Moreover, Gârleanu et al. (2009) assumed that economy is populated by two agents: dealers and end-users. Firstly, they have constant absolute utility function and their “linear” wealth evolved. Further, they assumed that prices are underpinned by competitive Markov process.

The first lemma in Gârleanu et al. (2009) assumed that prices are determined at equilibrium level. Followed by two propositions; proposition one is one demand pressure for end-users and proposition two is when demand is zero, given a certain price. The theoretical results on effects of demand pressure illustrate that demand affects option prices. On a hedge position, based on theorem one, they found that hedge position has an optimal level with a certain expectation. The pricing kernel is “unheageable”.
