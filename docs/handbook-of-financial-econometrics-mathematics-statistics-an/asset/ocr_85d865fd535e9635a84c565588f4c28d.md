Instead, the positive relationships between gold and stocks returns are often observed especially in lower frequencies since the early 2000s. These findings call into question the role played by gold in a diversified portfolio of stocks. On the other side, the coherency between returns of gold and bond shows that the relation of gold and bond exhibits different behaviors both in the short-term and long-term relationships since the early 2000s. A significant positive coherency is observed in the higher frequency, indicating the existence of a positive relationship between gold and bond in the short-term. The difference in long-term and short-term relationships has an important implication in portfolio management. We can conclude that the investor’s decision horizon plays a crucial role in determining whether it includes gold in a diversified portfolio of bonds. In sum, gold has been an efficient diversifier in investment portfolio because of its low correlation between gold and other financial assets, which is an important feature in an era of globalization in which correlations increased dramatically among most financial asset types. However, the financialization of gold like other commodities has led to increased correlation between gold and other financial assets since the early 2000s. Moreover, the relation of gold and stock as well as that of gold and bonds exhibits different behaviors both in the short-term and long-term relationships. These findings have important implications for portfolio managers in determining whether gold is included in a diversified portfolio.

Finally, we investigate the role play by gold in a diversified portfolio. The optimal portfolio weights of gold are obtained from minimizing the global variance of portfolio at time–frequency scales. By this setting, for a given frequency, we can trace the time variability of the allocation weightings of gold. Our empirical findings show little evidence that gold has been an efficient diversifier in traditional stock and bond portfolio whether measured by minimizing portfolio variance in the short term or the long term. The gold acts as an attractive alternative to assets investment does not seem to be supported by the data. The results suggest that the diversification benefits of gold are unreliable. As expected, our results show that, both over the short-term and long-term, the optimal allocation weightings of gold in a diversified portfolio comprised of bonds and stocks have been declined. Only exception is during the times of crisis periods, the relatively lower volatility in gold compared to stocks and bonds increases the attractiveness of gold relative to bond and stock. This finding supports that the main contribution of gold is during the times of crisis periods.
