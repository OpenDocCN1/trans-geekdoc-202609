First, the outer model loadings are assessed. The algorithm needs only six iterations instead of the possible 300 ones that demonstrate the high quality of our model (Wong, 2013). All the coefficients are above 0.4 and most of them are above 0.7. This indicates the acceptable level of reliability (Hair et al., 2014). Moreover, all the loadings are significant at 0.01 level. A suitable level of internal consistency is also reached because all the composite reliability coefficients are between 0.7 and 0.9. In addition, we examine the convergent validity with the average variance extracted (AVE) coefficients which are higher than 0.5. We also use the Fornell–Larcker criteria to check the discriminant validity. All the results are summarized in Appendix 71B.

Next, we analyze the structural model and test the hypotheses. We check VIF-coefficients and find that there is no multicollinearity problem in the model. Using bootstrapping, we verify the significance of the structural coefficients. The results support six hypotheses (structural model loadings are significant to least at 5%), while three hypotheses are not accepted. We present these results in Table 71.2 and Figure 71.2.

We find three factors out of five that are supposed to have a direct impact on the intention to use mobile banking that are statistically significant. Thus, the most significant factor (0.287) in our model is perceived usefulness, which is comparable and corresponds to empirical studies (Luo et al., 2010; Kim et al., 2009; Wang et al., 2006; Zhou, 2011) particularly for the Russian case
