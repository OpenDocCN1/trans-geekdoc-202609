# 38.5 Conclusion

The major contributions of this chapter are threefold. First, we comprehensively summarize the typical features of the dependence structure between various types of bank risks. Second, we thoroughly analyze the major risk integration approaches’ capabilities of capturing these typical features. Third, we propose a mixture copula framework to capture the overall typical features of dependence structure between bank risks. In the empirical analysis, the constructed mixture copulas are applied to describe the dependence structure between credit, market and operational risks of Chinese banks and integrate these risks into total risk. The results of mixture copulas are compared with seven other major risk integration approaches to verify its effectiveness.

The empirical results provide strong evidence of the effectiveness of the constructed mixture copulas. Compared with other major approaches, the three mixture copulas perform the best in goodness-of-fit test, in particular the t-Gumbel copula, which demonstrates that the mixture of proper copulas can better model the dependence structure between risks. Moreover, the empirical results also help to uncover some important pitfalls and misunderstandings in risk dependence modeling. The copula approach is highly popular at present and is widely recognized as the leading risk integration approach, particularly the t copula. However, in this empirical analysis, none of the five popular individual copulas pass the goodness-of-fit test, indicating that the copula approach should not remain unchallenged in bank risk integration.
