Thereafter, they used econometric analysis, and the Granger causality test confirmed positive correlation between S&P500 and TA100 indices. Central to the econometric analysis, was the question “are all terror attacks alike?” for the September 27, 2000, the Chow test confirmed structural breaks which can be interpreted “the general effect of the terror attacks on the stock market”. Nikkinen and Vähämäa (2010) explored the impact of terrorism on stock market sentiments. Unlike prior studies, they focused on the behavior of implied option prices based on utility probability density functions. The latter principle will be adopted in hedging strategies in this chapter. To assess the impact of terrorism, Nikkinen and Vähämäa (2010) used implied density functions on the FTSE100 index around September 11, 2001 attacks in New York and Washington D.C., the March 11, 2004 attacks in Madrid and the July 7, 2005 attacks in London. The period of the data is from January 4, 2000 to December 30, 2005. The data covers 1,535 trading days. In order to reduce trading noise, firstly, the excluded options with less than five trading days, secondly, used only at-the-money (ATM) and OTM options as in-the-money (ITM) options have the lowest liquidity. Just like Scott (1997), Nikkinen and Vähämäa (2010) agree that “noise” cannot be hedged.

For modeling, they used a discounted risk-neutral probability density function. Given that the equation in the latter statement is discrete, they transformed it into a continuous form by using a cubic spline. Moreover, the cubic spline is able to handle minimization problem. In presenting their results; firstly, they present basic descriptive statistics which illustrate that terrorist attacks are not remotely executed. Broadly, the implied FTSE option prices are negatively skewed with fat tails. The volatilities for those implied options soared. The probabilities were large and sharper for the key dates. The changes for the key dates (i.e., September 11, 2001, March 11, 2004 and July 7, 2005) were all negative and negatively skewed.
