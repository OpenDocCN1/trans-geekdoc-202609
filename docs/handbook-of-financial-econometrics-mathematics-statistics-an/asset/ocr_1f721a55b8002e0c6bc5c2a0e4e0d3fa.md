# 28.11 Random Effects (RE) Models

The efficiency of the FE estimator can be improved upon by applying the RE estimator if the **X** and $v_i$ are independent (recall what is required with the FE model is that the $\varepsilon_{it}$ are independent of **X** and $v_i$). RE also allows the possibility of estimating parameters associated with variables that are fixed across the cross-section (these variables are perfectly correlated with the fixed effects so their parameters can’t be estimated with FE).

RE is not consistent if the random effect is correlated with the **X** variables. For example, consider a panel of observations of individual workers, an observed variable like compensation may be correlated with an unobserved (to the econometrician) variable such as effort or ability, which implicitly enters the individual specific error $v_i$.

If **X** and $v_i$ are independent, then

$$E(e_{it} | \mathbf{X}_{it}) = E(v_i + \varepsilon_{it} | \mathbf{X}_{it}) = 0, \tag{28.26}$$

since both $v_i$ and $\varepsilon_{it}$ are independent of **X**. Note that (28.26) is the condition that implies that the pooled OLS model, (28.22), provides unbiased parameter estimates.

However, OLS estimation of (28.22), the pooled regression model, is not efficient because the $e_{it}$ are not iid. We can compute the covariance matrix of the $e_{it}$ if the $v_i$ are iid $(0, \sigma_v^2)$, hence the term “random effects.” From the error components, $e_{it} = u_t + v_i + \varepsilon_{it}$, assuming shocks are independent for simplicity and $u_t = 0$, it follows that

$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Var}(e_{it}) &= \sigma_v^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2 \\
\text{Cov}(e_{it}e_{jt}) &= \sigma_v^2 \forall t \text{ and } i \neq j, \\
\text{Cov}(e_{it}e_{js}) &= 0 \forall i,j \text{ and } t \neq s,
\end{aligned}\tag{28.27}$$

which define the elements of the $NT \times NT$ covariance matrix, $\Omega$, necessary for GLS estimation. This assumption allows heteroskedasticity and contemporaneous correlation between the errors across the cross-sections, but rules out lagged correlations between the errors (no autocorrelation). If the data are ordered by c-s units in $N$ blocks of $T$ observations each, this matrix has the form

$$\Omega = \begin{bmatrix}
\sum_1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\
0 & \sum_2 & \ddots & \vdots \\
\vdots & \ddots & \ddots & 0 \\
0 & \cdots & 0 & \sum_N
\end{bmatrix}, \tag{28.28}$$
