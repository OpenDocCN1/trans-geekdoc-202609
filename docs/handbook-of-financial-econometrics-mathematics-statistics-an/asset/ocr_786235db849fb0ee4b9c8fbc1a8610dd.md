Shanken (1987) derived a Bayes factor $B_{10}$ based on the sufficient statistics $V_u$ and the prior distribution $\pi(\bullet)$ as

$B_{10} = (1 + Tc)^{-N/2} \times \left[ \frac{1 + N (T - N - 1)^{-1} V_u}{1 + N (T - N - 1)^{-1} (1 + Tc)^{-1} V_u} \right]^{(T-1)/2}.$ (93.6)

The derivation of the Bayes factor $B_{10}$ is given in Appendix 93C.

Later, a more complicated Bayesian test based on the posterior-odds ratio is proposed by Harvey and Zhou (1990) by imposing a joint prior distribution for all the parameters in the time-series regression models (93.1), namely, the $N$ intercepts $\alpha = (\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_N)$, the betas $\beta = (\beta_1, \dots, \beta_N)$, and the non-singular covariance matrix $\Sigma$. However, the difficulties in implementing the test lie in the evaluation of the posterior distribution involving high-dimensional integration that is analytically intractable.

Instead of assuming the *ex post* Sharpe ratio $SR$ equals the *ex ante* Sharpe ratio $SR$ without the sampling error as in Shanken (1987), a Bayesian test by Kandel, McCulloch, and Stambaugh (1995) is based on the posterior distribution of the ratio $\rho = SR / SR^*$ using Monte Carlo method by drawing the non-centrality parameter $\lambda$ and the *ex ante* Sharpe $SR$ from their marginal posterior distributions independently. By imposing a diffuse prior distribution on the means and the covariance matrix of the excess returns $(R_t, r_t)$, the posterior distribution of the *ex ante* Sharpe ratio $SR$ is derived as the distribution of the sum of the two independent random variables $Z$ and $\chi_{T-N}$:

$(\hat{SR} \times \chi_{T-N} + Z) / \sqrt{T},$ (93.7)

where $Z$ is a standard normal variate, $\chi_{T-N}$ is the squared root of a central chi-square variate with $T - N$ degrees of freedom. Unfortunately, the posterior distribution (93.7) is derived based on the two postulates regarding the population mean $\mu_R$ and variance $\sigma_R^2$ of portfolio $p$'s excess returns $R_1, \dots, R_T$: *(i)* the posterior distribution of $\sigma_R^2$ is proportional to the inverse of a central chi-square $\chi_{T-N}^2$ distribution with $T - N$ degrees of freedom; *(ii)* the posterior distribution of the $\mu_R$ and $\sigma_R^2$ are independent.

The above two postulates contradict with the common Bayesian analysis. In addition, the sampling errors associated with the *ex post* Sharpe ratio $\hat{SR}$ is not considered. In the following section, a Bayesian test of the null $H_0$: $\rho =1$ versus the alternative $H_1$: $\rho < 1$, which is based on the Bayes factor that considers the measurement errors associated with the *ex post* Sharpe ratio $\hat{SR}$, is given.
