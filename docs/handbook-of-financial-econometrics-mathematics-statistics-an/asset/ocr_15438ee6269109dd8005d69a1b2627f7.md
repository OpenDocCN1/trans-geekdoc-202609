This study contributes to the literature in several aspects. Firstly, the current study provides new evidence to determine the quality of financial advisors on the influence of deal outcomes in M&As. Secondly, the analysis takes into account the potential self-selection bias by controlling for inverse mill's ratio. This allows the current study to explore the relationship between announcement returns and the role of financial advisors precisely. More importantly, this study examines domestic and foreign advisors on the influence of deal outcomes in M&As. The empirical evidence indicates that domestic advisors can outperform their foreign counterparts leading to higher announcement returns to targets and bidders. This provides additional insights to reveal the importance of domestic and foreign advisors on M&As. Hence, the empirical evidence in this study enhances our understanding and knowledge to reveal the importance of the role of financial advisors on the influence of deal outcomes in M&As.

# 76.2 Literature Review

A number of prior studies have examined the performance of financial advisors in M&As (e.g., McLaughlin, 1992; Servaes and Zenner, 1996; Hunter and Jagtiani, 2003; Schiereck et al., 2009; Wang and Whyte, 2010; Golubov et al., 2012; Ma, 2013; Song et al., 2013). However, prior studies report inconclusive results and none of prior studies takes into account domestic and foreign advisors to examine the impact of deal outcomes in M&As. Servaes and Zenner (1996) argue that bidders are more likely to hire financial advisors when the transactions are more complex. Song et al. (2013) report that bidders are more likely to hire boutique advisors in hostile deals as hostile deals are more difficult to complete.

(2008) report that firms hired by more prestigious financial advisors can complete the deals faster as these advisors have more experience and expertise to advise the transactions.
