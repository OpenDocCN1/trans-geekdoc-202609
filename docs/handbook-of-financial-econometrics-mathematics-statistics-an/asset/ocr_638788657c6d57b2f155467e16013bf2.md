When only community banks and credit unions compete in the same market the following patterns occur. The cost efficiency of both institutions decreased when more credit unions are present. The cost efficiency from the presence of more community banks is unchanged for credit unions but increased for community banks. The probability of a large bank entering in the market has a negative effect on the efficiency of community banks and credit unions. The findings again indicate mixed results of efficiency by population size. Higher county level population is associated with greater cost efficiency for community banks and credit unions. Only community banks are affected by the population growth rate, becoming less cost efficient as the growth rate increases. Both types of financial institutions become more cost efficient as assets increase. As the loan-to-asset ratio increases community banks become more cost efficient.

# 131.6 Concluding Remarks

Recent legislation is associated with substantial transformation of the community banking industry during recent decades. Product offerings and risk management behavior have changed. The competitive balance between different sizes of institutions has also changed, leading to an evolution in industry structure. This chapter provides an overview of these changes and models used to analyze them in the context of changes in bank production costs and efficiency.

The Sarbanes-Oxley Act and Dodd-Frank Act were passed with the intent to facilitate transparency in financial transactions. Compliance with the DFA impacted the cost efficiency of financial institutions, with the magnitude depending on the size of the institution. Compliance costs have an asymmetric effect on institutions of different sizes. Since the smallest institutions may be unable to afford the additional overhead costs of compliance, some financial institutions choose not to enter markets requiring additional compliance expense.

As the competitive landscape in lending becomes more structured financial institution managers in this market segment must adjust to the set of informational and cost realities. Firms that operate in this market space have to maintain regulatory currency as well as technology to provide sustained products for consumers. An added burden is then placed upon the lender to be aware of legislative changes to maintain and attract quality borrowers.
