Focusing on key technological advantages, most of the organizations introduce developments for facilitated job tasks, improved communication. A successful technology adoption contributes to the higher organizational performance (Zaheer et al., 2010). At the same time, the technological development influences labor productivity. Imran et al. (2014) revealed that technological advancement has significant impact on motivation and training of employees in the banking sector. Motivation significantly affects employee productivity but training has no substantial impact on it. Sengupta et al. (2015) concluded that organizational resources, such as training and technology have an impact on labor productivity through the perception of person–job fit. Meanwhile, Rubel et al. (2017) argued that effective technology adaptation and practice of digital skills and special knowledge can finally promote employee’s competitiveness. The technology adaptation behaviors may influence employee performance and their level of job satisfaction.

Accordingly, labor productivity plays an important role in business success (Kaufman, 2015). Human resource management system is an indirect driver of company performance and prospers or fails to the extent that they provide employees with the most efficient human capital and motivate them to ensure peak pro-performance behaviors. Employee skills, education and physical–mental ability concern to the productivity attributes of human capital. Motivation relates to the act of stimulating employees to maximally use their ability to enhance firm performance. Firms which gain more ability-motivation-opportunity from employees will reach higher performance through consequences such as productivity, creativity, and discretionary effort (Rauch and Hatak, 2016).

At the moment, as much of the operations of employees in organizations are supported by financial technologies, the labor productivity and the quality of service often depend on how the new technology is adopted. The process of technology adaptation may depend on many factors, in particular on internal factors such as the personal qualities of the employee (Walcuch et al., 2007), and external factors like work environment (Elie-Dit-Cosaque et al., 2011), which should be taken into account in the study of technology adoption.

Internal and external factors affecting the acceptance of the high-tech product have been practically studied in many empirical studies but have not considered concerning to employees of financial institutions in general and from the point of view of the list of advanced technologies which we focused on.
