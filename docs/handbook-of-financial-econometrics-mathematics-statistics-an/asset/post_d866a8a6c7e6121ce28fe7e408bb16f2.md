
## 82.7 Economic Rationale of the Treynor Performance-Measure Method

Cheung and Kwan (1988) have derived an alternative simple rule for optimal portfolio selection in terms of the single-index model. First, Cheung and Kwan relate \(C^*\) as defined in equation (82.13) to the correlation coefficient between the portfolio rates of return with \(i\) securities \(R_i\) and market rates of return \(R_m\) (\(\rho_i\)):

\[
\rho_i=\frac{C^*}{\sigma_m\Theta_i}, \tag{82.19}
\]

where \(\rho_i=\sigma_{im}/\sigma_i\sigma_m\), \(\sigma_{im}\) is the covariance between \(R_i\) and \(R_m\), \(\Theta_i=(\overline{R_i}-R_f)/\sigma_i\), the Sharpe performance measure associated with the \(i\)th portfolio, and \(\sigma_i\) and \(\sigma_m\) are the standard deviations for the \(i\)th portfolio and the market portfolio, respectively.

Cheung and Kwan show that \(\rho_i\) and \(C_i^*\) display the same functional behavior for optimal portfolio selection. In other words, if portfolios are formed by adding securities successively from the highest rank to the lowest rank, the optimal portfolio is reached when the correlation of the portfolio returns and the index is at its maximum. Since the expected return on the index must be positive if the investor is to invest in stocks, an objective of the investors using the single-index model to establish the risk–return trade-off is to pick securities that benefit the most from a market upswing. Hence, the role of the index in the selection of securities for an optimum portfolio is demonstrated explicitly.

Based upon the single-index model and risk decomposition, the following relationships can be defined:

- 1. \(\rho_i=\dfrac{\sigma_{im}}{\sigma_i\sigma_m}\),
- 2. \(\sigma_{im}=\beta_i\sigma_m^2\),
- 3. \(\sigma_i^2=\beta_i^2\sigma_m^2+\sigma_{\epsilon i}^2\). \tag{82.20}

From equation (82.20), Cheung and Kwan define \(\rho_i\) in terms of \(\beta_i^2\), \(\sigma_m^2\), and \(\sigma_i\).

\[
\rho_i=\sqrt{\beta_i^2\sigma_m^2/\sigma_i^2}=\sqrt{1-\frac{\sigma_{\epsilon i}^2}{\sigma_i^2}}
\]

in which \(\sigma_{\epsilon i}^2\) is the non-systematic risk for the \(i\)th portfolio. They use both \(\rho_i\) and \(\Theta_i\) to select securities for an optimum portfolio, and they conclude that

## Appendix 82A: Derivation of Equation (82.6a)

\[
\begin{aligned}
&(\bar{R}_i-R_f)\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_i^2\sigma_i^2+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\substack{j=1\\j\ne i}}^{n}W_iW_j\sigma_{ij}\right]^{-1/2}\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_i(\bar{R}_i-R_f)\right]\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_i^2\sigma_i^2+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\substack{j=1\\j\ne i}}^{n}W_iW_j\sigma_{ij}\right]^{-3/2}\\
&\quad\times\left[2W_i\sigma_i^2+2\sum_{\substack{j=1\\j\ne i}}^{n}W_j\sigma_{ij}\right]=0 \quad (i=1,2,\dots,n). \tag{82A.1}
\end{aligned}
\]

Multiplying equation (82A.1) by \(\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_i^2\sigma_i^2+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\substack{j=1\\j\ne i}}^{n}W_iW_j\sigma_{ij}\right]^{1/2}\) and rearranging yields

\[
\bar{R}_i-R_f=\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_i(\bar{R}_i-R_f)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_i^2\sigma_i^2+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\substack{j=1\\j\ne i}}^{n}W_iW_j\sigma_{ij}}\right)\left(W_i\sigma_i^2+\sum_{\substack{j=1\\j\ne i}}^{n}W_j\sigma_{ij}\right). \tag{82A.2}
\]

Defining

\[
k=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_i(\bar{R}_i-R_f)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_i^2\sigma_i^2+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\substack{j=1\\j\ne i}}^{n}W_iW_j\sigma_{ij}},
\]

yields

\[
-k\left(W_i\sigma_i^2+\sum_{\substack{j=1\\j\ne i}}^{n}W_j\sigma_{ij}\right)+(\bar{R}_i-R_f)=0.
\]

Therefore,

\[
\frac{dL}{dW_i}=-\left(kW_1\sigma_{1i}+kW_2\sigma_{2i}+\cdots+kW_i\sigma_i^2+\cdots+kW_n\sigma_{ni}\right)+(\bar{R}_i-R_f)=0.
\]

Define \(H_i=kW_i\), where \(W_i\) are the fractions to invest in each security and \(H_i\) are proportional to this fraction. Substituting \(H_i\) for \(kW_i\),

\[
\bar{R}_i-R_f=H_1\sigma_{1i}+H_2\sigma_{2i}+\cdots+H_i\sigma_i^2+\cdots+H_n\sigma_{ni}.
\]

There is one equation like this for each value of \(i\):

\[
\bar{R}_1-R_f=H_1\sigma_1^2+H_2\sigma_{12}+\cdots+H_n\sigma_{1n},
\]

\[
\bar{R}_2-R_f=H_1\sigma_{21}+H_2\sigma_2^2+\cdots+H_n\sigma_{2n},
\]

\[
\vdots
\]

\[
\bar{R}_n-R_f=H_1\sigma_{n1}+H_2\sigma_{n2}+\cdots+H_n\sigma_n^2.
\]

This is equation (82.6a).

## Appendix 82B: Derivation of Equations (82.10)

Following the optimization procedure for deriving equation (82A.2) in Appendix 82A,

\[
\frac{dL}{dW_i}=(\bar{R}_i-R_f)-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_i(\bar{R}_i-R_f)}{\sigma_p^2}\left(W_i\beta_i^2\sigma_m^2+\beta_i\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_j\beta_j\sigma_m^2+W_i\sigma_{\epsilon_i}^2\right)=0,\qquad j\ne i.
\]

Let \(H_i=[(\bar{R}_p-R_f)/\sigma_p^2]W_i\) and, solving for any \(H_i\),

\[
H_i=\frac{\bar{R}_i-R_f}{\sigma_{\epsilon_i}^2}-\frac{\beta_i\sigma_m^2\sum_{j=1}^{n}H_j\beta_j}{\sigma_{\epsilon_i}^2}. \tag{82B.1}
\]

Multiplying both sides of the equation by \(\beta_i\),

\[
H_i\beta_i=\frac{(\bar{R}_i-R_f)\beta_i}{\sigma_{\epsilon_i}^2}-\frac{\beta_i^2\sigma_m^2\sum_{j=1}^{n}H_j\beta_j}{\sigma_{\epsilon_i}^2}. \tag{82B.2}
\]

Adding together the \(n\) equations of this form yields,

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{n}H_j\beta_j=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(\bar{R}_j-R_f)\beta_j}{\sigma_{\epsilon_j}^2}}{\displaystyle 1+\sigma_m^2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\beta_j^2}{\sigma_{\epsilon_j}^2}}. \tag{82B.3}
\]

By substituting equation (82B.3) into equation (82B.1), we can obtain (82.10):

\[
H_i=\frac{\bar{R}_i-R_f}{\sigma_{\epsilon_i}^2}-\left(\frac{\displaystyle\sigma_m^2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(\bar{R}_j-R_f)\beta_j}{\sigma_{\epsilon_j}^2}}{\displaystyle 1+\sigma_m^2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\beta_j^2}{\sigma_{\epsilon_j}^2}}\right)\frac{\beta_i}{\sigma_{\epsilon_i}^2}.
\]

## Appendix 82C: Derivation of Equation (82.15)

This appendix discusses the use of performance measure to examine the optimal portfolio with short sales not allowed. Therefore, equation (82B.1) from Appendix 82B must be modified:

\[
H_i = \frac{\bar{R}_i - R_f}{\sigma_{\varepsilon i}^2} - \frac{\beta_i \sigma_m^2}{\sigma_{\varepsilon i}^2} \sum_{j=1}^{n} \beta_j H_j + \mu_i, \tag{82C.1}
\]

where \(H_i \ge 0\), \(\mu_i \ge 0\), and \(\mu_i H_i = 0\) for all \(i\).

The justification of this equation can be found in Elton et al. (1976). Assuming all stocks that would be in an optimal portfolio (called \(d\)) can be found, and then arranging these stocks as \(i = 1,2,\dots,d\), for the subpopulation of stocks that make up the optimal portfolio:

\[
H_i = \frac{\bar{R}_i - R_f}{\sigma_{\varepsilon i}^2} - \frac{\beta_i \sigma_m^2}{\sigma_{\varepsilon i}^2} \sum_{j=1}^{d} H_j \beta_j \quad \text{and} \quad \mu_i = 0. \tag{82C.2}
\]

Multiplying both sides by \(\beta_i\), summing over all stocks in \(d\), and rearranging yields

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{d} H_j \beta_j = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{d} \left( \frac{\bar{R}_j - R_f}{\sigma_{\varepsilon j}^2} \right) \beta_j}{1 + \sigma_m^2 \sum_{j=1}^{d} \frac{\beta_j^2}{\sigma_{\varepsilon j}^2}}. \tag{82C.3}
\]

Note since the set \(d\) contains all stocks with positive \(H_i\),

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{n} H_j \beta_j = \sum_{j=1}^{d} H_j \beta_j,
\]

and let

\[
C^* = \sigma_m^2 \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{d} \frac{\bar{R}_j - R_f}{\sigma_{\varepsilon j}^2} \beta_j}{1 + \sigma_m^2 \sum_{j=1}^{d} \frac{\beta_j^2}{\sigma_{\varepsilon j}^2}}. \tag{82C.4}
\]

Using (82C.4), the following equation for \(H_i\) is obtained after substitution and rearranging from equation (82C.1):

\[
H_i = \frac{\beta_i}{\sigma_{\varepsilon i}^2} \left( \frac{\bar{R}_i - R_f}{\beta_i} - C^* \right) + \mu_i. \tag{82C.5}
\]

Since \(\mu_i \ge 0\), the inclusion of \(\mu_i\) can only increase the value of \(H_i\). Therefore, if \(H_i\) is positive, \(\mu_i = 0\) can never make it zero, and the security should be included. If \(H_i < 0\) when \(\mu_i = 0\), positive values of \(\mu_i\) can increase \(H_i\). However, because the product of \(\mu_i\) and \(H_i\) must equal zero, as indicated in equation (82C.1), positive values of \(\mu_i\) imply \(H_i = 0\). Therefore, any security with \(H_i < 0\) when \(\mu_i = 0\) must be rejected. Equation (82.15) in the text can therefore be used to estimate the optimal weight of a portfolio.

## Appendix 82D: Quadratic Programming and Kuhn–Tucker Conditions

The quadratic programming algorithm is based on a technique from advanced calculus called the Kuhn–Tucker conditions. This technique can solve the optimal portfolio with short sales not allowed. The problem can be stated as

\[
\max_{\{W_i,\,i=1,2,\dots,n\}} F(W) = \frac{\overline{R_p} - R_f}{\sigma_p},
\]

subject to

\[
\sum_{i=1}^{n} W_i = 1,
\]

\[
W_i \ge 0, \quad i = 1,2,\dots,n,
\]

where

\[
\overline{R_p} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} W_i \bar{R_i},
\]

\[
\sigma_p = \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{n} W_i^2 \sigma_i^2 + \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} W_i W_j \sigma_{ij} \right]^{1/2}.
\]
