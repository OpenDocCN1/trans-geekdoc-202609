Compared to the GARJI model, the VG NGARCH model is more informative parsimoniously. In addition, an *ex ante* probability for the occurrences of large price movements can be obtained. As the GARJI model can provide only an *ex post* filter for the probability of large price movements, one can expect the VG NGARCH model is more informative than the GARJI model.

The performance of the proposed VG NGARCH model with the benchmark GARJI model is compared using the equity returns of five financial companies in S&P 500. By introducing a random business time to the GARCH framework, an extra complication in model calibration and parameter estimation is added on. A Monte Carlo method, namely, the Monte Carlo EM algorithm, is used together with the Metropolis algorithm for parameter estimation in the VG NGARCH models. This method provides maximum likelihood estimates of the model parameters via the iterative sampling of the unknown random business times. The remainder of this chapter is organized as follows. Section 70.2 specifies the two GARCH models under consideration, namely, the VG NGARCH model and the GARJI model. Section 70.3 outlines the Monte Carlo EM algorithm and the Metropolis algorithm. Empirical analysis and the performance of the two types of GARCH models are then presented in Section 70.4. Finally, Section 70.5 presents conclusions.

# 70.2 GARCH-Jump Versus Variance-Gamma Model

The most significant influence on financial markets may be the news arrival (e.g., see Clark, 1973; Rose, 1989; Andersen, 1996; Jones *et al.*, 1994). According to Andersen (1996), two or more types of news arrival processes that have different implications for asset returns exist. In Maheu and McCurdy (2004), it is postulated that there are two types of news events, namely, normal and extreme news events. Usually, normal news events cause smooth changes in price movements; while extreme news events, such as earnings surprises, crashes, terrorist attacks, etc., cause infrequent, unanticipated and large price movements. To account for both types of price movements, a GARCH-jump model that combines a GARCH-type model with a Poisson jump process for the dynamics of log-returns was proposed (Jorion, 1988).
