$r_t = \delta_o + \delta_1^T x_t.$
(61.8)

In our estimation, we fix $\delta_o$ to be the unconditional mean of the three-month Treasury-bill rate. As regards to $\delta_1$, we obtain the coefficients $\delta_{11}$ corresponding to the macro factors from linear regressions of the three-month rate on the macro factors and leave $\delta_{12}$ corresponding to the latent factors to be estimated in the maximum likelihood estimation procedure. Finally, the market prices of risk are estimated through the following standard relation,

$\lambda_t = \lambda_0 + \lambda_1 x_t.$
(61.9)

In this case, $\lambda_0$ is a 27-dimensional vector and $\lambda_1$ is a $27 \times 27$ matrix with affiliated parameters corresponding to the lagged macro factors being set to zero. This allows us to simplify the estimation procedure greatly, while still ensuring that risk in the macro and latent factors are priced. This means that the upper $2 \times 1$ row (corresponding to the current macro factors) and the bottom $3 \times 1$ row (corresponding to the latent factors) of $\lambda_0$ are free parameters. The upper-left $2 \times 2$ corner, upper-right $2 \times 3$ corner, lower-left $3 \times 2$ corner, and the lower-right $3 \times 3$ corner of $\lambda_1$ are also all free parameters. The aforementioned steps characterize the time-series dynamics of how the factors evolve through time in the model.

As regards the cross-sectional part of our maximum likelihood estimation procedure, we begin by assuming that $P_{t,n} = \exp (\bar{A}_n + \bar{B}_n^T x_t)$ (e.g., see Duffie and Kan, 1996). To derive the coefficients used for computing the recursive bond prices, we begin by noting that for an $n$-period bond,

$P_{t,n+1} = E_t[\xi_{t+1} P_{t+1,n}]$
$= E_t\left[ \exp\left\{ -r_t - \frac{1}{2} \lambda_t^T \lambda_t - \lambda_t^T \varepsilon_{t+1} + \bar{A}_n + \bar{B}_n^T x_{t+1} \right\} \right].$
(61.10a)

Since the expectation is taken at time $t$, the information is known at time $t$ and this expression can be evaluated without uncertainty. Thus, we take these known quantities out of the expectation as shown in equation (61.10b) and in subsequent steps,

(61.10b)
