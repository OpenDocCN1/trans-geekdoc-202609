# Chapter 13: What Drives Variation in the International Diversification Benefits? A Cross-Country Analysis

In this chapter, we show that, as the world becomes increasingly integrated, the benefits of global diversification still remain positive and economically significant over time. Both regression analysis and explanatory power tests show that international integration, measured by adjusted R² from a multifactor model, has more profound impact on the diversification benefits than correlation. Our results support Roll's (2013) argument that R², but not correlation, is an appropriate measure of market integration. We examine the impact of market integration determinants such as default risk, inflation, TED spread, past local equity market return, liquidity, and the relative performance of domestic portfolio on the potential diversification benefits.

Keywords: Diversification benefits, Investment constraints, International portfolio.
