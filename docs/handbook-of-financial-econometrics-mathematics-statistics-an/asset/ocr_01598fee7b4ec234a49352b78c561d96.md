# 31.5.2 Regression analysis on analyst career development

Results in subsection 31.5.1 indicate that investors respond more to new earnings forecasts announced by analysts occupying more structural holes. Analysts exhibiting ability to provide investors valuable information are also more likely to be rewarded with better career development in their profession. Hypothesis 4 predicts that analysts occupying more structural holes are more likely to move to work for larger brokerages than other analysts. We test Hypothesis 4 by estimating the following regression model:

```
Career_{i,t+1} = α0 + α1 Hole_{i,t} + α2 Experience_{i,t} + α3 Accuracy_{i,t}
+ α4 Cover_{i,t} + α5 Cons_{i,t} + α6 Timeliness_{i,t} + e_{i,t}.
(31.10)
```

In model (31.10), the dependent variable Career_{i,t+1} is a dummy variable with a value of 1 if analyst i is hired by bigger brokerage in year t + 1, and zero otherwise. We use both the network constraint and the rank variable of constraint of analyst i in year t as the structural hole measures. Accuracy_{i,t} is calculated following Cooper et al. (2001) and by an average of the accuracy of all earnings forecasts made by analyst i in year t. Cons_{i,t} is the average forecast consistency measures (calculated in (31.7)) of analyst i in year t. Timeliness_{i,t} is the average LFR (calculate in (31.6)) of analyst i in year t. We also add the control variables following Hilary and Hsu (2013): Experience_{i,t} is the logarithm of the number of years between the year the analysts has appeared in the I/B/E/S earning forecast database and year t; and Cover_{i,t} is the logarithm of the number of firms followed by analyst i in year t.
