Social media are increasingly taking up a high share of company information online in the same way that bank relationships provide companies’ financial information. That is, social media and bank relationships both overcome problems of asymmetric information in firm value.

The business press provides equity market participants with fundamental information about firms that is incremental to that provided by other information intermediaries and accounting data and reduces information asymmetries between equity investors (Telock *et al.*, 2008; Bushee *et al.*, 2010). Information asymmetry is a condition in which one participant in an exchange possesses more or better information than the other (Akerlof, 1970). A defining feature of equity markets is that investors do not generally have privileged access to the firms’ confidential information as the securities law prohibits unequal access to such information. This feature creates an opportunity for the media to serve as an information intermediary for equity investors seeking information from sources external to firms (Bushee *et al.*, 2010; Bushman *et al.*, 2017).

In general, firms provide information to investors through financial statements and other disclosures such as annual reports; numerous studies have suggested the existence of asymmetric information between firms and investors (Healy and Palepu, 2001). To overcome this asymmetry, investors seek additional information from multiple sources, such as new product announcements, expert reviews, and user-generated content (Chen *et al.*, 2012; Tellis and Johnson, 2007; Tirunillai and Tellis, 2012). However, social media communication between a firm and its consumers provides one such source of information to investors, which can then infer the firm’s level of involvement with its customer base and the firm’s commitment to improving its relationships with customers from its social media efforts.

Social media have opened new ways to communicate, collaborate, share content and advertise online in support of branding, sales, customer service, and product development. In the same way, online investors are largely involved in browsing firm and product webpages and exploring information on search engines and social media platforms. This environment reduces the information asymmetry between firms and investors and widens the application of associated investments. In addition, some website visitors may be current or potential investors who access websites to obtain information that is relevant to investment decision-making (Chung *et al.*, 2014; Luo *et al.*, 2013). Social media content is updated instantly and spreads virally at an unprecedented speed, thus providing first-hand information to investors ahead of any other sources.
