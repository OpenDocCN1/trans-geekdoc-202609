The derivations of upper bounds for European and American call options are trivial. If the current underlying asset is not the value of these upper bounds, then arbitrageur can easily earn riskless profit by purchasing the current underlying asset of call options and selling call options. The upper bounds for European and American put options can also be proven by the same arbitrage trading strategy.

To derive the lower bounds of European options, we consider the following investments:

**Portfolio A.** *Purchase a European call option at price $C_0$ with strike price $K$ and purchase $K e^{-rT}$ value of bond*

**Portfolio B.** *Purchase the underlying stock at price $S_0$*

**Portfolio C.** *Purchase a European put option at price $P_0$ with strike price $K$ and purchase the underlying stock at price $S_0$*

**Portfolio D.** *Purchase $K e^{-rT}$ value of bond*

At maturity, portfolio B and portfolio D are worth $S_T$ and $K$ respectively. The value of portfolio A and portfolio C will be $\max(S_T, K)$ where portfolios A and C is worth $S_T$ if $S_T > K$ and worth $K$ if $S_T \le K$. Therefore, unless the current value of portfolio A is at least as large as portfolio B, there will be arbitrage opportunity. By the same way, portfolio C should be always worth as much as portfolio D. Hence, we can obtain the relations as follows:

$C_0 + K e^{-rT} \ge S_0 \quad \text{and} \quad P_0 + S_0 \ge K e^{-rT},$ 
 or 
 $C_0 \ge S_0 - K e^{-rT} \quad \text{and} \quad P_0 \ge K e^{-rT} - S_0. \quad (7.5)$

Then, the lower bounds of European options are derived. Since American call options are never early exercised if the underlying asset without dividend payment, therefore the upper and lower bounds for American call options are same as the bounds of European call options. However, an American put option should be larger than $K[-S]_0$ since it can be exercised at any time. Handley (2005) investigate the extension of Merton’s (1973) approach to the options with dividends. The tightened upper bound for European and American call options with dividends under arbitrage opportunity framework are determined by the comparison between dividend payment and strike price.
