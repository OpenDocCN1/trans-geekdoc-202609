Next, we proceed with the wavelet multiple cross-correlation (Figure 36.7) of 11 Asian stock market returns (Japan, Hong Kong, Singapore, India, Indonesia, South Korea, Taiwan, Sri Lanka, Thailand, China and Malaysia).

Figure 36.7 shows that the Asian stock markets are positively correlated at all wavelet scales and at all lags. The cross-correlation coefficients for scale one associated with an investment horizon of two to four days are zero. However, when the wavelet scales are increased to level two, the lead–lag effect between the Asian stock markets becomes evident where the Japan market is the potential leader or follower of the other Asian markets across all wavelet levels. As in the EU stock markets, we notice that the correlation gets weaker with successive lags and tends to zero for short-term investment horizons while it remains high for long-term investment horizons.

The wavelet multiple cross-correlation of the Asian stock markets (Figure 36.8) without Japan (Hong Kong, Singapore, India, Indonesia, South Korea, Taiwan and Malaysia) shows that the Hong Kong stock market acts as a potential leader or follower of the other Asian markets for investment horizons of two to four days and four to eight days while the Singapore stock market is the potential leader or follower of the other Asian markets for investment horizons of eight to 16 days, 16-32 days, 32-64 days and 64-128 days.

Figure 36.10 shows the degree of similarity and phase relations between the Hong Kong and Singapore stock market returns.

We notice that since the beginning of 1994 until the end of the sample, some strong linkage appeared at medium frequencies, which indicates that the process of contagion began with the 1994 Mexican crisis. Besides, we notice at all times and scales a positive correlation between the Hong Kong and Singapore index returns, indicating that the Asian markets tend to move together where sometimes the Hong Kong index leads and at other times it is the Singapore index that leads. For example, from January 1995 to July 2005 at a scale of about 1024 days, the Singapore index led while from July 2005 to the end of the sample the Hong Kong index led. A phase difference of zero is observed especially at scales of 512-1024 days and 1024-2048 days.
