# 70.4.1 Model calibration

Maximum likelihood estimation is employed for the estimation of the GARJI model (70.1)–(70.6), while the Monte Carlo EM and the Metropolis chain algorithm are used together for the estimation of the VG NGARCH model (70.8)–(70.12), where the instantaneous mean return rate m is set to the mean of the log-returns Y1, . . . , YT, namely, m̂ = Ȳ.

Tables 70.4 and 70.5 list the parameter estimates and model calibration for the VG-NGARCH and GARJI model, respectively. All the companies exhibit significant time-variation in the shape parameters of the VG-NGARCH model and the jump intensity of the GARJI model, particularly after July 7, 2008. As the two models are non-nested, their goodness-of-fit is measured using the following three criteria: Akaike information criteria (AIC), Schwarz criteria (SC), and Hannan–Quinn criteria (HQ). The three criteria are calculated as follows:

AIC = −2(k/T) + 2(k/T); SC = −2(k/T) + k log(T)/T;
HQ = −2(k/T) + 2k log(log(T))/T, (70.19)

where k is the number of parameters and T is the sample size. The model that minimizes these information criteria values has the best fit.

The residual-based diagnostic test based on the Ljung-Box statistics (with lag=25) shown in Tables 70.4 and 70.5 reveals no remaining serial correlation in the squared standardized residuals, except for the Bank of America and Wells Fargo in the GARJI model. Comparing Tables 70.4 and 70.5 reveals that the VG-NGARCH model not only has higher log-likelihood values, but also has smaller values of all the three information criteria in (70.19), implying that it provides a parsimonious model specification. The VG-NGARCH model reduces to the VG model by Madan et al. (1998) when the NGARCH parameters a = b = c = 0.
