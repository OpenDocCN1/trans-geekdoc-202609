Given $N(0)=1/2$, $N'(0)=1/\sqrt{2\pi}$, $N'''(0)=-N'(0)$, $N''(0)=N''''(0)=0$, we expand $N(L')$ and $N'(L')$ at 0 respectively:

$$N(L') = N(0) + N'(0)L' + N''(0)L'^2/2 + e_3$$
$$= \frac{1}{2} + L'/\sqrt{2\pi} + e_3,\tag{106.58}$$
$$N'(L') = N'(0) + N''(0)L' + N'''(0)L'^2/2 + e_4$$
$$= 1/\sqrt{2\pi} - L'^2/2\sqrt{2\pi} + e_4.\tag{106.59}$$

Substituting equations (106.56)–(106.59) into equation (106.54), dropping all of remainder terms, equation (106.54) becomes

$$C = (S' - K)/2 + (\ln(S'/K)/\sigma\sqrt{2\pi T})[(S' - K)(1 + [\ln(S'/K)/4]^2)$$
$$- \ln(S'/K)(S' + K)/4]$$
$$+ (\sigma\sqrt{T}/2\sqrt{2\pi})[S' + K - \ln(S'/K)(S' - K)/4].\tag{106.60}$$

Equation (106.60) is a quadratic equation of $\sigma\sqrt{T}$ and can be rewritten as

$$\sigma^2 T[8(S' + K) - 2(S' - K)\ln(S'/K)] - 8\sigma\sqrt{T}\sqrt{2\pi}(2C - S' + K)$$
$$+ \ln(S'/K)[(S' - K)(16 + (\ln(S'/K))^2) - 4(S' + K)\ln(S'/K)] = 0.\tag{106.61}$$

Solving $\sigma\sqrt{T}$ from equation (106.61) yields

$$\sigma\sqrt{T} = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a},\tag{106.62}$$

where
$$a = 8(S' + K) - 2(S' - K)\ln(S'/K),$$
$$b = -8\sqrt{2\pi}(2C - S' + K),$$
$$c = \ln(S'/K)[(S' - K)(16 + \ln(S'/K))^2] - 4(S' + K)\ln(S'/K).$$

A merit of equation (106.62) is to circumvent the ad hoc substitution present in Corrado and Miller (1996) and improve the accuracy of the ISD's estimation. Other methods to calculate the implied volatility can be found in Lai et al. (1992).
