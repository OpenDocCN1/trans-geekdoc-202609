# 72.6  Conclusion

In this chapter, we first review the non-parametric estimator of the VaR and the CTE for long-horizon equity returns proposed in HCT-1 and HCT-2. The estimator is based on an asymptotic formula of the VaR and the CTE when the integration length goes to infinity at some rate related to the ratio of the sample size and the integration length. The estimator is further used to construct new statistics to test the equality of two CTEs from integrated returns. Extensive simulation studies show that the test works well. In (72.9), the null hypothesis $C_\alpha^{1,T}=C_\alpha^{2,T}$ is too restrictive as the two CTEs are both time-varying. It is more realistic in practical applications to allow for a small gap between the two CTEs that vanishes as $T$ diverges to infinity. To take this into account, the following local-type test

$$\left\{\begin{aligned} H_0 &: C_\alpha^{1,T}=C_\alpha^{2,T}+c_1/T^{\lambda_1} \\ H_1 &: C_\alpha^{1,T}=C_\alpha^{2,T}+c_2/T^{\lambda_2}, \end{aligned}\right.$$

is worthy of investigation, where $c_1$ and $c_2$ are non-zero, and $0<\lambda_2<\lambda_1$.
