Table 35.7 presents the results of the structural model using SEM with CFA to determine the capital structure. We find that the profitability has a significant effect on the capital structure in either a short-term or long-term aspect. The positive relationship between the profitability and the capital structure is inconsistent with the pecking order theory (Myers, 1977) and the free cash flow theory (Jensen, 1986). It is more likely that profitable firms may utilize both internal sources and debt to invest profitable opportunities and to avoid agency costs (Stulz, 1990). Volatility and financial rating only influence the market-based short-term leverage ratio. This evidence implies that, although the credit risk affects the fluctuation of stock price, bankruptcy costs may not change long-term target leverage ratios. This evident is also consistent with Welch’s (2004) argument that firms do not readjust target leverage ratios responding to their fluctuated stock prices.

# 35.4.3 Determinants of capital structure by MIMIC model

To enhance the explanation of the factors of capital structure, we use the MIMIC model to reduce the latent variable. The standardized estimates of the determinants of the capital structure on a market value basis are illustrated by the path diagram in Figure 35.7. In the MIMIC model, there is only one latent variable (attribute), Capital Structure (CapStruc), and all observed variables are indicators of the capital structure. To compare the performance between MIMIC model and SEM with CFA, we use the same indicators as those used in SEM with CFA to determine the capital structure. Table 35.8 presents the results of the MIMIC model in terms of the market value basis and the book value basis. The goodness-of-fit measures, the root mean square residual, and the root mean square error of approximation show that the MIMIC model performed better than SEM with CFA because of the decrease in latent variables.13
