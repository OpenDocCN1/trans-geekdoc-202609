## 121.5 Conclusions

Pástor and Stambaugh (2003) forcefully argue that aggregate liquidity in theory should be a priced state variable in the equity market. A large empirical literature has developed to test if aggregate liquidity is priced. This study extends the literature along two dimensions. First, while previous research typically focuses on one cross-section of stock returns (i.e., the portfolios sorted on liquidity betas), we examine various cross-sections of returns. Second, unlike prior research that typically employs only one liquidity measure, we take into account various measures of aggregate liquidity. In particular, we employ both stock and money market liquidity measures. With the time-series regression approach of Fama and French (2015), we find that aggregate liquidity is not priced in the cross-sections of stock returns. That is, adding the liquidity factor to common asset-pricing models does not improve the performance of models significantly. Therefore, our results call for more research on aggregate liquidity.
