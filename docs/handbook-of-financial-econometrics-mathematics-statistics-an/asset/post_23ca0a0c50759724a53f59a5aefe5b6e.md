Besides that, we introduce the turning point method to increase the forecast accuracy.

### 6A.1 Principles of turning point method

The Turning point method obeys the following four concepts, if $P_t<P_{t-1}>P_{t-2}$ then represent as peak turning point (PTP); if $P_t>P_{t-1}<P_{t-2}$ then represent as trough turning point (TTP); if $P_t>P_{t-1}>P_{t-2}$ then represent as upward no turning point (UNTP); if $P_t<P_{t-1}<P_{t-2}$ then represent as downward no turning point (DNTP). According to the four concepts, we can build up a 4*4 matrices as follows:

|  | **Real** |  |  |  |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Prediction** | **PTP** | **TTP** | **UNTP** | **DNTP** |
| PTP | $f_{11}$ | $f_{12}$ | $f_{13}$ | $f_{14}$ |
| TTP | $f_{21}$ | $f_{22}$ | $f_{23}$ | $f_{24}$ |
| UNTP | $f_{31}$ | $f_{32}$ | $f_{33}$ | $f_{34}$ |
| DNTP | $f_{41}$ | $f_{42}$ | $f_{43}$ | $f_{44}$ |

### 6A.2 Measurable indexes of turning point method

The measurable indexes of the 4*4 matrices are (a) ratio of accurate forecasts (RAF), (b) ratio of worst forecasts (RWF), (c) ratio of accurate to worst forecasts (RAWP), (d) ratio of inaccurate forecasts (RIF).

The RAF is $\frac{f_{11}+f_{22}+f_{33}+f_{44}}{\sum\sum f_{ij}}$, $i,j = 1,2,3,4$; RWF is $\frac{f_{12}+f_{21}+f_{34}+f_{43}}{\sum\sum f_{ij}}$, $i,j = 1,2,3,4$; RAWP is $\frac{f_{11}+f_{22}+f_{33}+f_{44}}{f_{12}+f_{21}+f_{34}+f_{43}}$, $i,j = 1,2,3,4$; RIF is $\frac{f_{13}+f_{14}+f_{23}+f_{24}+f_{31}+f_{32}+f_{41}+f_{42}}{\sum\sum f_{ij}}$, $i,j = 1,2,3,4$. The most accuracy of prediction model, that the higher RAF and RAWP is needed, and the lowest RWF and RIF is also needed is to be considered.

## Abstract

Based upon Ritchken (1985), Levy (1985), Lo (1987), Zhang (1994), Jackwerth and Rubinstein (1996), and others, this chapter discusses the alternative method to determine option bound in terms of the first two moments of distribution. This approach includes stochastic dominance method and linear programming method, then we discuss semi-parametric method and non-parametric method for option-bound determination. Finally, we incorporate both skewness and kurtosis explicitly through extending Zhang (1994) to provide bounds for the prices of the expected payoffs for options, given the first two moments and skewness and kurtosis.

### 7.2 Parametric and Mathematical Methods to Derive Option Bounds

In this section, major mathematical methods used to derive option bounds are discussed in detail. The major techniques are classified into four broad categories: searching for arbitrage conditions, stochastic dominance, linear programming, semi- and non-parametric approaches. For each category, we review its influential papers and describe the basic concepts of the derivation process to obtain option bounds.

### 7.2.1 Arbitrage theory

Merton (1973) derives option bounds by searching for arbitrage conditions. Given no arbitrage assumption and the constant risk-free interest rate, r, the upper and lower bounds for European options can be obtained without any particular assumptions as follows:

$$C^{E}_{\min}=\begin{cases}\max(S_0-Ke^{-rT},0),&\text{without dividend case}\\\max(S_0-D-Ke^{-rT},0),&\text{with dividend case}\end{cases},$$

and $C^{E}_{\max}=S_0$, \tag{7.1}

$$P^{E}_{\min}=\begin{cases}\max(Ke^{-rT}-S_0,0),&\text{without dividend case}\\\max(D+Ke^{-rT}-S_0,0),&\text{with dividend case}\end{cases},$$

and $P^{E}_{\max}=Ke^{-rT}$, \tag{7.2}

where $C^{E}_{\min}$ and $C^{E}_{\max}$ represent the lower and upper bounds for a European call option, respectively; $P^{E}_{\min}$ and $P^{E}_{\max}$ are the lower and upper bounds for a European put option. $S_0$ is the current price of the underlying asset, $K$ denotes the strike price, and $D$ represents the dividend payment.

Without any assumptions of the distribution of the underlying asset’s price, the bounds for American options written on stock with non-dividend can be derived as follows:

$$C^{A}_{\min}=\max(S_0-Ke^{-rT},0),\quad\text{and}\quad C^{A}_{\max}=S_0, \tag{7.3}$$

$$P^{A}_{\min}=\max(K-S_0,0),\quad\text{and}\quad P^{A}_{\max}=K. \tag{7.4}$$
