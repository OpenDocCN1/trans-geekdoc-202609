In the model of hedge fund returns that was presented in this chapter, the lagged previous month’s hedge fund returns had a statistically significant impact on the current month’s performance results. This finding, in similar studies, has led to a barrage of studies that attempt to illustrate why hedge fund pricing and performance are stale. Studies have claimed that managers purposely misreported performance results and used the serial correlation in hedge fund performance results as evidence that they are misleading investors. This general argument does not alone prove that hedge fund managers are maliciously deceiving investors and as we have shown, serial correlations of similar magnitudes are found in publicly traded investment vehicles. Studies that aim to evaluate the performance of hedge funds based on the delay in reporting performance results seem to be a useful pursuit to add to the body of literature on hedge fund performance. Moreover, in terms of estimating or identifying the determinates of hedge fund performance, it is useful to start by examining the relationship between hedge fund performance and investment vehicles that are investable. This chapter reviewed examples of attempts to model hedge fund returns and illustrated why it is helpful to use an approach that focuses on developing a model that employs investable indices. Using these model estimates as a basis, investors and researchers could explore more complicated replication strategies in an attempt to construct passive hedge fund indices.

The current body of literature on hedge fund performance provides a solid basis to think about hedge fund returns and how to interpret the returns reported by hedge fund managers. The ultimate goal to replicate hedge fund returns and passive exploit investment factors that automate the hedge fund experience should start with an exploration of determinates of hedge fund performance that can be obtained using passive and investable indices. Once that basis is provided, as this chapter illustrates, investors and practitioners may be able to experiment with advanced replication strategies to attempt to generate a similar experience with less cost, which would lead to an improved investment experience. In conclusion, this chapter offers key suggestions for financial market participants such as hedge funds managers, portfolio managers, risk managers, regulatory bodies, financial analysts, and investors in regard to their evaluation and interpretation of hedge fund performance. In addition, this critical review chapter can benefit investors, portfolio managers, and researchers in the establishment of a yardstick for the assessment of hedge fund performance and the performance of assets that have stale pricing and are relatively opaque.
