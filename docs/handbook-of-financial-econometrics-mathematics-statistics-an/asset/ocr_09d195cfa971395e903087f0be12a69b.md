# 31.2.2 Social network research on structural hole

The three approaches in the literatures (forecast accuracy, forecast timeliness and forecast consistency) can be somewhat problematic. First, forecast accuracy may not be an effective way to identify high-ranked analysts because follower analysts can free ride on the information released by high-ranked analysts. However, ranking forecast timeliness is highly model dependent with the parameter specification in forecast timeliness calculation subjective and arbitrary. Measure of forecast consistency requires calculation of standard deviation, which entails a long history of earnings forecasts. Such a data requirement inherently biases against identification of young and new analysts as top performers.

Our paper utilizes a new approach for analyst ranking. We use the measure of structural hole, a concept developed in the social network research, to rank analysts. We review prior studies on structural hole below.

The idea of “groups”, formed by people with kinship, similar behavior or opinion, often focusing on activities within rather than outside has been well-documented in social science studies (Granovetter, 1973; Blau, 1977; Burt, 2003). Granovetter (1973) puts forward the concept of “weak ties” to represent the between-group relations: with agents with strong ties as a part of a close-knit group who largely knows one another, while agents with weak tie referring to acquaintances far less likely to know one another. Granovetter (1973) proposes that only weak ties can build bridges² between different groups and transmit the information among groups, within strong ties strengthening connections within groups. Blau (1977) examines the importance of weak ties and indicates that the whole society may fall apart without weak ties.

Burt (1992) delves further into the effect of weak ties and develops the concept of structural hole. The paper indicates that as people prefer to focus on activities inside their own groups, there is a “structural hole” as a gap in the information flow between groups. Meanwhile, agents standing at the position of structural holes and building bridges to connect two independent groups, can facilitate the information sharing among groups of agents over time.
