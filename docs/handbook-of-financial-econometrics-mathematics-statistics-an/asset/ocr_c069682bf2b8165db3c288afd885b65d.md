C_{Gaussian–Gumbel}(u1, ..., un | wGa, wGu, R, α)
= wGa C_{Gaussian}(u1, ..., un | R) + wGu C_{Gumbel}(u1, ..., un | α),  (38.15)

where wGa, wGu ∈ [0,1] and wGa + wGu = 1. The parameters (R, α) are the association parameters, and (wGa, wGu) are the weight parameters.

By analogy, the upper and lower dependence coefficients of Gaussian–Gumbel copula are shown in equations (38.16) and (38.17), respectively. It can only capture upper tail dependence.

λ^U_{Gaussian–Gumbel} = wGa λ^U_Ga + wGu λ^U_Gu = wGu · (2 − 2^{1/α}),  (38.16)

λ^L_{Gaussian–Gumbel} = wGa λ^L_Ga + wGu λ^L_Gu = 0.  (38.17)

# (3) Gaussian–Gumbel–Clayton copula

Three or more than three copulas can be mixed for embodying more flexibility. Generally, the more the component copulas are, the more flexible their mixture is. For example, to further cover possible lower tail dependence, the Clayton copula can be added into the mixture of the Gaussian and Gumbel copula. In comparison with the Gaussian–Gumbel copula, this three-copula mixture actually nests a greater capability of capturing sensitive lower tail dependence and has all “√” in Table 38.2. The mixture of the Gaussian copula, Gumbel copula and Clayton copula is named the Gaussian–Gumbel–Clayton copula and defined as equation (38.18).

C_{Gaussian–Gumbel–Clayton}(u1, ..., un | wGa, wGu, wC, R, α, β)
= wGa C_{Gaussian}(u1, ..., un | R) + wGu C_{Gumbel}(u1, ..., un | α)
+ wC C_{Clayton}(u1, ..., un | β),  (38.18)

where wGa, wGu, wC ∈ [0,1] and wGa + wGu + wC = 1. The parameters (R, α, β) are the association parameters, and (wGa, wGu, wC) are the weight parameters.
