Specifically, there is no study that examines if there is a change in the differential impact of the use of foreign versus domestic bank debt on firm performance. This paper attempts to make a contribution in this area.

To examine this issue, we require data from an emerging market that has both sufficient foreign bank penetration and has undergone substantial financial reforms. Our market of choice is Taiwan. Taiwan is unique in that it offers access to trustworthy, detailed credit data that is not available in many other emerging markets.³ Taiwan, even in the early years of our data sample, also has enough foreign bank penetration for a meaningful exploration. Moreover, in 1994, the Taiwanese government set in motion a strategy that would slowly begin the transformation of Taiwan from an emerging financial market into a regional Asian financial center. To that end, the government proposed the strategy “Manufacturing Industry is the Root and Financial Industry is the Leaf”, which gradually introduced reforms into the financial sector.⁴ As helpful as the 1994 reforms were, it was the Taiwanese banking reforms of 2002 and 2004 that were the most critical events in the transformation of Taiwan from an emerging market into a developed one. Since the implementation of the 2002 and 2004 financial reforms, Taiwan has evolved into one of the world’s most important economies and a significant regional financial center.
