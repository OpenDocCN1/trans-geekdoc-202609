In addition, the empirical evidence from Conover *et al.* (2009) shows that among the precious metals, gold provides the greatest portfolio performance improvement of the US equity portfolios. As such, the role of gold in a diversified portfolio has been an important issue in financial literature.

Gold in asset management is currently very topical. Gold is commonly known as a safe haven commodity to avoid risk in financial market, especially to hedge the currency value in the short run. Reboredo (2013b) adopts copulas approach to examine the role of gold as a hedge or safe haven for the US dollar. However, from a long-term perspective, a fairly wide consensus exists that gold retains inflation hedging properties despite considerable fluctuations in the shorter term. Thus, gold functions as the risk management tool in hedging and diversifying asset portfolios (Sherman, 1986; Aggarwal and Soenen, 1988; Jaffe, 1989; Chua *et al.*, 1990; Dooley *et al.*, 1995; Johnson and Soenen, 1997; Capie *et al.*, 2005; Baur and Lucey, 2010). Obviously, the lack of correlation with other financial assets made gold an ideal tool for portfolio diversification. However, the financialization of gold and other commodities since the early 2000s has integrated the commodity markets with other financial markets, such as the bonds’ and stocks’ markets (Tang and Xiong, 2012). On the other side, the relative volatility of stocks and bonds has changed over time (Reilly, Wright, and Chan, 2000; Jones and Wilson, 2004). Therefore, it raises our interests to investigate how the changing nature of stocks’ and bonds’ volatility alters the role of gold in a diversified portfolio.

If the purpose of investment is to accumulate funds to finance future expenditures, investors then need to meet different investment planning horizons due to the different life cycle of investment goals. In literature, Marshall (1994) confirms that the length of the investors’ investment horizon reflects their willingness to bear risk, the investors’ perception of riskiness changes as their horizon grows shorter. In other words, as time passes and the investors’ investment horizon grows shorter, the investors will find it desirable to move from a more risky portfolio to a less risky one. In addition, Gunthorpe and Levy (1994) state that the difference in investment horizons mainly reflect the difference in investors’ transaction cost. Therefore, the role played by the investment horizon in optimal portfolio construction has been an important issue of portfolio management.
