The above analyzes show that none of the major risk integration approaches is able to capture all the typical features of risk dependence at the same time. Among these approaches, the copula approach, in particular the t copula, comes closest to the goal. We notice that although no single copula is flexible enough to capture all features, they have their own characteristics, so their mixture might be applicable to all situations. Nelsen (1999) demonstrates that mixtures of copulas are also copulas. By carefully choosing the component copulas in the mixture, a model that is flexible enough to generate most dependence patterns can be constructed (Lai and Tseng, 2010; Chen and Tu, 2013). Therefore, this chapter proposes a mixture copula framework to comprehensively capture the typical features to accurately model the dependence structure between risks.

# 38.3.1 Illustration of the mixture copula

The idea of a copula was mooted as early as the 19th century in statistics, and modern theories about copulas date back to about 50 years ago when Sklar (1959) defined and provided some basic properties of a copula. Despite the long history in statistics, copulas have been applied in financial markets only very recently. Embrechts et al. (1999) and Li (2000) were among the first to popularize the use of copulas in the field of risk management. After that, research in this area has grown very fast. The mixture copula has emerged much later than did the copula and has attracted much less attention so far. Nelsen (1999) demonstrates that mixtures of copulas are also copulas.
