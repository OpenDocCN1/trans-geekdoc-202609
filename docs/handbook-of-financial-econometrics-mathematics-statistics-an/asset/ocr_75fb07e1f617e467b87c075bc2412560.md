As a result, agents sitting in the position of structural holes and having multiple contacts with different independent groups will be more likely to have early access to diverse and comprehensive information, giving them a competitive advantage in seeing good ideas (Burt, 1992).

Burt (2003) uses a survey in American electronic companies, and reports that people occupying more structural holes can give more valuable ideas. Ahuja (2000), Goyal and Redondo (2007), Leonardi and Bailey (2011), and Burt et al. (2013) provide similar evidence that people occupying structural holes provide non-redundant ties. The diverse and unique information makes them more likely to come up with new ideas.

Recent research has started to exploit the idea of structural hole in finance. Ahuja (2000) builds a network with detailed archival research on the chemicals and materials sector, with three types of data sources to identify linkage activity. The paper reports that accessing to diverse information type is beneficial for firms. Zaheer and Bell (2005) examine the network of Canadian mutual fund firms based on both ties inboard and management. The results indicate that firms occupying more structural holes are more capable of using their internal capabilities to enhance their performance.

Our paper utilizes a network constructed with analysts providing overlapping research coverage on firms. For many analysts, their assignments are industry-based, focusing on similar firms of interests and particular sets of the market and economy information. In other words, analysts can exhibit patterns of “group” behavior documented earlier in social science studies. If this is the case, analysts occupying more structural holes in the network can build bridges to connect groups. Those analysts are able to access information and knowledge of their peer analysts from different groups and obtain more diverse information about market, industries and firms. We therefore hypothesize those analysts (occupying more structural holes) are more likely to release more valuable forecasts.
