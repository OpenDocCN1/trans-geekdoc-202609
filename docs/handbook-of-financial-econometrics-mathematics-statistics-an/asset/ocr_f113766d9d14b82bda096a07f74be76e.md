The detailed procedure of mixture copula-based risk integration is shown in Algorithm 38.1, which consists of three Stages, with a total of seven Steps. Stage 1 prepares marginal risk distributions and the copula for Monte Carlo simulation. Stage 2 simulates total risk distribution by Monte Carlo simulation. Stage 3 calculates VaR from total risk distribution. Assume there are n types of individual risks (r1, r2, ..., rn) to be integrated and the number of Monte Carlo simulations is N. Next, each step is explained in detail, as follows.

Stage 1 is the preliminary stage, comprising Steps 1 and 2. Step 1 estimates the marginal distributions (F1, F2, ..., Fn) for risk types (r1, r2, ..., rn). Generally, a goodness-of-fit test such as the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test (KS test for short) is used to find the most proper distributions for empirical risk data series, and maximum likelihood estimation is used to estimate the parameters. Step 2 uses the most suitable copula C to model the dependence structure across risk types (r1, r2, ..., rn). Similar to Step 1, there are many types of copulas, so first, the goodness-of-fit test is needed to determine the best fit for the dependence structure. Popular test methods for mixture copula include the χ² test, Cramer-von Mises test and Anderson-Darling test (Hu, 2006; Kole et al., 2007; Genest et al., 2009; Grundke and Polle, 2012; Weiß, 2014). +   **Stage 1: Preliminaries**
+   Step 1: Estimate marginal risk distributions (F1, F2, ..., Fn)
+   Step 2: Estimate mixture copula C
+   **Stage 2: Monte Carlo simulation**
+   Step 3: Draw a joint sample of uniform random variables (u1, u2, ..., un) from the mixture copula C
+   Step 4: Transform the sample (u1, u2, ..., un) into the sample of risk losses (s1, s2, ..., sn) = (F1⁻¹(u1), F2⁻¹(u2), ..., Fn⁻¹(un))
+   Step 5: Sum all the losses from Step 4 to derive integrated loss L = s1 + s2 + ⋯ + sn
+   Step 6: Repeat Step 3 to Step 5 N times to derive N simulated integrated losses L1, L2, ..., LN
+   **Stage 3: Calculation**
+   Step 7: Calculate VaR from integrated losses L1, L2, ..., LN
