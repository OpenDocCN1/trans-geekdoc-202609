# 131.6.1 Regulation and discretionary loan default risk management

The SOX and DF legislation affect the risk management decisions made in bank, and each piece of legislation affect loan loss expense volume distinctly. Loan loss provisioning with current regulatory controls has been deterred. Increased interest income and successful collections or delinquency reduction reduce loss provision expense. Increased specialization in lending to specific industries tends to increase loss provision expenses, but the advent of the DFA offsets much of this finding for commercial of loans. This observation presumes that DFA regulations affect bank manager proclivity to accept more risky loan opportunities. This in itself will result in a reduced allocation for risk reserves. The reduced risk reserve cushion is complemented by a reduction in loan loss provision expenses associated with improved governance activities as required by the Sarbanes-Oxley Act.

Banks experience a complementary effect between SOX and DFA on loan loss provisions. Improved governance procedures to establish policy responses to non-performing loans result in one of the largest estimated coefficient values in the statistical model while still providing incentives for growth. Yet DFA affects loan loss provisions distinctly, with estimated coefficients for DFA having the opposite signs when compared to corresponding SOX counterparts for delinquent loan volume and for asset growth. This argues for careful policymaking which generates, or enforces, attention to risk as part of the lending culture of a bank. Indeed, these results indicate increased lending activity reduces loan loss provisions only when making relatively profitable loans regardless of the regulatory environment. The distinct roles of controls impacting non-performing loan volume management. Incentives for firm growth, income, capital, and loan specialization under the two regulatory environments, make the loan risk management process more complex and under increasing influence by federal banking regulation.
