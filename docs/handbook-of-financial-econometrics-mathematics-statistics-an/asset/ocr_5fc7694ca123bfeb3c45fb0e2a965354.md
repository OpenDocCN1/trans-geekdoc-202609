Furthermore, prior studies, such as Golubov et al. (2012), argue that the use of financial advisors may endogenously relate to deal and firm specific characteristics. This may cause the problem of endogeneity that obtain biased coefficients. Thus, this study uses Heckman’s (1979) two-stage procedure to take into account the issue of self-selection bias. In the first stage, this study conducts the probit regression analysis by controlling for deal and firm specific characteristics in order to obtain inverse mill’s ratio for different types of financial advisors.

In addition, as high-reputable financial advisors are more likely to be involved in large deals, the probit regression model also controls for the log of transaction value as an additional variable in order to contend for identification restriction. Accordingly, this study obtains four variables of inverse mill’s ratio in terms of high- and low-reputable financial advisors and domestic and foreign advisors. In the second stage, I further run OLS regression analysis with additionally controlling for inverse mill’s ratio to take into account the potential self-selection bias. This can reveal a clear picture to address the relationship between different types of financial advisors and announcement returns in M&As.

As shown in Table 76.6, the results in model specification (1) show that there is negative relationship between target announcement returns and high-reputable financial advisors. On the contrary, the results in model specifications (2)–(4) reveal that targets obtain higher announcement returns when hiring low-reputable financial advisors and domestic and foreign advisors. However, the results are not statistically significant. With regard to control variables, the results indicate that targets obtain lower announcement returns when targets are financial firms.
