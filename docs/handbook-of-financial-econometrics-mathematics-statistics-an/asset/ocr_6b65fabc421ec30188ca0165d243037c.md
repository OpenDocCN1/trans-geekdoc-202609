The goal of a predictive model like our bankruptcy prediction study is to predict future outcomes based on past financial data. Bankruptcy prediction is an interesting topic because various stakeholders such as regulators, investors, lenders, auditors, management, bankers, employees, and the public are interested in how bankrupt firms proceed. We use Korean financial data and try to predict bankrupt firms in the Korean capital market environment that may or may not be the same as the US capital market, especially since the financial crisis of 1997 in Korea.

The neural networks approach and the decision tree approach for bankruptcy prediction were introduced recently, but our study uses the MCLP approach in data mining for firm bankruptcy prediction. Our MCLP data mining approach has been applied to credit card portfolio management (Kou et al., 2005). This approach has proven to be robust and powerful for even a large sample size using a huge financial database (Peng et al., 2008). The results of the MCLP approach in our firm bankruptcy prediction study are promising as this approach performs better than traditional multiple discriminant analysis or logistic regression analysis using financial data.

# 21.2.2 Background

Altman (1968) had a seminal work in a bankruptcy prediction study using multiple discriminant analysis. Mossman et al. (1998) later compared financial ratios with cash flow variables and confirmed that cash flow variables are also useful in predicting bankruptcy.

Another major study is Ohlson’s (1980) study that used a logit model. His approach may be simple to apply since this model does not require any assumptions about the prior probability of bankruptcy or the distribution of predictor variables. Grice and Ingram (2001) tested Altman’s (1968) study using a small sample of 33 manufacturing firms and the use of equal sample size of bankrupt and non-bankrupt firms using a sample from 1958 to 1961 and reported a 83.5% overall accuracy.
