# 48.4 Conclusion

In this chapter, we introduce a consumption-based asset pricing model based on prospect theory. To complement a deficiency of the original prospect theory, habit formation and “catching up with the Joneses” are incorporated to determine endogenously the reference point in prospect utility. This modification is due to the similarity between prospect theory and habit formation that investors actually care about gains or losses in wealth and excess or shortage in consumption. Moreover, in our model, the power utility functions of the original prospect theory are replaced with exponential utility functions. After this modification, our prospect utility function is still similar to the original one, but the first-order derivative of our prospect utility function becomes well-defined everywhere. With this desired property, we can readily apply prospect theory to many issues in economics and finance.

There are three major contributions in this research. First, it is the first model that tries to apply the value function in prospect theory as the utility function over consumption under the traditional consumption-based asset pricing framework. Second, the concept of habit formation and “catching up with the Joneses” is endogenously built into the reference point of our prospect-theory-oriented consumption-based asset pricing model. Third, we prove that investors’ behavior of evaluating their excess or shortage amounts in consumption relative to their habit consumption levels according to prospect theory is the key factor to solve the equity premium puzzle.
