The above system implies that equation (58.1), $A_i(L) = I_2 - A_i L$, where $A_i = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & \gamma_{ii}\phi_i \\ 0 & \phi_i \end{bmatrix}$. In addition, $\boldsymbol{u}_{i,t+1}$ is a matrix that contains both errors, $\varepsilon_{i,t+1}$ (noise in returns) and $v_{i,t+1}$ (innovation to downside risk), and these errors are not necessarily independent, since they are correlated with the downside risk aversion parameter, $\gamma_{ii}$.$^9$ As a result, efficient estimation of this model would require the simultaneous estimation of both equations, since the system errors in $\boldsymbol{u}_{i,t+1}$ are contemporaneously correlated. The maximum likelihood method is applied here to efficiently and simultaneously estimate the parameters in this system. After obtaining the parameters in the $A_i$ matrix using the maximum likelihood method, $\gamma_{ii}$ can be calculated through $\gamma_{ii} = A_{i,12} / A_{i,22} = \gamma_{ii}\phi_i / \phi_i$.$^{10}$

However, if our interest is on investigating a “cross-market” intertemporal downside risk–return relation, the specification in equation (58.5) can be modified as

$$\begin{bmatrix} R_{i,t+1} \\ V_{j,t+1} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & \gamma_{ij}\phi_j \\ 0 & \phi_j \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} R_{i,t} \\ V_{j,t} \end{bmatrix} + \boldsymbol{u}_{ij,t+1} \quad \text{and}$$

$$\boldsymbol{u}_{ij,t+1} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \gamma_{ij} \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \varepsilon_{ij,t+1} \\ v_{j,t+1} \end{bmatrix}. \tag{58.6}$$

The cross-market downside risk aversion parameter, $\gamma_{ij}$, measures the degree to which investors in country $i$ expect higher stock returns to compensate for the higher downside risk from country $j$.$^{11}$ This specification helps us to capture the contagion effect or a spillover effect induced by the risk from country $j$.$^{12}$

## 58.3.2 The leverage effect

Black (1976) and Christie (1982) argue that bad news in the financial market will produce an asymmetric effect on future volatility relative to good news. This leverage effect has been shown by Bekaert and Wu (2000) and Feunou and Tédongap (2012). To address this issue, we specify equation (58.7) to highlight the market behavior that bad news about stock returns will produce a higher downside risk, such that

$$V_{i,t+1} = \lambda_i R_{i,t} + \epsilon_{i,t+1}, \qquad (58.7)$$

where $\lambda_i$ measures the leverage effect and this coefficient is expected to bear a negative sign. By substituting equation (58.7) into the right-hand side of the downside risk–return relation in equation (58.2), $R_{i,t+1} = \gamma_{ii}E(V_{i,t+1}) + \varepsilon_{i,t+1}$, we have $R_{i,t+1} = \gamma_{ii}\lambda_i R_{i,t} + \varepsilon_{i,t+1}$, with $E(\epsilon_{i,t+1}) = 0$, entailing the complicated VaR-return dynamics embodied in an autocorrelation process. Having incorporated the leverage effect into a VAR system, equations (58.5) and (58.6) are replaced by equations (58.8) and (58.9), respectively, as

$$\begin{bmatrix} R_{i,t+1} \\ V_{i,t+1} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \gamma_{ii}\lambda_i & \gamma_{ii}\phi_i \\ \lambda_i & \phi_i \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} R_{i,t} \\ V_{i,t} \end{bmatrix} + \mathbf{u}_{i,t+1}. \qquad (58.8)$$

The appeal of equation (58.8) is that this system consists of both the leverage effect and the VaR feedback effect. The leverage effect shows that a negative return leads to higher subsequent volatility/VaR, whereas the volatility/VaR feedback effect captures how an increase in volatility/VaR may cause negative contemporaneous returns, which, in turn, results in an increase in future returns (Bollerslev et al., 2006). The dynamics of risk-return relation (consisting of $R_{i,t-1} \rightarrow V_{i,t}$ (negative sign), $V_{i,t} \rightarrow R_{i,t}$ (negative sign), $V_{i,t} \rightarrow R_{i,t+1}$ (positive sign)) can be tested by examining the significance of the off-diagonal of the coefficient matrix, while the autocorrelations of stock returns ($R_{i,t} \rightarrow R_{i,t+1}$) and/or risk ($V_{i,t} \rightarrow V_{i,t+1}$) can be verified by testing the significance of the coefficients on the diagonal of the coefficient matrix in equation (58.8). Obviously, as we impose the restriction $\lambda_i = 0$, equation (58.8) reduces to equation (58.5).
