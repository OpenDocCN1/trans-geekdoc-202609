# 6.6 Conclusion

In Taiwan, a stable stock market is an important indicator of economic development. In this study, the researchers used intervention analysis integrated into the ARIMA-GARCH model, ECM, intervention analysis integrated into the transfer function model, the simple average combination forecasting model, and the minimum error combination forecasting model to establish a TAIEX prediction model.

According to the results, with regard to the quantity index, the minimum error combination forecasting model has the best predictive power, followed by intervention analysis integrated into the transfer function model; with regard to the quality index, the minimum error combination forecasting model and intervention analysis integrated into the transfer function model are best. The intervention analysis integrated into the transfer function model has a more accurate prediction than ECM and intervention analysis integrated into the ARIMA-GARCH model. The minimum error combination forecasting model can improve prediction accuracy much better than non combination models.

In the intervention analysis integrated into transfer function model, the TAIEX will be affected by external factors and macroeconomic factors. External factors included the temporary changes of July 1997 and July 2007 and additive outliers of November 1993 and November 2001 primarily because of international economic shock and policy change in Taiwan. Macroeconomic factors include the INDU, the exchange rate, and the consumer price index. In this section, we discuss issues regarding government policy.

Based on intervention analysis integrated into the transfer function model in this paper, the government could maintain a strong currency and keep the inflation down if the government wants to maintain a strong TAIEX. To maintain a strong currency, the government could implement a tight monetary policy. To keep inflation down, the government could use tight fiscal policy.

In addition, if government officials believe that a stock market bubble can prevent problems like those that occurred in the Asian financial crises of 1990 and 1997, the government could weaken the currency by using expansionary monetary policy.

In view of various TAIEX issues, the policies mentioned in this section could be workable; for instance, a strong TAIEX would be maintained by strong currency and would keep inflation down, and the problem of the stock market bubble could be avoided by weakening the currency.
