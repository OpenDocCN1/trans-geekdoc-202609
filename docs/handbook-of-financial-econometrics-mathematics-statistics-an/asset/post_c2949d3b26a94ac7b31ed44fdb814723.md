## 131.5.2 Key results

A sample of financial data for individual bank branches and credit unions that operate in any number of non-metro counties was collected. These are compiled from Call Reports available from the Federal Financial Institutions Examination Council (FFIEC) or National Credit Union Administration. These data were subset into six competitive scenarios based on which types of financial institutions were operating within a county: credit unions as the only type of financial institution, community banks as the only type financial institution, large banks and community banks competing, large banks and credit unions competing, credit unions and community banks competing, and all three types of financial institutions competing. No cases of large banks being the only type of institution in a county were considered as these were not observed in the data. The predominant competitive situation is a community bank as the sole institution headquartered in a county. An increasing number of counties with only a credit union or only a community bank in operation in the county appears during the sample period.

The results for the second-stage regression are provided. Average ROA is greatest when all three institutions compete in the same market (Tables 131.6 and 131.7). Greater average values of X_EFFI when institutions compete are observed in this analysis. Efficiency is lowest when community banks are the only institution in the market, and second lowest when credit unions are the only institution. The loan to asset (LTA) ratio is greatest when credit unions and large banks compete in a market.

The cost efficiency of all institutions in general is higher when more community banks are present, but lower when more large banks and credit unions are present. Nevertheless, as the probability of a large bank entering the market increases it is associated with greater average cost efficiency. This suggests a tradeoff between profitability and efficiency. Cost efficiency of large banks is less when more community banks are present within the market. Cost efficiency is greater with more large banks in a market. Community bank efficiency is reduced when more community banks enter a market where all three financial institution types are present, but increase as more credit unions enter and decline as large banks enter said market. ## 131.6 Concluding Remarks

Recent legislation is associated with substantial transformation of the community banking industry during recent decades. Product offerings and risk management behavior have changed. The competitive balance between different sizes of institutions has also changed, leading to an evolution in industry structure. This chapter provides an overview of these changes and models used to analyze them in the context of changes in bank production costs and efficiency.

The Sarbanes-Oxley Act and Dodd-Frank Act were passed with the intent to facilitate transparency in financial transactions. Compliance with the DFA impacted the cost efficiency of financial institutions, with the magnitude depending on the size of the institution. Compliance costs have an asymmetric effect on institutions of different sizes. Since the smallest institutions may be unable to afford the additional overhead costs of compliance, some financial institutions choose not to enter markets requiring additional compliance expense.

As the competitive landscape in lending becomes more structured financial institution managers in this market segment must adjust to the set of informational and cost realities. Firms that operate in this market space have to maintain regulatory currency as well as technology to provide sustained products for consumers. An added burden is then placed upon the lender to be aware of legislative changes to maintain and attract quality borrowers.
