# 16.2.3 Self-control

Self-control bias causes investors fail to pursue their long-term and overarching goals because of insufficiency of self-discipline (Thaler and Shefrin, 1988; Thaler and Benartzi, 2004; Pompi an, 2006). Thaler and Shefrin (1981), Shefrin and Statman (1985), and Statman (1995) argue that investors could be rational planners and emotional doers at the same time and reveal intrapersonal conflicts when making decisions. Shefrin and Statman (1985) point out the disposition effect to be a self-control problem or a conflict between the part of rational planner and the part of emotional doer. They argue that the doer is related to emotions of regret and pride, which leads investors to hold losing stocks to defer the emotion of regret and realize winning stocks to hasten the feeling of pride.

To prevent the doer from adopting a myopic behavior, the planner needs some psychological techniques to influence the doer’s behavior. These techniques alter the doer’s incentives by modifying their preferences and monitoring their behaviors explicitly. However, when the monitoring costs are high, investors are advised to adopt self-imposed rules of thumb that limit the range of doer discretion (Thaler and Shefrin, 1981).
