# 82.7  Economic Rationale of the Treynor
Performance-Measure Method

Cheung and Kwan (1988) have derived an alternative simple rate of optimal portfolio selection in terms of the single-index model. First, Cheung and Kwan relate $C^*$ as defined in equation (82.13) to the correlation coefficient between the portfolio rates of return with $i$ securities $R_i$ and market rates of return $R_m$ ($\rho_i$):

$$\rho_i=\frac{C^*}{\sigma_m\Theta_i}, \tag{82.19}$$

where $\rho_i=\sigma_{im}/\sigma_i\sigma_m$, $\sigma_{im}$ is the covariance between $R_i$ and $R_m$, $\Theta_i=(\overline{R_i}-R_f)/\sigma_i$, the Sharpe performance measure associated with the $i$th portfolio, and $\sigma_i$ and $\sigma_m$ are the standard deviation for $i$th portfolio and market portfolio respectively.

Cheung and Kwan show that $\rho_i$ and $C_i^*$ display the same functional behavior for optimal portfolio selection. In other words, if portfolios are formed by adding securities successively from the highest rank to the lowest rank, the optimal portfolio is reached when the correlation of the portfolio returns and the index is at its maximum. Since the expected return on the index must be positive if the investor is to invest in stocks, an objective of the investors using the single-index model to establish the risk–return trade-off is to pick securities that benefit the most from a market upswing. Hence, the role of index in the selection of securities for an optimum portfolio is demonstrated explicitly.

Based upon the single-index model and risk decomposition, the following relationships can be defined:

+   1. $\rho_i=\dfrac{\sigma_{im}}{\sigma_i\sigma_m}$,
+   2. $\sigma_{im}=\beta_i\sigma_m^2$,
+   3. $\sigma_i^2=\beta_i^2\sigma_m^2+\sigma_{\epsilon i}^2$.  \tag{82.20}
