(2010) using copulas on four currencies including US dollar, euro, yen and Hong Kong dollar vis-à-vis Chinese yuan. The risk structure of the portfolios of these exchange rates was considered based on the VaR and conditional VaR measures. They introduced the GARCH-EVT (extreme value theory) copulas and found that the risks of the multiple asset portfolio are best described by the Student’s t copulas.

On the other hand, Cech (2006) has established a copula-based top-down approach to study the dependence structure between risk factor changes for financial risk aggregation, such as interest rate and credit risk. The researcher found that Gaussian copula may under-estimate the dependence structure when there is a strong joint risk factor changes due to extreme events, especially in times of crisis. For this reason, the Archimedean copulas that are more suitable for capturing asymmetrical upper and lower tail dependence were introduced for applications in finance, mainly in the stock and bond market, as well as the currency market.

Copulas with symmetrical densities were also used to study the dependence structure of financial markets with other markets. For example, Gronwald et al. (2011) applied the Gaussian and Student’s t copulas to examine the dependence between the financial, carbon and commodity markets, and found the results to be significant. They also reported greater dependence between these markets during the period of financial crisis. Aloui et al. (2013) modeled the dependence structure between the oil and exchange rate markets using daily crude oil prices and five US dollar exchange rates. They compared a variety of time-varying copulas, but the Student’s t copulas provide the best model for the conditional dependence structure of the extreme co-movements in these markets.

# 73.2.2 Applications of Archimedean copulas

Patton (2002) has applied the copula theory to financial econometrics in his PhD dissertation. He found evidence that copula models usually outperform the multivariate normal distribution models, as a copula process under the Sklar’s (1959) theorem allows an n-dimensional joint distribution function to be formed from different marginal distributions. Consequently, Archimedean copulas can better address the asymmetric dependence structure of non-normally distributed financial data, which is more useful than linear correlation.
