
## Consumption-Based Asset Pricing with Prospect Theory and Habit Formation

This research adopts a simulation method to derive the returns of assets. In real life, at each point in time t, the representative agent chooses the consumption level ct based on the information of the reference point v_t^{10} to maximize his expected utility, Et[Ut]. During the maximizing phase, the returns of the riskless and risky assets between today and the next period are determined. The equity premium is derived by calculating the difference between the arithmetic average of the returns of the riskless and risky assets over time.

Our simulation algorithm is formulated to mimic the above situation. First, assuming the reference point vt and the chosen consumption level ct are given, the conditional rates of return R^B_{t+1}(v_t, c_t) and R^s_{t+1}(v_t, c_t) are derived. Second, because vt and ct changes over time, the arithmetic average over time actually means the arithmetic average over the distribution of the pair of the values of vt and ct. So we obtain the unconditional rates of return R^B_{t+1} and R^s_{t+1} through evaluating the expectations of them over vt and ct. The difference between these two values is the equity premium. The details about how to derive the conditional and unconditional expected rates of return are as follows.

Based on the given values of the reference point vt and the consumption level ct, the steps to derive the conditional rates of return R^B_{t+1}(v_t, c_t) and R^s_{t+1}(v_t, c_t) are as follows:

First, following the assumptions in equations (48.14) and (48.15), 5000 sets of random samples of (g^{t+1}_{t+1}, g^{t+2}_{t+1}, y^{t+1}_t) are generated.11 Based on the information of vt and given the value of ct, then 5000 sets of (c_{t+1}, c_{t+2}, v_{t+1}, v_{t+2}) can be derived.

> Of course, the information of P^s_t / D_t is known too. However, because when calculating the rate of return of the risky asset in our algorithm, what should be derived is the ratio (P^s_{t+1}+D_{t+1})/P^s_t = (D_{t+1}/D_t)(P^s_{t+1}/D_{t+1})/(P^s_t / D_t), the effect of P^s_t / D_t is normalized in our model. Therefore, the expected returns are not conditional on this information.

> These 5000 sets of random samples are used to describe the possible nature of the world in our model. They are sampled once, and kept to calculate all the results under different combinations of the values of the parameters. If different random samples are used to calculate the results of different values of the parameters, it is not able to tell the differences are caused from different values of the parameters or from different random samples.

Second, for each set, equations (48.12) and (48.13) are applied to derive the conditional $R_{t+1}^B(v_t,c_t)$ and $R_{t+1}^s(v_t,c_t)$. However, the value of $M_t^{t+1}(l,m,m)$ for each set should be decided first. Because there is a term $E_t[e^{-\beta\phi_{t+1}(c_{t+1}-v_{t+1})}]$ inside $M_t^{t+1}(l,m,m)$, this expectation is needed to be calculated before deriving the value of $M_t^{t+1}(l,m,m)$ for each set. In this model, the arithmetic average of $e^{-\beta\phi_{t+1}(c_{t+1}-v_{t+1})}$ is derived as the approximation of this expectation. Once we have the value of $E_t[e^{-\beta\phi_{t+1}(c_{t+1}-v_{t+1})}]$, and for each set of $(c_{t+1},c_{t+2},v_{t+1},v_{t+2})$, we judge whether $c_{t+j}-v_{t+j}$ (j = 0, 1, 2) is larger than zero, then we can settle on the right form of $M_t^{t+1}(l,m,m)$ for each set. Given the information of $v_t$ and the chosen value of $c_t$, for each set of values $c_{t+1},c_{t+2},v_{t+1},v_{t+2}$, and $y_t^{t+1}$,^{12} the corresponding values of conditional $R_{t+1}^B(v_t,c_t)$ and $R_{t+1}^s(v_t,c_t)$ can be derived according to equations (48.12) and (48.13). Calculate the arithmetic average of rates of return over the 5000 sample sets, and we can obtain the values of the conditional expected rates of return, $R_{t+1}^B(v_t,c_t)$ and $R_{t+1}^s(v_t,c_t)$.

### 48.2.4.2 Unconditional expected rates of return

In our model, we calculate the unconditional expected rates of return by numerical integration over possible values of $v_t$ and $c_t$. If we assume the probability density function of $v_t$ and $c_t$ to be $f(v_t,c_t)$, the unconditional $R_{t+1}^B$ and $R_{t+1}^s$ are as follows.

$R_{t+1}^B = \int_{v_t}\int_{c_t} R_{t+1}^B(v_t,c_t)f(v_t,c_t)dc_t dv_t,$ $R_{t+1}^s = \int_{v_t}\int_{c_t} R_{t+1}^s(v_t,c_t)f(v_t,c_t)dc_t dv_t.$

In our model, $v_t \sim N(\mu_c,\sigma_c)$ is assumed, where $\mu_c$ and $\sigma_c$ are the mean and the standard deviation of the consumption level calculated from the historical quarterly data of per capita consumption in the US. However, knowing $v_t$ is equivalent to knowing the last period's consumption data, $c_{t-1}$ and $\overline{C}_{t-1}$, there must be some correlation between $v_t$ and today's consumption level, $c_t$. To avoid introducing extra parameters to describe the correlation between $c_t$ and $v_t$, an assumption is adopted so that $\ln(c_t/v_t)=g_t^{t-1}$, where $g_t^{t-1}$ is the consumption growth rate from $t-1$ to $t$ and its distribution is from

## 48.3 Numerical Results

In this section, the values of parameters in our model are discussed first. Then we report the results based on our model. Finally, we show the sensitivity analysis of several important parameters in our model.

### 48.3.1 The settings of parameters

Table 48.1 lists the parameters and their assumed values in our model. The meaning of each parameter value is explained in detail. First, following the dataset of Campbell and Cochrane (1999), we assume both the expected mean of logarithmic consumption growth, $g_t^{t+1}$, and dividend growth, $y_t^{t+1}$, to be 0.0189. As for the standard deviations of these two random variables, we assume the standard deviation of $g_t^{t+1}$ to be 0.015, and the standard deviation of $y_t^{t+1}$ to be 0.112. As for the correlation between $y_t^{t+1}$ and $g_t^{t+1}$, $\rho_{yg}$, one may expect the correlation to approach 1 because dividends and consumption processes ultimately share the same long-term trend. However, in fact, the growth rates of stock market dividends and consumption are only weakly correlated in US data. Following Campbell and Cochrane (1999), the value of the parameter is assumed to be 0.2. In addition, $\rho_{gg}$, which is the correlation between $g_t^{t+1}$ and $g_{t-1}^t$, is taken into consideration, too. Following Mehra and Prescott (1985), the value of this parameter is assumed to be $-0.14$.

Third, the primary feature of our model is to employ prospect theory to describe the behavior of the representative consumer. Hence, there is one more parameter $\lambda$ to capture the loss averse behavior in our model. When the value of $\lambda$ becomes larger, the consumer is more loss averse and therefore more afraid of incurring the loss. In our model, according to the suggested
