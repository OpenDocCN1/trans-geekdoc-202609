+   - **Step 3:** Selecting leaders (or laggers) among the stocks of selected sectors or clusters, still in terms of price strength of each stock relative to a common reference time.
+   - **Step 4:** Grouping the selected stocks with price strength in a multi-stock portfolio.
+   - **Step 5:** Constructing a multi-strategy portfolio for each selected stock, so in total, a multi-stock multi-strategy portfolio is constructed.
+   - **Step 6:** Along with the market moving forward, the comparative strength of each sector or cluster and each stock will change, and even the clustering will produce new clusters. Therefore, there is a need to reiterate the whole process by going back to Step 1.

This procedure is hierarchical in nature, so the stocks with strength, i.e., showing stronger trends are selected with higher quality and reliability of trend because the multi-level strengths from sectors or clusters to individual stocks confirm the trend of the selected stocks with larger background market forces which may come from value appreciation, strategic intent of strategic investors, or herding. The principle here is to stay always with the stronger market forces, so big mistakes or gross errors can be avoided.

This methodology of strength investing can also be called stronger trend following, which is strategically superior than traditional single (asset or market) trend following with following advantages: (1) it shifts investment dynamically to assets (or markets) with stronger trend so the stoploss on the existing positions need not to be triggered, and so the loss due to stoploss triggered is either avoided or minimized; (2) it always concentrates capital on the market leaders with a minimal portfolio, so superior return of investment can be automatically realized.
