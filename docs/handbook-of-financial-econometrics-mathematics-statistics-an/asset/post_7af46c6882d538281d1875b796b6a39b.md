## Appendix 97A: Derivation of Equation (97.16)

This appendix presents a detailed derivation of the solution to the variable partial differential equation, equation (97.14), which is similar to Gould’s (1968) equation (97.9) in investigating the adjustment cost. Following Gould’s (1968) approach, we first derive a general solution for a standard variable partial differential equation. Then we apply this general equation to solve equation (97.14). The standard variable partial differential equation can be defined as

$$\dot{p}(t)+g(t)p(t)=q(t). \tag{97A.1}$$

As a particular case of equation (97A.1), the equation

$$\dot{p}(t)+g(t)p(t)=0\quad\text{or}\quad\frac{\dot{p}(t)}{p(t)}=-g(t), \tag{97A.2}$$

has a solution

$$p(t)=c\cdot\exp\left(-\int g(t)dt\right). \tag{97A.3}$$

By substituting constant $c$ with function $c(t)$, we have the potential solution to equation (97A.1)

$$p(t)=c(t)\cdot\exp\left(-\int g(t)dt\right). \tag{97A.4}$$

Taking a differential with respect to $t$, we obtain

$$\begin{aligned}
\dot{p}(t)&=\dot{c}(t)\cdot\exp\left(-\int g(t)dt\right)-c(t)\cdot\exp\left(-\int g(t)dt\right)g(t)\\
&=\dot{c}(t)\cdot\exp\left(-\int g(t)dt\right)-p(t)g(t).
\end{aligned} \tag{97A.5}$$

Therefore,

$$\dot{p}(t)+p(t)g(t)=\dot{c}(t)\cdot\exp\left(-\int g(t)dt\right). \tag{97A.6}$$

From equations (97A.1) and (97A.6), we have

$$\dot{c}(t)\cdot\exp\left(-\int g(t)dt\right)=q(t). \tag{97A.7}$$
