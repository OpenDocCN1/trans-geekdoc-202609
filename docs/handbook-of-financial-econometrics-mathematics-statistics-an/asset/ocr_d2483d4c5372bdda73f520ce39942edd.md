# (2) Model-Based Passive Trend Following (MPTF):

This type of strategy is based on some stochastic dynamic model of the market price. A typical form is based on geometric Brownian motion and regime-switching models. Dai et al. (2011) developed an optimal trend following trading rule in a bull–bear switching market, where the drift of the stock price switches between two parameters corresponding to an uptrend (bull market) and a downtrend (bear market) according to an unobservable Markov chain. They showed that the optimal trading strategy is a trend following system characterized by the conditional probability in the uptrend (or downtrend) crossing two threshold curves. The thresholds can be obtained by solving the associated Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equations. Naturally, this type of model-based trend following can be extended to more realistic models such as Levy process with heavy tails, stochastic volatility, and jump-diffusion process.

# (3) Prediction-Based Active Trend Following (PATF):

Whether the past or historical price data are useful to predict future price trend has been a controversy since the beginning of quantitative financial theory since Bachelier (1900). Nonetheless our own first-hand modeling and testing of financial market prediction have confirmed that stock indexes and forex rates are predictable probabilistically with a hit rate of daily prediction up to 65%–85% depending on the position in the market ecology.
