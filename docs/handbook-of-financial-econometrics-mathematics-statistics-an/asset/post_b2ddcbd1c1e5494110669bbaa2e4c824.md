## 46.6 Performance Metrics and Their Optimization

Once the equilibrium pmf vector $\pi$ of the Markovian cash conversion process $\{(I(t),C(t),R(t)) : t \ge 0\}$ is computed, we are in a position to compute several key financial metrics associated with the cash conversion process, which are of interest to the firm’s management. These metrics include the equilibrium net profit rate and equilibrium rate of return, which will be defined in the sequel. In the remainder of this chapter, we will assume that the firm’s borrowing is constrained by a maximal debt-to-equity ratio, $H/G$, imposed as a covenant in the loan agreement by the lender. We will then treat a number of optimization problems pertaining to the aforementioned metrics, where the decision variables are three system design parameters: the inventory base stock level, $S$, and the firm’s capital structure, i.e., the allocation of capital investment $G$ and credit limit $H$. More specifically, the following optimization problems will be addressed:

- (A) Given a cash conversion process with available funds maximum, $A = G + H$, optimize the financial metrics with respect to the components, $G$ and $H$.
- (B) Given a cash conversion process, optimize the financial metrics with respect to the parameters $S$, $G$, and $H$.

Naturally, if an optimal financial metric turns out to be non-positive, this would indicate that the system is not financially viable in the long run.

The following parameters will be used in the remainder of this chapter:

- $\omega$ is the gross profit (in PEMUs) from a sale of one inventory item to an arriving customer, that is, a demand that arrives at the inventory and finds it to be non-empty.
- $\eta$ is the holding cost per PEMU in inventory per unit time, excluding financing costs.
- $\gamma$ is the cost rate of capital investment (per unit time).
- $\alpha$ is the fixed cost rate (per unit time) of maintaining a line of credit.^3^
- $\beta$ is the cost rate of borrowing (per unit time).
- $\ell$ is the maximal debt-to-equity ratio, satisfying $\frac{H}{G} \leq \ell$.

### 46.6.1 Equilibrium net profit rate

Let $\pi_I(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{G+H-n}\pi(n,k,G+H-n-k)$ denote the equilibrium marginal pmf of inventory size, and let $\pi_C(k)=\sum_{n=0}^{\min\{S,G+H-k\}}\pi(n,k,G+H-n-k)$ denote the equilibrium marginal pmf of available cash. The *equilibrium net profit rate* is a function of the parameter triple $(S,G,H)$, defined by

$$\phi(S,G,H)=\lambda[1-\pi_I(0)]\omega-\eta\sum_{n=1}^{S}n\pi_I(n)-\gamma G-\alpha H$$
$$\qquad\qquad\quad-\beta\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}(H-k)\pi_C(k). \tag{46.24}$$

Here, the first term on the right-hand side is the equilibrium rate of net operating revenue, the second is the equilibrium rate of the inventory holding cost, the third is the equilibrium rate of the cost of capital investment, the fourth is the fixed cost rate of maintaining the line of credit, and the fifth is the equilibrium cost rate of credit debt. Furthermore, $(1-\pi_I(0))\lambda$ is the rate of demand satisfactions (expected number of items purchased per unit time). Note that the demand satisfaction rate should actually be $(1-\pi_I^{A-}(0))\lambda$, where $\pi_I^{A-}$ is the equilibrium pmf of the inventory size “seen” by arriving demands (that is, the inventory size embedded just before demand arrival times). However, one has $\pi_I^{A-}=\pi_I$ by the PASTA Theorem, which is a special case of the ASTA Theorem (Melamed and Whitt, 1990a, 1990b; Melamed and Yao, 1995).

³This fixed cost represents a borrower’s cost of maintaining a line of credit. Such cost may be considered as a periodic fee, or the borrower’s cost of depositing a so-called compensating balance with the lender as collateral.

Business considerations would preclude systems with a non-positive net profit rate. That is, one needs only search for the solution space of $(S, A)$ that gives rise to $\phi > 0$. From equation (46.24), we have

$$\lambda[1 - \pi_I(0)]\omega - \eta \sum_{n=1}^{S} n\pi_I(n) - \gamma G - \alpha H - \beta \sum_{k=0}^{H-1} (H - k)\pi_C(k) \ge 0$$
$$\Rightarrow \lambda\omega \ge \gamma G + \alpha H \ge \min\{\gamma, \alpha\}(G + H).\tag{46.25}$$

Consequently, the following (loose) bounds on $S$ and $A$ serve to reduce the search space:

$$S \le A = G + H \le \frac{\lambda\omega}{\min\{\gamma, \alpha\}}.\tag{46.26}$$

### 46.6.1.1 Optimization problem (A)

Recall that for a cash conversion process with fixed available funds maximum $A = G + H$ and inventory base stock level $S$, Corollary 46.1 states that the equilibrium statistics of the process depend on $A$ alone regardless of its components, $G$ and $H$, everything else being equal. Lemma 46.6 and Theorem 46.4 below provide useful properties that speed up this optimization by obviating the need to recompute associated equilibrium probabilities.

## Lemma 46.6:

*The function $\Delta\phi(G, H) = \phi(G, H) - \phi(G+1, H-1)$ has the representation*

$$\Delta\phi(G, H) = \gamma - \alpha - \beta \sum_{k=0}^{H-1} \pi_C(k),\tag{46.27}$$

*and is strictly decreasing in $H$ for any $A \ge 2$ and $1 \le H \le \mathcal{B}$, where $\phi(G, H)$ is the equilibrium net profit rate defined in equation (46.24) for given $S, \lambda, \mu, \nu, \omega, \eta, \gamma, \alpha, \beta, \ell$, $\mathcal{B} = \min\{A - 1, \lfloor \frac{\ell}{\ell+1} A \rfloor\}$ and $\lfloor \cdot \rfloor$ is the floor operator.*

**Proof.** For a given $A = G + H$, the constraint $\frac{H}{G} \le \ell$ yields $H \le \frac{\ell}{\ell+1} A$. Since $G \ge 1$ and $H$ is an integer, $H$ is bounded from above by $\mathcal{B}$.

we have for $A \ge 2$ and $1 \le H \le \mathcal{B}$,

$$\Delta\phi(G,H)=\phi(G,H)-\phi(G+1,H-1)$$ $$=\Big[\lambda[1-\pi_I(0)]\omega - \eta\sum_{n=1}^S n\pi_I(n) - \gamma G - \alpha H - \beta\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}(H-k)\pi_C(k)\Big] - \Big[\lambda[1-\pi_I(0)]\omega - \eta\sum_{n=1}^S n\pi_I(n) - \gamma(G+1)-\alpha(H-1)-\beta\sum_{k=0}^{H-2}(H-k-1)\pi_C(k)\Big]$$ $$=\gamma - \alpha - \beta\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}\pi_C(k).$$

Since $\pi_C(k)>0$ for $0\le k\le A$, it follows that $\Delta\phi(G,H)$ is strictly decreasing in $H$.

## Theorem 46.4:

*Given inventory base stock level $S$, rate parameters $\lambda,\mu,\nu$, and financial parameters $\omega,\eta,\gamma,\alpha,\beta,\ell$, let $G$ and $H$ be any capital investment and credit limit parameters, respectively, such that their sum $A=G+H$ is constant. Then, the equilibrium net profit rate $\phi$ of equation (46.24) is maximized at $(G^*,H^*)$, given by*

$$H^* = \begin{cases} 0, & \text{if } A=1 \text{ or } \pi_C(0)\ge \dfrac{\gamma-\alpha}{\beta} \\ \max\Big\{ H:1\le H\le\mathcal{B}, \sum_{k=0}^{H-1}\pi_C(k)<\dfrac{\gamma-\alpha}{\beta}\Big\}, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\tag{46.28}$$

and

$$G^* = A - H^*,\tag{46.29}$$

where $$\mathcal{B}=\min\Big\{A-1,\Big\lfloor\frac{\ell}{\ell+1}A\Big\rfloor\Big\}.$$

**Proof.** Since $G$ is determined by $H$, it suffices to optimize on $H$ alone. To this end, consider the following cases.

**Case 1:** $A=1$. Then clearly $H^*=0$ and $G^*=A-H^*=1$.
