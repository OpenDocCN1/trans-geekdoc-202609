# 54.5.1 Numerical example

As an illustration of the multiple factor transfer pricing model, the Union Plastic Corporation has two divisions that process raw materials into intermediate or final products. Division 1, which is located in Taipai, Taiwan, manufactures two kinds of plastics, called Products 1 and 2. Product 1 in Division 1 is an intermediate product and cannot be sold externally. It can, however, be processed further by Division 2 into a final product. Division 2, which is located in Omaha, Nebraska, USA, processes Product 2 and finalizes Product 1 in Division 1. Central management and two divisional managers agree on the following multiple objectives:

+   (i) Maximize the overall company’s after-tax profit.
+   (ii) Maximize the market share goal of Product 2 in Division 1, and Products 1 and 2 in Division 2.
+   (iii) Maximize the utilized production capacity of the company so that each division manager can avoid any underutilization of normal production capacity.

The data related to these objectives is given in Table 54.1.

In Table 54.1, Product 1 of Division 1 is a byproduct which has no sale value in Division 1 at all. The unit profit −$4 of this product means its production cost. All other products generate profits. We use a market price of each product to maximize the market share goal. Note that the market prices of three products in Table 54.1 are different.
