# 22.5.2.2 Sample from 2003 to 2005

As there are only 128 observation firms in the post-SOX period (2003–2007), we also test the sample year from 2003 to 2005 in the post-SOX period. There are 1,953 observation firms in this post-SOX period, the results of the threshold effect test show that there are two thresholds when FEERATIO = 0.185584 (p-value = 0.05) and FEERATIO = 0.190323 with p-value = 0.087. The threshold values are smaller than those from 2003 to 2007. The regression results are shown in Table 22.9, RANKNON and RANKTOT are positive significantly when FEERATIO is higher than 0.190323. The coefficients of RANKAUD are not significant, but on the contrary it is negative significantly in the subsample which under the threshold value since 2003-2007.

# 22.5.2.3 Sample for big five auditors

Prior research contended that Big Five auditors are less likely to acquiesce to client pressure than non-Big Five ones (Francis et al., 1999). We thus investigate the relation between the Big Five and their clients since 2000–2002, the pre-SOX period. The threshold test is significant (p-value = 0.03667) when FEERATIO = 0.68872. Table 22.10 shows that RANKAUD is negative and significant in the subsample of FEERATIO<=0.68872, which implies that audit fees only reduce the earnings management under certain circumstances. Nevertheless, RANKTOT and RANKNON are significantly positive in both subsamples, and the coefficients in the FEERATIO>0.68872 regressions are almost the double of those in the FEERATIO<=0.68872 regressions. The results suggest that auditors could tolerate more discretionary accruals when clients purchase more nonaudit services.
