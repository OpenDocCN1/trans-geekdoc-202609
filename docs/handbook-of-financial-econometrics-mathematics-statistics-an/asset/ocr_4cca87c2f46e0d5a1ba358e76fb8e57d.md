To determine the quality of financial advisors, prior studies used leagues from the SDC database to measure the level of financial advisors (e.g., Walter et al., 2008; Wang and Whyte, 2010; Golubov et al., 2012). Several studies use a static ranking system to measure the quality of financial advisors (McLaughlin, 1992; Rau, 2000; Hunter and Jagtiani, 2003). However, Da Silva Roas et al. (2004) and Walter et al. (2008) argue that the static ranking system does not take into account the dynamics of the M&A advisory market. As Walter et al. (2008) argue that the reputation of financial advisors depends on its past performance, this study uses market share for each financial advisor in the previous year prior to the announcement date to measure the rank of financial advisors. The market share is measured as a fraction of the total value of transactions advised by investment banks from the previous year.

To determine the quality of financial advisors, this study collects the top 30 investment banks from the league table in the SDC database. Financial advisors are divided into two groups in terms of high- and low-reputable financial advisors to determine the quality of financial advisors. If the market share of financial advisors ranks the top eight banks in the previous year prior to the announcement date, these financial advisors are classified as high-reputable financial advisors; otherwise, as low-reputable financial advisors. If firms hire multiple financial advisors, this study uses the highest rank of financial advisors to measure the quality of financial advisors.²
