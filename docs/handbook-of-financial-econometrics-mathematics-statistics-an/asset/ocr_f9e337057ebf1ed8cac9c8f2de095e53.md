Gupta et al. (1990) proposed linear goal programming as an alternative to discriminant analysis. However, this approach may not be realistic for large-scale databases at this time. Sung et al. (1999) proposed the decision tree approach to develop bankruptcy prediction models for normal and crisis economic conditions. They found different ratios are significant in different economic conditions. Therefore, economic conditions are important factors in bankruptcy prediction studies. The accuracy of their model is 72.4% for bankruptcy under normal conditions while it was 66.7% under crisis conditions using 1991–1998 Korean data. Shin and Lee (2002) proposed a genetic algorithm (GA) in bankruptcy prediction modeling using 264 mid-sized Korean manufacturing companies from 1995 to 1997. Their approach is capable of extracting rules that are easy to understand for users like expert systems. Their accuracy rate is 80.8% in both training and holdout samples. However, this study used only the manufacturing industry and there may be an upward bias for prediction accuracy. In addition, like most GA studies, computational costs of this model can be expensive and there is no guarantee of optimality. Park and Han (2002) proposed a case-based reasoning (CBR) with feature weights derived by an analytic hierarchy process (AHP) for bankruptcy prediction since AHP incorporates both financial ratios as well as non-financial variables into the model. They reported an 84.52% accuracy rate even after incorporating non-financial data. Chi and Tang (2006) used 24 variables to apply logit analysis and the prediction rate was 85%. The bankruptcy law in Korea is governed by the Corporate Reorganization Act of Korea. The idea behind this act is to support firms in financial distress and encourage reorganizing. Most firms are reorganized successfully and continue to exist afterwards (Kim and Kim, 1999). Therefore, the definition of a bankrupt firm in Korea is that the firm filed a bankruptcy petition under the Corporate Reorganization Act of Korea and failed to reorganize.

All previous bankruptcy studies are criticized because of lack of theories, but our focus should be prediction accuracy, flexibility of the model, and easy to apply using real-world data without difficulty of computational complexity. The most prominent bankruptcy studies are Altman’s and Ohlson’s and our study uses their financial variables. These variables are pulled from each bankrupt firm’s financial statements the year before they file bankruptcy. If we want to improve the prediction accuracy, we could easily add cash flows variables or chaebol as a dichotomous variable since chaebols or business groups dominate Korean economy (Bongini et al., 2000).
