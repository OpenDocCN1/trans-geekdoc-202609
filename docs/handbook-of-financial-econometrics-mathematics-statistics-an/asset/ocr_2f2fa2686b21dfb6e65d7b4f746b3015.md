As you can see in the last column of Panel B, all variables between bankrupt and non-bankrupt firms are significantly different except for the size, total liability/total assets ratio, working capital/total assets ratio, market value of equity/book value of total debt ratio, and sales/total assets ratio. The size variable is not significantly different since we matched the control firms based on the size and industry. Since Korea was already in a foreign currency crisis in 1997, the other non-significant variables are similar to either bankrupt or non-bankrupt firms. The market value of equity/book value of total debt is significantly different from Kwak et al.’s (2006b) Japanese bankruptcy study. Generally, Korea and Japan share the same cultural and socio-economic background and the capital market environment is very similar. For example, keiretsu in Japan is similar to chaebol in Korea. Interestingly, the current liability/current assets ratio for Korean bankrupt firms is 1.25 and 1.19 for Japanese bankrupt firms. Net income to total assets are −8.9% for Korean bankrupt firms (minus 5.4% for Japanese), net income is negative for consecutive years (86% vs. 76%), and sales/total assets is 81.34% (80.80%). These variables are similar since Japan and Korea both went through a financial crisis.
