The ex post relationship examined in the present study is based on the sampling distributions of the estimators of performance measures and their risk proxies while Levy (1972) and Levhari and Levy (1977) investigate the relationship in terms of ex ante parameters without specifically considering the sample variation in the estimators. Moreover, their results determine the relationship between n-period Treynor (or Sharpe) measure and its one-period risk measure as the length of the investment horizon varies. In contrast, we examine the m-period correlation between the estimated m-period performance measure and its m-period estimated risk proxy. This m-period correlation can be explained by means of one-period market conditions (R_f and μ) and varying investment horizons. Thus, their studies and our analysis examine two different relationships.12 Nevertheless, the result of our analysis has strengthened the understanding of this important relationship in ex post terms.

In sum, a shorter observation horizon and a large sample size should be used in empirical research to test the relationship between estimated Treynor measure and estimated systematic risk.

It is important to note that the estimated Treynor's measure is a biased estimator of ex ante Treynor's measure, as indicated by equation (68.9)13:

$$E\left(\frac{\bar{R}-R_f}{\hat{\beta}}\right)=\left(\frac{\mu-R_f}{\beta}\right)e_{\beta},\qquad(68.9)$$

$$e_{\beta}=\frac{\beta}{k\sqrt{2\pi}\left(m^*\sigma_{\hat{\beta}}+\beta\right)}$$
