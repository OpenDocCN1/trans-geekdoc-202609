Although, Titman and Wessels (1988) apply SEM to analyze the factors of the capital structure choice, most of their results are not significant in explaining the determinants of the capital structure. Maddala and Nimalendran (1996) note the problematic model specification as the reason for Titman and Wessels’ (1988) poor findings and propose a multiple indicators and multiple causes (MIMIC) model to improve the results. Therefore, Chang et al. (2009) use the MIMIC model to estimate capital structure determinants and compare the results with those obtained by Titman and Wessels (1988). Chang et al. (2009) find that MIMIC model performs better than Titman and Wessels’ (1988) SEM model in estimating the capital structure determinants. In addition, using SEM with confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) approach, Yang et al. (2010) incorporate the stock returns into the simultaneous equations of capital structure determinants. Based on the evidence of Taiwan stock markets, the researchers find that a firm’s capital structure and its stock return are correlated and should be determined simultaneously.

The main purpose of this chapter is to investigate whether the decision regarding the capital structure is associated with financial theories documented in the literature and whether the capital structure interrelates to stock rate of returns simultaneously. Specifically, we attempt to confirm the trade-off theory between the financial distress and agency costs, the pecking order theory, and the signaling theory with asymmetric information in the corporate finance literature. To test these theories, we employ SEM with a CFA approach to classify the observed variables into several groups (attributes) and to test McDonald’s (1983) endogenous supply relationship between short-term public debt and private debt through macroeconomic factors. In addition, the theory of managerial investment autonomy introduced by Dittmar and Thakor’s (2007) can be verified via simultaneous equations of the capital structure and the stock returns.

There are three major findings in this chapter. First, we find that the interaction of a firm’s leverage and its stock return should be explicitly considered in the capital structure research. That is, we can only find significant theoretical attributes regarding the decision of capital structure when the stock return is incorporated into the capital structure decision. Second, our empirical results also confirm the endogenous supply relationship between public and private debts. The negative relation between firms’ leverage ratio and the issue of total public debts is consistent with McDonald’s (1983) theory.
