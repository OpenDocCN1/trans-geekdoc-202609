# Figure 125.1: Logistic and exponential transition functions.

Notes: The figure shows the form of the transition functions used in smooth transition regression. Panel A shows the logistic transition function, $f(z_t)=[1 + \exp(-\gamma(z_t - c))]^{-1}, \gamma > 0$ and Panel B shows the exponential function, $f(z_t) = 1 - \exp(-\gamma(z_t - c)^2), \gamma > 0$.

The alternative exponential transition function is

```
$f(z_t) = 1 - \exp(-\gamma(z_t - c)^2), \gamma > 0. \quad (125.4)$
```

Again $\gamma$ is the smoothness parameter while the function is symmetric around $z_t = c$. This function produces the E-STR model. The E-STR model becomes linear for both $\gamma \to 0$ and $\gamma \to 0$ as $f(z_t)$ becomes constant. Otherwise, the model displays nonlinear behavior. Unlike the L-STR model, E-STR is symmetric, as $(z_t - c) \to \pm\infty, f(z_t) \to 1$ and the behavior of $r_t$ is given by $r_t = (\beta' + \varphi')x_t + e_t$. As $z_t \to c, f(z_t) \to 0$ and the behavior of $r_t$ is given by $r_t = \beta' x_t + e_t$.

# 125.2.2 Specifying and estimating an STR model

Granger and Terasvirta (1993) set out three stages to specify a STR model, specify a linear model, test the linearity and select the transition function.

+   (a) *Specification of a linear model.* The initial step requires the specification of the linear model. The linear model should be appropriate to the strategy under investigation and is not dependent on the use of STR.
+   
+   $r_t = \beta' x_t + u_t, \quad (125.5)$
+   
+   where $r_t$ is the excess return on the hedge fund index, $\beta'$ is the funds exposure to the risk factors, including the intercept $\beta_0$, and $x_t$ is an $n+1 \times t$, consisting of a $1 \times t$ constant array and an $n \times t$ matrix of risk factor returns.
