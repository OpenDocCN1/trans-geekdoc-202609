The bias that results from using non-randomly selected samples to estimate behavioral relationships arises from the ordinary problem of omitted variables. Heckman (1979) thus suggests a simple method to deal with the specification error due to the omitted variable, $E(u_{1i}|u_{2i} > -x_{2i}\beta_2)$ — that is, an appropriate estimate of the omitted variable can be used to solve the problem of sample selection bias. Assuming that $u_1$ and $u_2$ are bivariate-normally distributed, we can evaluate the omitted variable in the regression model as

$$E(u_{1i}|u_{2i} > -x_{2i}\beta_2) = \frac{\sigma_{12}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{22}}}\lambda_i, \tag{111.10}$$ $$E(u_{2i}|u_{2i} > -x_{2i}\beta_2) = \frac{\sigma_{22}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{22}}}\lambda_i, \tag{111.11}$$

where $\lambda_i = \frac{\varphi(z_i)}{\Phi(-z_i)}$ and $z_i = -\frac{x_{2i}\beta_2}{\sqrt{\sigma_{22}}}$.

Here, $\lambda$ is the inverse of Mill's ratio. Based on these results, we have

$$E(y_{1i}|x_{1i}, y_{2i} > 0) = x_{1i}\beta_1 + \frac{\sigma_{12}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{22}}}\lambda_i, \tag{111.12}$$ $$E(y_{2i}|x_{2i}, y_{2i} > 0) = x_{2i}\beta_2 + \frac{\sigma_{22}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{22}}}\lambda_i, \tag{111.13}$$ $$y_{1i} = E(y_{1i}|x_{1i}, y_{2i} \ge 0) + v_{1i},$$ $$y_{2i} = E(y_{2i}|x_{2i}, y_{2i} \ge 0) + v_{2i}, \tag{111.14}$$

where

$$E(v_{1i}|x_{1i}, \lambda_i, u_{2i} \ge -x_{2i}\beta_2) = 0,$$

$$E(v_{2i}|x_{2i}, \lambda_i, u_{2i} \ge -x_{2i}\beta_2) = 0. \tag{111.15}$$

As shown above, the new error terms $v_1$ and $v_2$ retrieve the property of zero mean to ensure the unbiasedness of the OLS estimator. Therefore, if we know $z_i$ and hence $\lambda_i$, then we can enter $\lambda_i$ as a regressor and estimate the equation by OLS. In practice, we do not know $\lambda_i$. If we have the case of a censored sample for $y_2$ — i.e., we know $x_{2i}$ for observations with $y_2 \le 0$ — then we can use the two-stage estimation procedure as proposed in Heckman (1979) to solve the problem of sample selection bias as follows.

**First stage:** Estimate $\Pr(y_2 > 0)$ by Probit to yield a consistent estimator of $\beta_2/\sqrt{\sigma_{22}}$. Next, estimate $z_i$ and hence $\lambda_i$ based on these estimates.

**Second stage:** Use the estimated $\lambda_i$ as a regressor, and OLS will yield unbiased estimators of $\beta_1$ and $\sigma_{12}/\sqrt{\sigma_{22}}$.
