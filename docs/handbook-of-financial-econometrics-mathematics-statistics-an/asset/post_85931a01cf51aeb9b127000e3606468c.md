## 13.5.3 What affects the realized benefits?

To check the robustness of our finding, we run regressions showing how the realized mean-variance efficiency benefit determined by equation (13.9) is affected by the above economic and financial factors. The out-of-sample results should be of interest to asset managers as our empirical findings show whether superior profitability can be generated by the ex ante diversifying portfolio strategies.

Table 13.8 presents the regression results of an increase in the realized risk-adjusted premium. The results from out-of-sample test confirm our overall results. That is, R² is more associated with diversification benefit than correlation. Inflation, TED spread, country risk, turnover, and the relative performance of the local portfolios in the world market (RSR) have significant explanatory power on the benefits of mean-variance efficiency. In contrast to the in-sample test in Table 13.6, country risk increases diversification benefit. The p-value suggests R² is a more appropriate measure of market integration than correlation; although, the significance is lower than that from the in-sample tests. We get the same conclusion from the F-test of instruments and the Hansen–Sargan test.

## 13.6 Which Economic Determinants Matter in Diversification Benefits?

Section 13.5 shows several variables that determine diversification benefits. However, what loading factors are more critical to the size of diversification benefits? What is explanatory power of the economic determinants on diversification benefits? Understanding this issue helps investors prioritize variables in formulating international investment strategies with other countries of differing characteristics. In this section, we apply the methodology proposed by Bekaert et al. (2011) and perform two examinations on the effects of each independent variable to the diversification benefit.

Further, we examine how much variation in the diversification benefit is explained by each independent variable and what are their individual contributions. We estimate R² as

$\frac{\text{Var}(\hat{\delta}_{j,t})}{\text{Var}(\delta_{j,t})}$

where

$\hat{\delta}_{j,t}= \hat{a}_k + \hat{\beta}\delta_{j,t-1} + \sum_{i=1}^{N} \hat{\gamma}_{j,i,t}$

and $x_{j,i,t}$ is an independent variable $i$ at time $t$ of country $j$. The denominator is

$\text{Var}(\delta_{j,1}) = \frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{1}{T_j}\sum_{t=1}^{T_j}\left(\hat{\delta}_{j,t} - \bar{\hat{\delta}}\right)^2$

where

$\bar{\hat{\delta}} = \frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{1}{T_j}\sum_{t=1}^{T_j}\hat{\delta}_{j,t}$

and the numerator is defined as $\text{Var}(\hat{\delta}_{j,t}) = \frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T_j}\left(\hat{\delta}_{j,t} - \bar{\hat{\delta}}\right)^2$, where $\bar{\hat{\delta}} = \frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T_j}\hat{\delta}_{j,t}$

We then estimate the contributions of each independent variable for the overall diversification benefit from the following covariance. Let

$\text{Cov}(\hat{\delta}_{j,t}, \hat{\gamma}_i x_{j,i,t}) = \frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{1}{T_j}\sum_{t=1}^{T_j}\hat{\gamma}_i\left(\hat{\delta}_{j,t} - \bar{\hat{\delta}}\right)^2\left(x_{j,i,t} - \bar{x}_i\right), \qquad (13.13)$
