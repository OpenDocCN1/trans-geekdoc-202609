## Theorem 41.3.5: Let $D_1$ be the differential operator with the characteristic polynomial

$P_1$ given by (41.19). Then $D_1\Phi\equiv0$ on $R_{++}$.

Proof. Let $L_2=L_2(R)$ be the space of square-integrable functions defined on $R$, and set $(f_1,f_2)=\int f_1(x)f_2(x)dx$. For an operator $A$ on $L_2$, write $A^*$ for its adjoint (i.e., $\langle Ak,h\rangle=\langle kA^*h\rangle$ for all $k$, $h$ in $L_2$). By integration by parts, we have $\langle k',h\rangle=-\langle k h'\rangle$ whenever $k$ or $h$ has compact support. Write $Th(x)=\int h(x-y)f(y)dy$. By a change of variables and Fubini's theorem, we have

$\langle Th,k\rangle=\int k(x)\int h(x-y)f(y)dydx=\int h(z)\int k(z-y)f(-y)dydz=\langle h,T^*k\rangle$

where $T^*k(x)\equiv\int k(x-y)f(-y)dy$.

Let $D_0$ be the differential operator with the characteristic polynomial $P_0$ and $\phi\in C_c^\infty(R_{++})$ a test function. Recall that, by Theorem 41.2.4, $(L-r)\phi\equiv0$ on $R_{++}$. Therefore, we have

$0=\langle D_0(L-r)\phi,\phi\rangle=\langle\phi(L^*-r)D_0^*\phi\rangle.\quad(41.24)$

Here $L^*$ is given by

$L^*h(x)=D h''(x)-c h'(x)+\lambda T^*h(x)-\lambda h(x).$

Set $L_D=L-r-\lambda T$. Then $(L^*-r)D_0^*\phi=\lambda T^*D_0^*\phi+L_D^*D_0^*\phi=\lambda T^*D_0^*\phi+(D_0L_D)^*\phi.\quad(41.25)$

Write $D_2$ as the differential operator corresponding to the polynomial $P_2(\xi)=P_0(\xi)\psi(\xi)$. We prove that $T^*D_0^*\phi=D_2\phi$ a.e. by showing that both $T^*D_0^*\phi$ and $D_2^*\phi$ are in $L_2\cap L_1$ and have the same Fourier transforms. (Here $L_1=L_1(R)$ is the space of integrable functions on $R$.)

First, we show that $T^*D_0^*\phi$ and $D_2^*\phi$ are in $L_1\cap L_2$. Since $\phi\in C_c^\infty(R_{++})$, we obtain $D_2^*\phi\in L_1\cap L_2$. Also, $\int|T^*D_0^*\phi(x)|dx\le\iint|D_0^*\phi(x-y)|dx f(-y)dy\le\|D_0^*\phi\|_{L_1}\|f\|_{L_1}<\infty.$

And hence $T^* D_0^* \phi \in L_1$. We show that $T^* D_0^* \phi \in L_2$. Since $D_0^* \phi \in C_c^\infty(R_{++})$ and $T^* h(x) = \int h(x-y)f(-y)dy$, it suffices to show that $T k \in L_2$ for any $k \in C_c^\infty(R_{++})$. Note that, by Theorem 41.B.1, $f$ has tails that decay exponentially and, hence, $\int f^2 dx < \infty$. Let $k \in C_c^\infty(R_{++})$ and write $H$ for the compact support of $k$. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality we get

$$\int [T k(x)]^2 dx = \int \left( \int k(x-y)f(y)dy \right)^2 dx = \int \left( \int_H k(Y)f(x-y)dy \right)^2 dx \le \int \left( \int k(y)^2 dy \right) \left( \int_H f(x-y)^2 dy \right) dx \le \|k\|_{L_2}^2 \|f\|_{L_2}^2 \int_H dx < \infty.$$

Next, we show that the Fourier transforms $F(T^* D_0^* \phi)$ and $F(D_2^* \phi)$ coincide, where $Fh(\theta) = \int e^{-2\pi i \theta x} h(x)dx$. Recall that $\psi_1(\xi) = \int e^{-\xi y} f(y)dy$ and notice that $F(D_0^* \phi)(\theta) = P_0(-2\pi i \theta)F(\phi)(\theta)$ (see Stein and Shakarchi (2005), Section 5.3). Since $T^* D_0^* \phi \in L_1 \cap L_2$, we have, for all $\theta \in R$,

$$F(T^* D_0^* \phi)(\theta) = \int e^{-2\pi i \theta x} \left( \int D_0^* \phi(x-y)f(-y)dy \right) dx = \int \left( \int D_0^* \phi(x-y)e^{-2\pi i \theta (x-y)}dx \right) e^{-2\pi i \theta y} f(-y)dy = \psi_1(-2\pi i \theta)P_0(-2\pi i \theta)F(\phi)(\theta) = P_2(-2\pi i \theta)F(\phi)(\theta) = F(D_2^* \phi)(\theta).$$

By the Fourier inversion formula, we deduce that $T^* D_0^* \phi = D_2^* \phi$ almost everywhere.

By (41.25) and the fact that $T^* D_0^* \phi = D_2^* \phi$ a.e., $(L^* - r)D_0^* \phi = \lambda D_2^* \phi + (D_0 L_D)^* \phi = D_1^* \phi$ a.e. Hence, by (3.11), we have $$0 = \langle \Phi, (L^* - r)D_0^* \phi \rangle = \langle \Phi, D_0^* \phi \rangle = \langle D_1 \Phi, \phi \rangle.$$ Since $\phi \in C_c^\infty(R_{++})$ is arbitrary, we have $D_1 \Phi = 0$ a.s. on $(0,\infty)$. Since $\Phi \in C_c^\infty(R_{++})$, $D_1 \Phi \equiv 0$ on $R_{++}$. This completes the proof.

## Example 41.3.9 (Optimal capital structure).

We shall assume that, under a risk-neutral measure $Q$, the value of the firm’s assets is given by $V_t = V e^{c t + \sigma W_t - Z_t}$. The setting of debt issuance and coupon payment follows the convention in Leland and Toft (1996). In the time interval $(t, t + dt)$, the firm issues new debt with face value $a dt$ and maturity profile $k(t) = m e^{-m t}$. With the exponential maturity profile, the face value of the debt maturing in $(t, t + dt)$ is the same as the face value of the newly issued debt. Thus, the face value of all pending debt is equal to the constant $A = a \int_0^\infty e^{-m t} dt = a / m$. All bondholders will receive coupons at rate $b$ until default. All debt is of equal seniority and, once default occurs, the bondholders get the rest of the value, $\beta V_\tau$, after the bankrupt cost $(1 - \beta) V_\tau$. We assume that there is a corporate tax rate $\delta$, and the coupons paid can be offset against tax. We further assume that the default occurs at time $\tau = \inf\{t \ge 0; V_t \le L\}$, where $L$ will be determined optimally later on. As in Hieberink and Rogers (2002), we assume that the market has a risk-free rate $r > 0$, and $\mu > 0$ is the proportional rate at which profit is disbursed to investors. Then the total value at time 0 of all debt is

$$D(V, L) = \frac{bA + mA}{m + r} E_Q\left[1 - e^{-(m+r)\tau}\right] + \beta E_Q\left[V_\tau e^{-(m+r)\tau}\right] \qquad (41.29)$$

and the value of the firm is

$$v(V, L) = V + \frac{bA\delta}{r} E_Q\left[1 - e^{-r\tau}\right] - (1 - \beta)E_Q\left[V_\tau e^{-r\tau}\right]. \qquad (41.30)$$

The value of equity of the firm is given by

$$S(V, L) = v(V, L) - D(V, L), \qquad (41.31)$$
