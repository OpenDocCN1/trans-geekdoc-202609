where $\tilde N_j = N - L_j + 1 > 0$ is the number of maximum overlap coefficients at scale $\lambda_j$ and $L_j = (2^j - 1)(L - 1) + 1$ is the length of the wavelet filter for level $j$.

The wavelet covariance between two stochastic processes {$X$ and $Y$} at scale $\lambda_j$ with level $j$ is defined as the covariance between wavelet coefficients of {$X$ and $Y$}, that is

$\gamma_{XY}(\lambda_j) = \mathrm{Cov}\left(\tilde W_{j,t}^X, \tilde W_{j,t}^Y\right). \tag{36.21}$

An unbiased estimator of the covariance is (Whitcher et al., 1999, 2000; Gallegati, 2012):

$\tilde\gamma_{XY}(\lambda_j) = \frac{1}{\tilde N_j} \sum_{t=L_j-1}^{N-1} \tilde W_{j,t}^{(X)} \tilde W_{j,t}^{(Y)}. \tag{36.22}$

The wavelet correlation between two stochastic processes {$X$ and $Y$} at scale $\lambda_j$ with level $j$ is defined as the ratio of the wavelet covariance, $\gamma_{XY}(\lambda_j)$, and the square root of their wavelet variance, that is

$\rho_{XY}(\lambda_j) = \frac{\gamma_{XY}(\lambda_j)}{\nu_X(\lambda_j) \nu_Y(\lambda_j)}. \tag{36.23}$

where $\nu_X^2(\lambda_j)$ and $\nu_Y^2(\lambda_j)$ are the wavelet variances of $X$ and $Y$, respectively, and $|\rho_{XY}(\lambda_j)| \le 1.$

An unbiased estimator of the wavelet correlation is

$\tilde\rho_{XY}(\lambda_j) = \frac{\tilde\gamma_{XY}(\lambda_j)}{\tilde\nu_X(\lambda_j) \tilde\nu_Y(\lambda_j)}. \tag{36.24}$
