Nonetheless, while the DCC-GARCH model is appealing for modeling the DCC in a parsimonious way, it has raised some controversy among researchers. For instance, Caporin and McAleer (2013) provide several caveats about the practical use of the DCC-GARCH model, while Aielli (2013) undertakes a thorough investigation of the properties and estimation methods of the DCC model and clarifies logically as well as numerically the contributions and limitations of Engle (2002). Aielli (2013) also suggests a correction of the DCC model to a more tractable form as the cDCC model. However, the numerical performance of the DCC model is almost identical to that of the cDCC model over a typical range of dynamic parameters (Aielli, 2013).

Second, we investigate how the DCC-GARCH model works in practice through two empirical applications to the integration process of bond and stock markets in the emerging East Asian countries, comprising the ASEAN5 (Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, and Thailand), China, Hong Kong and Korea. Following Kim and Lee (2012, p. 333), we take a fully integrated financial market as representing the situation in which traders can transact financial assets freely within the area.² Based on this definition, our analysis focuses on the commonality of bond yield and stock price movements between different markets in the region over time. The degrees of commonality are measured numerically by the DCCs and dynamic variance decompositions. By way of these measures, we assess the levels of bond and stock market integration in the region. In addition, we also assess the trends in integration during the past 15 years in the region. East Asia has grown rapidly during the past 25 years and is currently recognized as the growth center of the world economy, despite the experiences of the Asian financial crisis in 1997–98 and the global financial crisis in 2007–08. The emerging East Asia local currency bonds and stocks listed in the markets in this region have thus become indispensable asset classes for global investors. Hence, the investigation of financial market integration in the region is of great interest for practitioners as well as academics.³
