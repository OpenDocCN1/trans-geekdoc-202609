The factors would include the three latent factors described above. But, in our implementation we focus on the yield curve with macro factors in DRA and add three key measures of our macroeconomy to our vector of factors; capacity utilization (CU_t), inflation (INFL_t), and the fed funds rate (FFR_t), with data on all three measures for the US economy being collected from the Federal Reserve Economic Database (FRED). Assuming that the yields still load only on the three latent factors, we assume that the matrix in the abovementioned measurement error equation is N × 6 with the three right most columns assumed to be zeros. Thus, the structure of the model remains parsimonious and estimation via a Kalman filtering algorithm is simple in principle.7 We assume that as many yields as factors are measured with error and include the six-month, one-year, two-year, five-year, seven-year, and 10-year in our estimation. We also follow the authors and assume that ε_t and η_t are orthogonal to each other and the initial state from which we begin the filtering algorithm. We also assume that these two error processes follow Gaussian white noise dynamics with zero mean and diagonal covariance structure. This means that we have 55 parameters to estimate in our state space formulation (1 for Γ, 6 for μ, 36 for A, 6 for factor covariance matrix, 6 for for measurement error covariance matrix). So, although this model has a parsimonious structure, due to the fact that it requires 55 parameters to estimate, it is challenging to estimate this model. Fortunately, the authors provide, in Table 7 of their paper (Ang and Piazzesi, 2003), their parameter estimates and note that they initialize λ in the filtering algorithm at the value specified in Diebold and Li (2006) which is 0.0609. Using these initial conditions greatly shortened the computation time and resulted in remarkably small cross-sectional fitting errors for the six examined yields with varying maturities measured in months; 7.0466 basis points for the bond yield with a six-month maturity, 5.9957 basis points for the one-year bond yield, 5.8202 for the two-year bond yield, 6.6047 basis points for the five-year bond yield, 7.2423 basis points for the bond yield with a seven-year maturity, and 17.3465 basis points for the 10-year bond yield. These errors are also quite close to those reported by DRA.8 We present a plot of the model fitted dynamics over the sample period under examination implied by the DRA model in Figure 61.1.
