Past studies showed that the dependence structure of financial returns cannot be accurately estimated using these methods when extreme events occur, especially in times of crisis. A solution to overcome this problem is to use copulas for examining dependence between financial asset returns. Generally, copula is premised on probability theories used to describe the dependence structure between variables. Copulas are derived based on Sklar’s theorem. They are multivariate probability (joint) distributions combined by a number of univariate (marginal probability) distributions, where the marginal distribution of each variable (continuous) is uniform.

The copula approach requires that the standardized financial returns be transformed into a standardized Student’s t-distribution to change the domain of the distribution to [0,1] that approximates a uniform distribution through the probability integral transform. Subsequently, a nonlinear copula model can be applied to the nonlinear and non-normally distributed financial data to examine their tail dependence structure. Typically, elliptical copulas such as the Gaussian and Student’s t copulas are used to capture two-tailed dependence structure, while Archimedean copulas such as Gumbel (upper tail) and Clayton (lower tail) are used to capture one-tailed dependence.

The two-step full MLE method is easy for practical applications. All the parameters of marginal densities are firstly estimated, and then the estimated parameters are used in the subsequent step to estimate the copulas. The estimated copula parameters are used to determine the lower and upper tail dependence coefficients. In general, elliptical copulas have symmetric tail dependence. Gaussian copulas have zero or very weak tail dependence, while Student’s t copulas have relatively strong tail dependence. The latter are often more relevant for applications in finance, as financial returns are often known to have fatter tails. Conversely, Archimedean copulas allow for asymmetric tail dependence. The Clayton copulas have zero upper tail dependence but a positive lower tail dependence coefficient, while the Gumbel copulas have zero lower tail dependence but a positive upper tail dependence coefficient. As the Archimedean copulas do not assume symmetrical tail distribution, they are suitable for examining the dependence structure between financial assets where return distributions are typically asymmetrical due to non-symmetrical responses to good and bad market news.
