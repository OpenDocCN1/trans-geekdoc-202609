In the digital era, banking services have become more closely connected to mobile devices. Another significant factor in the model (−0.185) is the perceived effort that negatively influences the intention to use mobile banking. It is also similar to the results of the existing study on mobile banking in different countries (Luarn and Lin, 2005; Hanafizadeh *et al.*, 2014). The size of screens and keypads of the mobile device requires customers to concentrate on them more carefully, while using mobile apps, lowering the ease of use. Mobile banking helps banks offer many new services; however, this also makes mobile apps more complex. The easier an app is to use, the more likely the customer will use the app.

The next factor is perceived financial costs (−0.144). The same results are obtained in Luarn and Lin (2005) and Hanafizadeh *et al.* (2014). They showed that this factor was important in different countries. Extra costs have a strong negative impact on the intention to use mobile banking. Moreover, even small fees could be crucial for young people when deciding whether or not to use mobile banking (TASS, 2016).
