# Corollary 46.1:

Given rate parameters λ, μ, ν > 0, the equilibrium probability vector π depends only on the inventory base stock level, S, and the firm's available funds maximum, A = G + H, and not on the individual values of the capital investment G and credit limit H.

# Proof.

The state space of {(I(t),C(t),R(t)) : t ≥ 0} is determined by S and A. The corollary follows since for any fixed λ, μ, ν, S, and A, the transition rate matrix remains unchanged regardless of the values of G and H, as long as their sum is fixed.

Next, the balance equations corresponding to equation (46.7) in the unknown equilibrium probability vector π = [π(n,k,j)], (n,k,j) ∈ S, can be written as the following system of equations:

1̄_{S}(n) 1̄_{0}(j)π(n+1,k,j−1)λ  (a)
+ 1̄_{0}(n) 1̄_{G+H}(k)π(n−1,k+1,j)L_{n−1,k+1}μ  (b)
+ 1̄_{0}(k)1̄_{G+H}(j)π(n,k−1,j+1)(j+1)ν  (c)
=  (46.8)
1̄_{0}(n)1̄_{G+H}(j)π(n,k,j)λ  (d)
+ 1̄_{S}(n)1̄_{0}(k)π(n,k,j)L_{n,k}μ  (e)
+ 1̄_{G+H}(k)1̄_{0}(j)π(n,k,j)jν.  (f)

Here, line (a), (b) and (c) correspond to transitions into state (n,k,j) ∈ S due to demand arrival at the inventory, replenishment arrival at the inventory and receivable arrival at the available cash pool, respectively, while lines (d), (e) and (f) correspond to transitions out of state (n,k,j) ∈ S due to the same events, respectively.

# Lemma 46.1:

Equation (46.8) has a unique solution, which is the equilibrium probability pmf of the Markov process {(I(t),C(t),R(t)) : t ≥ 0}.

# Proof.

The equilibrium solution is unique because all states (n,k,j) ∈ S are (1) irreducible, namely, all states communicate, and (2) positive recurrent and non-null since the state space is finite. It follows that there exists a unique equilibrium pmf.
