Next, we study the association with domestic financial market development. To measure domestic equity market development, we consider dividend yield, market turnover, price/earnings per share (PE) ratio, market capitalization per GDP, and number of listed stocks in national exchange(s). To capture the development of the banking sector, we include the amount of private credit per GDP. Higher domestic financial market quality implies lower diversification benefits. Further, we include the domestic economic conditions relative to international markets on the diversification benefits. The variables include exchange rate risk, correlation coefficient between a domestic market and other countries, integration measure R² from Pukthuanthong and Roll (2009), and relative Sharpe ratio (RSR) of the domestic portfolio compared to international markets. We construct a RSR of country X’s portfolio relative to the world market as follows:

RSR_Country X = [SR_Country X − SR_min)/(SR_Max − SR_Min)], (13.10)

where SR_Max and SR_Min represent the maximum and minimum of the national Sharpe ratios in the world market, respectively. As shown in Figure 13.1, the cross-country comparative gauge specifies the risk-adjusted performance of a domestic portfolio relative to the rest of the world and can vary over time. Exchange rate risk should increase diversification benefits whereas high RSR should decrease potential diversification benefits.
