# 69.2 Statistical Relationship Between the Estimated Sharpe Measure and its Risk Proxy

Following Sharpe (1966), the Sharpe performance measure can be defined as

$$SP = \frac{\bar{R}_i - R_f}{S_i},\tag{69.1}$$

where $\bar{R}_i$ is the average rate of return on a portfolio (or an asset) $i$, $R_f$ is the risk-free rate of interest, and $S_i$, the sample standard deviation of ex post data for portfolio $i$, can be defined as

$$S_i = \left[\frac{\sum_{t=1}^n (R_{it} - \bar{R}_i)^2}{n}\right]^{1/2}.\tag{69.2}$$

Before deriving the empirical relationship between the estimated Sharpe's measure and its risk proxy ($S_i$), the distributions of $\bar{R}_i$ and $S_i$ are first discussed briefly. If the rate of return of securities in the population is normally distributed with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$,³ then a set of sample rates of return for portfolio $i$ can be considered as random sample drawn from the normal population. Assume that the holding period coincides with the true investment horizon. The “investment horizon” concept will be explored in detail in section 69.3. It follows that the sampling distribution of the average rate of return, $\bar{R}_i = \sum_{t=1}^n R_{it}/n$, is normally distributed with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2/n$, where $n$ is the sample size. The random variable $n S_i^2/\sigma^2$ has a $\chi^2$ distribution with $(n - 1)$ degrees of freedom (see Hogg and Craig (1970)). For simplicity, $\bar{R}$ and $nS^2/\sigma^2$ denote $\bar{R}_t$ and $n S_i^2/\sigma^2$, respectively.

To investigate the relationship between the estimate of Sharpe's measure and the estimated risk measure, the probability density function (p.d.f.) of the estimated risk ($S$) is derived first. Following Hogg and Craig (1970), the p.d.f. of the random variable, $n S^2/\sigma^2 (y_1)$, can be defined as

$$f_1(y_1)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{(n-1)}{2}\right)2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\cdot y_1^{((n-1)/2 -1)}\cdot e^{-y_1/2}, & \text{for }0<y_1<\infty\\0, & \text{elsewhere}\end{cases}\tag{69.3}$$
