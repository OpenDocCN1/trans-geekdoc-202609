# 13.7 Conclusion

We analyze the impact of various economic determinants, including market integration, on the time-variation of diversification benefits while considering feasibility of the optimal portfolios. Our measure of diversification benefits specifically considers portfolio diversity and our measure of market integration contemporaneously considers time-varying return and risk structure across markets. We find that the benefits of international diversification to domestic investor still survive despite an increasing integration of the global financial market. The results are consistent across measures of diversification benefits using ex ante and realized mean-variance and volatility. The regression analysis and explanatory power tests confirm the superior impact of market integration to correlation, on diversification benefits. In addition, we identify that some important factors, such as RSR and default risk, play a critical role in determining the benefits. The robust empirical evidence suggests investors prioritize the decision variables in global investments.

We evaluate the benefits of international diversification to the local investor in various countries while considering strategy feasibility. Diversification has traditionally been regarded to be effective when assets or portfolios are weakly correlated. Our result supports the literature that R² from the multifactor model is a more appropriate measure of market integration than correlation. Although R² and correlation significantly decrease diversification benefit, R² is more statistically and economically significant. Investors and academicians should benefit from our findings as to the impact of business cycle on the effectiveness of global diversification. Future research may take transaction costs into account.
