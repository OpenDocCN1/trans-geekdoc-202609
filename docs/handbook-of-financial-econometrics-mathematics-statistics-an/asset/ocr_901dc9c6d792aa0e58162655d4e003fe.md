# Proof of Proposition 41.4.1.

Let $x > 0$. Similar to the proof of (41.10), we can pick a sequence of uniformly bounded functions $(\phi_n) \in C_0^2(R)$ such that $\phi_n \equiv \phi$ on $R_+$ and $\phi_n \to \phi$ a.e. on $(-\infty, 0)$. By Dynkin's formula, we have

$$E_x\big[e^{-r(t\wedge\tau)}\phi_n(X_{t\wedge\tau})\big] = E_x\left[\int_0^{t\wedge\tau} e^{-ru}(L - r)\phi_n(X_u)du\right] + \phi(x).$$ (41A.6)

Note that $\phi_n$ and $\phi$ only differ on $(-\infty,0)$ and $X_u > 0$ for all $u < t\wedge\tau$. Moreover, the jump distribution of $Z$ is absolutely continuous. Hence, similarly to the proof of (41A.5), we have

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} E_x\left[\int_0^{t\wedge\tau} e^{-ru}(L - r)\phi_n(X_u)du\right] = E_x\left[\int_0^{t\wedge\tau} e^{-ru}(L - r)\phi(X_u)du\right].$$

By dominated convergence and Lemma 41.A.2(1), letting $n \to \infty$ on both sides of (41A.6) gives

$$E_x\big[e^{-r(t\wedge\tau)}\phi(X_{t\wedge\tau})\big] = E_x\left[\int_0^{t\wedge\tau} e^{-ru}(L - r)\phi(X_u)du\right] + \phi(x) = \phi(x).$$ (41A.7)

Note that the last equality follows from the assumption that $(L - r)\phi \equiv 0$ on $R_{++}$. Let $t \to \infty$ on both sides of the last equality. Since $r > 0$, the result follows by the fact that $e^{-rt}1_{[\tau > t]} \to 0$ as $t \to \infty$. This completes the proof.
