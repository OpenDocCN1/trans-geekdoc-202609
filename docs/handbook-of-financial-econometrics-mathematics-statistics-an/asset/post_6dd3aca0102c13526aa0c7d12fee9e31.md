
that $\Phi(x,T) \le (1 - e^{\lambda T}) + G_A(x,T)$. Therefore, we obtain

$$\limsup_{T\to0+} s(V,T) = \limsup_{T\to0+} \frac{1}{V} \log\left(\frac{1}{1-\Phi(x,T)}\right)$$ $$\le \lim_{T\to0+} \frac{1}{T} \log\left(\frac{1}{e^{-\lambda T} - G_A(x,T)}\right).$$

Note that $G_A(x,T) \le \Phi(x,T) \to 0$ as $T \to 0+$ and, thence, $\lim_{T\to0+}(e^{-\lambda T} - G_A(x,T)) = 1$. By L'Hospital's rule, we have

$$\lim_{T\to0+} \frac{1}{T} \log\left(\frac{1}{e^{-\lambda T} - G_A(x,T)}\right) = \lim_{T\to0+} \frac{-(-\lambda e^{-\lambda T} - \frac{\partial G_A}{\partial T}(x,T))}{e^{-\lambda T} - G_A(x,T)}$$ $$= \lim_{T\to0+} \left(\lambda + \frac{\partial G_A}{\partial T}(x,T)\right).$$

To compute $\lim_{T\to0+} \frac{\partial G_A}{\partial T}(x,T)$, we note that $G_A(x,T) = \psi(0)e^{-\lambda T} P[\min_{s\le T} s + c s + \sigma W_s \le 0]$.

By Lemma 110.7, we get $\lim_{T\to0+} \frac{\partial G_A}{\partial T}(x,T) = 0$. We have obtained the upper bound in (110.21). This completes the proof. $\square$

## Appendix 110A Infinite Series Expression for Bond Price

Now, consider the space $E_T \triangleq R_+ \times [0,T]$, $T > 0$. Take $E = R_+ \times [0,\infty)$ for notational convenience. For every bounded measurable function $f$ on $E$, the operator $L$ is defined by the formula,

$L f(x,t) = \int_0^t dF_{J_1}(s) \int dF(y) \int_{y^+}^{\infty} dw\, f(w - y, t - s) H(x,w,s),$

where $H$ is defined in (110.15). Then by Theorem 110.4, we can write $\Phi$ as

$\Phi(x,t) = G(x,t) + L\Phi(x,t), \quad \forall (x,t) \in E. \tag{110A.1}$

Now, after $n$th iteration of (110A.1), we have

$\Phi(x,t) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} L^k G(x,t) + L^{n+1}\Phi(x,t), \tag{110A.2}$

where $L^0 = Id$ and $L^{k+1} f = L(L^k f)$. From the definition of $f$, one sees that

$L f(x,t) = E_x\Big[ f(X_{J_1}, t - J_1) 1\big(J_1 \le t, \min_{0\le s\le J_1} X_s > 0\big)\Big].$

In general, after $n$th iteration, we have the following result.

### Lemma 110A.1. Let $f$ be a nonnegative measurable function. Then, for any $n \in N$,

$L^n f(x,t) = E_x\Big[ f(X_{J_n}, t - J_n) 1\big(J_n \le t, \min_{0\le s\le J_n} X_s > 0\big)\Big]. \tag{110A.3}$

Moreover, if $f$ is bounded, $L^n f$ converges to zero as $n \to \infty$ uniformly on $E_T$.

### Proof.

The proof proceeds with induction. We already have the case $n = 1$ by the definition of $L$.

Assume that for $n = k$ the conclusion of the lemma holds. Then for $n = k + 1$,

$L^{k+1} f(x,t) = E_x\Big[ L^k f(X_{J_1}, t - J_1) 1\big(J_1 \le t, \min_{0\le s\le J_1} X_s > 0\big)\Big] = E_x\Big[ E_{X_{J_1}}\big[ f(X_{J_k}, v - J_k) 1\big(J_k \le t, \min_{0\le s\le J_k} X_s > 0\big)\big] \Big|_{v = t - J_1} 1\big(J_1 \le t, \min_{0\le s\le J_1} X_s > 0\big)\Big]$

where we have applied the case of (110A.3) for n = k in the last line. On the other hand, by the Strong Markov Property of X, we have

$$E_x\left[f(X_{J_{k+1}},t-J_{k+1})1\left(J_{k+1}\le t,\min_{0\le s\le J_{k+1}}X_s>0\right)\right]$$

$$=E_x\left[E_x\left[f(X_{J_{k+1}},t-J_{k+1})1\left(J_{k+1}\le t,\min_{0\le s\le J_{k+1}}X_s>0\right)\big|F_{J_1}\right]\right]$$

$$1\left(J_1\le t,\min_{0\le s\le J_1}X_s>0\right)$$

$$=E_x\left[E_{X_{J_1}}\left[f(X_{J_k},v-J_k)1\left(J_k\le t,\min_{0\le s\le J_k}X_s>0\right)\right]\bigg|_{v = t - J_1}1\left(J_1\le t,\min_{0\le s\le J_1}X_s>0\right)\right].$$

Hence, we have proved that (110A.3) holds for n = k + 1. By induction hypothesis, we have proved the first part of the lemma.

Note that for any $(x,t)\in E_T$, we have

$$0\le L^n f(x,t)=E_x\left[f(X_{J_n},t-J_n)1\left(J_n\le t,\min_{0\le s\le J_n}X_s>0\right)\right]$$

$$\le\|f\|_\infty P[J_n\le t]\le\|f\|_\infty P[J_n\le T].$$

Since $J_n$ is the $n$th jump time of the compound Poisson process, we have $P[J_n\le T]=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda T}\frac{(\lambda T)^m}{m!}$. This implies that $L^n f$ converges to zero uniformly on $E_T$ as $n\to\infty$.

From this, we obtain from (110A.2) that for every bounded $\psi$,

$$\Phi(x,T)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}L^kG(x,T)+L^{n+1}\Phi(x,T)\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}L^kG(x,T).$$

Write $x=\log(V_0/K_0)$. By (110.6), we get the following infinite series expression for bond prices. $\square$

### Theorem 110A.2. For the general nonnegative bounded writedown function $\psi$, the bond price (110.6) is given by the formula

$$D(V_0,T)=e^{-rT}-e^{-rT}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}L^mG(\log(V_0/K_0),T),\qquad(110A.5)$$

*where G = G_A + G_B + G_C and G_A, G_B, and G_C are given as in (110.12), (110.13), and (110.14), respectively. Moreover, the series converges uniformly on E_T.*

### Remark.

The result of Theorem 110A.2 is quite intuitive if we see Φ as an infinite sum of securities. We have discussed in the remark following Theorem 110.4 that the expected penalty part Φ(x,T) can be seen as a sum of four securities. In particular, the part G = G_A+G_B+G_C contributes to the expected cash flow ψ(X_{τ(T)}) arising from all possibilities except that J_1 < τ(T) ≤ T. From (110A.1) and the definition of G_D, G_D(x,T) = LΦ(x,T) can be seen as the time-0 price of a security issued at J_1 which is almost the same as Φ except the time to maturity is T - J_1. The new security has Φ(log(V_{J1}/K_{J1}), T - J_1). Following the similar decomposition of Φ(x,T), at time J_1, Φ(log(V_{J1}/K_{J1}), T - J_1) can be decomposed mainly into two securities, the one whose payment is made after the first jump (after J_1): its time-(J_1) price given by

G_D(log(V_{J1}/K_{J1}), T - J_1) = LΦ(log(V_{J1}/K_{J1}), T - J_1),

the other for the remaining possibilities: its time-(J_1) price given by G_D(log(V_{J1}/K_{J1}), T - J_1). That is, the price Φ has the decomposition

Φ(x,T) = G(x,T) + LΦ(x,T) = G(x,T) + L[G + LΦ](x,T)
= G(x,T) + LG(x,T) + L²Φ(x,T).

Continuing the above, we have, after n steps, equation (110A.2). Since the number of jumps in a finite period of time is finite (J_m → ∞), this step-by-step analysis must cover all possibilities of τ < T. We are led to the conclusion (110A.4).

### Abstract

In many occasions of regression analysis, researchers may encounter the problem of a non-random sample that leads to a biased estimator when using the OLS method.
