The risk-free rate proxies from 1926 to 1983 are the monthly rates on US Treasury Bills in Stocks, Bonds, and Inflation (1989). As defined above, $X=(1+R_m)/(1+R_f)$ is the excess return, the return in the market portfolio over the risk-free rate. Brown and Gibbons (1985) proposed two estimators of the relative risk aversion parameter based on the following Euler condition for the dynamic consumption portfolio problem faced by the representative individual under uncertainty.

$$E\left[(X-1)X^{-\beta}\right]=0, \tag{65.15}$$

where $\beta$ is the relative risk aversion parameter.

Their first estimator is a so-called parametric one; namely the approximately maximum likelihood estimator given by (65.2). It is based on the lognormality assumption for $X$. It is easy to show that the asymptotic variance of this estimator is $(\sigma^2+2\mu^2)/\sigma^4$ (see Appendix). Their second estimator is nonparametric, which is robust to departure from any particular distribution.
