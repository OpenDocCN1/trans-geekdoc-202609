$E[T_C] = \sum_{n=1}^{S} \pi_I^{\Theta}(n) \frac{n}{\lambda} + \frac{1}{\nu},$ (46.22)

$\mathrm{Var}[T_C] = \sum_{n=1}^{S} \pi_I^{\Theta}(n) \frac{n(n+1)}{\lambda^2} - \left(\sum_{n=1}^{S} \pi_I^{\Theta}(n) \frac{n}{\lambda}\right)^2 + \frac{1}{\nu^2},$ (46.23)

*where $\pi_I^{\Theta}(n)$ is given by equation (46.18).*

# 46.6 Performance Metrics and Their Optimization

Once the equilibrium pmf vector $\pi$ of the Markovian cash conversion process $\{(I(t),C(t),R(t)) : t \ge 0\}$ is computed, we are in a position to compute several key financial metrics associated with the cash conversion process, which are of interest to the firm’s management. These metrics include the equilibrium net profit rate and equilibrium rate of return, which will be defined in the sequel. In the remainder of this chapter, we will assume that the firm’s borrowing is constrained by a maximal debt-to-equity ratio, $H/G$, imposed as a covenant in the loan agreement by the lender. We will then treat a number of optimization problems pertaining to the aforementioned metrics, where the decision variables are three system design parameters: the inventory base stock level, $S$, and the firm’s capital structure, i.e., the allocation of capital investment $G$ and credit limit $H$. More specifically, the following optimization problems will be addressed:

+   (A) Given a cash conversion process with available funds maximum, $A = G + H$, optimize the financial metrics with respect to the components, $G$ and $H$.
+   (B) Given a cash conversion process, optimize the financial metrics with respect to the parameters $S$, $G$, and $H$.

Naturally, if an optimal financial metric turns out to be non-positive, this would indicate that the system is not financially viable in the long run.

+   - $\omega$ is the gross profit (in PEMUs) from a sale of one inventory item to an arriving customer, that is, a demand that arrives at the inventory and finds it to be non-empty.
