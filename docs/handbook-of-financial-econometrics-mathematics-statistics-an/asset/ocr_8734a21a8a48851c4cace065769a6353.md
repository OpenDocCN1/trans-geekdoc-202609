For a discount bond, an investor will pay ordinary income tax on the total amortized amount of the discount when the bond matures. The value of the discount bond to the buy-and-hold investor can be expressed as

$$U(t,T,v(T),\hat{t})=E_t^P\{e^{-(1-\tau)r_t}[U(t+1,T,v(t+1,T),\hat{t})+(1-\tau)c_t1_{t^*>t+1}]\}

+E_t^P\{e^{-(1-\tau)r_t}[\delta_t+\tau_x(t+1,\hat{t})(v(t+1,T))-\delta_t]1_{t^*\in\{t,t+1\}}\}.$$

At maturity, the difference between face value and original purchase price will be taxed with ordinary income tax rate

```
v(T)=1,
```

```
U(T,\hat{v}(T),\hat{t})=1-(1-\hat{v}(t_0,T))\tau+(1-\tau)c_t.
```

# 128.3.2 Optimal trading strategy

We next consider that investors trade before bond maturity. In each period, investors evaluate the market condition to see if they would be better off by selling their bonds. If the value of holding the bond is greater than that of selling it, the investor will continue to hold the bond. Otherwise, the investor will sell the bond and repurchase it back from the market. Thus, the worth of the bond to the investor will be the maximum of the no-trading (U_N) or trading (U_T) value:

```
U(t,T,v(T),\hat{t}(t))=\max\{U_N(t,T,v(T),\hat{t}(t)),U_T(t,T,v(T),\hat{t}(t))\}.
```

For a premium bond, if the bondholder chooses not to trade, the bond's value to this investor at time t would be:

```
U_N(t,T,v(T),\hat{t}(t))=E_t\{e^{-(1-\tau)r_t}[U(t+1,T,v(t+1,T),\hat{t}(t+1))+(1-\tau)c_t-a_N(t+1)\tau1_{t^*>t+1}]\}

+E_t\{e^{-(1-\tau)r_t}[\delta_t+\tau_x(t+1,\hat{t})(v(t+1,T))-\delta_t]1_{t^*\in\{t,t+1\}}\}.
```

As there is no trading at time t, the trading time indicator does not change; that is,

```
\hat{t}(t+1)=\hat{t}(t)
```
