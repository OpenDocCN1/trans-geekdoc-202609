$y_t = \mu + \sum_{i=1}^{k} \gamma_i y_{t-i} + \varepsilon_t, \quad t = 1, \dots, T, \qquad (123.1)$

where $\varepsilon_t = h_t^{1/2} z_t$, $h_t > 0$, and $z_t$ are independently identically distributed (i.i.d.) with $E(z_t) = 0$ and Var$(z_t) = 1$. We assume that all solutions of the characteristic equation

$1 - \sum_{i=1}^{k} \gamma_i \lambda^{i} = 0, \qquad (123.2)$

are greater than unity in absolute value. Under this condition, the process $\{y_t\}$ is stationary if $\{\varepsilon_t\}$ is stationary. We do not assume any specific distribution of the standardized random variable $z_t$. The process $\{\varepsilon_t\}$ in equation (123.1) is then a GARCH$(p,q)$ model if its first two conditional moments exist and satisfy$^4$

+   (i) $E(\varepsilon_t | I_{t-1}) = 0, \qquad (123.3)$
+   (ii) $h_t = \text{Var}(\varepsilon_t | I_{t-1}) = \omega + \sum_{i=1}^{q} \alpha_i \varepsilon_{t-i}^{2} + \sum_{j=1}^{p} \beta_j h_{t-j}, \qquad (123.4)$

where $\omega > 0$, $\alpha_i$ and $\beta_j \ge 0$ ($i = 1, \dots, q$; $j = 1, \dots, p$), and $I_{t-1}$ in (123.3) and (123.4) denotes the information available up to time $t - 1$. If $\beta_j = 0$ for $j = 1, \dots, p$, the GARCH$(q,0)$ model is known as an autoregressive conditional heteroscedastic (ARCH)$(q)$ model. Engle (1982) first introduced the ARCH$(q)$ model, with Bollerslev (1986) extending it to GARCH$(p,q)$ model. The conditional expectation of $y_t$ given the information $I_{t-1}$ is

$E(y_t | I_{t-1}) = \mu + \sum_{i=1}^{k} \gamma_i y_{t-i}. \qquad (123.5)$
