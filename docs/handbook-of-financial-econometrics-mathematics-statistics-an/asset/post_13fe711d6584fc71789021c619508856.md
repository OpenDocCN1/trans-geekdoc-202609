FinTech has become one of the major investments for the most competitive financial firms due to driving forces (Gai et al., 2018). Financial Technology describes the high-tech products adopted by the financial service institutions, which predominantly focuses on the improvement of the service quality via Information Technology (IT) applications. This term contains a large scope of technical aspects, which includes security and privacy, data techniques, hardware and infrastructure, applications and management, and service models (EY, 2017). The main possibilities concerning the rise of FinTech are cost reduction, differentiation, retention of clients and extra revenues (PwC, 2016a).

Evidently, disruptive technologies have a disproportionately strong impact on the financial services sector. Technology forces that matter are as follows:

- blockchain will cause a radical restructuring of the industry;
- big data analytics will be the most important predictor of revenue growth and profitability;
- advances in artificial intelligence will start a wave of ‘re-shoring’ and localization;
- cloud computing will have strategic importance to organizations (PwC, 2016b);
- machine learning is increasing firms’ opportunity to analyze transactional data and forecast customers’ possible behavior outcomes;
- information security technologies provide an opportunity to protect organizations from the rising risk of cyber threats and make them more secure (EY, 2017).

Relying on these facts, in the current study, we will consider the same list of emerging technologies in financial organizations as the most influential and relevant to invest.

Like any new technology, companies need to understand the benefits of adoption. Due to blockchain, transactions with financial instruments can be implemented with greater efficiency through faster data processing and lower costs and also with security. Using big data and analytics, firms may predict customer needs and find new paths to get profit. With artificial intelligence, companies can unlock powerful insights and move staff to higher-value work. Robots provide consistent, convenient, low-cost service and customers have grown to trust them (PwC, 2016b). The advantages of cloud-based solutions are enabling financial businesses to closely connect to the target markets by improving system performance (Gai et al., 2015) and also it makes it much easier to manage and analyze ‘big data’. Benefits of augmented and virtual reality may include faster prototyping, enhanced design and training, risk-free exploration, increased efficiencies, improved safety and cost savings (PwC, 2018).

Focusing on key technological advantages, most of the organizations introduce developments for facilitated job tasks, improved communication. A successful technology adoption contributes to the higher organizational performance (Zaheer et al., 2010). At the same time, the technological development influences labor productivity. Imran et al. (2014) revealed that technological advancement has significant impact on motivation and training of employees in the banking sector. Motivation significantly affects employee productivity but training has no substantial impact on it. Sengupta et al. (2015) concluded that organizational resources, such as training and technology have an impact on labor productivity through the perception of person–job fit. Meanwhile, Rubel et al. (2017) argued that effective technology adaptation and practice of digital skills and special knowledge can finally promote employee’s competitiveness. The technology adaptation behaviors may influence employee performance and their level of job satisfaction.

Accordingly, labor productivity plays an important role in business success (Kaufman, 2015). Human resource management system is an indirect driver of company performance and prospers or fails to the extent that it provides employees with the most efficient human capital and motivates them to ensure peak performance behaviors. Employee skills, education and physical–mental ability concern to the productivity attributes of human capital. Motivation relates to the act of stimulating employees to maximally use their ability to enhance firm performance. Firms which gain more ability-motivation-opportunity from employees will reach higher performance through consequences such as productivity, creativity, and discretionary effort (Rauch and Hatak, 2016).

At the moment, as much of the operations of employees in organizations are supported by financial technologies, the labor productivity and the quality of service often depend on how the new technology is adopted. The process of technology adaptation may depend on many factors, in particular on internal factors such as the personal qualities of the employee (Walczuch et al., 2007), and external factors like work environment (Elie-Dit-Cosaque et al., 2011), which should be taken into account in the study of technology adoption.

Internal and external factors affecting the acceptance of the high-tech product have been practically studied in many empirical studies but have not considered concerning to employees of financial institutions in general and from the point of view of the list of advanced technologies which we focused on. With reference to internal factors, for instance, personal innovativeness leads individuals towards technology because there are expectations that all technologies fulfill the highest demands (Walczuch et al., 2007), high ambitions lead to higher expectations to use computers in the workplace (Lee et al., 2013). Psychological barrier such as computer anxiety is negatively linked with IT adoption, IT use, end-user satisfaction, productivity and general individual performance in the workplace with computers (Elie-Dit-Cosaque et al., 2011). After all, motivation improves the efficiency level of the employees and leads to performing organizational goals (Ali et al., 2012). Concerning external factors, formal training enhances job performance of employees by improving their skill set required to handle customer demands and reduce their turnover intentions (Sengupta et al., 2015).

Internal and external factors influence the technology adoption. Effective acceptance of technologies in turn has a positive effect on labor productivity, accelerated workflow, and also increased the efficiency of production processes and output amounts (Kurt and Kurt, 2015). Kurt and Kurt (2015) researched the effects of innovation on labor productivity for the five countries defined as BRICS (Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa) which have drawn attention in recent years due to their economic similarities by using panel data and dynamic panel data methods. The authors confirmed a positive relationship between innovation and labor productivity in these countries. However, the introduction of technology in Russia is not as active as in other countries. According to EY, the average percentage of FinTech services penetration in Russia — 43% — is slightly less than the same indicator on emerging markets (Brazil, China, India, Mexico and South Africa) — 46% (EY Russia, 2017).

The aim of our study is to examine the employees’ attitudes and acceptance of disruptive technologies in the Russian financial institutions. This analysis might give a deeper insight into the problem of the impact of advanced technologies on labor productivity and company performance. First of all, this study is at the junction of science and business. The interaction of business and science is important, because the growth of innovation activity in the industry is possible when industrial production is based on the modern scientific research, and various innovations find application in business without losing relevance (Schueffel and Vadana, 2015). Meanwhile, we look at organizational structure, prior training regarding specific technologies and user satisfaction (Lee et al., 2013). The detailed study of the impact of the advanced technologies on employee performance represents also the novelty from referring to the list of emerging technologies as the most influential and relevant to invest (PwC, 2017).
