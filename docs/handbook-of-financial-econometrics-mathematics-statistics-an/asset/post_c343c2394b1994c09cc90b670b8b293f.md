$$\frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 S^2 C_{SS}(t,S) + r S C_S(t,S) - r C(t,S) + C_t(t,S) = 0, \qquad (102.81)$$

where

- $t$ = passage of time;
- $S$ = stock price, which is a function of time $t$;
- $C(t,S)$ = call price, which is a function of time $t$ and stock price $S$;
- $C_t(t,S)$ is the first order partial derivative of $C(t,S)$ with respect to $t$;
- $C_S(t,S)$ is the first order partial derivative of $C(t,S)$ with respect to $S$;
- $C_{SS}(t,S)$ is the second order partial derivative of $C(t,S)$ with respect to $S$;
- $r$ = risk-free interest rate;
- $\sigma$ = stock volatility.

We rewrite it in a simpler way, as shown in equation (102.81):

$$\frac{\partial C}{\partial t} + r S \frac{\partial C}{\partial S} + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 S^2 \frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial S^2} = r C, \qquad (102.82)$$

where

$\frac{\partial C}{\partial t} = C_t(t,S)$; $\frac{\partial C}{\partial S} = C_S(t,S)$; $\frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial S^2} = C_{SS}(t,S)$ in equation (102.80).

To derive the Black–Scholes model, we need to solve this differential equation under the boundary condition:

$$C(S,T) = \begin{cases} S - X & \text{if } S \ge X, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases} \qquad (102.83)$$

where $T$ is the maturity date of the option, and $X$ is the exercise price.

By introducing boundary constraints and making variable substitutions, they obtained a differential equation, which is the heat-transfer equation

Since $D = e^{r\tau} C$, and it is a function of $Z$ and $\tau$, then we explicitly rewrite $D$ as $D^Z(Z,\tau)$. Equation (102.91) implies that $D$ is also a function of $Y$ and $\tau$. We define $D^Z(Z,\tau)$ and $D^Y(Y,\tau)$ as follows.

$D^Z(Z,\tau) = D^Z\!\left(Y - \left(r - \tfrac12\sigma^2\right)\tau + \ln X,\ \tau\right),\qquad (102.93)$

$D^Y(Y,\tau) = D^Y\!\left(Z + \left(r - \tfrac12\sigma^2\right)\tau - \ln X,\ \tau\right).\qquad (102.94)$

Taking partial derivatives of $D^Z(Z,\tau)$ with respect to $Z$, we obtain

$\displaystyle\frac{\partial D^Z(Z,\tau)}{\partial Z} = \frac{\partial D^Y(Y,\tau)}{\partial Y}\frac{\partial Y}{\partial Z} = \frac{\partial D^Y(Y,\tau)}{\partial Y}.\qquad (102.95)$

Similarly, we have

$\displaystyle\frac{\partial^2 D^Z(Z,\tau)}{\partial Z^2} = \frac{\partial^2 D^Y(Y,\tau)}{\partial Y^2},\qquad (102.96)$

$\displaystyle\frac{\partial D^Z(Z,\tau)}{\partial \tau} = \frac{\partial D^Y(Y,\tau)}{\partial Y}\frac{\partial Y}{\partial \tau} + \frac{\partial D^Y(Y,\tau)}{\partial \tau} = \frac{\partial D^Y(Y,\tau)}{\partial Y}\frac{\partial\left(Z + \left(r - \tfrac12\sigma^2\right)\tau - \ln X\right)}{\partial \tau} + \frac{\partial D^Y(Y,\tau)}{\partial \tau} = \frac{\partial D^Y(Y,\tau)}{\partial Y}\left(r - \tfrac12\sigma^2\right) + \frac{\partial D^Y(Y,\tau)}{\partial \tau}.\qquad (102.97)$

Substituting equations (102.94)-(102.96) into equation (102.90), we can get

$\displaystyle\frac{\partial D^Y}{\partial \tau} - \frac12\frac{\partial^2 D^Y}{\partial Y^2}\sigma^2 = 0.\qquad (102.98)$

Equation (102.97) is almost close to the heat transfer equation used by Black and Scholes.

Let $u = \tfrac{2}{\sigma^2}\left(r - \tfrac12\sigma^2\right)Y$, $v = \tfrac{2}{\sigma^2}\left(r - \tfrac12\sigma^2\right)^2\tau$, and re-denote $D(u,v)$ as the function of $u$ and $v$:

$\displaystyle\frac{\partial D^Y(Y,\tau)}{\partial Y} = \frac{\partial D(u,v)}{\partial u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial Y} = \frac{\partial D(u,v)}{\partial u}\frac{2}{\sigma^2}\left(r - \tfrac12\sigma^2\right),\qquad (102.99)$ $\displaystyle\frac{\partial^2 D^Y(Y,\tau)}{\partial Y^2} = \frac{\partial^2 D(u,v)}{\partial u^2}\left(\frac{2}{\sigma^2}\left(r - \tfrac12\sigma^2\right)\right)^2,\qquad (102.100)$

We finally reach the heat transfer equation derived by Black and Scholes, when we substitute equations (102.99) and (102.100) into equation (102.97):

∂D(u, v)/∂v = ∂²D(u, v)/∂u² .

Equation (102.101) is identical to equation (102.10) of Black–Scholes (1973). In terms of the Black–Scholes notation, equation (102.101) can be written as

y₂ = y₁₁ .

Now, we need to find a function D(u, v) that satisfies both of the boundary conditions and the partial differential equation as shown in equation (102.101). The general solution given in Churchill (1963) is as follows:

If

v_t(x, t) = k v_xx(x, t), −∞ < x < ∞, t > 0,

v(x, 0) = f(x), −∞ < x < ∞,

then the general solution for v_t(x, t) is

v(x, t) = 1/√π ∫_{-∞}^∞ f(x + 2η√kt) e^{-η²} dη .

In our notation, D(u, v) = v(x, t), and k = 1, which makes equation (102.102) equivalent to the partial differential equation as shown in equation (102.101). Moreover, we have the boundary condition:

C(S, T) = { S - X if S ≥ X, 0 otherwise.

At maturity date, t = T, v = 0. Then we have D(u, 0) = C(S, T). f(u) must be determined to make equation (102.103) satisfied. Black and Scholes

choose:

$f(u)=\begin{cases}X\big(e^{u(\frac12\sigma^2)/(r-\frac12\sigma^2)}-1\big) & \text{if } u\ge0,\\0 & \text{if } u<0.\end{cases}\tag{102.106}$

Note that, when $t=T$, $u=\frac{2}{\sigma^2}\big(r-\frac12\sigma^2\big)\ln(S/X)$, then we have

$D(u,0)=\begin{cases}X\big(e^{\ln(S/X)}-1\big)=S-X & \text{if } u\ge0,\\0 & \text{if } u<0,\end{cases}\tag{102.107}$

which is identical to the boundary condition. Therefore, the determined $f(u)$ makes equation (102.103) hold.

Now that equations (102.102) and (102.103) are satisfied, the solution to the differential equation is given by

$D(u,v)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{-u/2\sqrt{v}}^{\infty} X\Big(e^{(u+2\eta\sqrt{v})(\frac12\sigma^2)/(r-\frac12\sigma^2)}-1\Big)e^{-\eta^2}d\eta.\tag{102.108}$

Let $\eta=q/\sqrt2$, and substitute it and $C=e^{-r\tau}D$ into equation (102.107), then we have

$C(S,t)=e^{-r\tau}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-u/\sqrt{2v}}^{\infty} X\Big(e^{(u+q\sqrt{2v})(\frac12\sigma^2)/(r-\frac12\sigma^2)}-1\Big)e^{-q^2/2}dq.\tag{102.109}$

Note that $-u/\sqrt{2v}= -\frac{\ln(S/X)+(r-\frac12\sigma^2)\tau}{\sigma\sqrt{\tau}}=-d_2$. Therefore, equation (102.108) can evolve into

$C(S,t)=Xe^{-r\tau}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-d_2}^{\infty}e^{[(u+q\sqrt{2v})(\frac12\sigma^2)/(r-\frac12\sigma^2)]-q^2/2}dq - Xe^{-r\tau}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-d_2}^{\infty}e^{-q^2/2}dq.\tag{102.110}$

We observe the second term in equation (102.109). Recall that the cumulative standard normal density function is defined as

$N(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-t^2/2}dt.\tag{102.111}$

Therefore, the second term of equation (102.109) is

$Xe^{-r\tau}\int_{-d_2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-q^2/2}dq = Xe^{-r\tau}\big(1-N(-d_2)\big)=Xe^{-r\tau}N(d_2).\tag{102.112}$

Deriving the first term in equation (102.109) is much more tedious and difficult. Recall the expressions for u and v, u = \frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}(r - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2})\tau(\ln(S/X) + (r - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2})\tau), v = \frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}(r - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2})^{2}\tau. Therefore, equations (102.112) and (102.113) hold.

u \left(\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\right) \bigg/ \left(r - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\right) = \left(\ln(S/X) + \left(r - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\right)\tau\right),

q\sqrt{2v} \left(\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\right) \bigg/ \left(r - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\right) = q\sigma\sqrt{\tau}.

Therefore, the first term in equation (102.109) is

X e^{-r\tau} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-d_2}^{\infty} e^{[u + q\sqrt{v} (\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2})/(r-\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2})] - q^2/2} dq

= X e^{-r\tau} e^{\ln(S/X)} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-d_2}^{\infty} e^{r\tau - \frac{1}{2}(q^2 - 2q\sigma\sqrt{\tau} + \sigma^2\tau)} dq

= S \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-d_2}^{\infty} e^{-\frac{1}{2}(q - \sigma\sqrt{\tau})^2} dq. (102.115)

Here, again we apply variable substitution. Let q' = q - \sigma\sqrt{\tau}, then dq' = dq. Therefore, equation (102.114) evolves to

S \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-d_2 - \sigma\sqrt{\tau}}^{\infty} e^{-\frac{1}{2}q'^2} dq'. (102.116)

Let d_1 = d_2 + \sigma\sqrt{\tau}, then we obtain

S \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-d_1}^{\infty} e^{-\frac{1}{2}q'^2} dq' = S \int_{-d_1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{1}{2}q'^2} dq' = S(1 - N(-d_1)) = SN(d_1). (102.117)

Finally, when combining the first and second terms in equation (102.109), simplified by equations (102.116) and (102.111) respectively, we reach the Black–Scholes formula:

C(S, t) = SN(d_1) - X e^{-r\tau} N(d_2), (102.118)

where

d_1 = \frac{\ln(S/X) + (r + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2)\tau}{\sigma \sqrt{\tau}},

d_2 = \frac{\ln(S/X) + (r - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2)\tau}{\sigma \sqrt{\tau}} = d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{\tau},

\tau = T - t. (102.119)
