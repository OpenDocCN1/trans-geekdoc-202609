Consistent with other parts of the CAPM literature, some results provide strong support for the CAPM. Others provide strong evidence against it. An infinite number of SMLs at a point in time would seem to make it more likely that the CAPM holds. Yet, with some specifications of the SML, the riskfree rate exceeds the market portfolio's equilibrium mean, which is clearly inconsistent with CAPM theory. Moreover, while statistical tests are the gold standard in empirical work, they are far from fool proof. Somewhat paradoxically, in high interest rate periods, statistical tests may not reject SMLs, even though other MV criteria may indicate they are not economically reasonable.
