First, large jump spillover effects from each sector seem to coincide with sector-related major events that happened around that time. For example, XLI, XLK and XLP had the strongest large jump spillover effects in 2006, and large jumps spillovers in XLI and XLP might be related to the volatile housing market at the time. According to a report published by RealtyTrac, the number of total foreclosure filings nationwide rose from about 885,000 in 2005 to 1,259,118 in 2006, which is more than 42% increase. For the same reason, the large jump spillover effects for XLF in 2006 was quite strong as well. In terms of the XLK, 2006 is often called a “tech bubble” year. For example, YouTube was sold for $1.65 billion during 2006. Also, six prominent tech companies filed their IPOs in 2006, but only one of them was profitable. Moreover, quite a few tech companies experienced skyrocketing stock prices until early 2006, and slid dramatically afterwards. In 2009, XLF, XLV and XLY exhibited their most spillovers. Similar news events can be used to explain many of the other incidences of large spillover effects.
