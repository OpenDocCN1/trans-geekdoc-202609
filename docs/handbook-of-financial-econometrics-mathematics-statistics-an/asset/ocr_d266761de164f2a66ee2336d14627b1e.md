# 43.3.3 Objective functions of intelligent portfolio optimization

The Asset Portfolio: Suppose, after asset selection, a set of n assets are selected, forming an asset list A = (A1, A2, ..., An). The given total capital C is about to be allocated over this asset list with a corresponding list of weights w = (w1, w2, ..., wn), in which wi, i = 1, 2, ..., n, is the weight of capital allocation onto the asset Ai. Note that the allocation weight is defined at a predefined initial time to, i.e., wi = Ci0 / C0, where C0 is the total capital at to, Ci0 is the capital allocation at to on asset Ai. The weights satisfy two constraints: ∑_{i=1}^n wi = 1 and 0 ≤ wi ≤ 1, i = 1, 2, L, n. Thus the asset portfolio is a long-only portfolio. For a portfolio with given weights, there is a holding period during which the weights are fixed. When the weights are changed, the portfolio is considered a new one. For a portfolio with weights w, its return of investment Rt at time t is defined and calculated as

$$R_t = \frac{C_t}{C_0} = \sum_{i=1}^n w_i \frac{C_{it}}{C_{i0}} = \sum_{i=1}^n w_i R_{it}, \tag{43.1}$$

where

$$R_{it} = \frac{C_{it}}{C_{i0}}$$

is the return of investment of asset Ai at time t relative to the initial time to, Cit is the current capital (wealth) value of the investment on asset Ai at time t.

We call such a portfolio of trading strategies the strategy portfolio on asset Ai. The weights of trading strategies satisfy two constraints: ∑_{j=1}^m wij = 1 and 0 ≤ wij ≤ 1, j = 1, 2, L, m. Obviously, the strategy portfolios so constructed are also long-only portfolios. However, it must be pointed out at this stage that each trading strategy in a strategy portfolio is long-short, i.e., the trading strategy is free to trade long or short.
