Manzon and Plesko (2002) argue that book-tax differences are positively associated with a firm's profitability, its growth rate, the proportion of tangible assets in its assets base and the age of its property, plant and equipment. We briefly explain the rationale underlying the explanatory variables in equation (32.2). Profitable firms can make more efficient use of tax deductions and tax credits. Accruals from sales are likely to differ between tax and financial reporting due to revenue recognition differences between both systems. The tax depreciable lives of property, plant and equipment are set by tax rules whereas the depreciable lives of property, plant and equipment for financial reporting purposes are determined by managerial judgment. If depreciable lives for tax purposes are lower than those used for financial reporting purposes, taxable income will be lower than accounting income in the early years of an asset's life and higher in the later years of an asset's life. Thus, we expect BTD to be positively associated with the utilization of depreciable assets measured using the ratio of gross property, plant and equipment to assets. Moreover, firms with younger assets (proxied by the ratio of net property, plant and equipment to gross property, plant and equipment) are more likely to have larger book-tax differences.⁵

⁵Unlike Manzon and Pleasko (2001), we exclude net operating losses as a determinant of book-tax differences because our sample selection procedures have already explicitly excluded firms with net operating losses.
