Because the optimal portfolio weights are determined by the means, variances, and covariances of assets returns, investors with different horizons should concern the different return characteristics in the return intervals. However, the return characteristics are varying with different time intervals (Chua et al., 1990; Gunthorpe and Levy, 1994; Jones and Wilson, 2004). Thus, investors with different investment horizon will have different optimal portfolio compositions.

As discussed above, the returns characteristics of financial assets are often not time-invariant, the characteristics follow different patterns at various investment horizons. Empirically, researchers could apply rolling-window method or multivariate GARCH family models to capture the time-varying relation structures at a predetermined frequency (Jones and Wilson, 2004; Chiang, Li, and Yang, 2015). The short-run and long-run interdependences can be investigated by using data with different frequencies. In this setting, however, the number of observations used to calculate the optimal weights will vary with specified frequency. Moreover, no matter whether low frequency or high frequency data is used, the realizations of observation at a predetermined frequency simultaneously contain both long-term and short-term information. In a departure from traditional approaches using data with different frequencies (Chua et al., 1990; Gunthorpe and Levy, 1994), wavelet analysis decomposes data into different time scale components to capture different cyclical features of the data in this study. From this perspective, wavelet analysis can provide the investors necessary data to investigate the long term and short term dynamics simultaneously, and help to deepen the understanding of the true nature of these dynamics across different frequencies. By this setting, this study helps us in identifying the portfolio diversification opportunities for different type of investors with different investment horizons.

As a whole, the purpose of this chapter is to explore the role of gold in a diversified portfolio comprised of stocks and bonds. Moreover, we further apply wavelet analysis to investigate portfolio allocation over different time-frequency scales. That is, this chapter examines how the investment horizon affects portfolio allocation by comparing the vector of optimal portfolio weights at different time-frequency scales. The results therefore should be of interest to both individual investors with either long or short term investment horizons, and to long-term investors such as pension plans and insurance companies.
