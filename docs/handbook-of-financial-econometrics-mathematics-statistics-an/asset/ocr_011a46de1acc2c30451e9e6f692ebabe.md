Although, the Margrabe formula is continuous, one of thing that prior studies never explicitly explained is the parameters of the Margrabe formula. Prices are assumed to follow a log-normal process (i.e., follow geometric BM) such that it is impossible to get negative prices. All exponential parameters are continuous. Given that inputs of d1(d2) and N(d1){N(d2)} are continuous, it follows that d1(d2) and N(d1){N(d2)} are continuous as well. Thus, the two-asset option formula will be modified such that it is suitable for pricing options in incomplete markets.

Given that S1 and S2 are continuous in the complete world, then in the incomplete one should adjust them such that they incorporate jumps. A stock price that follows a Lévy process can be represented as

```
S(t + Δt) = S(t) e^x
(34.5)
```

where S(t) is price at time t, S(t + Δt) is price at time t + Δt and x is the size of the movement in log price of a small time interval Δt, in addition, −∞<⟨x⟩+∞. The x in equation (34.5) follows an exponential movement. The elegance of exponents is that they are applicable to logarithm movements which is consisted with the movement of options. The advantage of using a Lévy process is that on one hand the Margarbe formula maintains its continuous form and on the other hand it incorporates stock jumps. In the first case, the chosen martingale measure Q is a Poisson process. A Poisson process Nλ = (Nλ(t), t ≥ 0) with intensity λ > 0 is a Lévy process with traits (0, 0, λδ1) where δ1 is a Dirac mass concentrated at 1.
