Chiras and Manaster (1978) went further and investigated trading strategies during that particular period. Other unique jumps include Variance Gamma (V.G.) and one study that explored V.G. is Madan and Seneta (1990). Basically, V.G. fills in the gaps that BM and Praetz t distribution⁵ fail to take into account which are (i) accounting for long-tailedness and (ii) consistency on the underlying asset when there is a continuous-time stochastic process, respectively. Although V.G. accounts for long tailedness, it cannot account for non-sequential jumps. This is one angle that e2AO addresses. Madan and Seneta (1990) simply describe V.G. as a BM when a path is continuous. In building V.G. model, they focus on seven issues; (i) the V.G. unit period distribution, (ii) moments of the V.G. distribution and parameter interpretation, (iii) effects of varying kurtosis, (iv) relationship of V.G. to Praetz t, (v) moment methods estimation, (vi) maximum likelihood estimation of parameters, (vii) empirical relevance of the V.G. model for stock market returns, (viii) power series representation for density, and (ix) multivariate extension for the V.G. For (i), central issues are that the distribution of gamma has a density and function of that density with no closed-form solution. On moments of the V.G. distribution and parameter interpretation, the key thing is that the distribution has excess kurtosis and V.G. is exponential in nature.
