# MLE: The Theory:

Whatever the distribution of the random components, we can write the log likelihood function. We then find its log and differentiate it, and solve it to find the estimator “g”: $D\log L(g_{\text{MLE}})=\frac{\partial D\log L(g_{\text{MLE}})}{\partial g_{\text{MLE}}}=0$ and $D^2\log L(g_{\text{MLE}})=\frac{\partial D^2\log L(g_{\text{MLE}})}{\partial g_{\text{MLE}}\partial g'_{\text{MLE}}}<0$.

# MLE for the Linear Model:

We now find the values of the parameters that maximize the likelihood function given “y”. $\log L(\beta,\sigma^2)=-\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\log 2\pi-\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\log\sigma^2-\left(\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\right)(y-X\beta)'(y-X\beta).$ (115.26)

Differentiating with respect to “β” gives $-\left(\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\right)[-2X'(y-X\beta)]=0$.

Similarly differentiating equation (115.26) and with respect to “σ²” we get $-\left(\frac{n}{2\sigma^2}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{2\sigma^4}\right)(y-X\beta)'(y-X\beta)$.

Solving these and evaluating for the MLE estimators gives $b_{\text{MLE}}=(X'X)^{-1}X'Y$ and $s^2_{\text{MLE}}=\frac{e'e}{n}$. The MLE estimator of “β” is just the OLS estimator. However, the MLE of “σ²” has “n” as the denominator rather than “n−k”. In large samples “n” and “n−k” are going to be almost exactly the same, and since MLE is a large sample estimator only, it cannot distinguish between them: both the OLS and MLE estimators are consistent. The second derivative is derived as $D^2\log L(g_{\text{MLE}})=-\begin{pmatrix}\sigma^{-2}(X'X)&0\\0&T/2\sigma^4\end{pmatrix}$.
