This study is focusing on the credit-scoring models for bond rating evaluation based on financial ratios. Though dominating the bond rating literature (e.g., see Altman, 1968, 1983, 2013; Altman and Saunders, 1998; Pinches and Mingo, 1973; Molinero et al., 1996; Pinches and Mingo, 1975; Pogue and Soldofsky, 1969; Ohlson, 1980; Van Gestel et al., 2003; Wang and Campbell, 2010; Li, 2012), few financial ratios-based credit-scoring models for bond ratings had addressed the two construction principles of a bond rating system mentioned in the revised Framework (e.g., see Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, 1999, 2005). The first construction principle is the selection of meaningful financial ratios that are key inputs to the credit-scoring model among hundreds of correlated financial ratios (e.g., see Chen and Shimerda, 1981; Horrigan, 1966). The second construction principle, i.e., the so-called rating quantification principle (e.g., see Crouhy et al., 2001; Falkenstein, 2000; Sobeheart et al., 2000), under which the ratings produced by the underlying credit-scoring model are mapped successfully to an external and independent benchmark ratings assigned by rating agency that is of special credibility, e.g., Moody’s or S&P. To meet the two construction principles, efforts must be made for the selection of financial ratios, as well as the improvement in classification methodology.

Pinches and Mingo (1973, 1975) are the pioneers to develop a credit-scoring model that addressed the two construction principles using “stepwise discriminant analysis”, in which a subset of key financial ratios with significant explanatory power is chosen first using factor analysis. After the selection process, Fisher’s discriminant analysis (e.g., see Fisher, 1936) is implemented to combine and weigh the key financial variables to produce credit scores with maximal discriminatory power that discriminate obligors among different benchmark ratings ex ante as much as possible.

Nevertheless, the two objectives, namely, the maximization of the explanatory power and the maximization of the discriminant power are conflicting that variables with significant explanatory power might have very low discriminatory power, or variables with insignificant explanatory power might have very high discriminatory power (e.g., see Johnson and Wichern, 2002). Therefore, the stepwise approach does not guarantee the chosen discriminatory function is optimal.
