Suppose a random sample of size T is available with sample values of R_m, denoted R_mt and the sample values of R_f, denoted R_ft, for t = 1,2,…,T. Then X_t = (1 − R_mt)/(1 − R_ft) is the tth sample observation on X and Y_t is the tth sample observation on Y. We identify

Y_t = log X_t = log(1 − R_mt)/(1 − R_ft) = log(1 − R_mt) − log(1 − R_ft), (65.1)

as the excess market rate of return for the tth sample observation. Finally, we denote the sample mean excess market rate of return by

Ȳ = log X̄ = overline{log(1 − R_m)} − overline{log(1 − R_f)} = Σ_{t=1}^T Y_t / T

and the sample variance of excess market rate of return by

S² = Σ_{t=1}^T (Y_t − Ȳ)² / (T − 1).

In investigating utility-based asset pricing models, Brown and Gibbons (1985) assumed that Y is normally distributed, or that X is lognormally distributed, with parameters μ and σ², the mean and variance of Y, respectively, and estimate the relative risk aversion parameter, β = (μ/σ²) + 1/2, with the estimator

β̂_ML = Ȳ / S² + 1/2. (65.2)

Note that β̂_ML would be the maximum likelihood estimator if, instead of using S² in (2), ((T − 1)/T) S² was used.

It is important to note that β̂_ML is a function of Ȳ / S².¹ It shall be shown that the uniformly minimum variance unbiased estimator of β is also a function of Ȳ / S², and, therefore, we derive first the probability density function (pdf) of Ȳ / S², under the normality assumption of Y.
