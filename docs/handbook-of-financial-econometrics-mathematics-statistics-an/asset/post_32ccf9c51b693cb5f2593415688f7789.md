
Now, for each $n$, define a $C_0^2(R_-)$-function $h_n$ such that $h_n = g_n$ on $(-\infty,-1/n)$, $h_n(0)=g(0)$ and $(h_n)$ is uniformly bounded. Then it is obvious that $(h_n)$ is the desired sequence. This completes the proof.

## Lemma 41.A.2

The following distributions are absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure:

- (1) The distribution $P_x[X_{\tilde\tau}<0, X_{\tilde\tau}\in\bullet]$, where $X$ is defined by (41.4), and $\tilde\tau$ is defined as in the proof of Theorem 41.2.4.
- (2) The distribution $P_x[X_\tau<0, X_\tau\in\bullet]$ for $x>0$, where $X$ is defined by (41.45).

**Proof.** We only show (2), and the proof of (1) follows similarly. Let $N$ be a Borel set in $(-\infty,0)$ with Lebesgue measure zero. Write $J_n$ for the $n$th jump time of the compound Poisson process $Z$. Observe that, for $x>0$,

$$P_x[X_\tau<0, X_\tau\in N] = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P_x[X_\tau<0, X_\tau\in N, \tau=J_n].$$

We first show that $P_x[X_\tau<0, X_\tau\in N, \tau=J_1]=0$. Note that since $X^{(+)}$, $J_1$ and $Y_1$ are independent, so are $X_{J_1}^{(+)}$ and $Y_1$. Hence, we have

$$
P_x[X_\tau<0, X_\tau\in N, \tau=J_1]
= P_x\big[X_{J_1}^{(+)}-Y_1\in N, X_{J_1}^{(+)}-Y_1<0, \tau=J_1\big]
\le P_x\big[X_{J_1}^{(+)}-Y_1\in N\big]
= \int P_x[z-Y_1\in N]\,dG(z),
$$

where $G$ is the distribution of $X_{J_1}^{(+)}$. Since $Y_1$ has an absolutely continuous distribution, from the last inequality we deduce that $P_x[X_\tau<0, X_\tau\in N, \tau=J_1]=0$. In general, for each $n\ge2$, we have by the strong Markov property that

$$
P_x[X_\tau<0, X_\tau\in N, \tau=J_n]
= E_x\big[\mathbf{1}_{\tau>J_{n-1}}P_{X_{J_{n-1}}}[X_\tau<0, X_\tau\in N, \tau=J_1]\big]
=0,
$$

since $J_{n-1}$ is an $(F_t)$-stopping time and $X_{J_{n-1}}>0$ on $\{\tau>J_{n-1}\}$. This implies that $P_x[X_\tau<0, X_\tau\in\bullet]$ is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure for every $x>0$.

### Proof of (41.2.8)

As in the proof of Theorem 41.2.4, let $\epsilon > 0$ and let $\tilde{\tau}$ be the first exit time for $X$ from $(\epsilon, x+\epsilon)$. Pick a sequence of uniformly bounded functions $(g_n) \subset C_0^2((-\infty,0])$ that converges to $g$, except on a set $N$ of Lebesgue measure zero in $\mathbb{R}_-$, and $g_n(0)=g(0)$ for all $n$. (See Lemma 41.A.1.) Therefore, we may consider a sequence of uniformly bounded functions $(\Phi_n; n \ge 1)$ that satisfies the following conditions:

- **C1** $\Phi_n = g_n$ on $(-\infty,0]$;
- **C2** $\Phi_n = \Phi$ on $(1/n,n)$;
- **C3** $\Phi_n \in C_0^2(R)$.

By (C1), (C2), and the fact that $g_n \to g$ except on $N$, we have that $\Phi_n \to \Phi$ pointwise on $R$ except on $N$.

Now, pick $n$ large such that $(\epsilon, x+\epsilon) \subset (1/n,n)$. Then $\Phi_n(x)=\Phi(x)$ by (C2). By Dynkin's formula, for all $t > 0$,

$$
E_x\left[e^{-r(\tilde{\tau}\wedge t)}\Phi_n(X_{\tilde{\tau}\wedge t})\right]
= E_x\left[\int_0^{\tilde{\tau}\wedge t} e^{-ru}(L - r)\Phi_n(X_u)du\right] + \Phi(x). \tag{41A.1}
$$

Since $(\Phi_n)$ is uniformly bounded, $\Phi_n \to \Phi$ pointwise on $R_+$, and $\Phi_n \to \Phi$ a.e. on $(-\infty, 0)$, by Lemma 41.A.2(1) and dominated convergence,

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty} E_x\left[e^{-r(\tilde{\tau}\wedge t)}\Phi_n(X_{\tilde{\tau}\wedge t})\right]
= E_x\left[e^{-r(\tilde{\tau}\wedge t)}\Phi(X_{\tilde{\tau}\wedge t})\right]. \tag{41A.2}
$$

On the other hand, for every $u < \tilde{\tau}\wedge t$, $X_u \in (\epsilon, x+\epsilon)$, and hence $\Phi_n(X_u)=\Phi(X_u)$ for all large $n$. These give

$$
(L - r)\Phi(X_u) - (L - r)\Phi_n(X_u)
= \int[\Phi(X_u - y) - \Phi_n(X_u - y)]dF(y). \tag{41A.3}
$$

By (41A.3) and dominated convergence, we have

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}E_x\left[\int_0^{\tilde{\tau}\wedge t}e^{-ru}(L-r)\Phi_n(X_u)\,du\right]
= E_x\left[\int_0^{\tilde{\tau}\wedge t}e^{-ru}(L-r)\Phi(X_u)\,du\right]. \tag{41A.5}
$$

By (41A.2) and (41A.5), letting $n\to\infty$ for both sides of (41A.1), we get (41.10).

### Proof of Proposition 41.3.6

Denote by $Z_{(+)}$ the collection of all solutions to $\psi(\xi)-r=0$ with non-negative real parts, counting their multiplicity. Since $\mathcal{L}_1\Phi=0$ on $\mathbb{R}_{++}$ and $\psi(\xi)-r$ and $P_1(\xi)$ have the same zero set, $\Phi$ is of the form

$$
\Phi(x)=\sum_{\rho_i\in Z_{(-)}}Q_i(x)e^{\rho_i x}+\sum_{\rho'\in Z_{(+)}}R_{\rho'}(x)e^{\rho'x}.
$$

For some polynomials $Q_i$ and $R_{\rho'}$ (see Tenenbaum and Pollard (1985), Theorem 19.3 and Lesson 20). We show that $R_\rho=0$ for all $\rho\in Z_{(+)}$. Assume that $R_\rho(x)\ne0$ for some $\rho\in Z_{(+)}$. Let $a\ge0$ be the maximum of all real parts of members in $Z_{(+)}$, and let $\{\rho'_1,\ldots,\rho'_k\}$ be the set of all elements in $Z_{(+)}$ such that $\operatorname{Re}(\rho'_j)=a$. Now, let $m\ge0$ be the smallest integer such that the order of $R_{\rho'_j}$ is $\le m$ for all $j$, and select those $\rho'_j$ such that the order of $R_{\rho'_j}$ is equal to $m$. Still call the selected members $\{\rho'_1,\rho'_2,\ldots,\rho'_k\}$. Let $a_j\ne0$ be the coefficient of $x^m$ in $R_{\rho'_j}$ for all $j$. Then

$$
\Phi(x)x^{-m}e^{-ax}=\sum_j a_je^{i\operatorname{Im}(\rho'_j)x}+h(x),\quad\text{where }h(x)\to0\text{ as }x\to\infty.
$$

However, $\sum_j a_je^{i\operatorname{Im}(\rho'_j)x}$ is the discrete Fourier transform of a nonzero function and hence is not identically zero, and it has period $2\pi$. So,

$$
= \lambda p \alpha + \left(\int_{0}^{\infty} g(-y) e^{B_{+} y} dy\right) e^{x B_{+}} b_{+}^{T}
-\lambda p \sum_{i=1}^{S} Q_{i} e^{\rho_{i} x} \alpha_{+} (\rho_{i} I - B_{+})^{-1} e^{x (B_{+} - \rho_{i} I)} b_{+}^{T}.
$$

So, the solution of the proposition follows.
