Therefore, the continuous assumption of the structural model doesn’t seem suitable for the evaluation of deposit insurances. Another example of the contract’s limitation elaborated in a practical provision is that the FDIC protects the depositors to a certain extent. Deposit insurances don’t cover other financial products when a bankruptcy occurs.

Taking the aforementioned into consideration, we provide a novel discrete structural model based on the Stair Tree model and the concept of BT model to price the FDIC in a barrier option framework. In other words, our extended structural approach can modify the BT model by adding regulation limitations. This model not only allows the adjustment of the insurance premium in different financial institutions based on a risk-based assessment system, but also takes account of the loss from bankruptcy costs and the limited indemnification for depositors. We are then able to build a fair insurance premium system and calculate the reasonable implied barrier critical points to determine whether FDIC’s supervisory policy is strict or forbear ing.

The bank asset is discretely monitored for the regular declaration of financial reports and significant announcements. Therefore, we combine the techniques for constructing the Stair Tree (see Dai, 2009) and its generalization (see Dai and Lyuu, 2010) to monitor the bank asset at the time of auditing and to deal with bank failure when its asset meets the closure barrier.
