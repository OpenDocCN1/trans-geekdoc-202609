Liu (2006) showed that financial data, especially exchange rate, are better explained by Archimedean copulas that are able to detect currency tail risk in advance. This is supported by Lai and Goh (2019) who applied the Archimedean copulas to investigate the impact of Chinese yuan devaluation on a group of major and regional currencies. They reported a higher degree of lower tail dependence after the devaluation. Huang et al. (2014) employed the Archimedean copulas to examine the crash sensitivity of global currencies, which habitually exhibit nonlinear dependence structure. Again, they discovered that the linear model always underestimates the tail dependence structure, particularly in extraordinary situations, such as government policy uncertainties. One of the GARCH-EVT copulas introduced by Wang et al. (2010) for examining exchange rate dependence structure is based on the Clayton copula. It did not, however, perform as well as the Student's t copula in the analysis of the risk structure of exchange rate portfolios comprising of four major currencies in China considered by the researchers.

In line with the findings that support the use of Archimedean copulas, Shamiri et al. (2011) found that the tail dependence between stock returns will be underestimat ed when extreme events occur. They applied the Clayton-Gumbel copula approach to measure the lower and upper tail dependence between Singapore (STI), Malaysia (KLCLI) and the US (S&P 500) composite stock index due to extreme events. Their results showed significant dependence between STI and KLCLI, with around 35% conditional probability that the return of STI or KLCLI will move in tandem to either the lower or upper tail of their return distributions. Naifar (2011) warned against the underestimation of tail dependence that may cause non-trivial default risk together with losses in stock markets. This may arise due to the co-movements of the CDS and stock markets in extreme market conditions, as shown in his study of the dependence between credit default swap and equity returns using a family of Archimedean copulas.

Kvasnakova (2009) applied the Archimedean copula approach for modeling the returns of pension funds based on the stock and bond market performance. His results showed stronger left tail dependence between these two markets, which indicates that there is a higher conditional probability that the stock or bond returns will drop to a record low, when the other experiences a similar drop. Based on some model selection criterion, the researcher found that the rotated Gumbel copula offers the best fit, which further supported that the returns in stock and bond markets register higher dependence during the times of crisis.
