and the parameter vector is $\theta = (\mu, \Gamma_1, \dots, \Gamma_k; \omega, A_1, \dots, A_q; B_1, \dots, B_p; R)$. Minimizing the (negative) quasi-log-likelihood with respect to $\theta$, we obtain the QMLE:

$\hat{\theta}_T = \hat{\theta}(Y_1, \dots, Y_T) = \arg\min_{\theta} l_T(\theta). \quad (123.40)$

This method of estimation is theoretically applicable to the earlier VEC-GARCH and BEKK-GARCH models. However, the numerical calculations for the quasi-log-likelihood function of those models are often not stable in practice when the dimension of the variables $(n)$ is large.

# 123.3.3 Asymptotic properties of the QMLE for the CCC-GARCH

We can derive the asymptotic properties of the QMLE of the CCC-GARCH in a similar manner to those of the univariate GARCH model as stated in Section 123.2.4. Under certain suitable regularity conditions, the QMLE is strongly consistent:

$\hat{\theta}_T \to \theta_0\text{(almost surely)}, \text{ as } T \to \infty \quad (123.41)$

and asymptotically normally distributed

$\sqrt{T}(\hat{\theta}_T - \theta_0) \to N(0, \Sigma) \quad \text{as} \quad T \to \infty, \quad (123.42)$

where $\Sigma = J^{-1} I J^{-1}$,

$I = E_{\theta_0}\left( \frac{\partial l_t(\theta_0)}{\partial \theta} \frac{\partial l_t(\theta_0)}{\partial \theta'} \right), \quad \text{and} \quad J = E_{\theta_0}\left( \frac{\partial^2 l_t(\theta_0)}{\partial \theta \partial \theta'} \right). \quad (123.43)$

While the mathematical formula of (123.41)–(123.43) is identical to (123.18)–(123.20), the number of elements in $\hat{\theta}_T$ is much larger in the former than in the latter.
