On the other hand, the assumption is not realistic if structural models assume continuous monitoring because the accounting information of financial institution only can be observed quarterly. Although in a practical provision the FDIC would cover insurance funds in many kinds of deposit accounts, including checking and savings accounts, money market deposit accounts, and certificates of deposit (CDs). The FDIC does not cover other financial products such as stocks, bonds or securities and only protects each depositor with limitation.²

The purpose of this chapter is to combine all of the above mentioned real provisions of the FDIC contract and to take into account the cover limitation of insurance for depositors. When a bank asset fails to closure barrier, the FDIC would take over the bank; therefore, the value of the bank's shareholders transfers to the FDIC. At this moment, the indirect bankruptcy cost occurs and the bank asset suddenly drops. At the maturity time, the FDIC would gain the profit if the bank asset was enough to pay deposits; otherwise, the FDIC would lose the insurance fund because of the proportion of promised payment to deposits insurance due to limited coverage regulation.

Furthermore, future research is being conducted to find the implied barrier as the optimal barrier that does not make the FDIC gain or lose on average, that is

$P(X) = DIC(H, X).$ (15.4)

If we take into account bankruptcy and insurance premiums, the determination of implied barrier follows the equation:

$P(X) + BC(H, X) = DIC(H, X) + IP(H).$ (15.5)
