Note that the $x$ in equation (34.38) represents variable that affects and/or influences the risk premium. That is equation (34.38) illustrates that the risk premium in equation (34.38) is exponential in shape. Further, the risk premium is dynamic in the sense it is influenced by $x$. Note the $x$ series is chosen in such a way that the integral converges. The same principle was adopted by Sebehela (2015b). When $a$ in equation (34.38) is replaced by the actual risk premium, equation (34.38) changes to

$$f(x):=F^{-1}[\hat{f}(a_{it})](x)=\frac{1}{4\pi}e^{i(\pm y_1\pm y_2)^2_{it}x}. \tag{34.39}$$

Equation (34.39) is a continuous risk premium that causes the exchange options to jump. On the other hand, equation (34.39) illustrates that the risk premium takes a quadratic shape, i.e., geometry type formula. In geometry, correct parametrization of a quadratic equation leads to $x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$. This would imply that the risk premium can be written as $x$ where $x$ represents an unknown, while $a$, $b$, and $c$ are constants with $a$ not equal to 0. The latter principle is novel.

# 34.5 Conclusion

To sum up; first, this chapter illustrates a formula for pricing an exchange option under a Poisson process. Unlike, in the first case, when the option is priced under a Poisson process, the asymmetry decreases further. Second, the Poisson process increases option premium. Third, the dynamism in the two-asset option increases. Fourth, hedging of a jumpy two-asset option is dynamic as it incorporates both traits of dynamic and static hedging. Fifth, the risk premium as illustrated by the inverse Fourier transform is further dynamic both in terms of applicable and illustration. Finally, e2AO is not formulaic or stereotypical like other options models-confined returns/volatilities with specific distribution, shape-specific, circumstantial relevant and inflexibility.

The first implication of this study is that there is a positive premium when exchange options are priced in incomplete markets. That positive premium enhances value of an exchange option. Second, the dynamic Margrabe formula is not industry and/or sector-specific. The latter statement implies that it is usable for a variety of products from different industries. Third, the bivariate phenomenon that is included in the Margrabe formula is extended to include premium emanating from stock jumps. The dynamism of the jumpy options, allows one to price, hedge and calibrate jumpy Margrabe options.
