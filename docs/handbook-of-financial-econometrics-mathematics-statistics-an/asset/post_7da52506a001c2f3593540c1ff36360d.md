Panel A of Figure 4.3 illustrates the wavelet coherency between returns of gold and bond. There is a significant negative coherency in the higher frequency band but positive coherency in the lower frequency is observed before 1994. However, the coherency between returns of gold and bond exhibits different behaviors both in the short-term and long-term relationships since the early 2000s. A significant positive coherency is observed in the higher frequency band, indicating the existence of a positive relationship between gold and bond in the short-term. This finding implies the diversified function of gold included in a portfolio of bonds is no longer reliable as the investor’s decision horizon is short. Instead, a significant negative relationship between gold and bond is observed in the medium-term (two-year frequency) since the early 2000s. The difference in long-term and short-term relationships has an important implication in portfolio management. We can conclude that the investor’s decision horizon plays a crucial role in determining whether to put gold into a diversified portfolio of bonds.

## 4.4.3 The weights of gold in minimum-variance portfolios

Based on the framework of wavelet analysis, it is possible to evaluate the short-term and long-term roles of gold in the traditional stock and bond portfolio over time. The short-term and long-term optimal weights of gold, S&P 500 index, and ten-year US government bond are obtained from the minimum variance portfolio at one year and four year frequencies, respectively. Table 4.2 provides the preliminary statistics for the short-term and long-term optimal weights of gold, S&P 500 index, and ten-year US government bond. As shown in Panel A of Table 4.2, the means of short-term optimal weights of gold, stock, and bond over the entire sample period are 0.257, 0.504, and 0.240, respectively. Moreover, the means of short-term optimal weights of gold, stock, and bond over the entire sample period are 0.021, 0.335, and 0.645, respectively. Overall, the results suggest that an investor who invests for a short period of time should focus on stock, while one who invests for a longer period should focus on bond. Moreover, the variabilities of short-term and long-term optimal weights of gold are captured by the standard deviation, which equal 0.319 and 0.335, respectively. The fluctuations in the short-term and long-term optimal weights of gold are both greater than that of stock and bond weights within the entire sample period.

As we divide our sample into two sub-sample periods shown respectively in Panel B and Panel C of Table 4.2, the following implications are observed. First, the average value of the short-term and long-term optimal weights of gold during the period of 1990–2000 are 0.411 and 0.578 as observed in Panel B of Table 4.2, which are much greater than that during the periods of 2000–2013.

The significant drop in the long-term weight of gold reflects the evidence that gold loses much of its role in the investment portfolio since the early 2000s.

In addition, the time path of gold weights in short-term and long-term is shown in Figure 4.4. First, in terms of short-term global minimum variance (GMV) portfolio, we can find that the short-term optimal weights of gold are obviously high during crisis periods of the study: 1997–1998, 2001–2002, 2008–2009 and 2011. When we compare the pattern of short-term GMV between the tranquil and turmoil periods, we observably find that the weight of gold decreases after every turmoil period. This outcome is possibly explained with the lowest variation of gold during the turmoil periods that is one of the main factors in GMV framework, which attempts to construct the different assets. Investors have long believed that gold can offer benefits of diversification because their returns characteristics and fundamental driver are different than those of other risky assets like stocks and bonds. However, the financialization of commodities has integrated the commodity markets with other financial markets like bond and stock markets since the early 2000s. Therefore, an interesting question arises how the increasing correlations between commodity and financial returns affect the role played by gold in a diversified portfolio comprised of bonds and stocks. Here, this study applies continuous wavelet transform analysis to capture the short-term and long-term correlation features between gold and other risky assets like stocks and bonds. This chapter also uses the US stock, bond, and gold data from 1990 until 2013 to investigate the optimal weights of gold obtained from the minimum variance portfolio. The following conclusions have been derived from our analysis.

Firstly, the wavelet power spectrum analysis is employed to capture the short-term and long-term volatilities of gold, stocks and bonds. The evidence from the short term volatility of gold indicates significant differences in volatility over time, with a significantly increasing trend since the early 2000s. A comparison with the short term volatilities for gold and stock confirms that the relatively low volatility of gold in times of market turmoil, that is consistent with the fact that gold provides the need for a safe haven for investors. Notably, the pattern of long term wavelet variances differs markedly for gold. The long term volatility of gold has been increasing more than that of stock, whereas the long-term volatility of bond is relatively stable. In addition, a comparison of the short term volatilities for bond and stock support that the volatility of bond has been increasing more than that of stock since the mid-1990s. The finding is in line with the result of Reilly, Wright and Chan (2000) and Jones and Wilson (2004). Therefore, at least in the short term, the argument that stock is riskier investment than bond does not seem to be well grounded. However, the long term volatilities of bond and stock exhibit different pattern that stock fluctuates more than bond in the long term. In sum, the findings included the change in nature of assets volatility and the difference in short term and long term volatilities, have particular implications for analysts and portfolio managers.

Secondly, the wavelet coherency analysis is employed to capture the short-term and long-term dependencies among gold, stocks and bonds. The empirical finding from the wavelet coherency between returns of gold and stock shows that there is a significant negative coherency at higher frequencies in the periods of crisis.
