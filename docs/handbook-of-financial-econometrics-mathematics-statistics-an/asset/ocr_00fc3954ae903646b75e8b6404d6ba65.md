```
Y = α + β₁RELL + β₂Ln(Asset) + β₃ROA + β₄CR + δ₁Med + δ₂Lg
+ β₅(DF × RELL) + β₆(DF × CR)

```

Y is either non-interest expense to assets (NIEA) or non-interest income (NIEI), α is the average NIEA or NIEI value for all observations over time, and ε is the idiosyncratic error term for all observations. The same model is used for NIEA or NIEI. The variables RELL, ROA, and CR are ratios. The variable Asset is measured in natural logarithm and its associated coefficient is the elasticity of the non-interest ratio to changes in assets.

The dependent variable NIEA is the ratio of non-interest expense to total assets. The dependent variable NIEI is the ratio of non-interest to the sum of interest income and non-interest income. To the extent that relatively financial institutions have adopted compensation or other expenses in response to DFA, these should be observed as statistical relationships between the NIEA and NIEI variables and others if present.

RELL is the variable depicting the ratio of outstanding residential real estate loans a financial institution has or the ratio of first mortgage loans a bank has outstanding as a portion of all outstanding loans. The variable DF is a dummy variable that takes a value of one for observations from the years 2010–2014 and a zero for other years before the Dodd–Frank Act in this data series, 2007–2009. Interacting DF with RELL allows for the detection of whether the time period after the passage of DFA created a different relationship between the ratio of non-interest expenses and assets, or income, and the fraction of loans generated. It is anticipated that this value will be positive, indicating that, all else equal, when the fraction of outstanding loans increases within a financial institution that the incremental expenses of compliance will increase as well.

The variable Asset is the total assets held by a financial institution and represents firm size. It is further anticipated that banks will experience economies of scale in Dodd–Frank compliance and expect this variable to be negative in both models. The variable ROA is the ratio of after-tax net income to total assets and serves as a proxy for profitability of community banks. It is assumed that the ROA variable would to be negative, indicating that more profitable institutions are more cost efficient, including DFA compliance, relative to less profitable firms.
