the other hand, changes in time create consistency. A common phenomenon in the real estate investment trust (REIT) industry is that there is only one jump in stock price. Hence, both period and intensity are one, equation (34.8) changes to

```
= (e^{-τ}(τ))(K S1 e^{y1 τ} N(d1) - S2 e^{y2 τ} N(d2)) (34.9)
```

Multiplying throughout equation (34.9) yields:

```
= K S1 e^{y1 τ - τ}(τ) N(d1) - S2 e^{y2 τ - τ}(τ) N(d2) (34.10)
```

Factorizing equation (34.10) in an ideal world (i.e., K = 1 : 1) yields:

```
= S1 e^{τ(y1 -1)}(τ) N(d1) - S2 e^{τ(y2 -1)}(τ) N(d2). (34.11)
```

Note the cost of carries are predominantly positive; however, it can be negative in declining economies. Therefore, net cost of carries either positive or negative, and equation (34.11) can be re-written as

```
= S1 e^{± y1 τ}(τ) N(d1) - S2 e^{± y2 τ}(τ) N(d2). (34.12)
```

The derivation in equation (34.12) is similar to derivation in Finnerty (2015, Appendix A). Factorizing equation (34.12) yields:

```
C_pp = τ e^{τ(± y1 ± y2)} (S1 N(d1) - S2 N(d2)), (34.13)
```

where e^{τ(± y1 ± y2)} is the discounting factor, which is similar to a bond discounting factor. Equation (34.13) is when the Margrabe formula follows a Poisson process and it is suitable for pricing exchange options when there are stock jumps only and only if stock jumps follow a Poisson process. Equation (34.13) is similar to the formula for a constant-volatility GBM for pricing currency options (see Bates, 1996). Tau in the equation illustrates that C_p accounts for stock jumps at different periods of C_p, a phenomenon which is inconsistent with equation (34.1). Second, the overall cost of carry in equation (34.13) is positive. It follows from here that C_p in equation (34.13) approaches zero slower than C in equation (34.1) ceteris paribus because overall positive cost of carry has maximization effect. When one compares equations (34.13) and (34.1), the “key” difference between those two equations is positive premium, e^{τ(± y1 ± y2)}. That is, this chapter illustrates how the change in discounting factor affects the value of equation (34.13). Equation (34.13) is indifferent to utility preferences.
