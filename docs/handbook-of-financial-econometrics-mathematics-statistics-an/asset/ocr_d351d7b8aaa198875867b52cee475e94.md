Effectively those critics claim that the CDS market functions in an inefficient way due to the lack of transparency, as a result of the absence of standardization, the inadequate central clearing of contracts as well as the speculative nature of the “naked” contracts which create huge incentives for defaults. On the other hand there are those who claim that the CDS market functioned effectively during the GFC in spreading risk, as it was intended to do, and furthermore it did not wreak havoc to the markets after the collapse of Lehman Brothers’, as many feared, since the net payouts were at the region of $7 billion far below the estimated $400 billion outstanding contracts.

Even if CDS spreads price efficiently the risk of default for the reference loans, an issue of paramount importance for the financial markets stability supervisory authorities is the identification of the driving factors behind the CDS spread changes. It is clear that micro-prudential regulation is needed if the main sources for defaults are to be found within the institutions issuing the reference bonds while a macro-prudential regulation is more useful if systemic risk factors are behind the increase of the default probabilities. This study empirically investigates the determinants of CDS spread changes for Euro-zone’s FI over the period January 2005 to October 2015, covering both tranquil times, the GFC and the Euro-area sovereign debt crisis. We use as a benchmark, the structural model developed by Merton (1974) to investigate the influence of theoretical determinants on the quarterly changes in bank CDS spreads during an eleven-year period between January 2005 and October 2015. The literature suggests that the impact of variables from structural models seem to be strongly time varying (see Alexander and Kaeck, 2008; González-Hermosillo, 2008; Chiaramonte and Casu, 2012) and that they lose their explanatory power when applied to FI (see Raunig and Scheicher, 2009; Grammatikos and Vermeulen, 2012).

The empirical investigation is conducted by means of Panel Vector Autoregressive (PVAR) models that allow for the efficient estimation of parameters in systems with endogenous variables and a limited number of observations while at the same time they make possible the use of dynamic models. The choice of the PVAR models has been necessitated from the need to address the issue of endogeneity of equity returns and their volatility which traditionally are treated as exogenous determinants of CDS spreads. Moreover, in the presence of thin trading for some CDS contracts and information asymmetries generated by the absence of central clearing and standardization of the CDS contracts it is reasonable to expect frequent corrections of the quoted CDS prices, that rationalize the choice of dynamic representations.
