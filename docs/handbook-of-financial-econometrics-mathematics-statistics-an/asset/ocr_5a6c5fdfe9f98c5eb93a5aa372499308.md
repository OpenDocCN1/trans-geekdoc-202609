We further analyze the impact of the economic variables that are used to characterize the business cycle on the diversification benefits in 21 developed countries and 13 emerging markets.

We find market integration is more statistically and economically significant than correlation in explaining diversification benefits when controlling various economic variables. Diversification is believed to be effective when assets have low or negative correlation in a portfolio. However, correlation and other correlation-based metrics have been subject to a severe criticism. Bekaert et al. (2009) argue that correlations are an important concept in the analysis of international diversification benefits and international financial market integration, but are not a perfect measure of either concept. Pukthuanthong and Roll (2009) also mention the simple correlation between broad financial market index returns from two countries can be a poor measure of their economic integration. Carrieri et al. (2007) and Christoffersen et al. (2012) suggest that correlations do not provide a complete and accurate measure of overall market integration. When using the correlation with a single factor, one just simplifies but fails to fully account for the structure of risk by incorporating the full covariance matrix. Carrieri et al. (2007) and Pukthuanthong and Roll (2009) agree that there are multiple factors that should be used to measure integration. We then include both market integration and correlation as determinants of diversification and compare their impact.

To examine the relation between diversification benefit and market integration, we develop direct measures of the global diversification gains that model both returns and correlations, examine their dynamics, and further analyze how global and local economic factors affect diversification benefits in a wide range of countries. De Roon et al. (2001), Eun et al. (2010), Ehling and Ramos (2006), Eiling et al. (2012), Harvey (1995), Li et al. (2003), Mullen and Berrill (2017), and Pástor and Stambaugh (2000) have shown that domestic investors can enhance their investment performance by including assets from other markets when short-sales are not allowed. Similar to Bekaert and Urias (1996), Chiou (2009), and Driessen and Laeven (2007), we use an increase in Sharpe ratio and a reduction in volatility to estimate the mean-variance benefits. Our measure considers the infeasibility of strategies associated with low asset diversity in diversification. The non-negative-weight optimal strategies are widely employed in previous empirical research; nevertheless, these portfolios discount the heterogeneity in market size and investability around the world.
