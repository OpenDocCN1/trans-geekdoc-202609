Bali, Brown, and Caglayan (2014) claim that macroeconomic risk factors also have a significant relationship with hedge fund returns and should be considered when analyzing hedge fund return performance. Although Bali et al. (2014) make a good argument, an attempt to illustrate the drivers of hedge fund performance, which may lead to efforts to model hedge fund performance, should begin with factors that are proxies for investable assets. This analysis is interesting because in Model I, without the use of the lagged variables, the explanatory power of the model is significant. In Model II, Smith et al. (2017) incorporate the lag of the Global Stock Market Index and in Model III, they incorporated the lag of the Hedge Fund return series. Finally, in Models IV–VI, Smith et al. (2017) use the backward elimination technique to reduce the model. In the final model, the explanatory power of the model is not reduced, it is actually maximized, and the number of variables used to explain hedge fund performance is minimized. Interestingly, none of the lagged variables was included in the final model as it seems as though other variables provide better descriptions of hedge fund performance when compared to the lagged variables. As outlined in Smith et al. (2017), a factor model of hedge fund performance is developed as follows:

$R_{i,t} = \alpha_i + \beta_{i,1} X_{1,t} + \cdots + \beta_{i,K} X_{K,t} + \epsilon_{i,t}, \quad (91.7)$

where $R_{i,t}$ is the return for the individual hedge fund return series (i.e., $i$) at time $t$, $\alpha_i$ is the slope coefficient associated with the hedge fund return series, $\beta_{i,1}$ and $\beta_{i,K}$ signify the regression coefficient associated with factor 1 and factor $K$, respectively, and $X_{1,t}$ and $X_{K,t}$ are risk exposures 1 and $K$ that researchers believe have an influence over the hedge fund return series $i$. The formula in equation (91.7) is then modified to allow us to discuss the expectation associated with the model of hedge fund performance.

$E[R_{i,t}] = \alpha_i + \beta_{i,1} E[X_{1,t}] + \cdots + \beta_{i,K} E[X_{K,t}] + \epsilon_{i,t}. \quad (91.8)$

Finally, the full model of hedge fund performance is developed as follows:

$E[R_{i,t}] = \alpha_i + \beta_1 R_{Micro} + \beta_2 R_{Global Stocks} + \beta_3 R_{NASDAQ} + \beta_4 R_{SmallCap}$

$+ \beta_5 R_{Gold} + \beta_6 R_{Global HY Bonds} + \beta_7 R_{CrudeOil} + \beta_8 R_{Commodities}$

$+ \beta_9 R_{GlobalCorpBonds} + \beta_{10} R_{USAggBond} + \beta_{11} R_{GlobalAggBond}$

$+ \epsilon_i, \quad (91.9)$
