## 76.5.4 The announcement returns

### 76.5.4.1 Target announcement returns

This section presents empirical results for target announcement returns taking into account the use of financial advisors. If financial advisors have more ability to advise the deals, firms hired by more prestigious financial advisors can obtain higher gains to their shareholders. In addition, if domestic advisors have information advantages to advise their clients, firms that hire domestic advisors can create higher value for their shareholders. As shown in Table 76.4, the results reveal that targets earn positive announcement returns around the announcement date. For example, targets earn positive announcement returns at 4.98% for a 3-day (−1,+1) event window. Taking into account the presence of financial advisors, the evidence reveals that targets hired by financial advisors on average earn lower announcement returns relative to those without using financial advisors. As targets need to pay advisory fees to their advisors, this can then reduce gains to target firms as a result of lower announcement returns.

In a further analysis, the evidence reveals that targets hired by more prestigious financial advisors on average obtain slightly lower announcement returns at 4.53% over a 3-day (−1,+1) event window in comparison to 5.09% for those with less prestigious financial advisors. While more prestigious financial advisors may require higher advisory fees, this can result in lower announcement returns for targets. The results are consistent with the study of Walter et al. (2008) and Schiereck et al. (2009), indicating that targets with the use of more prestigious financial advisors obtain lower returns.
