
The performance of the combined investment strategy involving extreme portfolios, that is, portfolios ($Q_{M5},Q_{B1},Q_{F5}$) and ($Q_{M1},Q_{B5},Q_{F1}$), for holding periods of 3, 6, 9, and 12 months after the portfolio formation date, are examined following the trading strategy suggested by Jegadeesh and Titman (1993). Specifically, at the end of each month, we form a zero investment portfolio by having a long position for stocks with a top-quintile return for the previous 12 months, the lowest BOS ratio, and the highest FSCORE and by having a short position for stocks with the lowest quintile return for the previous 12 months, the highest BOS ratio, and the highest FSCORE. We hold this zero investment portfolio for the subsequent $K$ months ($K = 3,6,9,$ and 12), with a 1-month skip from the formation date, and rebalance it after $K$ months.

## 95.3.2 Correlation between sorting variables

Table 95.1 provides the summary statistics for the major financial characteristics of all sample stocks, value stocks, and growth stocks. Of 1,115,284 firm-months, 552,632 belong to value stocks, and 562,652 belong to growth stocks. The means (medians) of the book-to-market ratio are 1.3354 (1.0652) and 0.3947 (0.3572) for the value and growth stocks, respectively. Growth stocks have a greater market value of equity compared with value stocks. The sales and sales growth of growth stocks are greater than those of value stocks. This confirms that firms with lower book-to-market ratios experience higher growth rates; moreover, the R&D intensity of growth stocks is also higher, indicating larger future potential growth opportunities for these firms.
