And hence $T_0^* D_0^* \phi \in L_1$. We show that $T_8 D_0^* \phi \in L_2$. Since $D_0^* \phi \in C_c^\infty(R_{++})$ and $T^* h(x) = \int h(x-y)f(-y)dy$, it suffices to show that $T k \in L_2$ for any $k \in C_c^\infty(R_{++})$. Note that, by Theorem 41.B.1, $f$ has tails that decay exponentially and, hence, $\int f^2 dx < \infty$. Let $k \in C_c^\infty(R_{++})$ and write $H$ for the compact support of $k$. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality we get

$$\int [T k(x)]^2 dx = \int \left( \int k(x-y)f(y)dy \right)^2 dx = \int \left( \int_H k(Y)f(x-y)dy \right)^2 dx \le \int \left( \int k(y)^2 dy \right) \left( \int_H f(x-y)^2 dy \right) dx \le \|k\|_{L_2}^2 \|f\|_{L_2}^2 \int_H dx < \infty.$$

Next, we show shat the Fourier transforms $F(T^* D_0^* \phi)$ and $F(D_2^* \phi)$ coincide, where $Fh(\theta) = \int e^{-2\pi i \theta x} h(x)dx$. Recall that $\psi_1(\xi) = \int e^{-\xi y} f(y)dy$ and notice that $F(D^* \phi)(\theta) = P_0(-2\pi i \theta)F(\phi)(\theta)$ (see Stein and Shakarchi (2005), Section 5.3). Since $T^* D_0^* \phi \in L_1 \cap L_2$, we have, for all $\theta \in R$,

$$F(T^* D_0^* \phi)(\theta) = \int e^{-2\pi i \theta x} \left( \int D_0^* \phi(x-y)f(-y)dy \right) dx = \int \left( \int D_0^* \phi(x-y)e^{-2\pi i \theta (x-y)}dx \right) e^{-2\pi i \theta y} f(-y)dy = \psi_1(-2\pi i \theta)P_0(-2\pi i \theta)F(\theta)(\theta) = P_2(-2\pi i \theta)F(\phi)(\theta) = F(D_2^*)(\theta).$$

By the Fourier inversion formula, we deduce that $T^* D_0^* \phi = D_2^* \phi$ almost everywhere.

By (41.25) and the fact that $T^* D_0^* \phi = D_2^* \phi$ a.e., $(L^* - r)D_0^* \phi = \lambda D_2^* \phi + (D_0 L_D)^* \phi = D_1^* \phi$ a.e. Hence, by (3.11), we have $$0 = \langle \Phi, (L^* - r)D_0^* \phi \rangle = \langle \Phi, D_0^* \phi \rangle = \langle D_1 \Phi, \phi \rangle.$$ Since $\phi \in C_c^\infty(R_{++})$ is arbitrary, we have $D_1 \Phi = 0$ a.s. on $(0,\infty)$. Since $\Phi \in C_c^\infty(R_{++})$, $D_1 \Phi \equiv 0$ on $R_{++}$. This completes the proof.
