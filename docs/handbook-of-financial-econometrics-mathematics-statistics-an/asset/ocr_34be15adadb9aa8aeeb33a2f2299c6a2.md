the quadratic equation $ax^4 - 4x^3 + 6x^2 - 4x + b = 0$ and $\overline{\omega}_4$ is the largest real root of the quadratic equation $ax^4 + 4x^3 + 6x^2 + 4x + b = 0$, $b = 1 - \phi F^3(K)$, $\phi = \frac{\eta \sigma^4}{(\mu - K)^4} + \frac{4K \sigma^3}{(\mu - K)^3} + \frac{6 \sigma^4}{(\mu - K)^2} + 1$.

Besides Zhang (1994) and Lee et al. (2002), several papers try to extend or generalize Lo's (1987) approach. Grundy (1991) derive option bounds by considering higher moments of the underlying asset's distribution. Grundy (1991)'s upper bound of a European call option can be derived by given knowledge that the value of the $n$th moment multiplied by the $n$th power of current price of underlying asset should be equal to the $n$th power of the value of underlying asset at maturity date. Boyle and Lin (1997) apply Lo (1987)'s semi-parametric method to a European call option on maximum of several assets and a lookback option. Bertsimas and Popescu (2002) also extend Lo (1987)'s upper bound of a European call option by considering the correlation between European call options with different strike price. Lo's (1987) approach is also improved by Gotoh and Konno (2002). They propose an efficient cutting plane algorithm which can efficiently solve the semi-definite programming problem associated to the bound depending on the first $n$ moments. Chazal and Jouini (2008) put constraints on the two first risk neutral moments and use some qualitative information on the risk-neutral measure in order to improve the bound of Lo (1987).

# 24.2.4.2 Non-parametric method

The advantages of non-parametric method used in derivation of option bounds are no assumptions of the underlying stock's distribution, interest rates or volatility in option pricing framework. Rodriguez (2003) uses a non-parametric method and a single analytical framework to derive the option bounds. Rodriguez (2003) states that the disparate methods applied to option bounds can be viewed as a single analytical framework with the synthesis lying in the derivation of call option bounds which depends on the discount factor of its corresponding put option. The general expression for the value of a European call option in a single analytical framework can be written as

$C_0 = S_0 - K e^{-rT} + R_p \int_{0}^{K} F(S_T) d(S_T), \qquad (24.23)$

where $r$ is risk-free rate, $F(S_T)$ is the cumulative distribution function of underlying stock price, and $R_p$ is the discount factor of its corresponding put option. Given the suitable lower bounds of the put's discount factor, Rodriguez (2003) provides the tighter lower bound than previous work.
