# 67.4 Events that Affect IPO Performance and Issuance

Benninga, Helmantel, and Sarig (2005) and Alti (2005) liken IPO performance to waves and cascades. This seems to be a good general description of IPO performance and volume; however, when we attempt to evaluate or estimate these cycles, researchers (i.e., Brailsford, Heaney, Powell, & Shi, 2001 and Guo, Brooks, & Shami, 2010) seem to have focused in on these two types of markets (i.e., hot and cold) as defined previously in the literature (e.g., Ibbotson, 1975; Ritter, 1984; Helwege & Liang, 2004) and have not questioned whether the market’s cycles are more complex. Hu and Wang (2013) seemed to have broken out of this mold and considered a three-regime change model, which spurred Smith and Mum taz (2018) to consider whether we should constrain ourselves to three types of markets or whether we should attempt to isolate the effects of events on IPO performance and volume and then attempt to identify the cyclical nature of IPO performance.
