## Appendix 42A

We employ the optimization method introduced by Rubinstein (1994) to estimate the implied risk-neutral distributions from TAIEX call and put option prices. The prior risk-neutral distribution is the lognormal probability distribution derived from an n-step standard binomial tree in the study of Cox, Ross, and Rubinstein (1979, CRR henceforth). The average implied volatilities calculated by the Black–Scholes (1973) formula in the CRR binomial tree for the two nearest-the-money TAIEX call options is denoted as implied volatility in this chapter. In the CRR binomial tree, the probability distribution and price of the TAIEX futures at each nodal of the last period are defined as equations (42A.1) and (42A.2).

```
$$P_i' = \left[ \frac{n!}{i!(n-i)!} \right] q'^{i}(1 - q')^{n-i}, \tag{42A.1}$$
$$F_i = F_0 u^{i} d^{n-i}. \tag{42A.2}$$
```

In equation (42A.1), $P_i'$ represents the lognormal risk-neutral probability of TAIEX futures price at the $i$th nodal of the last period, and $q'$ is the probability that the price of TAIEX futures moves upward over the binomial periods, $i = 0, \dots, n$. In equation (42A.2), $F_0$ is the price of TAIEX futures at the initial period; u and d denotes the up and down multiple of the futures price, and F_i symbolizes the price of TAIEX futures at the i th nodal of the last period.

We minimize the quadratic function in equation (42A.3) given three constraints to infer the posterior risk-neutral probability implied by TAIEX call options.

min_{P_i ≥ 0} Σ_{i=0}^n (P_i - P_i')²,  (42A.3)
s.t. Σ_{i=0}^n P_i = 1,  (42A.4)
F_b ≤ Σ_{i=0}^n (P_i × F_{T,i}) × e^{-rT} ≤ F_a,  (42A.5)
c_{b,j} ≤ Σ_{i=0}^n (P_i × c_{T,i,j}) × e^{-rT} ≤ c_{a,j},  j = 1,...,m,
c_{T,i,j} = max(F_{T,i} - K_j, 0).  (42A.6)

The implied risk-neutral probability P_i is the approximation of lognormal probability P_i'. The aggregation of implied risk-neutral probabilities at each ending nodal equals 1 shown in the constraint (42A.4). In the constraint (42A.5), the bid price F_b and the ask price F_a of TAIEX futures at the initial period represent the lower and upper bounds of expected value of TAIEX futures prices at the maturity date discounted by the riskless interest rate r. The constraint (42A.6) represents that the present value for expected payoff of TAIEX options is between the bid price c_{b,j} and the ask price c_{a,j} observed in TAIEX call options. Let c_{T,i,j} denote the payoff of TAIEX call options for the jth striking price at the ith ending nodal; F_{T,i} is the price of TAIEX futures at the ith ending nodal of the maturity date T; and K_j is the jth striking price of TAIEX call and put options.

We use similar methodology to derive the implied risk-neutral probabilities from TAIEX put options \tilde{P}_i:

min_{\tilde{P}_i ≥ 0} Σ_{i=0}^n (\tilde{P}_i - P_i')²  (42A.7)
s.t. Σ_{i=0}^n \tilde{P}_i = 1,  (42A.8)

$$F_b \le \sum_{i=0}^{n} (\tilde{P}_i \times F_{T,i}) \times e^{-rT} \le F_a, \tag{42A.9}$$ $$p_{b,j} \le \sum_{i=0}^{n} (\tilde{P}_i \times p_{T,i,j}) \times e^{-rT} \le p_{a,j}, \quad j=1,\dots,m,$$ $$p_{T,i,j}=\max(K_j - F_{T,i}, 0). \tag{42A.10}$$

The explanations of three constraints (42A.8), (42A.9), and (42A.10) are analogous to the constraints (42A.4), (42A.5) and (42A.6). In the constraint (42A.10), $p_{T,i,j}$ represents the payoff of TAIEX put options for the $j$th striking price at the $i$th ending nodal, and $p_{b,j}(p_{a,j})$ symbolizes the bid (ask) prices found in TAIEX put options.
