We infer immediately that the empirical performance of the JSZ model was impacted greatly during the global financial crisis and continued to be greatly impacted by the near zero environment. Estimation of the JSZ normalization led to several violations of the ZLB. At the six-month maturity, there were 40 observations that were below zero. At the one-year maturity, there were 42 observations that were below zero. At the seven-year maturity, there was one observation that violated the ZLB. Finally, at the ten-year maturity, there were 11 observations that violated the zero lower bound. In each case, all violations occurred in the “low interest rate environment”. The JLS model seemed more stable under all environments. In this case, the six-month maturity was associated with nine violations of the ZLB, and all were during the “low interest rate environment”. These numbers are similar to Bauer and Rudebusch (2016) who find that for the 46 months, between December 2008 and December 2014, affine term structure models severely violate the ZLB constraints.

To assess the out-of-sample forecast performance for each model, we follow JSZ and perform a rolling re-estimation of each model in the following manner. Based on the results from the F-test and the patterns in the results for the in-sample fitting error for the JSZ normalization displayed in Table 61.1, we use the data from the first two sub-periods to predict, out-of-sample, the principal components over the following period (i.e., six-months). First, we estimated each model using data on the first 78 months (these are data in the first two sub-periods). Then, we used the transition dynamics in the P-measure to forecast the realization of the first principal component in month 79. We repeated these two steps for principal components two and three. Then, we estimated the model using data on the first 79 months and repeated the above steps to arrive at forecasted realizations of the first three principal components for month 80.

We repeated these steps until we got to the second to last month, month 215, and used this to produce a forecast for month 216, which is the last month. We provide a plot of these forecasted PCs in Figure 61.3.

We repeat these steps for the JLS model and provide a plot of the forecasts of the five predicted principal components in Figure 61.4.

According to both the models, the predicted principal components seem to be strongly impacted during the near zero environment and crisis. According to the JSZ model, the period of strongest volatility in all three components seems to be during the global financial crisis around May 2007 and seems to begin to stabilize in November 2009.
