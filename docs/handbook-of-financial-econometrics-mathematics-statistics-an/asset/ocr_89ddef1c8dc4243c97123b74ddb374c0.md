Merton (1973) derives the equilibrium market clearing conditions for the model of the stochastic opportunity set and the equilibrium relationship between the expected return on an individual asset and the expected return on the market.

$$\alpha_i - r = \frac{\sigma_i[\rho_{iM} - \rho_{in}\rho_{nM}]}{\sigma_M(1 - \rho_{nM}^2)}(\alpha_M - r) + \frac{\sigma_i[\rho_{in} - \rho_{iM}\rho_{nM}]}{\sigma_n(1 - \rho_{Mn}^2)}(\alpha_n - r).\tag{122.14}$$

Let the first fund hold the same proportions as the risky fund. Let the second fund hold only the nth asset and the third fund only the riskless asset. Given $(\alpha, \alpha_n, r, \sigma, \sigma_n, \rho)$ where $\alpha$ and $\alpha^2$ are the expected return and variance on the first fund’s portfolio and $\rho$ is its covariance with the return on the second fund and $\rho = \sigma(\alpha_n - r)/\sigma_n(\alpha - r)$. $M$ is the (equilibrium) value of all assets, the market.

The (instantaneous) expected return $= \alpha_M \equiv \sum_1^n w_j(\alpha_j - r) + r$; covariance $= \sigma_{iM} \equiv \sum_1^n w_j \sigma_{ij}$; variance of market portfolio $= \sigma_M^2 \equiv \sum_1^n w_j \sigma_j \sigma_{jM}$. Equation (122.14) states that, in equilibrium, investors are compensated in terms of expected return for bearing market risk and for bearing the risk of unfavorable shifts in the investment opportunity set; and it is a natural generalization of the security market line of the classical capital asset pricing model. Note that if a security has no market risk (i.e., $\beta_i = 0 = \rho_{iM}$), its expected return will not be equal to the riskless rate as forecast by the usual model.

Merton (1973) shows that if investment opportunities vary over time, then long-term investors generally care about shocks to investment opportunities — the productivity of wealth — and not just about wealth itself. They may seek to hedge their exposures to wealth productivity shocks, and this gives rise to intertemporal hedging demands for financial assets.

The intertemporal CAPM model of Merton (1973) with stochastic investment opportunities states that the expected return on assets is derived from a multi-beta version of the CAPM with numbers of betas being equal to one plus the number of state variables needed to describe the relevant characteristics of the investment opportunity set.
