
Based on these constraints, the upper bound, $C_{\max}^E$, and lower bound, $C_{\min}^E$, of a European call option can be obtained as:

$$C_{\max}^E = \frac{1}{2}\left[ \mu - K + \sqrt{(\mu - K)^2 + \sigma^2} \right],$$

$$C_{\min}^E = \begin{cases}
\mu - \dfrac{K\mu^2}{\mu^2 + \sigma^2} & \text{if } K \le \mu + \dfrac{\sigma^2}{\mu} \\
0 & \text{if } K > \mu + \dfrac{\sigma^2}{\mu}.
\end{cases}$$\tag{7.21}

Lee, Zhang, and Lee (2002) further extend Zhang (1994)'s moment conditions by incorporating third and fourth moment to capture skewness and kurtosis characteristics of the underlying stock price since most empirical findings indicate that the distribution of stock return is often fat-tailed and has excess kurtosis. The results for the upper bounds of a European call option of Lee et al. (2002) can be categorized into three parts:

$$C_{\max}^E = \begin{cases}
\sigma \left( \dfrac{\eta\left(F_{S_T}(K)\right)^3}{a} \right)^{1/4} & \text{when } \mu = K \\
\kappa_4(\mu - K) & \text{when } \mu > K \\
\varpi_4(K - \mu) & \text{when } \mu < K,
\end{cases}$$\tag{7.22}

where $a = 1 + \left( \dfrac{F_{S_T}(K)}{1 - F_{S_T}(K)} \right)^3$ and $\eta$ is the kurtosis of the underlying asset price distribution. $F_{S_T}(K)$ denotes the cumulative function of any distribution for the underlying asset when $S_T$ equals the strike price $K$ at maturity; $\kappa_4$ is the largest real root of the quartic equation $a x^4 - 4 x^3 + 6 x^2 - 4 x + b = 0$ and $\varpi_4$ is the largest real root of the quartic equation $a x^4 + 4 x^3 + 6 x^2 + 4 x + b = 0$, $b = 1 - \phi F^3(K)$, $\phi = \dfrac{\eta \sigma^4}{(\mu - K)^4} + \dfrac{4 K \sigma^3}{(\mu - K)^3} + \dfrac{6 \sigma^4}{(\mu - K)^2} + 1$. Lee et al. (2002)'s higher moment approach will be discussed in Sections 7.4–7.7.

Besides Zhang (1994) and Lee et al. (2002), several papers try to extend or generalize Lo's (1987) approach. Grundy (1991) derives option bounds by considering higher moments of the underlying asset's distribution. Grundy (1991)'s upper bound of a European call option can be derived by given the knowledge that the value of the nth moment multiplied by the nth power of current price of underlying asset should be equal to the nth power of the value of underlying asset at maturity date. Boyle and Lin (1997) apply Lo (1987)'s semi-parametric method to a European call option on a maximum of several assets and a lookback option. Bertsimas and Popescu (2002) also extend Lo (1987)'s upper bound of a European call option by considering the correlation between European call options with different strike price. Lo's (1987) approach is also improved by Gotoh and Konno (2002). They propose an efficient cutting plane algorithm which can efficiently solve the semi-definite programming problem associated to the bound depending on the first n moments. Chazal and Jouini (2008) put constraints on the two first risk neutral moments and use some qualitative information on the risk-neutral measure in order to improve the bound of Lo (1987).

## 7.3.2 Non-parametric method

The advantages of non-parametric method used in derivation of option bounds are no assumptions of the underlying stock's distribution, interest rates or volatility in option pricing framework. Rodriguez (2003) uses a non-parametric method and a single analytical framework to derive the option bounds. Rodriguez (2003) states that the disparate methods applied to option bounds can be viewed as a single analytical framework with the synthesis lying in the derivation of call option bounds which depends on the discount factor of its corresponding put option. The general expression for the value of a European call option in a single analytical framework can be written as

$C_0 = S_0 - K e^{-rT} + R_p \int_0^K F(S_T) d(S_T), \qquad (7.23)$

where $r$ is risk-free rate, $F(S_T)$ is the cumulative distribution function of underlying stock price, and $R_p$ is the discount factor of its corresponding put option. Given the suitable lower bounds of the put's discount factor, Rodriguez (2003) provides the tighter lower bound than the previous work.

Lin et al. (2012) derived a tighter lower bound and re-interpret Ritchken (1985) upper bound of European options under non-parametric framework. The only variable used in these bounds is the dollar beta of an option which is defined as the covariance between an option price and its underlying asset's price over the variance of its underlying asset's price. Lin et al. (2012) utilize the general classical asset pricing model with a stochastic kernel to derive the lower bound at time $t$ for a European call option as follows:

$C_{t,min}^E = D_{t,T} E[C_T] + \beta_C \{ S_t - D_{t,T} E[S_T] \}, \qquad (7.24)$
