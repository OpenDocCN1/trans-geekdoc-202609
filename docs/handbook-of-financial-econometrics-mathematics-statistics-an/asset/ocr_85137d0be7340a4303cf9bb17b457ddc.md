Taken collectively, the effects of the SOX and DFA legislation indicate that substantive regulatory change in governance (SOX) and monitoring of internal controls (DFA) by bank regulators can result in an operating environment that economizes on loan loss provision expenses, which provides a measure of management’s perception of loan risk. However, with the passage of the DFA, financial and depository institutions had to make specific changes to business processes, which changed risks, and therefore altered the internal controls and loan loss provisions of banks. These led to the development of regulations whose purpose is to generate information that informs investors and other stakeholders about control weaknesses in the firm that could lead to fraud. Disclosure requirements support both risk management practices and safety and soundness of operations within the banking sector.

# 131.3.1 Requirements of DFA and SOX for risk management

The combined effects of improved governance and enhanced lending expertise facilitate a change in optimal risk taking in the banking industry; SOX and DFA affected banking industry production costs. DFA imposed new duties and obligations on loan originators. Among the new residential mortgage loan origination standards imposed by Dodd-Frank are the following. Mortgage loan originators (MLOs) must be licensed as described by applicable law. Dodd-Frank attempts to eliminate originators from having borrowers pursue unsustainable loans that are sold to the secondary market. Originators are prohibited from receiving compensation that varies based on any component of the loan or from the amount of principal. MLOs cannot receive compensation from the consumer and a third party. Dodd-Frank prohibits originators from steering consumers towards residential mortgage loans that the borrower lacks the ability to repay as well as not allowing originators to direct the consumer toward loans that erode equity. Dodd-Frank reduces excessive fees during the loan origination process, does not allow for loan conditions that are abusive to the consumer, steer consumers to non-qualified mortgages and away from a qualified mortgage, mischaracterize a consumer’s credit history.

Another aspect of the Dodd-Frank legislation prevents originators from discouraging consumers from looking for a cheaper loan with another lender.
