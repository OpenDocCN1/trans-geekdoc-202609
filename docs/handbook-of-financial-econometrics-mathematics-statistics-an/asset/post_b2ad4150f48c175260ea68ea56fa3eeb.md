
## 25.4.1 Empirical results and analysis

The results of the OLS regression analysis are presented in Table 25.3. The coefficient on LOGNB, the logarithm of the number of banking relationships is negative and significant, which suggests that, controlling for other relevant factors, there is a negative relationship between the number of banking relationships and firm performance. This result jibes with Yu et al. (2011), Degryse and Ongena (2001) and Bhattacharya and Chiesa (1995) and is in contrast to Von Rheinbaben and Ruckes (1998). Our result implies the existence of a potential free-rider problem when the number of banking relationships increases.

Foreign bank debt has a positive differential impact on performance relative to domestic bank debt. The coefficients on all of the debt variables except for one are negative and significant. Only the coefficient of the ratio of foreign bank debt to total debt is positive and significant. This implies that better performing firms may be moving from other sources of financing to foreign bank debt. It is interesting that, controlling for the use of foreign bank debt, all other types of debt — and the use of public, bank debt in particular — are associated with poorer performance. This result is not consistent with the Blackwell and Kidwell (1988) and Johnson (1997), which argue that the use of public debt is associated with better performance, however these papers do not examine emerging markets nor do they consider the differential impact of foreign bank debt.

There is a negative relationship between the concentration of a firm's debt that comes from any given bank and the firm's performance. The coefficient on CONCENT, the ratio of bank debt over total assets divided by the number of banking relationships, is negative and significant implying that firms with more concentrated borrowing tend to perform more poorly.

The coefficient on domestic bank collateral is not significantly different from zero. There seems to be little information contained in domestic bank collateral, since it is nearly universally required.13 In contrast, however, the coefficient on the foreign bank collateral indicator variable is positive and significant. This result is consistent with the notion that foreign bank collateral might be a signal of high quality.

The coefficients on the control variables are, for the most part, what one would expect. The coefficients on age, leverage and the proportion of fixed assets are negative and significant. In contrast, the coefficients on size, growth rate, capital expenditure ratio and research and development expenditure ratio are positive and significant. EBITSD and payout are not significant. The sign on the managerial ownership percentage is positive and significant, while the coefficient on its square is negative and significant. This implies that the relationship between managerial ownership and Tobin's Q is concave (a result which has been well documented in the literature).

## 25.4.3 Dealing with outliers: Quantile regression analysis

As seen in Table 25.1, the distribution of Tobin’s Q is skewed, consequently one might be concerned that a straightforward OLS regression analysis may be problematic due to the potential concern that outliers may be driving the result. To address this concern, we employ quantile regression analysis. Mean-based analytical procedures (such as ordinary least squares regression) are more sensitive to outliers than median-based procedures (such as quantile estimators which place less weight on outliers than do OLS estimators). Hence, any potential outlier-related problems will be substantially mitigated using a median-based quantile estimator.

The use of quantile regression analysis was pioneered by Koenker and Bassett (1978). The general form of their equation is $y_i = \beta'_{q}x_i + u_{qi}$, with the innovation that the coefficients differ by quantile. Depending on the quantile, the procedure assigns weights to the residuals in order to lessen the impact of outliers. This can result in coefficients that differ substantially from ordinary least squares, where the only constraint on the residuals is that their sum equals zero.
