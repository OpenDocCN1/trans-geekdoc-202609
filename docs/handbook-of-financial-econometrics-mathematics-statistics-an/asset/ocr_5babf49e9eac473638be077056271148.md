# 29.4.2 Empirical findings

See Sections 29.3.1 and 29.3.2 for a discussion of our empirical setup. As discussed in that section, tabulated results in Tables 29.3 and 29.4 collect coefficients on jumps variables in a given sector that are significantly different from zero at a 95% level of confidence (based on application of t-tests), take the absolute value of these, and report the sum thereof, for each regression in our VAR.⁴ Thus, for each of our nine market sectors one can assess the impact each of the other eight sectors has on that sector. Our results based on QVJL5 and QVJS5 were found to be un-informative, so that tabulated results are presented only for regressions that include QVJ, QVJL3 and QVJS3 in this paper.⁵ This is not surprising, given the findings presented in Table 29.2, where it can be seen that QVJL5 is often 0, suggesting that the cut-off level used in the calculation of QVJL5 is not informative.⁶ Interestingly, Table 29.2 also indicates that jumps can either contribute as much as 80% of quadratic variation or as little as 20% on a given trading day. This suggests that market sectors are frequently beset by shocks that cause jumps. However, it should be noted that, large jumps usually dominate the quadratic variation, with a few exceptions, such as on January 11 and February 8.
