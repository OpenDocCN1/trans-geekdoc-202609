For a more rounded evaluation of the mixture copulas, computational efficiencies of these approaches are also analyzed. The longer the computational time is, the lower the efficiency is. As mentioned in Section 38.3.1, compared with individual copulas, mixture copulas not only contain the association parameters of all component copulas but also hold extra weight parameters. Therefore, the increase in the number of parameters inevitably leads to additional estimation time. Table 38.8 shows the number of parameters and the corresponding estimation time of each type of copula. All parameters are estimated by the maximum likelihood method with the EM algorithm.

As shown in Table 38.8, the number of parameters of the Gaussian copula, t copula, Gumbel copula, Clayton copula and Frank copula are 3, 4, 1, 1 and 1, which cost estimation time of 0.01, 0.05, 0.13, 0.03 and 0.08 seconds, respectively. All have estimation time much smaller than 1 second. In contrast, the time cost of the three mixture copulas is significantly higher. The t-Gumbel copula comprising two weight parameters and five association parameters, a total of seven parameters, costs 15.42 seconds. Compared with the t-Gumbel copula, there is one more weight parameter for the Gaussian–Gumbel–Clayton copula and a total of eight parameters are needed to be estimated, costing 16.37 s, which is a little longer than for the t-Gumbel copula. The Gaussian–Gumbel copula needs to estimate 2 weight parameters and four association parameters, a total of six parameters, costing 13.18 s, which is less than for the former two mixture copulas.

To sum up, the goodness-of-fit test demonstrates that compared with individual copulas, the capability of the mixture copula to capture dependence between different types of risks is significantly improved. More component copulas can bring more flexibility; however, the computational cost also increases because more parameters are involved. Therefore, the correct way to construct a proper mixture copula is to cover the overall features with the least possible number of copulas.
