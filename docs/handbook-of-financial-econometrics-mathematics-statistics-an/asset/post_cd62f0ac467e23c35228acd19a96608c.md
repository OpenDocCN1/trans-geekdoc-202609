where \(\hat{\alpha} = (\hat{\alpha}_1, \dots, \hat{\alpha}_N)\) and \(\hat{\Sigma}\) are the maximum likelihood estimators of the intercepts \(\alpha = (\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_N)\) and the covariance matrix \(\Sigma\).

Under the multivariate normal system in (93.1), Gibbons, Ross and Shanken (1989) showed that the F-test statistics \(V_u\) of (93.2) has a non-central F distribution with \(N\) and \((T - N - 1)\) degrees of freedom, respectively. The non-centrality parameter of the non-central F distribution is

\[ \lambda = \frac{T(SR^{*2} - SR^2)}{(1 + \hat{S}_R^2)}, \quad (93.3) \]

where \(SR\) is the ex ante Sharpe ratio of the test portfolio \(p\), and \(SR^*\) is the ex ante maximum Sharpe ratio attainable by the \(N\) risky assets and the test portfolio \(p\). A brief derivation of the F-test statistics \(V_u\) is given in Appendix 93A.

Note that if \(\alpha = 0\), as the test portfolio \(p\) coincides with the tangent portfolio on the mean-variance efficient frontier, the ex ante Sharpe ratio \(SR=SR^*\), thus the non-centrality parameter \(\lambda = 0\). Conversely, as the ex ante Sharpe ratio \(SR<SR^*\), the non-centrality parameter \(\lambda\) is positive and the likelihood to reject the null hypothesis \(H_0: \alpha = 0\) increases as the non-centrality parameter \(\lambda\) increases due to the ex ante Sharpe ratio \(SR\) deviates further from the maximum Sharpe ratio \(SR^*\). Therefore, the multivariate F-test for the null hypothesis \(H_0: \alpha = 0\) versus the alternative hypothesis \(H_1: \alpha \neq 0\) is equivalent to \(H_0: \lambda = 0\) versus \(H_1: \lambda \neq 0\). Nevertheless, the non-centrality parameter \(\lambda\) in (93.3) depends on the ex post Sharpe ratio \(\hat{SR}\), which is subject to the measurement errors. In case the ex post Sharpe ratio \(\hat{SR}\) overestimates the ex ante Sharpe ratio \(SR\), the non-centrality parameter \(\lambda\) is underestimated and the power of the F-test declines. In another word, the power of the F-test depends on the measurement error associated with ex post Sharpe ratio \(\hat{SR}\). Instead of a conditional F-test in Gibbons, Ross and Shanken (1989), this study develops an unconditional test in the Bayesian framework, which is not subject to the measurement errors associated with the ex post Sharpe ratio \(\hat{SR}\). The development of the Bayesian test is given in the following section.

### 93.3 Portfolio’s Efficiency Test in Bayesian Framework

Consider the ratio ρ = SR/SR*, where SR is the ex ante Sharpe ratio of the test portfolio p, and SR* is the ex ante maximum Sharpe ratio attainable by the N risky assets and the test portfolio p. Since the ratio ρ = 1 (ρ < 1) is equivalent to the non-centrality parameter λ = 0 (λ ≠ 0), then the null hypothesis H0: λ = 0 versus the alternative hypothesis H1: λ ≠ 0 is equivalent to H0: ρ = 1 versus H1: ρ < 1. In the following, Bayesian tests for the null hypothesis H0: ρ = 1 versus the composite alternative H1: ρ < 1 are reviewed. After the review, a Bayesian test based on the Bayes factor for H0: ρ = 1 versus H1: ρ < 1 is developed.

### 93.3.1 Bayesian tests without measurement error associated with ex post Sharpe ratio

The Bayesian hypothesis testing, developed by Jeffreys (1961), is based on the posterior-odds ratio that compares the probabilities that the null hypothesis is valid to that of the alternative hypothesis. The posterior-odds ratio reduces to the Bayes factor when an even prior-odds is assumed (Kass and Raftery, 1995). The details of the posterior-odds ratio and the Bayes factor are given in Appendix 93A.

In Shanken (1987), by assuming the ex post Sharpe ratio \(\hat{SR} = SR\) without measurement error, the test statistics Vu of (93.2) is a sufficient statistics for the non-centrality parameter λ of (93.3), which can be rewritten as

λ = T × SR²/(1 + \(\hat{S}_R\)²) × (1 - ρ²)/ρ².

Thus, Vu is also a sufficient statistics for the ratio ρ. A prior distribution of the non-centrality parameter λ is given by

π(•) ∝ Tc × χ²_N(•),  (93.4)

where χ²_N(•) is a chi-squares distribution with N degrees of freedom, N is the number of risky assets, T is the time series sample size, and the constant c is

c = S R̂²/(1 + S R̂²) × [1 - [median(ρ)]²]/[median(χ²_N) × [median(ρ)]²].  (93.5)

Shanken (1987) derived a Bayes factor \(B_{10}\) based on the sufficient statistics \(V_u\) and the prior distribution \(\pi(\bullet)\) as

\[ B_{10} = (1 + Tc)^{-N/2} \times \left[ \frac{1 + N (T - N - 1)^{-1} V_u}{1 + N (T - N - 1)^{-1} (1 + Tc)^{-1} V_u} \right]^{(T-1)/2}. \] (93.6)

The derivation of the Bayes factor \(B_{10}\) is given in Appendix 93C.

Later, a more complicated Bayesian test based on the posterior-odds ratio is proposed by Harvey and Zhou (1990) by imposing a joint prior distribution for all the parameters in the time-series regression models (93.1), namely, the \(N\) intercepts \(\alpha = (\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_N)\), the betas \(\beta = (\beta_1, \dots, \beta_N)\), and the non-singular covariance matrix \(\Sigma\). However, the difficulties in implementing the test lie in the evaluation of the posterior distribution involving high-dimensional integration that is analytically intractable.

Instead of assuming the *ex post* Sharpe ratio \(\hat{SR}\) equals the *ex ante* Sharpe ratio \(SR\) without the sampling error as in Shanken (1987), a Bayesian test by Kandel, McCulloch, and Stambaugh (1995) is based on the posterior distribution of the ratio \(\rho = SR / SR^*\) using Monte Carlo method by drawing the non-centrality parameter \(\lambda\) and the *ex ante* Sharpe \(SR\) from their marginal posterior distributions independently. By imposing a diffuse prior distribution on the means and the covariance matrix of the excess returns \((R_t, r_t)\), the posterior distribution of the *ex ante* Sharpe ratio \(SR\) is derived as the distribution of the sum of the two independent random variables \(Z\) and \(\chi_{T-N}\):

\[ (\hat{SR} \times \chi_{T-N} + Z) / \sqrt{T}, \] (93.7)

where \(Z\) is a standard normal variate, \(\chi_{T-N}\) is the square root of a central chi-square variate with \(T - N\) degrees of freedom. Unfortunately, the posterior distribution (93.7) is derived based on the two postulates regarding the population mean \(\mu_R\) and variance \(\sigma_R^2\) of portfolio \(p\)'s excess returns \(R_1, \dots, R_T\): *(i)* the posterior distribution of \(\sigma_R^2\) is proportional to the inverse of a central chi-square \(\chi_{T-N}^2\) distribution with \(T - N\) degrees of freedom; *(ii)* the posterior distribution of the \(\mu_R\) and \(\sigma_R^2\) are independent.

The above two postulates contradict with the common Bayesian analysis. In addition, the sampling errors associated with the *ex post* Sharpe ratio \(\hat{SR}\) is not considered. In the following section, a Bayesian test of the null \(H_0\): \(\rho =1\) versus the alternative \(H_1\): \(\rho < 1\), which is based on the Bayes factor that considers the measurement errors associated with the *ex post* Sharpe ratio \(\hat{SR}\), is given.
