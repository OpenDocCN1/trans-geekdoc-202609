
From (41.4). Then we have

$$D(V,L)=\frac{A(m+b)}{m+r}\bigl(1-G_{m+r}(x)\bigr)+\beta L H_{m+r}(x),\tag{41.32}$$

and

$$v(V,L)=V+\frac{A\delta b}{r}\bigl(1-G_{r}(x)\bigr)-(1-\beta)L H_{r}(x),\tag{41.33}$$

where

$$G_{s}(x)=E_{x}\bigl[e^{-s\tau}\bigr].\tag{41.34}$$

and

$$H_{s}(x)=E_{x}\bigl[e^{-s\tau}e^{X_{\tau}}\bigr].\tag{41.35}$$

Hence, we obtain that

$$\frac{\partial S}{\partial V}(V,L)=\frac{\partial}{\partial V}\bigl(v(V,L)-D(V,L)\bigr)\\=1-\frac{\delta A b}{r}G'_{r}(x)\frac{1}{V}-(1-\beta)L H'_{r}(x)\frac{1}{V}\\ +\frac{A(m+b)}{m+r}G'_{m+r}(x)\frac{1}{V}-\beta L H'_{m+r}(x)\frac{1}{V}.$$

As in Hilberink and Rogers (2002), Leland and Toft (1996), by imposing the smooth-pasting condition (i.e., $\left.\frac{\partial S}{\partial V}(V,L)\right|_{V=L}=0$), we get the optimal default-triggering level

$$L^{*}=\frac{\frac{\delta A b}{r}G'_{r}(0)-\frac{A(m+b)}{m+r}G'_{m+r}(0)}{1-(1-\beta)H'_{r}(0)-\beta H'_{m+r}(0)}.\tag{41.36}$$

Assume further that X has only downward jumps and $\sigma>0$. Indeed, in this case, Kyprianou and Surya (2007) proved that the optimal default-triggering level does satisfy the condition of smooth-pasting. By taking $g(y)=e^{y}\mathbf{1}_{y\le0}$ in (41.29), we get

$$H'_{s}(0)=\frac{d}{dx}H_{s}(x)\Big|_{x=0}=-\frac{c}{D}-\rho_{s}^{*}+\frac{\lambda}{D}\int_{0}^{\infty}dv\int_{v}^{\infty}dF(y)e^{-\rho_{s}^{*}v}e^{v-y}$$

$$=-\frac{c}{D}-\rho_{s}^{*}+\frac{\lambda}{D}\int_{0}^{\infty}dF(y)\int_{0}^{y}e^{(1-\rho_{s}^{*})v}e^{-y}dv$$

$$=-\frac{c}{D}-\rho_{s}^{*}+\frac{\lambda}{D(1-\rho_{s}^{*})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl(e^{\rho_{s}^{*}y}-1+1-e^{-y}\bigr)dF(y)$$

$$=-\frac{c}{D}-\rho_{s}^{*}+\frac{1}{D(1-\rho_{s}^{*})}\bigl[-D(\rho_{s}^{*})^{2}-c\rho_{s}^{*}+s-(\psi(1)-D-c)\bigr]$$

$$=\frac{s-\psi(1)}{D(1-\rho_{s}^{*})}+1.\tag{41.37}$$

Here in the fourth equation we use the fact that $\rho^*_s$ is a positive number satisfying $\psi(\rho^*_s)-s=0$. Similarly, by taking $g(y)=1$ for all $y\le0$ in (41.28), we get

$G'_s(0)=\frac{d}{dx}G_s(x)\Big|_{x=0}=-\frac{c}{D}-\rho^*_s+\frac{\lambda}{D}\int_0^\infty dv\int_v^\infty dF(y)e^{-\rho^*_s v}=-\frac{c}{D}-\rho^*_s+\frac{\lambda}{D}\int_0^\infty dF(y)\int_0^y e^{-\rho^*_s v}dv=-\frac{c}{D}-\rho^*_s+\frac{\lambda}{D\rho^*_s}\int_0^\infty(1-e^{-\rho^*_s y})dF(y)=-\frac{c}{D}-\rho^*_s+\frac{1}{D\rho^*_s}(D(\rho^*_s)^2+c\rho^*_s-s)=-\frac{s}{D\rho^*_s}.\tag{41.38}$

Plugging (41.38) and (41.37) into (41.36) and using the fact that $\psi(1)=r-u$ (since $V=E_Q[E^{-(r-u)}V_1]=Ve^{-(r-u)+\psi(1)}$), we obtain the optimal default-triggering level

$L^*=[A(m+b)/\rho^*_{m+r}-\delta Ab/\rho^*_r]/[\beta(m+\mu)/(\rho^*_{m+r}-1)+(1-\beta)\mu/(\rho^*_r-1)].\tag{41.39}$

Assume that the jump distribution $F$ is a phase-type distribution $PH(\alpha,B)$ in $(0,\infty)$. By using (41.31)–(41.33), we compute $D(V,L)$, $v(V,L)$ and $S(V,L)$ in terms of $G_s(x)$ and $H_s(x)$. Note that

$G_s^{(k+2)}(0)=-\frac{c}{D}H_s^{(k+1)}(0)+\frac{\lambda+s}{D}H_s^{(k)}(0)-\frac{\lambda}{D}I_k(0)\tag{41.40}$

and $H_s^{(k+2)}(0)=-\frac{c}{D}H_s^{(k+1)}(0)+\frac{\lambda+s}{D}H_s^{(k)}(0)-\frac{\lambda}{D}J_k(0),\tag{41.41}$ where $I_k(0)=\alpha\bigl(B^k\int_0^\infty e^{By}dy+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}B^jG_s^{(k-1-j)}(0)\bigr)b^T$ and $J_k(0)=\alpha\bigl(B^k\int_0^\infty e^{-y}e^{By}dy+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}B^jG_s^{(k-1-j)}(0)\bigr)b^T$.

Using (41.37), (41.38), and $G_s(0)=H_s(0)=1$ in (41.40) and (41.41), we get higher-order derivatives of $G_s$ and $H_s$ at zero. Then, by Theorem 41.3.7, we compute the constant vector $Q$ and, hence, obtain solutions for $G_s(x)$ and $H_s(x)$.

To illustrate our method, we consider the particular case $\mathrm{dF}(x)=\eta e^{-\eta x}\mathbf{1}_{x>0}\mathrm{d}x$. Then $\psi(\xi)=D\xi^2+c\xi+\lambda\frac{\eta}{\eta+\xi}-\lambda$ and, for every $s>0$, there exist $\rho_1<\eta<\rho_2<0<\rho^*$ such that $\psi(\rho_1)=\psi(\rho_2)=\psi(\rho^*)=s$ (i.e., $Z^s_-=\{\rho_1,\rho_2\}$), and we write $\rho^*$ for $\rho_s^*$. Recall that $G'_s(0)=\frac{s}{D\rho^*}$. Hence $G_s(x)=Q_1e^{\rho_1x}+Q_2e^{\rho_2x}$, where $Q$ is the unique column vector satisfying the equation

$$\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\\rho_1&\rho_2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}Q_1\\Q_2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\\frac{-s}{D\rho^*}\end{bmatrix}.$$

Simple algebra shows that
$$\begin{bmatrix}Q_1\\Q_2\end{bmatrix}=\frac{1}{\rho_1-\rho_2}\begin{bmatrix}\rho_2+\frac{s}{D\rho^*}\\\ -\rho_1-\frac{s}{D\rho^*}\end{bmatrix}.$$

Since $\rho_1\rho_2\rho^*=\frac{s\eta}{D}$, we get
$$G_s(x)=\frac{\rho_2+\frac{s}{D\rho^*}}{\rho_2-\rho_1}e^{\rho_1x}+\frac{-\rho_1-\frac{s}{D\rho^*}}{\rho_2-\rho_1}e^{\rho_2x}$$
$$=\frac{\rho^*(\eta+\rho_1)}{\eta(\rho_2-\rho_1)}e^{\rho_1x}+\frac{-\rho_1(\eta+\rho_2)}{\eta(\rho_2-\rho_1)}e^{\rho_2x}.\qquad(41.42)$$

Recall that $H_s'(0)=\frac{s-\psi(1)}{D(1-\rho^*)}+1$. Hence $H_s(x)=P_1e^{\rho_1x}+P_2e^{\rho_2x}$, where $P$ is the unique column vector satisfying the equation
$$\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\\rho_1&\rho_2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}P_1\\P_2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\\frac{s-\psi(1)}{D(1-\rho^*)}+1\end{bmatrix}.$$
Simple algebra shows that
$$\begin{bmatrix}P_1\\P_2\end{bmatrix}=\frac{1}{\rho_1-\rho_2}\begin{bmatrix}\rho_2-1-\frac{s-\psi(1)}{D(1-\rho^*)}\\\1-\rho_1+\frac{s-\psi(1)}{D(1-\rho^*)}\end{bmatrix}.$$

Using the fact that $(1+\eta)(\psi(1)-s)=D(1-\rho_1)(1-\rho_2)(1-\rho^*)$. We obtain
$$H_s(x)=\frac{\rho_2-1-\frac{s-\psi(1)}{D(1-\rho^*)}}{\rho_2-\rho_1}e^{\rho_1x}+\frac{1-\rho_1+\frac{s-\psi(1)}{D(1-\rho^*)}}{\rho_2-\rho_1}e^{\rho_2x}$$
$$=\frac{(\rho_2-1)(\eta+\rho_1)}{(1+\eta)(\rho_2-\rho_1)}e^{\rho_1x}+\frac{(1-\rho_1)(\eta+\rho_2)}{(1+\eta)(\rho_2-\rho_1)}e^{\rho_2x}.\qquad(41.43)$$

### Remark 41.3.10
The optimal level $L^*$ in (41.39) coincides with that in Hilberink and Rogers (2002). Our ODE approach also works for the perpetual American put option. Indeed, we determine the optimal stopping times when only downward jumps are allowed. Also we calculate exactly the price of the perpetual American put option.

For general two-sided jump distributions, we get a necessary condition for the coefficient constant $Q$. This necessary condition will play an important role in Section 41.4.

### Proposition 41.3.11
*If $(\alpha_+,B_+)$ is minimal, $Z_{(-)}$ is separable, and $p>0$, then the constant vector $Q$ for $\phi$ satisfies $\sum_{i=1}^S Q_i=g(0)$ and*

$\alpha_+\Bigl(\int_0^\infty g(-y)e^{B_+y}dy\Bigr)e^{xB_+}b_+^T=\alpha_+\Bigl[\sum_{i=1}^S Q_i(\rho_i I-B_+)^{-1}\Bigr]e^{xB_+}b_+^T,\quad\forall x>0.\tag{41.44}$

**Proof.** Please refer to Appendix 41A.

## 41.4 The Constant Vector $Q$: The Second Method
In this section, we consider a more general process whose upward jumps are determined by a general Levy measure and downward jumps by a compound Poisson process with a mixture of exponential jump distributions, we show that a vector $Q$ satisfies a system of linear equations, then a conjectured function must be the desired function $\phi$. To find a solution to the system of equations, instead of directly inverting the system of equations, we exploit the technique in Dufresne and Gerber (1989) to obtain an explicit solution for $\phi$. With this explicit formula, we compute especially in Example 41.4.6 the optimal default-triggering level. We also provide an example (Example 41.4.7) which shows that we can calculate certain option prices with finite maturity up to a Fourier transform.

Let $X=(X_t,\{P_x\}_{x\in R})$ be the Levy process given by

$X_t=X_0+X_t^{(+)}-Z_t,\;t\ge0.\tag{41.45}$

Here $X^{(+)}=(X_t^{(+)};t\ge0)$ is a Levy process on $R$ such that it starts at and has a nontrivial diffusion part and no downward jumps, and $Z$ is a compound Poisson process that is independent of $X^{(+)}$ with jump distribution given by $f_{(+)}=PH(\alpha_+,B_+)$. As before, under $P_x,X_0=x$ a.s. Clearly, the process in (41.45) is a generalization of (41.4).

The characteristic exponent $\psi$ of $X$ is given by

$\psi(\xi)=D\xi^2+c\xi+\int_0^\infty\bigl[e^{\xi z}-1-\xi z\mathbf{1}_{|z|\le1}\bigr]v(dz)+\lambda\int_0^\infty e^{-\xi y}f_{(+)}(y)dy-\lambda,$

where $v$ is an arbitrary Levy measure on $(0,\infty)$ and $\int_0^\infty\min(1,z^2)v(dz)<\infty$. Moreover, the infinitesimal generator of $X$ has a domain containing $C_0^2(R)$ and is given by

$Lh(x)=Dh''(x)+ch'(x)+\int_0^\infty\bigl[h(x+z)-h(x)-h'(x)z\mathbf{1}_{|z|\le1}\bigr]v(dz)+\lambda\int_0^\infty h(x-z)f_{(+)}(z)dz-h(x).$

Recall that $\phi(x)=E_x[e^{-r\tau}g(X_\tau)]$, where $\tau=\inf\{t\ge0;X_t\le0\}$. The following proposition gives a converse to Theorem 41.2.4.

### Proposition 41.4.1
If $\phi\equiv g$ on $(-\infty,0]$, $\phi\in C_0^2(R_+)$, and $(L-r)\phi\equiv0$ on $R_{++}$, then $\phi\equiv\Phi$ on $R$.

Proof. Please refer to Appendix 41A.

We consider below the special case where $f_{(+)}$ is a mixture of exponential distribution. Namely, there exist constants $(p_j)_{j=1}^m$ and $(\eta_j)_{j=1}^m$ such that $p_j>0$, $\eta_j>0$, $\sum_{j=1}^m p_j=1$, and

$f_{(+)}(y)=\sum_{j=1}^m p_j\eta_j e^{-\eta_j y},\tag{41.46}$

on $y>0$ and $f_{(+)}(y)=0$ otherwise. Without loss of generality, assume that the $\eta_j$ are distinct.

It is worth noting that $Z_{(-)}=(\rho_i)_{i=1}^s$ is separable and $S=m+1$ (see Asmussen et al. (2004), Lemma 1(1) or Mordecki (2002), Corollary 2). Now, based on Theorem 41.3.7, we conjecture that $\Phi(x)=E_x[e^{-r\tau}g(X_\tau)]=\sum_{i=1}^S Q_i e^{\rho_i x}$ for some $Q_i$. By Proposition 41.3.11, we further assume that $Q$ satisfies (41.44). More precisely, for $f_{(+)}$ given by (41.46),

$\sum_{j=1}^m p_j\eta_j e^{-\eta_j x}\int_{-\infty}^0 g(y)e^{\eta_j y}dy=\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}Q_i\sum_{j=1}^m p_j\eta_j e^{-\eta_j x}/(\rho_i+\eta_j).\tag{41.47}$

(Note that $f_{(+)}=PH((p_1,\dots,p_m),\operatorname{diag}(-\eta_1,\dots,-\eta_m))$.) Recall that $\sum_{i=1}^S Q_i=g(0)$. Then, by comparing the coefficients of $e^{-\eta_j x}$ in (41.47),
