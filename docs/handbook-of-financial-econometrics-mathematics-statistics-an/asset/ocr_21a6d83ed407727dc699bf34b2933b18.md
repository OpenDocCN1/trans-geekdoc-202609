The arbitrage strategy for American call options is like a trade-off decision between the underlying asset’s dividend received and the value of an American call option exercised on ex-dividend date before maturity. Based on this insight, Handley (2005) derive the upper bounds for American call options which depends on the value of dividends received compared to the strike price of the contract.

In addition, some studies obtain the tighten bounds for American option in incomplete market. Perrakis and Lefoll (1997, 2000, 2004) investigate the American option bounds with proportional transaction costs in lattice method framework. Constantinides and Perrakis (2007) extend the bounds by Constantinides and Zariphopoulou (1999, 2001) to American option markets with transaction costs in stochastic dominance framework.

# 24.4.2 Asian option bounds

An Asian option is a kind of exotic options and its prices are determined by the average underlying prices over whole period, not only dependent on the price of the asset at the maturity date. Even though there is no closed-form solution for an Asian option, the bounds of an Asian option can be derived by replication of a portfolio incorporated with other related financial derivatives. Nielsen and Sandmann (2003) express bounds of an Asian call option as the portfolio of delayed payment European call options which depend on the exercise price of the Asian option. By using put-call parity, the bounds of an Asian put option can be derived as well. Vanmaele et al. (2006) investigate bounds for Asian options under the assumption that the underlying asset follows lognormal distribution. They employ the bounds approach proposed by Kaas et al. (2000) for sums of random variables and then obtain computable bounds for Asian options. Asian option bounds not only can appropriately evaluate the prices of Asian options, but also can be applied to hedging strategies. Nielsen and Sandmann (2003) and Vanmaele et al. (2006) also discuss hedging positions based on their Asian option bounds.
