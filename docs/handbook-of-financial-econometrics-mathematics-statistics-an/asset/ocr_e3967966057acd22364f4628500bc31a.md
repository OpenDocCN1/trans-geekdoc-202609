$$Q_t = (1 - a - b)\bar{Q} + a u_{t-1} u'_{t-1} + b Q_{t-1}, \tag{123.59}$$

where $\bar{Q}$ is a matrix of location parameters. If $a \ge 0$, $b \ge 0$, $a + b < 1$ and $\bar{Q}$ is positive definite, then $R_t$ is also positive definite. Hence, $R_t$ can be expressed in terms of $Q_t$ as $R_t = (\rho_{ij,t}) = \text{diag}(q_{11,t}, \dots, q_{nn,t})^{-1/2} Q_t \text{diag}(q_{11,t}, \dots, q_{nn,t})^{-1/2}$, where $\rho_{ij,t}$ is given by (123.49). The conditional correlations are dynamically driven by the process of $Q_t$. The conditional covariances are obtained accordingly as $h_{ij,t} = \rho_{ij,t}\{h_{ii,t}h_{jj,t}\}^{1/2}$.

This model enables us to investigate the three aspects of interdependence of different financial markets in a single unified model. These are: (i) mean spillover effects, (ii) DCCs, and (iii) volatility spillover effects. Each of the three quantities measure some aspect of the degree of integration in the East Asian financial markets. We employ the one-step maximum likelihood method proposed by Bauwens and Laurent (2005) as explained in Section 123.4.

# 123.5.1.2 Estimates of parameters

We focus on the mean spillover effects from the global (US), Japan, and aggregate regional markets and from the individual local market itself to the individual local market, which correspond respectively to the parameters of $\gamma_{41}$ (Global to Local), $\gamma_{42}$ (Japan to Local), $\gamma_{43}$ (Regional to Local) and $\gamma_{44}$ (Local to Local) in the last row of $\Gamma_1$. These coefficients represent the intertemporal dependency across the markets. The estimated results of the parameters are shown in Table 123.2. The results reveal the following facts. (i) The effects of global markets ($\gamma_{41}$) on the conditional mean of the local market are significant for all the emerging East Asian countries except for Indonesia and China. The global market yields in the previous period affect the local market yields positively in the present period except for Indonesia and China. In particular, given the small absolute value of $t$-statistic, China is not affected by the global market. (ii) The Japanese effect ($\gamma_{42}$) is significant only for Singapore. Movements in the Japanese bond market barely affect the emerging East Asian markets. (iii) The aggregated regional effects ($\gamma_{43}$) are significant for the three markets of Singapore, Thailand, and China, but regional effects on China are negative and significant. One of the most striking findings is that China differs substantially from other emerging markets.
