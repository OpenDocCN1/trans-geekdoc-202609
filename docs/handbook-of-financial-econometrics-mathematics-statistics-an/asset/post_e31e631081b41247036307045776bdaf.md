
Before the provision is made (Dou et al., 2018; Jin et al., 2018; Kanagaretnam et al., 2017), the following equation is estimated:

```
LLP_{i,t} = INTINC_{i,t} + DNPL_{i,t} + NPL_{i,t-1} + CAPRATIO_{i,t-1}
+ Assets_{i,t-1} + Concentration_{i,t}
+ Commercial_{i,t} + DF_t + SOX_t + ε. (131.1)
```

The dependent variable, LLP_{i,t}, is the loan loss provision for year t, the year of the call report scaled by lagged total loans. This variable represents the institutional assessment concerning the riskiness of the loan portfolio of the firm. As this value increases there is the view that the risk tolerance of the bank has been elevated. A smaller provision reflects the opposite risk awareness by bank management. Prior research has identified (Bushman and Williams, 2012, 2015; Dahl, 2013; Dou et al., 2018; Jin et al., 2018; Morris et al., 2016; Ng and Roychowdhury, 2014) relationships between management control and the loan loss provision. INTINC_{i,t} is net interest income divided by lagged total assets of the bank. It is presumed a lagged relationship between some management controls and loan loss expense (Marton and Runesson, 2017; Nicoletti, 2018). DNPL_{i,t} is the change in non-performing loans between the ends of the lagged and current years, scaled by lagged total loans. The volume of non-performing loans, at the end of the prior period (NPL_{i,t-1}) also scaled by lagged total loans, are also included in the model formation. CAPRATIO_{i,t-1} is equity capital in year t divided by lagged total assets. ASSETS_{i,t-1} is the natural logarithm of total assets from the lagged year t − 1. Loan concentration is a key driver of a bank’s overall portfolio’s credit risk, with larger values indicative of greater overall risk as risk is increasingly concentrated in one industry instead of being relatively diversified across multiple loan categories. To account for management perception of loan portfolio risk, CONCENTRATION_{i,t} is a measure of loan concentration between consumer and personal real estate loans within each bank in year t.

A variable of interest is DF_t, and its associated cross terms. These detect and measure the statistical significance of a change in regulatory treatment on loan loss provisions. The DF_t variable takes a value of one for years 2011 and later and serves to introduce an observed shift in the average loan loss provision expense-to-lagged total loans ratio. This formulation belies the assumption that bank managers change their loan loss provision management behavior due to the exogenous change in regulation and become more willing to lend to relatively risky borrowers since DFA requires lending practices such as contractual provisions and development of other expertise designed to mitigate the probability of lending to a borrower who will default.

A positive estimated coefficient implies that loan loss provisions increased as a share of lagged total loans after the implementation of DFA in 2011, while a negative coefficient would indicate that loan loss provisions, as a share of lagged total loans, decreased after the implementation. A positive coefficient could suggest that bank management considered the pre-DFA loans from an adverse selection perspective: the provisions of the DFA prevent the negative outcomes of loan defaults, thereby encouraging relatively risky lending that would not have otherwise occurred. A negative coefficient could suggest that the bank management considers the DFA from a moral hazard perspective: prior to the DFA operational mechanisms were not in place to reduce access to relatively risky borrowers; now these are present and expected charge-off expenses are reduced. Interaction terms between DF_t and the earnings, non-performing loans, capital, and size control variables are present. A statistically significant coefficient indicates a change in the marginal effect of these variables on loan loss provisions, as a share of lagged total loans, during the period SOX and DFA are in effect, while controlling for the implementation of SOX.

SOX_t is a second variable of interest. This variable takes a value of one for years 2002 and later. A positive coefficient indicates loan loss provisions increased after the passage of SOX, while a negative coefficient indicates loan loss provisions decreased after the implementation. A positive coefficient, again, suggests an adverse selection interpretation of SOX implementation; SOX prevents weakness related to governance or culture. Interaction terms between SOX_t and the earnings, non-performing loans, capital, and size control variables mentioned previously are also included. A statistically significant coefficient indicates a change in the marginal effect of these variables on loan loss provisions during the period SOX is in effect, prior to 2011. Effects of SOX and DFA on the provision for loan loss, as a share of total loans, were realized after 2011 (Table 131.3).

Several results emerge from the parameter estimates (Table 131.4). Increases in earnings are associated with decreases in loan loss provision expense. For the bank to earn additional income entails greater overall exposure to default risk, but successful risk management generates more income per dollar loan than increased total loss provision, all else equal. The statistical significance of earnings when setting the loan loss provision accentuates the challenge of determining strategies to manage the lending process as well as raising firm income in the existing regulatory environment.

Loan loss provision is impacted by loan quality, measured in terms of the volume of delinquent balances and changes in delinquent balances. This finding reinforces the importance of earnings and loan quality within the banking sector. Financial institutions should continue to deploy a strategy for attracting relatively high-margin loans given potential loan quality declines within this application.

Increased equity offsets loan loss provision expenses and loan loss provisions are inversely related to asset volume. Interestingly, (average) bank assets grew during the sample period. This finding implies that an average increase in loan loss provision via growth is occurring through purchases of progressively less delinquent loans as banks grow in size.

Average bank loan concentration declined since the DFA was implemented.
