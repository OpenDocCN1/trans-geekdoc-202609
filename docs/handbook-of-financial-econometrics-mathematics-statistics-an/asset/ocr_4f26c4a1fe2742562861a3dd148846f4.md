where γ*_{ii} is the estimated downside risk aversion parameter of country i using the long memory adjusted downside risk; Δ^d V_{i,t+1}; φ*_i is the coefficient of the first-order serial correlation in the adjusted downside risk process based on the semi-parametric method; and λ*_i measures the financial leverage effect of local market i. Referring to the system in equation (58.1), we have A*_i = [γ*_{ii}λ*_i γ*_{ii}φ*_i; λ*_i φ*_i] and the parameters in the A*_i matrix are estimated by the maximum likelihood method. Note that γ*_{ii} is calculated by γ*_{ii} = A*_{i,12}/A*_{i,22} = γ*_{ii}φ*_i/φ*_i. Equation (58.12) is a more general representation of the VAR process as it constitutes the leverage effect, the VaR feedback effect, the long memory effect, and the autocorrelations in stock returns and downside risk. Thus, the parameters in the VAR system not only contain information about modeling a long memory process, but they also capture the market dynamics associated with the risk-return interactions. In particular, risk-return relations can be tested by examining the significance of γ*_{ii}=0, whereas the leverage effect can be tested by examining the restriction on λ*_i=0. If λ*_i=0 is true, then equation (58.12) becomes

⎡ R_{i,t+1} ⎤   ⎡ 0      γ*_{ii}φ*_i ⎤ ⎡ R_{i,t} ⎤       
⎣ Δ^d V_{i,t+1} ⎦ = ⎣ 0      φ*_i      ⎦ ⎣ Δ^d V_{i,t} ⎦ + u_{i,t+1}.  (58.12)'

Equations (58.12) and (58.12)' allow us to examine the difference with and without the leverage effect, and its impact on the parametric relations in the system.

However, in a highly globalized financial system, equity prices in Asian markets often covary with those in a dominant market. Similar to the specification of equation (58.9), we introduce the cross-market risk effect into the VAR model while at the same time considering the long memory factor as

⎡ R_{i,t+1} ⎤   ⎡ γ*_{ij}λ*_jρ_{ij}   γ*_{ij}φ*_j ⎤ ⎡ R_{i,t} ⎤       
⎣ Δ^d V_{j,t+1} ⎦ = ⎣ λ*_jρ_{ij}        φ*_j       ⎦ ⎣ Δ^d V_{j,t} ⎦ + u_{ij,t+1}.  (58.13)

where γ*_{ij} represents the cross-country downside risk aversion. This coefficient measures the compensation for taking on the downside risk from the dominating country j, Δ^d V_{j,t+1}. The coefficient of φ*_j indicates a first-order serial correlation in the dominating downside risk process after taking the fractional difference. The cross-market leverage effect, λ*_j can be calculated by λ*_j = λ*_j ρ_{ij} / ρ_{ij} and its significance can be examined by testing λ*_j = 0. If we impose the restriction λ*_j = 0, equation (58.13) is reduced to

⎡ R_{i,t+1} ⎤   ⎡ 0      γ*_{ij}φ*_j ⎤ ⎡ R_{i,t} ⎤       
⎣ Δ^d V_{j,t+1} ⎦ = ⎣ 0      φ*_j      ⎦ ⎣ Δ^d V_{j,t} ⎦ + u_{ij,t+1}.  (58.13)'
