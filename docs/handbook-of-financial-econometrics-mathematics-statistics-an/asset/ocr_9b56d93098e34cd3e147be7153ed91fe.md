# 18.3.2.1 Tensor decomposition and reconstruction

In this part, we briefly introduce the mathematical notations and the tensor operations. Tensors are high-order arrays that generalize the notions of vectors and matrices. In this work, we use a 3rd-order tensor, representing a 3-dimensional array. Scalars are 0th-order tensors and denoted by lowercase letters, e.g., $a$. Vectors are 1st-order tensor and denoted by boldface lowercase letters, e.g., $\mathbf{a}$. Matrices are 2nd-order tensor and denoted by boldface capital letters, e.g., $\mathbf{X}$, and 3rd-order tensors are denoted by calligraphic letters, e.g., $\mathbf{A}$. The $i$th entry of a vector $\mathbf{a}$ is denoted by $a_i$, element $(i, j)$ of a matrix $\mathbf{X}$ is denoted by $x_{ij}$, and element $(i, j, k)$ of a 3rd-order tensor $\mathbf{A}$ is denoted by $a_{ijk}$. The $i$th row and the $j$th column of a matrix $\mathbf{X}$ are denoted by $\mathbf{x}_i :$ and $\mathbf{x} : j$, respectively. Alternatively, the $i$th row of a matrix, $a_i :$, can also be denoted as $\mathbf{a}_i$.

The norm of a tensor $A \in P^{N \times M \times L}$ is defined as

$\|\mathbf{A}\| = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{j=1}^{M} \sum_{k=1}^{L} a_{i,j,k}^{2}}$

This is analogous to the matrix Frobenius norm, which is denoted as $\|\mathbf{X}\|$ for a matrix $\mathbf{X}$. The $n$-mode product of a tensor $X \in P^{I_1 \times I_2 \times I_3}$ with a matrix $\mathbf{U} \in \mathbf{R}^{I_n \times J}$, denoted by $X \times_n U$, is a tensor of size $I_1 \times \cdots \times I_{n-1} \times J \times I_{n+1} \times \cdots \times I_N$ with the elements $(X \times_n U)_{i_1 \dots i_{n-1} j i_{n+1} \dots i_N} = \sum_{i_n=1}^{I_n} a_{i_1 i_2 \dots i_N} u_{i_n j}$.

The Tucker factorization of a tensor $A \in P^{N \times M \times L}$ is defined as:

$\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{X} \times_1 \mathbf{U} \times_2 \mathbf{V} \times_3 \mathbf{W}$

Here, $\mathbf{U} \in P^{N \times R}$, $\mathbf{V} \in P^{M \times S}$ and $\mathbf{W} \in P^{L \times T}$ are the factor matrices and can be thought of as the principal components in each mode. The tensor $X \in P^{R \times S \times T}$ is the core tensor and its entries show the level of interaction between the different components. The reconstructed tensor is derived by multiplying the core tensor and the three factor matrices. It can be observed that tensor decomposition and reconstruction has updated the value for each existing entries indicating its importance and fill some new entries showing the latent relationships. Generally speaking, tensor factorization can be regarded as an extension of the matrix decomposition. During the process of decomposition, data can be projected in the subspaces, which includes latent significance.
