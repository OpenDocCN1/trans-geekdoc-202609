To perform the Monte Carlo EM algorithm, at each iteration of the algorithm, a set of N samples of the unobservable random time-changes g₁, …, g_N, where g_s = (g_{s1}, …, g_{sT}), 1 ≤ s ≤ N, are drawn from the posterior distribution p(g|Y; Θ_{i−1}) (e.g., see Wei et al., 1990; McCulloch, 1997). However, no available random number generators can be used to sample the random time-changes g₁, …, g_N from the posterior distribution p(g|Y; Θ_{i−1}). Therefore, the independent Metropolis chain approach is considered to generate a random sample from the target distribution p(g|Y; Θ_{i−1}) (Metropolis et al., 1953; Hastings, 1970). In the following lemma, the posterior distribution p(g|Y; Θ_{i−1}) is derived.

# Lemma 70.3.1. The posterior distribution p(g|Y; Θ) for the random time-changes g = (g₁, …, g_T)′ is proportional to

$$p(g|Y;Θ) ∝ ∏_{t=1}^T exp{−κ g_t − δ_t / g_t + (ν_t − 1.5) log(g_t)}, \tag{70.15}$$\nwhere ν_t is the time t-shape parameter, the coefficient ζ = θ² / 2σ² + 1 and\n$$δ_t = \frac{(Y_t − \bar{Y} − φ_t)^2}{2σ²}. \tag{70.16}$$

Proof. See Appendix 70C.

In the independent Metropolis chain algorithm, a chain of size L is generated as follows: at the nth step of the chain, if the chain is at a point X_n = x, a candidate value y is sampled from a proposal transition density f(y) for the next location X_{n+1}. The candidate X_{n+1} = y is accepted with probability\n$$b(x, y) = \min\left\{ \frac{π(y) f(x)}{π(x) f(y)}, 1 \right\}.$$

An independent uniform random variate U is generated, if U < b(x, y), then X_{n+1} = y; otherwise, the step is rejected and the chain remains at X_{n+1} = x. After L such steps, where L is sufficiently large, a realization can be obtained from the target distribution.

Based on Lemma 70.3.1, the proposal transition density f of the target distribution, i.e., the posterior distribution p(g|Y; Θ), is chosen to be the distribution of T independent gamma random variables with shape parameters ν₁ − 0.5, …, ν_T − 0.5, respectively, and scale parameters 1/κ.
