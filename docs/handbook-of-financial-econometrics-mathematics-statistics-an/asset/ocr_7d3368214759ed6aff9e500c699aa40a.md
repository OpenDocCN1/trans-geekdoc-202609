From the property of the multivariate normal distribution, we have $Y_i\sim N(\mu_i,\sigma_{ii})$. Hence, $X_i$ is log-normal with

$E(X_i)=e^{\mu_i+\frac{\sigma_{ii}}{2}},$ (85.33)

$\operatorname{Var}(X_i)=e^{2\mu_i}e^{\sigma_{ii}}\big[e^{\sigma_{ii}}-1\big].$ (85.34)

Furthermore, by the property of the moment generating for the bivariate normal distribution, we have

$E(X_1X_2)=E(e^{Y_1+Y_2})=e^{\mu_1+\mu_2+\frac12(\sigma_{11}+\sigma_{22}+2\sigma_{12})}=E(X_1)E(X_2)\cdot\exp\big(\rho\sqrt{\sigma_{11}\sigma_{22}}\big).$ (85.35)

Thus, the covariance between $X_1$ and $X_2$ is

$\operatorname{Cov}(X_1,X_2)=E(X_1X_2)-E(X_1)E(X_2)=E(X_1)E(X_2)\cdot\big(\exp(\rho\sqrt{\sigma_{11}\sigma_{22}})-1\big)=\exp\!\Big(\mu_1+\mu_2+\frac12(\sigma_{11}+\sigma_{22})\Big)\cdot\big(\exp(\rho\sqrt{\sigma_{11}\sigma_{22}})-1\big).$ (85.36)

From the property of conditional normality of $Y_1$ given $Y_2=y_2$, we also see that the conditional distribution of $Y_1$ given $Y_2=y_2$ is log-normal.
