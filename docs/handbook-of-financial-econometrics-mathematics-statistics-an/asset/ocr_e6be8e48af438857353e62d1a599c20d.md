$$e_{it}^* = [e_{it}^{1*} e_{it}^{2*} \cdots e_{it}^{k-1*} e_{it}^{k*}]$$
$$A' = [A_1' A_2' \cdots A_{p-1}' A_p' B'],$$ (60A.2)

where the asterisk denotes some transformation of the original variable. If the original variable is presented as $m_{it}$, then the first-difference transformation imply that $m_{it}^* = m_{it} - m_{it-1}$, while for the forward orthogonal deviation $m_{it}^* = (m_{it} - \bar{m}_{it}) \sqrt{T_{it}/(T_{it}+1)}$, where $T_{it}$ is the number of available future observations for panel $i$ at time $t$, and $\bar{m}_{it}$ is the average of those future observations. Notice that this transformation gives larger weight to observations closer to the beginning of the sample, so these observations have equal variance.

Suppose these observations are stacked both over panels and over time, then the GMM estimator is given by

$$A = (\overline{Y^{*'}} Z \hat{W} Z' \overline{Y^*}) (\overline{Y^{*'}} Z \hat{W} Z' \overline{Y^*})^{-1},$$ (60A.3)

where $\hat{W}$ is a $(LxL)$ weighting matrix assumed to be non-singular, symmetric and positive semi-definite. Assuming that $E[Z'e] = 0$ and rank $E[\overline{Y^{*'}} Z] = kp + l$, the GMM estimator is consistent. According to Hansen (2002), the weighting matrix $\hat{W}$ may be selected to maximize efficiency. Joint estimation of the system of equations makes cross-equation hypothesis testing straightforward. Wald tests about the parameters may be implemented based on the GMM estimate of $A$ and its covariance matrix. Granger causality tests, with the hypothesis that all coefficients on the lags of the variable of interest are jointly zero, may likewise be carried out using this test.

# Appendix 60A.3: Lag-order selection

Panel VAR analysis is predicated upon choosing the optimal lag-order in panel VAR specification.
