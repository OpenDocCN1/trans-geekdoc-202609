where $f_1$, is an $n$-element vector and $b_1$ a $p$-element vector. The sum of squares of $f_1$, is

$f_1' f_1 = f_1' Y' Y f_1.$
(77C.2)

We wish to choose $b_1$ to maximize $f_1' f_1$. Clearly, some constraint must be imposed on $b_1$, or $f_1' f_1$, could be made infinitely large, so let us normalize by setting

$b_1' b_1 = 1.$
(77C.3)

The problem now is to maximize equation (77C.2) subject to equation (77C.3). Define $\phi$ as

$\phi = b_1' Y' Y b_1 - \lambda_1(b_1' b_1 - 1),$

where $\lambda_1$ is a Lagrange multiplier. Thus,

$\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial b_1} = 2 Y' Y b_1 - 2 \lambda_1 b_1.$

Setting $\partial \phi / \partial b_1 = 0$ gives

$(Y' Y) b_1 = \lambda_1 b_1.$
(77C.4)

Thus, $b_1$ is a latent (eigen) vector of $X' X$ corresponding to the root of $\lambda_1$. From equation (77C.2) and (77C.4), we see that

$f_1' f_1 = \lambda_1 b_1' b_1 = \lambda_1,$

and so we must choose $\lambda_1$, as the largest latent root of $Y' Y$. The $Y' Y$ matrix, in the absence of perfect collinearity, will be positive definite ($Y' Y > 0$) and thus have positive latent roots. The first principal component of $Y$ is then $f_1$.

Now define $f_2 = Y b_2$. We wish to choose $b_2$ to maximize $b_2' Y' Y b_2$ subject to $b_2' b_2 = 1$ and $b_1' b_2 = 0$. The reason for the second condition is that $f_2$ is to be uncorrelated with $f_1$. The covariation between them is given by

$b_2' Y' Y b_2 = \lambda_1 b_1' b_2$
$= 0$ if and only if $b_1' b_2 = 0.$

Define

$\phi = b_2' Y' Y b_2 - \lambda_2(b_2' b_2 - 1) - \mu(b_1' b_2),$

where $\lambda_2$ and $\mu$ are Lagrange multipliers:

$\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial b_2} = 2 Y' Y b_2 - 2 \lambda_2 b_2 - \mu b_1 = 0.$

Premultiply by $b_1',$

$2 b_1' Y' Y b_2 - \mu = 0.$
