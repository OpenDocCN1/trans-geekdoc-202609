A positive value of Y₁,ₖ indicates that new equity is issued in the interval k and a zero value of Y₁,ₖ is indicative of no equity being issued in that time interval.

Second, a firm can buy back its own equity in the market. Let Y₂,ᵢ be defined as the treasury stock acquired in the time interval i such that Y₂ = [Y₂,₁, Y₂,₂, Y₂,₃, …, Y₂,ₙ]′ is the vector of treasury stock acquisition over the N period planning horizon. If no treasury stock is acquired in any time interval k, Y₂,ₖ is then zero. Thus, the cumulative treasury stock acquired over i intervals of time is given by the expression (49.1b).

∑ₖ₌₀ⁱ Y₂,ₖ,  where Y₂,ₖ ≥ 0 for all k and i = 1, …, N. (49.1b)

It is not considered desirable, though probably permissible, that the cumulative treasury stock acquired at any time should exceed the cumulative retained earnings. Thus, the constraint on treasury stock acquisition is

φ ∑ₖ₌₀ⁱ Y₃,ₖ − ∑ₖ₌₀ⁱ Y₂,ₖ ≥ 0  for all i, (49.1c)

where φ is a constant greater than zero and less than one. In general, firms use only a portion, φ at most, of their retained earnings for acquiring treasury stock.

Third, the cumulative funds available from retained earnings over i time intervals are

∑ₖ₌₀ⁱ Y₃,ₖ,  for all i = 1, …, N, (49.1d)

where Y₃ = [Y₃,₁, Y₃,₂, Y₃,₃, …, Y₃,ₙ]′ is the vector of retained earnings over the N period planning horizon. The retained earnings in time interval i may be expressed as

Y₃,ᵢ = Eᵢ − Zᵢ, (49.1e)

where Eᵢ is the earnings for the time interval i and Zᵢ is the dividends paid during the interval. Thus, the incremental retained earnings for that interval equals the difference between the earnings, Eᵢ, and the dividends paid, Zᵢ.
