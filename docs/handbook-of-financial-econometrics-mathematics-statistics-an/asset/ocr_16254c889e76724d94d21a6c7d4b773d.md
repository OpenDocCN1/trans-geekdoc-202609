$$y(t, \tau) \approx \sum_{i=1}^{3} f_k(\tau) P_k(t) = f_1(\tau)P_1(t) + f_2(\tau)P_2(t) + f_3(\tau)P_3(t). \quad (61.31)$$

In this approximation, $P_k(t)$ is the time-$t$ realization of the $k$th principal component and $f_k(\tau)$ is the factor loading obtained from PCA as a function of $\tau$. The derivative of this yield curve function for the $n$th order is given by

$$\frac{\partial^n y(t, \tau)}{\partial \tau^n} \approx \sum_{i=1}^{3} \frac{\partial^n f_k(\tau)}{\partial \tau^n} P_k(t) = \frac{\partial^n f_1(\tau)}{\partial \tau^n} P_1(t) + \frac{\partial^n f_2(\tau)}{\partial \tau^n} P_2(t) + \frac{\partial^n f_3(\tau)}{\partial \tau^n} P_3(t).$$

To construct the nth order derivative of the factor loading function, CDGJ (2008) employ a linear polynomial to approximate $f_1(\tau)$, a quadratic for $f_2(\tau)$, and a cubic for $f_3(\tau)$. Then, they run a regression of $f_1(\tau)$ on a constant and a vector that is linear in $\tau = \{1,3,6,9,12\}$ months and evaluate the derivative at $\tau = 0$ for each day in the sample. Next, they run a regression of $f_2(\tau)$ on a constant and a vector that is linear in $\tau = \{1,3,6,9,12\}$ months and then one that is quadratic in the same vector and evaluate the derivative at $\tau = 0$ for each day in the sample. Finally, they run a regression of $f_3(\tau)$ on a constant and a vector that is linear in $\tau = \{1,3,6,9,12\}$ months, one that is quadratic in it, and one that is cubic in it, and evaluate the derivative at $\tau = 0$ for each day in the sample. The resulting coefficients are used to construct the first and second derivatives of the yield curve at short maturities. The above approximate expression for the yield curve will be used to form the level of the yield curve.
