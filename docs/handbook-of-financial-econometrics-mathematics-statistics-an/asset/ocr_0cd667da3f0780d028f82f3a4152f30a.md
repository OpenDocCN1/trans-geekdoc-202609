Monthly rates of return for JNJ, IBM, and BA are used to perform the analysis in this section. The sample period is from January 2001 to September 2007. The Markowitz model determines optimal asset allocation by minimizing portfolio variance using a constrained optimization procedure as follows:

Min Var$(R_p) = \sum_{i=1}^{3} \sum_{j=1}^{3} W_i W_j \sigma_{ij}$,  (80.16)

+   subject to
+   - (i) $\sum_{i=1}^{3} W_i E(R_i) = E^*,$
+   - (ii) $\sum_{i=1}^{3} |W_i| = 1.0,$

where the $E^*$ is the investors' desired rate of return, and where the absolute value of the weights $|W_i|$ allows for a given $W$ to be negative (sold short) but maintains the requirement that all funds are invested or their sum equals one.

The Lagrangian function is

Min $L = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} W_i W_j + \lambda_1 \sum_{i=1}^{n} [W_i E(R_i) - E^*] + \lambda_2 \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} W_i - 1 \right)$.  (80.17)

By using IBM, JNJ, and JPM, a three-security portfolio will be formed and optimal weights will be solved for.
