This process can be used to derive the standard error of reverse regression estimates. Since the large sample variance and covariance of regression coefficients of equations (75.2) and (75.3) can be readily estimated by the application of the standard formula, this will facilitate estimating the large sample variance of reverse regression estimates of equations (75.4) and (75.5). These variances are

$$\operatorname{var}(-b_0/b_1) \approx (-1/b_1)^2\operatorname{var}(b_0) + (b_0/b_1^2)^2\operatorname{var}(b_1)$$
$$+2(-1/b_1)(b_0/b_1^2)\operatorname{cov}(b_0,b_1), \qquad (75.7)$$

$$\operatorname{var}(1/b_1) \approx (-1/b_1^2)^2\operatorname{var}(b_1), \qquad (75.8)$$

$$\operatorname{var}(-b_2/b_1) \approx (-1/b_1)^2\operatorname{var}(b_2) + (b_2/b_1^2)^2\operatorname{var}(b_1)$$
$$+2(-1/b_1)(b_2/b_1^2)\operatorname{cov}(b_1,b_2), \qquad (75.9)$$

$$\operatorname{var}(-c_0/c_2) \approx (-1/c_2)^2\operatorname{var}(c_0) + (c_0/c_2^2)^2\operatorname{var}(c_2)$$
$$+2(-1/c_2)(c_0/c_2^2)\operatorname{cov}(c_0,c_2), \qquad (75.10)$$

$$\operatorname{var}(-c_1/c_2) \approx (-1/c_2)^2\operatorname{var}(c_1) + (c_1/c_2^2)^2\operatorname{var}(c_2)$$
$$+2(-1/c_2)(c_1/c_2^2)\operatorname{cov}(c_1,c_2), \qquad (75.11)$$

$$\operatorname{var}(1/c_2) \approx (-1/c_2^2)^2\operatorname{var}(c_2). \qquad (75.12)$$

This method of computing variances of reverse regression estimates can be generalized to a $k$-variable case very easily as reverse regression estimates are always either ratio of two coefficients (for example, $c_1/c_2$) or inverse of one coefficient (for example, $1/c_2$) of the reverse regression equation. Now, these variances can be used to compute $t$-ratio for reverse regression estimates of equations (75.4) and (75.5) for the purpose of testing their statistical significance. Without these $t$-ratios, the conclusion drawn as to whether or not direct and reverse regression estimates consistently bound the true coefficients will not be meaningful and reliable. Appendix 75A derives and presents the reverse regression estimates of a four-variable model and their variances as an illustration.
