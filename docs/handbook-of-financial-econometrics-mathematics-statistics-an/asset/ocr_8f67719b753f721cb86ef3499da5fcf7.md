We notice that, from Figure 36.15, the strongest wavelet correlation values are localized at a first time lag of zero and persist from the lowest time scale to an investment horizon of 64–128 days and with correlation values close to 1 for all investment horizons. This implies that the major world stock markets are almost perfectly integrated at a time lag equal to 0 and tend to move together. That can be interpreted as a result of growing economic and financial globalization in the world, which leads to reducing diversification opportunities between the global world stock markets and, consequently, the international diversification benefits suggested in the early works of Grubel (1968), Levy and Sarnat (1970), Solnik (1974) have largely disappeared in globalized world markets.
