The investor (consumer) sentiment proxy is correlated with investors’ (consumers’) irrational sentiment, which is the aggregate error in investors’ (consumers’) beliefs regarding underlying asset prices (general economic prospects). Moreover, the investor (consumer) sentiment proxy may reflect investors’ (consumers’) rational sentiment, which is the rational updating of investors’ (consumers’) expectations as new information appears. Based on the work of Baker and Wurgler (2006), we decompose the sentiment proxies to study the influence of irrational and rational sentiment on risk-neutral moments. Each sentiment proxy is regressed on the rational predictors of the underlying asset return and indicators of investment opportunities, analyzed by running regression (42.3). In this chapter, the rational control variables include an industry production index (IP), the difference in yield between 10-year Treasury bonds and the 90-day repurchase agreement rate (Yield), the 30-day repurchase agreement rate (RP), the TAIEX dividend yield (DY), and difference in returns between the TAIEX and a one month repurchase agreement (Risk Premium).13 The risk-neutral moment is then regressed on the residual of the sentiment proxy ε*_t, shown in regression (42.4)

IX_t = β0 + β1 IP_t + β2 Yield_t + β3 RP_t + β4 DY_t

Moment_t = α'_0 + α'_1 Moment_{t-1} + α'_2 ε*_t + ε'_t.   (42.4)

We find that the relation between the residual of the consumer sentiment proxy and the pre-crisis risk-neutral confidence intervals becomes insignificant. The implication is that the significant influence of the consumer sentiment proxy shown in Panel 1A-1 is driven by a rational sentiment component.
