This is strong support for the argument that corporations build up their supplies of liquid assets because of a planned increase in dividends. Long-term investment, on the other hand, seems to adjust downward. Eight of the ten industry coefficients are negative or zero, and one of the positive coefficients is insignificant. Thus the evidence suggests that long-term investment might fall in the face of a planned increase in dividends. Since investment has to compete with dividends for funds, this result is not altogether surprising. Only the very large positive coefficient for the paper industry provides conflicting evidence.
