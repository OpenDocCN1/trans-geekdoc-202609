In addition to the variables described above, (LOGNB and CONCENT) the regression employs a traditional set of control variables. Older firms are often more stable and may have smaller growth opportunities (and lower Q values), so we control for age with the variable AGE, which is the number of years since the firm's first incorporation. Firm size is included because large firms may be more successful in developing new technology than small firms (Chauvin and Hirshey, 1993) thus yielding different Q scores. In addition, Rajan and Zingales (1995) find that firm size is a proxy for the probability of default. Our proxy for size is LOGTA, the log of the firm's total assets. We control for overall leverage by including the traditional leverage ratio (LEVERAGE). More efficient firms may have superior performance, so we control for efficiency by including FA/TA, the ratio of fixed assets to total assets. We control for sales growth by including the variable GROWTH, the past year's growth in sales. Houston and James (1996) find that Tobin's Q depends on future growth opportunities, which are often affected by capital expenditures; consequently, we control for this by including the capital expenditure ratio, CAPEX/TA, which is capital expenditure divided by total assets. Lastly, Lang and Stulz (1994) and Chen and Steiner (2000) find that R&D expenditure is also positively related to Tobin's Q; to for this, we include the R&D expenditure ratio, RD/SALES, which is the ratio of research and development expenditure to total net sales. Additional control variables include ROA, which is the return to assets; earnings volatility, EBITDA, which is the standard deviation of EBIT during the previous 3 years; and the dividend payout ratio, DIV/TA, measured by total cash dividends divided by total assets. We also include the percentage of public debt to total debt (PD/TD), the percentage of non-bank debt to total debt (NBD/TD), and the percentage of foreign bank debt to total debt (FBD/TD). To control for variations in performance that might be attributable to managerial ownership, we include the percentage of equity that is owned by management and its square (MO and MO2, respectively).

> 12 This item excludes all non-financial liabilities, such as accounts payable, provisions for pensions, deferred taxes, and other provisions for future liabilities.
