to its variable vector (chromosome). Let $(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$ be the chromosome involving $n$ variables. Then a mutation operator is defined as:

$$p_t^{g+1}=p_t^g+N(0,\varepsilon_t^{g+1}), \tag{44.16}$$

where strategy parameters $\varepsilon_t$ are actually step sizes of mutation, and $g$ is the number of generations. The $\varepsilon_t$ will also evolve in their own way:

$$\varepsilon_t^{g+1}=\varepsilon_t^g\exp(\tau,N(0,1)), \tag{44.17}$$

where $\tau$ is the learning rate controlling the self-adaptation speed of $\varepsilon_t$.

**(2) Recombination:** Mutation performs a search step which is only based on the information of only one parent, while recombination can share several parents’ information. It reproduces only one offspring from $\rho$ parents. ES has two versions of the recombination technique: discrete recombination and intermediate recombination. Discrete recombination selects every component randomly for the offspring, from relevant components of $\rho$ parents. In contrast, the intermediate recombination gives an equal right to all $\rho$ parents for reproduction. The offspring takes the average of $\rho$ parent vectors as its value. When the variables are discrete, we need to perform a rounding operation.

**(3) Selection:** The selection operation is necessary to direct the search to the promising range of the object parameter space, and to produce optimization. There are two versions of this selection technique, i.e., comma selection, denoted by $(\mu,\theta)$, and plus selection, denoted by $(\mu+\theta)$. For $(\mu,\theta)$ selection, only $\theta$ offspring individuals can be selected, while parental individuals are not selected in the set. This selection technique will forget the information of the parent generation. Thus, we can avoid pre-converging on local optimal points. However, the plus selection $(\mu+\theta)$ gives a choice from the selection set, which comprises the parents and the offspring. It can ensure the best individuals survive, and then preserve them.

Moreover, the fitness function is a criterion for evaluating search results, and is also very important to the ES. The chromosome with high fitness value has high probability of being preserved to the next generation. So in this study we use this test’s overall hit rate as the fitness measure.
