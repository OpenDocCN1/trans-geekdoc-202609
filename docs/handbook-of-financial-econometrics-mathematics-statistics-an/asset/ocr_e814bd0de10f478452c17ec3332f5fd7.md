To the extent that large positive book-tax differences signal low accounting earnings quality, the results suggest that CEOs who exercise their discretion over the financial accounting process to inflate earnings do not seem to earn excessive compensation. In contrast, the positive coefficient for NEGBTD suggests that firms with large negative book-tax differences are more likely to have higher CEO compensation. To the extent that large negative book-tax differences signal high accounting earnings quality, CEO are rewarded with higher compensation even if accounting earnings are below taxable income. Taken together, the results support the efficiency hypothesis. The board of directors does not appear to be fixated narrowly at accounting earnings in determining CEO compensation. Hence, directors seem to use the information in book-tax differences in setting CEO compensation.
