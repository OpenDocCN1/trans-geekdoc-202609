**Proof.** First, we show that Δ > 0. Since all eigenvalues of B₋ have strictly negative real parts by Theorem 41B.1(3), so do the eigenvalues of B₋ + ξ I for all ξ in an open interval in (0, ∞). For all these ξ, from Theorem 41B.1(4) we obtain that

$$\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{\xi y} f_{(-)}(y) dy = \alpha_{-} (-\xi I - B_{-})^{-1} b_{-}^{T} < \infty,$$

and hence, $\int e^{-\xi y} dF(y) < \infty$.

Next, we show that $\psi(\Delta-) = +\infty$. If $\Delta < \infty$, $\Delta$ must be a pole of $\psi(\xi)$ on $C$ so that $\psi(\Delta-) = +\infty$ or $-\infty$. Since $\psi$ is strictly convex on $[0, \Delta)$, we have $\psi(\Delta-) = +\infty$. If $\Delta = \infty$, we have $\psi(\Delta-) = +\infty$ by the fact $D > 0$. This completes the proof. $\square$
