# 85.10 Price Bounds for Options

# 85.10.1 Options written on non-dividend-paying stocks

To derive the lower price bounds and the put–call parity relations for options on non-dividend-paying stocks, simply set the cost-of-carry rate, b, equal to the risk-less rate of interest, r. Note that the only cost of carrying the stock is interest.

The lower price bounds for the European call and put options are

c(S,T;X) ≥ max[0, S − X e^{−rT}], (85.79a)

and

p(S,T;X) ≥ max[0, X e^{−rT} − S], (85.79b)

respectively, and the lower price bounds for the American call and put options are

C(S,T;X) ≥ max[0, S − X e^{−rT}], (85.80a)

and

P(S,T;X) ≥ max[0, X e^{−rT} − S], (85.80b)

respectively. The put–call parity relation for non-dividend-paying European stock options is c(S,T;X) − p(S,T;X) = S − X e^{−rT}, (85.81a) and the put–call parity relation for American options on non-dividend-paying stocks is S − X ≤ C(S,T;X) − P(S,T;X) ≤ S − X e^{−rT}. (85.81b)

For non-dividend-paying stock options, the American call option will not rationally be exercised early, while the American put option may be done so.
