$$\begin{vmatrix}
A_{13}\pi_{13}+A_{23}\pi_{21}+A_{33}\pi_{31}+A_{43}\pi_{41}+A_{53}\pi_{51}\\
A_{13}\pi_{12}+A_{23}\pi_{22}+A_{33}\pi_{32}+A_{43}\pi_{42}+A_{53}\pi_{52}\\

\end{vmatrix}$$

$$\Longrightarrow\begin{vmatrix}
A_{33}\pi_{31} & A_{15}\pi_{11}+A_{25}\pi_{21}+A_{35}\pi_{31}+A_{45}\pi_{41}+A_{55}\pi_{51}\\
A_{33}\pi_{32} & A_{15}\pi_{12}+A_{25}\pi_{22}+A_{35}\pi_{32}+A_{45}\pi_{42}+A_{55}\pi_{52}
\end{vmatrix}=0.$$

That is,

$$\begin{aligned}
&\{(1-\theta_p\phi_{44})\theta_q\phi_{51}+\theta_p\phi_{41}\theta_q\phi_{54}\}(\pi_{11}\pi_{32}-\pi_{12}\pi_{31})\\
&+\{(1-\theta_p\phi_{44})\theta_q\phi_{52}+\theta_p\phi_{42}\theta_q\phi_{54}\}(\pi_{21}\pi_{32}-\pi_{22}\pi_{31})\\
&+\theta_q\phi_{54}(\pi_{41}\pi_{32}-\pi_{42}\pi_{31})+\{1-\theta_p\phi_{44}\}(\pi_{51}\pi_{32}-\pi_{52}\pi_{31})\\
&=0. \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad (116.68)
\end{aligned}$$

Equations (116.67) and (116.68) state that the two unknown measurement error variances, $\theta_p$ and $\theta_q$, can be solved by the parameters $\phi_{ij}$ and $\pi_{ij}$ which can be obtained from observations by regressing **Y** on **X**. Obviously, we can solve the two equations in two unknowns for $\theta_p$ and $\theta_q$, given $\phi_{ij}$ and $\pi_{ij}$ are estimable. As a consequence, we can conclude that in a simultaneous equations model, the measurement error variance need not destroy the identifiability provided that the model is otherwise over-identified. In fact, the over-identifying restrictions have been traded off against the under-identifiability introduced by measurement error (Hsiao, 1976).
