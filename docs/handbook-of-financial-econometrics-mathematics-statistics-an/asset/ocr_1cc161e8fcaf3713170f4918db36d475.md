Then, we can express the joint log-likelihood function in terms of the transition density of the state variables $x_t$ and the cross-sectional error term $u_t^m$ for the yields measured with error,

$$\log(LG(\theta)) = \sum_{t=2}^{T} -\log|\det(J)| + \log f_x(x_t^o, x_t^u|x_{t-1}^o, x_{t-1}^u) + \log f_{u^m}(u_t^m)$$ $$= -(T-1)\log + |\det(J)| - (T-1)\frac{1}{2}\log(\det(\Sigma\Sigma^T))$$ $$- \frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=2}^{T} (x_t - \mu - \Phi x_{t-1})^T (\Sigma\Sigma^T)^{-1} (x_t - \mu - \Phi x_{t-1})$$ $$- \frac{T-1}{2}\log\sum_{i=1}^{4}\sigma_i^2 - \frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=2}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\frac{(u_{t,i}^m)^2}{\sigma_i^2}. \qquad (61.17)$$

In this expression, $\sigma_i^2$ is the variance of the $i$th measurement error term and it is applicable for a given parameter vector $\theta = (\mu, \Phi, \Sigma, \delta_0, \delta_1, \lambda_0, \lambda_1)$ and the total number of parameters including measurement error terms to be estimated is 31 (6 for $\Phi$, 3 for $\delta_1$, 5 for $\lambda_0$, 13 for $\lambda_1$, 4 for $\sigma_i^2$). The remaining parameters are estimated via ordinary least squares regressions as described above in prior steps. We maximize the aforementioned log-likelihood function selecting the parameter vector $\theta$ provided above using the parameters given in Table 6 on Page 765 of their paper (Ang and Piazzesi, 2003) initial conditions for our estimation. The reason we use those parameters as initial conditions is the remark by the authors that owing to the complexity of their model, they design a very specific algorithmic procedure to estimate it and implement it over several iterative rounds.
