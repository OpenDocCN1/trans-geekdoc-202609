However, inconsistent with the theory of financial distress cost, we do not find a negative association between uniqueness attribute and the capital structure. Burdett (1978) claims that workers quit only because a better wage offer is found. In his theory, quit rates can be viewed as wage quit rates. Workers do not accumulate firm-specific human capital because they know a job before starting employment. Therefore, quit rates is negatively related to growth firms with high salary offers. Combined with the previous finding that growth firms usually issue less debt, the lower quit rates may therefore be associated with lower debts. Thus, the relationship between uniqueness attribute and firms’ leverage would be positive.

We also find a significantly positive association between the profitability and the capital structure. This finding indicates that, consistent with the pecking order theory, firms may raise debt to profitable investment opportunities if the retained earnings are not sufficient to invest. In addition, the profitable firms have lower transaction and issuing costs on debts. Therefore, the profitable firms would utilize more leverage to invest in profitable opportunities in accordance with the decrease in agency costs and the inexpensive issuing costs of debts.

In addition to attributes of firm characteristics, we find that macroeconomic factors have significantly negative effects on the capital structure. The negative relationship confirms McDonald’s (1983) theory that the increase in government leverage will lead to the decrease in private debt. In addition, the negative association between the manager attribute and firms’ leverage is consistent with the theory of agency costs. The strong managerial entrenchment and a manager’s ability would lead to the decrease in debt because managers prefer private benefits to the shareholder’s profits.

For the simultaneous relationship between capital structure and stock rate of returns, the usage of debt can lower information asymmetry problem and therefore debt issuance can be viewed as a positive signal to shareholders. Therefore, Ross (1977) suggests that the increase of leverage will lead to the increase of stock returns. However, high stock prices would lead to the decrease of debt usage in accordance with “managerial investment autonomy” theory suggested by Dittmar and Thakor (2007) and the “market timing behavior” phenomenon in Yang (2013).
