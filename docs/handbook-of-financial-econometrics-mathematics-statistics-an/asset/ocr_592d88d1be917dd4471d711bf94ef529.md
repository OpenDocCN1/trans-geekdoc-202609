The GRS and Hotelling’s T² tests of four specifications of the SMLs soundly reject the CAPM in the 25 size and book-to-market portfolios datasets — over two thirds of the time with the expanding window. The CAPM fares better with the fixed 120-month window with the rejection percentage ranging from 37% to 54% of the 852 months. The contrast between the results in the 25 size and book-to-market portfolios and the 10 size portfolios datasets is striking. The different ways of estimating the equilibrium means are rejected at most 2.46% of the time with mean p-values ranging from a low of 0.77 to a high of 0.97. I expected that the GRS test and Hotelling’s T² test, with the intercept set equal to the average risk-free rate and the slope set equal to the average excess return on the market, would yield similar results. For the most part, they do. But there are clear differences in the 12 industry portfolios dataset with the GRS test rejecting far more often than the Hotelling’s T² test. We explore this further in the following paragraphs.

While the tabular results are informative, there is a time-series component of the results not captured in the table.
