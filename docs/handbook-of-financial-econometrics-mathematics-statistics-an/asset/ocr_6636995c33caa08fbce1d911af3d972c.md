This study adopts a questionnaire survey to collect quantitative data and further employs an SEM approach to examine the proposed conceptual model. An SEM approach is broadly utilized in marketing research, which is usually related to consumers’ cognitive dissonances (e.g., perceived value, perceived service quality, etc.). These dissonances are latent variables measured by observed variables (i.e., question items). Many marketing studies adopt the SEM approach to examine the influences of cognitive dissonances on acceptance of specific products and services (Kleijnen et al., 2004; Olorunniwo et al., 2006; Soureli et al., 2008; Theron et al., 2008; Tu et al., 2011; Fernando and Alben, 2015). Like cognitive dissonances, cognitive/emotional biases are latent variables, which means that the SEM approach is appropriate in examining whether these biases influence investor’s behavior (MacCallum and Austin, 2000; McDonald and Ho, 2002). To the best of my knowledge, only a very few studies to date have utilized the SEM approach when examining the disposition effect.

The results of this study find that mental accounting and regret avoidance are significant biases that influence the disposition effect, resulting consequently in underperformance. Mental accounting is the most significant factor influencing the disposition effect. As such, prospect theory (an S-shaped value function) could be an alternative to expected utility theory (expected utility function) to account for investors’ behavioral decisions. However, there is no evidence to show that investors’ self-imposed disciplines work efficiently, which implies that investors have self-control bias. The findings of moderating analysis indicate that female investors and married ones are more likely to be influenced by mental accounting, whereas male investors and single ones are more easily affected by regret avoidance. Moreover, female investors seem to have greater disposition effect bias.

This study offers several substantial contributions. Firstly, unlike prior literature, this study examines multiple cognitive/emotional biases to uncover the relationship between these biases and the disposition effect. Secondly, this study employs an appropriate approach to conduct the estimation procedure. The SEM approach can provide more precision for model specification, and increase confidence in the proposed measures for decision-making and substantive hypothesis testing. Finally, based on the empirical results, this study highlights several strategic implications that might assist investors, financial institutions, and regulatory authorities in developing their investment strategies, promoting their financial products and services, and improving financial discipline, respectively.
