for any sequence of partitions $t_0 = 0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_n = t$, with $\sup_i\{t_{i+1}-t_i\} \to 0$ for $\delta \to 0$. Thus,

$$\mathrm{RV}_t \xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}} [Y]_t$$

where $P$ denotes convergence in probability. Thus, realized quadratic variation (QV) is expressed as:

$$\mathrm{QV} = [Y_\delta]_t = \sum_{i=1}^{t/\delta} r_{i,t}^2. \tag{29.4}$$

Another important measure is called integrated volatility, which is defined as $\int_0^t \sigma_u^2 du$. When asset prices are continuous on a fixed interval $[0,T]$:

$$[Y]_t \xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}} \int_0^t \sigma_u^2 du, \tag{29.5}$$

and when asset prices also have a discontinuous component on $[0,T]$ (like in equation (29.1)):

$$[Y]_t \xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}} \int_0^t \sigma_u^2 du + \sum_{u \le t} (\Delta Y_u)^2, \tag{29.6}$$

where $\sum_{u \le t} (\Delta Y_u)$ is a pure jump process and a jump at time $s$ is defined as $\Delta Y_t = Y_u - Y_{u-}$. Here, $\sum_{u \le t} (\Delta Y_u)^2$ is the variation of the jump component.

# 29.2.2 Jump testing

The literature on jump testing has been active since 2002. Testing whether or not jumps are present in a process is particularly useful to do prior to constructing realized measures of jump and continuous components of a variable. For early relevant discussions in this area, see Andersen et al. (2002) and Chernov et al. (2003), as well as Aït-Sahalia (2002) and Johannes (2004). In this paper, we discuss three different tests including: the bipower-variation-based tests of Barndorff-Nielsen and Shephard (2006a, 2006b, 2006c), Huang and Tauchen (2005), Andersen et al. (2007), and Lee and Mykland (2008); the swap-variance-based test due to Jiang and Oomen (2008); and the truncated-power-variation based tests due to Aït-Sahalia and Jacod (2008, 2009a, 2009b) and Lee and Hanning (2010). We also discuss a so-called long-time span jump test due to Corradi et al. (2018), which is consistent (the above fixed time span tests are not consistent, in the sense that power does not go to unity as the sample size increases).
