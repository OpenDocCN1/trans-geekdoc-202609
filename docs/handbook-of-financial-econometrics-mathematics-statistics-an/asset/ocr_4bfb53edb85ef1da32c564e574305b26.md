# 24.2.2.2 Second degree stochastic dominance with riskless asset (SSDR)

Distinct with Perrakis and Ryan’s (1984) option bounds, which are obtainable by applying second degree stochastic dominance method, Levy (1985) applies the concept of second degree stochastic dominance with the riskless asset (SSDR) to derive the upper and lower bounds for European options. Levy (1985) assumes that the investors’ preferences belong to the set of all non-decreasing concave utility function and the underlying assets are those with non-negative consumption beta. Let F_c and F_s be the cumulative distributions of the terminal wealth investing in an European call option and its underlying asset. If borrowing and lending at the risk free rate are allowed, which is crucial assumption to derive relative narrower bounds, then an investor may find that any combination of the underlying asset with the riskless asset is dominated by some combination of European call option with the riskless asset even though neither F_c nor F_s dominates to each other without the riskless asset. That is, in the presence of a riskless asset, if every risk-averse investor prefers to hold the investment F_c rather than the investment F_s, then F_c is said to dominate F_s by SSDR.

Therefore, the European call option bounds can be obtained by using the intuition that the lower (upper) bound of an European call option is derived in such a way that F_c(F_s) exactly does not dominates F_s(F_c). The criterion of SSDR is stated as follows:

Let F_c and F_s be the two cumulative distributions with the p^{th} quantile, Q_{F_C}(p) and Q_{F_S}(p), respectively. Then F_c dominates F_s in the SSDR if and only if

$$\inf_{0\le p<p_C}\frac{\int_0^p[Q_{F_S}(w)-r]dw}{\int_0^p[Q_{F_C}(w)-r]dw}\ge\sup_{p_C<p\le1}\frac{\int_0^p[Q_{F_S}(w)-r]dw}{\int_0^p[Q_{F_C}(w)-r]dw}$$ where $p_C$ is the value which solves $\int_0^{p_C}[Q_{F_C}(w)-r]dw=0$.

Levy (1985) proves that the cumulative distribution of the return of a European call option, F_c, dominates the cumulative distribution of the return of its underlying asset, F_s, in the SSDR if and only if $p_c \le p_s$, where $p_c$ and $p_s$ are the values that solve $$\int_0^{p_c}[Q_{F_C}(w)-r]dw=0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_0^{p_S}[Q_{F_S}(w)-r]dw=0.\tag{24.8}$$
