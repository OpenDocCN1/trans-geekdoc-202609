Basso and Pianca (1997) reconsider the following maximization and minimization problem:

$$\text{Minimize (or Maximize)}\quad C_0=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_i e_i,$$

$$\text{subject to}\quad \sum_{i=1}^{n}s_i e_i=S_0\ \text{and}\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}e_i=B_0\ \text{with}\ (e_1,e_2,e_3,\ldots,e_n)\in D, \tag{7.17}$$

where the set $D$ depends on the stochastic dominance rule adopted and is determined by the assumptions made on the utility function. For example, given utility function with decreasing absolute risk aversion (DARA) character, the set $D$ is constrained by:

$$e_i\ge 0,\quad i=1,2,\ldots,n$$

$$e_i\ge e_{i+1},\quad i=1,2,\ldots,n-1$$

$$e_i e_{i+2}\ge e_{i+1}^{2},\quad i=1,2,\ldots,n-2. \tag{7.18}$$

Recently, Peña, Vera and Zuluaga (2009) show that the problem of computing sharp upper and lower static-arbitrage bounds on the price of a European basket option, given the prices of other similar options, can also be cast as a linear program. The linear program formulations readily yield super-replicating (sub-replicating) strategies for the upper (lower) bound problem. The dual counterparts of the linear program formulations in turn yield underlying asset price distributions that replicate the given option prices, and the bound on the new basket option’s price. In the special case when the given option prices are those of vanilla options on the underlying assets, they show that the linear program formulations admit further simplifications. In particular, for the upper bound problem they derive closed-form formulas for the basket’s price bound and for the corresponding super replicating strategy. In addition, their linear program approach admits efficient modeling of additional features such as basket options with negative weights, bid-ask spreads, transaction costs, and diversification constraints.
