Instead of the N univariate t tests, multivariate tests were developed (e.g., see Gibbons, 1982; Jobson and Korkie, 1985; Shanken, 1985; Gibbons et al., 1989; MacKinlay, 1987; MacKinlay and Richardson, 1991). Among these, the multivariate F-test by Gibbons, Ross and Shanken (1989) has become the standard. Tests in the Bayesian framework were developed by Shanken (1987) and Kandel et al. (1995) that is based on the ratio ρ = SR/SR* between the ex ante Sharp ratio SR of the test portfolio p and the ex ante maximum Sharp ratio SR* attainable by the N risky assets and the test portfolio p. However, either the multivariate F-test by Gibbons, Ross and Shanken (1989), or the Bayesian tests by Shanken (1987), the test statistics depends on the ex post Sharpe ratio \hat SR of the test portfolio p. Therefore, the powers of these tests depend on the precision of the ex post Sharpe ratio \hat SR. However, the ex post Sharpe ratio \hat SR is calculated empirically based on the test portfolio p’s historical excess returns R1, . . . , RT that are subject to measurement errors. In case the ex post Sharpe ratio \hat SR overestimates the ex ante Sharpe ratio SR, the power of the test decreases. For this reason, the measurement errors associated with the ex post Sharpe ratio \hat SR need to be taken into consideration.

This study develops a Bayesian test of a test portfolio p’s mean–variance efficiency. In contrast to the previous mean–variance efficiency tests, the measurement errors associated with test portfolio p’s ex post Sharpe ratio \hat SR is taken into consideration using the derived asymptotic distribution of \hat SR under stationary and ergodic excess returns (Christie, 2005; Opdyke, 2007). The proposed Bayesian test is based on the Bayes factor B10 that compares the joint likelihoods under the null hypothesis H0 and the alternative hypothesis H1, respectively (e.g., see Kass and Raftery, 1995). Therefore, the proposed Bayesian test is unconditional and the power of the proposed Bayesian test is independent of the measurement errors associated with the ex post Sharpe ratio \hat SR.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 93.2, the multivariate F-test of a portfolio p’s efficiency by Gibbons et al. (1989) is reviewed. In Section 93.3, Bayesian tests from the previous literature are reviewed. An unconditional Bayesian test based on the Bayes factor is developed. An empirical study that uses monthly return data of 10 industrial portfolios and a test portfolio, namely, the CRSP value-weighted index, from January 1941 to December 1973 and January 1980 to December 2012, respectively, is given in Section 93.4. An independent simulation study is also given. The performance of the proposed Bayesian test is compared to the conditional F-test of Gibbons, Ross, and Shanken (1989) and Shanken’s Bayesian test (Shanken, 1987). Section 93.5 concludes.
