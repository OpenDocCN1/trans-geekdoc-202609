Structure asymmetry is the fourth typical feature of bank risk dependence. It refers to interactions between different pairs of bank risks being distinctly different and asymmetric. Banks face many types of risks, such as credit, market and operational risks, and any two of them are relevant to each other (Baker and Filbeck, 2015). However, due to their diverse sources and interaction mechanisms, the dependencies between them are usually disparate (Brockmann and Kalkbrenner, 2010). For example, it is widely recognized that the correlation between credit risk and market risk is quite different from correlations between operational risk and the two risk types (Rosenberg and Schuermann, 2006; International Financial Risk Institute Foundation and Chief Risk Officers Forum, IFRI/CRO, 2007). Generally, structure symmetry often exists only among risks that are uniform and interact in the same manner, such as credit portfolios of homogeneous risks (BCBS, 2010).

This chapter proposes a mixture copula framework to describe the dependencies between various types of bank risks, which is able to comprehensively capture the four typical features of the dependence structure. To be specific, by reasonably combining individual copulas with different feature capturing capabilities, a mixture copula that is able to capture all features can be constructed. Then, the mixture copula can be used to describe the dependence structure between different risk types and integrate them into the total risk of banks. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work that constructs mixture copula to model bank risk dependence. In the empirical study, the proposed approach is applied to describe the dependencies between credit, market and operational risks of Chinese banks. The comparisons with seven other major approaches provide strong evidence of the effectiveness of the constructed mixture copulas and help to uncover some important pitfalls and misunderstandings in risk dependence modeling.
