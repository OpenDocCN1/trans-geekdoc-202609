The classical mean-variance model of the portfolio theory actually implies the simplest “Buy-and-Hold” trading strategy, and the then subsequent multi-period portfolio models are just multi-period “Buy-and-Hold”. The set of trading strategies considered here for the Intelligent Portfolio Theory (IPT) includes all existing trading strategies with qualified profit-generating capability and effective risk control mechanism, and all other theoretically possible trading strategies with good profit-risk ratios.

On the basis of these two hypotheses that actually express our understanding of how the trading markets work, three principles are introduced, which represent our fundamental tenets in developing the Intelligent Portfolio Theory (IPT):

## Principle 1: Invest in Trading

In a capital market, the price of each financial asset is a result of trading among many intelligent agents. Instead of investing on the asset price — an aggregate phenomenon of trading agent interactions, we should invest in trading as a process. In other words, we should invest in a portfolio of capable trading strategies which are always readily prepared to operate on the selected assets. For a given portfolio of selected assets, that asset portfolio is to become a portfolio of trading strategy portfolios, where the capital allocation on each asset is diversified through investing in a portfolio of trading strategies dedicated to that asset.

## Principle 2: Divide and Conquer the Complexity of Portfolio Management

The complexity of asset portfolio investment should be divided into two parts — the portfolio management and trading strategies for dynamic implementation (control) of the portfolio management. Actually, the division of asset portfolio and trading strategy portfolio divides and conquers the complexity of asset portfolio investment into three levels: the asset portfolio, the (trading) strategy portfolios, and the trading strategies themselves. The first two levels diversify the investment risk among different assets and among different trading strategies. On the third level, each trading strategy is designed with its own risk control mechanism such as using a proper combination of take-profit and stop-loss for each position entered. Trading has its own value more than just for implementation of an asset portfolio.

## Principle 3: Financial Information Fusion

The division of asset portfolio and trading strategy portfolios and their natural integration provides a three-level framework for investment management. On this framework, multiple sources of financial information can be naturally connected (or docked) into proper nodes in this framework, so financial information fusion can be realized properly.

The Intelligent Portfolio Theory (IPT) is meant to enable the investigation on developing intelligent portfolio trading systems, taking the two hypotheses and the three principles as the theoretical bases and methodological guidelines.

## 43.3.2 A framework of intelligent portfolio management

Generally speaking, a complete approach to financial market investment management should include at least four major steps in a loop:

**Step 1: Predictive Analysis:** This is a nonstop process of surveillance, monitoring, modeling and prediction of global and domestic macroeconomies and financial markets for mapping and maintaining a comprehensive picture of the market trends, volatility, liquidity, policy changes, market ecology, market sentiment, and strategic games.

**Step 2: Asset Selection:** After predictive analysis, a set of assets are to be selected out, each meets certain criteria of liquidity, value and/or growth, or preferences or other considerations.

**Step 3: Portfolio Construction:** The weight of capital allocation on each selected asset is then to be determined through a global optimization process. Note this optimization should be dynamic and rerun whenever scheduled or triggered by conditions.

**Step 4: Trade Execution:** For each selected asset with its capital allocation, long or short trade on the asset is then to be executed with market timing for entry, exit and position management. Note that, after the capital allocation is done on an asset, trading on this asset is not necessarily be just “buy-and-hold”, instead, successive entry-and-exit procedures should be the general norm. The trading logic for when to enter and when to exit is specified by the corresponding trading strategy.

In every step, either asset selection, portfolio construction, or trade execution, risk management should be implemented throughout. In the step of asset selection, assets with big risk are directly ruled out. This is a hard measure of risk control. In the stage of portfolio construction, assets with bigger risk are assigned with smaller weights, or with zero weights. This is a soft measure of risk control. In the stage of trade execution, the timing of entry and exit as well the way of entry and exit such as trading the entire position at once, scaling in or out, or algorithmic trading, and the tradeoff between the profit target and stop-loss levels, are all parts of risk control. The equity curve of a trading strategy is also an important source of information, which can be used as a feedback signal for starting or stopping the trading strategy on the given asset. This showcases the technical-level risk control. In addition, there are trading strategies that encompass predictive analysis in the trading decision and control. Therefore, such a complete process of investment management comprises three-level dynamic optimization processes — asset selection, portfolio construction and trading strategy.

For the five aspects of a general financial investment management — predictive analysis, asset selection, portfolio construction, technical trading and risk management, the Intelligent Portfolio Theory (IPT) first of all integrates portfolio construction and technical trading into a framework of portfolio trading. This framework extends left to the information fusion-based predictive analysis of the markets and relative strength analysis of the assets for selecting assets with relatively strong trends, and it reaches right to the portfolio adjustment and rebalance as well as the total risk management.
