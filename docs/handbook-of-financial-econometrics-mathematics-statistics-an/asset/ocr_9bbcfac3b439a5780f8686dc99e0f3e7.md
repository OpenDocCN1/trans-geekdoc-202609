This study revisits this issue to examine the role of financial advisors in M&As. This study firstly examines what factors influence the choice of financial advisors for targets and bidders. Specifically, this study looks into what factors influence the choice of different types of financial advisors in terms of high and low-reputable financial advisors and domestic and foreign advisors. Secondly, this study explores the relationship between completion time and different types of financial advisors. Thirdly, the current study examines whether firms hired by high reputable financial advisors can create higher value to their shareholders relative to those with low-reputable financial advisors and whether domestic advisors can outperform their foreign counterparts. This can shed lights on the importance of financial advisors in M&As.

Using the standard event study methodology with 333 targets and 949 bidders during the period of 1995–2011, the evidence reveals that firm size is an important determinant in the choice of financial advisors. When large firms involve themselves in M&As, firms are more likely to hire financial advisors. The evidence also shows that targets hired by low-reputable financial advisors spend more time in completing the deals. On the contrary, bidders hired by low-reputable financial advisors complete the deals faster than those with high, reputable financial advisors. In addition, bidders that hire domestic advisors can take longer time to complete the deals in comparison to those with foreign advisors. With regard to the impact of firm performance, the results reveal that targets hired by low- reputable financial advisors earn slightly higher announcement returns than those with high-reputable financial advisors. In addition, domestic advisors create higher gains to targets relative to foreign advisors. Taking into account the evidence for bidding firms, bidders hired by less prestigious financial advisors obtain higher gains around the announcement date relative to those with more prestigious counterparts. When looking at the post-announcement period, the results are reverse, showing that bidders hired by low-reputable financial advisors experience higher losses. Interestingly, I find that bidders hired by domestic advisors obtain higher announcement returns both around the announcement date and during the post-announcement period compared to those with foreign advisors. In the regression analysis, the results show that bidders obtain higher post-announcement returns when bidders hire domestic advisors.
