and

$$\hat{H} = \frac{\delta^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{\eta QPV_t / BV_t^2}} \left( \frac{\mu_1^{-2} BV_t}{QV_t} - 1 \right) \xrightarrow{d} N(0,1).$$

Inference using these tests is straightforward, as both test statistics have limiting standard normal distributions. Clearly, the ratio $\frac{\int_0^t \sigma_u^4 du}{\mu_1^{-4} BV^2} \ge 1/t$, and Barndorff-Nielsen and Shephard (2006a) suggest replacing $\hat H$ by the adjusted ratio jump test

$$\hat{J} = \frac{\delta^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{\eta \max\left(t^{-1} \frac{QPV_i}{BV_i^2}\right)}} \left( \frac{\mu_1^{-2} BV_t}{QV_t} - 1 \right) \xrightarrow{d} N(0,1). \tag{29.10}$$

Huang and Tauchen (2005), Andersen et al. (2007) analyze the statistical properties of bipower variation-based jump tests using S&P index data, exchange rates, and bond yields; as well as via Monte Carlo simulation. They suggest using a daily statistic, $z_{TP,t}$ to test for jumps on a daily basis, where

$$z_{TP,t} = \frac{RV_t - BV_t}{\sqrt{(v_{bb} - v_{qq}) \frac{1}{N} TP_t}} \xrightarrow{d} N(0,1) \tag{29.11}$$

with $v_{qq}=2$, $v_{bb}=\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^2 + \pi - 3$, Here, realized tripower quarticity (TP) is defined and estimated as follows:

$$TP_t = \delta^{-1} \mu_{4/3}^{-3} \sum_{j=3}^{1/\delta} |r_{i-2,t}|^{4/3} |r_{i-1,t}|^{4/3} |r_{i,t}|^{4/3} \xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}} \int_0^t \sigma_u^4 du. \tag{29.12}$$

Additionally, the asymptotic covariance of

$$\delta^{-1/2} \begin{pmatrix} RV_t - \int_0^t \sigma_u^2 du \\ BV_t - \int_0^t \sigma_u^2 du \end{pmatrix}$$

is $\Pi \int_0^t \sigma_u^4 du$, where

$$\text{Var}(u^2) \cdot 2 \mu_1^{-2} \text{Cov}(u^2, |u||u'|)$$

$$\Pi = (2\mu_1^{-2} \text{Cov}(u^2, |u||u'|) \mu_1^{-4} (\text{Var}(|u||u'|) + 2\text{Cov}(|u||u'|, |u'||u''|)))$$

$$= \begin{pmatrix} v_{qq} & v_{qb} \\ v_{qb} & v_{bb} \end{pmatrix}$$
