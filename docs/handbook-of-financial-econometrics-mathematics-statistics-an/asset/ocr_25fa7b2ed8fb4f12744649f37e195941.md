In this chapter, to investigate the coherence between stock market returns, the time scale axis is divided into high, medium and low frequencies (Bogdanova, 2014). Indeed, the high frequencies are considered to span from two days to three months and represent the random component in a given time series. This random component is in its turn driven by the news. Consequently, if two markets are affected by the same news, strong co-movements at the high frequencies would be observed in both crisis and non-crisis periods. The medium frequencies which span from three months to one year indicate the presence of a contagion phenomenon. The low frequencies, which are driven by the fundamentalists and which indicate the interdependence of the markets, are considered for investment horizons above one year.

From Figure 36.5, we notice that a strong co-movement at low frequencies is observed since the beginning of the investigated period and lasts until the end of the period under study. The high degree of co-movement between France and Germany in long-term investment horizons indicates a fundamental relationship and a high degree of integration between stock market returns in long-run frequencies, which increases the probability of crisis propagation.
