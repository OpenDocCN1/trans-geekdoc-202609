The correlations between Tobin’s Q and social media are all positive, with the highest of 0.084 between Tobin’s Q and Facebook. We explain this from the theorem that social media can reduce information asymmetry and indirectly enhance firm value.

By contrast, the correlations between Tobin’s Q, bank relationships and bank debt ratio are all negative, with the highest negative correlation of −0.168 between Tobin’s Q and bank relationships. This inverse relationship suggests that firms that rely more (less) on bank relationships have lower (higher) firm value.

Interestingly, the correlations between social media, bank relationships and bank debt ratios are mostly negative but insignificant. Among them, the correlation between Facebook and bank relationships is the highest at −0.057. These results imply that social media and bank relationships have a potential substitution effect on reducing the information asymmetry problem.

The social media variables also correlate with each other. The correlation between YouTube and Facebook is the highest at 0.57, followed by Twitter and YouTube, at 0.382, and by Twitter and Facebook, at 0.303.

## 66.4.3 Empirical results

Table 66.3 presents the results of OLS analysis based on equation (66.1). Column 1 shows that the coefficient on Facebook is 0.09, which is significant and better than the 1% level, implying that the use of Facebook increases the firm value. The magnitude of this effect is economically significant; that is, the use of Facebook increases Tobin’s Q by 8.4% (= 0.09/1.071) from the sample mean of Tobin’s Q of 1.071. Hence, the use of Facebook contributes to 8.4% of Tobin’s Q. By contrast, the coefficients of YouTube and Twitter are insignificant.

The impact of bank relationships and bank debt ratios on Tobin’s Q is just the opposite. The former has a negative coefficient of −0.027, while the latter has a positive coefficient of 0.429, both are significant at the 1% level. In terms of economic magnitude, it implies that a one standard deviation increase in bank relationships from their mean value (an increase of 0.901) decreases Tobin’s Q by 0.4% (= 0.027*0.901/5.989, recall that 5.989 is the average number of bank relationships). On the contrary, one standard deviation increase in bank debt ratio from its mean value (an increase of 0.263) increases Tobin’s Q by 67% (= 0.429*0.263/0.168, recall that 0.168 is the average bank debt ratio).
