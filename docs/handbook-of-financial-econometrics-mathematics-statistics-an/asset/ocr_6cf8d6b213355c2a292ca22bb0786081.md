Lin et al. (2012) derived a tighter lower bound and re-interpret Ritchken (1985) upper bound of European options under non-parametric framework the only variable used in these bounds is the dollar beta of an option which is defined as a covariance between an option price and its underlying asset’s price over the variance of its underlying asset’s price. Lin et al. (2012) utilize the general classical asset pricing model with a stochastic kernel to derive the lower bound at time t for a European call option as follows:

```
$$C_{t,\min}^{E} = D_{t,T} E[C_T] + \beta_C \{S_t - D_{t,T} E[S_T]\},$$
```

where $\beta_C = \frac{\text{Cov}(C_T,S_T)}{\text{Var}(S_T)}$ denotes the dollar beta of the call option, $D_{t,T}$ is the risk-free discount factor and can be written as $D_{t,T} = e^{-r(T-t)}$ if the risk free rate is non-stochastic. The lower bound of a European put option can be derived by the put-call parity as follows:

```
$$P_{t,\min}^{E} = C_t + D_{t,T} K - S_t \geq C_{t,\min}^{E} + D_{t,T} K - S_t = P_{t,\min}^{E}.$$
```

By substituting the lower bound formula of a European call option, then we can get

```
$$P_{t,\min}^{E} = D_{t,T} E[P_T] + \beta_P \{S_t - D_{t,T} E[S_T]\},$$
```

where $\beta_P = \frac{\text{Cov}(P_T,S_T)}{\text{Var}(S_T)} = 1 - \beta_C$ denotes the dollar beta of the put option and can be calculated by the dollar beta of the call option. By using histogram method to calculate the expected payoffs of the stock and the option, then lower bounds of European options can be obtained under non-parametric framework.
