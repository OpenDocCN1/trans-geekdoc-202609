+   (4) **Event-Based Active Trend Following (EATF):** Mature market economies of developed countries have developed a conventional information disclosure mechanism. Release of financial and economic policies or readings of economic indicators which may impact the financial markets are conventionally scheduled with exact dates and times, often as precise as to the minute. Therefore the market calendar has emerged. When and which market authorities will release what market-impacting information have already arranged into the market calendar, although the polarity (negative or positive) of each information or data and the polarity of its market impact are unknown in advance. A trader needs to enter into the attention state, closely monitor the market activities before, during and after the release, take appropriate trading actions accordingly. This type of event-based trading strategies based on known market calendar but unknown polarity of information and that of market impact is feasible in developed countries where the market calendar exists. According to our long-term observation to international financial markets, about a half of profitable trading opportunities belong to this type of event-driven situations. As for emergent events with breaking news outside the market calendar, it is generally assumed in academics to be unpredictable. Or from a viewpoint of big science, prediction of emergent events outside the financial markets and economies requires far bigger and more complex models encompassing political, economic, financial, and environmental information. A group of European cross-disciplinary scientists were reported pushing for a grand project aiming to develop a Living Earth Simulator, which belongs to this type of big science prediction models. Apparently this type of complex models would require connecting to the technologies of the Internet, the Internet of Things, and deep intelligence. This is beyond the scope of this chapter.
