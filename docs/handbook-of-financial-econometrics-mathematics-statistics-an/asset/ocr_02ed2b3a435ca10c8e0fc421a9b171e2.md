# 24.4 The Option Bounds for American Options and Asian Options

In this section, the previous studies that investigate the option bounds for American options, Asian options, currency cross-rate options, and real options are reviewed in detail.

# 24.4.1 American option bounds

American option valuation requires considering not only the option pricing problem itself but also simultaneous the determination of the early exercise boundary. Most previous papers use lattice model to estimate American options because there doesn’t exist closed-form function for American style options. A lattice model is a tree model with nodes to represent the time and state spaces of underlying assets in order to approximate the American option price. Practical implementation involves numerical methods that may become computationally burdensome, in particular, when there are multiple state variables. Carr, Jarrow and Myneni (1992) derive the upper bound for American put options as a European put option plus a fixed premium. Chaudhury and Wei (1994) studied the bounds on American future options. Broadie and Detemple (1996) derive the lower bound of the American call option by using lattice model where the optimal exercise boundary of the American call option is set as one of nodes on binomial tree. Perrakis (1986) apply the bounds by Perrakis and Ryan (1984) to obtain the bounds for American put option that can be represented in the binomial model.

In contrast with heavy computational burden in lattice method, Chen and Yeh (2002) derived closed-form upper bounds under stochastic interest rate, stochastic volatility and jumps model. Chung and Chang (2007) further generalize Chen and Yeh’s (2002) upper bounds to solve the case of inadequate upper bounds for American options when the interest rate is smaller than the dividend yield.
