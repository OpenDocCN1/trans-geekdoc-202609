
Then, we can express the joint log-likelihood function in terms of the transition density of the state variables $x_t$ and the cross-sectional error term $u_t^m$ for the yields measured with error,

$$\log(LG(\theta)) = \sum_{t=2}^{T} -\log|\det(J)| + \log f_x(x_t^o, x_t^u|x_{t-1}^o, x_{t-1}^u) + \log f_{u^m}(u_t^m)$$ $$= -(T-1)\log|\det(J)| - (T-1)\frac{1}{2}\log(\det(\Sigma\Sigma^T))$$ $$- \frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=2}^{T} (x_t - \mu - \Phi x_{t-1})^T (\Sigma\Sigma^T)^{-1} (x_t - \mu - \Phi x_{t-1})$$ $$- \frac{T-1}{2}\log\sum_{i=1}^{4}\sigma_i^2 - \frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=2}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\frac{(u_{t,i}^m)^2}{\sigma_i^2}. \qquad (61.17)$$

In this expression, $\sigma_i^2$ is the variance of the $i$th measurement error term and it is applicable for a given parameter vector $\theta = (\mu, \Phi, \Sigma, \delta_0, \delta_1, \lambda_0, \lambda_1)$ and the total number of parameters including measurement error terms to be estimated is 31 (6 for $\Phi$, 3 for $\delta_1$, 5 for $\lambda_0$, 13 for $\lambda_1$, 4 for $\sigma_i^2$). ## 61.2.2.2 The model of Joslin, Singleton, and Zhu (2011)

Within the Gaussian setting, JSZ develop a normalization of PCDTSM in which pricing factors are taken to be portfolios of yields with weights obtained from PCA. To provide an overview of the development of their normalization, we begin by specifying the following discrete-time characterization of latent factors $x_t \in R^N$:

$$\Delta x_t = \kappa_{0x}^{\mathbb{P}} + \kappa_{1x}^{\mathbb{P}} x_{t-1} + \Sigma_x \epsilon_t^{\mathbb{P}}, \qquad (61.18)$$

$$\Delta x_t=\kappa_{0x}^{Q}+\kappa_{1x}^{Q}x_{t-1}+\Sigma_x\epsilon_t^{Q},$$

$$r_t=\rho_{0x}+\rho_{1x}x_t.$$

In equations (61.18)–(61.20), $r_t$ is the one-period spot rate, $\Sigma_x\Sigma_x^T$ is the conditional covariance matrix of $x_t$, and $\epsilon_t\sim N(0,I_N)$. Based on this characterization, prices for a zero coupon yield of maturity $m$ can be expressed as

$$D_{t,m}=E_t^Q\left[e^{-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}r_{t+i}}\right]=e^{A_m+B_mx_t}.$$

Taking the natural logarithm of equation (61.21) leads to the following expression for zero coupon yields as shown

$$y_t=A_m+B_mx_t.$$

In equation (61.22), $(A_m,B_m)$ satisfy the well-known Riccati difference equations

$$A_{m+1}-A_m=\kappa_0^{Q\prime}B_m+\frac{1}{2}B_m^{\prime}H_0B_m-\rho_0,$$

$$B_{m+1}-B_m=\kappa_1^{Q\prime}B_m-\rho_1,$$

subject to the initial conditions $A_0=0$ and $B_0=0$ and with loadings for the corresponding bond yields given as $a_m=-A_m/m$ and $b_m=-B_m/m$⁴.

⁴JSZ remark that although all their results are specified in discrete-time, without loss of generality, all of their results apply to continuous-time models as well. In regards to the Riccati ODE, since the structure of the model is similar whether we estimate it in continuous-time or discrete-time, we can demonstrate how to obtain the Riccati ODE in continuous-time and show how it parallels the discrete-setting. The steps that follow largely follow Duffee and Kan (1996), Fisher and Gilles (1996), and Piazzesi (2010) and so we refer the reader to these papers for more information. We begin with the class of affine term structure models, such as JSZ, for which bond prices have the following form:

$$P(x,\tau)=\exp(A(\tau)+B(\tau)^Tx).$$

In the expression above, A and B are coefficients defining how bond prices and the state variable $x$ (e.g., the principal component) are related. In this footnote, we provide an explanation for how one would obtain A and B for use in estimating an affine term structure model. To obtain the state factor coefficients, we note that by the absence of arbitrage the short rate R(x) should equal the instantaneous bond return $\frac{dP}{P}$. Thus, by Itô’s lemma, we derive the process for the instantaneous bond return for which we need the following derivatives:

$$P_\tau(x,\tau)=\frac{\partial P(x,\tau)}{\partial\tau},\quad P_x(x,\tau)=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial P(x,\tau)}{\partial x_1}\\\vdots\\\frac{\partial P(x,\tau)}{\partial x_d}\end{bmatrix},\qquad P_{xx}(x,\tau)=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial^2P(x,\tau)}{\partial x_1^2}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^2P(x,\tau)}{\partial x_1\partial x_d}\\\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\\frac{\partial^2P(x,\tau)}{\partial x_d\partial x_1}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^2P(x,\tau)}{\partial x_d^2}\end{bmatrix}.$$

The parameters used for pricing are

$$\Theta_x^{\mathbb{Q}} = (\kappa_0^{\mathbb{Q}}, \kappa_1^{\mathbb{Q}}, \Sigma_x, \rho_0, \rho_1).$$

Then,

$$P_\tau(x,\tau)=P(x,\tau)\bigl(A'(\tau)+B'(\tau)^T x\bigr)$$

$$\frac{P_\tau(x,\tau)}{P(x,\tau)}=A'(\tau)+B'(\tau)^T x$$

$$P_x(x,\tau)=P(x,\tau)B(\tau)$$

$$\frac{P_x(x,\tau)}{P(x,\tau)}=B(\tau)$$

$$P_{xx}(x,\tau)=P(x,\tau)B(\tau)B(\tau)^T$$

$$\frac{P_{xx}(x,\tau)}{P(x,\tau)}=B(\tau)B(\tau)^T.$$

So, for process $dx_t = \mu_x(x_t)dt + \sigma_x(x_t)^T dW_t$

$$\frac{dP}{P}=\mu_x^T \frac{P_x(x,\tau)}{P(x,\tau)}+\frac{1}{2}tr\left[\frac{P_{xx}(x,\tau)}{P(x,\tau)}\sigma_x^T\sigma_x\right]-\frac{P_\tau(x,\tau)}{P(x,\tau)}$$ 
$$=R(x)\quad\text{(absence of arbitrage)}.$$

Plugging in the aforementioned derivatives into this expression,

$$\mu_x^T B(\tau)+\frac{1}{2}[B(\tau)^T\sigma_x\sigma_x^T B(\tau)]-A'(\tau)-B'(\tau)^T x=R(x).$$

For the JSZ normalization of the Gaussian dynamic term structure model, let $H_0=\sigma_x^T\sigma_x$ and $R(x)=\rho_0+\rho_1 x$ and $\mu_x=\kappa_0^{Q^T}+\kappa_1^{Q^T}x$, then

$$(\kappa_0^{Q^T}+\kappa_1^{Q^T}x)B(\tau)+\frac{1}{2}[B(\tau)^T H_0 B(\tau)]-A'(\tau)-B'(\tau)^T x=\rho_0+\rho_1 x.$$

Collecting terms in $x$ as well as the constant term and recognizing that both must identically be zero,

$$\kappa_0^{Q^T} B(\tau)+\frac{1}{2}[B(\tau)^T H_0 B(\tau)]-A'(\tau)-\rho_0=0$$

$$(\kappa_1^{\mathbb{Q}^T} B(\tau))x-\rho_1 x-B'(\tau)^T x=0.$$ 

Thus,

$$A'(\tau)=\kappa_0^{Q^T} B(\tau)+\frac{1}{2}[B(\tau)^T H_0 B(\tau)]-\rho_0.$$ 

Equivalently, for discrete-time and without loss of generality for time period $m$, 

$$A_{m+1}-A_m=\kappa_0^{Q^T} B_m+\frac{1}{2}[B_m^T H_0 B_m]-\rho_0.$$ 

And,

$$B_{m+1}-B_m=\kappa_1^{Q^T} B_m-\rho_1.$$
