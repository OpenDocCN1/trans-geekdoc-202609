# Appendix 38A: Individual Copulas

Assume $u_1, u_2, \dots, u_n$ follow univariate standard uniform distribution, and then, the five popular copulas mentioned in this chapter are introduced as follows.

(1) The Gaussian copula is presented as

$C_{\text{Gaussian}}(u_1, u_2, \dots, u_n | R) = \Phi_R(\Phi^{-1}(u_1), \Phi^{-1}(u_2), \dots, \Phi^{-1}(u_n)), \quad (38A.1)$

where $\Phi_R$ denotes the standard multivariate Gaussian distribution function with correlation coefficient matrix $R$ and $\Phi^{-1}$ denotes the inverse function of the standard univariate Gaussian distribution. The Gaussian copula has zero tail dependence and is generally viewed as a benchmark for comparison.

(2) The $t$ copula is presented as

$C_t(u_1, u_2, \dots, u_n | \nu, R) = t_{\nu,R}(t_{\nu}^{-1}(u_1), t_{\nu}^{-1}(u_2), \dots, t_{\nu}^{-1}(u_n)), \quad (38A.2)$

where $t_{\nu,R}$ denotes the $t$ distribution function with a degree of freedom $\nu$ and correlation coefficient matrix $R$, and $t_{\nu}^{-1}$ denotes the inverse function of $t$ distribution function. The $t$ copula exhibits the same dependence in both right and left tails. The larger is the degree of freedom, the weaker is the tail dependence for a $t$ copula. The Gaussian copula is, in fact, a limiting case of the $t$ copula as the degree of freedom approaches $\infty$.

(3) The Gumbel copula is presented as

$C_{\text{Gumbel}}(u_1, u_2, \dots, u_n | \alpha) = \exp\left\{ -\left[\sum_{n=1}^{N}(-\ln u_n)^{\alpha}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right\}, \quad \alpha \in [1, \infty), \quad (38A.3)$
