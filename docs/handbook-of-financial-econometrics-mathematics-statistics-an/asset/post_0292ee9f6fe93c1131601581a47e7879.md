
$$\Delta\text{LnGold}_{futures,t}=\alpha+\eta\hat{e}_{t-1}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{i}\Delta\text{LnGold}_{futures,t-i}
+\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{j}\Delta\text{LnGold}_{spot,t-j}+e_{t},\tag{92.3}$$

where Δ denotes the first difference of variables, and N and M are the lag lengths. The variable \hat{e}_{t-1} is the lag-1 variable of the estimated error-correction terms given from equation (92.1), and e_t denotes a residual. In our empirical work, we set N and M the same and choose them with Schwarz information criteria (SIC) in vector auto-regression (VAR) models.

### 92.2.2.2 Quantile co-integration

The traditional co-integration model describes the following equation:

$$y_{t}=\alpha+\beta'_{t}x_{t}+\mu_{t}\tag{92.4}$$

where y_t and x_t are integrated with order 1(I(1)) and μ_t is stationary in level. We followed Xiao (2009) who applies the method proposed by Saikkonen (1991) to decompose μ_t into the lead-lag terms $\sum_{j=-K}^{K}\Delta x_{t-j}$ and a pure innovation component ε_t in order to deal with endogeneity in the traditional co-integration model given below.

$$y_{t}=\alpha+\beta'_{t}x_{t}+\sum_{j=-K}^{K}\Delta x'_{t-j}\Pi_{j}+\varepsilon_{t}.\tag{92.5}$$

If the τth quantile of ε_t is Q_ε(τ), let Ω_t = σ {x_t, Δx_{t-j}, ∀j}, the τth quantile of y_t is given by

$$Q_{y_t}(τ|Ω_t)=α+β(τ)'x_t+\sum_{j=-K}^{K}\Delta x'_{t-j}\Pi_j+F_{ε}^{-1}(τ),\tag{92.6}$$

where F_ε(.) is the cumulative distribution function of ε_t. Let Z_t be the vector of regressions consisting of (1, x_t) and (Δx'_{t-j}, j = -K, ..., K), θ = (α, β'_t, Π'_{-K}, ..., Π'_K)', hence, we arrive at

$$θ(τ)=(α(τ),β(τ)',Π'_{-K},...,Π'_K)',\tag{92.7}$$

where α(τ) = α + F_ε^{-1}(τ). We then rewrite the regression as

$$y_t = \theta' Z_t + \varepsilon_t \tag{92.8}$$

and

$$Q_{y_t}(\tau \mid \Omega_t) = \theta(\tau)' Z_t, \tag{92.9}$$

as the τth conditional quantile function of y_t, where Koenker and Bassett (1978) has ρ_τ = μ(τ − I(μ < 0)); thus, the τth quantile regression delivers the least absolute deviation (LAD) estimate of the co-integration model.

### 92.3.3 Gold spot prices and futures prices with quantile co-integration

We further examine whether co-integrating coefficients are constant in equation (92.1). Results show a statistically significant existence of nonlinear co-integrating relationships between gold spot and various maturities of futures prices.² We then estimate the co-integrating coefficients between gold spot process and different maturities of futures prices across different quantiles. Table 92.5 and Figures 92.2(a)-92.2(e) present the co-integrating coefficients and their corresponding tracks across various quantiles. We find that future prices have stronger co-integration relationships with gold spot prices at high quantiles. By contrast, futures prices have lower co-integration with gold spot prices at low quantiles. That means the co-integration between gold spot and futures is increasing with quantiles. These findings suggest that when gold spot prices are at low quantiles, the response of spot prices to futures prices is miniscule. However, when spot gold prices are at high quantiles, the responses become considerable.

Moreover, long-term futures prices have lower co-integration than short-term futures prices at the same quantile.
