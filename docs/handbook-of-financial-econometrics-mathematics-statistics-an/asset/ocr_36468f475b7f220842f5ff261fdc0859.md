# Figure 102.1: Three-period binomial decision tree of stock price.

If we assume that r, u, and d will remain constant over time, deriving the option’s fair value with two or more periods to maturity is a relatively simple process of working backwards from the possible maturity values. Using the same procedure, we can extend the two-period model to a three-period model as in equation (102.11):

C = [p³C_uuu + 3p²(1-p)C_uud + 3p(1-p)²C_udd + (1-p)³C_ddd]/r³. (102.11)

A graphical interpretation of equation (102.11) is presented in Figures 102.1 and 102.2. Figure 102.1 presents the stock price binomial decision tree and Figure 102.2 presents the call option decision tree.

In Figure 102.1, S represents stock price per share in period 0. In period 1, stock price can either go up (uS) or go down (dS).

Similarly, in period 2, stock prices can be u²S, udS, duS or d²S. In period 3, stock price has eight possible cases as presented in Figure 102.1 in detail.
