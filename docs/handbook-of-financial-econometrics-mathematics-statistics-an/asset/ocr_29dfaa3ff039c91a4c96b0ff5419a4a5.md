Similarly, Habiboellah (2007) has conducted a study on modeling dependence structure in financial risk management, focusing on market, credit and operation risk in the banking field. The researcher pointed out that the Gaussian copula model has become a benchmark model for valuating collateralized debt obligations in the financial market, which usually have skewed distributions due to occasional losses. The Gaussian copula model that does not rely on normally-distributed data captures well both the thickness of the tails and risk dependence structure, compared to correlation, which always assumes a multivariate normal distribution.

Kharoubi-Rakotomalala and Maurer (2013) agreed with Habiboellah (2007) that Gaussian copula usually serves as a benchmark model to capture dependence structure in the financial market, as this copula “underlines the main theoretical financial models” (p. 1561). With an analysis on a sample of daily data from 2002 to 2011, they found that the Gaussian copula is “optimal” for explaining the dependence structure between the returns of the stock market index S&P 500 and CAC 40. Nevertheless, they highlighted that “the choice of a copula should be related to the problem at hand and not on mathematical convenience” (p. 1564). This supports the importance of understanding the behavior of each asset returns and the typical economic fundamentals affecting the asset prices before applying the copula method to study their dependence structure.

Rossi, Ehlers, and Filho (2011) employed the copula-GARCH models on modeling financial returns, where the errors are assumed to follow a standardized Student’s t-distribution, so that the Gaussian and Student’s t copula functions can be applied to examine the dependence structure between financial time series. Nevertheless, the researchers discovered that the copula parameters may change over time due to different contagion regimes. Likewise, Wang et al. (2014) also applied the copula-GARCH models for modeling exchange rate risk for commercial banks in China, and subsequently to determine the optimal holding ratio of foreign currency assets under different risk situations. The researchers generated exchange returns by using Monte Carlo simulation to compute the VaR. Then, the joint distribution of two exchange rate returns was studied by using Gaussian copula to find the optimal proportion of two currency assets for a minimum risk portfolio. Using autoregressive and GARCH specifications as marginals, Patton (2006) examined the dependence structure of mark-dollar and yen-dollar exchange rates based on constant as well as time-varying Gaussian and symmetrized Clayton copulas.
