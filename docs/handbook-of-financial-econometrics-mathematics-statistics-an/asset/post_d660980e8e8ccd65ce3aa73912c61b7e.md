## Appendix 23B: Robinson’s (1995) Semiparametric Whittle Method

Throughout this method, the estimate of $d$ is implicitly defined by

```
\hat{d} = \arg\min_d \left( \log \overline{C}(d) - 2d \frac{1}{m} \sum_{j=1}^m \log \lambda_j \right),
```

for $d \in (-1/2, 1/2)$; $\overline{C}(d) = \frac{1}{m} \sum_{j=1}^m I(\lambda_j) \lambda_j^{2d}$, $\lambda_j = \frac{2\pi j}{n}$, $\frac{1}{m} + \frac{m}{n} \to 0$,
where $m$ is the bandwidth parameter, and $I(\lambda_j)$ is the periodogram of the time-series, $x_t$, given by:

```
I(\lambda) = \frac{1}{2\pi n} \left| \sum_{t=1}^n x_t e^{i \lambda_j t} \right|^2 .
```

Under finiteness of the fourth moment and other mild conditions, Robinson (1995a) proved that:

```
\sqrt{m} (\hat{d} - d_o) \to_d N(0, 1/4) as n \to \infty,
```

where $d_o$ is the true value of $d$ and with the only additional requirement that $m \to \infty$ slower than $n$. Velasco (1999a) and Phillips and Shimotsu (2004) showed the consistency and the asymptotic normality of this estimator for $d \in (-0.5, 1]$ and $d \in (-0.5, 0.75)$, respectively with $m^{1/2}(\hat{d}-d_o) \to_d N(0, 1/4)$. Velasco (2003) showed the consistency of $\hat{d}$ when applied to estimated cointegrated errors. Other semiparametric methods, some based on the Whittle function (Kim and Phillips, 1999; Velasco, 1999a; Abadir et al., 2007) and others based on the log periodogram (Robinson, 1995b; Velasco, 1999b) were also employed producing very similar results.

## Appendix 23C: Fractional Cointegration: Marinucci and Robinson (2001)

Marinucci and Robinson (2001) show that:

```
H_{ix} = 8m \left( \hat{d}_* - \hat{d}_i \right)^2 \to_d \chi^2_1 as \frac{1}{m} + \frac{m}{n} \to 0,
```

where $n$ refers to the sample size and $m < [n/2]$ is the bandwidth number going to infinite though at a slower rate than $n$; $\hat{d}_i$, $i = 1$ and $2$ are the
univariate estimates of the parent series, and $\hat{d}_*$ is a restricted estimate obtained in the bivariate context under the assumption that $d_x = d_y$. In particular,

$\hat{d}_* = -\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{s^*} 1_2' \hat{\Omega}^{-1} Y_j v_j}{2 1_2' \hat{\Omega}^{-1} 1_2 \sum_{j=1}^{s^*} v_j^2}$

where $1_2$ indicates a $(2 \times 1)$ vector of 1s, $\hat{\Omega}$ is the estimated variance covariance matrix, and with $Y_j = [\log I_{xx}(\lambda_j), \log I_{yy}(\lambda_j)]^T$, with $\lambda_j = 2\pi j/n$, $j = 1,2,\dots n$, as the Fourier frequencies, and where for arbitrary sequences $\xi_t$ and $\zeta_t$ we define the discrete Fourier transform and the (cross)-periodogram

$w_\xi(\lambda) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi n}} \sum_{t=1}^n \xi_t e^{i\lambda t},\quad I_{\xi\zeta}(\lambda) = w_\xi(\lambda) w_\zeta^T(-\lambda),\quad I_\xi(\lambda) = I_{\xi\xi}(\lambda)$

and $v_j = \log j - \frac{1}{s} \sum_{j=1}^{s^*} \log j$. The limiting distribution above is presented heuristically, but the authors argue that it seems sufficiently convincing for the test to warrant serious consideration.
