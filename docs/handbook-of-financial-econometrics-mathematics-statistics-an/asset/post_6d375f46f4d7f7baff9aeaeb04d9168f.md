
Similarly, Habiboellah (2007) has conducted a study on modeling dependence structure in financial risk management, focusing on market, credit and operational risk in the banking field. The researcher pointed out that the Gaussian copula model has become a benchmark model for valuing collateralized debt obligations in the financial market, which usually have skewed distributions due to occasional losses. The Gaussian copula model that does not rely on normally-distributed data captures well both the thickness of the tails and risk dependence structure, compared to correlation, which always assumes a multivariate normal distribution.

Kharoubi-Rakotomalala and Maurer (2013) agreed with Habiboellah (2007) that Gaussian copula usually serves as a benchmark model to capture dependence structure in the financial market, as this copula “underlines the main theoretical financial models” (p. 1561). With an analysis on a sample of daily data from 2002 to 2011, they found that the Gaussian copula is “optimal” for explaining the dependence structure between the returns of the stock market index S&P 500 and CAC 40. Nevertheless, they highlighted that “the choice of a copula should be related to the problem at hand and not on mathematical convenience” (p. 1564). This supports the importance of understanding the behavior of each asset returns and the typical economic fundamentals affecting the asset prices before applying the copula method to study their dependence structure.

Rossi, Ehlers, and Filho (2011) employed the copula-GARCH models on modeling financial returns, where the errors are assumed to follow a standardized Student’s t-distribution, so that the Gaussian and Student’s t copula functions can be applied to examine the dependence structure between financial time series. Nevertheless, the researchers discovered that the copula parameters may change over time due to different contagion regimes. Likewise, Wang et al. (2014) also applied the copula-GARCH models for modeling exchange rate risk for commercial banks in China, and subsequently to determine the optimal holding ratio of foreign currency assets under different risk situations. The researchers generated exchange returns by using Monte Carlo simulation to compute the VaR. Then, the joint distribution of two exchange rate returns was studied by using Gaussian copula to find the optimal proportion of two currency assets for a minimum risk portfolio. Using autoregressive and GARCH specifications as marginals, Patton (2006) examined the dependence structure of mark-dollar and yen-dollar exchange rates based on constant as well as time-varying Gaussian and symmetrized Clayton copulas. His findings that the returns exhibited greater correlation during market downturns rejected the possibility of symmetrical dependence structure in these exchange rate series. Dependence in exchange rate markets is also investigated by Wang et al. (2010) using copulas on four currencies including US dollar, euro, yen and Hong Kong dollar vis-à-vis Chinese yuan. The risk structure of the portfolios of these exchange rates was considered based on the VaR and conditional VaR measures. They introduced the GARCH-EVT (extreme value theory) copulas and found that the risks of the multiple asset portfolio are best described by the Student’s t copulas.

On the other hand, Cech (2006) has established a copula-based top-down approach to study the dependence structure between risk factor changes for financial risk aggregation, such as interest rate and credit risk. The researcher found that Gaussian copula may underestimate the dependence structure when there is a strong joint risk factor changes due to extreme events, especially in times of crisis. For this reason, the Archimedean copulas that are more suitable for capturing asymmetrical upper and lower tail dependence were introduced for applications in finance, mainly in the stock and bond market, as well as the currency market.

Copulas with symmetrical densities were also used to study the dependence structure of financial markets with other markets. For example, Gronwald et al. (2011) applied the Gaussian and Student’s t copulas to examine the dependence between the financial, carbon and commodity markets, and found the results to be significant. They also reported greater dependence between these markets during the period of financial crisis. Aloui et al. (2013) modeled the dependence structure between the oil and exchange rate markets using daily crude oil prices and five US dollar exchange rates. They compared a variety of time-varying copulas, but the Student’s t copulas provide the best model for the conditional dependence structure of the extreme co-movements in these markets.

## 73.2.2 Applications of Archimedean copulas

Patton (2002) has applied the copula theory to financial econometrics in his PhD dissertation. He found evidence that copula models usually outperform the multivariate normal distribution models, as a copula process under the Sklar’s (1959) theorem allows an n-dimensional joint distribution function to be formed from different marginal distributions. Consequently, Archimedean copulas can better address the asymmetric dependence structure of non-normally distributed financial data, which is more useful than linear correlation. Weiss (2013), for instance, found that the quality of risk estimates of bivariate portfolios can be improved with the use of Archimedean copulas. Liu (2006) showed that financial data, especially exchange rate, are better explained by Archimedean copulas that are able to detect currency tail risk in advance. This is supported by Lai and Goh (2019) who applied the Archimedean copulas to investigate the impact of Chinese yuan devaluation on a group of major and regional currencies. They reported a higher degree of lower tail dependence after the devaluation. Huang et al. (2014) employed the Archimedean copulas to examine the crash sensitivity of global currencies, which habitually exhibit nonlinear dependence structure. Again, they discovered that the linear model always underestimates the tail dependence structure, particularly in extraordinary situations, such as government policy uncertainties. One of the GARCH-EVT copulas introduced by Wang et al. (2010) for examining exchange rate dependence structure is based on the Clayton copula. It did not, however, perform as well as the Student's t copula in the analysis of the risk structure of exchange rate portfolios comprising of four major currencies in China considered by the researchers.

In line with the findings that support the use of Archimedean copulas, Shamiri et al. (2011) found that the tail dependence between stock returns will be underestimated when extreme events occur. They applied the Clayton-Gumbel copula approach to measure the lower and upper tail dependence between Singapore (STI), Malaysia (KLCI) and the US (S&P 500) composite stock index due to extreme events. Their results showed significant dependence between STI and KLCI, with around 35% conditional probability that the return of STI or KLCI will move in tandem to either the lower or upper tail of their return distributions. Naifar (2011) warned against the underestimation of tail dependence that may cause non-trivial default risk together with losses in stock markets. This may arise due to the co-movements of the CDS and stock markets in extreme market conditions, as shown in his study of the dependence between credit default swap and equity returns using a family of Archimedean copulas.

Kvasnakova (2009) applied the Archimedean copula approach for modeling the returns of pension funds based on the stock and bond market performance. His results showed stronger left tail dependence between these two markets, which indicates that there is a higher conditional probability that the stock or bond returns will drop to a record low, when the other experiences a similar drop. Based on some model selection criterion, the researcher found that the rotated Gumbel copula offers the best fit, which further supported that the returns in stock and bond markets register higher dependence during the times of crisis.
