# 42.4.3 Alternative measure of risk-neutral skewness

It is well known that the skewness measure based on the sample average is sensitive to extreme values. Therefore, we use Pearson’s (1895) skewness measure to investigate the robustness of the relation between sentiment effect and diversity in call and put option prices. Pearson’s coefficient of skewness is defined as

$$SK = \frac{\mu - M_0}{\tilde{\sigma}}, \tag{42.6}$$

where $M_0$ is the mode of the risk-neutral distribution. The relation between sentiment effect and alternative measurement of risk-neutral skewness is reported in Table 42.6. The regressions of Table 42.6 have the same specifications as the skewness regressions in Table 42.4. The difference between Tables 42.4 and 42.6 is the dependent variable, which is the measurement of risk-neutral skewness. The result in Panel 1A of Table 42.6 indicates that investors’ irrational sentiment and consumers’ rational sentiment positively influence the alternative measurement of pre-crisis risk-neutral skewness, consistent with the evidence in Panel 2A of Table 42.4. Panel 1B of Table 42.6 reports the significant influence of investors’ irrational sentiment on the crisis risk-neutral skewness. Furthermore, rational and irrational components of consumer sentiment proxy have different impacts on the crisis risk-neutral skewness separately derived from calls and puts. Therefore, using Pearson’s measurement to calculate the risk-neutral skewness in the crisis period does not alter the result in Panel 2B of Table 42.4.
