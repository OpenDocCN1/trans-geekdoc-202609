# Appendix 23B: Robinson’s (1995) Semiparametric Whittle Method

Throughout this method, the estimate of $d$ is implicitly defined by

```
\hat{d} = \arg\min_d \left( \log \overline{C}(d) - 2d \frac{1}{m} \sum_{j=1}^m \log \lambda_j \right),
```

for $d \in (-1/2, 1/2)$; $\overline{C}(d) = \frac{1}{m} \sum_{j=1}^m I(\lambda_j) \lambda_j^{2d}$, $\lambda_j = \frac{2\pi j}{n}$, $\frac{1}{m} + \frac{m}{n} \to 0$,
where $m$ is the bandwidth parameter, and $I(\lambda_j)$ is the periodogram of the time-series, $x_t$, given by:

```
I(\lambda) = \frac{1}{2\pi n} \left| \sum_{t=1}^n x_t e^{i \lambda_j t} \right|^2 .
```

Under finiteness of the fourth moment and other mild conditions, Robinson (1995a) proved that:

```
\sqrt{m} (\hat{d} - d_o) \to_d N(0, 1/4) as n \to \infty,
```

where $d_o$ is the true value of $d$ and with the only additional requirement that $m \to \infty$ slower than $n$. Velasco (1999a) and Phillips and Shimotsu (2004) showed the consistency and the asymptotic normality of this estimator for $d \in (-0.5, 1]$ and $d \in (-0.5, 0.75)$, respectively with $m^{1/2}(\hat{d}-d_o) \to_d N(0, 1/4)$. Velasco (2003) showed the consistency of $\hat{d}$ when applied to estimated cointegrated errors. Other semiparametric methods, some based on the Whittle function (Kim and Phillips, 1999; Velasco, 1999a; Abadir et al., 2007) and others based on the log periodogram (Robinson, 1995b; Velasco, 1999b) were also employed producing very similar results.
