that long-term futures contracts usually need longer time to wait and trade compared with short-term futures contracts.

Third, we use the quantile co-integration to estimate the relationships of gold spot and different maturities of futures prices at low and high quantiles. This approach not only uncovers the relationships between precious metals in the short and long run, but also identifies return relationships from the cross-sectional dimension. Moreover, we discovered that returns at lower quantiles and co-integration in gold are less stronger than those at higher quantiles. As such, using the traditional linear Johansen co-integration, which is the average of all quantiles, may result in some biases. That is, it would underestimate (overestimate) the co-integration for high (low) quantile memberships. Investors should be aware of this in order to make a better decision on hedging.

Our findings add another dimension of cross-section (returns) to the extant literature, which uses the time-series dimension to examine the co-integrated relationships. However, during the crisis period, the co-integration of gold with all assets is weak due to the uncertainty reflected in the price fluctuations. Future studies may separate the entire sample period into a few sub-periods based on different events, such as the subprime crisis and the financial crises, to further explore the details.
