If the parent nation of financial advisors is in Australia, financial advisors are grouped as “domestic” advisors; otherwise, they are grouped as “foreign” advisors. The regression analysis controls for deal and firm specific characteristics in terms of cash payment, private targets, friendly deals, relatedness, market-to-book ratio, ROA, and ln(total assets). A dummy equals one if firms pay cash, acquire private targets, involve in friendly deals, engage in diversifying acquisitions. The market-to-book ratio is the market value of the equity to the book value of the equity. ROA is measured as net income to total assets. Ln(total assets) is measured as the log of total assets. The regression analysis also controls for whether firms are financial firms. White’s (1980) heteroskedasticity is used to compute p-value.

With regard to the evidence for bidders, I consistently find that large bidding firms are more likely to hire financial advisors. Looking at model specifications (7) and (8), the evidence reveals that the magnitude of the coefficient for firm size is larger for the use of high-reputable financial advisors relative to the use of low-reputable counterparts. The coefficients for hiring high- and low-reputable financial advisors are 0.134 and 0.066, respectively. Comparing with the coefficients for domestic and foreign advisors, I find that the coefficient of firm size for foreign advisors is larger than that for domestic advisors, at 0.106 and 0.050, respectively. The results indicate that large bidders are more likely to hire foreign advisors and small bidders are more likely to hire domestic advisors.

In addition, the results also show that bidders are less likely to hire financial advisors when bidders pay cash, acquire private targets and involve in friendly deals. Compared with the magnitude of the coefficients, bidders are less likely to hire high-reputable financial advisors relative to low-reputable financial advisors when bidders pay cash and acquire private targets. When transactions involve friendly deals, bidders are less likely to hire low-reputable financial advisors relative to high-reputable financial advisors.

Interestingly, the evidence indicates that bidders are more likely to hire domestic advisors in comparison to foreign advisors when bidders acquire private targets. The coefficients for hiring domestic and foreign advisors are −0.419 and −0.690, respectively. The results suggest that domestic advisors may possibly have superior information to identify potential private targets. Domestic advisors can have more ability to assist bidders to complete the deals. In addition, the results show that bidders are less likely to hire domestic advisors when bidders pay cash and engage in friendly deals.
