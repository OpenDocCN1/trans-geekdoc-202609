Taking expectations on both sides, given all available information at time t - h including Fith and dth, we get the following conditional relationship between uncertainty, the variance of mean forecast errors and observed dispersion:

U_th = E(A_t - F_•th)^2 + d_th. (2.8)

The first term on the right-hand side of equation (2.8) can alternatively be written as (Markowitz 1959, p. 111):

E(A_t - F_•th)^2 = 1/N^2 \sum_{i=1}^N E(A_t - F_ith)^2 
+ 1/N^2 \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{j \neq i} E(A_t - F_ith)(A_t - F_jth). (2.9)

Under Assumption 3, that is, the independence between the common and idyosyncratic error components, equation (2.9) can be expressed as:

E(A_t - F_•th)^2 = \sigma^2_{\lambda th} + 1/N^2 \sum_{i=1}^N \sigma^2_{\varepsilon ih}. (2.10)

Note that as the number of forecasters gets large, the second term on the right hand side will be close to zero and the uncertainty about the mean forecast E(A_t - F_•th)^2 will reflect only the uncertainty in common information, \sigma^2_{\lambda th}.

Substituting equation (2.10) in (2.8), we obtain

U_th = \sigma^2_{\lambda th} + d_th + 1/N^2 \sum_{i=1}^N \sigma^2_{\varepsilon ih}. (2.11)

For large values of N, the last term on the right-hand side of equation (2.11) will be close to zero and can be ignored.
