Table 91.2 seems to provide evidence that when hedge fund managers experience a negative performance event, they provide more weight to the market’s influence over their returns and seem to integrate them more fully as compared against positive performance events. Further, Bollen *et al.* (2008) provide a framework for researchers to examine the conditional performance of hedge fund returns to attempt to generate an idea of how hedge fund managers are reacting to specific performance events.

An adaptation of the Bollen *et al.* (2008) technique used to capture conditional effects was provided in Smith *et al.* (2017) and is illustrated as follows:

$$R_{\mathrm{Hedge},t}=\beta_0+\left(\beta^{-}_1(1-I_{t-1})+\beta^{+}_1I_{t-1}\right)R_m+\varepsilon_t, \qquad (91.4)$$

where

$$I_{t-j}=1\quad\text{if}\quad R_{t-j}\ge c\quad\text{for }j=0,\ldots,k, \qquad (91.5)$$

$$I_{t-j}=0\quad\text{if}\quad R_{t-j}<c\quad\text{for }j=0,\ldots,k, \qquad (91.6)$$

$R_{\mathrm{Hedge},t}$ is the return on the hedge fund at time $t$, $I_{t-j}$ is a binary variable that indicates whether the return associated with the hedge fund was above or below some constant $c$, $R_{t-j}$ is the return of the hedge fund in period $t-j$, $\beta^{-}_1$ is the beta coefficient attached to the market factor if the hedge fund return was below the constant c variable in period t − j, $\beta^{+}_1$ is the beta coefficient attached to the market factor if the hedge fund return was above or equal to the constant c variable in period t − j, $R_m$ is the return on the market proxy in time period t, $\beta_0$ is the intercept for the regression, $\varepsilon_t$ is the error term associated with the regression.

This section illustrates that hedge fund managers seem to smooth their prices to some extent and similar to the conclusions reached in Asness et al. (2001), this smoothing may provide investors with a somewhat muted estimate of the total market risk that investors are bearing when investing in these instruments. Further, Smith et al. (2018) illustrate that the sign and magnitude of the previous month’s performance also seem to have an impact on the level of systematic risk that these managers integrate into their estimates of performance.
