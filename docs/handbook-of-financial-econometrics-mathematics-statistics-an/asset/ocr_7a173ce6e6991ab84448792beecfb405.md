If aggregate liquidity is priced, cross-sectional differences in expected stock returns should be related to the sensitivities of returns to fluctuations in aggregate liquidity (i.e., liquidity betas). Pástor and Stambaugh (2003) develop an aggregate liquidity measure based on order-flow-induced return reversals of individual stocks, and construct ten portfolios sorted on liquidity betas as their test assets. Empirically, Pástor and Stambaugh (2003) find that expected returns of their test assets are indeed related cross-sectionally to their liquidity betas.

A large empirical literature on liquidity has developed. Our study extends the literature along two dimensions. First, while previous research (e.g., Pástor and Stambaugh, 2003) typically focuses on one cross-section of stock returns (i.e., the portfolios sorted on liquidity betas), we examine various cross-sections of returns, as, if aggregate liquidity is a true state variable in the equity market, it should price other cross-sections of returns too. More importantly, as Lewellen, Nagel and Shanken (2010) point out, using test assets with tight factor structure could yield spurious results. Second, unlike prior research that typically employs only one liquidity measure² (e.g., Bali et al. 2014), we take into account various measures of aggregate liquidity. In particular, we employ both stock and money market liquidity measures, as (1) Chordia, Sarkar, and Subrahmanyam (2005) find that fluctuations in liquidity are correlated across stocks and bond markets and (2) a large literature (e.g., Schularick and Taylor, 2012; Gilchrist and Zakrajšek, 2012; Krishnamurthy and Muir, 2016; López-Salido, Stein, and Zakrajšek, 2017) suggests that banking and bond market conditions can exert significant influences on real economy.

Empirically, our test assets include seven cross-sections of stock returns, commonly used in the empirical asset pricing literature (e.g., Fama and French, 2015): 10 portfolios formed on momentum, 25 portfolios formed on size and momentum, 25 portfolios formed on size and book-to-market (BM), 32 portfolios formed on size, book-to-market, and operating profitability, 32 portfolios formed on size, book-to-market, and investment, 32 portfolios
