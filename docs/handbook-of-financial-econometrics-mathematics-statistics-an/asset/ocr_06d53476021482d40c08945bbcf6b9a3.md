Company, 2016). In such a case, the main focus of the m-banking literature is the process of its adoption mechanism.

This field of study attracts more and more researchers. It has led to an increasing number of studies and published empirical studies. Scholars have examined different factors and IT adoption theories affecting mobile banking adoption commonly. However, most studies are based on three main adoption theories: Technology Adoption Model (Koenig-Lewis et al., 2010; Mortimer et al., 2015; Koksal, 2016), Innovation Diffusion Theory (Chen, 2013; Koksal, 2016; Laukkanen, 2016), Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (Zhou et al., 2010; Baptista and Oliveira, 2015).

Actually, some factors that drive mobile banking adoption are more widespread in the literature than others. Ha et al. (2012) found perceived usefulness, risk, compatibility and cost as the most occurring ones in empirical studies. Later, Shaikh and Karjaluoto (2015) reported that the most frequent factors were perceived ease of use and perceived usefulness followed by trust, social influence, perceived risk and self-efficacy. In the network of constructs and their relationships that are discussed in mobile banking studies, the most important constructs included intention, use, perceived ease of use, perceived risk, perceived usefulness, social influence, trust (Baptista and Oliveira, 2016).

The extant study has tried to explain mobile banking adoption using these theories, but the forms of mobile banking are changing. However, “virtually no studies address the use of m-banking applications via smartphones or tablets or consider the consequences of such usage” (Shaikh and Karjaluoto, 2015, p. 129). Later, Munoz-Leiva et al. (2017) who studied the determinants of the intention to use m-banking apps in Spain argued that this area is totally underexplored and only a scarce number of relevant papers are available (Hsu and Lin, 2016; Hew et al., 2015) existed. In turn, we try to fill this gap in the literature by focusing on mobile banking via special apps for smartphones and tablets in the case of an emerging market (Russia). This market is characterized by the lack of quick and easy access to banking services by half of customers, so mobile users have a great potential to increase the level of financial inclusion (Plaksenkov and Korovkin, 2015).
