Moving to the control variables, the results are as expected. The coefficient on AGE is negative, significant, and monotonically decreasing in Q. The older firms in our sample tend to underperform their younger counterparts. Size, as proxied for by the log of total assets, has a statistically positive but economically weak impact on value for Q25, but has a substantial economic impact in quantile 95. For the highest Q firms, a larger asset base is associated with better performance. As expected, the coefficient on CAPEX/TA and R&D expenditure are positive, significant, and monotonically increasing in Q. The coefficient on the ratio of fixed assets to total assets is everywhere negative, statistically significant, and monotonically decreasing in Q. The coefficient on the dividends to total asset ratio is positive and significant for all quantiles except the 75th and 95th. It is negative and significant for the 95th quantile. Lastly, the coefficient on the managerial ownership percentage is positive and significant for every quantile except Q5, and its square is everywhere negative but only significant for Q25 and Q50.

Table 25.5 demonstrates that Tobin’s Q is concave and monotonically decreasing in the leverage ratio. For Q5, which are the low growth firms, the coefficient on the leverage ratio is positive and significant, but it quickly turns negative for Q25, and then negative and significant for all the other quantiles. The relationship in Q95 is particularly economically important. The market seems to reward low growth firms that use more debt in their capital structure, but punish severely the most highly growing ones. Similarly, the coefficient on the ratio of public debt to total debt is also concave and monotonically decreasing by quantile. It is positive and significant for Q5 and Q25, not significant for Q50, and negative and significant for Q75 and Q95.

We find convincing evidence that foreign bank monitoring adds value to shareholders. The coefficient on the ratio of foreign bank debt to total debt is everywhere positive, monotonically increasing, and is significant for all quantiles except for the extreme quantiles, Q5 and Q95. It seems that foreign bank debt — controlling for the existence of local, public and non-public debt — is performance enhancing.

Lastly, the coefficient on domestic bank collateral is not significantly different from zero for every quantile except for Q95. This implies that the use of collateral for domestic bank debt carries with it little information about the firm’s performance, except for the highest growth firms.
