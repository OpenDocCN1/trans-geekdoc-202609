
## Appendix 109A Proof of Feller’s Lemma

We need some preliminary results in order to prove equation (109.6).

**Proposition 109A.1.** $f(z)=e^{Av/z}z^{-1}$ is the Laplace transformation of $I_0(2(Avx)^{1/2})$, where $I_k(x)$ is the Bessel function

$$I_k(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x/2)^{2r+k}}{r!\,\Gamma(r+1+k)}.$$

**Proof.** By the definition of Laplace transformation and the monotone convergence theorem, we have

$$\begin{aligned}
f(z)&=\int_0^{\infty}e^{-zx}I_0\bigl(2(Avx)^{1/2}\bigr)\,dx\\
&=\int_0^{\infty}e^{-zx}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(Avx)^r}{r!\,\Gamma(r+1)}\,dx\\
&=\int_0^{\infty}e^{-zx}\left\{1+\frac{Avx}{\Gamma(2)}+\frac{(Avx)^2}{2!\,\Gamma(3)}+\cdots+\frac{(Avx)^n}{n!\,\Gamma(n+1)}+\cdots\right\}\,dx\\
&=\frac{1}{z}+\frac{Av}{z^2}+\frac{(Av)^2}{2!z^3}+\cdots+\frac{(Av)^n}{n!z^{n+1}}+\cdots\\
&=\frac{1}{z}\left\{1+\frac{Av}{z}+\frac{(Av)^2}{2!z^2}+\cdots+\frac{(Av)^n}{n!z^n}\right\}\\
&=e^{Av/z}z^{-1}.
\end{aligned}$$

**Proposition 109A.2.** *Consider the parabolic differential equation*

$$P_t=(axP)_{xx}-((bx+h)P)_x,\qquad 0<x<\infty \tag{109A.1}$$

where $a,b,h$ are constants, $0<h<a$, then the Laplace transformation of $f(t,x,x_0)$ with respect to $x$ takes the form

$$\begin{aligned}
w(t,s;x_0)&=\int_0^{\infty}e^{-sx}f(t,x;x_0)\,dx\\
&=\left(\frac{b}{sa(e^{bt}-1)+b}\right)^{h/a}\exp\left\{\frac{-sbx_0e^{bt}}{sa(e^{bt}-1)+b}\right\}\\
&\quad\times\Gamma\left(1-\frac{h}{a};\frac{b^2x_0e^{bt}}{a(e^{bt}-1)\bigl(sa(e^{bt}-1)+b\bigr)}\right),\tag{109.50}
\end{aligned}$$

where $\Gamma(n;z)=\Gamma^{-1}(n)\int_0^z e^{-x}x^{n-1}\,dx$.

Proof. The proof of the lemma is too tedious and hence is omitted. For more details, see Lemma 7 of Feller (1951).

We now turn to prove equation (109.56). From equation (109.50), let $A = \frac{b x_0}{a(1 - e^{-bt})}$ and $z = \frac{1}{b}(s a (e^{bt} - 1) + b)$. The $w(t, s; x_0)$ in equation (109.50) can be rewritten as

$$w(t,s;x_0)=\frac{z^{-h/a}e^{-(1-1/z)A}}{\Gamma(1-h/a)}\int_0^{A/z} e^{-x}x^{-h/a}dx$$
$$=\frac{z^{-h/a}e^{-(1-1/z)A}}{\Gamma(1-h/a)}\int_0^1 e^{-Ax'/z}(Ax'/z)^{-h/a}(A/z)^{1-h/a}dx'$$
$$=\frac{A^{1-h/a}e^{-A}}{\Gamma(1-h/a)}\int_0^1 e^{A(1-x')/z}(x')^{-h/a}z^{-1}dx'$$
$$=\frac{A^{1-h/a}e^{-A}}{\Gamma(1-h/a)}\int_0^1 e^{Av/z}(1-v)^{-h/a}z^{-1}dv.$$

By Proposition 109A.1, we know that $f(z)=e^{Av/z}z^{-1}$ is the Laplace transformation of $I_0(2(Avx)^{1/2})$ and by the Fubini theorem, we have

$$w(t,s;x_0)=\frac{A^{1-h/a}e^{-A}}{\Gamma(1-h/a)}\int_0^1 (1-v)^{-h/a}\left[\int_0^{\infty} e^{-zx}I_0\left(2(Avx)^{1/2}\right)dx\right]dv$$
$$=\frac{A^{1-h/a}e^{-A}}{\Gamma(1-h/a)}\int_0^{\infty}\int_0^1 (1-v)^{-h/a}e^{-zx}I_0\left(2(Avx)^{1/2}\right)dxdv$$
$$=\frac{A^{2-h/a}e^{-A}}{\Gamma(1-h/a)x_0e^{bt}}\int_0^{\infty}\int_0^1 (1-v)^{-h/a}e^{-sx'}e^{-Ax'/(x_0e^{bt})}\times I_0\left(2A(e^{-bt}vx'/x_0)^{1/2}\right)dvd x'.$$

Hence, upon comparing the two formulae for $w(t,s;x_0)$ and by the monotone convergence theorem, we have

$$f(t,x;x_0)=\frac{b}{\Gamma(1-h/a)a(e^{bt}-1)}\left[\frac{bx_0}{a(e^{bt}-1)}\right]^{1-h/a}$$
$$\times \exp\left\{\frac{-b(x+x_0e^{bt})}{a(e^{bt}-1)}\right\}\int_0^1 (1-v)^{-h/a}I_0\left(\frac{2b(e^{-bt}vx x_0)^{1/2}}{a(1-e^{-bt})}\right)dv$$

$$= \frac{b}{\Gamma(1 - h/a) a (e^{bt} - 1)} \left[ \frac{b x_0}{a (e^{bt} - 1)} \right]^{1 - h/a} 
\times \exp\left\{ \frac{-b(x + x_0 e^{bt})}{a(e^{bt} - 1)} \right\} \int_{0}^{1} (1 - v)^{-h/a} dv 
\times \sum_{r=0}^{\infty} \frac{\left[ b(e^{-bt} v x x_0)^{1/2} / \left(a(1 - e^{-bt})\right) \right]^{2r}}{r! \Gamma(r + 1)} $$

$$= \frac{b}{\Gamma(1 - h/a) a (e^{bt} - 1)} \left[ \frac{b x_0}{a (e^{bt} - 1)} \right]^{1 - h/a} \exp\left\{ \frac{-b(x + x_0 e^{bt})}{a(e^{bt} - 1)} \right\} 
\times \sum_{r=0}^{\infty} \frac{\left[ b(e^{-bt} x x_0)^{1/2} / \left(a(1 - e^{-bt})\right) \right]^{2r}}{r! \Gamma(r + 1)} \int_{0}^{1} (1 - v)^{-h/a} v^{r} dv $$

$$= \frac{b}{\Gamma(1 - h/a) a (e^{bt} - 1)} \left[ \frac{b x_0}{a (e^{bt} - 1)} \right]^{1 - h/a} \exp\left\{ \frac{-b(x + x_0 e^{bt})}{a(e^{bt} - 1)} \right\} 
\times \sum_{r=0}^{\infty} \frac{\left[ b(e^{-bt} x x_0)^{1/2} / \left(a(1 - e^{-bt})\right) \right]^{2r}}{r! \Gamma(r + 1)} \frac{\Gamma(r + 1) \Gamma(1 - h/a)}{\Gamma(r + 1 + 1 - h/a)}.$$

This completes the proof.

### 110.1 Introduction

We want to price bonds issued by a firm. Assume that the firm issues a zero coupon bond with one dollar face value which matures at time $T$. First we consider the basic extension of the Merton model due to Black and Cox (1976). The idea is to capture the possibility that defaults may occur at any time before maturity for this zero coupon bond. Black and Cox considered an asset value process which, under the risk neutral measure, follows the dynamic:

$dV_t = (r - a)V_t dt + \sigma V_t dW_t.$

Here $a$ is the payout rate of the firm and $r > 0$ is the risk free interest rate. Moreover, they defined the default boundary as

$K_t = K_0 e^{\kappa t}, \quad 0 \le t \le T, \qquad (110.1)$

where $\kappa \in R$. (Longstaff and Schwartz (1995) adopted the constant boundary, that is, $\kappa = 0$.) More precisely, the default time is defined by

$\tau(T) \triangleq \inf\{0 \le t \le T; V_t \le K_t\}, \qquad (110.2)$

with the usual convention that $\inf\{\emptyset\} = +\infty$. Then, in the Black–Cox model, the payoff at maturity is $B(V_t,T) = \min(V_t,1)$, if there is no default up to time $T$. If boundary is hit before or at the maturity of the bond, bond holders will take over the firm and receive the remaining value of the firm at the default time: $B(V_{\tau(T)},\tau(T)) = K_{\tau(T)}$. To get the price of the bond payout at maturity if there is no default, we use results on pricing of barrier options. To get the price of the bond payout on the default barrier, one can use the density of the hitting time of Brownian motion and the joint density of Brownian motion and its maximum process. (For details, see Lando (2004)).
