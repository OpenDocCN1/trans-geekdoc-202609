# 73.5 Tail Dependence

In finance, the co-movements of two asset return series are often of interest. The application of copula allows tail dependence to be estimated. Generally, higher values (densities) in the copula tails indicate stronger dependence between the returns. Tail dependence is useful for examining the association between two random variables in the extremity of a joint distribution. In other words, it provides a measure of the likelihood that the movement of one variable will follow the movement of another variable, i.e., whether they tend to increase (decrease) when both variables assume extreme values in their upper (lower) tail.

For a bivariate copula, the $i$, $j$th lower tail dependence coefficient is defined as

$$\lambda_{ij}^l = \lim_{q\to 0} P(X_i < F_i^{-1}(\xi) | X_j < F_j^{-1}(\xi)); \quad \lambda_{ij}^l \in [0,1], \tag{73.20}$$

where $\xi$ represents the quantile of the distribution. When $\lambda_{ij}^l > 0$, the lower tail dependence between $X_i$ and $X_j$ exists. In addition, a higher $\lambda_{ij}^l$ indicates a stronger lower tail dependence. Conversely, the $i$, $j$th upper tail dependence coefficient is defined as

$$\lambda_{ij}^u = \lim_{q\uparrow 1} P(X_i > F_i^{-1}(\xi) | X_j > F_j^{-1}(\xi)); \quad \lambda_{ij}^l \in [0,1]. \tag{73.21}$$

When $\lambda_{ij}^u > 0$, the upper tail dependence between $X_i$ and $X_j$ exists. Similarly, a higher $\lambda_{ij}^u$ suggests a stronger upper tail dependence. If the value of a variable falls in a lower (upper) tail of the distribution, the tail dependence coefficient gives the conditional probability that the value of the other variable also lies in the lower (upper) tail of its distribution.

As mentioned earlier, elliptical copulas have symmetric tail dependence. Therefore, $\lambda_{ij}^l = \lambda_{ij}^u$ or $\lambda_L = \lambda_U$ for these copulas. Since $C(u_1,u_2) = C(u_2,u_1)$, we can conclude that

$$\lambda_{ij}^l = \lambda_{ji}^l \quad \text{and} \quad \lambda_{ij}^u = \lambda_{ji}^u \text{ for all } i,j. \tag{73.22}$$

Gaussian copulas have zero or very weak tail dependence unless the correlation coefficient ($\rho$) is 1. The tail dependence coefficient is given by

$$\lambda_L = \lambda_U = 0 \quad \text{if } \rho < 1 \tag{73.23a}$$

and

$$\lambda_L = \lambda_U = 1 \quad \text{if } \rho = 1. \tag{73.23b}$$

Hence, Gaussian copulas are not appropriate for modeling dependencies between financial assets, which regularly have asymmetric tail dependence.
