where we have applied the case of (110A.3) for n = k in the last line. On the other hand, by the Strong Markov Property of X, we have

$$E_x\left[f(X_{J_{k+1}},t-J_{k+1})1\left(J_{k+1}\le t,\min_{0\le s\le J_{k+1}}X_s>0\right)\right]$$

$$=E_x\left[E_x\left[f(X_{J_{k+1}},t-J_{k+1})1\left(J_{k+1}\le t,\min_{0\le s\le J_{k+1}}X_s>0\right)\big|F_{J_1}\right]\right]$$

$$1\left(J_1\le t,\min_{0\le s\le J_1}X_s>0\right)$$

$$=E_x\left[E_{X_{J_1}}\left[f(X_{J_k},v-J_k)1\left(J_k\le t,\min_{0\le s\le J_k}X_s>0\right)\right]\bigg|_{v-t-J_1}1\left(J_1\le t,\min_{0\le s\le J_1}X_s>0\right)\right].$$

Hence, we have proved that (110A.3) holds for n = k + 1. By induction hypothesis, we have proved the first part of the lemma.

Note that for any $(x,t)\in E_T$, we have

$$0\le L^n(x,t)=E_x\left[f(X_{J_n},t-J_n)1\left(J_n\le t,\min_{0\le s\le J_n}X_s\ge0\right)\right]$$

$$\le\|f\|_\infty P[J_1\le t]\le\|f\|_\infty P[J_n\le T].$$

Since $J_n$ is the $n$th jump time of the compound Poisson process, we have $P[J_n\le T]=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda T}\frac{(\lambda T)^m}{m!}$. This implies that $L^n f$ converges to zero uniformly on $E_T$ as $n\to\infty$.

From this, we obtain from (110A.2) that for every bounded $\psi$,

$$\Phi(x,T)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}L^kG(x,T)+L^{n+1}\Phi(x,T)\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}L^kG(x,T).$$
