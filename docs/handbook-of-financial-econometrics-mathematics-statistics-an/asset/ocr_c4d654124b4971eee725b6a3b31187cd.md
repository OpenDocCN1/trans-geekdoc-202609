If a winner (loser) stock suffers a higher level of information asymmetry, uninformed investors will not trade with informed traders, and a lower (higher) BOS ratio can be observed. Therefore, the winner (loser) stocks with lower (higher) BOS ratios may indicate that those stocks subject to a higher degree of information asymmetry and large momentum returns are expected to be observed.² In addition, our argument is closely related to the literature on the relationship between the valuation uncertainty and the momentum effect. For example, Daniel et al. (1998) show that investors may overweight information that is consistent with their views and underweight information that is not consistent with their original estimates, and the valuation with biased information will lead to the momentum effect. Moreover, Daniel and Titman (1999) demonstrate that the momentum effect is stronger for stocks with a lower book-to-market ratio due to the valuation uncertainty. In this study, our empirical results show that the BOS momentum strategy can obtain an enhanced performance for both high book-to-market ratio stocks and low book-to-market ratio stocks. This indicates that although the BOS ratio and book-to-market ratio can represent price inefficiency and have substantial effects on momentum profits, the BOS ratio and book-to-market ratio can capture a certain extent of unrelated information content.
