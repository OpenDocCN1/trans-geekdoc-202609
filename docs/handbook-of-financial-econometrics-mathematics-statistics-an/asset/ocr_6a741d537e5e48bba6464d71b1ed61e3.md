r_{ijt}^+ and r_{ijt}^- are estimated, denoted by \hat{\sigma}_{ijt}^+ and \hat{\sigma}_{ijt}^-, respectively. Then, we estimate V(r_{it} + r_{jt}) and V(r_{it} - r_{jt}) by

$$V(r_{it} + r_{jt}) = \hat{\sigma}_{ijt}^+, \tag{14.3.o}$$ $$V(r_{it} - r_{jt}) = \hat{\sigma}_{ijt}^-. \tag{14.3.p}$$

Finally, we estimate \hat{\Sigma}_{ijt} by

$$\hat{\Sigma}_{ijt} = \frac{\hat{V}(r_{it} + r_{jt}) - \hat{V}(r_{it} - r_{jt})}{4}. \tag{14.3.q}$$

# 14.4.1 Study plan

To begin with, we consider three categories of risk preferences for investors: mildly risk averse (λ = 1), moderately risk averse (λ = 5), and severely risk averse (λ = 10).

Now, we propose a dynamic rolling window scheme as follows. A rolling window consists of two parts: the training period of size N_train days and the holding period of size N_hold days. Hence, the size of the rolling window is of (N_train + N_hold) days. Given a rolling window, we use the data in the training period to estimate parameters and obtain optimal portfolio weight, then we form the portfolio and hold it during the holding period to measure its performance in terms of various strategy summary statistics. Then, we move the rolling window N_hold days forward, and repeat the above procedures till the end of the study period.
