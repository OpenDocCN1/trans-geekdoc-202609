# 61.1 Introduction

Interest rate models are very useful to both academicians and practitioners in completing many tasks. Lately, at the forefront of these tasks has been the performance of interest rate models around crises (e.g., Global Financial Crisis of 2007–2010). Between December 2008 and December 2015, the US Federal Reserve Bank kept its Federal Funds Target rate essentially at zero to stimulate growth in its economy with the hopes of pulling it out of the recession. Since it has been rare for researchers to imagine that interest rates could fall below zero, thoughts of negative interest rates becoming a reality, though admissible, traditionally have been taken lightly. In this chapter, we examine the empirical performance of dynamic term structure models (DTSMs) characterized by principal components as factors in a near zero environment.

Among researchers, one of the most popular and widely studied types of DTSM is affine in nature and appropriately called the class of affine term structure models. Duffie and Kan (1996) developed it and shortly thereafter Dai and Singleton (2000) gave it a taxonomy to aid researchers in their examinations of it.
