Results in Table 32.6 indicate that after controlling for endogeneity concerns, CEO compensation is still negatively associated with book-tax differences. Our results are robust to the alternative estimation methods: two-stage least squares (column 1), three-stage least squares (column 2) and seemingly unrelated regression (column 3) method. The results of the other variables in the regressions are similar to those reported in Table 32.3. Therefore, our findings of the effect of book-tax differences on CEO compensation are robust to treating book-tax differences as endogenous.

# 32.4.2 Additional tests

We perform several robustness checks. First, we winsorize all variables at the 1st and 99th percentiles and we find that our results are not sensitive to outliers. Second, we consider the effect of mergers on CEO compensation. Bliss and Rosen (2001) find that bank mergers have a net positive effect on CEO compensation, even if mergers cause the acquiring bank’s stock price to decline. They find that the majority of mergers increase the overall wealth of the CEO, often at the expense of shareholders. To address the effect of mergers on CEO compensation, we identify all completed mergers from the Securities Data Corporation database and delete all mergers from our sample. Our results (not tabulated) are qualitatively similar — CEO compensation is negatively associated with temporary book-tax differences. Third, we have multiple observations for our firms, which suggests that the t-statistics may be overstated. To mitigate this issue, we averaged all the observations available for a given firm and then use the average of the observations in the regressions. Our inferences are not affected. Fourth, it is possible that CEO changes may affect accounting earnings and hence, compensation. Pourciau (1993) provides evidence of earnings management associated with top executive changes. She finds that incoming executives manage accruals in a way that decreases earnings in the year of executive change and increases earnings in the following year whereas departing executives record accruals that decrease earnings during their last year of tenure. To address the concern that executive change may affect our results, we exclude from our sample all CEOs who joined or left the firm during the sampling period. CEO compensation still exhibits a statistically significant negative association with book-tax differences.
