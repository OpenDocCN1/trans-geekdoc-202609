# 66.5 Conclusions

The usage of social media and establishing banking relationships are beneficial to firms. This chapter explores how social media and bank relationships mitigate information asymmetry and affect firm value. Using 855 listed Taiwanese firms and with 6,636 observations over the period 2008–2015, the following results are obtained.

First, we find that Facebook, Google+ and LinkedIn positively influence firm value, whereas YouTube and Twitter affect firm value insignificantly. The use of Facebook contributes to Tobin’s $Q$ by 12.6%, whereas the use of Google+ and LinkedIn contribute to Tobin’s $Q$ by 9.3%.

Second, the two measures of bank relationships affect firm value differently. While the higher numbers of banks firms borrow from reduces the value, high bank debt creates value. Interestingly, although one-unit increase in bank relationship reduces Tobin’s $Q$ by 2.24%, using Twitter may reverse the result and create value. Twitter and bank relationships are complementary. Again, although the use of YouTube does not affect value, the use of bank debt may reverse the result and add value to a firm. The use of YouTube and bank debt is complementary as well. Besides, substitution effects exist between other types of social media and banking relationships on firm value.

Third, the use of Facebook, Google+ and LinkedIn adds value to a firm that was driven by firms that are having Tobin’s $Q$ at low to median quantiles. For those firms whose Tobin’s $Q$ are at high quantile, the results are insignificant.

Fourth, the results remain robust after conducting a DID analysis where the exogenous shock of the Facebook IPO in 2012 is taken into consideration. The endogeneity concerns mostly expel.
