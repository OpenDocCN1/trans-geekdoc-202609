Investors have long believed that gold can offer benefits of diversification because their returns characteristics and fundamental driver are different than those of other risky assets like stocks and bonds. However, the financialization of commodities has integrated the commodity markets with other financial markets like bond and stock markets since the early 2000s. Therefore, an interesting question arises how the increasing correlations between commodity and financial returns affect the role played by gold in a diversified portfolio comprised of bonds and stocks. Here, this study applies continuous wavelet transform analysis to capture the short-term and long-term correlation features between gold and other risky assets like stocks and bonds. This chapter also uses the US stock, bond, and gold data from 1990 until 2013 to investigate the optimal weights of gold obtained from the minimum variance portfolio. The following conclusions have been derived from our analysis.

Firstly, the wavelet power spectrum analysis is employed to capture the short-term and long-term volatilities of gold, stocks and bonds. The evidence from the short term volatility of gold indicates significant differences in volatility over time, with a significantly increasing trend since the early 2000s. A comparison with the short term volatilities for gold and stock confirms that the relatively low volatility of gold in times of market turmoil, that is consistent with the fact that gold provides the need for a safe haven for investors. Notably, the pattern of long term wavelet variances differs markedly for gold. The long term volatility of gold has been increasing more than that of stock, whereas the long-term volatility of bond is relatively stable. In addition, a comparison of the short term volatilities for bond and stock support that the volatility of bond has been increasing more than that of stock since the mid-1990s. The finding is in line with the result of Reilly, Wright and Chan (2000) and Jones and Wilson (2004). Therefore, at least in the short term, the argument that stock is riskier investment than bond does not seem to be well grounded. However, the long term volatilities of bond and stock exhibit different pattern that stock fluctuates more than bond in the long term. In sum, the findings included the change in nature of assets volatility and the difference in short term and long term volatilities, have particular implications for analysts and portfolio managers.

Secondly, the wavelet coherency analysis is employed to capture the short-term and long-term dependencies among gold, stocks and bonds. The empirical finding from the wavelet coherency between returns of gold and stock shows that there is a significant negative coherency at higher frequencies in the periods of crisis.
