# # 65.1 Introduction

Brown and Gibbons (1985) developed a theory of relative risk aversion estimation in terms of average market rates of return and the variance of market rates of return. However, the exact sampling distributions of the relative risk aversion estimators have not been derived. The main purpose of this chapter is to derive the exact sampling distribution of an appropriate relative risk aversion (RRA) estimator. Statistical inference based on the sampling distribution has significant implications for the study of many important issues in finance. For instance, the appropriateness of a log utility function which is crucial for some important results in finance can now be tested based on the null hypothesis of a unitary relative risk aversion (see, e.g., Cox et al., 1985; Hakanson, 1970; Kraus and Litzenberger, 1975; Merton, 1971; Rubinstein, 1977; Samuelson, 1969); the effects of changes in the degree of risk aversion on the demand for risky assets and the saving decisions (see, e.g., Rothschild and Stiglitz, 1971) can now be empirically evaluated; the contribution of changes in aggregate risk aversion to the controversial issue of excessive volatility of stock prices (see, e.g., Grossman and Shiller, 1981) can be correctly estimated.

Section 65.2 will derive the exact sampling distribution of the uniformly minimum variance unbiased RRA estimator, as well as the maximum likelihood estimator, assuming the lognormal model. Applications of the sampling distribution of the RRA estimator will be explored in Section 65.3. Section 65.4 summarizes the results.
