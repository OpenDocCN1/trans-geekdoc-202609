For constant μ, σ > 0 and W is a standard Weiner process. Ilhan and Sircar (2006) adopted the same principle when markets are complete. Given that this chapter deals with incomplete markets, the Weiner process changes into

$$ dS_t = e^{\tau(\pm y_1 \pm y_2)} \left( \underbrace{\mu S_t dt}_{\text{part 1}} + \underbrace{\sigma S_t dW_t}_{\text{part 2}} \right), \quad S_0 = S, \tag{34.16} $$

where S is the spot price. And S0 = S implies that S can be at any point in time. Equation (34.16) is Weiner process when discrete jumps are taken into account. Options are forward looking and an option writer would want to hedge that risk premium in “future”. Therefore, this chapter incorporates a standard forward contract for hedging reasons. Similar phenomenon is adopted in Ilhan and Sircar (2006), and Carr and Wu (2014). Equation (34.16) has some traits of the JD model. A typical JD model is given by the formula;

$$ dS_t = \underbrace{\mu S_{t-} dt}_{\text{similar to part 1 of eq. (34.16)}} + \underbrace{\sigma S_{t-} dZ_t}_{\text{similar to part 2 of eq. (34.16)}} + S_t dJ_t, \; i \in \{1,2\} $$

where Z_t is the Brownian motion and J_t = \sum_{i=1}^{N_t} Y_i is a compound Poisson process where the jump prices sizes Y_i are independent and identically distributed with distribution F and the number of jumps N_t is a Poisson process with jump intensity λ. The difference between equation (34.16) and JD model is that S follows Brownian motion — any type of randomness while in JD model S follows geometric Brownian motion. That is, equation (34.16) is more encompassing in terms of movements that JD model. The J_t part JD model is compound parameter (which implies jumps double every time); however, not every jump doubles the preceding jumps. In certain cases, jumps might increase by decimal places (see Wurgler and Zhuravskaya, 2002). That is, the brilliance of equation (34.16) is that it can account for any stock jump of any form and nature, under any environment. The JD model is incapable of the latter phenomenon. On hedging, the strategy applicable to this study postulates a subset of hedging strategies that broadly appropriate for hedging any asset class under a Poisson process.
