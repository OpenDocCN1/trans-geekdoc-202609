# 70.5 Conclusion

This chapter proposes the VG NGARCH model, which is based on a purely jump VG process that considers the volatility clustering and leverage effect of assets’ prices by imposing a nonlinear asymmetric NGARCH process on the time-varying shape parameter of the VG process. Furthermore, the parameterization of the shape parameter allows return innovations to feedback into higher moments of log-returns, thus the skewness and excess kurtosis frequently observed in log-return distributions are explained. The model differs from the GARCH-jump models in that a single VG process is sufficient to describe the price movements resulting from normal and unusual news events. The result is a more parsimonious model than the GARJI model of Chan et al. (2002) and Maheu et al. (2004). The empirical performance of the proposed VG NGARCH model is evaluated and compared with the benchmark GARJI model. The likelihood ratio test favors the hypothesis of an autoregressive conditional shape parameter versus a constant shape parameter assumed by the VG model of Madan (1998). The VG NGARCH model outperforms the GARJI model in terms of the three goodness-of-fit indices: AIC, SC, and HQ. This improved performance is evident from the plots of the predicted probabilities of large price movements for the years 2006 and 2008 in Figs. 70.1 and 70.2, respectively. The VG NGARCH model provides persistent low probabilities of large price movements for the first period and persistent higher likelihood of large price movements during the second period.
