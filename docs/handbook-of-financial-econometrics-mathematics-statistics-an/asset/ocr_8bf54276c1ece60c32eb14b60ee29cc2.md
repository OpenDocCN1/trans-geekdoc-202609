# 34.2.3 Hedging in incomplete markets

Buraschi and Jackwerth (2001) priced volatility smiles pre- and post-1987 stock market crash. They opined that deterministic volatility models accommodated flexible volatility surface and no additional risk factors are introduced. On one hand, that trait of deterministic volatility is suitable for hedging but when there are jumps deterministic volatility models might be unsuitable for dynamic hedging. Prior studies on hedging argue that the BS model should be relaxed such that it is suitable for hedging in different scenarios. Buraschi and Jackwerth’s (2001) study focus loosely on two approaches; (i) deterministic volatility and (ii) stochastic models. This chapter combines traits from two approaches.
