The conditional $F$-test statistics $V_u$ in (93.2), Shanken's Bayes factor in (93.3), and the proposed Bayes factor $B_{10}$ of (93.8) are calculated for each of the $L - T + 1 = 336$ sampling windows, respectively. To calculate Shanken's Bayes factor in (93.3), the median of the ratio $\rho = SR / SR^*$ between the ex ante Sharpe ratios SR and the ex ante maximum Sharpe ratio SR* is set to 0.75 and 0.45, for the first and second time periods, respectively. To calculate the Bayes factor of (93.8), a uniform distribution within the interval ($\hat{SR}$, $\hat{SR}^*$) is chosen for the prior distribution $\pi(\cdot)$ of the ex ante Sharpe ratio SR and K = 10,000 realizations $w_1, ..., w_K$ of the ex ante Sharpe ratio SR are drawn from the prior distribution $\pi(\cdot)$. When the sampling window length T = 60, the asymptotic variance $V_{GMM}$ in (93.9) of the test portfolio p’s excess returns are 0.0236 and 0.0193 for the two time periods, respectively. Note that if the Bayes factor $B_{10}$ is greater than 50, then it is set to 50.

According to Kass and Raftery (1995), the null hypothesis H0 is rejected “not worthy more than a bare mention” as the Bayes factor B10 > 1, H0 is rejected “substantially” as the Bayes factor B10 > 3.2, and H0 is rejected “strongly” as the Bayes factor B10 > 10. Here, a threshold h* is chosen so that the null hypothesis H0: ρ = 1 is rejected as the Bayes factor B10 of (93.8) is greater than h*. The threshold h* is chosen so that the likelihood of rejecting the null hypothesis H0: ρ = 1 is identical to that under the conditional F-test when the ex post difference $\hat{SR}^* - \hat{SR} \le 0.4$. The threshold h* are 12 and 18 for the first and second time periods, respectively. Similarly, a threshold h** is chosen so that the null hypothesis H0: ρ = 1 is rejected as the Bayes factor B10 of (93.3) is greater than h**. The threshold h** are 2 and 1 for the first and second time periods, respectively.

The p-values of the conditional F-tests of the L − T + 1 = 336 sampling windows are illustrated in panel B of Figure 93.1 (Figure 93.2) from January 1941 (January 1980) to December 1973 (December 2012), where the p-values are compared to the significant level α = 0.10. The corresponding Bayes factors B10 of (93.5) for the L − T + 1 = 336 sampling windows are illustrated in panel C of Figure 93.1 (Figure 93.2). In panel D of Figure 93.1 (Figure 93.2), the Shanken’s Bayesian factors for the L − T + 1=336 sampling windows are plotted. The null hypothesis H0: ρ = 1 is rejected when the Bayesian factor is greater than the threshold h*. It can be seen from Figures 93.1 and 93.2, the proposed Bayesian test has the highest likelihood of rejecting the null hypothesis H0: ρ = 1 as the deviation between the ex post maximum Sharpe ratio $\hat{SR}^*$ and the test portfolio p’s Sharpe ratio $\hat{SR}$ is larger.
