The cost efficiency of community banks in general has declined in the post-Dodd–Frank period. Since the smallest institutions may be unable to afford the additional overhead costs of compliance, this suggests that the smallest community banks have exited, or chosen not to enter, the product markets requiring additional compliance expense.

# 131.3 Regulation and Discretionary Loan Default Risk Management

Section 131.2 demonstrated the ways legislation has affected the competitive environment of community banks. This section describes the consequences of these changes on a single parameter: loan and lease loss expenses. An important component of the earnings quality for financial institutions is the loan loss provision, an annual expense set aside to create an allowance for loan and lease losses for uncollected loans and loan payments. It is often used as an accounting measure of risk management as well as a surrogate for portfolio quality. Loan loss provisioning is a key financial statement item that reveals management’s responses to changes in loan portfolio risk.
