As Table 38.2 shows, the candidate copulas include four popular copulas, i.e., the Gaussian copula, t copula, Gumbel copula and Clayton copula. These copulas have their own strengths and weaknesses, denoted by symbols “√” and “×”, respectively. The Frank copula is excluded as it does not have any advantage over the four other copulas. The mixture copula nests the dependence structures of its component copulas. In this case, the mixture of copulas is the combination of their feature capturing capabilities. For example, if a copula has a “×” in some feature, another copula with “√” in this feature will make their mixture also have “√” in this feature. Therefore, by carefully choosing component copulas, the mixture copula with all “√” can be constructed.

# (1) T-Gumbel Copula

Among these candidate copulas, we start with the t copula because it can capture most types of features. The only “×” in Table 38.2 shows its only weakness of tail symmetry. Therefore, a copula that can capture tail symmetry will make a good partner. As Table 38.2 shows, the Clayton copula and Gumbel copulas, with high sensitivities to lower and upper tail dependence, respectively, are marked as “√” in the row of tail asymmetry. Significantly stronger dependencies are often observed when bank risks are at their large extremes, so the Gumbel copula is finally chosen. The mixture of the t copula and Gumbel copula is expected to be able to capture all the typical features of bank risk dependence. It is named t-Gumbel copula and defined as equation (38.2).

C_{t-Gumbel}(u1, u2, ..., un | w_t, w_{Gu}, ν, R, α)
= w_t C_t(u1, u2, ..., un | ν, R) + w_{Gu} C_{Gumbel}(u1, u2, ..., un | α),  (38.2)

where w_t, w_{Gu} ∈ [0,1] and w_t + w_{Gu} = 1. Parameters (ν,R,α) are the association parameters reflecting the degree of dependence and (w_t,w_{Gu}) are the weight parameters reflecting the structure of dependence.

According to Nelsen (1999), the upper and lower tail dependence coefficients λ^U and λ^L in terms of copula C are:

λ^U = lim_{u→1} (1 - 2u + C(u,u,...,u)) / (1 - u),  (38.3)
λ^L = lim_{u→0} C(u,u,...,u) / u.  (38.4)
