Therefore, the total risk integrated by the t-Gumbel copula is deemed to be the most accurate among these approaches. The closer is the result of an approach to the t-Gumbel copula, the better this approach can capture the dependence structure.

Among all approaches, the results of the other two mixture copulas, the Gaussian–Gumbel–Clayton copula with 3.67% and the Gaussian–Gumbel copula with 3.62%, are the closest to the t-Gumbel copula, which indicates that dependence capturing capabilities of the two approaches are just next to the t-Gumbel copula. This also exactly accords with the analyzes that the two approaches can capture most of the features in Table 38.2 and rank the second and the third in goodness-of-fit test. The result of the Gaussian–Gumbel copula is not as close as the Gaussian–Gumbel–Clayton copula because adding the Clayton copula brings more flexibility to the mixture, and so improves its capability of capturing dependence structure.

For individual copulas, it is easy to observe that none of them are very close to the t-Gumbel copula. The total risks estimated by the Gaussian copula, Clayton copula and Frank copula are 3.49%, 3.50% and 3.51%, respectively, which are very close to each other. The results of the t copula and Gumbel copula are 3.55% and 3.76%, respectively, which are much larger than results of the former three copulas, mainly because they can capture tail dependence while those three copulas cannot. Among all individual copula approaches, the result of the t copula is the closest to the t-Gumbel copula, mainly because it is able to capture more typical features (Section 38.2). The goodness-of-fit test results also show the t copula is superior to other individual copulas.

For the last two major approaches, the result of the variance–covariance approach is 3.16%, which is significantly smaller than all other approaches, mainly because it can only capture linear correlation between risks and ignores the consequences of nonlinear dependence. It is noteworthy that the result of the simple summation approach is 3.63%, which is very close to the results of the t-Gumbel copula. However, this does not mean that the simple summation approach is able to capture the dependence structure very well; it is only a special case in this empirical analysis.

The integration results tally highly with our theoretical analysis and the validity test outcomes. The more features the approach can capture, the better it can model the dependence structure, and the more accurate the risk integration is.
