Thereafter, Ilhan and Sircar (2006) hedged barrier options in incomplete markets using combination static–dynamic strategies. Broadly, they argue that the “proper” hedge should include continuous trading of put options and underlying asset. The latter strategy would work perfectly in complete markets but not in incomplete ones. In order to deal with the incompleteness of the market, Ilhan and Sircar (2006) introduced the following measures; (i) investor should sell additional put options for the market price in dollars at time zero, and (ii) elimination of duality problem by creating a maximum expected utility function.

Then, they apply static–dynamic hedging strategies on stochastic volatility models where the Weiner process is driven by two stochastic processes. They capped the stochastic volatility models because the unbounded scenario might lead to the option being zero. In order to deal with indifference in pricing they adopted Radon–Nikodym derivative formula. The results illustrate that optimal static strategy is characterized by (i) Fenchel–Legendre transform, and (ii) optimal dynamic strategy is characterized by indifference price combined with options. Thus, the optimal strategy is a combination static and dynamic strategy. Finally, given the presence of path-dependent traits, lookbacks and Asian options should be considered as well.

Carr and Wu (2014) explored static hedging on standard options. The elegance of their study is the fact that findings can be applied to other exotic options, most likely path-dependent options. They argue that static hedging works well for continuous and discontinuous dynamic prices. The latter principle is appropriate to this chapter as it explores hedging strategies when there are stock price jumps. Further, Carr and Wu (2004) argue that when liquidity dries up, dynamic hedging is inappropriate. In designing their model, they make numerous assumptions including frictionless markets and no arbitrage. They use a simple notation for long call option and the hedging technique is delta.
