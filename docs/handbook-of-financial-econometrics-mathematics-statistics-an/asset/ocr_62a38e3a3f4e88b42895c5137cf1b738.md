Based on these constraints, the upper bound, $C_{\max}^E$, and lower bound, $C_{\min}^E$, of a European call option can be obtained as:

$$C_{\max}^E = \frac{1}{2}\left[ \mu - K + \sqrt{(\mu - K)^2 + \sigma^2} \right],$$

$$C_{\min}^E = \begin{cases}
\mu - \dfrac{K\mu^2}{\mu^2 + \sigma^2} & \text{if } K \le \mu + \dfrac{\sigma^2}{\mu} \\
0 & \text{if } K > \mu + \dfrac{\sigma^2}{\mu}.
\end{cases}$$\tag{7.21}

Lee, Zhang, and Lee (2002) further extend Zhang (1994)'s moment conditions by incorporating third and fourth moment to capture skewness and kurtosis characteristics of the underlying stock price since most empirical findings indicate that the distribution of stock return is often fat-tailed and has excess kurtosis. The results for the upper bounds of a European call option of Lee et al. (2002) can be categorized into three parts:

$$C_{\max}^E = \begin{cases}
\sigma \left( \dfrac{\eta\left(F_{S_T}(K)\right)^3}{a} \right)^{1/4} & \text{when } \mu = K \\
\kappa_4(\mu - K) & \text{when } \mu > K \\
\varpi_4(K - \mu) & \text{when } \mu < K,
\end{cases}$$\tag{7.22}

where $a = 1 + \left( \dfrac{F_{S_T}(K)}{1 - F_{S_T}(K)} \right)^3$ and $\eta$ is the kurtosis of the underlying asset price distribution. $F_{S_T}(K)$ denotes the cumulative function of any distribution for the underlying asset when $S_T$ equals the strike price $K$ at maturity; $\kappa_4$ the largest real root of the quadratic equation: the largest real root of the quadratic equation $a x^4 - 4 x^3 + 6 x^2 - 4 x + b = 0$ and $\varpi_4$ is the largest real root of the quadratic equation $a x^4 + 4 x^3 + 6 x^2 + 4 x + b = 0$, $b = 1 - \phi F^3(K)$, $\phi = \dfrac{\eta \sigma^4}{(\mu - K)^4} + \dfrac{4 K \sigma^3}{(\mu - K)^3} + \dfrac{6 \sigma^4}{(\mu - K)^2} + 1$. Lee et al. (2002)'s higher moment approach will be discussed in Sections 7.4–7.7.

Besides Zhang (1994) and Lee et al. (2002), several papers try to extend or generalize Lo's (1987) approach. Grundy (1991) derives option bounds by considering higher moments of the underlying asset's distribution. Grundy (1991)'s upper bound of a European call option can be derived by given knowledge that the value of the nth moment multiplied by the nth power of current price of underlying asset should be equal to the nth power of the value of underlying asset at maturity date.
