where P_{t,h} and P_{t,l} represent the intraday highs and lows, respectively (Parkinson, 1980; Maheu and McCurdy, 2004). Parameter χ was calibrated to the data such that the range estimate of the unconditional variance equals the unconditional variance of daily returns.

Following the approach by Maheu and McCurdy (2004), the range-based realized volatilities are regressed on the out-of-sample forecasts of the conditional variances as

Range_t = β₀ + β[Var(Y_t | ℱ_{t-1})]^{1/2} + error_t, (70.20)

where Var(Y_t|ℱ_{t-1}) is the out-of-sample forecast for the time t variance conditional on the information set ℱ_{t-1}. The coefficient of determination, R², measures the forecasting performance: higher R² implies the variability of the realized volatilities, which is explained by the out-of-sample forecasts of the conditional variances and thus the proposed model is a superior forecasting tool. Table 70.6 lists the R² for the regression model (70.20) for all companies, respectively. As can be seen from Table 70.6, the VG-NGARCH model produces higher out-of-sample R² for all firms, which indicates that the VG-NGARCH model outperforms the GARJI model in the forecasting performance.
