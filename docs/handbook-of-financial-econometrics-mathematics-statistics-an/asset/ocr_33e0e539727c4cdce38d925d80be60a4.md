# 71.4 Methodology and Data Collection

Our empirical model contains eight constructs and for each construct, a set of multiple items was developed. To ensure the content validity, all the items were developed from recent empirical studies. The data from Russia were used to test the model, so all the items were translated into Russian and modified in order to make them more understandable for Russian respondents. We also check the clarity of the questionnaire on a small sample of seven respondents, including experts in sociology and economic sociology. According to their recommendations, we made some corrections to the questionnaire. Each construct was measured by three items and each item was measured with a five-point Likert scale, ranging from strongly disagree (1) to strongly agree (5). However, three items were measured conversely from strongly agree (1) to strongly disagree (5). The list of final items and their sources are presented in Appendix 71A.

The survey also includes two other parts. The first part identifies respondents from the focus group. The focus group includes users of mobile Internet through smartphones and tablets. Applications for smartphones and tablets are the most advanced technologies for mobile banking. The second part of the survey collects demographic characteristics, such as gender, age, education, income and employment.

To test the model, we collected data via an online survey in March 2015. We used a snowball approach (Koenig-Lewis et al., 2010) and sent invitations with links to an online survey through social networks. We also sent emails with invitations to students of campuses of the National Research University Higher School of Economics (HSE University)¹ located in four cities (Moscow, Saint-Petersburg, Perm and Nizhny Novgorod).
