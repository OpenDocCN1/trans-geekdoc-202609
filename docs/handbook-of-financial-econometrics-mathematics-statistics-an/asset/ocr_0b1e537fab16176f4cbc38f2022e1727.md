univariate estimates of the parent series, and $\hat{d}_*$ is a restricted estimate obtained in the bivariate context under the assumption that $d_x = d_y$. In particular,

$\hat{d}_* = -\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{s^*} 1_2' \hat{\Omega}^{-1} Y_j v_j}{2 1_2' \hat{\Omega}^{-1} 1_2 \sum_{j=1}^{s^*} v_j^2}$

where $1_2$ indicates a $(2 \times 1)$ vector of 1s, $\hat{\Omega}$ is the estimated variance covariance matrix, and with $Y_j = [\log I_{xx}(\lambda_j), \log I_{yy}(\lambda_j)]^T$, with $\lambda_j = 2\pi j/n$, $j = 1,2,\dots n$, as the Fourier frequencies, and where for arbitrary sequences $\xi_t$ and $\zeta_t$ we define the discrete Fourier transform and the (cross)-periodogram

$w_\xi(\lambda) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi n}} \sum_{t=1}^n \xi_t e^{i\lambda t},\quad I_{\xi\zeta}(\lambda) = w_\xi(\lambda) w_\zeta^T(-\lambda),\quad I_\xi(\lambda) = I_{\xi\xi}(\lambda)$

and $v_j = \log j - \frac{1}{s} \sum_{j=1}^{s^*} \log j$. The limiting distribution above is presented heuristically, but the authors argue that it seems sufficiently convincing for the test to warrant serious consideration.
