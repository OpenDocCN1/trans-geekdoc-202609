| Study | Model | Description and results |
|---|---|---|
| Cready et al. (2001) | Multiple Regression | To overcome bias caused by measurement error, this study developed an alternative means of applying reverse regression techniques in multi-interacted variable settings of the earnings/returns relation with a specific focus on the earnings response coefficient (ERC) analysis of Collins and Kothari (1989). They replicated cross-sectional ERC determinant analysis of Collins and Kothari (1989) and showed that the relationship between ERC and growth and between ERC and beta were both positive. |
| Goldberger (1984) | Multiple Regression | Considered several alternative stochastic models of salary discrimination to assess discrimination by gender or race. The models differ with respect to their specification of the structural relationship between regressors and the latent variable which is best interpreted as the employer's assessment of productivity. The model that justifies reverse regression based on the validity of estimators was identified and selected. Testable implications are deduced, and the analysis is illustrated with empirical material. |
| Klepper and Leamer (1984) | Multiple Regression | Extended Firsch's (1934) work by presenting a complete k-variable generalization of direct and reverse regression and demonstrated how direct and reverse estimates together bound the true coefficients if certain conditions are met. For the unbounded case, they show that considerable prior information concerning the seriousness of the measurement errors in the observed regressors can be used to conditionally bound the estimates. Two statistics are developed to indicate the sensitivity of conventional inferences to measurement error in the regressors, and an illustrative example is presented. |
| Klepper (1988a) | Multiple Regression | Developed a series of diagnostics based on the large-sample properties of the classical errors-in-variables model to assess the sensitivity of regression coefficient estimates to measurement error in the regressors when the set of feasible values of the coefficients are initially be unbounded in reverse regression procedure. The process identifies which measurement error variance(s) must be constrained, and by how much, to bound the set of feasible values of the coefficients to an acceptable range. Algorithms to compute the diagnostics are developed and an illustrative example for the application of the diagnostics is provided. |
