### 123.2.2.2 Exponential GARCH (EGARCH) (p, q) model

Nelson (1991) proposed another model to capture the leverage effects

$$\log(h_t) = \omega + \sum_{i=1}^{q} \alpha_i g(z_{t-i}) + \sum_{j=1}^{p} \beta_j \log(h_{t-j}), \tag{123.12}$$

where \(g(z_{t-i}) = \kappa z_{t-i} + \varsigma(|z_{t-i}| - E(|z_{t-i}|))\), and \(\omega\), \(\alpha_i\), \(\beta_j\), \(\kappa\) and \(\varsigma\) are real numbers. This specification allows asymmetry in the volatility, and also avoids positivity restrictions on the parameters of \(\omega\), \(\alpha_i\), and \(\beta_j\) by way of specifying the log-volatility. The process \(\{h_t\}\) is stationary if all solutions of the characteristic equation

$$1 - \sum_{j=1}^{p} \beta_j \lambda^{j} = 0 \tag{123.13}$$

are greater than unity in absolute value.

## 123.2.3 Estimation of the GARCH(p, q) model

The parameter vector in the GARCH(p, q) model for equations (123.1) and (123.4) is \(\theta = (\theta_1, \dots, \theta_{k+p+q+2}) = (\mu, \gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_k; \omega, \alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_q; \beta_1, \dots, \beta_p)'\). The true value of parameters is unknown and denoted as

$$\theta_0 = (\theta_{01}, \dots, \theta_{0k+p+q+2}) = (\mu_0, \gamma_{01}, \dots, \gamma_{0k}; \omega_0, \alpha_{01}, \dots, \alpha_{0q}; \beta_{01}, \dots, \beta_{0p})'.$$

The orders of the GARCH(p, q) model (namely k, p, and q) are assumed known to estimate the parameters. Though we do not assume any specific distribution of the standardized random variable \(z_t\), we work with the normal distribution. Then the quasi-likelihood is

$$L_T(\theta) = L_T(\theta; y_1, \dots, y_T) = \prod_{t=1}^{T} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi \tilde{h}_t}} \exp\left(-\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}_t^{2}}{2\tilde{h}_t}\right), \tag{123.14}$$

where

$$\tilde{\varepsilon}_t = \tilde{\varepsilon}_t(\theta) = y_t - \mu - \sum_{i=1}^{k} \gamma_i y_{t-i}, \quad \text{and} \quad \tilde{h}_t = \tilde{h}_t(\theta) = \omega + \sum_{i=1}^{q} \alpha_i \tilde{\varepsilon}_{t-i}^2 + \sum_{j=1}^{p} \beta_j \tilde{h}_{t-j}. \tag{123.15}$$

If the initial values are given as \(y_0,\ldots ,y_{1-k-p},\tilde{\varepsilon}_0,\ldots ,\tilde{\varepsilon}_{-q},h_0,\ldots ,h_{1-p}\), then, for any \(\theta\), the values of \(\tilde{h}_t\) and \(\tilde{\varepsilon}_t\) for \(t=1,\ldots ,T\), can be computed from equation (123.15). The quasi-maximum likelihood estimator (QMLE) is defined by a solution \(\hat{\theta}_T\) of \(\hat{\theta}_T = \arg \max_{\theta} L_T(\theta)\). Taking the logarithm of \(L_T(\theta)\), the solution is equivalent to

$$\hat{\theta}_T = \hat{\theta}(y_1,\ldots ,y_T) = \arg\min_{\theta} l_T(\theta), \tag{123.16}$$

where

$$l_T(\theta) = l(\theta; y_1,\ldots ,y_T) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} l_t, \quad \text{and} \quad l_t = \log(\tilde{h}_t) + \frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}_t^2}{\tilde{h}_t}. \tag{123.17}$$

This method of estimation is applicable to other models of GJR and EGARCH, although we do not explicitly discuss these in this paper.

## 123.2.4 Asymptotic properties of the QMLE for the GARCH(p,q)

Under suitable regularity conditions, the QMLE is strongly consistent:

$$\hat{\theta}_T \to \theta_0 \text{ (almost surely), as } T \to \infty \tag{123.18}$$

and asymptotically normally distributed<sup>5</sup>:

$$\sqrt{T}(\hat{\theta}_T - \theta_0) \to N(0,\Sigma), \quad \text{as} \quad T \to \infty, \tag{123.19}$$

where \(\Sigma = J^{-1} I J^{-1}\),

$$I = E_{\theta_0}\left(\frac{\partial l_t(\theta_0)}{\partial\theta}\frac{\partial l_t(\theta_0)}{\partial\theta'}\right), \quad \text{and} \quad J = E_{\theta_0}\left(\frac{\partial^2 l_t(\theta_0)}{\partial\theta\partial\theta'}\right). \tag{123.20}$$

<sup>5</sup>See Theorems 7.4 and 7.5 in Chapter 7 in Francq and Zakoian (2010) for rigorous regularity conditions and proofs. Proofs of consistency and asymptotic normality of the QMLE are mathematically much involved.

## 123.3.1 Representative multivariate GARCH models

We commence with a multivariate (vector) autoregressive of order k or VAR(k) model

$$Y_t = \mu + \sum_{i=1}^{k} \Gamma_i Y_{t-i} + \varepsilon_t, \quad t = 1, \ldots, T, \tag{123.21}$$

where \(Y_t = (Y_{1t}, \ldots, Y_{nt})'\) is an n-dimensional vector and \(\mu, \Gamma_i, \varepsilon_t\) are conformably defined. We assume that all solutions of the characteristic equation

$$\left| I - \sum_{i=1}^{k} \Gamma_i \lambda^i \right| = 0 \tag{123.22}$$

are greater than unity in absolute value. Under this condition, the process \(\{Y_t\}\) is stationary if \(\{\varepsilon_t\}\) is stationary.

A process \(\{\varepsilon_t\}\) in equation (123.21) is a multivariate GARCH model if its first two conditional moments exist and satisfy:

$$(i) \ E(\varepsilon_t \mid I_{t-1}) = 0, \quad (ii) \ H_t = \mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_t \mid I_{t-1}), \tag{123.23}$$

where \(\varepsilon_t = H_t^{1/2} z_t\), \(H_t > 0\) (positive definite), \(z_t \sim \text{i.i.d.}\), \(E(z_t) = 0\), \(\mathrm{Var}(z_t) = I\), and \(I_{t-1}\) in (123.23) denotes the information available up to time \(t - 1\). The conditional expectation of \(Y_t\) is

$$E(Y_t \mid I_{t-1}) = \mu + \sum_{i=1}^{k} \Gamma_i Y_{t-i}. \tag{123.24}$$

The \(n \times n\) conditional variance–covariance matrix \(H_t\) is positive definite and has \(n \times (n + 1)/2\) different elements. Many different ways of specifications concerning the process of \(H_t\) are proposed in the literature.
