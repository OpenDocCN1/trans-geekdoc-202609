Numerous researchers have explored the use of enterprise social media as a method for transforming businesses and improving firm value. As firms look to forge strong connections with their customers in a competitive marketplace, the use of social media can dramatically influence their performance through customer engagement and the value created from customer interactions (Trainor, 2012). For example, businesses use social media as a tool to develop and maintain strong relationships with customers for WOM marketing, i.e., the intentional influence of customer-to-customer communication (Kozinets et al., 2010); for community-based customer support (Greenberg, 2010) and for innovation co-creation (Sawhney et al., 2005). Firms create community support for their brands by sharing information, perpetuating the history and culture of the brand and assisting consumers. In addition, the creation and maintenance of long-term customer relationships, instead of emphasizing individual transactions, has been introduced as a competitive advantage and strategic resource for firms. Thus, social media-based online communities enhance and build brand loyalty and trust (Ba, 2001). That is, these communities decrease uncertainty and information asymmetry and make customers feel comfortable with their brands (Chiu et al., 2010; Pavlou et al., 2007; Doney and Cannon, 1997). Hence, when firms use social media to engage with customers, they enhance their relationship with them and create additional marketing value.

In addition, social media can assist companies to create online resources containing the accumulated wisdom of the organizations by allowing knowledge to be codified, searched and shared (Cairncross, 2001; Tajudeen et al., 2017). Moreover, companies can collect general information about the market and information regarding new trends and industry through social media. Companies can also easily deliver organizational information to the public in a timely manner (Parveen et al., 2015). However, social media platforms have shifted the direction of information flow such that the roles of users have transformed from content consumers to producers. This situation creates the potential for asymmetric information conditions between conversation partners. Information asymmetry is a condition in which one participant in an exchange possesses more or better information than the other (Akerlof, 1970).

Numerous studies have examined the effects of the specific types of social media (e.g., recommender systems) and marketing outcomes (e.g., brand perception, customer satisfaction and even product sales) (Chevalier and Mayzlin, 2006; Dellarocas et al., 2007; Duan et al., 2008; Forman et al., 2008).
