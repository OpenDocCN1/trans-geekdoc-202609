For example, the Asian financial crisis began in Thailand before spreading to other countries in Southeast Asia. Even countries with relatively strong economic systems, such as Singapore, Taiwan, and Hong Kong, were affected (Lee, 2001). Evidence has shown that the 2007 global financial crisis in the Taiwanese financial industry affected banks, security firms, and insurance companies, which suffered serious distress as a result of the crisis (Chen et al., 2011). Forbes and Rigobon (2002) warned that economic or financial volatility in America would shock other countries through the contagion effect; in addition, Wang and Huang (2004) also indicated that financial volatility in America would impact Taiwan via the contagion effect or spillover effect. Outlier detection and intervention analysis should, therefore, be integrated into the modeling process. Taiwan successfully recovered from the financial crisis in 1997 because of effective and proper fiscal and monetary policies put in place in response to the crisis (Chen, 2000; Hsu, 2001). In fact, the policies implemented were important in overcoming the financial crisis; therefore, the way in which the Taiwan government made policies in response to the shock of the financial crisis is worthy of close study.

In accordance with Liao and Lei (2012), the researchers further measured the rate of prediction accuracy of the all models and selected those with the best prediction capability as the TAIEX prediction model using mean absolute percentage error (MAPE), root mean square error (RMSE), and the turning point method. In addition, in order to increase the accuracy of the prediction model, the combination forecasting method proposed by Bates and Granger (1969), Granger and Ramanathan (1984), and Wei and Yang (2012) is also discussed in this study. The model in this study suggests fiscal and monetary policies in response to the shock of financial crisis that may be useful to the Taiwanese government.
