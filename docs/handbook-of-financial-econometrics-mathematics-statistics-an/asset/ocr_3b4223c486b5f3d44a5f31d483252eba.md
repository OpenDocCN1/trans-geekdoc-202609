# 62.4 An Alternative Hypothesis Testing Procedure for the CAPM

BJS and MS have also used an alternative hypothesis testing procedure. After the measurement errors are considered, the null used to test the predictive ability of the systematic risk is

H₀ : c = τ − B

d = \bar{R}_m − \bar{R}_b = (\bar{R}_m' − \bar{R}_T) + (τ − B),  (62.20)

where c and d are the intercept and the slope of a linear relationship

\bar{R}_j = c + d \hat{β}_j.  (62.21)

The effects of ψ_j and s_j on the estimates of both c and d can be analyzed using (62.13a). If we assume that ψ_j is normally distributed with mean (1 − \bar{β})(σ_e² + σ_η²)/(σ_M² + σ_e² + σ_η²) and finite variance σ_{β_j}² [σ_e² + σ² η/(σ_M² + σ_e² + σ_η²)]², then the probability limit of \hat{c} and \hat{d} is

Plim_{j→∞} \hat{d} = d[(σ_M² + σ_e² + σ_η²)/σ_M²]/(1 + σ_s²/σ_{β_j}²),  (62.22a)

Plim_{j→∞} \hat{c} = −B + d(1 − \bar{β}_j)(σ_e² + σ_η²)/(σ_M² + σ_e² + σ_η²) + λ \bar{β}_j + λ(1 − \bar{β}_j)(σ_e² + σ_η²)/(σ_M² + σ_e² + σ_η²),  (62.22b)

where λ is the asymptotic bias of \hat{d}, i.e., Plim_{j→∞} \hat{d} − d.

Comparing (62.16) with (62.22a), it appears that regressing α on β will produce a higher bias on estimated parameters than the method described in this section. The reason is that estimate of b is also affected by constant measurement errors of R_b and R_m in addition to the sample variation of β_j and the random measurement error of R_b and R_m. However, both \bar{R}_m and \bar{R}_b are not observable and (\bar{R}_m' − \bar{R}_T) is employed to substitute for (\bar{R}_m − \bar{R}_b) in the null hypothesis. Therefore, the hypothesis to be tested is not precisely stated. Hence, even if the bias in the estimated parameters of smaller, it does not mean that the test is statistically more powerful. Incidentally, (62.22a) is the general case of the result derived by Black, Jensen and Scholes (1972) who considered only the effect of sampling variation.
