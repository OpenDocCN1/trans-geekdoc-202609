In our case, the cash conversion systems under study include an inventory, treasury and receivables, and as such, this chapter integrates two key sub-areas of supply chain management (SCM): supply chain operations management (SCOM) and supply chain financial management (SCFM). SCOM includes operations planning and scheduling, inventory management (mainly replenishment), etc. SCFM (also known as supply chain finance (SCF)¹) comprises the activities of planning, management and optimization of financial flows across the supply chain. In a modern supply chain, SCOM and SCFM need to be tightly integrated and coordinated. Figure 46.1 depicts schematically the relation between SCOM and SCFM and their key interactions. Here, the firm’s boundary is indicated by a dotted frame, where the firm’s SCOM sub-area is represented at the bottom by an inventory component with its product inflows and outflows, and the firm’s SCFM sub-area is represented at the top as a finance component with its monetary inflows and outflows. The two components are coupled through interactions with outside entities (customers, vendors, owners and a funding entity). Solid arrows represent flows of tangibles (customers, product and funds), while dashed arrows represent flows of information.

¹The term SCF is also used in a restricted sense to mean the practice of reverse factoring. Here, we mean the term in the broader sense.
