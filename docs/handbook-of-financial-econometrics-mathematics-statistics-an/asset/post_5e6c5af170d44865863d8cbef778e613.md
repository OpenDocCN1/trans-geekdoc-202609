For individual copulas, it is easy to observe that none of them are very close to the t-Gumbel copula. The total risks estimated by the Gaussian copula, Clayton copula and Frank copula are 3.49%, 3.50% and 3.51%, respectively, which are very close to each other. The results of the t copula and Gumbel copula are 3.55% and 3.76%, respectively, which are much larger than results of the former three copulas, mainly because they can capture tail dependence while those three copulas cannot. For the last two major approaches, the result of the variance–covariance approach is 3.16%, which is significantly smaller than all other approaches, mainly because it can only capture linear correlation between risks and ignores the consequences of nonlinear dependence. It is noteworthy that the result of the simple summation approach is 3.63%, which is very close to the results of the t-Gumbel copula. However, this does not mean that the simple summation approach is able to capture the dependence structure very well; it is only a special case in this empirical analysis.

The integration results tally highly with our theoretical analysis and the validity test outcomes. The more features the approach can capture, the better it can model the dependence structure, and the more accurate the risk integration is. The Basel Capital Accord II in 1996 and Accord III in 2011 retain the key elements of the 1988 capital adequacy framework, including

the general requirement for banks to hold total capital equivalent to at least 8% of their risk-weighted assets. The CBRC also adopts this standard to regulate Chinese banks. It is notable that the total risk values calculated by these approaches in this empirical study are all smaller than 4%. From this perspective, the economic capital allocated by Chinese listed banks might be able to cover their total risks. However, strictly speaking, these results might underestimate the total risk of Chinese banks, because the data used in this study are from the financial statements of banks, and the accounting subjects cannot be guaranteed as the perfect proxies of these risks.

## 38.5 Conclusion

The major contributions of this chapter are threefold. First, we comprehensively summarize the typical features of the dependence structure between various types of bank risks. Second, we thoroughly analyze the major risk integration approaches’ capabilities of capturing these typical features. Third, we propose a mixture copula framework to capture the overall typical features of dependence structure between bank risks. In the empirical analysis, the constructed mixture copulas are applied to describe the dependence structure between credit, market and operational risks of Chinese banks and integrate these risks into total risk. The results of mixture copulas are compared with seven other major risk integration approaches to verify its effectiveness.

The empirical results provide strong evidence of the effectiveness of the constructed mixture copulas. Compared with other major approaches, the three mixture copulas perform the best in goodness-of-fit test, in particular the t-Gumbel copula, which demonstrates that the mixture of proper copulas can better model the dependence structure between risks. Moreover, the empirical results also help to uncover some important pitfalls and misunderstandings in risk dependence modeling. The copula approach is highly popular at present and is widely recognized as the leading risk integration approach, particularly the t copula. However, in this empirical analysis, none of the five popular individual copulas pass the goodness-of-fit test, indicating that the copula approach should not remain unchallenged in bank risk integration.
