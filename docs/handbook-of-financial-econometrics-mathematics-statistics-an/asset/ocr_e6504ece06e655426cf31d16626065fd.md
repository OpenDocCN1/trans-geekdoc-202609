Facebook and Twitter in the world exceeded 2.45 billion as of 2019.¹ Social media platforms allow users to communicate and interact on the Internet instantly. People can easily send messages through a messenger or a tweet and obtain a fast response (Correa et al., 2010; Edosomwan et al., 2011). Similarly, companies can create firm value by engaging with customers and other stakeholders through social media. A firm can potentially reach new customers, increase interaction with the existing customers and generate word of mouth (WOM) for new and existing product offerings (Aral et al., 2013; Hitt et al., 2014). Besides, companies can use social media to reduce information asymmetry which affects the pricing of financial assets and securities (Blankespoor et al., 2013).

The effects of social media on economic activity are extensive and pervasive, with social networks playing a role in the transmission of information on jobs, products and technologies. Some research shows that regularly sharing information with peers via social networks plays a vital role in investment decision-making, including stock market participation and portfolio choices. Organizations can improve their competitive advantages through social media by learning about the customer’s tastes, sentiments and competitors’ tactics (Tajudeen et al., 2017). While customer satisfaction, brand recognition and even product sales can be good indicators for predicting firm performance, very little evidence exists detailing how the usage of social media affects firm value (Hitt et al., 2014).

In this chapter, we examine the triangular relationships between social media, bank relationships and firm value. Specifically, we aim to answer the following questions: How do social media influence corporate valuation? How do bank relationships affect firm value? Does a firm’s social media usage affect its banking relationships, or does the interaction of social media and bank relationships affect a firm’s value? Moreover, what are the implications for consumers and investors? To address these questions, we design research pathways using big data from social media platforms and the associated financial and lending databases from the Taiwan Economic Journal (TEJ).

The social media platforms include Facebook, YouTube, Twitter and Google+. Data are collected from the official online forums that companies use for communication and interaction.
