# Figure 46.4: Equilibrium marginal pmfs for various μ and ν parameters.

Consider the first column of Figure 46.4, where the receivables payment rate is held fixed and low, and the replenishment rate increases from top to bottom. Note that (1) the pmfs of inventory size peak at n = 0, but this probability decreases with higher replenishment rate; (2) the probability mass of receivables moves progressively to the right; and (3) the probability mass of available funds moves progressively to the left. This is consistent with the observation that low receivables payment rates tend to starve the inventory, and as the replenishment rate increases, the inventory is better stocked, but even more PEMUs get stuck in the receivables pool.

On the other hand, consider the first row of Figure 46.4, where the replenishment rate is held fixed and low, and the receivables payment rate increases from left to right. Note that (1) the pmfs of inventory size peak at n = 0, but this probability decreases with higher receivables payment rate; (2) the probability mass of receivables moves progressively to the left; and (3) the probability mass of available funds moves progressively to the right. This is consistent with the observation that low replenishment rates also tend to starve the inventory, and as the receivables payment rate increases, the inventory is better stocked, but even more PEMUs accumulate in the available funds pool.
