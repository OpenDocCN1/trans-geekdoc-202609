where

$$\sigma_{\hat{\beta}} = \operatorname{Var}(\hat{\beta}) = \frac{\sigma_{\varepsilon}^2}{\sum_{t=1}^n (R_{Mt} - \bar{R}_M)^2}$$

$$\beta = E(\hat{\beta}), \text{ the expected value of non-truncated } \hat{\beta},$$

and

$$k = \int_a^b \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_{\hat{\beta}}} \exp\left[ -\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\hat{\beta} - \beta}{\sigma_{\hat{\beta}}}\right)^2 \right] d\beta = a\text{ constant } > 0.$$

Since $\hat{\beta}$ and $(\bar{R} - R_f)$ are independently distributed, the truncated distribution of $\hat{\beta}$ will also be independent of the distribution of $(\bar{R} - R_f)$. With this independence property, the covariance between the estimated Treynor's measure and its estimated risk measure can be easily obtained as follows⁴:

$$\operatorname{Cov}\left(\frac{\bar{R} - R_f}{\hat{\beta}}, \hat{\beta}\right) \approx E(\bar{R} - R_f) - E(\hat{\beta}) E\left(\frac{\bar{R} - R_f}{\hat{\beta}}\right) \approx E(\bar{R} - R_f) - \beta^+ E(\bar{R} - R_f) E\left(\frac{1}{\hat{\beta}}\right),$$

by using independence property,

$$(68.5)$$

$$\approx (\beta^+ C - 1) [E(\bar{R} - R_f)]$$

$$\approx (\beta^+ C - 1) (R_f - \mu),^5$$

where

+   - $\mu = E(\bar{R})$, the population (market) mean rate of return,
+   - $C = E(1/\hat{\beta})$ is a constant defined in equation (68B.5) of Appendix (68B),
+   - $\beta^+ =$ the expected value of the truncated $\hat{\beta} = \beta + l,$⁶
+   - $l = \frac{1}{k} \left[ Z\left(\frac{\alpha - \beta}{\sigma_{\hat{\beta}}}\right) - Z\left(\frac{b - \beta}{\sigma_{\hat{\beta}}}\right) \right] > 0,$
+   - and $Z\left(\frac{\alpha - \beta}{\sigma_{\hat{\beta}}}\right) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\left[ \frac{-(\alpha - \beta)^2}{2\sigma_{\hat{\beta}}^2} \right].$

⁴ The distribution function of the estimated Treynor's measure can be easily obtained from Fieller's (1932) results. See Appendix (68A) for the derivation.

⁵ Following Friend and Blume (1970), $\bar{R}$ is the average rate of return of a random sample portfolio drawn from a population containing all securities with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$. In other words, $\mu$ represents the population (market) mean return and $\sigma^2$ denotes the population variance associated with market rates of return. Thus, $E(\bar{R}) = \mu.$
