The Herfindahl–Hirschman Index (HHI) of the portfolio weights and the maximum weight among assets are used to measure the portfolio diversity. To evaluate how the candidate assets are utilized, the percentage that assets are included, the number of assets in the portfolio divided by that of the all available assets, is presented.

The results are summarized in Table 82.1. For the MSR portfolios, the diversification with short-selling constraint yields a maximum Sharpe ratio of 0.51. When the investment constraints are added and become more restrictive, the MSR gradually diminishes. For the most restrictive case, i.e., SS+UB (10%), the mean is 0.0239 with a standard deviation of 0.0515. The fact that the HHI of portfolio weights decreases from 0.297 to 0.092 as the upper bounds are increasingly restrictive suggests the portfolio is more diverse. As the upper limits are imposed, the maximal weight in the portfolio decreases, but more available assets are included in the optimal selection.

For the minimum-variance portfolios, the mean–variance efficiency slightly improves (from 0.39 to 0.41) as the investment constraints are more restrictive, though the volatility of the MVPs increase (from 0.0434 to 0.0461). According to the HHI, maximum of portfolio weights, and the percentage of assets included in the optimal asset allocation, the portfolios are increasingly diverse when the upper bounds are included.
