# 7.3.2 Non-parametric method

The advantages of non-parametric method used in derivation of option bounds are not assumptions of the underlying stock's distribution, interest rates or volatility in option pricing framework. Rodriguez (2003) uses a non-parametric method and a single analytical framework to derive the option bounds. Rodriguez (2003) states that the disparate methods applied to option bounds can be viewed as a single analytical framework with the synthesis lying in the derivation of call option bounds which depends on the discount factor of its corresponding put option. The general expression for the value of a European call option in a single analytical framework can be written as

$C_0 = S_0 - K e^{-rT} + R_p \int_0^K F(S_T) d(S_T), \qquad (7.23)$

where $r$ is risk-free rate, $F(S_T)$ is the cumulative distribution function of underlying stock price, and $R_p$ is the discount factor of its corresponding put option. Given the suitable lower bounds of the put's discount factor, Rodriguez (2003) provides the tighter lower bound than the previous work.

Lin et al. (2012) derived a tighter lower bound and re-interpret Ritchken (1985) upper bound of European options under non-parametric framework. The only variable used in these bounds is the dollar beta of an option which is defined as the covariance between an option price and its underlying asset's price over the variance of its underlying asset's price. Lin et al. (2012) utilize the general classical asset pricing model with a stochastic kernel to derive the lower bound at time $t$ for a European call option as follows:

$C_{t,min}^E = D_{t,T} E[C_T] + \beta_C \{ S_t - D_{t,T} E[S_T] \}, \qquad (7.24)$
