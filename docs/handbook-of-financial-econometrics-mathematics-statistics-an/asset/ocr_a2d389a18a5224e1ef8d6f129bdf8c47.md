Several results emerge from the parameter estimates (Table 131.4). Increases in earnings are associated with decreases in loan loss provision expense. For the bank to earn additional income entails greater overall exposure to default risk, but successful risk management generates more income per dollar loan than increased total loss provision, all else equal. The statistical significance of earnings when setting the loan loss provision accentuates the challenge of determining strategies to manage the lending process as well as raising firm income in the existing regulatory environment.

Loan loss provision is impacted by loan quality, measured in terms of the volume of delinquent balances and changes in delinquent balances. This finding reinforces the importance of earnings and loan quality within the banking sector. Financial institutions should continue to deploy a strategy for attracting relatively high-margin loans given potential loan quality declines within this application.

Increased equity offsets loan loss provision expenses and loan loss provisions are inversely related to asset volume. Interestingly, (average) bank assets grew during the sample period. This finding implies that an average increase in loan loss provision via growth is occurring through purchases of progressively less delinquent loans as banks grow in size.
