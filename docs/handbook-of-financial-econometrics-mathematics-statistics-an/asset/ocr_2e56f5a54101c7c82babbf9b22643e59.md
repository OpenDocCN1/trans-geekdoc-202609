# 75.2 Errors-in-Variables Problems

EIVs and measurement error are commonly encountered in asset prices and returns in capital market as well as other regression studies. EIVs and measurement errors in financial asset (security as well as portfolio) and benchmark returns have been very well documented in the extant literature (see, for example, Roll, 1969, 1977; Lee and Jen, 1978; Keim and Stambaugh, 1984; Chen et al., 1986; Cheng and Lee, 1986; French and Roll, 1986; Chen et al., 2015). The T-bill rate is used as a proxy for unobserved risk-free rate and this proxy is likely to measure the true variable with an error (Roll, 1969; Jen, 1970; Lee and Jen, 1978). The use of this proxy for generating risk premium on assets transfers the measurement error to asset excess returns. Roll (1969, 1977) and Lee and Jen (1978) show that the observed market returns in terms of stock market index are measured with error since the stock market index does not include all assets which can be traded by investors.

Roll (1969, 1977) argues that testing capital asset pricing model suffers from an EIVs problem and concludes that no correct and unambiguous test of the theory can be accomplished without correcting for these errors. For the issue of the capital asset pricing test, Lee and Jen (1978) argue that both market return and beta coefficient are subject to measurement error (Chen et al., 2015). Measurement error is also caused by private information which affects prices during the trading session when informed investors trade (French and Roll, 1986). Keim and Stambaugh (1984) note that the distribution of stock returns varies across days of week and the average return for Monday (Friday close to Monday close) appeared to be significantly negative. It is plausible that these pricing errors and weekend effects would spill over to asset returns calculated from observed prices. Even when monthly returns are used in empirical studies, these pricing errors and anomalies occurring at the beginning or end of the month would significantly affect estimated monthly returns.
