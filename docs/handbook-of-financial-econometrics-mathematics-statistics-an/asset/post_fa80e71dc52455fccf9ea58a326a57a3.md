
## 64.4.2 Importance Sampling (IS)

To apply the algorithm of particle filter, we need to first review the importance sampling procedure to estimate the expectation of any function f(·) of x0:t,

E[f(x0:t)] = ∫ f(x0:t)p(x0:t|y1:t;θ)dx0:t
= ∫ f(x0:t) \frac{p(x0:t|y1:t;θ)}{g0:t(x0:t|y1:t;ψ)} g0:t(x0:t|y1:t;ψ) dx0:t,

where p(x0:t|y1:t;θ) is the updated joint distribution of x0:t given y1:t, while g0:t(x0:t|y1:t;ψ) is a proposal (trial) distribution. The importance sampling algorithm is given by

- 1. Take N draws {x0:t(i)}N i=1 from the proposal distribution;
- 2. Calculate the importance weights {w(i)t}N i=1, where w(i)t ∝ p(x0:t(i)|y1:t;θ)/g0:t(x0:t(i)|y1:t;ψ);
- 3. Given the draws {x0:t(i),w(i)t}N i=1, the importance sampling estimator E[f(x0:t)] ≈ ∑i=1N f(x0:t(i)) \hat{w}(i)t , where \hat{w}(i)t = w(i)t / ∑j=1N w(j)t .

The updated joint distribution of x0:t given y1:t, p(x0:t|y1:t;θ), can be derived as follows:

p(x0:t|y1:t;θ) = p(x0:t,y1:t|θ)/p(y1:t|θ) = p(yt|x0:t,y1:t−1;θ)p(x0:t|y1:t−1)p(y1:t−1|θ)/p(yt|y1:t−1)p(y1:t−1|θ)
= p(yt|x0:t,y1:t−1;θ)p(xt|x0:t−1,y1:t−1;θ)/p(yt|y1:t−1;θ)p(x0:t−1|y1:t−1;θ)
= p(yt|xt;θ)p(xt|x0:t−1;θ)/p(yt|y1:t−1;θ)p(x0:t−1|y1:t−1;θ).

## 64.4.3 Sequential Importance Sampling (SIS)

The particle filter implements the above importance sampling sequentially at each iteration, which is denoted as sequential importance sampling (SIS). At time t, we generate {x0:t(i)}N i=1 = {x0:t−1(i),xt(i)}N i=1 by the sequential proposal distribution,

g0:t(x0:t|y1:t;ψ) ≡ gt(xt|x0:t−1;y1:t;ψ)g0:t−1(x0:t−1|y1:t−1;ψ).

Then the weight update process becomes

$$
w_t=\frac{p(x_{0:t}\mid y_{1:t};\theta)}{g_{0:t}(x_{0:t}\mid y_{1:t};\psi)}
$$

$$
=\frac{p(y_t\mid x_t;\theta)p(x_t\mid x_{t-1};\theta)p(x_{0:t-1}\mid y_{1:t-1};\theta)}{p(y_t\mid y_{1:t-1};\theta)g_t(x_t\mid x_{0:t-1},y_{1:t};\psi)g_{0:t-1}(x_{0:t-1}\mid y_{1:t-1};\psi)}
$$

$$
=w_{t-1}\widetilde{w}_t\frac{1}{p(y_t\mid y_{1:t-1};\theta)}\propto w_{t-1}\widetilde{w}_t.
$$

where

$$
\widetilde{w}_t=\frac{p(y_t\mid x_t;\theta)p(x_t\mid x_{t-1};\theta)}{g_t(x_t\mid x_{0:t-1},y_{1:t};\psi)}
$$

is defined as the incremental importance weight.

The updated joint distribution of $x_{0:t}$ can be approximated through

$$
p(x_{0:t}\mid y_{1:t};\theta)\approx\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{w}_t^{(i)}\delta_{x_{0:t}^{(i)}}(x_{0:t}),
$$

where $\delta_{x_{0:t}^{(i)}}$ denotes a Dirac measure located at $x_{0:t}^{(i)}$. Therefore, the filtered state is

$$
x_{t\mid t}\approx\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{w}_t^{(i)}x_t^{(i)}.
$$

According to the above weight update process, we have

$$
\widehat{w}_t^{(i)}=\frac{p(x_{0:t}^{(i)}\mid y_{1:t};\theta)}{g_{0:t}(x_{0:t}^{(i)}\mid y_{1:t};\psi)}p(y_t\mid y_{1:t-1};\theta).
$$

This implies

$$
p(y_t\mid y_{1:t-1};\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widetilde{w}_{t-1}^{(i)}\widetilde{w}_t^{(i)},
$$

which is the contribution to the likelihood at time $t$.

## 64.4.4 The Particle Filter

By summarizing the above subsection, the algorithm of Particle Filter is given as follows: at $t=0$, for $i=1,\ldots,N$, draw $x_0^{(i)}\sim g_0(x_0)$ and get

$$
w_0^{(i)}=\frac{p(x_0^{(i)})}{g_0(x_0^{(i)})}.
$$

At $t=1,\ldots,T$, for $i=1,\ldots,N$,

- 1. Draw $x_t^{(i)}\sim g_t(x_t\mid x_{0:t-1}^{(i)},y_{1:t};\psi)=g_t(x_t\mid x_{t-1}^{(i)},y_t;\psi)$ and compute the importance weights

$$
w_t^{(i)}\propto w_{t-1}^{(i)}\frac{p(y_t\mid x_t^{(i)};\theta)p(x_t^{(i)}\mid x_{t-1}^{(i)};\theta)}{g_t(x_t^{(i)}\mid x_{t-1}^{(i)},y_t;\psi)}.
$$

- 2. Normalize the importance weights:

$$
\widehat{w}_t^{(i)}=\frac{w_t^{(i)}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_t^{(j)}}.
$$

Thus the estimate of any function $f$ of $x_{0:t}$ is

$$
\mathbb{E}[f(x_{0:t})]\approx\sum_{i=1}^{N}f(x_{0:t}^{(i)})\widehat{w}_t^{(i)},
$$

where

$$
\widehat{w}_t^{(i)}=\frac{w_t^{(i)}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_t^{(j)}}.
$$

The joint updated distribution of $x_{0:t}$ is given as

$$
p(x_{0:t}\mid y_{1:t};\theta)\approx\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{w}_t^{(i)}\delta_{x_{0:t}^{(i)}}(x_{0:t}),
$$

where $\delta_{x_{0:t}^{(i)}}$ denotes a Dirac measure located at $x_{0:t}^{(i)}$. Based on this distribution, we can compute the filtered state vector $x_{t\mid t}$;

- 3. Resample $N$ particles with probabilities $\{\widehat{w}_t^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{N}$ and for $i=1,\ldots,N$ set

$$
w_t^{(i)}=\frac{1}{N}.
$$

As a result,

$$
\mathbb{E}[f(x_{0:t})]\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f(x_{0:t}^{(i)}),\qquad p(x_{0:t}\mid y_{1:t};\theta)\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{0:t}}^{(i)}(x_{0:t}),\; p(y_t|y_{1:t-1};\theta)\approx\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat w_{t-1}^{(i)}\hat w_t^{(i)}.
$$

The contribution to the likelihood at time $t$ is $p(y_t|y_{1:t-1};\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat w_{t-1}^{(i)}\hat w_t^{(i)}$. Finally, the log likelihood can be computed as $\log L(\theta|y_{1:T})=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log p(y_t|y_{1:t-1};\theta)\approx\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log\big[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat w_{t-1}^{(i)}\hat w_t^{(i)}\big]$.

## 64.4.5 The Classification of Particle Filter based on Importance Distribution

Based on the choice of importance distribution $g_t(x_t|x_{t-1},y_t;\psi)$, we can divide particle filter into several classes. The first type is the standard (bootstrap) particle filter that uses the blind proposal, $g_t(x_t|x_{t-1},y_t;\psi)=p(x_t|x_{t-1};\theta)$, which implies $\tilde w_t=p(y_t|x_t;\theta)$. It does not use the information in the current observation $y_t$. When the variance of the measurement density is large, the incremental weights are expected to be unbalanced.

The second type is the conditionally optimal importance distribution which uses the proposal, $g_t(x_t|x_{t-1},y_t;\psi)=p(x_t|x_{t-1},y_t;\theta)=\frac{p(y_t|x_t,x_{t-1};\theta)p(x_t|x_{t-1};\theta)}{p(y_t|x_{t-1};\theta)}=\frac{p(y_t|x_t;\theta)p(x_t|x_{t-1};\theta)}{p(y_t|x_{t-1};\theta)}$, which leads to $\tilde w_t=p(y_t|x_{t-1};\theta)$. The weakness of this filter is that for most models, $p(y_t|x_{t-1};\theta)$ is rarely known in closed-form except in special circumstances, e.g., when the observation equation is linear and Gaussian.

The third type is the independent particle filter which uses the proposal $g_t(x_t|x_{t-1},y_t;\psi)\propto p(y_t|x_t;\theta)$. This implies that $\tilde w_t\propto p(x_t|x_{t-1};\theta)$. This choice is convenience when the information from state equation is weak and that from observation equation is strong. It is called independent particle filter because the samples $x_t^{(j)}$ are independent to each other and to the past particles $x_{t-1}^{(i)}$. The weakness of the algorithm is that it involves higher computational complexity. The corresponding algorithm uses the following multiple matching algorithm,

- 1. Generate $x_{t+1}^{(j)}$ from $g_t(x_t|x_{t-1},y_t;\psi)\propto p(y_t|x_t;\theta)$;
- 2. Select $L$ different permutations of $(1,\dots,N)$: $K_l=(k_{l,1},\dots,k_{l,m})$, $l=1,\dots,L$. Compute incremental weight $u_{t+1}^{(k_{l,j},j)}\propto p(x_t^{(j)}|x_{t-1}^{(k_{l,j})};\theta)$ for each permutation; and
- 3. Compute multiple matching weight $\hat w_{t+1}^{(j)}=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{l=1}^{L}u_{t+1}^{(k_{l,j},j)}\hat w_t^{(k_{l,j})}$.

The weakness of the algorithm is that it involves higher computational complexity.

## 64.4.6 Application

Due to the high computational cost of the particle filter, the application of the particle filter in parameter estimation has not been applied very widely. However, as the development of high performance computing such as parallel computing and GPU computing technologies, this method is becoming increasingly popular in economics and finance from dynamic stochastic general equilibrium models in macroeconomics to option pricing. For example, Li and Zinna (2018) examine the properties of the variance risk premium (VRP) and estimate a flexible asset pricing model that captures co-jumps in prices and volatility, and self-exciting jump clustering using the particle filter. Bardgett, Gourrier and Leippold (2018) use the auxiliary particle filter to estimate a flexible affine model based on a joint time series of underlying stock indexes and option prices on both markets and find that including VIX option prices in the model estimation allows better identification of the parameters driving the risk-neutral conditional distributions and term structure of volatility, thereby enhancing the estimation of the variance risk premium.
