# The Evolution of Capital Asset Pricing Models: Update and Extension

Aggregating equation (122.74) over N firms, the supply function is given by

ΔQ_{t+1} = A_i^{-1}(BP_t - E_t D_{t+1}),

where

A^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} A_1^{-1} & A_2^{-1} & \cdots & A_N^{-1} \end{bmatrix}, B = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1 I & \lambda_2 I & \cdots & \lambda_N I \end{bmatrix}, and

Q = \begin{bmatrix} Q_1 \\ Q_2 \\ \vdots \\ Q_N \end{bmatrix}

Equation (122.73) implies that a lower price for a security will increase the amount retired of that security. In other words, the amount of each security newly issued is positively related to its own price and negatively related to its required return and the prices of other securities.



The aggregate demand for risky securities presented by equation (122.74) can be seen as a difference equation. The prices of risky securities are determined in a multiperiod framework. Clearly, the aggregate supply schedule has similar structure. As a result, the model can be summarized by the following equations for demand and supply, respectively:

Q_{t+1} = cS^{-1}(E_t P_{t+1} - (1 + r^*)(P_t + E_t D_{t+1})) and

ΔQ_{t+1} = A_i^{-1}(B P_t - E_t D_{t+1}).

Differencing equation (122.74) for period t and t + 1 and equating the result with equation (122.75), a new equation relating demand and supply for securities is

cS^{-1}[E_t P_{t+1} - E_{t-1} P_t - (1 + r^*)(P_t - P_{t-1}) + E_t D_{t+1} - E_{t-1} D_t ]
= A^{-1}(B P_t - E_t D_{t+1}) + V_t,

where V_t is included to take into account the possible discrepancies in the system. Here, V_t is assumed to be random disturbance with zero expected value and no autocorrelation.
