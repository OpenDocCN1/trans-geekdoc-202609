The results of Carr and Wu (2014) illustrate that hedging errors of two (i.e., static with options and dynamic with underlying) strategies hedging errors of dynamic strategy are larger than the static one. As quadrature nodes decline, the approximation error increases. Based on hedging comparison under the non-Markovian diffusive Heston–Nandi (from here HN–Steven Heston and Saikat Nandi derived a closed-form GARCH option valuation model in 2000) model and Heston stochastic volatility (from here HV–Steven Heston derived a formula for evolution of the volatility of underlying asset in 1993) model show “discretization error becomes dominating source of the hedging error”. Further, dynamic hedging fails miserably when prices are random. When effects of model uncertainty and misspecification are taken into account, dynamic hedging still fails. For Merton jump model, the dynamic hedging produces negative errors irrespective of the direction of large movements.
