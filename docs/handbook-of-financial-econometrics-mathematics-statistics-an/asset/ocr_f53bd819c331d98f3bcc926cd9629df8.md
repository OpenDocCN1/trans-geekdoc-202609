When targets and bidders are in the same industry, completion time for the deals is longer (Walter et al., 2008).

Furthermore, prior studies have documented the importance of deal and firm specific characteristics on firm performance in M&As. Morck et al. (1990) report that the market reacts negatively to diversifying acquisitions. Similarly, Lang and Stulz (1994), Servaes (1996) and Doukas and Kan (2004) find that diversifying acquisitions may reduce the gains to bidders in M&As. However, Jensen and Ruback (1983), Bradley et al. (1988) and Hadlock et al. (2001) report that diversifying acquisitions are in general related to small positive abnormal returns.

Hostile deals can be more complex to complete relative to friendly deals. Servaes (1991) finds that bidders obtain lower returns when the transactions are hostile deals. Hunter and Tagtiani (2003) find that bidders in hostile deals obtain higher post-announcement gains. In addition, bidders may be relatively easy to negotiate the deals when acquiring private targets. Kohers (2004) reports that bidders obtain higher gains when acquiring private targets. Conn et al. (2005) report similar results, showing that bidders in private acquisitions result in positive announcement returns. In addition, Moeller et al. (2004) report that bidders paid by cash obtain positive announcement returns. Hagendorff et al. (2008) similarly find that bidder announcement returns are positively associated with cash payment. However, Loughran and Vijh (1997) find that stock payment results in lower bidder returns and bidders with cash payment break-even on the announcements of takeover deals.

Several studies have reported that firm characteristics can be important determinants to influence firm performance in M&As. Mock et al. (1990) report that firms with superior prior performance make better acquisitions. Rau and Vermaelen (1998) document that acquirers with low book-to-market ratio (glamour acquirers) outperform those with high book-to-market ratio (value acquirers). Dong et al. (2006) report positive relationship between targets’ book-to-market ratio and target abnormal returns. In addition, Moeller and Schlingemann (2005) find that bidder announcement returns are positively associated with their market-to-book ratio. Hunter and Tagtiani (2003) find that bidders obtain higher post-merger gains when the bidder size is large.
