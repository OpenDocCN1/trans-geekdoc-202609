
P* on Z. Comparing equation (116.35) and equation (116.38) shows that σ²_{P*|DIV_{t-1}} is equal to σ²_{P*|Z} only when P* and Z are uncorrelated. Otherwise, σ²_{P*|DIV_{t-1}} < σ²_{P*|Z}, implying that the collinearity increases the bias in the OLS estimate of earnings. Moreover, as shown in equation (116.39), the parameter estimate of Z measured without error is biased in general unless Z is independent of P*.

## 116.3.1.2 Asymptotic bias of 2SLS estimator in the simultaneous equations model

Dhrymes and Kurz (1967), Fama (1974), Smirlock and Marshall (1983), and Lee et al. (2016) have studied empirical relationships between investment and dividend decisions of firms. In this study, a simultaneous equations system is constructed as follows:

```
ΔDIV_t = γ1 ΔK_t + α0 + α1 DIV_{t-1} + α2 P*_t + ε1t,
ΔK_t = γ2 ΔDIV_t + β0 + β1 K_{t-1} + β2 Q*_t + ε2t,
```

where ΔDIV_t and ΔK_t are the change in the dividend and capital stock of firm i from year t − 1 to year t, respectively. DIV_{t-1} and K_{t-1} are the dividend and capital stock of firm i at year t. P*_t is the expected earnings of firm i at year t. Q*_t is the marginal q of firm i at year t. This system (116.40) may be rewritten in the matrix form as follows:

```
Y = X* β + ε,
```

where Y = (ΔDIV, ΔK) is a T × 2 matrix for two endogenous variables, X* = (1 DIV_{t-1} K_{t-1} P* Q*) is a T × 5 matrix for all exogenous variables in the system including a constant term in the first vector of X*. Here, ΔDIV_{-1} and K_{-1} are one-period-lagged data matrices of ΔDIV and K, respectively. ε = (ε1, ε2)' is a T × 2 matrix for two disturbances with mean vector zeros and covariance matrix Σ. The parameters matrices Γ and β are defined as

```
Γ = [1 −γ1 ; −γ2 1] and β = [α0 α1 0 α2 0 ; β0 0 β1 0 β2 ]'
```

Post-multiplying the structural form model (116.32) by Γ^{−1}, we have the following reduced-form model:

```
Y = X* β Γ^{−1} + ε Γ^{−1} = X* Π* + e,
```

where Π* = β Γ^{−1} is the parameters matrix of reduced form model. Disturbances vector e has mean vector zeros and covariance matrix Ω = Γ^{−1} Σ Γ^{−1}.

Here, we rewrite the jth equation in our simultaneous equation model (116.41) in terms of the full set of T observations:

y_j = Y_j γ_j + X_j* β_j + ε_j* = Z_j* δ_j + ε_j,   j = 1,2,   (116.44)

where y_j denotes the T × 1 vector of observations for the endogenous variables on left-hand side of jth equation. Y_j denotes the T × 1 data matrix for the endogenous variables on right-hand side of this equation. X_j* is a data matrix for all exogenous variables in this equation. For example, consider the dividend equation in our simultaneous equation model, y_j and Y_j are data matrices defined as ΔDIV and ΔK, respectively. X_j* = [1 ΔDIV_{-1} P*] is a T × 3 data matrix for the explanatory variables, where ΔDIV_{-1} denotes one-period-lagged data matrix of ΔDIV. Z_j* = [ΔK 1 ΔDIV_{-1} P*] is a T × 4 data matrix for all variables on right-hand side of this equation. Since the jointly determined variables y_j and Y_j (ΔDIV and ΔK) are determined within the system, they are correlated with the disturbance terms. This correlation usually creates estimation difficulties because the ordinary least squares estimator would be biased and inconsistent (e.g., Johnston and DiNardo, 1997, Greene, 2018). The two-stage least squares (2SLS) approach is the most common method used to deal with this endogeneity problem resulting from the correlation of Z_j* and ε_j.

However, in reality, the expected earnings (P*) and marginal q (Q*) are not observable. Now we assume that there is an error of measurement in P* and Q* as follows:

P = P* + v_p   and   Q = Q* + v_q,   (116.45)

where P and Q denote observed data matrix of accounting earnings and Tobin's q, respectively. Assume that the measurement errors v_p and v_q are independent of all explanatory variables and random disturbances ε:

y_j = Y_j γ_j + X_j β_j + ν_j = Z_j δ_j + ε_j,   j = 1,2,   (116.46)

where Z_j = [Y_j X_j]. The 2SLS estimation uses all the exogenous variables in this system as instruments to obtain the predictions of Y_j. In the first stage, we regress Y_j on all exogenous variables X = [1 ΔDIV_{-1} K_{-1} P Q] in the system to receive the predictions of the endogenous variables on right-hand side of this equation (116.46), Ŷ_j = X(X'X)^-1 X'Y_j. In the second stage, we regress y_j on Ŷ_j and X_j to obtain the estimator of δ_j in equation (116.46). Using Ŷ_j in place of Y_j in Z_j yields W_j = [Ŷ_j X_j]. Note that

**W**'_j **W**_j = **W**'_j **Z**_j, the 2SLS estimator for **δ**_j in equation (116.46) is then

$$\hat{\delta}_j^{2SLS} = (\mathbf{W}'_j\mathbf{W}_j)^{-1}\mathbf{W}'_j\mathbf{y}_j$$

$$= \begin{bmatrix} \hat{\mathbf{Y}}'_j\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_j & \hat{\mathbf{Y}}'_j\mathbf{X}_j \\ \mathbf{X}'_j\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_j & \mathbf{X}'_j\mathbf{X}_j \end{bmatrix}^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} \hat{\mathbf{Y}}'_j\mathbf{y}_j \\ \mathbf{X}'_j\mathbf{y}_j \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{\mathbf{Y}}'_j\mathbf{Y}_j & \mathbf{Y}'_j\mathbf{X}_j \\ \mathbf{X}'_j\mathbf{Y}_j & \mathbf{X}'_j\mathbf{X}_j \end{bmatrix}^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} \hat{\mathbf{Y}}'_j\mathbf{y}_j \\ \mathbf{X}'_j\mathbf{y}_j \end{bmatrix}.$$
(116.47)

Replace **Ŷ**_j by **X**(**X**'**X**)^(-1)**X**'**Y**_j in (116.46), this turns out to be equivalent to

$$\hat{\delta}_j^{2SLS} = (\mathbf{Z}'_j\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{Z}_j)^{-1}\mathbf{Z}'_j\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{y}_j,$$
(116.48)

where **y**_j = ΔDIV, **Z**_j = [ΔK 1 ΔDIV_{-1} P], and **X** = [1 ΔDIV_{-1} K_{-1} P Q] are observed data matrices.

Here, the effect of measurement error on the 2SLS estimates of the structural parameters is investigated. To investigate the asymptotic bias of 2SLS estimator, we rewrite 2SLS estimator in equation (116.48) as follows:

$$\hat{\delta}_j^{2SLS} = (\mathbf{Z}'_j\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{Z}_j)^{-1}\mathbf{Z}'_j\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'(\mathbf{Z}^*_j\delta_j + \varepsilon_j)$$

$$= \delta_j + (\mathbf{Z}'_j\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{Z}_j)^{-1}\mathbf{Z}'_j\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}'([\mathbf{0}\,\nu_j]\delta_j + \varepsilon_j).$$
(116.49)

Here, we define that **Z**_j = [**Y**_j **X**_j] = [**Y**_j **X***_j + ν_j] = **Z***_j + [**0** ν_j], and ν_j is the submatrix of measurement error corresponding to included exogenous variables on the right-hand side of the jth equation. Thus, we can find that the sampling error of 2SLS estimator depends on three parts: T^{-1}(**X**'**X**), T^{-1}(**X**'**Z**_j), and T^{-1}(**X**'([**0** ν_j]δ_j + ε_j)). We start with the first term T^{-1}(**X**'**X**)

- (i) $T^{-1}(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})$:

$$\operatorname{plim}T^{-1}(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X}) = \operatorname{plim}T^{-1}((\mathbf{X}^* + \nu)'(\mathbf{X}^* + \nu))$$

$$= \operatorname{plim}T^{-1}(\mathbf{X}^{*\prime}\mathbf{X}^*) + \theta \equiv \Sigma + \theta,$$
(116.50)

where **θ** is the covariance matrix of measurement error **v**. For simplicity, we assume that the measurement errors are mutually independent, i.e., matrix **θ** is diagonal. By equation (116.50), we predict that the biases of **Ŷ**_j = **X**(**X**'**X**)^(-1)**X**'**Y**_j in the first stage will carry over to second stage and hence the estimates of structural parameters will be affected by the measurement error.

- (ii) $T^{-1}(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{Z}_j)$: To solve the variable of interest, we divide the $\mathbf{Y}$ matrix in two parts corresponding the one-dependent endogenous variable $\mathbf{y}_j$, and the explanatory endogenous variables $\mathbf{Y}_j$ in equation (116.46). The $\mathbf{X}^*$ matrix can be similarly partitioned in two parts corresponding the included exogenous variables $\mathbf{X}^*_j$ and the excluded exogenous variables $\mathbf{X}^*_{-j}$. The $j$th equation of reduced form model (116.43) can be rewritten as

$(\mathbf{y}_j|\mathbf{Y}_j) = [\mathbf{X}^*_j\ \mathbf{X}^*_{-j}]\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{\Pi}^*_1 & \mathbf{\Pi}^*_j \\ \mathbf{\Pi}^*_2 & \mathbf{\Pi}^*_{-j}\end{bmatrix} + (\mathbf{e}_j|\mathbf{e}_{-j}).\tag{116.51}

Then, we rewrite the reduced-form model (116.12) corresponding to explanatory endogenous variables $\mathbf{Y}_j$ in the $j$th equation as follows:

$\mathbf{Y}_j = \mathbf{X}^*_j\mathbf{\Pi}^*_j + \mathbf{X}^*_{-j}\mathbf{\Pi}^*_{-j} + \mathbf{e}_{-j},\tag{116.52}$

where $\mathbf{X}^*_j$ is explanatory exogenous variables appeared in equation (116.44). $\mathbf{\Pi}^*_j$ denotes the submatrix of $\mathbf{\Pi}^*$ corresponding to $\mathbf{X}^*_j$ appeared in equation (116.44). $\mathbf{X}^*_{-j}$ and $\mathbf{\Pi}^*_{-j}$ are the submatrices of $\mathbf{X}^*$ and $\mathbf{\Pi}^*$, respectively, corresponding the explanatory variables that do not appear in the $j$th equation. We rearrange the columns of $\mathbf{X}$ such that the first part is $\mathbf{X}_j$, that is

$\mathbf{X} = [\mathbf{X}_j\ \mathbf{X}_{-j}],\tag{116.53}$

where $\mathbf{X}_{-j}$ is the observed exogenous variables that do not appear in the $j$th equation. From eqs. (116.45), (116.52), and (116.53) we have

$\mathbf{Y}_j = \mathbf{X}_j\mathbf{\Pi}^*_j + \mathbf{X}_{-j}\mathbf{\Pi}^*_{-j} + \mathbf{e}_{-j} - \boldsymbol{\nu}_j\mathbf{\Pi}^*_j - \boldsymbol{\nu}_{-j}\mathbf{\Pi}^*_{-j},\tag{116.54}$

where $\boldsymbol{\nu}_j$ and $\boldsymbol{\nu}_{-j}$ are measurement errors corresponding to $\mathbf{X}^*_j$ and $\mathbf{X}^*_{-j}$, respectively.

$\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{Z}_j = [\mathbf{X}_j\ \mathbf{X}_{-j}]'[\mathbf{Y}_j\ \mathbf{X}_j] = \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{X}'_j\mathbf{Y}_j & \mathbf{X}'_j\mathbf{X}_j \\ \mathbf{X}'_{-j}\mathbf{Y}_j & \mathbf{X}'_{-j}\mathbf{X}_j\end{bmatrix}$
$= \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{X}'_j\mathbf{X}_j & \mathbf{X}'_j\mathbf{X}_{-j} \\ \mathbf{X}'_{-j}\mathbf{X}_j & \mathbf{X}'_{-j}\mathbf{X}_{-j}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{\Pi}^*_j & \mathbf{I} \\ \mathbf{\Pi}^*_{-j} & \mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{X}'_j\boldsymbol{\nu}_j\mathbf{\Pi}^*_j + \mathbf{X}'_j\boldsymbol{\nu}_{-j}\mathbf{\Pi}^*_{-j} & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{X}'_{-j}\boldsymbol{\nu}_j\mathbf{\Pi}^*_j + \mathbf{X}'_{-j}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{-j}\mathbf{\Pi}^*_{-j} & \mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}$
$+ \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{X}'_j\mathbf{e}_{-j} & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{X}'_{-j}\mathbf{e}_{-j} & \mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}.\tag{116.55}$
