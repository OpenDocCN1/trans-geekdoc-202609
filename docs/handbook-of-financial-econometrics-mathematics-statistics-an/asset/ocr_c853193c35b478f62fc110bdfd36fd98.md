SOX and the DFA were both passed to curb business fraud and corruption by reducing the risky behavior of financial institutions, requiring certification and disclosure of internal various elements of internal information. SOX (2002) requires management to certify its financial reports are free from errors and weaknesses. Secondarily the results of an externally conducted audit of the internal control environment has to be reported. DFA was passed in 2010 with the goal of imposing additional market-based discipline on the risk management behavior in the financial services, including requirements for protection of investors and consumers from risky bank practices as well as providing stakeholders with information about the link between executive compensation and bank performance. The intended benefits of SOX and DFA are to reduce informational asymmetry between financial institutions and their stakeholders which should decrease firm risk. Critics claim that uniform application of the regulations associated with this legislation has resulted in various, negative, unintended consequences which may increase systemic risk among financial institutions.

Regulatory compliance, as measured by traditional indicators include capital requirements, changes in credit risk expenses, differential size effects. Another major component of financial institution performance is net non-interest expense as a percentage of assets, or the net non-interest expense as a percentage of income. Compliance requirements impose non-interest expenses, reflected as changes in overhead expense, in general, and overhead expense, in particular.

In order to meet the compliance obligations of the regulations extending from this legislation, changes in overhead expense, in general, and personnel expense in particular have occurred. Community banks appear to have changed their total workforce, experiencing a 14.4% decrease in non-interest expense between 2002 and 2011, and then a 55.4% increase between 2011 and 2017. Compensation expenses at these institutions increased 66.1% between 2011 and 2017. These increases outpaced changes in assets in community banks, which grew 23.8% between 2002 and 2011 and then 65.4% after 2011. Therefore, increases in personnel expense are an important component in the change in performance of community banks, resulting in changes in product offerings, costs, and efficiency for individual firms (Table 131.2.2).
