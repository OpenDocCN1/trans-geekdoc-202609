# **Proof of (41.2.8).**

As in the proof of Theorem 41.2.4, let $\epsilon > 0$ and $\tilde{\tau}$ the first exit time for $X$ from $(\epsilon, x + 1)$. Pick a sequence of uniformly bounded functions $(g_n) \subset C_0^2((\infty,0])$ that converges to $g$, except on a set $N$ of Lebesgue measure in $0 R_{-\infty}$, and $g_n(0)=g(0)$ for all $n$. (See Lemma 41.A.1) Therefore, we may consider a sequence of uniformly bounded functions $(\Phi_n; n \ge 1)$ that satisfies the following conditions:

+   - **C1** $\Phi_n = g_n$ on $(-\infty,0]$;
+   - **C2** $\Phi_n = \Phi$ on $(1/n,n)$;
+   - **C3** $\Phi_n \in C_0^2(R)$.

By (C1), (C2), and the fact that $g_n \to g$ except on $N$, we have that $\Phi_n \to \Phi$ pointwise on $R$ except on $N$.

Now, pick $n$ large such that $(\epsilon, x + \epsilon) \subset (1/n,n)$. Then $\Phi_n(x)=\Phi(x)$ by (C2). By Dynkin's formula, for all $t > 0$,

$$E_x\left[e^{-r(\tilde{\tau}\wedge t)}\Phi_n(X_{\tilde{\tau}\wedge t})\right] = E_x\left[\int_0^{\tilde{\tau}\wedge t} e^{-ru}(L - r)\Phi_n(X_u)du\right] + \Phi(x). \tag{41A.1}$$

Since $(\phi_n)$ is uniformly bounded, $\Phi_n \to \Phi$ pointwise on $R_+$, and $\Phi_n \to \Phi$ a.e. on $(-\infty, 0)$, by Lemma 41.A.2(1) and dominated convergence,

$$\lim_{m\to\infty} E_x\left[e^{-r(\tilde{\tau}\wedge t)}\Phi_n(X_{\tilde{\tau}\wedge t})\right] = E_x\left[e^{-r(\tilde{\tau}\wedge t)}\Phi(X_{\tilde{\tau}\wedge t})\right]. \tag{41A.2}$$
