Given that exchange option occurs in merger and acquisition (M&A) case, when firms bargain in M&A in early stages, shareholders of the target firm will do everything in their power to be “over-compensated”, after terms of the M&A deal are agreed upon, the risk moves to shareholders of the acquiring firm in making sure that the M&A deal is closed successfully. One of the ways of minimizing pricing errors in options is by dividing option values by their spot prices. This chapter adopts the same principle in minimizing total hedge errors from the total hedge values in Table 34.2. The total hedge values are divided by their respective spot prices. Table 34.3 illustrates those hedging errors before and after minimization.

The errors in Table 34.3 are assumed to be independent as this eliminates clustering effect. Unlike, Pan (2002) who measures errors as the difference between implied and market volatilities, this chapter measures periodical change errors as one wants to find error in the errors. Panel A (B) of Table 34.3 illustrates that before errors minimization, errors tend to be lower (larger) when tau is longer (shorter) and errors increases (decreases) as tau approaches maturity. Although minimization principle of dividing total hedge values by their respective spot prices seems not to decrease errors, which is contrary to prior studies of derivatives error minimization, however the technique as illustrated in panel B of Table 34.3, minimizes errors as tau approaches the maturity.
