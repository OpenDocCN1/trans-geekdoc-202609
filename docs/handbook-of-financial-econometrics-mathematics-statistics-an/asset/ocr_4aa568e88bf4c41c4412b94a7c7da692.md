But from $(Y'Y)b_1 = \lambda_1 b_1,$

$b_2'(Y'Y)b_1 = \lambda_1 Y_1'Y_1 = 0.$

Thus, $\mu = 0$ and we have

$(Y'Y)b_2 = \lambda_2 b_2,$
(77C.5)

and $\lambda_2$ should obviously be chosen as the second largest latent root of $Y'Y$. We can proceed in this way for each of the $p$ roots of $Y'Y$, and assemble the resultant vectors in the orthogonal matrix:

$B = [b_1\ b_2\ \dots\ b_p].$
(77C.6)

The $p$ principal components of $Y$ are then given by the $n \times p$ matrix $F$,

$F = YB.$
(77C.7)

Moreover,

$F'F = B'Y'YB = \Lambda = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1 & 0 & \dots & 0 \\ 0 & \lambda_2 & \dots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \dots & \lambda_p \end{bmatrix},$ 
(77C.8)

showing that the principal components are indeed pairwise uncorrelated and that their variances are given by

$f_i'f_k = \lambda_i\ (i = 1, \dots, p).$
(77C.9)

This appendix has already shown the major concepts and procedures of principal-component analysis. For further details, we suggest that readers consult Anderson (2003), Johnston and Dinardo (1996), and Stuart (1982).
