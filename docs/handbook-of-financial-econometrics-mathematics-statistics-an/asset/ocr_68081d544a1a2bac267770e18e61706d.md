where $y = \alpha K^{2-\beta}$; $Q[s; v, \lambda]$ is the complementary distribution function of the noncentral $\chi^2$ distribution with $v = 2 + 2/(2 - \beta)$ degrees of freedom, noncentral parameter $\lambda = 2\xi$, and lower integrated limit $s$, and $Q[s; v, \lambda] = 1 - F[s; v, \lambda]$.

Using the density function given in equation (7.43), the noncentral moments of the stock prices can be expressed

$M_i = S_t^i e^{i \mu \tau} \lambda^{-\frac{i-1}{2-\beta}} E\left(Y^{\frac{i-1}{2-\beta}}; v, \lambda\right), \quad (7.46)$

where $i = 1, 2, 3, 4, \dots$, $E\left(Y^{\frac{i-1}{2-\beta}}; v, \lambda\right)$ is the $\frac{i-1}{2-\beta}$-th moment of the noncentral $\chi^2$ distribution with $v = 2 + 2/(2 - \beta)$ degrees of freedom and noncentral parameter $\lambda = 2\xi$.

The noncentral moments of the noncentral $\chi^2$ distribution $E\left(Y^{\frac{i-1}{2-\beta}}; v, \lambda\right)$ can be calculated using the moment generating function of the distribution$^{8,9}$

$M(\theta; v, \lambda) = (1 - 2\theta)^{-\frac{v}{2}} \exp\left(\frac{\lambda \theta}{1 - 2\theta}\right). \quad (7.47)$

With the noncentral moments given in equation (7.46), we can express the necessary moment parameters as follows (for simplicity, we drop the degree of freedom and noncentral parameters in the noncentral moment expressions)

$\mu(t) = S(t) e^{\mu \tau}, \quad (7.47a)$

$\sigma(t) = S(t) e^{\mu \lambda} \left(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2-\beta}} E\left(Y^{\frac{1}{2-\beta}} - 1\right)\right)^{1/2}, \quad (7.47b)$

$\kappa = \frac{\left(\lambda^{-\frac{2}{2-\beta}}\right)E\left(Y^{\frac{2}{2-\beta}}\right) - 3\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2-\beta}}E\left(Y^{\frac{1}{2-\beta}}\right) - 1}{\left[\left(\lambda^{-\frac{2}{2-\beta}}\right)E\left(Y^{\frac{2}{2-\beta}}\right) - 1\right]^{3/2}}, \quad (7.47c)$

$\eta = \frac{\left(\lambda^{-\frac{3}{2-\beta}}\right)E\left(Y^{\frac{3}{2-\beta}}\right) - 4\lambda^{-\frac{2}{2-\beta}}E\left(Y^{\frac{2}{2-\beta}}\right) + 6\lambda^{-\frac{2}{2-\beta}}E\left(Y^{\frac{1}{2-\beta}}\right) + 1}{\left[\left(\lambda^{-\frac{2}{2-\beta}}\right)E\left(Y^{\frac{2}{2-\beta}}\right) - 1\right]^2}. \quad (7.47d)$
