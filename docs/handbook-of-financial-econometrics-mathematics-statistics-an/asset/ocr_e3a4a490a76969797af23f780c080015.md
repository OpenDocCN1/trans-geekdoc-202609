The prices of different stocks listed, for example, in the New York Stock Exchange move correlatively and the conditional covariances change over time. In particular, risk management critically depends on the conditional covariance structure of the assets in a portfolio. Hence, it is important to introduce the concept of the multivariate GARCH model. A wide variety of extensions of univariate to multivariate GARCH models have been dully proposed including the vector, BEKK (Baba, Engle, Kraft and Kroner), constant conditional correlation (CCC) and dynamic conditional correlation (DCC) GARCH models. Bauwens et al. (2006), Silvennoinen and Terasvirta (2009), and Franq and Zakoian (2010, Chapter 11) provide recent useful reviews of these multivariate extensions.

However, unlike the progression from the univariate autoregressive moving average (ARMA) model to the multivariate (vector) ARMA model, there is no direct extension of the univariate GARCH model to a multivariate framework. Two main difficulties arise in a multivariate extension from the statistical viewpoint. First, the conditional covariance matrix entails positive definiteness condition. Second, the specification of conditional covariance matrix generally requires quite a large number of parameters if the number of variables is large, and this often makes the estimation procedure numerically intractable. The DCC-GARCH model proposed by Engle (2002) ingeniously solves two contrary requirements for constructing a model. On the one hand, the model should be sufficiently flexible to capture the behavior of the actually observed data process. On the other hand, it must be sufficiently parsimonious to allow statistical analysis in practice. The DCC-GARCH model is also able to trace the changes in comovements between the different variable elements (DCC). Moreover, the time varying conditional covariance matrix enables us to conduct the variance decomposition of Sims (1980) for each period of the sample observations (the dynamic variance decomposition).

The purpose of this paper is twofold. First, we review the theoretical aspects of the DCC-GARCH model and demonstrate how this model is of promising practical application. This model is not only parsimonious and tractable, but also yields a flexible representation for large-scale multivariate financial time series in the sense that it permits time-varying conditional correlations.
