The data are from July 1926 to July 2012. In the size and book-to-market dataset, there are no missing data from July 1931 to July 2012. Therefore, we use this period as the full sample period.

The datasets contain the number of firms in each portfolio and the average firm size in each portfolio at each point in time. The market capitalization of a portfolio is equal to the number of firms in the portfolio times the average firm size of the firms in the portfolio. The sum of these market caps is the total market cap of the portfolios. The jth element of the market portfolio x_m is

x_{mj} = (market cap of portfolio_j) / (∑ market cap of portfolio_k).

The market portfolio weights differ in magnitude and vary over time — sometimes in opposite directions. For example, the values of the mean, maximum and minimum, the large-growth portfolio's weight (in percent) are 35.1, 67.9 and 17.3, respectively. The time-series correlation between the portfolio's weight and the risk-free rate is negative 0.47. The corresponding values of the small value portfolio's weight are: 0.50, 1.45 and 0.11. And, the time-series correlation between this portfolio's weight and the riskfree rate is positive 0.67.
