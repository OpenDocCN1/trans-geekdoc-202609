(2) The student's t distribution is presented as

$$f(x) = \frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\nu+1}{2}\right)}{\sqrt{\nu\pi}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)} \left(1 + \frac{x^2}{\nu}\right)^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}}, \qquad (38B.3)$$

where ν is degree of freedom and Γ is the Gamma function. The student's t distribution is a common distribution in statistics whose shape is decided by parameter ν.

(3) Lognormal distribution is presented as

$$f(x|\mu,\sigma) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma x} \exp\left\{ -\frac{(\ln x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} \right\}, \; x > 0, \mu > 0, \sigma > 0, \qquad (38B.4)$$

where μ and σ are the mean and standard deviation of logarithmic x, respectively. The lognormal distribution is one of the standard distributions used in insurance and operational risk to model larger claims and operational risk loss.
