# 10.2  Multi-Factor Models

Next, we briefly describe several models that examine the relationship between the asset pricing and factors, which we use as a comparison in our study. First, Fama and French (1993) developed a 3-factor model to explain the cross-sectional variation in stock returns. In their model, the excess return on a portfolio or security is explained by sensitivity to three-factors: broad market excess return, a factor based on market capitalization, and a factor based on the book-to-market equity ratio (B/M):

$$R_{it} - R_{ft} = a_i + b_i(R_{mt} - R_{ft}) + c_iSMB_t + d_iHML_t + e_{it}, \tag{10.1}$$

where *R*ᵢₜ is the return on security or portfolio *i* for period *t*, *R*ᶠₜ is the risk-free return, *R*ₘₜ is the return on the value-weighted market portfolio, SMBₜ is the difference between the returns on diversified portfolios of small and large cap stocks, HMLₜ is the difference between the returns on diversified portfolios of high B/M stocks (value stocks) and low B/M stocks (growth stocks), and *e*ᵢₜ is a zero-mean disturbance term.

Jegadeesh and Titman (1993) show that intermediate-term returns tend to persist; stocks with higher returns in the prior year tend to have higher future returns.
