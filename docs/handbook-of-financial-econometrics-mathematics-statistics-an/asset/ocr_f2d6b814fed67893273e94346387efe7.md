# Entropic Two-Asset Option

In deriving the model, Goldstein et al. (2013) focused on (i) environment, (ii) asset payoffs and information structure and (iii) timeline. For the environment section, time is discrete and had three dates (t = 0, 1, 2). They set dates as follows: date 1, financial markets open and there are three tradable assets (i.e., one riskless asset and two risky assets). At date 2, each unit delivers one dollar and riskless asset is zero net supply. More at date 2, pay-off for asset A is v̂_A and for asset B is v̂_B. They assume that two assets are correlated. For asset payoffs and information structure, Goldstein et al. (2013) assumed that proper focus will yield fundamental value of assets (θ̂). And the expectations of the two assets are independent. This is surprising given that the authors assumed that the two assets are correlated. Probably, they assumed independence of expectations in order to make easier for hedging positions. The timeline is as stated earlier in the paragraph.

In order to verify their model with the real world, they harmonize with different markets including speculation and hedging in a commodity futures market. They assume that L-traders are primary crop suppliers while S-traders are speculators. Other markets (international markets, CDS, convertible bonds, index futures, and human capital and entrepreneurship) were listed earlier on. In advancing and matching their model to the real world, they explored (i) trading and prices, and (ii) speculation, hedging and price informativeness. They put forward the notion that informed S-traders know the fundamental and prices of assets A and B while uniformed S-traders only know prices of assets A and B. Based on constant-absolute-risk-aversion (CARA) and normal distributions, optimal investment in risky assets are D^L_A( θ̂, p̂_A, p̂_B) = \frac{\hat{	heta}\hat{p}_A}{\gamma(1-ho^2)\sigma_A^2} - \frac{ho(\phi\hat{	heta} - \hat{p}_B)}{\gamma(1-ho^2)\sigma_A\sigma_B} and D^L_B( θ̂, p̂_A, p̂_B) = \frac{\hat{	heta}\hat{p}_B}{\gamma(1-ho^2)\sigma_B^2} - \frac{ho(\phi\hat{	heta} - \hat{p}_A)}{\gamma(1-ho^2)\sigma_A\sigma_B}. They state that in both formulas, first term represents speculation-based trading and second term is hedging-motivated trading. The informed S-traders have information set but limited trading set while uniformed S-traders observe prices. On the market clearing, they stated that there are multiple equilibria. On speculation, hedging and price informativeness, Goldstein et al. (2013) stated that as new information get disseminated in the market; informed traders interpret information faster than uniformed traders. Then they state that cost of capital is similar to futures risk premium per share of cost of carry for most asset classes. In order to make speculation, hedging and price informativeness real, they incorporate futures risk premium in their model. Although in Goldstein et al. (2013) risk premium is more a risk factor, for this study risk premium has implications for arbitrage opportunities. Thus, higher the risk premium.
