Especially today, quantitative investment and intelligent trading systems have become an important type of market participants. It is reported that over 70% trading volume in the US stock markets is actually generated by program trading systems. The force of quantitative investment in China has also been in a quick growth in recent years.

Naturally, financial theory and technologies must keep pace with the times, i.e., evolving and advancing along with the changing and evolving financial systems and market ecology. We pay attention on the changes of the market ecology, which directly leads to the reflection and reconstruction of the fundamentals of financial theory such as the “adaptive market hypothesis” proposed by Lo (2004) and the “dynamic market hypothesis” proposed by Pan (2004, 2011), both hypotheses can be considered as deepening of or surpassing over the famous “efficient market hypothesis” of Fama (1965, 1970).

The necessity of keeping pace with the times can be clearly seen on modern portfolio theory. The mean-variance model of Markowitz is basically a static portfolio model. It was then further developed up to multi-period portfolio models which are a kind of mechanic dynamic portfolio models, and which are still to be lifted up to the level of adaptive intelligent dynamic portfolio models and systems, i.e., the “intelligent portfolio theory”. Even so, we recognize that the portfolio theory solely aiming at optimization of asset portfolio weights has its innate limitedness in its footing not yet on the real-time changing asset prices in financial markets. Here we believe that the complexity of financial market price dynamics has been severely overlooked if not underestimated deliberately. In the financial literature since Bachelier (1900), such complexity was simplified to stochastic process models, predominantly the random walk model (Brownian motion and geometric Brownian motion) and more sophisticated extensions such as Levy models with fat tails and jump-diffusion models for gapping or breaks in price process. Implementation of a portfolio in fact corresponds to technical trading in financial industry. No matter how precise, delicate or sophisticated the theoretical portfolio models are, the implementation still has to be realized with technical trading.
