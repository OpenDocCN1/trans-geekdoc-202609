Assume that the measurement error ν_it is independent of P*_it, DIV_it−1, and ε_it. Carroll et al. (2006), and Chen, Lee and Lee (2015) show the asymptotic biases of traditional OLS estimated coefficients are as follows:

$$\operatorname{plim}\{\hat\beta_1 - \beta_1\} = \left(\frac{-\sigma_\nu^2}{\sigma_{P_t^*|\mathrm{DIV}_{t-1}}^2 + \sigma_\nu^2}\right)\beta_1, \qquad (116.35)$$

$$\operatorname{plim}\{\hat\beta_2 - \beta_2\} = \left(\frac{b_{P_t^*|\mathrm{DIV}_{t-1}}\sigma_\nu^2}{\sigma_{P_t^*|\mathrm{DIV}_{t-1}}^2 + \sigma_\nu^2}\right)\beta_1, \qquad (116.36)$$

where σ²_ν is the variance of measurement error, σ²_{P*_t|DIV_{t-1}} is the variance of P*_t conditional on DIV_{t-1}, and b_{P*|DIV_{t-1}} is the coefficient of DIV_{t-1} in the auxiliary regression of P*_t on DIV_{t-1}. Equation (116.36) shows that the OLS estimate of β̂₁ is downward biased. In addition, equation (116.36) shows that the estimated coefficient of DIV_{t-1} measured without error is also biased, the direction of bias of estimated coefficient of DIV_{t-1} will rely on the dependence of P*_t and DIV_{t-1}. We can conclude that the estimated coefficient of variable measured without error is also biased in general, unless the variable is independent of the variable measured with error.
