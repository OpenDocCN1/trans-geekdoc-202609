The average number of banking relationships is 5.59, but similar to the results for Q there is a significant difference between those firms the use foreign debt and those that do not use it. The average number of banking relationships for firms that borrow exclusively from Taiwanese banks is 4.42, while the corresponding number for firms that also borrow from foreign banks (7.97) (significantly higher).

The average firm in our sample has a leverage ratio of 25%, and the average ratio of bank debt to total debt is 61%. Firms that borrow only from domestic banks have a slightly higher average bank debt to total debt ratio (65%) than firms that also borrow from foreign banks (52%). The opposite is true for public debt. Although, the average ratio of public debt to total debt is 24%, firms that borrow from foreign banks have a greater proportion of their debt coming from public markets (28% relative to 22%). Non-bank private debt is on average a much smaller component of the capital structure of the firms in our sample. The average percentage of non-bank private debt to total debt is only 10%. Once again, we see a difference in this ratio between firms that borrow from foreign banks and those that do not do so. The average percentage of non-bank private debt to total debt is 13% for firms that do not borrow from foreign banks, while only being 3% for firms that do borrow from foreign banks. The average ratio of foreign bank debt to total debt is 6%, but one needs to interpret this number with some care, since for firms that have no foreign bank debt the ratio is obviously zero. For those firms that do have foreign bank debt, the average percentage of foreign bank debt to total debt is 17% — a non-trivial percentage. The fact that foreign bank debt is not easily obtainable by all Taiwanese firms suggests that the use of such debt might be a signal of quality from those firms that are able to obtain it.

Collateral seems to be used almost universally by the firms in our sample: 84% of the firms in the sample use collateral in their local banking relationships. This ratio is remarkably stable regardless of whether firms do or do not borrow from foreign banks. The average age of the firms in the sample 21.18 years, with very little differentiation between firms that do not borrow from foreign banks (20.79 years) and those that do (21.67 years). The average log of total assets is 6.50, with firms that borrow from foreign banks being slightly larger (6.60) relative to those that borrow only from local banks (6.43).
