Conversely, the difference in the pre-crisis risk-neutral kurtosis between calls and puts is negatively related to consumer sentiment. When consumer sentiment becomes pessimistic, the diversity in uncertainty about market volatility between call and put option traders is more significant during the pre-crisis period. Finally, during the crisis period, arbitrage is more likely to be constrained. Our evidence of the sentiment effect under different market conditions suggests that the influence of irrational sentiment is more significant during the crisis period, which is similar to Han’s (2008) result, that the impact of aggregate error in investors’ beliefs is stronger for greater arbitrage limits in the options market.

# 42.2 Data and Variables

# 42.2.1 Option data

We obtain the daily prices of TAIEX call and put options from the TAIFEX. The sample period is from January 3, 2005, through December 31, 2010, including 1,493 trading dates, 39,119 call options, and 40,959 put options. The TAIEX options are European options and the expiring month can be in the next three calendar months or the next two calendar-quarter months (March, June, September, or December). TAIEX options that expire in the current month are characterized by higher liquidity and heavier trading volume than options that expire the following month. This study therefore uses concurrent-month TAIEX options. When the options approximate the maturity dates, implied volatility is sensitive to option prices and the trading volumes of the option contracts for the following month subsequently increase, which is often referred to as the maturity effect.
