# 46.4.3 Cash conversion model with S < G + H

For the generalized case of cash conversion model with S < G + H, the solution of the balance equations (46.8) provides the equilibrium pmf of the system. However, solving these equations analytically is much harder compared to the balance equations (46.10) for the case S = G + H, since for S < G + H, L_{n,k} = min{S − n, k} depends on both n and k. Consequently, we resort to a numerical solution approach. More specifically, we will use equation (46.4) to approximate the jump chain’s equilibrium pmf π^J, and then derive an approximation for the requisite equilibrium pmf π using the approximation of π^J. To this end, we first prove some properties of the jump chain corresponding to the cash conversion process.

# Lemma 46.3: The jump chain of the Markovian cash conversion process {(I(t),C(t),R(t)) : t ≥ 0} is irreducible, positive recurrent, non-null and periodic with period 3.

Proof. See Chen et al. (2017, proof of Lemma 2).

# Theorem 46.2: The Markov process {(I(t),C(t),R(t)) : t ≥ 0} has a unique equilibrium probability vector π, given by

π(s) = π^J(s) / [ q(s) Σ_{u∈S} π^J(u)/q(u) ],  s ∈ S,  (46.13)

where the equilibrium probability vector π^J of the corresponding jump chain is unique, and for any probability vector ψ over the state space S, π^J is given by

π^J = ψ (P + P^2 + P^3)/3 lim_{n→∞} (P^3)^n .  (46.14)

Proof. The existence and uniqueness of π and π^J follow from Lemma 46.3. Equation (46.13) follows readily from equation (46.3), while equation (46.14) follows from equation (46.4) as a special case for period K = 3.
