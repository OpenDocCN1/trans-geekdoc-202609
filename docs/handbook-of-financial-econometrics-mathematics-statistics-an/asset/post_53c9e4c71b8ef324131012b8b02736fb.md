## 114.2 Time Aggregation on Systematic Risk Coefficient

### 114.2.1 Impacts of time aggregation on beta under additive assumption

Following Schwartz and Whitcomb (1977a,b), the market model for any $i$th firm or portfolio for a $T$-year period is defined as

$$R_{Tij} = \alpha_{Ti} + \beta_{Ti}R_{TMj} + U_{Tij},\ i = 1,2,\dots,I\ \text{and}\ j = 1,2,\dots,J,$$
(114.1)

where $R_{TMj} = \log(I_{Tj}/I_{Tj-1})$ is the “market” continuously compounded rates of return per annum over the $j$th subperiod of length $T$ and $R_{Tij} = \log_e((P_{Tj}+D_{Tj})/P_{Tj-1})$, the continuously compounded rate of return per annum over the $j$th subperiod of length $T$ for the $i$th firm or portfolio.

Then for any $t$th short period of duration $n$ years ($n < T$), write the model (dropping the firm index $i$, and the observation index $j$ for compactness) as follows:

$$r_t = \alpha_n + \beta_n r_{Mt} + u_t,\quad t = 1,2,\dots,N\ \text{where}\ N = T/n.$$
(114.2)

The relationship between $R_T$ and $r_t$, $R_{MT}$ and $r_{MT}$ is defined as follows:

$$R_T = \sum_{t=1}^{T/n} r_t,$$
(114.3a)

$$R_{MT} = \sum_{t=1}^{T/n} r_{Mt}.$$
(114.3b)

Equations (114.3a) and (114.3b) show that the return of long period can be presented by the summation of rates of return of every individual subperiod which are disjoint. And this property is called additivity. For example, if $r_t$ and $r_{Mt}$ represent monthly rates of returns and $T/n = 3$, then $R_T$ and $R_{MT}$ will represent the quarterly rates of return.

To simplify the analysis, the market model deviation from the mean in terms of monthly rates of return is defined as follows¹:

$$Y_t = \beta X_t + u_t, \quad t = 1,2,\dots,N \tag{114.4}$$

where $Y_t = r_t - \bar{r}_t$, $X_t = r_{Mt} - \bar{r}_{Mt}$, $\beta$ is scalar parameter, and $u_t$ is a non-autocorrelated error term with $E(u_t)=0$ and $E(u_t^2)=\sigma^2$ for all $t$.

Following Zeller and Montimarquette (1971) and the definitions defined in (114.3), the market model without intercept in terms of $N$-period rates of return is given as follows:

$$\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{Y} = \boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{X}\beta + \boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{U} \tag{114.5a}$$

where $\boldsymbol{Y}' = (Y_1, Y_2, \dots, Y_{(J\times N)})$, $\boldsymbol{X}' = (X_1, X_2, \dots, X_{(J\times N)})$, $\boldsymbol{U}' = (u_1, u_2, \dots, u_{(J\times N)})$, and $\boldsymbol{A}$ is a $J \times (J \times N)$ matrix of the form

$$\boldsymbol{A} = \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{I}' & \boldsymbol{o}' & \dots & \boldsymbol{o}' \\ \boldsymbol{o}' & \boldsymbol{I}' & \dots & \boldsymbol{o}' \\ \dots & \dots & \dots & \dots \\ \boldsymbol{o}' & \boldsymbol{o}' & \dots & \boldsymbol{I}' \end{bmatrix},$$

and $\boldsymbol{I}' = (1,1,\dots,1)$ and $\boldsymbol{o}' = (0,0,\dots,0)$ are row vectors of $1 \times N$.

In addition, we specify

$$\boldsymbol{A}_j'\boldsymbol{Y} = \beta \boldsymbol{A}_j'\boldsymbol{X} + \boldsymbol{A}_j'\boldsymbol{U} \tag{114.5b}$$

where $\boldsymbol{A}_j'$ is the $j$th row of $\boldsymbol{A}$.

Given the assumptions made about the elements of $\boldsymbol{U}$ in connection with (114.4), we have $E(\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{U}) = 0$, and the $J \times J$ covariance matrix for $\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{U}$ in (114.4) is

$$E(\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{U}\boldsymbol{U}'\boldsymbol{A}') = \sigma^2 \begin{bmatrix} N & & \\ & N & \\ & & \ddots & \\ & & & N \end{bmatrix}. \tag{114.6}$$

The OLS estimate of β is defined as

\[ \tilde{\beta} = (\boldsymbol{X}' \boldsymbol{A}' \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{X})^{-1} \boldsymbol{X}' \boldsymbol{A}' \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{Y}. \tag{114.7} \]

The minimum variance linear unbiased (MVLU) estimator for β is defined as

\[ \beta^* = [\boldsymbol{X}' \boldsymbol{A}' (\boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{A}')^{-1} \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{X}]^{-1} \boldsymbol{X}' \boldsymbol{A}' (\boldsymbol{A}' \boldsymbol{A})^{-1} \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{Y} \]
\[ = [\boldsymbol{X}' \boldsymbol{A}' \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{X}]^{-1} \boldsymbol{X}' \boldsymbol{A}' \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{Y} = \tilde{\beta}. \tag{114.8} \]

This result indicates that the ordinary least squares (OLS) estimator of systematic risk is equivalent to the generalized least squares (GLS) estimator. Hence the OLS estimator is an MVLU estimator.

Equations (114.4) and (114.5) are simple regressions without intercepts. The slope associated with aggregated data can be defined as

\[ \beta_a^2 = \left[ \frac{\text{Cov}(\boldsymbol{A}'_j \boldsymbol{Y}, \boldsymbol{A}'_j \boldsymbol{X})}{\text{Var}(\boldsymbol{A}'_j \boldsymbol{X})} \right]^2 = r_a^2 \frac{\text{Var}(\boldsymbol{A}'_j \boldsymbol{Y})}{\text{Var}(\boldsymbol{A}'_j \boldsymbol{X})}, \tag{114.9} \]

where $r_a$ is the correlation coefficient between $\boldsymbol{A}'_j \boldsymbol{Y}$ and $\boldsymbol{A}'_j \boldsymbol{X}$.

Equation (114.9) can be used to show that the ex ante systematic risk associated with additive rates of return is not affected by time aggregation even if there exists autocorrelation in the market rates of return. Following equation (114.9), it can be shown that

\[ \text{Var}(\boldsymbol{A}'_j \boldsymbol{X}) = \boldsymbol{A}'_j \text{Var}(\boldsymbol{X}) \boldsymbol{A}_j = \mathbf{I}' \text{Var}(\mathbf{X}_j) \mathbf{I} \]
\[ = \text{Var}(X_t) \left[ N + 2 \sum_{s=1}^{N-1} (N - s) \rho_X^s \right], \tag{114.10} \]

where $\boldsymbol{X}_j$ is column vector containing N market rates of return in aggregated period, and the above equations are derived by assuming that Var$(X_t)$ = Var$(X_{t-s})$ for s = 1,2,...,N-1, the lag s autocovariances of $X_t$ are all equal, which are Cov$(X_t, X_{t-s})$ = Cov$(X_{t-k}, X_{t-k-s})$ for all k and s. And $\rho_X^s$ denotes autocorrelation coefficients with order s for the explanatory variable, which is the excess expected return of market. For simplicity, we denote that $P_N = 2 \sum_{i=1}^{N-1} (N - i) \rho_X^s$.

Furthermore, as shown in appendix to this chapter, it can be obtained that

\[ \text{Cov}(\boldsymbol{A}'_j \boldsymbol{Y}, \boldsymbol{A}'_j \boldsymbol{X}) = \text{Cov}(X_t, Y_t)[N + \Gamma_N], \tag{114.11} \]

where

\[ \Gamma_N = \sum_{s=1}^{N-1} (N - s) \frac{\rho_{XY}^s + \rho_{XY}^{-s}}{\rho_{XY}} = \sum_{s=1}^{N-1} (N - s) q_{XY}^s. \tag{114.12} \]

Equation (114.11) is the Covariance-Time Function (C-T Function) as denoted in Hawawini (1980). The ratio $\frac{\rho_{XY}^{s}+\rho_{XY}^{-s}}{\rho_{XY}}$ is called the $q$-ratio of order $s$. It is the sum of lead ($\rho_{XY}^{s}$) and lag ($\rho_{XY}^{-s}$) intertemporal cross-correlation coefficients for order $s$ in unit (unaggregated) returns divided by the contemporaneous cross-correlation coefficient ($\rho_{XY}$). And we denote this ratio as $q_{XY}^{s}$.

From equations (114.10), (114.11) and definitions of the coefficient of systematic risk, we obtain

$$\beta_{a}^{2} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X_{t}, Y_{t})[N + \Gamma_{N}]}{\text{Var}(X_{t})[N + \text{P}_{N}]} = \beta_{u}^{2} \times \frac{N + \sum_{s=1}^{N-1} (N - s) q_{XY}^{s}}{N + 2 \sum_{s=1}^{N-1} (N - s) \rho_{X}^{s}}. \tag{114.13}$$

Denote the time aggregation factor as $\Phi(N) = \frac{N+\sum_{s=1}^{N-1} (N-s) q_{XY}^{s}}{N+2 \sum_{s=1}^{N-1} (N-s) \rho_{X}^{s}}$. Therefore, the ex ante systematic risk associated with additive rates of return is frequency-dependent. The direction and the magnitude of the time aggregation effect on the estimation of systematic coefficient is then given by the sign and absolute value of the difference between $\Phi(N)$ and 1. It is reasonable to imply that the time aggregation effect is positive (aggregated beta is larger than the unaggregated one) if $\Phi(N)$ is larger than 1. While the effect is believed to be negative if $\Phi(N)$ is smaller than 1. Furthermore, we can speculate that for the variation in time interval, larger difference between the Phi multiplier, $\Phi(N)$, and 1 would indicate bigger gap between aggregated and unaggregated estimations of beta.

In Section 114.3, we will test our result by calculating the $\Phi$'s for each firms and different time frequency and compare it with the actual changing trend of beta.
