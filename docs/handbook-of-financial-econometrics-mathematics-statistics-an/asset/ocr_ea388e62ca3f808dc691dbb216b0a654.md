# 37.3.1 Classical estimation method

# 37.3.1.1 The classical method to a simple regression analysis

In general, the classical method considers three cases: (i) either σ₁² or σ₂² is known; (ii) λ = σ₂² / σ₁² is known; and (iii) σ₁² and σ₂² are known. We can obtain the estimate for β from equation (37.2) under every possible situation as:

(i)  $\hat{\beta} = \frac{S_{XY}}{S_{XX} - \sigma_1^2}$, when $\sigma_1^2$ is known. (37.14)

$\hat{\beta} = \frac{S_{YY} - \sigma_2^2}{S_{XY}}$, when $\sigma_2^2$ is known. (37.15)

(ii) $\hat{\beta} = \frac{(S_{YY} - \lambda S_{XX}) + \{ (S_{YY} - \lambda S_{XX})^2 + 4\lambda S_{XY} \}^{1/2}}{2 S_{XY}}$, when $\lambda = \frac{\sigma_2^2}{\sigma_1^2}$ is known. (37.16)

(iii) When both σ₁² and σ₂² are known, Kendall and Stuart (1961) regarded it as an over-identified situation unless a non-zero covariance between U_i and V_i is introduced. Barnett (1967) followed Kiefer's (1964) suggestion and derived a consistent estimator of $\hat{\beta}$ as one of the real roots of equation (37.8).

$\hat{\beta}^4 - \left( \frac{1}{b_2} - \frac{\lambda}{b_1} - 2\lambda b_2 \right) \hat{\beta}^3 - 3\lambda \left( 1 - \lambda \frac{b_2}{b_1} \right) \hat{\beta}^2 + \lambda^2 \left( \lambda \frac{b_2}{b_1^2} - \frac{1}{b_1} - 2b_2 \right) \hat{\beta} - \lambda^3 \frac{b_2}{b_1} = 0$, (37.17)

where $S_{XX} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \bar{X})^2}{n}$, $S_{YY} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n (Y_i - \bar{Y})^2}{n}$, $S_{XY} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \bar{X})(Y_i - \bar{Y})}{n}$, $m_{XX} = Var(X) = \sigma_U^2 + \sigma_1^2$, $m_{YY} = Var(Y) = \beta^2 \sigma_U^2 + \sigma_2^2$, $m_{XY} = Var(Y) = \beta \sigma_U^2$, $b_1 = \frac{m_{XY}}{m_{XX}}$, $b_2 = \frac{m_{XY}}{m_{YY}}$, and $\lambda = \frac{\sigma_2^2}{\sigma_1^2}$.

It has been shown that the meaningful solution of equation (37.17) is identical to equation (37.12a). The advantage of the knowledge of both σ₁² and σ₂² is that one obtains a more efficient estimator of $\hat{\sigma}_2^2$.
