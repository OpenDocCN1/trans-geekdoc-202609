To illustrate, consider the classical linear in parameters model

**Y** = **X**β + **e**  (28.8)

where
**Y** = (Y₁,…,Y_N)' is a N × 1 vector,
**X** = (**X**₁,…, **X**_N)' = N × K matrix with at least one X_i correlated with e_i (contemporaneous correlation),
β = (β₁,…,β_K)' is a K × 1 vector,
**e** = (e₁,…,e_N)' is a N × 1 vector, and
**e** ∼ IID (0,σ²).

Since we are considering OLS, **B** is specified as a T × T identity matrix in (28.5), so β̂_OLS = (**X**'**X**)⁻¹ **X**'**Y**.

Let H₀ denote the null hypothesis of no misspecification, and H_A denote the alternative hypothesis of a particular form of misspecification.

In order to use OLS on model (28.8) and obtain unbiased parameter estimates, we specify **X** independent of **e**, so the specific null and alternative hypotheses are

H₀ : **X** is independent of **e**.
H_A : **X** is not independent of **e**.

To implement the DWH test to compare OLS to an alternative estimator, compute the vector of contrasts in the respective parameters

β̂_A − β̂_OLS = (**X**'**A** **X**)⁻¹ **X**'**A** **Y** − (**X**'**X**)⁻¹ **X**'**Y** = (**X**'**A** **X**)⁻¹ **X**'**A** **M**_X **Y**,  (28.9)

where **M**_X = **I** − **P**_X and **P**_X = **I** − **X**(**X**'**X**)⁻¹ **X**' are a T × T symmetric and idempotent projection matrices. That is,

+   - for some T × L matrix **Z**, **M**_Z **X** is the T × K matrix of residuals from the regression of each column of **X** on **Z**, and
+   - **P**_Z **X** is the T × K matrix of predicted values from regressing each column of **X** on **Z**.

The choice of **A** depends on the problem under consideration, e.g.,

+   - **A** = **P**_Z ⇒ β̂_A is 2SLS estimate of β using **Z** as instrumental variables.
+   - **A** = **M**_D ⇒ β̂_A is the panel data FE estimator.
