The relationship between the CML and the SML can be seen by rearranging the definition of the beta coefficient:

β_i = Cov(R_i, R_m) / Var(R_m) = σ_im / σ_m² = ρ_{i,m} σ_i σ_m / σ_m² = σ_{i,m} σ_i / σ_m, (79.4)

where σ_i is the standard deviation of a security’s rate of return, σ_m is the standard deviation of the market rate of return, σ_{i,m} is the correlation coefficient of R_i and R_m and, σ_im is the σ_{i,m} / σ_i σ_m.

If σ_im = 1, then equation (79.3) reduces to

E(R_i) = R_f + σ_i / σ_m [E(R_m) - R_f]. (79.3)

If σ_{i,m} = 1, this implies that this portfolio is an efficient portfolio. If i is an individual security, it implies that the returns and risks associated with the asset are perfectly correlated with the market as a whole. There are several implications:

+   (1) Equation (79.3) is a generalized case of equation (79.3).
+   (2) The SML instead of the CML should be used to price an individual security or an inefficient portfolio.
+   (3) The CML prices the risk premium in terms of total risk, and the SML prices the risk premium in terms of systematic risk.
