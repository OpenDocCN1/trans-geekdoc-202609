The Taiwan Weighted Stock Index (hereafter TAIEX) is calculated with the value weighed method. The TAIEX rose over 1,000 in 1987 and over 10,000 in 1990; at the time of this writing, 12,682.41 was the highest index. Historically, its volatility has caused ups and downs in various economic, societal, and political environments. The TAIEX is an important leading indicator of the Taiwanese economy; therefore, predicting its trend is worthy of close attention. This study introduces time-series analysis to establish TAIEX prediction models and prediction accuracy to find the best TAIEX prediction model.

According to Umstead (1977), empirical research has indicated that economic growth and stock prices are positively correlated in America (Cochrane, 1991; Fama, 1981; Kim, 2003; Schwert, 1990; Spiro, 1990). The passive informant hypothesis, the active informant hypothesis, the financing hypothesis, and the stock market pressure hypothesis account for this phenomenon (Mauro, 2003). In America about 52% of stock price volatility can be attributed to market factors¹ (King, 1966); in Taiwan that the figure is 69% (Lin, 1978). Other factors include industry factors and company factors. In some advanced countries² macroeconomic factors are important in stock price volatility (Gabe, 2008); therefore, most researchers have used different time-series methods to analyze each country’s stock prices (Chen, 2009; Chen and Chen, 2010; Hartmann and Pierdzioch, 2007; Jung and Boyd, 1996; Kanas, 2002; Mestel and Gurgul, 2003; Narayan, 2009; Li and Yang, 2017; Shang 2017).

The researchers established a TAIEX prediction model, using the autoregressive integrated moving average model (ARIMA) proposed by Box and Jenkins (1976), generalized autoregressive conditional heteroskedasticity (GARCH) proposed by Bollerslev (1986) and Hansen and Lunde (2005), the error correction model (ECM) proposed by Johansen (1988), intervention analysis proposed by Box and Tiao (1975), outlier detection proposed by Fox (1972), the transfer function model proposed by Liu and Hanssens (1982), and combination forecasting methods proposed by Bates and Granger (1969)
