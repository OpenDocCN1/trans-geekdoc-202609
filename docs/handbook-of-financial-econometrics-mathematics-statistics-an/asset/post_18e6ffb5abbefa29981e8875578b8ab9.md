## 66.4.4 *Quantile regression*

OLS regression estimators are more sensitive to outliers than are median-based quantile estimators, thus to minimize the impact of outliers on our coefficient estimates, we employ a quantile regression methodology to control for extreme Tobin's Q across low to high Tobin's Q quantiles. Quantile regression was first proposed by Koenker and Bassett (1978). The general form of the quantile regression equation is given as follows: y_i = β'_q x_i + u_{q i} (Koenker and Hallock, 2001). This equation implies that the regression coefficients differ across various quantiles. The target for quantile regression estimates is the parameter specified before estimation. Let e_{it} be the residuals and q represent the target quantile from the distribution of the residuals. Quantile parameter estimates are, thus, the coefficients that minimize equation (66.2) as follows:

```
\sum_{e_{it}>0} 2q |e_{it}| + \sum_{e_{it} \leq 0} 2(1-q) |e_{it}|.
```

If q = 0.5, then equal weighting is given to the positive and negative residuals, and the parameters are estimated in order to minimize the sum of absolute errors. Depending on the quantile, varying weights are assigned to the residuals to lessen the impact of outliers. This result differs from OLS measures where the only constraint on the residuals is that their sum equals zero. Thus, this methodology is routinely employed to examine if and how relationships vary between the independent and dependent variables across quantiles. Our quantile regression estimates are based on 100 replications of a bootstrapping algorithm at target quantiles of q = 0.25, 0.50 and 0.75, where the quantiles are based on Tobin's Q.

### 66.4.4.1 Results and analysis of quantile regression

Table 66.4 presents the results of quantile regression of the model presented in equation (66.1). Although the OLS results show that the relationship between Tobin's Q and Facebook is positive and significant, the quantile results in Columns 2-4 show that this result is driven primarily by q = 0.25 and 0.5. It means that when firms have a low-median level of Tobin's Q, the use of Facebook is increasing with Tobin's Q and raises the firm value. Similar positive impacts were observed on Google+ and LinkedIn, where the result was positive, driven by the firms' Tobin's Q that are at low to median quantiles (q = 0.25 – 0.5). As for banking relationships, similar results appear as in OLS and no significant quantile effects were found.

These results verify that investments in social media create values that appear in those firms whose Tobin's Q are located at low to median Tobin's Q quantiles of q = 0.25 - 0.5. For those firms at high quantile (q = 0.75), the results are insignificant.
