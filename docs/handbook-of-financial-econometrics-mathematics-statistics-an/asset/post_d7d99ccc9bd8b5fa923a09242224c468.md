## 35.3 Methodologies and the LISREL System

In this section, we introduce the structural equation modeling (SEM) approach and present an example of a path diagram to show the structure of the structural model and the measurement model in the SEM framework. Subsequently, a MIMIC model and its path diagram show alternative means to investigate the determinants of capital structure. Finally, CFA is provided to improve the explanation of the relations between the indicators and the latent variables in the measurement model of the SEM framework.

### 35.3.1 SEM approach

The SEM incorporates three equations as follows:

Structural model : η = βη + Φξ + ζ    (35.1)
Measurement model for y : y = Λy η + v    (35.2)
Measurement model for x : x = Λξ + δ    (35.3)

In this paper, the accounting items can be viewed as the observable independent variables (x), which are the causes of attributes as the latent variables (ξ), and the debt–equity ratios representing the indicators of the capital structure are the observable dependent variables (y).

The fitting function for the maximum likelihood estimation method for the SEM approach is F = log |Σ| + tr (S Σ^{-1}) − log |S| − (p + q), (35.5)

where S is the observed covariance matrix, Σ is the model-implied covariance matrix, p is the number of independent variables (x), and q is the number of dependent variables (y).

### 35.3.2 Illustration of SEM approach in LISREL system

SEM consists of two parts, the measurement model and the structural model. The measurement model analysis is the presumed relation between the latent variables, which are viewed as the attributes, and the observable variables, which are viewed as the indicators. For example, capital expenditures over total assets (CE_TA) and research and development over sales (RD_S) are the indicators of the growth attribute (Growth). In the measurement model, both indicators are assumed to have measurement errors associated with the growth attribute. In addition, the structural model presents the relationship between the unobserved variables and the outcome. For instance, the relationship between attributes and the capital structure is represented by the structural model, while the relationship between the capital structure and its indicators, which are estimated by debt–equity ratios and debt–asset ratios, is modeled by the measurement model.

### 35.3.3 Multiple indicators multiple causes (MIMIC) model

The MIMIC model is the specified SEM that uses latent variables to investigate the relationship between the indicators and the causes of these latent variables. The MIMIC model incorporates two equations as follows:

η = γx + ζ,  (35.6)
y = λ η + v,  (35.7)

η is the matrix of latent variables that link the determinants of capital structure (x) to capital structure (y); x is the matrix of observed independent variables as the causes of η. y is the matrix of observed dependent variables as the indicators of η; ζ and ν are error terms. In this chapter, there is only one latent variable in η, which is called leverage.
