## Appendix 69B: Statistical Distribution of Sharpe Performance Measure

Determine the distribution of the estimated Sharpe's performance measure:

Since \((\bar{R} - R_f)\) is normally distributed with mean \(\mu - R_f\) and variance \(\sigma^2/n\), \(\frac{(\bar{R} - R_f)}{\sqrt{\sigma^2/n}}\) is normally distributed with mean \(\frac{(\mu - R_f)}{\sqrt{\sigma^2/n}}\) and variance 1. By the stochastic independence of the average excess rate of return and the estimated risk measure, the estimated Sharpe's performance measure can be written as

\[
\frac{\bar{R} - R_f}{S} = \frac{(\bar{R} - R_f)/\sqrt{\sigma^2/n}}{\sqrt{\frac{nS^2/\sigma^2}{n-1}}} \times \frac{1}{\sqrt{n-1}} = t'(\delta) \times \frac{1}{\sqrt{n-1}},
\]

where \(t'(\delta) = \frac{(\bar{R} - R_f)/\sqrt{\sigma^2/n}}{\sqrt{\frac{nS^2/\sigma^2}{n-1}}}\) is a non-central \(t\)-distribution with non-centrality parameter \(\delta = \frac{(\mu - R_f)}{\sqrt{\sigma^2/n}}\) (see Hogg and Craig, 1970). Thus, the estimated Sharpe's performance measure is a non-central \(t\)-distribution. If \(\mu = R_f\), the distribution of the estimated Sharpe's performance reduces to a central \(t\)-distribution with \((n - 1)\) degrees of freedom (since \(\delta = 0\)).

Then, Rohatgi (1976) has shown that

\[
E[t'(\delta)] = \delta \times \frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)} \sqrt{\frac{n}{2}}, \quad n > 1.
\]

Based upon this result, an unbiased estimator for the Sharpe performance measure can be derived by substituting equation (34) into equation (33), i.e.,

\[
E\left(\frac{\bar{R} - R_f}{S}\right) = \left[ \delta \times \frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)} \sqrt{\frac{n}{2}} \right] \times \frac{1}{\sqrt{n-1}} = \left(\frac{\mu - R_f}{\sigma}\right) \times \left(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}\right) \times \left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{2}}\right) \times \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n-1}}\right) = \left(\frac{\mu - R_f}{\sigma}\right) \times e(n)
\]

where

\[
e(n) = \left(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}\right) \times \left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{n-1}}\right).
\]

The above equation implies that the sample estimate of the Sharpe measure is not an unbiased estimator if the security rates of return are normally distributed.

Thus, an unbiased estimator of Sharpe's performance measure can be defined as

\[
\left(\frac{\bar{R} - R_f}{S}\right) \left[ \frac{1}{e(n)} \right]
\]

### Abstract

This study proposes and calibrates the VG NGARCH model, which provides a more informative and parsimonious model by formulating the dynamics of log-returns as a variance-gamma (VG) process by Madan et al. (1998). An autoregressive structure is imposed on the shape parameter of the VG process, which describes the news arrival rates that affect the price movements. The performance of the proposed VG NGARCH model is compared with the GARCH-jump with autoregressive conditional jump intensity (GARJI) model by Chan and Maheu (2002), in which two conditionally independent autoregressive processes are used to describe stock price movements caused by normal and extreme news events, respectively. The comparison is made based on daily stock prices of five financial companies in the S&P 500, namely, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, J. P. Morgan, Citigroup, and AIG, from January 3, 2006 to December 31, 2009. The goodness of fit of the VG NGARCH model and its ability to predict the ex ante probabilities of large price movements are demonstrated and compared with the benchmark GARJI model.

### 70.1 Introduction

In order to explain the large empirical biases caused by the extreme events in traditional pricing theory, a candidate asset-return model needs to capture both the continuously small price movements as well as rare large price movements. On the other hand, an asset-return model must incorporate two frequently empirically observed phenomena: volatility clustering and the leverage effect (e.g., see Nelson, 1991; Campbell and Hentschel, 1992; Engle and Ng, 1993). The volatility clustering phenomena involve the propagation of previous news because of strategic trading, while the leverage effect refers to the phenomena that volatility following bad news exceeds that following good news. The volatility clustering and leverage effect together have led to the development of the family of nonlinear asymmetric GARCH models in financial forecasting and derivatives pricing (e.g., see Nelson, 1991; Engle and Ng, 1993; Glosten et al., 1993; Ding et al., 1993). By allowing heteroskedasticity in the log-normal distributions of asset returns, the traditional ARCH/GARCH models had successfully explained the volatility clustering and the leverage effect. However, Gaussian distributed log-return innovations in traditional ARCH/GARCH models fail to capture the excess skewness and kurtosis frequently found in the distribution of asset returns, and thus fail to capture infrequent large price movements caused by extreme news events.

In the literature, two approaches are proposed for improving the Gaussian GARCH model. The first approach applies leptokurtic and skewed non-Gaussian distributions on the log-return innovations in a GARCH-type model (e.g., see Duan, 1999). The second approach, characterized by excess skewness and kurtosis resulting from occasional large price jumps, imposes stochastic jumps on the log-return innovations in a GARCH-type model (e.g., see Jorion, 1988; Duffie et al., 2000; Pan, 2002; Eraker et al., 2003, 2004). In a more advanced GARCH-jump mixture model, namely, the GARJI model, the conditional variance of asset returns is divided into two components to describe moderate and large price movements resulting from normal and extreme news events, respectively (e.g., see Chan and Maheu, 2002; Maheu and McCurdy, 2004). More specifically, two conditionally independent autoregressive processes for the variability corresponding to normal and large price movements, respectively, are used to describe the dynamics of the two components in a discrete-time setting. Despite the explanatory power of the excess skewness and kurtosis provided by the GARCH-jump mixture model, the locally risk-neutral valuation relationship (LRNVR) for asset pricing proposed by Duan (1995), which provides a link between the risk-neutral distribution and the corresponding historical distribution, cannot be obtained under GARCH-jump models even by further restricting investor preferences (e.g., see Duan et al., 2006).

Rather than using two conditionally independent autoregressive processes, this study proposes a GARCH-type model, namely, a VG NGARCH model, which is constructed based on a variance-gamma (VG) process embedded with a generalized autoregressive conditional heteroskedasticity (GARCH) process on its shape parameter that describes the conditional variance of asset log-returns. Being a purely jump process that accounts for jump discontinuities in asset price dynamics due to the news arrivals having different impacts on the magnitudes of price movements (e.g., see Madan, 1998), the VG process has been applied in finance for asset price dynamics. However, as noted by Konikov and Madan (2002), a homogeneous VG model fails to explain volatility clustering phenomenon owing to the feedback effect. To account for the volatility clustering phenomena and the leverage effect, the proposed VG NGARCH model considers the use of a nonlinear asymmetric autoregressive structure on the shape parameter.
