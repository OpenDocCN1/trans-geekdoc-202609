## 35.4.2 Determinants of capital structure by SEM with CFA

Here, we conduct SEM with CFA to determine the capital structure. The conceptual diagram of the structural model for the determinants of the capital structure is illustrated in Figure 35.6. In the CFA test, we eliminate certain indicators to solve the singularity problems in the covariance matrix and combine certain attributes to satisfy discriminant and convergent validity measures.¹¹ We combine attributes, uniqueness and industry classifications, into one attribute called “Uni_Ind” to solve the collinearity problem since the indicators of these attributes are similar. We also combine volatility and financial rating attributes into one attribute, “Vol_Rate”, because both attributes are used to measure financial distress costs. After the adjustment of CFA, 23 indicators and nine attributes are introduced in SEM to determine the capital structure.¹² The estimates of the parameters of the measurement model and the estimated covariance matrix of attributes are presented in Tables 35.5 and 35.6, respectively.

Except for the indicators of Growth and Size, the indicators are significant as proxies of attributes. According to Table 35.6, asset structure and non-debt tax shield are highly correlated because their indicators are closely related. For example, depreciation indicator (D_TA) of non-debt tax shield is positively related to the gross plant and equipment indicator (IGP_TA) of asset structure. The high correlation of attributes will cause problems in estimating the model. Table 35.7 presents the results of the structural model using SEM with CFA to determine the capital structure. We find that the profitability has a significant effect on the capital structure in either a short-term or long-term aspect. The positive relationship between the profitability and the capital structure is inconsistent with the pecking order theory (Myers, 1977) and the free cash flow theory (Jensen, 1986). It is more likely that profitable firms may utilize both internal sources and debt to invest profitable opportunities and to avoid agency costs (Stulz, 1990). Volatility and financial rating only influence the market-based short-term leverage ratio. This evidence implies that, although the credit risk affects the fluctuation of stock price, bankruptcy costs may not change long-term target leverage ratios. This evidence is also consistent with Welch’s (2004) argument that firms do not readjust target leverage ratios responding to their fluctuated stock prices.

## 35.4.3 Determinants of capital structure by MIMIC model

To enhance the explanation of the factors of capital structure, we use the MIMIC model to reduce the latent variable. The standardized estimates of the determinants of the capital structure on a market value basis are illustrated by the path diagram in Figure 35.7. In the MIMIC model, there is only one latent variable (attribute), Capital Structure (CapStruc), and all observed variables are indicators of the capital structure. To compare the performance between MIMIC model and SEM with CFA, we use the same indicators as those used in SEM with CFA to determine the capital structure. Table 35.8 presents the results of the MIMIC model in terms of the market value basis and the book value basis. The goodness-of-fit measures, the root mean square residual, and the root mean square error of approximation show that the MIMIC model performed better than SEM with CFA because of the decrease in latent variables.13
