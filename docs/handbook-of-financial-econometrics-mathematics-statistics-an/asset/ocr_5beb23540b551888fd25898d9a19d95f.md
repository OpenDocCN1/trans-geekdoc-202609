# 34.2 Literature Review

What causes stock prices to jump varies from the actual events that occur in stock markets to the incompleteness of those stock markets. While there is no argument on events, which normally occur rarely, the incompleteness of stock markets tend to be a broad definition. Among the things that cause stock markets to be incomplete includes new information, asymmetry environment and non-availability of products that are supposed to be offered in those stock markets. For example, option products that are offered in South Africa are exclusively of index nature, i.e., single stock futures on equity indices. This implies that South African options market is incomplete in nature. Thus, both incompleteness and rare events cause stock prices to jump in stock markets. In criticizing prior studies, this chapter explores both literatures on incompleteness, rare events that cause stock price jumps and hedging in incomplete markets.

# 34.2.1 Pricing securities in incomplete markets

According to Smith, Jr. (1976), option formulas are either ad-hoc or equilibrium formulas. The exchange option formula falls within the equilibrium formulas group. In layman's terms, equilibrium models are models based on grounded theory. In exploring prior studies, this chapter focuses only on equilibrium option formulas given their similarity with the Margrabe formula. Benninga and Mayshar (2000), Shackleton and Wojakowski (2002), and Lambrecht and Perraudin (2003) explored equilibrium option formulas when stock markets are incomplete.
