# 75.1 Introduction

Measurement error in observed variables of regression models causes a biased estimate of regression coefficients. Measurement error results in coefficient estimates being biased downward in simple regression and biased downward or upward in multiple regression (Cready *et al.*, 2001). Reverse regression technique (Maddala, 1977; Leamer, 1978; Greene, 2012) is used to mitigate biases resulting from the measurement error. It provides a means of drawing inferences that are robust to measurement error biases. In an analysis of the errors-in-variables (EIVs) problem in which both dependent and independent variables are measured with error, Frisch (1934) demonstrated that direct and reverse regression estimates could be used together to consistently bound the true regression coefficients.

Klepper and Leamer (1984) extended Firsch’s (1934) work by presenting a complete *k*-variable generalization of direct and reverse regression. Several studies applied the *k*-variable direct and reverse regression technique (see, for example, Conway and Roberts, 1983, 1984; Goldberger, 1984; Klepper *et al.*, 1993; Cready *et al.*, 2001). Reverse regression technique has also been employed in examining information content of security prices with respect to accounting earnings (Beaver *et al.*, 1987), cross-sectional variations in earnings response coefficient (Collins and Kothari, 1989), the small-firm effect (Booth and Smith, 1985) and mutual fund performance evaluation (Rahman *et al.*, 1991), to overcome the bias-associated measurement errors in observed variables.^{1} Table 75.1 presents a list of studies that applied the reverse regression technique along with their models and empirical results.

Rahman *et al.* (1991) derived the standard error of reverse regression estimates of Firsch’s (1934) two-variable regression. However, there is no prior research in the extant literature calculating and reporting the standard error of reverse regression estimates for a *k*-variable case. This study fills the void in the literature. The purpose of this study is to derive standard error of reverse regression estimates of a *k*-variable generalization of Klepper and Leamer (1984).
