More specifically, a VG process X is a Brownian motion evaluated at a random business time g = γ(t;ν,ξ), where {γ(t;ν,ξ) : t > 0} is a stochastic gamma process with shape parameter ν (mean rate) and the scale parameter ξ (variance rate), respectively. To control for the dimensionality of parameters, set the scale parameter ξ = 1. At time s, the variance-gamma process X is given by

X(s) = θΔg + σW(Δg), (70.7)

where W represents a standard Brownian motion, and θ and σ are the drift and volatility parameters, respectively, and Δg = γ(s;ν,ξ) − γ(0;ν,ξ) is the extent of random time-change during the interval (0, s]. Note Δg is gamma-distributed with shape and scale parameters νs and ξ = 1, respectively.

Note the Levy density for the gamma process γ(t;ν,ξ) is given by π_γ(x) = (ν²/x) exp(−νx), x > 0, where the Levy measure π_γ(x)dx gives the arrival rate for jumps of size x. As the Levy density π_γ(x) has infinite integral and approximates 0 as x → ∞, this implies that a gamma process, being a purely jump process, has an infinite arrival rate of jumps of sizes near zeros and declining arrival rate for jumps with increasing sizes. Note that as the shape parameter ν increases, the arrival rate of jumps of large sizes increases. Our proposed VG NGARCH model for an asset’s log-return will be based on the VG process in (70.7), which is given in Section 70.2.3.

# 70.2.3 Variance-gamma NGARCH model for log-return

Let the trading period be partitioned into T sub-intervals (0, 1], (1, 2], ..., (T − 1, T]. For t = 1, ..., T, we consider the dynamics of the log-return Y_t = ln[S(t)/S(t − 1)], where S(t) is the time t price of an asset, which is modeled as

Y_t = m + φ_t + θ g_t + ε_t, (70.8)

where m denotes the instantaneous mean return rate, g_t is the random business time-change during the interval (t − 1, t] modulated by a gamma process {γ(s;ν_t,κ): t − 1 < s ≤ t} with shape parameter ν_t and scale parameter κ = 1. The innovation ε_t corresponds to the term σW(Δg) in (70.7), thus is Gaussian distributed as

ε_t | ℱ_{t−1} ~ N(0, σ² g_t). (70.9)

Note the variance of the innovation ε_t depends on the random business time-change g_t, which is gamma-distributed with a time-varying shape parameter ν_t, namely.

g_t | ℱ_{t−1} ~ gamma(ν_t, κ = 1). (70.10)
