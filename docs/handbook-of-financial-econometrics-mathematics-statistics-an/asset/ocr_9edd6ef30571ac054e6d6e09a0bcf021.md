External forces (or forces stemming from the environment) can be found in the context in which an individual acts and makes decisions, and these factors can alter individual perceptions. As external forces influencing the job performance of the employees in financial organizations, we will con-sider organizational support in order to examine how external conditions can alter individual perceptions. Lee et al. (2013) indicate that the lack of organizational support negatively influence on effective computer usage or utilization. Moreover, formal training enhances the job performance of employees by improving their skill set required to handle customer demands and reduce their turnover intentions (Sengupta et al., 2015).

# 56.2.1 Internal forces

# 56.2.1.1 Personal innovativeness

The concept (innovativeness) means that a person has a tendency to be the first to use the latest technologies. Personal innovativeness with technologies is related to the extension of positive perceptions about the advantage of decision support systems, greater computer ambitions, and convictions such as ease of use, compatibility, usefulness and intention to use new IT (Elie-Dit-Cosaque et al., 2011).

However, Walczuch et al. (2007) discovered that personal innovativeness is negatively related to perceived usefulness, using the empirical data from 810 employees of a multisite financial service provider. The authors explain that in the result obtained innovative persons are more critical towards tech-nology since they expect all technology to fulfill the highest demands.
