in such a way that $F_c$ ($F_s$) exactly does not dominate $F_s$ ($F_c$). The criterion of SSDR is stated as follows:

*Let $F_c$ and $F_s$ be the two cumulative distributions with the pth quantile, $Q_{F_C}(p)$ and $Q_{F_S}(p)$, respectively. Then $F_c$ dominates $F_s$ in the SSDR if and only if*

$$\inf_{0\le p < p_C} \frac{\int_0^p [Q_{F_S}(w)-r]dw}{\int_0^p [Q_{F_C}(w)-r]dw} \ge \sup_{p_C < p \le 1} \frac{\int_0^p [Q_{F_S}(w)-r]dw}{\int_0^p [Q_{F_C}(w)-r]dw},$$

*where $p_C$ is the value which solves $\int_0^{p_C} [Q_{F_C}(w)-r]dw = 0$.*

Levy (1985) proves that the cumulative distribution of the return of a European call option, $F_c$, dominates the cumulative distribution of the return of its underlying asset, $F_s$, in the SSDR if and only if $p_c \le p_S$, where $p_c$ and $p_s$ are the values that solve

$$\int_0^{p_c} [Q_{F_C}(w)-r]dw = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \int_0^{p_S} [Q_{F_S}(w)-r]dw = 0. \tag{7.8}$$

The lower bounds of a European call option, $C_{\min}^E$, then can be solved by using Levy (1985)'s dominance theory. In order to avoid such dominance, we must set $p_c > p_S$. Therefore, one can solve for a European call option price, $C_0$, that equates this condition, and then conclude that the market price of a European call option must be greater than such a value. Using this line of reasoning, it can be shown that:

$$C_0 \ge C_{\min}^E = S_0 - \frac{K}{r} + \frac{1}{r p_C} \int_0^K (K - S_T) f(S_T) dS_T, \tag{7.9}$$

where $C_0$ denotes current price of a European call option; $S_0$ denotes current price of the underlying asset, $S_T$ is the future price of the underlying asset at expiration day, $T$; $K$ and $r$ represent the strike price and risk-free rate, respectively; $p_c$ is the value that solves $\int_0^{p_c}[Q_{F_C}(w)-r]dw = 0$ and $F_c$ is the cumulative distribution of the return of a European call option.

Similarly, the upper bound of a European call option, $C_{\max}^E$, can be obtained by considering the dominance of the price of the underlying asset over the price of a European call option in SSDR. By using Levy (1985)'s dominance theory and setting $p_S > p_c$, the upper bound of a European call option, $C_{\max}^E$ can be shown as:

$$C_0 \le C_{\max}^E = \frac{S_0}{E(S_T)} \int_K^{\infty} (S_T - K) f(S_T) dS_T, \tag{7.10}$$
