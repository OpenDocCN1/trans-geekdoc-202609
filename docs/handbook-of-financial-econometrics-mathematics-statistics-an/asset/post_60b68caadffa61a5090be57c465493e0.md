CAR_i = α₀ + β₁(high) + β₂(low) + β₃(domestic) + β₄(foreign) + β₅(cash) + β₆(private targets) + β₇(friendly) + β₈(relatedness) + β₉(market-to-book) + β₁₀(ROA) + β₁₁(ln(total assets)) + β₁₂(financial firms) + ε_i. (76.4)

### 76.5.2 The choice of different types of financial advisors

This section runs the probit regression to explore what determinants can influence the choice of financial advisors for targets and bidders. If financial advisors have more ability to advise the deals, firms are more likely to hire financial advisors. In addition, the regression analysis further examines what factors can influence the choice of different types of financial advisors in terms of high- and low-reputable financial advisors and domestic and foreign advisors in M&As. This allows the current study to reveal the determinants that can influence the choice of different types of financial advisors. Dependent variable is a dummy, where the dummy equals 1 if firms hire financial advisors.

As shown in Table 76.2, the evidence reveals that firm size is an important determinant to influence the choice of financial advisors for both targets and bidders. The results in model specification (1) show that there is a positive relationship between firm size and the choice of target financial advisors. The coefficient is 0.398, indicating that large targets are more likely to hire financial advisors. When looking at model specification (2), the results show that large targets are more likely to hire high-reputable financial advisors, the coefficient at 0.343. In contrast, there is a negative relationship between the target size and low-reputable financial advisors in model specification (3). The coefficient is −0.109, indicating that small targets are more likely to hire low-reputable financial advisors. As large deals may be more complex, large targets are more likely to hire high-reputable financial advisors. This may be attributed to the fact that high-reputable financial advisors may have more M&A experience to assist targets in completing the deals. On the contrary, small targets may prefer to hire low-reputable financial advisors possibly because of lower advisory fees.

In addition, the results in model specification (3) also reveal that targets with superior performance prior to the transactions are more likely to hire low-reputable financial advisors. The coefficient is 0.732. This may suggest that target firms with better performance can be more attractive to bidding firms. Thus, targets can pay lower advisory fees to hire low-reputable financial advisors in order to complete the deals. Furthermore, when targets have higher growth potential, targets are more likely to hire domestic advisors. The coefficient in model specification (4) is 0.044. The results in model specification (5) also show that targets are less likely to hire foreign advisors when the transactions use cash payment. In addition, targets are more likely to hire foreign advisors when targets are financial firms.
