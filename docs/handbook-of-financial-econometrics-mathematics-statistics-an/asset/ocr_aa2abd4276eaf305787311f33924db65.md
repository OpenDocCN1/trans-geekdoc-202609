Forecast consistency (cons) is calculated as follows. First, we calculate forecast error of analyst i for firm j and the standard deviation of forecast errors over the years dating from the first coverage analyst i issued for firm j. Second, we rank all analysts covering firm j based on the standard deviation of those forecast errors. Finally, we calculate the ranking of forecast consistency (cons) for analysts covering firm j as the following:

cons = 1 − (rank − 1)/(number of analysts following the firm − 1). (31.7)

To test the relationship between earnings forecast consistency and structural hole measures, we calculate the average forecast error, average rank, average cons of each analyst across all the firms she follows. Our results indicate no significant correlation between measures of forecast consistency and structural hole. In other words, analysts occupying more structural holes do not release more consistent earnings forecasts in our sample period.

To sum up, our analysis indicates that timeliness leaders are more likely to occupy more structural holes. However, analysts associated with higher structural hole measures do not publish more accurate or more consistent earnings forecasts. Our results are more in line with those of Cooper et al. (2001) which indicate that forecast timeliness is a better method to rank analysts while forecast accuracy is not.
