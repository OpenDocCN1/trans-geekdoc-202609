# # Time-Frequency Wavelet Analysis of Stock-Market Co-Movement

To examine the index return distributions, we begin with descriptive statistics.⁴ During the period under study, except Greece, Japan and Thailand, all other markets showed positive average daily returns. The highest average daily return within the group came from Venezuela (0.001185) followed by Argentina (0.000498), Mexico (0.000492), India (0.000379), Denmark (0.000371), Indonesia (0.000347), Colombia (0.000327), Finland (0.000309), Sweden (0.000308) and Sri Lanka (0.000307). The lowest average daily return is recorded for Greece (−4,954 × 10−5), Japan (−2.946 × 10−7), Portugal (2.873 × 10−5), Malaysia (4.228 × 10−5), Austria (5.165 × 10−5), Singapore (5.615 × 10−5), Taiwan (6.793 × 10−5), Italy (8.513 × 10−5) and Hong Kong (9.899 × 10−5). These statistics suggest that the developing Latin American stock markets outperformed the other region stock markets.

The most volatile stock market is Argentina, with a standard deviation of 0.0217 followed by Greece (0.0199), China (0.0196), Thailand (0.01909), Finland (0.01901), Sweden (0.0179), South Korea (0.0172), Venezuela (0.0167), Hong Kong (0.0162) and Austria (0.0161). The least volatile market is Chile, with a standard deviation of 0.0086, followed by Australia (0.0096), Colombia (0.0101), Canada (0.0105) and Peru (0.0106). Furthermore, except Venezuela, Mexico, Chile, Sweden, Hong Kong, Thailand, China, Sri Lanka and Malaysia, all the other stock returns are negatively skewed. This means that the tremendous negative returns of equities are more evident than the enormous positive returns. The highest skewness belongs to Venezuela (2.3200) while the Kurtosis statistics show that all stock return series are leptokurtic. The highest excess Kurtosis is witnessed by Malaysia (55.069) followed by Venezuela (43.189) and China (23.975). The distribution of the returns series is not normal with a kurtosis exceeding three in all cases and showing a skewness different from zero. The Jarque–Bera statistics and p-values, on the other hand, strongly reject the null hypothesis of normal distribution.

The correlation matrix for the stock market returns shows that the correlation coefficients across all the stock market returns are positive.
