# 23.6 Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we have investigated the economic relationships among the ASEAN countries by looking at the statistical properties of the trade balances in the 10 countries that form the ASEAN economic community by means of using fractional integration and cointegration techniques. These techniques are more general than those that are based exclusively on integer degrees of differentiation and thus, include these standard techniques as particular cases of interest in the fractional framework.

We first conducted several unit root testing methods and the results were very inconclusive about the existence of unit roots, which may be due to the fact that the series are fractionally integrated with orders of integration between 0 and 1. In fact, our results (based on the Whittle function in the frequency domain) decisively reject the two hypotheses of I(0) stationarity and I(1) non-stationarity in all cases examined. This result was obtained independently of the assumptions made on the disturbance term.

An interesting implication of this result is that the trade balances in the ASEAN countries are mean reverting (d < 1) with shocks disappearing in the long run. Thus, in case of negative shocks there is no need of strong measures since the series will recover by themselves to their original trends.

In a second step, we also investigated the possibility of cointegrating relationships among the countries in a vis-à-vis relationship. For this purpose, a necessary condition is that the two series must display the same degree of integration. This condition was fulfilled in a large number of cases.

We observe some relations where cointegration could be satisfied, mainly involving countries such as Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia and the Philippines. Yet for other countries that only partial cointegration results can be found.

It is worth mentioning that fractional cointegration is only one possibility when looking at the relationships among the ASEAN countries. In addition, a fully multivariate fractional cointegrated approach like the one suggested by Nielsen and Johansen (2012) could be implemented with these data. It could also be appropriate to re-compute the tests for an updated sample size taking into account, for instance, the European debt crisis.
