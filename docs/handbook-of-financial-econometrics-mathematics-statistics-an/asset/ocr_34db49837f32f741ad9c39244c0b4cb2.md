Since the introduction of our model in Hung and Wang (2005), there are several papers trying to incorporate prospect theory (or merely loss aversion) into consumption-based asset pricing models. For example, Kőszegi and Rabin (2006) apply prospect theory to developing a reference-dependent, loss averse utility over consumption. They believe that the reference point that people use to compute gains and losses is their expectations. As a result, in addition to the traditional consumption utility stressed in economics, they incorporate another utility function from gains and losses compared with the expected consumption level, and this utility function also exhibits the characteristic of loss aversion and diminishing sensitivity proposed by prospect theory. In Kőszegi and Rabin (2007), they analyze risk attitudes of investors with such kind of utility function over consumption. Based on the same utility function, Kőszegi and Rabin (2009) derive the consumption plan associated with this utility function. However, Kőszegi and Rabin (2006, 2007, 2009) focus on theoretical analyzes but do not provide any empirical evidence for the utility function they propose. Following this stream, Andries (2012) uses the expected consumption level as the reference point to construct a consumption utility, which exhibits loss aversion when future consumption levels are lower than their expected values. Different from prospect theory, investors described in Andries (2012) are always risk averse regardless of facing gains or losses in consumption. Similar to our results, she finds loss aversion enhances expected returns substantially and the asset pricing model with this utility shows superior performance in matching moments of asset prices.
