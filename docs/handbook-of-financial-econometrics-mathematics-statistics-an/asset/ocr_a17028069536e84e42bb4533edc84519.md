# 60.5 Concluding Remarks

We rely on a structural approach model to investigate the influence of theoretical determinants on the quarterly changes of bank CDS spreads during a 10 year period between January 2005 and October 2015. Going beyond the structural model, this study incorporates features such as the role of common risk factors, bank-specific characteristics, monetary policy (unconventional) measures and credit rating issues. The suggestion in this chapter is based on the fact that the theoretical determinants of Euro-zone banks’ CDS do not perform the same in different market cycles (also see Alexander and Kaeck, 2008, González-Hermosillo, 2008 and Annaert et al., 2013). As the period under investigation changes from a tranquil one (January 2005 to December 2007) to the global financial crisis (January 2008 to December 2009) and then to the Euro-zone sovereign debt crisis, the influence of the structural model determinants increases. More specifically, the evidence shows that in tranquil periods stock markets have strong effects on both core and peripheral banks’ credit risk. Historical volatility plays a role only during the global financial crisis. Finally, during the Euro-zone debt crisis period all three theoretical determinants influence strongly banks’ credit risk. The evidence does not offer support to the argument that theoretical determinants that are found to affect CDS spreads of non-financial institutions lose their strength when applied to financial institutions CDS (see Raunig and Scheicher, 2008; Grammmatikos and Vermeulen, 2012). The main conclusion is that the structural model variables affect banks’ CDS spreads but their performance varies with the market conditions, and that the role of the structural model determinants is intensified during volatile periods, i.e., the global financial crisis and the European sovereign debt crisis.

In addition, it is remarkable that there exists a strong effect that comes from the systemic factor, in consistency to numerous other studies. Also, bank credit risk in euro-zone seems to be immune to changes in bank-specific characteristics. We do not support the result of others (e.g., Drago et al., 2017) for the peripheral banks that retail banks are less risky (especially during financial crisis), since they have lower market betas and generate more profits. Also, core banks only seem to be affected by the capital adequacy ratios and only for the turbulent 2008–2009 period. Finally, we observe that the too-big-to-fail issue failed to affect the CDS spreads. However, this might be biased evidence since our sample already includes the largest banks in Euro-zone.
