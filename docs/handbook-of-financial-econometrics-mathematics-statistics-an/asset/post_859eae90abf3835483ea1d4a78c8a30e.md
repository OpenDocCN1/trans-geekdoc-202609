However, after the reforms were enacted, the positive impact of foreign banking relationships on firm performance disappears, and turns out to be negative but not significantly different from zero. Interestingly, although pre-reform the use of collateral for local loans had a negative but not significant impact on performance, post-reform the coefficient remains negative but now becomes significant. This implies that the use of collateral by Taiwanese firms is viewed negatively by the market. In contrast, the coefficients on foreign bank collateral remain positive and significant both pre- and post-reform. The results are consistent with the notion that the Taiwanese banking reform increased the sophistication of Taiwanese banking institutions and the financial system overall. Taiwanese banks started to resemble their foreign counterparts, and consequently some of the value that might be obtained from the uniqueness of foreign banking relationships eroded.

## 25.7 Conclusion

This paper examines the impact of foreign banking relationships on the performance of emerging market firms; especially Taiwanese ones. This paper finds that, controlling for the other forms of debt financing and for firm characteristics, the use of foreign bank debt is associated with increased performance. Further, this paper examines the role of collateral in domestic versus foreign banking relationships. Although the use of collateral by domestic banks is not associated with better performance, the use of collateral by foreign banks is associated with better performance. This result supports the notion that foreign bank collateral is a signaling mechanism of firm quality, due to a separating equilibrium whereby high quality firms post collateral in order to obtain lower financing costs, while poor quality firms would be unwilling to follow suit for fear of losing the collateral.

This paper also provides a discussion of the Taiwanese Financial Reform and offers some evidence of how the reform impacted the relationship between foreign banking relationships and performance. The Taiwanese Financial Reform was instituted in order to increase the liquidity, safety, and sophistication of Taiwanese financial markets. It was an event that began the transformation of Taiwan’s financial markets from an emerging market to one that is more financially sophisticated. Not surprisingly, the positive, beneficial impact of foreign banking relationships on firm performance was mitigated substantially after the enactment of the Financial Reform. In conclusion, our results are consistent with the notion that foreign banking relationships create value for emerging market firms because foreign bank monitoring creates value above and beyond that which can be provided by domestic, emerging market banks. In addition, this Taiwanese data suggests that, as emerging markets mature and become more sophisticated, the performance enhancement derived from foreign banking relationships will be diminished.
