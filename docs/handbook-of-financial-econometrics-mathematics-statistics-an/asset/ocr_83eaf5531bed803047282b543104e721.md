This is the expression in the Hotelling's T² statistic, in equation (47.7), we employ to test the null hypothesis that α_t = (μ_t^s - μ_t^e) = 0. When W_t = D_t = Diag(Σ_t), where the variances of the asset returns are the diagonal elements and zeros are the off-diagonal elements, the Weighted Least Squares estimator minimizes α'_t D_t^{-1} α_t. Levy and Roll (2010) minimize a weighted average of the means and standard deviations, i.e., a weighted average of ∑ ((μ_it^e - μ_it^s)/σ_it^s)² and ∑ ((σ_it^e - σ_it^s)/σ_it^s)², with 75% of the weight on the means. See Levy and Roll (2010, equation (47.2)). Our diagonal distance is a special case of the Levy-Roll distance with 100% of the weight on the means.

Unfortunately, methods that minimize the distance between the sample and SML means often yield slopes of the SML that are negative or intercepts of the SML that are less than the risk-free rate. Therefore, we examine cases that restrict the distance methods' estimates of the intercept and slope to be consistent with MV theory. More specifically, the Isqlin command in the Matlab Optimization Tool Box allows us to run OLS regressions that restrict θ_Int ≥ r_ft and θ_Slt > 0. That is, the intercept (or zero-beta rate) is restricted be greater than or equal to the risk-free rate and the slope is restricted to be strictly positive. Specifically, we set θ_Slt > 0.0005. The Isqlin command constrains the coefficients of OLS regressions. To employ Isqlin for the GLS regressions we transform the variables and run OLS regressions. It is well known that a positive-definite matrix can be expressed as A = PP', where P is nonsingular. We find P using the Matlab command Chol, which performs the Cholesky factorization of Ω or Σ. Pre-multiplying the GLS model y=Xθ+e by P^{-1} converts the GLS regression into an OLS regression. Fourteen sets of equilibrium means are summarized in Table 47.1. At a point in time, we estimate the betas (using the estimated covariance matrix and observed market weights, i.e., β_mt = Σ_t x_mt / σ_mt²) and the sample means. The estimates of the covariance matrix and sample mean vectors are based on a fixed 120-month window or an expanding window beginning with 120 observations.
