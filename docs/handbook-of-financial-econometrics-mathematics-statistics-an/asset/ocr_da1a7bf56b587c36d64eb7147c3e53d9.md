The Kalman filter consists of the prediction equations and updating equations. The prediction equations can be written as

$\alpha_{t|t-1} = \phi \alpha_{t-1}, \qquad (53A.15)$

and

$\Omega_{t|t-1} = \phi \Omega_{t-1} \phi + Q, \qquad (53A.16)$

where $\alpha_{t-1}$ is the optimal estimator of $\alpha_{t-1}$ and $\Omega_{t-1}$ is the covariance matrix of the estimated error terms, which is just a quadric form of (53.A4). The prediction equations state that $\alpha_{t|t-1}$ the optimal estimator of $\alpha_t$ can be estimated at time $t-1$ if $\phi$ and $\alpha_{t-1}$ have been known or estimated.

The updated equations can be written as

$\alpha_t = \alpha_{t|t-1} + \Omega_{t|t-1} Z'_t F_{t-1} (y_t - Z_t \alpha_{t|t-1}), \qquad (53A.17)$

where

$F_t = Z_t \Omega_{t|t-1} Z'_t + G_t. \qquad (53A.18)$

The updated equations state that $\alpha_t$ cannot observed at time $t$, but can be predicted at time $t-1$ via the prediction equations. However, the variables $y_t$ is observable at time $t$ and can be used to update the prediction of $\alpha_1$ by incorporating the new information via updated equations. This process continues until the current state of interest is reached.
