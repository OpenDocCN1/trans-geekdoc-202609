the real Euler equation (12.21) can be represented as:

0 = θ log β − (θ/σ)E_t Δc_{t+1} + (θ − 1)E_t r_{m,t+1} + E_t r_{i,t+1} + θ((1/σ) − 1)E_t Δπ_{t+1} + ½[(θ/σ)² V_{cc} + (θ − 1)² V_{mm} + V_{ii} − 2(θ/σ)(θ − 1)V_{cm} − 2(θ/σ)V_{ci} + 2(θ − 1)V_{im}] + ½{[(θ((1/σ)−1))]² V_{ππ} − 2θ²(1/σ)((1/σ)−1)V_{πc} + 2θ(θ−1)((1/σ)−1)V_{πm} + 2θ((1/σ)−1)V_{πi}} (12.25)

where lowercase letters are used for logs and V_cc denotes var_t(Δc_{t+1}), V_jj denotes var_t(r_{j,t+1}) ∀j = i,m, V_cj denotes cov_t(Δc_{t+1}, r_{j,t+1}) ∀j = i,m, V_im denotes cov_t(r_{i,t+1}, r_{m,t+1}), V_iπ = cov_t(r_{i,t+1}, r_{π,t+1}), and r_{π,t+1} = Δ ln(π_{t+1}). In equation (12.25), we do not assume that asset prices and consumption are conditional homoscedastic. In other words, the conditional second moments in equation (12.25) are time-varying.

Replacing the asset i by market portfolio and rearranging, we obtain the relationship among expected consumption growth and the expected return on the market portfolio as well as expected price level growth:

E_t Δc_{t+1} = σ log β + ½[(θ/σ)V_{cc} + θσ V_{mm} + 2(1−σ)θ((1/σ)−1)V_{ππ}] − ½[2θ V_{cm} + 2θ((1/σ)−1)V_{πc} − 2θ(1−σ)V_{πm}] + σ E_t r_{m,t+1} + (1−σ)E_t r_{π,t+1} = θ log β + ½(θ/σ) var_t[Δc_{t+1} − σ r_{m,t+1} + (1−σ)Δπ_{t+1}] + σ E_t r_{m,t+1} + (1−σ)E_t r_{π,t+1}. (12.26)

As has been noted, the second term in equation (12.26) is not a constant now. Equation (12.26) indicates that after adjusting the first and second term in equation (12.26), consumption growth is linearly related to the expected market return and expected inflation. In addition, the coefficients of these two variables add up to one.
