| *Equity market openness* | This measure is based on the ratio of the market capitalization of the constituent firms composing the International Financial Corporation (IFC) Investable index to those that compose the IFC Global index for each country. The IFC Investable index represents a portfolio of domestic equities that are available to foreign investors while Global index measures the overall market portfolio of each country. The ratio of one implies all stocks in that market are available to foreign investors whereas a value of zero suggests the market is completely closed to foreigners. | − | Bekaert *et al.* (2012)/ Emerging Market Database, S&P/Compustat/Frequency: annual |
| *Trade/GDP* | The sum of exports and imports of goods and services divided by gross domestic product. | + | Campa and Fernandes (2006); Campbell *et al.* (2008); De Santis and Gerard (1998)/WDI/Frequency: annual |
| *Trade openness* | Wacziarg and Welch (2008) develop this measure considering five factors including average tariff rates of 40% or more, a black market exchange rate depreciated by 20% or more relative to the official exchange rate during 1970s and 1980s; non-tariff barriers covering 40% or more of trade; a state monopoly on major exports; a socialist economic system. A country gets a value of zero if it meets any of these criteria, otherwise one. | − | Bekaert *et al.* (2012)/Wacziarg and Welch (2008)/Frequency: annual |
