Among them, European countries became much more integrated with each other during these 20 sample years, exhibiting very strong time trends. France, Germany, Italy and Spain all have t-statistics in excess of eight. Some other notable increases in measured integration include South Korea, Sweden, and the United Kingdom. There are, however, some glaring exceptions to the general trend of enhanced integration. One country actually exhibits significantly negative time trends in their R², Malaysia, which might not be all that surprising. Overall, R² shows most countries in our sample have integrated with the world market over time.

To compare R² with correlation, we show t-statistics of slope from the regression of correlation of each country with the rest of the world on time trend. Fifteen of the twenty-one developed countries have positive t-statistics in excess of 2.0 with 4.21 of the mean time trend t-statistic. For the 13 emerging countries, seven have positive t-statistics in excess of 2.0, one has positive time trend significant at 10% level, three have positive but insignificant time trend, and two have negative time trends. The mean time trend t-statistic is 1.92. Overall, t-statistics of correlation time trend is lower than those of R² time trend. This evidence supports Roll (2013) and Pukthuanthong and Roll (2009)’s theoretical argument that the correlation can be conceivably quite low between markets; although, they are driven by the same common influences and are thus perfectly integrated or R² equal 1.0. The results suggest correlation and R² capture different dynamics of market integration.
