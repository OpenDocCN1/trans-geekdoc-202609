# 72.2 Models and Estimation

Let $\tilde{r}_t = (r_{1,t},\dots,r_{m,t})'$ be the equity return vector at time $t$, $\tilde{\mu} = (\mu_1,\dots,\mu_m)'$ be the mean of $\tilde{r}_t$, and $r_t$ be the return of the weighted portfolio,

$r_t = \sum_{i=1}^{m} w_i r_{i,t} = \mu + \sum_{i=1}^{m} w_i v_{i,t} u_{i,t}, \tag{72.1}$
where $\mu = \sum_{i=1}^{m} w_i \mu_i$, $\sum_{i=1}^{m} w_i = 1$, and the weights $\{w_i\}$ are allowed to be positive, zero, or negative. Let $\{S_t, t = 0,1,\dots\}$ denote price process formed by its log-returns $r_t = \ln(S_t/S_{t-1})$. HCT-1 considered the problem of estimating $Q_\alpha(T)$, the $\alpha$th quantile of the equity price $S_t$ at $T$, that is, $\alpha = P\{S_T < Q_\alpha(T)\}$. First, HCT-1 assumed the return process follows the multivariate SV (MSV) model defined by

$\tilde{r}_t = \tilde{\mu} + V_t U_t, \tag{72.2}$
where $V_t = \operatorname{diag}(v_{1,t},\dots,v_{m,t})$ is a diagonal matrix, with $v_{i,t} = \exp(Z_{i,t}/2)$, $i = 1,\dots,m$, $U_t = (u_{1,t},\dots,u_{m,t})'$ is a sequence of shocks comprised of independent identically distributed (i.i.d.) random vectors with mean 0 and a positive-definite covariance matrix $\sum_U = [\sigma_{U,ij}]$, $Z_t = (Z_{1,t},\dots,Z_{m,t})'$ follows an $m$-dimensional stationary short memory process.

Suppose both $\sigma = \{E(r_t - \mu)^2\}^{1/2}$ and $\mu$ are unknown. By assuming the normality of $U_t$, HCT-1 showed that $B_T/\sigma \approx \Phi^{-1}(\alpha)$, where $B_T = (\ln(Q_\alpha(T)/S_0) - T\mu)/\sqrt{T}$, and $\Phi^{-1}(\cdot)$ is the inverse of the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution. From this, the following estimate for $Q_\alpha(T)$ is suggested:

$\hat{Q}_\alpha(T) = S_0 \exp\big( \Phi^{-1}(\alpha) \sqrt{T} \hat{\sigma}_T + T \hat{\mu}_T \big), \tag{72.3}$
where $\hat{\sigma}_T = (T^{-1} \sum_{t=1}^T (r_t - \hat{\mu}_T)^2 )$ with $\hat{\mu}_T = \sum_{t=1}^T r_t$. Furthermore, HCT-1 established the asymptotical normality for the sequence of estimation errors $\ln(\hat{Q}_\alpha(T)/Q_\alpha(T))$, and then constructed the $100(1-\beta)\%$ confidence interval for $Q_\alpha(T)$:

$\hat{Q}_\alpha(T) \exp(-\sqrt{T} \hat{\sigma}_T U) \le Q_\alpha(T) \le \hat{Q}_\alpha(T) \exp(-\sqrt{T} \hat{\sigma}_T L), \tag{72.4}$
where $L$ and $U$ are the $100(\beta/2)\%$ and the $100(1-\beta/2)\%$ quantile of $N(0,1)$, respectively.

HCT-2 extended the results of HCT-1 to those for the CTE with two modifications. First, regarding the length of integration, it is the same as the sample size in the latter, whereas the sample size $n$ and the length $T$ satisfy $n = N \cdot T$ for some positive integer $N$ in the former. Second, the MSV model considered in HCT-2 is more general than that in HCT-1.
