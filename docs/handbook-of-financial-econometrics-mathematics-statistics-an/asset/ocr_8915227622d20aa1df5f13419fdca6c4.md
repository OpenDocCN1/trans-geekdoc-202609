# 23.4 Methodology

In the analysis of time-series, the issue of stationarity is crucial in order to make statistical inference. However, most macroeconomic time-series display non-stationarity in their behavior. The traditional approaches to remove the non-stationarity were usually based on deterministic functions of time or alternatively, taking first (or second) differences or a combination of the two. In fact, differencing (with or without deterministic components) became a standard practice, especially after the seminal paper by Nelson and Plosser (1982). In that paper, these authors found that, using tests of Fuller (1976) and Dickey and Fuller (1979) all except one of the 14 US macroeconomic series examined contained a unit root. Later on, many other more powerful tests statistics were developed for the analysis of unit roots (Phillips and Perron, 1988; Elliot et al., 1996; Ng and Perron, 2001; etc.), including in some cases one or more structural breaks (Zivot and Andrews, 1992; Perron, 1997; Lumsdaine and Papell, 1997; Lee and Strazicich, 2003; etc).
