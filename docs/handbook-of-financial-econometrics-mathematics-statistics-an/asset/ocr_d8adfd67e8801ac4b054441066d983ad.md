where $q(s) = \sum_{(s,u)\in\mathcal{S}_-^2} q(s,u)$. Under certain technical conditions (irreducibility, positive recurrence), a finite-state jump chain, by virtue of its Markovian property, has a unique equilibrium pmf row vector, $\pi^J = [\pi^J(s_i)]$, satisfying $\pi^J P = \pi^J$ (Çinlar, 1975, p. 153). The equilibrium vectors $\pi$ and $\pi^J$ are generally not equal, but are related by (see Kelly, 1979, pp. 4-5)

$\pi^J(s) = \frac{\pi(s)q(s)}{\sum_{u\in\mathcal{S}}\pi(u)q(u)},\quad s \in \mathcal{S}.\tag{46.3}$

Note that equation (46.3) is consistent with equation (46.1), since the corresponding denominators constitute different representations of the same expression, and the balance equations ensure that the corresponding numerators are equivalent in equilibrium. From Çinlar (1975) Theorem 3.10, for an irreducible positive recurrent non-null Markov chain, $\pi^J$ is also the limiting pdf vector satisfying for any initial row probability vector $\psi$,

$\pi^J = \begin{cases}
\psi \lim_{n\to\infty} P^n, & \text{if }\{M^J(t): t \ge 0\}\text{ is aperiodic}\\
\psi \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K P^k}{K} \lim_{n\to\infty} (P^K)^n, & \text{if }\{M^J(t): t \ge 0\}\text{ is periodic with period }K,
\end{cases}\tag{46.4}$

where $\psi$ is any probability vector over the state space $\mathcal{S}$.
