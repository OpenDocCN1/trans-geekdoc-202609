# 60.3 Data and Methodology

# 60.3.1 Data description

In this analysis, we use data on nine EMU (European Monetary Union) countries. We split the sample into two sub-groups. The first one consists of Italian, Spanish, Greek and Portuguese banks and stand for the Euro-zone’s periphery. Irish banks are not included in the sample due to the lack of data on CDS prices. The second one consists of German, Austrian, Dutch and French banks that form the “core” group. The data cover the period 1/2005 to 3/2015 and have a quarterly frequency since we make use, among others, of bank balance sheet variables, which are available on a quarterly basis only. Furthermore, thin trading of CDS contracts, for most of the testing period and banks in our sample, renders the analysis at a higher frequency as misleading since the revealed information from CDS prices does not reflect the market’s perception of risk. Finally, we focus here on identifying the factors that determine CDS spreads on a medium-term basis rather than their dynamic behavior at a higher frequency.

We break the sample into three sub-samples. Our intention is to measure the lasting effects of the chosen variables during the global financial crisis and the Euro-area sovereign debt crisis and compare them to those found during a pre-crisis period. The first period, which represents the tranquil period in the financial markets, is 1/2005–4/2007. The starting date is necessitated from the lack of data for the previous periods, while the end of this period is considered by many researchers as the time when the first signs of the incoming GFC appeared. The second period covers 2008 and 2009 and captures the GFC, while the last one is from 1/2010 to 3/2015 and captures the euro-area sovereign debt crisis.
