Green and Hollifield (1992) and Jagannathan and Ma (2003) evaluate portfolio bounds from the angle of risk control and document that weight constraints decrease volatility in portfolio return and increase feasibility of asset allocation. Brunnermeier and Pedersen (2005) and Wagner (2011) model liquidity in portfolio strategy and document its impact on the correlation structure and the effectiveness of risk management. To take infeasibility into account, we impose the restriction on the portfolio weight based on the market cap of each country.³ We measure diversification benefits using ex ante and realized mean-variance and volatility. The advantage of using MSCI data in this study is it directly deals with the issues of feasibility and diversity in optimal portfolios. Specifically, the MSCI take various requirements such as size, adjusted market capitalization, liquidity requirement, length of trading requirement, etc., into account when the indices are created.⁴

The major findings of this paper are as follows. The benefits of international diversification vary over time but still prevail despite an increasing integration of the global financial market. Both regression analyses and explanatory power tests show that market integration (adjusted R² and correlation are negatively associated with the benefits of diversification; however, R² is more statistically and economically significant. We find that variables associated with the business cycle, such as inflation, country risk, liquidity, size of equity market, and the relative performance of domestic portfolio in the world market, are significantly associated with the potential values from overseas investments.
