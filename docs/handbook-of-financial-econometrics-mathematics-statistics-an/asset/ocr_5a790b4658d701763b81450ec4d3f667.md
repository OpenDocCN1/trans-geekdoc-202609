Black et al. (1972) time-series test of the CAPM is based on the regression

$$r_{jt} - r_{ft} = \alpha_j + \beta_j(r_{mt} - r_{ft}) + e_{jt}, \qquad (47.4)$$

where $r_{jt}$ is the rate of return on asset (or portfolio) $j$ at time $t$, $r_{ft}$ is the riskfree rate of interest at time $t$, and $r_{mt}$ is the rate of return on the market portfolio at time $t$. The alphas are the deviations of securities’ sample means from the SML means. In the univariate and multivariate time-series tests, the intercept and slope of the SML in average excess return — beta space are zero and the average excess return on the market, i.e., $\mu_m^s - \bar{r}_f = \mu_m^e - \bar{r}_f$, where $\mu_m^s$ is the sample mean rate of return of the market portfolio. The alphas may be tested individually or together. The null hypothesis is that the alphas are equal to zero.

# 47.3.2 Gibbons, Ross and Shanken and Hotelling’s T² multivariate tests

The GRS multivariate test of whether the alphas are jointly equal to zero is

$$J = \frac{T - n - 1}{n} \left(1 + \frac{(\mu_m^s - r_f)^2}{(\sigma_m)^2}\right)^{-1} \alpha'\Omega^{-1}\alpha, \qquad (47.5)$$

where $T$ is the number of time-series observations, $n$ is the number of assets, $\alpha$ is an $n$-vector of the alphas from equation (47.4), and $\Omega^{-1}$ is the $n \times n$ inverse of the covariance matrix of the residual returns from equation (47.4). The Sharpe ratio of portfolio $j$ is $Sh_j = (\mu_j - r_f)/\sigma_j$. Therefore, the term in the brackets is one plus the squared Sharpe ratio of the market portfolio. GRS show that an equivalent test can be formulated in mean–standard deviation space as the difference in two-squared Sharpe ratios

$$J = \frac{T - n - 1}{n}(Sh_q^2 - Sh_m^2)/(1 + Sh_m^2), \qquad (47.6)$$

where $m$ is the market portfolio and $q$ is the ex post tangency portfolio constructed from the $n$-assets plus the market portfolio.
