Thus, at any stock price, the value of the portfolio invested in stock is the value of the call C plus the amount of borrowing B minus the amount borrowed B or C. The line CO shows how the value of the insured portfolio reacts to changes in the stock price. As the stock price increases (decreases), the slope of the curved line becomes steeper (flatter) and the amount of borrowing increases (decreases).

If the call is in the money at the expiration date, the investor will own one share in the replicating portfolio and owe an amount equal to the exercise price. If the call finishes out of the money, no stock will be owned and the borrowing will be fully repaid. This is equivalent to the position of the call buyer at expiration. Hence, a purchased call position can be replicated by a strategy of buying shares plus borrowing, where shares are bought (sold)
