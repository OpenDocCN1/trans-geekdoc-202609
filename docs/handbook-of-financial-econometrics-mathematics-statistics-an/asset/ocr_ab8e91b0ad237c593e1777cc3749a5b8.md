Ibboston et al. (1975), Ritter (1984), Guo et al. (2010), and Hu et al. (2013), the methodology of Smith et al. (2018) is able to be applied to estimate the heat of the market without having an idea about what the future performance and issuance might be.

```
NIPO_t = β₀ + β₁ D_Heat + β₂ D_Cold + ε_{i,t},  (67.1)
```

where NIPO_t is the volume experienced in IPOs during time period t. D_Heat is a heat variable which relies on a classification of heat based on the volume of IPOs issued in preceding periods. Helwege et al. (2004) obtained the three-month moving average of IPO volume of the entire sample and then classified the top quartile as ‘hot’ months. Smith et al. (2018) use the trailing 10 and 20 months of IPO volume, in two separate regressions, and segment them into quartiles for which the first quartile is classified as ‘hot’ months. D_Cold is a measure of the coolness of the IPO market. The variable relies on a classification of coolness based on the volume of IPOs issued in preceding periods. Helwege et al. (2004) obtained the three-month moving average of IPO volume of the entire sample and then classified the bottom quartile as ‘cold’ months. Smith et al. (2018) use the trailing 10 and 20 months of IPO volume, in two separate regressions, and segment them into quartiles for which the bottom quartile is classified as ‘cold’ months. β₁ is the beta coefficient associated with hot performance.
