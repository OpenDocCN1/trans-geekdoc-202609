These two papers both apply discrete wavelet transform to capture the time-frequency relations among various assets. Kim and In (2010) analyze the impacts of investment horizon on asset allocation between stocks and bonds. Likewise, In, Kim, and Gençay (2011) examine the portfolio allocation between value and growth stocks over various investment horizons. Their findings both confirm that investment horizons play an important role on the asset allocation decisions. This chapter differs from these two papers conceptually: we apply continuous wavelet transform to examine how the changes in the short-term and long-term correlation structures between gold with other assets affect the optimal weights of gold in a diversified portfolio. In this study, our empirical findings can provide more insight in the role played by gold in a diversified portfolio since continuous wavelet analysis is used here can provide a richer description on risk characteristics and correlation features among assets.

# 4.2 Methodology

The risk characteristics and correlation structure among various asset classes are the important features from the point of view of investors and portfolio managers. Here, wavelet analysis is applied to capture these characteristics among various assets over different time-frequency scales. As indicated above, investors may have different investment horizons due to their different patterns of consumption. Different investors have different portfolio management needs. Here, the impact of investment horizon on the optimal compositions of portfolio is also investigated by comparing the optimal portfolio weights in a minimum variance portfolio over different time-frequency scales.
