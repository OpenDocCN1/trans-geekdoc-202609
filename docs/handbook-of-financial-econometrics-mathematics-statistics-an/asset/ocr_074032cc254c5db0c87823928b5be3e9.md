That is, wavelet analysis presents the characteristics of the local behavior of the function whereas Fourier transform presents the characteristics of the global behavior of the function. The wavelet analysis has been successfully used for many studies in social science during the last decade. Scholars are usually familiar with the use of the discrete wavelet transform and the maximal overlap discrete wavelet transform such as In, Kim, and Gençay (2011) and Baruník, Kočenda, and Vácha (2016). More recently, the continuous wavelet transform is becoming more widely used in financial issue (Graham, Kiviaho, and Nikkinen, 2013; Lin, Chen, and Yang, 2016; Lin et al., 2018). One major benefit of continuous wavelet analysis has over discrete wavelet analysis, as Aguiar-Conraria et al. (2008) and Rua and Nunes (2009) pointed out, is that we need not define the number of wavelets (time-scales) in continuous wavelet analysis which generates itself according to the length of data. This allows us to easily identify and interpret patterns or hidden information. Therefore, continuous wavelet analysis is applied here to evaluate the time and frequency-varying features of assets returns relations.

Typically, the continuous wavelet transform decomposes a time-series in terms of some elementary functions which are derived from a time-localized mother wavelet by translation and dilation. These wavelets result from a mother wavelet, Ψ(t) that can be expressed as a function of the time position τ (translation parameter) and the scale λ (dilation parameter), which is related with the frequency. The wavelet can be defined as

$$\Psi_{\tau,\lambda}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\psi\left(\frac{t-\tau}{\lambda}\right),\tag{4.1}$$

where mother wavelet Ψ(t) is a continuous function in both the time domain and the frequency domain. τ acts to translate the function across the data and λ acts to vary the time scale of the mother wavelet function Ψ. Here, 1/λ is a normalization factor to ensure energy normalization that wavelet transform is comparable across time-frequency scale. Consequently, we have defined in the time domain a sequence of functions that are doubly indexed, once by location in the time domain, once by the scale. To be a mother wavelet, Ψ(t) must satisfy following conditions: it must have zero mean, i.e., ∫ ψ(t)dt = 0, its square integrates to unity, i.e., ∫ ψ²(t)dt = 1 and the so-called admissibility condition.
