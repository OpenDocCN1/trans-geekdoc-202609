In Figures 46.6(a) and 46.6(b), it is observed that the optimal net profit rate φ* increases as the operational rates, μ and ν, increase. Concomitantly, the optimal S* and A* decrease as the operational rates increase, indicating that fewer resources are needed for optimality when the system operates more efficiently. Figures 46.6(c) and 46.6(d) give rise to similar, though not identical, observations for the optimal rate of return ρ*. Here, as the operational rates increase, ρ* increases substantially, while S* and A* decrease slightly. Meanwhile, the values of G* and H* are bounded by the maximal debt-to-equity ratio ℓ, indicating a preference for borrowing.

To further explain the above observations, we carried out two experiments where we held one of the parameters μ and ν constant, and varied the other to elucidate the impact of the latter on the optimal design and associated optimal financial performance. The mean cash conversion cycles corresponding to the optimal designs were also computed from Theorem 46.3 and displayed.

Figure 46.7 studies the optimal net profit rate, φ* (first row), and the optimal rate of return, ρ* (second row), by fixing μ at 0.2 (an intermediate value between medium and high rates) and varying ν in the range of 0.05 (medium rate) to 0.2 (high rate) by increments of 0.01.

Figure 46.7(a) shows that as ν increases, the optimal sizes for funds (A*, G* and H*) decrease gradually, while the optimal inventory base level S* remains unchanged. The former behavior is consistent with the observation that as the receivables payment rate increases, the firm can rely less on its available funds. The latter behavior indicates that as the replenishment rate remains at medium level, the increase of the receivables payment rate does not have observable impact on the optimal inventory base stock level. As expected, Figure 46.7(b) shows that as ν increases, φ* increases and E[TC] decreases. The behavior of E[TC] is dominated by the decrease of the second term, 1/ν, in equation (46.22).

Figure 46.7(c) differs from Figure 46.7(a) in that G* remains at the minimal level 1, and H* is subsequently bounded by the maximal debt-to-equity ratio of 1. An examination of equation (46.30) provides an explanation: since the given financial parameters (η, γ, α and β) are much smaller than λω, ρ is dominated by the first term, λ[1 − π_I(0)] ω/Ḡ, in equation (46.30). Hence, maximizing ρ is roughly tantamount to minimizing G. Finally, Figure 46.7(d) gives rise to observations similar to those of Figure 46.7(b).
