## 82.2 Sharpe Performance-Measure Approach with Short Sales Allowed

In deriving the capital asset pricing model (CAPM), Lintner (1965) suggests a performance-measure approach for determining the efficient frontier. Lintner arrived at this approach through a sequence of logical steps.

Following Markowitz (1952, 1959), the objective function for portfolio selection can be expressed:

Max L = Σ_{i=1}^n W_i \bar{R}_i + λ₁ { [ Σ_{j=1}^n Σ_{i=1}^n W_i W_j Cov(R_i,R_j) ]^{1/2} - σ_p } + λ₂ ( Σ_{i=1}^n W_i - 1 )  (82.1)

where \bar{R}_i is the average rates of return for security i, W_i (or W_j) is the optimal weight for ith (or jth) security, Cov(R_i,R_j) is the covariance between R_i and R_j, σ_p is the standard deviation of a portfolio, and λ₁, λ₂ are the Lagrangian multipliers.

Markowitz portfolio-selection model minimizes the variance given the targeted expected rate of return. Equation (82.1) maximizes the expected rates of return given targeted standard deviation.

If a constant risk-free borrowing and lending rate R_f is subtracted from equation (82.1),

Max L' = Σ_{i=1}^n (W_i \bar{R}_i - R_f) + λ₁ { [ Σ_{i=1}^n Σ_{j=1}^n W_i W_j Cov(R_i,R_j) ]^{1/2} - σ_p } + λ₂ ( Σ_{i=1}^n W_i - 1 )  (82.2a)

Equations (82.1) and (82.2a), both formulated as a constrained maximization problem, can be used to obtain optimum portfolio weights W_i (i = 1,2,...,n). Since R_f is a constant, the optimum weights obtained
from equation (82.1) will be equal to those obtained for equation (82.2a). It can be shown that equation (82.2a) can be replaced by a non-constrained maximization method as follows. Incorporating the constant ∑ᵢ₌₁ⁿ Wᵢ − 1 into the objective function by substituting

$R_f = (1)R_f = \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} W_i\right)R_f = \sum_{i=1}^{n} W_iR_f$

into equation (82.2a),

$\operatorname{Max} L' = \sum_{i=1}^{n} W_i(\bar{R}_i - R_f) + \lambda_1\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n} W_iW_j\operatorname{Cov}(R_i,R_j)\right)^{1/2} - \sigma_p\right]$.  (82.2b)

A two-Lagrangian multiplier problem has been reduced to a one-Lagrangian problem, as indicated in equation (82.2b). By using a special property of the relationship between $\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\bar{R}_i-R_f)$ and $\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_iW_j\operatorname{Cov}(R_i,R_j)\right)^{1/2}$, the constrained optimization of equation (82.2b) can be reduced to an unconstrained optimization problem, as indicated in equation (82.3)

$\operatorname{Max}\ L = \dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_i(\bar{R}_i-R_f)}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_i^2\sigma_i^2+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_iW_j\sigma_{ij}\right)^{1/2}}\quad(i\ne j),$  (82.3)

where $\sigma_{ij}=\operatorname{Cov}(R_i,R_j)$. Alternatively, the objective function of equation (82.3) can be developed as follows. This ratio $L$ is equal to excess average rates of return for the ith portfolio divided by the standard deviation of the ith portfolio. This is a *Sharpe performance measure.*

Following Sharpe (1964) and Lintner (1965), if there is a risk-free lending and borrowing rate ($R_f$) and short sales are allowed, then the efficient frontier (efficient set) will be linear. ## 82.3 Sharpe Performance-Measure Approach with Short Sales and Upper Bound Constraints

We further present the approach to maximize Sharpe measure with short-sales and upper bound constraints. De Roon et al. (2001) and Li et al. (2003) suggest that the short-sale constraints to be considered in the portfolios of developing countries. However, the constraints should not be ignored in the portfolio analysis of developed economies because the change in the regulation, such as during the 2008 financial crisis in Australia and the US, may restrict investors to short sell stocks. We further consider the issue of concentration in small amount of assets, which leads to illiquidity and triggers significant changes in volatility and correlations of assets. We take into account issues in portfolio weights to enhance feasibility of strategies. Green and Hollifield (1992) question the usefulness of corner solutions of the non-constraint diversification. Jagannathan and Ma (2003) evaluate portfolio bounds from the angle of risk control and document that weight constraints decrease volatility in portfolio return and increase feasibility of asset allocation. The optimal portfolio can be more practical by setting the upper bounds of weights not larger than a certain percentage ($U$).

$$0 \le W_i \le U, \quad \text{for} \quad i = 1,2,\dots,n. \tag{82.8}$$
