It is noted that the moment sequence {$\mu'_r$} does not belong only to log-normal distribution. Thus, the log-normal distribution cannot be defined by its moments.

The CDF of $X$ is

$P(X \le x) = P(\log X \le \log x) = N\left(\frac{\log x - \mu}{\sigma}\right), \quad (85.11)$

because $\log X$ is normally distributed with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$.

The distribution of $X$ is unimodal with the mode at

$\operatorname{mod} e(X) = e^{(\mu - \sigma^2)}. \quad (85.12)$

Let $x_\alpha$ be the $(100)\alpha$ percentile for the log-normal distribution and $z_\alpha$ be the corresponding percentile for the standard normal, then

$P(X \le x_\alpha) = P\left(\frac{\log X - \mu}{\sigma} \le \frac{\log x_\alpha - \mu}{\sigma}\right) = N\left(\frac{\log x_\alpha - \mu}{\sigma}\right). \quad (85.13)$

Thus $z_\alpha = \frac{\log x_\alpha - \mu}{\sigma}$, implying that $x_\alpha = e^{\mu + \sigma z_\alpha}. \quad (85.14)$ Thus, the percentile of the log-normal distribution can be obtained from the percentile of the standard normal. From equation (85.13), we also see that $\operatorname{median}(X) = e^{\mu}, \quad (85.15)$ as $z_{0.5}=0$. Thus, $\operatorname{median}(X) > \operatorname{mod} e(X)$. Hence, the log-normal distribution is not symmetric.
