where $S = E(X_{t+1} - x_{t+1})(X_{t+1} - x_{t+1})' =$ the covariance matrix of returns of risky securities. Maximization of the expected utility of $W_{t+1}$ is equivalent to

Max $w_{t+1} - \frac{b}{2}V(W_{t+1})$.
(122.67)

By substituting equations (122.65) and (122.66) into equation (122.67), equation (122.67) can be rewritten as

Max$(1 + r^*)W_t + q_{t+1}'(x_{t+1} - r \times P_t) - \left(\frac{b}{2}\right) q_{t+1}' S q_{t+1}$.
(122.68)

Differentiating equation (122.68), one can solve for the optimal portfolio as

$q_{t+1} = b^{-1} S^{-1}(x_{t+1} - r \times P_t)$.
(122.69)

Under the assumption of homogeneous expectation, or by assuming that all the investors have the same probability belief about future return, the aggregate demand for risky securities can be summed as

$Q_{t+1} = \sum_{k=1}^{m} q_{t+1}^k = c S^{-1}[E_t P_{t+1} - (1 + r^*)P_t + E_t D_{t+1}]$,
(122.70)

where $c = \Sigma (b^k)^{-1}$.

In the standard CAPM, the supply of securities is fixed, denoted as $Q^*$. Then, equation (122.70) can be rearranged as $P_t = (1/r^*)(x_{t+1} - c^{-1} S Q^*)$, where $c^{-1}$ is the market price of risk. In fact, this equation is similar to the Lintner's (1965) well-known equation in capital asset pricing.
