respectively (e.g., see Fisher, 1936; Johnson and Wichern, 2002). Usually, it is assumed that the $K$ populations have a common variance-covariance matrix $\Sigma$. In FDA, a set of $r$ linear combinations of the observable variables $\boldsymbol{X} = (X_1, \dots, X_p)'$, or discriminant functions $D_1 = \boldsymbol{\theta}_1' \boldsymbol{X}, \dots, D_r = \boldsymbol{\theta}_r' \boldsymbol{X}, r \le \min(K-1,p)$, where the coefficient vectors $\boldsymbol{\theta}_1 = \Sigma^{-1/2} \boldsymbol{\xi}_1, \dots, \boldsymbol{\theta}_r = \Sigma^{-1/2} \boldsymbol{\xi}_r$ are determined sequentially as follows. Given the first $i-1$ coefficient vectors $\boldsymbol{\theta}_1, \dots, \boldsymbol{\theta}_{i-1}, i \le r$, the $i$th coefficient vector $\boldsymbol{\theta}_i$ is obtained by choosing $\boldsymbol{\xi}_i$ that maximizes the ratio comparing the variability between the groups to that within the groups subject to the constraints $\boldsymbol{\xi}_i' \boldsymbol{\xi}_i = 1$ and $\boldsymbol{\xi}_i' \boldsymbol{\xi}_j = 0$ for all $j < i$. That is, the vector $\boldsymbol{\xi}_i$ maximizes the objective function

$g_1(\boldsymbol{\xi}_i) = \boldsymbol{\xi}_i' A \boldsymbol{\xi}_i, \qquad (57.1)$

subject to the constraints $\boldsymbol{\xi}_i' \boldsymbol{\xi}_i = 1$ and $\boldsymbol{\xi}_i' \boldsymbol{\xi}_j = 0$ for all $j < i$, where the $p \times p$ positive-definite matrix

$A = \Sigma^{-1/2} \left( \sum_{j=1}^K w_j (\boldsymbol{\mu}_j - \bar{\boldsymbol{\mu}})(\boldsymbol{\mu}_j - \bar{\boldsymbol{\mu}})' \right)^{-1/2} \Sigma, \qquad (57.2)$

Here, $\bar{\boldsymbol{\mu}} = \sum_{i=1}^k w_i \boldsymbol{\mu}_i$ is the overall mean and $\Sigma$ is the common $p \times p$ variance-covariance matrix of the $K$ populations. By maximizing the objective function $g_1$, the $K$ population means of the $r$ discriminant functions $D_1 = \theta_1' \boldsymbol{X}, \dots, D_r = \theta_r' \boldsymbol{X}, r \le \min(K-1,p)$, are separated as much as possible so that the chance of misclassification of obligors is reduced and thereby the discriminant power is maximized (Ignizio and Cavalier, 1994).
