
Recall the Taylor series expansion approximating complex functions from calculus (Lee et al., 2009, Appendix 5B), which can be mathematically written as follows:

$$F_n(x) = F(a) + F'(a)(x - a) + \frac{F''(a)}{2!}(x - a)^2 + \cdots + \frac{F^{(n)}(a)}{n!}(x - a)^n, \tag{106.55}$$

where

- $F_n(x)$ is the function we are approximating;
- $F'(a)$ is the first derivative of the function;
- $F^{(n)}(a)$ is the $n$th derivative of the function;
- $n!$ is the factorial value of $n$, i.e., $n! = (n)(n - 1) \cdots (2)(1)$;
- $a$ is the value near which we are making the approximation to the function $F(x)$.

Let $L' = \ln(S'/K)/\sigma\sqrt{T}$. Here, we apply the Taylor series expansion to both cumulative normal distributions in the Black–Scholes formula at $L'$.

Then we have

$$N(L' + \sigma\sqrt{T}/2) = N(L') + N'(L')(L' + \sigma\sqrt{T}/2 - L') + N''(L')\frac{(L' + \sigma\sqrt{T}/2 - L')^2}{2!} + \cdots = N(L') + N'(L')\sigma\sqrt{T}/2 + N''(L')(\sigma\sqrt{T}/2)^2/2 + e_1 = N(L') + N'(L')(\sigma\sqrt{T}/2)[1 - \ln(S'/K)/4] + e_1 \tag{106.56}$$

and

$$N(L' - \sigma\sqrt{T}/2) = N(L') + N'(L')(L' - \sigma\sqrt{T}/2 - L') + N''(L')\frac{(L' - \sigma\sqrt{T}/2 - L')^2}{2!} + \cdots = N(L') - N'(L')\sigma\sqrt{T}/2 + N''(L')(\sigma\sqrt{T}/2)^2/2 + e_2 = N(L') - N'(L')(\sigma\sqrt{T}/2)[1 + \ln(S'/K)/4] + e_2. \tag{106.57}$$

where $e_1$ and $e_2$ are the remainder terms of Taylor's formulas. The above equations can be obtained by the fact that $N''(x) = -N'(x)x$.

Given $N(0)=1/2$, $N'(0)=1/\sqrt{2\pi}$, $N'''(0)=-N'(0)$, $N''(0)=N''''(0)=0$, we expand $N(L')$ and $N'(L')$ at 0 respectively:

$$N(L') = N(0) + N'(0)L' + N''(0)L'^2/2 + e_3$$
$$= \frac{1}{2} + L'/\sqrt{2\pi} + e_3, \tag{106.58}$$
$$N'(L') = N'(0) + N''(0)L' + N'''(0)L'^2/2 + e_4$$
$$= 1/\sqrt{2\pi} - L'^2/2\sqrt{2\pi} + e_4. \tag{106.59}$$

Substituting equations (106.56)–(106.59) into equation (106.54), dropping all of remainder terms, equation (106.54) becomes

$$\begin{aligned}
C &= (S' - K)/2 + \frac{\ln(S'/K)}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi T}}\left[(S' - K)\left(1 + \left(\frac{\ln(S'/K)}{4}\right)^2\right) - \frac{\ln(S'/K)(S' + K)}{4}\right] \\
&\quad + \frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}\left[S' + K - \frac{\ln(S'/K)(S' - K)}{4}\right].
\end{aligned} \tag{106.60}$$

Equation (106.60) is a quadratic equation of $\sigma\sqrt{T}$ and can be rewritten as

$$\sigma^2 T[8(S' + K) - 2(S' - K)\ln(S'/K)] - 8\sigma\sqrt{T}\sqrt{2\pi}(2C - S' + K) + \ln(S'/K)[(S' - K)(16 + (\ln(S'/K))^2) - 4(S' + K)\ln(S'/K)] = 0. \tag{106.61}$$

Solving $\sigma\sqrt{T}$ from equation (106.61) yields

$$\sigma\sqrt{T} = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}, \tag{106.62}$$

where
$$a = 8(S' + K) - 2(S' - K)\ln(S'/K),$$
$$b = -8\sqrt{2\pi}(2C - S' + K),$$
$$c = \ln(S'/K)[(S' - K)(16 + (\ln(S'/K))^2)] - 4(S' + K)\ln(S'/K).$$

A merit of equation (106.62) is to circumvent the ad hoc substitution present in Corrado and Miller (1996) and improve the accuracy of the ISD's estimation. Other methods to calculate the implied volatility can be found in Lai et al. (1992).

According to Lee et al. (2013), put–call parity can be defined in equation (106.63), and we can calculate implied volatility, stock price per share, and exercise price per share in terms of put option model:

$$P = C + X e^{-rT} - S e^{-qT}. \tag{106.63}$$

Let $X e^{-rT} = K$ and let $S e^{-qT} = S'$, then we have following equation:

$$P = C + K - S'. \tag{106.64}$$

Substituting equation (106.60) into equation (106.64), we obtain following equation:

$$P = (K - S')/2 + \frac{\ln(S'/K)}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi T}}\left[(S' - K)\left(1 + \left(\frac{\ln(S'/K)}{4}\right)^2\right) - \ln(S'/K)(S' + K)/4\right] + \frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{2\sqrt{2\pi}} \times [S' + K - \ln(S'/K)(S' - K)/4]. \tag{106.65}$$

Equation (106.65) is also a quadratic equation of $\sigma\sqrt{T}$ and can be rewritten as

$$\sigma^2 T[8(S' + K) - 2(S' - K)\ln(S'/K)] - 8\sigma\sqrt{T}\sqrt{2\pi}(2P - K + S') + \ln(S'/K)[(S' - K)(16 + (\ln(S'/K))^2) - 4(S' + K)\ln(S'/K)] = 0. \tag{106.66}$$

Solving $\sigma\sqrt{T}$ from equation (106.66) yields

$$\sigma\sqrt{T} = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}, \tag{106.67}$$

where $a = 8(S' + K) - 2(S' - K)\ln(S'/K)$, $b = -8\sqrt{2\pi}(2P - K + S')$, $c = \ln(S'/K)[(S' - K)(16 + (\ln(S'/K))^2)] - 4(S' + K)\ln(S'/K)$.

We rearrange equation (106.61) in terms of $S'$, then we obtain equation (106.68):

$$[8\sigma^2 T + 8\sigma\sqrt{2\pi T} + 2\sigma^2 T \ln(S'/K) - 16 \ln(S'/K) - 4(\ln(S'/K))^2 - (\ln(S'/K))^3]S' + [2\sigma^2 T K - 16K + 8K \ln(S'/K) - 3K(\ln(S'/K))^2]\ln S' + [16 - 2\sigma^2 T + 8 \ln(S'/K) + 3(\ln(S'/K))^2]S'\ln S' + (3K \ln(S'/K) - 4K)(\ln S')^2 - (3 \ln(S'/K) + 4)S'(\ln S')^2 - K(\ln S')^3 + S'(\ln S')^3 = 16C\sigma\sqrt{2\pi T} + 8\sigma\sqrt{2\pi T}K - 8\sigma^2 T K + 2\sigma^2 T K \ln(S'/K) - 16K \ln(S'/K) + 4K(\ln(S'/K))^2 - K(\ln(S'/K))^3. \tag{106.68}$$

Equation (106.68) can be used to estimate $S'$ if we have the information of the other five variables. The solution of $S'$ can only be obtained by the trial-and-error method.

Now, consider two call options, $C_1$ and $C_2$, on the same time to the maturity with exercise prices of $X_1$ and $X_2$. $q$ is an annual dividend yield, $S$ is the underlying asset value, and we denote $S' = S e^{-qT}$. We also denote the present values of the exercise prices $K_1 = X_1 e^{-rT}$ and $K_2 = X_2 e^{-rT}$, respectively.

For $C_1$, we apply Taylor's expansion to equation (106.54) at $K_2$. This yields the following equation:

$$C_1 = C_2 - N\!\left(\frac{\ln(S'/K_2)}{\sigma\sqrt{T}} - \frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{2}\right)(K_1 - K_2) + \varepsilon_1, \tag{106.69}$$

where $\varepsilon_1$ is the remainder term of Taylor's formula.

Similarly, for $C_2$, we apply Taylor's expansion to equation (106.54) at $K_1$, which yields the following equation:

$$C_2 = C_1 - N\!\left(\frac{\ln(S'/K_1)}{\sigma\sqrt{T}} - \frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{2}\right)(K_2 - K_1) + \varepsilon_2, \tag{106.70}$$

where $\varepsilon_2$ is the remainder term of Taylor's formula.

Rearranging the equations, dividing both sides by $(K_2 - K_1)$, then applying the inverse function of cumulative normal function on both sides, we have the following two equations:

$$N^{-1}\!\left[\frac{C_1 - C_2}{K_2 - K_1}\right] = \frac{\ln(S'/K_1)}{\sigma\sqrt{T}} - \frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{2} + \eta_1, \tag{106.71}$$

$$N^{-1}\!\left[\frac{C_1 - C_2}{K_2 - K_1}\right] = \frac{\ln(S'/K_2)}{\sigma\sqrt{T}} - \frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{2} + \eta_2. \tag{106.72}$$

Combining the two above equations, the effect of remainder terms may be partially offset. Then we get the quadratic equation of $\sigma\sqrt{T}$ as follows:

$$\sigma^2 T + 2N^{-1}\!\left[\frac{C_1 - C_2}{K_2 - K_1}\right](\sigma\sqrt{T}) - \ln(S'/K_1) - \ln(S'/K_2) = 0. \tag{106.73}$$

Then we can solve implied volatility as

$$\sigma\sqrt{T} = -N^{-1}\!\left[\frac{C_1 - C_2}{K_2 - K_1}\right] \pm \sqrt{\left[N^{-1}\!\left[\frac{C_1 - C_2}{K_2 - K_1}\right]\right]^2 + \ln\!\left(\frac{S'^2}{K_1K_2}\right)}. \tag{106.74}$$

Similarly, we consider two put options $P_1$ and $P_2$ on the same time to the maturity with exercise prices of $X_1$ and $X_2$, $q$ is the annual dividend yield, $S$ is the underlying asset value, we denote $S' = S e^{-qT}$. We also denote the present values of the exercise prices $K_1 = X_1 e^{-rT}$ and $K_2 = X_2 e^{-rT}$, respectively.

## Implied Variance Estimates for Black-Scholes and CEV OPM

According to put–call parity defined in equation (106.64), we have the following equations:

$$P_1 = C_1 + K_1 - S',$$

$$P_2 = C_2 + K_2 - S'.$$

If we substitute the above equations into equations (106.69) and (106.70), then we have the following equations:

$$P_1 = P_2 + (K_1 - K_2) - N\!\left(\frac{\ln(S'/K_2)}{\sigma\sqrt{T}} - \frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{2}\right)(K_1 - K_2) + \delta_1,$$

$$P_2 = P_1 + (K_2 - K_1) - N\!\left(\frac{\ln(S'/K_1)}{\sigma\sqrt{T}} - \frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{2}\right)(K_2 - K_1) + \delta_2.$$

Rearranging the equations, dividing both sides by $(K_2 - K_1)$, and then applying the inverse function of cumulative normal function on both sides, we have the following two equations:

$$N^{-1}\!\left[\frac{P_1 - P_2}{K_2 - K_1} + 1\right] = \frac{\ln(S'/K_2)}{\sigma\sqrt{T}} - \frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{2} + \gamma_1,$$

$$N^{-1}\!\left[\frac{P_1 - P_2}{K_2 - K_1} + 1\right] = \frac{\ln(S'/K_1)}{\sigma\sqrt{T}} - \frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{2} + \gamma_2.$$

By combining the two above equations, the effect of the remaining terms may be partially offset. Then we get the quadratic equation of $\sigma\sqrt{T}$ as follows:

$$\sigma^2 T + 2N^{-1}\!\left[\frac{P_1 - P_2}{K_2 - K_1} + 1\right](\sigma\sqrt{T}) - \ln(S'/K_1) - \ln(S'/K_2) = 0.$$

Solving the equation for $\sigma\sqrt{T}$, we obtain

$$\sigma\sqrt{T} = -N^{-1}\!\left[\frac{P_1 - P_2}{K_2 - K_1} + 1\right] \pm \sqrt{\left[N^{-1}\!\left[\frac{P_1 - P_2}{K_2 - K_1} + 1\right]\right]^2 + \ln\!\left(\frac{S'^2}{K_1K_2}\right)}.$$
