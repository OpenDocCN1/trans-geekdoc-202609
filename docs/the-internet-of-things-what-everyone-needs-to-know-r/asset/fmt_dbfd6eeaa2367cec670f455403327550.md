
The failure of self-governance is not a foregone conclusion, though. Indeed, some argue that there is not, in fact, a market failure in cybersecurity despite the continued prevalence of data breaches without much in the way of penalties or corrective action.[^(41)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-513) Others point to the lack of sustained stock price dips following breaches, or penalties for repurposing collected data, as examples of the market failing to take account of lax cybersecurity.[^(42)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-514) Much depends on the type of community in question, along with its capacity for effective communication, conflict resolution, norm building, and enforcement of collective rules as are laid out in the Ostrom Design Principles.[^(43)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-515) The varieties of these types of online communities abound—within the IoT context and more generally—as does their potential for successful self-regulation, which depends in large part on the size and scope of the group of IoT manufacturers under consideration.[^(44)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-516) In some of these communities, such as eBay or Facebook—which Professor Murray describes as “Lockean” because users have given over power to a central administrator—some degree of democratic governance can coexist with an established authority such as by empowering users to police and report errant behavior.[^(45)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-517) This state of affairs may be compared to so-called Rousseauean communities in which power remains decentralized; this continues to be the current state of affairs in much of the IoT context.[^(46)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-518) However, such groupings are often ineffective owing to their sheer size and complexity.[^(47)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-519) If, however, such communities could increase collaboration by taking the example of the Internet Engineering Task Force working groups or the EU’s General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and create industry codes of conduct, then power may not have to be centralized to the degree that it is in Lockean communities such as Facebook. This may be accomplished through forming micro communities to build trust such as in the financial services joint Security Operations Center.

Polycentric theorists, including Professor Elinor Ostrom, have extolled the benefits of small, self-organized communities in the context of managing common resources,[^(48)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-520) and anthropological evidence confirms that groups of humans function more efficiently along certain metrics when they are kept relatively small in scale.[^(49)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-521) However, micro-communities can ignore other interests, stakeholders, and the wider impact of their actions.[^(50)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-522) To overcome such apathy, these communities must have a defined stake in the outcome to effectuate good governance, which can be accomplished by educating users about the cyber threat and their power to help manage it.[^(51)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-523)

The IoT comprises both types of communities, but a hybrid model favoring organic, bottom-up governance composed of small cohorts with a role for centralized coordination that can codify and enforce best practices as well as protect against free riders may be most appropriate to enhance IoT security.[^(52)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-524) Such self-regulation has the flexibility “to adapt to rapid technological progress”[^(53)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-525) arguably better and faster than black letter law, which often changes incrementally. It also has the potential to be relatively more efficient and cost-effective than alternative one-sized-fits-all approaches while instilling civic virtue.[^(54)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-526) Nevertheless, it is only one piece of a very big and complex puzzle, which is why self-governance is but a single component of polycentric governance.[^(55)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-527) Yet as Professor Murray argues, that does not make it any less vital: “In cyberspace the power to decide is, it seems, vested ultimately in the community. We have the power to control our destiny.”[^(56)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-528)

One organization that is trying to create such a community is `Consumer Reports`, which has been at the forefront of testing consumer products and advocating for improvements in their safety since it was founded 1936.[^(57)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-529) More recently, in March 2017 `Consumer Reports` launched its Digital Standard, which is designed “to measure the privacy and security of products, apps, and services . . . \[to\] put consumers in the driver’s seat as the digital marketplace evolves.”[^(58)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-530) Once it matures, the Digital Standard will empower consumers to be able to select products—including in the IoT context—that meet rigorous privacy and security requirements. Over time, the Standard holds the promise of helping the market function more efficiently. It does so by rewarding those firms that take cybersecurity and data privacy seriously, and penalizing those that do not through lower scores and, as a result, less revenue. Already, these efforts are having an impact, such as when they helped expose privacy risks in the pregnancy and fertility app Glow.[^(59)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-531) As the Digital Standard is continually refined and globalized, it will likely further impact the trajectory and rate of global IoT privacy and security standards.[^(60)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-532) `Consumer Reports` is reportedly working with European colleagues to harmonize its Standard, which could lead to further norm-building efforts around cybersecurity due diligence in the IoT context.[^(61)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-533) However, obstacles remain, given the diverging regulatory stances of the United States and the European Union when it comes to IoT governance.[^(62)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-534)

## What is the US approach to regulating the IoT?

The US government has long favored a largely voluntary, sector-specific approach to managing threats to privacy and cybersecurity, unlike other jurisdictions, such as the European Union, discussed next, that prefer more comprehensive measures. IoT governance is no exception to this trend. Overall, federal cybersecurity law is largely unprepared to mitigate security or privacy problems arising in the IoT context.[^(63)](#17_Notes.xhtml#actrade-9780190943813-miscMatter-10-note-535) Governance gaps are all too common, only some of which are being filled by relevant state and international law, as we will see.
