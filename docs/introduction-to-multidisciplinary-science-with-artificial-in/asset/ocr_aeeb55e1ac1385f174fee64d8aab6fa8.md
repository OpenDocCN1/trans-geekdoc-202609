Planck's distribution function of photons, as predicted in quantum mechanics, the specific intensity can be written as (Planck, 1897):

I(λ,T) = 2hc²/λ⁵ n_photon = 2hc² / [λ⁵ (exp(hc/(λk_B T)) - 1)] = a₁ / [π λ⁵ (exp(a₂/(λT)) - 1)] , (2.72)

with

n_photon = 1 / [exp(hc/(λk_B T)) - 1] , (2.73)

where n_photons, which is Planck's distribution, provides the average number of photons in a single wavelength (or frequency), c is the speed of light, h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s = 4.136 × 10⁻¹⁵ eV·s is the Planck constant, k_B is the Boltzmann constant, a₁ = 2πhc² = 3.74 × 10⁻¹⁶ W/m⁻², and a₂ = hc/k = 1.44 × 10⁻² m K.
