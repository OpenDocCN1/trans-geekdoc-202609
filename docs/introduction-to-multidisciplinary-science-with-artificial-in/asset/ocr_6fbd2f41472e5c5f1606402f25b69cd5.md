This result leads to the common definition of an effective temperature of stars:

$T_{\text{eff}} = \left( \frac{L}{4\pi\sigma R^2} \right)^{1/4}$  (2.80)

Other important related quantities to the Planck blackbody function are energy intensity (i.e., the energy density of photons),

$U(T) = \frac{4\pi}{c} E(T)$ ,  (2.81)

and the radiation pressure,

$P(T) = \frac{4\pi}{3c} E(T) = a T^4$ ,  (2.82)

where $a = 4/3c$. Note also that $P(T) = U(T)/3$ is valid whenever $I(\lambda,T)$ is isotropic, regardless of whether we have blackbody radiation.
