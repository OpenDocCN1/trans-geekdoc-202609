# Box 6.3: Equations of States (EoS)

Equation state relates ρ, T and p as p = p(ρ,T). Most astrophysical fluids can be roughly approximated as ideal gases. The corresponding EoS is

p = n k_B T = \frac{k_B}{\mu m_H} \rho T = \frac{\mathcal{R}}{\mu} \rho T , (6.154)

where μ is the mean particle mass in units of the proton mass m_H. Fluids for which p is only a function of ρ are called barotropic fluids. In the isothermal case, T is constant so that p ∝ ρ. In the adiabatic case, ideal gas undergoes reversible thermodynamic changes such that p = K ρ^γ, where K and γ are constants. In many circumstances, stars behave as polytropes, e.g. fully convective stars with p-ρ relation close to the adiabatic relation. In such a star, assuming monatomic gas with γ = 5/3, we have p = K ρ^{5/3}.
