In the Fourier domain, this expression becomes

P(x, t) = ε₀ χ^(e;1)(ω) E(x, ω) + ε₀ ∫_{-∞}^{+∞} χ^(e;2)(ω - ω₂, ω₂) E(x, ω - ω₂) E(x, ω₂) dω₂ + ε₀ ∫∫_{-∞}^{+∞} χ^(e;3)(ω - ω₂ - ω₃, ω₂, ω₃) E(x, ω - ω₂ - ω₃) E(x, ω₂) E(x, ω₃) dω₂ dω₃ + ... (3.68)

The frequency of the nonlinear response is always the sum of the frequencies of the input fields.

# An example of nonlinear wave equation.

We here start from Maxwell’s equations in Table 3.1. Taking the curl of Faraday’s law leads to

∇ × ∇ × E = - 1/c₀ ∂/∂t (∇ × B) , (3.69)

where c₀² = 1/(ε₀ μ₀). Inserting Ampere’s law into this equation yields,

∇ × ∇ × E + 1/c₀² ∂²E/∂t² = - μ₀ ∂²P/∂t² , (3.70)

or after breaking P as linear and nonlinear electric polarizations, P = P_L + P_NL, we arrive at

∇ × ∇ × E + ε^(1)/c₀² ∂²E/∂t² = - μ₀ ∂²P_NL/∂t² , (3.71)
