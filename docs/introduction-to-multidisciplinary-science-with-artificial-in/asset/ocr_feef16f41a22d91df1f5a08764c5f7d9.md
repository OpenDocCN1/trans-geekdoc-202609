$-i\omega\tilde{\tau}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf{x},\omega)=\frac{\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2\varepsilon_3}{\varepsilon_\beta\varepsilon_\eta}c_{\alpha\beta\xi\eta}(\mathbf{x})\frac{\partial v_\zeta(\mathbf{x},\omega)}{\partial x_\eta}$ , (A.109)

with

$\tilde{\tau}_{ab}(\mathbf{x},\omega)=\frac{\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2\varepsilon_3}{\varepsilon_b}\tau_{ab}(\mathbf{x},\omega)$ . (A.110)

Note that the tensor $\tilde{\tau}$ is nonsymmetric (i.e., $\tilde{\tau}_{ab}\neq\tilde{\tau}_{ba}$). We can also notice that $\tilde{\tau}=\tau$ across the boundary between the non-PML region and the PML region, which means that $\tilde{\tau}$ is just a continuation of $\tau$ in the PML region. Therefore, we can replace $\tilde{\tau}$ by $\tau$ in equations (A.108)-(A.109) and arrive at the following PML equations:

$-i\omega v_a(\mathbf{x},\omega)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2\varepsilon_3}\sigma(\mathbf{x})\frac{\partial\tau_{ab}(\mathbf{x},\omega)}{\partial x_b}$ (A.111)

$-i\omega\tau_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf{x},\omega)=\frac{\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2\varepsilon_3}{\varepsilon_\beta\varepsilon_\eta}c_{\alpha\beta\xi\eta}(\mathbf{x})\frac{\partial v_\zeta(\mathbf{x},\omega)}{\partial x_\eta}$ . (A.112)

Because our finite-difference modeling is carried out in the time domain, we have to transform equations (A.111)-(A.112) back to the time domain by taking their inverse Fourier transforms with respect to time. Due to the frequency dependence of the coefficients $\varepsilon_j=\varepsilon_j(x_j,\omega)$, time-convolution operators will appear in the resulting equations. These time-convolution operators must be eliminated or modified to accommodate for the fact that our finite-difference solution here is recursive in time (i.e., the computation of the wavefield values at a given time depends explicitly on the wavefield values at earlier times), but the standard time-convolution operations are not recursive. They are summations over time. One way to eliminate the time-convolution operators from the PML equations is to factorize equations (A.111)-(A.112) into new equations, with only one factor $\varepsilon_b$ per equation. To illustrate this approach, let us consider the following expression:

$w_{ab}(\mathbf{x},\omega)=\varepsilon_b(x_b,\omega)v_a(\mathbf{x},\omega)=\left[1+i\frac{d_b(x_b)}{\omega}\right]v_a(\mathbf{x},\omega)$ , (A.113)

which is a convolution in the time domain. By multiplying this expression by $-i\omega$, we eliminate the convolution operator; i.e.,

$-i\omega w_{ab}(\mathbf{x},\omega)=-i\omega v_a(\mathbf{x},\omega)+d_b(x_b)v_a(\mathbf{x},\omega)$ , (A.114)

which corresponds to

$\frac{\partial w_{ab}(\mathbf{x},t)}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial v_a(\mathbf{x},t)}{\partial t}+d_b(x_b)v_a(\mathbf{x},t)$ . (A.115)

This idea was proposed by Oskooi and Johnson (2011) for the PML solutions of Maxwell's equations. To extend this idea to the elastic case, we need to factorize equations (A.111)-(A.112).
