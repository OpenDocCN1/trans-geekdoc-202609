SP may deflect to the right (negative) or right (positive) of the shale base line, depending on the relative salinities of formation water and mud filtrate; these occur only in porous, permeable beds. Limestone is low in permeability unless porous or fractured. Sandstone deflects left. If the sonde encounters a fluid that is a better conductor than drilling mud (such as salt water), the SP will deflect to the left; if the fluid is a poor conductor (such as fresh water or oil), it will deflect to the right. That is because shale are cation exchangers (i.e., negatively charged) and repel anions such as chlorine ions. As sodium ions migrate from non-invaded zone to borehole, potential is induced. The current then flows through shale from the non-invaded zone of the permeable formation to the borehole. Such case is true for more saline formation water. The magnitude of diffusion potential is usually 20 percent of membrane potential.
