Thus the toroidal-magnetic field inhomogeneity causes the following drifts; i.e.,

```
$$\boldsymbol{v}_d = \frac{mv_{\parallel}^2 + 0.5\,mv_{\perp}^2}{qB_{\mathrm{tor}}}\frac{\boldsymbol{B}_{\mathrm{tor}}\times\nabla B_{\mathrm{tor}}}{B_{\mathrm{tor}}^2}$$ $$= \frac{mv_{\parallel}^2 + 0.5\,mv_{\perp}^2}{qB_{\mathrm{tor}}R}[\boldsymbol{i}_\phi\times(-\boldsymbol{i}_r)] = \frac{mv_{\parallel}^2 + 0.5\,mv_{\perp}^2}{qB_{\mathrm{tor}}R}\boldsymbol{i}_z\ .\qquad(6.68)$$
```

This drift leads to charge separation. Because the sign of drift depends on the sign of the charge $q$, ions and electrons drift parallel to the symmetry axis, in opposite directions, thus leaving their magnetic field line and to an additional vertical electric field. A second drift appears now in the crossed-field arrangement of vertical electric and toroidal magnetic fields. In other words, an $\boldsymbol{E}\times\boldsymbol{B}_{\mathrm{tor}}$ drift appears perpendicular to both $\boldsymbol{E}_{\mathrm{tor}} = -E_{\mathrm{tor}}\boldsymbol{i}_z$ and $\boldsymbol{B}_{\mathrm{tor}} = B_{\mathrm{tor}}\boldsymbol{i}_\phi$ directions. This drift can be expressed as follows:

```
$$\boldsymbol{v}_E = \frac{\boldsymbol{E}_{\mathrm{tor}}\times\boldsymbol{B}_{\mathrm{tor}}}{B_{\mathrm{tor}}^2} = -\frac{E_{\mathrm{tor}}}{B_{\mathrm{tor}}}(\boldsymbol{i}_z\times\boldsymbol{i}_\phi) = -\frac{E_{\mathrm{tor}}}{B_{\mathrm{tor}}}\boldsymbol{i}_r\ .\qquad(6.69)$$
```

It is directed outward and thus causes the plasma torus to expand radially. So the drifts in (6.68), associated with the gradient of $\boldsymbol{B}_{\mathrm{tor}}$, set up an electric field and this electric field results in $\boldsymbol{E}\times\boldsymbol{B}_{\mathrm{tor}}$ drifts, which drive plasma (electrons and ions) to the chamber wall, destroying the confinement.

Let us now turn our attention to the solution to the toroidal confinement problem. We adopt the idea of preventing the charge separation resulting from gradient-B drifts and thereby avoid the $\boldsymbol{E}_{\mathrm{tor}}\times\boldsymbol{B}_{\mathrm{tor}}$ drift. The gradient-B drifts and resulting charge separation can be canceled out by twisting the toroidal field lines to form helices—that is, the twisting of the field lines in Figure 6.23. The basic idea is that the tokamak device includes a poloidal magnetic field along the toroidal magnetic field. The net magnetic field is $\boldsymbol{B}=\boldsymbol{B}_{\mathrm{tor}}+\boldsymbol{B}_{\mathrm{pol}}$, where $\boldsymbol{B}_{\mathrm{pol}}=B_{\mathrm{pol}}\boldsymbol{i}_\theta$ is the poloidal magnetic field. Let us verify that we indeed have that we have spiral magnetic field lines by introducing $\boldsymbol{B}_{\mathrm{pol}}$. The equation of motion of a particle generated by a poloidal magnetic field is:

$$r\frac{d\theta}{dt}=\frac{B_{\mathrm{pol}}}{B_{\mathrm{tor}}}v_\phi=\frac{B_{\mathrm{pol}}}{B_{\mathrm{tor}}}\frac{B_{\mathrm{tor}}}{B}v_{\parallel}=\frac{B_{\mathrm{pol}}}{B_{\mathrm{pol}}}v_{\parallel}\qquad(6.70)$$

and $r=$ constant, where $B^2 = B_{\mathrm{pol}}^2 + B_{\mathrm{tor}}^2$. Now add on to this motion the cross field drift in the $\boldsymbol{i}_z$ direction; i.e.,

$$r\frac{d\theta}{dt}=\frac{B_{\mathrm{pol}}}{B}v_{\parallel}+v_d\cos\theta\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{dr}{dt}=v_d\sin\theta\ .\qquad(6.71)$$

By eliminating time from these two equations we arrive at

$$\frac{dr}{dt}\frac{d\theta}{dt}=\frac{dr}{d\theta}\implies\frac{dr}{r}=\frac{v_d\sin\theta}{(B_{\mathrm{pol}}/B)v_{\parallel}+v_d\cos\theta}d\theta\ .$$

Let us assume $B_\theta$, $B$, $v_{\parallel}$, $v_d$ are constants. By integrating the above orbit equation, we obtain

$$\ln r = -\ln\left|\frac{B_{\mathrm{pol}}}{B}v_{\parallel}+v_d\cos\theta\right|+C\ .\qquad(6.72)$$
