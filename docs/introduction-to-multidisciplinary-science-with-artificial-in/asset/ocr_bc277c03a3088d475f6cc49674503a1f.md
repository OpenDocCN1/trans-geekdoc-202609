# 4.2. Quantum Entanglement: Nature Inseparability

In this section, we introduce the key principles of quantum entanglement. Quantum entanglement, first recognized by Einstein, Podolsky, and Roson (1935) and Schrödinger (1935), is a physical phenomenon in which the quantum states of a many-particle system cannot mathematically be factorized into a Kronecker product of single-particle wave functions, even when the particles are separated by large distances. Entangled states have been produced in laboratories (e.g., Wu and Shaknov, 1950; Freedman and Clauser, 1972; and Aspect et al., 1983) and exploited to test the contradiction between classical local hidden variable theory and quantum mechanics by using Bell's inequality (Bell, 1964). To begin, we introduce the Dirac notation, which is used in most mathematical descriptions of quantum mechanics, including quantum entanglement.
