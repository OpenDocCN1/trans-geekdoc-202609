When we are limited to two dimensions (2D), we have to assume that both electric and magnetic fields have no dependence on one of the three classical Cartesian dimensions x, y, and z—for example, y; i.e., ∂E/∂y = ∂H/∂y = 0 . (3.161)

The fact that E is perpendicular to H means that we have two possible versions of Maxwell's equations known as transverse electric (TE) mode [H = [0, H_y, 0]^T, E = [0, 0, E_y]^T] and transverse magnetic (TM) mode [H = [H_x, 0, H_z]^T, E = [0, E_y, 0]^T]. So in TE mode, all the electric field components are transverse to the direction of propagation. In TM mode there is only an electric field along the propagation direction. Figure 3.31 shows Maxwell's equations corresponding to these two modes.
