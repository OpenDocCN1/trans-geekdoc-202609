Therefore, the proof of the second law is equivalent to showing that dA/dt is constant; which is our task here.

Let us start by introducing a physical quantity known as angular momentum. It measures the quantity of rotation of a rotating body just like linear momentum (simply known as momentum) measures the quantity of motion of a moving body. The angular momentum of a mass m2 with position vector x and velocity v is L = x × (m2 v), where × is the cross product defined in Ikelle (2023b). The derivative of L with respect to time, is

dL/dt = d x / dt × (m2 v) + x × (m2 dv/dt)
= x × (m2 γ) = x × F = 0 . (1.36)

In these derivations we used the fact that the cross product of two parallel vectors is zero. Because F is collinear with x (i.e., the force is along the direction of the line joining the centers of the Sun and the orbiting planet), dL/dt is zero and therefore L = |L| is a constant with respect to time, which means in physics that angular momentum is conserved.
