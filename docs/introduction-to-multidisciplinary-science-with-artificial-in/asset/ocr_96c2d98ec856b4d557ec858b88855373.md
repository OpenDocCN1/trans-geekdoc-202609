where $C_\phi$ is a constant of integration. We can divide the second equality by $\sin^2\theta$ to arrive at

$$
\frac{1}{R} \frac{d}{dr} \left( r^2 \frac{dR}{dr} \right) + \frac{2m}{h^2} r^2 \left[ U + \frac{e^2}{4\pi \epsilon_0 r} \right] = - \frac{1}{\theta} \frac{d}{d\theta} \left( \sin \theta \frac{d\Theta}{d\theta} \right) - \frac{m^2}{\sin^2 \theta} = l(l+1) . 
$$ (4.28)

The first term only depends on the radius $r$, and the second term depends on the angle $\theta, \phi$. The separating constant is $l(l+1)$. We can deduce the second and third Schrödinger equations for hydrogen

$$
\frac{d}{dr} \left( r^2 \frac{dR}{dr} \right) + \frac{2 m_e r^2}{h^2} \left[ U + \frac{e^2}{4\pi \epsilon_0 r} \right] R - l(l+1)R = 0 
$$ (4.29)

and

$$
\sin \theta \frac{d}{d\theta} \left( \sin \theta \frac{d\Theta}{d\theta} \right) + [l(l-1)\sin^2 \theta - m^2] \Theta = 0 \implies \Theta(\theta) = P_l^m (\cos \theta) , 
$$ (4.30)

where again $l$ is a non-negative integer and $m$ is an integer. Equation (4.30) is the well known to have Legendre polynomials with argument $\cos\theta$ which is generally denoted as $P_l^m(\cos\theta)$. The product of $r Y(\theta,\phi) = \Theta(\theta)\Phi(\phi)$ actually represents spherical harmonics; i.e.,

$$
Y(\theta,\phi) = \Theta(\theta)\Phi(\phi) = \sqrt{ \frac{2l+1}{4\pi} \frac{(l-m)!}{(l+m)!} } P_l^m (\cos \theta) \exp [i m \phi] . 
$$ (4.31)

Derivations of the solution of the radial equation in (4.29) are quite lengthy and do not provide any significant educational benefits. So we borrow the results from Liboff (2003) and Sullivan (2012). The radial equation solution can be written as

$$
R_{nl}(r) = \left( \frac{2Z}{na} \right)^{3/2} \sqrt{ \frac{(n-1-l)!}{2n [(n+l)!]} } \left( \frac{2Zr}{na} \right)^l L_{n-l-1}^{2l+1} \left( \frac{2Zr}{na} \right) \exp \left( - \frac{Zr}{na} \right) , 
$$ (4.32)

where

a = \frac{4\pi \epsilon_0 \hbar^2}{m_e e^2} and $L_n^k = \frac{1}{n!} \exp(x) x^{-k} \frac{d^n}{dx^n} [\exp(-x) x^{n+k}]$ (4.33)
are Bohr radius and associated Laguerre functions, respectively. The full normalized hydrogen wave function is

$$ \psi_{nlm}(r,\theta,\phi) = R_{nl}(r) \Theta(\theta) \Phi(\phi) = R_{nl}(r) Y_l^m (\theta, \phi) . $$ (4.34)

So when you solve the Schrödinger equation of hydrogen, the key outcomes are quantum numbers $n$, $l$, and $m$; $n = 1,2,3,...$ is known as the principal quantum number (energy level), $l = 0,1,2,3,...,n-1$ as the angular momentum quantum number, and $m = -l, -l+1, ..., 0, ..., l-1, l$ as the magnetic quantum number.
