where φ is the electrostatic potential, as introduced in Chapter 3. Energy conservation becomes

$\left[ m \frac{d\mathbf{v}}{dt} \right] \cdot \mathbf{v} = (-q \nabla \phi) \cdot \mathbf{v} = -q \frac{d\phi}{dt} \implies \frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{1}{2} m v^2 + q \phi \right) = 0$, (6.19)

thus $\frac{1}{2} m v^2 + q \phi = \text{constant}$. That is, a particle gains kinetic energy $q \phi$ when it falls through a potential drop-φ. For an accelerator system with the electric potential of the particle source, $\phi_s$, and two parallel conductors that carry equal and opposite potential, $\phi_a$, we have

$\frac{1}{2} m v_x^2 + q \phi_s \implies v_x = \sqrt{\frac{-2 q \phi_s}{m}}$ (6.20)

and

$m \frac{d v_z}{dt} = q E_a \implies v_z = \frac{q}{m} E_a t = \frac{q}{m} E_a \frac{x}{v_x}$. (6.21)

where $\mathbf{E} = E_a \mathbf{i}_z$ is the electrostatic field of the accelerator. So we have $\frac{dz}{dt} = v_z$

or

$z = \int_0^t v_z dt' = \frac{q}{m} E_a \int_0^t t' dt' = \frac{q}{m} E_a \frac{t^2}{2} = \frac{q}{m} E_a \frac{x^2}{2 v_x^2}$. (6.22)

Hence the deviation of the particle at the output of the accelerator is

$z_0 = \frac{q}{m} E_a \frac{L^2}{2 v_x^2} = \frac{1}{4} \frac{\phi_a}{\phi_s} \frac{L^2}{d}$, (6.23)

where $d$ is the distance separating the two conductors in the $z$-direction and $L$ is the conductor's length in the $x$-direction. We used the fact that $E_a = -\phi_a / d$ (see Chapter 5).

To summarize, the motion of a charged particle in a uniform magnetic field is the sum of uniform motion, at constant velocity $\mathbf{v}_\parallel$ in a direction parallel to $\mathbf{B}$, and uniform circular motion, with velocity $\mathbf{v}_\perp$ in a plane orthogonal to $\mathbf{B}$. The specific frequency of revolution of circular motion, known as gyrofrequency (or cyclotron frequency), and the specific radius, known as gyroradius (or Larmor radius), depend on the charge and mass of particles and the magnitude of the magnetic field. In other words, charged particles move freely along a constant magnetic field, but any velocity perpendicular to the field causes them to orbit around the magnetic-field lines. This particle motion is often referred to as gyromotion because its path is a helix. The center of gyromotion is known as the guiding center or Larmor center.
