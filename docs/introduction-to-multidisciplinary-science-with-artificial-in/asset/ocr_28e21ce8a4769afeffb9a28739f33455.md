The other reason melting occurs beneath the crust is mantle convection. Mantle convection, which brings heat to the surface, causes hotspots to occur. Heat is delivered to the base of the lithosphere by mantle convection, which brings hot material to shallower depths, thus, releasing pressure, enabling the melting of both the rising material, and the lithospheric rocks that it contacts. The sites where volcanoes form from this sort of deep-seated heat delivery are called hot-spots.
