One of the other major problems that makes space unfriendly to human bodies is the high radiation levels. On Earth, we are protected by the atmosphere and its structure from most of the harmful sun's radiation. In other words, without a strong magnetic field and atmosphere, such as those provided by the Earth, we cannot escape high levels of radiation in other space environments in our solar system. As illustrated in Figure 2.45c, the high radiation is so penetrating that it can reach internal organs, the human's entire body, in a matter of minutes and even seconds because high radiation is accompanied by significant changes in blood perfusion. As we can see in Figure 2.45d, in a matter of seconds, organs are affected and death may occur nearly instantaneously. On the surface of the Moon, for example, the lack of an atmosphere exposes human bodies to radiation without mitigation. On Mars, with a thin atmosphere, only about 1 percent of the Earth (see Ikelle, 2023a), the radiation level is so intense that underground habitats (e.g., caves/lava tubes) are being considered, along with strongly shielded vehicles for excursions on Mars's surface. In fact, harmful radiation is more of a deterrent to human space travel than variations in gravity fields.
