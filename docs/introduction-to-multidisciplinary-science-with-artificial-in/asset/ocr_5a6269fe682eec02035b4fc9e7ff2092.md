where **v**^(0) = 0 because the reference MHD is static. We assume no gravity. Subscript and superscript 1 are used for perturbed quantities. When substituting (6.122) into the MHD equations, the equilibrium terms vanish naturally, and those terms in which a perturbation appears more than once will be relatively small. Hence, we are left with only terms in which a perturbation quantity appears once. In essence the MHD equations have been linearized. We then decompose all perturbed quantities into Fourier modes in the form of exp[i(ωt - **k**·**x**)] (e.g., **v**^(1) = \hat{**v**}^(1) exp[i(ωt - **k**·**x**)]) to arrive at

$$\frac{\partial\rho_1}{\partial t}+\rho_0\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}^{(1)}=0\Longrightarrow -\omega\hat\rho_1+\rho_0\mathbf{k}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{v}}^{(1)}=0\qquad(6.123)$$ $$\rho_0\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(1)}}{\partial t}=-\nabla p_1+\frac{(\nabla\times\mathbf{B}_1)\times\mathbf{B}_0}{\mu_0}$$

or $$-\omega\rho_0\hat{\mathbf{v}}^{(1)}=-\hat p_1\mathbf{k}+\frac{(\mathbf{k}\times\hat{\mathbf{B}}_1)\times\mathbf{B}_0}{\mu_0}\qquad(6.124)$$

$$\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}_1}{\partial t}=\nabla\times(\mathbf{v}^{(1)}\times\mathbf{B}_0)\Longrightarrow -\omega\hat{\mathbf{B}}_1=\mathbf{k}\times\left(\hat{\mathbf{v}}^{(1)}\times\mathbf{B}_0\right)\qquad(6.125)$$ $$\frac{\partial p_1}{\partial t}+\gamma p_0\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}^{(1)}=0\Longrightarrow -\omega\hat p_1+\gamma p_0\mathbf{k}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{v}}^{(1)}=0\ ,\qquad(6.126)$$

and ∇·**B**₁ = 0, where **k** is the wavevector (it is perpendicular to wavefronts). Taking another derivative in time of the momentum equation yields, substituting (6.125) and (6.126) then in the new momentum equation, and assuming that **B**₀ = B₀**i**_z, we arrive at:

$$\frac{\partial^2\mathbf{v}^{(1)}}{\partial t^2}= -\frac{1}{\rho_0}\nabla\left(\frac{\partial p_1}{\partial t}\right)+\frac{1}{\rho_0\mu_0}\left[\nabla\times\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}_1}{\partial t}\right)\times\mathbf{B}_0\right]$$ $$=c_s^2\nabla\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}^{(1)}\right)+c_A^2\left[\nabla\times\left(\nabla\times(\mathbf{v}^{(1)}\times\mathbf{i}_z)\right)\times\mathbf{i}_z\right]\ ,$$ or after taking the time-Fourier transform at $$\omega^2\hat{\mathbf{v}}^{(1)}=c_s^2\left(\mathbf{k}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{v}}^{(1)}\right)\mathbf{k}+c_A^2\left\{\mathbf{k}\times\left[\mathbf{k}\times\left(\hat{\mathbf{v}}^{(1)}\times\mathbf{i}_z\right)\right]\times\mathbf{i}_z\right\}\ ,\qquad(6.127)$$

where c_s = √{γp₀/ρ} is the adiabatic sound speed and c_A = √{B₀²/(μ₀ρ)} is known as the Alfvén speed, in honor of Hannes Alfvén, Swedish scientist who first observed MHD waves. Sound speed and Alfvén speed are the basis of all wave phenomena described by magnetohydrodynamic equations. Other speeds also play an instrumental role, but they can be described in terms of c_s and c_A as we will discuss later.
