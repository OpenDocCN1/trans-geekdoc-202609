with

$$\rho_E(\boldsymbol{x},t)= -\int_{0}^{t} [\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{J}(\boldsymbol{x},t')] dt' \quad\text{and}\quad g(\boldsymbol{x})= -\int_{-\infty}^{0} [\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{J}(\boldsymbol{x},t')] dt', \qquad (3.36)$$

we can now deduce the non-causal version of Gauss’s law. By taking the derivative of (3.36) with respect to time $t$, we can see that the continuity equation in (3.29) is unchanged. In other words, the conservation of charges is unchanged by non-causal source actions.

The non-causal Gauss’s law for the magnetic displacement field is given in (3.34), i.e.,

$$\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{x},t) = f(\boldsymbol{x}) , \qquad (3.37)$$

where

$$f(\boldsymbol{x}) = \int_{-\infty}^{0} \left[ \frac{\partial[\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{x},t')]}{\partial t'} \right] dt' . \qquad (3.38)$$

Basically, in non-causal Maxwell equations, Gauss’s law for magnetism can produce a time-independent magnetic monopole because the total magnetic flux through a closed surface can be nonzero. We can generate the magnetic field using a magnetic monopole and by moving an electric field generated by an electricity charged particle.
