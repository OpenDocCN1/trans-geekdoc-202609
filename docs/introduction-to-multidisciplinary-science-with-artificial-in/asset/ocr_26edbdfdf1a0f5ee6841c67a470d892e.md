By substituting the boundary conditions in (5.8) and the first in (5.7) into (5.9), we obtain (Geselowitz, 1967 and 1970):

φ = 1/(4πσ) ∫_V J^(a) ∇ (1/|x|) dV + Σ_m ∫_{S_m} (σ_m^(+) − σ_m^(-)) φ ∇ (1/|x|) · n_m dS_m. (5.10)

This equation evaluates the electric potential anywhere within the brain.

The current density throughout a volume conductor produces a magnetic field given by the following relationship (Stratton, 1941; Jackson, 1968):

4πH = ∫_V J × ∇ (1/|x|) dV = ∫_V J^(a) × ∇ (1/|x|) dV − ∫_V σ ∇φ × ∇ (1/|x|) dV = ∫_V J^(a) × ∇ (1/|x|) dV − Σ_m ∫_{V_m} σ_m ∇φ × ∇ (1/|x|) dV = ∫_V J^(a) × ∇ (1/|x|) dV − Σ_m ∫_{V_m} σ_m ∇ × [ φ ∇ (1/|x|) ] dV .

In the last equality here, we use the vector identity ∇ × (φ a) = φ ∇ × a + ∇ φ × a, with a = ∇ (1/|x|), and the fact ∇ × ∇ Ψ = 0, where Ψ is an arbitrary function of x; i.e.,

∇ × [ φ ∇ (1/|x|) ] = φ ∇ × ∇ (1/|x|) + ∇ φ × ∇ (1/|x|)

or

∇ × [ φ ∇ (1/|x|) ] = ∇ φ × ∇ (1/|x|) . (5.11)

Finally, applying this divergence theorem (see Appendix A of Ikelle, 2020), we arrive at

4πH = ∫_V J^(a) × ∇ (1/|x|) dV + Σ_m ∫_{S_m} (σ_m^(+) − σ_m^(-)) φ ∇ (1/|x|) × n_m dS_m . (5.12)

This equation describes the magnetic field outside a finite volume conductor (brain) containing internal (electric) volume sources J^(a) and inhomogeneities (σ_m^(+) − σ_m^(-)). Equations (5.10) and (5.12) are the basis of electrophysiological activity and measurements. These equations were first derived by Geselowitz (1970).
