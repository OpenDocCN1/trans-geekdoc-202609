Also, one can define electric charge density in macroscopic Gauss’s law for electric displacement as the sum of the electric charge density of free electrons, ρ_{E,f}, and the electric charge density of bound electrons, ρ_{E,b}; i.e.,

∇ · D = ρ_E ⟺ ε₀ ∇ · E + ∇ · P = ρ_{E,f} + ρ_{E,b} , (3.31)

with ε₀ ∇ · E = ρ_{E,f} and ∇ · P = ρ_{E,b}. Because each particle is treated separately in microscopic scale, the electric charge density of the bound electrons, ρ_{E,b}, associated with the electric polarization is ignored at this scale as P is zero. So, in microscopic scale, Gauss’s law for the electric field is

∇ · E = ρ_{E,f} / ε₀ . (3.32)
