The resting potential is reached when the total membrane current is zero; i.e.,

$I_{\mathrm{Na}^+} + I_{\mathrm{K}^+} + I_{\mathrm{Cl}^-} = 0 \tag{5.2}$

or

$g_{K^+}[\Delta V_{\mathrm{res}} - \Delta V_{K^+}] + g_{Na^+}[\Delta V_{\mathrm{res}} - \Delta V_{Na^+}] + g_{Cl^-}[\Delta V_{\mathrm{res}} - \Delta V_{Cl^-}] = 0 , \tag{5.3}$

where

$g_{Na^+} = \frac{I_{Na^+}}{\Delta V_{\mathrm{res}} - \Delta V_{Na^+}} , \quad g_{K^+} = \frac{I_{K^+}}{\Delta V_{\mathrm{res}} - \Delta V_{K^+}},$

and

$g_{Cl^-} = \frac{I_{Cl^-}}{\Delta V_{\mathrm{res}} - \Delta V_{Cl^-}} , \tag{5.4}$

where $\Delta V_{\mathrm{res}}$ is membrane voltage [mV], $\Delta V_{Na^+}$, $\Delta V_{K^+}$, $\Delta V_{Cl^-}$ are Nernst voltage [mV] for sodium, potassium, and chlorine, respectively, $I_{Na^+}$, $I_{K^+}$, $I_{Cl^-}$ are the electric current carried by sodium, potassium and chlorine (leakage current) per unit area mA/m², respectively, $g_{Na^+}$, $g_{K^+}$, $g_{Cl^-}$ are membrane conductance (1/resistance) per unit area for sodium, potassium, and chlorine, respectively—also referred to as the leakage conductance S/m². We can alternatively express the resting membrane potential in (5.3) as follows:

$\Delta V_{\mathrm{res}} = \frac{g_{K^+}\Delta V_{K^+} + g_{Na^+}\Delta V_{Na^+} + g_{Cl^-}\Delta V_{Cl^-}}{g_{K^+} + g_{Na^+} + g_{Cl^-}} . \tag{5.5}$

Thus, the membrane’s resting potential is dominated by the most permeable ions. In these derivations, we only used the three ions that influence bioelectrical phenomena: K⁺, Na⁺, and Cl⁻. Note that Ca⁺⁺ also contributes to bioelectricity to a few tissues, including the heart.
