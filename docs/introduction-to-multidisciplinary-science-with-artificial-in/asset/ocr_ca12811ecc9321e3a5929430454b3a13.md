By using the definitions in Figure 3.22c, we have

$$z-h=\frac{a}{\tan\theta}\Longrightarrow dz=-\frac{a}{\sin^2\theta}d\theta\tag{3.138}$$

and

$$\frac{a^2}{[a^2+(z-h)^2]^{3/2}}=\frac{\sin^3\theta}{a}\tag{3.139}$$

and integrating (3.137) over the entire length of the solenoid, we obtain

$$\boldsymbol{B}=\boldsymbol{i}_3\frac{\mu_0 n I}{2}\int_{-l/2}^{l/2}\frac{a^2}{[a^2+(z-h)^2]^{3/2}}dz$$

$$= -\boldsymbol{i}_3\frac{\mu_0 n I}{2}\int_{\theta_1}^{\theta_2}\sin\theta\,d\theta=\boldsymbol{i}_3\frac{\mu_0 n I}{2}[\cos\theta_1-\cos\theta_2]\tag{3.140}$$

For an infinite solenoid, $\theta_1=0$ and $\theta_2=\pi$, we obtain

$$\boldsymbol{B}=\boldsymbol{i}_3\mu_0 n I=\boldsymbol{i}_3\frac{\mu_0 N I}{l}\tag{3.141}$$

These results are valid for points inside the solenoid but $|\boldsymbol{B}|=0$ outside the solenoid.

We can also use Ampere’s law in (3.122) to calculate the magnetic field strength inside an ideal infinite solenoid. We choose a rectangular path of length $l$ and width $w$, as illustrated in Figure 3.22d, with one part of the path inside the solenoid and one part of the path outside. The line integral of $\boldsymbol{B}$ along this loop is

$$\oint_C \boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot d\boldsymbol{l}(\boldsymbol{x})=\int_h^i \boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot d\boldsymbol{l}(\boldsymbol{x})=B l\tag{3.142}$$

assuming that the $\boldsymbol{B}$-field is constant. The contributions along paths $ij$ and $kh$ are zero because $\boldsymbol{B}$ is perpendicular to $d\boldsymbol{l}$ along these paths. We assume that the path $jk$ on the outside is sufficiently far from the solenoid, compared to the diameter of the solenoid, that the field is approximately equal to zero for an ideal solenoid, which is infinitely long with turns tightly packed. We can deduce that

$$\oint_C[\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot d\boldsymbol{l}(\boldsymbol{x})]=\mu_0 N I\Longrightarrow B=\frac{\mu_0 N I}{l}\tag{3.143}$$

We used the fact that the total current passing through the area defined by the integration path is $N I$ where $N$ is the number of turns and $I$ is the current in the loop. So the right-hand side of Ampere’s law is $\mu_0 N I$.
