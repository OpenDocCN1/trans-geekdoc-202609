As illustrated in Figure 4.2, particles vary between states. We are most likely to find our particle in the center of the box in the ground state. In the (n = 2)-state, there is zero probability of finding the particle in the center of the box. As n increases the quantum mechanical probability density oscillates more and more. As n nears infinity, the quantum mechanical probability becomes constant and the particle has equal probability to be found at any location within the box.
