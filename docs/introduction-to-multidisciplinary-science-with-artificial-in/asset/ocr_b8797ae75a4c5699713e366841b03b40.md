*As described, in Ikelle (2022a), the surface of Mercury changes in temperature are the most drastic in our entire solar system. Why?*

Because the planet has no atmosphere. Temperatures on the surface of Mercury are both hot and cold. During the day, temperatures on the surface can reach up to 430 degrees Celsius. Because the planet has no atmosphere to retain that heat, nighttime temperatures on the surface can drop to -180 degrees Celsius. These changes in temperature are the most drastic in the entire Solar System.

*White et al. (1997) show that the expected temperature change, ΔT, caused by the 11-year periodic change in solar activity can be described by the following equation:*

ΔT(t) = ΔS / [ρ c_P √(K² + ω_S²)] cos(ω_S t + θ_S) , (B.5)

*where ΔS ≈ 0.2 W/m² describes the change in the sun's constant (nearly all the energy that the Earth receives), ω_S = 2π/T_S = 2π/(11 years) is the angular version of the 11-year period, ρ = 1.0 g/cm³ is the average density of ocean water, K = 1/year is a dissipative timescale for energy loss to the deep ocean and to the atmosphere, h = 50 m is the average depth of the mixed layer of the oceans, c_P = 4.2 × 10³ Ws/(K kg) is the average heat capacity of ocean water at constant pressure, and θ_S = tan⁻¹(ω_S/K).*

*(1) Determine the range of temperature change during a solar cycle. (2) Are the changes in solar activity have significant impact on the Earth’s surface climate? Hint: Shaviv (2008).*

(i) Using ω_S = 1.8 × 10⁻⁸ 1/second, K = 3.1 × 10⁻⁸ 1/second, and ρ = 1000 kg/m³, we can verify that the temperature change during a solar cycle ranges from -0.026 to 0.026 K. (ii) This range suggests that solar activity can have a significant impact on the Earth’s climate. This result is in contrast to the official consensus from the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC). The difference is due to the fact that the IPCC assumes that ΔS ≈ 0.05 W/m², which is small relative to the effect of greenhouse gases, estimated at around 2.5 W/m². Shaviv (2008) suggests ΔS ≈ 0.2 W/m² and maybe higher.

*(1) Define a process where a parcels temperature changes due to expansion or compression, but no heat is added or taken away from the parcel. (2) Explain why Africa and Antarctica have numerous hotspots (see Ikelle, 2022a).*

Hot spots can and do occur anywhere on the Earth’s surface. However, because the lithosphere is thicker in some places than others, and because lithospheric plates moves laterally relative to the mantle convection system at various rates, some plates and parts of plates have more hot spots than others. As a result, the slow moving plates that include continents such as Africa and Antarctica have numerous hot spots, as do thin plates, such as the Pacific plate, which is entirely composed of thin ocean lithosphere.
