
## Get started with Android Studio

Welcome to your new developer environment! This is the prescribed setup for your training materials. To begin, make sure the Android Studio IDE is installed on your computer.

### Install Android Studio

Download and install Android Studio for your operating system from the official developer website.

1. Download the latest version of Android Studio for your OS.
2. Open the downloaded installer file.
3. Follow the on-screen instructions to complete the installation.
4. Launch Android Studio after installation completes.

### Set up Android Studio

When you first start Android Studio, you'll be asked to import settings. Choose "Do not import settings" if you are using a fresh installation.

The setup wizard will guide you through downloading the Android SDK components. Make sure to accept the license agreements and install the recommended SDK packages.

> **Note:** Some components may require additional disk space. Ensure you have at least 8 GB of free space recommended.

- Android SDK
- Android SDK Platform
- Performance (Intel® HAXM)
- Android Virtual Device

After the SDK components are installed, you are ready to create your first Android app project.

You can find more documentation and resources on the official Android Developer website.

(1) Converting the temperature of 0°C into 273 K, we find

ΔS = ΔQ / T = 3.35 × 10^7 J / 273 K = 1.23 × 10^5 J/K

(2) The entropy change is

ΔS = Q (1/T_cold − 1/T_hot) > 0.

Because ΔS > 0, the heat transfer is irreversible. No work, W, is produced by this process. When the two reservoirs are not allowed to come into direct contact and, instead, an engine is used to convert some of the heat Q_H into work and exhausts the remaining heat Q_C to the low temperature reservoir, we have the basic principle of the heat engine.

(3) To find the work done by the gas as it expands adiabatically from V_A to V_B, for the adiabat P V^γ = P_A V_A^γ = P_B V_B^γ,

W = ∫_{V_A}^{V_B} P dV = P_A V_A^γ ∫_{V_A}^{V_B} dV / V^γ = P_A V_A^γ (V_B^{1−γ} − V_A^{1−γ})/(1 − γ) = (P_B V_B − P_A V_A)/(1 − γ).

By using the ideal gas law, P V = R T, we arrive at

W = R (T_B − T_A)/(1 − γ) = c_V (T_B − T_A).

Consider an atmosphere that is completely transparent to shortwave (solar) radiation but very opaque to infrared terrestrial radiation. Specifically, assume that it can be represented by n layers of atmosphere, each of which completely absorbs infrared radiation. How many layers would be required to obtain the actual average temperatures of Earth, Venus, and Mars? Explain why N is zero for some planets.

By considering the energy balance of the n-th layer, we can show that

2T_n^4 = T_{n+1}^4 + T_{n-1}^4,

where T_n is the temperature of the n-th layer, for n > 1. So the ground temperature is

T_g = (N + 1)^{1/4} T_e,

where T_e is the planetary emission temperature and T_g is the ground temperature, and T_a is the actual ground temperature. N = 0 for planets without atmosphere. Before sunlight reaches the Earth it has to pass through the atmosphere. As described in Chapter 1, Earth's atmosphere has several layers. The troposphere is where weather occurs and where climate is determined. There is also a scenario in which the atmosphere can absorb visible light rather than letting it pass through. This scenario can occur on Earth if the upper atmosphere is filled with dust resulting from nuclear accidents, nuclear wars, or any artificial design to reduce Earth temperatures. The Earth will be too cold; that is why this scenario is known as nuclear winter.
