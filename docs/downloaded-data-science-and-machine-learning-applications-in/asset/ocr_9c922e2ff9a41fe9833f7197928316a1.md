During the Mesozoic period, the area continued to experience tectonic activity, which caused the land to rise and form mountains. These mountains, now long gone, were made up of sandstone, shale, and limestone. Later, these rocks were buried in sedimentary layers made up of shale, sandstone, and coal.

During the Cenozoic period, the area experienced further tectonic activity and experienced several periods of erosion. These activities caused the land to be reshaped, exposing the sedimentary layers to the surface. The area is now home to a variety of rock formations, including sandstone, shale, and limestone, as well as coal and oil deposits.

Overall, the geological history of Opunake Field is a complex and dynamic geological system reflecting the millions of years of tectonic activity, volcanic eruptions, and sedimentary processes in the Taranaki region. These activities have resulted in a wide range of rock formations and sedimentary layers that have formed over the years. These rocks and sediments provide a rich resource for geological study and exploration. The Opunake Field is a valuable oil and gas production resource that has also played an important role in developing New Zealand’s energy industry (McBeath, 1977).
