The 1956 Dartmouth Artificial Intelligence conference marked the beginning of the field of AI and proved succeeding generations of scientists with their first sense of the potential technology had to be of benefit to human beings in a profound way. In the summer of 1955, John McCarthy, associate professor of mathematics at Dartmouth College, decided to organize a group to clarify and develop ideas about thinking machines. In the early 1950s, there were various names for the field of “thinking machines”: cybernetics, automata theory, and complex information processing. However, instead of focusing on automata theory, John McCarthy chose a new name, artificial intelligence. The project was formally proposed by McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Nathaniel Rochester and Claude Shannon on September 2, 1955. The proposal is credited with introducing the term ‘artificial’.
