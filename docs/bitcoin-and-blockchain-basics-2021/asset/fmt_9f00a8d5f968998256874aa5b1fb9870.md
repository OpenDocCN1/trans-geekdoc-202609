## What are the Differences between Trading and Investing in Bitcoin?

Investing in bitcoins is different from trading bitcoins the same way trading and investing in stocks differ physically.

Therefore, trading is a short-term process. Someone begins the market and trades for some months. He withdraws if the price gets to its highest. Those that trade bitcoin are sensitive of prices and leaves when they feel that trading is not gainful.

Then, you might have heard the saying that one day trader is not a serious trader. But in bitcoin trading, one day trader is a serious trader that maximizes the issues of volatility and fluctuation of prices.

Investing in bitcoin is a long-term transaction. Here, an investor has access to various portfolios of cryptocurrencies. Bitcoin investment also involves fiat risk, hedging, and other roles. Price volatility does not affect bitcoin investors. They are ready to hold onto the investment for a longer time.

In this scenario, they continue and hold onto their investment until a good time to sell. This investment may last for years on the exchange.

## Minimizing Exposures in Trading and Investment in Bitcoin

Trading and investing in bitcoin comes with a lot of risks. Due to the explosion in the usage of bitcoins and other cryptocurrencies, fraudulent-minded persons have devised different means of ripping innocent investors of their hard-earned cash. Individuals and organizations are opting for digital currencies making it scarce and valuable like a piece of gold.

Here are different ways to minimize exposures or risks involved in trading and investment in bitcoin:

1.  **Distribute your investment over many bitcoin exchanges** – this will prevent you from losing all your investible funds as a result of fraud on the exchange.
2.  **Research about the bitcoin exchanges you want to handle your portfolios** – read the reviews on them before investing.
3.  **Don't expose your private keys to bitcoin exchangers** – remember that whoever has your private keys will collect your cryptos. When you transact business with bitcoin exchangers, don't allow them to have your keys.
4.  **Avoid storing large amounts of cryptocurrencies on a bitcoin exchange** – put enough amount of bitcoin on your hot wallet. Don't keep big amounts of funds with your bitcoin exchange to prevent losses.

## Managing the Risks in Trading Bitcoin

Managing the risks that comes with bitcoin trading will help you to succeed in the venture. However, risks are the possibility or probability of a bad incident occurring in your actions. The event can go against your expectations.

A risk could lead to losses on the crypto market. But we need to know some types of risk associated with bitcoin trading. These include:

1.  **Liquidity risk** – this occurs when an investor is unable to change their cryptos into fiat currencies.
2.  **Operational risk** – this can cause a trader not to deposit, withdraw, or trade with funds in their bitcoin wallets.
3.  **Market risk** – the instances of the prices of coin fluctuating against your desires in an open market situation.
4.  **Credit risk** – this risk causes cryptocurrency project holders not to execute their obligations. This may be caused by fraudulent minds in the cryptocurrency network.
5.  **Legal risk** – policies and regulations by authorities could affect the crypt market leading to losses in the business.

Here are ways to manage these risks:

1.  **Stop Loss and take Profit approaches** – Stop loss occurs when an order of execution ends an open position if a price reduces to particular point. This prevents you from embarking on unprofitable transactions. Also, take profit refers to an order of execution liquidating open orders if the values go to a certain level. This approach enables you to pull out of trading before the system can hit you badly.
2.  **Position Sizing approach** – this approach helps in determining the amount of tokens or bitcoins an investor can buy comfortably. Most times, a trader can invest up to one hundred percent of their trading capital but when things go bad, it puts them in a bad position financially.
3.  **Risk or Reward Ratio approach** – in this method, you compare the potential risk with the probable reward. Sometimes, if a trading position is riskier, it has the potentials of becoming more profitable. A proper understanding of this system will help you to know when to begin a trade and when to quit if it is unprofitable.

## The Legality of Trading and Investing in Cryptocurrencies

Despite the boom in cryptocurrency trading and investment, one needs to be concerned about its legality. This new technology is still a mystery to several individuals, governments, central banks, and other financial institutions all over the world. Therefore, if you want to embark on digital investments in cryptos, you will be liable to different legal risks that may arise.

Here are some of the legal implications of investing and trading in cryptocurrencies:

1.  **The risk of fraud and money laundering** – One dangerous belief nowadays is that some criminally minded persons are using bitcoins and other cryptos for money laundering across the borders. But investors committing these financial crimes may not face the same legal consequences as those caught in conventional money laundering situations. But if you are not inclined to committing financial crimes using the cryptocurrency network, you may not face the same legal risks.
2.  **Exchange hacking risk** – Another implication of investing and trading on digital currencies is that the bitcoin exchange could be hacked leading to the loss of investors' funds. There is no insurance or legal means of recovering such cryptoassets. This is one of the problems of decentralizing the blockchain technology as against the centralized method of traditional financial institutions. Due to this legal risks, exchanges are developing new types of wallets and other security measures to combat these crimes on the network.
3.  **The risk of decentralization** – Cryptofinance has no regulatory body to manage the legal responsibilities arising from the transactions. There is also the issue of ownership. Who owns bitcoin? The assumed ownership (Nakamoto) is still anonymous. Therefore, no one can predict the issues that may arise tomorrow and lead to loss of investments due to ownership tussle.
4.  **Tax implications in the USA** – In the United States of America, the IRS has stated that bitcoins and other cryptos are properties rather than currencies. Therefore, owners and investors of cryptocurrencies are liable to pay capital gains tax.
5.  **Counterparty risk** – The risk of the particulars of investors initiating transactions could become a challenge tomorrow. Unlike traditional financial homes where an organization takes responsibility for resolving and settling debt or deposit claims. Online crypto exchangers cannot take such responsibility if deposit issues occur.
