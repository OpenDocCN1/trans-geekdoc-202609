
## SOL-78 CH.SOL- 4.12.

An important set of properties of the entropy follows from the concavity of the entropy, which follows from the concavity of the logarithm. Suppose we cannot decide whether the actual probability of ‘heads’ is $p_{1}$ or $p_{2}$. We may decide to assign probability $q$ to the first alternative and probability $1-q$ to the second. The actual probability of ‘heads’ then is the mixture $qp_{1}+(1-q)p_{2}$. The corresponding entropies satisfy the inequality:

$$S(qp_{1}+(1-q)p_{2}) \geq q S(p_{1})+(1-q) S(p_{2}) \tag{4.16}$$

with equality in the extreme cases where $p_{1}=p_{2}$, or $q=0$, or $q=1$.

## 4.3.3 Kullback-Leibler Divergence

### SOL-81 CH.SOL- 4.15.

For discrete probability distributions $P$ and $Q$, the Kullback-Leibler divergence **from** $P$ to $Q$, the KLD is defined as:

$$
\begin{align}
D(P \| Q) &= \sum_x P(x) \log \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} \tag{4.19} \\
&= \mathbb{E}_P \left[ \log \frac{1}{Q(x)} - \log \frac{1}{P(x)} \right] \\
&= \underbrace{H_P(Q)}_{\text{Cross Entropy}} - \underbrace{H(P)}_{\text{Entropy}}.
\end{align}
$$

### SOL-82 CH.SOL- 4.16.

One interpretation is the following: the KL-divergence indicates the average number of **additional bits** required for transmission of values $x \in X$ which are distributed according to $P(x)$, but we erroneously encoded them according to distribution $Q(x)$. This makes sense since you have to “pay” for additional bits to compensate for not knowing the true distribution, thus using a code that was optimized according to other distribution. This is one of the reasons that the KL-divergence is also known as relative entropy. Formally, the cross entropy has an information interpretation quantifying how many bits are wasted by using the wrong code:

$$H_P(Q) = \sum_x \underbrace{P(x)}_{\text{Sending } P} \overbrace{\log \frac{1}{Q(x)}}^{\text{code for } Q} . \tag{4.20}$$

### SOL-83 CH.SOL- 4.17.

- 1. True KLD is a non-symmetric measure, i.e. $D(P \| Q) \neq D(Q \| P)$.
- 2. False KLD does not satisfy the triangle inequality.
- 3. True KLD is not a distance metric.
- 4. True KLD is regarded as a measure of the information gain. Notice that however, KLD is the amount of information lost.
- 5. True The units of KL divergence are units of information (bits, nats, etc.).
- 6. True KLD is a non-negative measure.
- 7. True Performing splitting based on highly informative event usually leads to low model generalization and a less accurate one as well.

### SOL-84 CH.SOL- 4.18.

Formally, mutual information attempts to measure how correlated two variables are with each other:

$$
\begin{aligned}
I(X;Y) &= \sum_{x,y} P(x,y) \log \frac{P(x,y)}{P(x)P(y)} \tag{4.21} \\
&= \mathbb{E} \left[ \log \frac{1}{P(x)} + \log \frac{1}{P(y)} - \log \frac{1}{P(x,y)} \right] \\
&= H(X) + H(Y) - H(X,Y).
\end{aligned}
$$

Regarding the question at hand, given two distributions $f_1$ and $f_2$ and their joint distribution $f$, the mutual information of $f_1$ and $f_2$ is defined as $I(f_1, f_2) = H(f, f_1, f_2)$. If the two distributions are independent, i.e. $f=f_1 \cdot f_2$, the mutual information will vanish. This concept has been widely used as a similarity measure in image analysis.

$$
\begin{aligned}

$$
\begin{aligned}
