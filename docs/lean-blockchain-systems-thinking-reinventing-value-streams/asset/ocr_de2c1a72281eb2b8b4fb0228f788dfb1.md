# Lean Transformation: Kaizen, Kaakuan, and Kakushin

Lean provides three distinct approaches to improvement, which have been named by the Japanese as Kaizen, Kaakuan, and Kakushin. These three Japanese words represent the following:

+   - Kaizen: Small and regular changes for improvement; incremental improvement often in an iterative way.
- Kaakuan: Radical change that replaces a previous design/process/business-model with a new design/process/business model.
- Kakushin: The use of innovation for transformational change, disruption of the status quo. It involves a paradigm shift, a new way of thinking and behaving.

# Lean Kaizen

The term “Kaizen” is understood as “Change for small incremental steps”, Kaizen (1986). In the context of Lean manufacturing, Lean Kaizen represents a culture and mindset within organizations for “Everyday, Everywhere, Every Day” Masaaki Imai “Kaizen, The Key to Japan’s Competitive Success” (1986) to be identifying and making small improvements to the way in which work is being done.

# Lean Kaakuan

The term “Kaakuan” translates to “radical change” or “revolution” in Japanese. In the context of Lean manufacturing, Lean Kaakuan represents a deliberate and strategic effort to bring about substantial and transformative changes within a process, system, or organization.
