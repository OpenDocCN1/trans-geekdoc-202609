## Systems Thinking

Systems Thinking is the concept of focusing on the goals and outcomes of the whole system, including society, the earth, suppliers, producers, and end-users instead of the goals of the individual stakeholders or the component parts of the system. Systems Thinking looks for balance, collaboration, and cooperation between the component parts of systems, which results in better and more sustainable outcomes for all stakeholders. Systems Thinking requires that we break down barriers between organizational units and remove silos of information by providing greater interoperability.

### Lean Transformation: Kaizen, Kaakuan, and Kakushin

Lean provides three distinct approaches to improvement, which have been named by the Japanese as Kaizen, Kaakuan, and Kakushin. These three words represent the following:

- Kaizen: Small and regular changes for improvement; incremental improvement often in an iterative way.
- Kaakuan: Radical change that replaces a previous design/process/business-model with a new design/process/business model.
- Kakushin: The use of innovation for transformational change, disruption of the status quo. It involves a paradigm shift, a new way of thinking and behaving.

### Lean Kaizen

The term “Kaizen” is understood as “Change for small incremental steps”, Kaizen (1986). In the context of Lean manufacturing, Lean Kaizen represents a culture and mindset within organizations for “Everyday, Everywhere, Every Day” Masaaki Imai “Kaizen, The Key to Japan’s Competitive Success” (1986) to be identifying and making small improvements to the way in which work is being done.

The term “Kaakuan” translates to “radical change” or “revolution” in Japanese.
