# 8.2 INTRODUCTION

Coronaviruses are positive-sense RNA viruses that are enveloped and non-segmented and belong to Coronaviridae family, Nidovirales order. The highly contagious beta-coronavirus has the potential to cause COVID-19, named by WHO on February 11, 2020. The entire population is highly prone to this alarming disease; contact and respiratory droplets are the primary routes of transmission [3,4]. COVID-19 targets the epithelial cells of the alveoli of lungs by using receptor-mediated endocytosis like all other viruses via ACE2 (angiotensin-converting enzyme II) as an opening receptor. In the case of symptomatic patients, symptoms start appearing after not more than a week, including cough, fever, fatigue, nasal congestion, and other signs associated with the infection of the upper respiratory tract. The infection could progress to a severe state with dyspnea and pneumonia with severe chest pain, symptoms that could be seen by computer tomography which have been seen in case of 75% of infected individuals approximately. After second and third weeks in the case of symptomatic infection, pneumonia occurs mostly [5].
