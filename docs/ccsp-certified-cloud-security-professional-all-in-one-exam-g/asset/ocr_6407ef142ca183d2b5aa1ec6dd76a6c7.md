> EXAM TIP Remember that a gap analysis and the presentation of audit findings should be from an impartial and independent actor. While many organizations conduct internal audits, these should be considered for their own purposes or fact-finding efforts, and should never be used for certifications or compliance. Only findings done by someone else and independent, who has no financial or other interests in the results of the audit, should be considered valid and trustworthy. Under virtually all regulatory and certification programs, only audits performed by an independent actor, sometimes with certification requirements of their own, will be accepted as a valid for the purposes of compliance.

# Audit Plan

The overall audit plan falls into a series of four steps, each with important and sequenced components that drive the overall process to meet the objectives and requirements. Here are the four steps in the audit plan:

+   - Define objectives
- Define scope
- Conduct the audit
- Lessons learned and analysis

# Define Objectives

Defining Objectives of the audit includes several steps to lay the groundwork for the entire audit process, and will drive the process of defining the scope for actually conducting the audit. This step clearly defines them and articulates the official objectives of the audit and produces a document contesting them. The objectives take into account the management priorities and risk acceptance to ensure they are aligned with what is desired from the audit. They also define the format and formats of the audit report and any other deliverables that will be produced as a result of the audit. Based on the requirements and the scale of the audit, this process also defines the number of staff needed to conduct the audit, not only on behalf of the auditing group, but also the systems and application teams that need to be available to produce data, answer interview questions, and run whatever scripts or grant access needed from the audit team’s perspective.
