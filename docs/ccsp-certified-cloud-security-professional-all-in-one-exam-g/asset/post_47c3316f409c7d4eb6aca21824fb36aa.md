## Mapping and Definition of Controls

With the various requirements mapped out in privacy acts, a central role of the Cloud Security Professional is the mapping of those requirements to actual security controls and processes in place with both the application and cloud environment. For applications that span multiple jurisdictions, this will be of importance. The customer and cloud provider will need to work together through appropriate SLA requirements to ensure compliance for both parties in regard to privacy acts.

## Application of Defined Controls

With the complexity of cloud environments, it can sometimes be difficult to ensure that applicable privacy requirements are met by both the customer and cloud provider. The Cloud Security Alliance Controls Matrix (CCM) provides a strong framework and applicable security domains within cloud environment that encapsulate various requirements, as well as industry certification and regulatory bodies. Here are the security domains outlined by the CCM, which can be found at https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/cloudcontrols/:

- Application Security
- Audit Assurance and Compliance
- Business Continuity and Operational Resilience
- Change Control and Configuration Management
- Data Security and Information Lifecycle Management
- Data Center Security
- Encryption and Key Management
- Governance and Risk Management
- Human Resources
- Identity and Access Management
- Infrastructure and Virtualization Security
- Interoperability and Portability
- Mobile Security
- Threat and Vulnerability Management

NOTE: Not all controls will be applicable to all regulatory privacy acts; the CCM serves as a holistic approach to controls and definitions.

## Data Rights Management

Data rights management (DRM) is an extension of normal data protection where additional controls are placed onto data sets that require access and use. This fits within the framework to ensure that applicable privacy acts are met by both the customer and cloud provider, whereas DRM applies to the organizational side to protect intellectual property rights and control the extent of distribution.

NOTE: Many are familiar with the concept of information rights management (IRM). DRM applies to the protection of consumer media, such as publications, videos, and so on.

## Data Rights Objectives

With DRM, few main concepts are important. In a typical environment, access controls are placed on data object that determines who can read or modify that file. Also, providing read permissions to file allows a user to inherently perform operations such as copying, renaming, printing, and sending. With DRM, additional control layers are applied to file to allow for granular and powerful control over what can be done. With DRM, all functions mentioned can be further controlled and restricted, providing additional layers of security over documents. This also allows for more flexibility in choosing hosting platforms and providers.

IRM can also be used as a means for data classification and control. Controls are placed immediately upon data at the time of creation, including where the data is created, when it is created, and who created it. This allows for granularity in the initial stages of the data lifecycle and enables an organization to maintain stringent data control toward satisfying some aspect of regulatory requirements, as part of the auditing and compliance process.

## Data Retention, Deletion, and Archiving Policies

For any data policy to be effective and ensure compliance with regulatory or corporate compliance requirements, the concepts of retention, deletion, and archiving are of the utmost importance. Whereas other data policies and practices are focused on the security and production usage of data, these three concepts are typical of those that fulfill regulatory obligations, which can come in the form of legal requirements or mandates from certification or industry oversight bodies.

## Data Retention

Data retention involves the keeping and maintaining of data for a period of time as well as the methods used to accomplish these tasks. Typically, the retention requirements have set periods of time mandated as the minimums. Corporate policies for data retention can sometimes require longer periods of time, but the regulatory requirements will serve as the basis for retention. The data retention policy also addresses the means of storage and the ability to access the data when required for a variety of reasons, including regulatory requirements, corporate history, and legal discovery. A data retention policy that does not address the access and availability of it will miss the focus and points of data retention because the data will effectively be useless or cost prohibitive.

CAUTION: While regulations and policies determine the length of time for which data must be preserved, some caution is also warranted for data that may end being kept past stated policies. This can come into play particularly with eDiscovery requirements for evidence preservation and searching.

## Tools

Rather than focusing on specific software technologies or implementations for DRM, we will instead focus on the typical attributes and features of those tool sets and what they can provide for security:

- **Auditing**: IRM technologies allow for robust auditing of who has viewed information, as well as proof of when and where they accessed the data. Data can aid greatly with compliance requirements where an organization needs to ensure that an appropriate audience has a policy or document.
- **Expiration**: IRM technologies allow for the expiration of access to data. This gives an organization the ability to set a lifetime for the data and enforce policy controls that disable it to be accessible, as is the case in most systems data been presented to users or is allowed to leave its host system.
- **Policy control**: IRM technologies allow an organization to have very granular and detailed control over how their data is used. The ability to control different audiences to copy, save, print, forward, or access any data is far more powerful than what is afforded by traditional security mechanisms. An DRM implementation also gives an organization the ability to change policy at any time, revoking or expiring data down the road if necessary. This ensures the organization that users are always viewing the most current copy of data or policies as well as to expire the old ones once they are available.
- **Protection**: With the implementation of IRM technologies and controls, information under their protection is secure at all times. Unlike many systems, which require separate practices and technologies for data at rest versus in transit, IRM provides persistent protection at all times that is integrated with the data regardless of its current state.
- **Support for applications and formats**: Most IRM technologies support a range of data formats and integration with application packages commonly used within organizations, such as e-mail and office suites. This enables the integration of IRM systems and processes within the organization needing to make major changes to their current operational practices.
