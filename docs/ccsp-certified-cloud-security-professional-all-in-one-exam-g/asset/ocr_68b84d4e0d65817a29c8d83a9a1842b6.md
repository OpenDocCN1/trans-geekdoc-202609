Most modern applications, especially those typically found in cloud data, are built on components, services, and APIs that consume other services and data, the selection and verification of appropriate pieces that meet security requirements is essential. The old adage about the weakest link is clearly valid within this context, as weakness of a single component could expose an entire application or system to threats and exploits. Domain 4 covers the software development lifecycle (SDLC) as it relates to cloud environment, including in-depth walk through each phase, what it entails, and the key components to be addressed moving to the next stage, as well as the cyclical nature of SDLC. The main threats and vulnerability concepts from STRIDE and DREAD models are introduced, including their applicability to cloud environments.

On top of secure development concepts and methodologies, other commonly used technologies and paradigms within cloud computing are introduced. These include XML applications, web application firewalls (WAFs), and systematic approaches such as sandboxing and application virtualization. Their importance in regard to cloud computing as well as their essential use and reliance on cryptography are discussed. Lastly, Domain 4 covers the strategic approaches to identity and access management and building them into applications during development, including multifactor authentication technologies.

# Domain 5: Operations

The operations begins with a discussion of planning for data centers, including both the physical and logical layers and technologies employed. This also incorporates how environmental concerns and needs are addressed along physical requirements, including reliable and redundant access to cooling and electrical resources, as well as adequate protection from natural disasters and environmental issues.

Although cloud environments are mostly virtualized in the manner that most think, they still have the burden of a traditional data center, even if those burdens are being handled and managed by the cloud provider and are largely abstracted from view and concern of the users and cloud customers. The physical layer of the servers that are hosting the virtualized environment, as well as storage and network components. From the network perspective, all the concerns of a traditional data center are still present, such as firewalls, routing, and network intrusion protection and detection systems. Physical devices can be operated in a stand-alone or clustered configuration as well, depending on specific concerns for the environment or specific requirements and expectations.
