# ISO/IEC 27050

ISO/IEC 27050 strives to establish an internationally accepted standard for discovery processes and best practices. It encompasses all of the eDiscovery process, including the identification, preservation, processing, collection, review, analysis, and the final production of the requested archive. It attempts to address the enormous volume of data that organizations produce and hold, the complex and diverse methods by which this data is stored and processed, as well as the challenges with how quickly and easily electronic data and records can be produced, stored, shared, and destroyed. With cloud computing crossing jurisdictional boundaries—either the distributed nature of cloud computing and the cloud provider using geographically diverse locations—an internationally published and accepted standard will give both cloud providers and cloud customers a contractual basis for standardization. This allows cloud providers to structure their support and operations around the framework and best practices forward, and to advertise that to cloud customers and potential customers.

# CSA Guidance

The Cloud Security Alliance has published, as part of their “CSA Security Guidance” series, a publication that eDiscovery titled “Domain 3: Legal Issues, Contracts, and Electronic Discovery.” This document outlines specific cloud-based concerns for eDiscovery, issues specific to the cloud provider and cloud customer, and approaches to ensure compliance with eDiscovery orders and the pitfalls and challenges that occur because of them.

A big challenge with eDiscovery compliance is that orders for data collection and preservation are located on data that an organization owns and controls. With a traditional data center, this is a lot clearer because the organization controls and owns the systems—from the hardware and network level, all the way through the application level. With a cloud environment, even with SaaS, the cloud customer only owns and controls a subset of data, with the cloud provider maintaining ownership and control over the rest, usually automatically to the cloud customer. With this setup, in many jurisdictions, one eDiscovery order may be required for the cloud customer and another for the cloud provider, in order to get the entire set of data. The contract between the cloud customer and cloud provider should clearly delineate the responsibilities of both parties as far data ownership and collection, and what mechanisms and requests are necessary should an eDiscovery order be received. In some jurisdictions, depending on the contracts and expectations of other tenants within the same cloud, separate court orders or eDiscovery orders may be necessary to gain access to the infrastructure-level data and logs.
