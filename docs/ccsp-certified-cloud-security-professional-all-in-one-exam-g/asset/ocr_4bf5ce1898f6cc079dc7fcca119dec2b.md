Given the access and privileges with which the management plane operates, concerns
over security are of the highest level. A compromise of the management plane would give
an attacker complete control of the entire environment and make the entire cloud vul-
erable, which is well above the risk and threat a compromised hypervisor would provide
because the management plane controls multiple hypervisors. The most highly vet-
ed limited set of administrative staff should have access to the management plane,
an all access and functions should be tightly audited and reviewed on a regular basis.

# Risks Associated with Cloud Infrastructure

Cloud-based systems have the same level of risk as other hosting models, but with the
addition of risks specific to cloud hosting. A cloud-based system should be approached
and managed by any other outsourced platform, with the same types of concerns, risks,
and/governance requirements as an external hosting environment.

# Risk Assessment and Analysis

Cloud hosting has the same areas of risk as all systems and applications,
with cloud-specific risks on top of these risks or as key aspects expanding upon them.
From an organizational and regulatory perspective, there are risks related to lock-in,
governance, data security and privacy, and any legal and regulatory controls and report-
ired for a system or application. One of the biggest benefits of the cloud hosting
model is portability and the ability move between providers at will. If an organization
locked into that provider and incur a lot of property requirements, they may get
locked in substantial costs if they decide to switch.

With any external hosting arrangement, a company loses substantial control over their
systems and their governance. Even with strong contractual requirements and SLAs in
place, the level of control and access will be much less than what would be in their
proprietary data centers. Depending on the regulatory requirements for the type of appli-
cation and data to be hosted, the choice of cloud provider may be limited, or even
extent. Cloud providers have to serve a large number of customers with their business
model, which makes them complying with various types of certifications and requirements di-
cicult. A primary concern for any company is where their data is stored and whether
sufficient protections are in place to ensure their confidentiality and integrity. A cloud-
environment presents many challenges for appropriate governance, made even more compli-
cated by eDiscovery requirements, depending on the nature and type of the data,
that need to be carefully evaluated and weighed before making a cloud hosting decision.
