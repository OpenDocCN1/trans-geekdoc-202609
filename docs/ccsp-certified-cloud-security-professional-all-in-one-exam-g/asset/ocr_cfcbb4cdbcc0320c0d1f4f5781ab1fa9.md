# Account Hijacking

Account Hijacking is not unique to cloud environments, nor new, but the threat in a cloud environment is often more significant than in a traditional data center model. Within a cloud with many hosted environments and customers, and given the large scale and viable nature of many cloud environments, especially public clouds, systems are a particularly ripe target for attackers. If an attacker is able to gain access through either exploiting your system or another system in the same cloud environment, they can use to eavesdrop or capture your traffic or it to attack others in the cloud or the underlying cloud infrastructure. Each occurrence could harm the reputation of the exploited system. Multifactor authentication methods and strong account provisioning controls can minimize the risk. The account hijacking threat applies to IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS models.

# Malicious Insiders

The malicious insider is centered on an individual who has (or had) access and uses for unauthorized purposes to exploit systems or data. This is related to the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of systems. Within a cloud environment, the malicious insider threat is further compounded by the employees who works for the cloud provider itself. In a cloud infrastructure, the administrators of the system have access to the hypervisor, web portals, deployment and provision systems, as well as the actual virtual images. This is above and beyond what the customer has access to their own employees, and it is dependent on the security policies and practices of the cloud provider to mitigate the risk of its own fault. The malicious insider threat applies to the IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS models.

# Advanced Persistent Threats

Advanced persistent threats are where attackers target systems with the intent of establishing themselves and stealing data over the long term. Social engineering tactics are often employed by attackers to gain access to a system and establish themselves. These types of attacks can be very difficult to detect and eliminate, so even advanced technology solutions to detect methods in normal traffic and traverse detect these attacks as well as a focus on minimizing problems as social engineering are common methods used in their prevention and elimination. Advanced persistent threats can be used against the IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS models.

# Data Loss

Data loss occurs when the data an organization relies on becomes lost, unavailable, or destroyed when it should not have. This can happen through encryption keys, accidental deletion or corruption of data. The threat of data loss can be largely mitigated by a robust and regular backup design and schedule, and should always include having the data backed up off-premises from the cloud environment for the most protection. Of course with any backup system, especially an offsite one, the possibility of a data breach increases because a lot more possible points are exposed for one to occur.
