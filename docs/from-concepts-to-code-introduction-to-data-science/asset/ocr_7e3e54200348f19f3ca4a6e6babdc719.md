This will help you trust you, actually, because they will get the message that if you are stuck, you will get work done, not to get stuck. This is preferable to counting on people to get work done, waiting for weeks as you struggle in silence, and ultimately having nothing delivered.

In my early years, I faced steep learning curves and my managers were very busy people. They usually couldn't provide help at a moment of notice, so I asked them to suggest how I could get help. Answers came, of course, but had one thing that was collecting all the blockers, or where I was stuck, and thinking through them. I found that for some, I could break them down into subproblems and then tackle the overall problem. For other problems, there was information that I needed to move forward. This helped my list of blockers and it made me more independent. As people freed up to help (typically after the financial markets closed), we had things to work through. The second big thing that was developing an internal network of people in similar roles. There was a good chance that someone near my level could offer to help.

The current environment is for crowdsourcing your questions. Instant message boards like Slack or Teams are conducive to posing questions to groups of people and getting answers in a timely fashion. If you have a coding question, there is a great chance that web resources like Stack Overflow will have an answer. In the other direction, I encourage you to share your knowledge and help others when you can.

Beyond getting help for yourself and staying productive on projects, you should also think about what you want to do and to love your work. This might include challenging projects, headcount, a promotion, or a raise. Whatever the result may be, be just that you really want it, that you are ready for it, and you can document why you deserve it. Some of these things could come with more responsibility, or different responsibilities. For example, changing from individual contributor (IC) to manager will require a different set of skills. ICS need to produce work, while managers need to motivate and coach others to produce the work. Not every IC will enjoy being a manager? Some data scientists remain ICs for the duration of their careers.
