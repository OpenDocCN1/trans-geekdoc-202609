```
$P(Y = 5) = \frac{\#(\text{ways of achieving 5 given a 1})}{\#(\text{possible outcomes})} = \frac{\{|{(4, 1), (1, 4)\}|}{12} = \frac{2}{6}$
```

When we compute the probability given available information, we call this the *conditional probability*. Mathematically, this is written $P(Y|X)$ and read "the probability of $Y$ given $X$." In the case above, $Y=5$ represented two dice summing to 5, and 1 represented one of the dice having value 1. More, the formula is

```
$P(Y|X) = \frac{P(X \cap Y)}{P(X)}$
```

where $P(X \cap Y)$ denotes the probability of both $X$ and $Y$ occurring. For our example, the numerator represents the probability that the 2 rolls sum to 5, with restriction that at least one is 1. We saw there are two outcomes out of 36 possible outcomes, for a probability of 2/36. The denominator works out to 12 possible involving one or more of 36 possible outcomes, for a probability of 12/36. The calculation is done like this:

```
$P(Y=5|X=1) = \frac{(2/36)}{(12/36)} = \frac{2}{6}$
```

When the outcome of $X$ does not influence the outcome of $Y$, we say that $X$ and $Y$ are *independent*. This can be written $P(Y|X) = P(Y)$. In data, the goal is to find random variables $X_1, X_2, \dots, X_p$ which predict $Y$. Predictors which are independent of $Y$ would not be helpful. We will see later that prediction relies heavily on conditional probability, as important data is included to produce refined results.
