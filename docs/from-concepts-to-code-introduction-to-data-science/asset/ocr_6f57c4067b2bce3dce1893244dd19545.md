# 2.7 Help Others

I want to briefly make the case for helping others through knowledge sharing and displays of empathy. Earlier, I mentioned that you will need help from others, so it's only fair that I ask that you help others whenever possible. Helping others is not only good karma, but it will increase your satisfaction and value to the organization. Ultimately we feel good when we can be helpful, and it promotes a message of respect and care for our teammates.

There are many ways to be helpful. If you see someone struggling and you can help them, please go ahead and help before they ask. Some organizations groups of employees with specialized expertise helping on a common goal. At UVA, there was a desire to scale the number of online programs. A team of leaders from each university would regularly convene to share best practices. Such groups provide several benefits, including opportunities for networking and accelerated learning.

I have worked in all kinds of organizations, from startups to massive enterprises, from competitive places to cooperative. One of course is entitled to their opinion, but I find that cooperative organizations promote stability, support, and well-being. You might ask why people choose not to help others. There can be two common factors that I have found are from leadership and a scarcity mindset.

People generally respond to incentives. Many salespeople quota carry quota, as a certain amount of revenue they need to bring in each quarter. The quota is a dangling carrot that leads to planned behaviors. If a team of scientists is a dangling carrot to mentor, then you may find that the ones that want this role will respond to the incentive. When leadership rewards workers for their values, and the workers agree with these values, they tend to propagate them [8].

Now let's think about a scarcity mindset and an abundance mindset. If you have a great deal of resource (e.g., money, opportunity, knowledge), then you might feel to donate money or train a junior employee. If you worry that you don't have enough for yourself or your family then you will be reluctant to give it away. When on Wall Street, I met several people who were afraid of colleagues aiming to take their job. As a result, they selectively helped, as they feared that indiscriminately helping people would make them replaceable. In certain situations, this seems understandable. In general, however, playing defense is a strategy based in anxiety, and anxiety is draining. I encourage playing offense: learn as much as you can and focus on your growth. Learning will yield you an abundance of knowledge, and sharing this knowledge will make teammates better and deliver value to the organization.
