# Mathematics Preliminaries

ValueError indicating a dimension mismatch calling the matmul method (the @ operator is shorthand for this method). Operations with invalid dimensions are common source of bugs involving matrix algebra. Checking the dimensions of each matrix is highly recommended.

# Transpose

The transpose of matrix A is a matrix denoted A^T and A'.T is obtained by taking the rows (or columns) of A to be the columns (or rows) of A^T. Here is an example:

```
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 5 & 0 \ 2 & 12 & -1 \ 4 & 12 & 3 \end{pmatrix}
```

```
A^T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 4 \ 5 & 12 & 12 \ 0 & -1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}
```

For example, the first row of A, [1, 5, 0], became the first column of A^T. Note that [1, 5, 0] is a row vector, and taking its transpose made it a column vector. Taking the transpose of a column vector makes it a row vector.

When a matrix M is equal to its transpose M^T, it is said to be symmetric. An example is

```
M = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 4 \ 2 & 12 & -6 \ 4 & -6 & 3 \end{pmatrix}
```

Symmetric matrices can reduce the amount of storage and computation, due to repeated values in predictable locations (here, where m12 = m21, m13 = m31, and m23 = m32).

# Matrix Diagonal

The diagonal of an n x n matrix includes the elements m11, m22, ..., mnn. That is, they begin at the top left and proceed down to the bottom right. From the above example, the elements on the diagonal of M are 1, 2, and 3.

# Identity

The identity matrix I_n is a square n x n matrix with 1s on the diagonal and 0s elsewhere. The subscript n is sometimes suppressed. Multiplying an appropriately sized matrix M by I_n (on the left or right) will reproduce M.
