As director of an online master's program in data science, I watched many of my students struggle to balance a demanding degree, family responsibilities, and a full-time job. Several projects involved group work, and it was difficult for teammates to work at a constant pace. This was the first time I heard mention of the term *positive intent*, which suggested that students assume others are trying their best and mean no harm, particularly in ambiguous circumstances. One might imagine, it can be very easy to interpret a Slack message negatively (especially when it's missing the right emoji). Moreover, once things take a negative turn, situations can escalate quickly. The advice was very powerful, assuming positive intent brushes off things that may mean nothing. It means giving the benefit of the doubt to the other person, and can induce empathy.

In *Conversations* [5], the authors suggest that we invent a story to explain behavior. When things don't align with ours, the story is often negative. We assume a manager has a low opinion of our capabilities, or that a colleague doesn't like us. The tragedy is that the damaging story may not be correct. As an example, I had an excellent intern who was very driven and results-oriented. I'll call her Christine. As the summer was ending, a colleague mentioned that Christine working for my team and that she was leaving since she wasn't offered the option to return - some of her friends working for larger firms had received offers - she was feeling increasingly discouraged. She made up a story to explain what was happening, and this story included the message that her work was not valued. Of course, this was far from the truth. The truth was that we worked at a startup which was long on work and process - our timing simply didn't align. I was very grateful to her in this instance, as it provided me the opportunity to eradicate the negative story. We made Christine an offer, and she still happily works for the company.

My story was about an intern not asking for a full-time position. This specific scenario may not apply to you, but a larger scenario that may apply during your career is a pay raise or promotion is in order. A sad but true fact is that many companies are not thinking about how to give more money to their employees, even when they highly value their efforts. This is another example where assuming positive intent and having a conversation, even when uncomfortable, is preferable to inventing a negative story. In the space between conversations, we build up stories and feelings to explain differences between reality and expectations. Asking for things, including honest conversations, will help with alignment.
