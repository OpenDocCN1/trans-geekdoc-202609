In addition to a “why,” you will need a strong belief in yourself. You\may worry about the computer science requirements of the field, public speaking,
or some other type of data science. From the start, keep an open mind,
stay positive, keep a list of topics for exploration, and gradually chip away
at the challenges. Even for accomplished professionals, feelings of self-doubt
not deserving a “seat at the table” are very common. The phenomenon
is so prevalent it has a name: imposter syndrome. A meta-analysis in the
Journal of General Internal Medicine shows imposter syndrome prevalence
rates from 9% to 82% over 62 studies and 14,161 participants [7].

Having some wins in your areas can help build confidence.
For the areas that need improvement, plan a plan for moving forward. You\may be able to get coaching from a mentor or more senior colleague. It is perfectly to be honest about things you don’t know and ask for help. Finally,
a mentor can help greatly. In the highest ranks of large organizations
use mentors to share ideas, identify weaknesses, and identify opportunities for
growth.

Let’s imagine that you’ve been working in data science for a few years\and you feel confident doing things like coding, preparing, and building
machine learning models. While these functions may not change, the underly tools and techniques will constantly change. I mention this because I
want you to be prepared for this certainty. Data science is a field
that constantly evolves, as is driven by technology and changing
customer demands. If you can accept the challenge of learning new things, and
the idea that you’re never done, this will keep you relevant and engaged. For support take part in activities that you enjoy, as joining a data science community, attending conferences or talks,
or reading online blogs.
