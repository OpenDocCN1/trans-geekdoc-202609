Equipped with light low-power electric motors, rechargeable lightweight batteries and a flight control system with GNSSs, rotor-winged or multirotor drones have become increasingly popular due to their ability to take off and land vertically and to maintain position. Figure 3.5 shows a multirotor drone, DJI Phantom 4, used to capture the stereopair of aerial photos in Figure 3.4. They can either fly by themselves using flight plans guided by flight planning software or be remotely controlled by an operator. Photogrammetry software allows users to process drone-collected

stereoscopic images into orthorectified images and create surface and ground elevation data. Drones are the most affordable platform for acquiring aerial data over a small project area. In addition, acquiring stereoscopic images with the latest satellites offers an alternative to aerial photogrammetry for medium-scale mapping.

In recent years, airborne LiDAR (light detection and ranging) has increasingly become an important source of elevation data.
