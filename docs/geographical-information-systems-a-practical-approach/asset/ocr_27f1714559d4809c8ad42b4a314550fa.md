When incoming diffuse radiation is assumed to be the same from all sky directions (for the uniform sky diffuse model):

```
$$ W_{\theta, \alpha} = \frac{\cos(\theta_2) - \cos(\theta_1)}{d_a} $$ (8.27)
```

where $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$ are the bounding zenith angles of the sky sector and $d_a$ is the number of azimuth divisions in the keymap. When diffuse radiation flux is considered to vary with zenith angle (for the standard overcast sky diffuse model):

```
$$ W_{\theta, \alpha} = \frac{2\cos(\theta_2) + \cos(2\theta_2) - 2\cos(\theta_1) - \cos(2\theta_1)}{4d_a} $$ (8.28)
```

With this model, global solar radiation is calculated as the sum of direct and diffuse irradiance (that is, $I_{dir} + I_{diff}$) from all sky directions over a given time interval, assuming that the contribution of reflected radiation is negligible.

The preceding process of the construction of a hemispherical viewshed, sunmap and skymap, identification of unobstructed sky sectors in the sunmap and skymap and calculation of direct, diffuse and global radiation can be repeated for every location on a topographic surface, resulting in a solar radiation or insolation map for a region. The ArcGIS Solar Radiation toolset allows incoming solar radiation to be derived for a set of specific locations (point-specific) or mapped for every location over a landscape (area-based).
