Landsat 8, launched in February 2013, carries two sensors: the Operational Land Imager (OLI) and the Thermal Infrared Sensor (TIRS). Landsat 9, launched on 27 September 2021, replaces Landsat 7 (launched in 1999), taking its place in orbit (eight days out of phase with Landsat 8). Landsat 9 carries two sensors that largely replicate the instruments on Landsat 8: the Operational Land Imager 2 (OLI-2) and the Thermal Infrared Sensor 2 (TIRS-2). The OLI sensor is similar to the ETM+ sensor but provides enhancements over

prior Landsat sensors by adding two more spectral bands: a deep-blue visible band (Band 1) designed for water resource and coastal zone investigation, and a new infrared band (Band 9) for cirrus cloud detection. It collects data at a 30 m resolution in eight short-wave spectral bands and in one panchromatic band at a 15 m resolution.
