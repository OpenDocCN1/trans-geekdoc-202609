# Box 5.1 Identification of the Critical Areas of Riparian Zones in the Woor Yallock Catchment, Victoria, Australia

Agricultural activities, settlements and septic tanks on rural properties are major sources of pollution of aquatic environments. These point and non-point source pollutions have significant impacts on the water quality of rivers and streams. Riparian vegetation can play a significant role in protecting water quality by filtering runoff, trapping and removing nutrients, sediment, pesticides and other chemicals originating on land. The establishment and maintenance of riparian vegetation zones or buffers have been considered as an effective means of preventing pollutants from reaching rivers and streams and maintaining water quality. Monash University conducted a study on the use of GIS in the identification of the critical areas of riparian zones in the Woor Yallock catchment, Victoria, Australia, which are susceptible to the threat of pollution from adjacent land uses and septic tanks. The study aimed to help build consensus among the stakeholder groups in the catchment in developing water resource management strategies and improve the decision-making process in identifying high-priority sites for riparian vegetation plantation or restoration.

The Woor Yallock catchment is located about 57 km to the north-east of Melbourne. The east portion of the catchment area is covered by a mix of forest, intensive horticulture and waters. The middle and northern part have been used for grazing and horticulture. Most of the urban areas are located in the west and south-west of the catchment. In this area, water resource is mainly used for domestic use, irrigation, stock, on-stream dam filling and off-stream dam filling. The key management issues for this catchment include the loss of native vegetation, bed and bank erosion, water quality deterioration, weeds, fish barriers and maintaining stream flows. Many parts of the river system (especially near the residential areas) are not protected by natural vegetation buffers, and the water quality standards leave the river health ratings rather low on the river health scale. Identifying the critical areas of riparian zones that are susceptible to the threat of pollution is the first step in addressing those environmental issues and developing the water resource management and biodiversity conservation strategies for the catchment.

The study involved three major activities: data preparation, delineation of riparian buffers and identification of critical areas.
