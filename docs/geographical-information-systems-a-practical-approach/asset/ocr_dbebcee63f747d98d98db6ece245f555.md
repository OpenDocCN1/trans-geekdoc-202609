CLARA is an extension to PAM to support k-medoids clustering for large datasets in order to reduce computing time. Instead of finding medoids for the entire dataset, this algorithm selects samples from the dataset, applies PAM on each sample and outputs the best clustering out of these samples (Kaufman and Rousseeuw 1990). This is more effective than PAM for a large dataset. However, the algorithm may give wrong clustering results if one or more sampled medoids are away from the actual best medoids. CLARANS deals with the concerns of both PAM and CLARA and maintains a balance between the computational cost and the influence of data sampling on clusters’ formation. In CLARANS, the process of finding k medoids from a dataset is viewed abstractly as searching through a certain graph (Ng and Han 2002). In the graph consisting of a set of connected nodes, a node is represented by a set of k locations (bin time series) as selected medoids. Two nodes are neighbours if their sets differ by only one location. In each iteration, CLARANS considers a sample of randomly chosen neighbour nodes instead of samples from the dataset and examines the neighbours of every node. It moves to the neighbour node if the neighbour is a better choice for medoids. Otherwise, a local optimum is discovered. The entire process is repeated multiple times to find the best local optimum choice of medoids. These medoids are then used for clustering.

# 6.7.3.1.2 Similarity in Shape

Time series are considered similar if they tend to stay in proportion to each other and increase and decrease in value at the same time. For example, cities can be clustered based on their population growth rates. Although their actual population sizes are quite different, cities will be grouped into clusters if they follow similar patterns of population growth. If such time series data are plotted as curves in a chart similar to that shown in Figure 6.24, the curves would have a similar shape. The degree of similarity in shape can be measured according to the linear correlation between two time series.
