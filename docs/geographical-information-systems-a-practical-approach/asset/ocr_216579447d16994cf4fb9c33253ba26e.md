+   - Moderate: when \( 5 \ge P_{post} / P_{priori} \ge 1 \) and \( P_{post} / \sigma(P_{post}) > 1.5 \)
+   - Low: when \( P_{post} / P_{priori} < 1 \) and \( P_{post} / \sigma(P_{post}) > 1.5 \)
+   - Uncertain: when \( P_{post} / \sigma(P_{post}) \le 1.5 \)

where \(\sigma(P_{post})\) is the standard deviation of the posterior probabilities. \( P_{post} / \sigma(P_{post}) \) is called studentised posterior probability.

WoF E modelling with GIS generally involves eight steps (Figure 9.10): (1) collecting evidence and generating evidence themes, (2) collecting training sites (sample data), (3) categorising the evidence themes, (4) calculating weights for each class on all evidence themes, (5) selecting significant evidence themes, (6) calculating posterior probabilities and creating the response theme, (7) conducting the test for conditional independence among the evidence themes and (8) validating the result.

Practical 9.2 provides an example of WoF E for landslide susceptibility assessment based on the evidence involving slope, distance to rivers, distance to faults, lithology, soils and land cover. Case Study 11 in Chapter 10 gives another example of habitat potential mapping with WoF E.
