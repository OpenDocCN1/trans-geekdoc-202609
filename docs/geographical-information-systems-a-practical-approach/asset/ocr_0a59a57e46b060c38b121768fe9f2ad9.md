# 1.3.1 Inventory and Monitoring

It is a well-accepted principle that spatial data and information at an appropriate spatial scale are required to underpin the complex decision-making in environmental and resource management and planning. A compilation of spatial data pertinent to a specific geographical area is called **spatial inventory**. Examples include land resource, land account, wetland, wildlife, pollutant and landslide inventories. A spatial inventory typically comprises data that represent both the thematic and locational attributes of environmental and socio-economic phenomena or resources. For example, an air emissions inventory is a database containing detailed information about pollutants discharged into the atmosphere by each source type during a given time period and at a specific location. A forest inventory contains forest attribute data for each forest stand in a given area, including stand attributes of a unique identifier, stand age, stand species, stand size, stocking, soil type, the level of reconnaissance (the method used to inventory the stand) and other forest attributes or variables, depending on the purpose of the inventory. Maps, imagery and related tabular data are the centrepiece of most spatial inventories. Traditionally, spatial inventories were stored in paper maps, orthophotographs and tally sheets and updated by aerial photography, field sampling

methods and manual drafting. GIS has been used to store, organise, document, retrieve, integrate, summarise, update and map inventory data since the 1960s, when early GIS systems were mainly developed for land and natural resource inventory. Modern GIS not only provides spatial database management tools for compiling, managing and maintaining spatial inventories but also allows the use of alternative source data, such as high-resolution digital satellite imagery and LiDAR data, to automatically capture inventory data over a large and/or inaccessible area, which would be infeasible to collect using traditional field inventory and mapping methods.

GIS-based spatial inventory ensures accurate reporting with improved data collection and supports monitoring key environmental and socio-economic variables that are essential for resource and environmental management. Spatial inventory is often a foundation for environmental and socio-economic analysis and modelling.
