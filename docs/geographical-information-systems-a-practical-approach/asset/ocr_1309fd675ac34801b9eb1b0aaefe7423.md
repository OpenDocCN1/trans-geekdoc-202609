# # CHAPTER 10 Applications of GIS

This chapter provides a selection of case studies of representative applications of GIS. It is by no means comprehensive but is designed to illustrate how GIS is applied to improve our understanding of environmental systems and to showcase GIS solutions to some environmental issues. The case studies are selected from the literature and cover the areas of hydrological modelling, land use analysis, atmospheric science research, ecological modelling and landscape valuation.

# ## Learning Objectives

After studying this chapter, you should be able to

+   - comprehend the breadth and versatility of GIS applications;
+   - explain how value can be added to various studies with GIS and spatial data;
+   - understand how GIS can be applied in hydrology, ecology, land use science, atmospheric science and other representative areas of environmental science;

+   - appreciate the roles of GIS in decision-making and
+   - develop your own applications of GIS.

# ### 10.1 Hydrological Modelling

Hydrology is concerned with the study of the movement, distribution and quality of water through the hydrological cycle, as well as the transport of constituents such as sediment and pollutants in the water as it flows. Numerous models have been developed for simulating hydrological systems in order to facilitate the understanding of hydrological processes. They range from full-catchment models dealing with all components of the hydrological cycle to smaller-scale models focusing on the processes in particular regions within a catchment, such as a hillslope. Some models describe the catchment as a single entity, assuming that rainfall falls evenly throughout the catchment, the hydrological parameters are uniform everywhere and the rainfall–runoff processes are linear.
