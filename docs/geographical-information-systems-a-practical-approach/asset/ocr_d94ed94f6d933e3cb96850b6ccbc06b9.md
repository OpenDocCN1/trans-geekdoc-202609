In summer, for example, the mean centre trended significantly north-eastward, away from Missouri and toward Iowa and Illinois. The mean centres in spring and autumn also migrated eastward over time, but the trends are not as noticeable or statistically significant.

The angle of rotation ($\theta$) represents the orientation of the major axis of the SDEs, about which tornado activity concentrates. The mean and median angles of rotation both varied between 85° and 98° in all seasons, indicating that tornado activity tended to orient around a north-west-to-south-east major axis. As revealed in Figure 6.9, the mean and median angles of rotation did not vary much across the seasons, but the angle of rotation was more variable in winter and autumn (winter: min=25.3°, max=141.7°, standard deviation (SD) = 25.1°; spring: min=58.6°, max=119.3°, SD = 15.3°; summer: min=75.2°, max=124.7°, SD = 12.2°; autumn: min=14.0°, max=149.3°, SD = 28.5°). The angle of rotation also changed over time, most notably in summer, when the angle increased (that is, rotated clockwise) at a rate of 0.25° per year between 1954 and 2017 (Figure 6.9g). The angle of rotation also increased in spring and autumn, but trends are not significant (Figure 6.9f, h). In winter, the angle of rotation was rather stable until the late 1990s, at which time a decline (that is, rotated counter-clockwise) is noticeable (Figure 6.9e).
