# CHAPTER 1

# GIS Overview

This chapter provides an overview of GIS. It introduces the history, general framework and functionality of GIS for problem-solving and decision-making, discusses the roles of GIS in research, management and planning and presents a brief introduction to several popular GIS software packages.

# Learning Objectives

After studying this chapter, you should be able to

+   - distinguish GIS from other types of computerised systems;
+   - understand the purposes for which GIS may be applicable;
+   - discuss the roles of GIS in research, management and planning;
+   - describe the process of problem-solving with GIS and
+   - outline the key features of a range of proprietary and open-source GIS software.

# 1.1 Introduction to GIS

The fundamental goal of environmental research and sustainability studies is to understand and solve environmental problems and sustainable development issues, focusing on how humans use natural resources, how human activities change the environment and what actions humans can take to solve environmental problems and support sustainable development. For example, an ecologist might like to study the consequences of landscape fragmentation caused by agricultural intensification and habitat loss for wildlife. A wildlife biologist might want to analyse the movement patterns of migratory animals. A geographer might be interested in land use changes and their environmental impacts in a region. A climatologist might want to predict the responses of ecosystems to climate change in order to provide options for sustainable landscape management.
