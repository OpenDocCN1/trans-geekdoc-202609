For instance, the time series (2, 1, 2, 1, 2) has very different values from the time series (20, 10, 20, 10, 20), but they are perfectly correlated as their point-by-point values are scaled. The correlation between the two time series is valued at 1. The degree of dissimilarity between two time series is calculated by subtracting the correlation from 1. Thus, time series that are perfectly positively correlated (correlation = 1) have a dissimilarity value of 0, time series that are uncorrelated (correlation = 0) have a dissimilarity value of 1 and time series that are perfectly negatively

correlated (correlation = -1) have a dissimilarity value of 2. Dissimilarity values range from 0 to 2. The larger the value, the higher the dissimilarity.
To cluster a set of time series into groups with high similarity in shape, a dissimilarity matrix needs to be created, whose elements are pair-wise dissimilarity values between two time series.
