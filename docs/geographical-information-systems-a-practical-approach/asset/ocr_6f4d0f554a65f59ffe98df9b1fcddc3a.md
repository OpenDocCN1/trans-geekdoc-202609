The greyscale or colour or intensity of the colour of a hexagon represents the Euclidean distance between the weight vector of the hexagon and the weight vectors of its immediate neighbouring hexagons. Conventionally, light colours depict closely spaced node weight vectors (that is, similar multivariate data vectors) and darker colours indicate more widely separated node weight vectors (that is, dissimilar multivariate data vectors). Thus, areas of light colours are considered as clusters, and the dark areas as the boundaries between the clusters. This kind of graphic display is called a unified distance matrix, commonly known as a U-matrix (Ultsch and Siemon 1990). It provides a similarity graph of the input data and can help visualise the clusters in the high-dimensional, multivariate datasets.
