Some of the zoom controls include + and – buttons, zoom level slider (such as the one in Figure 4.38), mouse wheel zoom, ‘Home’ icon to return to initial zoom state, double-click zoom and so on. The common method to navigate around the map is panning by dragging the mouse or by using the arrow keys.
In an online map, moving the mouse over or clicking on a map symbol may lead to further information or data shown in pop-ups. Pop-ups are small windows that appear in the foreground of the online map and display data or information about the features or locations on a layer where a mouseover or clicking event is fired. They are an equally essential and important part of crafting the complete map information experience. A general principle is to keep pop-ups concise and only have one on the screen at a time. They should appear where the user clicks or moves the mouse rather

than at the centre of the screen or the centre of the feature.
Usually, complicated online maps have a few filters or search functions. However, the panels should be kept to the absolute minimum size possible. Different panel opacity and colours need to be explored to make the panels to feel integrated with the map.

# 4.6.3.3 Layer Design

Layer design needs to consider the amount of data to be displayed. In order to speed up downloading and draw the data on the screen of a map user in sub-second speeds, the content of an online map should be simple or organised in separate layers, each brought in as one or more separate web services.
