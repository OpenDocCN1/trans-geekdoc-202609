This edition represents a complete overhaul of the practicals in the first edition. All the practicals have been re-written to use ArcGIS Pro and QGIS. ArcGIS Desktop used in the first edition will be phased out in 2026, and the users of the book will transition to ArcGIS Pro. QGIS is an increasingly popular open-source GIS. This is one of the main drivers for the second edition. With the new edition, readers can choose one of the GIS software packages, which they can access or prefer to use. In response to feedback from the reviewers on the first edition, all practical boxes from the text have been removed and placed at the book's support material together with the needed data so that all the practical materials can be updated when the new versions of the software are released. Practicals are provided for almost every topic, and they are flagged up close to the associated concepts or topics in the text to bring practicals on the book's companion site to reader's attention. To get the most out of this book, readers are encouraged to follow the practicals in this book. This 'learning by doing' approach will help students become capable and competent practitioners and support scientists and practitioners to enhance their knowledge and skills with new GIS techniques and tools.

In addition, case study and technical boxes have been updated, highly technical explanations in the text have been moved to new technical boxes, web links have been updated and inactive web links have been removed and review questions for new contents have been added.

# Organisation of the Book

The book is organised into ten chapters. Chapters 1–9 cover the fundamental concepts, principles and techniques in spatial data, spatial data management, spatial visualisation, spatial analysis, spatial statistics, remote sensing data analysis, terrain analysis and spatial decision analysis and modelling. Each of these chapters includes examples and practicals that show how the GIS principles, techniques and tools introduced can be implemented. The order of the chapters allows the book to smoothly take readers from the basics of GIS to advanced topics. Although boxed case studies are provided in the first nine chapters, Chapter 10 contains a collection of more detailed case studies organised under the themes of hydrological modelling, land use analysis and modelling, atmospheric modelling, ecological modelling and ecosystem service valuation. These case studies, although not covering all application areas, show some typical environmental applications of GIS with an emphasis on how GIS has been used in the analysis, modelling or solution processes. They are fully cross-referenced to relevant sections in the text and provide additional case studies to those sections.
