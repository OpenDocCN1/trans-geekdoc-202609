Temporal object types may be classified into two kinds: once only and repeatable. A *once-only temporal object* is either a single *instant* or a single *period* of time, measured to a specified temporal granularity (relative to an assumed calendar and time zone). Some simple examples are depicted in Fig. 10.9(a). For example, Albert Einstein was born in the year 1879 CE. In this sense, a year is a 12-month period in the time stream. Likewise, a date is a period of 1 day in the time stream (e.g., 2023 June 5), a month is a period of approximately 30 days in the time stream (e.g., 2023 June), an hour is a period of 60 minutes (e.g., 2023 June 5, 9–10 a.m.).

A time is an instant in the time stream measured to a specified granularity, such as a second, for example, 2023-06-05 14:35:27 (i.e., 2023 June 5, 14h 35m 27 s). Object types such as these are useful for recording when an individual event happened or will happen (e.g., the birth of Einstein, or the next Olympic Games).

Each *repeatable temporal object* corresponds to a set of instants or time periods, measured to a specified granularity (relative to an assumed time zone). Some simple examples in ORM notation are depicted in Fig. 10.9(b). Here, a weekday is a day slot of any week excluding the weekend. (e.g., Monday), a MonthOfYear instance is a month slot of any year (e.g., January, or Month 1), and an hour slot is a 1-h slot of a day. Object types such as these are useful when modeling schedules (e.g., a workout routine or a conference program).

Periods may be modeled in more detail by specifying their start and end times to the desired temporal granularity (year, month, day, hour, etc.). For example, if periods are closed (we know both start and end) and the granularity is day, then they may be modeled in ORM as shown in Fig. 10.10(a) and in UML as Fig. 10.10(b).

*Intervals* (unanchored durations of time) may be modeled as a simple object type with a unit for the chosen granularity. For example, if we measure ages of fossils in year units, this may be modeled in ORM as shown in Fig. 10.11(a) and in UML as shown in Fig. 10.11(b). In ORM, the colon “:” indicates that “y” is a unit of a given type (display can be expanded to “y: Interval”).
