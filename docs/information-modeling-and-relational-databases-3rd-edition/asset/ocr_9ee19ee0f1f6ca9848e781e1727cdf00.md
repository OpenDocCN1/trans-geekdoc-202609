We are assuming here that the letter consists mainly of “boilerplate” text with the client information, the case reference, and dates inserted at the appropriate points. To identify the relevant facts, the activity requires two primary objects to be identified: a Case and a LetterType. From the Case, we can uniquely identify the Client and hence the Client’s Title, Name, and Address. From the LetterType, we can identify the appropriate text to form the body of the letter. The current date is assumed to be available from the activity’s environment. This information is sufficient for the activity to create and send the standard letter.

Fig. 15.42 shows a relational view of the information. This can be used as the basis for defining the necessary bridge between process and information.

From the process perspective, the necessary link is provided by the SendLetter Data Store reference in the process model of Fig. 15.40. The SendStandardLetter activity requires Read access (R) to the LetterType, Client, and Case relations but full access (F) to the Client-WasSentLetterOnDate relation.
