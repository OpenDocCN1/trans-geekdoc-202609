Fig. 3.15 includes a sample population. No matter how high the *arity* (number of roles) of the fact type, we can easily populate it for checking purposes. Each column in the fact table is associated with one role in the predicate.

An earlier example transformed unaries into a binary. Another kind of schema transformation is **objectification** or **nesting**. This effectively *treats a relationship between objects as an object itself*. Consider again the top row of Table 3.9. Instead of expressing this as a single sentence, we might convey the information in two sentences:

> Student “1001” enrolled in Course “CS100”.
> This Enrollment resulted in Rating 4.

Here, “This Enrollment” refers back to the enrollment relationship between the specific person and the specific subject mentioned in the first sentence. Any such enrollment may be treated as an object in its own right.

The act of making an object out of a relationship is called *objectification* or *reification* and corresponds to the linguistic act of nominalization (making a noun out of a verb phrase).
