```
Role( roleId, predicateId, [roleName] )
r4   P2      name
```

This relational notation underlines uniqueness constraints; if more than one exists, the primary key is doubly underlined. Optional columns are marked with square brackets. The external uniqueness constraint appears as a compound internal uniqueness constraint. Because we only require roles to have unique names in a predicate if they are named, the conceptual join between the roles of Role in Fig. 10.2(a) is an inner join, resulting in the single row population shown.

Using "?" to depict a null value, the outer join result would include four tuples: (r1, P1, ?), (r2, P1, ?), (r3, P2, ?), and (r4, P2, name). The first two tuples would then violate the compound uniqueness constraint, so outer join semantics should not be used with this example.

The external uniqueness constraints in Fig. 10.2(b) and (c) are depicted with an inner "o" through the uniqueness bar. This indicates that the external uniqueness constraint has outer join semantics, with the added proviso that nulls produced in the outer join are treated as actual values.

Hence, each course may be referenced by exactly one of the following patterns: the combination of the course title and its department (where the course is offered by a department); or its course title (where the course is offered by no department, e.g., a course offered by a visiting lecturer).

External uniqueness constraints of this nature are verbalized in two sentences, one for the inner join semantics and one for the additional semantics of the outer join. The external uniqueness constraint in Fig. 10.2(b) verbalizes thus:

+   **For each CourseTitle and Department**
+   at most one Course has that CourseTitle and is offered by that Department.
+   
+   **For each CourseTitle**
+   at most one Course has that CourseTitle and is offered by no Department.

Consider a sample population with three courses, C1, C2, and C3, with the same title, one offered by the physics department, one by the mathematics department, and one offered by no department. The join of the title and department fact types results in a compound ternary fact type equivalent to the following relational table scheme. The external uniqueness constraint appears as the internal uniqueness constraint spanning courseTitle and departmentCode.

```
Course( courseCode, courseTitle, [departmentCode] )
C1 Mechanics PY
C2 Mechanics MA
C3 Mechanics ?
C4 Mechanics ?   -- violates constraint
```
