It is with great enthusiasm and profound honour that I contribute to the foreword of the third edition of this exceptional book, a comprehensive and illuminating guide to information modeling and relational databases, underscoring its profound importance in the realm of enterprise modeling and data management. Even more in this new edition, one cannot help but acknowledge the sheer breadth and depth of knowledge included within its pages.

This book serves as an indispensable resource for comprehending the object-role modeling (ORM) language and its fact-oriented modeling methodology. ORM stands as a beacon in the realm of data modeling, offering a rigorous and yet user-friendly approach. This book meticulously dissects the semantic complexity of enterprises with strict attention to detail. The authors’ dedication to exploring and evaluating various conceptual modeling notations against ORM sets this book apart, providing readers with a panoramic view of the landscape of data modeling paradigms while presenting an accurate understanding of conceptual modeling and evidence of the advantages of ORM.

The chapters clarify the nuances of conceptual modeling, emphasizing the necessary precision of a modeling language. As someone from a logic-based knowledge representation and reasoning background, I greatly appreciate the formal rigor of the ORM notation, where ambiguities and sloppiness find no place, unlike many other (widely known) conceptual modeling notations. Furthermore, the book doesn’t merely describe the precise meaning of the modeling constructs but also elucidates why these constructs exist in the language. A meticulous analysis of their use cases and a methodology for their design hold equal importance in the book.

I find it fascinating how ORM offers a powerful and precise yet understandable conceptual modeling approach, and of course also how ORM is presented in this book in a precise yet understandable manner. As expected, the fact-oriented modeling methodology associated with ORM is thoroughly detailed in a clear way and serves as another key pillar of this book. It guides the understanding of data semantics and the design of data structures that authentically and correctly mirror the business reality and needs. The book focuses also quite a lot on the relationship between a conceptual model and its implementation in some database structure, emphasizing correctness and effectiveness while not forgetting the intricacies of real-world SQL.

The fact-oriented modeling methodology as explained in the previous editions of the book has influenced a lot of my research. Indeed, it was after a first reading of this book that I started working on the intersection between classical logic-based knowledge representation and ORM with its fact-oriented modeling methodology. I was also influenced by the textual version of the ORM language (FORML), also discussed in this book. I met Terry Halpin to discuss the outcome of my research, only to realize that there was much more for me to learn beforehand! Terry is a humble scientist who, after every discussion, always seems to be right.

The lifelong dedication of Terry Halpin and Tony Morgan to developing enterprise modeling concepts substantiates the significance of this work. The updated and expanded coverage, along with a plethora of exercises embedded in the chapters, enhance the book’s value. The book stays current with the latest versions and trends, ensuring readers have access to the most recent developments in the field. The content encompassing ORM, ER, UML, SQL, and OWL has undergone comprehensive updates, incorporating their most recent iterations. Additional chapters addressing NoSQL technologies, such as key-value, column-oriented, and graph databases, have been integrated in this edition of the book. A chapter covering contemporary popular data file formats, such as XML and JSON, has been added. Extensive revisions have been made to all other chapters, enhancing the depth of coverage for numerous topics.

Finally, I want to emphasize the book’s relevance to a diverse audience, including data modelers, data engineers, data analysts, enterprise architects, and programmers, highlighting its inclusivity and accessibility. The incorporation of exercises throughout the book offers readers an interactive and immersive learning experience, fostering a deeper understanding of conceptual modeling.

## Foreword

by Dr. Herman Baltsters
Partner at NorthChain, Groningen, The Netherlands.

I first became acquainted with ORM in 2005 at an ORM Workshop in Cyprus as part of the OTM Confederated International Workshops and Conference. I had just presented a paper on data federations at the OODB conference and out of pure curiosity, I paid a short visit to the ORM Workshop to learn a bit more about the ORM language. When I wandered in, Terry Halpin was offering a presentation. I was amazed with the lively discussions that went on during this presentation, and was surprised not only by the enthusiasm of the workshop participants but also by the high quality of comments and questions. Instead of paying only a short visit, I stayed at the ORM Workshop during the complete remainder of my stay in Cyprus, and I have been a great fan of ORM ever since.

During my work as a researcher and associate professor at the University of Groningen, I was heavily involved with languages such as unified modeling language (UML) and business process model and notation (BPMN), trying to capture the essence of information modeling. I had a constant struggle with these languages, which were considered state of the art, because I just could not get a full grasp of what models written in these languages really meant. I have a background in pure mathematics (in particular, mathematical logic) and computer science, and I wanted to have a clear idea of the semantics and completeness of the data and process models that I was trying to specify. I also wanted to see a clear connection between a model and how that model is implemented. In both aspects, I felt uncomfortable with the prevailing modeling languages of that time. And it was not only in research that I felt this lack of precision and completeness, but also in teaching. Nothing is harder to explain to students than something that you as a teacher do not completely understand.

All that changed the day I entered into that ORM Workshop in Cyprus. It took me a while to master all the details of ORM, but after that, I was able to actually write models with complete understanding and with such precision that the models could directly be tied to implementations. Even today, I still use ORM with deep respect and satisfaction.

This means that you specify objects and relations between these objects, resulting in certain statements about the world. ORM does this in such a way that even nontechnical domain experts will be able to model facts about their domain of expertise.
