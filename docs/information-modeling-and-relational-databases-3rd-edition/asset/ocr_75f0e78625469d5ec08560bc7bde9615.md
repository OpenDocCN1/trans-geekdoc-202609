Once you objectify the ternary, the NORMA tool requires one of the two UCs to be preferred, and displays the preferred UC with a double uniqueness bar. Here, SportRanking is primarily identified by the athlete, sport combination, but NORMA requires the athlete, sport, and rank to identify each sport ranking so the fact entry is not atomic. To avoid confusion and nonatomic fact entries, we prefer to model such cases objectification of a ternary with a nonspanning UC, using one of the approaches shown in Fig. 10.54.

Now consider objectification of an n:1 fact type with multiple roles played by the objectification. Suppose we need to record for each moon the planet it orbits and the period of that orbit. Optionally, we also record for one or both of the axes (minor and major) of that elliptical orbit.

Fig. 10.55(a) models this case in ORM by treating MoonOrbit as an objectification of the Moon orbits Planet association. Fig. 10.55(b) shows an equivalent model in UML.

If you enter the ORM schema in Fig. 10.55(a) in NORMA, it internally creates implicit fact types linking MoonOrbit to Moon and Planet, using the 1:1 link fact type MoonOrbit involves Moon as the preferred identification scheme for MoonOrbit. There is no need to drag the link fact types onto the drawing window. Although a moon orbit is identified by just its moon, populating the period and axis fact types requires both the moon and planet to identify each moon orbit.

Fig. 10.55(c) shows the relational schema view NORMA generates from the ORM model in Fig. 10.55(a). The moonName primary key clearly shows that NORMA identifies a moon's orbit just by the moon. Personally, we do not like the practice of hiding the preferred identification scheme at the conceptual level, and we prefer to model such cases in ORM without objectification, using an alternative schema such as that shown in Fig. 10.55(c) or (d).

However, some users might still prefer the objectification approach over such alternatives. Objectification of a binary or longer fact with just one UC spanning all but one role where the objectification plays other multiple roles is the only kind of case where one might possibly have a legitimate reason to ignore our advice to never objectify an association without a spanning UC.
