Note that although Fred and Sue both own car 123BOM, only Fred drives it. Perhaps it is a “bomb” of a car and Sue refuses to drive it! Tina owns a car but doesn’t drive it (maybe she has a chauffeur). Tom neither owns nor drives a car.

For the population shown, people own every car that they drive. Although unusual, for discussion purposes let us assume the sample database is significant in this respect. This rule is indicated in Fig. 6.23 by the *pair-subset constraint* running from the middle of the Drives predicate to the middle of the Owns predicate. This asserts that, for each state of the database, the set of (Person, Car) pairs represented in the Drives fact table is a subset of the set of pairs listed in the Owns table. We may think of each pair as a tuple of values from a row of a fact table.
