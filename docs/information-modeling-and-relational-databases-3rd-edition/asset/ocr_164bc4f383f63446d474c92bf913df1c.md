# Foreword

by Dr. Gordon C. Everest, Professor Emeritus of MIS and “Data” Modeling Carlson School of Management, University of Minnesota, USA.

I am delighted and honored to again write a foreword, this time to the third edition of this book. It gives me another opportunity to convince those in the world of data modeling that there is a better way. I am absolutely convinced that object-role modeling (ORM) is a better way to do data modeling. My goal here is to sufficiently perk your interest to seriously study ORM. This book is the best resource available.

The Greek philosopher Socrates advised “Know thyself” to live a worthwhile life. Similarly, we can say “Know your world”, the reality in which you live, operate, and make decisions. Because it is impossible for any one person to know everything about their world, we need a representation of that world, a model that captures information about the things, relationships, activities/processes, and rules in that world. This representation consists of two parts: the definition of the structure and format, called the schema, and the actual data stored consistent with its definition. A collection of “databases” of information becomes an important surrogate for that world.

We make decisions in and about our world based on the surrogate databases we build. That is an awesome responsibility and thus demands a high degree of integrity, quality, and availability of our data stores. That requires a data modeling schema that can capture a rich set of semantics, and ORM is the best of anything out there today.

The use of the term “data modeling” is often misleading. It suggests we are modeling data, when we are really modeling the things and relationships (objects and roles in relationships) in some user domain of interest—modeling in data, using data constructs, which eventually becomes a “data” model after the model is populated with data.

Data modeling is the foundation of information systems development—if you don’t get the database design “right”, then the systems you build will be like a house of cards, collapsing under the weight of inevitable future forces for enhancement, integration, and quality improvement. Thus, we need a scheme to guide our data modeling efforts to produce data models that clearly and accurately represent the users’ domain of discourse and facilitate human communication, a shared understanding, and validation for quality control. The people who live in and know the user domain must be directly involved in the modeling process, supported by data modeling specialists who understand the design methodology.
