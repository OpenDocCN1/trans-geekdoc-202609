Fig. 7.25(a) shows one way to display three textual rules. Rule 1 declares that employees are hired after their birth. This is the same as the value-comparison constraint in Fig. 7.24(a), but in Fig. 7.25(a) the constraint is expressed textually. The footnote number 1 is appended to the name of the Employee type. A pair-exclusion constraint also exists between the birth and hire predicates, but is omitted because it is now implied.

Rule 2 says that nonexecutives use at most one company car. This is a restricted uniqueness constraint because it strengthens the uniqueness constraint on Employee uses CompanyCar, but restricts this to a subtype of Employee.

Rule 3 says that each executive must have use of a company car. This is a restricted mandatory role constraint because it makes an optional Employee role mandatory for a subtype of Employee.

Fig. 7.25(b) shows another way to model the restricted mandatory role constraint by explicitly defining Executive as a subtype of Employee, and adding a subset constraint from the subtype link (Executive is an Employee) to the Employee role in Employee uses CompanyCar to declare that each executive must play the role of using a company car.
