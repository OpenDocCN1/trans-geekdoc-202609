In this example, the nested or coreferenced object type plays just one fact role, and this role is mandatory. In such a situation, the relational mapping is the same whether we nest, coreference, or flatten. In this case, each maps to the table scheme Result (studentNr, subjectcode, rating ). So, we may choose which of the three approaches appeals most to us. Although this choice is partly subjective, there are a few guidelines that may be helpful.

First, let's consider choosing between nesting and coreferencing. Both approaches always have the same relational mapping, no matter what the situation. Choose what seems most “natural”. If in thinking about the real world, you first “see” a relationship and later want to talk about it, then nesting is probably the best choice. On this basis, more people would probably nest Enrollment rather than coreference it. However, it often seems unnatural to think of a visible object as an objectified relationship.
