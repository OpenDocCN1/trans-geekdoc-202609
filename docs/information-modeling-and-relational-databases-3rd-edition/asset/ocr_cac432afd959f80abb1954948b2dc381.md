As explained in Section 10.8, an arrow from a subtype to a supertype displays a type-level relationship that implies instance-level, identity relationships linking subtype and supertype instances. So, the four subtype-to-supertype connections in Fig. 11.44 may be understood as implicit link fact types displayed in Fig. 11.46. For example, the subtyping link from StudentEmployee to Employee implies the link fact type StudentEmployee is Employee. The implicit is-associations between Student and Person and between Employee and Person allow us to express facts such as “The employee with EmployeeNr 23 is the person with PersonNr 507” and “The Person with PersonNr 507 is the Student with StudentNr 300”.

By default, derived fact types are not stored. Instead, their derivation rules are used to compute the derived values on request. Apart from saving storage space, this “derive on query” (lazy evaluation) approach ensures that every time the derived information is requested, it will be based on the latest values of the stored data.
