Another way to support negated instances of an open unary is to add a separate unary fact type with the opposite meaning (its instances are equivalent to negated instances of the original unary). The opposite meaning implies an exclusion constraint between the two unaries. For example, to record that student 1002 is not intelligent, include that student in the population of the fact type Student isUnintelligent where there is an exclusion constraint between its role and the role of the Student isIntelligent fact type.

A third option is to replace the unary with a third way to model. Suppose that we know that Australia (AU) and the United States (US) are monolingual and that Canada is multilingual, but don’t know the language status of Cyprus. This corresponds to the OWN approach, as modeled directly in Fig. 10.60(a). The rest of Fig. 10.60 shows alternative ways to model this in ORM and UML.

A detailed procedure for mapping unaries for the various open/closed world approaches is discussed in Chapter 11.

In principle, support for the various closure options could be extended beyond unary fact types, but this would add considerable complexity, especially for nonfunctional fact types.
