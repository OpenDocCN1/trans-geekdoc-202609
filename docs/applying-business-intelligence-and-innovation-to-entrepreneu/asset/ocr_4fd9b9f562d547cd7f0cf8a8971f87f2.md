Thus, it is urgent to formulate the concept of salon and spa Sharia amid the development of health tourism under secularisation, where there is an attempt to separate religion from the various aspects of human life, including tourism and health.

Islamic Practices Spa provides spa services, beauty therapists based on sex, different rooms and services for male and female clients, and services per Islamic law (Yaman et al., 2012). Based on this, Bafadhal (2017) states, “definition of sharia salons and spas are a form of business that provides promotive and preventive wellness services and products combining aesthetic, cosmetics, and spiritual treatments of the body, face, hair, and soul for both men and women, especially Muslims in conformity with Islamic law as the main determinant.”

Even so, scholars still differ in opinions about the halal or haram of salons and spas. Some scholars consider treating and dressing in salons and spas are wasteful, and it is feared to lead to haram actions such as imitating the barbarian “Jahiliyah style.” Furthermore, suppose the salons and spas employ male staff. In that case, Khalwat (excessive closeness between persons of the opposite sex and illicit contact with the opposite sex), which is forbidden in Islam, may occur. Ulama and Fatwa Council, headed by Sheikh Abdullah ibn Baz, once issued a Fatwa regarding the Haram of the beard-shaving profession, as the profession is helping people sinning. Therefore, the salon services can be said to be haram as it generally provides various haram services such as hair extension and eyebrow trimming, tattooing, and changing Allah creation, that is haram for Muslims according to Islamic law (Hadith narrated At-Tirmidzi).

Some Islamic scholars also consider having treatments in a public bath or spa haram if done at home. At-Tirmidzi narrated Hadith that the Prophet said, “Whoever believes in Allah , and the last day, his wife should not enter the public baths. “At-Tirmidzi narrated that some women from Homs or Syria entered upon ‘Aisyah رضي الله عنها and she said, “Are you the ones whose women folk go to the public baths? I heard Muhammad , the Messenger of Allah , say, “There is no woman who takes off her garments anywhere but in the house of her husband, but she has torn the veil that is between her and her lord.” However, if the treatments cannot be done at home, it is permissible for women to go to public baths. The women must still be careful and keep the aurat (body parts that the opposite sex must cover) closed.
