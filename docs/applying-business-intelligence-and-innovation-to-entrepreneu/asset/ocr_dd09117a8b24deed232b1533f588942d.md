Then, the large number of Muslims in the world may be assumed that every Muslim has the potential to be facilitated as consumer needs. Although Muslim health tourism facilities, products, and services are almost the same as conventional ones, adjustment is still necessary for understanding markets’ demand, product development, segmentation, and marketing (Bafadhal, 2022). Therefore, health tourism must focus on sub-sectors or niches because health tourists do not seek the same service (Smith & Puczkó, 2014).

In addition, consumers of the halal industry are universal with shared values. Consumers of the halal industry are mainly Muslims but also include non-Muslims of the same values. However, business in Islam is essentially universal and can be enjoyed by Muslim and non-Muslim consumers. This is because Islam is raḥmatan lil alamin (a mercy to all creation), a religion that respects human beings. Muslim health tourism specialises in delivering healthcare products and services to Muslim customers. However, it is undeniable that Muslim health tourism also attracts non-Muslim tourists who want to enrich their health, knowledge, and travel experiences. This then leads to the term “borderless patient” for health tourists. Muslim health tourism is, therefore, not just an issue of ethics and equity at a personal level. More inclusive health marketing towards the Muslim community is a pivotal part of global environmental and public health (Bafadhal, 2022). Making the demand for halal products become a religious obligation and a powerful market force. It has been established that boosting Muslim travelers’ mental health and satisfaction is vital for minimizing the impact of inconvenient tourism experiences (Han et al., 2021).

Countries of non-Muslim majority are expected to maintain tolerance and to keep the faith of Muslim tourists visiting those countries by providing Muslim-friendly facilities, services, and accommodations. The availability of Islamic attributes will not cause inconvenience to non-Musltos to any extent. Managers of tourist destinations can provide services that meet the needs of Muslims by allocating unique places and facilities for Muslims. Thus, Islamic branding within a destination or tourist product would not become merely a name to attract the Muslim market (Battour & Ismail, 2015). Hence, both Muslim and non-Muslim tourists may decide not to travel to a healthcare provider if there are strict rules, specific attributes, and restrictions. Tourism operators face a more significant challenge, particularly those promoting halal tourism destinations (Rahman et al., 2020).
