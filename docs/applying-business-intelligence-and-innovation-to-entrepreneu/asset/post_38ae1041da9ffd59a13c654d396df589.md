
## LITERATURE REVIEW AND HYPOTHESES DEVELOPMENT

The variables most widely used in the literature to describe the impact of corporate governance on ESG performance are related to board size, board gender diversity, the number of board meetings per year, board compensation, and the score of ESG controversies. In the sub-sections below, we develop our hypotheses for each of these characteristics of corporate governance.

## Board Size

Board size has mostly been studied as to its relationship to firm performance. Empirical studies can be classified into two categories: a first stream of research is in favour of smaller boards, whereas a second school of thought advocates larger boards. Since the firms in our sample are listed, and are therefore large and complex financial institutions that work in a context of intense regulation, we expect that larger boards will be more efficient, especially in terms of workload and responsibility allocation, and of greater diversity in terms of stakeholder representation, when improving ESG performance (Albitar et al., 2020; Zubeltzu-Jaka et al., 2020; Arayssi et al., 2020). Our hypothesis agrees with the findings of previous studies that show a positive relationship between board size and the breadth of sustainability practices (Baraiabar-Diez & Odriozola, 2019; Harjoto & Wang, 2020; Albitar et al., 2020). Based on the previous considerations, the following hypothesis is proposed for testing:

**Hypothesis 1.** *Board size affects positively the ESG performance.*

## Board Meetings

For the number of board meetings per year, which is usually used as a proxy for the level of board activity and board diligence (Baraiabar-Diez & Odriozola, 2019; Bravo & Reguera-Alvarado, 2019), there is also no consensus about impact on non-financial performance. Since the firms in our sample work in a dynamic business environment, which requires increased meeting frequency to ensure legitimacy, legitimacy theory provides the basis for proposing a positive relationship between board meeting frequency and ESG performance. Our hypothesis aligns with the studies that find a positive relationship between the number of board meetings and sustainability practices (Ismail et al., 2019; Bravo & Reguera-Alvarado, 2019; García Martín & Herrero, 2020). Based on the previous considerations, the following hypothesis is tested:

**Hypothesis 2.** *The number of board meetings affects positively the ESG performance.*

## Women on the Board

Interpretations of the relationship between women on the board and ESG performance are connected to the various features of the women themselves. There seems to be a general consensus in the literature regarding the positive impact of female directors on sustainability performance. More in detail, women on boards positively impact the firm’s charitable contributions (Bettinelli et al., 2019; Furlotti et al., 2019; Abd Rahman et al., 2019), apprehension for climate change (Birindelli et al., 2019) and reputation-based CSR measures (Ajaz et al., 2020). Similarly, many studies find that board gender diversity increases the extent of social and environmental reporting and decreases environmental lawsuits (Yuan et al., 2019; Peng, 2020). A likely explanation of these inconsistent results could be the presence of a non-linear relationship between board gender diversity and social and environmental performance. Based on their findings, some studies such those of (Birindelli et al., 2019; Furlotti et al., 2019; Ajaz et al., 2020; Peng, 2020) point out that a high level of presence of women on the board improve the ESG performance. In consideration of this evidence, we advance the following hypothesis:

*Hypothesis 3. A high level of gender diversity affects positively the ESG performance.*

## Executive Compensation

The provision of ESG based sustainable compensation policy is likely to play a critical role in motivating corporate executives to undertake carbon initiatives, which can in turn enhance both organizational legitimacy and market value. As part of sustainable corporate strategies, corporations are increasingly using sustainable compensation policy to motivate executives to pursue social and environmental projects. Empirically, Baraiabar-Diez et al. (2019); Eklund (2019); Haque & Ntim (2020) find that sustainable compensation schemes improve the ESG performance. We argue, therefore, that in the presence of a sustainable compensation policy, the board of directors and the compensation committee of the board might be in a better position to evaluate the environmental, social and governance attributes of a firm to design a more effective which may enhance corporate carbon performance. Therefore, any symbolic ESG compensation policy, without requiring explicit guidelines and mandatory emission reduction targets, might enhance process-oriented ESG performance of the firm (Westling & Mazhari, 2019; Baraiabar-Diez et al., 2019). Thus, we advance the following hypothesis:

**Hypothesis 4.** Executive compensation affects positively the ESG performance.

## ESG Controversies

ESG score is traditionally seen as a tool to influence stakeholders. The topic of the ESG controversies related to environmental, social and governance issues was investigated in the literature because of the assumed negative impact that the ESG problems or weaknesses may have on the corporate financial results and the need to deal with them (Iamandi et al., 2018; Markopoulos et al., 2020; Li et al., 2020). The more a company is seen as virtuous, the more likely it is to be appreciated by consumers, employees, and investors. Most importantly, when such virtues are disclosed by external sources such as the media, they build both reputation and credibility (Javedt et al., 2020). The ESG controversies are usually based on public or media disclosure of ESG-related corporate scandals, Utz (2019); Aouadi & Marsat (2018) interestingly found a negative impact of ESG controversies over the firm ESG performance. Taking as a whole, one could expect the following hypothesis.

**Hypothesis 5.** ESG controversies have a negative impact on ESG performance.

## RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

### Sample Selection and Data Sources

Our initial sample was based on the availability of ESG data in the Thomson Reuters Asset4 database and Thomson Reuters Eikon. We started with all the available sample of 35000 firm-year observations from nonfinancial firms listed on the main stock exchanges of 60 countries included in the panel A of table 1. We then removed 4960 observations with missing firm-level data relating to the dependent or independent variables used in our explanatory models. Our final sample is based on an unbalanced panel dataset consisting of 31040 firm-year observations from 5500 listed firms, covering a period of 16 years (2002–2017). Table 1 shows the annual- and country-wise distribution of the sample. We used the Asset4 dataset and the Thomson Reuters Eikon to collect ESG score and corporate governance data. The ASSET4 database from Thomson Reuters is utilized in our research because it is readily available for many investors and scholars.

### Dependent Variable
