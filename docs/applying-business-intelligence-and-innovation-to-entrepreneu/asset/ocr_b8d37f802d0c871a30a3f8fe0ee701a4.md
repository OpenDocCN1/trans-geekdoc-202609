Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) holds significant importance in social sciences research because it analyzes complex relationships among observed and latent variables. The substantive use of SEM in psychology and social sciences highlights its growing importance in empirical research (Anderson & Gerbing, 1988). Moreover, SEM emerged as a key statistical tool in social sciences and medical research, indicating its widespread adoption for improved analysis (Shaheen et al., 2017). Moreover, recent research highlights the wide usage of SEM in social sciences research, particularly in attitudes, behavior, and performance measurement (Al-Qadasi et al., 2021; Ehidiamen et al., 2023). Some authors also discussed the methodological advancements and guidelines for using SEM, such as the updated guidelines for using PLS path modeling in new technology (Ali et al., 2018; Hair, 2017; Henseler et al., 2016). These methodological advancements contribute to refining and standardizing SEM practices in social sciences research. Thus, the application of SEM in exploring and validating performance measurement domains and modeling predictors of environmental sustainability by forming path analysis demonstrates its versatility and relevance in this research.
