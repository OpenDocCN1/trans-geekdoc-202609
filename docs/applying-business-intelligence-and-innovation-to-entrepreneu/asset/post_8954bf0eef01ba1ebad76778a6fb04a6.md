
## MUSLIM HEALTH TOURIST BEHAVIOUR MODEL

Muslim health tourism behavior model is a reasonably straightforward means of identifying the level of Muslim tourist commitment towards selecting and purchasing a product or service for health tourism both for the purposes of preventing disease (Muslim wellness tourism) and treating disease (Muslim medical tourism). Making this model more specific and different from other types of halal tourism. Amid the urgency to understand Muslim health tourists as a potential market with such unique characteristics, terms, and specific needs, it is unfortunate that we have not developed a unique model that describes the decision-making behaviour of Muslim health tourists regarding choosing a Muslim-friendly health destination. Therefore, the author proposes a “Muslim Health Tourist Behavior Model,” shown in Figure 1. The model shows three main things underlying the behaviour of Muslim health tourists. The three are the testament of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ (Hadith narrated At-Tirmidzi). As part of worship, Allah ﷻ commands healthy travel to increase the balance in the vertical relationship (humans and Allah ﷻ), the horizontal relationship (humans to humans), and the diagonal (humans and nature).

The three relationships consist of the following. First, it is called Habluminaallah (God-driven). The significant driving force is:
- religious piety (Taqwa)
- religious obedience (Taat)
- piousness (Saleh)

each of which is of different levels. These are the main drivers of Muslims to choose Muslim-friendly health travel over conventional health travel or even controversial one, as to maintain the relationship with Allah ﷻ. Second, it is called Habluminnannas (socio-driven). These factors contribute to human relationships, including:
- stimuli of Muslim-friendly strategic marketing, such as product quality, promotion, price, location, image, halal and sharia compliance services, and so forth;
- Muslim community references which refer to recommendation from the Muslim community that may include nuclear family, peers, community, scholars and even the government—as the duty of all Muslims are Amar makruf nahi munker (enjoying the good and forbid the evil); and
- the motive of Muamalah transactions (business) for health purposes also affect the behavior of Muslims.

Third, it is known as habluminal’alam (environment-driven), which refers to the relationship between humans and nature, i.e., the location and the destination of health tourism. The things to consider include:
- the merits of a health destination, in which the more efficacious treatment or prevention of disease in that destination, the more encouraging it is for tourists to visit;
- Muslim-friendly health destination also plays an essential, in which the better the facility accommodates the needs of Muslim tourists, the more encouraging it is for Muslim tourists to choose.

Muslim health tourist behavior model is formed through three stages. First is the pre-decision stage, taking the form of needs recognition. When Muslims are ill, they may decide to take corrective action through Muslim medical tourism. Meanwhile, when Muslims are in good health and want to improve their health to avoid diseases, they can choose to perform preventive actions through Muslim wellness tourism.

Second, it is the decision stage. This stage starts with:
- *niyyah* (worship intention), in which every Muslim must see and do any activities as a form of worship to Allah ﷻ. Hence, it is necessary to straighten the intention to worship Allah ﷻ in the form of health tourism.

- evaluation of *Maslahah* (profit here and in the afterlife) by selecting products of good utility. At the same time, the products do not violate Islamic law for them to be blessed by Allah ﷻ.

## DISCUSSION AND FINDINGS

The current study’s findings aim to provide an overview of one of the Muslim Health Tourism services to prevent disease (wellness), particularly Sharia Salon and Spa. Islam teaches people to beautify themselves in a simple and balanced way because Allah ﷻ in Holy Qur’an, “Verily We have created man in the best form. (Surat At-Tin: 4). Allah ﷻ has created humans in the best possible form, and changing Allah’s ﷻ creation makes us ungrateful people and changes the nature that Allah ﷻ has given. Allah ﷻ in Holy Qur’an said, “It may be that you hate something, even though it is perfect for you, and it may be (also) you like something, even though it is awful for you; Allah ﷻ knows, while you do not know,” (Surah Al-Baqarah: 216).

For the treatment to prevent physical and social illnesses, Islamic civilisation built various public health services. The Islamic hammam was the first public bathhouse in the Muslim world. There were two types of hammam in the Middle Ages, for men and women. Hammam was first introduced in Syria during the era of the Umayyad Caliphate in the 6th century, during the Roman period. During the Umayyad period (661-750 AD), public baths, called Islamic hammams, proliferated in the Arabian Peninsula. In particular, the Islamic hammam is based on a prevalent religious and cultural background, derived from the wudhu (ablution) ritual mentioned in the Quran and the use of a steam room for baths by the Turkish, called the Turkish hammam. Therefore, Islamic hammams were generally built adjacent to the mosque in the medieval period. The Islamic hammam was also evolving as a gathering place for religious scholars, travellers, former detainees, and sick people hoping to recover from their illnesses; they came to perform ritual cleansing and to get closer to Allah ﷺ. Hammam is the embryo of spas these days. (Aaland, 1978).

Hammam had such complex sanitation and drainage, completed with fresh water supplies (even though it was more comprehensive than the famous Roman public baths), contributing to public health (Pormann & Savage-Smith, 2007). The staff of the public baths consisted of several professionals such as guards (Hammāmīn), barbers (Salmānīn), masseurs (Āstijānān), cupping therapists (Hajjāmīn) and others (Mez, 1992). The hammam is part of the Islamic purification ritual, and the concept has been exported to modern Sharia salons, spas, and beauty clinics (Mez, 1992; Smith & Puckzo, 2013). The services include Ghusl baths, massages, Ismid kohl, Henna art, cutting moustache, siwak toothbrush, spa services, beauty therapists, hairdressers, therapists, masseurs, and make-up artists (Bafadhal, 2017; Bafadhal, 2022).

Women love to look beautiful and are very concerned about their beauty, such as cutting their hair, having body and facial treatments, and cleaning their nails and hands. Women often go to beauty salons to make their appearance even more beautiful. However, how does Islam perceive this? Islam highly upholds and respects the values of beauty, cleanliness, and neatness. Islam always encourages Muslim women (Muslimah) to adorn and beautify themselves acceptably in order to worship and seek the blessing of Allah ﷺ (Holy Quran Surah Al-A'raf: 26; An-Nur: 31; and An-Nahl: 14).

Al-Ghamidi (2013) states that in the past, women helped each other to comb, oil, tidy, and cut their hair, yet ﷺ these all were done. Such activities already existed in the time of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. At this time, beauty salons were widespread. However, Muslims were suggested to select those whose service and operation complied with Islamic values. Furthermore, the spa comes from the Latin word “Solus per Aquam” (SPA). Water is one of the methods of treatment recommended by the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, and it is suggested to be used to relieve fever, as the saying of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ: “Fever is from the heat of Hell, so abate fever with water” (Hadith narrated Al-Bukhari). Callen (2007) claims that in their development, spas are no longer just associated with mineral springs and the therapeutic use of water. Spas now present in the form of chic services and products in a beauty salon, are available in various department stores, and may also involve places to eat delicious and healthy food or even become the ultimate destination for a vacation.
