Attention is paid to all branching and interconnected aspects of the idea of adopting business intelligence systems, known as Business Intelligence Opportunities. The distinctive features of small and medium-sized industrial enterprises compared to larger ones also give a special character to the idea of adoption. Therefore, an analytical framework will be adopted in this regard, dividing adoption requirements into three main groups: technological requirements, organizational requirements, and human requirements. # Organizational Requirements for Adopting Business Intelligence

According to the literature of management science and as per the thinker Peter Drucker, organization is considered the second stage of practice within the framework of any managerial process, following planning. Organizing is, therefore, an activity that involves designing the organizational structure, in addition to achieving coordination and harmony among its various parts and separate administrative units. This is to make the organization fully adaptable to the continuous changes occurring in its environment, providing it with organizational agility. Consequently, organizational requirements refer to the compatibility, harmony, and coordination that must be achieved between business intelligence as a system and the organizational structure of the entire institution. This ensures the smooth flow and proper distribution of data and information. Managers and executives must real, useful, detailed, and timely information concerning all practiced activities and external environmental conditions to achieve high performance and organizational efficiency. Thus, the operational business intelligence system is integrated with the overall operational system of the organization. (Howson, Successful Business Intelligence: Secrets of making BI a killer app, 2008, p. 23) Among the prominent reasons leading to the emergence of these organizational requirements are the modern organizational structures adopted by industrial enterprises, known as flat structures, horizontal structures, or structures centered around operations. Additionally, the new distribution of decision-making authority, by enabling individuals responsible for business operations to make various decisions, played a crucial role. (Olszak, 2020, p. 3) The urgent transformations in the work environment, the emergence of the digital economy, and competition based on quality and innovative competition played a decisive role in the need for enterprises to achieve high levels of internal coordination.
