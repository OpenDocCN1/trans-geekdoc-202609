One of the study (López-Robles et al., 2019) topics that has the most potential for growth in the upcoming years is business intelligence. The study offers a conceptual analysis of the definitions of performance that are currently available in the literature, quantifies bibliometric performance indicators, identifies key researchers and research topics, and uses SciMAT, a bibliometric analysis programme, to assess the field’s evolution. BI is strongly connected to other theme areas including Big Data, text mining, queries, data warehouses, and business intelligence systems. It appears that the demand to use and analyse vast volumes of data is rising, which might lead to this potential rise. It is crucial to stress that business intelligence, in its broadest sense, is solely concerned with the creation and application of information technology, not with specifics pertaining to business organisation. Modern business intelligence solutions can capture and analyse almost any kind of data. BI has been a rising star in the information systems industry, and it is presently experiencing a new boost within the Big Data Analytics movement (Wanda & Stian, 2015). A revolutionary new way to store intelligence and analyse it is being made possible by big data. Big data-related entrepreneurship entails the founding of new businesses and the application of novel ideas while utilising data analytics to spur success and innovation (Sedkaoui, 2020). However, the distribution of market intelligence (another component of BI, it particularly relates to gathering and evaluating data about the market and client demands.) promotes acceptable decision making, incorporates creative entrepreneurial orientation into already-existing technology or expertise, and lessens the negative effects of overly proactive behaviour by reducing the number of poor judgements made (H. Chen & Hsu, 2013). Exposure of small businesses to market intelligence fosters the development of marketing competencies that maximise adaptability and client response. Market intelligence supplements the experience-based informal market knowledge of small businesses, guiding the intuition of owners and managers and boosting their confidence in creative ideas (Carson et al., 2020).

Furthermore, in a study (Sen et al., 2016), it has been stated that big data has great promise and potential for SMEs. Through the development of real-time solutions to problems in every industry, big data may foster alliances in SMEs. Utilising openness for decision-making can help achieve this. SMEs are chosen in this particular situation for two primary reasons: Because of their general economic situation, SMEs can react to modest changes more quickly and flexibly than bigger companies; and adapt to changes in an efficient manner. SMEs may get more precise and thorough performance data on anything from product inventories to sick days as they generate and maintain more transactional data digitally. This allows them to uncover variability and improve performance. Big data enables considerably more accurate and ever-narrower client segmentation customised goods and services. SMEs tend to be more quickly adaptable, have fewer legacy problems, and have more flexible IT infrastructure. Big Data may help SMEs focus on innovation, increase efficiency, and launch new ventures. That means that some of the main obstacles can be removed. It is anticipated that SMEs would be able to assess the usage and potential of big data by concentrating on the growth prospects it presents. Big Data can be used by SMEs for a variety of purposes, including market segmentation, demand forecasting, process optimisation, predictive inventory planning, predictive maintenance, and in-depth data analysis to identify correlations, risks, and opportunities. SMEs may be able to view the broad picture as a result of the extensive usage of big data. Also, a study shows how crucial business function (information systems) strategy is to enabling overall business strategy-making for bringing forth innovations within the company (Yoshikuni & Dwivedi, 2023).

According to another study’s authors (Oesterreich et al., 2022), future theory development should be guided by their increased grasp of the mechanisms resulting in big data analytics (BDA)-induced economic value. Prioritising the sociotechnical aspect of BDA is important for managers and decision-makers starting new BDA initiatives. The significance of social and technological elements in generating economic value from BDA is investigated in this research using a meta-analysis of 107 individual studies.

A bibliometric study (Rialti et al., 2019) has revealed that organisational performance and decision-making processes are greatly impacted by big data and BDA systems. Managers can utilise the forecasts generated by BDA systems regarding future trends to decide on sales, revenues, and production needs. Real-time consumer analytics from BDA enable managers to respond quickly to changing customer preferences. Managers can be better equipped to make decisions that improve organisational efficiency in the production and supply chain by using insights from BDA. BDA systems may provide managers with information about sales, manufacturing, and supply, allowing them to react quickly to changes and make wise choices. Also, enhancing the quality of information and facilitating its efficient exchange across supply chain networks makes an enterprise more capable of quickly and effectively responding to interruptions (Hyun et al., 2023). For example a study (Nayak et al., 2019) noted that wearable technology relies heavily on big data and big data analytics since these technologies may provide commercial value for the healthcare sector and extract value from vast amounts of information.

In accordance with the aspects of the idea of business intelligence use, a study (Gnizy, 2019) based on Israeli firms implements Big Data (BD) utilisation widely. Using pertinent components from previous research, it evaluates international market orientation (IMO), international entrepreneurial orientation (IEO), and international learning orientation (ILO). BD is essential to improving IEO (International Entrepreneurial Orientation) in businesses; yet, the sources included do not specifically address the link between BD and IEO. Using BD increases a company’s ability to compete, willingness to take chances, and desire for innovative leadership—all of which are indicators of their IEO. Businesses may use BD to support their decisions and actions by analysing data, which is in line with the entrepreneurial spirit of looking for and seizing global market possibilities. Businesses may use BD to get and evaluate market intelligence, spot new trends, and make data-driven decisions—all crucial elements of an entrepreneurial orientation in a global setting. In order to differentiate BD apart from typical datasets, a paper (Ciampi et al., 2020) outlines the characteristics of BD, such as size, diversity, velocity, variability, value, authenticity, and visualisation. Crucial resources, including human skills, data-driven organisational learning, and integration methodologies, storage, processing, and other physical and intangible resources, are needed for the successful application of BD analytics capabilities (BDAC).

Also, a study (Ciasullo et al., 2022) aims to explore the connections between organisational resilience and big data analytics (BDA) skills in 192 European big data SMEs. Improving small and medium companies’ (SMEs) ability to withstand adversity and pivotal moments is the goal of this article. It is suggested that practitioners improve their responsiveness in times of crisis by identifying the micro foundations of SMEs’ resilience-building capabilities. For small and medium-sized businesses (SMEs), radical innovation results from the effective integration of big data usage (BDU) in business operations, which aligns company strategy and boosts operational efficiency. Radical innovation and the creation of cutting-edge goods that satisfy consumer demands are the results of BDU’s assistance in helping SMEs match their business strategy with organisational objectives (Wang et al., 2023).

Moreover, in another study conducted among 192 Bangladesh companies identified that a firm’s performance and agility are greatly impacted by its ability to use business analytics (an extension of business intelligence) skills to enhance resources like information quality and inventive capability. The link between performance, agility, and business analytics (BA) capabilities is moderated by industry dynamism. The objective of using data to inform business decisions and boost performance is one that both BI and BA strive to achieve. It is evident that BA goes beyond the technical elements of data characteristics and emphasises how organisational resources must change in order for an organisation to become data-driven (Khan et al., 2022).
