## RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

Through a “PRISMA 2020” systematic review of existing literature, this study aims to identify how business intelligence can be influencing entrepreneurial orientation among the ventures that need to be addressed for future research agendas.

## Identification

In order to identify the resources, the first stage entails filtering out journals from the various databases, such as Science Direct, Emerald Insight, Ebscohost, and JStor, since they are some of the main chosen databases for evaluation and financing in Indian social science research. Utilizing keywords such as “Business Intelligence” AND “Entrepreneurship Orientation” will allow to search the four database journals for the resource. This study includes only English-language research papers, review articles, early-cite publications, and peer-reviewed articles that were published between 2010 and 2023.

## Screening

In order to find relevant articles, keywords are used to search for resources. The screening process led to the retrieval of 306 articles (59 from Science Direct, 235 from Emerald Insight, 4 from EBSCOhost, and 8 from JStor). A duplicate article was identified during the compilation process and removed. 37 articles were sorted after reading the abstracts and the titles, and 269 were discarded. There were 13 articles that had no full-text available, as well.

## Included

The titles of the papers were screened before moving on to the abstracts as part of the selection process. Articles were chosen in the next phase according to the research question. Nevertheless, the research did not include any papers whose full texts were not accessible. To decide if the publications in this round should be a part of our research, we carefully reviewed each one. The study of our research topics did not include any articles that did not include a process or result examination. After reviewing the whole text of the study, 23 articles were included in response to the research question.

The term “business intelligence” is quite extensive. Covers a range of approaches, processes and technological resources that are used to gather, store and analyse data in order to support decision making (Wanda & Stian, 2015). The field of intelligence research provides an opportunity to explore advancements in collecting and analysing data, managing information making decisions and enhancing capabilities. Intelligence focuses on extracting value from data, information and the dynamic nature of organizations (López-Robles et al., 2019). Business Intelligence (BI) is a valuable tool that has to be created and utilised for information collecting, analysis, and distribution to enable improved decision-making (Caseiro & Coelho, 2018). To provide information and insights, for decision making strategic intelligence combines various types of intelligence such as business intelligence, competitive intelligence and knowledge management. Intelligence, in its entirety, analytical and practical intelligence significantly influences entrepreneurship by affecting numerous aspects of the process. This includes recognizing opportunities making decisions and employing thinking (Ahmadi et al., 2020). A study demonstrates how the interaction between digital technology and entrepreneurs’ everyday activities shapes the nature of their business. The qualities and dynamics of the surrounding environment influence how various digital tools are used, and their use is interrelated. The digital revolution in entrepreneurship is not the product of deliberate design, but rather an evolutionary, practice-based phenomena. The results have important ramifications for software development companies that cater to entrepreneurs, universities that are interested in entrepreneurial education, and entrepreneurs who want to comprehend the dynamics of their working practices (Corvello et al., 2022). One way to find various strategic levers for encouraging innovation and taking advantage of technology opportunities is through the strategic integration of data-driven orientation, skills upgrading, and innovation development (Visvizi et al., 2022).

On the other hand, the methods, procedures, and choices used throughout the decision-making process that result in a new market entrance are referred to as entrepreneurial orientation (EO) (Lumpkin & Dess, 1996; Covin & Miller, 2014). EO is crucial to a firm’s entrepreneurial process, which is in identifying opportunities, innovation processes, and opportunity exploitation. It is an effective way to handle competitive risks and minimise competitive pressures (Y. C. Chen et al., 2012). Proactiveness, inventiveness, and risk-taking comprise the three components that make up entrepreneurial orientation as a combined construct (Covin & Miller, 2014). The concept of entrepreneurial orientation centres on an organization’s procedures, leadership perspectives, and enterprising actions, encompassing the generation of novel ideas via digital transformation. Businesses may increase the speed and accuracy of business intelligence operations by digitising data collecting and analysis processes and minimising manual labour. Therefore, it is concluded in a study that digitization improves business intelligence via streamlining procedures related to data collecting, analysis, and decision-making; it also boosts productivity and gives researchers access to insightful information from a variety of sources (Ismail, 2023). When it comes to leveraging information systems (IS) to identify chances for innovation, EO which entails actions that bring about change in the company or market is a crucial component. With autonomy, managers may think through more possibilities and alternatives, which improves their capacity to spot chances when utilising IS. Using IS to identify possibilities also requires creativity and taking calculated risks. Decision support systems (DSS) may give managers the information and understanding they need to spot possible areas for innovation. Examples of DSS include business intelligence tools and analytics (Roberts et al., 2016).
