## Proxies of Environmental Sustainability in Organizations: Sustainability Expenditures and Measurement of Nitrogen Emissions

Wang et al. (2022) considered the yearly increase in environmental protection spending as a proxy variable for environmental protection investment, indicating a potential link between education and environmentally friendly behaviors among CEOs. Moreover, Lu et al. (2020) highlighted the significance of leveraging the synergistic effect of environmental protection investment and technological innovation to promote corporate performance, underscoring the strategic implications of integrating environmental protection expenditure with innovation efforts.

Yang et al. (2020) found that organizational management has a significant positive impact on enterprise environmental protection investment and financial performance, emphasizing the importance of leadership in driving environmental protection expenditure.

The production-based nitrogen emissions of the textile industry have a significant impact on environmental sustainability. This sector faces considerable challenges due to the substantial emissions of sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxide during the production phases. These emissions can have negative effects on air quality, climate change, and human health. Therefore, it is essential to address these issues and work towards sustainable and eco-friendly production methods in the textile industry. Guo et al. (2023) emphasize the environmental significance of nitrogen emissions in textile manufacturing. Knowledge-based nitrogen management is crucial in mitigating greenhouse gas emissions and reactive nitrogen pollution. Xia et al. (2017) found the significance of this approach for environmental sustainability in agricultural systems. This understanding can be applied to the textile sector, as effective nitrogen control can help lessen negative environmental effects. Qian et al. (2020) examined how textile products affected the environment and stressed the need to evaluate and account for the chemical footprint—including nitrogen emissions—during the production phase of the textile life cycle.

### Effects of Transformational Leadership on Environmental Sustainability

The role of leadership in promoting sustainability extends beyond the internal organizational environment. It has been suggested that leadership support can compensate for a lack of clear sustainability goals and direction, emphasizing the crucial role of leadership in driving sustainability strategies (Wolf, 2011). In sustainable competitive advantage, good leadership and effective strategy implementation have been proposed as key factors that can lead corporate environmental activities to sustainable competitive advantage (Amoako, 2019).

Suphattanakul (2017) identified the impact of transformational leadership on organizational performance and its role in creating strategic organizational change, emphasizing its relevance for strategic implementation. Additionally, Schlosser and Volkova (2022) emphasized the alignment of transformational leadership with the greater good, indicating its potential to drive strategic leadership practices that deliver shared value. Transformational leadership has the potential to influence strategic outcomes within firms, and transformational leadership also influences the environmental sustainability outcomes of the firms.

Transformational leadership is in the figure gathered under the so-called four I's. These four I's (Idealized Influence (II), Inspirational Motivation (IM), Intellectual Stimulation (IS), and Individual Attention (IO)) in transformational leadership are effective in environmental sustainability.

Idealized Influence (II) (Attribution and behavior) contains charisma or idealized influence. Idealized Influence, a key component of transformational leadership, has significantly influenced environmental sustainability within organizations (Singh et al., 2020). Furthermore, Zong-Bo et al. (2020) emphasized the role of environmentally specific transformational leaders in acting as role models for employees by demonstrating environmental sustainability ideas and encouraging environmentally friendly actions, which aligns with the concept of idealized influence (Li et al., 2020). As a result, leadership support by transformational leaders, including actively demonstrating the importance of sustainability by managers-attribution, impacts pro-environmental behavior in the workplace, indicating the potential influence of idealized influence on promoting environmental sustainability (Wesselink et al., 2017).

The relationship between Inspirational Motivation (IM) and environmental sustainability has been a topic of interest in the literature. Xenikou and Simosi (2006) emphasized the indirect positive impact of transformational leadership on performance, suggesting its potential influence on environmental sustainability through IM. Additionally, some writers proposed that environmental innovation indirectly impacts sustainable leadership on environmental performance, suggesting the potential contribution of IM to the promotion of environmental innovation and, by extension, sustainability (Cho & Kao, 2022; Iqbal et al., 2021). Additionally, they discovered that transformational leadership has a strong positive impact on organizational commitment, which may use IM to support environmental sustainability (Cho & Kao, 2022). Furthermore, Erat et al. (2020) emphasized the connection between sustainable organizational identity and transformational leadership, suggesting that IM may impact forming organizational values that align with sustainability.

Intellectual Stimulation (IS) means that the manager stimulates employees to become more creative and innovative through, among other things, linguistic guidelines. The intricate and diverse topic of the relationship between individual creativity and environmental sustainability has attracted interest from a wide range of academic fields. Awan et al. (2019) emphasized the critical role of creativity in addressing environmental sustainability, particularly in the context of green innovation. They offer a broadly applicable paradigm that illustrates how creativity fosters green innovation and can positively impact sustainability. This suggests that individual creativity, when integrated into organizational practices, can contribute significantly to environmental sustainability (Souto, 2022). Additionally, Moraru (2021) highlights the potential link between individual creativity and environmental sustainability, suggesting that creativity can be linked to environmental and economic sustainability and the dimensions of social and human sustainability. In conclusion, Intellectual Stimulation (IS) can positively impact environmental sustainability, highlighting the potential of individual creativity as a driver for sustainable development.

Individual Attention (IO) is about individual attention and seeing the employees. Transformational leaders focus on employees' needs for growth and development by acting as mentors or coaches. In that sense, mentorship plays a crucial role in fostering environmental sustainability within organizations. Reddy et al. (2021) emphasized the importance of peer-to-peer or collaborative mentorship relationships in promoting sustainable practices among employees. Similarly, Erhabr (2018) underscores the integral role of mentorship programs in developing effective environmental leaders, indicating the potential of mentorship in cultivating individuals who can drive sustainability initiatives within organizations. These references collectively suggest that mentorship, particularly in peer-to-peer or collaborative relationships, is instrumental in influencing employee adoption of sustainability practices.

Furthermore, the literature in this chapter suggests that transformational leadership can indirectly affect organizational performance through various mediating variables. For example, Çop et al. (2021) emphasized that transformational leaders provide environmentally sustainable results by engaging employees in both the organization's activities and the environment. Moreover, Suliman et al. (2023) found that transformational leadership increases the total environmental sustainability performance of the organizations; however, they did not specify the environmental performance indicators.
