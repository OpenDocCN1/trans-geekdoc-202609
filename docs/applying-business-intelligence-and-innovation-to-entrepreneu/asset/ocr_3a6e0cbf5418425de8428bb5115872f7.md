# Women on the Board

Interpretations of the relationship between women on the board and ESG performance are connected to the various features of the women themselves. There seems to be a general consensus in the literature regarding the positive impact of female directors on sustainability performance. More in detail, women on boards positively impact the firm’s charitable contributions (Bettinelli et al., 2019; Furlotti et al., 2019; Abd Rahman et al., 2019), apprehension for climate change (Birindelli, et al., 2019) and reputation-based CSR measures (Ajaz, et al., 2020). Similarly, many studies find that board gender diversity increases the extent of social and environmental reporting and decreases environmental lawsuits (Yuan et al., 2019; Peng, 2020). A likely explanation of these inconsistent results could be the presence of a non-linear relationship between board gender diversity and social and environmental performance. Based on their findings, some studies such those of (Birindelli et al., 2019; Furlotti et al., 2019; Ajaz et al., 2020; Peng, 2020) point out that a high level of presence of women on the board improve the ESG performance. In consideration of this evidence, we advance the following hypothesis:

*Hypothesis 3. I high level of gender diversity affects positively the ESG performance*

# Executive Compensation

The provision of ESG based sustainable compensation policy is likely to play a critical role in motivating corporate executives to undertake carbon initiatives, which can in turn enhance both organizational legitimacy and market value. As part of sustainable corporate strategies, corporations are increasingly using sustainable compensation policy to motivate executives to pursue social and environmental projects. Empirically, Baraiabar-Diez et al., (2019); Eklund, (2019); Haque, & Ntim., (2020) find that sustainable compensation schemes improve the ESG performance. We argue, therefore, that in the presence of a sustainable compensation policy, the board of directors and the compensation committee of the board might be in a better position to evaluate the environmental, social and governance attributes of a firm to design a more effective which may enhance corporate carbon performance. Therefore, any symbolic ESG compensation policy, without requiring explicit guidelines and mandatory emission reduction targets, might enhance process-oriented ESG performance of the firm (Westling, & Mazhari, 2019; Baraiabar-Diez et al., 2019).
