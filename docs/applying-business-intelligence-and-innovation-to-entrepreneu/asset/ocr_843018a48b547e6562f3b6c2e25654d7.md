At its core, design thinking emphasizes empathy, problem definition, ideation, prototyping, and collaboration (Lor, 2017). These fundamental principles form the theoretical foundations that underpin its application in various domains, including entrepreneurship.

Empathy, the cornerstone of design thinking, revolves around deeply understanding the needs, desires, and pain points of end-users (Portnoy, 2019). This empathetic perspective closely aligns with the essence of entrepreneurship, where identifying unmet market needs is often the ignition for a venture’s journey. Entrepreneurs who embrace design thinking immerse themselves in the world of their target audience. By adopting the perspective of their potential customers, they gain invaluable insights that can lead to the creation of innovative and compelling solutions. Understanding the user’s experience becomes not just a goal but a driving force behind entrepreneurial endeavors. Through empathetic research methods, such as user interviews, observations, and surveys, entrepreneurs can uncover hidden pain points and unarticulated desires. Armed with this deep understanding, they are better equipped to develop products or services that genuinely resonate with their target market. This empathetic approach ensures that entrepreneurial ventures are built upon a foundation of user-centricity, increasing the likelihood of success in the competitive business landscape.

Entrepreneurship often presents challenges that are ambiguous and complex (Amit, 1993). Entrepreneurs must grapple with identifying the right problems to solve to drive innovation and growth. In this context, design thinking offers a structured framework for effective problem definition. Design thinking encourages entrepreneurs to articulate problem statements clearly and comprehensively (Ihugba, 2014). This process involves breaking down complex challenges into manageable components and exploring the root causes of issues. By defining problems with precision, entrepreneurs gain clarity and focus on the aspects of their venture that require attention. Effective problem definition is crucial for resource allocation, prioritization, and decision-making. It allows entrepreneurs to channel their creative energy and resources toward solving the most critical challenges. Moreover, it ensures that the entrepreneurial journey remains aligned with the needs and aspirations of the target audience, reducing the risk of developing solutions that miss the mark.

Ideation, the phase of generating ideas, is a pivotal aspect of design thinking that is highly relevant to entrepreneurship. Entrepreneurs thrive on innovation, and design thinking provides a structured approach to fostering creativity. In design thinking, ideation encourages entrepreneurs and their teams to generate a multitude of ideas without judgment. This approach promotes a culture of creativity where no idea is deemed too far-fetched or impractical.
