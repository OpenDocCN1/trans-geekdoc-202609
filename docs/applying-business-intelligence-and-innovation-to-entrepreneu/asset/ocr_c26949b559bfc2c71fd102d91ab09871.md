BD is essential to improving IEO (International Entrepreneurial Orientation) in businesses; yet, the sources included do not specifically address the link between BD and IEO. Using BD increases a company’s ability to compete, willingness to take chances, and desire for innovative leadership—all of which are indicators of their IEO. Businesses may use BD to support their decisions and actions by analysing data, which is in line with the entrepreneurial spirit of looking for and seizing global market possibilities. Businesses may use BD to get and evaluate market intelligence, spot new trends, and make data-driven decisions—all crucial elements of an entrepreneurial orientation in a global setting. In order to differentiate BD apart from typical datasets, a paper (Ciampi et al., 2020) outlines the characteristics of BD, such as size, diversity, velocity, variability, value, authenticity, and visualisation. Crucial resources, including human skills, data-driven organisational learning, and integration methodologies, storage, processing, and other physical and intangible resources, are needed for the successful application of BD analytics capabilities (BDAC).

Also, a study (Ciasullo et al., 2022) aims to explore the connections between organisational resilience and big data analytics (BDA) skills in 192 European big data SMEs. Improving small and medium companies’ (SMEs) ability to withstand adversity and pivotal moments is the goal of this article. It is suggested that practitioners improve their responsiveness in times of crisis by identifying the micro foundations of SMEs’ resilience-building capabilities. For small and medium-sized businesses (SMEs), radical innovation results from the effective integration of big data usage (BDU) in business operations, which aligns company strategy and boosts operational efficiency. Radical innovation and the creation of cutting-edge goods that satisfy consumer demands are the results of BDU’s assistance in helping SMEs match their business strategy with organisational objectives (Wang et al., 2023).

Moreover, in another study conducted among 192 Bangladesh companies identified that a firm’s performance and agility are greatly impacted by its ability to use business analytics (an extension of business intelligence) skills to enhance resources like information quality and inventive capability. The link between performance, agility, and business analytics (BA) capabilities is moderated by industry dynamism. The objective of using data to inform business decisions and boost performance is one that both BI and BA strive to achieve. It is evident that BA goes beyond the technical elements of data characteristics and emphasises how organisational resources must change in order for an organisation to become data-driven (Khan et al., 2022).

Therefore, a study (Zeng & Khan, 2019) looks at how EO and resource orchestration work together to create value in emerging economies using big data.
