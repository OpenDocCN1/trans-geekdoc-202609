It involves gathering various market information and choosing the markets in which the organization will sell, as well as providing after-sales services.

- **Support Operations:** The organization carries out a set of support activities for its stakeholders both internally and externally. These activities mainly consist of legal operations associated with government entities, as well as providing various financial information and innovation support activities.

Proponents of reengineering believe that the horizontal organization of the enterprise’s activities, according to operations rather than functions and departments, ensures the smooth and immediate flow of data and useful information for decision-making. This occurs among the responsible individuals for those operations and the individuals affiliated with them, considering they belong to diverse functional specialties and are granted high authority for decision-making. This ultimately guarantees solving cost, quality, and timeliness issues instantly and before they occur.

In line with this, Lila Rao-Graham, Maurice McNaughton, and Gunjan Mansingh believe that the adoption of Business Intelligence by small and medium-sized industrial enterprises makes them more flexible towards sudden changes in their external environment. This is achieved by providing the necessary coordination levels and ensuring a constant and immediate flow of data and information between the parts of their organizational structure. This is especially advantageous compared to larger enterprises due to the simplicity of their organizational structures. (Rao-Graham, McNaughton, & Mansingh, 2020, p. 4)

## Technical Requirements for Adopting Business Intelligence

The technical requirements for Business Intelligence systems serve as their underlying infrastructure, enabling enterprise management to acquire, handle, manage, and use data and information in the areas of business analysis and decision-making. These requirements vary with Information Technology and Communication (ITC) assets but are closely integrated with them. The technical requirements for the successful adoption of Business Intelligence in small and medium-sized industrial enterprises differ from larger organizations. This is primarily due to the lower maturity level of Information Technology and Communication practices in small and medium-sized enterprises in general. This can be attributed to various factors such as limited resources, lower levels of expertise among individuals in the field of Information Technology and Communication, and a lack of a clear vision from management regarding the utilization of Information Technology and Communication in creating value.

Therefore, technical success requirements can be categorized into two main groups:

- Requirements associated with the maturity of Information Technology and Communication practices (ITC).
- Technical requirements specific to the Business Intelligence system itself.

## The Set of Requirements Related to the Maturity of Information Technology and Communication Practices

Michael Gendron asserts that the success of an enterprise’s adoption of Business Intelligence is strongly linked to the maturity of its practices in the field of Information Technology and Communication (ICT). This is evident through the ICT infrastructure, enabling the creation, aggregation, and transfer of data, and subsequently, its utilization in the realm of smart business solutions. (Gendron, 2013, pp. 4, 5) More precisely, enterprises generally have access to a vast and diverse array of ICT assets, enabling them to engage in electronic management practices. These technological assets are commonly used for general purposes and, in many cases, are functionally suitable for Business Intelligence purposes. (Williams, 2016, p. 189)

For the enterprise to maximize the benefits of ICT and BI, it is essential not only to use them to support internal operations but also to employ them in a well-organized manner that ultimately leads to value creation. To achieve this, the ICT infrastructure within the small and medium-sized industrial enterprise must encompass the following components:

- **Enterprise-wide components:** These components operate across all organizational departments but function as a unified system. Users interact with Information and Communication Technology (ICT) applications and enterprise systems, such as ERP, enterprise document management, enterprise content management, or knowledge management systems, through the front-end interface of each computer program. This interaction is consolidated at a central level known as the “Enterprise-wide component.” It comprises a large set of servers, databases, computers, communication networks, front-end software, etc.

- **User Components:** This category includes all parts that users interact with directly and have direct daily control over. Individuals use these components to carry out their functional activities, such as computers, printers, scanners, and various software.

- **Service Components:** Represented by the network service used directly by individuals in the enterprise, consisting of network hardware and software. Managed and monitored by the Information and Communication Technology department, service components encompass various services such as printing, office communications, network storage service, internet protocol telephony, etc.

- **Network Components:** Comprising a set of networking and telecommunication equipment, including network devices, software, cables, media, routing hardware, network switching, and associated items.
