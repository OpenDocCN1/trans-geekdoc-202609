# ## BACKGROUND

The evolution of marketing communication has been a dynamic journey, shaped by societal changes, technological advancements, and shifts in consumer behavior. Traditional marketing approaches, once the bedrock of brand promotion, now find themselves grappling with the transformative forces of the digital age. As businesses navigate this complex landscape, Neuromarketing emerges as a promising paradigm, offering a deeper understanding of consumer cognition and emotional responses to marketing stimuli.

The roots of Neuromarketing trace back to the early 2000s when Ale Smidt’s coined the term, signifying a convergence of neuroscience, psychology, and marketing (Smidt’s, 2002). Drawing inspiration from the field of neuroeconomics, which explores the neural underpinnings of economic decision-making, Neuromarketing pioneers the application of neuroscientific tools to decipher the intricate dance between the consumer’s brain and marketing messages (Lee et al., 2007). This interdisciplinary approach positions Neuromarketing as a revolutionary force in understanding consumer behavior.

Digitalization has ushered in a new era of marketing communication, necessitating a reevaluation of strategies. The rise of social media, online advertising, and influencer marketing has transformed the way brands connect with their audience. In this context, Neuromarketing serves as a bridge between the traditional and the digital, offering insights into how the human brain responds to the rapidly evolving landscape of online communication.

One key aspect of this evolution is the growing emphasis on personalization in marketing. Consumers, inundated with a deluge of information, seek personalized experiences that resonate with their individual preferences and needs (Hubert & Kenning, 2008). Neuromarketing, with its ability to decode neurodata, provides marketers with the tools to tailor content and messages, creating a more personalized and engaging consumer journey.
