+   **Mining (Proof of Work):**
+   
+   In a proof-of-work (PoW) blockchain, miners compete to solve complex mathematical puzzles. The first miner to solve the puzzle gets the right to add the new block of transactions to the blockchain. This process is resource-intensive and requires significant computational power.

+   **Block Confirmation:**
+   
+   Once the block containing the transaction is confirmed by the network, it is added to the blockchain. The transaction is now considered finalized, and the data is recorded in an immutable and transparent manner.

+   **Immutable Records:**
+   
+   Blockchain's immutability means that once a transaction is recorded on the blockchain, it cannot be altered or deleted. This feature ensures the integrity of transaction records, reducing the risk of fraud and disputes.

+   **Transaction Finality:**
+   
+   The transaction is considered final when it is added to the blockchain. This finality is one of the key benefits of blockchain technology, as it reduces the need for trust in a centralized authority to verify transactions.

+   **Blockchain Networks:**
+   
+   Different blockchain networks may have varying transaction processing speeds, costs, and consensus mechanisms. Public blockchains, like Bitcoin and Ethereum, are open to anyone and can handle a global volume of transactions. Private or consortium blockchains may have faster transaction speeds and controlled access for specific participants.

+   **Smart Contracts:**
+   
+   In addition to simple transactions, entrepreneurs can also deploy smart contracts on certain blockchain platforms. Smart contracts are self-executing programs that automatically perform actions based on predefined conditions. These can streamline complex business processes and reduce the need for intermediaries.
