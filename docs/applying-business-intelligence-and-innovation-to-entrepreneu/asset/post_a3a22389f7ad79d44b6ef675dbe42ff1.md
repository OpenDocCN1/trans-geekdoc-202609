## THE THEORETICAL FOUNDATIONS OF DESIGN THINKING IN ENTREPRENEURIAL INNOVATION

The idea and practise of “design thinking,” which has roots in many fields including industrial design, engineering, and architecture, has developed into a crucial strategy in the entrepreneurial world (Johannsson-Sköldberg, 2013). Its rise to prominence is ascribed to its human-centric approach to problem-solving, which has a profound resonance with the opportunities and challenges faced by entrepreneurs in today’s fast-paced business climate. In this thorough investigation, we will delve into the theoretical underpinnings of design thinking and how it fits with the entrepreneurial process, offering insights into how it enables businesspeople to innovate, create, and succeed. Design thinking is an all-encompassing strategy that draws influence from various fields of study. Its roots can be seen in the innovative and iterative methods used in industrial design, where the demands and experiences of users are crucial to the development of products. Additionally, it shares similarities with engineering and architectural problem-solving methodologies, which prioritize user-centric solutions. At its core, design thinking emphasizes empathy, problem definition, ideation, prototyping, and collaboration (Lor, 2017). These fundamental principles form the theoretical foundations that underpin its application in various domains, including entrepreneurship.

Empathy, the cornerstone of design thinking, revolves around deeply understanding the needs, desires, and pain points of end-users (Portnoy, 2019). This empathetic perspective closely aligns with the essence of entrepreneurship, where identifying unmet market needs is often the ignition for a venture’s journey. Entrepreneurs who embrace design thinking immerse themselves in the world of their target audience. By adopting the perspective of their potential customers, they gain invaluable insights that can lead to the creation of innovative and compelling solutions. Understanding the user’s experience becomes not just a goal but a driving force behind entrepreneurial endeavors. Through empathetic research methods, such as user interviews, observations, and surveys, entrepreneurs can uncover hidden pain points and unarticulated desires. Armed with this deep understanding, they are better equipped to develop products or services that genuinely resonate with their target market. This empathetic approach ensures that entrepreneurial ventures are built upon a foundation of user-centricity, increasing the likelihood of success in the competitive business landscape.

Entrepreneurship often presents challenges that are ambiguous and complex (Amit, 1993). Entrepreneurs must grapple with identifying the right problems to solve to drive innovation and growth. In this context, design thinking offers a structured framework for effective problem definition. Design thinking encourages entrepreneurs to articulate problem statements clearly and comprehensively (Ihugba, 2014). This process involves breaking down complex challenges into manageable components and exploring the root causes of issues. By defining problems with precision, entrepreneurs gain clarity and focus on the aspects of their venture that require attention. Effective problem definition is crucial for resource allocation, prioritization, and decision-making. It allows entrepreneurs to channel their creative energy and resources toward solving the most critical challenges. Moreover, it ensures that the entrepreneurial journey remains aligned with the needs and aspirations of the target audience, reducing the risk of developing solutions that miss the mark.

Ideation, the phase of generating ideas, is a pivotal aspect of design thinking that is highly relevant to entrepreneurship. Entrepreneurs thrive on innovation, and design thinking provides a structured approach to fostering creativity. In design thinking, ideation encourages entrepreneurs and their teams to generate a multitude of ideas without judgment. This approach promotes a culture of creativity where no idea is deemed too far-fetched or impractical. By suspending judgment, entrepreneurs open the door to unconventional and groundbreaking concepts. Techniques such as brainstorming, mind mapping, and idea sketching are integral to the ideation process. These methods encourage diverse thinking and collaboration among team members, leading to a rich pool of ideas. Entrepreneurs can explore a wide range of possibilities, allowing for the discovery of unique and innovative solutions. Ideation is not confined to the early stages of entrepreneurship; it permeates throughout the venture's lifecycle. It inspires entrepreneurs to continually seek fresh perspectives and explore novel approaches to address evolving challenges. By integrating ideation into their entrepreneurial mindset, innovators are better prepared to adapt to changing market dynamics and seize emerging opportunities.

Prototyping and testing are central components of design thinking (Liedtka, 2018), offering a structured approach to validating ideas and concepts. In the entrepreneurial context, these principles align seamlessly with the development of minimum viable products (MVPs) and the iterative process of innovation. Entrepreneurs often embark on their ventures with bold ideas and visions. However, translating these concepts into tangible products or services requires a systematic approach. Design thinking advocates for the creation of prototypes – scaled-down, functional representations of the envisioned solution – to gather user feedback and insights. Prototyping enables entrepreneurs to test their ideas in a real-world context, allowing them to identify strengths and weaknesses. This iterative approach ensures that entrepreneurs remain agile and responsive to evolving user needs and preferences. It also minimizes the risk associated with investing substantial resources in fully-fledged products or services that may not align with market demands. Testing is a continuous cycle of learning and improvement. Entrepreneurs collect feedback from users, refine their prototypes, and iterate on their solutions. This process of refinement and enhancement is the lifeblood of entrepreneurial innovation. It enables entrepreneurs to evolve their offerings in sync with market dynamics and emerging trends, increasing the likelihood of long-term success.

Design thinking places a strong emphasis on collaboration and interdisciplinary teamwork, which resonates profoundly with the entrepreneurial ecosystem (Taajamaa, 2013). Entrepreneurial ventures often require diverse skills, perspectives, and expertise to thrive and innovate. Entrepreneurs are rarely solo actors; they work with multifaceted teams comprising individuals with varied backgrounds, experiences, and skill sets. Design thinking methodologies enhance team dynamics and foster a culture of innovation within these entrepreneurial teams. One of the key benefits of design thinking is its ability to break down silos and encourage open communication. Cross-disciplinary teams collaborate seamlessly, drawing from their unique strengths and viewpoints to tackle complex problems. This interdisciplinary approach is particularly valuable in addressing multifaceted challenges that demand a holistic understanding of business, technology, user experience, and more. Furthermore, design thinking promotes a mindset of empathy and active listening within teams.
