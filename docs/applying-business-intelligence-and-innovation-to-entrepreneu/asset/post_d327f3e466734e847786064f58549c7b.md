Using one of the largest datasets to date, Chapter 10 investigates the impact of board characteristics on ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) performance. Findings reveal relationships between board size, meeting frequency, women on the board, executive compensation, and ESG performance, offering valuable insights for businesses worldwide. The chapter emphasizes the importance of diverse and responsible board practices in enhancing ESG performance.

Addressing the relationship between leadership styles and sustainability, Chapter 11 explores the impact of transformational and transactional leadership on organizational sustainability. The study employs path analysis to reveal the influence of leadership styles on sustainability outcomes, shedding light on the complexities of leadership in promoting sustainable practices. It highlights the need for a nuanced understanding of leadership’s role in fostering sustainability.

Drawing on Public Sphere theory, Chapter 12 compares the behavior of the British government in protecting British business in China with the Chinese government’s approach to Chinese business in the Philippines in the 19th century. Using private business groups as representatives, the chapter highlights the role of public sphere interaction in shaping economic foreign policies and promoting government-business consensus.

Investigating the growth of health tourism, particularly Muslim health tourism, Chapter 13 proposes a Muslim Health Tourist Behavior Model. The study explores decision-making behaviors when selecting Muslim-friendly health destinations and identifies Sharia salons and spas as forms of Muslim health tourism businesses. The chapter emphasizes the need for a comprehensive understanding of the unique characteristics and needs of Muslim health tourists.

### ABSTRACT

Business intelligence has been a recent information tool which was introduced in the entrepreneurship world. The present study is an extensive systematic literature review based on PRISMA framework which aims to explore the influence of business intelligence (BI) on the entrepreneurial orientation (EO) among the ventures.
Four journal databases have been used, namely Emerald, JSTOR, ScienceDirect, and EBSCOhost to gather the articles for the study. “Business intelligence” and “Entrepreneurial orientation” were the keywords used to identify studies from the journal databases. 23 articles were finalised for the study after going through the identification, screening and inclusion process which comes under the PRISMA (preferred reporting items for systematic reviews and meta-analyses) framework. It has been found that very little research has been done in this field. So, this systematic literature review will give an insight into the possible gaps that can be covered by the upcoming researchers in this field.

Businesses have to work hard to get the data they need to make better decisions in a highly competitive, dynamic, and unstable market. Gathering and evaluating data are two steps in the creation of intelligence. Top-performing organisations use sophisticated information and analytics techniques five times more frequently than low-performing organisations, according to a global study of 3000 company managers, executives, and analysts from a range of industries and regions (Lavalle et al., 2011).

The business environment is ever-changing due to advancements in technology, industry trends, and economic fluctuations. As BI technologies and approaches advance, it becomes more crucial than ever to comprehend how they affect entrepreneurial orientation. ### THE CONNECTION BETWEEN BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE (BI) AND ENTREPRENEURIAL ORIENTATION (EO)

Since entrepreneurial orientation and business intelligence are relatively new topics in the academic arena, there aren’t many empirical studies that demonstrate their direct correlation. A study (Caseiro & Coelho, 2018) explored among various firms located in different European countries and stated that how business intelligence (BI), entrepreneurial orientation (EO), and startup competitiveness are related to one another. It does this by looking at how EO influences BI’s impact on startup competitiveness as well as how BI directly influences it. Because business intelligence entails using information to make better decisions, which can affect a firm’s entrepreneurial orientation, the study predicts a favourable association between EO and BI. The competitiveness of a business, which is impacted by EO, might benefit from BI techniques and attributes.
