the proposal to increase the tariff rate to 15%. To address this issue, the Shanghai
branch held a plenary meeting on March 3rd to discuss the plan and vote on it. The
vote at the meeting rejected the Mackay’s plan (Mackay 1902a). Without the support
of British businessmen in China, there would be a lack of practical implementers
of policies towards China. The British government adopted a cooperative attitude
after recognizing the importance of the Shanghai branch.

The British government actively attracted committee members, attempting to
use the support of the elite class to control the outcome of the rally. Both Charles
John Dudgeon and the new Shanghai branch chairman James L. Scott had reached
a cooperative relationship with the British government. The committee declared
the resolution invalid on the grounds of excessive abstention and reconvened the
meeting (Dudgeon 1902a). On the other hand, Mackay submitted to Scott the whole
plan of canceling likin and rising tariff, suggesting to express details at the next
meeting to win the recognition of members (Mackay 1902c). Given the difficulty in
reaching an agreement with the British government at this time, the Shanghai branch
had also actively sought support from other organizations with common interests.
When the Manchester Chamber of Commerce, Worcester Chamber of Commerce,
and other chambers of commerce with direct interests learned about the situation
in Shanghai, they successively objected to the British government’s plan (Corbett
1902; Manchester Chamber of Commerce 1902).

The Manchester Chamber of Commerce wrote to the British Foreign Office,
pointing out that raising the tariff rate to 15% would pose a serious burden on trade.
The statements of local businessmen made the British government realize that
opposition was not limited to China. The actions of the Shanghai branch represented
the will of a large part of the British bourgeoisie. The British government didn’t
intervene forcefully in the second assembly of members of the Shanghai branch.
After rejecting the original plan again at the second meeting, the British government
chose to listen to public opinion and invited representatives from the chambers of
commerce to jointly adjust the plan of canceling likin and raising the tariff formulated
by Mackay. Under the coordination of Robert Edward Bredon, the Deputy General
Tariff Department of Customs, the tariff rate increase was reduced to 12.5% (中
1994). Despite taking so many methods, the British government was unable to
completely shake the stance of British businessmen in China due to the existence of
the Shanghai branch. The British government had ultimately to make compromises.

Secondly, the Shanghai branch intervened in British government decision-making
by appealing its claims to the media and creating public pressure. The North China
Herald, an influential newspaper founded by British businessmen in China, had
become one of the most powerful public opinion tools for the Shanghai branch.
