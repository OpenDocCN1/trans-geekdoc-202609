## BENEFITS AND POSITIVES OF BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE FOR INDUSTRIAL SMALL AND MEDIUM SIZED ENTERPRISE

The term Business Intelligence indicates the combination of data, information, technologies, and tools used for multidimensional data collection and analysis to improve decision-making processes. Business Intelligence systems primarily aim to achieve many benefits for industrial small and medium sized enterprise management, especially when employed correctly. The benefits can be summarized as follows:

- Providing necessary information and knowledge within the enterprise, contributing to improved decision-making, performance, and operational efficiency.
- Offering a wide range of essential information in management operations when needed, providing flexibility to external environmental changes and quick responsiveness to customers. (Grossmann & Rinderle-ma, 2015, p. 2)
- Enhancing the quality of the enterprise’s experience related to making various decisions.
- Providing the ability to predict scenarios of different variables associated with decision-making industries.
- Discovering new knowledge, enabling the enterprise to build its competitive capabilities and enter new markets.
- Creating opportunities to gain new customers.
- Introducing new changes to the enterprise, enabling the possibility of innovating new products and services.
- Improving the relationship with suppliers, customers, and stakeholders.
- Providing opportunities to improve adopted strategies and, consequently, achieving a competitive advantage.
- Improving the enterprise’s performance by enhancing its operations and reducing various associated risks. (Olszak, 2020, pp. 38, 39)

## CHALLENGES OF BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE SUCCESS

Over the past two decades, the success of adopting economic enterprises in general, and small and medium-sized industrial enterprises in particular, faces many challenges related to the ambiguity that characterizes business intelligence systems in the ability to enhance the performance of various operational processes and other administrative aspects. The most important challenges are highlighted within the following points:

- The urgent need for enterprise managers to deal with business intelligence as more than just a provider of reports related to the operational activities of the enterprise.
- The necessity to adapt the components and parts of Information and Communication Technology (ICT) with business intelligence systems, so that the latter can operate more efficiently and effectively.
- The absence of a clear strategy and sound plans to integrate business intelligence systems with the operations of the enterprise, especially operational processes is affected by the sudden changes in the external environment. (Williams, 2016, p. 161)
- The lack of sufficient maturity of Information and Communication Technology (ICT) assets available in the enterprise, which does not allow them to adopt business intelligence systems in a way that supports business processes. This challenge necessitates improving the management and governance processes of information and communication technology.
- The lack of a precise and logical understanding by the management of the enterprise of how to deploy information and communication technology and business intelligence systems in the value creation process. (Gendron, 2013, pp. 5, 6)
- The limited financial capabilities that characterize small and medium-sized industrial enterprises, which may hinder their ability to allocate appropriate budgets to meet investment requirements in the field of business intelligence.
- The low maturity level of Information and Communication Technology (ICT) systems in small and medium-sized industrial enterprises poses a significant challenge to these enterprises in adopting business intelligence.
- The success of adopting business intelligence requires the availability of high-level human skills and organizational capabilities in the control of data collection, analysis, storage, extraction, and integration according to organizational requirements. These capabilities may be lacking in small and medium-sized industrial enterprises due to their limited resources.
- The weakness of available and acquired knowledge levels within small and medium-sized industrial enterprises due to their lack of experience in knowledge management. This adds a kind of challenge to the success of adopting business intelligence, considering that the latter is one of the most important sources of shaping knowledge.

## REQUIREMENTS FOR SMALL AND MEDIUM-SIZED INDUSTRIAL ENTERPRISES TO ADOPT BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE SYSTEMS

Most managers and business leaders have specific and precise management and operational goals, along with constrained performance rates that must be achieved within programmed periods. Therefore, attempting to convince them of the idea of investing in business intelligence systems is fundamentally related to the real benefit that these systems will provide in various areas. This includes increasing revenue, reducing costs, improving profitability, enhancing customer relationships, increasing supply chain flexibility, improving productivity rates, enhancing customer services, and improving competitive positioning. (Williams, 2016, pp. 162, 163) The adoption of a business intelligence system is, thus, associated with a set of adoption requirements that must be met to ensure the desired benefit. These requirements ultimately lead to integrating business intelligence with the strategy, plans, and business practices of small and medium-sized industrial enterprises. More specifically, business intelligence systems and various Information and Communication Technologies (ICT) should play an innovative role by integrating them into all business processes of the enterprise to improve performance, rather than merely managing the costs of these technological systems and considering them as tracking only business operations. (Gendron, 2013, pp. 6, 7)

For example, Presthus believes that business intelligence is a socio-technical system where technical aspects are equal to human aspects.
