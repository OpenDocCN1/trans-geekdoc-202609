# # Definition Of Business Intelligence in Industrial Small and Medium Sized Enterprises

In our search for the conceptual roots of the term Business Intelligence, we found the first definition of H.P Luhn in 1958 where he defined it as the ability to understand the interrelationships among presented facts and how they lead to directing work towards desired goals. In the business field, Business Intelligence is an automatic system created and developed to disseminate information across various departments and parts of the organizational structure (Grossmann & Rinderle-ma, 2015, p. 1). Laura Zapata-Cantú, Teresa Treviño, and Flor Morton see Business Intelligence as referring to a wide range of methodologies, processes, infrastructures, architectures, and technologies that work together to provide solutions for managers to make sound and quick decisions. Eventually, it relies on the real-time analysis and aggregation of data, transforming it into useful information (Zapata-Cantu, Trevino, & Morton, 2020, p. 15). It is also know as a comprehensive concept for data structuring, analysis tools and software which is embedded in a specific way to enable entrprise managers for intrantical reach of data and information simoltanously. Therefore, decision maker can have crystal clear visions for a sane administrative decision (Sharda, Delen, & Turban, 2018, p. 16). Business Intelligence comprises different activities, analysis means, works, and the necessary techniques to transform data into useful information, which in turn becoms knowledge. Thus, this knowledge is used in the enterprise to find plans which lead eventually to the adoption of operational and managerial procedures economic profits. (Loshin, 2013, p. 7)

Accordingly, Business Intelligence is a large structure of computerized media devices, means, technologies, computer software, and infrastructures. These elements are integrated according to a specific configuration, aligning with the activities and operations of the organization and its managerial levels and adopted organizational structure. The main purpose is to obtain vast data, in various forms and from different sources, both internal and external to the enterprise environment, associated with its field of activity. This data is then organized, analyzed, processed, and transformed into useful information in the management process and the making of correct decisions, thus creating knowledge. The following sequential stages are followed:

+   * Data collection and integration from various available sources, relying on ETL, data warehouses, and databases.
+   * Data analysis, organization, information production, and the preparation of various reports related to the enterprise's activities and operations. This relies on OLAP, query languages, and ad hoc analyses.
+   * Knowledge acquisition through data exploration, utilizing various data mining techniques.
