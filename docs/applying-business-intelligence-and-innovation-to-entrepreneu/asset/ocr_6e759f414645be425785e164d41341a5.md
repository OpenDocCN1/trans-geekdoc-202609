# ## WHAT IS NEUROMARKETING?

Neuromarketing, at its core, represents a multidisciplinary field that converges insights from neuroscience, psychology, and marketing to comprehend the intricacies of consumer decision-making and behavior. At the inception of this innovative discipline, Ale Smidt’s (2002) coined the term, marking a significant departure from traditional market research methodologies. Neuromarketing harnesses advanced neuroscientific tools, including electroencephalography (EEG), functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), and eye-tracking, to delve into the neural underpinnings of consumer responses to marketing stimuli (Lee et al., 2007; Plassmann et al., 2012).

By adopting these neuroscientific methods, Neuromarketing transcends the limitations of conventional market research. EEG, for instance, enables the measurement of electrical brain activity, offering real-time insights into cognitive processes related to attention, engagement, and emotional responses (Lee et al., 2007). fMRI provides a glimpse into the brain’s functional areas, aiding in the identification of neural correlates associated with preferences and decision-making (Plassmann et al., 2012). Eye-tracking, another integral tool, records eye movements, uncovering visual attention patterns and aiding in the optimization of visual elements in marketing materials.

The foundation of Neuromarketing rests on the premise that consumer decisions are often influenced by subconscious processes and emotions (Lee et al., 2007). Traditional market research techniques, reliant on self-reported data, may fall short in capturing these underlying factors. Neuromarketing, by directly accessing neural responses, provides a more accurate and comprehensive understanding of consumer behavior.
