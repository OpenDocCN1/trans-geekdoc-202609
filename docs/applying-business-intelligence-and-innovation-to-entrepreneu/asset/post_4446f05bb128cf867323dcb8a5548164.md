## Shanghai Branch of the China Association

## Proposal of the Issue of Canceling Likin and Rising Tariff: Positive Interaction Between the Shanghai Branch of the China Association and the British Government

In terms of economy, the Shanghai branch requested abolishing the likin barriers. To solve the financial crisis caused by the suppression of the Taiping Rebellion (太平天国运动), the Chinese government established likin barriers, which were similar to donation but could be implemented for a long time (Luo 2010). From then on, indiscriminate collection of likin, and extortion by officials seriously hindered the smooth flow of goods throughout the state. To further develop trade with China, British businessmen in China were eager to abolish likin barriers. They seek support from the British government. After the Boxer Rebellion (义和团运动), the British Consulate-General in Shanghai consulted with the Chamber of Commerce on the negotiations between British and China. The Shanghai branch strongly demanded to abolish likin barriers (Dudgeon 1901). Faced with the global decline of the British Empire, responding to the United States’ demand for an “open door”, the British government supported the demands of the Shanghai branch and promoted the issue of Canceling likin into the negotiation of the Renewed Treaty of Commerce and Navigation between Britain and China.

In terms of diplomacy, the Shanghai Branch of the China Association required that tariff increases must be based on Cancellation. To obtain the Boxer Indemnity (庚子赔款) as soon as possible, Germany, Russia, France, and other countries proposed increasing import tariffs on China to 10% (Liu & Liu 1984). This move would increase the cost of the Shanghai Branch’s trade with China. After learning about it, the Shanghai branch called the London headquarters of the Chinese Association to oppose any tariff increase without any return (Dudgeon 1902b). If commercial benefits such as canceling likin could be transferred, the Shanghai branch was willing to bear the burden of tariff increases. It was difficult for individual units to obtain public power. But when many individual units acted together, it was possible to compete with public actors. The Shanghai branch, through the London headquarters of the China Association, liaised with many British chambers of commerce engaged in trade with China to jointly exert pressure on the British government to influence government decision-making. The Chinese Association had extensively sought support from the Bristol Chamber of Commerce, Leeds Chamber of Commerce, and Liverpool Chamber of Commerce for the proposal of Canceling likin and increasing tariffs (Spear 1901; Calvert 1901; Barker 1901). Under pressure from the British business communities, the British government representative stated at the negotiation of the Boxer Protocol that if the tariff was raised to more than 10%, the Chinese government should cancel likin and give other commercial concessions (Wang 1985). The issue which support by the British government for the Shanghai branch had also been recognized by the major powers. But at this moment, the focus of the negotiation was the aftermath of the Boxer Rebellion (义和团运动). The issue of canceling likin and increasing tariffs was scheduled to be carried out in the negotiation of the Renewed Treaty of Commerce and Navigation with China.

## Planning About Canceling Likin and Rising Tariff: A Partial Consensus Between the Shanghai Branch and the British Government

In terms of economy, the Shanghai branch required that the import tariffs on China should not be too high. Firstly, the Shanghai branch launched a collective appeal to the British government through an organizational motion. To exert pressure on the British government, the power of individual businessmen/enterprises was limited. However, using the Shanghai branch as a platform, the demands of British businessmen in China were concentrated. In January 1902, the British treaty envoy Mackay and the committee of the Shanghai branch jointly formulated a plan for canceling likin and increasing the tariff. However, most ordinary members were dissatisfied with the proposal to increase the tariff rate to 15%. To address this issue, the Shanghai branch held a plenary meeting on March 3rd to discuss the plan and vote on it. The vote at the meeting rejected the Mackay’s plan (Mackay 1902a). Without the support of British businessmen in China, there would be a lack of practical implementers of policies towards China. The British government adopted a cooperative attitude after recognizing the importance of the Shanghai branch.

The British government actively attracted committee members, attempting to use the support of the elite class to control the outcome of the rally. Both Charles John Dudgeon and the new Shanghai branch chairman James L. Scott had reached a cooperative relationship with the British government. The committee declared the resolution invalid on the grounds of excessive abstention and reconvened the meeting (Dudgeon 1902a). On the other hand, Mackay submitted to Scott the whole plan of canceling likin and rising tariff, suggesting to express details at the next meeting to win the recognition of members (Mackay 1902c). Given the difficulty in reaching an agreement with the British government at this time, the Shanghai branch had also actively sought support from other organizations with common interests. When the Manchester Chamber of Commerce, Worcester Chamber of Commerce, and other chambers of commerce with direct interests learned about the situation in Shanghai, they successively objected to the British government’s plan (Corbett 1902; Manchester Chamber of Commerce 1902).

The Manchester Chamber of Commerce wrote to the British Foreign Office, pointing out that raising the tariff rate to 15% would pose a serious burden on trade. The statements of local businessmen made the British government realize that opposition was not limited to China. The actions of the Shanghai branch represented the will of a large part of the British bourgeoisie. The British government didn’t intervene forcefully in the second assembly of members of the Shanghai branch. After rejecting the original plan again at the second meeting, the British government chose to listen to public opinion and invited representatives from the chambers of commerce to jointly adjust the plan of canceling likin and raising the tariff formulated by Mackay. Under the coordination of Robert Edward Bredon, the Deputy General Tariff Department of Customs, the tariff rate increase was reduced to 12.5% (Zhong 1994). Despite taking so many methods, the British government was unable to completely shake the stance of British businessmen in China due to the existence of the Shanghai branch. The British government had ultimately to make compromises.

Secondly, the Shanghai branch intervened in British government decision-making by appealing its claims to the media and creating public pressure. The North China Herald, an influential newspaper founded by British businessmen in China, had become one of the most powerful public opinion tools for the Shanghai branch.
