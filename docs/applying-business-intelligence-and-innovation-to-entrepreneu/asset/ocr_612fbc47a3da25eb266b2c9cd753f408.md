Currently, businesses rely heavily on data and information, leading to informational chaos and noise due to the increasing volume and flow of data. The inconsistency and incompleteness of data used by decision-makers create uncertainty and reliance on diverse assumptions. To address this, successful companies establish specialized organizational units focusing on data collection and analysis, leading to the development of BI and Business Analytics (BA) fields. As a results, these fields concentrate on extracting useful data and information through the innovation of specialized software (Olszak, 2020, p. 3).

As a result of disruptive digital technologies, including the emergence of cloud computing, mobile computing, big data analytics, advances in machine learning and artificial intelligence, and increased reliance on social media, small and medium-sized industrial enterprises are compelled to rely on Information Technology and Communication (ITC) to adapt to current environmental conditions. There is a widespread belief among many researchers that information and communication technology plays a pivotal role in helping small and medium-sized industrial enterprises improve their operational performance and enhance their competitiveness. # Historical Evolution of Business Intelligence

The term Business Intelligence was coined by the Gartner Group in the mid-1990s, but its historical roots trace back to Management Information Systems (MIS) in the 1970s and Executive Information Systems (EIS) in the 1980s. These systems improved computerized management support, with MIS offering static two-dimensional reports, and EIS introducing dynamic multidimensional reports and trend analysis. In the 1990s, BI emerged, providing executives with all the information they needed (Sharda, Delen, & Turban, 2018, p. 16). Celina Olszak identifies three generations of BI systems: BI 1.0, BI 2.0, and BI 3.0. BI 1.0, which emerged in the 1970s and 1980s, and was closely tied to MIS, EIS, and Decision Support Systems (DSS). BI 2.0, which emerged from 1990 to 2005, had witnessed a continuous development in advanced data warehouses, OLAP techniques, and data extraction methods. It enabled enterprises to highlight their work online, aligning with market requirements. The latest generation, BI 3.0, introduced proactive solutions available to a large number of individuals within the enterprise, which was integrated with enterprise operations and offering cloud-based BI services (Olszak, 2020, pp. 42, 43).
