# BASIC CONCEPTS OF BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE IN INDUSTRIAL SMAL AND MEDUIM SIZED ENTERPRISES

The current business environment is characterized by its complexity and instability. To achieve a quick response to consumer demands, economic institutions face the inevitability of using information technology and various decision support systems. This necessitates acquiring technological communication tools and methods to gather a variety of data from different sources, analyze it, and transform it into new and useful knowledge for decision-making processes. Business Intelligence (BI) systems play a crucial role in this regard, serving as a set of practices, methods, tools, and applications that assist enterprise management in collecting, integrating, analyzing, and presenting data in an understandable and usable format, enhancing overall performance and decision-making (Olszak, 2020, p. 35). Therefore, BI includes practices and tools such as data warehousing, data mining, and reporting and querying tools contribute to informed decision-making (Deepmala, Anurag, Amizan, & S.B, 2023, pp.32,33). Therefore, Lila Rao-Graham, Maurice McNaughton, and GunjanMansingh argue that a business intelligence system provides small and medium-sized industrial enterprises with high organizational flexibility, in addition to their inherent organizational flexibility, derived from their small size. These enterprises completely lack the formal and bureaucratic organizational structures that largely constrain large enterprises. (Rao-Graham, McNaughton, & Mansingh, 2020, p. 4)

# Conditions and Reasons for the Emergence of Business Intelligence in Industrial Small and Medium Sized Enterprises

The deep economic and technological transformations that have occurred in the industrial business environment since the early 21st century have significantly reshaped internal production activities. Globalization, technological advancements, and changes in communication, data storage, and information management have shifted industrial enterprises from traditional hierarchical models to horizontal structures focused on process-oriented organization. This shift is part of the reengineering philosophy introduced by James Chenpey and Michael Hammer, emphasizing process-centric organizations.
