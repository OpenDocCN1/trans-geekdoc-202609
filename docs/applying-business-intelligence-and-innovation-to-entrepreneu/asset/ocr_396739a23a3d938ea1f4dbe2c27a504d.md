As it is known, the large enterprise differs significantly from the small and medium-sized enterprise in terms of its organizational structure, financial and human resources, expertise levels, and production and technological capabilities. Consequently, the factors influencing the definition and adoption of Business Intelligence (BI) systems in SMEs differ radically from those in large enterprises. Therefore, it is crucial to establish a specific conceptual framework for BI, accurately encompassing the key factors driving the definition and adoption of BI in SMEs.

In Algeria, an industrial SME is defined as an economic entity with a workforce ranging from 01 to 250 employees and an annual turnover not exceeding four (04) billion Algerian dinars or a total annual turnover of one (01) billion dinars, meeting the conditions of independence, and engaging in productive activities by transforming raw materials and supplies into semi-finished or finished products. (People’s Democratic Republic of Algeria, 2017, p. 5) According to this definition, the characteristics of industrial SMEs in Algeria do not differ globally due to the convergence of classification criteria, including the number of employees, annual turnover, and conditions of independency. Unlike large enterprises, which largely rely on extensive capital, technological resources, and human capital, SMEs are quite the opposite. Their owners, managers, and employees typically possess low levels of awareness and training in effectively utilizing information and communication technology (ICT), which is a fundamental pillar in defining and adopting BI systems and activating their role in value creation and overall performance improvement. Additionally, their ability to finance IT and BI projects is limited due to financial constraints.

Another key factor in defining BI systems suitable for industrial SMEs is the low levels of organizational and human knowledge, which determine their ability to collect, integrate, analyze, and organize necessary data and information. Furthermore, their capacity to design and manage operational processes horizontally and correctly integrate them with BI systems to effectively improve value creation is crucial.
