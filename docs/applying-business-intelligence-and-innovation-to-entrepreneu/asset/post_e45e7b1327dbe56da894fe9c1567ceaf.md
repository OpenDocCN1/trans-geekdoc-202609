In 1906, the Chinese government ordered the consul in the Philippines to gather chambers of commerce such as the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. to discuss the joint organization of overseas Chinese corporations and the establishment of banking, shipping, insurance, and other business (Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. 1977; Yung 1996; LI 2023).

In terms of diplomacy, the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. requested the consul to negotiate with the United States government to ban discriminatory regulations against overseas Chinese. In 1906, the United States government issued regulations requiring all Chinese returning from China to Manila to stay for a week to test for illness. To prevent overseas Chinese from being extorted, the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. protested to the consul of China in the Philippines (Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. 1977). However, this matter remained unresolved until the downfall of the Chinese government. Overseas Chinese still faced humiliation upon entering Manila. They are subjected to physical examination and interrogation like prisoners being locked up for several days (Arturo 1986; Guo 1999; Yu 1998). In 1913, the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. elected representatives to protest against the harsh treatment of customs quarantine doctors during entry inspection (Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. 1977). To further fight the elimination of unfair treatment, the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. gathered the strength of other chambers of commerce to collectively exert pressure on the colonial government of the United States in the Philippines. In 1924, in response to the new immigration bill, the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc., together with the Chinese Chamber of Commerce and the Guangdong Huiguan (广东会馆), submitted a protest to Leonard Wood, the United States Governor-General in the Philippines. Under pressure from the Chinese business communities in the Philippines, Wood rejected the case as the reason for violating the United States Constitution (Huang & He 2009; Zhuang 2009).

## DISCUSSION

From the interactions between the two overseas private business groups and their home states, we can find four forms of interaction that correspond to different results. When the public sphere existed, the Shanghai branch and the British government could engage in positive interaction around public issues. The Shanghai branch influences the decisions made by the British government through collective pressure, newspaper opinion, rallies, and voting. When the Shanghai branch obeyed and trusted the British government, the British government supported it. The Shanghai branch became a colonial organization of the British government. The British government developed imperialism. When the Shanghai branch only obeyed but did not trust the British government, the British government reached a partial consensus with the Shanghai branch to promote mercantilism.

When the public sphere didn't exist, the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. and the Chinese government couldn't engage in positive interaction around public issues. When Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. obeyed and trusted the Chinese government, Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. could only use the submission of petitions and private meetings with Chinese officials to make interest demands to the Chinese government without affecting its decision-making. Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. had become an informal colonialism of the Chinese government. When the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. obeyed but did not trust the Chinese government, it didn't seek support from the Chinese government, instead taking private measures to solve its problems. We believe that the presence or absence of the public sphere will affect the interaction and outcomes between overseas private business groups and their home states.

## CONCLUSION

By comparing the interaction between the Shanghai branch and the British government, as well as between the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. and the Chinese government, we find that the presence or absence of the public sphere has a significant impact on the interaction between the overseas private business group and the home state. It is precisely because of a public sphere between the Shanghai branch and the British government that they can establish public issues, using media opinions, and gatherings to promote the development of public affairs. On the other hand, due to the absence of a public sphere between the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. and the Chinese government, they were unable to establish public issues. There was no space for discussion and coordination. Therefore, the public sphere is where overseas private business groups can interact positively with their home state. In the public sphere, the two can exchange their demands, and ultimately reach partial consensus through compromise, promoting common development.
