Wu Tingfang (伍廷芳), only suggested the Foreign Office(外务部) state that if the Spanish government still refused to establish a consulate of China in the Philippines, it would be stipulated how to compensate the damage to the lives and property of overseas Chinese. China wouldn’t bear any responsibility for the troubles made by overseas Chinese who were without consul constraints (Huang IV, 1989). This statement, similar to an exemption from liability, was far from meeting the needs of Chinese businessmen in the Philippines to ensure their safety during the war. At this moment, Manila had been surrounded by the United States military. The Spanish government almost lost control. To save the current crisis, the Spanish government agreed to the temporary establishment of a consul by the Chinese government in Manila (Wilson 2004). On July 29th, the Foreign Office appointed Chen Gang ( 陈纲), the son of Chen Qianshan (陈谦善), as the consul in the Philippines (Liang 2004). After the United States military occupied Manila, the Foreign Office turned to negotiate with the United States government. On October 4th, Wu Tingfang (伍廷芳) informed the Foreign Office that the United States government had approved China’s establishment of a consulate in Manila (Huang 1989).

The attitudes of the ambassadors to the United States, Spanish, and Peru could be seen in their previous actions with the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs. It should be said as lax and even lazy in politics. Moreover, when negotiating with the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs to establish a consulate in the Philippines, Zhang Yinhuan (张荫桓) emphasized that the China Merchants Steam Navigation Company (招商局) intended to carry cargo to expand the scope of the Chinese government business (Ren & Ma 2015). The various considerations of the Chinese officials were different from the consul role that Chinese businessmen in the Philippines hoped for. The Chinese officials’ support for the establishment of a consulate was limited.
