In 1906, the Chinese government ordered the consul in the Philippines to gather chambers of commerce such as the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. to discuss the joint organization of overseas Chinese corporations and the establishment of banking, shipping, insurance, and other business (Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. 1977; Yung 1996; LI 2023).

In terms of diplomacy, the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. requested the consul to negotiate with the United States government to ban discriminatory regulations against overseas Chinese. In 1906, the United States government issued regulations requiring all Chinese returning from China to Manila to stay for a week to test for illness. To prevent overseas Chinese from being extorted, the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. protested to the consul of China in the Philippines (Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. 1977). However, this matter remained unresolved until the downfall of the Chinese government. Overseas Chinese still faced humiliation upon entering Manila. They are subjected to physical examination and interrogation like prisoners being locked up for several days (Arturo 1986; Guo 1999; Yu 1998). In 1913, the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. elected representatives to protest against the harsh treatment of customs quarantine doctors during entry inspection (Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. 1977). To further fight the elimination of unfair treatment, the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. gathered the strength of other chambers of commerce to collectively exert pressure on the colonial government of the United States in the Philippines. In 1924, in response to the new immigration bill, the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc., together with the Chinese Chamber of Commerce and the Guangdong Huiguan (广东会馆), submitted a protest to Leonard Wood, the United States Governor-General in the Philippine. Under pressure from the Chinese business communities in the Philippines, Wood rejected the case as the reason for violating the United States Constitution (Huang & He 2009; Zhuang 2009).
