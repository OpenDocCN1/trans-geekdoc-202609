Examining these interactions is crucial to fully comprehend how the environment influences the development and expansion of new employment in the residential environment (Acs et al., 2012; Ács et al., 2014, 2016; Alvedalen & Boschma, 2017). These components are knitted together in a way to empowers the entrepreneur. Van De Ven, (1993) states that several components interconnectedly contribute to the development of the ecosystem, some of which primarily relate to the role entrepreneurs play.

Certain characteristics of the entrepreneurial ecosystem make it an attractive setting for a variety of supporting activities. These include the promotion of present and future knowledge, the intense attention of investment firms, institutions for skill improvement and training, governance, access to legal and financial resources, and the desire to invent and create. Recent studies, particularly theoretical contributions, provide a useful framework for identifying qualities essential to the success of a startup environment. Van De Ven, (1993) is one of the prominent researchers who has extensively worked with business enterprises and advocates that industrial infrastructure can help to produce an enabling environment that contributes to the entrepreneurial ecosystem. This infrastructure consists of functional arrangements for technology management and implementation, the provision of basic scientific information services, the estimation of the amount of financing, skilled labor, research facilities, and marketing and logistics support that are readily available. The university, the government, the professionals, support services, capital services, and the talent team are all cited as important elements of the business environment (Cohen, 2006; Neck et al., 2004).

# DEFINING ENTREPRENEURIAL ENVIRONMENT AND ENTREPRENEURSHIP ECOSYSTEM

The entrepreneurial environment, as defined by Spiegel, (2017), consists of eleven traits that give entrepreneurs security, encouragement, and access to resources. Aspects include supporting culture, entrepreneurial history, investor talent, networks, advisers, and successful businesses, as well as academics, virtual infrastructure, and market support (Spiegel, 2017; Spiegel & Harrison, 2018). Researchers have put forth various frameworks for explaining the role of the entrepreneurial journey which should be a combination of different elements including affirmative culture, streamlined policies and leadership, access to exclusive funding, fair utilization of labor, user-friendly product markets, and a variety of institutional and infrastructure support (Isenberg, 2014).
