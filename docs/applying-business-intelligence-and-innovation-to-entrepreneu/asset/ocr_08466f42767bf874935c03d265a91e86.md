Intellectual Stimulation (IS) means that the manager stimulates employees to become more creative and innovative through, among other things, linguistic guidelines. The intricate and diverse topic of the relationship between individual creativity and environmental sustainability has attracted interest from a wide range of academic fields. Awan et al. (2019) emphasized the critical role of creativity in addressing environmental sustainability, particularly in the context of green innovation. They offer a broadly applicable paradigm that illustrates how creativity fosters green innovation and can positively impact sustainability. This suggests that individual creativity, when integrated into organizational practices, can contribute significantly to environmental sustainability (Souto, 2022). Additionally, Moraru (2021) (Moraru, 2021) highlights the potential link between individual creativity and environmental sustainability, suggesting that creativity can be linked to environmental and economic sustainability and the dimensions of social and human sustainability. In conclusion, Intellectual Stimulation (IS) can positively impact environmental sustainability, highlighting the potential of individual creativity as a driver for sustainable development.

Individual Attention (IO) is about individual attention and seeing the employees. Transformational leaders focus on employees' needs for growth and development by acting as mentors or coaches. In that sense, mentorship plays a crucial role in fostering environmental sustainability within organizations. Reddy et al. (2021) emphasized the importance of peer-to-peer or collaborative mentorship relationships in promoting sustainable practices among employees. Similarly, Erhabr (2018) underscores the integral role of mentorship programs in developing effective environmental leaders, indicating the potential of mentorship in cultivating individuals who can drive sustainability initiatives within organizations. These references collectively suggest that mentorship, particularly in peer-to-peer or collaborative relationships, is instrumental in influencing employee adoption of sustainability practices.

Furthermore, the literature in this chapter suggests that transformational leadership can indirectly affect organizational performance through various mediating variables. For example, Çop and friends (2021) emphasized that transformational leaders provide environmentally sustainable results by engaging employees in both the organization's activities and the environment. Moreover, Suliman et al. (2023) found that transformational leadership increases the total environmental sustainability performance of the organizations; however, they did not specify the environmental performance indicators.
