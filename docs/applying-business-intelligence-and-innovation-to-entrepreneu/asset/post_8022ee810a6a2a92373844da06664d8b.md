
Businesses that strategically design and optimize their supply chains gain an edge. A managed supply chain ensures operations, timely deliveries, and cost efficiency, setting businesses apart from others in the market. This competitive advantage goes beyond cost reduction; it also involves creating an agile structure that can quickly adapt to changing market conditions. This phenomenon aligns with resource-based views and dynamic capabilities theory. The resource-based view suggests that competitive advantage stems from valuable resources possessed by firms. Efficient OSCM then becomes a resource that’s not easily replicable by competitors. Dynamic capabilities theory emphasizes a company’s capacity to effectively integrate, develop, and adapt both external competencies to navigate rapidly changing environments. Within this context, operational supply chain management (OSCM) serves as a capability that empowers entrepreneurs to continuously adapt and innovate, thereby contributing to the attainment of competitive advantage (Tönnissen & Teuteborg 2020).

### Enhancing Operational Efficiency and Cost Reduction

For ventures aiming to optimize resource utilization and minimize costs, operational efficiency stands as a metric. OSCM plays a role in enhancing efficiency by streamlining processes, reducing waste, and optimizing resource allocation. The implications extend beyond cost savings; they encompass the establishment of an agile operational framework that bolsters overall adaptability for the business. From a perspective, the relationship between OSCM and operational efficiency aligns with principles derived from systems theory and efficiency theories. Systems theory asserts that organizations operate as systems with interconnected components that rely on one another for functioning. By implementing OSCM practices, entrepreneurs effectively optimize the system by ensuring each component works harmoniously together. Efficiency theories emphasize the significance of minimizing resource wastage while maximizing output. Through the integration of OSCM practices, entrepreneurs adhere to these principles of efficiency, ensuring optimal resource utilization (Inman & Green 2018).

### Prioritizing Customer Satisfaction and Fostering Loyalty

Within the ecosystem, prioritizing customer satisfaction is crucial for establishing a customer base. OSCM plays a role in ensuring that products are available and delivered on time and consistently of quality. The impact of OSCM on customer satisfaction aligns with the Service Profit Chain theory, which suggests that satisfied customers result in increased loyalty, repeat business, and positive word of mouth. Efficient OSCM contributes to a seamless supply chain, enhancing customer experiences and fostering brand loyalty (Mahmoud et al., 2018). This relationship is emphasized by the principles of customer relationship management (CRM) and service quality theories. CRM highlights the significance of managing and nurturing long-term customer relationships for profitability. When designed with a customer approach, OSCM aligns with CRM principles to ensure that the entire supply chain is focused on meeting and surpassing customer expectations. Furthermore, service quality theories emphasize that consistent delivery of high-quality service leads to customer satisfaction and retention. Within the context of OSCM, service quality extends beyond the product to encompass the supply chain process.

### Mitigating Risks and Building Resilience

Entrepreneurial ventures inherently face risks including supply chain disruptions and market uncertainties. OSCM serves as a tool for identifying, assessing, and mitigating risks. The impact of Operations and Supply Chain Management (OSCM) on risk management is in line with the principles of resilience theory and risk management frameworks. Resilience theory emphasizes a system’s ability to absorb shocks, adapt, and reorganize to maintain functionality. Effective OSCM practices contribute to resilience by enabling responses to disruptions, ensuring continuity and minimizing risks. From a perspective, risk management frameworks like the COSO Enterprise Risk Management framework provide an approach to identify and mitigate risks. Efficient OSCM aligns with these frameworks, offering entrepreneurs a way to evaluate and address supply chain risks. This not only protects businesses from disruptions but also contributes to long-term sustainability.

### Innovation and Adaptability

Innovation and adaptability are also implications of OSCM for ventures operating in dynamic markets. OSCM becomes a catalyst for improvement, allowing businesses to adapt to emerging trends, seize opportunities, and stay ahead in an ever-changing marketplace (Kim & Marcouiller 2018). The scientific foundation of this relationship lies in innovation theories and organizational learning principles. Innovation theories suggest that businesses must continuously innovate to remain competitive. When viewed as a capability, OSCM aligns with this idea by fostering an environment of ongoing improvement. Organizational learning theories highlight the importance of adaptability and knowledge acquisition for success. OSCM, as a way to learn from market feedback and adapt operations, aligns with these principles, ensuring that businesses adapt to changing environments.

### Expanding Globally and Reaching Markets

Entrepreneurial ventures aiming for expansion face challenges regarding logistics, customs, and distribution (Moshood et al., 2021). The impact of OSCM on reaching global markets aligns with business theories and strategies for market entry. International business theories emphasize the importance of adaptability and responsiveness to markets. Efficient OSCM practices equip entrepreneurs with the tools needed to navigate these challenges and ensure that supply chains are tailored to the demands of markets. Market entry strategies such as exporting, licensing, and strategic alliances are influenced by the efficiency of supply chain operations. From a perspective, the connection between OSCM and global expansion is in line with transaction cost economics, which suggests that the choice of market entry mode is influenced by transaction efficiency. Effective OSCM reduces transaction costs associated with operations, making international expansion more viable for ventures.

### Sustainable and Ethical Practices

In an era where sustainability and ethical considerations are fundamental in business practices, the implications of OSCM extend into responsibility. Entrepreneurs who embrace ethical practices in their supply chains do not meet the expectations of socially conscious consumers but also foster long-term trust and reputation for their brands. The connection between these practices and corporate social responsibility (CSR) theories as ethical supply chain management frameworks is supported by scientific research (Mani et al., 2020). CSR theories emphasize the significance of businesses contributing to societal well-being beyond profit making (Brin & Nehme 2019). By implementing practices in operations and supply chain management that consider social impacts, entrepreneurs position themselves as responsible corporate citizens. Ethical supply chain management frameworks, such as the UN Global Compact, offer guidance to businesses in ensuring practices throughout their supply chains. Entrepreneurs who incorporate these frameworks into their strategies contribute to ethical entrepreneurship.

### Data-Driven Decision-Making

Furthermore, the implications of operations and supply chain management extend to data-driven decision-making for entrepreneurs. By leveraging advancements and utilizing data analytics within their supply chains, entrepreneurs gain insights. This scientific relationship aligns with the principles of data-driven decision-making, which emphasize using data and analytics to inform choices. From a perspective, integrating data analytics into operations and supply chain management resonates with business intelligence principles and decision support systems. Business intelligence utilizes data analysis tools to provide insights for decision making, whereas decision support systems (Nikiforova et al., 2022).
