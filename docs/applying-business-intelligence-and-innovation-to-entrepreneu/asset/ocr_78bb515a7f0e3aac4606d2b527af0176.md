It is anticipated to result from lower levels of outsourcing technologies when choosing sales and marketing initiatives that focus on technology-related goods and services (Koza et al., 1998; Koza & Lewin, 1998; Lavie & Rosenkopf, 2006). Marketing-related activities are frequently left out and unreliable (such as product performance information and expected benefits), leaving outsourcing to at least sales and marketing activities for products and services that contain high levels of technical knowledge and uncertainty. Because market outsourcing and sales are frequently less susceptible to the degree of exploration activity, we anticipate that improved levels of technical exploration activity will have a major negative impact on R&D outsourcing.

The performance measurement tool concept – “startup cockpit” introduced by Ripsas, Shaper, and Toger, (2018) focused on indicators such as customer service, financial vision, and procedure. These three indicators are relevant while investigating startup efficiency. Further indicators such as economic survival and profitability are believed to be assessed while evaluating financial forecasts. Liquidity, burn rate, margin analysis, and return on investment are further sub-layers of performance evaluation. From investors’ perspective, client acquisition expenses, customer lifetime value, and learning curve can be other indicators.

As a startup expands, making decisions need more information. It is therefore frequently stated that startups should specialize in fields that are thought to be of utmost relevance to spend time and resources effectively and efficiently. Startups are therefore expected to perform better in areas that management deems more crucial and less significant.

# Sustainability

Now when it comes to assessing the sustainability aspect of the startups, researchers have suggested evaluating the implementation phases. Building, measuring, and learning were the initial three stages as stated by Eric Ries, (2016). Successful startups complete the business iteration loop model in the early stages until training and insights from client feedback offer sufficient proof that the business model is successful and pertinent (Ries, 2016; Ries & Euchner, 2013; Ripsas et al., 2018a, 2018b; Ripsas & Hentschel, 2017)

Croll and Yoskovitz (2017),identified five basic categories that can be investigated when assessing the sustainability of startups, these include adherence, empathy, average customer experiences, vitality of product/service, and income generated. Empathy is the stage at which the needs of actual customers that cannot be satisfied are recognized. The most important aspect is when a startup learns how to address its demands and retain clients.
