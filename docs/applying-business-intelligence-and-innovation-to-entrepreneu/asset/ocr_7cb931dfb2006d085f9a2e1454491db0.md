As shown in figure (1), we can say that Business Intelligence (BI) systems have emerged and evolved through five chronological stages since the early seventies of the twentieth century, with the emergence of Decision Support Systems. The seventies were characterized by relying on Management Information Systems (MIS) in decision-making. This involved preparing reports concerning various operational, organizational, and strategic variables over past time periods. The executive management relied on these reports for making future decisions. Additionally, Operations Research and Expert Systems techniques were used to solve complex problems faced by enterprise management. The second stage extended through the eighties and witnessed significant changes in how enterprise management obtained data related to its activities and fields. Traditional systems used to capture data from various units, such as marketing, accounting, and manufacturing, were integrated into a unified system at the enterprise level, known as Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP). During this stage, Relational Database Management Systems (RDBMS) were also introduced to improve data capture, storage, and computerized access. In the nineties, due to the need for more diverse reports for top management, Executive Information Systems (EISs) and Decision Support Systems (DSSs) were introduced. These systems were specifically designed for executive managers to meet their decision-making needs. They included visual information dashboards with performance metrics and essential indicators, relying on data warehouses to facilitate and expedite decision-making (Sharda, Delen, & Turban, 2018, pp. 13, 14, 15).
