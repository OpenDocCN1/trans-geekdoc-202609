## TECHNOLOGY BUSINESS INCUBATORS (TBIs) IN ENHANCING ENTREPRENEURIAL ECOSYSTEMS

For many years, science, technology, and innovation have advanced together. The entrepreneurial input and the surrounding support structure, which includes institutional setup, have a significant impact on the creation of new technology-based inventions. The development of mobility indicators between innovators and other organizations, across sectors that produce and use research, and inside businesses is necessary. The market incentives that drive technology endeavors like research and development by businesses and result in a competitive advantage have been recommended to be mapped. Such work also produces knowledge spillover, which boosts overall economic growth and increases economic competitiveness.

It essentially illustrates the precise function that technology entrepreneurs play by developing new concepts, obtaining patents, and entering markets.

Technology Business Incubators (TBIs) support the early stage and development of startups by offering various technological, managerial, and other administrative support. National Business Incubation Association (NBIA) has identified main goal of an incubator is to create profitable, self-sustaining businesses. The goal of the incubator is to help aspiring business owners get off the ground. A business consultant can help bridge the gap that exists in many areas from mentoring to funding support. By offering entrepreneurs fundamental training, the chance to launch a firm, and in certain circumstances, the chance to interact with other potential future investors in the company, incubator programs fill the gap (Burns & Jim, 1996).

TBIs aim to use technology, resources, and first-hand experience to foster entrepreneurial talent, speed up the growth of the art industry, and ultimately speed up the selling of technology (Hackett & Dilts, 2004a, 2004b). They collaborate closely with colleges, research facilities, and technological parks. Technology business incubators are an endeavor to offer a supportive environment for technology-based businesses, new product and service development for the technology sector, and new market delivery.

### Technology Business Incubation: Gujarat

Gujarat is making significant investments in creating a solid ecosystem for fostering innovation and startups to promote job growth and drive economic expansion. As per the information from GoG, the state has an ambitious goal to build about 200,000 square meters of incubator space, construct about 100 incubators, and support at least 2,000 companies over the next few years. To date, Gujarat has given 175 businesses financial support totaling INR 220 million (USD 3 million) during the last two years. By 2021, the government had assisted 2,000 startups. 26 firms in the state are being sponsored by High-Net-Worth Individuals in addition to the 175 startups with government funding. The government has distributed institutional incubation support through several programs and projects that come under different departments (see Figure 2).

A typical model of incubation support in Gujarat state relates to helping startups in the form of mentoring, counsel, and resources. One of these is a very well-known event called a “Demo-Day,” which aims to draw the startup investor community’s attention to cutting-edge startup ideas by hosting a series of investment pitches from innovators to investors. TBIs typically provide physical places for shared resources and other incubation events in addition to offering dedicated office space or co-working space to startups (and sometimes even encouraging companies to locate their own dedicated space). Business incubators frequently provide businesses with exclusive office and development space for a predetermined amount of time.

While having a discussion with TBI heads from the state, it was observed that business incubators get involved from the very beginning of a startup's development, from ideation to the stage where revenue is generated, and they can even go as far as the later growth stage. However, because this is the time at which most businesses normally benefit from outside assistance, most incubators tend to concentrate on relatively early-stage entrepreneurs. Incubated startups are often admitted in batches offering 1-3 batches every year during the incubator and accelerator's usual incubation procedure. While some are more generalist, some incubators concentrate on a particular industry, market, technology, stage, or other thrust areas. The majority attempt to conduct a screening and application process before incubation.

### TBI's Role in Enhancing Operational Performance and Sustainability in Startups

#### Operational Performance

When it comes to assessing the performance of the startups, several elements (or factors) are highlighted which can be considered. The higher levels of technological exploration are predicted to slow down the outsourcing of research and development (R&D) activities and make it more expensive for new enterprises to achieve since doing so has a substantial influence and can contribute to long-term uncertainty and knowledge. It is anticipated to result from lower levels of outsourcing technologies when choosing sales and marketing initiatives that focus on technology-related goods and services (Koza et al., 1998; Koza & Lewin, 1998; Lavie & Rosenkopf, 2006). Marketing-related activities are frequently left out and unreliable (such as product performance information and expected benefits), leaving outsourcing to at least sales and marketing activities for products and services that contain high levels of technical knowledge and uncertainty. Because market outsourcing and sales are frequently less susceptible to the degree of exploration activity, we anticipate that improved levels of technical exploration activity will have a major negative impact on R&D outsourcing.

The performance measurement tool concept – “startup cockpit” introduced by Ripsas, Shaper, and Toger (2018) focused on indicators such as customer service, financial vision, and procedure. These three indicators are relevant while investigating startup efficiency. Further indicators such as economic survival and profitability are believed to be assessed while evaluating financial forecasts. Liquidity, burn rate, margin analysis, and return on investment are further sub-layers of performance evaluation. From investors’ perspective, client acquisition expenses, customer lifetime value, and learning curve can be other indicators.

As a startup expands, making decisions need more information. It is therefore frequently stated that startups should specialize in fields that are thought to be of utmost relevance to spend time and resources effectively and efficiently. Startups are therefore expected to perform better in areas that management deems more crucial and less significant.

#### Sustainability

Now when it comes to assessing the sustainability aspect of the startups, researchers have suggested evaluating the implementation phases. Building, measuring, and learning were the initial three stages as stated by Eric Ries (2016). Successful startups complete the business iteration loop model in the early stages until training and insights from client feedback offer sufficient proof that the business model is successful and pertinent (Ries, 2016; Ries & Euchner, 2013; Ripsas et al., 2018a, 2018b; Ripsas & Hentschel, 2017)
