In 1889, the new ambassador to the United States, Spanish and Peru, Cui Guoyin (崔国因), was preparing to resume negotiations on establishing a consulate in the Philippines during his visit to Madrid. However, due to an unexpected incident in the United States, Cui Guoyin (崔国因) returned in a hurry. There was no follow-up on it afterward (Wang & Wang 2015). From the attitudes of the two ambassadors, it can be seen that the Chinese government attaches limited importance to this issue.

During the Katipunan revolt, Chinese businessmen in the Philippines were subjected to bullying by rebels. The Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. once again proposed to the Chinese government to establish a consulate in the Philippines and pleaded for the dispatch of warships to protect overseas Chinese. They also begged for imploring other countries to protect overseas Chinese they were afraid that it would be difficult to expedite the establishment so soon (Wilson 2004). The Chinese government had made no mention of the dispatch of warships. It is unwilling to take any military action on behalf of overseas Chinese. The request to establish a consulate was only mentioned by the Chinese government in response to the Spanish Ambassador to China. The Chinese government proposed that overseas Chinese were deeply persecuted. If they could establish a consulate like other countries, it would not be so tragic (Huang III, 1989). This hypothetical euphemism naturally couldn't be a legitimate expression for requesting the establishment of a consulate. In response to the request for assistance from other countries, the Foreign Office called Yang Ru(杨儒), the ambassador to the British to request the British Consul in the Philippines to protect overseas Chinese (Huang & He 2009). But when the Spanish Ambassador to China opposed the British act of protecting the Chinese government didn't mention it again (Huang III, 1989). However, the British government took on the responsibility of protecting overseas Chinese, ignoring the opposition from the Spanish government. Under the protection of the British consul in the Philippines the cases of two Chinese businessmen named Cai Zao (蔡燥) and Chen Dang (陈党) who were treated harshly in the Philippines were won (Huang & He 2009). The Chinese Office of Foreign Affairs expressed great gratitude to the British ambassador to China for this. It was also hoped that the British consul in the Philippines could protect overseas Chinese in the future by international law (Huang 1989; Long 2007). In other words, the Chinese government had completely forgotten its responsibilities towards overseas Chinese.

During the Spanish-American War, Chinese businessmen in the Philippines were heavily implicated. However, the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. no longer requested the Chinese government to dispatch warships. It only pleaded for the establishment of a consulate in the Philippines as soon as possible to provide protection (Huang IV, 1989). The lives of Chinese businessmen in the Philippines were in danger.
