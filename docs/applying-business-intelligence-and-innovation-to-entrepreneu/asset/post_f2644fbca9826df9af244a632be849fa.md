Further discussion on establishing a consulate would be taken after verifying that stable funds could be provided (Yuan et al. 1998). The subsequent investigation results showed that Chinese businessmen in the Philippines were willing to raise funds for the consulate establishment. However, the Foreign Office was still concerned that the consulate would be like the ones in Cuba and Singapore. Overseas Chinese businessmen initially actively raised funds, but became weak later (First Pavilion 2003). In essence, the Chinese government did not want to bear any economic burden of a consulate in the Philippines.

After the outbreak of the Katipunan revolt, the Philippine government forced Chinese businessmen to transport grain. The rebels hated Chinese businessmen for doing it. Chinese businessmen's shops in the Philippines were looted by the rebels. Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. once again requested the Chinese government to establish a consulate to protect the security of the Chinese business industry in the Philippines (Wilson 2004). However, the Spanish ambassador to China claimed that the rebels' hatred of Chinese businessmen was due to normal trade competition which was very common (Huang III, 1989). The persecution faced by overseas Chinese in the Philippines daily was evident from this. Under Spanish colonial rule, the economic capacity of overseas Chinese was greatly restricted (Zhu & Liu 2009). Faced with such excuses, the Chinese government had neither pursued nor held accountable the security of the Chinese business industry in the Philippines (Huang III, 1989).

In April 1898, the Spanish-American War broke out (Yang 2019). Under the precarious situation of the property safety of Chinese businessmen in the Philippines, the Chinese government which had gained some understanding of international law (Yu 1998; Zhu 2017), requested the British Consul in the Philippines to protect Chinese businessmen (Huang & He 2009). The Chinese government also required the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. to raise funds to pay the expenses of British in protecting Chinese businessmen in the Philippines (Huang III, 1989). It could be seen that the Chinese government was unwilling to bear the cost of protecting the Chinese in the Philippines.

In terms of diplomacy, the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. requests the Chinese government to establish a consulate to improve the political status of Chinese businessmen in the Philippines and protect life safety. In 1886, the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. sent representatives to China to request the establishment of a consulate in the Philippines. The Philippine Ministry of Foreign Affairs declined it with the pretext that the issue belonged to the colonial administration after meeting Zhang Yinhuan (张荫桓). However, taking these verbal excuses so easily, Zhang Yinhuan (张荫桓) wrote in his diary that he was well aware of the difficulties faced by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. He didn't keep on persisting (Ren & Ma 2015). In 1889, the new ambassador to the United States, Spanish and Peru, Cui Guoyin (崔国因), was preparing to resume negotiations on establishing a consulate in the Philippines during his visit to Madrid. However, due to an unexpected incident in the United States, Cui Guoyin (崔国因) returned in a hurry. There was no follow-up on it afterward (Wang & Wang 2015). From the attitudes of the two ambassadors, it can be seen that the Chinese government attaches limited importance to this issue.

During the Katipunan revolt, Chinese businessmen in the Philippines were subjected to bullying by rebels. The Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. once again proposed to the Chinese government to establish a consulate in the Philippines and pleaded for the dispatch of warships to protect overseas Chinese. They also begged for imploring other countries to protect overseas Chinese they were afraid that it would be difficult to expedite the establishment so soon (Wilson 2004). The Chinese government had made no mention of the dispatch of warships. It is unwilling to take any military action on behalf of overseas Chinese. The request to establish a consulate was only mentioned by the Chinese government in response to the Spanish Ambassador to China. The Chinese government proposed that overseas Chinese were deeply persecuted. If they could establish a consulate like other countries, it would not be so tragic (Huang III, 1989). This hypothetical euphemism naturally couldn't be a legitimate expression for requesting the establishment of a consulate. In response to the request for assistance from other countries, the Foreign Office called Yang Ru (杨儒), the ambassador to the British to request the British Consul in the Philippines to protect overseas Chinese (Huang & He 2009). But when the Spanish Ambassador to China opposed the British act of protecting the Chinese government didn't mention it again (Huang III, 1989). However, the British government took on the responsibility of protecting overseas Chinese, ignoring the opposition from the Spanish government. Under the protection of the British consul in the Philippines the cases of two Chinese businessmen named Cai Zao (蔡燥) and Chen Dang (陈党) who were treated harshly in the Philippines were won (Huang & He 2009). The Chinese Office of Foreign Affairs expressed great gratitude to the British ambassador to China for this. It was also hoped that the British consul in the Philippines could protect overseas Chinese in the future by international law (Huang 1989; Long 2007). In other words, the Chinese government had completely forgotten its responsibilities towards overseas Chinese.

During the Spanish-American War, Chinese businessmen in the Philippines were heavily implicated. However, the Philippine Chinese Charitable Association Inc. no longer requested the Chinese government to dispatch warships. It only pleaded for the establishment of a consulate in the Philippines as soon as possible to provide protection (Huang IV, 1989). The lives of Chinese businessmen in the Philippines were in danger. The ambassador to the United States, Spanish, and Peru, Wu Tingfang (伍廷芳), only suggested the Foreign Office (外务部) state that if the Spanish government still refused to establish a consulate of China in the Philippines, it would be stipulated how to compensate the damage to the lives and property of overseas Chinese. China wouldn't bear any responsibility for the troubles made by overseas Chinese who were without consul constraints (Huang IV, 1989). This statement, similar to an exemption from liability, was far from meeting the needs of Chinese businessmen in the Philippines to ensure their safety during the war. At this moment, Manila had been surrounded by the United States military. The Spanish government almost lost control. To save the current crisis, the Spanish government agreed to the temporary establishment of a consul by the Chinese government in Manila (Wilson 2004). On July 29th, the Foreign Office appointed Chen Gang (陈纲), the son of Chen Qianshan (陈谦善), as the consul in the Philippines (Liang 2004). After the United States military occupied Manila, the Foreign Office turned to negotiate with the United States government. On October 4th, Wu Tingfang (伍廷芳) informed the Foreign Office that the United States government had approved China's establishment of a consulate in Manila (Huang 1989).

The attitudes of the ambassadors to the United States, Spanish, and Peru could be seen in their previous actions with the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs. It should be said as lax and even lazy in politics. Moreover, when negotiating with the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs to establish a consulate in the Philippines, Zhang Yinhuan (张荫桓) emphasized that the China Merchants Steam Navigation Company (招商局) intended to carry cargo to expand the scope of the Chinese government business (Ren & Ma 2015). The various considerations of the Chinese officials were different from the consul role that Chinese businessmen in the Philippines hoped for. The Chinese officials' support for the establishment of a consulate was limited.
