### Human Requirements for Adopting Business Intelligence

The human requirements for Business Intelligence encompass a diverse set of skills that should be possessed by business analysts, the individuals forming the technical team responsible for designing and operating the enterprise’s Business Intelligence system. Small and medium-sized industrial enterprises often face limitations in having individuals with high-level technical skills due to their limited human resources. Additionally, Business Intelligence, business analysis, and data analysis are considered highly specialized and precise fields, making training individuals in these areas a significant challenge for management.

Bert Brijs identifies five types of skills that should be present in a business analyst:

- **Data Knowledge:** Business analysts should master linking data quality to master data management, starting from data profiling to analyzing data proportions. This requires in-depth knowledge of processes responsible for creating, using, or modifying data.
- **Application Knowledge:** involves proficiency in working with Business Intelligence applications capturing real events and transactions within the organization, such as invoices, purchase and sales orders, etc. Familiarity with Customer Relationship Management (CRM), Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP), sales channels, and Online Analytical Processing (OLAP) is crucial as they are primary data sources.
- **Process Knowledge:** acquiring knowledge related to the nature of the organization’s processes is one of the most critical skills for business analysts. Understanding business processes is vital as they are significant sources of data.
- **BI Skills:** encompasses skills to maximize enterprise value from using Business Intelligence. These skills are associated with integrating Business Intelligence components with the activities and processes practiced by the organization to contribute to maximizing the produced value.
- **Interpersonal Skills:** involves skills such as conducting interviews, gathering requirements, conducting workshops, brainstorming, and mediating between opposing factions. (Brijs, 2013, p. 343)

## Research Methodology

Among 20 fieldwork visits to 20 small and medium-sized industrial enterprises located in the industrial zone of Setif district in Algeria, we interviewed 32 managers, and executive managers (the chief information officer (CIO), chief financial officer (CFO), chief operating officer (COO), and the chief executive officer (CEO) over a period of two months, through which we administered a questionnaire which contains two important sections. The first section is about the interest of the administration of small and medium-sized enterprises towards the adoption of Business Intelligence Systems and it was administered to 20 enterprises. The second section of the questionnaire contains 14 questionnaires and it was administered to 11 enterprises. It was administered to the enterprises that have already adopted Business Intelligence Systems, and the managers were asked questions about the requirements of adopting Business Intelligence, and the activities which can be enhanced using BI Systems. These questions are selected on the basis of its correlation with enterprises strategy, in addition to its contribution to creating value. Henceforth, detailed questions were asked about practical application of BI systems which happened or could happen in the future at the level of the enterprise.

## Data View and Analysis

The first section of the questionnaire contained five questions administered to 20 small and medium-sized industrial enterprises, and it is related to how interested the administration of the enterprise is towards adopting BI system (questions are set according to the technical basis of BI system). The results indicated that only 11 enterprises of the total number are interested in adopting BI systems, against 9 enterprises which show no interest at all. Based on the results obtained, we set 14 questions which correlates with the requirements of those responsible for adopting BI in the enterprises. 85% of the 32 questioned managers indicated funding is not a difficult requirement for adopting BI and it does not determine its success. In addition, 90% see that designing the operational BI is considered as the basic elements for its success, as it works well when it is integrated with the enterprises' activities and operational activities. However, 77% admit that offering complete freedom to executive individuals by benefiting from data and information provided by BI systems is one of the most successful requirements for its adoption. Meanwhile, 61% believe that relying on e-administration and ICT is key factor to adopt Business Intelligence systems, and 100% admit that the skillfulness of the technical team responsible for designing and operating business intelligence system determines to a large extent the success of adopting BI systems

## Findings

It is clear that the requirements and success factors for adopting a business intelligence system are numerous and diverse, as a result of its connection to all parts of the organization. These are primarily organizational requirements, centered around the design of the operational business intelligence system, in addition to requirements related to management, control, and coordination. They are embodied in the method of integrating the business intelligence system into the organizational structure to ensure vertical and horizontal flow of data and information to enhance coordination levels. In addition to technical requirements, which are concentrated in a high level of maturity of information technology and communication within the enterprise. Moreover, a crucial requirement is the skills that individuals responsible for designing and operating the business intelligence system must possess.
