BI can provide an upward value for the enterprise when it is used in the right way by individuals. Cindi Howson sees that there is a positive correlation between the efficint use of business intelligence and the performance of the enterprise, since reaching data does not necessarily lead to efficiency and good results, but dealing, understanding, and using it in the right way is the key criterion. This chapter will attempt to study the nature of Business Intelligence (BI) systems that are compatible with small and medium-sized industrial enterprises, considering the distinctive characteristics of this type of institution compared to large enterprises. It will identify and analyze the various organizational, technical, and human requirements necessary for the successful adoption of BI systems by these enterprises, making it an effective tool for creating value, improving performance, and making the right decisions.

# CHAPTER BACKGROUND

Field experiments have shown that small and medium-sized industrial enterprises tend to use various technologies encompassed within BI, seeking to acquire a level of flexibility towards market requirements and to respond to continuous changes in their external business environment. The use of these technologies enables them to have organizational capabilities that lead to a constant sensing of available opportunities and threats. Consequently, they have begun to support the idea of acquiring and exploiting data and information assets (internal and external) as factors that effectively contribute to creating and strengthening competitive advantages in a sustainable manner. (Rao-Graham, McNaughton, & Mansingh, 2020, p. XIII) As is known, small and medium-sized industrial enterprises possess a nature characterized by agility and mobility, primarily resulting from the simplicity of their organizational structure due to their small size. This makes it easier for them to adopt process-centered organization, which is more adaptable to external environmental conditions over time. Additionally, small and medium-sized industrial enterprises mostly adopt one of three types of manufacturing systems: the job shop, the flow shop, and the Cellular shop. These manufacturing systems endow them with many characteristics associated with the strategic triangle: low cost, high quality, and timeliness.
