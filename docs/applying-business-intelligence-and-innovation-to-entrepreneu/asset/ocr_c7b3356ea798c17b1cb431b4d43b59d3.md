Ultimately, scholars do not see going to salons as haram if Muslims as users use the services as commanded by Islamic laws. In addition, it is also haram if the salon and spa managers provide and offer treatments and services that are prohibited in Islam.

The main problem in dealing with beauty treatments and spa operations from the perspective of Islam is the issue of Mahram, aurat, and the separation of different sexes. The Quran commands, “...and not expose their adornment except to their husbands, their fathers, their husbands’ fathers, their sons, their husbands’ sons, their brothers, their brothers’ sons, their sisters’ sons, their women, that which their right hands possess, or those male attendants having no physical desire, or children who are not yet aware of the private aspects of women....” (Holly Quran Surah An-Nur: 31).

There are also controversies and conflicts related to halal haram for women to open their Aurat in front of non-Muslim women. Some scholars declare that this is haram except for emergency and life-threatening conditions such as a medical operation or accident and treatment of diseases. However, when it comes to health care that is not urgent, most scholars agree that Muslim women should not open their aurat in the presence of non-Muslim women. Therefore, Muslim women should go to a salon and spa managed and owned by Muslims. Meanwhile, non-Muslims interested in opening a salon and spa targeting Muslim users are suggested to provide Muslim women therapists and staff.
