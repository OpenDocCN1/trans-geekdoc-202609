Hypothesis 3 predicts a positive relationship between gender diversity and ESG performance. In this case, the hypothesis seems to be confirmed. In all Models presented in the (Table 4), GENDERD is significant and positively related to ESG SCORE. Hence, consistent with correlation results (see Table 3), the econometric model indicates that the high shares of female in the board are associated with better sustainability performance. This positive effect is broadly consistent with the findings of (Birindelli et al., 2019; Furlotti et al., 2019; Ajaz et al., 2020; Peng, 2020). It is more likely that higher share of gender diversity are served by female with different skills and attitudes, including directors characterized by a strong propensity towards a culture of sustainability. Therefore, this result does corroborate Hypothesis 3.

On the contrary, we reject Hypothesis 4, which predicts a positive relationship between the executive compensation and the extent of sustainability performance. In our regression results (Models Full set, America and Australia), ECOMPENS shows a positive coefficient, but it is never significant for these contexts. For the area of African, Asian and European countries, the hypothesis 4 is verified very strongly (Westling, & Mazhari, 2019; BaraiBar-Diez et al., 2019).
