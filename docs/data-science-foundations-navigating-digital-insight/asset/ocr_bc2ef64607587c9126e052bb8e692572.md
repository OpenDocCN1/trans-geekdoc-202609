Correlation quantifies the strength and direction of a linear relationship between two variables, expressed as a correlation coefficient between –1 and +1.

+   - **Positive correlation**: Both variables move in the same direction. As one goes up, so does the other one.
- **Negative correlation**: Variables move in opposite directions. As the temperature drops, more gas is used for heating.
- **Zero correlation**: No linear relationship exists.

# The formula

Pearson’s correlation coefficient, $r$, is calculated as:

$r = \frac{n \sum x_i y_i - \sum x_i \sum y_i}{(n \sum x_i^2 - (\sum x)^2)(n \sum y_i^2 - (\sum y)^2)}$

where $x_i$ and $y_i$ are data points for variables $x$ and $y$, respectively.
