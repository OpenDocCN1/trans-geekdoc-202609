+   **Performance metrics**: Common metrics include accuracy, precision, recall and F1-score. However, it's essential to monitor these across both the training and test sets. Significant disparities between the two may indicate overfitting or underfitting.

Striking the right balance between these sets ensures that your model explains the data well and can generalise effectively.

False positives and false negatives

In many real-world applications, not all errors are created equal. If you build a model that predicts something will happen and it doesn't, this is an error. The impact of this error depends on the context of a problem. Imagine predicting that someone will catch the flu, but they don't. You may have given them an unnecessary vaccine, which is not likely to be the end of the world. Being able to label the errors in your model is key to understanding its impact.

+   **False positive (Type I error)**: This occurs when the model predicts a positive outcome incorrectly. For example, diagnosing someone as having a disease when they don't.
