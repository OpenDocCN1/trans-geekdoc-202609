Second, researchers understand ITG as involving the strategic alignment between IT and business in order to achieve full business value. Gremberg and Webb et al. (2006) define ITG as activities maximizing business value through bringing about this strategic alignment. In achieving this goal, they emphasize the control of resources, performance management, and risk management.
Korak-Kabadse, and Kakabadse (2001) describe IS/IT as dealing with the structures and processes aiming to develop, direct, and control IS resources such that IT adds value to the pursuit of strategic objectives. The IT Governance Institute (ITGI, 2003) defines ITG as the responsibility of corporate executives and the board of directors, referring inclusively to the leadership, organizational processes, and structures to ensure that IT supports the organization’s goals and strategies. ITG concerns IT decision-making, which is, preparation for, making, and implementing decisions, processes, people, and technology on a tactical and strategic level (Simsson, & Johnson, 2006).

Verhof (2007) presents an Architecture Theory Diagram (ATD) and a framework for defining IT governance based on an extensive literature study. Ploesser et al. (2008) defines IT as the organizational measures exercised by the Board, executive management, and IT management to control the preparation and implementation of IT strategy. IT governance aims to ensure that the organization and board of directors or governing body are aware of managing the organization's IT investments efficiently, and effectively (Bart & Turl, 2010). Scholl et al. (2011) define the ITG as a set of IT standards, methodologies, rules, and practices that limit, prescribe, and enable the implementation and uses of ICTs to support government activity.

In recent works, Aasi et al. (2014) define IT governance as a phase of developing, and implementing decisions on objects, processes, people, and technology at tactical and strategic levels. Elhasnai al. (2015) define IT governance as the responsibility of the Board of Directors and Executive Management. In her thesis, Valentine (2016) confirms that IT governance includes the leadership, alignment, and oversight of enterprise technologies with the organization’s strategy, systems, policies, and governance processes. IT governance seeks to facilitate data-driven decision-making and minimize risk throughout the enterprise. IT governance creates value by optimizing stakeholder engagement and strategic investments and in deriving results. Felix et al. (2017) propose that the positive effect on performance can be achieved through IT governance mechanisms that are formally defined, operate with defined rules and standards widely disseminated, and are periodically monitored and improved.

In short, IT governance can be commonly defined as the clarification of decision-making rights and responsibilities as companies seek to leverage assets to business goals (Males et al., 2019). This alignment is designed to allow organizations to achieve their goals by installing a systematic series of activities establishing structures and processes. Research suggests that governance works on three levels in developing IT governance frameworks, designing ‘structures’, ‘processes’, and ‘communication protocols or approaches’ (Gremberg, 2004; Weil & Ross, 2004). Structures refer to organizational units and roles responsible for making IT decisions, such as committees, executive teams, and business/IT relationship managers. Processes involve the arrangement of formal decision-making and the design of forms checking that daily behavior is consistent with firm IT policy.
