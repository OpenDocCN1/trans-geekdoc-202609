## 7.3.4 Assessing Capability Maturity

The framework's assessment tool provides a granular and focused view of an organization's current maturity state for each SPO, desired or target maturity state for each SPO, and importance attributed to each SPO. These maturity and significance scores are primarily determined by an online survey conducted by the organization's key IT and stakeholders, as shown in Appendix 1 (Table 7.7). The survey typically takes each assessment 30–45 minutes to complete. The data collected can be augmented by qualitative interviews focus on such as key security-related business priorities, successes achieved, and initiatives taken or planned. The assessment provides valuable insight into the similarities and differences in how they view both the importance and maturity of individual SPOs and the overall vision for success. Figure 7.4 shows the organization's ISG capability assessment results, outlining its current and target SPO maturity across all 21 SPOs. For each SPO, the maturity results are automatically generated by the proposed assessment tool based on averaging the survey participants' scores across all questions about that SPO. Based on this average score achieved, the organization highlighted in Figure 7.4 reflects a level 1.4 (initial) current maturity state of ISG overall. It is less mature in some SPOs, such as security budgeting, resource effectiveness, security threat profiling, and security risk handling. Based on the average across all SPOs, their desired ISG maturity state is maturity level 2.4 (Basic) for the first 6 months after the first organization assessment.

## 7.3.5 Developing Improvement Action Plans

The output from the framework's assessment supports understanding the actions to drive improvement and enables the organization to transition from its current to target maturity systematically. This is achieved by implementing a series of industry-validated practices that allow organizations to improve incrementally and monitor track progress over time using a number of industry-validated metrics. Table 7.4 includes sample practices and metrics for the five SPOs highlighted for prioritized improvement. For each of these SPOs, the figure outlines the currently reported maturity and the practices required to transition to the next maturity state. Note that additional practices are available to support transitioning to the desired maturity state.

To reach the target maturity levels score, the organization adopted an action plan planned over three phases in 2 years, as presented in Table 7.5. To reach the desired level of maturity, the organization implemented some programs during each phase of the rollout. The following initiatives were adopted in the first phase:

- Construct a security plan for the technologies used;
- Create a risk process and conduct high-level business assessments;
- Prepare initial security standards for developers;
- Conduct security awareness training for platforms that pose significant risk to the organization.
