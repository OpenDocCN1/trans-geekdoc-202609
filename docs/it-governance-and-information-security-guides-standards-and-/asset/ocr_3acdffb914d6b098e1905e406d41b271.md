Published mixed-methods studies (De Haes & Van Gremberg, 2006; Hiererra, 2012; McEvoy & Richards, 2006; McGuire, 2016) suggest that social researchers used mixed-methods approaches for one or more of the following purposes: providing a complete picture, improving accuracy, compensation for strengths and weaknesses, and, more importantly, developing robust analysis (Denscombe, 2014).

In the first research activity, a quantitative survey is used to develop an evaluation framework for IT governance in medium and large organizations. An questionnaire was developed to gather respondents' perceptions of the importance of each of the 37 high-level IT processes from the COBIT framework. Given the findings from the previous research activities, the second research activity was designed to evaluate IT governance processes using the adapted case study in a medium and large organization in Morocco. This case study was selected for a number of reasons. (i) According to Hancock and Algozanine (2016), case research emphasizes studies in natural settings. It allows for a greater understanding of the context in which a phenomenon exists by collecting rich data to conclude. IT governance is a phenomenon that occurs within the context of the organization and is the unit of analysis. (ii) Case studies allow the exploration of the individual participant's viewpoint and various groupings of participants (Cronin, 2014). The use of multiple sources of data from the perspective of various stakeholders was required to ensure accurate evaluation of IT governance processes. (iii) Case study research is suitable for dynamic organizations evolving phenomenon. The examined company is considered a dynamic organization, with IT governance being an emergent and rapidly evolving phenomenon. (iv) Case studies can investigate and describe the processes and meaning of current events by collecting and integrating quantitative survey data, facilitating understanding of the phenomenon studied using applied methods (Lewis, 2015). This research could have utilized a number of data collection techniques based on the goal of the research activity, initial discussions with potential participants in the MENA Port organizations revealed that they opposed participating in interviews and would prefer to respond to anonymous questionnaires. As a result, the two research activities utilized questionnaires as a main data collection technique. In this research, it was applied to research activities 1 and 2 to analyze data obtained from the questionnaires. This mainly involved measures related to relative location, such as rankings, and those related to the center, such as means. Structural equation modeling is better known as a data analysis tool for testing and estimating causal relationships in quantitative research studies.
