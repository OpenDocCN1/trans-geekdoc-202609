# # 1.1.15 Flexibility

The significance of IT organizational infrastructure capacity as a critical component of the organizational's survival and competitiveness continues to grow. Sharing IT infrastructure is considered the basis for shared IT capabilities to develop IT applications and support business processes (Chung et al., 2003; Darke et al., 1998). IT infrastructures are generally the foundation of shared IT capabilities that enable the development of IT applications and support business processes (Lim, 2014). According to Zhu (2004), IT infrastructure is a set of resources and capabilities shared across the organization and form the foundation on which IT applications are developed and business processes are supported. IT infrastructure capabilities are usually used by IT/IS(service) organizational functions, and may also include public or outsourced facilities by organizations (Chin et al., 2003). The main reason for developing IT infrastructure capabilities is to support similarities among different uses, or, through facilitating information sharing across organizations for cross-functional integration (Broadbent & Weil, 1997).
Literature defines flexibility as the ability to relate to environmental change in information systems, organizational theory, strategic management, or operational management (Lee & Xia, 2005). However, many researchers have characterized IT flexibility as the organizational capacity to support various information technologies and services based on four dimensions: compatibility, connectivity, modularity, and IT staff flexibility.
Compatibility is the ability to share any information between any technological components. Connectivity is the ability of technology to interact with other technological components. In other words, connect each person, functional area, and application in organizations.
