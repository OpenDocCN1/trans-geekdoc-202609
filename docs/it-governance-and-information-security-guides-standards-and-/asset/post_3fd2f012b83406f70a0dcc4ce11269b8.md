# IT Service Management as a Key Pillar for IT Governance A Maturity Framework Based on ITIL v4

## 4.1 INTRODUCTION

In the age of digitization, the world is evolving at a constant pace. Organizations need to respond to changing conditions and agility is the only guarantee of survival. Globalization means that there is more competition (Kumar, 2008). The lifecycle of products is shorter than ever. disruptive technology can change markets overnight. The company faces challenges in maintaining security and compliance while achieving its business objectives, complying with current regulations, and managing staff and technology. We understand that the IT staff must react quickly to changing business while maintaining the existing infrastructure. We also know that the management objective so quoted, ‘Doing more with less’, is not only a goal but also a corporate commitment (Mesquita, 2012).

Most CIOs believe that IT management is simply about filling security gaps, collecting inventory, and automating application delivery, and they are not convinced that it is also necessary to define and better manage the assets, processes, and services that determine resource allocation, cost control, performance measures, and service delivery.

With improved Service Management (ITSM) processes and the adoption of best practice guides and benchmarks as ITIL (van Bon, 2007) and ISO/IEC 20000 (2010), compliance appears to be a need rather than a strategic choice to improve rapidly and easily decisions about IT and business processes. Get better agility, which allows the business to benefit from a faster Return On Investment (ROI) and a constant competitive advantage.

Recent developments in the field of IT governance and ITSM have led to the introduction of new technology and concepts such as service-oriented architecture (SOA), decision-making, and agility (Uebernick, 2006).

Over the last decade, agility has been proposed primarily around methods, such as Scrum (Ken Schwab, 2004) and Extreme Programming XP (Beck, 1999), for lifecycle management of software and IT developments. However, with the strategic changes that companies are currently experiencing, agility is a more strategic than a simple development method, affecting all business activities.

The management of IT services implies the adoption of an agile vision. Resilience adds a layer of flexibility and dynamism to this management (Sahid et al., 2021).

The IT department's responsibility for maintaining and securing the IT environment now includes all departments employees use, but budgets and IT resources are limited. Agility is the right solution for IT departments to streamline processes and manage all aspects of end-user productivity. The objective is to refine the traditional IT services of the 'control' type, oriented peripheral by a complete integrated approach, oriented user. ITSM increases communication efficiency between business departments and implement services. The needs of ITSM in organizations can change by how they do business, communicate, and also develop and innovate, gain a market advantage (Brooks, 2006). In addition, ITSM allows companies to internally govern and follow the global standards (Miao, 2008). To better understand the ITSM concept in the organization, reviewing the ITSM component would be helpful. ITSM components consist of Process, Technology, Management (people), Organization, and Security, which is recently added to agile construction to improve the security parity (Park, 2008), (Mcaughton, 2010).

At first glance, the Agile and IT principles seem to contradict each other because of the values they promote. However, these two share common values that should be put in synergy to get the most out of them. Version 4 of ITIL, published in February 2019 (Shekhor, 2020), will go in this by adopting ITSM best practices for agile operating methods.

In literature and industry, there is no practical, concrete, and agile method for implementing IT services and assets management in the literature and even references as ITIL, ISO 20000, and COBIT (Adelkbir et al., 2017). This chapter proposes a global, practical, and agile framework to support ITSM and IT Asset Management (The proposed framework surpasses the limitations of existing methods/referential and meets the needs of international standards in terms of flexibility and agility to improve and enhance ITSM/IT processes. We measure the proposed framework by adopting a continuous improvement process based on DevOps (DevOps is the concatenation of the first three letters of the word 'development' and the usual abbreviation 'ops' of the word 'operations') and the PDCA Deming cycle.
