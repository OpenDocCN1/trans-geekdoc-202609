The framework is structured around four poles or 'words'. The four words have been used in various engineering disciplines: IS engineering (Jeranke & Pohl, 1993), requirements engineering (Rolland, 1998), process engineering (Nurcan & Roland, 2003), and change engineering (Nehan & Deneckere, 2007). The framework has been used for IT governance engineering and component-based situational method engineering (Prieto-Diaz, 1991).

Facets supplement it according to the approach in 'Information Security Governance: Guidance for Boards of Directors and Executive Management Guidance for Boards of Directors and Executive Management' (2006). The aim is to allow a flexible and precise classification of software components is based on the enumeration of component descriptors, their association with a lexicon of terms (thesaurus), and a graph of facets. The initial framework of the four words has been adapted by facets that describe elements. Each facet can define a predefined value by a 'value domain':

+   * A simple value domain refers to a predefined primitive type. This is the case of integer or real value;
* An overall value domain (SET[b...]) refers to a structured type. For example, a vector with n dimensions is typically structured on n elements;
* A listed value domain (Enum[a, b...]) refers to a listed type. Thus, a mention for a diploma is from a listed field and can take its value among the values defined on Enum('Fairly Good', 'Good', 'Very Good').
