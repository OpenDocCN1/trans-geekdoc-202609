On the emergent side of the strategic paradigm, researchers have examined how problems are dealt with creating an IS security policy. It has been noted that 52% of all logistical and physical security breaches arise from the activities of personnel within the organization (Ahmad & Ruighfer, 2003). Research has sought to determine the most optimal control method to handle these breaches. IS security policy formally defines security requirements, outlines the main security objectives, and allocates resources. To maximize the probability of compliance, the enlightenment of staff to their responsibilities as outlined in the IS security policy is one potential solution.

Also, from the emergent perspective, there has been a call for the improvement of audit management to allow administrators to configure the software to reflect an organization's security needs as defined in the IS security policy. This demonstrates a dynamic approach to the policy in that it can be reactive to how an audit trail affects an information system. Changing the configuration from the status quo bottom-up approach to a policy-centric top-down approach would help the configuration closely match an organization's security goals (Coyne & Kluksdahl, 1994).

While not explicitly approaching the issue from an emergent perspective (Coye & Kluksdahl, 1994), failing a IS security policy implementation demonstrates an analysis from an emergent perspective. The implication remains in how elements could have adapted to how the actual was different from the rational plan. They found that compliance-based approaches are more prone to failure than risk-based approaches. De facto compliance-based policy led to the reaction of all security-related matters being answered (Walsham, 1993).

This research was conducted via an interpretive study in the Information Technology Department of a large university in the south-eastern portion of the United States. The interpretive tradition perceives that the knowledge of reality is a social construction by human actors (Howcroft, 2005). In contrast to the assumptions of objective science, this knowledge of reality applies equally to researchers. It leads to the perception that there is no objective reality researchers can discover. This perspective is also described whereby interpretive research provides in-depth insights into social, cultural and historical contexts wherein particular events and actions are described and interpreted as the authentic experiences of the people studied (Reich & Benbasat, 2000).

Approximately 45 employees worked for the department under study. Of these, 20 participated in the interviews. The subjects involved in the formulation and implementation of the IS security policy. They included the Chief Information Officer, Security Officer, and a group of operational level employees who were members of a Security Planning Team (SPT). SPT members included systems analysts, web developers, a database administrator, two school administrators, and three faculty members. The employees within the department who did not participate in the study included those who were not stakeholders in the IS policy formulation and implementation process.
