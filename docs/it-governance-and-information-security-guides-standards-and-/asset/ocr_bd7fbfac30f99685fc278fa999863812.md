Immediately after each of the interviews, the investigator debriefed. This immediate 'debriefing' process helped the researcher's interpretations and deepen his level of understanding (Walsham, 1993).

Besides gathering data, the interviews served as subject recruitment opportunities. The process of building the network of interviewees was done in a 'referral' manner. This means that the interviewees themselves will point the researcher to the next contacts to continue the interview process. The point of saturation became apparent when the same names began to appear. In this process, the totality of the stakeholders were involved in the IS security policy became clear (Walsham, 1993).

Once the process was complete, the researcher interpreted the data (Fomin, 2008). This involved systematic analysis and categorization of the data by emergent elements that the researcher identified. These themes were not known priori but as thematic principles categorized the data. These thematic principles, which included such topics as security awareness, deterrence, and resistance, emerged in part from existing themes in the security literature and the data gathered in the study.

Gikas (2010) is trying to find the reasons for the low adoption of ISO/IEC 27000 on information security management. The author compares ISO/IEC 27001 with the two widely applied management systems –ISO 9001 for quality management and ISO 14001 for environmental management – and shows that in addition to adoption rates, ISO/IEC 27001 has attracted less academic interest, as evidenced by the number of scholarly publications on the subject. The author compares the reasons for applying ISO/IEC 27001 with those of ISO 9001 and concludes by listing possible factors and obstacles for the dissemination of standards and suggesting a roadmap for future research on the subject.

Gillies (2011) discusses two types of legislation (HIPAA and FISMA) that focus on information security needs of US government agencies and private sector standards (PCI-DSS and ISO 27000) that address the information security needs of a broader range of institutional users of information technology (IT). It will provide a brief description of the four entities, a high-level comparison of standards andor prescribed guidelines to identify gaps and overlaps, and suggest a possible threshold model that could incorporate safety parameters that meet the requirements of the four.

ISO & Std (2005) is looking at global adoption of the ISO27000 standards and compared them with ISO09000 and ISO14000 adoption rates. They compare the barriers to the adoption of different standards. Adoption ISO27001 has been slower than ISO9001 and ISO14001 management systems, with about half of ISO14001 certifications. In response to the questions raised in this analysis, the paper examines how a maturity model approach can overcome these barriers, particularly in small businesses. The 2008 survey of ISO27001 certified companies that responded had found that 50% of the certified organizations that responded fewer than 200 employees and therefore fell into the SME category. The framework used the ISO code of process 27002 to define the elements that should be taken into account in ISMS. Each element is then developed through a maturity model life cycle to processes where an ISO27001 compliant ISMS can be implemented.
