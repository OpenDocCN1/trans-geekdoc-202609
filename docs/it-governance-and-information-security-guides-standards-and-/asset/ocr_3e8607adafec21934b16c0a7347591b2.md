# 5.17 THE PROPOSED CLOUD GOVERNANCE FRAMEWORK

A key success factor for governance is not simply managing cloud impacts on the business but also governing the cloud. By defining enterprise architecture (EA), you must have defined how it will be managed, using a governance framework in which accountability controls are established to ensure appropriate adoption and use of EA. # 5.18 POLICIES AND PRINCIPLES

Policies and principles are the company's rules and laws for cloud adoption. A principle is a belief statement that serves as the base for establishing decision-making policies. In contrast, a policy outlines the underlying rules that an organization must consider when making decisions about cloud adoption.

Usually, policies are defined at the strategy level. For example, they may relate to decisions about cloud vendor selection (e.g., contractual restrictions, which aren't allowed, processes for managing required services, and specification of what business applications and data can be moved in the cloud). Policies define management's direction to support the company's business purposes and needs, ensure the company's compliance with applicable legislation, reflect management's commitment, and clarify responsibilities. As such, you may have a data encryption or separation policy that must apply to all clouds, whether private, public, hybrid, or community.
