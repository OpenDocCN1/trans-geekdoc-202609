# 3.2 BACKGROUND AND LITERATURE REVIEW

IT governance is considered a complex system as it includes several critical aspects, namely, leadership, organization and decision rights, scalable processes, enabling technologies (Selig, 2008). Early conceptualizations of IT governance, often considered a subset of corporate governance (De Haes et al., 2013; Posthumus et al., 2010), recognized the role of IT governance in ensuring a valuable contribution from the organization's to its overall business strategy. More specifically, the role of IT governance is to ‘ensure’ that the organization's IT sustains and extends the organization's strategies and objectives (ITGI, 2003).

Several respected organizations and authors have attempted to define IT governance(Simonson & Johnson, 2006). Still, there is no universally definition of IT governance at the date of this chapter. IT governance can be defined as the process of controlling an organization's IT resources (Hunton et al., 2004). The International Standard for ICT Corporate Governance extends this definition to indicate that IT governance is the system by which the current and future use of ICT is directed and controlled. This involves assessing and guiding ICT use to support the organization and monitoring ICT to achieve the plans. It includes strategies and policies for ICT within an organization.

As a result, governance has become a standard component of most organizations’ governance, oversight, and control landscapes (Schubert, 2004). With most social phenomena, the increasing importance of IT governance has risen to several industry frameworks, tools, best practices, and maturity models, offering a prescriptive and deterministic approach to establishing effective governance. Nonetheless, the significant role of frameworks has been established as a practical approach to IT governance (Guldents, 2002; Webb et al., 2006) by providing guidance to organizations and offering an advantage over compliance with standards best practices and complying with regulatory rules (Brown et al., 2005a). For example, prominent meta-frameworks such as ISO 38500 and ITIL provide a comprehensive subset of best practices for standardizing, monitoring, and controlling IT activities (Wallhoff, 2004). However, guidance on IT governance can perhaps be found through the Information Systems Audit and Control Association (ISACA) and as its professional organization, the IT Governance Institute (ITGI) (Moeller, 2011).

BIT is a set of best practices developed by ITGI and is widely accepted as the main IT governance framework for establishing control over the IT environment, performing measurement of IT processes, and allowing executives to bridge the gap between control requirements, technical issues, and business risks (van Wyk & Rudman, 2019).
