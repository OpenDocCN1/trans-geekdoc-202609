# IT Governance and Information Security

IT governance consists of structures, processes, and operational mechanisms that work together to ensure IT investments and business objectives are aligned (De Haes & Van Gemenberg, 2005). The cornerstone of IT governance is to provide decision-makers an acceptable level of assurance that an organization's strategic objectives are not jeopardized by IT failures (Benarach & Chernobai, 2021). A conventional or, rather, invariable approach for attaining a level of assurance includes evaluating the IT governance system in place. The evaluation was born to assess the degree of conformance with standard practice by utilizing methodologies and frameworks (Vlietland et al., 2016). In this particular means that by engaging in IT governance, organizations can periodically measure IT governance performance using well-known worldwide frameworks or methods such as Control Objectives for Information and Related Technology (COBIT), IT Framework Library ITIL, or the International Standards Organization's ISO 38500, to name few.

A range of literature examines IT governance structures and mechanisms (De Haes & Van Gemenberg, 2005; Guldentos et al., 2002; McKay et al., 2003; Peterson et al., 2002; Wim Van Gemberg & de Haes, 2009) explores factors influencing adoption and implementation of IT governance systems (Aasi et al., 2014; Reich & Benbasat, 2000), and the use of codified frameworks and their impact on IT governance (El-Mawy et al., 2015; Guldentos, 2002; Wim Van Gemberg & de Haes, 2009; Weber). The literature also indicates that, while there is the widespread use of governance frameworks, more research is needed to investigate how these frameworks could be modified to fit a specific circumstance or context (Maleh et al., 2018). Aspects involving behavior in IT governance, although they have been long acknowledged (Grunwal & Sahama, 2016; Herath & Rao, 2009), have received less attention from academics (Smits & Hillebersberg, 2015).

Several IT frameworks can guide the implementation of an IT governance program. Although frameworks and guidelines such as CoBIT, ITIL, VaIT, and ISO 38500 (Prieto-Diaz, 1991) have been widely adopted, there is no absolute standard IT governance framework; the combination that works best for an organization depends on business factors, corporate culture, maturity, and staffing capacity. The level of implementation of these frameworks will also vary by organization (Maleh et al., 2019).

This chapter presents the state of the research on the implementation of information technology (IT) governance through an analysis of the various proposed standards. This analysis was conducted on the basis of a meta-model of four frameworks. This chapter is organized as follows. The first part describes the proposed reference framework for IT governance. Five recognized approaches are then evaluated under this framework. Finally, this chapter highlights the gaps in current approaches, our positioning concerning the literature, and its contribution to IT governance.
