Recent developments in the field of IT governance and ITSM have led to the introduction of new technology and concepts such as service-oriented architecture (SOA), decision-making, and course agility (Uebernick, 2006).

Over the last decade, agility has been proposed primarily around methods, such as Scrum (Ken Schwab, 2004) and Extreme Programming XP (Beck, 1999), for lifecycle management of software and IT developments. However, with the strategic changes that companies are currently experiencing, agility is a more strategic than a simple development method, affecting all business activities.

The management of IT services implies the adoption of an agile vision. Resilience adds a layer of flexibility and dynamism to this management (Sahid et al., 2021).

The IT department's responsibility for maintaining and securing the IT environment now includes all departments employees use, but budgets and IT resources are limited. Agility is the right solution for IT departments to streamline processes and manage all aspects of end-user productivity. The objective is to refine the traditional IT services of the 'control' and control' type, oriented peripheral by a complete integrated approach, oriented user. ITSM increases communication efficiency between business departments and implement services. The needs of ITSM in organizations can change by how they do business, communicate, and also develop and innovate, gain a market advantage (Brooks, 2006). In addition, ITSM allows companies to internally govern and follow the global standards (Mior, 2008). To better understand the ITSM concept in the organization, reviewing the ITSM component would be helpful. ITSM components consist of Process, Technology, Management (people), Organization, and Security, which is recently added to agile construction to improve the security parity (Park, 2008), (Mcaughton, 2010).
