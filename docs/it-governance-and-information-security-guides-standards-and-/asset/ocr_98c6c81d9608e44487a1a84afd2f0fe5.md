# 8.2.1.2 ISO/IEC 27002:2005 (Revised by ISO/IEC 27002:2013)

ISO/IEC 27002:2005 is another generic standard applied to health information systems to ensure security. It establishes general principles and guidelines for effective initialization, implementation, maintenance, and improvement of information security management. The objectives therein provide general guidance on the commonly accepted goals of information security management. Thus, any organization seeking to adopt a comprehensive information security management program or improve its existing information security practices can use the standard. The ISO standard asserts that information can be protected using a wide variety of controls. Controls include hardware and software functions, processes, policies, and organizational structures. Organizations including healthcare organizations must develop, implement, monitor, evaluate, and improve these security practices (PRGL, 2011).

# 8.2.1.3 ISO/IEC 27002:2005

ISO/IEC 27002 2005 is entitled Information technology–Security techniques–Code of practice for information security management. It is published by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC).
ISO/IEC 27002:2005 was developed from BS7799, a British standard that was published in the 1990s. ISO/IEC adopted this as ISO/IEC 17799:2000 in December 2000. In June 2005, the standard was revised and officially published as ISO/IEC 17799:2005. On July 1, 2007, it was renamed ISO/IEC 27002:2005 to align with the other ISO/IEC 27000-series standards (ISO/IEC, 2013). On the other hand, ISO/IEC 27002:2005 has been revised by ISO/IEC 27002:2013 (ISECT, 2012).

In this research, ISO 27002 serves as the framework for measuring information security in organizations. It is well suited for this work, as the idea behind ISO 27002 is to have a list of controls that should mitigate all possible information security risks.
