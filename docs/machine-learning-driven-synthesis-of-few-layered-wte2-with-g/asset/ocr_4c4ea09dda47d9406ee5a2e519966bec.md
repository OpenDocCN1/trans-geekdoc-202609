# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

# Optimization of CVD Synthesis Parameters by ML.

The space-confined CVD method has been adopted to synthesize WTe2 NRs, and the corresponding setup is illustrated in Figure 2A. Empirically, five CVD parameters were identified as significant input features: source ratio (RTe/W), the molar ratio of tellurium power and tungsten power), reaction temperature (T, in °C), ramp time (tR, in min), deposition time (tD, in min), and H2 gas flow rate (fH2, sccm). A total of 255 experiments were carried out in the lab with different combinations of growth parameters. The detailed feature overview is shown in Table S1. Among these experiments, WTe2 were successfully synthesized in 141 experiments, whereas the remaining 114 experiments showed the negative result (no WTe2 can be observed), as identified by the optical microscopy and Raman spectroscopy. A binary classification model was thus developed by defining “Can grow” as a positive class and “Cannot grow” as a negative class. XGBoost is an optimized gradient boosting algorithm that can fit complicated relationships through the ensemble of decision trees. In addition, it regularizes the complexity of the model to avoid overfitting and thus makes it suitable for small data sets. In this study, XGBoost together with 10-fold nested cross-validation has been applied, as reported in our previous work23,27 (see more details in Experimental Methods and

Supporting Information Notes 1 and 2 and Figures S1–S3). The excellent performance of the as-selected model has been demonstrated through plotting the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, as shown in Figure 2B. The area under the ROC (AUROC) is commonly used to evaluate the ability of the model to successfully differentiate between two classes given different decision thresholds. AUROC of 1.0 represents perfect differentiation, whereas an area of 0.5 (indicated by the dashed line) is equivalent to a random guess. As displayed, our trained model possesses a high AUROC of 0.93, indicating great discriminating capability between “Can grow” and “Cannot grow” classes of the trained model.

To gain further insight into the system of CVD-grown WTe2, feature importance has been extracted from the trained XGBoost model, with the result shown in Figure 2C (see details in Experimental Methods). As indicated, the H2 gas flow rate (fH2) is the most important feature affecting the synthesis of WTe2, followed by the reaction temperature (T) and ramp time (tR), whereas the source ratio (RTe/W) is the least significant one. Due to the difficulty of achieving a controllable synthesis of the intriguing few-layered WTe2, optimization of the synthesis condition has been carried out.27 Based on the input ranges of each feature (Table S2), respective “Can grow” probabilities (P) of the resulted 720 720 combinations have been predicted by the trained ML model. The correlation of the output with each feature has been individually examined, as presented in Figure 2D. It can be clearly observed that the values of H2 gas flow rate, reaction temperature, and ramp time have the dominant effect on the distribution of P. This is consistent with the feature importance result discussed above (Figure 2C). Among all the 720 720 synthesis conditions, 8 combinations with the highest P have been chosen for experimental verification. Table S3 summarizes the corresponding experimental results, which are all positive. This suggests that the controllable synthesis of few-layered WTe2 can be realized with our trained ML model.
