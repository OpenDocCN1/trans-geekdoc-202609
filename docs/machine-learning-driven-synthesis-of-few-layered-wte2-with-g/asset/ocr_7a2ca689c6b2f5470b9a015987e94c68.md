expense of smaller ones due to the energy difference as per Ostwald ripening.⁴⁷,⁴⁸

According to the binary Te–W phase diagram, as the $R_{\mathrm{Te/W}}$ increases, more W can be dissolved in the liquid Te.⁴⁹,⁵⁰ As a result, more migrated W atoms would be in the liquid Te in the high $R_{\mathrm{Te/W}}$ system. Based on the DFT calculation, the WTe₂ NRs with increased length and length–width ratio can be obtained. It is worth mentioning that the growth mechanism we proposed was established through a large number of experiments. Although it is discussed under the specific reaction condition, we believe it can be extended to the entire Te/W/NaCl reaction system. Additionally, this growth mechanism could also be used to understand many other 2D to 1D TMC synthesis processes.

# ■ CONCLUSIONS

In summary, ML has been successfully applied to guide the CVD synthesis of quasi-1D few-layered 1T′ WTe₂ NRs. Relevant characterizations have demonstrated the high quality of as-grown samples. A high AUROC of 0.93 indicates the excellent performance of the well-trained XGBoost model. Feature importance extracted from the model suggests that H₂ gas flow rate is the most important feature governing the synthesis of 1T′ WTe₂ NRs, while $R_{\mathrm{Te/W}}$ dominates the sample morphology. Optimization of synthesis parameters has been performed to achieve both high “Can grow” probability and large length–width ratio. Our experimental verifications further support the strong correlation between the $R_{\mathrm{Te/W}}$ and the geometric structure of WTe₂ NRs. Additionally, a growth mechanism of WTe₂ NRs has been proposed, where quasi-1D WTe₂ was found to be evolved from the 2D WTe₂ with the aid of H₂, providing new insight into the study of the intriguing tellurides family. Our work, integrating ML analysis and experimental results, will shed light on the accelerated development of 1D materials and boost the diversified nanostructures derived from 2D TMC family.

# ■ EXPERIMENTAL METHODS

**Synthesis of Few-Layered WTe₂ NRs.** A total of 2 mg of powders (Te:W:NaCl = 16:1:0.1) were mixed by a gate mortar and then placed on the 10 mm × 30 mm Si/SiO₂ substrate (with a 285 nm oxidation layer). Another 10 mm × 30 mm Si/SiO₂ substrate was put on top of the powders with the polished surface down, forming a sandwiched structure. They were then transferred into a closed (inner quartz tube (with a diameter of 12 mm and length of 300 mm). The (inner) quartz tube was placed in the quartz tube (with a diameter of 2 in.) and then positioned at the center of the furnace. The quartz tube was cleaned with Ar gas (99.99%) for 20 min before heating. After that, the mixed gas of Ar/H₂ (100/40 sccm, respectively) was used as the carrier gas. The furnace was heated to 750 °C in 20 min and then kept for 7 min. Then, the furnace cooled down to room temperature naturally, and the WTe₂ NRs can be obtained on the substrate.

**Characterization.** The microstructures and morphology of as-grown WTe₂ were characterized by optical microscopy (Olympus BX51), Raman (WITEC Alpha 200R), XPS (Kratos Analytical, Manchester), AFM (Asylum Research Cypher S), and STEM (JEOL ARM-200F (S)). The STEM characterization was carried out with CEOS CESKOR aberration corrector, operated at an accelerating voltage of 80 kV. The EDX mapping results were carried out with the TEM. The Raman characterization was carried out with a 532 nm laser (the laser power used was less than 1 mW, and the system was calibrated with the Raman peak of Si at 520 cm⁻¹). The XPS analysis was performed by a Kratos Axis Supra instrument, and the binding

energy correction for charging was performed by assigning a value of 284.8 eV to the adventitious C1 s line.

# **Feature Importance.** *Shapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP)*

computes theoretically sound and unique feature importance values, belonging to the class of additive feature attribution.⁵¹ The explanation model $g(z′)$ satisfies $g(z′)=\phi_0+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\phi_i z_i′$, where $z′\in\{0,1\}^{M}$, $M$ is the number of input features, $\phi_i$ denotes the feature importance value, and $\phi_i\in\mathbb{R}$. $z_i′=1$ for a feature that is being observed, otherwise 0.

To compute SHAP values, $f_x(S)=E[f(x)|x_S]$ is defined where $f$ is the ML model to be explained, $S$ is the set of nonzero indexes in $v′$, and $E[f(x)|x_S]$ is the expected value of the function conditioned on the input features of subset $S$. The SHAP value is computed for each feature using these conditional expectations:

$$\phi_i=\sum_{S\subseteq N\setminus\{i\}}\frac{|S|!(M-|S|-1)!}{M!}\left[f_x(S\cup\{i\})-f_x(S)\right] \tag{3}$$

where $N$ is the set of all input features.

**XGBoost.** XGBoost, derived from Gradient Boosting Decision Tree (GBDT),⁵²,⁵³ makes a decision with an ensemble of $M$ decision trees as base estimators $f_m$, $m=1,\ldots,M$:

$$\hat{y}_i=\sum_{m=1}^{M}f_m(x_i) \tag{4}$$

Given $N$ training data $\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$, the objective is to minimize

$$\operatorname{obj}(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}l(y_i,\hat{y}_i)+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\Omega(f_m) \tag{5}$$

where $\sum_{i=1}^{N}l(y_i,\hat{y}_i)$ denotes the training loss and $\sum_{m=1}^{M}\Omega(f_m)$ penalizes complexity of the base estimators. In addition, it adopts an additive training strategy that adds one new tree at a time, that optimizes the objective at step $t$:

$$\operatorname{obj}(\theta)^t=\sum_{i=1}^{N}l(y_i,\hat{y}_i)+\sum_{m=1}^{t}\Omega(f_m) \tag{6}$$

whereas

$$\hat{y}_i^t=\sum_{m=1}^{t}f_m(x_i)=\hat{y}_i^{t-1}+f_t(x_i) \tag{7}$$
