## ABSTRACT:

Reducing the lateral scale of two-dimensional (2D) materials to one-dimensional (1D) has attracted substantial research interest not only to achieve competitive electronic applications but also for the exploration of fundamental physical properties. Controllable synthesis of high-quality 1D nanoribbons (NRs) is thus highly desirable and essential for further study. Here, we report the implementation of supervised machine learning (ML) for the chemical vapor deposition (CVD) synthesis of high-quality quasi-1D few-layered WTe₂ NRs. Feature importance analysis indicates that H₂ gas flow rate has a profound influence on the formation of WTe₂, and the source ratio governs the sample morphology. Notably, the growth mechanism of 1T′ few-layered WTe₂ NRs is further proposed, which provides new insights for the growth of intriguing 2D and 1D tellurides and may inspire the growth strategies for other 1D nanostructures. Our findings suggest the effectiveness and capability of ML in guiding the synthesis of 1D nanostructures, opening up new opportunities for intelligent materials development.

## INTRODUCTION

In the past few decades, two-dimensional (2D) transition metal chalcogenides (TMCs) have demonstrated excellent performance in nanoelectronic and spintronic applications and showed great potential for investigating quantum physics and fabricating quantum devices.¹⁻⁴ Recently, one-dimensional (1D) nanostructures derived from 2D TMCs have received increasing attention, attributed to the intriguing properties that arise from the reduced dimensionality and enhanced quantum confinement effect.⁵⁻⁹ Among TMCs, tungsten ditelluride (WTe₂) with rich structural variations and strong spin−orbit coupling is predicted to be type-II Weyl semimetals and 2D topological insulators, providing an ideal platform for the investigation of dimensionality-related properties, such as superconductivity, quantum spin Hall Effect, and Majorana Fermion.³,⁴,¹⁰⁻¹⁶ Even though chemical vapor deposition (CVD) has been widely adopted to grow high-quality 2D materials, controllable synthesis of few-layered WTe₂ remains challenging, not to mention the dimensionality control. This is due to the lower reactivity of Te and small electronegativity difference between Te and W, as well as the poor stability of telluride.¹⁷⁻²¹ Effective identification of optimal synthesis conditions for WTe₂ with dimensional tunability is therefore urgently required. Machine learning (ML) has recently demonstrated great capability in materials study.²²⁻²⁸ It has been successfully applied to accelerate the design and discovery of novel materials, including catalytic materials,²⁴ inorganic−organic hybrid materials,²⁸,²⁹ thermoelectric materials,³⁰ superhard materials,³¹ low-dimensional materials,²⁶,²⁷,³² phase-change memory material,²⁵ organic photovoltaic material,³³ etc., through effectively learning from the past and even failed data.³⁴⁻³⁸ For instance, Zhong et al.²⁴ reported the discovery of Cu−Al electrocatalysts with the combined density functional theory calculations and active machine learning, which can efficiently reduce CO₂ to ethylene with the highest Faradaic efficiency reported so far. On top of the materials discovery, the successful synthesis of novel materials is critical toward its future application, whereas substantial effort is generally required with traditional laboratory exploration.²³,²⁷,³⁹ Therefore, the feasibility and potential of introducing ML into guided synthesis and even structure engineering of advanced materials to expedite the exploration process and reduce the cost are worth exploring.

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

### Optimization of CVD Synthesis Parameters by ML.

The space-confined CVD method has been adopted to synthesize WTe2 NRs, and the corresponding setup is illustrated in Figure 2A. Empirically, five CVD parameters were identified as significant input features: source ratio (RTe/W), the molar ratio of tellurium powder and tungsten powder), reaction temperature (T, in °C), ramp time (tR, in min), deposition time (tD, in min), and H2 gas flow rate (fH2, sccm). A total of 255 experiments were carried out in the lab with different combinations of growth parameters. The detailed feature overview is shown in Table S1. Among these experiments, WTe2 were successfully synthesized in 141 experiments, whereas the remaining 114 experiments showed the negative result (no WTe2 can be observed), as identified by the optical microscopy and Raman spectroscopy. A binary classification model was thus developed by defining “Can grow” as a positive class and “Cannot grow” as a negative class. XGBoost is an optimized gradient boosting algorithm that can fit complicated relationships through the ensemble of decision trees. In addition, it regularizes the complexity of the model to avoid overfitting and thus makes it suitable for small data sets. In this study, XGBoost together with 10-fold nested cross-validation has been applied, as reported in our previous work23,27 (see more details in Experimental Methods and

Supporting Information Notes 1 and 2 and Figures S1–S3). The excellent performance of the as-selected model has been demonstrated through plotting the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, as shown in Figure 2B. The area under the ROC (AUROC) is commonly used to evaluate the ability of the model to successfully differentiate between two classes given different decision thresholds. AUROC of 1.0 represents perfect differentiation, whereas an area of 0.5 (indicated by the dashed line) is equivalent to a random guess. As displayed, our trained model possesses a high AUROC of 0.93, indicating great discriminating capability between “Can grow” and “Cannot grow” classes of the trained model.

To gain further insight into the system of CVD-grown WTe2, feature importance has been extracted from the trained XGBoost model, with the result shown in Figure 2C (see details in Experimental Methods). As indicated, the H2 gas flow rate (fH2) is the most important feature affecting the synthesis of WTe2, followed by the reaction temperature (T) and ramp time (tR), whereas the source ratio (RTe/W) is the least significant one. Due to the difficulty of achieving a controllable synthesis of the intriguing few-layered WTe2, optimization of the synthesis condition has been carried out.27 Based on the input ranges of each feature (Table S2), respective “Can grow” probabilities (P) of the resulted 720,720 combinations have been predicted by the trained ML model. The correlation of the output with each feature has been individually examined, as presented in Figure 2D. It can be clearly observed that the values of H2 gas flow rate, reaction temperature, and ramp time have the dominant effect on the distribution of P. This is consistent with the feature importance result discussed above (Figure 2C). Among all the 720,720 synthesis conditions, 8 combinations with the highest P have been chosen for experimental verification. Table S3 summarizes the corresponding experimental results, which are all positive. This suggests that the controllable synthesis of few-layered WTe2 can be realized with our trained ML model.
