## Characterization of Few-Layered 1T′ WTe₂ NRs.

The structure and quality of the synthesized few-layered WTe₂ NRs have been carefully examined, with results shown in Figure 4. The top and side views of the atomic structure of 1T′ WTe₂ NRs are presented in Figure 4A. Figure 4B shows the typical optical image of the few-layered WTe₂ NRs with a length of ~50 μm. The width and thickness were further determined by the atomic force microscopy (AFM) (Figure 4C). The width of the WTe₂ NRs is ~0.8 μm, and the thickness is ~2.5 nm, indicating its triple-layer nature. The reproducible synthesis results (Figure S6) and corresponding statistical analysis (Figure S7) suggest the high reproducibility of the space-confined CVD method, attributed to its stable fluid field velocity distribution (Supporting Information Note 3, Figure S8, and Table S5).

Raman spectroscopy was carried out to investigate the quality of as-synthesized WTe₂ NRs, with the Raman spectrum displayed in Figure 4D. Six vibrational modes can be clearly observed, B₁¹⁰, A₂⁴, A₂³, A₁⁴, A₁⁷, and A₁⁹ at 87.3, 108.6, 113.7, 132.4, 161.1, and 210.6 cm⁻¹, respectively, which are in good agreement with previously reported values, suggesting the successful preparation of high-quality WTe₂ NRs.¹⁰,¹⁷ To further demonstrate the quality and uniformity of WTe₂ NRs, Raman intensity mapping (A₁⁹) has been performed, as shown in Figure 4E. The Raman mapping region is highlighted by the red dashed border in the optical image with a size of 6 μm × 60 μm. The uniform color distribution of the mapping region indicates the high homogeneity and low defect of the synthesized 1T′ WTe₂ NRs.

We have also conducted X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) to examine the chemical composition of as-synthesized NRs (Figure S9). The peaks of Te, W, Si, O, and C can be clearly identified in the survey spectrum with no extra peaks. The high-resolution XPS spectra of Te 3d and W 4d are shown in Figure 4F. The Te 3d spectrum can be well fitted by two peaks located at 583.7 (Te 3d₅/₂) and 573.3 eV (Te 3d₃/₂), respectively, which are associated with W–Te bonds.¹⁷,¹⁹ And the prominent W 4d peaks can be well fitted by two peaks located at 256.9 (W 4d₅/₂) and 243.6 eV (W 4d₃/₂), respectively.¹⁷ The results agree well with previous reports, suggesting that WTe₂ NRs are not oxidized.⁴¹,⁴² The chemical composition was calculated to be WTe₂.₀₈, indicating our space-confined-CVD-grown WTe₂ NRs are considerably stoichiometric.

The atomic structure of as-synthesized WTe₂ NRs was further revealed by scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM). The typical bright-field TEM image of WTe₂ NRs is shown in Figure 4G. According to the STEM image in Figure 4H, 1T′ WTe₂ consisting of 1D zigzag W–Te chains along the unit cell axis belongs to space group P2₁/m (Pmn2₁). To demonstrate the single crystallinity of our CVD-grown WTe₂, four different positions from the same WTe₂ NRs have been measured by using STEM (Figure S10). The results reveal that all of the WTe₂ NRs possess the same crystal orientation, thereby indicating the single-crystalline nature. EDX mappings in Figure 4I further verify the uniform distribution of elements. All the results together indicate that high-quality few-layered WTe₂ NRs have been successfully synthesized by our space-confined CVD method. The prepared WTe₂ NRs also demonstrate better stability than those obtained by previously reported methods (Figure S11).¹⁷ It is worth mentioning that the space-confined CVD method has been extended to prepare other telluride NRs. Both MoTe₂ NRs (Figure S12) and MoₓW₁₋ₓTe₂ NRs (Figure S13) have been grown using similar procedures.

## Growth Mechanism Governing the Geometrical Transformation from 2D to 1D.

Understanding the growth mechanism of tellurides is key to the controllable synthesis of high-quality tellurides and may inspire the growth strategies for other 1D nanostructures. Considering the high melting point and low reactivity of W, the mixed powder of NaCl/Te/W is adopted as the precursor and placed into the confined space (inner tube) for the growth of few-layered WTe₂ NRs under H₂ atmosphere. Notably, the confined space, salt, H₂, and mixture of Te/W/NaCl are indispensable for the synthesis of WTe₂ NRs, as demonstrated in Supporting Information Note 4, Figures S8 and S14, and Table S6. Conventionally, the deposition time of only a few minutes is required for the CVD synthesis of monolayer or few-layered TMCs as the synthesis process is quite fast.¹⁰,¹⁷–¹⁹ In order to clearly observe the morphological evolution, the mild synthesis conditions with high P from the ML results but moderate temperature and H₂ gas flow rate (see Figure 2E and Table S4) have been chosen to slow down the reaction rate and investigate the growth mechanism of WTe₂ NRs. The detailed parameters are as follows: H₂ gas flow rate of 30 sccm, reaction temperature of 650 °C, ramp time of 18 min, source ratio of 16, and reaction time of 5 min. Here, the reaction time of H₂ is the only variable studied. Three controlled experiments have been carried out:
- H₂ gas was introduced into the system only when the temperature reaches 650 °C and turned off after 5 min.
- H₂ gas was introduced at room temperature and turned off after a 5 min deposition at 650 °C.
- H₂ gas was kept throughout the whole process.

The corresponding optical images of as-synthesized few-layered WTe₂ are shown in Figures S15 and S16. The morphological evolution from 2D to 1D can be clearly observed. Based on the experimental results, the growth model of WTe₂ NRs has been further proposed (Figure 5A–C). The synthesis of WTe₂ NRs could be divided into three stages: the formation of 2D WTe₂, H₂ etching of 2D WTe₂, and the formation of WTe₂ NRs, as well as the ripening regrowth of WTe₂ NRs.

The following reaction mechanism is proposed to explain the formation of WTe₂ with the Te/W/NaCl mixed powder under the Ar/H₂ atmospheric pressure (Figure 5A).⁴³,⁴⁴ Te(l) + H₂(g) ↔ H₂Te(g) (1) W(s) + H₂Te(g) ↔ WTe₂(s) + H₂(g) (2)

The Gibbs free energy (G) of the proposed conversion reaction has been calculated to investigate the role played by H₂Te in reducing the barrier height (Figure 5E, Figure S17, Table S7, and Supporting Information Note 5). As calculated, with the aid of H₂, the energy required to synthesize WTe₂ from W and the intermediate product H₂Te is ~135 kJ/mol less than the direct reaction between W and Te. Moreover, considering the negative ΔG value, the formation of WTe₂ by W and H₂Te is thermodynamically more favorable. Similar findings were also found for the synthesis of MoTe₂ NRs with and without H₂ (Figure S18). In this regard, H₂Te serves as an efficient Te carrier as well as a catalyst. Under the Te-rich condition, the H₂ obtained in Reaction 2 could then react to form H₂Te (Reaction 1), ensuring the continuous tellurization process. Liquid W in the W–Te system then reacts with H₂Te to form the layered WTe₂. Both reactions are reversible, and the direction depends on the concentration of the source and the reaction temperature. Therefore, the synthesis and decomposition of WTe₂ can occur simultaneously.

In the first stage, the growth process is much faster than the decomposition of WTe₂ owing to the sufficient source supply, and WTe₂ can be quickly synthesized. As the chemical reaction proceeds, the growth rate drops due to the reduced source concentration, and the H₂ etching of layered WTe₂ becomes significant. Previous works have also reported that excessive H₂ can decompose TMCs at high temperature.⁴⁵,⁴⁶ The etching will prefer the [100] direction of 1T′ WTe₂ structure due to the different bond lengths of W–Te (Figure 5D), which is conducive to the formation of NR structures (Figure 5B). The surface energies of the (100) and (010) facets of 1T′ WTe₂ are calculated to be 0.0315 and 0.0158 eV/Å², respectively, according to density functional theory (DFT) calculations. The higher energy of the (100) facet than that of (010) indicates that the [100] direction is the preferred growth direction for 1T′ WTe₂ NRs (Figure 5F and Supporting Information Note 6). For the 2D layered WTe₂, etching always occurs at the two ends of 1T′ WTe₂ (Figure S19), and thus the prototype of NR structures can be established. In the third stage, H₂ etching and ripening occur concurrently (Figure 5C). The wide WTe₂ can be etched into W and H₂Te, and W then migrates into the liquid Te.
