# # Determining What to Measure

Recall in our dance problem that measuring distance was easy. You could just pull out the tape measure! (What, you don't have one at the ready at all times?)

But things get more complicated in this example. For instance, we know there were 32 deals offered in the last year. And, we have a list on the Transactions tab of the 324 purchases, broken out by customer. In total, there are 100 customers in our dataset. To measure the distance between each customer and a cluster center, we need to understand where they sit in a 32-dimension space! That's because we need to understand the deals they did not take.

This may sound like a lot of data, but it's not too hard in Excel. We'll need to create a matrix of deals by customers, where each customer gets their own 32-deal column. If they took the deal, they'll get a 1 for the deal; otherwise, they'll get a zero. Thankfully, this is easy to construct with a PivotTable.
