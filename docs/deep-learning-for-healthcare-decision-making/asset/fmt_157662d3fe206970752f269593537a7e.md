
# Related Work

The authors in [1](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_1) had designed a smart healthcare system with the integration of `blockchain 3.0` and `healthcare 4.0`. The designed network has been implemented in `Ethereum` network along with other programming languages and tools like `Solidity` `Web3.js`. Further, to handle the challenge of trust building, `smart contracts` have been used.

The work [2](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_2) presents a blockchain-based secure, efficient, and easily maintainable framework for medical data access between patient and doctor. The proposed framework allows the patient to have full control of its data.

The work [13](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_13) also develops a framework for secure, robust, stable, and patient-centric health information exchange. `Distributed ledgers` and `smart contracts` are used for the purpose.

The work [43](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_43) develops a patient-centric application named `PACEX` that allows the sharing of `EHR` data. `Ethereum blockchain` and `smart contracts` have been used.

The authors in [3](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_3) extended work of [9](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_9) and designed a hybrid architecture using blockchain and edge node for `EHR` data management. This architecture authenticates the user signature by using `attribute-based multi-signature (ABMS)` scheme and encrypts `EHR` data by using `multi-authority attribute-based encryption (ABE)` scheme. `Hyperledger Fabric` platform and `Hyperledger Ursa` library have been used for implementation of the scheme respectively.

The authors in [4](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_4) first proposed a cross blockchain based `EHR` storage solution to handle the interoperability issue of blockchain in healthcare. A `relay-chain as a service (Raas)` has been used for this purpose and it allows the deletion of the `EHR` information by the patient when it enters different hospitals.

The work [5](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_5) develops a blockchain-based platform for enhanced `EHR` in the area of diabetes as it is the hastily growing disease in today’s world. This platform generates an alert message to patients and caretakers in case of emergency.

The work [6](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_6) proposes a blockchain- and hash-based new strategy for enhancing the security in distributed `EHR`. This strategy uses `collision-resistant hash function` for fast generation of unique patient IDs, `PoW` for reducing scalability, `smart contracts`, and cryptography techniques for consent management.

The works [7](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_7) and [40](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_40) address the scalability of blockchain in `EHR`. For increasing the scalability, the proposed architecture uses `multi-hyperledgers`: one for patients and another for health institutes. To store a large amount of data and fulfill more requests, each channel maintains its own ledger.

The work [8](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_8) integrates `AI` with blockchain for more secure `EHR`, efficient data management, and reliable diagnosis of disease. A `constraint goal model (CGM)` is used to stimulate the system requirement.

The work [50](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_50) addresses the scalability of `NHIA’s NMR` used in Taiwan. The developed framework can integrate the `NHIA’s NMR` data with `EMR` and `EHR`.

The work [10](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_10) develops a consent model for data sharing and access control. `Smart contracts` are used for representing consent. This model uses `data use ontology (DUO)` and `automatable discovery and access matrix (ADA-M)` for modeling consent of users and description of queries from requesters, respectively. The `Ethereum` platform is used for data validation.

The papers [11](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_11) and [55](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_55) propose a scheme for secure multi-authority `EHR` in cloud storage environments. This scheme uses an `attribute signcryption scheme` for achieving security. Moreover, the use of `verifiable outsourcing computational mechanisms` reduces the computational burden of the user.

In addition to this, [22](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_22) develops an efficient authentication scheme for resolution of centralized problems of the cloud.

The work [12](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_12) uses `fuzzy decision models` for evaluation of blockchain models for secure and trustworthy `EHR`. This evaluation helps in the selection of the most suitable blockchain for trustworthy `EHR`.

The work [14](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_14) uses `zk-proofs` to authenticate the beneficiaries.

However, [29](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_29) uses `keyless signature infrastructure` for securing `EHR` data over the cloud. The proposed framework is evaluated and compared with existing frameworks on different parameters.

The work [42](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_42) uses a `revocable attribute scheme` to protect privacy. `Attribute signing key` is calculated with the combination of `attribute master key` and `update master key`.

The paper [16](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_16) proposes a `multi-tier architecture` for privacy preservation. This novel architecture uses `public blockchain` between different `EHR` cloud providers and another between patient sensors and patient systems.

The papers [28](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_28) and [33](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_33) use `cryptographic functions` for maintaining the pseudonymity of the patient data.

The paper [49](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_49) uses various `utility functions` to evaluate privacy preserving frameworks. `Multiple smart contracts` are used for evaluation of access control and transaction evaluation.

The paper [35](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_35) uses blockchain for securing decentralized `EHR` sharing.

The work [18](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_18) proposes a hybrid architecture that combines blockchain and edge nodes to facilitate `EHR` access control. This architecture maintains a `tamper-proof log` of the access events. `Hyperledger Fabric` is used for the evaluation of the hybrid architecture.

In addition to this, [19](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_19) and [44](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_44) also propose a novel `EHR` framework for trustworthy access control mechanisms between various users of `EHR`. `Ethereum` is used for prototype implementation.

Furthermore, [20](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_20) focuses on patient controlled sharing schemes instead of third parties. Some medical institutes are used for storing the `PES chain`. These are used for storing the hash values of the encrypted patient record. `Stealth authorization scheme` is constructed for privacy preservation and sharing of data.

The work [21](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_21) addresses the problem of privacy preservation and security over cloud sharing of `EHR` records. `Searchable encryption` and `conditional proxy re-encryption` based scheme is developed for the resolution of problems. `Proof of authorization` is used as a consensus protocol.

The paper [24](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_24) presents a novel audit mechanism for exchanging e-health data across the borders in Europe, while [47](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_47) proposes an architecture for `EHR` data in Peruvian health organizations. For off-chain storage of medical data and providing logs of transactions, laws maintained by Peruvian authorities are followed.

The paper [48](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_48) proposes a framework for sharing and management of cancer patients. The data validation is done on the data of `Stony Brook University Hospital`.

The paper [25](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_25) presents a blockchain-based solution for secure `EHR` inter-exchange. Security analysis is done in terms of inside and outside threats on the system.

The work [41](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_41) proposes a `permissioned blockchain` for cross-organizational data sharing.

The paper [26](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_26) proposes a novel and secure scheme named `MedSBA`, an `attribute-based encryption` scheme for sharing of `EHR`. `Private blockchain` is utilized.

The work [27](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_27) develops real, operational, and secured `PHRs`. Three design principles were developed for secure `PHR`.

The work [36](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_36) proposes a `certificate-less scheme` and `elliptical curve cryptography` scheme for the security of data.

The paper [30](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_30) integrates `Hyperledger blockchain` with `deep learning` for secure data management and diagnosis model, respectively. The developed model works in five different stages. `SIMON block cipher` technique is applied for encryption of data and `variational autoencoder` is applied for detection of disease.

The work [31](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_31) utilizes `smart contracts` for sharing of data between patients, doctors, and caretakers. `Modified Merkle tree` data structure is used for secure and fast access of data. `Cryptographic hash functions` are used for security of data.

The paper [32](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_32) uses `attribute and identity-based encryption` for security of data over the cloud.

The paper [37](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_37) develops a novel model `MedRec` for data access and permission management of medical data.

The work [39](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_39) proposes a `granular access control mechanism` that can give different levels of authorization while maintaining compatibility with underlying blockchain architecture.

The work [45](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_45) focuses on the `EHR` data of emergency wards of the hospitals. This work uses the `P2P network` for analyzing data transmission of `smart contracts`.

The paper [46](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_46) builds a unified solution for migrating the existing `EHR`. Off-chain storage of assets is controlled through patient and policy transactions.
