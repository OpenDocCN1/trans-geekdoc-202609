We have two important assumptions which are related to Bayesian inference. 

# Bayesian Inference

+   * Ad i gets rewards from Bernoulli distribution p(y_i) ~ B(θ_i).
* θ_i is unknown but we know its uncertainty by having a uniform distribution p(θ_i) ~ U([0,1]), which is the prior distribution.
* Bayes Rules: approach θ_i by the posterior distribution
* We get p(θ_i) ~ β(number of successes + 1, number of failures + 1)
* At each round n we take a random draw θ_i(n) from this distribution p(θ_i), for each ad i.
* At each round n we select the Ad that has the highest θ_i(n).

p(θ_i|y_i) = p(y_i|θ_i)p(θ_i) / p(y_i) ∝ p(y_i|θ_i) * p(θ_i)
posterior distribution likelihood prior distribution

So the first assumption is in this line where we are showing the Ad_i gets rewards from a Bernoulli distribution of parameter θ_i. Where θ_i is the probability of success.

And you can consider this probability of success by showing the Ad to a huge amount of users like 100000 and could be interpreted as the number of times the outcomes were z (i.e. the number of successes) divided by the total number of times we selected the Ad.

θ_i = number of times we were / total number of times we selected the Ad

So basically it is the probability of success that is the probability of getting Reward 1 when we select the Ad.
