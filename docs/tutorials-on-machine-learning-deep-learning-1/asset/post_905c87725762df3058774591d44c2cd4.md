### 12.4.1 RMSE to evaluate the model performance

In the previous sections, the RNN built a regressor, indeed we were dealing with Regression because we were trying to predict continuous outcome (the Google Stock Price). For Regression, the way to evaluate the model performance is with a metric called RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error). It is calculated as the root of the mean of squared differences between the predictions and the real values.

> RMSE not help: For our specific Stock Price Prediction problem, evaluating the model with the RMSE does not make sense, since we are more interested in the directions taken by our predictions, rather than the closeness of their values to the real stock price. We want to check if our predictions follow the same directions as the real stock price and we don't really care whether our predictions are close the real stock price. The predictions could be close but often taking the opposite direction from the real stock price

If you are interested in the code that computes the RMSE for our Stock Price Prediction problem, please find it just below:

```python
import math
from sklearn.metrics import mean_squared_error
rmse = math.sqrt(mean_squared_error(real_stock_price, predicted_stock_price))
```

Then consider this RMSE by the range of the Google Stock values of January 2007 (that is around 800) to get a relative error, as opposed to an absolute error.

It is more significant if you example if an RMSE of 50 then this error would be very big if the stock price values around 100, but it would be small if the stock price values ranged around 10000.

### 12.4.2 Parameter tuning to improve RNN model

- [1]. Getting Training Data: we trained our model on the past 5 years of the Google Stock Price but it could be better to train it over the past 10 years.
- [2]. Increasing the number of Timesteps: we modeled the stock prices from the previous 60 financial days to predict the stock price of the next day. That is we chose a number of 60 timesteps (3 months). You could try to increase the number of timesteps, by choosing for 120 timesteps (6 months).
- [3]. Adding some Indicators: if you have the financial insight that the stock price of some other companies might be correlated to one of Google, you could add this stock price as a real indicator in the training data.
- [4]. Adding LSTM layers: we built a RNN with 4 LSTM layers and you could try even more.
- [5]. Adding more Neurons in the LSTM layers: we highlighted the fact that we need a high number of neurons in the LSTM layers to respond better to the complexity of the problem and we chose 50 neurons in each of our 4 LSTM layers. You could try an architecture with more neurons in each of the 4 (or more) LSTM layers.

### 12.4.1 GRU : Gated recurrent units

- Gated recurrent units (GRUs) are a gating mechanism in RNN, introduced in 2014 by Cho et al. The GRU is a long short-term memory (LSTM) with a Forget gate, but with fewer parameters than LSTM.
- GRU's performance on certain tasks of polyphonic music, speech modeling and natural language processing (NLP) was found to be similar to LSTM.
- Better for Smaller dataset: GRUs have been shown to exhibit better performance on smaller and less frequent datasets.
