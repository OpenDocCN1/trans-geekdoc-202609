# ### 9.3.5 Fully-Connected-Layers Work

Let's look at a more realistic example. Here we've got an ANN in the **Inputs** that

+   - five attributes on the Inputs that
- we have in the first *hidden-layer* [fully-connected-layer/]we have six neurons,
- in the second fully connected layer we have eight neurons
- then we have two outputs, one for Dog and one for Cat.

![](bbox=[0.198, 0.145, 0.802, 0.403])

**Why do we have two outputs here?** Before in ANN we used to have one output.

Note that, **one output** is for kind of when you're predicting a *numerical value* and running a *regression type of problem*

But when we're doing classification, we have more than **two categories** for instance **dogs, cats and birds** then you have to use a *neuron* for *each category* and that's why we're going to *practice* with **two categories**.

![](bbox=[0.298, 0.638, 0.902, 0.813])

So we've already done the *convolution*, *pooling* and *flattening* and now the information is gonna go through the ANN.

+   - a) At the first step the *convolution*, *pooling* and *flattening* happens and the information going through the ANN and a *prediction* is made.

+   - b) And for instance let's say, it predicted 80%, given image is a *dog's* image. But it turns out that actually a *Cat*.

+   - c) Then an error is calculated. *Cost function* in ANN, where we used to calculate *mean square error*. In CNN it's called a *Loss Function* and we use a *Cross Entropy Function* for (that we will talk *Cross Entropy Function* later).

> *This Loss Function* tells us how well our network is performing and we're trying to optimize or minimize that *Loss Function* to optimize our Network.
