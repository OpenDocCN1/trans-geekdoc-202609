# ## 13.3.1 How SOM works

In this section we want to find out how SOMs learn.

+   - Here we've got a very simple example of SOM. We've got **three features [3 columns]** in our *input vector*, and we've got **nine nodes** in the output. **Each node** represents a *data-point (single row)*.

+   - Don't let this representation confuse your understanding of SOM. Here we have **3 columns of features** and we might have thousands of rows (each row has 3 columns, represents a data-point).

+   - We may have **rows** (data-points) in a **2D map** and **Group (cluster)** them using SOM.

It means that our **input data set** is actually **three dimensional** (we reduce this dimension), our **output data set** in *SOM* is always a **two-dimensional map**, and we are reducing the dimensionality from 3D to 2D.

We're going to turn this SOM into a **Network representation**. Right-hand image shows us what it would look like

+   - It is the **same network** (above), above is how we've **positioned** the nodes.

+   - We still have the **same amount of connections, inputs** and **outputs**, it's the visual representation has changed so that it would be easier for us to understand what's going on.

Also note that SOMs are different from **NN**.

+   - First of all, SOMs are much easier than NN-supervised techniques. The concept becomes simple and straightforward.

+   - Secondly it's also important to note that SOMs are different than **ANN/CNN/RNN**.

+   - The concepts they have the same names/terms (as *weights* and *synapses*) have different meanings so don't get confused with ANN/CNN/RNN terms.

+   - Just be careful when we're talking about like weights and synapses and other things, those are different from *ANN/CNN/RNN*.

Let's consider the **top node** of our **output-nodes**. Notice the **three synapses** connecting to our **three input-nodes**

+   - We saw in **ANN**, weights were used to multiply the value of a node, we added them up and then we applied *activation function*.

**Weights in SOM**: The weights in SOMs are different and there is no activation function. Here weights act as *coordinates*. The node itself, For example, **W_{1,1}, W_{1,2}** and **W_{1,3}** as an *input vector*, in *3D space*.

+   - There are **three weights** for those 3 synapses **W_{1,1}, W_{1,2}** and **W_{1,3}** Now

*First index* means it's the first-node. **Second index** means the input nodes.
