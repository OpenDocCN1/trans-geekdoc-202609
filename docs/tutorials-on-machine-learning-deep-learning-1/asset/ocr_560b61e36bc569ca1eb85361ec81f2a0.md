# 3.2.1 Logistic Regression

Let's imagine that we have a scenario where we have two categories already present in our data set we've identified two categories: Green and Red.

For simplicity let's consider two variables or two columns x₁ and x₂ in our data-set so all of this is happening based on these columns.

Now let's say we add a new data point into our data set. The question is should it fall into the Red category or should fall to the Green category.

K-Nearest Neighborhoods: The steps are given below:

+   i. STEP 1: Choose the number K of neighbors (select the no. of points) which will be the Neighborhood.
ii. STEP 2: Choose the Euclidean distance of K number of neighbors
iii. STEP 3: Take the nearest neighbors of the new data point, according to the Euclidean distance (we can also use other kind of distances)
iv. STEP 4: Among these K neighbors, count the Number of data points in each category (you might even have more than two categories in your data set).
vv. STEP 5: Assign the new data point to the category where you counted the most neighbors
vi. Final: Your Model is Ready

Euclidean distance : The formula is √((x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²), where the points (x₁, y₁) are (x₂, y₂).

How we classify: Let's no. of points in our data is K=5.

+   * Take the K nearest neighbors of the new data point by their Euclidean distance.
* Once you've reached the neighbors among these K you need to count the number of data points in each category.
* So many data points fell into each category (you might even have more than two categories in your data set).
* Just calculate how many fall into each category and then need to assign the new data point to the category where you counted the most neighbors.
