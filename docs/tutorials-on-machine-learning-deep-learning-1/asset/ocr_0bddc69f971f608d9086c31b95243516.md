Parameters: These arguments which you will apply on your business problem, depend on the number of observations you have in your data-set. Of course your minimum support, confidence and lift are not going to be the same as you have 1000 transactions and 10000 transactions.

```
# Training apriori
from apriori import apriori
rules=apriori(transactions=transactions, min_support=0.003, min_confidence=0.2, min_lift=3, min_length=2)
results=list(rules)
for asocitm,item in results:
    print(f"{asocitm\n}")
```

In the code above, the first line is to import the apriori function. In the code, the apriori function returns the output as the rules. It takes the following parameters:

+   **transactions**: A list of transactions. We pass our list of lists data-set **Transactions**.
- **min_support**: To set the minimum support float value.
- **min_support**: To set the minimum support float value. We have used 0.003 to calculate by 3 transactions per customer each to the total number of transactions.

+   - We need to look at the products that are purchased frequently lately like those that are bought at least three or four days.
- It depends on your business goal. Now, we find some rules about items that are bought at least three or four times a day by associating them and putting together customers will be more likely to put them in their basket and before these products will be purchased and the sales will increase.
- Now, to set the **minimum support** we go to consider the products that are purchased three or four times a day and then we will look at the rules. And if we're not convinced/satisfied by we will change this value of the **minimum support**.

If a product is bought 3 times per day in 1 week it is $3 \times 7 = 21$. And we have found 7500 transactions.

$$min_support=\frac{21}{7500}=0.0028 \approx 0.003$$

All the products of our rules will have a higher support than this support.

+   **min_confidence**: To set the minimum confidence value. Here we have taken 0.2. It can be changed as per the business problem.
- confidence of 0.8 means that the rules has to be in 80 percent of the time.

+   - But if you get some rules containing some properties of example **Mineral-water** and **Eggs**. In hot day bought them frequently. But these two products have no connection between them. They end up in the same basket. Right reason: not because they associate well together but they're purchased all the time.
- There is no logical association between these two products and that's not very relevant. And unfortunately that's what we'll get if we set the **confidence high**
- Hence we set the confidence 20% i.e.min_confidence = 0.2.

+   **min_lift**: To set the minimum lift value.
- We also try **different values** of the **minimum lift**.
- So now we try to get some rules that have lift above 3. These are actually some good rules because you know the left is a great insight of the relevance and the strength of rule. We're hoping to find some rules having lift to four, five or six.
