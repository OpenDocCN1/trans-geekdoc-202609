Lets say Red curve represents the model. This Red line here is the cumulative accuracy profile of your model. The better your model the Larger will be the area between Red and Blue line.

If your model is worse, this red line will be closer to the blue line (random line). And this is how the CAP-curve is normally represented.

Consider another model represented by Green curve. Let's say we had less-access to independent variables and we didn't see there's a multicollinearity effect in our model or something else went wrong. And that making this model worse.

By plotting the CAP CURVE (Green and Red) you'll be able to compare models to each other and understand how much we gain in this is also sometimes called the Gain Chart.

+   - How much gain you get in each of these models compared to the random scenario or how much you get additional gain from switching from one model to the next or from the Green to the Red for instance. This is how you're improving your ratio and therefore you're improving your return on investment.
