# 2.7. Interpreting Linear Regression Coefficients

How do we find/explain the Coefficient of the final model. Consider the previous Venture Capitalist example,

+   Notice the following:
R.Spend 7.966
Marketing.Spend 2.9912

So these coefficients are $B_1 = 0.7966$ and $B_2 = 0.0299$. What are they telling us is,

+   First of all you look at the sign. If the sign is positive means your variable is correlated with your independent variable. Meaning that if you change the value of your independent variable then the value can say that the dependent variable will change in the same direction.

+   So basically you'll be spending on R.Spend then you'll be profitable increasing spending of marking then your profit is also increasing.

+   If the sign is negative then it's the opposite effect. So basically increase your independent variable and you decrease variable. If any one of them has -ve value then the corresponding feature will decrease the dependent variable (it is the basic math)

+   Always remember the magnitude. You might think that, Coefficient for this R Spend is bigger than the Market Spend coefficient. But that's not the case, the value of measurement is important here, to compare something like that we have to measure all in a measurement scale.

+   Even, though coefficient for this R Spend is bigger but of its unit you can say 1 Cent i cents ($0.01), and the magnitude of unit for Market Spend is is lower than R Spend.

+   Use the term 'Per Unit Change'. R Spend could be in dollar (unit) or it could be in cents. The magnitude could be different for different feature variables R Spend could be in Dollars or Celcius. The Marketing could be in Million Dollars. In all of those case we should say 'Per Unit Change'

So here we use 1 for measurement for R Spend and Market Spend. Then we can say, for every dollar (every unit) of R Spend that increase your model profit will increase by 79 cents or $0.79. For every unit that decrease in your spend your profit will decrease by 7.9unit of profit.

Similarly for every (every unit) of Market Spend that you increase, increasing to model your profit will increase by 2.99 cents or 0.0299.

# 2.7.6 FAQ

I understand that in order to evaluate linear models using the Adjusted R or the Pearson matrix. How can I evaluate Polynomial regression or Random Forest regression models which are not linear?

+   You can evaluate regression models and random forest regression models, by using the 'Mean of Squared Residuals' the mean of squared error. You can easily that with a sum function or using a for loop, computing the sum of the squared differences between the predicted outcomes and the real outcomes, over all the observations of the test set.
