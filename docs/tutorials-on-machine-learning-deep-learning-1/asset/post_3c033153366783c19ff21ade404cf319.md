### 9.1.1 Convolutional-Neural-Networks (CNN)

Look at this image. Do you see a person looking at you or looking to the right. Here your brain is struggling to adjust. If you look to the right side of the image you'll see a person looking to the right. If you look at the left side of the image you'll see a person looking at you.

This proves that we see things is actually its **features**. Depending on the features our brain process.

- * So you look to the **right side of the image** you see certain **features** of a **person looking to right** because they're closer to your center of focus and therefore your brain **classifies it** as a person looking to the right.

- * When you look to the **left side of the image** you see more **features** of a **person looking at you** and therefore your brain classifies it as such.

Most of this kind of illusion image we can see two in one and depending on which features our brain picks up it will switch between classifying each image as one or the other. The oldest one of these illusions recorded in the printed work is "duck or the rabbit". Here is also "Young girl or Old lady".

For this kind of image your brain is trying to understand what is it, what it is like it's trying to. This is a classic example of when there are certain features where your brain cannot decide.

All these examples illustrate us how the brain processes certain features on an image or whatever you are in real life and classifies that as.

You probably have been in situations when you look over your shoulder quickly and you see something-it's like a Ball but it turns out to be a Cat because you don't have time to process those features or you don't have enough features to classify.

The CNN works in very similar way. Computers can interpret them as we do.
