- Role of SOM: The first thing to understand here is that the columns are the attributes, so these are the informations of the customers. There are 16 clusters, which are different features as the lines rows are the customers.
- The unsupervised learning model is good to identify some patterns among the customers. It's good to do some kind of customer segmentation to identify segments of customers and one of the segments will contain Fraud customers.
- All those customers are going to be on the self-organizing map (SOM). It will actually very explicit, it corresponds to one specific range of values of the SOM.
- We are the inputs of our neural network. These input points are to be mapped to a new output space.

Between the input space and the output space, weight vector has NN neurons, each neuron being initialized as a vector of weights has same size as the vector of inputs (i.a vector of 15 elements).

For each observation point the output will be the neuron that is closest to the point. Basically, in the network, we pick the neuron that is closest to the customer, this is called the winning node.

For each customer, the winning node is the most similar neuron to the customer. We use a neighbourhood function like the Gaussian Neighbourhood function, to update the weight of the neighbors of the winning node to move closer to the point.

We do this all for the customers in the input space. It is a repeating process. And each time we repeat it, the output space decreases and loses its shape.

It reduces its dimension by little by little. And it reaches a point where the neighbourhood stops decreasing, where the output space stops decreasing and become steady.

And that's the moment where we obtained our SOM in 2d map, with all the winning nodes that were eventually identified.

- How we detect the frauds: The frauds are outliers, because the fraud is basically defined by something that is far from the general rules. The rules must be respected when applying to the credit card.
- The frauds are the outlying neurons in this 2-SOM, because they are far from the majority of neurons that follow the rules.
- How can we detect the outliers in the SOM?
- For this, we need the Mean Interneuron Distance.
- That means in our self-organizing map for each neuron, we're going to compute the mean of the Euclidean distance between this neuron and neurons in its neighborhood.
- We define a neighborhood for each neuron manually. We use the mean of the Euclidean distance between this neuron we picked and all the neurons in the neighborhood we defined.
- And by that we can detect outliers, because outliers will be far from all neurons in their neighborhood. That's how we detect fraud from SOM.
- Also we use an Inverse mapping function to identify which customers in the input space is an outlier that is associated to a winning node,
