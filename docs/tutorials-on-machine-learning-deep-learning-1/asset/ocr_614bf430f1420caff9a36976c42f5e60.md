# $\log p(v^0) = \sum w_{ij}^0 h_j^0 - \sum v_i^\infty h_{ij}^\infty$

Gradient Formula: This is a gradient formula, can see the gradient on the left here We've got the gradient of the log probability of a certain state of our system, based on the weights in the system.

Weights are Constant: Remember, through this process, the weights are constant. We're not changing the weights, we just using random weights.

When we talked about energy based systems we said in RBM energy is defined through the weights. Following is the curve for the weights, 'Energy' is actually weights.

![](bbox=[0.288, 0.196, 0.715, 0.363])

Since we are using random weights, reconstructed input-values won't be same to our real-input-values even though the system is converged. We need to adjust the weights in such a way that the system reach to the low energy state.

It's telling us how the weights affect the log probability.

$v_i^0$ is the initial state of the system, $v_i$ is visible vector, $h_j^0$ is hidden vector. $v_i^\infty$ represents next states.

+   The weights are fixed and we're going to define this energy/curve based on the weight.
- Weights dictate the shape of this energy curve,
- For our first pass through the RBM we place our inputs (the green point, since the weights are initialized randomly it could be anywhere).

![](bbox=[0.498, 0.486, 0.913, 0.642])

Since our second pass, we end up at the red point. Since a system is governed by its energy will always try to end up in the lowest energy state possible.

So as you can see, this ball/point is rolling towards the bottom(minima).

That's exactly what's happening through Contrastive Divergence process, where reconstructed input values converges to some values.

We're going closer and closer to our lowest energy state

![](bbox=[0.498, 0.651, 0.913, 0.811])

But remember, the weights are not changed, but we get reconstructed input values and hidden values, that bring the system to the minimum energy state at the end of this Contrastive Divergence process.

And what this formula is telling us, that you have lowest energy state if you subtract $v_i^\infty h_{ij}^\infty$ value from $v_i^\infty$, it will tell you how adjusting your weights will affect the log probability of the system being in its lowest energy state.

So basically, this is a recipe for adjusting your curve or modifying your energy curve, so that you can make that initial state (where we have initial-values) is an lowest energy state, so that you can get an desired effect. With this formula we adjust the weights in such a way that the reconstructed values are same as input-values.
