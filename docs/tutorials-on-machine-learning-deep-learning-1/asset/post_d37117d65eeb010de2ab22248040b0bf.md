### 13.1.2 SOM (Self-Organizing Maps)

We already talked about **ANN, CNN, and RNN** and those are collectively called *supervised* deep learning.

> However, SOM is a *unsupervised* deep learning method. Self-organizing maps (SOM) were invented in the 1980s by Teuvo Kohonen. Sometimes SOMs are even called the *Kohonen maps.*

![](img/0aa092657a6eb1b9f2dd65bbb79055a3_422_0.png)

- **Usage of SOMs:**
  - SOMs are used for reducing *dimensionality.*
  - SOMs can be used in *astronomy.* Here's a great example from the paper.

![](img/0aa092657a6eb1b9f2dd65bbb79055a3_422_1.png)

Image Adapted From: arxiv.org/pdf/1312.5753.pdf

Here we got a beautiful visualization of how *self-organizing maps* actually work.

SOMs take a multi-dimensional data set (with lots of columns which are the dimensions of the data set, and lots of rows) and they reduce the dimensionality of these data sets.

- Basically, instead of using 20, 30 or a hundred or more columns (20, 30 or 100 dimensions), we end up with a map.
- That's why they are called Self-Organizing Maps (SOMs)—we end up with a two-dimensional representation of your data set.
- The purpose of the SOM is to reduce the amount of columns and represent the data in a 2D-map.

Example: Here is an actual SOM, which was produced, from the data of the different states of prosperity and poverty in different countries.

![](img/0aa092657a6eb1b9f2dd65bbb79055a3_423_0.png)

Image Source: cis.hut.fi

- Those names actually represent countries of the world and this SOM has put them into clusters based on lots of different indicators.
- In this example, 39 indicators were used. And indicators are parameters describing such things as quality of life, the state of health in a country, nutrition, education, services, and so on.
- We can see in the top left corner, we have countries with the best or the least alarming state of poverty. Those countries are Belgium, Sweden, Japan, Spain.
- We also notice that it is slowly going towards the other end of the spectrum where we have countries with the most alarming state of poverty, like Ethiopia or Zimbabwe.
- If we have a huge data set containing 200 plus countries as rows, and 39 different columns (dimensions), indicators in SOM—it's impossible to visualize.
- But using a SOM, we can reduce the dimensionality and present it as a map like above.
- Remember that SOMs are unsupervised techniques: it has training data but it doesn't have any labels in the training data. So its learning is on its own.
  - Just using given data, and it learns to group these data (countries). It is much more like clustering in ML.
- What else can we do with this map: we take the color-codes for the different countries and color them in the world map. In this world map we can determine first world countries and third world countries, where countries are developed, where countries are developing.

To summarize, we had the data, which you can get from the World Bank—you can just download data sets from there. We reduce the columns using SOM and get a 2D-map with different countries grouped together according to their color code. It would help group your data set. SOM map is still a good representation for the data but we can use the colors in the world map.

### 13.1.2 SOM (Self-Organizing Maps)

We already talked about **ANN, CNN, and RNN** and those are collectively called *supervised* deep learning.

> However, SOM is a *unsupervised* deep learning method. Self-organizing maps (SOM) were invented in the 1980s by Teuvo Kohonen. Sometimes SOMs are even called the *Kohonen maps.*

![](img/0aa092657a6eb1b9f2dd65bbb79055a3_422_0.png)

- **Usage of SOMs:**
  - SOMs are used for reducing *dimensionality.*
  - SOMs can be used in *astronomy.* Here's a great example from the paper.

![](img/0aa092657a6eb1b9f2dd65bbb79055a3_422_1.png)

Image Adapted From: arxiv.org/pdf/1312.5753.pdf

Here we got a beautiful visualization of how *self-organizing maps* actually work.

SOMs take a multi-dimensional data set (with lots of columns which are the dimensions of the data set, and lots of rows) and they reduce the dimensionality of these data sets.

- Basically, instead of using 20, 30 or a hundred or more columns (20, 30 or 100 dimensions), we end up with a map.
- That's why they are called Self-Organizing Maps (SOMs)—we end up with a two-dimensional representation of your data set.
- The purpose of the SOM is to reduce the amount of columns and represent the data in a 2D-map.

Example: Here is an actual SOM, which was produced, from the data of the different states of prosperity and poverty in different countries.
