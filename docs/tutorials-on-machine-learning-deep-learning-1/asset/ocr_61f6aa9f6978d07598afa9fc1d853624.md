We're going to have a look at how to read advanced SOM. You might come across a SOM that looks like above. There's actually a lot of representations inside this SOM. This is an example from Wikipedia, and is a SOM of voting results/ patterns in the US Congress.

The input data for this map was a data sets of Members of Congress in the US Congress over 500 or 535 Members. Each Member belongs some class that they were voting on. Did they say yes/no/absent voting.

And based on that information, the SOM group of Members of Congress are similar to each other, which are dissimilar, and place them onto a map.

+   CLUSTERS: So we've got a map of the Members of Congress, where SOM splits the set into two classes.
- This is the overall cluster, but it is different. How the SOM splits the Members of Performance, just based on all of the things they voted on all of the different questions (it is not solely based on Republican-democrat).
- Here red doesn't mean Republicans or blue doesn't mean Democratic, just red and blue two other colors are used to identify the two clusters.

This cluster is based on overall voting about: 17 topics –Bankruptcy Abuse Prevention, Border Protection Anti-terrorist, Broadcast Decency Enforcement, Class Action Fairness Act, Continuity in Representation, Personal Responsibility inf, Private Property Rights Prot etc (these are the actual topics that are being voted).
