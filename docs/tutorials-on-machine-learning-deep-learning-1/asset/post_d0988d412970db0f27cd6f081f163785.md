### 6.1.2 The Multi-Armed Bandit

**Multi-armed bandit**: In probability theory and machine learning, the Multi-armed bandit problem (sometimes called *K* or *N-armed bandit*) is a problem in which a fixed limited set of resources must be allocated to competing choices that maximizes expected gain, when each choice's properties are only partially known at the time of allocation, and may become better understood as allocating resources.

**Reinforcement Learning Problem**: This is a classic reinforcement learning problem that exemplifies the exploration-exploitation dilemma. The name comes from imagining a gambler at a row of slot machines (Those slot machines are known as "one-armed bandits"), who have to decide which machines to play, how many times to play each machine, in which order to play them, and whether to continue with the current machine or try a different machine.

The multi-armed bandit problem also falls into the broad category of stochastic scheduling.

- **What is a multi-armed bandit?**: First thing that comes to mind is like a robber into a bank and so on. But actually one armed bandit is a slot machine.
- **Why is it called the arm bandit?**: Notice the handle on the right. These machines, and you, there are 50-50 chance to win/lose. But in some casino machines are designed with a button, so that the user lose their money very fast (i.e. more than 50% chance to lose). And they became the quickest to lose your money in a casino. Here the name bandit because it is basically robbing you.
