Then we take sampled hidden nodes as input. Let's call them $h^{(1)}$ to sample the visible nodes with the probabilities $p(v^{(1)})$.

Then again use these sample visible nodes for 2nd iteration, let's call $v^{(0)}$, to sample the hidden nodes with the probabilities $p(h^{(1)})$ and then again sample the visible nodes and hide the hidden nodes and we do this $K$ times.

And that is exactly how this CD-K algorithm (K-step contrastive divergence) is. The algorithm is below:

Algorithm 1. K-step contrastive divergence
Input: $RBM(v_1, ..., v_n; h_1, ..., h_m)$, training batch $S$
Output: gradient approximation $\Delta v_i, \Delta c_j$ for $i=1, ..., n, j=1, ..., m$
$j=1, ..., m$
1 initialize $\Delta v_i = \Delta c_j = 0$ for $i=1, ..., n, j=1, ..., m$
2 for $v=S$
3 $v^{(0)} \leftarrow v$
4 for $k=1, ..., K-1$ do
5 for $i=1, ..., n$ sample $h_i^{(k)} \sim p(h_i|v^{(k)})$
6 for $j=1, ..., m$ sample $v_j^{(k+1)} \sim p(v_j|h^{(k)})$
7 for $i=1, ..., n, j=1, ..., m$ do
8 $\Delta v_i \leftarrow \Delta v_i + p(h_i|v^{(0)}) - p(h_i|v^{(k)})$
9 $\Delta c_j \leftarrow \Delta c_j + p(v_j|h^{(0)}) - p(v_j|h^{(k)})$
10 $c_i \leftarrow c_i + p(h_i|v^{(0)}) - p(h_i|v^{(k)})$

So, in our **train()** function of the RBM, we will simply implement these three steps **8, 9, 10**. And we will simply update our **tensor** of weights. Our visible-node-bias $b$ and hidden-node-bias $a$.
