Note that **$(RR^T)^{-1}$** is the covariance matrix for **Z**:

$Cov[Z] = E[ZZ^T] = E[RRU^TR^T] = RE[UU^T]R^T = R/R^T = RR^T$

Thus the density function of **Z** can be written as

$f_Z(\mathbf{z}) = \frac{1}{|\det(\mathbf{R})|(\sqrt{2\pi})^n} e^{-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{z}^T\Sigma_Z\mathbf{z}}$

Furthermore, we know that

$|det(\Sigma_Z)| = |det(R^T)|$
$= |det(R) \cdot det(R^T)|$
$= |det(R) \cdot det(R)| = |det(R)|^2$

and therefore

$f_Z(\mathbf{z}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\det(\Sigma_Z)}(\sqrt{2\pi})^n}} e^{-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{z}^T\Sigma_Z\mathbf{z}}$

# 2.5 MLE with Dependent Noise

Up to this point, we have been able to easily view regression problems from an optimization perspective. In the context of dependent noise however, it is much easier to view the problem from a probabilistic perspective. Note as before that:

$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{Xw} + \mathbf{Z}$

Where **Z** is now a jointly Gaussian random vector. **$\mathbf{Z} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \Sigma_Z)$**, and **$\mathbf{y} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{Xw}, \Sigma_Z)$**.

$\mathbf{w}^* = \arg \max_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^n} \frac{1}{\sqrt{\det(\Sigma_Z)}(\sqrt{2\pi})^n}} e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{Xw})^T\Sigma_Z^{-1}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{Xw})}$ \quad (2.1)

$\arg \min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^n} (\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{Xw})^T\Sigma_Z^{-1}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{Xw})$ \quad (2.2)

Note that **$\Sigma_Z$** is symmetric, which means it has a good eigen structure, we can take the interpret geometrically. **$\Sigma_Z$** can be written as

$\Sigma_Z = \mathbf{Q} egin{pmatrix} \sigma_1^2 & 0 & \dots & 0 \ 0 & \sigma_2^2 & \dots & 0 \ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \ 0 & 0 & \dots & \sigma_n^2 \end{pmatrix} \mathbf{Q}^T$

where **Q** is orthonormal. This means a multivariate Gaussian can be thought of having its level sets by the ellipsoid axis given by **Q** and amount of stretch given by **$\sigma$**.

$\mathbf{R}_Z = \sqrt{\Sigma_Z} = \mathbf{Q} egin{pmatrix} \sigma_1 & 0 & \dots & 0 \ 0 & \sigma_2 & \dots & 0 \ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \ 0 & 0 & \dots & \sigma_n \end{pmatrix}$
