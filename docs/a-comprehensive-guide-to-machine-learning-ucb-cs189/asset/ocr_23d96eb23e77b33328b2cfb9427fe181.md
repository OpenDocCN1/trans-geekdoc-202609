This looks like ridge regression, with a **negative regularization constant**! Why does this make sense? One of the motivations of ridge regression was to ensure that the matrix being inverted is in fact nonsingular, and subtracting a scalar multiple of the identity seems like a step in the opposite direction. We can make sense of this by recalling our original model:

```
$$\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{X}_{true} + \mathbf{Z}$
```

where $\mathbf{X}_{true}$ are the actual values before corruption, and $\mathbf{Z}$ is a zero-mean noise term. Then

```
$$\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}^	op\mathbf{X}] = \mathbb{E}[(\mathbf{X}_{true} + \mathbf{Z})^	op(\mathbf{X}_{true} + \mathbf{Z})]$\nn = \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_{true}^	op\mathbf{X}_{true}] + \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_{true}^	op\mathbf{Z}] + \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{Z}^	op\mathbf{X}_{true}] + \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{Z}^	op\mathbf{Z}]$\nn = \mathbf{X}_{true}^	op\mathbf{X}_{true} + \mathbf{X}_{true}^	op\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{Z}] + \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{Z}]^	op\mathbf{X}_{true} + \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{Z}^	op\mathbf{Z}]$\nn = \mathbf{X}_{true}^	op\mathbf{X}_{true} + \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{Z}^	op\mathbf{Z}]$
```

Observe that the off-diagonal terms of $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{Z}^	op\mathbf{Z}]$ terms are zero because the $i$-th and $j$-th rows of $\mathbf{Z}$ are independent for $i \neq j$, and the on-diagonal terms are essentially variance. Thus the $-\sigma_{j+1}^2$ term is there to compensate for the extra noise introduced by our assumptions regarding independent variables.

# Eckart-Young Theorem

The Eckart-Young theorem essentially says that the best-rank approximation (in terms of the Frobenius norm) is obtained by throwing away the smallest singular values.
