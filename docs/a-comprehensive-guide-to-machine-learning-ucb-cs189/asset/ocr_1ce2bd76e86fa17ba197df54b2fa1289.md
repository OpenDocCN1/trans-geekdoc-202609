Recall that this implies that multiplication by $\mathbf{U}$ or $\mathbf{V}$ does not change the Frobenius norm, so minimizing $|\mathbf{E} \mathbf{f}|_F^2$ is equivalent to minimizing $|\mathbf{E}' \mathbf{f}'|_F^2$ where $\mathbf{E}', \mathbf{f}'$ are $\mathbf{E}, \mathbf{f}$ expressed in the SVD coordinates. Now our problem reduces to finding

```
$$\begin{bmatrix} \sigma_1 & & & 0 \ & & \vdots & \vdots \ & & \sigma_{d+1} & [\mathbf{E}' \mathbf{f}'] \ & & & \vdots \ 0 & & & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ (9)
```

is not full rank and $|\mathbf{E}' \mathbf{f}'|_F^2$ is as small as possible. Since the matrix on the left is diagonal, we can reduce its size by simply zeroing out one of its diagonal elements. Therefore our perturbation $[\mathbf{E}' \mathbf{f}']$ will have $-\sigma_j$ in the $(j,j)$ position for some $j$, and zeros everywhere else. To minimize the size of the perturbation, we decide to eliminate the smallest $\sigma_j$ by taking

```
$[\mathbf{E}' \mathbf{f}'] = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & & & 0 \ & & \vdots & \vdots \ & & &-\sigma_{d+1} \ & & & \vdots & \vdots \ 0 & & & 0 \end{bmatrix}$
```

```
$[\mathbf{E} \mathbf{f}] = [\mathbf{u}_1 \quad \dots \quad \mathbf{u}_{d+1} \quad U_{rest}] \begin{bmatrix} 0 & & & 0 \ & & \vdots & \vdots \ & & &-\sigma_{d+1}\mathbf{v}_{d+1}^T \ & & &\mathbf{v}_{d+1}^T \ 0 & & & 0 \end{bmatrix} =-\sigma_{d+1}\mathbf{u}_{d+1}\mathbf{v}_{d+1}^T$
```

in the original coordinate system. It turns out that this choice is optimal, as guaranteed by the Eckart-Young theorem, which is stated at the end for reference.
