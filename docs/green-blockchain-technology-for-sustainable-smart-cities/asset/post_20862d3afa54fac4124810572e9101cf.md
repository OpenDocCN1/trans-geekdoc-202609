
### 3.1 Countries investing in green technologies

The potential of green technology initiatives across worldwide is to enhance green finance for sustainable development. Green technology encompasses a wide range of innovations, from exploring new clean and renewable sources of energy to pioneering new and clean materials to sustainable agriculture. Worldwide, green technology investments reached $755 billion in 2021, a sharp rise from the previous year. In order to meet the goal to eliminate carbon emissions by 2050, investment in green technology is rising worldwide, both by governments and private investors.

The Asia-Pacific regions grow into the largest amount of green tech investment in 2021, as well as the highest growth. Over $368 billion were invested in Asia–Pacific green tech industries, a 38% increase from 2020. Investment in the Middle East/North Africa region grew 16%, and in the Americas, it reached 21%. Corporate investments in climate tech companies also increased, reaching $165 billion in 2021 (Report by United Nations Climate Change Conference, 2021).

The UNEP (2022) is supporting the implementation of the National Green Development Policy and focuses on the integration of green economy into local level development plans. An initiative in the greening of key sectors to obtain the SDGs indicators (Green Construction, Green Bonds, Green Insurance, and Sustainable Transportation) creates awareness, knowledge, and capacities to promote sustainable economies. Additionally, government organizations have increased its engagement with the private sector, which is a very important factor in promoting resource efficiency and green economy. The UN Environment has been working with countries’ financial regulators to direct the financial flows to acquire the SDGs.

The World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO, 2022) (Tang, 2022) is most actively and directly encouraging the green tech initiatives, which enables innovation to develop greener and more efficient technologies. Today, innovation in green technology has become an important tool for businesses to increase their market share to facilitate organizations toward attaining sustainable competitive advantages. These technological solutions play an important role in allowing us to achieve sustainability targets around the globe with finite natural resources and an expanding healthy global population. A successful green innovation in technology improves the company’s market position, attracts customers, provides green services, and gains competitive advantage. Because of these benefits, innovative green technologies are on the agenda for many organizations and researchers in the world.

The UN Secretary-General has recently mandated the establishment of a Task Force on green finance for the SDGs. The Task Force, which will be coming up with high-level members from the UN private and public sectors, aims to identify the opportunities for digital finance to support all SDGs, suggests how public and private partners can leverage these opportunities, and highlights the added value role of the United Nations (According to the Secretary-General, 2019).

The World Bank launched a blockchain lab in 2017 to serve as a forum for learning, experimentation, and collaboration on distributed ledger technology (DLT). This blockchain lab brings together internal and external participants to work on blockchain use cases, and its core focus areas include land registry, digital identity, aid distribution, and financial infrastructure. The lab's objective is to explore and build with its nonprofit and technology partners and to produce proofs of concept that can then be rolled out in the field. It also enables greater understanding of the technology itself, as well as its accompanying security, legal, and policy implications.

The initiative of the Programme is to increase the access of banking services through developing innovative and sustainable retail payments services as a point of entry for low-income populations. The Programme supports the development of innovative and sustainable retail payment solutions using technologies such as mobile phones, electronic funds transfer at point of sale, and payment card infrastructure to deliver payment services at low cost to the base of the pyramid as well as other users. Implementation is in close cooperation with the World Bank Payment Systems (Global Innovative Retail Payments Program).

### 3.2 Innovative green technology for a greener economy and restoration

Global energy investment is set to increase by 8% in 2022 to reach USD 2.4 trillion, with the anticipated rise coming mainly in clean energy. Although encouraging, the growth investment is still far from enough to tackle the multiple dimensions of today's energy crisis and pave the way toward a cleaner and more secure energy future (Report of International Energy Agency 2022).

The global green tech and sustainability market through across the globe investments in green technologies hit a whopping $755 billion in a year, a significant increase from the previous year. Governments and private organizations are investing more heavily in green tech, leading to more advancement, innovation, and overall market growth (Report of Allied Market Research, 2021).

The G20 countries are expected to provide $97 trillion by 2040 for green infrastructure improvements and sustainable developments (United Nations Office for Project Services, 2019). Even before the COVID-19 pandemic, sustainable investments were insufficient and much lower than the required amount. Since the pandemic, in the case of developing countries, the $2.5 trillion annual SDG financing gap is predicted to increase due to global economic uncertainty (Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development, 2020).

Clean energy investment grew by only 2% a year in the five years after the Paris Agreement was signed in 2015. But since 2020, the pace of growth has accelerated significantly to 12%. Spending has been underpinned by fiscal support from governments and aided by the rise of sustainable finance, especially in advanced economies. Renewable grids and storage now account for more than 80% of total power sector investment. Spending on solar photovoltaics, batteries, and electric vehicles is now growing at rates consistent with reaching global net zero emissions by 2050. Investment in battery energy storage is expected to more than double to reach almost $20 billion in 2022 (Report of World Energy investment, 2022).

The growth of green finance seems conclusive to continue as most of the governments in worldwide focus on how to cut pollution and greenhouse gases. The value of sustainability-themed funds traded on exchanges hit a record $41.6 billion last year (Report of Bloomberg Intelligence, 2022).

The current investments in sustainable management amount to $133 billion, most of which comes from public sources and calls for investments to triple by 2030 and to increase fourfold by 2050 from the current level. If the world is to meet targets for climate change, biodiversity, and land degradation, it needs to close a $4.1 trillion financing gap by 2050 (Report of United Nations Environment Program, 2021).

Tapping green finance credibly signals commitment to environmental impact that has turned its attention in recent years to more sustainable and inclusive growth. Asian firms that issue green bonds typically improve their environmental performance by 17% within a year after issuance and by 30% within two years. Evidence further suggests that green finance signals increased awareness of and commitment to the SDGs (Report of Asian Development Outlook, 2021).

Cash inflow into any kind of asset is labeled green or sustainable. At least $30.7 trillion of funds is held in sustainable or green investments, up 34% from 2016. Overall, these money flows account for one-third of the tracked assets under management and seek to financing environmentally sound and sustainable projects that foster a net-zero emissions economy and protect the environment (Report of Global Sustainable Investment Alliance, 2019).

An investment opportunity on the use of Green Bond promotes a change in transparency that facilitates the tracking of funds to environmental projects, while simultaneously aiming to improve insight into their estimated impact. Green credentials alongside provide transparency and disclosure and promote integrity in the development of the Green Bond market by clarifying the approach for issuance of a Green Bond (Green Bond Principles, 2021).
