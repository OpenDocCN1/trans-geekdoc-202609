The other way blockchain technology can facilitate the advent of new green financial products is to package the returns of environmental financial products with those of other instruments and debt applied to financing environmentally friendly assets, and to do so entirely digitally. Financial exclusion is estimated to affect around 1.7 billion people worldwide who lack access to even the most basic of financial services. There are many reasons for financial exclusion including geographic barriers, lack of identity documents, and lack of trust in local financial institutions. Blockchain can help to achieve the solutions by supporting the creation of a trusted framework for transactions between parties who do not know each other and the use of smart contracts and to make the relation between fundamental design and blockchain applications robust (Krishnan et al., 2020).

In addition, blockchain technology can improve the carbon asset transaction system and provide global investors with a seamless and frictionless transaction solution. It can also make trading schemes more efficient through new approaches to price carbon credits and connect distributed carbon markets, besides decentralizing the trade of renewable energies and carbon credits (Thaker et al., 2022). The efficient use of smart contracts in the process will also facilitate automation, high-speed execution, traceability, and transparency in transactions.

# 2. Green finance

Sustainable finance or environmental finance is a financial innovation aiming to connect financial industry to meet the needs of environmental protection and imply financial tools designed for developing, promoting, and supporting positive and durable production activity, which has committed to solve problems like environmental pollution and the greenhouse effect and promote coordinated economic society. Green finance is becoming increasingly sophisticated. As it becomes more complex, it becomes harder to trace the financial flows and ensure investment is reaching its desired destiny. It includes many financial products and services, such as green bonds, green investment funds, carbon market, and energy trading, alongside initiative financial institutions, including green banks and green funds (Taghizadeh-Hesary and Yoshino, 2019).
