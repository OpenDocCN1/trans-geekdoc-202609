## 4.1 Blockchain technology gear to financial innovation

Blockchain technology will revolutionize green finance through digitized monitoring of proceeds in green bonds and loans as well as new technology-enhanced financial products with many real-world applications, particularly in green finance where transparent and traceable information is critical in measuring and monitoring climate impact (Dorfleitner et al., 2021). Blockchain can help a bank or brokerage digitally monitor the proceeds of green investments in a transparent manner, so investors can verify not just the growth but also the environmental impact of their investments. Blockchain security, immutability, and irreproducibility all provide investors greater confidence in the bank or brokerage delivering this information and recommending or managing these green assets.

## 4.2 Bonding with green

An emerging way to raise debt capital for greener projects is through green bonds. Green bonds are fixed income, liquid financial instruments dedicated exclusively to climate change mitigation and adaption projects, and other environment-friendly activities. Over the last 14 years, green bonds have become an important tool to address the impacts of climate change and related challenges (Agliardi and Agliardi, 2019). The growth of green bonds in the capital markets has been explosive and is increasingly attracting attention from investors. Climate change threatens communities and economies, and it poses risks for agriculture, food, and water supplies. A lot of financing is needed to address these challenges. Green bonds are a way to make the connection between environmental projects with capital markets and investors and channel capital toward sustainable development. Investors have been mainly focusing on the risks of climate change to their portfolios and through mechanisms such as the Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (Liu et al., 2021).

Furthermore, stakeholders are pressuring the investment community to employ ESG policies. Green bonds address some of these changes to the new landscape by offering a platform to engage in good practices, influencing the business strategy of bond issuers. In this way, the growth in green bonds and green finance also indirectly works to disincentives high carbon-emitting projects. Green bonds enjoyed a 49% growth rate in the 5 years before 2021; the green bond market annual issuance could exceed the $1 trillion mark by 2023.

## 4.3 The potential of blockchain technology to unlock the mobilization of green finance

The blockchain technology refers to a digitally distributed data structure that records and shares data and information between any two parties (peer-to-peer network) in a verifiable and tamper-proof way that applies globally. It enables the evolution from the traditional centralized to the distributed network, which can be achieved securely without the need for a trusted authority (Mohd and Kaushal, 2018). In response to rising environmental concerns, blockchain technology is transforming green innovation, culminating in green economic practices and well-established business models. Technological advancements have made significant contributions to the development of the economy and the advancement of humanity, such as through blockchain open innovation.

Green finance is instrumental in achieving the objective of SDGs which deliberately enhance green growth and pave the way to increase the level of financial flows (banking, microcredit finance, insurance, and investment activities) from the public, private, and not-for-profit sectors to promote sustainable development priorities. A sustainable financial system is one that creates values and transacts financial assets in ways that shape real wealth to serve the long-term needs of an inclusive, environmentally sustainable economy (Falcone, 2020). Green finance is used for sustainable development projects and initiatives, environmental products and policies under the single goal of promoting a green economic transformation toward low-carbon, sustainable, and inclusive pathways.

## 5. Blockchain innovations in green bond market

Blockchain technologies offer great opportunities for more efficient green bond markets; however, the opportunities offered for green bonds in increasing investor confidence, facilitating wider capital flows, and reducing the cost of reporting are even more significant. The adoption of DLT enables green bonds to reach and create new markets at both ends of the investment pipeline. Banks have traditionally been walled gardens, but blockchain technology initiatives have engaged them in collaborative projects. DLT is a poor fit with traditional centralized models, which means adoption in general markets, still heavily centralized, will be slow, but with the opportunity to reduce costs and improve investor communications, issuers are likely to drive the adoption of plug and play solutions as they emerge (Laut & Fliaster, 2020). Banks have an opportunity to support this by collaborating on cross-industry solutions, which can be supported by the growth of security-specific blockchain platforms being built by financial technology companies. Extending the reach of green bond financing can grasp greater efficiencies and confidence, and new markets and new sources of capital can be created for the SDGs.

### 5.1 Green bonds an opportunity to align finance with ESG goals

Global green bonds issuance accelerated to over $500 billion in 2021, continuing the green market expansion of the last 10 years. Issuance for green bonds is currently predicted to hit $1 trillion between 2022 and 2025. Notwithstanding the significant growth in the green bond market, lack of transparency is one of investors’ main concerns. Utilizing green blockchain technology as a financial instrument will streamline the process of green finance with the utmost trust, transparency, cost reduction, mobilization, and immutable traits. Banks can list tokenized green assets in the primary or secondary market and make them accessible to small/retail investors through a public blockchain exchange or a stock exchange that supports security token trading. There will be no difference in the costs for bonds on the blockchain between a $10 and a $10 million investment, which will open up the green bond market to a broader investor base (Report of HSBC & Green Digital Finance Alliance, 2019). Tokenizing green assets and bonds will engage a wide range of investors in the market and make it possible to involve more individuals interested in sustainable investment.

- **Bonds as a service:** Many platforms provide do-it-yourself (DIY) bonds, which permit the issuers to build their own blockchain-based green bonds at reduced cost. DIY bonds allow investors to build and manage their portfolios without intervention or guidance from a professional financial advisor (HDFC Bank, 2022). The new platforms can offer these bonds via the issuance of security tokens. They help small or medium-sized enterprises or communities to issue green bonds without a bank’s traditional costly services.

- **Fractional asset ownership:** One of the greatest use cases of tokenizing green assets is provision of fractional asset ownership. Green bonds and other assets can be converted into smaller units and owned by the investors. Amalgamating fractional asset ownership with automated reporting, small investors will be able to supervise the real-time progress of their investments.
