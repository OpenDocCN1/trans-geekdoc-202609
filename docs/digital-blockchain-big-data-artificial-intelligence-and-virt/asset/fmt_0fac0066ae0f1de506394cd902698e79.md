
## 3.5 AI-assisted pharma collaborations

Even though industry-academia cooperation is on the rise, certain problems and obstacles still affect the mechanisms of collaboration, as shown in [Fig. 3.2](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_fig_002). A distinct time horizon, confidentiality, and exclusivity; organizational variances may exist, and these can directly affect funding levels, university budgets, academic incentives, and research priorities. Industry is driven by profit-driven solutions to issues and outcomes, while university researchers are motivated by academic prestige and interest. There are certainly cultural differences between university and industrial R&D research, with the former concentrating on applied problem-solving research and the latter more exploratory. Due to divergent values and misunderstandings, it could lead to a serious conflict among academic and industrial partners [[82](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_082), [83](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_083)]. Collaborative and partnership research is impeded by the diversity of perspectives on knowledge production and use, as well as the connection between researcher and practitioner from top-down to bottom-up, sequential to interactive, and multidimensional [[84](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_084), [85](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_085)]. Based on research traditions and/or experiences, researchers and stakeholders may possess distinct fundamental assumptions regarding knowledge and learning [[86](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_086)]. Researchers discovered that social relationships could be weakened by role ambiguity, various positions, and role conflicts. They also identified barriers such as organizational change, cultural differences, and standards on research output, as well as positive effects on actual usage [[87](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_087)]. The development of the collaborative knowledge platforms that serve as the foundation for many of these innovative working practices is also beset by numerous real-world obstacles. Creating a critical mass of contributors, standardizing and ensuring the quality of the data entered, curating the databases that are produced, utilizing the right tools for analysis and visualization, and protecting clinical sample data are a few examples [[88](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_088)].

The absence of accountability and transparency in PPPs is an additional impediment to equitable health care on a global scale. Public inspection is frequently prohibited for partnership agreements with the private sector. The selection of private partners, the establishment of objectives to be achieved, and the development of management policies are all opaque processes. The apparent lack of transparency makes it difficult to ascertain the equity objectives that have been established and the individuals who should be held accountable for their fulfillment. Due to their intricate structures, differing processes of accountability between both the private and public sectors, and complicated governance, private companies can be challenging to hold accountable for failed PPPs [[89](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_089)]. The achievement of the university-industry collaboration is impacted by multiple challenges. Organizational differences present a variety of challenges as well. However, the most frequent problems in university-industry (UI) partnerships are those brought on by organizational differences between universities and industries. Industries and academic institutions have distinct institutional settings and prioritize different things [[90](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_090)]. The organizational structure of a university is more rigid and less adaptable than that of a business. Even though the challenges arise from these structural differences, we still don’t fully understand how various collaborations address these challenges. There is a dearth of understanding about how these organizational differences impact collaboration, particularly when students are involved [[91](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_091), [92](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_092)]. Another issue that affects these collaborations is an elevated level of heterogeneities, usually during the execution stage [[90](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_090), [91](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_091)]. The complexity of collaboration arises from the diverse characters, values, and traditions that come together to work together. Researchers frequently lament that the business isn’t giving them the information they need to carry out their work. Prior studies indicate that there are fewer organizational differences the more closely industry and academia interact. Organizational differences have an impact on the collaboration process since the parties involved in it are accustomed to seeing things from the other partner’s perspective [[92](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_092), [93](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_093), [94](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_094), [95](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_095)].

Partnerships between public/governmental, private/commercial, and civil society organizations can enhance the health of the impoverished by creatively integrating the diverse resources and talents of various organizations. Public agencies benefit from collaborating with the private sector in areas such as product development, manufacturing, marketing, distribution, and production process development, where the public sector is weak. Partnerships seem to be the most suitable option in the following scenarios: The impact of traditional independent methods of addressing a problem is limited. Potential collaborators can reach a consensus on the specific desired objectives. Both sectors possess relevant complementary expertise. The long-term interests of each sector are met, and the contributions of resources and expertise are fairly balanced. It is not advisable to assume that PPPs will fulfill obligations that are located elsewhere. In general, PPPs should be considered social initiatives that aim to identify more effective strategies for addressing complex health issues [[97](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_097)].
