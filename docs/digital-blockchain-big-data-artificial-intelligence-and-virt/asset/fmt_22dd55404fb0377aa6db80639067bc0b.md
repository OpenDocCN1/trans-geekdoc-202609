
# AI Applications in Healthcare: Clinical Decision Support Systems and Machine Learning Techniques

The healthcare sector has experienced a significant rise in the use of AI applications, driven by the swift advancement of AI and other emerging technologies, which possess the capacity to generate new opportunities and eliminate current obstacles through effective data processing and analysis [[26](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_026)]. Today’s healthcare industry is better off avoiding many mistakes and unfortunate incidents due to clinical decision-making technologies and electronic health records (EHR). Examples of these technologies include those for entering pharmaceutical orders, predicting several critical diseases, and more [[27](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_027), [28](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_028)].

Healthcare institutions in wealthy nations are adopting AI with enthusiasm, as evidenced by a review of several real-world applications in the medical field. These facilities are utilizing AI-enabled systems to help medical staff diagnose and treat patients for a range of illnesses, as well as to maximize the effectiveness of nursing and administrative duties related to hospital services.

## Selecting and Evaluating Clinical Decision Support Systems (CDSS)

When choosing a CDSS, there are two primary factors to take into account: the CDSS’s quality and its suitability for bridging the clinical quality gap. A CDSS’s quality must be evaluated at least on two levels: the technological platform level and the data or knowledge level utilized in its construction. Generally accepted quality assurance techniques for developing software used in the field of medicine should be used in the design, implementation, testing, and documentation of CDSSs, as they are software that may also be medical devices [[29](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_029)].

In the instance of CDSS built around models learned through machine learning or statistical analysis, an evaluation of the source data’s quality is required [[30](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_030), [31](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_031)]. Further hazards linked to AI-based CDSSs encompass bias and overfitting to the training data, among other factors. It may lead to undetected poor performance at the local location, as the CDSS cannot extrapolate from the training data. Therefore, considerable attention must be paid to alleviate any possible negative effects of CDSSs. It is crucial to carefully choose the CDSS that aligns with the site’s requirements. CDSSs necessitate rigorous acceptance testing, commissioning, and quality assurance at the local facility, similar to any other medical technology [[32](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_032), [33](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_033)].

## Machine Learning Techniques for CDSS

### Support Vector Machines (SVM)

ML is a discipline that examines the creation and advancement of algorithms capable of learning from and predicting outcomes based on data. These algorithms generate a model from sample inputs, enabling data-driven predictions or judgments rather than relying solely on static program instructions. A technique known as `SVM` constructs a hyperplane to identify outliers and analyze data for regression and classification. The technique utilizes a set of labeled training samples to create the maximum separation between two linearly distinguishable categories. The instances are depicted as points in a spatial framework, and new instances are categorized according to the side of the gap they occupy when projected into the same space. The kernel trick employs the dot product of vectors in a designated space to convert non-linear data into a linear representation. `SVM` is a popular substitute for AI in clinical management decision models. Nonetheless, the limitations of `SVM` encompass its incapacity to provide direct probability estimations and suboptimal performance when the feature count surpasses the sample size [[34](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_034), [35](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_035), [36](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_036), [37](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_037), [38](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_038)].

### Decision Trees and Random Forests

Decision tree learning is a predictive modeling technique that employs statistics, data mining, and machine learning to create a decision tree from item observations, resulting in inferences about the item’s target value. Decision trees are classified into two main types: (1) discrete decision tree models, in which the goal variable assumes only discrete values, and (2) continuous decision trees. Decision-making and execution can be clearly and visually depicted through a decision tree.

Random forests, also known as random decision forests, are ensemble learning methodologies employed for classification, regression, and various other applications. They function by generating many decision trees during the training phase and producing a class that represents the average prediction (for regression) or the most frequent class (for classification) from the individual trees. Random decision forests reduce the tendency of decision trees to overfit their training datasets [[39](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_039), [40](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_040)].

### Deep Learning Neural Networks (DLNN)

A machine learning technique called `Deep Learning Neural Network (DLNN)` uses representation learning to enable systems to automatically find the necessary representations. Deep artificial neural networks and other methods are used for identifying and categorizing features from unprocessed data. `DLNN` may acquire intricate features from data using a hierarchical feature representation by employing several hidden layers. In contrast to networks with fewer layers, `DLNNs` execute input data (features) via a higher volume of mathematical operations, rendering their training more computationally intensive [[41](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_041), [42](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_042)].

## Applications in Medical Imaging

The `ANN`, `CNN`, and `DLNN` models have been thoroughly analyzed as AI-based CDSS engines. Radiology is frequently employed for the diagnosis of medical conditions (e.g., breast cancer through mammography), stratification/classification (e.g., lung diseases), imaging-based prediction (e.g., CT imaging in smokers), imaging outcome prediction (e.g., pulmonary edema on V/Q scans), and multi-omics genotype-phenotype evaluation of disease behavior (e.g., multiple sclerosis). In medical imaging applications of AI, `DLNN` have been especially favored. These applications encompass picture segmentation, expedited image capture, lesion detection, and imaging-based clinical decision support [[43](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_043), [44](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_044), [45](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_045)].
