## 4.2.1 Big Data in Smart Pharma

Digitalization of healthcare data has aided in the expansion and advancement of big data. Due to the data's explosive growth in the last several years, a new field known as "big data" was established. Health care is becoming more mobile and intelligent thanks to big data analysis and other smart healthcare solutions that also reduce costs and increase efficiency. Chemists can use statistical analysis to predict pharmacologic outcomes and recognize the patients who might benefit most from pharmaceutical treatments, which can aid them in better understanding the risks and side effects of pharmaceuticals [[21]](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_021), [[22]](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_022), [[23]](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_023). Big data has emerged as a popular research topic in recent years due to its possible uses in health and medicine. It has demonstrated promise in the treatment of conditions like Alzheimer's disease by utilizing big data-driven systems for clinical decision-making. Electronic medical record optimization has also been achieved through the application of big data techniques. Toxicology, pharmaceuticals, and pharmacology have produced data regarding chemical properties, pharmacokinetics, clinical behaviors, and drug toxicity. High-throughput screening (`HTS`) and next-generation sequencing (`NGS`) have led to a significant increase in the amount of data [[24]](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_024), [[25]](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_025).

By reducing the need for in-person visits, enhancing patient outcomes, and guaranteeing that clinicians have immediate access to all patient medical records, big data analysis will enable digital health care. Through digital health and aggregated real-time data, big data analysis, which offers more chances for proactive intervention and a more comprehensive picture of patients' circumstances, will become a crucial tool for healthcare providers [[26]](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_026), [[27]](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_027). The present epoch is defined by the incessant proliferation of data streams, attributable to the extensive use of electronic medical records, the vast quantities of data relayed by various sensors, and the information patients disseminate on social media platforms. The healthcare sector consistently produces significant volumes of data, including genetic information, medical records, imaging data, and health-related behaviors. Effective data utilization by healthcare organizations can aid in tracking illnesses, medical decision-making, and public health management [[28]](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_028). To solve the problem, electronic health records, or `EHRs`, are digital collections of electronic patient health data. The use of `EHRs` is growing in developing nations. These records can be shared between various healthcare settings via a robust network connection. Since digital health records can be shared, revised, and altered across various healthcare systems, there is less chance of missing any important paperwork, which contributes to the reduction of duplicate data. `EHR` initiatives provide instant benefits to hospital management, physicians, and patients [[29]](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_029).

## 4.2.2 Blockchain in Smart Pharma

Pharmaceutical development and research is a multi-year, complex process that includes drug discovery, drug development, and regulatory approval. Blockchain is a decentralized computer network that provides its members with access to a secure ledger of transactions and operates without a central server. By timestamping each transaction made within the network, it eliminates the need for an outside organization. Every network node maintains its local ledger and maintains a local copy of the record. Modest changes made to the localized ledger are reflected across the network. Beyond just cryptocurrencies, blockchain technology enables the creation and monitoring of any sort of digital asset. Within the pharmaceutical supply chain, a product created by a manufacturer and registered on the network can be transferred to other participants like cryptocurrency, as it is considered a digital asset. Once again, blockchain ranks first for privacy protection. Each member of a blockchain network is given a key pair that serves as their identity [[30]](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_030). The blockchain approach maintains patient privacy and data security while offering a special benefit for patient recruitment. Patient recruitment times are shortened by using blockchain distributed ledgers because anonymously registered patients can be linked to various clinical trial sites. As a result, the researcher can select the necessary subjects from a group of patients by the study plan. Proposals have also been made for a blockchain structure that involves multiple contracts for clinical trials to manage the studies and involve patients [[31]](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_031). Also, the clinical trials are tracked using the blockchain technique, which encourages others to participate in clinical research while also instilling confidence in the outcomes. Because it can result in fewer clinical trials where positive results are covered selectively, transparency is an essential part of drug discovery research. Consequently, applying blockchain technology to clinical trials encourages improved relationships between sponsors and different clinical research sites in addition to enhancing data integrity and transparency [[32]](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_032).
