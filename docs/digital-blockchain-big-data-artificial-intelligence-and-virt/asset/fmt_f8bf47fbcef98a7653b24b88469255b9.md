
# 6.3 AI-powered target determination and validation for clinical trials and drug development

The substantial increase in the utilization of in-silico methodologies, including chemoinformatics, molecular simulation, and AI, over recent decades is rational, given the advancements in computing technology and the exponential proliferation of structural, chemical, and biological data related to a widening spectrum of therapeutic targets.

Molecular docking is a preferred and effective structure-based in-silico technique for forecasting interactions between molecules and biological targets [[49](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_049), [50](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_050)]. The technique typically begins with predicting the molecular orientation of the ligand within the receptor, followed by assessing their complementarity by a scoring function. A ligand is positioned within a protein’s active region with matching algorithms that use chemical and shape information. Pharmacophores are used to illustrate the ligand and the protein. The distance matrix that links the pharmacophore, as well as the corresponding ligand atoms, governs new ligand conformations. For each match, the pharmacophore’s distance within the ligand as well as the protein is computed. Programs like `DOCK` [[54](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_054)], `FLOG` [[55](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_055)], `LibDock` [[56](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_056)], and `SANDOCK` [[57](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_057)] offer algorithms for matching ligand docking. A more recent approach that integrates multiple scores to evaluate the docking conformation is consensus scoring [[58](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_058)]. A ligand or potential binder may be approved if it exhibits good performance across multiple scoring systems. Consensus scoring usually produces notable gains in bound conformation, pose prediction, and virtual screening enrichments [[59](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_059)]. Nonetheless, the prediction of binding energies may still be off. Receptor flexibility continues to be a significant barrier in docking studies, especially when it comes to the movement of several crucial extra components of the receptor that are involved in ligand and catalyst binding in addition to the flexibility of the core structure. Certain techniques to address side chain flexibility are suitable and successful in specific circumstances. A popular solution for global flexibility, which makes sense from the standpoint of conformer selection, is a collection of proteins. Protein structures capable of accommodating the ligand should be included in the ensembles, as docking requires an efficient approach for acquiring and selecting reliable protein structures. Furthermore, the method’s computing expense is an additional disadvantage. The scoring function is a critical component for enhancing those merits of docking. Effective examples demonstrate the utilization of computational methods to engineer novel small compounds and evaluate results from an extensive database. However, experimental technology is still needed to achieve realistic interactions between small molecules and receptors. Docking applications may be enhanced by precise and inexpensively computed scoring functions [[60](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_060)].

Creating new medications is a costly and time-consuming process. The pharmaceutical industry faces challenges in maintaining a satisfactory return on investment due to exponential cost growth. Precise forecasting of clinical trial results can streamline pharmaceutical companies’ pipelines by cutting down on failure costs. It can also help representatives of investment banks and hedge funds make better decisions when managing investment portfolios [[61](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_061), [62](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_062), [63](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_063), [64](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_064)]. Early clinical trial outcome prediction may enhance the efficacy of pharmaceutical R&D, facilitate the creation of effective funding sources, and lead to the creation of innovative financial instruments for biotechnology research financing. Deep learning methods are quickly spreading into the field of biomedicine since they have begun to outperform humans in various tasks, such as image recognition. `DL` is widely applied in drug discovery and repurposing [[65](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_065), [66](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_066), [67](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_067)]. Pharmaceutical companies employ `ML` extensively in clinical trial operations; however, no validated reports have been published regarding the utilization of `ML/AI` predicted chances for technical as well as regulatory success methods throughout pharma R&D processes [[68](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_068)]. Although there are numerous methods and resources available for predicting clinical trial outcomes, the main techniques used in these methods were readouts from previous phases of clinical trials or trial design [[69](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_069), [70](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_070), [71](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_071)]. The goal was to integrate all scoring strategies, biological backgrounds, and multimodal data sources with the most recent deep-learning models.
