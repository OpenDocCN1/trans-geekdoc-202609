
## 2.6 Conclusion

Healthcare relies on the pharmaceutical industry, which researches, develops, manufactures, and markets drugs, biological compounds, and medical devices. Traditional manufacturing methods have reached their limits, and AI can help eliminate prescription errors and improve care. Blockchain technology, particularly Bitcoin, has gained interest in the pharmaceutical sector due to its decentralization, transparency, trust, confidentiality, and stability. It has been utilized to avoid counterfeit drugs, track drug origins, improve drug transparency and quality control, and aid the pharmaceutical supply chain. Blockchain allows safe transactions as well as privacy protection, decreasing patient recruitment times and enhancing the precision of data and transparency. Platforms like `LifeCrypter`, `GCoin`, `Hyperledger`, and smart contracts have been developed to improve security and credibility in the pharmaceutical supply chain. Blockchain technology is utilized to create, exchange, and safeguard EHR data, ensuring the validity and accountability of healthcare institutions. AI has a significant potential to transform the pharmaceutical sector, especially in drug discovery and regulatory approval processes. The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the European Commission’s “RoboLaw Project” have provided technical standards and guidelines for AI algorithms. However, blockchain technology faces challenges such as data management, accessibility, redundancy, privacy, and integration with existing healthcare systems.

## Challenges of Blockchain Integration in Healthcare

Researchers assert that the integration of blockchain technology with the existing healthcare system presents significant challenges. Since numerous hospitals still use legacy systems, careful planning is required to guarantee backward compatibility and a smooth transition. Furthermore, it is imperative to tackle the energy consumption issues associated with blockchain technology, specifically with proof-of-work consensus. Blockchain solutions that are energy-efficient and environmentally friendly should be taken into consideration by healthcare organizations [[73](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_073), [74](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_074), [75](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_075), [76](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_076), [77](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_077)]. Also, managing storage capacity is another challenge. Blockchain, engineered to document and manage transactional data, possesses a constrained scope, hence necessitating minimal storage capacity. As technology evolved and penetrated the healthcare sector, storage challenges emerged. Daily, substantial data from the healthcare sector need processing. In a blockchain context, all data, such as patient records, medical histories, test results, MRI scans, X-rays, and other images, will be available to every node in the network, requiring substantial storage capacity.

As blockchain technology advances, it encounters not only the previously noted technological issues but also social obstacles, including cultural transformations. Embracing and implementing a technology that is entirely disconnected from conventional work methodologies is invariably challenging. The medical profession is progressively transitioning to digitalization; however, substantial advancements are required before it completely adopts technology, particularly blockchain, which now lacks clinical confirmation. To get doctors to rely on technology rather than paperwork, it will take time and work. Because the healthcare industry has been slow to adopt new technology and policies, the policies offered are not widely trusted [[78](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_078), [79](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_079), [80](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_080), [81](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_081)].

Blockchain technology remains in its early stages of development and refinement. The technology will initially cost institutions more and will need to be embraced and integrated over time. It’s also necessary to address issues with non-standardization, scalability, information decay risk, and integration with current legacy systems. A related issue is blockchain transaction verification time. Technical implementation challenges include unproven systems, hidden operating costs, and the need for a network of connected computers (nodes) to process transactions and create blocks [[82](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_082), [83](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_083)]. No incentives exist to use blockchain technology to build a vast network of interconnected information “blocks” to replace national record-keeping. Blockchain could improve the current system but not replace it. The majority of medical data would not be stored on the blockchain. To describe a particular patient or procedure, a small amount of information would be retained in each “block” [[84](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_084)].

In some scenarios, the growing volume of daily transactions causes the blockchain to get heavy, which causes scalability problems. It makes developing blockchain-based real-time healthcare applications difficult because they cannot process millions of medical records. Furthermore, because of the limited block capacity at the moment, miners may choose to verify transactions with higher transaction rewards before completing smaller ones. On the other hand, large-size blocks decrease transaction speed. Blockchain networks necessitate security as users generate addresses rather than utilizing their actual identities. Due to the visibility of each private key’s balance and activities to all network participants, blockchain-based solutions are unable to provide transaction confidentiality. In the medical field, safeguarding sensitive patient data is essential, which makes the issue crucial [[85](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_085), 86].

As a result, blockchain technology is still developing and addressing social issues like a culture shift. Technology that differs from traditional work methods is hard to accept. Blockchain technology is gradually making its way into the pharmaceutical industry, but full adoption of the technology takes time. Cultural resistance may occur because most patients dislike having their data shared. To use blockchain in the framework, some people must change their behavior to accept distributed data sharing [87].
