
# 8.5 Regulatory aspects of AI in pharmacovigilance and drug safety

An agreement between the public and private sectors that is cooperative is known as a “public-private partnership,” or PPP. In this arrangement, the two sectors work together to accomplish a goal that is compatible or mutually beneficial by sharing resources, risks, and duties. The premise of public-private partnerships (PPPs) is founded on the conviction that, from the bench to the bedside, neither the public nor the private sectors can successfully monitor and sustain innovation across an extended period on their own [[62](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_062)]. A research and development procedure created exclusively for the public sector could be excessively sluggish and ineffectual. The ability of the public and private sectors to work together and share resources, including financial resources, knowledge, data, samples, medical knowledge, patient base, facilities, risks, and rewards, is one of the main benefits of a public-private partnership (PPP). More importantly, they create wider networks of experts with diverse viewpoints. In addition to making the innovation process more efficient, cost-effective, and time-bound, a public-private partnership (PPP) can also help to facilitate and improve the overall fluidity and holistic nature of the research and growth process [[63](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_063)]. The private sector routinely uses medications at a substantially higher frequency than the public sector does. Adverse drug reactions, or ADRs, are one of the leading causes of death and are predicted to become a major public health concern in the not-too-distant future [[64](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_064), [65](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_065)]. In both urban and rural areas, private physicians are the only providers of healthcare, according to the findings of the National Sample Survey Office (NSSO). Furthermore, due to the private pharmaceutical industry’s explosive growth, the private sector urgently needs handholding support to overcome obstacles like the underreporting of ADRs, the creation of a national patient safety database, as well as the pursuit of mutual benefits like recognition and avoiding regulatory action [[66](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_066), [67](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_067), [68](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_068)]. Pharmacovigilance requires the involvement of healthcare professionals to be successful. Consequently, a strong system and tools for the tracking, reporting, and assessment of ADRs have been established as a result of the collaboration between the PvPI and academic medical institutions, public and private hospitals [[69](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_069), [70](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_070)]. Consequently, the PvPI tackles the problems by informing and arguing with the hospital administration and the medical staff that reporting ADRs is entirely voluntary and does not result in disciplinary action. Reports obtained from the public sector even have higher quality completion scores than those from the private sector. The private sector’s reports have a low overall rating because they withhold information about alternative dispute resolutions out of fear of lawsuits and a desire to maintain confidentiality [[71](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_071)].

Historically, the primary goal of NRAs has been to collaborate with healthcare providers and the industry to report passive ADRs through hard copy and online reporting channels. NRAs are essential regulators in the field of pharmacovigilance. Pharmacovigilance has looked into the possibilities of using artificial intelligence and keeping electronic health records over the last few years [[72](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_072)]. More people are coming to understand and accept the idea that patients and caregivers should be involved in the process of creating and regulating health products. Since they will ultimately be using the products in the healthcare industry, patients should be educated, engaged, and treated as participants in their care. They also possess the greatest depth of knowledge regarding their perspectives, experiences, and insights. Therefore, the viewpoints of patients and their life experiences can help to improve a health product at different stages of its life cycle [[73](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_073)]. Regulatory approvals, post-approval monitoring, clinical trial designs, research priorities, and technology evaluations are a few of these stages. The involvement of patients in pharmaceutical surveillance is crucial because it provides more context than the reports that healthcare providers present. One illustration of this would be the simple fact that patient reports usually include a more thorough description, especially when it comes to the impact on daily life, which may not be fully understood by medical professionals. It enhances patient participation in their care, adds to the reports provided by healthcare providers, and raises consciousness about ADRs [[74](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_074), [75](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_075)].

In the digital age, patients can now more easily report ADRs due to online reporting via the Internet and smartphones [[76](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_076)]. In the aforementioned French study, which examined the variables influencing patient participation in pharmacovigilance observations and the development of patient involvement, it was found that the availability of digital solutions affected the rise in patient reporting of adverse drug reactions [[77](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_077)]. In the field of drug safety communication, which is a vital part of pharmacovigilance, a further significant collaborative partnership among patients is essential. A study that examined the pharmacovigilance environments in eight ASEAN nations discovered that a common problem is communication [[78](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_078)]. This is even though open and honest communication about the advantages, drawbacks, and efficacy of medications increases the likelihood of safe medication use among patients. For the sake of the general public’s health, information regarding drug safety must be made accessible, easily accessed, as well as successfully communicated.
