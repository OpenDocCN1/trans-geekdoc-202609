
# 7.5 Future trends of AI in the pharmaceutical industry

The implementation of AI technologies in healthcare increases the likelihood of the system becoming autonomous, hence jeopardizing human autonomy. Artificial intelligence in healthcare possesses the capacity to enable machines to make decisions. Medical decision-making and AI-driven healthcare systems must remain entirely under human oversight. AI technology must not, under any circumstances, compromise patient autonomy. The patient may be uncertain about which authority to trust regarding clinical judgments made by the AI system and the physician due to these discrepancies. The patient must be presented with both alternatives in such situations. All research initiatives and evaluation programs must adhere to a consent protocol before the deployment of any AI technology within the healthcare sector. Patients must be fully acquainted with the advantages of using AI technologies as well as the risks – social, psychological, and physical – that come with using them. The ability to accept or reject AI technologies must be fully granted to patients. At every stage of the development and application of AI, moral considerations and human values should be effectively and transparently monitored [[98](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_098)]. The most ideal feature for any prognostic or diagnostic tool used in AI healthcare is trustworthiness. Similar to how they must develop confidence in other tools they use, clinicians must also develop confidence in AI technologies. Clinicians and healthcare providers want a straightforward and reliable method to evaluate the validity and reliability of AI technologies for optimal use. A reliable AI-driven system must deliver precise health data analysis while also being legitimate, ethical, dependable, and authentic. AI-driven technology must ensure data security and privacy during the complete development and implementation phases. Securing the confidence of all stakeholders, particularly medical receivers, in the safe and secure use of their data is essential for the effective and extensive deployment of AI [[99](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_099)]. The primary aim of data privacy is to avert unauthorized access, alteration, and destruction of personal information. The use of personal data by artificial intelligence must not excessively limit individuals’ perceived or actual freedom. These procedures are essential in the healthcare business, as medical information contains sensitive data that could be exploited against patients or result in prejudice, even inadvertently. Data about specific patients should ideally be anonymized unless clinical or research needs require that it be kept in an identifiable format. The duty of an individual or organization to take responsibility for their actions, account for their activities, and transparently disclose the outcomes is known as accountability. AI systems that are meant to be used in the healthcare industry need to be prepared to be examined at any time by relevant authorities. For AI technologies to operate at their best, frequent internal and external audits are required. These audit reports have to be released to the general public. The data used to train and test the system is what essentially determines the outcome of artificial intelligence, as it is a data-driven technology. It is particularly important when it comes to AI for health, as biased and skewed datasets can cause issues with bias and discrimination, among other issues. Artificial intelligence for health and other data-driven technologies are thought to be most vulnerable to data bias [[100](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_100)]. It is imperative to implement measures to guarantee that the “training data” is free from established biases and accurately represents a substantial segment of the intended audience.

Artificial intelligence will usher in a transformational era for the pharmaceutical industry. Given the potential side effects and toxicity, AI algorithms are anticipated to be crucial in tailored dosage formulation and safety assessments, refining pharmaceutical compositions, and improving treatment outcomes. Wearable technologies, sensors, and artificial intelligence-enabled monitoring devices will enhance remote patient care and optimize medication adherence via tailored treatment recommendations. Artificial intelligence impacts patient selection, recruitment, and the design of clinical studies. Genetic profiles and electronic health records are employed to find qualifying patients, reduce trial costs, and accelerate approval procedures. Furthermore, it is anticipated that AI’s continuous surveillance and oversight capabilities will optimize pharmaceutical manufacturing processes, guaranteeing efficiency and uniformity [[101](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_101)]. As stated in certain current conceptualizations, strategic decisions are not limited to plant design or global supply chain management but rather affect all functional areas and levels of the organization. In a similar vein, decisions made in the short and medium term now span far larger domains and are not limited to production scheduling and planning tasks. Furthermore, to better meet the demands along with the dynamics of the real world, it is now imperative to integrate the various decision levels [[102](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_102)].

Therefore, a company’s current decision structure is far more intricate and wide-ranging, encompassing various time scales as well as enterprise functions and activities, impact and scope variations, physical network variations, and finally, multiple technological settings. In the future, the patient will be the primary influencer in determining the pertinent operational performance metrics as well as the specifications for the product or service [[103](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_103)]. To improve the client experience from initial discovery to therapy delivery, companies must integrate the patient’s perspective into their decision-making processes. Decision-support models will proficiently tackle challenges such as a broader range of products and volumes, patient-focused distribution networks, improved service level precision, and real-time evaluation of the whole customer experience via closed-loop information systems [[104](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_104), [105](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_105)]. All of the company’s technological assets are housed in the technological layer. The organization must make use of all of those capabilities to optimize the value of all assistances that can be provided with decision models that have been developed. Therefore, whenever possible, economical and/or environmentally friendly alternatives should be considered when making decisions about the most pertinent future features and particular technology selections. At the manufacturing level, production expenses, lead times, and inventory levels will inevitably diminish as more economical technologies, such as continuous manufacturing, are adopted and advanced decision-support tools, based on intricate optimization and simulation methods, are developed. Enhancements in resource and energy efficiency, along with increased flexibility and responsiveness to market variations, are anticipated to result in elevated capacity utilization [[106](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_106), [107](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_107)].
