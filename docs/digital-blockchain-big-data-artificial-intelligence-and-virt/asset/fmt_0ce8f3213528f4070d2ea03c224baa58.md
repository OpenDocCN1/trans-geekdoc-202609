
# Effectively encrypting data prevents unauthorized access to confidential information

Effectively encrypting data prevents unauthorized access to confidential information. The ownership of data is safeguarded across the whole data lifecycle, encompassing the data center, endpoints (including mobile devices of administrators, clinicians, and physicians), and the cloud. Encryption safeguards against security breaches such as packet sniffing and theft of storage devices [[34](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_034)]. Efficient methods with minimal time and cost constraints should be employed, as the encryption of medical images is an expanding application of cryptographic systems. To convert an input image into a cipher image, an encryption method, either symmetric or asymmetric, must be employed. A variety of techniques and environments can be employed to encrypt medical images. Medical images can be encrypted by several techniques, including bitwise XOR diffusion, chaotic approaches, edge mapping, high-speed scrambling, and others [[35](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_035)]. Furthermore, most of the time, encryption methods stop encrypted data from being processed, so data is processed in its original format. On the other hand, after computing encrypted data, homomorphic encryption allows the user to obtain encrypted results. Homomorphic encryption not only makes it possible to process encrypted data but also safeguards privacy throughout [[36](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_036)].

To provide more effective healthcare services and care, it is now feasible to quickly access data due to the digital age of healthcare. Electronic health record (`EHR`) systems are currently used by a large number of medical professionals. Most medical practices have shifted to using third-party clouds for the storage of patient data in centralized data warehouses, which are insecure and unreliable. Concerns regarding information preservation, access security, and privacy emerge when `EHR` data is stored on a potentially unreliable third-party cloud. Given that healthcare organizations routinely manage sensitive data, encryption is a key source of security and reliability. Numerous apprehensions have been raised regarding cloud storage’s ability to provide scalable, regulated, and adaptable access [[37](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_037)]. Researchers authored a study article addressing security and privacy concerns associated with cloud-based personal health record systems. The research analyzed various attribute-based encryption (`ABE`) methods applicable to cloud systems for the provision of fine-grained, scalable, and flexible access control. Contemporary cryptographic techniques, such as `ABE`, are included in the `PHR` system to mitigate security and privacy concerns related to cloud-based `PHRs` [[38](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_038)].

## 9.3.3 Data masking

The original value cannot be obtained from the masked value, as masking does not constitute a genuine encryption method. It substitutes sensitive data items with indistinguishable values. It utilizes a method for de-identifying or obscuring data sets, suppressing or generalizing quasi-identifiers such as zip codes and dates of birth, and concealing personal identifiers including names and social security numbers. Data masking is hence one of the most prevalent techniques for real-time data anonymization. Initially, certain researchers introduced the k-anonymity approach, which safeguards against attribute disclosure but fails to protect identity revelation [[39](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_039), [40](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_040)]. The target application is deemed indifferent to the masked data in numerous prevailing data masking strategies. It may pertain to identifying information such as social security numbers (`SSNs`), but depending on the intended application, especially in the healthcare context, it may also encompass other sensitive information such as age. In such instances, the data is often encrypted prior to transmission to prevent unauthorized access. Nonetheless, encryption methods impair performance and often lead to considerable cost concerning reaction time and storage capacity. Moreover, they do not prevent the exploitation of information post-decryption by reputable external `AI/ML` researchers; hence, when such material is disseminated, legal agreements are often incorporated to prohibit the disclosure or inference of private data [[41](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_041), [42](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_042), [43](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_043)].

## 9.3.4 Access control

An access control policy, typically founded on the privileges and rights conferred to each practitioner authorized by the patient or a reliable third party, will continue to regulate users’ access to the information system. Consequently, the policy evolves into an efficient and flexible instrument for bestowing permissions upon users. It offers advanced authorization controls to guarantee that users can only execute tasks for which they are permitted, including data access, job submission, cluster administration, and other responsibilities. Security and access control concerns have been mitigated through several methods, with Role-Based Access Control (`RBAC`) and Attribute-Based Access Control (`ABAC`) being the most used models for `EHRs` [[44](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_044), [45](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_045), [46](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_046)]. Furthermore, blockchain technology is a viable solution for enhancing healthcare privacy due to its inherent advantages. Data is dispersed across several network nodes owing to the ledger’s distributed and decentralized characteristics. As a result, the probability of a singular point of failure or assault target diminishes. Secondly, blockchain technology can facilitate the creation of smart contracts that autonomously disseminate data while complying with privacy regulations. Blockchain technology has the potential to enhance patient privacy; nevertheless, some issues remain to be addressed [[47](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_047)]. A primary issue is the incompatibility among various blockchain platforms, potentially resulting in healthcare providers utilizing fragmented and non-integrated systems. Blockchain technology integrates many privacy-enhancing technologies to bolster the security of sensitive patient information. Encryption enables the secure transfer and storage of healthcare data, restricting access to authorized individuals who possess the necessary decryption keys. Patients can determine who accesses their data using consent management in smart contracts, thereby ensuring that healthcare data governance honors patient preferences [[48](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_048), [49](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_049)].
