
## 5.5 Regulatory compliance in AI-based manufacturing

The secret to success is ingraining readily available technologies like AI, blockchain, and other technologies into a company’s core competencies [[92](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_092)]. To enhance clinical research and attain patient-centricity, the industry must employ technology to create user- and customer-centric interfaces, make decisions based on data for innovative data processing methods, and enhance outcomes [[93](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_093), [94](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_094)]. AI could help, for instance, with patient identification and prioritization for medication development and monitoring, which is necessary for standardized drug production and expedited schedules [[92](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_092)]. AI and numerical drug design techniques were used to analyze clinical trial data to determine dosage, evaluate the efficacy of medication formulations, and repurpose pharmaceuticals that were already on the market. By incorporating secure and automated data analysis, blockchain facilitates the creation and maintenance of ledgers, or blocks of content, in systems design. Secure recording and analysis of all health-related data allow for prompt updates to be provided to payers, medical professionals, and doctors. Nevertheless, the blockchain would be exceedingly inefficient for storing a substantial volume of records, such as comprehensive electronic medical records or genetic data records, due to the substantial computational resources that would be necessary. It is a substantial defect in blockchain technology, as it complicates the process of querying data within a blockchain [[95, 96](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_095)].

However, there are ways to work around the issue with the blockchain and AI algorithms integrated. Risk assessment must be the initial phase in protecting sensitive personal information and PHI within the life sciences and healthcare sectors. Implementing the Cyber Security Framework (CSF) from the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) is advised for establishing a cybersecurity program. The NIST Cyber Security Framework encompasses standards, recommendations, and best practices for managing cyber security in critical infrastructure. But cloud- and AI-based products, services, and technologies are employed to construct business processes utilizing frameworks to develop security initiatives that will enable those employed in the sector of the economy to be ready to defend the sensitive data and able to respond promptly to any incidents or security threats [[97](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_097), [98](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_098), [99](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_099)]. To create a comprehensive security program from process and policy viewpoints, researchers began with the NIST Cyber Security Framework (NIST CSF). They then used technology from SAP Cloud, specifically `SAP S4/HANA` and its AI-based cloud services, to make up for it. A source of veracity and a record system would be the `SAP S4/HANA`. Digitization and digital transformation of the life science industries are more important than ever. `SAP S4/Cloud` offerings provide all required products and services along with control features like private cloud services, enabling life science companies to access state-of-the-art technologies and solutions that leverage cloud and AI services. They are still in charge of their private information, and it helps them comply with global privacy and data laws. With the public, private, and hybrid models that `SAP S4/HANA` cloud offering offers, users can select the one that best suits their needs and yields the highest return on investment (ROI) while maintaining all the controls required to protect their company’s objectives, safeguard sensitive data, and carry on innovating and producing new medications through clinical research and R&D [[100](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_100), [101](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_101), [102](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_102)]. Blockchain technology and AI powered by the IoT will improve biomedical and healthcare data ledgers, accelerating biological and clinical research. These assessments are predicated on fundamental aspects of blockchain and AI, including improved security, resilience, decentralized administration, unchangeable audit trails, and above all the reinstatement of characters’ rights. Innovative and state-of-the-art techniques that will improve medical data handling, collaboration, processing, assessment, and result-based classification are being developed using blockchain and AI. The healthcare industry, which is driven by the IoT, is being redefined by blockchain technology. It is achieved through enhanced productivity, access control, advances in technology, safeguarding privacy, and operational data process safety [[103](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_103)].

IoT solutions’ security and privacy are rapidly approaching but largely ignored. The research on privacy and security measures in the IoT is reviewed by the study’s authors, who also look at common misconceptions about technologies that claim to be incredibly secure. Researchers discuss user authentication methods and safe key management for fog computing-based IoT environments. The `SAKA-FC` security system comprises a dual-component framework featuring three-factor key management and a user authentication mechanism [[104](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_104)]. In another study, researchers [[105](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_105)] examined the safety of implanted medical devices (IMDs), including pacemakers and insulin pumps. The study’s authors propose a three-factor lightweight remote user authorization approach for implantable medical devices, utilizing elliptic curve cryptography. The security scheme illustrates how the recommended scheme should be implemented using the Dolev-Yao threat structure. It is a system model that is linked to the user (U), the Controller Node (CN), and the IMD. The security of remote monitoring is improved by employing a three-factor verification strategy. To guarantee secure communication, it necessitates the establishment of bilateral keys between CN and IMD, as well as mutual authentication between U and CN through the use of a session key. Other researchers have suggested a lightweight data-sharing plan with traceability, or `LiST`, for mobile health solutions. The system in the model is comprised of healthcare professionals (the data user), the public cloud, WBSN (the data owner), and the Key Generation Center (KGC), which is considered a completely dependable organization that generates public characteristics for the entire system and provides secret keys to data users. The authors assert that `LiST` provides lightweight user revocation, lightweight test algorithms, lightweight encryption, lightweight backdoor generation using keywords, and lightweight traitor tracing [[106](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_106)]. The scheme implements a lightweight revocation mechanism to eradicate unauthorized users and their decryption privileges to safeguard the privacy of the EHR.
