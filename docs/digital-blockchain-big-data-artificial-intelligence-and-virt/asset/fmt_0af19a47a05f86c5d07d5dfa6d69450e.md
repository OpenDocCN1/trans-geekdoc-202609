
Researchers have proposed utilizing ANNs’ ability to reveal concealed information and values to enhance care efficiency and support the healthcare sector in adopting “lean thinking” or value-based decision-making [[70](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_070)]. An example of how ANNs aid in the incorporation of Lean thinking in healthcare is through the modeling of “information flow” among cancer patients, demonstrating the relationship between quality of life and clinical outcomes [[71](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_071), [72](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_072)]. Support vector machines are considered advanced methodologies for addressing issues that conventional statistical analysis cannot answer. They are utilized for modeling high-dimensional data. Despite their analytical capabilities, broad implementation remains challenging, mostly due to methodological complexities and scalability concerns [[73](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_073)]. A systematic examination of deep learning models employing data from electronic health records identified issues associated with information inconsistency and timeliness, including concealed correlations among clinical features arising from both short-term and long-term events. The models’ performance may decline if these difficulties are not sufficiently addressed [[74](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_074)]. Research demonstrates that ANNs are extensively utilized in business applications, especially in decision assistance, stock price prediction, and financial distress and bankruptcy matters. Furthermore, hybrid networks have been developed to improve the efficacy of traditional models in commercial environments. The integration of ANNs with secondary AI and meta-heuristic approaches, including fuzzy logic, genetic algorithms, bee colony optimization, and artificial immune systems, has been suggested to address challenges related to ANNs, such as network structure selection, initial weight assignment, and control parameter optimization. Hybrid intelligent systems are employed in various fields, including natural language processing, robotics, medical evaluation, and manufacturing process oversight [[75](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_075)].

The efficacy of AI-CDSS deployment is influenced not only by technological progress but also by human issues; including explainability, privacy, and equity, together with the social and organizational dimensions of medical practice [[76](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_076), [77](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_077), [78](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_078), [79](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_079), [80](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_080)]. For example, because end-user clinicians in the following case do not trust or comprehend AI-CDSS’s capabilities, they may be reluctant to adopt or use it. One reason could be that many AI-CDSS operate like “black boxes,” meaning that clinicians are unable to understand how they come up with the recommendations. These problems might result in low system uptake and user acceptance [[81](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_081), [82](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_082)]. Researchers involved in the project assert that the success of a cooperative alliance between human decision-makers and AI-integrated systems heavily relies on the trust that users have in these systems. It indicates several variables influencing confidence in CDS systems [[83](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_083)]. For instance, the two sources from which the participants sought validation of medication recommendations were the guidelines published by professional societies and the articles published in reputable clinical journals. Although guidelines are regarded as more reliable recommendations, physicians are aware that guidelines change more slowly than findings from publications. Fundamentally, faith is placed in the underlying processes of clinical research, particularly randomized clinical trials [[84](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_084)]. The randomized clinical trial method has long been subject to criticism due to its purportedly tenuous connection to the actual medical experience and is not without controversy [[85](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_085)]. It has been demonstrated that participants in clinical trials tend to be younger, sicker, and more male than those who would use the experimental treatment or medication in question.

Validating and putting AI to use in medicine are still major tasks that need to be completed. AI technology currently requires significant innovation in governance and regulatory oversight before it can be widely used in a safe and morally responsible manner. It is imperative that AI be validated in clinical settings and that algorithms be published in peer-reviewed journals [[86](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_086)]. Perhaps in the future, AI should collaborate with scientists and medical professionals to give patients the best care possible rather than trying to take the place of specialists. AI is unlikely to handle all aspects of patient admission, diagnosis, and treatment selection. Therefore, it is imperative to attend to a patient’s spiritual and social needs as well. It is doubtful that machines will be able to provide these kinds of care [[87](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_087)]. For any algorithm to be validated in a clinical setting, some training is necessary. Patient data must therefore be exchanged between healthcare facilities and occasionally across international borders [[88](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_088)].
