
# 5.4 Data management and security

All validation procedures that confirm the outcomes of AI-based applications created specifically for each patient or machine are included in case-specific quality assurance (QA). The output can be used in subsequent stages of the RT workflow once the QA results are deemed satisfactory. If the quality check’s case-specific assurance is unsuccessful, the output will go through verification. The effectiveness of certain applications may be assessed using a separate secondary algorithm [[43](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_043)]. Additionally, recent research has been done on auto-segmentation quality assurance using ML. Using distinct features for every ROI, decision trees can be created to classify the morphology of a particular auto-segmentation input [[44](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_044)].

Research and development in the fields of DL and ML have significantly increased in recent years. Researchers have developed increasingly complex algorithms and models owing to the enhanced availability of data and computational capacity, which has markedly progressed in various areas. One area of research that has advanced significantly is natural language processing (NLP) [[45](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_045), [46](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_046), [47](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_047)]. Researchers have used DL models such as `transformers` and `recurrent neural networks (RNNs)` to construct more accurate and efficient language models. Improved human-machine connection, simpler process automation for customer service, and more sophisticated search engines and automated assistants could all result from these models.

Another area that has made significant strides is computer vision [[48](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_048), [49](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_049), [50](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_050), [51](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_051)]. More accurate image classification and object recognition have been made possible by `convolutional neural networks (CNNs)`. These models have the potential to improve autonomous driving, manufacturing quality control, and medical imaging analysis [[52](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_052), [53](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_053), [54](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_054), [55](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_055), [56](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_056), [57](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_057), [58](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_058)].

DL approaches may autonomously identify and generate abstract features, while conventional ML methods generally necessitate domain expertise for feature construction or engineering [[59](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_059)]. In a variety of inspection applications, DL techniques that utilize `recurrent neural networks (RNNs)`, `autoencoders`, and `CNNs` demonstrate exceptional outcomes [[60](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_060)]. Researchers use a `deep ensemble learning model` to examine surface flaws on automobile bodies. When the identical task is finished, their method performs better than human inspectors [[61](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_061)]. A `CNN` is used by researchers in [[62](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_062)] to locate flaws in textured surfaces. The results demonstrate high defect detection accuracy on a multi-class dataset. Researchers [[63](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_063)] identify manufacturing defects in semiconductors using a `CNN-based transfer learning technique`; however, other researchers [[64](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_064)] suggest using a `CNN model` that can handle time series data. Researchers [[65](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_065)] recommend using `deep CNNs` for sewer system inspection. They claim that `CNN-based techniques` perform better than manual feature extraction methods using highly variable image data. An optimized `VGG model` is used by researchers [[66](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_066)] for laser welding inspection. A `transfer learning methodology` is utilized when a diverse array of images is employed to pre-train the `VGG model`. Researchers [[67](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_067)] describe a system for defect identification that integrates `Random Forest (RF)` and `Support Vector Machines (SVM)` with a pre-trained `AlexNet` for feature extraction.

The `smart quality inspection (SQI)` method seeks to improve the model’s efficacy and address many elements affecting the visual inspection process. Outsourcing segments of the inspection process enables the regulation of various task aspects, external influences, and other variables. Research gaps are found through the literature review process, which is closely linked to the development of the assessment algorithm. The strategy for creating `SQI` proposes a methodology for utilizing AI-driven visual inspection on-site. The inspection task was allocated to `CNN`, an AI model selected for its unique architecture. The application enables users to upload images of the product as it exits the assembly line. The `CNN model` will subsequently analyze the images to ascertain whether the product is adequate or defective and whether it has passed or failed the required inspection. Furthermore, the operator has the option to add the results to the inspection record by utilizing the `SQI application` [[68](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_068), [69](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_069), [70](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_070)].
