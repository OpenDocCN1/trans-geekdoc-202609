# Chapter 12 Challenges and ethical considerations

## Abstract

In the healthcare industry, artificial intelligence (AI) is crucial because it helps with disease diagnosis, treatment planning, and operational efficiency. However, there are issues with biases, data breaches, and ethical consequences. The chapter aims to provide some knowledge on the challenges faced by the researchers of AI in healthcare along with some ethical considerations. Collaboration between AI clinicians can succeed if appropriate governance frameworks are in place. An overview of the challenges and ethical quandaries surrounding AI in healthcare is given in the chapter. Explainable AI techniques address issues with bias and transparency. Data privacy, algorithmic transparency, and ethical issues are among the difficulties. Up skilling medical staff, addressing biases, and obtaining patient consent are all necessary before implementing AI in healthcare. Furthermore, healthcare professionals need to believe in AI for it to be adopted. Research suggests that AI holds promise for enhancing patient outcomes and healthcare delivery. More research is necessary to fully grasp how AI will affect healthcare professionals’ roles and responsibilities.

**Keywords:** challenges, ethics, XAI, data privacy, safety, algorithms, patient perspective, patient compliance

## 12.1 Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI) is applied as a system that consists of hardware and software. In terms of software, algorithms are the primary focus of AI. Limited resources, a rise in patient demand, and chronic illness put pressure on healthcare systems. As digital health technologies advance, so does the amount of data in every healthcare facility [[1]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_001). When appropriately applied, medical professionals could monitor the effectiveness of interventions and preventative measures while concentrating on the causes of illness. Therefore, decision-makers, legislators, and others must be aware of it. Data scientists, computer scientists, along clinical entrepreneurs all agree that AI, particularly machine learning (ML), is important to the success of healthcare reform [[2]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_002), [[3]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_003). In computing, the term “artificial intelligence” describes a computer program’s capacity to carry out operations akin to those of a human intellect, such as learning and reasoning. When it comes to its application, new technology raises concerns as it can result in data breaches and errors. Errors in the high-risk medical domain can yield deadly consequences for the affected patient. It is crucial to recognize that patients engage with doctors during moments of heightened vulnerability in their daily life [[4]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_004), [[5]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_005). Collaborations between AI and clinicians, where AI assists in guiding medical decisions and delivering evidence-based management, can be effective if appropriately utilized (AI-Health). It can provide healthcare services in the domains of diagnosis, medication discovery, epidemiology, personalized care, and operational efficiency. Researchers indicate that a robust governance system is essential to protect individuals from harm, particularly that arising from unethical conduct, when integrating AI solutions into medical practice. The Hippocratic Oath was established due to Hippocrates, the physician, who formulated the ethical foundation of medicine [[6]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_006).

In general, AI in medicine can be divided into three categories: virtual, physical, along hybrid (which combines robots and virtual reality). In both clinical and research contexts, each of these fields has proven to have extraordinary advantages and/or promise [[7]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_007). A subset of AI known as virtual AI, as well as ML-based AI, consists of a collection of techniques for automatically identifying patterns in data to forecast trends or make decisions in unpredictable circumstances. Machine learning is still being used to help with research in genetics and molecular medicine [[8]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_008), [[9]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_009), [[10]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_010), [[11]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_011). Furthermore, by combining genotype data from “omics” with phenotypic data from electronic health records (EHRs), AI can support precision medicine. The advent of high-throughput sequencing in recent times has enabled the acquisition of extensive data on proteins, DNA, and molecular interactions. The majority of therapeutic targets and diagnostic biomarkers identified today can be found directly or through interaction with algorithms. These techniques can also be used to optimize research on the creation of novel drugs and treatments, thereby lowering the complexity and expense of these studies. These investigations may encompass data-driven, accurate treatment planning, clinical outcome prediction, and process management simplification (e.g., patient recruitment and retention) [[12]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_012), [[13]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_013), [[14]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_014), [[15]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_015), [[16]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_016). Furthermore, AI has proven to be very beneficial in the digital healthcare era with the extensive use of EHRs and high-throughput sequencing. AI may be able to actively advise doctors when they make clinical judgments. Pathological diagnoses and treatments, imaging, and prediction all show promise and success stories. Robots are the best example of the physical AI branch in healthcare; they help senior patients and support surgeons. Senior care facilities are increasingly using care robots. Sensible Surgeons, a US corporation, manufactures surgical systems that can be operated from a console using a method that is minimally invasive to perform complex surgeries [[16]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_016), [[17]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_017), [[18]](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_018).
