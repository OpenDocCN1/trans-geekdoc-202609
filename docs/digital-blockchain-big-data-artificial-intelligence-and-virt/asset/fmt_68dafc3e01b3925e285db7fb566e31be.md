
## 12.5 Patient perspective and engagement

It is well-known how the public feels about sharing health data for research purposes [101](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_101), [102](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_102), [103](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_103). While worries about data sharing’s security, confidentiality, and commercial implications are often raised, people generally favor the research when they believe that it will benefit society or the public at large and when they have faith in the institutions carrying it out [104](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_104). Nonetheless, the perceptions of the public and patients on the dissemination of medical information for AI research are inadequately articulated [105](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_105). Conditional public support for data sharing cannot be presumed to apply to the subject of study, as data sharing for AI research is a contentious issue. The medical literature in the field is rapidly expanding, and AI has the capacity to significantly transform patient interactions with healthcare practitioners. A recent journal issue analyzed the merits and drawbacks of AI technology, including enhancements in healthcare efficacy and quality, as well as threats to patient autonomy, informed consent, and privacy. However, there is a lack of depth and clarity in the present knowledge of how patients view AI and its use in healthcare [106](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_106). According to other researchers, people will likely lose faith in AI systems’ abilities. People might not trust AI’s diagnostic and predictive capabilities, for example, when it comes to medical interventions. According to a different study, users’ perceptions of AI systems’ trustworthiness are influenced by their autonomy [107](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_107), [108](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_108), [109](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_109). Furthermore, fewer participants signed up for an AI diagnosis in a survey some researchers conducted to learn about consumer views of AI in medicine than for a diagnosis from a healthcare provider. The majority of respondents in the same study preferred a human provider over AI, even if there was a greater chance of a misdiagnosis. Additionally, they showed that patients are more inclined to select AI if the doctor makes the final treatment decision rather than just the AI [110](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_110).
