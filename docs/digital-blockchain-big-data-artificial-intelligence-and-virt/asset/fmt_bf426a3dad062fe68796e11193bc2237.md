
# Key Components of AI-Driven Decision Support Systems

Artificial neural networks, decision trees, random forests, naïve Bayes, logistic regression, support vector machines, and numerous other AI methodologies have been successfully utilized in drug development. The integration of various models enhances and optimizes the drug discovery pipeline more efficiently than conventional machine learning methods. This is where collective education proves advantageous.

Random forest analysis is an ensemble method for decision trees that produces individual trees by picking random samples using the bagging technique [[5]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_005). The support vector machine (SVM) is an additional tool that improves predictive accuracy by utilizing a unique approach to minimize structural risk throughout the learning process while increasing the complexity of the mathematical functions necessary for categorization. The multilayer perceptron, characterized by a complex arrangement of concealed layers, constitutes the many hidden layer architecture in deep learning neural networks. It enhances model generalization by reducing overfitting and optimizing hyper-parameter selection, resulting in superior performance [[6]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_006).

Artificial neural networks (ANNs) are widely employed in drug release studies, pharmacokinetics/pharmacodynamics (PK/PD) modeling, and the simulation of biological processes. The ANN is employed to develop controlled release formulations by determining the appropriate dissolution properties, bioavailability, pharmacokinetic profile, and in vitro–in vivo correlation (IVIVC) [[7]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_007). Implantable microchips are effectively used in continuous/pulsed drug delivery methods as well as drug reservoirs for regulated drug delivery. According to reports, AI technology can provide integrated implantable robots for gene and drug delivery applications, allowing for the optimization of target-specific drug delivery. Ongoing research on magnetized microrobots for ocular drug delivery may prove beneficial for ocular targeting therapy. Nanorobot path optimization is supported by quorum sensing and bacterial foraging strategies. Bio-nanorobots are used to count the number of biomarkers associated with cancer as shown in [Fig. 7.1](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_fig_001) [[8]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_008), [[9]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_009). Theranostics agent delivery along with disease monitoring is made easier with medical capsule robots (MCR), which use artificial intelligence systems in place of wireless capsule endoscopy (WCE) [[10]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_010), [[11]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_011), [[12]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_012).

AI and ML algorithms are becoming more and more important in biomedical research, and they are influencing clinical decision-making processes. Consequently, they directly affect medical practice and the exchange of information between doctors and patients [[13]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_013). The foundation of the traditional doctor-patient relationship is open communication about medical advice and treatment choices, as well as personal trust. Decisions become opaque if the doctor is no longer the only one making the diagnosis or choosing the best course of treatment, but is instead heavily influenced by a machine using AI and learning algorithms.

Clinical decision support systems (CDSS) come in a variety of forms and classifications, with the ability to be delivered actively or passively and the timing of interventions among their many subcategories. They are frequently separated into categories based on knowledge and those based on non-knowledge [[14]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_014), [[15]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_015). Knowledge-based systems generate rules based on patient, practice, or literature-based evidence. The system subsequently collects data to evaluate the rule and produces an output or action. Non-knowledge-based CDSS require a data source; however, rather than utilizing specialist medical knowledge for decision-making, they depend on statistical pattern recognition, ML, or AI. Despite being a quickly expanding use case for AI in medicine, CDSS is fraught with difficulties, such as those relating to data collection and comprehending the logic that the AI employs to generate recommendations (black boxes) [[16]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_016).

Pharmaceutical formulations are extremely complex structures with a wide range of variables that can occasionally have poorly defined effects on how they behave. Pharmaceutical formulations are extremely difficult to characterize using conventional analytical methods due to their complex preparation technology. Hundreds of well-defined physicochemical parameters are being reduced to theoretically useful descriptions that will not influence decision support in the future. The characterization of specific difficulties in pharmaceutical technology continues to depend significantly on empirical data. Because of this, empirical modeling is the method of choice in the field to create the right model when numerical analysis of the data is used [[17]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_017). It makes it possible to build the model solely from the data, negating the need for prior knowledge and a priori assumptions. The data, which encapsulates the current understanding of the issue, is utilized to construct the model. The primary advantage of a data-driven model is its impartiality, as it is free from established theories. In the absence of well-founded theories, partially validated theories or hypotheses from other domains may lead to misconceptions [[18]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_018). ANNs exemplify an empirical modeling tool that has become highly effective for empirical modeling applications.

Additionally, CDSS are employed for triage, detecting alterations in health-related symptoms, extracting patient data from medical records, providing in-patient assistance, evaluating treatments, and monitoring. As researchers and developers prepare to implement CDSS, they must consider the five “rights” of CDSS: the right information, the right person, the right intervention format, the right channel, and the right workflow time, as shown in [Fig. 7.2](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_fig_002) [[19]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_019), [[20]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_020).
