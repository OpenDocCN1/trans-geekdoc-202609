
# Chapter 5 Drug manufacturing and quality control with artificial intelligence

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence (AI) uses advanced instruments and networks to mimic human intelligence. Artificial intelligence is increasingly used in the pharmaceutical industry to automate and manage large data sets. Quality by design (QbD) and AI can improve product quality, reduce waste, and speed up production in the pharmaceutical industry. The chapter provides information on drug manufacturing and quality control using AI in the pharma industry. The medical research sector must integrate technologies like blockchain and AI into its core competencies to thrive. The objective of the European Union’s AI-based manufacturing compliance regulations is examined, aiming to align technology with the EU’s core values, rights, and principles. The body of research on the Internet of Things privacy and security measures was reviewed, along with the myths surrounding widely used technologies that make extreme security promises. To raise the bar for pharmaceutical handling of data, sharing, processing, assessment, and classification based on outcomes, the chapter discusses cutting-edge blockchain and AI methods.

**Keywords:** drug, production, quality, management, security, regulatory compliance, quality-by-design

## 5.1 Introduction

The application of artificial intelligence (AI) is growing across many domains in society, chief among them being the pharmaceutical industry. As processes become more digital, it’s harder to acquire, evaluate, as well as apply knowledge to complicated clinical problems [1]. It encourages AI use because it can automatically handle large amounts of data. AI simulates human intelligence using advanced tools and networks [2], [3]. However, it doesn’t threaten to completely replace people in the physical world. AI makes use of hardware and software to perform independent analysis, learning, and decision-making to accomplish predefined objectives [4]. After training, the ability to learn over time is frequently lost when an AI system is used to control procedures like clinical trials or manufacturing. Since the recent adoption of quality by design (QbD) techniques, the pharmaceutical industry has grown, and Industry 4.0 initiatives indicate rapid development [5]. Successful treatment requires choosing the right target for drug molecule synthesis. Numerous proteins, a few of which are overexpressed, are involved in the disease’s progression. Therefore, predicting the desired protein’s molecular structure is crucial to designing a drug molecule specifically targeting that disease. AI can facilitate drug discovery through structural analysis by modeling the three-dimensional configuration of proteins that corresponds to the chemical milieu of the target protein site. It aids in forecasting a compound’s target impact and ensuring safety before synthesis or manufacture [6].

AI has the potential to supplant trial-and-error methodologies. QSPR enables many computational techniques to address formulation design challenges, including stability, dissolution, porosity, and more [7], [8]. A feedback mechanism monitors the entire process and makes occasional adjustments to power decision-support tools. Based on the medication’s physicochemical properties, they choose the excipient type, amount, and type using rule-based systems [9]. AI offers a plethora of options for streamlining development and production manufacturing processes. AI can result in improvements in quality. Additionally, by using AI in several ways, including accelerating processes, it can reduce material waste and increase production efficiency [10]. To ensure quality and produce the pharmaceutical composition within the intended timeframe, a well-planned, scientific approach utilizing AI is required for quality assurance and control. Researchers examine the characteristics of pharmacological substances as well as how they interact with equipment, unit activities, and other substances. Applications include correlations, the first-principle model, heuristics, and decision trees. Among other things, decisions about machine sizes, excipients, and manufacturing processes are made using the information. To increase product quality and reduce waste, AI is used in conjunction with related networks, methods, and tools. Pharmaceutical companies will benefit as a result.
