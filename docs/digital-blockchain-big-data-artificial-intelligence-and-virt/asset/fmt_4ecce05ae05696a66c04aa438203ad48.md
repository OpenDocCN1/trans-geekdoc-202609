
## AI and Blockchain Challenges in Pharmaceutical Development

### Data Quality and Ethical Concerns in AI

The reliability and accuracy of the results can sometimes be compromised by uneven quality, insufficient quantity, or substandard quality of the available data. Another ethical problem is the possibility that AI-based solutions may provoke inquiries over prejudice and equity. An ML system may produce inaccurate or unfair forecasts if the training set contains biased or unrepresentative data. It is imperative to consider the ethical and equitable implementation of AI in the development of novel pharmaceutical products. The knowledge and experience that human researchers have acquired, as well as traditional experimental procedures, cannot be supplanted by current AI-based methods. As AI is limited to making predictions based on available data, human researchers are subsequently required to validate and interpret the results [[91](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_091), [92](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_092), [93](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_093), [94](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_094), [95](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_095), [96](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_096), [97](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_097)].

Pharmaceutical companies have access to millions of compounds in their databases for drug development, which could prove too numerous for conventional machine-learning tools to manage. A computational model can employ the quantitative structure-activity relationship (QSAR) to rapidly forecast several chemicals or essential physicochemical characteristics, such as `log P` or `log D`. Nonetheless, these models exhibit little proficiency in forecasting intricate biological characteristics, like the effectiveness and side effects of certain drugs. The lack of experimental validations, the data derived from experimental errors in training sets, and the restricted size of the training sets hinder the advancement of QSAR-based models [[98](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_098), [99](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_099)].

### Blockchain Technology Applications and Challenges

The problem of counterfeiting is one of the main application areas for blockchain technology, which has been most recently adopted by the pharmaceutical industry. Information security and privacy are two benefits of blockchain technology, which is largely decentralized. More processing power is needed. Roughly half of the power is dedicated to thwarting malicious user attacks; without efficient tracking and tracing, users risk losing goods and becoming victims of robbery [[100](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_100)].

Blockchain technology can enable safe, international banking transactions, but interoperability issues present a significant obstacle. Blockchain operations can be standardized through the use of distributed ledger technology (DLT). Additional research is necessary to determine the most effective methods for implementing DLT applications, despite the International Telecommunication Union (ITU)’s efforts to identify and standardize DLT. The deployment of smart IoT applications, cloud services, and periphery computing has significantly improved the availability of data [[101](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_101)].

The deployment of blockchain faces numerous challenges as a result of the massive growth, including issues with data management, privacy, redundancy, and accessibility. Furthermore, it has attempted to use blockchain technology to expedite medical transactions, protect patients, and enable tracking and monitoring due to misbehavior and poorly managed supply chains [[102](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_102), [103](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_103)].

It’s also critical to guarantee interoperability amongst various healthcare systems and equipment. Strong interoperability solutions are required for effective communication and data exchange among the different hospitals and systems. The blockchain network must be capable of accommodating increased transaction rates without experiencing performance degradation as the volume of healthcare data increases. Careful design and enforcement procedures are required for blockchain deployments to abide by strict data protection laws like GDPR and HIPAA [[104](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_104), [105](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_105), [106](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_106), [107](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_107), [108](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_108)].

### Integration, Energy, and Storage Challenges

The researchers claim that it is challenging to integrate blockchain technology with the present healthcare system. Since many hospitals continue to operate with legacy systems, careful planning is necessary to ensure a seamless transition and backward compatibility. In addition, it is critical to address blockchain’s energy consumption problems, particularly those related to proof-of-work consensus. Healthcare organizations should consider blockchain solutions that are environmentally friendly and energy-efficient [[109](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_109), [110](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_110), [111](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_111), [112](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_112)].

Managing storage capacity presents another difficulty on the front. Because blockchain was developed to store and analyze transactional data, which is restricted in scope, it does not necessitate a significant amount of storage.

### Ethical Concerns and Mitigation Strategies

Ethical concerns also pose a challenge, as AI-based solutions may raise concerns regarding impartiality and bias [[113](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_113)]. For example, the forecasts generated by ML system may be erroneous or inequitable if the training data is biased or unrepresentative [[114](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_114)]. Considering the ethical and fair implementation of AI in the creation of new medicinal molecules is crucial [[115](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_115)].

Various strategies and techniques can be utilized to overcome the challenges that AI faces in the domain of chemical medicine. One approach is to employ data augmentation, which involves generating synthetic data to enhance current datasets. The accuracy and consistency of outcomes can be enhanced by increasing the quantity and diversity of data utilized to train ML algorithms [[116](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_116)]. An alternative method that seeks to deliver succinct and intelligible elucidations for the predictions produced by ML algorithms is the application of explainable AI (XAI) approaches [[117](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_117)]. It has the potential to reduce concerns regarding the impartiality and bias of AI-based techniques [[113](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_113)] and to provide a more thorough understanding of the fundamental assumptions and mechanisms that underlie the predictions.
