
Historically, medical practitioners predominantly determined the health decisions of their patients. It constrains the opportunities for collaboration and collective decision-making between patients and healthcare providers. Studies have shown that digital technology has transformed the relationship between patients and medical personnel [[111](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_111), [112](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_112), [113](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_113), [114](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_114)]. The implementation of these technologies has fostered more collaborative ties between patients and doctors, enabling people to more readily express their concerns over their care. Patients with more awareness of their condition are those that commence self-management. It is characterized as the systematic distribution of knowledge and supportive measures by healthcare professionals to improve individuals’ abilities and confidence in managing their health issues [[115](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_115)]. In an ideal scenario, patients would possess the confidence to address their social, emotional, and physical challenges. Digital technology is enhancing patient engagement and preventive medicine within the healthcare sector. Research indicates that the incorporation of digital technology in healthcare enhances patients’ awareness of their lifestyle choices and encourages them to engage more proactively in their health management. Researchers found that veterans exhibited increased motivation to manage their health when they had access to their health data. Moreover, personalization is modifying a digital platform, product, or service’s user experience to align with an individual’s specific needs, interests, behaviors, and characteristics [[116](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_116), [117](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_117), [118](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_118)]. It entails leveraging data and algorithms to produce interactions, recommendations, and user-specific content that are pertinent and helpful. It facilitates the change in the power dynamic from physicians making decisions for the sake of their patients to a collaborative process in which the needs of the patients are taken into account. Developing a user-friendly online interface for patients may be essential to boosting their engagement with their care [[119](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_119)].

## 12.6 Conclusion

AI is a potent instrument in the field of healthcare, especially when it comes to ML, which helps to monitor the efficacy of interventions and understand illnesses. Healthcare organizations may be resistant to implementing AI due to issues with data accessibility and organizational resistance, so a shift toward overall patient outcomes is required. For data privacy, patient consent is essential, particularly since AI relies heavily on massive datasets. Responsible AI implementation in healthcare requires ethical considerations that prioritize patient trust, accountability, and transparency. Healthcare outcomes trained on biased data by AI systems can be discriminatory, highlighting the need to address biases in data collection as well as model training. Limitations on algorithmic transparency have been the focus of legal discussions; however, in high-risk scenarios, transparent, equitable, and ethical AI design is essential. Creating reskilling programs as a priority is essential to ensuring that medical professionals can use AI technologies. The need for more education on the benefits and drawbacks of AI is highlighted by healthcare professionals’ limited grasp of digital systems. Although worries about criminal penalties for AI errors remain, healthcare professionals are becoming more and more interested in using AI to handle the massive amount of data needed for diagnosis. Ultimately, AI in healthcare possesses the capacity to improve patient outcomes and the delivery of care; yet, factors such as transparency, data privacy, and ethical concerns must be addressed. The successful use of AI necessitates collaboration among medical experts. Further research is necessary to comprehensively understand the impact of AI on patients and healthcare professionals for the appropriate and effective integration of AI in the healthcare sector.
