## 8.6 Conclusion

Pharmacovigilance (PV) is a scientific and applied endeavor that attempts to identify, evaluate, understand, and minimize the negative effects of drugs or potential drug-related issues. Natural language processing and machine learning are two distinct features of AI used in web searches, clinical settings, treatment, surveillance, outcome prediction, and medical diagnosis. Data intake, anomaly detection, and duplicate detection have all benefited from AI/ML. To gather information on ADEs and ADRs, process reports according to international clinical standards, obtain drug-drug interactions, predict adverse reactions to drugs, represent clinical trials, and incorporate estimation unpredictability into diagnostic classification algorithms, AI is being utilized for pharmacovigilance and patient safety. Machine learning techniques are becoming more and more useful for predicting medication side effects, identifying high-risk populations for adverse drug reactions, and incorporating prediction unpredictability into diagnostic classifiers. A developing area in pharmacovigilance is text mining, which combines NLP and data mining. The classification of ADRs, side-effect prediction, and drug toxicity prediction are all done with artificial neural networks (ANNs). Nevertheless, there are obstacles in the way of pharmacovigilance and drug safety, including the scarcity of opportunities for cross-specialty training, privacy concerns, and data models for healthcare facilities, as well as limited training and education in LMICs. PPPs, or public-private partnerships, can aid in resolving these problems and enhancing the creative process.
