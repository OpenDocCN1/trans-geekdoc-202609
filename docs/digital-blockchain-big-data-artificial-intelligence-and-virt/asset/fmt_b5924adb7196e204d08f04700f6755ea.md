
# AI Methods and Applications in Healthcare

AI methods and their applications can be used to diagnose diseases more accurately, care for and monitor the elderly with robots, detect cancer earlier and more quickly than in the past, etc. Massive volumes of data can be processed by ML techniques, which also enable increasingly precise evaluations and forecasts [[19](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_019), [20](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_020), [21](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_021)]. While ML has aided in the advancement of AI, it also raises ethical concerns, particularly in the field of healthcare. Prioritizing ethics in the creation and use of AI across industries. The analytical process of evaluating and selecting alternatives in accordance with social, ethical, and legal standards is referred to as ethical decision-making by AI systems [[22](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_022)]. The subsequent ethical considerations affect the ongoing development and implementation of AI, especially in the healthcare sector, where technology must adhere to regulations, laws, and privacy norms to safeguard the public interest. Advanced ML algorithms employing deep learning and other complex techniques can generate opaque and challenging-to-interpret models referred to as "black boxes". The makers of black box models encounter difficulties in elucidating the decision-making processes of AI systems [[23](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_023), [24](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_024)]. Data augmentation is a technique that entails generating synthetic data to improve existing datasets. As a result, a broader and more varied dataset may be utilized to train ML algorithms, resulting in the generation of more accurate and dependable outcomes. An alternate tactic that seeks to provide clear reasons for the predictions made by machine learning algorithms is the implementation of explainable AI (XAI) tools. It enhances comprehension of the core processes and assumptions that support the predictions, while also alleviating concerns regarding the fairness and bias of AI-based methodologies [[25](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_025), [26](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_026), [27](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_027), [28](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_028)].

## 12.2 Challenges in Implementing AI in Healthcare

To reliably define or predict a variety of tasks, ML as well as deep learning models requires large datasets. Machine learning has advanced most in industries where large datasets are available for backing more complex, precise algorithms. However, data accessibility in the healthcare industry is a complicated problem. Health data is costly for organizations, and sharing it amongst hospitals is viewed with reluctance because each hospital views it as its property, used to manage its patients [[29](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_029)]. The data's continuous availability after the algorithm that analyzes it was introduced is another problem. Ideally, training with ever-larger datasets would necessitate constant improvement for ML-based systems. However, it is frequently challenging to accomplish because of organizational resistance. To achieve it, it has been suggested that for information technology along with AI to make advancements in the healthcare sector, a drastic shift in emphasis from treating specific patients to all-around patient outcomes is required. It will promote data sharing to improve clinical outcomes [[30](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_030), [31](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_031)]. Applications using AI create privacy and data security concerns. Security breaches often target sensitive health data. Protecting patient data is therefore essential. People may begin to mistake AI systems for humans as AI develops, which would increase worries about data privacy and allow for more inadvertent data collection. Patient consent is crucial for addressing data privacy issues, as without sufficient individual consent, healthcare organizations may permit extensive utilization of patient data for AI training [[32](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_032), [33](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_033)].

Extensive datasets are a fundamental element of AI-driven systems and technologies, prompting privacy concerns related to data sharing and gathering. Due to the inclusion of personal information in patient records, the interchange and management of illness-related data across several databases is exceedingly challenging [[34](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_034), [35](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_035)]. It indicates that software development organizations must adhere to secrecy regulations, potentially obstructing AI development. AI technology relies on machine learning to make conclusions based on available information, rather than taking into account the unique circumstances of each patient, hence raising moral, ethical, and legal concerns. AI technology should conform to the moral standards, legal obligations, and personal convictions that govern individuals' conduct in public areas. Numerous industries have transitioned from isolated silos in the digital era. It is also applicable to the medical domain [[36](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_036)]. The healthcare sector was previously perceived as involving medical professionals, including doctors and nurses, providing patient care. In addition to access to superior medical treatment, contemporary health is also achieved via the adoption of healthy lifestyle choices, including a balanced diet, regular exercise, and daily self-care practices. It has rendered preventive medicine increasingly vital, complicating the differentiation between the roles of a physician, the utilization of technology, and the maintenance of a healthy lifestyle. AI is not limited by temporal or spatial parameters. Consequently, the concept of a closed healthcare system has become obsolete [[37](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_037)].
