
## 13.3 Future trends and innovations of AI in drug manufacturing

Furthermore, since sufficient huge quantities of data to train them are necessary for the successful development of algorithms, the amount of data that is available directly impacts the effectiveness of AI/ML models. Finding novel targets or medications to treat a variety of neurological disorders for which there is no known treatment presents a unique challenge. Transfer learning technology, which applies knowledge from one task to another, can address the lack of training examples for a given drug discovery task. However, information sharing within the field of science appears to be the most promising long-term solution to data scarcity [[20](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_020)]. The process of developing drugs would greatly benefit from such widespread data sharing, and shortly, faster devices could result from hardware advancements like quantum computing. The difficulty in identifying targets is a particular limitation of using AI/ML for drug discovery, as many neurological disorders have unclear pathophysiology. More sophisticated AI and ML tools that can extract information from data sets created across the different biological layers – such as transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics – are required to study the intricate disease mechanisms and identify the appropriate biological targets [[21](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_021)]. Capsule networks are a futuristic AI construction that can help in the situation. It is a networked module that houses CNNs. The remarkable ability of capsule networks to predict the cardiovascular side effects of materials during initial application highlights their unique potential in drug discovery efforts. Due to CNNs’ modular design, capsule neural networks can learn from a variety of data sets while maintaining the data’s ordered features. Because illness records have a highly adaptable structure, capsule networks can be used to study the way various layers of protein molecules, metabolites, and genes connect and shift in function concerning different diseases [[22](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_022)].

The resurgence of AI in organic synthesis can be attributed to developments in big data, computational power, deep neural networks, and optimization algorithms. Their primary drawback is their dependence on unambiguous chemical changes and reactions [[23](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_023), [24](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_024), [25](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_025)]. These have to be built and assembled manually. Transformer depictions and sequence-to-sequence models are two methods for natural language processing that have recently gained traction in the area. The line of investigation is driven by the discovery that the distribution sequence of organic molecule fragments is comparable to the English word sequence. When rule-free approaches are applied, the products are usually conceived as part of a text-based representation. After processing the products, encoder–decoder architecture is used to predict their corresponding artificial precursors at a single-step response distance [[26](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_026)]. Moreover, data indicates that the application of AI to the research and development of pharmaceuticals is beginning to gain traction. These methods are gradually living up to some of the community’s expectations and are demonstrating remarkable advancements in synthesis planning, de novo molecule design, and quantitative structure-activity relationship (QSAR) modeling, among other fields. It’s unclear, though, if these methods will help scientists create and synthesize “better drugs more quickly”. Techniques based on more “raw” chemical pictures (e.g., neural networks with graphs and SMILES-based recurrent neural networks) ought to work at least as well as standard descriptor-based models in the context of ligand-based property prediction. Furthermore, a greater range of chemical materials and modeling tasks can be easily applied with deep learning techniques, enabling more effective data utilization through online and multitask learning. Presently, conformation-aware deep learning is in its infancy, particularly when approaches that make use of three-dimensional imbalances in their construction are taken into account. Nevertheless, they should advance quickly in the field of drug discovery as well as related fields like material science and quantum mechanics, especially when used to replace more computationally intensive first-principle calculations [[27](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_027), [28](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_028)].

Using generative models and deep learning, scientists can produce novel substances with target-binding properties that increase drug efficacy and decrease side effects. With AI, individualized dose formulations for each patient will be feasible. AI algorithms will take into account patient-specific factors like age, weight, genetics, and illness status to optimize drug pieces and delivery techniques and improve treatment outcomes. The application of AI algorithms to predict the toxicity and side effects of suggested medications will revolutionize safety evaluation. Medication adherence and remote patient care will be made possible by AI-powered monitoring systems. Wearable technology and instruments will make it easier to collect data continuously, which will allow AI algorithms to create more individualized treatment plans and improve adherence. AI enhances patient recruitment, patient selection, and clinical trial design. AI algorithms will identify eligible patients, reduce trial costs, and expedite approval by utilizing genetic profiles, biomarkers, and electronic health records [[29](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_029), [30](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_030)]. Continuous manufacturing operations will be optimized by AI models’ real-time monitoring and oversight of critical variables. Through data analysis and feedback, AI algorithms will make pharmaceutical manufacturing consistent and effective. Massive data sets will be analyzed by AI to support regulatory decision-making. It will expedite the approval process for pharmaceuticals by regulatory agencies and enhance safety. More creative approaches and procedures must be employed since the creation and study of pharmaceuticals necessitates such a significant time and financial commitment [[31](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_031)]. The medical field is seeing significant opportunities due to AI. These include extensive multivariate data analysis; complex problem-solving in the development of workable medication delivery systems; enhanced decision-making; disease classification; dosage ratio optimization; quick drug development; prediction of drug reactions and bioactivities; cellular response; the efficacy of combination medications; and a host of other topics. As each section has shown, AI and machine learning have enormous potential to transform the way that medications are administered and improve the efficacy of therapies for infectious diseases [[32](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_032)].
