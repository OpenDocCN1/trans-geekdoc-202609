
# Foundation of AI in Pharmacovigilance

The Greek word “pharmakon” (meaning drug), as well as the Latin word “vigilare” (meaning to keep watch), are the sources of the term “pharmacovigilance.” Detecting, evaluating, comprehending, and minimizing drug side effects or potential drug-related issues are the scientific and practical endeavors known as “pharmacovigilance” (PV) [[1]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_001). On a global scale, pharmacovigilance systems are gathering a substantial volume of data daily. Processing the extensive dataset poses a significant challenge. Recently, the program’s scope was extended to include herbals, blood-related products, medical devices, traditional and complementary medicines, herb vigilance, hemovigilance, and materio vigilance [[2]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_002).

Two examples of the unique characteristics of artificial intelligence (AI) are machine learning (ML) and natural language processing (NLP). ML techniques examine structured data, including genetic and imaging data. Unstructured and free-text forms can be distinguished by NLP, an interpretive system that can understand and process human language. AI is also utilized in search engines, clinical settings for treatment, detection, and outcome prediction, and medical diagnosis. With appropriate monitoring, it is also utilized in the healthcare industry to prevent health issues in people [[3]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_003).

Prioritizing patient safety was the primary objective of PV’s design since patients’ exposure to treatment medications throughout clinical trials as well as studies is limited. It allows for the long-term observation and use of drug profiles. It also covers specific populations, including the elderly, racialized, pediatric, and pregnant populations. PV studies are necessary due to the lack of complete data from long-term drug exposure [[4]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_004). Lifesaving medications like antiretroviral, antitubercular, and anticancer drugs are approved under a fast-track system, making them readily available to patients. PV is in charge of weighing the advantages and disadvantages of these medications.

After a new drug is introduced to the market, drug safety is currently facing significant risks. The main cause of erosion during a clinical trial or post-marketing is the drug’s unpredictable toxicities, which can result in morbidity and mortality even at normal dosages. ADR data must be gathered from pharmaceutical companies by the license holder and submitted to the local drug regulatory body. ADR detection and reporting, technical AE coding, safety individual report preparation, severity assessment, and linkage to suspected drugs are the most crucial tasks in the PV industry. As a result, a new technology is required for the detection of ADRs, as current methods rely on time-consuming human intervention. An AI and ML system was created by a multinational pharmaceutical company and a consulting firm to make it easier to process and maintain quality and compliance standards [[5]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_005)−[[7]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_007). AI and ML are being investigated for their potential to influence PV throughout its whole lifecycle [[6]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_006), [[7]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_007). Furthermore, ML has been used to find complex patterns, or syndromes, in safety data and to perform a range of predictive modeling tasks. Furthermore, pharmacoepidemiology, the study of drug interactions with human populations, is using machine learning (ML) extensively to examine the advantages, drawbacks, and practical applications of drug use [[8]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_008), [[9]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_009).

Pharmacovigilance also uses machine learning to assess how well NLP skills classify unstructured text in patient safety reports of incidents. NLP can serve as a layer of protection by identifying cases that result in severe harm or death, based on researchers’ testing of the system’s capacity to classify free text within patient security incidents autonomously and decide the severity of harm outcomes. Several studies looked at the possibility of using machine learning to filter reports on patient safety from sources such as electronic health records [[10]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_010). Researchers have discovered that near-miss reports gathered by passive surveillance monitoring systems, as well as large, semi-structured, or disorganized collections of adverse events, can be screened and evaluated effectively using text mining and machine learning algorithms [[11]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_011). A more focused strategy was employed by the researchers, who created a deep-learning model and tested it on various data sets to identify allergic reactions in the free-text narration of hospital safety reports and assess the model’s generalizability [[12]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_012). According to the study, the model may be applied to enhance allergy treatment, possibly allowing for system enhancement and real-time monitoring events for medical errors. The absence of risk to a patient that can be avoided and the decrease in the possibility of harm coming from receiving medical care are known as patient safety. The healthcare system is integrating AI, which is changing current dynamics (like the role of medical providers) and creating new opportunities to improve patient safety [[13]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_013), [[14]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_014).

Due to machine learning, computer systems can now identify latent information and make predictions about the data using both labeled (supervised learning) and unlabeled (unsupervised learning) data, all without the need for explicit programming. Among AI’s many applications, ML, along with natural language processing, stands out for its social impact and frequent use in the medical field [[15]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_015). Reinforcement learning is the third type of machine learning, where an algorithm learns from its successes and failures as it works to complete a task ([Fig. 8.1](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_fig_001)). Deep learning and artificial neural networks are also included in machine learning. The goal of natural language processing is to enable computers to comprehend human language and convert text into machine-readable, organized information that can be further studied using machine-learning techniques [[16]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_016), [[17]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_017), [[18]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_018). Electronic health records (EHRs), which may be gathered from mobile devices and regular medical attention, also contain real-time, practical health data. It might result in a potentially proactive monitoring and safety signal identification strategy. The advent of ML and NLP techniques has created new opportunities for identifying drug safety signals in the free-text EHR data, which is primarily unstructured [[19]](#9783111573861_011894.xhtml#b_9783111574288-008_ref_019).
