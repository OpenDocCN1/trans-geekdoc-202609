
## 3.4 Challenges in pharmaceutical collaboration

The most well-known type of relationship between for-profit and not-for-profit organizations in the healthcare context is interactions among pharmaceutical companies, healthcare providers (which include professionals and healthcare organizations), and large-scale interactions [[57](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_057), [58](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_058), [59](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_059), [60](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_060)]. Over most of the doctors interact with pharmaceutical companies, according to research from multiple countries, and a few percentages of US biomedical researchers accept funding from the industry [[61](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_061), [62](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_062), [63](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_063), [64](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_064)]. Academics, professionals, the media, and politicians are scrutinizing pharmaceutical company-healthcare provider interactions more and more, but experts are divided on whether these interactions improve social value and have positive effects [[65](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_065), [66](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_066), [67](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_067), [68](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_068)].

There are two categories of interactions: (a) interactions focused on education, and (b) interactions focused on research. Education-oriented interactions are those that aim to improve professionals’ (such as doctors or nurses) awareness, knowledge, and mindset toward new goods and services through the dissemination of factual and instructive information [[69](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_069), [70](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_070), [71](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_071), [72](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_072)]. Research-oriented interactions are those that are specifically designed to sponsor, fund, or provide assistance with research initiatives. Research indicates that pharmaceutical companies and healthcare providers that prioritize education influence physicians’ prescribing behaviors. In other words, physicians who participated in educational interactions prescribed a greater quantity of name-brand medications and administered a specific injection on a more frequent basis [[73](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_073), [74](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_074)]. Finally, the prescribing behavior of physicians is binary, influenced by education-oriented interactions in both investigations. In other words, the prescribing practices of physicians are not significantly affected by the increased monetary value of the education-oriented interaction [[75](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_075)]. Further research on research-oriented interaction reveals that doctors who identify primarily as entrepreneurs and whose professional identities are closely aligned with pharmaceutical companies are more likely to allow pharmaceutical companies to exert greater control over different aspects of research projects [[76](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_076)].

The corporate pharmaceutical sector is characterized by intense competition, with each organization striving to emerge as the winner in the sprint to introduce innovative blockbuster compounds. The industry knows for a fact that companies that enter the market first profit more than those that introduce follower molecules. Moreover, early adopters have enough time to refine their offerings and determine the going rate. Pharma companies are always searching for and developing new compounds that target similar or very similar drug targets [[77](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_077)]. These repeated research initiatives ultimately use resources that could be better allocated to the R&D of other diseases with unmet medical needs, given the enormous funding directed toward pharmaceutical R&D. Several cooperative agreements have been put forth and used to improve the performance of pharmaceutical R&D [[78](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_078), [79](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_079)]. Pharmaceutical companies, biotechnology firms, and academic drug discovery divisions share the goal of conducting fundamental research that will facilitate future drug discovery and innovation, even though they would otherwise compete. Consequently, they collaborate on precompetitive research. Precompetitive research, in its simplest form, determines the scientific feasibility of pursuing a specific therapeutic pathway before a full-scale research and development drug campaign is launched [[80](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_080)]. Precompetitive research can be conducted in several areas, such as target validation and identification, compound library sharing, and assay and biomarker development. Precompetitive cooperation has many advantages, such as lower research expenses because businesses pool resources and knowledge, increased productivity because businesses concentrate on their core skills and improve their quality, and the exchange of ideas among scientists [[81](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_081)]. Therefore, precompetitive studies, partnerships, and collaboration strategies have improved drug discovery efficiency. Research on rare and neglected diseases should be expanded into underserved therapeutic areas. It would protect companies with blockbuster drugs that are on the verge of expiring.
