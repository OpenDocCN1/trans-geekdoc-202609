
# Post-Marketing Surveillance and AI Applications

Within the domain of pharmaceutical sciences, the process of drug innovation is an ongoing endeavor that frequently results in the identification of novel therapeutic agents. A medicine’s development, from the idea to the final product, is thought to be an extremely intricate process that examines every facet of the medication to ensure sufficient safety at the time of approval.

**Post-marketing surveillance (PMS)** can be defined as the process of monitoring a drug’s safety following successful market release from clinical trials [[1]](#9783111573861_015802.xhtml#b_9783111574288-011_ref_001). The primary objective of PMS research is to identify novel beneficial and detrimental effects. Other important factors include off-label use of drugs, orphan drug concerns, and difficulties in conducting international research studies in the pediatric population. It is a crucial tool that helps paint a clearer picture of the benefits and drawbacks that a drug could start to pose over an extended period of administration by tying the strength of the drug-exposed to the cause of the adverse events [[2]](#9783111573861_015802.xhtml#b_9783111574288-011_ref_002).

**Phase IV studies** are those related to PMS because most medications cannot be commercialized without first completing clinical trials in phase III. Rare adverse drug reactions (ADRs) will only be partially disclosed by clinical trials conducting safety and efficacy evaluations of novel pharmaceuticals. Results are more realistic because they take place in more natural environments and provide proof to support or improve the safety of authorized medications [[3]](#9783111573861_015802.xhtml#b_9783111574288-011_ref_003), [[4]](#9783111573861_015802.xhtml#b_9783111574288-011_ref_004).

**Pharmacovigilance** and **pharmacoepidemiology** are the two areas of pharmacology that are covered by post-marketing drug monitoring programs. Pharmacovigilance, also known as medication safety surveillance, has as its main objective the “prompt detection” of “novel” adverse drug reactions (ADRs) that differ from one another in terms of the “clinical nature, severity, as well as frequency.” “Population-based investigation into the use of drugs and the risks associated with these uses” is the definition of pharmacoepidemiology. It is important to stress the importance of pharmacovigilance as well as the idea that a drug’s practical application begins the moment it is marketed [[5]](#9783111573861_015802.xhtml#b_9783111574288-011_ref_005), [[6]](#9783111573861_015802.xhtml#b_9783111574288-011_ref_006), [[7]](#9783111573861_015802.xhtml#b_9783111574288-011_ref_007).

## Regulatory Landscape

The World Health Organization suggests that companies look for information in literature and other sources to find out about any new risks associated with using or operating a device. The new **Medical Device Regulation (MDR)** and the **In Vitro Diagnostics Medical Device Regulation (IVDR)** from the European Union (EU) demonstrate the substantial increase in PMS demands placed on in vitro diagnostic and medical device companies. Gathering and assessing pertinent specialized, scientific, and technical sources is one requirement [[8]](#9783111573861_015802.xhtml#b_9783111574288-011_ref_008). The regulations do not specify the use of literature records or the format in which the documentation of the search process should be done; they only call for a thorough description of the chosen data sources, gathering strategies, and evaluation techniques.

New EU regulations mandate that companies that produce in vitro diagnostics and medical devices actively search for information regarding the safety and/or efficacy of their products. It will ensure that gadgets remain current and improve early problem detection. The industry will undoubtedly benefit from the shift to proactive surveillance [[9]](#9783111573861_015802.xhtml#b_9783111574288-011_ref_009), [[10]](#9783111573861_015802.xhtml#b_9783111574288-011_ref_010).

To comply with the new regulations, businesses will need to invest a significant amount of money in new procedures, staff, and rules. It is noteworthy that many small and medium-sized businesses, particularly those with outdated or fragmented data systems, find it difficult to adequately review and analyze the massive volumes of data offered from various sources.
