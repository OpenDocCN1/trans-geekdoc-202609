
## 12.4 Impact on healthcare professionals

Another researcher explains that privacy is a key ethical factor to consider when using AI. Concerns about how AI systems gather, store, and use personal data are known as privacy issues in AI. AI systems can gather and analyze substantial amounts of personal data, and improper management of the data may lead to significant privacy concerns [[68](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_068)]. AI systems often collect substantial amounts of personal data from individuals, which are subsequently utilized to make determinations about them. It prompts enquiries on the methodologies employed for data collection, storage, and utilization. The implementation of AI in surveillance systems may infringe upon privacy rights. Four principal ethical issues must be addressed for AI in healthcare to achieve its maximum potential: (1) informed permission for data utilization; (2) safety and integrity; (3) fairness in algorithms alongside biases; and (4) protection of personal data privacy. The majority of legal discourse on AI has been on the constraints of algorithmic transparency. The application of AI in high-risk situations has necessitated transparent, equitable, and accountable AI design and governance. Accessibility and comprehensibility of information are the two essential elements of transparency. It is standard practice to deliberately obscure the mechanisms of algorithmic operations [[69](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_069), [70](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_070)]. The potential for bias in AI algorithms, which may lead to disparities in healthcare results, represents another ethical problem. An unrepresentative training dataset can result in biased AI models that disproportionately affect specific demographic groups. It may result in inequitable access to healthcare and intensify pre-existing disparities in medical outcomes. To ensure fairness and equity, developers must actively combat bias through inclusive and diverse data sets, constant monitoring, and iterative algorithm improvement [[71](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_071)]. An important ethical consideration in AI decision-making is interpretability, particularly in the medical field where decisions affect patients’ quality of life. Deep learning networks and other complex AI models can be hard to interpret, resulting in it being hard to understand the way the system comes to a particular conclusion [[72](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_072)].

Moreover, another ethical cornerstone in AI applications for healthcare that needs particular consideration is informed consent. Patients ought to be made aware of how AI technologies are being used in their medical treatment and given the choice to accept or reject AI-driven diagnoses or treatments. It entails outlining the advantages of AI in healthcare as well as their drawbacks and possible hazards [[73](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_073)]. AI’s ability to automate some tasks in the healthcare industry could result in job displacement, which raises ethical questions about how it will affect the workforce. Maintaining human roles while taking advantage of AI’s efficiency gains requires careful consideration. Prioritizing reskilling and up skilling initiatives are necessary for healthcare organizations to guarantee that their workforce is capable of adjusting to and cooperating with AI technologies [[74](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_074), [75](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_075)].

AI has applications in healthcare that include task automation, anomaly detection, correlation analysis, unstructured data analysis, and human worker support. These are accomplished by using `DL`, `ML`, and `NLP` algorithms. `NLP` algorithms identify key terms and phrases in unprocessed electronic medical record data to assist with decision-making. The way AI can transform data into outcomes that could enhance clinical practices and the caliber of care given by medical professionals in innovative ways is fascinating to watch [[76](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_076), [77](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_077), [78](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_078), [79](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_079), [80](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_080)]. Research has examined whether or not AI will be accepted or used, depending on how beneficial the technology is viewed to be and how to make it work better. Research on AI has mostly concentrated on the technology – where to connect it and how to best utilize it. Nonetheless, the human being is the other essential element in all of it. It is now necessary to take into account how the technology will affect the user, the healthcare professional – and how it will influence their capacity to provide care [[81](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_081), [82](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_082), [83](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_083)]. The use and capability of AI will vary amongst healthcare professionals and disciplines. Since AI in health has no widely accepted definition, healthcare professionals might not be aware that they are utilizing AI in their work. It is crucial to comprehend how clinicians view AI in healthcare delivery to guarantee a long-term healthcare workforce prepared to manage its quickly evolving digital healthcare landscape. More research is required to determine the interactions between clinicians and technology [[84](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_084), [85](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_085), [86](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_086)]. Researchers investigating the underlying factors influencing healthcare professionals’ endorsement of an `AIMDSS` met the criteria. Medical practitioners are less inclined to utilize AI in their routine practice when they harbor skepticism about the technology or lack awareness of its capacity to enhance patient outcomes or care delivery, as indicated by the researcher’s limited observational study. The conclusion pertains specifically to the healthcare sector, highlighting similarities in aspects of digital health technology that do not incorporate AI [[87](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_087), [88](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_088), [89](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_089), [90](#9783111573861_017182.xhtml#b_9783111574288-012_ref_090)].
