
# Computational Methods in Drug Development

Computational methods are vital and practical tools in the drug development process. Finding a chemical compound that can both geometrically and chemically fit into a particular cavity of a target protein is the aim of drug development. Computational methods have made many tasks easier, as shown in [Fig. 6.2](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_fig_002), including lead optimization, design, search, comparison, modeling, binding energy estimation, and pharmacokinetic predictions. Many advancements in the field of developing and discovering drugs have been made possible by advances in computational power and analytical algorithms and approaches [[29](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_029)]. High-throughput virtual screening, also known as in-silico virtual screening (`HTVS`), has produced a valuable alternative to the costly and time-consuming high-throughput screening experimental methods. The capability to virtually screen catalogs of compounds to enhance ligand enrichment and move forward with experimental validation has resulted in the production of many lead compounds. Large databases of virtual compounds that are either complementary with the solved receptor structure or similar to a known inhibitor (ligand-based) are screened computationally by `HTVS`. It enables researchers to search through big databases or compound libraries to find a narrowly targeted subset that can be used for experimental confirmation of activities [[30](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_030)]. But instead of requiring an exhaustive list, traditional `HTS`, de-novo drug design relies on methods of search to efficiently scan the chemical space. `HTS` can handle thousands of library members, verify binders, reproduce findings via biophysical experiments with high precision, and remove false positive hits, irrespective of the target 3D structural information. The subsequent, time- and money-efficient phase of potentially enhancing the physicochemical characteristics of hits to a lead medication depends heavily on `HTVS` [[31](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_031)].

## High-Throughput Screening (HTS)

In `HTS`, various biological effectors and modulators are evaluated and tested against distinct, particular targets. Consequently, `HTS` is generally favored when target knowledge is insufficient and structure-based drug design is impractical. `HTS` may also be utilized in conjunction with alternative tactics, including computational methods and fragment-based drug creation [[32](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_032), [33](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_033)]. Target discovery, molecule management, chemical preparation, assay optimization, and screening are the elements of high-throughput screening (`HTS`). High-throughput screening, when combined with multi-well cell-based platforms, enables the identification of small molecule modulators of associated signaling and metabolic pathways. The development of new medications for central nervous system disorders, especially neurodegenerative diseases, is fraught with risk due to the elevated failure rate of candidates after long and expensive clinical and non-clinical research. Consequently, the establishment of accurate assays for quantifying neurodegeneration is an essential initial step in the formulation of therapies for neurodegenerative diseases. The ability to pinpoint the related processes that initiate and result in cell death is essential to the development of effective assays. To this aim, assays have been developed to identify biochemical occurrences, such as energy metabolism, metabolic fluctuation, and DNA fragmentation that lead to neuronal death [[34](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_034), [35](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_035)]. Historically, cytoprotective studies utilizing fluorescent markers or dyes have been crucial for classifying therapies that induce neuronal death [[35](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_035), [36](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_036), [37](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_037)]. Pharmaceutical companies regularly employ several tests, which may be readily adapted for high-throughput screening methods, to assess the neurotoxicity of medications and their possible adverse effects. Researchers devised a high-throughput screening (`HTS`) method for primary neurons suitable for extensive investigations, encompassing compound libraries and whole-genome RNA interference (`RNAi`) [[38, 39](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_038)]. The method may be utilized for the screening of mammalian cell lines, including `CHO` cells, for minute particles [[40](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_040), [41](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_041)]. The selection of the readout for a cell-based experiment targeting a signaling pathway can be flexible. Target-oriented and cell-oriented screening procedures have been utilized to explore various innovative drug delivery systems for Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease, bipolar disorder, autism, and schizophrenia. Cell-based screening concurrently assesses many targets, with the results indicating the effects on a cellular pathway or network. The models’ failure to precisely represent the pathophysiological environment of neurodegenerative diseases is a primary challenge faced by high-throughput screening in its quest for prospective therapeutic candidates. High-throughput screening is crucial to the drug discovery process for neurodegenerative diseases [[42](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_035), [43](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_035), [44](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_037)]. The efficacy of high-throughput screening (`HTS`) is constrained by both the quality and amount of the chemical library evaluated, as well as the absence of human-relevant neurodegenerative disease (`NDD`) models. Thus, increasing the number of tiny molecules accessible through high-throughput screening improves the likelihood of identifying effective disease-modifying therapies for neurodegenerative conditions. The advancement of cheminformatics in the pharmaceutical industry, the rise of `AI` in drug discovery, and the development of dynamic combinatorial chemistry (`DCC`) are anticipated to produce numerous compounds that could result in more targeted pharmaceuticals exhibiting efficacy in preliminary high-throughput screening (`HTS`) assays [[46](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_046), [47](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_047), [48](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_048)].
