
# Article 15 of the TRIPS Agreement

Article 15 of the TRIPS Agreement stipulates that “any sign, or any combination of signs, capable of distinguishing the goods or services of one enterprise from those of others, is necessary to constitute a trademark.” Due to industrialization, the trademark has gained prominence in contemporary international trade [[27](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_027)]. A trademark is a unique symbol or logo that signifies the manufacture or provision of a specific product by an individual, organization, or economic sector. Service marks, akin to trademarks, serve to differentiate service providers from their competitors. A business may employ various types of trademarks for distinct items, while trade names serve to differentiate it from competing enterprises. Trade names and trademarks assist companies in establishing their brand, enhancing awareness, and fostering trust with consumers. Consumers typically depend on trademarks due to the challenges associated with swiftly evaluating a product or service to determine its quality [[28](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_028)]. Any distinguishable mark, or one that can be graphically represented and used to differentiate the goods and services of one company from another, is eligible for trademark registration under the Indian Trademarks Act. Trademarks don’t grant exclusive rights that could be abused, so their validity shouldn’t be restricted. However, if there were no time limit on trademark validity, there would be an excessive number of registered trademarks that have no use [[29](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_029)].

## 9.2.4 The trade secret

A trade secret is any knowledge that is kept confidential, not widely known to the public, and gives its owner a financial or competitive advantage. Trade secrets have a high value – tens or even hundreds of millions of dollars – and large damage awards have been given in trade secret lawsuits. A trade secret is misappropriated when it is obtained through “improper means.” Nonetheless, if the claimed trade secret was created independently, became known to the public, or was not kept a secret, then those defenses may be able to successfully contest a claim of misappropriation of trade secrets. Trade secrets are not as well known as other IPRs, even though they are an intrinsically valuable intangible asset for any business [[30](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_030)]. Its unpopularity is attributed to numerous factors: First off, registration is not necessary for trade secrets. Trade secrets are generally credited with developing and being preserved due to standard business practices and business ethics. Even though trade secret laws are based on evolving legal theories, there is currently a weak enforcement system. Third, disputes involving such secrecy are seldom open to public discourse, implying that access to information regarding such matters is usually limited, rendering trade secrets comparatively unpopular. Fourth, trade secrets do not grant their owner the sole right to utilize the information, in contrast to other types of intellectual property. The right may belong to others when knowledge is produced independently or obtained through the process of reverse engineering. Trade secrets provide the business with several financial advantages [[31](#9783111573861_013307.xhtml#b_9783111574288-009_ref_031)]. Ensuring appropriate rewards for any invention that is supported by appropriate trade secret protection gives the company financial stability. To prevent breaches, no expensive system needs to be set up. Moreover, it always worries about knowledge spillovers leading to the misuse of company information. Trade secrets strike a balance between appropriate disclosures and secrecy, even though they rely on reasonable methods to maintain confidentiality. As a result, trade secrets may lead to independent invention or knowledge diffusion. The non-exclusivity restriction strikes the perfect balance between maintaining the essential distinction from absolute secrecy and ensuring confidentiality.

# 9.3 Data privacy in pharmaceutical industry

Healthcare organizations send, store, and manage substantial volumes of data to ensure the delivery of effective and appropriate care. For many years, safeguarding the data has been a challenging imperative. To make matters worse, the healthcare sector is still among the most vulnerable to data breaches that are made public. Indeed, data breaches occur when hackers discover sensitive information through data mining techniques and procedures and make it publicly available. The complexity of putting security measures in place doesn’t change, but the stakes keep getting higher as sophisticated methods of getting around security controls are developed. Consequently, organizations must adopt healthcare data security measures that comply with regulatory standards and safeguard critical assets. In recent years, the incidence of advanced persistent threats has risen, characterized by focused assaults on information systems aimed primarily at exfiltrating recoverable data by the attacker. Consequently, breaches of patient privacy are regarded as a significant concern in big data analytics, complicating organizations’ efforts to address these varied, interconnected, and pressing challenges. Data privacy delineates data access in accordance with privacy rules and regulations, specifying who is permitted to access financial, medical, personal, or confidential information. Numerous cryptographic methods incorporate endpoint authentication to mitigate man-in-the-middle (MITM) attacks. Authenticity generally relates to the truthfulness of one’s origins, claims, commitments, and motives. Most cryptographic methods use endpoint authentication to mitigate MITM attacks in various forms.
