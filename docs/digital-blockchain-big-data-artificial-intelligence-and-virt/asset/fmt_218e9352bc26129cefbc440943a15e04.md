
AI-driven CDDS can aid physicians, nurses, patients, and other carers in making more informed choices. It analyzes patient data from the past, present, and future to identify faults or deficiencies in the treatment route and propose enhancements or safety issues to the user. The two types of decision support systems are ML systems and rule-based expert systems (ES) [[21](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_021)]. Many hospitals’ electronic health records (EHR) have been incorporated into decision support systems, which employ pre-established rules to guide decision-making. Because these systems only require binary choices, they are simpler to configure. ML systems, on the other hand, rely on statistics to make decisions. It necessitates a thorough comprehension of the standard process by which medical professionals evaluate patient data and reach important conclusions [[22](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_022)]. An AI system designed to diagnose *Helicobacter pylori* infection during endoscopy was shown to detect the infection with good accuracy and exploitability in a recent case-control study. These tools can assist pathologists in identifying *H. pylori* infections or take their place. When it comes to gastric cancer, AI’s diagnostic precision and efficacy are on par with or even better than those of general pathologists. Through convolutional neural networks, deep learning models can identify and classify esophageal squamous cell carcinoma and its depth of invasion in a matter of minutes or seconds. It works well for detecting adenocarcinomas as well [[23](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_023)]. AI models have been effectively developed to assess prognosis in patients with colorectal cancer (CRC), biliary tract cancers, and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [[24](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_024)]. The prognosis for gastric cancer is presently uncertain. Whole slide images (WSIs) were utilized to develop a deep learning model in cohort research that produced the Tumor Risk Score (TRS) as a prognostic marker. Clinical staging approaches failed to equal TRS’s capacity to uniformly categorize patients into up to five groups with markedly different prognoses, attributable to its predictive efficacy. Though they have a lot of potential, AI-driven decision support systems still need to be worked out before being applied in clinical settings. These challenges include data uniformity, effectiveness, morality, government approval, as well as medical acceptability. For gastroenterology to begin to utilize AI technology, these issues must be resolved. AI-powered decision support systems can enhance gastroenterology by offering quick and accurate diagnosis, individualized treatment regimens, and enhanced patient care [[25](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_025)].
