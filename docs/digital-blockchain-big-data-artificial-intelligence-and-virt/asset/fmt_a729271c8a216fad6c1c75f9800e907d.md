When it comes to creating, planning, implementing, and supervising pharmaceutical policies, they are the key participants and, in a way, the pioneers. When creating new pharmaceutical policies, governments must take the initiative and confer with all pertinent stakeholders before issuing a policy framework. The political will of the government in each nation is a critical factor in the development of effective policies for the safe and efficient utilization, procurement, distribution, and quality control of pharmaceuticals, as well as their equal availability, affordability, and financial risk mitigation [[80]](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_080), [[81]](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_081). Drug regulators have a crucial role in the development and application of pharmaceutical laws in India, particularly in licensing, marketing, distribution, storage, clinical trials, manufacturing, import, export, and import-export. Drug regulators are not, however, at the forefront of the process in nations like India, where the ministries are directly responsible for creating pharmaceutical policy. Because they are knowledgeable about the difficulties and roadblocks that occur in putting these policies into action, they are crucial to the implementation of robust, thorough, and feasible policy creation. Drug regulators are the key players in India for the creation of comprehensive, practical, and robust regulations since they carry out these policies on the ground and have knowledge of the challenges and barriers that arise in doing so [[82]](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_082).

Moreover, producers can make their goods available for consumption by physicians, pharmacists, and patients due to pharmaceutical supply networks and loosely regulated marketplaces. That puts manufacturers in a position to evaluate a drug’s quality and, in turn, its suitability for treating common ailments. They also have the authority to determine drug costs, which lowers the cost of prescriptions for people from lower socioeconomic backgrounds. However, pharmaceutical companies are profitable enterprises whose main objective is to maximize shareholder wealth, as is widely known [[83]](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_083), [[84]](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_084). A vital conduit between the pharmaceutical industry and prescribers is provided by pharmaceutical traders, such as super-stockists, distributors, transportation and forwarding representatives, retailers, wholesalers, and medical representatives. Due to their commercial interests, they stand to gain a great deal from the advertising of branded medications and can significantly influence prescription practices [[85]](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_085). Patients are the last, but certainly not the least, significant stakeholders in the development of pharmaceutical policies since they are the focus of all other stakeholders. As the last users of medications made by licensed, regulated pharmaceutical businesses, they fall under government regulation. Pharmacists distribute these medications, while physicians prescribe them, and pharmaceutical merchants market, supply, and sell them. They serve as the pivot, providing support for all of the other chain links’ tasks and endeavors. The efficacy of a policy framework is determined by its capacity to alleviate patients’ distress and assist them in managing their sickness. As a result, throughout the formulation process, it is essential to carefully analyze the requirements, desires, and ambitions of patients to build a pharmaceutical strategy that is outcome-oriented and patient-driven [[86]](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_086). Other stakeholder groups include chemists, health care funds, health insurance plans, and pharmaceutical distributors. They should be considered clients in a wider sense, given their influence on prescription decisions. The three main clientele in the pharmaceutical sector are payers, physicians, and patients. Consequently, doctors, hospitals, wholesalers, pharmacies, end users, and patients are the three customer sectors that pharmaceutical firms presently focus on with their sales efforts [[87]](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_087), [[88]](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_088), [[89]](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_089).
