
### 3.2.2 Public-private partnerships

Such initiatives, which vary from two-party agreements to large, both privately and publicly financed consortia, involve companies, colleges, universities, governments, and nonprofit and nongovernmental organizations within the life science sector. Open access and controlled collaborations can facilitate sharing, which can be highly beneficial for both presently under-researched and under-focused areas of study. It might create avenues for novel discoveries, which would then benefit public health. While the pharmaceutical industry offers numerous asset-sharing models, the PPP model has garnered attention as a potent means of stimulating innovation across the spectrum of drug discovery and development. To further research in specific disease and therapeutic areas, or to achieve specific objectives, such as target identification, target confirmation, biomarker growth, and drug safety and efficacy evaluation, PPPs have also been introduced. PPPs that are specific to certain diseases or therapeutic areas include the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI), Dundee Kinase Consortium, Accelerating Medicines Partnership (AMP), National Cancer Institute (NCI) Community Cancer Centers Program (NCCP), and Coalition against Major Diseases (CAMD) [[27](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_027), [28](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_028), [29](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_029), [30](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_030), [31](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_031)]. Furthermore, pharmaceutical companies have developed publicly accessible screening libraries, such as the Tres Cantos Antimalarial Set (TCAMS), which was assembled by GlaxoSmithKline (GSK). Numerous individuals within the malaria community have successfully implemented TCAMS [[32](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_032), [33](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_033), [34](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_034), [35](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_035), [36](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_036), [37](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_037)]. The Structural Genomics Consortium (SGC), a nonprofit PPP that was established to identify protein structures that may serve as targets for drug discovery initiatives, receives the backing of numerous pharmaceutical companies [[38](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_038)].

### 3.2.3 Academic-industry partnership

Academic medical centers are investing resources to guarantee successful collaborations with pharmaceutical companies, as they have rekindled interest in doing so. These collaborations shouldn’t be one-sided, with information flowing both ways. Their pharmacological counterparts can provide academic colleagues with valuable insights. The majority of academic centers could benefit from the exchange of experiences regarding technologies, knowledge management, project management, and operational excellence. Pharma must establish a method to provide academicians with access to its extensive chemical libraries, confidential data sets, cutting-edge tools, and technological platforms. It is essential to participate in strategic, cooperative innovation in biomedicine to fully leverage the potential of novel biomarkers or disease targets and to stay up-to-date with the rapid tempo of discovery. Pharma’s present business strategies are predicated on the identification of clinical candidates through acquisitions, partnerships, and mergers. It is essential to forge active partnerships with academia to address critical scientific inquiries and convert novel targets into therapeutic programs, thereby replenishing the future drug pipeline with innovative medications [[39](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_039), [40](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_040)].

New translational models, enabling technologies, and therapeutic mechanisms are being discovered at an ever-increasing rate. The practice of segregating pharmaceutical research within an organization was common in the past. Similarly, academic drug discovery has changed dramatically in recent years, and working with the private sector has become more valuable. The worth goes beyond money. These multidisciplinary collaborations bring together the most innovative concepts and methods for locating new therapeutic targets, solving shortcomings in translational chemical biology, and creating new chemistry and technologies to increase the practical applications of these publicly funded discoveries [[41](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_041), [42](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_042)].

### 3.2.4 Collaboration with start-up

To remain competitive, expand into new markets, manage the mobility of qualified professionals, abbreviate product life cycles, and address the increasing complexity, specialization, and convergence of technologies, businesses have consistently sought new innovative approaches. The number of enterprises is increasing steadily. Startups are brimming with young talent who possess a strong sense of entrepreneurship, a willingness to take risks, a willingness to adapt to change, and the power of digitization, even though they lack resources, brand credibility, and easy access to the market. They are also awash in exciting ideas. To improve their chances of success, they typically need to collaborate with other businesses. Several organizations, including those in the pharmaceutical industry, are collaborating with startups to pursue innovation outside of their boundaries. These initiatives are well-designed and mutually beneficial, generating value for clients [[43](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_043), [44](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_044), [45](#9783111573861_003373.xhtml#b_9783111574288-003_ref_045)].
