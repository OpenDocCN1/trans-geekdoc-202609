
# Healthcare Data Digitization and Big Data Analytics

Healthcare data has been digitized with the help of the growth and development of big data. A new domain called "big data" was introduced because of the data's rapid expansion over the past few years. Big data analytics and other intelligent healthcare solutions, which also save costs and boost efficiency, are making healthcare more mobile and smarter. By employing predictive analytics to forecast pharmacological outcomes and pinpoint the patients who would benefit most from pharmaceutical treatments, chemists can get a deeper understanding of the dangers and adverse effects of drugs [[22](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_022), [23](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_023), [24](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_024)]. Big data's potential benefits in the medical and health sectors have made it a prominent study topic in recent years. Utilizing big data-driven clinical decision support systems has shown promise in treating ailments like Alzheimer's disease. Utilizing big data techniques, electronic medical record optimization has also been accomplished. Data about chemical characteristics, pharmacokinetics, clinical behaviors, and drug toxicity have been created in the fields of toxicology, pharmaceutics, and pharmacology. High-throughput screening (`HTS`) and next-generation sequencing (`NGS`) have significantly increased data output and correspondingly increased the amount of data [[25](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_025), [26](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_026)].

Digital health care will be enabled by big data analysis, which will reduce the requirement for in-person visits, enhance patient outcomes, and guarantee that clinicians have immediate access to all patient medical records. Big data analysis will become a vital tool for healthcare organizations via digital medicine with aggregated real-time data, offering more chances for proactive action and a more comprehensive picture of patients' circumstances [[27](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_027), [28](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_028)]. Data streams are constantly expanding in the modern era as a result of the utilization of electronic medical records, the vast amounts of data transmitted by diverse types of sensors, and the information that patients post on social media. The healthcare sector has always generated vast amounts of data. The medical sector also generates substantial quantities of data, such as genetic information, clinical notes, imaging data, and health-related behavior. Healthcare organizations can assist in disease surveillance, medical decision-making, and public health management by effectively utilizing the data [[29](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_029)]. The problem can be addressed with the use of electronic health records (`EHRs`), which are electronic patient health data compilations in digital format. Increasingly, `EHRs` are being implemented in poor countries. Through a strong network connection, these records may be exchanged between different healthcare settings. Digitally recorded health records are less likely to include duplicate data as they can be shared, updated, and altered throughout different healthcare systems, reducing the chance of missing any necessary paperwork. Physicians, patients, and hospital management all immediately benefit from `EHR` initiatives [[30](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_030)]. The electronic medical record is a critical element of e-health utilization, as it contains information regarding a patient's health. An electronic medical record is comprised of legal documents that are generated in hospital contexts. Consequently, these data serve as the electronic health record's principal information source. The term "e-health" was first coined in the early twenty-first century and denotes the integration of contemporary communication and information technologies into the provision of healthcare services, according to a researcher. Effective management of electronic health and the ability to share medical information over greater geographic distances require a multidisciplinary team with experience in computer science, instrumentation, and communications [[31](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_031), [32](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_032)]. In big analytics, it is one of the crucial steps, consisting of five stages: data production, acquisition, preservation, analytics, and visualization [fig 1.1.](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_fig_001) The main source of data production is the Internet, which is followed by data acquisition, which entails gathering, sorting, and exchanging enormous volumes of data. Large infrastructure is used to store data efficiently to guarantee its use in the future. Utilizing `ML`, computational intelligence, and data mining techniques, data analytics can extract data from vast volumes of data, guaranteeing effective use and improved use in the future [[33](#9783111573861_000727.xhtml#b_9783111574288-001_ref_033)].
