
# University-industry collaborative research and development (UIC R&D)

University-industry collaborative research and development (UIC R&D) is a term that encompasses pharmaceutical innovation processes that are more transparent. In the process, scientists and industry-based organizations collaborate to identify technological breakthroughs and knowledge, and subsequently develop new drugs based on these discoveries [[76](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_076), [77](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_077), [78](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_078)]. Molecular, biological, and chemical pharmacology knowledge is advanced through formal or informal collaborations between universities and industry groups. The goal of these collaborations is to translate the knowledge into new drug creation and commercialization initiatives that are beneficial to society and the economy. It is known as pharmaceutical UIC R&D [[79](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_079), [80](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_080)]. Using diverse UIC research and development strategies, such as establishing technology transfer offices (TTOs) and initiating academic start-up company incubation programs, universities endeavor to enhance and fortify the ties between academia and industry to advance an efficient and productive innovation ecosystem for novel drug discovery [[81](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_081)]. However, as academic and industrial organizations have different goals, guiding principles, and schedules, there are still very few drug development processes that are fully and flawlessly driven by university-industry collaboration. These elements play a part in conflict resolution and negotiation issues. There is little, if any, comprehensive research on the composition and coordination of pharmaceutical R&D partnerships between academic institutions and private businesses. Due to the phenomenon, a large number of scholarly contributions have focused on the outcomes of pharmaceutical R&D in one or a small number of university-industry collaborations. Gaining insight into the causes and factors influencing university-industry R&D in pharmaceuticals can help increase productivity and effectiveness [[82](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_082)]. The potential for productive specialization in a variety of institutional contexts may be presented by collaborating with others. Rather than incorporating industry practices into academia, industry collaboration could improve the academic framework by decreasing patenting rates and increasing publication rates. Collaboration between academia and industry in research areas with both scientific and commercial potential may result in a more productive division of tasks and responsibilities, enabling each party to concentrate on the aspects of their institutional environments that are most rewarding: for academia, it would be the development of deeper scientific discoveries, while for business, it would be the commercialization of these discoveries. Previous research has suggested that academic institutions have a comparative advantage in the investigation of more fundamental, basic concerns, while companies have a relative advantage in development and commercialization. Depending on their interests, scientists tend to specialize in either industry or academia. Scientists who are more inclined to enter academia are those who have a stronger “taste for science” or who prefer non-financial rewards; on the other hand, scientists who are more inclined toward applied research and financial incentives tend to enter the industry. Furthermore, the intellectual and material exchange that occurs within academic institutions is especially conducive to curiosity-driven research, while the resources and experience of businesses facilitate effective commercialization [[83](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_083), [84](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_084)].

## 4.5 Challenges faced by smart pharma

[Figure 4.2](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_fig_002) illustrates the hurdles and downsides that must be considered, notwithstanding the potential benefits of AI in drug discovery. The acquisition of pertinent data is a major challenge. Extensive data aggregations are generally necessary for the training of AI methodologies. The reliability and precision of the results can sometimes be compromised by the limited number, inconsistent quality, or poor quality of the available data [[85](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_085), [86](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_086), [87](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_087)]. It offers an important quantity of data, which is used by models that are trained gradually. The company suffered greatly from the data acquisition model via different databases because it was more expensive. Furthermore, a substantial amount of trustworthy data is required to guarantee the pharmaceutical analysis. Another obstacle keeping the pharmaceutical and biological sectors from fully adopting AI is the shortage of skilled personnel to oversee the AI-based platforms [[88](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_088)]. The lack of funding for small organizations and the use of AI and ML by large corporations, such as pharmaceutical companies, to replace their human workforce, lead to job losses. In ML processes, decisions are generated by the input data; however, the processes from input to output are not transparent. The input data is transformed through several intricate layers in CNNs, leading to unpredictable behavior in the end [[89](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_089)]. Furthermore, the absence of a rationale for predictions that are specific to black-box ML models has an impact on ML predictions as a result of human error in data interpretation. But logical models and decision trees, like relational ML, don’t show the fundamental problem. The dependence on model performance is impacted by human users’ incapacity to understand anticipated outcomes. There are still concerns regarding prediction confidence despite the ML models’ encouraging initial results. When an algorithm attempts to search domains outside of its applicability domain, it is more likely to result in a pathological failure in the hypothesis produced by ML techniques [[90](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_090)].
