# Chapter 7 AI-driven clinical decision support systems for pharma executives

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence (AI) is a powerful tool that makes data processing, analysis, and interpretation possible. The following chapter offers information on the AI algorithms employed by pharmaceutical corporations in their clinical decision support systems. There are difficulties in implementing AI-CDSS, and future possibilities are also covered. AI-based algorithms such as `logistic regression`, `support vector computations`, `naive Bayes`, `random forests`, `decision tree models`, and `ANN` are expected to be critical in optimizing medication compositions and enhancing treatment outcomes, while also anticipating potential toxicity along with side effects in patient-specific dose formulation and safety evaluations. AI-powered CDSS helps doctors, nurses, and patients make informed decisions by evaluating patient data. `NLP` improves patient communication and electronic health records. Through meticulous analysis of the patient’s signs, symptoms, medical records, and contextual information, an `NLP`-driven Clinical Decision Support System can propose potential diagnoses, treatment strategies, and pharmacological interventions. These difficulties include organizational and social factors, human factors, and a lack of confidence among medical professionals.

**Keywords:** clinical decision support, pharma, support vector machine, artificial neural network, decision tree, ethical considerations

## 7.1 Introduction

The rising significance of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML), along with the expanding accessibility of clinical data, has intensified interest in AI applications, namely computerized clinical decision support systems (CDSS). Initially, CDSSs were regarded as capable of supplanting doctors in decision-making processes. By analyzing significant data, a more sophisticated and contemporary understanding of the role of CDSSs is to aid physicians in making better decisions than the healthcare provider or the CDSS could accomplish separately. Contemporary Clinical Decision Support Systems (CDSSs) often offer recommendations to doctors, who are anticipated to make independent decisions and dismiss any suggestions they consider unsuitable [[1]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_001). Large multinational pharmaceutical corporations have led the industry’s vigorous use of AI, ML, and data mining technology to minimize the duration and expenses associated with medication research and development. During the business and management planning stage of drug development, artificial intelligence technologies must be strategically employed in addition to the exploratory drug discovery procedure [[2]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_002). Pharmaceutical companies wonder what kind of medications to target for their next research project, taking into account the managerial and market conditions of their business [[3]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_003). The majority of the time, choices about drugs under development may have been made qualitatively based on the opinions of the board of directors of the company or the technologies that the companies own. In other words, human judgment has historically played a larger role in drug development decision-making than formal analytic or data-based methodologies. An efficient model or approach that can raise the success rate of drug development is therefore desperately needed [[4]](#9783111573861_010289.xhtml#b_9783111574288-007_ref_004).
