
## 5.2 AI application in pharmaceutical manufacturing

The complexity of manufacturing has grown compared to previous years due to notable advancements and recent advancements in pharmaceutical technology. The quality and efficacy of a tested product are also essential for the intricate process. Modern pharmaceutical manufacturing systems incorporate human expertise into machines to improve processes. AI in pharmaceutical production may boost the industry [[11](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_011)]. AI tools will help mechanize many pharmaceutical activities. Not only can it help with manufacturing process issues, but it can also support numerical simulation. By utilizing other state-of-the-art manufacturing technologies, AI can be incorporated into the medication manufacturing process to achieve the desired level of outcomes. Potential applications of AI in pharma production processes were listed in a discussion paper released by the U.S. FDA. AI-based pharmaceutical quality control and assurance systems are anticipated to yield benefits [[12](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_012)]. Pharmaceutical companies use bands to ensure product quality, even though they may be inaccurate. AI applications in the pharmaceutical sector can forecast minute changes that may affect the efficacy and security of the products. AI-based pharmaceutical quality assurance and control can enhance process accuracy and product quality [[13](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_013)]. Moreover, preclinical lead molecule failure rates can be decreased by using computational methods with multi-objective refinement. AI in drug design uses computer software to evaluate, explore, and learn from enormous quantities of pharmaceutical data to find new therapeutic compounds. Combining ML advances in a unified, completely automated way achieves it [[14](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_014)]. Machine learning (ML)-based methodologies, a derivative of AI, emphasizes the transformation of extensive biomedical large data into novel insights and practical knowledge rather than the theoretical advancement of intricate physicochemical principles [[15](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_015)]. These days, deep learning (DL) paradigms and other AI technologies have incredible feature extraction and generalization capabilities that make them highly intriguing for drug design. While DL approaches can automatically extract characteristics from the input data and reorganize fundamental traits into complex characteristics via multi-layer feature extraction, conventional ML approaches use manually generated attributes [[16](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_016), [17](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_017)].

The pharmaceutical sector is urged to innovate via process analytical technology (PAT) and QbD methodologies. The QbD approach underscores the importance of understanding the product and process in determining the key material attributes (KMAs) and critical process parameters (CPPs) that substantially affect the critical quality attributes (CQAs) of both the product and process. It results in the creation of a design area with acceptable quality standards. Operating inside a space that has been approved by regulations for design does not constitute a change; therefore, production flexibility can be increased. AI primarily refers to computational techniques that carry out tasks that are commonly associated with cognitive processes similar to those of humans, like pattern recognition as well as decision-making. These tasks are carried out by ML techniques in AI, which learn from a dataset and generate a response without explicit programming. As the training dataset expands, ML can adapt the model behavior to continually enhance its performance, which is particularly useful for data-driven manufacturing applications [[18](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_018), [19](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_019), [20](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_020), [21](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_021), [22](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_022)]. Additionally, the medical regulatory bodies are becoming more receptive to AI/ML techniques. However, more investigation is needed to prepare for smart manufacturing concerning the ML process’s real-time applicability in the pharmaceutical manufacturing process. ML can use past process data to estimate the crystallization result. For example, researchers used data from various industrial batch crystallizations to create ANN and GA models. Nine descriptors were used to model the crystal density, which, after optimization, led to a notable improvement in the final product and assisted in identifying the critical parameters. Based on a few experimental runs as well as four input variables, the design area of a co-crystallization process could be investigated in a different study. ANN inputs for the operating variables (moisture, supersaturation, agitation speed, along with seeding properties) can also yield a more accurate crystal growth rate estimate than numerous non-linear regressions [[23](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_023), [24](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_024)]. Furthermore, the behavior of the compacted powdered mixture within the tablet press must be considered initially when constructing a tableting process. For each filling, the blend’s flowability must be sufficiently high to ensure that almost identical quantities of powder enter the die. Certain researchers employed a feed-forward and back propagation artificial neural network (FF-BP ANN) to forecast the rate at which specific powders would traverse a circular aperture. The input parameters for powders typically include bulk density, tapped density, particle size, aspect ratio, roundness, convexity, and true density.
