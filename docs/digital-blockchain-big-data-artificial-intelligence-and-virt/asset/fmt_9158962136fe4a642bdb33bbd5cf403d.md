
The initial phase of conventional drug development is the identification of a target protein that is related to a disease. The process is resource-intensive as well as time-consuming. The procedure entails the examination of extensive chemical libraries in search of compounds that interact with the target. As a result, the drug-like properties are improved through iterative optimization procedures [[33](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_033)]. The strategy is typically distinguished by a protracted implementation process and a high rate of attrition. Nonetheless, the paradigm is seeing a transition due to the application of ML techniques. The forecasting of biological activity, dissolution, stability, toxicity, absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination (ADMET) characteristics of prospective drug candidates exemplifies an effective application of ML. Using the analysis of extensive datasets that include physicochemical properties, activity profiles, and molecular structures, ML algorithms can detect connections and patterns that humans may have overlooked. Because of this, it is no longer necessary to conduct costly and time-consuming experimental studies to ascertain how a substance will operate in biological structures [[34](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_034)]. Compound screening and selection are acknowledged applications of ML in drug discovery. Novel molecules can be anticipated to demonstrate activity against a given target by ML models trained on existing active and inactive compounds [[35](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_035)].

## 4.2.4 Deep learning in smart pharma

Across a variety of domains, such as computer gaming, speech recognition, natural language processing, computer vision, and autonomous vehicles, DL has achieved remarkable success. It has reaped the rewards of the endlessly expanding availability of computational capacity and the expansion of the volume of data. DL is distinguished from the majority of other ML methods by the adaptability of its neural network architecture. The designs consist of fully connected feed-forward networks, recurrent neural networks (RNNs), and convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The natural application of multilayered feed-forward neural networks for biological function predictions has resulted from the continued application of single-layer NNs in QSAR modeling, as data size as well as processing power have increased [[36](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_036)]. It was not anticipated a few years ago that RNNs would be used in de novo design, which has been a somewhat unexpected development. CNNs have demonstrated amazing success in computer vision since high-throughput imaging technology has become widely available, and they are currently a popular choice for processing biological images. In recent years, DL applications have significantly accelerated pharmaceutical research and drug discovery. Their utility has exceeded expectations and demonstrated promise in addressing various drug discovery problems. Their numerous applications in scientific and technological fields, particularly in biomedicine and bioinformatics, are apparent. The automated synthesis of novel chemical compounds with defined characteristics, the prediction of molecular structure and function, and the sophisticated image analysis skills of DL render it an attractive contender for drug discovery. The application of DL can benefit every stage of the drug discovery process, including target identification, prognostic biomarker discovery, drug development, design, and prediction of synergistic antagonism [[37](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_037)].

## 4.2.5 Internet of things in smart pharma

The pharmaceutical industry is one where Internet of Things (IoT) is important. Scholars worldwide are endeavoring to implement this fascinating technology within the pharmaceutical sector. Because of their mutual internet connectivity, most researchers predict how much work they are going to do in the field of the next-generation medicinal product revolution. The network uses dynamic data to connect to things, including appliances, cars, trucks, and industrial equipment, as well as other Internet-connected utilities [[38](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_038)]. The IoT is the term used to describe the physical connection of software, embedded devices, and electronic sensors, which enables the sharing of data across a network from any location. IoT’s primary contributions to the pharmaceutical industry are its ability to track health issues, forecast patient-related future events through the use of evolutionary technologies, transform data, save costs, and save lives [[39](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_039)]. Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) technology has shown great promise for the pharmaceutical industry’s manufacturing and supply chain processes. But unlike bar codes, Internet of Things (IoT) devices are never scanned because they communicate real-time data to other devices through the Internet. IoT is essential to the management of all services because it provides medical services in the field of pharmaceuticals [[40](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_040)]. In addition, IoT could be helpful in monitoring and streamlining various departments for real-time control and monitoring to improve production efficiency during the pharmaceutical product’s manufacturing. IoT can be used in pharmaceutical supply chain management and warehousing to monitor the drug product’s storage conditions in real-time and to improve visibility, which will increase operational effectiveness [[41](#9783111573861_004895.xhtml#b_9783111574288-004_ref_041)].
