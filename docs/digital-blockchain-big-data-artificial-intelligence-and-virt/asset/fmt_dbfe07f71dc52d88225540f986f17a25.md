
## 6.5 Ethical challenges AI-enabled for clinical trials

Studies on the ethics of AI in healthcare are blatantly unbalanced, emphasizing topics other than global health, and more especially disease surveillance. There are few explanations of the ethical, legal, or practical issues needed for widespread adoption or large-scale implementation of AI-driven treatment in global health, according to a large portion of research in this area. Also, there is the interdependence of sources and data types needed for reporting and decision-support, as well as the surveillance of diseases at the individual and population levels [[117](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_117), [118](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_118), [119](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_119)].

Applications of AI in imaging, electronic medical records (`EMR`), laboratory assessment and treatment, novel drug discovery, preventive strategies, precision medicine, biological data analysis, procedural optimization, and information management for healthcare organizations have profoundly transformed the medical sector. Nevertheless, the scientific domain must confront several ethical and legal issues. It is essential to consider several parameters, as depicted in [Fig. 6.4](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_fig_004). The elements encompass social disparities, data protection and privacy, informed permission, medical consultation, empathy, and sympathy [[120](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_120), [121](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_121), [122](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_122), [123](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_123), [124](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_124)].

Union-based data processors or controllers manage personal information in compliance with these requirements, alongside the operations of international organizations and corporations to guarantee sufficient protection of personal data [[125](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_125)]. The Genetic Information Non-Discrimination Act (`GINA`) prohibits employers in the United States from making discriminatory decisions based on an individual’s genetic health information. AI can assess consumer health data and medical device imagery, thereby expediting health research and improving diagnosis and outcomes. Moreover, the procedure of engaging with a healthcare professional regarding ethical disclosure, decision-making ability, and documentation is referred to as informed consent [[127](#9783111573861_008368.xhtml#b_9783111574288-006_ref_127)].

Ethical duty dictates that patients are entitled to information regarding their diagnosis, health status, planned care, therapy outcomes, test results, charges, insurance plans, and other pertinent medical details. Patient consent must be unequivocal, explicit, and suitable for the intended purpose. Concerns about the issue have intensified when AI is implemented in healthcare applications.
