## Smart-connected healthcare (SCH) and innovative solutions

More creative and ingenious solutions will be used to improve patient-physician interactions as a result of smart-connected healthcare, or SCH. Mobile apps will enable the network to hyperlink geographically to the closest hospital in the event of an accident and transfer data before admission, potentially saving lives [[13](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_013)]. It is anticipated that new self-care methods made possible by technological advancements in the digital realm will be adopted in the future. Continuous as well as flash glucose monitoring systems, for instance, allow the sensors to serve as display devices, without wires transmit glucose data to a reader or mobile application, and generate hypo- and hyper-glycemia alerts [[14](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_014)]. Eye-tracking can be used to guide patient-focused product development of new systems for drug delivery (self-injection systems) through the procedure of usability testing. The development could greatly improve how the various parts of the linked system like the sensors, pumps for infusion, handheld devices, and mobile apps interact with one another, increasing the product’s overall efficacy [[15](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_015)]. Cutting-edge technologies like virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) can raise the bar for medical and healthcare practices by enabling users to interact with and completely lose themselves in these worlds. These technologies include head-mounted displays, haptic devices, AR glasses, software, and tracking sensors. In plastic surgery, virtual reality (VR) has become popular and has potential uses in navigation, training, and surgical preparation. Training using VR has been well-studied. Currently, the market is filled with several commercial VR goods for medical education [[16](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_016)].

# 13.2 Future trends and innovation of AI in drug discovery

Drug development is still a lengthy, costly, ineffective, and difficult process with a low rate of new-effective therapeutic discovery, especially in light of the complexity of disorders. Researchers have overcome obstacles in drug discovery with the help of AI/ML-based techniques, and these approaches have a wide range of applications in drug discovery programs for different diseases. In general, researchers from academia and business have expressed a great deal of interest in the application of AI/ML to pharmaceutical development [[17](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_017)]. Furthermore, a large number of pharmaceutical companies have collaborated with colleges and universities and AI start-ups, as well as invested in their AI-based research initiatives. In reaction to the COVID-19 pandemic, academia and industry have recently shown an impressive level of cooperation. Due to open-sourced platforms, data and pipelines to AI labs and businesses can now be shared. For instance, the public can now view the 3D models of SARS-CoV-2 proteins that Google Deepmind’s AlphaFold system predicted. The application of AI technology to enhance drug discovery is still in its infancy, even though AI has become a vital instrument for revolutionizing the medical development process [[18](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_018)]. Because most next-generation AI structures are “black boxes,” finding new drugs becomes more challenging. Because AI/ML results are difficult to interpret, their applications are restricted. It isn’t true for ML models that are simpler (like XGBoost, TensorFlow, Lasso, Ridge, and Elastic Net) or more complex (like DNN). As a result, scientists are unable to understand the biological processes that underlie the model’s output. Further complicating troubleshooting is the unpredictable malfunctions of these models. As a result, creating strategies for unlocking the DL black boxes is crucial [[19](#9783111573861_018790.xhtml#b_9783111574288-013_ref_019)].
