
# Role of Big Data Analytics in Post-Marketing Surveillance

In the medical field, clinical accounts are the primary means of communication. Compared to the generically coded portions of electronic health records (EHRs), narrative notes offer a more comprehensive and distinct account of the patient’s history and assessments, which enhances the context for clinical decision-making. The study of natural language processing, or NLP, is a branch of artificial intelligence that examines the analysis and synthesis of data expressed in natural language. It has repeatedly demonstrated that evidence can be taken out of clinical narratives and made available for large-scale analysis at a later time. The primary justification for machine learning’s popularity as a panacea for the knowledge-gathering information obstacles is that manually annotating data is a simpler task than actually eliciting knowledge [39]. However, the amount of data needed to train a machine-learning model might take just as long to annotate as the process of eliciting knowledge [40]. Similar to the principle of energy conservation, it appears that the information needed to guide the development of a precise computational model is merely converted from one format to another. Machine learning relies on implicit knowledge in the form of annotations and their distribution, with acquiring moments of these annotations being almost constant, as opposed to explicit understanding in the form of rules. The accessibility of clinical narratives poses a challenge to the machine learning approach due to the sensitive nature of medical data and security concerns [41]. Due to these issues (i.e., lack of manually annotated data), the training data may not be representative enough, which could lead to poor performance from the associated ML models [42].

As a component of governmental control over the distribution of medical devices (MDs), safety monitoring is crucial both globally [43, 44, 45] and in Russia [46, 47]. To guarantee the effectiveness and safety of medical devices used daily, the following kind of monitoring is carried out. Greater oversight is necessary when using high-risk (class III) MDs. Therefore, even in the case that there are no risks or adverse events associated with the product, the Board of the Eurasian Economic Commission’s Decision [48] mandated that risk class III medical devices be inspected annually for three years following the issuance of a registration certificate. Software as a medical device (SaMD-AI), which is based on machine learning technologies, uses technological solutions to mimic human cognition and produce outcomes that are at least equivalent to human performance [49, 50]. SaMD-AI cannot interpret data, so it needs extra supervision while in use. When applied to a population that differs from that used in machine learning, etc., it could lead to bias [51, 52]. Furthermore, through ongoing (self-)learning, it may integrate deep neural networks, in contrast to SaMD without AI technologies. The expeditious updating of SaMD-AI as an invisible medical device and the ensuing extension of the SaMD-AI re-registration period have raised concerns among researchers. The FDA (Food and Drug Administration) states that a unique registration system covering the entirety of the product life cycle is necessary for the following kind of medical device. The plan needs to be flexible enough to accommodate changes during SaMD-AI adaptation and use, all the while preserving the medical device’s efficacy and safety [53]. Other authors go into detail about the procedures that must be followed to guarantee that SaMD-AI is promptly and securely registered and that these products are integrated into actual clinical settings. The authors emphasize that the only way to rule out any potential scatter of SaMD-AI features is to assess SaMD-AI performance using actual clinical data. SaMD-AI’s developers should be aware of any potential risks, such as difficulties when interacting with novel patient populations and inadvertently harmful health outcomes. It is imperative to examine both the predictive indicators, such as positive or negative predictive values, and the SaMD-AI main performance indicators (metrics), including the area under the feature curve [52].

## 11.3 Role of Big Data Analytics in Post-Marketing Surveillance

The enormous amount of structured and unstructured data that is produced daily by machines, groups, and people is referred to as “big data.” It includes enormous volumes of data that are difficult to handle or evaluate with conventional data processing methods. Big data is growing exponentially and has several advantages (Fig. 11.2). One major factor contributing to the growth is the introduction of social media, the web, and connected devices. Big data’s velocity is one of its distinguishing features. To gain actionable insights into the unprecedented rate at which data is being generated, organizations must be able to gather, process, and evaluate data in real time. Social media platforms, for example, produce enormous amounts of data from interactions between users, posts, likes, and shares [53]. Businesses can make better business decisions and increase customer engagement by utilizing the data to better understand trends, sentiment analysis, and customer preferences. The diversity of big data is another important feature. It includes an extensive range of data types, for example, transaction records, text, photos, videos, and sensor data. Such diversity is beyond the capabilities of conventional databases and information processing methods. However, due to technological developments, such as distributed computing frameworks such as Hadoop and Spark, businesses are now able to store, handle, and analyze sizable and diverse datasets. Organizations can improve decision-making and innovation by integrating and analyzing multiple data sources to find previously hidden correlations, patterns, and insights [54].
