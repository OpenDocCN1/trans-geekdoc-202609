
## 5.6 Conclusion

Today’s legislators and regulators have considerably higher standards for corporate governance and compliance. There is a noticeable shift in favor of obligatory and complete corporate trust and transparency. Legislators and regulators in the European Union are considering creating compliance regarding the use of digital technologies, specifically AI, for the first time. A few years ago, the EU Commission released an AI regulation to guarantee that technology is applied in a way that upholds EU ideals, principles, and fundamental rights [[107](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_107), [108](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_108)]. An illustration of a measure implemented to ensure the security, transparency, ethics, neutrality, and human oversight of AI is the EU Commission’s AI Act. The EU now categorizes AI applications according to their risk levels, and corresponding norms and regulations have been established based on the classification. For example, social scoring is too dangerous to use, and transparent rules are all that low-risk chatbots need to abide by. Despite the significance of compliance in the framework of digital technologies, neither legal nor digital technology experts are currently providing thorough scientific approaches or focused attention to the issue. Consequently, AI continues to be the sole notable instance in which the European legislature has looked into these matters. The EU Medical Device Regulation imposes stringent regulatory requirements before medical devices, including AI software devices, may be used in clinical practice. The EU MDR [[109](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_109)], which replaced the EU Medical Device Directive, must be fulfilled. For the first time, many of the terms and specifications of the EU MDR also apply to medical device software created by healthcare organizations for internal use.

Software meant to offer data for any of the objectives quantified in the explanation of a medical device [[109](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_109)], including monitoring, treatment, prevention, diagnosis, and alleviation of disease, is subject to EU MDR regulation. It covers both software that offers a diagnosis or treatment on its own and software that only offers data to help a medical practitioner arrive at the best diagnosis or course of treatment. The EU MDR remains applicable regardless of whether the software is an executable program, dynamic website, web service, script, or even a simple spreadsheet macro. Additionally, the software is considered a medical device if it is intended for medical purposes and is intended to be used with humans or their data, regardless of the complexity of the processing, the risk it poses to the patient or user, whether or not a healthcare professional uses it, or the computing platform on which it is running [[110](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_110), [111](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_111), [112](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_112)]. AI could potentially make predictions regarding the complex relationships between drug efficacy and immunological responses to substantially improve drug discovery [[113](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_113), [114](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_114)]. AI could also be beneficial for more intricate tasks, such as the analysis of multiple genomic issues, or for simpler ones, such as the best practices for electronic documentation [[115](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_115), [116](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_116)]. AI has been deployed to enhance medical reporting related to oncology and radiology [[117](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_117), [118](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_118)]. For analytical labs, where protocols and procedures can occasionally total hundreds or even thousands of pages, and where the quantity of pages grows with every new treatment, it is encouraging. AI can quickly create useful methods and predict text based on input data [[119](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_119)]. AI language translation tools can also be applied to worldwide shared methods to improve interlaboratory reproducibility. Furthermore, data produced during stability and new batch cGMP testing needs to be carefully examined, and any inconsistencies need to be looked into through quality investigations. AI tools are capable of conducting investigations and proofreading analytical data to find errors [[120](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_120)].

In delicate areas, AI systems should be employed with extreme caution, as judgments made using skewed data or incorrect criteria may have far-reaching effects [[121](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_121)]. Consequently, these systems necessitate extensive validation that effectively addresses issues that are distinctive to AI, such as the black box problem. Numerous writers speculate that XAI might be useful in the validation, but particular strategies have not yet been created and assessed. Four phases make up the validation concept, which can be carried out repeatedly or step-by-step. The typical design tasks involved in creating and implementing a new IS are handled in the first phase, known as the setup phase. The technical setup and performance phase that follows covers the steps specifically related to AI development to create a dependable AI system. Thus far, the process bears similarities to validation methodologies or black-box AI system performance assessments [[122](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_122)]. Predefined performance measures may suffice for AI systems operating in insensitive environments; however, further effort is necessary to verify that the AI system has been developed and is functioning as intended under the proposed validation concept. The third phase of the XAI Assessment offers the recommended XAI approach, which gives an example of how to use decision rules and/or spaces appropriately. The final stage, the stress test, is designed to ascertain the limits of the AI system’s use, building upon the evaluation. A validation approach was developed in a research and development project with the assistance of specialists in technology, academia, industry, and GMP auditing. This strategy was applied with success in the pharmaceutical manufacturing sector [[123](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_123)]. The business putting the AI systems into place must still evaluate a few elements of the created validation strategy, though. It was therefore recommended that future research focus on creating and establishing guidelines and standards for sensitive use cases, such as the pharmaceutical industry. It might result in less validation work being needed, which would then make comprehensive validation techniques more feasible for use cases involving less sensitive data [[124](#9783111573861_006599.xhtml#b_9783111574288-005_ref_124)].
