With every new development, discovery, and invention, people in every country on Earth deal with rising levels of social inequality as well as a dwindling conviction in social justice. Artificial intelligence exacerbates social inequality, despite making it easier to access more information regarding science and technology, international politics, environmental issues, and world events [[88](#9783111573861_015802.xhtml#b_9783111574288-011_ref_088)]. It’s also probable that medical machines and people won’t evolve simultaneously because human feelings are different. Unlike autonomous (robotic) systems, physicians, as well as other health care providers, should consult with or refer colleagues for advice. It is not possible in autonomous systems [[89](#9783111573861_015802.xhtml#b_9783111574288-011_ref_089)]. Robotic physicians and nurses cannot perform the following kind of work. Because robotic nurses and doctors lack human qualities, patients will no longer interact with them with compassion, kindness, or appropriate behavior. Patients won’t be able to behave appropriately either. Additionally, mobile devices are used in the majority of studies on mobility problem detection techniques to gather relevant signals, send them to other devices for analysis, visualize the outcomes, and notify users of the results [[90](#9783111573861_015802.xhtml#b_9783111574288-011_ref_090), [91](#9783111573861_015802.xhtml#b_9783111574288-011_ref_091)]. Transparency is the capacity to understand how an artificial intelligence system makes decisions. More precisely, the black box’s basic analytical procedures and decision-making process are opaque. Algorithms that are programmed on biased data or that disregard particular demographic traits make these problems worse [[92](#9783111573861_015802.xhtml#b_9783111574288-011_ref_092)].

The sub-issue focuses primarily on the unnecessary or possible harm that AI tools or risky AI in the healthcare industry can cause. Enhancing the quality of care and patient safety, expanding healthcare access, giving patients and doctors local real-time advice, and spotting medical emergencies such as sepsis are just a few advantages of integrating AI into healthcare. However, AI-enabled support services are not error-proof, and their algorithms may suggest dangerous options or advice to patients that could endanger their health [[93](#9783111573861_015802.xhtml#b_9783111574288-011_ref_093)].

## 11.5 Conclusion

A crucial procedure in the pharmaceutical sciences, post-marketing surveillance (PMS) keeps an eye on a drug’s safety after it has been put on sale following clinical trials. It assists in identifying adverse and advantageous effects, off-label usage of drugs, orphan drug issues, and difficulties encountered when carrying out global clinical trials on pediatric patients. The Medical Device Regulation (MDR) of the European Union (EU) and the In Vitro Diagnostic Medical Device Regulation (IVDR) have increased PMS requirements for in vitro diagnostic and medical device companies. Compliance with these regulations requires a significant financial investment in new policies, procedures, and skilled staff. Natural language processing, machine learning, and deep learning are examples of artificial intelligence (AI) technologies that can cover a wider range of data and improve signal detection speed and accuracy. Deep-learning methods outperformed the accuracy rate threshold in a Celgene and IBM study, indicating that artificial intelligence (AI) may be able to increase data efficacy, consistency, and reporting efficiency. AI solutions can assist in the development of flexible, evidence-based quality control frameworks that promote creativity and better-informed decision-making. They can also assist in meeting EU MDR/IVDR regulations for PMS practitioners and manufacturers of medical devices. In 1966, automatic essay scoring (AES) technology was first introduced to simplify and expedite essay scoring. It can either supplement or replace human scorers. However, there are a lot of obstacles to privacy protection when using AI for post-marketing surveillance. If businesses can profit through or monetize the data, they might not be inspired to constantly uphold privacy protection, and the existing legal penalties are not enough to deter such behavior. Since healthcare AI applications involve complex algorithms, giving private AI companies access to patient health data presents privacy risks, even in situations where “anonymization” is used. Because of the speed at which these technologies are developing and being used, ethical issues are becoming more important.
