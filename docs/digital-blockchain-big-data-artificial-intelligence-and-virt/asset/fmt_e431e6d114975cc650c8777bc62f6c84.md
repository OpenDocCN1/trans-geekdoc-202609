
# Laboratory Medicine and Digitalization

Laboratory medicine consistently ranks among the medical specialties with the highest degree of digitalization. Since their beginnings, automation, electronic data transfer, and electronic reporting have become increasingly prevalent [[51](#9783111573861_014526.xhtml#b_9783111574288-010_ref_051)]. Medical laboratories maintain extensive databases comprising quality control reports and test results. Moreover, they typically have complex quality management systems established. Thus, it is not unexpected that laboratory medicine exemplifies a model discipline for the digitization of healthcare.

Conversely, recent advancements in data science, including `ML` and artificial intelligence, have not yet completely permeated the field of laboratory medicine. The experience-based opinion paper does not primarily concentrate on the latest and forthcoming breakthroughs in AI and `ML` within laboratory medicine, since several recently published publications offer a comprehensive assessment.

## Big Data in Laboratory Medicine

Big Data encompasses various sources, including diagnostic data, laboratory information systems (`LIS`), proteomics, and Next Generation Sequencing (`NGS`) [[52](#9783111573861_014526.xhtml#b_9783111574288-010_ref_052), [53](#9783111573861_014526.xhtml#b_9783111574288-010_ref_053), [54](#9783111573861_014526.xhtml#b_9783111574288-010_ref_054), [55](#9783111573861_014526.xhtml#b_9783111574288-010_ref_055)].

Data that can be easily found must be stored to facilitate its retrieval. To assign individual results to the respective patients and collection times for “standard” examinations is typically accomplished through the use of a patient identifier (`PID`) and date. It is easier said than done, depending on how the laboratory is set up. Some potential pitfalls include the possibility that different patients at various branch laboratories may have the same `PIDs` assigned to them, or that analyses done on emergency patients who are not identified may not be able to be linked to the correct individual once their identity is known. Furthermore, for research purposes, results from various patients may be consolidated under a “collective” `PID` [[56](#9783111573861_014526.xhtml#b_9783111574288-010_ref_056)].

Moreover, data may get convoluted when samples are recorded based on the scheduled collection date instead of the actual collection date, resulting in analysis timestamps preceding the collection. Specialized examination equipment is frequently not integrated with the `LIS`, complicating its retrieval.

### Semantic Interoperability

Semantic interoperability represents a significant and potentially important facet of Big Data in laboratory medicine. Each data item must be assigned distinctly and semantically; the optimal method for it is the utilization of standardized coding, such as `LOINC` (Logical Observation Identifiers Names and Codes) [[57](#9783111573861_014526.xhtml#b_9783111574288-010_ref_057)].

The specifications of research efforts employing laboratory analysis require precise granularity; it is insufficient to identify them merely by their superficial title. Thus, any type of creatinine measurement may be enough for a “safety lab measurement,” but it would be inadequate for technique comparison studies or for determining reference intervals [[58](#9783111573861_014526.xhtml#b_9783111574288-010_ref_058)].

# 10.4 Applications of AI in Regulatory Affairs

AI can aid `RA` by supplying knowledge on the latest advancements in the industry, particularly about the prospective benefits of AI for drug research and regulatory approval. By finding new opportunities, automating tasks, and enhancing decision-making, artificial intelligence (`AI`) can assist professionals in drug discovery in making decisions more quickly and effectively. It enables them to launch new medications faster. Pharmaceutical companies’ `RA` departments or groups act as a channel to national and international regulatory bodies whose purpose is to approve products following current laws and regulations. Consequently, products are approved by regulatory agencies based on data and records submitted during the drug discovery and product development process [[59](#9783111573861_014526.xhtml#b_9783111574288-010_ref_059), [60](#9783111573861_014526.xhtml#b_9783111574288-010_ref_060)].

A different secondary algorithm may be used to evaluate the efficacy of some applications [[61](#9783111573861_014526.xhtml#b_9783111574288-010_ref_061)].
