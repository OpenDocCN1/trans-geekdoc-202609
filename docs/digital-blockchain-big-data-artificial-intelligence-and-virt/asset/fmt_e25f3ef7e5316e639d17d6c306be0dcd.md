The FDA’s Pre-Cert program presently implements fundamental measures toward the approach [[61](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_061)]. The regulation lays out the following: (i) standard operating procedures for the sale, use, and functioning of AI systems within the Union; (ii) limitations on the application of specific AI techniques; (iii) unique specifications for high-risk AI systems along with operator responsibilities; (iv) standard operating procedures for the application of AI and pharmacovigilance with COVID-19; rules governing transparency for AI systems used to generate or modify image, sound, or video content; emotion recognition and biometric classification systems; and AI systems intended to interact with people; and (v) laws governing market surveillance and monitoring [[62](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_062)]. The European Commission is prepared to create a legal framework that focuses on the risks associated with the specific applications of AI systems. Three categories of AI applications are distinguished by the risk-based approach: (1) unacceptable risk, (2) high risk, and (3) moderate to low risk [[63](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_063)]. Certain AI systems that pose risks deemed “unacceptable” would be forbidden. Numerous “high-risk” AI systems could get approved, but only after meeting certain conditions and fulfilling certain duties to be allowed to enter the EU market. Very minimal transparency requirements would apply to AI systems that pose only “limited risk.”

The Health Ministry has been working to fulfill the regulatory role in quality management, consolidation, data collection, and consent standard-setting with the NDHB (2019) and the EHR Standards (2016). Enabling the ecosystem to share data and compile it in a way that is suitable for AI will advance it technologically. To facilitate data sharing, the government should do more than just establish plans and standards; instead, it should establish data marketplaces and cooperative initiatives. Since the healthcare industry is still in its infancy, the regulations governing AI are still changing. Additionally, there is a chance to have adaptable regulation and gain knowledge from past mistakes in finding the ideal balance between excessive regulation, which could impede the implementation of meaningful public health initiatives on a large scale, and insufficient regulation, which could jeopardize user trust, safety, effectiveness, and adoption. As a result, the regulator must take into account two things: the clinical assessment of AI models and the post-market surveillance and tracking of AI models that are being used [[65](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_065)].

## 2.5 Challenges of Blockchain in Pharma

The domain of blockchain technology research is expanding. Initially confined to the banking industry, blockchain technology has now been adopted by government agencies, the Internet of Things, reputational frameworks, and security services. The pharmaceutical business has lately implemented blockchain technology, mostly to address the issue of counterfeiting. Blockchain technology has two primary advantages due to its decentralization: information security and privacy. Nonetheless, it necessitates increased processing power. About fifty percent of the energy is allocated to thwarting hostile user attacks; without efficient tracking and tracing, users may suffer losses due to theft and misplaced items [[66](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_066)]. Global secure banking transactions can be facilitated by blockchain technology, but interoperability concerns pose a serious challenge. Distributed ledger technology can be used to standardize blockchain operations (DLT). Further research is necessary to ascertain the best approaches to implement DLT applications, even as the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) strives to identify and standardize DLT applications and their services. The utilization of edge computing, cloud computing, and advanced Internet of Things applications has markedly enhanced data accessibility [[67](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_067)]. The enormous growth presents many challenges for the implementation of blockchain, including data management, privacy issues, redundancy issues, and accessibility issues. Additionally, because of misbehavior and poorly run supply chains, it has tried to leverage blockchain technology to speed up medical transactions, safeguard patients, and track and trace [[68](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_068), [69](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_069)]. Ensuring interoperability between diverse healthcare equipment and systems is also crucial. For smooth exchange of information and interaction between the various hospitals and systems, robust interoperability solutions are required. The blockchain network must accommodate increased transaction rates without incurring performance degradation as healthcare data volume expands. To ensure blockchain implementations adhere to rigorous data protection requirements such as GDPR and HIPAA, meticulous design and enforcement protocols are essential [[68](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_068), [69](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_069), [70](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_070), [71](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_071), [72](#9783111573861_002146.xhtml#b_9783111574288-002_ref_072)].
