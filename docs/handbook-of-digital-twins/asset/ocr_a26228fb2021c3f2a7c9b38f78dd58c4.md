# ### 7.4.5 Topology

It clarifies how the observable manufacturing element and the digital counterpart are related. We extended the proposal of Schroeder et al. (2021) to the four following topologies:

+   - Disconnected, there is no direct connection between the DT and its entity, the information comes from external components or users. This configuration, also called pre-DT, actually foreshadows the actual DT in its early design phase to operate validation and tests before connecting the physical and virtual entities.
+   - Connected, the DT is directly connected to its entity:
+       - Embedded, the DT is included in its entity, the communication may be facilitated but the computational capabilities will be limited.
+       - Disjoint, even if the DT is linked to its OME, it remains physically distant from it.
+       - Joint, this represents an in-between of the two previously mentioned topologies, as one part of the DT is embedded in the OME while the other part remains physically distant from it.

# ### 7.4.6 Synchronization

Now that the DT and its entity are linked, they can speak to one another directly or indirectly. According to the ISO/DIS Standard, the next challenge is to ensure that this communication is carried out “at an appropriate rate of synchronization”; however, depending on the application, communication may take place asynchronously (event-based, conditional, on demand) or synchronously (real-time, near real-time, or periodically). Depending on the purpose, a variable synchronization may also be conceivable.
