## ABSTRACT

This work presents frequency reconfigurable Wideband Rectangular Dielectric Resonator Antenna (RDRA) using a Machine Learning (ML) approach for 5G (Sub-6 GHz) applications. The concept of ML is integrated with reconfigurable DRA for the first time. A novel approach has been proposed to obtain wideband and frequency reconfigurability. Frequency reconfigurability is achieved electronically (PIN diode switches) and operates in four different configurations, offering a maximum of 76.84 % wide tuning range. Hybrid structure and higher order mode $TE_{111}$ and $TE_{211}$ modes excitation is responsible for wideband operation. The Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGB) ML algorithm provides more than 90 % accuracy in all configurations for $S_{11}$ prediction.

## 1. Introduction

The idea of frequency reconfigurable antennas has been developed to make possible dynamic changes in the operating frequency without modifying the physical construction of the antenna. These antennas can adapt their operating frequency to changing system requirements or extend multiple frequency bands. Frequency reconfigurable antennas are essential in the 5G environment for addressing next-generation wireless devices changing requirements and difficulties. Multiple technologies, including cellular, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and IoT, are used concurrently in the Sub-6 GHz band. A single antenna may cover several frequencies due to a frequency-reconfigurable antenna, and it can provide an effective spectrum utilization. They improve spectrum utilization, compatibility, multiband capabilities, and cost-effectiveness, all of these are beneficial for wireless communication [1]. An advanced antenna technology known as a Dielectric Resonator Antenna (DRA) offers several desirable characteristics, such as excellent radiation efficiency, significant gain, and variable multi-mode capabilities. Due to these characteristics, the DRA is an excellent choice for antenna designs that need frequency flexibility and reconfigurability in applications. Additionally, there are several feeding mechanisms available in a DRA such as a coaxial probe, microstrip feeding, aperture coupled, and coplanar waveguides (CPW), and it may exist in several forms (rectangular, cylindrical, deformed shape, etc.). Dielectric resonators (DRs) show resonance behaviours at particular frequencies, and the resonant frequency may be tuned by varying the size of the DR. The ability to be tuned allows DRA to operate within a certain frequency band. Also, DRs have large bandwidths, enabling them to support various frequency bands over a broad frequency range. Frequency-reconfigurable antennas built on DRs can cover a large bandwidth range of frequencies, making them adaptable to various wireless communication protocols and applications. The small form factor of DRA allows for integration into space-limited devices without sacrificing performance. A compact wideband antenna with enhanced flexibility and performance may be developed by including frequency reconfigurability into DRAs. Traditional DRAs have demonstrated potential because of their excellent radiation efficiency, and compatibility with integrated circuits [2–6, 22–23].

To design frequency reconfigurable DRA, various studies were conducted, such as in [9], with chip capacitors or varactors, a rectangular differentially fed DRA was created, which provides narrow band tuning. One of the described configurations has a wide tuning range but adds complexity by combining three PIN diodes with a rectangular DRA that operates in six of the nine states [10]. In [11], Low tuning range and additional complexity are provided by a rectangular DRA powered by CPW that uses two PIN diodes to transition from narrowband to wideband operation. In [12], by combining a three-rectangular DRA with two PIN Diodes of more complexity, dual-band frequency-reconfigurability in one or both ON states is generated. In [13], four rectangular DRAs with three integrated PIN diodes provide a single band frequency reconfigurability, but only in two or more ON states. In [12] and [13], complexity increases due to the number of elements and biasing lines. In [14], a frequency agile hybrid DRA with two PIN diodes creates a reconfigurable circularly polarized wave across a certain frequency range; however, it has a limited tuning range. ### 2.2. Mathematical modelling

The antenna being proposed in this study is a hybrid antenna, which combines different features to enhance its performance. The resonant frequency of this antenna is determined by the presence of two dielectric layers: Epoxy FR4 (4.4) and Alumina (Al2O3). The effective height Heff and the effective permittivity εreff are crucial parameters that characterize the behaviour of the proposed antenna. According to [16], The effective height (Heff), and effective permittivity (εreff) is given by Eqs. (1) and (2):

H_eff = H + H_S

ε_reff = H_eff / (H/ε_RDRA + H_S/ε_sub)

According to [3] the transcendental equation is used to calculate the fundamental TE111 mode’s resonance frequency

k . tan(k * d / 2) = √((εreff - 1)k0² - kz²)

Where, kx, ky, and kz are wave numbers, they satisfy the following equation

kx² + ky² + kz² = (εreff)k0²

Where, kx = π/b and ky = π/b

According to [1], reconfigurable antenna parameters Tuning Range (TR) and Total Spectrum (TS) is given by Eqs. (5) and (6):

TR(%) = 2 × ((fol - fal) / (fon + fol)) × 100

Where, fol = Lower resonating frequency and foh = Higher resonating frequency

TS(%) = 2 × ((fmax - fmin) / (fmax + fmin)) × 100
