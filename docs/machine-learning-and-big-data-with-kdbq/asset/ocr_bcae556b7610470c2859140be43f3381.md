Complex numbers form the fundamental theorem of algebra: every non-constant
ynomial equation with complex coefficients has a complex solution. There is no
real number i which will solve i^2 = -1, this theorem also holds for a real number
whose imaginary part is equal to zero. Another interpretation of complex numbers is
geometric. Real numbers are defined in one dimension while complex numbers spans
two-dimensional plane. The real part of the complex number is a projection of
t complex number on the real axis (x-axis) while the imaginary part is a projection
to the imaginary axis, which is perpendicular to the real one (y-axis). The geometric
interpretation gives us an intuitive interpretation of how complex numbers extend real
algebraic operations on complex numbers then to operations on
two-dimensional vectors. Further, the dimensional interpretation of complex num-
results in a polar representation, where complex numbers are defined as vectors
orinating at (0,0) with a given radius and angle to the positive part of the real axis.
