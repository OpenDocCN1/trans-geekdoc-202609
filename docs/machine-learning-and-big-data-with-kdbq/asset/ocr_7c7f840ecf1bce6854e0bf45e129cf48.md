Figure 15.1 depicts the real part, the imaginary part and the modulus of the formed series. The series is short, and thus it is difficult to interpret the results from the figure. In the following paragraphs, we will move towards a more realistic example with an intuitive interpretation.

Let us consider an example, where the time series is characterised by both the index and signal. The signal is a real-valued series. In order to interpret the results, let us define the signal as a periodic function composed of two sinusoids with different frequencies. The time series is defined in a table:

```
t: 0:(1.%1000):* 501
pi: acos[-1]
xt: cos[2*pi*t]+0.5*cos[2*pi*4*t]
tabTab([t; xt]);
```

The signal xt consists of 5001 points spanning the interval t₀=0 to t₅₀₀=10. The function is periodic with a frequency of 2 and 4, respectively, where the component with frequency 2 has the amplitude compared to the other one.
