# **13.3.1** Moore-Penrose Pseudoinverse

Let's have a look at our $\beta$ in more detail:

$\hat{eta} = (mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{y},$

where $\mathbf{X}^+$ is the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse matrix of $\mathbf{X}$. In our example, we get

$\mathbf{X}^+ = $(\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^T = (1^2 + 3^2 + 4^2)^{-1} (1 \ 3 \ 4) = \begin{pmatrix} 1/26 \ 3/26 \ 4/26 \end{pmatrix}.$ (13.21)

One of its properties is $(\mathbf{XX}^+)\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{X}$; in other words, $\mathbf{XX}^+$ map all column vectors of $\mathbf{X}$ to themselves while not be the identity matrix. Namely,

$\(\mathbf{XX}^+)\mathbf{X} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \ 3 \ 4 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} 1/26 \ 3/26 \ 4/26 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1/26 & 3/26 & 4/26 \ 3/26 & 9/26 & 12/26 \ 4/26 & 12/26 & 16/26 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \ 3 \ 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \ 3 \ 4 \end{pmatrix}.$ (13.22)
