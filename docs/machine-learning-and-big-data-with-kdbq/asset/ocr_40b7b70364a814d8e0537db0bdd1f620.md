# 16.4.4 Terminology and Explained Variance

It is worth summarising the common terminology used when the PCA is applied. $\mathbf{W}$ be the matrix with weights - $\mathbf{C}$ eigenvectors - as its columns,

> $$\mathbf{W} = egin{pmatrix} \mathbf{w}_{(1)} & \mathbf{w}_{(2)} & \dots & \mathbf{w}_{(p)} \end{pmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{p 	imes p} \qquad (16.60)$

The transformation of the $p$-dimensional random vector $\mathbf{x}$, (corrected for its mean $oldsymbol{\mu}$)

> $$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{W}^T(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}) \in \mathbb{R}^{p 	imes 1} \qquad (16.61)$

the $p$-dimensional random vector $\mathbf{y}$, is called the vector of $\mathbf{x}$'s principal components or principal component (PC) scores. The $i$-th element of $\mathbf{y}$ is then the $i$-th principal component or the $i$-th PC score of $\mathbf{x}$.

For the centered data matrix $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{n 	imes p}$, the principal components or principal component (PC) scores are given by

> $$\mathbf{Z} := \mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{n 	imes p} \qquad (16.62)$

so that the $(i,j)$-th entry of $\mathbf{Z}$,

> $$\mathbf{Z}_{ij} = \mathbf{x}'_i^T\mathbf{w}_{(j)}, \qquad (16.63)$

with $1 \le i \le n, 1 \le j \le p$, where $\mathbf{x}'_i$ is the $i$-th row of $\mathbf{X}'$, is the $j$-th principal component ($j$-th PC score) of the $i$-th observation (the $i$-th row of $\mathbf{X}'$). The $j$-th column of $\mathbf{Z}$, $\mathbf{z}_{(j)}$, is sometimes referred to as the $j$-th principal component of the data. Thus the $j$-th weight vector, $\mathbf{w}_{(j)} \in \mathbb{R}^{p 	imes 1}$, maps the data to the $j$-th principal component (PC score).
