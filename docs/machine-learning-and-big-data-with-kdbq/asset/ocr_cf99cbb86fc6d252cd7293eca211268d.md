#  11.2 WEIGHTED MID FOR N LEVELS OF AN ORDER BOOK

Let us recall that in market, the interest in buying an asset or a realised trade of a certain amount of the underlying asset is as quote or a trade respectively. Our previous example saw how to calculate an N-tick moving VWAP on some of trades. In contrast, the quote data usually represents the state of a limit order book, or LOB: the limit order book stores the market interest in buying and selling this is expressed as a list of prices and corresponding amounts the market participants are willing to win (buy) or sell (at ask).

When analysing the LOB, a standard extension to the top-of-book mid, i.e. the point which is equidistant between the best bid (higher price the participants are willing to buy) and ask best (lowest price the participants are selling to at) of a LOB, the weighted mid, or WMID. This mid can take into account the size which is placed on each side of the book and is, therefore, a weighted average. In our case below, we generalise this concept to include two levels of depth, i.e. the best and second-best prices, respectively. In other words, we calculate the volume-weighted mid where we take into account the top 2 levels of the LOB.

We define the weighted mid at point in time as:

```
WMID_i = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^N bidSize_{i,j} \times bid_{i,j} + \sum_{j=1}^N askSize_{i,j} \times ask_{i,j}}{\sum_{j=1}^N bidSize_{i,j} + \sum_{j=1}^N askSize_{i,j}},
```

where N are levels of depth, in our case N=2. Let's creates a quote with 2 levels of depth:

```
n: 10;
quote: ([sym] time: asc n?10t; bid1: n?10f; bidSize1: n?100 );
update bid2: 0 | bid1 - .1 * n ? 10, bidSize2: n?100, ask1: bid1 + .2 * n ?
-- 10, ask1: n?100
from quote;
update ask2: ask1 + .1 * n ? 10, ask2: n?100 from quote;
quote
```
