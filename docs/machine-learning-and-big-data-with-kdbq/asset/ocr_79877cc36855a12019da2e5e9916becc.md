# 17.1 LOCAL OUTLIER FACTOR

In this section, we delve into the details of the local outlier factor – see Breuning et al. (2000) – and implement the method directly along with its explanation. The local outlier factor technique estimates the local density of data and determines the property of “being outlier” from the anomalous values of the data density. The local density is derived based on the k-nearest neighbours principles (subsequent chapters for more details). The density of a point set is derived from the distance between the point and its local neighbourhood relative to the neighbourhood itself. The values, which are far from their neighbours relative to the distance among the neighbours, are deemed to be considered outliers. On the other hand, one can define the inverted notion of “inliers” as observations, where the distance between the point and its local neighbourhood relative to the density of the neighbourhood is unusually small. The method calls the distance mentioned above as the reachability distance.

Let us assume a data set of N features, $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_N$ spanning the N-dimensional space. First, we need to define a notion of distance between two N-dimensional points. The distance has to be data-sensitive of the length between two observations, and it has to satisfy the usual properties imposed on distance metric. The suitable candidates are any of the $L_p$ norms:

```
$d(X, Y) = \left(\sum_{i=1}^N |X_i - Y_i|^p\right)^{1/p} \quad (17.1)$
```

where $X$ and Y are two N-dimensional points in the feature space.
Let us stress at this point that we cannot just take features as they are but rather to normalise them across the board.
