# 12.3.5 Sign Test

The sign test is a non-parametric test to assess the statistical difference between pairs of observations drawn from two related populations. For example, consider a set of investors who invest every month and report their Pn. The investors can subscribe to the advisory service. If the general market conditions remain the same, the effect of the advisory service can be estimated by comparing the performance – the Pn before and after the advisory service was obtained for a sample of investors. If the effect of the advisory service is not material, then Pn before/after comes for every investor from the same distribution. In particular, if every investor can have a different distribution of Pn – mean, sigma, or kurtosis, for example — we do not compare Pn across investors. Instead, we should assess the statistical properties of the difference of the Pn before/after the change. If the effect of the advisory service is irrelevant, the distribution of differences should be symmetric around zero. If the other hand, if the difference is positive/negative, the advisory effect is correspondingly negative/positive.

Let formulate the sign test explicitly: consider two related populations, $X$ and $Y$, and related pairs of draws, $(x_i, y_i)$, with $i=1\dotsN$. Each population is continuous, and the two populations are ordinal, i.e. we can order any pair $(x_i, y_i)$. We further denote the order of inequality between $x_i$ and $y_i$. For the sake of simplicity, we assume that $x_i=y_i$ is not possible (with probability 0), and discuss ties in subsequent tests. Further, every pair of draws are independent of each other.

The ordinal variables may not be necessarily numeric. Consider for example a string of characters, which is not numeric, but we can easily sort a test of string alphabetically. In any case. We define the difference between $x_i$ and $y_i$ as $\delta_i = x_i - y_i$ for non-numerical variables where the difference can be defined as:

> $\delta_i = \begin{cases} +1, x_i > y_i; \ -1, \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$ (12.20)

Then, the basic formulation of the sign test tests:

+   * $H_0$: The difference between $x_i$ and $y_i$, $\delta_i = x_i - y_i$, has the same probability to be positive as negative.
* $H_0$: The difference between $x_i$ and $y_i$ is skewed towards positive or negative values.
