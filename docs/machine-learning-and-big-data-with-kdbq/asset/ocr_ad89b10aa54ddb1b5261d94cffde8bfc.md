A further improvement to the MCTS algorithm relates to the use of heuristics, which affects the moves in Selection or Expansion steps mentioned above. The motivation for such improvement is to make the simulation more efficient and potentially to cut the situation time. In Bayesian formulation, this means that Bayesian priors can be introduced into the formula for $UCT$. Alternatively, we may modify the Expansion probability. It is essential to keep in mind that such extension be done in a way that still keeps the MCTS algorithm as well while making moves, which are locally suboptimal. This is the way the algorithm can overcome the human player and find innovative strategies.

We can roughly compare the MCTS to the ways are thinking. The human player explores the viability of a move in the game similarly as the MCTS: playing several moves placing herself into a position of the other and thus responding to her moves. The best players can form strategies several ahead. The human brain, however, does not allow to keep in memory the full tree and thus often only the best move in a given layer of the tree is kept in mind. The human builds a tree which is very narrow, not exploring all options.

On the other hand, the human relies heavily on heuristics. The ability of the MCTS to explore the suboptimal moves presents a significant improvement against human players. The heuristic is what stands between the human and the computer. Moreover, this is the element AlphaGo and its further extensions considered and closed the gap. The computer finally got better than human player – at least for the game of go.
