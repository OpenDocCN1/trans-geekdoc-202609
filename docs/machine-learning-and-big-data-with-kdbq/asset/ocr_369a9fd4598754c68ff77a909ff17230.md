# 16.2.2 QR Algorithm for Eigenvalues

We can proceed with the QR algorithm, which allows us to calculate all eigenvalues of a real square matrix A. The QR algorithm works as follows:

+   - Initialise the algorithm as $A_0 = A$.
- The algorithm proceeds in iterative steps, with $k$ denoting the iteration step, starting at $k=0$:
- Calculate the QR decomposition: $A_k = Q_k R_k$.
- Update the matrix: $A_{k+1} = R_k Q_k$.
- Increase $k$ and iterate.
- The diagonal of the matrix $A_{k+1}$ converges to the Schur form of $A$ with eigenvalues being on the diagonal.

The algorithm is based on the similarity of the matrix $A_k$ and $A_{k+1}$ within the iteration steps. The similarity can be shown as:

```
$A_{k+1} = R_k Q_k \quad (16.18)$$n$$= (Q_k^{-1} A_k) R_k Q_k \quad (16.19)$$n$$= Q_k^{-1} A_k Q_k. \quad (16.20)$
```

The matrices are similar; they represent the same operator in two different bases. The algorithm then transforms the matrix into a different basis. Further, the matrices have the same eigenvalues.

If the matrix A has distinct real eigenvalues, the QR algorithm converges to the upper triangular matrix – the Schur form. The QR algorithm cannot produce complex values as it involves only addition and multiplication of real numbers. If the real matrix has complex eigenvalues, or conjugate pairs of eigenvalues, the QR algorithm does not converge to the upper triangular matrix. In such a case, the matrix will converge to a block-upper triangular form. The elements in the block will never converge – they correspond to the oscillation in the complex plane which carry the values outside of the block carry eigenvalues.

The convergence of the algorithm cannot be proved by merely checking the upper diagonal form as the blocks may be present and perfectly valid. It is more suitable to monitor the performance of the algorithm in a controlled way with regularly overseeing the use some upper bound on the errors and construct the indicator for the shape of the matrix. The algorithm as formulated above is simple, but we have to pay the price by expensive calculations and oversight. There are specific techniques which speed up convergence in terms of numerical cost but they are beyond our scope.
