Including intercept within our framework is easy. We just prepend a column of ones to X, so it becomes the $n \times (p + 1)$-dimensional matrix:

```
$X = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \ 1 & 3 \ 1 & 4 \end{pmatrix} \quad (13.32)$
```

and prepend $\beta_0$ to the column vector $\hat{eta}$, so it becomes the $p+1$-dimensional column vector

```
$\hat{eta} = \begin{pmatrix} \hat{eta}_0 \ \hat{eta}_1 \end{pmatrix} \quad (13.33)$
```

We then proceed as before. In particular, for $n \in \mathbb{N}^*, p \in \mathbb{N}^*, i \in [1, \dots, n]$,

```
$y_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_{i1} + \dots + \beta_p x_{ip} + \epsilon_i, \quad (13.34)$
```

or, in matrix form,

```
$y = X\hat{\beta} + \epsilon,$
```

where

```
$y = \begin{pmatrix} y_1 \ y_2 \ \vdots \ y_n \end{pmatrix}, \quad X = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & x_1^T \ 1 & x_2^T \ \vdots & \vdots \ 1 & x_n^T \end{pmatrix}, \quad \begin{pmatrix} x_{11} & x_{12} & \dots & x_{1p} \ x_{21} & x_{22} & \dots & x_{2p} \ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \ x_{n1} & x_{n2} & \dots & x_{np} \end{pmatrix}, \quad \hat{\beta} = \begin{pmatrix} \hat{eta}_0 \ \hat{eta}_1 \ \vdots \ \hat{eta}_p \end{pmatrix}, \quad \epsilon = \begin{pmatrix} \epsilon_1 \ \epsilon_2 \ \vdots \ \epsilon_n \end{pmatrix}$
```

We can calculate the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of X as

```
$X^+ = (X^TX)^{-1}X^T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \ 1 & 3 & 1 \ 1 & 4 & 1 \end{pmatrix}^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \ 1 & 1 & 1 \ 1 & 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix} \quad (13.35)$
```

```
$= \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 8 & 1 \ 2 & 6 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \ 1 & 1 & 1 \ 1 & 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix} \quad (13.36)$
```

```
$= \frac{1}{14} \begin{pmatrix} 26 & -8 \ -8 & 3 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \ 1 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \quad (13.37)$
```

```
$= \frac{1}{14} \begin{pmatrix} 18 & 2 & -6 \ -5 & 1 & 4 \end{pmatrix}. \quad (13.38)$
```

Hence

```
$\hat{\beta} = X^+y = \frac{1}{14} \begin{pmatrix} 18 & 2 & -6 \ -5 & 1 & 4 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 13/7 \ -1/14 \ 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \hat{\beta}_0 \ \hat{\beta}_1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad (13.39)$
```
