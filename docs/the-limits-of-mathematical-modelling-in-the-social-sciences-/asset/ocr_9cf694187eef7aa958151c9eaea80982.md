$G_1^1, G_2^1, G_1^2, G_2^2$ are consistent = any member of $G$ is willing to associate with any member of his own group (the last two with members of the other group).

$G_1^2, G_2^2, G_0^1, G_0^2$ are inconsistent = no member of $G$ is willing to associate with any member of his own group (the last two with nobody).

$G_1^1$ and $G_2^2$ are antiphetic each other = any member of $G$ is willing to associate only with a member of his own group. $G_2^{1,2}$ and $G_2^{1,2}$ are mixed.

Menger's aim is to understand when is it possible to unify all the members of $G$ into a consistent group. The answer lies in the tolerance of the mixed groups $G_1^{1,2}$ and $G_2^{1,2}$ that makes possible the overlapping between. Kantian categorical imperative is unable to guarantee general harmony because it operates only in groups $G_1^1$ and $G_2^2$ i.e., only in groups which there is no need to find a tool of cohesiveness or peace, because they are formed by member who shared the same set of rules.

In the postscript to the English edition of his book (Menger, 1974), Menger summed up its purpose as an attempt to apply logico mathematical thought to ethical matters. He wrote that it was possible to construct an exact system of thought in regard not to dealing with personal dilemmas but treating social problems in what today is called group decision theory.
