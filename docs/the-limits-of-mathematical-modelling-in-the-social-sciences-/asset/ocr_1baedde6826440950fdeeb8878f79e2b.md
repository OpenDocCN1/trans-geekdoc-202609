# Hamiltonian mechanics

Hamiltonian mechanics is seen here as the dynamics of the “Hamiltonian fluid” (see Abraham and Marsden, 1978; Lanczos, 1977). Our ground structure for mechanics starts out of basic sets, which are a $2n$-dimensional real smooth manifold, and the real symplectic group $Sp(2n, R)$. Phase spaces in Hamiltonian manifolds are symplectic manifolds: even-dimensional manifolds like $M$ endowed with a symplectic form, that is, a nondegenerate closed 2-form $\Omega$ on $M$. The imposition of that form can be seen as the choice of a reduction of the linear bundle $L(M)$ to a fixed principal bundle $P(M, Sp(2n, R)); however, given one such reduction, it does not automatically follow that the induced 2-form on $M$ is a closed form.

All other objects are constructed in the same way as in the preceding example. However, we must show that we still have a Dirac-like equation as the dynamical axiom for the species of structures of mechanics. Hamilton’s equations are

```
$i_X\Omega = -dh$,
```

where $i_X$ denotes the interior product with respect to the vector-field $X$ over $M$, and $h$ is the Hamiltonian function. That equation is (locally, at least) equivalent to

```
$L_X\Omega = 0$,
```

or

```
$d(i_X\Omega) = 0$,
```
