The attitudes $Dx(y)$, $Ex(y)$, $Ix(y)$ are interpreted in terms of preferences between the pairs $(x,y)$ and $(x',y)$ respectively by

$(x',y) < (x,y)$, $(x,y) < (x',y)$, $(x,y) \sim (x',y)$

Hence, Menger introduced substitutive goods: when we consider two possible objects of desires, $p$ and $q$ (with their negations, $p'$ and $q'$) such that either affects the optative attitude toward the other. As before, $Ax(y)$ denotes the attitude toward $x$ under the condition $y$ (where $A$ is $D$ or $E$ or $I$, $A^*$ will denote $E, D$, or $I$, respectively, and $A^{**} = A$) then we have the following:

+   (1) If $Ax(y)$ then $Ay(y)$
(2) If $Ax(y)$ then $A^*x(y)$
