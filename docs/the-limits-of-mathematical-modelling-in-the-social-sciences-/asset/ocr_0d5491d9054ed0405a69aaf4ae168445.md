In his paper, he wrote: “the ordinary 2-valued logic divides propositions into two classes” (Menger, 1939, p. 91): *true or false*. Then, it combines into more complex propositions by connectives such as & (and), $ightarrow$ (implies) and so on. Proposition $p\&q$ belongs to the class of *true* if and only if both $p$ and $q$ are *true*. On the other side, the $n$-valued logics of Post and Lukasiewicz divides propositions into $n$ classes.

According to Menger, we must build a realistic logic, of everyday life, that is not 2-valued, but $n$-valued. Logic of everyday life in fact not share the feature that the class of the compound is uniquely determined by the class of components when it deals with doubtful propositions: $p\&q$ can be *doubtful* or false if both $p$ and $q$ are doubtful.

Logic of doubtful divides propositions into:

Asserted = $\mu_+
Doubtful = $\mu_0$
Negated = $\mu_-$

And $\mu(p)$ is the class of modality to which $p$ belongs: in contrast with 3-valued logic, “the modality of a compound is not determined by the modalities of the components”

Asserted = $\mu_+$ (deductive system) contains:

+   1. tautologies of 2-valued calculus of propositions
2. $p\&q$ if it contains $p$ and $q$
3. $q$ if it contains $p$ and $q ightarrow q$ or $(p\&q)'
4. $p, q$ and $p\&q$ if it contains $p\&q$
5. $q'$ if it contains $(p\&q)'$ and $p$.
6. $(p\&q)'$ if it contains $p'$

If $p$ belongs to $\mu_+$ then $p'$ belongs to $\mu_-$ and conversely; $P$ and $Q$ are tautologically equivalent if $P ightarrow Q$ and $Q ightarrow P$ are tautologies in 2-valued sense.
