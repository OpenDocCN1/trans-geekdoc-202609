Field spaces are:

+   - The first is the set (actually a manifold, with a natural differ-differentiable structure) of all pseudo-Riemannian metric tensors,

$$M \subset C^k(\odot^2T_*(M)),$

where $C^k(\odot^2T_*(M))$ is the bundle of all $C^k$ symmetric covariant
2-tensors over $M$.

Also out of $M$ and out of adequate associated bundles we get
$\mathcal{A}$, the bundle of all Christoffel connections over $M$, and $\mathcal{F}$, the
bundle of Riemann-Christoffel curvature tensors over $M$.

+   - We need the space of source fields, $\mathcal{I}$, which includes energy-momentum tensors, and arise out of adequate associated tensor
bundles over $M$.

+   - $\mathcal{G}$ is the group of $C^k$-diffeomorphisms of $M$.

+   - If $K$ is the group of all fields spaces, then $K/\mathcal{G}$ is the space of
physically distinct fields.

+   - Finally, the dynamics are given by Einstein’s equations (there is
also a Dirac-like formulation for those, first proposed by R. Penrose
in 1960 as a neutrino-like equation; as Doria (1975)).

The quotient $K/\mathcal{G}$ is the way we distinguish concrete, physically
diverse, fields, as for covariant theories, one has any that two fields
related by an element of $\mathcal{G}$ “are” the “same” field.

# Classical gauge fields

The mathematics of classical gauge fields can be found in Utiyama
(1956). We follow here the preceding examples, and, in particular,
treatment of general relativity:

+   - The basic sets are a spacetime $(M, g)$, and a finite dimensional,
semi-simple, compact Lie group $G$.

+   - The derived set is a fixed principal bundle $P(M, G)$ over $M$ with
G as the fiber.
