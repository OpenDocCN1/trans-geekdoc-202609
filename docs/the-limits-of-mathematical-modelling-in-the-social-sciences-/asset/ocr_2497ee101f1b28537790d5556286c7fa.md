The set of physical fields in our theory is obtained out of some of the bundles in $\mathcal{E}$: the set of electromagnetic field tensors is a set of cross-sections of the bundle $F=\Lambda^2 \otimes s^1(M)$ of all $s^1$-valued 2-forms on $M$, where $s^1$ is the group's Lie algebra. To be precise, the set of all electromagnetic fields is $\mathcal{F} \subset C^k(F)$, if we are dealing with $C^k$ cross-sections (actually a submanifold in the usual $C^k$ topology due to the closure condition $dF=0$).

Finally we have two group actions on $\mathcal{F}$: the first is the Lorentz-Poincaré action $L$ which is part of the action of diffeomorphisms of $M$; then we have the (trivial) action of the group $\mathcal{G}'$ of gauge transformations of $P$ when acting on the field manifold $\mathcal{F}$. As it is well known, its action is $not$ in the non-Abelian case. Anyway, it has a nontrivial action on the space $\mathcal{A}$ of all gauge potentials for the fields in $\mathcal{F}$. Therefore, we take as our symmetry group $\mathcal{G}$ the product $L \otimes \mathcal{G}'$ of the (allowed) symmetries of $M$ and the symmetries of the principal bundle $P$.

We must add the space $\mathcal{A}$ of potentials and of currents, $\mathcal{I}$, as structures derived from $M$ and $S^1$. Both spaces have the same underlying topological structure acts upon them. We differ in the way the group $\mathcal{G}'$ of gauge transformations acts upon them. We obtain $I=\Lambda^1 \otimes s^1(M)$ and $\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{I}=C^k(I)$. Notice that $\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{G}'=\mathcal{I}$ while $\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{G}' \neq \mathcal{A}$.
