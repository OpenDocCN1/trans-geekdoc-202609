In the next result, Z is the set of integers. Let $\mathbf{N}$ be a model, $\mathbf{N} \models T$, and $\mathbf{N}$ makes $T$ arithmetically sound.

**Proposition 13.6.** If $T$ is arithmetically sound, then we can arithmetically construct a polynomial expression

$q(x_1, \dots, x_n)$

over $Z$ such that

$\mathbf{N} \models \forall x_1, \dots, x_n \in \omega \ q(x_1, \dots, x_n) > 0$,

but

$T 
vdash \forall x_1, \dots, x_n \in \omega \ q(x_1, \dots, x_n) > 0$

and

$T 
vdash \exists x_1, \dots, x_n \in \omega \ q(x_1, \dots, x_n) = 0$.

**Proof.** $\xi \in \mathcal{L}_T$ is an undecidable sentence obtained from $T$ with the help of Gödel’s diagonalization; let $n_\xi$ be its Gödel number and $mt_T$ be the Gödel coding of proof techniques in $T$ (of the Turing machine that enumerates all the theorems of $T$). For an universal polynomial $p(m, q, x_1, \dots, x_n)$, we have

$q(x_1, \dots, x_n) = (p(m_T, n_\xi, x_1, \dots, x_n))^2. \square$
