# Chapter 4: Scientific foundations

## Abstract

This chapter summarizes the scientific foundations of empirical and experimental research in HCI. We define and examine the nature of research, empirical research, and the scientific method, while distinguishing research from design and engineering. Concepts such as observation, measurement, internal validity, external validity, and formulating testable research questions are explained and demonstrated through examples. Four tips for finding a research topic are presented.

### Keywords

research; empirical research; experimental research; the scientific method; observation; measurement; internal validity; external validity; research methods; research questions; circumstantial relationships; causal relationships; research topics

In the last chapter, we examined a variety of interaction topics in HCI. By and large, the research methodology for studying these topics is empirical and scientific. Ideas are conceived, developed, and implemented and then framed as hypotheses that are tested in an experiment. This chapter presents the enabling features of this methodology. Our goal is to establish the what, why, and how of research, with a focus on research that is both empirical and experimental. While much of the discussion is general, the examples are directed at HCI. We begin with the terminology surrounding research and empirical research.

## 4.1 What is research?

Research means different things to different people. "Being a researcher" or "conducting research" carries a certain elevated status in universities, colleges, and corporations. Consequently, the term "research" is bantered around in a myriad of situations. Often, the word is used simply to add weight to an assertion ("Our research shows that ..."). While writing an early draft of this chapter, a television ad for an Internet service provider was airing in southern Ontario. The ad proclaimed, "Independent research proves our Internet service is the fastest and most reliable – period."^([^(74)](#fn74))
