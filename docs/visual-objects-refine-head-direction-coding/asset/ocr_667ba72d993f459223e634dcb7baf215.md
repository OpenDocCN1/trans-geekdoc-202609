# HD tuning

HD tuning curves were calculated as previously described (4). Briefly, markers on the animal's head were tracked and connected to a horizontal orientation (yaw of direction). The yaw was measured in coordinates (i, aligned with the environment's axes, consistent throughout the study). HD tuning curves were generated as the ratio of time spent in each direction, using 1° bins and with a Gaussian kernel (3 standard deviation). The preferred direction of a cell is defined as the mean of the tuning curve.

To determine whether a cell is tuned to head direction, we used the head direction as previously described (6) with this method:

```
$i = \frac{\lambda(\theta)}{\sum\lambda(\theta)} \times p(\theta)$
```

Here, $n$ represents the total rate of the cell in the ith bin $	heta$, $\lambda(\theta)$ represents the neuron's average firing rate during election, and $p(\theta)$ denotes the occupancy (mean time spent in) $	heta$. The information rate (expressed in bits per second) was then adjusted by the mean firing rate to align with the cell metric (head direction, measured in bits).

# Cell-type classification

PoSB cells were classified as previously described (4). Briefly, cells with a higher firing rate than 10 Hz during the moving and a peak duration shorter than 0.4 ms were classified as fast-spinning neurons. In contrast, cells with firing rates below 10 Hz and durations longer than 0.4 ms were classified as slow-spinning neurons. This group was further divided into HD cells (HD cells with head direction information above 0.1 bit) or slow-HD cells (HD direction information below 0.1 bit). All other cells were excluded.
