**INTRODUCTION:** In day-to-day life, we use visual objects (e.g., a spatial clock when walking around town), or as spatial landmarks to help orient ourselves as we make our way through the world. We can use visual objects as spatial landmarks because our brains decode computational power when parsing the world into objects. However, our location in the world is processed by place cells, grid cells, and head direction cells in the brain. However, many remain unknown about how visual objects modulate the tuning of the neurons that encode spatial variables, because it is unclear how visual objects are processed by brains, where spatial navigation has been most extensively studied.

HD cells were differentially modulated by visual stimulation based on their preferred direction; HD cells pointing towards the visual stimulus were evoked, whereas HD cells pointing away from the visual stimulus were inhibited. The spatial navigation was observed in freeing conditions, when an object was displayed in the environment.
