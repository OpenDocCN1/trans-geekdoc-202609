## Electrophysiological recordings in awake animals
The probe was lowered below the postmusculature during the motor cruise of 2-8 hours before the start of recording. Neurophysiological signals were recorded at 20 kHz using a 26-channel system (Intan Technologies Headboard Interchanger and with) recording software.

### fUS acquisition
fUS-imaging was collected using a 32-channel matrix probe (15 MHz, 1024 channels, resolution: 220 x 175 µm; Vernon) connected to a Vantage 256 system (Vernon, Inc.) and connected to a custom fUS acquisition module (Intan AUTC). A multiplexer linked the 1024-channel probe to the 256-channel system with hardware and software buffering.

Behavior of the mice during freely moving assays was tracked using infrared markers that were mounted on the skull. Data were recorded using 30 infrared cameras and a 20 motion camera system (Optitrack). Behavior was captured by an additional mounted camera. Behavioral tracking was acquired at a rate of 120 Hz and synchronized with the fUS acquisition via the Intan USB Interface.

The head was fixed in a head-holding tube and the probe was moved by a manipulator. fUS recordings were performed under the FCM coil and kept at 37°C with a temperature control. The mouse was viewed through a small quartz window. The compound ultrasound image was generated from the summation of planes at -45°, -3°, -1°, 0°, 1°, 3°, and 45°. A Power-weighted fUS image was generated from the incoherent image of 160 compound images. Data reconstruction was performed in real time using the decomposition algorithm. Only 20% of significant signals were reconstructed.

### Visual stimuli
Images were presented at a size of 60° with the center of the image at -30° width and +20° elevation on a 60 cm monitor (Dell U2415). The monitor was placed in front of the mouse. For experiments, we employed a design of 12 groups of 8 stimuli for 12 seconds (12 minutes total, with a 30-second interval between groups). Stimuli were presented in random order. Each recording lasted 10 minutes for 5 s and interleaved with at least 0.5 s of each.

## Electrophysiology recordings under anesthesia
Data was recorded at 20 kHz using silicon probes (Cambridge NeuroTech, type III) connected via a SPI interface (Intan Technologies). The probe was inserted into a USB interface board. The FCM was fixed in a head-holding tube, to control the probe by the manipulator's coordinate system. Performance (0.5 minute) was measured after recording impedance stability. Recordings were performed for 2 brain at 10 days after the start of recording. For different days, sessions were secured with Wilsk-steel cannula. CM-DM (Thermo Fisher, Cambridge) was used to stabilize the probe before brain insertion or identifying recording sites post.

### Silicon probe insertion for (chronic) anesthesia
Animals were anesthetized with 5% isoflurane and prepared with a stereostatic apparatus. During the surgery we maintained with 1-2% isoflurane. We gave a subcutaneous injection (-0.8 mg body weight) and medobarbital (20 mg/kg, as a subcutaneous dose of 1 mL) at the incision site or as they were placed in the stereostatic.

A craniotomy was performed over the target area, creating a hole slightly larger than the probe insertion, at 0.1 mm lateral to the Bregma. We recorded coordinates: AP (from Lambda) +0.3, -2.35, -0.10. The probe was carefully fixed into position and anchored to the skull using dental cement (Unif Tread). To minimize electrical noise during recording, a mesh was attached to the skull using dental cement (Postite Funio).

After surgery, we gave a subcutaneous injection and placed on the head pad until fully recovered. Post-surgery, animals were housed individually and received daily carprofen injections for for three days. Animals were allowed to recover for 7 days post-surgery before further examination.

### Image preparation
Images were resized to 1024 x 1024 pixels and on a dark gray background. For these images we created a composite image and extracted statistics using the described texture statistics (36) with 25 generics. With this, we generated a scrambled image by matching permutation of features (vorticity, linear responses and many) of local features in the starting image without taking account the spatial location (33) and (34) for the description of alignment. Additionally, images were corrected and the SHINE toolbox (81) was used to enhance luminance. Finally, to avoid edge effects a small margin was used. For electrophysiological experiments we additionally demonstrated with two methods, the full amplitude spectrum (match.m) as well as the luminance (match.m).

### Pupil size
During functional recordings in awake, head-fixed mice pupil was observed via video. For this we used a light and a monochrome camera (BlackSentry) with an IR filter. Data was pooled at the level of the mouse. Videos were triggered by the fusion of visual stimuli and for analysis, each pool size was zeroed to account for fluctuations in the stimulus presentation. Then, the mean pool size was subtracted to get an average response for either stimulus or scrambled. a very short window (from stimulus onset to offset), a generic stimulus window was used.

## Data analysis
All analysis was performed using MATLAB or Python.

### fUS preprocessing
The fUS series was processed using custom scripts on voxel-by-voxel basis. A high-resolution power map was created by averaging 100 fUS frames from a single session, which was registered to the atlas. Temporal inversion was performed to calculate hemodynamic response. The BOLD signal was calculated by subtracting the baseline signal from each point and dividing by the baseline. Slow drifts were removed using a fifth-order Butterworth bandpass filter with a cutoff of 0.05 Hz. Movement artifacts occurring during awake sessions were removed using templates extracted from non-brain voxels or to preprocess, and trial-averaged fUS data of visual events were clustered using UMAP (implementation, Version 4.6) created with distance metric and the following parameters: n_neighbors=5, n=15. Trial-averaged fUS data of visual events was clustered using MATLAB dendrogram.m (distance metric + linkage method = used). For clustering at the level of voxels, processed fUS data of visual events were denoised using the silhouette method.

### Electrophysiological preprocessing
Electrophysiological records were processed with and times were analyzed using the Phy software (https://github.com/kelly/phy/). Only units that showed to the stimulus and exhibited high quality responses were included in the analysis. To estimate stimulus frames, single-unit events were binned with 100 ms bin size and a 2 ms zero-overlap. A Gaussian kernel with 10 ms was used for smoothing. For except trials, the rasters were averaged. Raster plots were plotted using the Graph toolbox.

### Reception field mapping
White and black squares (30×30 pixels) were flashed at 12 different positions (three elevation plus five azimuth) of the screen at random order. Each position was repeated 20 times. The stimulus duration was 200 ms with a gap of 200 ms. We used 0.5 as that a 2 ms bin had a larger response than 1 Hz for any of the areas. To analyze how many positions of the screen resulted in an activation, we tested binned the firing rates around the peak by comparing the firing rates from the stimulus (Wilcox signed rank, B-test).
