
To solve the air pollution problem in the city center, Oslo, Norway, will achieve "zero private cars" before 2019; Madrid, Spain, announced that by 2020, vehicles will be prohibited from entering an approximately 2-square-kilometer city center area. Of course, to meet people's personalized travel needs, private cars will not disappear for a considerable period of time, but experiencing the freedom of driving a private car does not necessarily require purchasing one. In the future, some service providers offering long-term leasing of driverless cars may emerge.

Autonomous taxis help the public enjoy high-quality shared mobility services at lower costs. In Asian markets (such as China, India, and Singapore), ride-pooling is popular among large populations, which is closely related to the level of economic development to a certain extent. In economically developed European countries (such as the UK, France, Germany, and other European markets), people's acceptance of ride-pooling is relatively lower. However, there are differences in acceptance across age groups. In a survey on willingness to ride-pool in European markets, 45% of respondents under 30 expressed willingness to share a taxi, while only 22% of those aged 50 and above were willing to accept taxi ride-pooling.

The cost of autonomous taxis has a huge impact on large-scale ride-pooling: when the fare is $20, only 11% of respondents are willing to ride-pool; when the cost drops 50% to $10, the proportion willing to ride-pool rises to 37%; when the cost further drops 75% to $5, the proportion rises to 52%. In addition, many respondents actively inquired whether the vehicle is equipped with glass partitions, cameras, and other personal safety features, stating that they would be more willing to ride-pool in autonomous taxis if these are handled properly.

The reason for using small vehicles like taxis instead of large buses is that smaller, more dispersed vehicles have a positive impact on meeting personalized travel needs, improving road traffic efficiency, epidemic prevention and control, and anti-terrorism security.

Some argue that bus environments may promote interpersonal interaction, but the fact is that, except for a few situations such as business meetings or traveling with relatives and friends, people travel more for self-entertainment and relaxation, rather than chatting with strangers with whom they have no connection. Therefore, large bus scenarios not only fail to promote interpersonal interaction but instead impair the travel experience.

In the future, as urban development levels continue to rise and autonomous driving travel costs decrease, more and more people will choose to ride-pool or ride alone in small autonomous vehicles.

### 12.4.3 Intelligent Traffic Management Based on Vehicle-Road Integration

Exploring intelligent traffic management models from the perspective of travel modes will align with the people-centered development philosophy. However, to establish a sound intelligent traffic management model, considering consumers alone is far from enough; it is also necessary to coordinate government departments, manufacturers, dealers, operators, and other entities. From the R&D and design of autonomous vehicles to their deployment and operation, it is inherently a huge and complex systems engineering project that requires collaborative interaction among all parties.

Communication and interaction between `V2V` (vehicle-to-vehicle), `V2I` (vehicle-to-infrastructure), and `V2P` (vehicle-to-pedestrian) require unified industry standards. At the same time, relying solely on a single Internet mobility platform or automaker is far from sufficient to tap into the deep potential value of big transportation data; this requires the sharing and deep cooperation of enterprise resources. In addition, the definition of responsibilities for vehicle quality, traffic accidents, etc., remains unclear, and a widely advocated view proposes that automakers should bear responsibility for the entire process of autonomous vehicle manufacturing and road operation.

Under this model, automakers will be responsible for vehicle R&D, production, configuration, as well as road construction and management, ultimately establishing an intelligent transportation industry model featuring vehicle-road integration and integrated operation, providing strong support for urban traffic development.

Regarding the R&D and production of autonomous vehicles, it is necessary to actively extend upstream and downstream along the industrial chain, which helps enhance the industrial chain's value creation capability and reduce costs and risks. Baidu leverages its resource integration advantages to deeply cooperate with government departments, parts manufacturers, mobility service providers, whole vehicle manufacturers, and industry associations to build and improve the "Smart Mobility" ecosystem, allowing the public to enjoy more convenient and efficient transportation services.

In August 2016, Velodyne, a Silicon Valley technology company in California, USA, announced that its LiDAR subsidiary, Velodyne LiDAR, received investments from Baidu and Ford, with a total investment of $150 million. The capital injection significantly reduced the price of LiDAR products. Benefiting from Velodyne LiDAR's strong supply capacity, the supply price of its 64-channel LiDAR dropped from the previous 700,000 yuan to 500,000 yuan. A Velodyne LiDAR spokesperson stated that when 64-channel LiDAR orders reach the million level, the cost will drop to $500.

In other words, price is expected to no longer be the main obstacle restricting LiDAR sales, and the commercialization process of autonomous driving technology will be further accelerated. Currently, many traditional automakers are investing in autonomous driving startups to seize the initiative. To ensure the safety of autonomous vehicles and improve regulatory capabilities, the service supply and operation management of autonomous vehicles in a certain region will be handled by a single enterprise in the future. At that time, the target customers contested by autonomous vehicle companies will no longer be individual consumers, but cities, regions, or even countries. From the perspective of industrial competition, this helps reduce internal friction within the industry and form a competition pattern dominated by external competition and region-based industrial competition.

Autonomous driving tools such as autonomous taxis are just one of many travel modes; people can also choose bicycles, e-bikes, buses, subways, and other methods. The large-scale popularization of mobile Internet and mobile smart terminals means that cities and regions with more diverse services and more complete infrastructure will attract more talent.

From a technical perspective, it is feasible to improve autonomous driving safety to a level suitable for large-scale application, but after reaching a certain level, raising safety from 80% to 99% is already difficult, and raising it from 99% to 99.99% is even more difficult. The analytical capabilities of big data technology and the computing power of cloud computing technology can indeed surpass humans, but new problems keep emerging. Properly solving these problems requires comprehensive consideration of factors such as emotion, morality, ethics, and law; solutions cannot be designed from a technical perspective alone.

In the long run, after autonomous driving enters the commercialization stage, for a considerable period of time, we cannot entrust safety entirely to autonomous vehicles and systems, but should combine them with more reliable and safer auxiliary measures such as classified and zoned driving.
