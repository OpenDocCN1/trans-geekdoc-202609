The label **Alice** (*priv*<sub>A</sub>, *pub*<sub>B</sub>) on the first line means that Alice holds her own private key, *priv*<sub>A</sub>, as well as Bob’s public key, *pub*<sub>B</sub>. This *priv/pub* key pair is called a *long-term* key because it’s fixed in advance and remains constant through consecutive runs of the protocol. Alice can use her key pair *priv*<sub>A</sub>/*pub*<sub>A</sub> with parties other than Bob, as long as they know *pub*<sub>A</sub> (how they know it is another question and one of the hardest operational problems in cryptography). These long-term private keys should be kept secret, while the public keys are considered to be known to an attacker.

Alice and Bob begin by picking random exponents, *a* and *b*, as in anonymous DH. Alice then calculates *A* and a signature *sig*<sub>A</sub> based on a combination of her signing function **sign**, her private key *priv*<sub>A</sub>, and *A*. Now Alice sends *A* and *sig*<sub>A</sub> to Bob, who verifies *sig*<sub>A</sub> with her public key *pub*<sub>A</sub>. If the signature is invalid, Bob knows that the message didn’t come from Alice, and he discards *A*.

If the signature is correct, Bob computes *g*<sup>ab</sup> from *A* and his random exponent *b*. He then computes *B* and his own signature from a combination of the **sign** function, his private key *priv*<sub>B</sub>, and *B*. He sends *B* and *sig*<sub>B</sub> to Alice, who attempts to verify *sig*<sub>B</sub> with Bob’s public key *pub*<sub>B</sub>. Alice computes *g*<sup>ab</sup> only if Bob’s signature is successfully verified.

# Security Against Network Attackers

Authenticated DH is secure against network attackers because they can’t learn any bit of information on the shared secret *g*<sup>ab</sup> since they ignore the DH exponents. Authenticated DH also provides forward secrecy: even if an attacker corrupts any of the parties at some point, as in the *breach* attack model discussed earlier, they’d learn the private signing keys but not any of the ephemeral DH exponents; hence, they’d be unable to learn the value of any previously shared secrets.
