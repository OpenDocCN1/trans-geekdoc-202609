
2. Vega with the parameter `γ = σ`: [(5.21)](#c05.xhtml#R_c05-disp-0103)

```
{
  d y_t = r y_t dt + (S_t^(θ/2) + σ · θ/2 · S_t^(θ/2 - 1) y_t) dw_t;
  d S_t = r S_t dt + σ S_t^(θ/2) dw_t,
}
```

with initial conditions `y_0 = ∂S_0 / ∂σ = 0` and `S_0`.

Suppose that we are only interested in approximating one Greek `∂f_γ(S_T) / ∂γ` of a derivative with the payoff `f_γ(S_T)`. For the finite differencing method, we have to simulate exactly two out of three different SDE dynamics simultaneously, depending on whether forward or central differencing is used, namely: the three dynamics for the stock price `S_t` with `γ - h`, `γ`, and `γ + h` for some desired small enough `h > 0`. For the pathwise differentiation method, two dynamics still have to be simulated simultaneously, namely the dynamics for `∂S_T / ∂γ` and `S_t`. In general, pathwise differentiation is generally faster and accurate enough, while it is at least as memory-efficient as the finite differencing method.
