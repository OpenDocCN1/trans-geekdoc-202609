To illustrate this, let us continue with our example of US zero‐coupon yield; their respective individual standard deviations are computed in [程序 9.15](#c09.xhtml#c09-sec-3015) via Python and [程序 9.16](#c09.xhtml#c09-sec-3016) via `R`. Suppose that we have a bond portfolio with the money exposure $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ shown in [表 9.1](#c09.xhtml#c09-tbl-0001), and we are interested in the 10‐day 99% V@R of this bond portfolio. As a benchmark from further calculations in [程序 9.15](#c09.xhtml#c09-sec-3015) via Python and [9.16](#c09.xhtml#c09-sec-3016) via `R`, the actual standard deviation of $\Delta \Pi$ is $\sqrt{\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\perp} \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \boldsymbol{\omega}} / 100 \approx 0.07786 \text{ M} = 77,860$, by using the sample one to estimate the population covariance matrix, and the corresponding 10‐day 99% V@R is $\sqrt{10 / 365} z_{0.99}(77,860) = 29,980$. Note that all computations hereafter are based on actual values from the programme outputs, and may be subject to rounding issues.
