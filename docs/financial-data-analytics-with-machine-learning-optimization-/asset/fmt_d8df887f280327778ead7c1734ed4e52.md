
As proposed in [\[8\]](#c12.xhtml#c12-bib-0008), we first seek for the “optimal” splitting boundary `t₁⁽ⁱ⁾`, usually selected among midpoints of consecutive values in `Xᵢ`, such that the corresponding binary partition of `Xᵢ`, say `X₁,₁⁽ⁱ⁾` and `X₁,₂⁽ⁱ⁾`, minimizes the class information entropy ([12.18](#c12.xhtml#c12-disp-0047)) when `kᵢ = 2`. We then repeat the same procedure to seek for the “optimal” splitting boundaries `t₁,₁⁽ⁱ⁾` and `t₁,₂⁽ⁱ⁾` for splitting `X₁,₁⁽ⁱ⁾` and `X₁,₂⁽ⁱ⁾`, respectively, again by minimizing ([12.18](#c12.xhtml#c12-disp-0047)). Say the “optimal” binary partition of `X₁,₁⁽ⁱ⁾` is `X₂,₁⁽ⁱ⁾` and `X₂,₂⁽ⁱ⁾`; while those for `X₁,₂⁽ⁱ⁾` is the pair of `X₂,₃⁽ⁱ⁾` and `X₂,₄⁽ⁱ⁾`. This procedure is carried out iteratively until some pre‐set stopping condition is fulfilled.
