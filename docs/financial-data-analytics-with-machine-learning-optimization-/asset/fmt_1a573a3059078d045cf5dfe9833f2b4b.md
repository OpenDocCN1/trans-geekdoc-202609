
Consider `z = x + y (mod 1)`, where `x` and `y` are continuous random variables on `[0, 1)` with respective density functions `f` and `g`. Then `z` is also a continuous random variable, and it has a density `h` such that

```
h(t) = ∫₀¹ f(u) g(t - u) du = (f * g)(t),
```

which is the usual convolution of `f` and `g` on a bounded range. The Fourier coefficient `ĥ(n)`, for `n ∈ ℤ`, can then be computed as the product of `f̂(n)` and `ĝ(n)`:

**Lemma 4.2** Let `f, g` and `h` be the continuous probability density functions of `x, y` and `z = x + y (mod 1)`, respectively, where `x` and `y` are continuous random variables on `[0, 1]`. Then, `ĥ(n) = f̂(n) ĝ(n)`.
