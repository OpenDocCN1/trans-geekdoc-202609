
Despite the apparent absence of the acceptance probability and the seemingly different parameter updating mechanism in Gibbs sampling (compared with the Metropolis–Hastings algorithm or its single component variant), there is a close connection between them as each updating substep `i` in a cycle in the former is actually a step in the latter with the corresponding acceptance probability `α_i ≡ 1`. To see this, we consider the `i`-th updating step in the cycle at the time `t`, where the joint distribution `π(θ̄ x₁, …, xₙ | θ₋ᵢ = θ₋ᵢ⁽ᵗ⁾)` is the target distribution, where `θ₋ᵢ⁽ᵗ⁾ := (θ₁⁽ᵗ⁺¹⁾, …, θᵢ₋₁⁽ᵗ⁺¹⁾, θᵢ₊₁⁽ᵗ⁾, …, θᵣ⁽ᵗ⁾)⊤`.
