
At the force of interest `δ`, a fund is immunized against a small change `ε` in the force of interest if (i) `P_A(δ; 0) = P_L(δ; 0)` and (ii) `P_A(δ + ε; 0) ≥ P_L(δ + ε; 0)` for `ε` sufficiently close to 0. Using the Taylor series expansion, these conditions translate to `P'_A(δ; 0) = P'_L(δ; 0)` and `P''_A(δ; 0) ≥ P''_L(δ; 0)`, i.e., the Macaulay duration (resp. convexity) of the assets should be equal to (resp. greater than) that of the liabilities. If we denote the difference of their time values at time `t` by `P(δ; t) := P_A(δ; t) - P_L(δ; t)`, the conditions above can be rewritten as `P'(δ; 0) = 0` and `P''(δ; 0) ≥ 0`.

Let us provide an example of achieving the *full immunization* against changes of any magnitude in the interest rate. Consider a single liability cash outflow of `L_T` at the time `T`. Full immunization can be achieved by holding two assets providing cash inflows of `A` at time `0 ≤ T - a` and `B` at time `T + b` respectively, for some `a, b > 0` and `a ≤ T`.
