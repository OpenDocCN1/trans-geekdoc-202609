
where the `i`‐th summand solely depends on the `i`‐th row of `J_r(β)` which is the gradient of the `i`‐th residual `r_i(β)` that in turn is just the residual with respect to the `i`‐th datum only; so each of these `J_i(β)`'s contributes to `S(β)` independently of the others. On the other hand, consider `(1/n) J_r(β)^⊤ J_r(β) p` as an average: in light of the law of large numbers, it stabilizes as `n` is large, and one can save much calculation by only coping with a small proportion `c ∈ (0, 1)` of the overall `n` residuals. More precisely, one can approximate the average of the total sum by only summing over a mini‐batch of the original sample as `(1/n) J_r(β)^⊤ J_r(β) p ≈ (1/(cn)) Σ_{i=1}^{cn} J_i(β) p J_i(β)^⊤` for some `c << 1`. As an aside, we note the independence among the summands in the representation ([10.7](#c10.xhtml#c10-disp-0018)) makes parallel computing feasible.
