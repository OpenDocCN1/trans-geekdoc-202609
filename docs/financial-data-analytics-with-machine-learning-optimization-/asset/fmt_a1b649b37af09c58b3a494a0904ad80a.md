
Now we include all the newly constructed `v_i`'s and `u_j`'s in matrices `V` and `U`, by letting `V = (V_r ; v_(r+1), ..., v_n)` and `U = (U_r ; u_(r+1), ..., u_m)`, and so `V` and `U` are both square matrices. Also form the `m × n` matrix `Σ` by adding to `Σ_r` with `m - r` new zero rows and `n - r` new zero columns. These definitions lead to `A v_i = 0` for `i = r + 1, ..., n`, and together with ([9.18](#c09.xhtml#c09-disp-0068)), we conclude that `A V = U Σ`. Finally, since `V` is orthogonal by construction, we also have `A = U Σ V^T`.

To summarize, `u`'s and `v`'s form the bases for the following four fundamental spaces:

*   `u_1, ..., u_r` form an orthonormal basis for the column space of `A`;
*   `u_(r+1), ..., u_m` form an orthonormal basis for the null space `N(A^T)`;
*   `v_1, ..., v_r` form an orthonormal basis for the row space of `A`;
*   `v_(r+1), ..., v_n` form an orthonormal basis for the null space `N(A)`.
