

The `Bitstamp_BTCUSD_2018_minute.csv` dataset contains intraday minute data for Bitcoin in 2018. Let us train an ARIMA model using the observations of `Close` in the fourth quarter of 2018, save for the last day, 31 December. Indeed, we want to use our ARIMA model to predict the closing prices every minute that day. Note that in 2018, unlike the rapid rise in the previous years, the value of a Bitcoin dropped drastically from USD 13,841 to USD 3,689. The upper plot in [Figure 7.5](#c07.xhtml#c07-fig-0005) via Python (resp. [7.6](#c07.xhtml#c07-fig-0006) via `R`) illustrates the movement of Bitcoin minute closing prices in the fourth quarter of 2018, and we note that there are many fierce jumps in the closing prices. These are likely to be regime-switching signals for parameters, and it would be reasonable to assume that the prices before and after the jumps should have different moving dynamics, and different statistical mean and volatility. Therefore, with this in mind, we choose only the time period after the most recent significant spike of standard deviation as the training period for our ARIMA model, that is after 00:21:00 on 30 Dec 2018 as indicated by the orange dashed line on the bottom plot in [Figure 7.5](#c07.xhtml#c07-fig-0005) via Python (resp. [7.6](#c07.xhtml#c07-fig-0006) via `R`).

We then select an appropriate ARIMA model by determining the values of the model parameters ![p comma d comma q](images/c07-i0118.png)<math display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mi>p</mi><mo>,</mo><mi>d</mi><mo>,</mo><mi>q</mi></math>; see the respective Python codes in [Programme 7.7](#c07.xhtml#c07-gra-0007) and `R` codes in [Programme 7.8](#c07.xhtml#c07-gra-0008), and the corresponding sample ACF and PACF plots are shown in [Figures 7.7](#c07.xhtml#c07-fig-0007) and [7.8](#c07.xhtml#c07-fig-0008), respectively. We observe that the sample ACF plot of the time series does not have a cut-off point, nor does it decay exponentially, indicating the non-stationarity of the time series and the need for differencing. After applying 1-lag differencing to the Bitcoin prices, i.e., ![d equals 1](images/c07-i0119.png)<math display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mi>d</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>1</mn></math>, both sample ACF and PACF plots of the resulting time series roughly exhibit the cut-off property at ![k equals 2](images/c07-i0120.png)<math display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mi>k</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>2</mn></math>. We therefore consider ARIMA(![2 comma 1 comma 0](images/c07-i0121.png)<math display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mn>2</mn><mo>,</mo><mn>1</mn><mo>,</mo><mn>0</mn></math>), ARIMA(![0 comma 1 comma 2](images/c07-i0122.png)<math display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mn>0</mn><mo>,</mo><mn>1</mn><mo>,</mo><mn>2</mn></math>), and ARIMA(![2 comma 1 comma 2](images/c07-i0123.png)<math display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mn>2</mn><mo>,</mo><mn>1</mn><mo>,</mo><mn>2</mn></math>) as the candidate models.

For the selection between these three candidates, the arguably most prevalent thresholds are various *information criteria*, which depend on both the likelihood ![ModifyingAbove upper L With caret](images/c07-i0124.png)<math display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mover accent="true"><mi>L</mi><mo>&#94;</mo></mover></math> of the fitted model and the number ![m](images/c07-i0125.png)<math display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>m</mi></mrow></math> of model parameters. Here we adopt the *Akaike Information Criterion* ![AIC colon equals minus 2 ln ModifyingAbove upper L With caret plus 2 m](images/c07-i0126.png)<math display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mtext>AIC</mtext><mo>:</mo><mo>=</mo><mo>&#8722;</mo><mn>2</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">ln</mi><mover accent="true"><mi>L</mi><mo>&#94;</mo></mover><mo>+</mo><mn>2</mn><mi>m</mi></math> as the decision criterion of model selection; a smaller value of AIC indicates a better model; also see [Section 10.6](#c10.xhtml#head-2-139) in [Chapter 10](#c10.xhtml) for more discussions on AIC and BIC. We can simply fit the candidate models above and prompt for their respective AIC values, as shown in [Programme 7.9](#c07.xhtml#c07-gra-0009) via Python and 7.10 via `R`. The AIC values of the fitted ARIMA(![2 comma 1 comma 0](images/c07-i0127.png)<math display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mn>2</mn><mo>,</mo><mn>1</mn><mo>,</mo><mn>0</mn></math>) and ARIMA(![0 comma 1 comma 2](images/c07-i0128.png)<math display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mn>0</mn><mo>,</mo><mn>1</mn><mo>,</mo><mn>2</mn></math>) are 8,319.95 and 8,316.41, respectively; both are higher than that of the fitted ARIMA(![2 comma 1 comma 2](images/c07-i0129.png)<math display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mn>2</mn><mo>,</mo><mn>1</mn><mo>,</mo><mn>2</mn></math>) model (less than 8,300), therefore ARIMA(![2 comma 1 comma 2](images/c07-i0130.png)<math display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mn>2</mn><mo>,</mo><mn>1</mn><mo>,</mo><mn>2</mn></math>) is our preferred model for this example.
