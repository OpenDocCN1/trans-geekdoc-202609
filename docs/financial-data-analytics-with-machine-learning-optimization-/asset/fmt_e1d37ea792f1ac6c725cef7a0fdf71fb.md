
Although the CH index as a clustering metric to evaluate the appropriateness of the hyperparameter `K` shows good performances in many practical examples such as those in this chapter, it still fails in an obvious manner in some cases; this issue is also briefly mentioned in `[4](#c14.xhtml#c14-bib-0004)`, while we shall provide a mathematical discussion as follows. For instance, consider the data points generated by uniform distribution with the support `[0, 1]`, and we assume that the true clusters are `[0, 1/K)`, `[1/K, 2/K)`, `...`, `[(K-1)/K, 1]` with points generated uniformly from each bucket for some positive integer `K`. In the limiting case of `n → ∞`, suppose that an `L`‐means clustering is applied to the dataset, then by symmetry, the resulting `L` clusters are the `L` equal‐length subintervals of `[0, 1]`, namely `[0, 1/L)`, `[1/L, 2/L)`, `...`, `[(L-1)/L, 1]`. Clearly, each point has an equal probability of `1/L` of falling into each of the `L` clusters, i.e. `p·l = 1/L` for all `l = 1, ..., L`, and given that the point is in the `l`‐th cluster `[(l-1)/L, l/L)`, the conditional distribution of its value is another uniform distribution `U((l-1)/L, l/L)` with the mean `(l-0.5)/L` and variance `1/(12L²)`.
