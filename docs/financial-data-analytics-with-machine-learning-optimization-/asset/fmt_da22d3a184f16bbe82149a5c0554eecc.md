
We observe that an additional factor `βₙ⁻¹` appears in the variance of this predictive distribution compared with its original conditional distribution `N(μ, σ²)` given `(μ, σ²)`; this reflects the additional uncertainty regarding the convergence of `μₙ` to `μ`. Recall that `βₙ⁻¹` is the posterior variance of `μ` given `x = x`, which decreases to zero with the increase of the sample size `n`, and so does the variance of this predictive distribution. That means as `n → ∞`, `βₙ⁻¹ → 0`, and the variance of `x` reduces to `β⁻¹ = σ²`, which echoes the convergence of `μₙ` towards the true value of `μ`. Altogether this implies that the predictive distribution of `xₙ₊₁` converges to the true distribution as `n → ∞`.

As an illustration, we revisit the example in [Figure 1.4](#c01.xhtml#c01-fig-0004), where the samples are drawn from `N(2, 1)`, and we perform Bayesian inference on the mean with the prior distribution `N(-1, 1)`. In [Figure 1.5](#c01.xhtml#c01-fig-0005), we plot the predictive distribution of `xₙ₊₁` for various values of sample size `n`, namely `N(μₙ, sₙ² := β⁻¹ + βₙ⁻¹)` (in solid lines), with the true distribution `N(2, 1)` (in dashed line), and clearly the former converges to the latter as `n` increases.
