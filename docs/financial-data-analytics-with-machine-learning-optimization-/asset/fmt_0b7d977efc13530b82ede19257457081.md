
Another approach for calculating V@R is based on the assumption that the corresponding relative returns of the `p` market variables `u_i,j = (v_i,j - v_i-1,j) / v_i-1,j` at time `i`, and for `j = 1, ..., p`, follow a `p`-variate independently and identically distributed normal random vector, i.e. `u_i ~ iid N_p(0, Σ)`. Assume that we have `$ω = (ω_1, ..., ω_p)^T` invested on these `p` market variables. We bought, for `j = 1, ..., p`, `ω_j / v_0,j` shares of the `j`-th market variable at time 0. When the market price changes from `v_0,j` to `v_1,j` at time 1, the market value of the newly acquired stock becomes `ω_j * (v_1,j / v_0,j)` and the monetary change is `ω_j * (v_1,j / v_0,j) - ω_j = ω_j * (v_1,j - v_0,j) / v_0,j = ω_j * u_1,j`.
