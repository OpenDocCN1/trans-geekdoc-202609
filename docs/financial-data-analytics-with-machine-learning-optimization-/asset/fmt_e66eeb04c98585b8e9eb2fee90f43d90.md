
3. **Student's t-distribution:** If $z \sim N(0, 1)$ is independent of $x \sim \chi^2_\nu$, then $t := \frac{z}{\sqrt{x/\nu}}$ is said to follow a $t$-distribution $t_\nu$ with $\nu$ degrees of freedom. As a result, if $y_1, \dots, y_n$ are iid $N(\mu, \sigma^2)$, then $\frac{\sqrt{n}(\bar{y} - \mu)}{s_y} \sim t_{n-1}$; indeed, since $\bar{y} \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2/n)$, we have $z := \frac{\sqrt{n}(\bar{y} - \mu)}{\sigma} \sim N(0, 1)$, and $x := \frac{(n-1)s_y^2}{\sigma^2} \sim \chi^2_{n-1}$, furthermore $\bar{y}$ is independent of $s_y^2$ in accordance with [Proposition 1.2](#c01.xhtml#c01-fea-0008). Therefore, $t = \frac{z}{\sqrt{x/(n-1)}} = \frac{\sqrt{n}(\bar{y} - \mu)}{s_y} \sim t_{n-1}$.
