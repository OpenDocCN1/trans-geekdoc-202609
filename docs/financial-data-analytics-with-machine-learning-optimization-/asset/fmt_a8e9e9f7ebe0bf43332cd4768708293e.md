
Finally, we have a similar asymptotic result for V@R under extreme-value copulae as that under Archimedean copulae. When the loss variables `x₁, …, x_d` have an identical distribution and their dependence is given by an extreme-value copula, [\[10\]](#c08.xhtml#c08-bib-0010) established the following results concerning the Value-at-Risk of the aggregate loss `s = x₁ + … + x_d` when the probability level approaches 1:

1. If `x₁ ∈ MDA(Φ_β)`, then `V@R` is (i) asymptotically subadditive when `β > 1`; (ii) asymptotically superadditive when `β < 1`; and (iii) asymptotically additive when `β = 1`.

2. If `x₁ ∈ MDA(Λ)` with an infinite right endpoint, then `V@R` is asymptotically subadditive.

3. If `x₁` has a finite endpoint, then `V@R` is asymptotically additive.

It is believed that the above claims, 1) to 3), remain valid by and large, even for heterogeneous distributions and when the dependence structures among the variables are modelled by general copulae exhibiting certain kinds of positive dependence, and this would be an interesting future research project.

Last but not least, we give a brief introduction to the trendy vine copula, which has found extensive use in finance, such as for the effective modelling of tail risk in portfolio optimization applications (see [\[1\]](#c08.xhtml#c08-bib-0001) for detailed discussions). A vine is a graphical tool used to label constraints in high-dimensional probability distributions. It is a nested set of connected trees, where the edges in one tree become the nodes of the next tree to be built. As a special case in which all constraints are two-dimensional or conditional two-dimensional, regular vines (a.k.a. R-vines) get more popular since the computation of joint probabilities is carried out in a sequential pairwise manner. Indeed, when the constraints are associated with conditional bivariate copulae, we impose the vine copula, such that any multivariate density can be represented as a product of marginal densities and conditional copula densities on an R-vine. One can interpret vine copulae as copulae that characterize the dependence structures among clusters of random variables. To this end, the Comonotone-Independence Bayes classifier (CIBer), which we shall introduce in [Chapter 12](#c12.xhtml), can be regarded as a special case of vine copula, in that the dependence structures there are handled in an all-or-nothing manner, namely either comonotonicity or independence. Due to space limitations, we shall not present its formulation and applications in detail.
