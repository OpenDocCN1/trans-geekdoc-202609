
CAPM is widely applied in modern finance. For instance, the linear relationship ([10.2](#c10.xhtml#c10-disp-0002)) between the `β`‐factor and the expected return of the security in the CAPM, a.k.a. the *Security Market Line* (SML), is often used to determine whether an asset is properly valued. Usually, `β_i` stays roughly constant at the equilibrium level for almost every industrial sector, and if the sample mean of `r_i` over the latest period becomes too high (resp. low) for a stock, it then receives an overall downward (resp. upward) pressure that pushes its price back to the corresponding equilibrium level on the SML. In the real market, these kinds of adjustments often take place in a relatively short time. Moreover, *Jensen's index* [\[11\]](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0011) `J := μ̂ − (r_f + β̂(μ̂_m − r_f))`, where `μ̂_m` is the empirical mean of the market index `r_m`, is a well‐known performance evaluation reference index that detects any possible mispricing via the difference between the estimated return of the stock `μ̂` observed from the market data and the “natural” return of `r_f + β̂(μ̂_m − r_f)` as predicted by the SML in CAPM. The *Sharpe ratio*, the empirical realization of `β_i`, `β̂_i = (μ̂_i − r_f) / σ̂_i`, measures the leverage effect of the `i`‐th stock, i.e. its sensitivity to changes of the underlying market risk. If `β̂_i` of a stock is equal to (resp. less than) the market beta `β_m` (or its empirical counterpart `β̂_m = (μ̂_m − r_f) / σ̂_m`), then the stock is said to be *efficient* (resp. *inefficient*); likewise if `β̂_i > β̂_m`, then the stock is considered more efficient than the market portfolio. Last but not least, the certainty equivalent pricing formula is frequently adopted in evaluating (single‐period) future projects for companies, and it is derived from CAPM as follows. To calculate the present value `P` of a project with a random terminal payoff `Q`, we apply CAPM to the expectation of its rate of return `r = (Q − P) / P`:

[(10.3)](#c10.xhtml#R_c10-disp-0003) `𝔼(r) = (𝔼(Q) − P) / P = r_f + β(μ_m − r_f).`
