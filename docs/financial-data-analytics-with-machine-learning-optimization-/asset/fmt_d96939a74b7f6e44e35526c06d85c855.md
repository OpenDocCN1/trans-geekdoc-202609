
For the estimation of `x_min`, we recall the notion of Kolmogorov–Smirnov (KS) distance as introduced in [Section 3.1](#c03.xhtml#head-2-19), and then follow the approach proposed in [[5]](#c04.xhtml#c04-bib-0005). Consider the truncated KS distance beyond `x_min` between the empirical and theoretical power law distributions defined by:

(4.20)`K(x_min) := max_{x ≥ x_min} |F̂(x) − F(x)|`

where `F̂(x)` is the empirical distribution of the data above `x_min`, and `F(x)` is the power law distribution of best fit for data above `x_min`; also see [[7]](#c04.xhtml#c04-bib-0007). To estimate `x_min`, [[5]](#c04.xhtml#c04-bib-0005) suggests the estimate `x̂_min` to be the minimizer of `K(x_min)`, which can be numerically obtained via the **R** package `poweRlaw`.

Having obtained an estimate `x̂_min`, the two most common approaches to estimating the parameter `β` in ([4.13](#c04.xhtml#c04-disp-0050)) are ordinary least squares (OLS) and Hill's estimator. As noted in [[16]](#c04.xhtml#c04-bib-0016), while OLS is the most common method employed in the empirical literature, it tends to underestimate the power exponent. An alternative estimator known as Hill's estimator was proposed by [[17]](#c04.xhtml#c04-bib-0017), and is defined by

(4.21)`β̂_Hills := (n_tail − 1) / Σ_{i=1}^{n_tail − 1} (ln(z_i) − ln(z_n_tail))`

A test for whether the underlying distribution of the data above this estimated minimal threshold `x̂_min` is a power law distribution with the estimated tail index `β̂` is proposed in [[5]](#c04.xhtml#c04-bib-0005); again, this can be implemented using the package `poweRlaw`. This test is motivated by the notion of *bootstrapping* (see alternative discussion in [Chapter 8](#c08.xhtml)), in the sense that the distribution of the test statistic, namely the truncated KS distance `K(x̂_min)`, is approximated by the empirical distribution of the truncated KS distances of the bootstrapped samples from the fitted distribution.
