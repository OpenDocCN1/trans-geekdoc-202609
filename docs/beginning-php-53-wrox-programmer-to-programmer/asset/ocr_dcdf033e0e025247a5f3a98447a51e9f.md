# Introduction

Chapter 15 introduces PEAR, the PHP Extension and Application Repository. It's a large collection of reusable code modules that can really help to speed up your application development. You look at how to install and use PEAR graphics, and explore three useful packages: Net_User_Detect, HTML_Table, and HTML_QuickForm.

Chapter 16 looks at various ways that your PHP applications can interact with the outside world. You take a detailed look at date and time handling, including various built-in date functions, as as the DateTime and DateTimeZone classes. You also look at how to work closely with HTTP request and response headers, to retrieve Web server information, and how to send email from your scripts.

Chapter 17 shows you how to use PHP to generate graphics on the fly. You study some graphics fundamentals, then look at how to create images, and as well modify existing images. Along the way you use colors, drawing functions, image formats, transparency, opacity, and generating text within images.

Chapter 18 looks at the power of regular expressions. These clever pattern-matching tools let you search for very specific patterns within strings. The chapter introduces regular expression syntax, and shows how to use PHP's regular expression tools to search for and replace patterns of text. Lots of examples are included to make the concepts clear.

Chapter 19 explores XML —extensible Markup Language — and shows how to manipulate XML from your PHP scripts. You learn about XML and its uses, and look at various ways to read and write XML with PHP, including XML Parser, the DOM extension, and SimpleXML. You also take a brief look at XML stylesheets, including XSL and XSLT.

Chapter 20 wraps up the book with discussion on good programming practices. You look at strategies for writing modular code; how to design and implement coding standards for consistency; ways to document your code; how to harden your applications against attack; how to handle errors gracefully; why it's important to separate application from presentation logic; and ways to test your application for robustness.

Appendix A answers to the exercises found throughout the book.

Appendix B looks at how to configure PHP, and lists all the configuration directives available.

Appendix C explores alternative databases to MySQL that are supported by PHP.

Appendix D shows you how to use PHP to write command-line scripts, further enhancing the flexibility and flexibility of your applications.
