We also run a six-fold CV routine, repeatedly fitting PLS($K$) for $K = 1$ , . . . , 10 and evaluating on the left-out fold. The results are shown in Figure 7.22 and we compare against a lasso for octane regressed onto the raw NIR values. PLS performance improves from $K = 1$ to $K = 3$ and flattens for $K > 3$. While the lasso is able to obtain a comparable MSE to PLS with $K = 3$, it may be preferable to work with (and build interpretation around) three PLS factors rather than a sparse set of direct effects at different wavelengths. This interpretability is often the main advantage for PLS over a raw lasso regression.
