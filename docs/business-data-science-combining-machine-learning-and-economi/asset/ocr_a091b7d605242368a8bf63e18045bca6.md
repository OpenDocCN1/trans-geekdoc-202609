But it is often the case that decision-makers want to understand the effects of policies that are not themselves randomized but are rather downstream of the things being AB tested. For example, suppose an algorithm is used to predict the credit-worthiness of potential borrowers and assign loans. Even if the process of loan assignment is never itself randomized, if the parameters in the machine learning algorithms used to score credit are AB tested, then those experiments can be used as instruments for the loan assignment treatment. Such “ upstream randomization” is extremely common, and IV analysis is your key tool for doing causal inference in that setting.
