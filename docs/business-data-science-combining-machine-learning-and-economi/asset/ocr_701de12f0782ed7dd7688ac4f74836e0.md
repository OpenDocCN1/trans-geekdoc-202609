However, this is a great setting for a diff-in-diff analysis. We have no reason to think that there was anything other than the SEM turnoff that would cause the relative difference between treatment and control group DMAs to change after May 22. Thus, if SEM works, the revenue difference between treatment and control groups should be larger after May 22. This pre-post comparison across groups is the logic underlying a diff-in-diff analysis.

Here we will focus on differences in log revenue because we anticipate that the treatment and control groups are related on percentage scale. The treatment DMAs have about 38% of the revenue of the control DMAs when SEM is on for both. Figure 5.3 shows the log difference between average revenues in each group (this is the log of the ratio of the top line over the bottom line from Figure 5.2). There does appear to be an increase in the log difference following May 22. Is it real (i.e., statistically significant), and what are the implications for the return on investment for SEM?
