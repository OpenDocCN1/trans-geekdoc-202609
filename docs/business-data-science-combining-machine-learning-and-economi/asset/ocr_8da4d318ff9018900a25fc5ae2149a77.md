As a round-up on PCR, we contrast it to the lasso regression techniques from Chapter 3. Lasso on raw x finds a sparse model (many β̂j = 0), whereas PCR assumes a dense model where all the xij values matter but only through the information they provide on a few simple factors. Both strategies rely upon dimension reduction; which is best will depend upon the application. Figure 7.18 shows results of a 20-fold (i.e., leave-out two shows each time) OOS prediction experiment for three regressions—a standard lasso for y on x, a PCR lasso for y on v, and a hybrid lasso onto both x and v. In this example, the PCR regression outperforms the lasso onto x. The hybrid regression does roughly as well as the PCR lasso, but with some added variance across folds (wider uncertainty bars).
