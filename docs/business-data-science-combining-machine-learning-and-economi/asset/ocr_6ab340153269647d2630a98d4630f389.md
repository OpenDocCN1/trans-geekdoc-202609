$$\sigma^2 = \mathbb{E}[(x-\mu)^2] \cdot$ You can show this by taking the expectation of $s \ 2$, which demonstrates that $\mathbb{E} [s \ 2] \ne \sigma 2:$

$$\mathbb{E}[s^2] = \mathbb{E}[(x-\mu)^2] - \mathbb{E}[(\bar{x} - \mu)^2] = \sigma^2 - \sigma^2/n.$$$$
