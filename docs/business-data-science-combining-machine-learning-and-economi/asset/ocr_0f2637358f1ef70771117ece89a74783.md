+   3. Most finance modeling works on units of *excess returns*: an asset’s rate of return minus a measure of the “risk-free rate” — in other words, what you would have made by holding low-risk debt. A common measure of the risk-free rate is the return on U.S. Treasury bills (T-bills) — say, $u$ at time $t$. You work with $r_{t}=(y_{t}-y_{t-1}) / y_{t-1}-u$, the return on equity $j$ at time $t$ minus the T-bill returns. Similarly, the market returns are the S&P 500 returns minus $u$. Since $u$ is small, this makes little difference, and the detail is often omitted.
