See Algorithm 14. In this algorithm, by estimating $\hat{\boldsymbol{d}}$ and including it in Equation 6.4, we’ve isolated where $d$ differs *randomly* from what is expected given the confounders. These random surprises are like experiments in $d$, and in the second step we estimate the effect of these surprise movements on $y$. That is, we are using the first “treatment regression” step to construct a near-experimental design.

# ALGORITHM 14 LTE Lasso Regression

+   i. Use a CV or AICc lasso to estimate $\mathbb{E}[d \mid x] = x^\prime \tau$ and collect the fitted values $\hat{d}_i = x_i^\prime \hat{\tau}$.
ii. Use a CV or AICc lasso to estimate

$\mathbb{E}[y \mid \boldsymbol{x}, \boldsymbol{d}] = \hat{\boldsymbol{d}}\vartheta + \boldsymbol{d}\boldsymbol{\gamma} + \boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{\beta}$ (6.4)

with *no penalty* on $\boldsymbol{\vartheta}$ (i.e., you’ve included $\hat{\boldsymbol{d}}$ unpenalized).
Then $\hat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}$ is your estimate for the treatment effect of $d$ on $y$.

To understand how this works, note that a theoretically equivalent (for big $n$) algorithm fits step i and then regresses $y$ onto $x$ and the fitted residuals, $\hat{v} = \boldsymbol{d} - \hat{\boldsymbol{d}}$. The coefficient on $\hat{v}$ then provides $\hat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}$, your treatment effect estimate. To see why the effect of $v$ is the same as the *random* effect of $d$, consider combining the information from the LTE system of Equation 6.2 into a single response equation:

$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E} \left[y \mid \boldsymbol{x}, \boldsymbol{d}\right] & = \boldsymbol{d} \boldsymbol{\gamma} + \boldsymbol{x} \boldsymbol{\beta} \\ & = \left(\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime} \boldsymbol{\tau} + \boldsymbol{v}\right) \boldsymbol{\gamma} + \boldsymbol{x} \boldsymbol{\beta} \\ & = \boldsymbol{v} \boldsymbol{\gamma} + \boldsymbol{x}^{\prime}\left(\boldsymbol{\gamma} \boldsymbol{\tau} + \boldsymbol{\beta}\right). \end{aligned}$

The last line is the *structural* version of the reduced-form regression,
$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{y} \mid \boldsymbol{x}, \boldsymbol{d}\right] &= \hat{\boldsymbol{v}} \boldsymbol{\gamma} + \boldsymbol{x} \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}, \end{aligned}$ (6.5)
