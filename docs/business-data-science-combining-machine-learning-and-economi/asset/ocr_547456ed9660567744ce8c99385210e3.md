where, $\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}} = \boldsymbol{\gamma}\boldsymbol{\tau} + \boldsymbol{\beta}$. Thus $\gamma$ is the same across both models—the effect of $\nu$ on $y$ is the same as the structural effect of $d$ on $y$. Controlling for $\hat{d}$ unpenalized in Equation 6.4 is essentially the same as estimating the treatment effect of residuals $\hat{\nu}$. In both cases, $\hat{\gamma}$ is based on variation in $d$ that is orthogonal to $\hat{d}$ (i.e., independent from $\hat{d}$ in the sample).
