# SUMMARY

The widespread adoption of deep learning to neural activity relies on "black-box" approaches that lack an interpretable connection between neural activity and network parameters. We propose using algorithm, a method for interpretable deep learning, to design the architecture of deconvolutional neural networks and obtain a direct interpretation of neural weights in stimulus-driven single-neuron activity through a generative model. We introduce method deconvolutional neural network (DUNL), and demonstrate its versatility by applying it to deconvolve single-trial signals across multiple areas and recording modalities. We uncovered multiple salience and prediction error signals in dopamine neuronsatosatosensory thalamus and characterize the heterogeneity of neural responses in the motor cortex and striatum under unstructured, naturalistic experiments. Our work leverages advantages in interpretable deep learning to provide a mechanistic understanding of neural activity.
