**Figure 3.** Decomposition of size, reward and variance in dopamine data
(A) Experimental setup: GRIN lens on the surface of the cortex allows for imaging of neurons in deep structures. GRIN lens track the VTA (bottom, scalebar: 600 µm) and projection of the field of view observed during an experimental acquisition under the two-photon microscope showing neurons (bottom right).
(B) Left: time-aligned of trials at outset, reward size by reward (one horizontal separates trial type). Right: averaged-time-aligned activity of an example neuron for each reward size.
(C) Kernel denconvolution of reward and times. Three kernels estimate the reward response: one for size (blue) and two non-concurrent kernels for positive and negative value (red).
(D) Neuronal spiking activity across time trials, stimulus number, kind, and probabilistic estimate for continuous data.
(E) Neural encoding as a function of reward size (unexpected) and expected (bottom) trials. Each line represents a neuron, with black lines showing averages for expected trials and red dashed lines for negative. Activity is normalized per neuron and across trials and for comparison subpopulations.
(F) Spatial correlation between codes (x axis) and unwanted activity (y axis) with respect to reward size. Dots represent neurons, and large markers show averages for expected (blue) and unexpected trials (Reward Combined has p = 0.008, and p = $3.5 \times 10^{-3}$ test, respectively). The third panel (brown) from the left combines the code from Reward Coupled kernels (positive and negative, depending on the trial). The final panel combines the right-ward related correlations (size-like and reward-value-coupled).
