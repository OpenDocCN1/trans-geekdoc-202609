# 2. Methods

# 2.1. DFT calculations

All electronic and vibrational frequencies were calculated using the Vienna ab initio software [38,39] (VASP 5.4) and the Atomic Molecular Environment [40]. We used the projected plane-wave (PAW) method [41,42] to describe the electrons and the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof generalized gradient approximation (GGA) to calculate the exchange-correlation energies. The D correction [43] was added to account for dispersion interactions. The Kohn-Sham electron-wave functions were expanded in a plane-wave basis. The convergence for the self-consistent electronic iteration was met with a threshold of $10^{-6}$ eV. The plane-wave mesh was converged with a force threshold of $10^{-2}$ eV/Å. The k-point mesh was constructed using the Monkhorst-Pack scheme [45]. The conjugate gradient algorithm with a force threshold of 0.02 eV/Å was used for ionic atoms.

All structures were relaxed with four-layer slabs, and in the top layers were allowed to relax in all directions. We stopped until 15 eV of vacuum was added to minimize the interaction between slabs, and dipole moments were converged to a potential energy. The reaction energy was calculated as the difference between the final state energy $E_{final}$ and initial state $E_{initial}$. The activation energy was defined as the difference between the transition state (TS) energy $E_{TS}$ and initial state $E_{initial}$.

The climbing dimer elastic band (CI-NEB method [47] was used with the dimer method [48] to search for the TS of reactions, and were verified by vibrational analysis searching for the existence of only one imaginary mode. The reaction path was determined and converged with a force below below 0.05 eV/Å. The TS determined by the CI-NE method was used as the input to the dimer method. The VIIST tools were used for CI-NE, dimer method, and frequency calculations [49].

Here we use these BEP relations for large hydrocarbon C-C and C-H bond cracking. We used three data sets to construct the BEP relations for all types of carbon species.

The BEPs are better for C-C and C-H cracking with a MAE of 0.19 eV for metals. For C-C cracking the accuracy is higher (0.2 eV). C-C cracking reactions have larger activation energies than C-H cracking reactions respectively. The reaction barrier depends not only on the metal, but also on the ionic bond [59]. The C-C and C-H bonds bond differently on surfaces.
