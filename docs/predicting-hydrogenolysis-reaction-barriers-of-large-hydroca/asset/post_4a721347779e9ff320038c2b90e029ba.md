| Keywords: | Calculating activation energies of chemical reactions for large reaction networks is computationally demanding. Brunsted-Evans-Polanyi (BEP) relations are used to predict activation energies, but they are prone to substantial errors. Here, machine learning is used to predict energies for reactions on metals, including Ru, Pt, and Rh. The dataset includes 380 DFT-calculated activation energies of C-C and C-H reactions from C1 to C65. The mean absolute error of BEP is 0.38 eV, providing predictions on hydrocarbons with much larger errors. In comparison, an XGBoost model achieves better accuracy for the DFT-calculated energies. The model selects the most features among reactions and the electronegativity and atomic mass of the metal atoms as keys. The model appears transferable across classes. This approach can provide rapid and accurate estimates of energies for recycling. We showcase the impact of hydrocarbon chain length and branching on barriers, discuss implications for the depolymerization rate of polypropylene and low-density polyethylene, and the feasibility of hydrolysis catalysis. |

# 2. Methods

## 2.1. DFT calculations

All electronic and vibrational frequencies were calculated using the Vienna ab initio software [38,39] (VASP 5.4) and the Atomic Molecular Environment [40]. We used the projected plane-wave (PAW) method [41,42] to describe the electrons and the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof generalized gradient approximation (GGA) to calculate the exchange-correlation energies. The D correction [43] was added to account for dispersion interactions. The Kohn-Sham electron-wave functions were expanded in a plane-wave basis. The convergence for the self-consistent electronic iteration was met with a threshold of $10^{-6}$ eV. The plane-wave mesh was converged with a force of $10^{-2}$ eV/Å. The k-point mesh was constructed using the Monkhorst-Pack scheme [45]. All structures were relaxed with four-layer slabs, and in the top layers were allowed to relax in all directions. We stopped until 15 eV of vacuum was added to minimize the interaction between slabs, and dipole moments were converged to a potential energy.

We consider the barriers of 266 C-C cracking and 114 C-H 380-dehydration reaction of C2 to C4 for a total of 380 dehydrogens. ## 2.3. Machine learning (ML) models

In developing models, we included all reaction 380 of the DFT activation barriers. The software used was scikit-learn (version 1.0.2) which we used for this work. We tested all models, 80% of the data were randomly selected and used to train the model, while the remaining 20% was used to test the model. K-fold cross-validation (k=10) was used to avoid overfitting. The XGBoost regression [58] showed the best performance. The hyperparameters were optimized using Bayesian optimization as used by Lee [53]. The optimal hyperparameters for each model are summarized in Table S1.
