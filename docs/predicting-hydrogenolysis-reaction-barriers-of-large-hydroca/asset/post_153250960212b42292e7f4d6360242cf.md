
## 3.5. Transferability across metals

To evaluate the transferability of the XGBoost model, we applied it to datasets containing various metals and employed a method learned from one metal to cross-validate. This worked well while training on two other metals. The model's performance was consistent across all tests, and details are presented in Table 55.

The best performance occurs when predicting Rh by a model trained on Pb and Au, achieving a test set MAE of 0.22. As shown in Fig. 4, the training data successfully enhances the test data performance. For the others, the overlap between training and test data is not as large as the dataset, leading to a higher test error. The error in these cases of 0.28 eV is higher than 0.19 eV of the original model with competitive baseline correlation. This result could be useful for screening other metal surfaces not included in the training data. Enhancing the dataset with more metals would likely improve its performance.

## 3.6. Implications for hydrogen catalysis

We demonstrate the utility of these ML models with examples. First, we provide insights into the transferability of hydrogen catalysis by cracking C-C bonds on the three catalysts (Rh, Pb, and Pt). Reactions of linear branched hydrocarbons exhibit different characteristics on different catalysts. On Rh, the reaction with the lowest barrier (0.14 eV) for C-C cracking of $CH_3CH_2CH(CH_3)CH_2CH_3 \rightarrow CH_3CH_2CH(CH_3)_2 + CH_3$ and $CH_3CH_2CH(CH_3)CH_2CH_3 \rightarrow CH_3CH_2CH(CH_3)CH_3 + CH_3$ with a barrier of 0.28 eV. ## 3.7. Application of the ML model to catalyst screening

To ascertain the utility of our ML model for catalyst screening the metals studied herein, we investigate the hydrogen cracking of ethane case study based on our previous work [37]. For ethane cracking, the C-C scission usually occurs on CH*; Therefore, we predict the activation barriers for bond $CH_3CH_2 \rightarrow CH_3 + CH^*$ and how reactions occurs across transition metals (Pb, Pt, Rh, Ru, Ag, Cu, Ir, Ni). The results show that among all metals, Ru shows the lowest activation barrier for C-C cracking, by Rh. This order in barriers demonstrates that Ru and Au-based catalysts provide the fastest catalysis, consistent with [74,75]. Based on previously measured turnover rates, the activity of noble metals decreases with Ru > Ir > Pt. Notably, of metals demonstrated lower barrier for C-H bond scission (CHCH* → C2H4 + H*), showing the significance of C-C scission from CHCH*. With a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.2 eV and only on three metals in the training dataset, our model could be valuable in search of other potential catalysts. Without selection, our model captures the general trends and shifts on potential catalysts but detailed parameters and reference.

## 4. Conclusions

Accurate prediction of activation energies is crucial for the performance of catalysts. In this study, we compiled a dataset of 2083 calculated activation barriers for reactions on various metals. We developed two methods to estimate activation energies. The first is an extension of traditional BE relationship, which applied to larger hydrocarbon species. However, BE relationship shows limitations, with correlations being more accurate than predicted-C-C cracking results. This finding shows that with the structural change the barriers are more accurate on Pt and Ru. However, when aggregating all data, BE results in limited results for previous studies [76,47].

To address shortcomings of traditional BE, we developed ML models using easy descriptors for catalyst, metal surface, and reaction type. Critical features include the homogeneous series (C-C vs. C-H reaction) and the metal's electronegativity and atomic mass.
