### Constructing the Sampling Distributions Group
In the proposed BDAS, each point $\alpha(i=1,2\cdots8; j=1,2\cdots14)$ of the sampling matrix $A$ is extended as a distribution, then the sampling distributions group $A$ is obtained. For a validation dataset $D_{out}=(x,y)$ and a trained hyper-network $H(w^*)=\argmin_w L_{train}(w)$, the variational posterior $q(\Lambda|\theta)$ is used to approximate the true Bayesian posterior $P(\Lambda|D_{out},H(w^*))$. In our work, the variational posterior is set to Gaussian distribution and its parameter $\theta$ needs to be found. This is solved by minimizing the KL divergence;

$$\Theta^* = \arg \min_0 KL[q(\Lambda|\theta)||P(\Lambda|D_{val}, H(w^*))]$
= \arg \min_0 \int q(\Lambda|\theta)\log \frac{q(\Lambda|\theta)}{P(\Lambda|D_{val}, H(w^*))} d\Lambda$
= \arg \min_0 \int q(\Lambda|\theta)\log \frac{q(\Lambda|\theta)P(D_{val}, H(w^*))}{P(\Lambda)P(D_{val}, H(w^*))|\Lambda)} d\Lambda$
= \arg \min_0 \int q(\Lambda|\theta)\log \frac{q(\Lambda|\theta)}{P(\Lambda)P(D_{val}, H(w^*))|\Lambda)} d\Lambda$
$+P(D_{val}, H(w^*)) \int q(\Lambda|\theta)d\Lambda$
= \arg \min_0 \int q(\Lambda|\theta)\log \frac{q(\Lambda|\theta)}{P(\Lambda)P(D_{val}, H(w^*))|\Lambda)} d\Lambda.quad (8.4)$

$\Lambda$ is the prior. This cost function is known as the expected lower bound. We follow the work of Blundell et al. (2015), who proposed an approximate form for the expected lower bound to allow more combinations of prior and variational families. We approximate Eq. (8.4):

```
$$\Theta^* = \arg \min_{j=1}^n \sum_{k=1}^s \log q((\Lambda^j)^k|\theta)|-\log P((\Lambda^j)^k)-\log P(D_{val}, H(w^*)|(\Lambda^j)^k)\quad (8.5)$$
```

where $(\Lambda^j)^k$ denotes the $k$th Monte Carlo sample from the distribution in the posterior group, $n$ is the Monte Carlo sampling number and $n$ is set to 1 in the implement. The order of summation in Eq. (8.5) has been switched as they are adequately independent and finite. In our experimental study, we found this approximate form can adequately achieve our purpose.

The reparameterization trick is used to calculate the unbiased Monte Carlo gradient for the optimization of Eq. (8.5). Each variational posterior in our work is set to a Gaussian distribution with a mean $\mu'$ and a standard deviation $\sigma'$. The standard deviation is parameterized as $\sigma' = \log(1+\exp(\rho'))$ to be positive. So the parameters $\theta'=(\mu',\rho')$ need to be estimated. A sample of $\Lambda$ can be obtained from a unit Gaussian and the transform is: $\hat{\Lambda}'=\mu'+\log(1+\exp(\rho'))\circ \epsilon'$ where $\hat{\Lambda}'$ is a sampling instance from $\Lambda'$, $\epsilon'$ is sampled from a unit Gaussian and $\circ$ represents pointwise multiplication. The forward calculation of the DAG is:

$f_{DAG}(x) = \left\{begin{matrix} \mu_1' & \dots & \mu_8' \\ \vdots & \vdots & \mu_{14}' & \dots & \mu_{18}' \end{matrix}\right\} + \left[\begin{matrix} \sigma_1' & \dots & \sigma_8' \\ \vdots & \vdots & \sigma_{14}' & \dots & \sigma_{18}' \end{matrix}\right] \circ \left[\begin{matrix} \epsilon_1' & \dots & \epsilon_8' \\ \vdots & \vdots & \epsilon_{14}' & \dots & \epsilon_{18}' \end{matrix}\right] \otimes \quad (8.6)$

### Scale Mixture Prior
In the implementation of DARTS, each row of the sampling matrix A is constrained by Softmax, leading to winner-takes-all evaluation. In order to overcome this phenomenon, a soft constraint based on MAP is given to replace Softmax, which assumes each point of the matrix A obeys a bimodal distribution:

```
$$A^{MAP} = \arg \min_A -\log P(A|D)=\arg \min_A -\log P(D|A) - \log P(A) \quad (8.7)$$
```

Note that the parameters in Eq. (8.7) are other than distributions. This is a coincidence that the regularization $-\log P(A)$ is an instance of the second $-\log P((\Lambda^j)^k)$ in Eq. (8.5) when $(\Lambda^j)^k$ is just the mean of the posterior distribution. Therefore, provided the prior $P(\Lambda)$ is a bimodal distribution, this soft constraint on the sampling group can be implemented in a broader sense. The prior is set to:

```
$$P(\Lambda) = \lambda N((\Lambda^j)^k|\mu_1, \sigma_1^2) + (1-\lambda) N((\Lambda^j)^k|\mu_2, \sigma_2^2) \quad (8.8)$$
```

$(\Lambda^j)^k$ is a sample point from the posterior distribution in the group, $N((\Lambda^j)^k|\mu, \sigma^2)$ is the Gaussian density evaluated at $(\Lambda^j)^k$ with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$, $\lambda$ is a hyperparameter of the ratio scaling Gaussian peaks. As shown in Figure 8.2, our prior is a bimodal distribution, then the group $\Lambda$ can be divided into two peaks, and $\lambda$ determines the proportion of allocation.

By optimizing the sampling distributions group, the posterior is obtained to estimate the uncertainty for model matching. Then the candidate architectures set can be sampled from the posterior via random sampling. There is no time sequence or dependency in the sampling, so it is convenient for parallel acceleration. In fact, strategic sampling approach is allowed, e.g., the margin-focused sampling strategy, but in practice, the random sampling is sufficient to fix the set. Two metrics, accuracy and sparseness, are used to evaluate the sampled architecture, where the accuracy is for the validation dataset (Hoyer 2004):

```
$$\text{Sparseness}(\Lambda) := \sqrt{m - \frac{\sum_{i,j} (\Lambda_{i,j})^2}{\sqrt{m-1}}} \quad\quad\quad (8.9)$$
```

$m$ is the dimensionality of $\Lambda$ and $m$ is equal $14 \times 8$. Sorting by the sparseness of the sampling instance matrix, the deviation caused by sampling from the continuous space is reduced. It is worth pointing out the difference between our method and the stochastic neural architecture search (SNAS (Xie et al. 2018)). The sampling in our work is subject to the $\partial o$ principle where the posterior is Gaussian, and the uncertainty estimation of model matching is also given, whereas sampling in SNAS is realized by a random one-hot mask.
