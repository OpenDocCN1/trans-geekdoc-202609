Negative Transfer. There are mainly four kinds of scenarios of UDTL-based IFD, but all experiments with two datasets are about transfer between different working conditions. To state that these scenarios are not suitable for generating the positive transfer, we use the PU dataset to design another transfer task considering the transfer between different methods of generating data. Each task consists of three health conditions, and detailed information is listed in Table 7.6. There are two transfer learning scenarios in total.

The transfer results are shown in Figure 7.15. We observe that each method has a negative transfer with the time or frequency domain inputs, and this phenomenon indicates that this constructed task may not be suitable for the transfer learning task. Actually, there are also some published papers designing transfer learning tasks which tackle transferring the gear samples to the bearing samples (it may not be a reliable transfer task) or transferring the experimental data to the real data (if structures of two machines are different, it may also not be a reliable transfer task). Thus, it is very important to first figure whether this task is suitable for transfer learning and which domains do have shared features.

Physical Priors. For UDTL-based IFD, many scholars only introduce methods, which have already existed in other fields, to perform IF tasks and pay less attention to the prior knowledge behind the data (lack of using special phenomena or rules in physical systems). Therefore, we suggest that scholars can learn from core ideas in the field of transfer learning (not just using the existing methods) and introduce prior knowledge of physical systems into the proposed method to construct more targeted and suitable diagnostic models with higher recognition rates in industrial applications.

Other Aspects. Although a large amount of data in different conditions can be collected, fault data in some conditions are still scarce. Due to the fact that most machines operate in a normal condition, the class-imbalanced problem naturally exists in real applications. Thus, imbalanced learning or few shot learning with transfer learning methods (Wu et al. 2020) might also be an important direction for better getting constructed algorithms off the ground.

Federated transfer learning (FTL) (Liu et al. 2020) provides a safer and more reliable approach for specific industries. At the same time, based on characteristics of transfer learning, FTL participants can own their own feature space without requiring all participants to own or use the same feature data, which makes FTL suitable for more application scenarios. FTL was used in IFD (Zhang and Li 2021) and for in-depth research is required.

Uncertainty quantification plays a critical role in assessing the safety of DL models construction, optimization, and decision-making processes. Bayesian networks (Gal and Gahramani 2016) and ensemble learning techniques (Lakshminarayanan, Pritzel, and Blundell 2017) are widely used uncertainty methods, and their effectiveness has been verified by different kinds of applications, such as self-driving car, etc. Thus, uncertainty as an auxiliary term can be used to further correct some inappropriate properties during transfer learning. For example, the prediction is explicitly estimated during training for semantic segmentation (Zheng and Yang 2021).

# CONCLUSION

This chapter constructs a taxonomy and provides a brief review of UDTL-based IFD according to different tasks. Two publicly available datasets are gathered to perform a comparative analysis of UDTL-based methods from several perspectives. Based on this systematic study, results of different methods indicate that the assumption of joint distributions and adversarial training are two helpful techniques for promoting the accuracy. Meanwhile, different input types often define what might be important to improve the accuracy. The stability and robustness of UDTL-based IFD need to be taken seriously. To sum up, it may be useful for scholars to think ahead of these results before developing new models.
