Applications to IFD

BDA was used by Wang and Wu (2018) to adaptively construct feature importance of the marginal and conditional distribution discrepancy between features learned by deep neural networks for IFD. The CORAL loss (Wang, He, and Li 2019) and maximum variance discrepancy (MVD) (Zhang et al. 2020) were used to reduce the distribution discrepancy between different domains. (Qian, Li, and Wang 2018) constructed the higher-order moments and proposed an HKL divergence to adjust distribution for rotating machine fault diagnosis. The distance designed to measure source and target representations was proposed in (Hu, Wang, and Gu 2020) to align tensor representations into the invariant subspace for bearing fault diagnosis.

Another metric distance, called MMD, was widely used in the field of intelligent diagnosis (Lu et al. 2016). (Tong et al. 2018) reduced marginal and conditional distributions simultaneously across domains based on MMD in the feature space by refining pseudo test labels for fault diagnosis. Wang et al. (2020) proposed a conditional MMD based on estimated pseudo labels to shorten the conditional distribution distance for fault diagnosis. The marginal and conditional distributions were aligned simultaneously in multiple layers by minimizing MMD (Li et al. 2020a

MK-MMD was used in (Li et al. 2019) to better transfer the distribution of learned features in the source domain to that the target domain for IFD. Han et al. (2020) used JDA to adapt both conditional and marginal distributions simultaneously to construct a more effective and robust feature representation for substantial distribution difference. Wang et al. (2021) further used the grey optimization algorithm to learn the parameters of JDA. Based on JMMD, Cao, Chen, and Zeng (2020) proposed a soft JMMD to reduce both the marginal and conditional distribution discrepancy with the enhancement of auxiliary soft labels.
