Besides, the above classifiers with shallow structures make it difficult to extract abstract features, leading to relatively low accuracy. Especially, with the arrival of the big data era of machinery monitoring, these methods further show their weakness in dealing with fault diagnosis.

Current deep learning (DL), which possesses a strong ability of feature learning, has attracted widespread attention in the field of machine fault diagnosis (Lei et al. 2016). With less manual intervention, these DL-based methods can realize the end-to-end diagnosis and have achieved excellent performance in fault diagnosis.

Besides, because vibration signal contains strong noise and nonlinear features in practice, researchers integrated time-frequency analysis (TFA) with DL for fault diagnosis to improve the diagnostic accuracy and robustness (Wang, Yan, and Gao 2017). Through the aforementioned analysis, DL has salient performance on the fault diagnosis of planetary gearbox to traditional ML methods. Most methods are supervised learning (SL) where the input is directly mapped to a fault type through a classifier in the training process and has strong feedback. Also, their learning ways are static, and unlike the human cognitive mechanism knowledge is learned by little through interaction with the environment. To a certain extent, these deficiencies reduce the generalization and intelligence level of DL-based fault diagnosis methods. Also, the diagnostic accuracy is influenced if the raw signal is directed as the model's input because of strong noise and nonlinear characters.

Deep reinforcement (DRL), as a breakthrough in AI, combines the strong perception of DL with the decision-making advantage of reinforcement learning (RL). However, DRL is used in classification because its solution is aimed at the sequential decision-making problem. For this purpose, Wiering et al. (2011) proposed a classification problem as a sequential decision-making problem and an MLP model trained in this framework outperformed a normal MLP trained by backpropagation. Some successful cases (Ding et al. 2019; Niyato, and Yuen 2020) prompt this chapter to explore a new planetary gearbox's fault diagnosis from the view of DL over the previously mentioned deficiencies of current fault diagnosis methods.
