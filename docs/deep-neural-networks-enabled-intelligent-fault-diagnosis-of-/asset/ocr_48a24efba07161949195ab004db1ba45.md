# Multi-Sensor Fusion for Intelligent Fault Diagnosis

# INTRODUCTION

Nowadays, induction motor is widely used in mechanical systems and it is an essential component of manufacturing machines where incipient faults during machine operation can pose threats to system reliability. The failures of induction motor in operation may cause accidents, which can bring huge economic losses and even casualties. Therefore, it is important to evaluate the working state of induction motors, detect potential faults, and then take proper action to prevent machine failure from happening (Elbouchikhi et al. 2017). Accordingly, induction motor fault diagnosis techniques plays a significant role in safety production and equipment maintenance, and it can improve machine quality, enhancing working safety, and reduce maintenance cost (Sun et al. 2017).

Many research studies have been carried out for developing reliable and efficient induction motor fault diagnosis (Hassan et al. 2018). For decades, many fault diagnosis methods have been based on motor current analysis which is called motor current signature analysis (MCSA). After data acquisition, critical features associated with the working state are extracted from motor current using signal processing techniques. MCSA has been one of the most widely used fault diagnosis techniques for motor as it is inexpensive and noninvasive (Tian et al. 2018), and it turns out to be successful in detecting stator faults and rotor faults (Da Silva, Povinelli, and Demeransch 2008). Sapena-Bano et al. (2015) proposed a framework using Fast Fourier transform (FFT)-based stator current analysis to diagnose the faults of electrical machines in transient conditions. Spectral analysis of the stator current based on Hilbert transform was also utilized (Noureddine, Hafaifa, and Kouzouz 2017). Although MCSA has achieved much success in various applications, its performance is influenced by voltage harmonics, induced harmonics, and other disturbances.
