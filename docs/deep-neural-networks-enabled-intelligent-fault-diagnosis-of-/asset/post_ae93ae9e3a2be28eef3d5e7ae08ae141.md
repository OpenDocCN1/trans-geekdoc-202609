# Reinforcement Learning for Intelligent Fault Diagnosis

## INTRODUCTION

The planetary gearbox as a critical transmission part of modern mechanical systems has been widely used in the fields of wind turbines, gas turbines, and helicopters because of its advantages of large transmission ratio, compactness, and strong load-bearing capacity (Chen et al., 2020). However, planetary gearbox usually operates in harsh environments, its key components, especially the gear and bearing, are frequently subjected to some defects such as gear root crack, chipped teeth, broken tooth, pitting, bearing inner race fault, outer race fault, etc. in long-run (Chen et al., 2019). The occurrence of such faults may cause unexpected downtime of the entire mechanical system, which may result in huge economic losses or even human casualties in accidents (Zhao et al., 2017). Thus, it is worth studying about fault diagnosis methods to ensure the operational safety and reliability of mechanical systems and reduce maintenance costs.

Traditional methods for gearbox fault diagnosis mainly use vibration analysis-based characteristic frequency identification. They utilize short-time Fourier transform (STFT) (Yu, Yu, and Xu 2017), wavelet transform (WT) (Yan, Gao, and Chen 2014), wavelet packet transform (WPT) (Fan and Zuo 2006), empirical mode decomposition (EMD) (Ziani et al., 2019), etc., to extract the failure fault from vibration signal and relate fault detection. This method can detect a fault, it requires extensive expert knowledge of signal processing and extracts the failure fault with difficulty under a complex environment (Widodo et al., 2014). With the rise of artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning (ML)-based data-driven approach provides a new way for the development of fault diagnosis. Some methods like neural networks (Khaze et al. 2013), support vector machine (SVM) (Peng et al. 2007), extreme learning (Yang, Wang, and Wong 2018), etc., have been widely used in fault diagnosis and have fruitful results. However, these methods need a hand-crafted feature extractor to obtain some time- and frequency-domain features for fault diagnosis, the diagnostic accuracy mainly depends on the effectiveness of extracted features (Zhao et al. 2018). Besides, the above classifiers with shallow structures make it difficult to extract abstract features, leading to relatively low accuracy. Especially, with the arrival of the big data era of machinery monitoring, these methods further show their weakness in dealing with fault diagnosis.

Current deep learning (DL), which possesses a strong ability of feature learning, has attracted widespread attention in the field of machine fault diagnosis (Lei et al. 2016). With less manual intervention, these DL-based methods can realize the end-to-end diagnosis and have achieved excellent performance in fault diagnosis.

Besides, because vibration signal contains strong noise and nonlinear features in practice, researchers integrated time-frequency analysis (TFA) with DL for fault diagnosis to improve the diagnostic accuracy and robustness (Wang, Yan, and Gao 2017). Through the aforementioned analysis, DL has salient performance on the fault diagnosis of planetary gearbox to traditional ML methods. Most methods are supervised learning (SL) where the input is directly mapped to a fault type through a classifier in the training process and has strong feedback. Also, their learning ways are static, and unlike the human cognitive mechanism is learned by little through interaction with the environment. To a certain extent, these deficiencies reduce the generalization and intelligence level of DL-based fault diagnosis.

Deep reinforcement (DRL), as a breakthrough in AI, combines the strong perception of DL with the decision-making advantage of reinforcement learning (RL). However, DRL is used in classification because its solution is aimed at the sequential decision-making problem. For this purpose, Wiering et al. (2011) proposed a classification problem as a sequential decision-making problem and an MLP model trained in this framework outperformed a normal MLP trained by backpropagation.
