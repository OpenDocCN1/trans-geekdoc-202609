In conclusion, this article proposed a deep learning approach to effectively capture the electronic properties of fluorescent molecules. Deep neural network, convolutional convolution, recurrent neural network, and message-passing models are able to predict the transition energies, oscillator strengths, and single-triplet TDFT materials. Satisfactory performances are achieved, with $R^2$ of the first oscillator above 0.7 and that of energy shifts above 0.8, demonstrating the robustness of deep learning methods in describing the TDFT mechanism. It showed that the 1-dimensional representation (represented by RNN) and 2-dimensional representation (represented by GNN) which we used can deliver comparable performances with the best 3-dimensional model (predicted by SchNnet), using atomic features and dynamic interactions, implying the diversity of the neural architectures that can be suitable for resolving the electronic transition. The investigation has provided a comprehensive ML framework for predictions of excited-state properties.

High-throughput methods implemented helped learn the transition properties of more than 60 conformers.
