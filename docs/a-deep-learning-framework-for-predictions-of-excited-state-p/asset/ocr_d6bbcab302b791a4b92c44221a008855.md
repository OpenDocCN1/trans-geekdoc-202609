Thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) materials, as the third generation of diodes, have been extensively studied in the past due to their high potential in making organic light-emitting diode (OLED) devices. The design of TADF materials requires managing singlet-triplet gaps. It is known that time-dependent density functional theory (TDDFT) can have reasonable accuracy in describing the excited state properties, and it has been extensively used in high throughput screening for the exploration of the TADF search space. However, high throughput calculation can suffer from the large number of molecules. With the advent of machine learning (ML), molecular discovery has entered a new era, which can significantly shorten the material research and development cycle. For particularly TADF molecules, deep neural network (DNN) has been used to predict the physical properties including the TADF luminescence
