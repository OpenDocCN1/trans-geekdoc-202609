# RMSE

In order to provide a comprehensive ML-assisted analysis of TAFD materials, this article investigates the predictive performance of state-of-the-art deep learning methods on the electronic properties that are directly related to the electronic applications of DFT. Deep neural network (DNN), graph convolutional network (GCN), message passing neural network (RNN) and SchNet models are employed to handle the 1-dimensional and 3-dimensional representations of molecules for the fitting of excited states and oscillator properties. A dataset (containing 1394 samples) with better accuracy structure than TDDFT calculations is used to label, as described in the supplementary material), is used for the model training, validation and testing. Prediction accuracies are compared between different models, where SchNet and message passing are also in to understand the others. The cut-off of the 3-dimensional models (including SchNet and message passing) explains how the long-range interactions can influence the electronic properties. We also examine how constructing models based on the original 1-dimensional features of TAFD so that more information can be included. It is observed that the predictive performance for the oscillator strengths can be significantly improved when models are applied on the conformer descriptor.

# MAE

Detailed descriptions regarding the TAFD database construction and the learning methodologies are given in the supplementary material.
Fig. 2 shows the performance details of the deep learning models for the excited states properties of TAFD materials. Statistical estimates indicate the R², mean squared error (RMSE) and mean absolute error (MAE) are evaluated. The R² for 1-dimensional models, demonstrating the satisfactory performance for the transition predictions when deep learning methods are applied, is in the range of 0.7-0.9. For 3-dimensional models, the MAE is around 0.2, which is acceptable, but for the higher excited states, it is significantly decreased. The observation is consistent with the works in Lu et al.⁵ and Tan et al.⁶ where uncertainties are encountered when deep learning is employed to predict the transition dipole. For the singlet-triplet energy splitting, all models achieve a R² above 0.85, improving the robustness of the models in simulating the fluorescence properties.
