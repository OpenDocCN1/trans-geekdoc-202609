# A deep learning framework for predictions of excited state properties of luminescent molecules†

Cite this: New J. Chem., 2023, 47, 9550

Accepted 29th April 2023.

Zheng Tan,a Yan Li,b Zying Zhang,c Thomas Penfold,d Weimei Shi,e Shiqing Yang†a and Wanli Zhang†b

We have implemented a deep learning framework to forecast the excited state properties for thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) molecules with satisfactory accuracy being achieved. In particular, for the oscillators, precisions have been significantly improved when the torsional profile of the D-A dihedral twisting is properly adjusted to realize the separation of the frontier orbitals (i.e., HOMO and LUMO), and the oscillator strength to be accurately simultaneously.

Thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) materials, as the third generation of diodes, have been extensively studied in the past due to their high potential in making organic light-emitting diode (OLED) devices. To simply demonstrate its operation mechanism upon excitation, TADF molecules can getters via reverse intersystem crossing (RISC) to the singlet energy gap. In Fig. 1, the triplet exciton can get with a high internal quantum efficiency approaching 100%. By the conventional fluorescent and phosphorescent materials, TADF have the advantages of larger material space, low fabrication cost without the use of heavy atoms, and more blue-light emission.

The design of TADF materials requires managing singlet-triplet gaps. It is known that time-dependent density functional theory (TDDFT) can have reasonable accuracy in describing the excited state properties, and it has been extensively used in high throughput screening for the exploration of the TADF search space. However, high throughput calculation can suffer from the large number of molecules. With the advent of machine learning (ML), molecular discovery has entered a new era, which can significantly shorten the material research and development cycle. For particularly TADF molecules, deep neural network (DNN) has been used to predict the physical properties including the TADF luminescence.

The design of TADF molecules is challenging, since it requires the optimization of opposing properties, i.e., the oscillator strength f and the first singlet-triplet gap $\Delta_{EST}$. An efficient emission in TADF requires f to be maximized and $\Delta_{EST}$ to be minimized constant,¹ singlet and triplet excitation and oscillator strengths².

It is observed that, for the transitions and the corresponding rate data, DNN can make high quality predictions with an accuracy comparable to that of TDDFT calculations. Nevertheless, for the oscillator strengths, the prediction is less accurate, especially for the excited states. The reason has been attributed to the two-dimensional fingerprint in DNN cannot figure out the 3-dimensional wishing, which is critical for the estimation of energy. Similar results have also been encountered in Lu et al.³ and Kang et al.⁴ work, in which it claimed that the ML should be used for the prediction of transition dipole moment.

![](img/33f80a751675bc430670c4c1179ca0b2_1_0.png)

In order to provide a comprehensive ML-assisted analysis of TAFD materials, this article investigates the predictive performance of state-of-the-art deep learning methods on the electronic properties that are directly related to the electronic applications of DFT. Deep neural network (DNN), graph convolutional network (GCN), message passing neural network (RNN) and SchNet models are employed to handle the 1-dimensional and 3-dimensional representations of molecules for the fitting of excited states and oscillator properties. A dataset (containing 1394 samples) with better accuracy structure than TDDFT calculations is used to label, as described in the supplementary material), is used for the model training, validation and testing. Prediction accuracies are compared between different models, where SchNet and message passing are also in to understand the others. The cut-off of the 3-dimensional models (including SchNet and message passing) explains how the long-range interactions can influence the electronic properties. We also examine how constructing models based on the original 1-dimensional features of TAFD so that more information can be included. It is observed that the predictive performance for the oscillator strengths can be significantly improved when models are applied on conformer descriptor.

![](img/33f80a751675bc430670c4c1179ca0b2_1_1.png)

Detailed descriptions regarding the TAFD database and learning methodologies are given in the supplementary material. Fig. 2 shows the performance of the deep learning models for the excited states of TAFD materials. Statistical estimates indicate the $R^2$, mean squared error (RMSE) and mean absolute error (MAE) are evaluated. The $R^2$ for 1-dimensional models, demonstrating the satisfactory performance for transition predictions when deep learning methods are applied, is in the range of 0.7-0.9. For 3-dimensional models, the MAE is around 0.2, which is acceptable, but for the higher excited states, it is significantly decreased. The observation is consistent with the works in Lu et al.⁵ and Tan et al.⁶ where uncertainties are encountered when deep learning is employed to predict the transition dipole moment. all models achieve a $R^2$ above 0.85, improving the robustness of the models in simulating the fluorescence properties.

Note that RMSE is defined as the root mean square displacement of atoms between a generated conformer and the reference structure. The difference denotes the mean error within the conformer generator. The cut-off of the 3-dimensional models (MP and SchNet) is also examined, with no correlation found between the prediction accuracy and cutoff radius. Detailed analysis is provided in the supplementary material. Considering the possibility of oscillator strengths, we developed a conformer dataset based on the original D-A molecules by extending the molecular forces, so that much more information (which is of importance for oscillator strengths) can be included in the training input to evaluate the robustness. The conformer set is built through a stochastic search algorithm (evolutionary algorithm) with distance constraints generating conformers that satisfy various constraints using lower/upper bounds on distances and torsional-angle preferences (using the module GetDKit as implemented in RDKit). As illustrated in Fig.3, the initial configurations are produced by twisting the molecular fragments along the torsional-angle preferences. As the rotatable bond is located the donor-acceptor molecule for most TADF molecules, the electrons are excited through a twisted D-A structure. A geometry optimization is then performed on the generated conformations using the MMP4 field¹⁸ to settle the conformers onto a local minimum. A configuration filtering procedure is implemented to rule out the generated conformers with conformational deviations (measured in terms of RMSE) from a cut-off value.

In total, 3650 conformers were generated. The conformers of the original D-A molecules, on average it is equivalent to producing around 2-300 conformers for each molecule. More details relevant to the conformer set construction are given in the supplementary material.
