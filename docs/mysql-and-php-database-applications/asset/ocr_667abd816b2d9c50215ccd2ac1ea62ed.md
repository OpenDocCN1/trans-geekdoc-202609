Which should you use? Well, this is a complex question, and the answer for many will be partially by religion. In case you're unaware of it, let's take a moment to talk about the broad topics in this religious war.

If you don't know what we are talking about, here are the basics. PHP and MySQL belong to a class of software known as open source. This means that the source code to the heart of their applications is available to anyone who wants to see it. They make use of an open-source development model, which allows anyone who is interested to participate in the development of the project. In the case of PHP, coders over the world participate in the development of the language and see immediate pay for their substantial work. Most of people who participate are passionate about good software and code for the enjoyment of seeing people like you and develop with their tools.

The method of development has been around for some time, but it has gained prominence as Linux has become increasingly popular. More than not, opensource is free. You can download the application, install it, and use it without getting permission from anyone or paying a dime.

Suffice to it that Microsoft, Oracle, and other traditional software companies do not make use of this method of development.

If you are not an open-source zealot, there are excellent reasons to choose NT/2000. Usually, the thing that steers people towards NT/2000 is out of inertia.
