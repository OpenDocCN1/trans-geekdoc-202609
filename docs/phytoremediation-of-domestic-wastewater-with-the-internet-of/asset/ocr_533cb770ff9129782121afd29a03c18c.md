Turbidity is the clarity of water (APHA, 2007), as a result of suspended matter found in water. P. stratiotes, S. molesta, and E. crasses plants in reduction of the influent water samples at varied detention times of 6, 12 and 24 hours for 2 weeks. At 6 hours retention, the overall turbidity value was 7.58 NTU (P. stratiotes), 12.8 NTU (S. molesta) and 9.6 NTU (E. crasses) against 22.2 NTU (influent turbidity), while the average value of 9.9 NTU (P. stratiotes), 6.2 NTU (S. molesta) and 9.8 NTU (E. crasses) was observed at 12 hours retention 28.9 NTU of the influent samples. At 24 hours retention time, the turbidity value of 7.1 NTU (N), 3.7 NTU (S. molesta) and 5.9 NTU (E. crasses) was recorded against 34.9 NTU of the influent samples. In the overall experiment, up to 93.9% reduction efficiency was observed. These results demonstrated the effectiveness of P. stratiotes, S. molesta and E. crasses plants in improving the water quality characteristics of the influent samples. Furthermore, S. molesta treatment system at 24 hours retention time provided the ideal conditions for lowering the turbidity level. However, it was discovered that the ability of S. molesta plants in absorbing the pollutants declined at 6 and 12 hours retention times. Beside, the roots were observed to retain and filter the suspended solids via rhizofiltration. The long hairy roots of the selected plants facilitated the rhizofiltration process. Plant roots have an electrical current which facilitates digestion and assimilation of the suspended particles by the plants (Johnson, 1993). Additionally, phytoremediation process using hydroponic system improved the quality of the influent samples by providing a conducive environment for the trapped suspended particle to sediment at the ground level of the pond. Polished/filtered water remained at the top level of the pond. These results demonstrated the efficacy of the test plants in refining the influent samples to Malaysian water standard of class I (5 NTU) from the 10th to the 14th days at 24 hours retention time. Similarly, Aswathy (2017) reported up to 85.66% turbidity reduction efficiency in phytoremediation of kitchen wastewater using P. stratiotes after 10 days detention time. Additionally, Parwin and Paul (2019) recorded up to 86.75% turbidity removal in wastewater remediation using E. crasses.
