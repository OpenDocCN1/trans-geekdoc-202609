## 6.3.6 Analysis of Phosphate

Phosphates are primarily found in domestic wastewater as a result of detergents, food waste, anthropogenic and animal activities (Kroiss et al., 2011). Orthophosphate (PO₄) in wastewater is a necessary nutrient for plant growth. Macrophytes absorb phosphates via root assimilation into shoots and xylem, thereby decreasing phosphate content in wastewater (Lambers & Colmer, 2005). The findings of phosphate tests performed in the influent and effluent samples are presented in Figures 6.6–16.

## 6.3.7 Analysis of Ammonia Nitrogen

The outcome of the ammonia tests performed on the water samples is presented in Figures 6.19–21.

From Figure 6.19, the ammonia nitrogen of the influent samples was observed to be inconsistent and ranged from 12.8 to 23.89 mg/L. During the 14-day period, a gradual decrease in the influent ammonia nitrogen level was observed. Additionally, the ammonia removal efficiency was observed to be 12.9%–20.4% (*P. stratiotes*), 4%–14% (*S. molesta*) and 4%–35.9% (*E. crassipes*) at 6 hours retention time. The ANOVA test revealed a statistically significant change (p<0.05) between influent and effluent samples.

From Figure 6.20, the ammoniacial nitrogen level of the influent samples falls within 13.4–21.8 mg/L. Similarly, on the second day, the plants reduced the nitrogen concentration of the influent samples to 13.4±0.1 to 12.5±0.1 mg/L (*P. stratiotes*), 10.1±0.0 mg/L (*E. crassipes*). The changes showed a little reduction efficiency of 6.71% for *P. stratiotes*, 2.68% for *S. molesta* and 24.6% for *E. crassipes*. Furthermore, the 23.2±0.04 mg/L ammonia nitrogen of the influent sample was drastically reduced to 8.0±0.0 mg/L (*P. stratiotes*).
