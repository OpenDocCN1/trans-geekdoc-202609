Despite SVM has only been used for a short time, it has proven to be a reliable and competent algorithm for regression and classification in different fields. SVMs are used in regression to estimate a single output variable from a collection of input variables (Khan & Couliblay, 2006). The advantage of this technique is that the prediction accuracy of SVM outperforms others approaches including decision trees, nearest neighbours and neural networks. Furthermore SVM algorithms involve the application of the principle of Fermat (1638), principle of Lagrange (1788) and principle of Kuhn-Tucker (1951) (Raghavendra & Deka, 2014). Additionally, SVM algorithms can be better understood through fundamental concepts: the soft-margin SVM, the hard-margin SVM, kernel function and hyperplane (Raghavendra & Deka, 2014).
