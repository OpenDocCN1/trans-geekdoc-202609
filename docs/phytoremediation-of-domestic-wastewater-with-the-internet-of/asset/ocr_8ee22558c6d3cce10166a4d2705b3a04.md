40 Phytoremediation of Domestic Wastewater

# 4.5.2.5 Nutrient Uptake by S. molesta Plants in Phytoremediation of Wastewater

S. molesta is a macrophyte that has been used in wastewater phytoremediation because of its rapid growth and potential to break down contaminants in wastewater (Hariyadi et al., 2013; Kumari et al., 2017). Pavithra and Kousar (2016) investigated the effectiveness of S. molesta in remediation of textile sewing. WQ et al. (2017) analysed the potentials of giant salvinia in remediation of wastewater for 16 days. Physicochemical parameters including turbidity, COD, NO$_3$, PO$_3^{−}$, and NH$_4^+$ were performed on the treated wastewater. The outcome of the research showed that the excess nutrients present in the wastewater were lower to 0.50 mg/L NO$_3$, 0.17 mg/L (95%) PO$_3^{3}$, and 2.62 mg/L NH$_4$. Macophytes could store and absorb phosphorus, and their development rate is dependent on the phosphorus content in the wastewater. Abeyawardhana et al. (2017) employed S. molesta and L. gibba in the remediation of industrial wastewater. WQ analysis was conducted on the wastewater at a detention time of 24 hours. The results demonstrated that up to 72.63%, 73.44% TN, 74.85% Pb, 66.39% Ni, 65.2% Fe, 69.81% Cu and 81.66% Cr was reduced by S. molesta plants. Despite the duration of the sampling study not being mentioned by the authors, the results of the heavy metals and nutrient reductions at 24 hours detention time revealed that S. molesta plants are effective in improving the contaminated wastewater. Furthermore, reduction efficiency evaluation is critical, as it has accepted as standard criteria for estimating the amount of nutrients removed during phytoremediation processes.

Additionally, Tangandu and Puri (2017) investigated the COD and BOD removal from batik wastewater using E. dense and S. molesta plants. It was observed that stress conditions were observed in the S. molesta plants on the fourth day of the cultivation period. This could be attributed to the exposure of S. molesta in the concentrated wastewater, implying that wastewater was hazardous to the plant. Another study, Hanafiah et al. (2018) investigated the performance of lettuce and giant salvinia in the treatment of wastewater. The findings established the effectiveness of S. molesta and P. stratiotes in polishing wastewater.

Furthermore, da Silva et al. (2018) evaluated the potentials of S. molesta in the remediation of polluted wastewater. They also investigated the functions of peroxidase, ascorbate peroxidase, catalase and superoxide dismutase in S. molesta resistance to arsenite. The results of the study indicated that S. molesta had a higher proficiency for reducing toxic effect in the polluted water. Sreekumar and John (2018) studied the potentials of giant salvinia in phytoremediation of pest water for bioaccumulation of Cd and Zn heavy metals. However, previous research failed to investigate the potentials of S. molesta in treatment of domestic wastewater, pharmaceutical, radioactive, nanoparticle and polymer-based materials using different weight (density) and retention times. Numerous reports have shown that the lipid content of giant salvinia was between 15.72% and 19.97% depending on the pre-treatment technique used. Kaur et al. (2018) stated the state of unsaturated fats in giant salvinia is C14:0; C14:1; C16:0; C16:1; C18:0; C18:1; C20:1; C20:4 and C22:0, comprising saturated (63.59%), polyunsaturated (0.73%) and saturated (33.16%) fats. Therefore, giant salvinia is a promising biomass for biofuel generation.
