# 4.5.2.2 Taxonomy of S. molesta

S. molesta belongs to a monogeneric family. The plant was originally thought to be a subspecies of S. auriculata. However, it reclassified in 1972 based on fruiting bodies or male sporocarps (Nelson, 2009). There are 12 species of S. molesta, seven of which originate from the new world tropics. # 4.5.2.3 Description of S. molesta

S. molesta is made up of fronds that come in whorls of three leaves, with the first being rooted, buoyant and emergent, and the third leaf is submerged and separated to root. The buoyant fronds are arranged in oblong form, while the matured plants develop in the form of eggs containing spores. Additionally, the top surface contains linear papillae, which is composed of four hairs at the distal end that are connected at their tips to create an inverted egg-bladder shape. The cage-like structure of the end ends is an active air trap that enables the plant to be resistant in water. The upper surface, papillae and end hairs of the plants are waterproof, while the lower layer of the plants has an affinity for water.
