## Cultivation of Aquatic Plants for Wastewater Treatment

### 4.1 INTRODUCTION

Pollution is among the core health-threatening issues affecting our environment. Natural water sources have been affected by organic and inorganic from agricultural and domestic waste (Dipu et al., 2010). Environmental problems in the atmosphere are a result of organic and inorganic soluble found in wastewater which leads to pollution (Thongtha et al., 2014). Similarly, water pollution has a detrimental effect on agriculture, resulting in metals into the food chain (George & Gabriel, 2017). Hence, effective wastewater treatment before discharge into the environment is critical (Davery et al., 2019). Furthermore, macrophytes (aquatic plants) have the capacity to absorb pollutants including heavy metals, organic and inorganic, nanoparticles, radioactive elements, and pharmaceutical compounds in industrial, agricultural and domestic wastewater either at secondary or tertiary processes. This could be through rhizofiltration, phytoextraction, phytostabilization, phytovolatilization, phytegradation or phytotransformation.

Besides, the construction of hydroponic systems for the cultivation of aquatic plants meets all prerequisites of environmentally friendly methods for wastewater.
