Additionally, it is possible to use different ML models, like the one of Kumar and Deswal (2020b) who used LR, ANN, RF and MSP to predicting phosphorous removal using four aquatic plants. Thus, Nadiri et al. (2018) reported that the application of different ML for forecasting and predicting of water parameters would allow robust comparison study than using single ML model.

The study by Guo et al. (2015) was restricted to the applications of two ML models (ANN and SVM) and three performance criteria in forecasting the TN concentration of effluent from treatment plant (WWTP) using MATLAB software. The data was normalized to 1 and -1, while Darajah et al. (2017), Elfansi et al. (2017) and Parveen et al. (2019) employed only one model in their study. Titah et al. (2018) employed response surface methodology (RSM) and ANN approaches in studying the phytoremediation of arsenic uptake by Ludwigia octovalis. Furthermore, Nourani et al. (2018) applied four ML models (FFNN, SVM, MLR and ANNFIS methods) in predicting the water quality indicators (TN, BOD and COD) of Nicosia WWTP. It was observed that ANNFIS outperforms all other models owing to the fuzzy concept capacity to deal with process uncertainty.

Vo et al. (2019) and Kumar et al. (2019) explored different regression models in their research. In contrast, Farzi and Borghei (2019) and Kumar et al. (2021) combined regression and ML models. Furthermore, Kumar and Deswal (2020a) conducted a comparative study on random forest (RF), MSP and ANN modelling techniques in predicting the reduction of phosphorus from rice mill wastewater. The data were divided into 70% and 30% for training and testing the model. The modelling findings suggest that ANN outperforms the MSP5 tree and RF modelling, while Kumar and Deswal (2020b) applied three statistical parameters (RMSE, R², and MAE) to analyse the model accuracy of linear regression (LR), ANN and MSP5.
