# 3.3 HIGH-RATE ALGAL PONDS (HRAPs)

High-rate algal ponds (HRAPs) can be described as shallow runway ponds used for microalgae cultivation and wastewater treatment (Crag et al., 2014). HRAPs are gaining renewed attention due to their ability to treat wastewater with low cost and low energy. These systems enhance microalgal biomass generation which can be used as feedstock and valuable biofuels production (Young et al., 2017). It is worth noting that they can be deployed where environmental conditions are favourable for optimal microalgae development. Additionally, HRAP is a natural system that promotes sustainable technique for wastewater treatment due to its potential in lowering greenhouse gas emissions during nutrient uptake (Acién, 2016). However, enormous surface area is required for effective wastewater treatment and biomass productivity is a key barrier to the adoption and proliferation of HRAP technology (Acién, 2016). Hence, it is necessary to redesign the maintenance and operational systems of HRAPs by eliminating the primary treatment from the entire process. Furthermore, the potentials of HRAP technology in remediation of wastewater and bioenergy generation have been the focus of recent research.
