The (\*) indicates the best model. The visual inspection of the data indicated that M1 was superior to M2 for all models. Additionally, ANFIS-M1* was observed to perform better than ANN, SVM and MLR. Additionally, it is worth noting that all the data-driven models employed for the prediction of TURBIT depicted reasonable accuracy in the performance criteria of the methods. However, a satisfactory outcome was obtained from the different combinations of M1 and M2. From the simulation of TURBIT, M1 with four combinations (TURB, ORP, TDS and TEMP) proved to be the best for all the models in comparison with M2 (TURB, ORP, TDS). 9.4 and 9.5 represent the time-series graphs between the observed and predicted TURBIT concentrations by M1 and M2 for ANN, SVM, ANFIS and MLR. Further evaluation of the time-series demonstrates that ANN-M1, SVM-M1, ANFIS-M1 and MLR-M1 gave a satisfactory outcome, while an acceptable precision was obtained for ANN-M2, SVM-M2, ANFIS-M2 and MLR-M2. The promising potential of the ANFIS model was not surprising, since it is an emerging non-linear hybrid model and demonstrated more predictive abilities in different fields (Ahmed et al., 2017; Alas et al., 2020; Gaya et al., 2014; Yaseen et al., 2018). The quantitative examination of ANFIS-M1 in terms of MSE=0.0001 and RMSE=0.0071 performed better than ANN, SVM and MLR. Similarly, this outcome demonstrated the influence of each independent model behaves differently. Additionally, time-series graphs are useful for representation of different scenarios.

Trends can easily easily identified using time-series graphs and can be used for future forecasting. It was evident that the time-series of the models vary from one combination to the other; this implies that the pattern of the model depends on how the models capture the relationship between the observed and the target parameters. From the above figures, it can be justified that ANFIS-M1 captures the patterns of the time-series more than the other models, with the worst model being LR-M2. The uniqueness of the goodness-of-fit could be indicated in the radar chart. Despite the overfitting reality, it was taken care by the other performance criteria of the models. On the other hand, it is important to involve two or more performance criteria in order to come up with a justifiable argument.

Furthermore, radar chart is a type of graph used to compare three or more values on a two-dimensional plane. Radar charts are easily used in depicting several variables without creating a clutter and are viewed as better for graphs. Thus, radar charts were used to display the goodness-of-fit and R of the simulated turbidity model. Figure 9.6 shows the goodness-of-fit and R for turbidity. Additionally, MSE was used in plotting the radar charts.

According to Figure 9.6, the radar chart ranges from 0 to 1, with the best value approaching 1. It was observed that ANFIS-M1 is on the last spider web for both the goodness-of-fit and R, proving the accuracy of the training and validation results presented in Table 9.2.

Additionally, boxplots are used to illustrate overall trends (range and other characteristics) of responses for a group. The boxplot illustration for M1 and M2 is presented in Figure 9.7.
