### 8.2 CONCEPT OF MACHINE LEARNING (ML) IN WASTEWATER

Artificial intelligence (AI) is a subfield of computer science that deals with human intellect. Machine learning (ML) is a branch of AI that applies to data for classification, pattern recognition, and prediction. AI approaches have been successfully used in water treatment due to their simplicity, accuracy, and speed. ML is divided into supervised and unsupervised learning. Supervised learning is used to develop the relationship between input and output values, whereas unsupervised learning does not provide outcomes. ML algorithms such as ANFIS, ANN, DLNN, SVM and decision tree are the black boxes being applied in water management.

### 8.3 ARTIFICIAL NEURAL NETWORK (ANN)

ANN is a mathematical model that is used to process information in a synaptic weight and brain-like learning process. Generally, ANN comprises tightly coupled units called neurons to analyse data in a manner comparable to the central nervous system. The development of ANN involves the collection and pre-processing of data, handling, network training and testing, data splitting, selection of inputs, activation function, and learning algorithm. The activation function could be linear, hyperbolic tangent, Gaussian, or logistic depending on the selected learning algorithm and scaling technique. The most frequently used activation functions in water and wastewater monitoring are the hyperbolic tangent and logistic sigmoid functions. Additionally, ANN can be applied in novel paradigms for function approximation owing to its great characteristics of adaptability, improved input and output mapping, fault tolerance, nonlinearity, and self-learning.
