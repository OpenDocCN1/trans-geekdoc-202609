# Cultivation of Aquatic Plants for Wastewater Treatment

# 4.5.3 Eichhornia CRASSIPES PLANTS

Eichhornia crassipes (Mart.) Solms also called water hyacinth is a notorious aquatic to the Amazon Basin of South America. The plant belongs to the pickerweed family (Pondertiaceae) and is considered one of the worst weed plants on the planet (Woldichael et al., 2011). Water hyacinth has been used in the bioaccumulation of pollutants by BOD, COD, phenols, pesticides, minerals, heavy metals and radioactive elements in phytoremediation (Khare & Lal, 2017).

# 4.5.3.1 Distribution of E. crassipes

E. crassipes are among the most distributed macrophytes in the world. They are usually found between 38°N and 38°S and were first identified as ornamental plant in the USA, South East Asia and South Africa in the 19th century. The weed plant believed to spread easily in ponds and lakes of different parts of the world because of its attractive lavender flowers. Additionally, in 1988 E. crassipes was first discovered in Lake Kyoga (Uganda) in the zones circumscribing Lake Victoria. The plants were able to survive and grow in Lake Victoria because of the favourable habitat and absence of physical disturbance such as pests, fish and other biota (Gichuki et al., 2012).

# 4.5.3.2 Taxonomy of E. crassipes

E. crassipes belongs to the Pondertiaceae family, a taxonomically complicated family that was reclassified as a member of the Comminales. E. crassipes is the only pantropical weed plant that has been identified. Except for Eichhornia natans (P. Beauv.), this family of mostly neropical freshwater aquatics has eight additional genera and species that originates from South America (Coetzee et al., 2009).
