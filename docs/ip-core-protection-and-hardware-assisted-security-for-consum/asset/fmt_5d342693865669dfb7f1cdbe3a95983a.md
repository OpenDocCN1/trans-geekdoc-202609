
Besides high- and low-power consumption, any consumer electronic system should also have the properties of trustworthiness and security. [Figure 2.15](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig15) shows different aspects of security, privacy, and protection ([Krishnamurthy *et al.*, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib43); [Mohanty, 2015](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib56)). There are different layers in the system, application, operating system (OS), firmware, digital signal processor (DSP), embedded processor, memory, and radio frequency (RF) components. At each of the stages, there are different kinds of attacks that can be successfully performed on the system. Application and OS attacks are possible on the OSs and its applications. Between the OS and applications or firmware and OS, there is a network which connects them to perform operations. This network can be attacked with different kinds of attacks: application attacks and network attacks. Some of them were discussed in the previous sections. Firmware is an integral part of the system. This acts as a bridge to the board or system to talk to other components or peripherals and describes its functionality. There are different kinds of firmware attacks possible on the devices.

A selective attributes of CE system to be trustworthy ([Krishnamurthy *et al.*, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib43); [Mohanty, 2015](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib56)):

1. The integrity of the data that is being processed by the system should be maintained.

2. The device will be prone to side channel and power analysis attacks once it is deployed in the field. So the data that is being processed should be secured and concealed during processing.

3. The functionality of the device should be restricted to the intended functionality. Additional operations should not be performed by the device.

4. The device should be reliable at all times and especially at times of critical operation.

5. The transparency of the device must be restricted to the owner and no one else.

6. Trusted vendors should be approached for different parts while manufacturing the device to avoid introduction of unwanted trojans.

7. Trusted fabrication facilities should be approached.

#### 2.2.3 Hardware-assisted security and protection

With the increased security threats, software-based security alone is not going to fight against them. Hardware-assisted security plays a crucial role in mitigating the threats caused by the attackers. There are two different kinds of attacks that can be performed on the system: software-based attacks and hardware-based attacks ([Chang *et al.*, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib15); [Krishnamurthy *et al.*, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib43)). The software attacks are performed usually from the communication channel. As IoT is connected through a network, this can easily be under threat by the attacker. These attacks are typically originated remotely and the device is affected. As the attacker need not be present at the device to perform them, they can be performed regularly and remotely. Denial of service attack, routing attacks, malicious injection, spoofing, snooping, and brute force are some of the software-based attacks.

Hardware-based attacks are typically on the device itself and most of them has to be performed at the location of the device. Some kinds of hardware attacks can also be performed remotely. In an IoT environment, the devices are deployed in areas where human interaction is very minimal and so it is highly prone to the attacks. Side channel attacks, backdoors, eavesdropping, inducing faults, and hardware trojans are some of the hardware attacks.

For the two kinds of attacks, software and hardware, the solutions available are also two different types: software solutions and hardware solutions ([Krishnamurthy *et al.*, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib43)). The software-based security solutions are easy to use, update, and also upgrade. This gives the flexibility to increase the robustness offered. As the software-based applications are dependent on the OS, they are widely supported and can be designed for cross-platform applications. The recurring operational costs are high in case of software securities. They can be used for general purpose processor but one of the main drawbacks is it will not be able to save the system against reverse engineering and other hardware attacks. In case of a hardware-security solutions, they are very fast when compared with software solutions, consumes less power, high flexibility in optimization, and low cost when an field programmable gate array or application-specific integrated circuit is used. They cannot be used to stop the software reverse engineering and are highly prone to side channel attacks.

[Figure 2.16](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig16) shows the security-aware design trade-offs and describes different aspects of the design. An example of hardware-assisted security is shown in [Figure 2.17](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig17). Major concerns when designing a system is low-power consumption and high performance, reliability, low cost of manufacturing and easy integration. In the design of a secure camera system, the watermarking chip is the main focus as most of the security is offered by it. The trade-offs that are present are power consumption, performance, chip area, integration into the system, and the memory. Integration of more memory into the system will increase the battery life, chip area, and design cost of the camera. Increased security will also increase the battery consumption and the other parameters as shown in [Figure 2.16](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig16). Thus, a design should be designed keeping all the parameters in view and trade-offs for security, and include additional hardware components, that will perform voltage and frequency scaling like technology for security, energy, and performance optimization ([Figure 2.17](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig17); [Krishnamurthy *et al.*, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib43)).
