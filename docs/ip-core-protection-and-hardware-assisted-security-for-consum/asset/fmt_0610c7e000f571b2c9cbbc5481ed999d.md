
In the case of a memristor-based crossbar array, the forward voltage applied will reduce the resistance offered by the device and the input voltage in opposite direction will increase the resistance of the device ([Lugli *et al*., 2013](#Chapter12.html#c12_bib39)) thus writing a “1” or “0” to the array. Another design of PUF is a super-high information content PUF (SHIC-PUF), which can be characterized by slow readout time and high information content ([Ruhrmair *et al*., 2011](#Chapter12.html#c12_bib62)). For storing information, a crossbar array is used in this case. But for using this, there are some requirements that should be met: the readout time should be low and this will be controlled by the construction of memory cell or slow-process modules. For higher security, the slow readout times should be due to the intrinsic construction of memory cell. Another requirement is that the circuit should be characterized completely and should be very large. The bits of information that are stored on the memory should also be random. Characterization time for a ![image](OEBPS/images/c12-Eqn-in-53.gif)bits (~10 GB) and *k* = 100 bits/s circuit is ![image](OEBPS/images/c12-Eqn-in-54.gif). This array should also be able to fit in the small area which reduces the occupied chip area.

The bit and word lines are used for the addressing of bits at the intersection of vertical and horizontal lines. This is where the current will flow through the crossing. The word and bit lines will be biased at ![image](OEBPS/images/c12-Eqn-in-55.gif)and ![image](OEBPS/images/c12-Eqn-in-56.gif)and during this period, and the word and bit lines that are not accessed will be biased at ![image](OEBPS/images/c12-Eqn-in-57.gif)and ![image](OEBPS/images/c12-Eqn-in-58.gif)voltages. So, the junctions that are at the accessed word and bit lines are only made to go into forward bias and others are reverse biased in case of a diode-based crossbar array. Word lines are connected with a sense resistor and depending on the voltage at the word line, there are two states that are shown, high or low resistance. In this case, the information in the bits is random and is also important to see that the structural features are irregular. To achieve this, aluminum-induced layer exchange is considered. This gives an advantage of randomizing the distribution of conductivity which will result in an inhomogeneous conductivity. This can be taken as an advantage in case of PUF. When the array size is increased, reverse bias junction current will increase, which in turn will increase the power dissipation of the module. But the parasitic paths will avoid the accessed bit and word line. This reduces the noise margin and makes it suitable for PUF design. And large generator resistance values are required for readout cycles to be slow and for limiting the capacitive peak current. As an alternative, high-*κ* material can be used.

A crossbar array can also be used and a weak PUF and SHIC PUF can be implemented as presented by [Lugli *et al*. (2013)](#Chapter12.html#c12_bib39). In this case, rewriting and erasing the PUF response can be performed without the other responses changed. This is only possible in case of a crossbar array but not in the case of other PUF designs. An increased crossbar array will be a huge issue as the sneak current path becomes a problem and this also decreases the noise margin and degrades power dissipation. A rectifying element can be implemented for reducing the crosstalk and leakage between the accessed and unaccessed cells. The design of a crossbar can also contain a memristor in series with a diode which can reduce the noise margin and power dissipation. There is a compatibility issue in case of a traditional silicon process and transitional metal oxide memristors ([Kim *et al*., 2010](#Chapter12.html#c12_bib29)).

When the floating biased scheme was implemented, the size of the array was reduced and noise margin was also reduced because of the increase in sneak current. This is also same in case of power dissipation. Due to this, the size of memory is limited to a few megabits. External resistance effect is minimal on the noise margin and reverse-biased scheme is suitable for the noise margin and power dissipation. But when compared to the floating point scheme, the power dissipation is high due to the higher off currents. It can be inferred that the memory size can be increased if power consumption is not a problem, which also decreases the noise margin. Scaling also helps in reducing the diode current and wire resistance.

In case of an ALILE, the diodes that come from a weak-doped wafer will have high rectification ratio and high randomness. But noise margin is low when compared to a ZnO junction because of low rectification of about 10⁸ at ±2 V.

An ALILE diodes of small size is suited for use in case of a weak PUF when compared to a ZnO Schottky diodes, as the randomness is high ([Ruhrmair *et al*., 2010](#Chapter12.html#c12_bib61)). The stability of ALILE diodes is also high over a long period and resistive to invasive readouts. Temperature dependency of the diode’s performance is high, which can be improved with the use of differential measurement techniques.

### 12.10 Carbon-based PUFs

In the current research trend, silicon devices are used widely for the development of PUF designs and are more focusing on the randomness, reliability, and robustness. But the power consumption of the devices is not being monitored or focused. Scaling of devices has led to a place where low-power consuming devices are more required and low-voltage operational devices are required, which becomes a challenge to the PUF design. Scaling of devices also makes it difficult to control the gate and charge carriers in the channel. This makes it necessary to explore post silicon device which can be used for scaling. Carbon derivative options such as the carbon nanotube (CNT) and graphene are promising candidates.

#### 12.10.1 CNT-based PUF

CNT FET-based PUFs can be implemented for various advantages such as low-power consumption, high reliability, and low energy ([Konigsmark *et al*., 2014](#Chapter12.html#c12_bib31)). In case of a CNTFET, a CNT is used as a channel in the transistor. The configuration of a CNTFET changes depending on various properties of the CNT, such as diameter, chirality, growth density, misalignment, and doping concentration. Chirality is used for the generation of secret bits. The direction in which the graphene sheet is rolled decides the chirality of a CNT which decides the metallic or semiconductor nature of the device. [Figure 12.22](#Chapter12.html#c12_fig22) shows the design of a CNPUF, which contains the CNTFETs connected in series. The parallel elements of the transistors, CNPUF-PEs, share a common gate voltage. In this case, there can be a large number of semiconducting CNTs and a small number of metallic CNTs. The ratio in which they are used is 10%–30% where the semiconducting current will dominate the metallic current. Each parallel element consists of a distinct state for high- and low-input gate voltages. Due to the process variation, this states will vary, which affects the output. The improvement of area can be achieved by 300% when compared to design of silicon-based arbiter PUF ([Konigsmark *et al*., 2014](#Chapter12.html#c12_bib31)). The interchip HD of 49.67% has been presented by [Konigsmark *et al*. (2014)](#Chapter12.html#c12_bib31). When the simulation was performed in different conditions, by giving random values to the temperature and the supply voltage of the devices, the performance of CNPUF-PE has been more reliable and stable compared to that of an RO PUF, current-based PUF, and SRAM PUF. For a higher security, a trade-off between security and power consumption should be made. An extended CNPUF adds an area overhead but is comparable to the Si PUF with higher security.
