
# Intrusion Detection System

Intrusion detection system is a way of stopping the attackers from getting into the system’s network ([Sedjelmaci *et al.*, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib79)). In the delay tolerance network, the communications are sparse and hence to deploy a distributed intrusion detection system is a better approach. When a distributed intrusion detection systems (IDS) is employed, different nodes will be able to fire the IDS and then monitor various behaviors of the incoming data. Each of the UAV will be able to monitor the neighbors and secure the entire network. When a malicious UAV is detected in the network, it then sends the data regarding the UAV and the malicious UAV loses the ability to be in the network. Hence, the entire network can be secured easily. Different rules are written for detecting the GPS spoofing and jamming ([Hartmann and Steup, 2013](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib31); [Kim *et al.*, 2012](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib40)). There are different characteristics of a spoofing attack: it generates a signal with a high strength and signal strength intensity (SSI) is very high from that of a satellite. The attacker will also send multiple signals from the same directions and from the same antenna. All of those signals will have the same signal. So while detecting such attackers, the UAV detection agent first selects all those signals and their signal strength, SSI, are determined and calculated. Then, the mean and variance of the signals are calculated and if they lie within the range of ±3σ, they are correct, and if not they are not correct and are from an attacker.

# 2.7 Smart healthcare security

IoT, the interconnection of various sensors that collect data, transmit it to the cloud, and store or process the data, is becoming an integral part of various industries including the healthcare. The idea of smart healthcare using the internet of medical things (IoMT) or internet of health things (IoHT) is presented in [Figure 2.43](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig43). It is estimated that there are more connected devices on this planet than there are people ([Li *et al.*, 2011](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib47)) and the number is keep on increasing day-by-day. Among all the data that is being collected by the sensors, health data is one of the most sensitive information. The health conditions of the patients should be kept under high security making sure the data does not leak. If medical records of patient are leaked, it can lead to major problems like social exclusion, insurance, adverse employment, and so on.

![image](OEBPS/images/PBCS0600-02-f43.jpg)

Figure 2.43 Internet of medical things (IoMT) or internet of health things (IoHT)

Previous sections discussed various security flaws that can be exploited and data stolen. This infers no system is 100% secure. An IoT network means network of connected devices and sensors. If one device is attacked and breached, the entire network will be in jeopardy. When it comes to a healthcare device, this can lead to catastrophic results where the patient’s life may fall into danger. Many healthcare devices will monitor a certain parameter of the patient and deliver the drug at a preprogrammed dosage. If such devices are attacked, the configuration can be changed leading to health deterioration. Hence, the health information of the patient should be confidential.

Thoughts around health information privacy and security automatically turn to the Health Information Privacy and Security Act (HIPAA), together with the Health Information Technology for Economic and Clinical Health (HITECH) Act amendments. Administered by the Department of Health and Human Services, Office for Civil Rights (OCR), the HIPAA Rules regulate privacy, security, individual rights, and breach notification for some (but not all) health information. Moreover, they require certain (but not all) persons who process that health information to put physical, administrative, and technical safeguards in place to protect it in all forms, and to institute a security management process (including risk analysis and risk management) to protect it in electronic form.

## 2.7.1 Smart healthcare security attacks

There are many different risks when it comes to a smart healthcare system ([Alamri, 2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib3); [Chaudhary *et al.*, 2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib16); [Ghoneim *et al.*, 2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib24); [Xiao *et al.*, 2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib108)). [Figure 2.44](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig44) shows different kinds of attacks that can be performed on a smart healthcare system. Data eavesdropping is the process where the attacker will listen to the communications between the medical device and the receiver and steal the data from the communication. This can be avoided using a secure communication. Data privacy, data confidentiality, and location privacy are major issues in healthcare. There is a lot of data that is collected in the case of a medical device ([Sundaravadivel *et al.*, 2016](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib97)). All this data needs to be securely transmitted to the doctor. The data should not fall into the hands of an attacker. It should be confidential and privacy should be given a highest priority. Every device will be given a unique identification which is also subject to counterfeiting attacks and spoofing attacks that were discussed in the case of RFID tags. RFID tags may also be used in the medical healthcare devices which are subject to different attacks that can lead to identity threats. Access and control of the device can be taken by the attacker which will be discussed further in the section.

![image](OEBPS/images/PBCS0600-02-f44.jpg)

Figure 2.44 Attacks on smart healthcare systems

[Figure 2.45](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig45) shows the design of an insulin delivery system that is used by various diabetes patients to monitor and control the insulin levels in the body ([Li *et al.*, 2011](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib47)). There are various components in the system, a glucose monitor, which can monitor the levels of glucose in the blood and a pump which is used to pump glucose into the blood when necessary and a remote control. The glucose monitor will have glucose sensors, which senses the levels of glucose in blood and transmit the data to the insulin pump and to the computer when necessary. The pump will autonomously administer the insulin in prescribed doses by the doctor and is programmed. There are two different ways of administration, bolus dose, which administers quickly when the glucose levels rise quickly and basal dose which administers the dose slowly during night and between meals. These are preprogrammed into the pump and are administered accordingly. There are different ways of programming the device, buttons on pump, through a computer with a wireless connection, remote control, and wireless connection to a blood glucose monitor. [Figure 2.46](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig46) shows two different kinds of attacks that are possible on the insulin pump which can affect the health of the patient. Passive attack is where the remote control is taken into control and send signal to the insulin pump ([Li *et al.*, 2011](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib47)). A universal radio peripheral is used in this scenario where the remote sends a malicious signal to the pump which can change the program of system and administer an unprescribed dosage of insulin. Active attack is where the same radio peripheral is used to send the signal directly to the pump and change the program of the system. Both these attacks are dangerously harmful to the patient.
