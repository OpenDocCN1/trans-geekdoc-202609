[Figure 2.29](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig29) shows the relay attack on the NFC module. As explained above, the attacks allow the data to be transmitted over a large distance. There are various parts of the attack, viz., honest prover, honest verifier, dishonest prover, and dishonest verifier. The cards of the victim and the point-of-sale terminal of the attacker are the honest prover and verifier in the scenario. The dishonest prover and verifiers are the NFC capable mobile phones of the victim and the attacker.

![image](OEBPS/images/PBCS0600-02-f29.jpg)

Figure 2.29 Relay attacks on the NFC modules of an IoT device

ISO/IEC14443 protocol of the NFC is taken as an advantage by the attacker in the relay attack. First, the attacker will forward the reader’s request to the victim and the answer from the victim is sent back to the reader in real-time. In the attack scenario, the attacker should be near the victim’s card and relay data over another communication channel which is sent to the second NFC reader. This secondary NFC reader has to be in the original readers’ proximity. For example, in case of cashless payment, an attacker could have an accomplice covertly holding one of the relay devices in range of somebody else’s payment card (i.e., by holding it near their wallet). Once this is done, the attacker could then use their relay device to purchase an item against the victim’s card. The disadvantage to this type of attack is the time taken to transmit the data. Due to the increased distance that the data has to travel, the time taken to transmit the data will also be increased.

In case of data-modification attack, the data that is being exchanged by the two parties is captured by the attacker and modified. The device used by the attacker will inhibit the data that is exchanged through the NFC channel for a brief amount of time. Another way of interfering with the data exchange over the NFC channel is to use an RFID jammer. The data modification can be easily detected by monitoring the strength of the signal that is being transmitted.

[Figure 2.30](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig30) shows the eavesdropping attack of the NFC applications. In this case, an antenna is used to monitor the traffic between two parties by the attacker. The small distance between the two devices does not reduce the possibility of this attack. In some cases of attacks, the data that is being exchanged will be corrupted rendering it as useless. Using a secure channel for transmission, the data integrity can increase.

![image](OEBPS/images/PBCS0600-02-f30.jpg)

Figure 2.30 Eavesdropping attacks on the NFC modules of an IoT device

In a spoofing attack, the attacker pretends to be a different entity and taps the device against the tag. This can be made possible if the tag is compromised allowing it to run the malicious code. Even though the distance between the sender and receiver is very small, in some cases, man in the middle attacks is also possible in NFC. The attacker can use the antennas to intercept the information and manipulate it before sending it to the original receiver.

In the design, E-Ink display was used for reducing the power consumption by the modules. The displays are bistable, and the power is consumed only when a change of state is required. A global system for mobile communication (GSM) modem, Adafruit Fona was implemented in the design for communications with a small size and a wide variety of capabilities. In the framework of Swing-Pay, the fingerprint of the payee is activated initially. The card will be activated once the fingerprint is authenticated. Once payer selects pay-money, the payee will select the receive money in the respective module. The payee is authenticated using the fingerprint sensor and tap his card with the payer’s card. Upon successful authentication, the Payee’s ID will be transferred using NFC P2P mode to the payer module. If the authentication fails, the transaction will be blocked. Upon successful authentication, a message is sent through GSM module. In this prototype, the Android application was used and data is put in a Web Service using HTTP.

Once the data is received by the cloud server, it proceeds with the transaction. The details of bank for both payer and the payee are stored in the cloud server. Details are sent to the respective bank servers using the API. The payer’s bank will transfer the money to the payee account.

A Crypta tag architecture is shown in [Figure 2.32](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig32) ([Plos *et al.*, 2013](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib71)). One of the main components of the tag is analog front-end. This is connected to the antenna and is responsible for various tasks, extracting power supply, modulation, and demodulation of data and providing stable reset and clock signals. The digital part is attached to the analog front-end. The received demodulated data is processed in the digital part of the tag. The requested operations are performed on the data received and the responses are prepared. The digital part of the tag consists of an 8-bit Microcontroller, crypto unit (CU), framing logic (FL), and a memory unit. Internal memory stores the microcontroller program and AMBA bus is used for the communication between the FL and memory unit. Various cryptographic operations are performed on the data by the CU. An interface to the microcontroller is provided by FL and also handles some of the commands that are at lower levels.

## 2.6 Smart transportation security

This section discusses the security threats and solutions for smart transportation. The category of vehicles that are discussed here, include smart cars and UAV. Research is being conducted where smart vehicles are being designed to reduce human interaction in driving the vehicle on road or in the air. This means the system will be doing all the driving and the dependence on circuitry increases exponentially. They are also connected to the internet for constant updates and also communicate with the servers. This makes them vulnerable to many kinds of attacks ([Storm, 2015](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib95); [Wu and Horng, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib106); [Xiao *et al.*, 2009](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib109)). If the smart car or a UAV are attacked and taken control by the attacker, the results can be catastrophic.
