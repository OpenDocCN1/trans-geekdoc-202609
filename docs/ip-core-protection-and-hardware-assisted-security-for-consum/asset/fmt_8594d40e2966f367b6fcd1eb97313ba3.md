### 11.1 IP Protection—a broad overview

With the advent of new technology, new devices are being introduced into the world and high-performance applications are being designed. With better systems coming out every day, there is also a problem that they are used for various malicious applications like counterfeiting, which is a major problem in hardware and in media also. Many people nowadays are producing content and sharing it with the world ([Krishnamurthy, 2018](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib47); [Mohanty, 2015](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib67)). But the content that they own is being illegally used by many commercially, without paying royalties to the owner and in some cases, the content is being misused also. This raises many problems pertaining to the IPR and their protection. [Figure 11.1](#Chapter11.html#c11_fig1) shows a broad overview on the area of counterfeiting. An image can be produced by the owner and watermarked but an attacker can attack, tamper, or remove the watermark from the image and can claim ownership. This creates many issues on the copyrights and ownership rights.

#### 11.1.2 Copyright protection of multimedia—a brief history

There are several advantages to digital media over the analog world. Higher quality, ease of processing, and editing are some of them. The most common forms of digital media are compact disc (CD), DVD, TV broadcast, and computers. But there are also many disadvantages to the digital media. Digital media counterfeiting has been a very big problem lately. It can easily be duplicated, which has raised the need for more secure and effective DRM tools ([Devillier, 2017](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib25); [Kougianos *et al*., 2009](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib46); [Lah and Lewis, 2016](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib48); [Ma, 2017](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib52); [Ma *et al*., 2016](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib53)). Watermarking is one of the ways of securing the digital media such as audios, photographs, and videos. There are different kinds of watermarking like visible or invisible and can be inserted into different domains like spatial or frequency. There are various software implementations of digital watermarking but very few hardware implementations ([Liu *et al*., 2017](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib50); [Sarreshtedari *et al*., 2015](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib94); [Zhao *et al*., 2015](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib129)). When designing a hardware implementation of watermarking, various issues are to be considered such as low-power consumption, high-performance, ease of integration, and reliability.

Emil Hembrooke invented electronic watermarking in 1954 ([Cox and Miller, 2002](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib19)). Since then there has been extensive research carried on by various experts from different domains, such as cryptography, computer science, communications, and signal processing. Digital watermarking was proposed to provide robust protection along with encryption and scrambling. Many of the following questions were raised by the community ([Cox and Miller, 2002](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib19); [Zhao *et al*., 1999](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib128); [Yeung, 1998](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib124)):

1. What is digital watermarking and what are its properties?

2. What is the process of implementing digital watermarking?

3. What are its disadvantages?
