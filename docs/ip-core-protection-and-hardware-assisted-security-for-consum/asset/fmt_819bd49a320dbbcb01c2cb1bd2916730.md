### 12.8 Memristor-based PUFs

Technology scaling has led to designing high-performance, low-power chips and SoCs ([Mohanty, 2015](#Chapter12.html#c12_bib53)). Transistor sizes have reached 12 nm ([AMD, 2018](#Chapter12.html#c12_bib1)) and are going low still. There are speculations of 7 nm chips being released commercially. But there is a threshold that can be reached and alternatives to the transistors are being explored to replace them. Scaling has also led to many issues, including power consumption, fabrication, and switching speeds. High switching speeds and low power consumption have been achieved using the RRAM, resistive random access memory technology, and 3D arrays ([Mohanty, 2015](#Chapter12.html#c12_bib53)). These RRAMs are also called “Memristors.” It is a two-terminal device, whose resistance depends on the amount of voltage and the direction of current that passes through it. So taking this property of a memristive device as an advantage, multiple resistive levels can be achieved. A continuous variable resistance can also be achieved by switching states from one resistive to other states. The resistance change will also be dependent on the state that it is already in, that is, the voltage/current that has already passed through the device in the previous iteration. This proves that a Memristor has the properties of a “Resistor” and “Memory.” Hence the name “Memristor” (Memory + Resistor).
