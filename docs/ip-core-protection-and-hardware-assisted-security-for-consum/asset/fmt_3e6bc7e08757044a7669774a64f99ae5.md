
Most of the modern CE device comprises a dedicated lens or camera to capture and/or display digital images such as smartphone, tablets, scanner, laptop, smartwatch ([Thavalengal and Corcoran, 2016](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib34); [Kim *et al.*, 2015](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib13); [Corcoran and Andrae, 2014](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib5); [Tang *et al.*, 2016](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib33)). Due to the enhancement of camera lenses, recording components, and displaying components, current digital imaging systems are capable of capturing and displaying high-resolution images ([Corcoran *et al.*, 2001](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib6); [Andorko *et al.*, 2011](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib3)). As high-resolution images contain too much detailed information, they are large in size. Storing or transmitting these large size images is a critical issue for storage space and transmission bandwidth, respectively. Reducing the size of an image while storing and/or transferring is one of the popular and commercially successful techniques to address this crisis. JPEG standard formed in 1992 proposed DCT-based image compression. Image compressions are of two types: (1) lossy and (2) lossless. In lossy image compression, less important information of an image is discarded permanently ([Mohanty *et al.*, 2003](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib18); [Mohanty, 1999](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib17)). Camera ([Corcoran *et al.*, 2001](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib6); [Andorko *et al.*, 2011](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib3)) in a smartphone, tablets, laptop, smartwatch, etc. uses lossy image compression ([Hnesh and Demirel, 2016](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib11)). In lossless image compression, no loss of information occurs. Camera in medical imaging ([Bilgin and Marcellin, 1998](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib4)), satellite imaging, forensic imaging, etc. uses lossless image compression ([Li *et al.*, 2017](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib16); [Scarmana and McDougall, 2015](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib24)).

DCT-based JPEG image compression is lossy by nature ([Obukhov and Kharlamov, 2008](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib21)). DCT segregates an image into multiple subparts or blocks based on the visual quality of the image and then converts each block to the frequency domain from spatial domain. It discards small high-frequency components; therefore, DCT-based JPEG image compression method is lossy.

Image compression and decompression (CODEC) is a major data-intensive/power-intensive computational process in the SoC of almost all smart CE devices. Therefore, in these CE products, JPEG-based CODEC is a vital function implemented through a dedicated IP core to perform the data-intensive/power-intensive computational process for image compression and decompression ([Roy and Sengupta, 2018](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib23)). The use of JPEG CODEC in the form of an IP core not only balances the trade-off between hardware area and processing speed but also enhances the design productivity. For these aforementioned advantages, JPEG CODEC IP core may face several threats such as IP piracy ([Sengupta *et al.*, 2018a](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib31)), IP infringement ([Sengupta and Roy, 2017a](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib26); [Roy and Sengupta, 2017](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib22); [Wong *et al.*, 2004](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib36); [Alkabani and Koushanfar, 2007](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib2)), malicious Trojan insertion ([Sengupta *et al.*, 2017a](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib29)), RE ([Sengupta and Roy, 2017b](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib27); [Lao and Parhi, 2015](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib15); [Vijayakumar *et al.*, 2017](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib35); [Sengupta *et al.*, 2017b](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib30)), which therefore demands its protection ([Sengupta, 2017](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib25)). The motivation for designing a protected JPEG CODEC IP core is explained in the rest of the section.

**Motivation:** Standard JPEG CODEC design process is not capable (also does not aim) to obscure the functionality from an adversary by transforming the architecture. This does not deter RE process for an adversary. Successful RE attack enables an adversary (in the foundry) to identify the functionality of the design to counterfeit the netlist of the design and make several copies of it without the knowledge of the IP vendor/owner. Further, if an adversary is able to identify the functionality of the design from its functional structure, he/she can make a malicious modification (i.e., insert Trojan) into the design and create malfunctioning. Both these threats can be avoided if RE process is made harder for an adversary. This is possible by structurally obfuscating the JPEG CODEC design such that the functionality of it becomes unobvious to an adversary, i.e., architecture designed to be hidden to an adversary. This strongly hinders the RE process, thus thwarting the threats. Therefore, a JPEG CODEC is considered secure, if its architecture is not obvious, i.e., its functionality is not easily discoverable by inspecting its structure ([Sengupta *et al.*, 2018b](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib32)). ## 9.2 Overview of JPEG compression and decompression

Typically, JPEG is a lossy image compression technique based on DCT. DCT is a Fourier-like transformation technique proposed by Ahmed *et al.* ([1974](#Chapter9.html#c09_bib1)). However, unlike Fourier transformation which represents a signal as a mixture of sine and cosine expansion, DCT represents a signal in only cosine expansion. Based on the image’s visual quality, DCT segregates it into multiple parts or spectral sub-bands. It converts an input image from the spatial domain to the frequency domain. The DCT is known for its high “energy compaction” property, meaning that the transformed signal can be easily analyzed using few low-frequency components. In simpler terms, DCT discards small high-frequency components, i.e., sharp transitions in intensity for grayscale images. Therefore, DCT-based image compression method is lossy, where less important information of an image is discarded. The process of discarding less important information is known as quantization. Image compression ratio with image quality can be controlled through quantization factor. The output bitstream is generated from the quantized coefficients through different encoding mechanisms and is then stored into a storage device. Reconstruction of the compressed image starts with decoding the bitstream data. De-quantization and inverse DCT (IDCT) is applied back to back on the decoded data to generate the decompressed image.
