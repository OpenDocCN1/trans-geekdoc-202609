Storage variables (`V0–V30`) are assigned to each operation of the scheduled DFG (according to step A.3). A CIG is constructed (according to step A.4) with 31 nodes, i.e., `V0–V30` and eight registers, i.e., register purple (`Reg_P`), register dark_blue (`Reg_DB`), register light_blue (`Reg_LB`), register green (`Reg_G`), register yellow (`Reg_Y`), register red (`Reg_R`), register orange (`Reg_O`), and register black (`Reg_B`). The corresponding CIG is shown in [Figure 4.12](#Chapter4.html#c04_fig12). The scheduled CDFG after performing storage variable assignment and register allocation is shown in [Figure 4.13](#Chapter4.html#c04_fig13). The number of control steps (`cs`) required to schedule the FIR-2 CDFG is 9. The register allocation table corresponding to the constructed CIG (shown in [Figure 4.12](#Chapter4.html#c04_fig12)) is reported in [Table 4.1](#Chapter4.html#c04_tbl1) (according to step A.5). All the storage variables (`V0–V30`) are sorted in ascending order according to variable number and a list is generated (according to step A.6). As an example, a 14-digits vendor signature: ‘`i I I T I TT I IT ! TTT`’ is selected which is a combination of four aforementioned signature variables (according to step A.7). All the pre-processing steps to embed single-phase watermark is performed now. The next step is to decode the 14-digits signature, based on aforementioned encoding rules (according to step B.1). The vendor’s signatures and its corresponding decoded meaning are shown in [Table 4.2](#Chapter4.html#c04_tbl2). According to the decoded signature shown in [Table 4.2,](#Chapter4.html#c04_tbl2) the list of additional edges are between nodes (`V2` and `V3`), (`V2` and `V4`), (`V2` and `V6`), (`V1` and `V2`), (`V2` and `V8`), (`V1` and `V4`), (`V1` and `V6`), (`V2` and `V10`), (`V2` and `V12`), (`V1` and `V8`), (`V0` and `V1`), (`V1` and `V10`), (`V1` and `V12`), and (`V1` and `V14`) (according to step B.2). Although according to the vendor’s signature, there are 14 additional edges to be added in the CIG, however, by default eight edges (i.e., edge between nodes (`V2` and `V3`), (`V2` and `V4`), (`V2` and `V6`), (`V1` and `V2`), (`V2` and `V8`), (`V1` and `V4`), (`V1` and `V6`), and (`V0` and `V1`)) are already present in the graph. Hence, the constraints for these eight constraints are satisfied by default. Therefore, the objective is to satisfy the other six remaining constraints (i.e., edge between nodes (`V2` and `V10`), (`V2` and `V12`), (`V1` and `V8`), (`V1` and `V10`), (`V1` and `V12`), and (`V1` and `V14`)) in the IP core design. It can be observed that edge between node (`V2` and `V12`) forces storage variable `V2` and `V12` to be executed through distinct register, therefore node color of `V2` and `V12` must be different which is green (`G`) now (see [Figure 4.12](#Chapter4.html#c04_fig12)). Hence to accommodate this constraint, the allocated register for storage variable `V2` (`Reg_G`) needs to swap with other storage variable of same control step. Scanning the storage variables from left to right, we can observe that swapping `V2` with `V0` is violating a constraint (due to edge between nodes `V2` and `V8`), similarly, swapping `V2` with `V1` is violating a constraint (due to edge between nodes `V1` and `V12`). However, swapping `V2` with `V3` does not violate any constraints; therefore, storage variable `V2` is allocated to `Reg_LB` and storage variable `V3` is allocated to `Reg_G`. Similarly, edge between nodes `V1` and `V14` forces storage variable `V1` and `V14` to be executed through distinct register which need swapping. Swapping `V14` with `V15` does not violate any constraints, therefore storage variable `V14` is allocated to `Reg_G` (previously allocated to `Reg_DB`) and storage variable `V15` is allocated to `Reg_DB` (previously allocated to `Reg_G`). (*Note: If no storage other variable is found to swap with the conflicting storage variable, a new register should be added in the CIG and assigned to that conflicting storage variable.*) After embedding all these additional edges in the CIG, the final modified graph (according to step B.3) is shown in [Figure 4.14](#Chapter4.html#c04_fig14).
