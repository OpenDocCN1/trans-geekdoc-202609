## Chapter 6

### Computational forensic engineering for resolving ownership conflict of DSP IP core

Some of previous chapters focused on watermarking and fingerprinting for ownership protection. This chapter focuses on another technique called forensic engineering (FE) for ownership protection. FE extracts the features of IP cores and matches to statistically suggest the original ownership. It also discusses various steps involved in FE of a hard IP core and presents specific details with case study examples.

#### 6.1 Introduction

The rapid proliferation of electronic/SoC industry along with the fierce competition should be considered to remain competent. To beat the competition, companies seek to reduce time-to-market and design cost. These goals can be easily met through utilization of reusable IP core(s) ([Sengupta, 2016](#Chapter6.html#c06_bib22); [Kachave and Sengupta, 2016](#Chapter6.html#c06_bib9)). This is because reusable IP core(s) minimize design time by reducing the manpower required to reproduce an IP. Therefore, reusable IP core has become a mandatory component of generic SoC/IC design flow. Moreover, a design process at higher level of design abstraction such as HLS is always crucial for complex SoCs such as DSP/multimedia cores (Sengupta and Sedaghat, [2010](#Chapter6.html#c06_bib29), [2011](#Chapter6.html#c06_bib30)). This is because a higher level of design abstraction reduces design complexity and identifies optimal (low-cost) design architecture based on several orthogonal design objectives. Therefore, reusable IP core(s), such as DSP/multimedia core generated through HLS, is an essential component for consumer electronics devices. However, an IP core is vulnerable to various threats such as IP piracy, Trojan insertion, IP overbuilding, and false claim of ownership ([Sengupta, 2017](#Chapter6.html#c06_bib23); [Yasin *et al.*, 2016](#Chapter6.html#c06_bib37); [Sengupta and Kundu, 2017](#Chapter6.html#c06_bib27)). An estimate based on the report presented in the [International Chamber of Commerce (2011)](#Chapter6.html#c06_bib8) shows that electronics industry lose roughly minimum 1.5 trillion USD annually due to piracy and counterfeiting ([Zhang, 2016](#Chapter6.html#c06_bib39)). Therefore, it becomes mandatory to devise methodologies that can safeguard an IP core from these aforementioned threats. There are several IP protection mechanisms to overcome these threats, as shown in [Figure 6.1](#Chapter6.html#c06_fig1).
