The effect of increasing fingerprint strength on the design latency, hardware area, and implanting cost is shown in [Table 5.9](#Chapter5.html#c05_tbl9). Three different fingerprint sizes is selected, viz. 10, 20, and 30 to observe the comparative outcome on various design metrics. It can be observed from [Table 5.9](#Chapter5.html#c05_tbl9) that for most of the benchmarks, the increment of design area is zero with the increment of fingerprint size. The reason is that out of three different fingerprint encoding variables only ‘`C`’ variable embeds fingerprinting constraints during register allocation phase, which may incur area overhead. Therefore, with the increase in fingerprint size, the possibility of incurring design area overhead is very minimal. On the other hand, minimal increment in design latency can be observed with the increment of fingerprint size from [Table 5.9](#Chapter5.html#c05_tbl9). The reason is that adding fingerprinting constraints during scheduling phase while resolving operation conflict. However, for both design area and design latency, the increment is minimal. Therefore, nominal increment in cost with increment in fingerprint size can be observed for some benchmarks.

On the other hand, minimal increment in design area can be observed by increasing the watermark size (in [Table 5.10](#Chapter5.html#c05_tbl10)). The reason is that augmenting more watermarking constraints during register allocation phase may result into register overhead. Therefore, marginal upsurge in cost by increasing watermark size can be witnessed for some benchmarks.

### 5.5.2 Comparative study between symmetrical and non-symmetrical technique

The DSP IP designs produced through Roy *et al.* ([2008](#Chapter5.html#c05_bib10)) are compared with baseline IP core design, where baseline design indicates an unprotected design (i.e. a design without fingerprint and watermark) in terms of design latency, design area, and implanting cost. As shown in [Table 5.11](#Chapter5.html#c05_tbl11), symmetrical IP protection can be achieved at minimal latency overhead (avg. 1.87%), at minimal area overhead (avg. 0.24%), and at very minimal design cost overhead (less than 1%) in comparison to original baseline.
