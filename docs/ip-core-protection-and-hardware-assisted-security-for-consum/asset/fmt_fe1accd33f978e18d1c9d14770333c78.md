
We discuss here one of the established methodologies to handle hardware Trojan (which corrupts the functional output of a DSP IP core) by generating a Trojan secured scheduling design. A Trojan secured schedule can be obtained in three consecutive steps: firstly, performing duplication of all the operations of the original CDFG to generate a duplicate CDFG, collectively known as a dual modular redundant (DMR) system; secondly, performing scheduling of operations of the DMR system based on the resource configuration (DSE-generated) to obtain the scheduled DMR; finally, achieving distinctness during hardware allocation of operations of the DMR schedule such that similar operations of the original CDFG and duplicate CDFG must have hardware of distinct vendors. Therefore, to achieve distinctness during hardware allocation, IP cores must be imported from a minimum of two distinct third-party vendors. As both the third-party vendors are not trustworthy, it may be possible that both the 3PIP cores are Trojan infected. However, having `exactly` the same Trojan payload in both the 3PIP cores is nearly impossible as both third-party vendors are mutually exclusive (unknown) to each other. Therefore, Trojan detection can be achieved by performing an equivalence check of the output of both the original CDFG and duplicate CDFG during the runtime. In other words, hardware Trojan detection can be achieved by assigning different sets of 3PIP vendors for similar operations in DMR design during the hardware-allocation step of HLS.

Trojan detection achieved through DMR for loop-based CDFG introduces duplication of all the operations, adding steering logic and internal buffering that results in incurring design overhead. Additionally, loop-based CDFGs can be implemented for different unrolling factor (UF) values, where different UF value have a different impact on the design latency, which may also incur design overhead (if not selected intelligently). Moreover, distinctness during vendor allocation can be achieved in several ways. However, each distinct vendor-allocation procedure may result in different area and execution delay of the Trojan secured design. The reason is that the same hardware imported from two different vendors may have different area and latency. Considering all these factors, to optimize the design overhead as well as to design a low-cost Trojan secured DSP IP core, an optimal design solution comprising of resource configuration, unrolling factor, and vendor allocation mode needs to be explored.

### 3.5.2 Goal

The goals of Trojan security aware design are as follows:

1. Generate a low-cost Trojan secured DMR schedule to detect hardware Trojan for DSP IP core.

2. The Trojan secured design should satisfy the user-specified design constraints (such as area, delay, and power).

3. For single- and nested-loop-based applications, an optimal unrolling factor should be additionally explored to optimize the Trojan secured DSP core design.

4. Perform distinct vendor allocation of the original and the duplicate unit of the DMR schedule.

5. Explore an optimal distinct vendor-allocation process out of several available possibilities.

6. Provide low-cost Trojan security aware solution.

7. Minimize the design overhead of Trojan secured DSP IP core.
