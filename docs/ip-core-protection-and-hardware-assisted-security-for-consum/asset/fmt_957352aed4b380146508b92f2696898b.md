
# Primary use of watermark

Primary use of watermark is to embed it into a digital media using different algorithms and extract it at a later time for authentication of ownership and so on. The digital media can be an image, a video, or an audio. For example, a watermark on media can be considered as a “seal” that is on an image to identify the ownership, the quality of media, and in some cases, the identity of purchaser ([Wang *et al*., 2018](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib121)). [Figure 11.7](#Chapter11.html#c11_fig7) shows the big picture of encoding and decoding techniques which consists of various parts such as encoder, comparator, and decoder ([Memon and Wong, 1998](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib60); [Mohanty, 1999](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib65)). In case of a visible watermarking technique, the watermark used will be an image. But in case of an invisible watermarking technique, the watermark can be an image, a random, or a pseudo random number. The owner may have a unique watermark of his/her own and can also embed different watermarks in different media objects. When needed, the watermark can be extracted for authentication.

## 11.2.2 The decoder

When the watermarked image is needed to be authenticated, it should sent through a decoder. [Figure 11.6](#Chapter11.html#c11_fig6) shows the big picture of the decoding process. The decoding process will depend on various factors, including the process of inserting a watermark into the original image and nature of watermarking algorithm. The watermark extraction process is where the entire watermark is extracted from the image. But in some cases, only a specific watermark signal can be detected in the media, and this process is called a watermark detection. Watermark extraction can prove the authenticity of ownership on the media, but only detection can verify the ownership. The input to the decoder `D` is an image, that is watermarked, nonwatermarked, or even corrupted, denoted as `J`, which needs to be challenged for ownership. The decoder will recover the watermark ![image](OEBPS/images/c11-Eqn-in-3.gif). ## 11.3.1 Spatial versus frequency domain watermarking

The digital watermark can be embedded in frequency or spatial domains ([Chen *et al*., 2015](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib13); [Mohanty *et al*., 2018](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib71)). There are also some alternatives implemented other than the frequency and spatial domain watermarking, like DCT, FT, and wavelet transform (WT). In all of the watermarking techniques, the spatial techniques are much simpler to implement and are less power hungry. For the frequency domain watermarking techniques also, there are several advantages ([Cox *et al*., 1996](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib37)). Nonsalient components in the image or an audio file will be eliminated by the lossy compression. So if the watermark has to be preserved after a lossy compression, it should be embedded in a region of the data that is significant and will not be damaged in the lossy compression. Such processing is performed in the frequency domain. High-frequency components are subjected to more loss. So embedding the watermark in a low-frequency region is preferred while watermarking the media. In the case of spatial domain watermarking, cropping is a major issue that might affect the watermark. It is highly difficult to analyze the effects of distortion in spatial and frequency domains.

## 11.3.2 Based on multimedia objects

There are four different types of watermarking techniques based on the type of media:

*   Image watermarking

*   Video watermarking

*   Audio watermarking

*   Text watermarking

Image watermarking is the most focused area of research for the past several decades. Developing a common type of watermarking technique for all the types of media is highly difficult. Secure Spread Spectrum algorithm was considered to be better for image, audio, and video media ([Cox *et al*., 1996](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib37)). But watermarking halftoned images has to be done in a different manner which was a contradiction to the previous claim ([Memon and Wong, 1998](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib60)). In such cases, the binary image is embedded with another binary watermark using visual cryptography. Text watermarking is much different compared to the image, audio, or video watermarking techniques ([Kamaruddin *et al*., 2018](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib40)).
