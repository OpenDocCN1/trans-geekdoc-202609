
Digital watermarking has come a very long way since its introduction. But so did the media community and devices that consume or distribute the content with watermarks with it. This has become a large system and watermarking algorithms should have integration capabilities into such huge systems. This is actually a high priority in case of hardware watermarking techniques where the compatibility of hardware module to be embedded in different applications should be increased. While implementing the watermarking chip, the target device, such as a DVD drive, digital camera, printer, and scanner, should be taken into consideration.

## 11.5.16 Characteristics specific to a watermark

As discussed in Section 11.3, there are different types of watermarking techniques. With the discussion that we had until now in the current section, the characteristics of different types of watermarks can be formulated.

### 11.5.16.1 Visible watermark

Visible watermarking technique is widely used in many applications and there are characteristics that need to be met for this type of watermark ([Mintzer *et al*., 1997](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib61); Mohanty *et al*., [2000a](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib73), [2000b](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib75)). The color and the monochrome images, when used for visible watermark, should be more obvious when used. When the visible watermark is embedded into an image, a significant portion of the image should be selected for the insertion so that the watermark is not deleted by cropping. The watermark should be transparent and the original image details should be visible. Watermark should be difficult to remove and the removal process should be more intensive compared to purchasing the original image from the owner.

### 11.5.16.2 Invisible-robust watermark

In the case of an invisible watermark, it should not be visible when embedded into the original image ([Mintzer *et al*., 1997](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib61)). When an invisible watermark is embedded into the media, the quality of the original media should not be affected and degraded. An invisible watermark should not be affected to any signal distortions and is also resistant to different kinds of attacks targeted to remove the watermark illegally. Identification of the owner should be unambiguous by retrieving the watermark. The decoder of the watermark scheme should be scalable and also be integrated into different generations of computers and systems. Pixel modification should be kept to a minimum when inserting the watermark into a high-quality artwork. The whole process of insertion of watermark should be autonomous with a little human intervention.

### 11.5.16.3 Invisible-fragile watermark

Similar to the previously discussed invisible watermark, the invisible-fragile watermark should not be visible or noticeable and also should not degrade the quality of the original image. When the pixel values of the image are modified, the watermark should not be removed from the watermarked image and should be secure. This means that it is impossible to recover the changes, or regenerate the watermark after image alternations, even when the watermarking procedure and/or the watermark itself is known. In case of a high-quality image as an input to the insertion algorithm, the pixels that are modified must be kept to a minimum. In some applications, reversing the tampering of watermark might be necessary, which is called error correction.

### 11.5.16.4 Video watermark

In case of a video watermark, the presence of the watermark in the video should not affect the viewing quality of the video or playback. Compression of the video should not affect the video and watermark and vice versa. Detection of watermark should be reliable and with less errors and false detection, it should be kept to a minimum, ideally zero. Attacks on the video watermarking may be intentional or unintentional like frame dropping and variable rate encoding. Detection algorithm hardware should be designed for low-cost implementations.

### 11.5.16.5 Watermarking for broadcasting applications

Watermarking applications also include broadcasting applications ([Busch *et al*., 1999](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib10)). The watermark used in that applications should be resistant to the attacks and the codec encoder should not be overworked. When the watermark is embedded into the image, it should not be affected by the low bit rate of MPEG-2, VHS tape recordings, and TV transmissions. For broadcasting applications, the capacity of 64 bits in 10-second video is used. Multiple watermarking schemes should be able to handle that with false positive detection probability less than 10^(−12) and watermark should be more secure.

### 11.5.16.6 Desired characteristics of hardware-based watermarking systems

Hardware watermarking chips are more secure and also can be optimized for better performance with low-power consumption ([Mathai *et al*., 2003a](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib57); [Mohanty *et al*., 2005a, 2005b](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib79)). In such cases, the chip area occupied by the chip should be low to reduce the cost of the entire component. The power consumption of the hardware watermarking chip should also be less so that the modules can be integrated in battery operated applications. The integration of the chip into other devices should not be an issue. The operational latency of the chip should be low so that it can be used for real-time applications. Blind watermarking schemes are preferred in such applications.

## 11.6 Technical challenges for watermarking

There are many technical issues when it comes to the implementation of watermarking. Security, robustness, and imperceptibility are the trade-offs of the digital watermarking and this is what makes the research in this area interesting. For an increased imperceptibility, the watermark must be added to a high-frequency component in the media object. But to attain high robustness, the low-frequency components should be used for watermarking. This becomes more difficult with added characteristics such that the watermark data should be resistant to different kinds of attacks on them. This section discusses different kinds of challenges faced during the watermarking algorithm design ([Delaigle *et al*., 2002](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib22); [Frattolillo, 2015](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib29); [Kougianos *et al*., 2009](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib46); [Mohanty, 2015](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib67); [Mohanty *et al*., 1999](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib72); [Peng *et al*., 2010](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib83); Singh *et al*., [2015](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib99), [2017](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib100); [Vo *et al*., 2017](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib114)).

### 11.6.1 Properties of visual signals

The images and the videos are the visual signals. Behavior of such signals has to be studied and understood to develop more robust and secure watermarking schemes and hide them well in the signals. The visual signals are considered as the intensity versus space delays of image information, amplitude plots, and the intensity versus space and time displays. When these waves are studied more, the information regarding the properties of the signals will be revealed. Some of the properties are discussed in the following text.

#### 11.6.1.1 Nonstationarity

The nonstationary property is highly common to almost all of the signals. There is a huge amount of segments of flat or slowly changing intensity in the image and video signals. Besides these there will also be edges and textured regions in the image and video signals. The edges are the important regions that need to be protected to maintain the quality of the image or video and the textured regions should be used for storing additional information.
