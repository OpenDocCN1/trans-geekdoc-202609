##### 10.2.2.5 Key scheduler for 128-bit AES

The main role of key scheduler in AES is to accept the original 128 bit key and derive the subkeys for the next rounds of AES. The number of subkeys needed is obtained by adding one to the number of rounds. Hence, for key length of 128 bits, the number of rounds is `n[r]` = 10, and there are 11 subkeys, each of 128 bits. The AES with a 192-bit key requires 13 subkeys of length 128 bits, and AES with a 256-bit key has 15 subkeys. The AES subkeys are calculated recursively, i.e., in order to derive subkey `k[i]`, subkey `k[i−1]` must be known. We will discuss the process of key scheduler in more details a little later. ### 10.3 AES digital watermarking

We have learnt about the motivation of embedding watermark in an IP core or hardware. Similarly, in order to provide protection (provide deterrent) against false claim of ownership, piracy, forgery, digital watermark needs to be implanted in AES hardware design. In the chapter, we show how to implant watermark in a certain portion of AES hardware structure. The selected portion of AES structure where watermarking is embedded in ‘`MixColumn diffusion layer 2` hardware block’ (shown in Figures [10.4](#Chapter10.html#c10_fig4) and [10.5](#Chapter10.html#c10_fig5), respectively).

This portion of AES hardware is selected for watermarking because of following desirable properties:

1. Option of embedding watermark without much design overhead.

2. Ease of implementation.

3. Seamless signature detection.

4. No chance of hardware functionality being affected.

5. Maximal opportunity of adding several signature digits corresponding to a watermark.

#### 10.3.2 Process of embedding watermark in AES

1. Sort the output variables of `MixColumn` hardware structure in ascending order, i.e., `Z[0]`, `Z[1]`, `Z[2]`,…,`Z[15]` and store in list `L[k]`.

2. Create a desired 16 digit watermark signature using random combinations of `α` and `β` digits.
