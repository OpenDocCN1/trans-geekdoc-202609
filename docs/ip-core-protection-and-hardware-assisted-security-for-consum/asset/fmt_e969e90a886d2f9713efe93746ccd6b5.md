
The memristor-based PUFs use the variation of resistance in the device, which depends on the thickness of device, “D.” Due to the manufacturing variations, the device thickness will vary which will affect the read and write times of the device in a memory cell. In case of the memristor, a logical “1” can be written to it when it is set from high resistive state (HRS) (![image](OEBPS/images/c12-Eqn-in-29.gif)) to a low resistive state (LRS) (![image](OEBPS/images/c12-Eqn-in-30.gif)) for a write time above a minimum threshold of time ![image](OEBPS/images/c12-Eqn-in-31.gif); else, a logical “0” will be written. `T` is called the SET time and when it is close to ![image](OEBPS/images/c12-Eqn-in-32.gif), the probability of having a “1” in the cell will be equal to that of a “0.” This uncertainty will make memristor much suitable for the design of PUF. Also, in this case of a binary memristor, where two states are considered, “1” and “0,” transition from one state to the other will take place very abruptly. In such cases, the tamper resistance of memristor-based PUFs will be very high. For the initialization process, a high voltage should be applied so that it will be in a SET state and then switching should be performed between the HRS and LRS at a lower voltage. Below this SET voltage threshold, the device will be operating as a normal resistor. This property can be used as a tamper resistive mechanism. [Figure 12.18](#Chapter12.html#c12_fig18) shows a 1-bit memristor memory-based PUF (McDonald *et al*., [2011](#Chapter12.html#c12_bib49), [2012](#Chapter12.html#c12_bib50)). This uses the write time variability of the devices for design of a PUF.

Reading can be done by supplying a “0” to ![image](OEBPS/images/c12-Eqn-in-37.gif). An XOR operation is performed on the output with the challenge input resultant of which will be the output of PUF.

In case of a lateral switching PUF cell designed using memristor (McDonald *et al*., [2011](#Chapter12.html#c12_bib49), [2012](#Chapter12.html#c12_bib50)), forming step is highly important for the operations. During the forming step, the conductive filament of the device is supplied with a large voltage in the dielectric sheet. This gives an advantage of values being hidden even during the fabrication state in the foundry. Therefore, if a filament is not present in the dielectric of the device, then it can be assumed that the device has not been tampered with and is secure. In the case of a lateral switching configuration, when the device is SET, during the RESET operation, only one device can go to HRS while other will stay in LRS. Which devices stays in LRS and which goes to HRS is completely random.

## 12.9 Diode-based PUF

There are various advantages to a crossbar array compared to other circuit designs which make them more suitable to be used in a PUF design. [Figure 12.21](#Chapter12.html#c12_fig21) shows the design of a memristor crossbar array. Regular structure, low energy consumption, high packing density, and simple implementation of the designs are some of the advantages. Besides these, there are also some disadvantages to this design, like sneak current path and crosstalk. The sneak current is dependent on the reverse junction current.

![image](OEBPS/images/PBCS0600-12-f21.jpg)

Figure 12.21 Memristor crossbar array

There are two sets of wires in the crossbar array, horizontal and vertical, as shown in [Figure 12.21](#Chapter12.html#c12_fig21). The intersection of the wires is separated by a thin material or by a molecular compound. The compound will be resistive to any voltage variations among the wires. Therefore, achieving different resistive states can be done using the intersection which is used for storing the data. Horizontal wires are the word lines and vertical are the bit lines with the voltages `*V*[word]` and `*V*[bit]`. The voltage applied for reading is high compared to that of the reading.

When the memristors in the figure are replaced with diodes, it becomes a diode-based crossbar array. In this case, there are three schemes: in floating schemes, the bit and word lines that are not accessed are kept floating. In the single-supply biasing scheme, the word and bit lines that are not accessed are kept at a voltage ![image](OEBPS/images/c12-Eqn-in-46.gif). When it is “0,” it is considered grounded scheme. When they are equal, it will be in a single-supply scheme and when they are opposite polarity, they are called reverse biasing scheme.
