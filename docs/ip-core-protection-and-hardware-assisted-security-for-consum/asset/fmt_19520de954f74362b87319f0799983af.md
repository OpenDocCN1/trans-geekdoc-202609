
There are various attacks that can be performed on a smart car or an autonomous car ([Petit and Shladover, 2015](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib70)). [Figure 2.33](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig33) shows a typical autonomous car system ([Storm, 2015](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib95)). Various parts of an autonomous car include global positioning system (GPS), LiDAR, on-board unit, Emaps, radar, camera, wheel encoder, etc. LiDAR is a ranging system which uses laser. It shoots laser pings which when echoed back, gives the readings from reflection of the objects that are present around the vehicle. Radars and camera are also used to detect the obstacles in the surroundings while the vehicle is in motion. Now-a-days, smart vehicles have around 100 electronic control units (ECUs) and 10 million lines of code. When such a massive system is in place there are various chances of security breaches possible. The software updates are usually Over-The-Air updates, which also means the communications of the car has to be protected. Advanced threats should also be mitigated and each module that is present in the vehicle should be secured and protected such that the attacker does not gain access to them.

### 2.6.1.1 Smart car security attacks

There are various threats when it comes to the smart transportation ([Petit and Shladover, 2015](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib70)). [Figure 2.34](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig34) shows some of the attacks that a smart car is vulnerable to. GPS spoofing and jamming are considered to be high threats in a smart transportation environment. Cost of the simple jamming equipment is very less, but highly effective ([Petit and Shladover, 2015](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib70)). Costlier jammers do not only jam the signal but also give fake coordinates which can increase the risk. This is called GPS spoofing where false locations are given to the car. The antitheft system of a car can be easily blocked by using a GPS jammer combined with a GSM jammer. Detecting this kind of threat is highly difficult due to the environmental constraints ([Petit and Shladover, 2015](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib70)). Using the GPS modules, the attacker will also be able to track the location of the vehicle affecting the privacy and security of the smart vehicle. Tracking a vehicle is also possible by merely eavesdropping the communications and enabling the remote identification and tracking ([Figure 2.35](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig35)).

Cameras are used in the smart car to constantly monitor the environment and perform the necessary operations. It is responsible for many processes like detecting pedestrians, nearby cars, and so on. A camera lens is highly susceptible to many issues. Like any camera, the lens can be distorted using a high-intensity light which may render it useless. There are various off-the-shelf components available, infrared light sources that are able to incapacitate the camera. This may lead to under performance of the vehicle or even cause an accident due to the malfunctioning of car. There are solutions available for this issue.

When an autonomous vehicle is designed, specific decision chains are also required to be incorporated. [Figure 2.36](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig36) shows the design chain of a smart car. When a human is driving the vehicle, the decisions can be made by the human, but when an autonomous vehicle is driven by the actuators and they are controlled by algorithms. This decision chain involves different sensors, perception, planning, and actuation. The sensors will collect the data from the surroundings and store it or send it for processing. Once the data is processed, the algorithms are used for planning the next operation that should be performed by the vehicle. Depending on the decision made, the appropriate actuators will be activated to perform the action.

### 2.6.1.2 Smart car security solutions

Smart transportation is a fast-growing area with many researchers working on it. Secure components development is also one of the main focus of this area ([Kong *et al.*, 2016](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib41); [Pacheco *et al.*, 2016](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib68); [Petit *et al.*, 2014](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib69); [Satam *et al.*, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib76)). Security of an autonomous vehicle cannot be compromised, or else, public can get into danger. There are many solutions available for most of the attacks performed on the smart vehicles. The cameras can be incapacitated using high-intensity IR emitters. This can be blocked by implementing the filters before the camera which can block the IR light. Securing the communication between the vehicles and to the server can help in fighting against the eavesdropping attacks. This can be achieved by implementing the hardware-assisted security which can increase the encryption strength (Yanambaka *et al.*, [2016](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib111)a, [2016](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib102)b, [2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib113)).

There are also various frameworks that are available to develop more secure services for the smart autonomous vehicles ([Ferreira *et al.*, 2013](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib22); [Jin *et al.*, 2014](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib38); [Soliman *et al.*, 2013](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib93)). There are four different layers when designing a smart car framework ([Satam *et al.*, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib76)). They are the application, services, communications, and devices layers. At each of these layer, attacks can be performed. But with the abstraction of the layers, the security can be increased and entire system can be made more robust to the attacks. The sensors and other physical devices are responsible for collecting the data that is required to control the vehicle. The next layer is responsible for the communications to the other vehicles and the servers for updates. Various communications can be used like WiFi, Bluetooth, etc., as discussed in the previous sections. The services layer is responsible for performing all the middleware operations and functions that are used in smart car between the communications and applications layer. The top layer is the applications layer which has different applications that the user will need.
