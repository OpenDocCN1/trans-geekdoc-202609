
Various sensors and devices are the driving technologies for IoT. There are various sensors and devices for IoT, and those are called as smart devices. One of the main concerns of these devices is security ([Sivaraman *et al.*, 2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib91)). Research is being conducted for a very long time in this aspect of IoT driving technology, various IoT smart devices, and their security ([Almajali *et al.*, 2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib4); [Sivaraman *et al.*, 2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib91); [Surendran *et al.*, 2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib98); [Valea *et al.*, 2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib103)). Another aspect of driving technologies in an IoT architecture is the communications. [Figure 2.8](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig8) shows various communication technologies that are used in the IoT environment ([RS Components, 2015](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib75)). Most of the components are using low-power Bluetooth and WiFi components in the single-board computers and boards for communication, but other communication protocols are also used in IoT. Low-power Bluetooth is not suitable for data transfer of large files, but can be used for smaller data packets. Zigbee is another protocol that is widely popular in the industrial scenario. It offers a low-power operation, robust security, and scalability. For home environments, Z-wave is employed. 6LowPAN is an Internet Protocol-based communication technology, unlike Bluetooth, operates over the network defining the encapsulation and header compression. WiFi and cellular are the options that are explored where ever they can be employed. Unlike other protocols, NFC does not have a wide range and also not capable of sending high chunks of data ([RS Components, 2015](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib75)). Long-range wide area network (`LoRaWAN`) is getting popular due to its low-energy consumption and long range.

### 2.1.3 IoT – applications

INTERNET and THINGS are the two main aspects of IoT architectures. Based on them, two different architectures can be proposed. When the Internet is given the main focus and network aspects are given a priority in the IoT architecture, it is called as an internet-centric architecture ([Gubbi *et al.*, 2013](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib27)). With the cloud at the center of the whole architecture, the full potential can be brought out. With the introduction of cloud, the entire system becomes highly scalable and also makes it simple and flexible to divide the costs associated. When the smart devices or ‘THINGS’ are the main focus of the architecture, it is called as an object-centric architecture ([Lopez *et al.*, 2012](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib50)). Internet of everything (`IoE`) as depicted in [Figure 2.9](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig9) will advance IoT to even more broader scope with human and process integrations.

### 2.1.4 IoT – challenges

Technological advancements have made developing IoT applications and devices much simpler, but there are a lot of challenges to overcome in this area. Connectivity is a major issue in this regard. IoT devices or Things needs to have a unique IP address of their own which means they should be connected to internet or cloud storage. But, a high-range wireless communication requires more energy consumption and low-power communication protocols will have a transmission rate or range as a trade-off ([RS Components, 2015](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib75)). Sensors have improved since the beginning of IoT, but a need for better sensing devices has not yet been developed. Moreover, accuracy of the sensors has to be improved in various ways as well. [Figure 2.11](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig11) shows various challenges that are present in an IoT environment ([Mohanty, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib54)). Various IoT architectures are proposed based on different aspects and focus of IoT ([Gubbi *et al.*, 2013](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib27)). Selecting an architecture based on the environmental conditions and devices being used in the environment is an uphill task and a challenge. There are various dependencies in the IoT architectures.

![image](OEBPS/images/PBCS0600-02-f11.jpg)

Figure 2.11 Challenges in designing IoT

When various devices are connected to each other through a network, they are vulnerable to various threats. Security issues are a great challenge and addressing them should be considered as a primary priority. Various solutions are available for IoT security but with the implementation of better security solutions, power consumption is going to increase for the entire module ([Yanambaka *et al.*, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib113)). Collecting the data and sending it to the cloud is part of the challenge for which the later part is going to be process the data that is being collected. First, the data is sent to the cloud where it can be processed, employing high-speed servers, and storing huge amounts of data will increase the cost of design and overall IoT design. Processing the data needs high-performance computing, wherein some cases, the data should be processed in real-time that will increase the operational and overall cost of the design.
