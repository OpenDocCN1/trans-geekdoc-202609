## Foreword by Peter Corcoran

We live in a time of continual change and nowhere is this more evident than in the consumer electronics (CE) industry. Nearly 6 years ago, I wrote an editorial for *IEEE Consumer Electronics Magazine* entitled “Change and the Consumer Electronics Industry” (`http://bit.ly/CEchange`). A key point of this article is that if you want to work in this industry you have to embrace change and go with it. Technology is constantly adapting to the market and the needs of the consumer and this was never truer than it is today.

Today, we live in a world of shortening product cycles, increasing NRE costs, and, unless your organization owns a global technology platform or manufactures’ high-value consumer device platforms, there are enormous challenges in building sustainable revenue from our engineering works. Add to these day-to-day concerns the constant struggle to keep up with an ever-changing technology landscape and new technical challenges.

Among these challenges we now find that hardware design is the “new” software are now being built in the same way that complex software products like operating systems were built in the 1990s—constructed from the work and contributions of different engineering teams with specialized skills and integrated onto a single platform—these increasingly complex “system-on-chip” designs that are the mainstay of today’s advanced CE products, from smartphones, to TVs, from autonomous vehicles to immersive AR/VR headsets.

With this growth in system complexity, it becomes challenging for smaller engineering companies to build their own integrated circuits and for many of our business model becomes that of the fabless IP supplier. We painstakingly build specialized hardware designs to license into larger SoC platforms owned by global corporations. And we face the same challenges faced by smaller software vendors in the 1990s—how can we prevent the theft of our engineering designs by copying from one IC design to another? After all they are just patterns of registers and transistors, albeit highly complex and sophisticated ones embodying many thousands of hours of engineering effort.

In this context, I am delighted to introduce this new book addressing the increasingly important topic of *IP Core Protection and Hardware-Assisted Security for Consumer Electronics*. This textbook is targeted to this increasingly important design challenge for today’s CE engineers.

This text starts with the fundamentals of IP core protection and proceeds to develop a number of alternative and complimentary approaches including a number of detailed case studies. The text examines Trojan security approaches, robust watermarking and symmetrical approaches that combine fingerprinting with watermarking. Forensic analysis techniques are presented to examine and provide proof of ownership, and several advanced techniques based on design and functional obfuscation methodologies are presented together with case studies on JPEG CODEC and DSP cores. Additional chapters consider the protection of digital media and the role of secure hardware design in IoT security.

This text appears at a very timely juncture when CE engineers are facing increasing challenges. It provides a detailed and comprehensive coverage of this increasingly important field and will be of great benefit to both practicing engineers looking to update their knowledge in this field and equally to graduate students working on advanced hardware R&D projects.
