
# Chapter 10: Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) and Its Hardware Watermarking for Ownership Protection

Chapter 10 presents “Advanced encryption standard (AES) and its hardware watermarking for ownership protection.” The salient features of this chapter include introduction to the AES algorithm, its description and custom hardware design, AES digital watermarking, process of embedding watermark in AES hardware, and finally ending with the design analysis.

# Chapter 11: Hardware Approaches for Media and Information Protection and Authentication

Chapter 11 presents “Hardware approaches for media and information protection and authentication.” The salient features of this chapter include introduction to digital rights management (DRM), copyright protection of multimedia, hardware versus media protection, general framework for copyright protection, types of digital watermarks, applications of digital watermarks, desired characteristics of watermarks, hardware-based approaches for watermarking, dynamic watermarking in smart car, side-channel information leakage attacks and countermeasures, attacks on watermarks and watermarking systems, and finally ending with the limitations of watermarking.

# Chapter 12: Physical Unclonable Functions (PUFs)

Chapter 12 presents “Physical unclonable functions (PUFs).” The salient features of this chapter include principles of PUFs, properties or characteristics of PUFs, classification of PUFs, description of different types of PUFs such as ring oscillator based, static random access memory based, memristor based, diode based, magnetic based, practical implementations of PUF, case study of PUF, and finally ends with discussion on its issues and challenges.

# Book Overview

This book aims to present novel solutions for any security/protection problems related to hardware used in CE. Authors believe that there is no book that presents details of numerous hardware-assisted security topics under one canopy. The theme is important for researchers and graduate students in different areas of specialization, as it encompasses overlapping contents of EDA-CAD, hardware design security, VLSI design (high-level synthesis, RTL, gate-level synthesis, etc.), IP core protection, optimization using evolutionary computing, system-on-chip design, and finally application-specific processor/hardware accelerator design for CE applications. All the aforesaid topics are encapsulated in the proposed theme where researchers, practitioners, industry experts, CAD/design engineers, system architects, and students are expected to show interest in reading.

The book offers theoretical exercise for each chapter. This presents abundant prospect to test the understanding for each security topic and hone readers understanding. The book is prepared keeping in mind that can be easily integrated to any master’s and Ph.D. level course as well as senior bachelor’s course. The book provides materials to train senior-level engineering students on hardware-assisted security topics. Furthermore, it also serves as designer’s handbook for a designer who is eager to learn designing secured hardware, especially IP core for DSP kernels. Each chapter also includes end-to-end design flow for secured IP core architecture from the perspective of different threat/attack models.
