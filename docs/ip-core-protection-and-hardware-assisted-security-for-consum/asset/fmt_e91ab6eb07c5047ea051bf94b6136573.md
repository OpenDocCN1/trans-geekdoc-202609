### 11.8 Dynamic watermarking in smart car or UAV

IoT has become a major focus of research nowadays. With all the new technologies coming out everyday, designing new THINGS has been possible and sensor integration into various devices has become an important task in every applications. Smart transportation and UAV technology have developed significantly since their advent ([Mohanty, 2015](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib67)). Autonomous vehicles and UAVs are being used for various purposes. Self-driving cars are not so far ahead in the future. With all these coming into picture, security is one of the main concerns that is bothering the research community. Both UAV and smart car will have video-recording capabilities. Watermarking that video will help in determining if the video that is recorded in the camera is tampered or not.

The video that is recorded in the vehicles is in some cases processed real time and in some cases, stored in the memory to be analyzed at a later time. Both these cases are important when it comes to a smart car and UAV. When the video is recorded, the cars and UAV might be subject to different kinds of attacks that let the attacker to tamper with the video surveillance footage of the vehicle. So, the watermark that is introduced in the vehicle should be tamper resistant ([Fallahpour *et al*., 2014](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib26)). Many video editing software lets the user to tamper with the video and make the stream unreliable which defeats the purpose of the recording ([Su *et al*., 2011](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib103)). So, detection and distinguishing of the tampered video is also necessary in such scenarios.

Hence the watermark should be able to provide transparency, robustness, and higher capacity so that it can be embedded into different kinds of video footage. A fragile watermarking is used in most cases for detecting the tampering of a video ([Fridrich *et al*., 2000](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib30)). The attacker will try to change the watermarked video or object in the case of tampering but without touching the watermark itself. This poses a huge threat as distinguishing the original untampered video will become difficult. A fragile watermarking will be able to protect the footage against such tampering attacks. In case of a fragile watermarking algorithm, the video footage will become highly sensitive to the modifications and this will make it difficult to perform any kinds of modifications to the video like re-compression. A semi-fragile scheme was also proposed by [Sang and Alam (2008)](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib93).

### 11.9 Medical signals authentication

As mentioned in the previous sections, the advancements of technology have allowed us to explore high-bandwidth communications which led to transmitting data globally ([Singh *et al*., 2017](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib100)). There has become no bounds for devices used for transmission, especially for medical use, such as the Internet, mobiles phones, wireless communications, and so on. There are different kinds of threats that are posed on the communication networks of such kind. The information that is being shared is also highly sensitive, which contains medical records of patients. This demands for higher levels of confidentiality and security. Medical image watermarking can provide a better service which can address these issues ([Acharya *et al*., 2001](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib1); [Coatrieux *et al*., 2006](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib15)). There have been few implementations of such security initiatives. For example, the use of watermarking in tele-medical applications in order to enhance the security of the transmitted sensitive medical data has been implemented. Tele-medicine watermarking will demand more care when the data is encrypted within the medical images as the information itself and its quality cannot be affected. The reliability, confidentiality, and availability are the major concerns of the security in the electronic patient record (EPR) exchange. Modern telecommunication infrastructure supports the possibility of delivering quality health care without the physical presence of medical experts. The integrity of biomedical signals being transmitted through communication channels must be established before their utilization. Investigations have been performed where digital watermarking techniques for signal integrity verification in an electroencephalogram (EEG) monitoring application were performed.
