
(ii) **Strong resiliency**: An ideal IP core watermark should be resilient against removal attack. Therefore, the watermarking constraints should be implanted as a core part of an IP core design. Both single- and triple-phase watermarks contain multi-variable encoding rules where the watermarking constraints are dispersed all over the design (especially in triple-phase watermarking process). Therefore, complete removal of watermarking constraints from the entire IP core design is enormously difficult for an attacker. Thus, substantial resiliency against removal attack is achieved in both watermarking processes.

(iii) **Strong tamper tolerance**: An ideal IP core watermark should have strong tamper tolerance ability, i.e., partial removal should not impact the proof of ownership of an IP core. The watermarking constraints of single- and triple-phase presented in this chapter are distributed using multiple encoding variables. Moreover, in triple-phase watermarking process, watermark constraints are embedded in three different phases of HLS. Additionally, as mentioned earlier, 1st and 3rd phase of triple-phase watermark is independent of other two phases. Thus, strong tamper tolerance ability is achieved in both watermarking process.

(iv) **Low-signature embedding and detection time**: A robust signature may incur complex signature decoding and high-embedding time; therefore, the total runtime to generate a watermarked IP core may increase massively. An ideal IP core watermark should be embedded with lower embedding time. Additionally, signature detection process for a genuine vendor (who has the thorough information of encoding rule and the signature) should be quick. Due to unobvious encoding rules and robust signature embedding process, the embedding time of both single- and triple-phase watermark phase presented in this chapter is low. Furthermore, signature detection time for a genuine entity is also straightforward and speedy.

(v) **Correct functionality**: In the process of embedding watermarking constraints, the correct functionality of an IP core must be preserved. Both single- and triple-phase watermarking processes phase presented in this chapter do not perform any alteration of IP core functionality while embedding watermarking constraints. Thus, functionality of an IP core does not get affected at all.

#### 4.3.5 Possible cases of dishonest claim of IP core/hardware ownership and its resolution

This section presents the possible threat scenarios of the false claim of IP core ownership and its resolution using watermarking process presented in this chapter. Let us assume ‘Alice’ is an IP vendor, designed a watermarked IP core with her signature and sold it to ‘Bob.’ In that case, Bob can claim his IP core ownership using any three possible scenarios as discussed below ([Koushanfar *et al.*, 2005](#Chapter4.html#c04_bib8)):

(i) **Claiming any arbitrary design information as the signature**: After purchasing the watermarked IP core, Bob can reverse engineer the IP core and extract the complete information of the IP core design. Then, he may claim any random design information as his watermarking constraints, thus may claim his ownership of that IP core. Bob’s claim may valid for a single design, but will invalid for other watermarked design. Thus, Bob’s claim is not strong enough to prove his ownership. On the other hand, Alice’s watermarking constraints will be more meaningful and consistent for all watermarked design. In such scenario, the entity having more meaningful and consistent watermark will be awarded to the original owner of the IP core. The presented watermarking in this chapter is capable to resolve this threat.

(ii) **Inserting illegal signature**: Bob can embed his own signature on the top of the watermarked design purchased from Alice and then claim for his IP ownership. In such scenario, Alice can prove her ownership easily because the watermarked design coming from Alice contents only Alice’s watermark; however, the watermarked design coming from Bob contains both Alice’s and Bob’s watermark. The presented watermarking in this chapter is also capable to resolve this threat.

(iii) **Tampering/partial removing of original watermark**: Though it is recommended that watermarking constraints should be an integral part of the design, still sometime it may be possible to identify some design information as the watermarking constraints of the design. In such case, Bob may perform some modification/alteration in the design to remove those watermarks. In such scenario, if the watermarking constraints are independent of each other and distributed throughout the design removal of the complete watermark is extremely difficult. Therefore, ownership of Alice can be proved. Additionally, sometimes tampering of embedded watermarking constraints may force to perform all the pre-synthesis steps from the beginning, which is a costly and time-consuming process. The presented watermarking in this chapter is also capable to resolve this threat.
