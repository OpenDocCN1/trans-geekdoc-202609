
# Security

Security has been one of the major issues that has been around for a very long time, and it is still the main focus of the research. New devices are developed every day with the advancements in technology, and better solutions are resulted in various domains as discussed in Section 2.1. Even with the introduction of many security primitives, attackers are finding ways to hack and perform successful attacks on various secured devices. This has been a major issue since many times. Cryptography has been the solution where the main text message intended for the end-user is encrypted with a key and the end user will decrypt the text with the same key or a different private key. This has been the in practice for a very long time ([Yanambaka *et al.*, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib113)). The first lock and key found was 4,000-year-old, and again now in this digital era; here, the locks and keys are also digital, however the underlying concept remains the same. It may be noted that security, privacy, confidentiality, and intellectual property rights (IPR) are not necessarily the same ([Bhargava *et al.*, 2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib10); Mohanty, [2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib54), [2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib55)) as depicted in [Figure 2.12](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig12). Data security is typically referred to as the confidentiality, availability, and integrity of data. System security often deals with trustful, safe, normal operations of a system (e.g., smart car), etc. Data privacy is defined as the appropriate use of data as agreed for different purposes. System privacy can be making location and functions of unknown system, e.g., location of a smart car should be unknown to adversaries. Confidentiality is assured only if the information is not made available or disclosed to unauthorized individuals, entities, or processes. IPR or ownership rights deals with the preservation of ownership of the owner of a particular hardware or media which is analogous to the copyright or authorship of a book that of a publisher or author.

## 2.2.1 IoT security – attacks and countermeasures

The debate of security and privacy has been going on for a very long time ([Notario, 2016](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib64)). The definitions of privacy and security are still in debate, and the most center-point is the confidentiality part. Both security and privacy are important parts of any system. In the case of an IoT environment, these are paramount as the devices in IoT are continuously connected to a network and online. This leaves much room for the attacker to attack the system in various ways. There has been a lot of research going on in this area ([Asplund and Nadjm-Tehrani, 2016](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib8); [Schinianakis, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib77); [Schneier, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib78); [Sivaraman *et al.*, 2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib91); [Unwala *et al.*, 2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib101)). There are various security attacks that are happening around the world. Number of cybercrime attempts have increased, and so have been the security solutions to fight against them ([Andrews, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib7); [Heartfield *et al.*, 2016](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib34); [Kundu *et al.*, 2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib44); [Mazurczyk *et al.*, 2016](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib52)).

[Figure 2.13](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig13) shows different types of attacks that are possible on an IoT environment ([Nawir *et al.*, 2016](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib60)). There are many threats where unauthorized and unrecognized malicious devices can enter the network and tamper the system both externally and internally. So, therefore, identifying the users that are in the IoT environment is an important prerequisite. Research is being conducted extensively in detecting and preventing the IoT botnets ([Moon *et al.*, 2015](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib59); [Prokofiev *et al.*, 2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib73)). The device should be tamper resistant. There are various types of attacks like side channel attacks and memory exploitations that can be successfully executed by tampering with the device itself ([Li *et al.*, 2011](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib47); [Sengupta and Kundu, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib83)). Hence naturally, the IoT device should be tamper proof, so that the attacker cannot perform such attacks. Software that is used in the IoT devices should be extensively scrutinized before executed on the device. This can lead to unauthorized usage of the device, data loss, or device failure. The network and the device security are of highest important. But increasing the security of the device may also increase the power consumption of the overall module. This should be avoided as the IoT devices are usually battery operated and cannot be used with a continuous power source. This makes it difficult for the development of security primitives for IoT devices. Low-power hardware security modules are being developed for these devices ([Yanambaka *et al.*, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib113)) so that the attacks are mitigated. Communication and device network authentication should also be given highest priority such that unauthorized devices cannot be in the network and the communication channel is not compromised ([Yanambaka *et al.*, 2016a](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib111)). Identity management is also a key factor in this scenario. The identity of the device should be secured such that cloning attacks are prevented.

Hardware trojans are another issue that should be dealt within the IoT environment. [Figure 2.14](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig14) shows various types of hardware trojans that are currently active, the location where they will be introduced, and the phase where there is a chance of insertion ([Mohanty, 2015](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib56)). A hardware trojan is an unwanted and malicious design that is introduced into the main design in different phases of manufacturing by the attacker to render the entire module useless or steal the data from the module. A hardware trojan can be introduced in processor, memory, I/O devices, power supply, and clock grid. Each of these components is the important modules of the device, which play a vital role in the overall functioning of the device. Introduction of a malicious design can prove fatal to the efficient working of device. There are two different types of trojans, one which is always on when the device itself is on and the other where it can be triggered with an event or with a signal. There are many ways in which the hardware trojans can be detected and avoided ([Dupuis *et al.*, 2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib20); [He *et al.*, 2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib33); Sengupta, [2016](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib81), [2017](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib82); [Shen *et al.*, 2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib86); [Xue and Ren, 2018](#Chapter2.html#c02_bib110)). There are many phases where the hardware trojans can be inserted as shown in [Figure 2.14](#Chapter2.html#c02_fig14). Changing functionality, downgrade performance, leak information, and denial of service are some of the effects of hardware trojans. These can prove fatal for many cases where the device cannot function at the full potential in times of need.
