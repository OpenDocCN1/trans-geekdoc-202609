
Broadcast monitoring is another application where watermarking can protect the content that is being distributed. Owner, broadcaster, and the user are the parties that are involved in this scenario. The owner and the protect rights can be identified using the invisible-robust watermarking in this case and the receiver end needs privacy monitoring stations. The monitoring station is responsible for watermark identification and not the owner in this case. Real-time detection also needs to be done without the original media content created being present in the scenario. A small visible watermark showing the ownership or TV broadcasting station logo can also be used.

#### 11.4.7 Content labeling

Most of the new digital cameras have GPS modules integrated in the camera that is used to stamp the photographs with the location. Time and date can also be stamped into the photograph automatically. During the management and retrieval of the data, this additional information is much helpful. A universal product code (UPC) can also be inserted by the watermarking scheme in the camera to identify the source of the media. Invisible or visible watermarking scheme can be used for this purpose. For successful operation of this scheme, the DSP of the camera has to be integrated with the watermarking blocks.

#### 11.4.8 Misappropriation detection

The misappropriate images and the identity of the person that has performed the act can be identified using the invisible-robust watermarks. The buyer of the shared content media, after buying the legit copy of the media, the mis-appropriator, may share the media for free, which can deprive the owner of legitimate licensing revenue. In this case, the watermark of the seller is retrieved and the availability of the seller’s objects are checked. The mis-appropriator might edit the original multimedia objects and distribute them without the payment of royalties. In this case, the owner needs to insert an invisible-robust watermark showing to whom has that object been sold.

#### 11.4.9 Anti-counterfeiting

Controlling the counterfeiting of media is another application of the watermarking techniques. Currency counterfeiting is a major issue that has to be addressed with high priority. With the availability of better performing scanners and printers, and open-source software, currency counterfeiting is becoming an issue every day. Inserting a digital watermark can save in such scenarios. The watermarking algorithms and schemes can be integrated into the printing hardware such that the currency can be saved from counterfeiting.

#### 11.4.10 UAV safety

UAVs have been around for a long time and are used for various applications including military. In some cases, UAVs are used for surveillance where it will have video recording capabilities. The video recorded by the UAV can be used for various purposes, but UAV itself is subject to different kinds of attacks including denial of service, spoofing, and so on. If the attacker gets access to the UAV, the data that is being recorded by the UAV can be tampered with, and the video recordings can be rendered useless. In such scenarios, watermarking the video can to some extent mitigate the threats and protect the integrity of the video recordings.

#### 11.4.11 Medical signals authentication

Medical field is a vast area and privacy protection is of the highest priority as it contains records of many patients. Many cases require confidential media such as videos, photos, or audio recordings, and also documents need to be stored where it may be subject to security threats. In such cases, invisible or visible watermark can be embedded into the media and can safeguard the media or documentation and mitigate the threats.

### 11.5 Desired characteristics of watermarks

This section discusses different characteristics of watermarks from a researcher point of view (Ahmaderaghi *et al*., 2018; [Bender *et al*., 1996](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib7); Bi *et al*., 2013; [Busch *et al*., 1999](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib10); [Kougianos *et al*., 2009](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib46); [Li *et al*., 2016](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib49); [Makbol *et al*., 2016](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib55); [Mohanty *et al*., 2004](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib68); [Swanson *et al*., 1998](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib105)). The general characteristics of digital watermarking—for example, cost, power consumption, tamper-resistance, perceptibility, orthogonality, robustness, unambiguity, universality, and so on—are discussed in this section. Trade-off between three different parameters is inevitable in the case of digital watermarking, data/information capacity, imperceptibility, and robustness. A single watermarking scheme cannot satisfy all three of these trade-off parameters simultaneously.

#### 11.5.1 Perceptibility

The viewer should not be able to see the invisible watermark and the embedded watermark should not degrade the quality of the image. The watermark should be imperceptible in ideal scenarios. But in case of the imperceptible signal, if it goes through a lossy signal, the watermark signal will be removed or lost. A just noticeable distortion (JND) is usually observed by comparing two signals: watermarked and original ([Cox and Miller, 1997](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib18); Delaigle *et al*., [1998](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib23), [2002](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib22); [Kankanhalli *et al*., 1998](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib41)). In case of visible watermark also, the watermark image should not degrade the quality of the original image.

#### 11.5.2 Robustness

A watermark image that is embedded into the original image should be robust to different transformations, which include lossy compressions, digital and analog conversions, and signal distortions. In case of images and video watermarking, the watermark should be resistive to geometric distortions such as cropping, scaling, and translation. The robustness of watermark can be increased by embedding in a significant portion of a signal. But, to make the watermark imperceptible, it should be embedded in perceptually insignificant regions of an image. There are two trade-offs that conflict in designing the algorithm. Ownership establishment need not be the only goal of a robust watermark scheme in an image or a video unless they are implicated legally on the schemes ([Craver *et al*., 1998](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib21)).

#### 11.5.3 Tamper resistance

Signal distortions are not the only type of transformations that a watermarked image is subjected to but also attacks that intend to remove the watermark image from the original image ([Voloshynovskiy *et al*., 2001](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib115); [Wang *et al*., 2018](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib121)). Hence, it is highly important that the invisible and visible watermarking schemes are resistant to such attacks. Detecting any attacks that are performed on the watermark should also be the quality of an invisible watermarking scheme. Using a private watermark, asymmetric encoder–decoder are some of the ways of protecting the image ([Bender *et al*., 1996](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib7); [Cox and Miller, 1997](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib18)).

#### 11.5.4 Bit rate

Watermark bit rate is the amount of information of watermark image that can be encoded into an original image, which is an important aspect in case of public watermarking schemes ([Cox and Miller, 1997](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib18)). Host signal modification and the quantity can vary depending on the application and its type. Some application will require more information to be embedded and in other cases, it requires large amount of data insertions. A watermark algorithm can embed huge data amounts or can be highly robust to different kinds of attacks but they are trade-offs ([Bender *et al*., 1996](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib7); [Swanson *et al*., 1998](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib105)).
