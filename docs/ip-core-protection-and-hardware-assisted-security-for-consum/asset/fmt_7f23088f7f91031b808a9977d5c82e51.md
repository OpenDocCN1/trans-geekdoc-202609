## 4.3.3 Design process of triple-phase watermarked IP core/hardware

In this section, we describe the design process of another watermarked DSP IP core/hardware. We describe a more robust triple-phase watermarking approach presented in Sengupta *et al.* ([2018](#Chapter4.html#c04_bib22)) to design a DSP IP core. This approach does not protect non-HDL IP cores such as standard/pre-defined blocks or symbols that are present in the in-built library of a logic CAD tool. The triple-phase watermarking methodology protects the ownership of an IP core (especially for DSP/multimedia applications). In the process of embedding triple-phase watermark, it is presumed that same type of micro-IPs are available from at least two different third-party vendors to design a macro-IP core (which is a common practice in the industry).

Unlike single-phase watermarking approach (Sengupta *et al.*, 2016), triple-phase watermarking approach ([Sengupta *et al.*, 2018](#Chapter4.html#c04_bib22)) for DSP core embeds watermarking constraints in multiple phases of HLS. More specifically, triple-phase watermarking approach embeds watermarking constraints in the scheduling, hardware allocation, and register allocation phase of HLS. In addition to implanting watermarking constraints in three different phases of HLS, the signature encoding scheme consists of seven different variables, where each variable carries a unique encoding rule. Thus, triple-phase watermarking approach enhances the robustness and minimizes the chance of malicious alteration or removal of the signature. Furthermore, for an adversary, it is also difficult to recognize the synthesis phase and in what way watermark constraints are implanted in the design.

In Sengupta *et al.* ([2018](#Chapter4.html#c04_bib22)), watermarking constraints are embedded during scheduling, hardware allocation, and register allocation phases by modifying three information: (a) ‘Non-critical operations (*μ*[*m*] > 0)’ timing table (*μ*[*m*] = the mobility of an operation), (b) ‘Functional unit allocation’ table, and (c) ‘Register allocation’ table (adopted from Sengupta *et al.*, 2016). As mentioned earlier, this watermarking approach has seven different variables, viz. ‘α’, ‘β’, ‘γ’, ‘i’, ‘I’, ‘T’, and ‘!’. Variable ‘γ’ modifies the timing table of ‘non-critical operations (*μ*[*m*] > 0)’ thus embedding watermarking constraints during scheduling (first phase). Variables ‘α’ and ‘β’ modifies the ‘functional unit allocation’ table thus embedding watermarking constraints during hardware allocation (second phase). Finally, variables ‘i’, ‘I’, ‘T’, ‘!’ modify the ‘register allocation’ table thus embedding watermarking constraints during register allocation (third phase). The signature encoding process of each variable is described below ([Sengupta *et al.*, 2018](#Chapter4.html#c04_bib22)).

### 4.3.3.1 Signature encoding scheme
