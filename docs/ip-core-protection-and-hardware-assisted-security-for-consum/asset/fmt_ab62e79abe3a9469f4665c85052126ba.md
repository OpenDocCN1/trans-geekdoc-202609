
There are frame-to-frame and line-to-line periodicities in the video and image signals. These are not precisely periodic in nature. There will be a little redundancy among the frames and the lines. In the case of the watermarking schemes, where additional content should be stored, this redundancy is taken as an advantage.

##### 11.6.1.3 Power spectral density

The visual signals mostly will have the low-pass frequency spectrum in the longtime average sense or a global sense. In the short-term frequency analysis of these signals in a local sense, when a certain region of the image or frame is selected, they tend to possess all-pass or high-pass frequency spectra. This can be highly useful in the watermark embedding for adding additional information.

##### 11.6.1.4 Properties of color signals

UV or IQ (chrominance) components of any kind of signal will have low energy with a lower bandwidth when compared with a luminance component Y. So when performing the data compression, a coarser quantization is used by subsampling them. This can be considered when developing algorithm for watermarking color and video signals.

#### 11.6.2 Properties of the human visual system

Any watermarking scheme can be called a success when the human visual system (HVS) is also considered and made a best use of it. Various properties of HVS should also be considered when developing watermarking schemes for the images and video ([Delaigle *et al*., 2002](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib22); [Kankanhalli *et al*., 1998](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib41); [Swanson *et al*., 1998](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib105)).

##### 11.6.2.1 Brightness sensitivity

Low-intensity signals are easily perceived by the human eye when different background intensities are present. The surrounding intensity is increased, dark area intensity is reduced when the light areas intensity is increased. When the background is the mean of noise square, it tends to be more visible to the human eye. This is called the Weber’s law. At low intensities, eye has more sensitivity; in case of highest intensity, it has reduced sensitivity.

##### 11.6.2.2 Frequency sensitivity

When psychovisual studies were performed on the HVS it was discovered that it has a general bandpass characteristics with peak sensitivity of 3 or 4 cycles per degree and the reduced sensitivity at higher and lower spatial frequencies. Watermark intensity in the spatial frequency should be inversely proportional to the sensitivity of the corresponding frequencies.

##### 11.6.2.3 Texture sensitivity

Background texture will control the visibility of distortion. When the background is a strong texture, the visibility will be less distorted. When the image block is highly textured, the energy is more evenly distributed among the DCT coefficients. When the image has a flat featured portion, energy is more concentrated in low-frequency components.

##### 11.6.2.4 JND and minimally noticeable distortion

The visual signals are studied and the properties are then analyzed for a short time or spatiotemporally local analysis was performed on the input signals. When the properties are studied, it was observed that they can be used for deriving the perceptual distortion threshold. This is the function of time, space, or frequency. When the distortion by the watermarking algorithm is below the threshold value, signal quality will degrade. A critical distortion profile is expressed by the threshold as JND. Similarly, minimally noticeable distortion (MND) defines a supra-threshold generalization of it, which is not transparent, but still perceptually optimum.

#### 11.6.3 How much watermark signal to add and where?

Watermarking an object is a communication process where the watermark signal should be inserted reliably into the host, the media object ([Ramkumar, 2000](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib88); [Ramkumar and Akansu, 2001](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib89); [Servette *et al*., 1998](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib98); [Sugihara, 2001](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib104)). It was already proven that the communication is reliably possible theoretically but when the information rate does not go beyond the threshold. The threshold value in this scenario is called the channel capacity. The information theory can be used here to derive the rules which will be helpful in deciding the strength of the watermark used and the location on the original media object.

The watermark should be imperceptible and SNR is also highly limited. Communication will be reliable with the increase in bandwidth compensation mechanisms for a poor SNR. So when the watermarking process is being performed, a number of transform domain coefficients should be high and are easy to exploit for hiding information in the object media. An application for the spread spectrum communications can be considered to be digital watermarking. The Shannon limit may be considered by applying the error control codes. If necessary, more robust error correction methods may also be used here. This discusses how much watermark strength to be added to an image or media object. For an answer to the question, where to embed, the watermark should be embedded in a place where the local noise variance is small compared to the threshold value.

#### 11.6.4 Spread spectrum communications

From the discussion until now, it has become more clear that the watermark should not be embedded into a place that is perpetually insignificant region on the image. A watermark that is embedded into a high-frequency domain can be removed using a little degradation of the quality directly or indirectly. Then the problem here is how a watermark can be embedded into the most significant part of the image without noticeable alterations to the image quality. Small changes can be made and spectral coefficient can be altered but the small changes are susceptible to high amounts of noise.

To avoid this issue, the frequency domain of the media object is considered as a communication channel and the watermark is the signal that is transmitted through it. The signal distortions and the attacks are considered as noise that affects the signal. So in this way, a watermarking can be considered as an application for spread spectrum communication ([Sequeira and Kundur, 2001](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib97)). In this case of a spread spectrum communications, a narrow band signal is transmitted over a broad bandwidth so that the signal energy is imperceptible. In the same manner, a watermark is spread over a large image such that it is imperceptible. Location of the watermark is known to the watermark detection systems.
