
A digital watermark which uses both the ECG and the patient demographic text as two-level watermarks was proposed by [Annadurai *et al*. (2012)](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib5). In the insertion process, `DWT` is applied to the object, and it is decomposed into three sub-bands. After that the texture matrix will be calculated for each of the sub-bands. The wavelet coefficients are then selected for the watermarks which are used as threshold values. Authenticating the watermarked object is the main goal of this watermarking scheme. A wavelet spread-spectrum watermarking mechanism for both text and image is presented by [Singh *et al*. (2015)](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib102). The watermark size is varied and the gain factor is obtained in this scenario for the experimental results. `BCH` codes are used for an increased robustness.

Researchers across the globe are using the hybrid watermarking schemes to improve the performance of the watermarking systems ([Fatima and Tuptewar, 2016](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib28); [Vo *et al*., 2017](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib114)). `DCT` and `DWT` can be used to embed the watermark into an image and the watermark can be selected at random. After a third level of decomposition, a `DCT` can be applied on them. A watermark with a zero mean and one variance is inserted in this process. The extraction process does not require the original image to be present in the memory.

Machine learning can also be used for the watermarking techniques. There are many variants of watermarking techniques that use machine learning and offer more imperceptibility and security compared to the other but demand high computational power. A blind watermarking technique which uses the multiwavelet and support vector machine (`SVM`) is presented by [Peng *et al*. (2010)](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib85). Initially the multiwavelet is performed on the each of the blocks of image. After this stage is complete, the lowest frequency sub-band is selected and the watermark is embedded into that using modulation. The watermark information contains a reference to the owner and the signature of binary logo image. The reference information of the owner will be helpful in training the `SVM` when extracting the watermark.

### 11.10 Side-channel information leakage attacks and countermeasures

Watermarking and cryptography hardware modules have an advantage of the low-power consumption along with a high performance and throughput. They can also be designed at a low cost per module and are integrated into different applications with ease. Besides these advantages, there are also some disadvantages to the hardware modules. They are made out of CMOS technology and are susceptible to various kinds of attacks such as side-channel attacks. The side-channel attacks collect the information from the hardware modules like the hardware watermarking chip directly unlike the software attacks.

During World War II, an engineer observed that the oscilloscope signal had a spike in it when a key was pressed in the secure typewriter Model 131-B2 at Bell Labs in 1943 ([Mohanty, 2015](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib67)). Based on that the first publicly known side-channel attack was reported in 1965. Any attack that uses the physical implementation of cryptosystem and gains information is called a side-channel attack. There are two types of attacks that are possible on a crypto system: nondestructive and destructive. The target of the first type is analyzing the target module and gain information from it without destroying or tampering the device itself. Side-channel attacks are included in this category of attacks. Different side-channel attacks are presented in [Figure 11.24](#Chapter11.html#c11_fig24) (Parameswaran, 2018). This section discusses in detail the `CPA` and `DPA` attacks on the modules. There are also fault attacks, acoustic noise attacks, elapsed time attacks, EM radiation attacks, and so on, which exploit the advantages of different components of a device, like the time taken to complete a process, injecting faults into the device algorithms, and so on.

#### 11.10.2 Side-channel analysis attacks

Two major side-channel analysis attacks are considered in this section, `CPA` and `DPA` ([Jayasinghe *et al*., 2015](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib38)). `CPA` is the analysis of relationship between the cipher-text and the plain-text and it’s correlation as well as the instantaneous power consumption of the cryptographic device. When compared to `DPA`, `CPA` is a more effective attacking method. `DPA` uses the relation between the power and the data. Using that `DPA` will try to look at the difference of the category averages for all the keys. As it uses the averages, more power correlation data is needed for successfully launching this attack. So it consumes more time and is less efficient compared to the `CPA`. [Figure 11.26](#Chapter11.html#c11_fig26) presents a big picture of `DPA` and `CPA` ([Zhang and Shi, 2011](#Chapter11.html#c11_bib125)).

A `DPA` attack is a side-channel attack which statistically analyzes the consumed power by the respective module in a cryptosystem. The module can be a watermarking chip or a cryptographic processor which performs encryption and decryption. [Figure 11.27](#Chapter11.html#c11_fig27) shows the overall process of `DPA` (Parameswaran, 2018). The biases varying power consumption is taken. Here, the microcontroller is monitored and the power analysis is performed when various operations are performed on the chip, which involve the secret keys. Signal processing and error correction properties are necessary for performing `DPA`, which are used for the extraction of secrets from the power consumption measurements. There is also noise that comes with the measurements so it must be filtered before the analysis is performed. A secret key can be obtained by the attacker by analyzing the power from the cryptographic operations on the chip using `DPA`.

A `CPA` uses the relation between the data and the power as mentioned before. [Figure 11.28](#Chapter11.html#c11_fig28) shows the overall picture of the `CPA` (Parameswaran, 2018). This uses a correlation between the guesses of the keys. Hence, low power is required compared to the `DPA`. `CPA` is used to find a key that is stored in memory of the victim device. Launching a `CPA` attack involves the following four steps.

*   The power consumption model of the victim device should be noted.

*   The victim should encrypt different files of plain text.

*   Record the trace of the power consumption on the victim device.
