# 16.2 Edge-BA AUTHENT AND AUTHORIZATION FRAMEWORK

Authentication and authorization are critical security measures that can help prevent various attacks, such as denied of service attacks [35]. IoT system architectures are anticipated to rely upon authentication and authorization mechanisms to ensure end-to-end security, but this is extremely difficult to achieve for various reasons. As an illustration, mutual authentication is complicated to set up due to the difficulty of establishing a direct trust. Moreover, numerous authentication methods, such as those utilizing digital signatures, are unsuitable for end devices in IoT. For example, Elseyed et al. (2023) proposed an illustration focusing on EGC servers as a proxy for authentication and authorization purposes [36]. In this concept, multi-factor authentication and authorization are implemented via control system pathways and an on-demand reputation monitoring service is used to confirm the identities of identities. To verify the information you know (such as a token or password), what you have, and/or what you are (such as biometrics), two-factor authentication often employs more than two separate authentication techniques. An IoT user sends an inquiry comprising physiological and identification data to the gateway during the authentication and authorization procedure. The gateway proceeds to authenticate the user and determine their level of authorization.

Elseyed et al. (2023) proposed a novel control system gateway that acts as a proxy for identity surveillance services, multiple authentication methods and authorization, or, multi-factor certification. Verification forms various aspects of identity, including knowledge (e.g. username/password and security questions), possession (such as a token, a key, a certificate, or a smart fob), and/or-biometric metrics. In the authentication and authorization process, the IoT user submits a request along their biometric and identity information to the gateway, which then authenticates the user and determines their authorization level [36]. To mutually authenticate an IoT user and an IoT device, Sha et al.'s (2020) research suggests a solution that leverages an edge device as an intermediate [38].
