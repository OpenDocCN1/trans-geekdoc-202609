Industry 4.0 aims to automate and streamline data processing across most industries. Healthcare uses Internet of Things (IoT) applications for fitness tracking. Technology collects, processes, and acts on health, and IoT is one of the leading technologies in this domain. Internet technology allows us to access data anywhere, and IoT has set a new bar for optimality by itself [1, 2]. As shown in Figure 9.1, IoT enables communication between integrated electronic devices to make human life more secure and comfortable.

IoT can succeed if the necessary hardware, software, and infrastructure are found. IoT requires a compatible configuration model; however, all configurations might not be valid. IoT academics and developers look for flexible answers to IoT’s diversity. Certain applications require real-time data processing with high definition in the shortest amount of time, and cloud computing does not fit into these applications. ### 9.1.2 Internet of Health Things (IoHT)

The introduction of IoT in healthcare encompasses pharmaceuticals, microbiology, and biomedical engineering through the integration of emerging technologies as AI, image processing, and big data, among others [12]. Sensors are required to drive physical objects through the internet in smart health, smart home, smart industrial, smart building, smart electrical, and smart traffic management.
