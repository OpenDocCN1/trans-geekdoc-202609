# 14.1 INTRODUCTION

The utilization of IoT in diverse industrial fields has undergoing rapid and expo-
ential growth over recent years. The impact of IoT applications, such as smart urban
systems, advanced healthcare solutions, intelligent transportation networks, environ-
tacking, wearable technology, the Industrial Internet, and more, has a signifi-
cance on the everyday lives of individuals [1]. Nevertheless, the widespread
adoption of IoT introduces numerous security and trust-related hurdles. Frequently
positioned in distant locales, IoT devices face susceptibility to data tampering,
resulting in the dissemination of deceptive data, incongruities, and potential
to privacy and security. Even if vouched for other layers of the IoT ecosystem,
data sourced from various devices might harbor malicious intent and remain untrust-
worthy. To maintain security and trust criteria such as authentication, access control,
diversity, integrity, and confidentiality, it is necessary to transform information into
a secure and reliable format. Relying on conventional security measures
like cryptographic methods such as RSA, AES, and ECC [3] fails short of ensuring
data integrity on this scale, thereby significantly impacting the potential future
integration of IoT. It is important to highlight that within this context, primary access
verification is typically carried out by a centralized entity, which is susceptible to a
single point of failure.

[4], offers a shared, decentralized ledger. It has already found extensive\application in cryptocurrency dealings as a pivotal technology for
fostering further advancements across diverse Internet of things (IoT) contexts [5]. As
dist, mutable, and tamper-proof ledger database, blockchain can tackle the
pressing security concerns within the realm of IoT, primarily focusing on ensuring data
