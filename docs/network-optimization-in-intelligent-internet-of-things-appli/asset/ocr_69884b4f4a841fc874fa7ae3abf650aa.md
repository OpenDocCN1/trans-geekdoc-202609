# 5.1.2 Features of AVs

Automated Vehicles have the following advantages:

+   - Automated Emergency Braking System (AEBS): The system is designed to detect pedestrians, cyclists, and other vehicles on the road and reduce the risk of collisions in busy urban areas.
- Night Vision Capability: AVs have night vision cameras that detect obstacles in low light conditions, keeping the passengers safe.
- Automated Parking: It means that the car can navigate tight spaces and can do parallel parking easily. Once the car is in position, it automatically adjusts its speed and steering to ensure it parks safely and accurately.
- Street Sign Recognition: It is a software program that enables it to identify road signals and sensor data.
- Adaptive Cruise Control: ACC helps the car to maintain a proper distance from the vehicle which will be moving in front of it. The sensor detects when the car slows down and then adjusts its speed according to it. It prevents rear-end collisions and sudden stops or starts.

# 5.1.3 Current State of Autonomous Car Industry

The Society of Automotive Engineers classified the vehicles that will enable us to understand the level of AV technology fully. The following classifications have as it stands: Level 0 to Level 5 phases illustrate the abilities of entirely and partially autonomous cars. These are:

+   - Level 0: No Automation: In this case, the driver is solely responsible for controlling the car.
- Level 1: Driver Assistance: It is the most basic grade of assistance. The driver receives aid with acceleration and steering. An excellent example of this is adaptive cruise control.
