## 16.2.2 ARCHITECTURE AT EDGE LAYER

EGC brings forth a platform to propose and devise comprehensive and innovative sanctuary strategies for IoT applications. Such strategies are premeditated to meet the security requirements of endpoint devices to minimize the security burden placed on these devices by shifting the protection to the layer. By keeping safety apparatuses EGC, it is possible to reduce the security risks associated with the limited resources available on IoT devices. Edge-based architecture consists of many security architectures device-centric, user and terminal security.

Device-centric: A device-oriented EGC security strategy for IoT devices is essential. Billions of IoT are tangible and physical objects that are embedded in the world and provide information, as well deciding to govern the physical realm. While user-centric IoT security focuses on protecting devices, device-centric IoT security tailors security measures to each specific device, considering factors such as resources, the sensitivity of the data it collects, and the potential impact of any actions. Moreover, device security can also be taken into account. Two notable

## 16.2. Edge Computing-Based Intrusion Detection System

A multitude of IoT devices were breached by attackers in 2016 and utilized to execute a Distributed Denial of Service(DDoS) assault on multiple Domain Name System (DNS) servers by Dye Inc. This resulted in a considerable loss due to the disruption of internet connectivity across a large region. The advanced system could have detected the DDoS attack early and minimized the resulting damage. Edge-based intrusion detection systems demonstrate various benefits with the additional information available at the edge [33]. Figure 16.4 illustrates a conceptual design for such a system. This design incorporates a distributed traffic monitoring service that gathers live traffic data. Intrusion detection algorithms are executed on each edge device to analyse the traffic. Additionally, traffic information from various edge devices is examined to detect collaboration intrusion. The outcomes of finding procedure are enforced by the network managers installed at the edge endpoints.

## 16.2 Edge-BA AUTH AND AUTHORIZATION FRAMEWORK

Authentication and authorization are critical security measures that can help prevent various attacks, such as denied of service attacks [35]. IoT system architectures are anticipated to rely upon authentication and authorization mechanisms to ensure end-to-end security, but this is extremely difficult to achieve for various reasons. As an illustration, mutual authentication is complicated to set up due to the difficulty of establishing a direct trust. Moreover, numerous authentication methods, such as those utilizing digital signatures, are unsuitable for end devices in IoT. For example, Elseyed et al. (2023) proposed an illustration focusing on EGC servers as a proxy for authentication and authorization purposes [36]. In this concept, multi-factor authentication and authorization are implemented via control system pathways and an on-demand reputation monitoring service is used to confirm the identities of identities. To verify the information you know (such as a token or password), what you have, and/or what you are (such as biometrics), two-factor authentication often employs more than two separate authentication techniques. An IoT user sends an inquiry comprising physiological and identification data to the gateway during the authentication and authorization procedure. The gateway proceeds to authenticate the user and determine their level of authorization.

Elseyed et al. (2023) proposed a novel control system gateway that acts as a proxy for identity surveillance services, multiple authentication methods and authorization, or, multi-factor certification. Verification forms various aspects of identity, including knowledge (e.g. username/password and security questions), possession (such as a token, a key, a certificate, or a smart fob), and/or-biometric metrics. To mutually authenticate an IoT user and an IoT device, Sha et al.'s (2020) research suggests a solution that leverages an edge device as an intermediate [38].
