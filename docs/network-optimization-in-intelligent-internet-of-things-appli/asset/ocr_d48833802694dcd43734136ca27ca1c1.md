# 5.2 FUTURE SCOPE

The we experience mobility is going to shift, thanks to AD. Driving will become simpler, less hazardous, and more satisfying as the outcome. Driving time can now be used to drive a book, talk on a cell phone, or do something productive. Hence, work productivity will increase. Since workers work from autonomous cars, most work will be done even if they live far from their offices. Hence, it will attract more people to the suburbs and rural areas. AD will improve mobility for elderly drivers and youngsters [12].

It can be clearly observed that AD will generate value for the auto industry. In particular situations, most cars will achieve SAE Level 4 or driverless control. Consequently, ADAS and AD could generate $300 billion and $400 billion in the passenger car market by 2035, based on a McKinsey consumer survey.

Based on different degrees of innovation availability, user uptake, and regulatory support, McKinsey additionally generated data on sales for autonomous passenger cars.

The above scenario projects how, in 2030, 4% of new passenger cars are installed with L3+ AD function, and this figure will increase to 17% in 2035 [13].

Furthermore, consumers will benefit from employing AD systems in a variety of ways, including increased safety, and are more open to the ideas. A quarter of people stated they would choose advanced AD when they bought their next car. The research clearly indicates the mobility poll by McKinsey 2021 of more than 25,000 people clearly the pricing inclinations. Twenty percent of poll participants indicated they were willing to purchase ADAS features via subscription, while the remaining 30% claimed they would pay for each time they used a function.
