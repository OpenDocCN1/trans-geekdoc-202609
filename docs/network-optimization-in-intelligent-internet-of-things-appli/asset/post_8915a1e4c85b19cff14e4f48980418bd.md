## 7.2.3 DATA OPTIMIZATION

To ensure efficient resource usage, lessen network congestion, improve system performance, efficient data optimization is essential in IoT networks. The exponential expansion of data creation brought on by the spread of IoT devices prevents management, processing, and analytical issues for network operators. Therefore, to reduce the amount of data carried over the network and enhance overall performance, effective data optimization techniques are needed. Recent years have substantially increased research in energy-efficient sensory data collection. Various strategies have been suggested to do this. One such is a two-step process that entails cluster construction and data collection [6]. IoT nodes take place in selecting a cluster head in the second stage and send it to the cluster process which then gets in touch with the base station. However, the cluster creation process becomes resource- and time-intensive in scenarios with frequent IoT node movement or large-scale IoT networks. Data compression is the focus of a different strategy to cut down on data transmission requirements. However, these techniques could cause data distortion and might not be appropriate when the network has a high data flow [7].

## 7.3.1 PARTICLE SWARM OPTIMIZATION (PSO)

PSO can be implemented in IoT networks to optimize data routing, and resource allocation. For instance, the path selection in data routing can be optimized using PSO. This is important for reducing energy consumption and maximising network lifespan. PSO can also be employed to optimize the energy usage of IoT devices by dynamically altering the duty cycle of the devices based on the network traffic and the device's battery level. With PSO, IoT networks can achieve higher performance, scalability, and efficiency. PSO is a flexible and successful optimization method for the IoT network since it permits the optimization of various parameters. It can assist the problems faced by IoT devices, such as limited processing power, memory, and storage capacity. PSO has the potential to greatly enhance IoT network performance, making them more dependable and long-lasting [8].

A swarm of particles traveling a multidimensional search space is what the Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm mimics. Each particle stands for a possible answer. The algorithm iteratively adjusts the particles' velocities and positions based on the particles' individual and collective best positions at that. The influence of the particle's optimum positions on its movement is governed by the cognitive and social learning ($c1$ and $c2$). Exploration and exploitation are balanced according to the inertia weight ($w$). The particles eventually arrive at the best outcome as they explore and update the search space. When a predefined termination condition is met, usually after a predetermined number of iterations, the algorithm ends.

# Algorithm: Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)

+   1. Initialize swarm of particles with arbitrary positions and velocities as $X(t)$ and $V(t)$, respectively.
2. Position and Velocity of the particle is given by $X(t)=(x(t),y(t))$ and $V(t)=(v_x(t),v_y(t))$, respectively.
3. Repeat until the termination condition is met:
Update each particle's velocity:
$V(t+1) = \omega V(t) + c_1 \cdot u_1(P_{best}-X(t)) + c_2 \cdot u_2(P_{gbest}-X(t))$
where: $u_1(t)$ is the random number which takes a value between 0 and 1.
$u_2(t)$ is the random number which takes a value between 0 and 1.
Update particle's position: $X(t+1)=X(t)+V(t+1)$, where $X(t)$ denote the position of particle $i$ in the search space at time step $t$; unless otherwise, $t$ denotes discrete time steps. The position of the particle is changed adding a velocity, $V(t)$, to the current position.
Update the best position and fitness value for each particle if necessary.
4. Return the best solution found.
