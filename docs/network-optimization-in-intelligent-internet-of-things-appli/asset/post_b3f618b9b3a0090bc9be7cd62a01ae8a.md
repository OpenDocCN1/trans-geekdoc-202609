## 3.3.1.3 Automatic Number Plate Recognition (ANPR)

ANPR, also known as Automatic Number Plate Recognition, is an image processing technology used to capture images and extract license plate information from them. This technology converts the extracted license plate into machine-readable formats, such as text strings, making it easier to process and store in a database. ANPR is widely used for various purposes, including vehicle identification, monitoring, and enforcement. By automating the recognition and tracking of license plate data, ANPR technology originated in 1976 when it was invented by the UK police. The first practical implementation of ANPR was a pilot system around 1979, specifically for monitoring traffic. Initially, ANPR was primarily used in policing and security applications. However, over time, it has progressed and changed along with other Information and Communications Technologies (ICTs). As a result, there has been a significant surge in new methods and groundbreaking applications of ANPR across various research fields.

## 3.3.1.4 Dedicated Short-Range Communication (DSRC)

DSRC stands for Dedicated Short-Range Communications. It is a wireless communication technology designed for short-range communication between vehicles and roadside infrastructure. DSRC operates in the 5.9 GHz frequency band and is specifically developed for ITS applications. DSRC enables vehicles and infrastructure to exchange data and information in real-time, facilitating communication for purposes such as vehicle safety, traffic management, and cooperative driving. It supports high-speed, low-latency communication, making it suitable for applications that require quick response times. Some key features and applications of DSRC include:

- Vehicle-to-Vehicle (V2V) Communication: By utilizing DSRC technology, vehicles can communicate their current speed, acceleration, and braking information to nearby vehicles. This enables cooperative driving and safety applications as collision avoidance and emergency vehicle warning systems.

- Vehicle-to-Infrastructure (V2I) Communication: DSRC enables vehicles to communicate with roadside infrastructure such as traffic signals, toll booths, and parking facilities. This facilitates applications like traffic management, collection, and providing guidance systems.

- Vehicle-to-Pedestrian (V2P) Communication: DSRC can also support communication between vehicles and pedestrians, enhancing safety by providing warnings and alerts to pedestrians in proximity to vehicles.

- Signal Phase and Timing (SPAT) Broadcast: DSRC can transmit real-time signal phase and timing information, allowing vehicles to receive data about traffic signal status. This can be used for applications optimizing traffic flow and providing driver assistance.

Overall, DSRC technology plays a significant role in enabling connected vehicle systems and improving transportation safety and efficiency. It has been widely used and tested in various pilot projects and research initiatives worldwide.
