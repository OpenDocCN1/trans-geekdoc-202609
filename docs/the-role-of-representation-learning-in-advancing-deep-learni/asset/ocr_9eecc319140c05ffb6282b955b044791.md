The sliding-window parser employs a two-step process to select nodes. First, it identifies the most relevant level in the tree. Starting at the highest level, it computes attention scores between each node and a query vector and selects the node with the highest score. It then descends the tree, level by level, computing attention scores between the children of the previously selected node and the query vector and selecting the best-scoring child. Once the attention score of the highest scoring child is lower than that of the parent, the level of the parent is selected. Once the level is identified, the parser expands its search area to encompass the children of both the best-scoring node and its one-hop neighbors. This broader scope helps to include semantically related nodes that may have been separated during the construction of the tree.
