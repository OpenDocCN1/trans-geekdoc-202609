To optimize memory usage and processing efficiency, the audio signals are converted to float32 format, with amplitude values normalized to the range 0.0 to 1.0. The processed data is then divided into training, validation, and test sets, consisting of 10k, 1k, and 1k samples, respectively.

**Video.** We employ the Adobe240 dataset [254], comprising 133 high-frame-rate videos captured at a resolution of 720p and a frame rate of 240 frames per second. Each video has a duration ranging from 1 to 10 seconds. For data pre-processing, the videos are divided into 25-frame segments, and any portrait-oriented videos are rotated to landscape orientation. Of the 133 videos, the first 100 are designated for training, 16 for validation, and 17 for testing. Following the approach in [178], we apply data augmentation by randomly cropping 512 × 512 patches. During 7-skip frame and x4 spatial interpolation, we use 4 input frames, each separated by 7 frames, providing a total of 25 frames as ground truth. Each spatial axis is further downscaled by a factor of x4.

**LiDAR.** To illustrate the adaptability of our framework, we further train our model on LiDAR data from the KITTI-360 dataset [164]. We disregard the global scene context and consider each vehicle at each timeframe as an independent sample. Following the methodology in [298], we define training, validation, and test splits of 20,000, 2,500, and 2,500 samples, respectively, by temporally down-sampling frames in the driving sequences. The point clouds are then converted into distance maps with a resolution of 64 × 1024. This data was captured using a Velodyne HDL-64E sensor, which provides a vertical field of view (FoV) of 26.8°, spanning from -24.8° to +2°, and a horizontal FoV of 360°. For consistency, the distance map is normalized based on the sensor’s maximum range of 120 meters.

**Motivation for downsampling.** Each of our dataset is mod 12 divisible. We motivate this to have the possibility to further downsample our data by a factor of x2, x3 and x4. Many Single Image Super Resolution Benchmarks degradations are compared in that range [65, 163, 124]. However, we did not test 8x degradation.
