Similar to GSVM, GALI is able to move the probability mass to higher efficiencies, with lower efficiencies being much less likely than with UF.

They show that the quality of the allocations produced by GALI is much more consistent than the ones produced by UF. This holds regardless of the number of initial queries. The fewer initial bundles are available, the bigger the difference in efficiency gets. Compared to the uniform sampling approach, GALI also uses on average fewer queries to produce its allocation. More specifically, GALI uses on average 16% fewer queries in GSVM and 18% fewer queries in LSVM compared to UF. GALI also performs better in the worst case. The minimal achieved efficiency for GALI with 20 initial queries on GSVM is 91.7% while UF produced an allocation with an efficiency of only 84.11%. These findings underline the advantage of GALI over UF in a practical setting, where consistent performance and a lower number of requested queries bring a large benefit.
