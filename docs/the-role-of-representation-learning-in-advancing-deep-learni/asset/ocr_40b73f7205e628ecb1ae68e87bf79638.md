170, 157, 275, 47, 215], and LiDAR data [149, 298, 116]. In the field of Single Image Super-Resolution (SISR), the integration of Vision Transformers (ViT) [67] and their variants [172], often combined with convolutional layers, has emerged as the predominant approach. For audio data, architectures based on U-Net [223, 170], enhanced with self-attention mechanisms and convolutional layers, or employing generative adversarial networks (GANs) [47], are commonly used.

Despite the success of these methods, there has been some research on the application of INRs for SISR tasks. Some existing INR-based approaches generate higher-resolution outputs by directly sampling from the fitted INR at a higher resolution [237, 226, 246]. However, this straightforward upsampling often results in only marginal improvements over traditional interpolation methods due to the lack of explicit modeling of high-frequency details. Other works [306, 249] use more advanced methods to refine the INR before sampling. Generally, INRs have gained more traction in more complex super-resolution settings such as for medical data [87, 77] or video [178, 46, 177].

# 5.2.2 Implicit Neural Representations

INRs have recently gained attention as a powerful technique for data representation across diverse modalities. Unlike traditional methods that rely on discrete data points, INRs model data as continuous functions parameterized by neural networks. This approach has been successfully applied to various domains, including images [245, 73, 16, 71, 72], manifold data [73, 72], audio [245, 72, 154], 3D shapes [245, 207, 188, 179, 96, 97], physical phenomena [245], and entire 3D scenes [190]. Our research extends this methodology by applying INRs to images, audio, 3D shapes, and manifold data, thereby contributing to the growing body of work in this area.

Traditionally, INRs use simple fully connected neural networks composed of multiple linear layers [245, 73, 16, 71, 72, 154, 207, 189]. However, recent advancements have introduced a decoupled approach that separates the fitting of the INR from its application in downstream tasks. This is achieved by encoding the network weights into a latent representation, which can then be utilized for various deep learning applications [73, 16, 179]. Following the methodology proposed in [73], we incorporate encoded weight representations into our super-resolution framework. This enables more efficient processing and improved performance, as the model can leverage the rich information encapsulated in the continuous representations while remaining adaptable across different data modalities.
