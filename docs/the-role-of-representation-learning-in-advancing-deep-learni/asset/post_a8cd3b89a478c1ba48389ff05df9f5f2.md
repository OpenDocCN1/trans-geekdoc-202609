
We further validate DAVA on three real-world datasets. CelebA [174] is a dataset commonly used in the disentanglement literature. It comprises over 200,000 images of faces of celebrities. As shown in Figure 4.14, DAVA achieves a competitive FID while still having a comparatively high PIPE metric score. This means that DAVA accomplishes a similar visual fidelity of its generated samples as the best baseline methods while better disentangling its latent space. ## 4.6 Conclusions

In this chapter, we presented a new way to understand and formalize disentangled representations from an unsupervised point of view. The main idea is that the distribution of reconstructed samples of a disentangled VAE should be similar to the distribution of generated samples. We quantify this notion in a new unsupervised disentanglement metric called PIPE metric for evaluating disentangled representations. Without access to ground truth, PIPE provides results consistent with supervised metrics. Furthermore, we apply the same idea to DAVA, a novel VAE training procedure. DAVA can self-tune its regularization strength and produces results competitive with existing models that require more dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning. Robust unsupervised disentanglement performance is an important requirement for practical applicability. With DAVA, we provide a method that can be used by practitioners with relatively low computational requirements.

## Domain-Agnostic Super-Resolution

## Abstract

Super-resolution techniques have made remarkable progress in recent years, especially in the vision domain. However, extending these advances to other data modalities has been challenging due to the need for specialized architectures tailored to each domain. In this chapter, we introduce FunctaFormer, a novel domain-agnostic super-resolution method capable of upscaling any arbitrary data type. Our approach leverages Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) to model data as continuous functions, providing a unified framework across different modalities. FunctaFormer obtains competitive performance across various domains, including images, audio, videos, 3D shapes, manifold data, and LiDAR scans.

## 5.1 Introduction

Super-resolution (SR) aims to reconstruct a high-resolution output from a given low-resolution input. While the specific notion of “resolution” varies across domains, such as pixel count in images, frame rate in videos, sampling frequency in audio signals, grid or mesh resolution in 3D shapes, measurement spacing on manifolds, or point density in LiDAR scans, the fundamental goal remains the same: enhancing the level of detail beyond what is present in the input data.

![](img/c742333bcb21933724ca00794dbfb657_75_0.png)

Figure 5.1: Samples of 4x super-resolution with FunctaFormer on low-resolution input (top) resulting in upsampled output (bottom). By using INRs, FunctaFormer can upsample any data modality (shown here as 2D images, 1D audio waveforms, 3D shapes, polar coordinates for manifold data, 2+1D videos, and LiDAR point clouds).

At first glance, these diverse data modalities, ranging from 1D to 3D spaces and represented in various forms, appear inherently different. This diversity poses a challenge for developing a unified SR approach applicable across modalities. However, Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) offer a promising solution by modeling data as continuous functions parameterized by a neural network. In an INR, the input to the network consists of coordinates—such as time, Cartesian coordinates, or polar coordinates—and the output is the corresponding feature value at that point. For instance, in the case of an image, the network outputs pixel color values for given spatial positions.

By leveraging INRs, the SR task can be reframed as transforming the INR fitted on low-resolution data to generate high-resolution output during inference. This perspective abstracts the SR problem away from the specifics of any particular data modality, allowing for a unified architecture that is adaptable to any data modality.

Previous work has demonstrated the potential of INRs for SR by sampling the fitted INR at a higher resolution than the training data [226, 237]. However, this approach yields only marginal improvements over basic interpolation techniques, primarily because standard INRs lack explicit spatial context in their representations.

To address this limitation, we leverage Spatial Functa [16], an approach that augments INRs with spatial information. Spatial Functa compute individual INR representations for spatially arranged subsets of the input data. These localized representations are then fed as tokens into a transformer backbone, which captures spatial dependencies and interactions between different regions. The transformer then produces a residual that is applied to the original INR, enhancing its capacity to generate high-quality, high-resolution outputs.

Our contributions can be summarized as follows:

- We propose FunctaFormer, a novel domain-agnostic super-resolution architecture that leverages Spatial Functa and transformer models to achieve competitive performance across multiple data modalities, including images, audio, videos, 3D shapes, manifold data, and LiDAR scans.
- We conduct extensive ablation studies to optimize the sampling process of high-resolution outputs from Spatial Functa, resulting in efficient SR performance.
- We explore various configurations of the transformer backbone, particularly focusing on the optimal attention window size for the SR task in conjunction with the chosen Spatial Functa representations.

The codebase is made publicly available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/functaformer/.

## 5.2 Related Work

### 5.2.1 Single Data Super Resolution

Neural networks are commonly used to upscale low-resolution data points, typically operating on discrete array representations. This technique has been applied across various domains, including images [65, 163, 313, 308, 293, 277], 3D shapes [247, 202, 267], video [178, 195, 312, 129, 292, 46], audio [148, 170, 157, 275, 47, 215], and LiDAR data [149, 298, 116]. In the field of Single Image Super-Resolution (SISR), the integration of Vision Transformers (ViT) [67] and their variants [172], often combined with convolutional layers, has emerged as the predominant approach. For audio data, architectures based on U-Net [223, 170], enhanced with self-attention mechanisms and convolutional layers, or employing generative adversarial networks (GANs) [47], are commonly used.

Despite the success of these methods, there has been some research on the application of INRs for SISR tasks. Some existing INR-based approaches generate higher-resolution outputs by directly sampling from the fitted INR at a higher resolution [237, 226, 246]. However, this straightforward upsampling often results in only marginal improvements over traditional interpolation methods due to the lack of explicit modeling of high-frequency details. Other works [306, 249] use more advanced methods to refine the INR before sampling. Generally, INRs have gained more traction in more complex super-resolution settings such as for medical data [87, 77] or video [178, 46, 177].

### 5.2.2 Implicit Neural Representations

INRs have recently gained attention as a powerful technique for data representation across diverse modalities. Unlike traditional methods that rely on discrete data points, INRs model data as continuous functions parameterized by neural networks. This approach has been successfully applied to various domains, including images [245, 73, 16, 71, 72], manifold data [73, 72], audio [245, 72, 154], 3D shapes [245, 207, 188, 179, 96, 97], physical phenomena [245], and entire 3D scenes [190]. Our research extends this methodology by applying INRs to images, audio, 3D shapes, and manifold data, thereby contributing to the growing body of work in this area.

Traditionally, INRs use simple fully connected neural networks composed of multiple linear layers [245, 73, 16, 71, 72, 154, 207, 189]. However, recent advancements have introduced a decoupled approach that separates the fitting of the INR from its application in downstream tasks. This is achieved by encoding the network weights into a latent representation, which can then be utilized for various deep learning applications [73, 16, 179]. Following the methodology proposed in [73], we incorporate encoded weight representations into our super-resolution framework. This enables more efficient processing and improved performance, as the model can leverage the rich information encapsulated in the continuous representations while remaining adaptable across different data modalities.
