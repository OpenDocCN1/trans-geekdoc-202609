And any good evaluator of MFA solutions needs to be aware that there are scenarios where MFA actually increase the risk of a successful attack. Many people are shocked to hear this. I, too, was taken aback the first time someone told me they went back to using names and passwords. After dozens of people told me the same, I understand that it isn't an uncommon reason and why they did so.

For example, if an MFA solution is SMS-based, it means that a SIM swap attack or social engineering my phone company's tech support folks may be enough to get around the MFA authentication defenses. SIM victims didn't do anything stupid. They relied on the MFA solution to provide them with what they were told "better" security than what they were previously using—and reliance ended up hurting them.

If they use SMS-based MFA, maybe they would use a regular name and password. If they use a unique name and password for each service, what are the odds that they will be phished over versus a SIM swapping happening? That risk comparison needs to be evaluated. Personally, I trust my ability not to be phished over my authentication to a phone company that hasn't so far (at least right now). So, I use a name and password only to protect some of my most valuable and important resources. And I don't regret it. It's absolutely the right security decision for me, and many in others, in many high-risk scenarios.

Of course, I think MFA can provide cybersecurity risk in many or most scenarios. The bigger problem I have is that no single MFA works in all my scenarios. That means I have to pick and choose scenarios I need to protect and decide among the available MFA solutions to protect them. When trying to pick the "best" MFA solution for me or one of my organizations, I use a proven process. It's one you can use, too.

## The Process for Selecting the Right MFA Solution

The process for selecting the “right” MFA solution for you and your organization consists of series of steps:

- 1. Create a project team.
- 2. Create a project plan.
- 3. Educate.
- 4. Determine what needs to be protected.
- 5. Choose required and desired details.
- 6. Research/select vendor solutions.
- 7. Conduct a pilot project.
- 8. Select a winner.
- 9. Deploy to production.

I'll cover each in more detail in the following sections.

## Chapter 23 Selecting the Right MFA Solution

### Create a Project Team

Picking MFA solutions is best not done by one person, even if you, the main person, are the most knowledgeable computer security and MFA person in your company. Moving from non-MFA solutions to MFA requires senior management support and comments and feedback from the people who will be helping you decide on the "right" solution. The more people you have involved with the process and supporting the ultimate selection, the more people you have on your side when things start to get a little rough, as they do in many computer security projects. Implementing MFA for the first time is never without at least some minimal operational intervention. Sometimes the issues are so small and end up being learned lessons for your next project. Having more people on your side from the beginning can only help you. And who knows? Maybe you will actually learn something from them that helps you, and the project team, the right decision and will be successful.

I remember a lesson I learned long time ago, when I was the regional IT director of a national health-care system. Just before I was hired, they chose a new main computer system to replace the old system. I hated the new system. It looked like a mainframe system, it had to have a lot of expensive customizations, and many of the current users spent their time telling me how the old system was better. And to me, it seemed weird. I developed a bit of attitude, and I complained to the project leader one day why they picked such an obviously bad system. Upon reflection, I now why he didn't take my criticism of the new system so well, especially since he had just a team of people over nine months in evaluating systems before they made their choice. He said something similar to this: "You can say you have the system and perhaps more than a few users do where a few features aren't as good as others they used to, but we spent nine months evaluating competing systems using a solid, thorough evaluation process and the majority of team members and end-user representatives chose this system. I'm sure each person involved some features they liked and some they thought were better in the system. But in the end, the majority of the team felt this particular system best overall for what we needed to do. Every system has haunts. You can argue about whether it was the best for you and some others, but you can't argue with the process." I gotta say, he was right.

Every MFA solution is a trade-off of features, each with its strengths and weaknesses, advantages and disadvantages. There are going to be people who do not like your MFA solution. So, make sure you put together a good project team and use a good selection process. That's the right choice.

If you do not have solid project management skills, get a great project manager assigned to your team or gain the necessary skills. Other team members should include the following:

- Senior management sponsor
- Project leader, knowledgeable with computer security, MFA, and other related topics (this should probably be you)
- IT security manager (if not you)
- Technical support representative
- IT employees as required
- End-user representative(s), possibly managers and/or stakeholders from different departments and business units
- Communications specialist
- Accounting/budgeting/purchasing

In course, in small companies, many of these roles may be represented by a single person. You may be the 'Team.' After you have your team, put together a project plan.

### Create a Project Plan

Every project leader needs to create a detailed project plan, likely using some project software like Microsoft Project (products.office.com/en-us/project/project-and-portfoliomanagement-software) or any of the other competing products (for suggestions, see www.pcmag.com/roundup/260751/the-best-project-management-software). It's important to figure out and document the tasks and critical paths. The more detailed and estimated, the better. Any project management plan should include the major milestones as steps in the process in this chapter.

### Create a Timeline

The ultimate objective is to move some or all people of the organization to an MFA solution from what they are using today (i.e., another MFA solution or non-MFA authentication).

### Create Project Phase Timeline Estimates

How long will it take you to complete the entire MFA migration project? Estimate how long it will take your organization to move all involved authentication systems to MFA once you start your MFA migration project so that senior management and project members can see expectations. The time estimates are unique for every organization and depend on what you have to move, and when you are able to move them. In experience at mid-sized companies, the average MFA project takes three to six months to finish, selecting the solution. In large, multistate or global companies, MFA projects start at six months long and easily last a year or two.
