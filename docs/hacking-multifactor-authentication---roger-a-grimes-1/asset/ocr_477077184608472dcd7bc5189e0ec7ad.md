# 410 Hacking Multifactor Authentication

# FIDO

As we covered in previous chapters, the Fast Identity Online FIDO specifications are a set of popular "passwordless" open authentication standards that rely on public–private key cryptography. FIDO supports a wide range of 1FA and MFA devices (computers, USB tokens, mobile phones, wired and wireless, etc.) and biometrics. FIDO2 (its and current version) has two main authentication paths and four specifications. Any FIDO2-participating software (OS, browsers, websites, services, etc.) must use the World Wide Web Consortium's (W3Cs) Web Authentication standard (WebAuthn) and APIs.

The Client to Authentication Protocol (CTAP) specification covers wireless devices. The Universal Authentication Framework (UAF) specification is a passwordless method, which is a physical device. The Universal 2nd Factor (U2F) specification covers MFA and requires a second factor and device of some type. The U2F standard is the one most adopted by MFA solution providers.

With U2F, users register their devices with the particular service or services to choose how to implement an authentication factor, such as a PIN or biometric ID. When connecting to the site or, conducting a transaction that requires strong authentication, the device performs local authentication (verifying the PIN or biometric identity) and passes the success or failure to the remote site. With U2F, an additional device (such as a cell phone or USB dongle) is used as the second factor after the password or PIN has been provided.

Traditional TLS guarantees authentication only to the client. FIDO authentication goes further by linking "registered" devices to their users and to the eventual websites or services. One authentication device can be linked to many (or all) websites and services. The registration prevents many, but not all, types of authentication attacks.

U2F prevents most-in-the-middle (MitM) attacks, as those discussed in Chapter 6, "Access Control Token Tricks," and summarized in Figure 6.3. For example, the Kevin Mitnick MitM demo attack against LinkedIn would not have worked. This is because U2F requires authentication of the device and server client from locations not previously registered, and the U2F authentication solution would not respond (or would respond with an error). You can read about FIDO MitM protections in this section of the FIDO specification document: fidoalliance.org/specs/fido-u2f-v1.2-ps-20170411/fido-u2f-overview-v1-2-ps-20170411.html.

> Part what I and others love about FIDO is that even in their specification section they prevent MitM attacks, they will tell you what would be needed to bypass those defenses (it's a pretty set of circumstances that are unlikely to be present in the real world) and how you to prevent even those. They aren't afraid to say they can't prevent every possible attack and don't pretend to. I wish more MFA solutions were as honest and direct. Transparency builds trust.

For more information on the FIDO2 standard and specifications, visit fidoalliance.org/specifications/download.
