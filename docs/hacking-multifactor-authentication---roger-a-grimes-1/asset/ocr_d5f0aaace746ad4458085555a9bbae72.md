# 11 Fake Authentication Attacks

This chapter covers a type of attack that's possible against nearly all forms of authentication, including MFA. It is difficult to stop and not all hard to pull off for the attacker. In a nutshell, malicious hackers can provide an experience to an end user that appears as if the user's authentication was successful and legitimate when it was not. The attacker can then trick the victim into revealing more confidential details or by performing actions that otherwise would not.

# About Fake Authentication Through UAC —

I often hear from MFA vendors and users who ask me if their favorite MFA solution can be hacked. My immediate response is always, "Yes! Anything can be hacked. More specifically, I know that "fake authentication" can be accomplished against nearly any MFA solution. I learned this firsthand over 15 years ago when my employer was trying to figure out how to mitigate it.
I worked for Microsoft for almost 12 years as a security consultant, and another three years later as a consultant working on Microsoft Windows Server 2003 and Microsoft SharePointsecurity for internal staff and customers. I loved my time working for Microsoft. It taught me a great deal, including that there are very smart people who know a lot about computer security. It is much more difficult to think of coming up with an idea that works at the scale that Microsoft works at. When I heard this first time, I thought it was great news and certainly my "great" idea would be implemented. But one at Microsoft's scale translates into millions and millions of problems, with as many angry users and vendors. A 99 percent solution isn't good enough. It causes too many operational issues.
