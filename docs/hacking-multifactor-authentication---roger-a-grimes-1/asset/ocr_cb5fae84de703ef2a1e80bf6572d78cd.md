I'll give you a real example. The Meltdown and Spectre vulnerabilities (meltdownattack.com) are likely the most threatening, high-risk, vulnerabilities to be announced .. The world's most popular CPU chips released since 1990's (Intel x86, ARM, and IBM Power) had the flaws. Hundreds of millions of computers and devices were susceptible and the vulnerabilities were exploitable no matter OS you ran and no matter what you implemented. And without patching those vulnerabilities, any involved computer would not only fail to stop the attacks, but would not even register an log record that documented anything had happened, much unless a successful exploit had occurred. After the announcement of Meltdown and Spectre in 2017, our hundred demonstration malware programs were created and released to show how easy it would be to “worm” and “weaponize” those vulnerabilities. Years later, there isn't a vulnerability report that lists devices not patched against Meltdown and Spectre as the highest risk threats you need to mitigate immediately. You've got to patch, patch, patch against them! Stop what you are doing and patch now!

But there is the very relevant fact that, to my knowledge, there hasn't been a single real-world exploit of Meltdown or Spectre vulnerabilities conducted against a single real-world attack by real criminals. Changes that the risk calculation immensely. The supposed high-risk threats created by Meltdown and Spectre are really a near-zero risk for most organizations. Until you have heard about one real world attack, is there really any risk? Perhaps your organization could be the first publicly known, but probably not.

There are a lot of things anyone can worry about, but something that hasn't ever occurred in the world probably isn't on the list. You can worry about a piece of a satellite falling to earth and landing on your roof top. All sorts accidents actually occur, but are extremely rare and even harder to predict. If a satellite fell on your roof it would probably cause a lot of damage. But most people don't worry about it or implement mitigations because it is such a rare event. With Meltdown and Spectre. You can worry about it. The “experts” and every vulnerability scanning software you could run indicate anything not patched against Meltdown and Spectre is at high risk of being compromised. The world is trying to tell you to worry about it. But no matter what they say, it isn't really critical risks—at least until the first public attack to it occurs.
