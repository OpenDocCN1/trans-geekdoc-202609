A related "control-type" of SMS message is known as "Flash SMS". Flash messages are sent by SMS, but they appear across your phone screen and are not even seen or stored in your regular SMS application or inbox. Flash messages are used to send SMS Alerts, weather warnings, and other types of official warnings. An attacker who is allowed to craft an SMS message that is not stopped by the network provider could likely pose as an otherwise trusted critical authority causing malicious.

# SMS Denial-of-Service Attack

An SMS-based attacker could also send a flood of SMS messages nearly anywhere in the system that supports and sends SMS messages and overwhelm the involved component. In the simplest form of the attack, a sender could send flood of messages to a receiver, which then overwhelms the user's ability to handle the incoming messages and obfuscates legitimate messages. An attacker could make it nearly impossible for an SMS user to be notified, view, or use an SMS message related to SMS-based MFA solutions.

And these attacks can be done silently. A more advanced attacker could flood the network provider's system as well. Here's a great whitepaper detailing how SMS works and some possible DDoS attacks: mo.co.za/open/slientdos.pdf.

Even without DoS, SMS is very unreliable. It is known as "connection-less," meaning its delivery from source to destination is not guaranteed. And some studies have shown that 10 to 5 percent of SMS messages are never delivered. That's a bad delivery rate when almost every type of authentication method is as near to 100 percent as you can get without just saying it's 100 percent.
