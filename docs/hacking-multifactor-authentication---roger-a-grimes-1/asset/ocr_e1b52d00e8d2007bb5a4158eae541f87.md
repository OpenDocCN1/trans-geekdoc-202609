# 188 Hacking Multifactor Authentication

Most cell phones also have an International Mobile Equipment Identity (IMEI), another 15-digit number that identifies the specific mobile device and stays with the device. Another common number is a Mobile Station Integrated Services Digital Network (MSISDN) number, which is the full phone number of a mobile device subscriber, beginning with including the country code (e.g., 1 for the United States and Canada, 44 for the UK, 61 for Australia).

These numbers are used to identify you and your device on a cellular network and to route and manage voice and message conversations to you and your device. In essence, these is numbers that allow your phone to be your phone. When some of these codes are moved from your current phone to yours, it's what makes the new phone your phone.

Most cell phone and cellular providers stored this information, and anything else they liked, in a physical small memory card (in Figure 8.5) known as the subscriber identity module (SIM) card or chip. Each SIM chip or storage area has a 19-digit or larger unique Integrated Circuit Identifier (ICCID) number.

If you ever had to move from one cell phone to another back then, your cellular provider would make you use the existing SIM card from the old phone to the new phone. Most cell phone users would spend time trying to find exactly where the SIM card was (often in a tiny slot on the end of the phone or underneath the removable battery, when batteries were removable), and get a paper clip to eject or pop the card out. It was an exercise in patience.

If you lost or replaced your old phone, SIM chip and all, the cell phone provider would provide you a new SIM chip and you place it in the new cell phone, and then you would dial a special short phone number (say #69# and that service would push down your SIM information to the new card and phone. Increasing, future cell phones will not come with SIM slots or cards anymore. Instead, the information that was normally stored on a SIM card will be stored on the cell phone's permanent memory in a protected location. This technology is known as virtual SIM. When the physical SIM is no longer present, people will still refer to the stored information as the SIM card or SIM information. For more information on virtual SIM, visit www.allspace.com/virtual-sim-card-es-the-full-guide and www.kaspersky.com/blog/virtual-sim-15572.

The SIM storage area can also act as storage for the phone, holding application data such as the user's pictures and contact information, although using a SIM for customer data storage is being deprecated. As I said earlier, it is the SIM information that makes your current phone your phone.
