# Chapter 23 Selecting the Right MFA Solution

# Create a Project Team

Picking MFA solutions is best not done by one person, even if you, the main person, are the most knowledgeable computer security and MFA person in your company. Moving from non-MFA solutions to MFA requires senior management support and comments and feedback from the people who will be helping you decide on the "right" solution. The more people you have involved with the process and supporting the ultime selection, the more people you have on your side when things start to get a little rough, as they do in many computer security projects. Implementing MFA for the first time is never without at least some minimal operational intervention. Sometimes the issues are so small and end up being learned lessons for your next project. Having more people on your side from the beginning can only help you. And who knows? Maybe you will actually learn something from them that helps you, and the project team, the right decision and will be successful.

I remember a lesson I learned long time ago, when I was the regional IT director of a national health-care system. Just before I was hired, they chose a new main computer system to replace the old system. I hated the new system. It looked like a mainframe system, it had to have a lot of expensive customizations, and many of the current users spent their time telling me how the old system was better. And to me, it seemed weird. I developed a bit of attitude, and I complained to the project leader one day why they picked such an obviously bad system. Upon reflection, I now why he didn't take my criticism of the new system so well, especially since he had just a team of people over nine months in evaluating systems before they made their choice. He said something similar to this: "You can say you have the system and perhaps more than a few users do where a few features aren't as good as others they used to, but we spent nine months evaluating competing systems using a solid, thorough evaluation process and the majority of team members and end-user representatives this system. I'm sure each person involved some features they liked and some they thought were better in the system. But in the end, the majority of the team felt this particular system best overall for what we needed to do. Every system has haunts. You can argue about whether it was the best for you and some others, but you can't argue with the process." I gotta say, he was right.

Every MFA solution is a trade-off of features, each with its strengths and weaknesses, advantages and disadvantages. There are going to be people who do not like your MFA solution. So, make sure you put together a good project team and use a good selection process. That's the right choice.

If you do not solid project management skills, get a great project manager assigned to your team or gain the necessary skills. Other team members should include the following:

+   - Senior management sponsor
- Project leader, knowledgeable with computer security, MFA, and other related topics (this should probably be you)
- IT security manager (if not you)
