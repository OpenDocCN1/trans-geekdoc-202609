# 388 Hacking Multifactor Authentication

Needlessly to say, there are many different ways to do DNS and namespace hijacking. When DNS
or another namespace is hijacked, it can lead to many different crimes, including:

+   - Bypassing MFA authentication
- Collecting email destined for the domain
- Collecting web traffic destined for the domain
- Collecting logon credentials used for the domain

And this has been done in the real world, many times. Let's get to some more examples.

# Example Namespace Hijacking Attacks

Let's look at example namespace hijacking attacks.

# DNS Hijacking Attacks

As covered earlier, one of the most popular types of DNS hacking is for a hacker to take control of
an entire DNS record administrator's server to change the IP address of a well-known
hostname to a malicious IP address. This is done thousands of times a year. Sometimes it's done
by individual hackers and by nation-states. In 2019, popular and respected security
vendor CrowdStrike reported ([crowdstrike.com/blog/widespread-dns-hijacking-activity-
targets-multiple-sectors]) that 28 organizations in 12 countries had their DNS hijacked. Once
hijacked, the hackers started receiving any traffic destined for those domains. The domains were
hijacked anywhere from less than a day to over a month. The hackers were highly sophisticated and
even created digital certificates for any connections requiring TLS. Security firm FireEye
concluded ([freeeye.com/blog/threat-research/2019/01/global-dns-hijacking-campaign-dns-
record-manipulation-at-scale.html) that at least some of the attacks were accomplished by Iran. You
read more details here: krebsonsecurity.com/2019/02/a-deep-dive-on-the-recent-widespread-
dns-hijacking-attacks.

# MX Record Hijacks

mail servers. If an attacker can move a victim's MX record to point to another IP address, they can\often capture email headed to the victim. Even if the victim's email has MFA protect-
ing their accounts.
