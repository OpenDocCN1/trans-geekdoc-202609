# 336 Hacking Multifactor Authentication

Confirming that a candidate is a particular, expected person is being used by many companies to confirm a person who is calling in for help really is the person calling. There is a huge industry offering voice identification solutions to save time by not requiring technical support folks to additionally verify a person's identity by offering help. Normally, the first factor in the voice identity is the phone number the person is calling from, followed by a second verification using the person's voice. If both match is already stored on the system, the person calling in may be considered automatically validated before they reach a tech support person. I have not personally seen voice recognition used inside companies for network logs or building entry, although Hollywood seems to think it is used at super-secret organizations.

In my experience, I have seen voice identification systems correctly validate me and not accept other people pretending to be in voice tests and presentations. Even witnessed tests where a person's voice was recorded and then played back in an attempt to fool voice-recognition systems without success. Hacker tools are available that will take a recorded person's speech and turn it into future, custom selected words or that the person never said. Demonstrations using these tools and methods are pretty fun to watch. In the ones where I've been physically present, even these hackers failed to fool the involved voice-recognition systems. These tests were limited in time, resources, and quality. The vendors of voice-recognition systems often indicate that if an attacker has time and effort, they can probably fool their systems. That's why voice-recognition systems are usually paired with another factor.

There are many other biometric factors that vendors and researchers have implemented or tried to implement. Most do not become widespread as they are not guaranteed to be unique, are too hard to measure, vary widely in measurements, or are too costly. As you recall from Chapter 4, “Usability vs. Security,” the world is waiting for a better biometric solution. We have plenty already. We just need more accurate solutions with the least amount of user friction. In any case, from a purely security view, any biometric paired with another factor (biometric or not) is widely seen as more secure than using a 1FA biometric factor as a sole authentication factor.
