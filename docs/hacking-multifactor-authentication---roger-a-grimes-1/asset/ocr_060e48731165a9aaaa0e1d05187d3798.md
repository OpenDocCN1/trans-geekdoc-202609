# Easy to Bypass Using Fake Traits

There isn't a biometric system that cannot be faked. Some are harder to fake than others, but they all can be bypassed using faked, simulated biometric traits. If someone is telling you that a biometric system can be faked, they are trying to sell it to you. There's often 16-year-old kid on YouTube showing who can fake it using $2 worth of materials. More on this in the example attacks later.

# Theft Causes Irreversible Damage

The majority of our most commonly used biometrics are easily spread wherever we've traveled/ or aren't particularly difficult to get if they really want them. Our fingerprints and DNA are everywhere, on our trash. Anyone can call and record our voice. Our geometry isn't a state secret. Getting our eye biometric data might be a bit more difficult, but a determined social engineer with the right equipment could offer a free glaucoma or premature retina detachment test, or simply break into your eye doctor's office to steal recorded eyes. With physical access, an adversary could steal a "skimming" device at any biometric reader to record submitted biometric traits just like gasoline pump/ATM criminals capture credit card details. Capturing internal blood vessels with enough accuracy would be might the exception, although it probably wouldn't be hard to trick anyone submitting to a rogue biometric reading to the needed biometric trait either.

Far more are the wholesale thefts of entire databases (several examples in a moment) used by vendors and customers. Once your biometric trait is stolen, how can any system that relies on that be assured it is really you to log in? For example, if someone steals your fingerprints (it happens), how can any system uses fingerprints for authentication be assured that a submitted fingerprint is really you? Theft of biometric traits is irreversible and likely to become more common as the use of biometrics increases. There is a chance one day that a few big biometric database breaches reveal everyone's biometric traits, and what do we do then?

Compare to non-biometric authentication factors like passwords or tokens, where we all need to replace a stolen one is delete the old one and enable a new one. Very easy to do. We do it all the time. The damage to theft is not permanent, unless that led to your biometric traits

# How to Compare Biometric Error Rates

Biometric error rates are essentially a fight between sensitivity to differences. As sensitivity is decreased, false rejections go up. False acceptance errors go up. Each biometric solution has its own error rate. What works in one environment may not work in another. For example, a high-security military system would tolerate far more false rejections than the average correction or cell user. False acceptance errors at a top-security military installation incur too much risk. But corporations and general consumer devices would rather have low rates of false rejections.
