# Side-Channel Leaks

Most living ethical things emit unintentional waves, signals, or currents that are different based on the type of information or activity they are involved with. It is not completely out of the range of possibility that someone will be able to point a scanner at them and determine what they are thinking. And if we break down to the smallest quantum levels, all things are composed of quantum particles and states that ultimately can be translated into 1s and 0s (information and data). All MFA devices are constantly generating unintended information; it's just a matter of looking for them and interpreting them.

A side-channel attack is accomplished by eavesdropping on an unexpected signal that is directly to operations or stored secrets. It can be due to a number of unintended leaks, including those from power consumption, electromagnetic waves, timing, light, temperature changes, and sound. Most electronic devices are one or more of these unintentional leaks.

My first exposure to a side-channel attack was when I was fairly new to computers in the mid-1980s and an acquaintance of mine worked in the U.S. Navy to protect our military ships against side-channel eavesdropping. He showed me how he could plug a voltage monitor into his home's wiring that could display what letters a nearly dot-matrix printer was printing. He explained that every strike of the "dots" of the printer that created individual character produced a minute current resistance change in the electrical circuit voltage the printer was connected to. I tested by sending some random characters that he could not see to the printer from a word processor. Sure enough, his voltage scanner reproduced the sentence I had sent. He made me a believer.
