On the negative side, any application can be hacked and attacked, and that includes phones apps. They are buggy like anything else. # SMS

Short Message Service (SMS) allows systems and users to send messages to one another. Many popular MFA solutions use SMS, which is limited to 140 characters per message, to send users "out-of-band" codes to be used during MFA logins. Figure 3.18 shows several legitimate instances of SMS being used to send MFA codes, from Bank of America, Microsoft, Google, Facebook, Marriott, and MyIDC (a credit monitoring service).

![](bbox=[0.095, 0.413, 0.712, 0.603])

Figure 3.18: Examples of SMS MFA codes

The most commonly used MFA on the Internet are SMS based. However, they are considered to be weak authenticators—and potentially vulnerable that in 2017 the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), stated in Special Publication 800-63, Revision 3, the Digital Identity Guidelines (pages.nist.gov/800-63-3/sp800-63-3.html), should not be used as an authenticator. Despite this, SMS continues to be one of the most popular MFA solution used in the world. More on this in Chapter 8, "SMS Attacks."
