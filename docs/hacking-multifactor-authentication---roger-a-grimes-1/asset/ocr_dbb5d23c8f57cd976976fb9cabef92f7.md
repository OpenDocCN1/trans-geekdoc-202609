# Pulling Off Top-Not Security Awareness Training

Security awareness training is supposed to give your coworkers a healthy level of skepticism to recognize, and report social engineering attacks. For years, we wondered how to pull off great security awareness training. I am fortunate enough to be working at the world's number one security training vendor. We have looked at how their customers have deployed security awareness training and determined what methods work best. I will share them with you.

Publicize the Security Awareness Training Program Let all your coworkers that you are doing a formal security awareness training program. Explain how you will be doing it and how you expect to participate. Don't surprise anyone. No one ever got promoted for surprising someone, least of senior management. Instead, let everyone know that your regular awareness training and how you will do it. This phase of the system is part of the training.

Establish a Baseline Send a simulated phishing email to all your coworkers. Create a phishing email that accurately represents a common phishing email that you've seen before. It is not crafted to be sneaky and hard to recognize. You want to send a phishing test that most people would recognize as a phishing test and not click. You'll likely be surprised by how many people click on this easy-to-spot fish. It is not uncommon for up to 30 percent of employees of an organization to fall victim to a simulated phishing test.

Train at Least Once a Month Send your coworkers to some sort of training at least once a month in addition to performing the simulated phishing tests. The training should vary, should be interesting enough that people won't hate it, and should be monthly. Monthly trainings shouldn't be longer than a few minutes each. Ideally, the training would be around current social engineering trends or focus on preventing social engineering for your implemented MFA solution.

Do Simulated Phishing Tests at Least Once a Month Send your coworkers a simulated phishing test (or SMS message or voice call) at least once a month. Like the training, the simulated phishing should be focused on current social engineering trends. If someone fails a simulated test, they should be provided with immediate feedback on what they should have noticed it was a phishing (test) and not a legitimate email. As your user base gets better at spotting phishing emails, you can increase the level of sneakiness. You want to ramp up the training as your end-user base knowledge of phishing increases.

Stay Current on Your Training Make a priority to keep the social engineering training current up-to-date real social engineering trends. When a world event happens that real phishers are using, model your simulated phishing tests and training after what the phishers are doing.
