
## Protect API Keys

If you have an API key, realize that it could be stolen by hackers to access your account and information. API keys are “keys.” Treat them as such. Don't leave them in email or lying around your hard drive. Instead, encrypt them or place them inside your password manager (if you use one).

### Amazon Mystery Device Bypass

This strange MFA bypass was reported by a U.S.-based Amazon user (www.reddit.com/r/sysadmin/comments/dpbt3t/the_perils_of_security_and-how_i_finally_resolved) in November 2019. This Amazon user woke up to discover several Amazon gift cards fraudulently charged to his Amazon account. This surprised him because not only did he not make the gift card purchases, but he, as an IT security professional, had protected his Amazon account with an OTP token. Amazon allows three different OTP options (see Figure 20.1): SMS Message, Voice Phone Call, and Phone App.

When enabled, the OTP code is required during the logon process to the Amazon user account, including any device the user connects to. The victim in this case thought that his account was thoroughly protected until he discovered that the fraudulent charges. He tried to contact Amazon fraud support and sadly discovered that there was no option other than that has promised a 48-hour turnaround time. So, he enabled another OTP, deleted his credit card information from his Amazon account, deleted all active sessions, removed all allowed devices, and changed his banking and credit card passwords.

After much research and talking to an offshore tech support person, he learned that (likely located in Asia) made the purchase from a Huawei smart television. He didn't own a Huawei television. To this day he does not know how his Amazon credentials were stolen. He was perplexed by two things: 1) how the foreign television connected to his Amazon account when he was OTP it, and 2) why he didn't see the television on his list of allowed and connected devices attached to his account when he allowed devices under his account options. He learned that not only are non-Amazon devices (such as smart televisions, game consoles, and Roku boxes) not shown in Amazon's account device list, but they aren't displayed to most Amazon tech support as well. ### Obtaining Old Phone Numbers

In Chapter 8, "SMS Attacks," you learned about SIM swapping attacks, where an attacker obtains a legitimate subscriber's SIM information and transfers it to a phone in their possession. In this similar scenario, an attacker legally obtains a victim's old phone number, and simply having the ability to use the old number is enough to take control of the victim's current MFA solution.

In one example, the MFA vendor, Authy (authy.com), allowed Authy users to register and use multiple devices (and phone numbers) with their MFA solution. This permitted any attacker knowing an Authy's old phone number (previously used with Authy's solution) to get that number and access the user's account using SMS (if the user did not disable Authy's Multi-Device feature, which was enabled by default). This oversight came to a head when Coinbase, the world's largest and most popular cryptocurrency trading site, recognizing Authy as a valid MFA solution (www.reddit.com/r/Bitcoin/comments/6f0nhb/coinbase_recommendation_migrate_from_authy_to) and told all its Authy-using customers to change to Google Authenticator instead. Further, an outside security research firm found a vulnerability (bitcoin.com/authy-vulnerability-exposed-users-affected) where attackers could learn Authy's personal information used to prove their identity to Authy. This information could have been used by an attacker to register a new fraudulent or phone number if the Multi-Device feature was enabled.

### Password Hints

Many software and phone-based MFA solutions are ultimately protected by a login name and password. Once the user logs on, the user can continue to use the MFA solution in perpetuity or for some interval of time. But logically it means the solution's strength is tied to login name and password. That by itself is an issue. But makes matters worse, some of these MFA solutions allowing login name and password to be used also allow a user to store, and anyone else to view, a password hint (see an example in Figure 20.2).
