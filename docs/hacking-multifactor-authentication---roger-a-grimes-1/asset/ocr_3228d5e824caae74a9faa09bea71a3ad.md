# Chapter 12 Social Engineering Attacks

Kevin is now co-owner and chief hacker officer of my current employer, KnowBe4, Inc., and he runs his own very successfully penetration testing company. I've come to know and love Kevin Mitnick. He's a solid, unassuming, moral person. He loves to teach others how to hack, especially using social engineering. If you ever get a chance to see one of Kevin's hackings or presentations in person, you'll see what I mean. Kevin's hacking demos are a combination of edgy hacker and nearly untouchable showmanship, which points back to his original line of magic and magicians.

One of my favorite demos and stories is from Kevin about how he clones building entry cards as part of his professional penetration testing engagements. Building entry card often works on principles similar to MFA solutions. This particular long video from one of my company's national conferences shows Kevin these hacks: https://youtube.com/watch?v=IFg5MUUNe

When Kevin is hired to penetrate a company, the purpose is to see whether he can penetrate the company's physical security. Most of the places he is to hack building entry cards that are required to enter the building and open doors. Kevin tells two stories about how he secretly clones building entry cards.

If the customer is located inside a big building that returns space to other customers, he will often call the rental office posing as a prospective future rental customer of that building. Usually he meets with a rental person on-site to "see the space." The building officer usually has a master entry key that lets them into the building and all the floors of the building. Unbeknown to the rental office, Kevin can clone card hardware that will make an exact copy of the building entry card when it gets within a few centimeters of Kevin's wireless scanner. Kevin places the card inside a thin, leather-bound notebook, the type of which you often seen used by business professionals around the world. He will ask to see the card used by the rental agent and "innocently" wipe it over his handheld pocket. Little does the rental agent know that their card has been completely duplicated.

The second story is how he tells him to enter the public bathroom of a company and put himself at the urinal right next to the building's head maintenance person. Any male knows that no other male should come up to a row of large vacant urinals and place themselves right next to the only other person going to the bathroom. In the world it's considered poor etiquette for a bunch of reasons. That's what Kevin did. He went up to the urinal next to the maintenance man, faked urinating, and in so placed his notebook close to the maintenance man's master key that he successfully cloned it—despite the nervous rolling of the maintenance man who felt Kevin had inside his personal space. Kevin regulates with the stories he tells as anyone from the audience to volunteer bringing up their own building entry card to use in a current hack demonstration of the space. Kevin has never been able to clone the volunteer's building entry card in any of the many demos I've seen him, although I'm sure there some models would not be so easy to clone.

The lesson here is that if you have a wireless MFA product that is not protected from cloning (smartcards anticloning protections), then it might be possible for a physical engineer to clone your MFA device.
