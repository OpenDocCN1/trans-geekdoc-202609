# User Defenses

The number defense is to use any MFA solution other than that relies on SMS. Outside of that, if you must use and rely on SMS-based MFA devices, here are some other defenses you can implement.

# Security Awareness Training

The best piece of defense advice I can give you to let users know they can still be hacked though they are using an MFA solution. Make sure users are educated about the risks and specifically associated with SMS-based MFA solutions. Youので want to cover SIM swap attacks, SMS impersonation, rogue SMS URL links that can lead to device takeovers. Educate users so that they understand they don't have all the protections they may have on a full-sized phone, such as the ability to hover a link. Make all SMS-based MFA users get this training at least once a month.

# Be Aware of Rogue Recovery Messages

Users should be aware of SMS recovery schemes and what they look like. Examples include unexpected SMS or voice calls warning of a possible compromise.

# Be Wary of SMS Recovery PINs

Users should be told that, if they are sent an SMS recovery PIN, it would be suspicious if they were instructed to type it back into another separate SMS message or to tell someone over a phone the PIN number.

# Try to Place a Lock on Your SIM Info

Some phone network providers allow high-risk users to place a "lock" or "block" on their SIM information. That is, if some callers requesting their SIM information be moved to another phone, that individual must know the additional piece(s) of information to get the lock removed. Be aware that this could frustrate users if they cannot remember the information needed to remove the block.

# Protect Your Cell Phone Account Logon with MFA

This may sound like circular logic, but it can't hurt to protect your cell phone network user account with MFA if that is an available option. Yes, MFA can be hacked, but protecting logon accounts with MFA usually provides you with more security. So use it.
