# Hacking Multifactor Authentication

Assistance Commission voting machine vendors in the United States: www.eac.gov/voting-equipment/registered-manufacturers. All of these manufacturers are working to create the most secure voting machines that they can.

Dozens of organizations and oversees are dedicated to improving the security of electronic voting. Many hundreds, if not thousands, white-hat hackers have been involved in penetration testing of electronic voting machines or conducting security reviews based on known information. The news so far is that many of the electronic voting machines are hackable—some easily so. Here is a story of a 11-year-old who hacked into a voting website replica in 10 minutes at a popular computer conference: time.com/536171/11-year-old-hacked-into-us-voting-system-10-minutes. Several hackers have proven they could, or have, broken into real electronic voting machines. When confronted with irrefutable evidence, many voting machine vendors have admitted their machines are vulnerable to hacking. Here is an example: www.theblaze.com/news/2018/07/17/largest-voting-machine-vendor-admits-using-software-vulnerable-to-hacking. The results when professional security and code reviews have been done on voting machines, the results have not been good. Here is an example of a voting machine's code review report: votingsystems.codn.ca.gov/vendors/hart-interic/verity/verity-sc.pdf.

As covered in Chapter 1, "Logon Problems, hacking" is so pervasive in such the prospect of any election using electronic voting, either at official polling places or remotely at homes, creates many people. If you understand what it is and how easy it is to hack most of today's electronic voting machines, you have to be crazy not to concerned. Almost all computer security professionals familiar with the issues feel there is way too much to go pure electronic voting any time soon, you don't even have to be a computer security professional to be wary of online voting scenarios. The general population, when asked about the pros and cons of electronic voting, are nearly as worried as the professionals. It's not a hidden secret that our electronic voting machines are fairly hackable with a lot at risk.
