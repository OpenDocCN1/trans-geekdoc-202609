# OTP Bypass Brute-Force Test

The first example comes from a white-hat hacker who successfully brute-forced a site using a one-time password (OTP) MFA solution (medium.com/bugountywriteup/how-i-bypassed-the-otp-verification-process-part-1-e5b33274ae9). He connected to the website and performed the normal logon/account registration process until the website requested the OTP code to validate his/registration. His previous knowledge and testing confirmed that the site used six-digit OTP.

He then used Burp Suite to capture the website's packet request around the OTP submission. He located the OTP submission field (see Figure 14.1) after the labeled codetextbox. The developer could have hidden the field or its value in many ways to at least obscure what purpose of the field and its value, but they did not. They didn't enable account lockout, since this is not a surprise.

The white-hat hacker used the “Intruder” mode/tab of Burp Suite to try different OTP combinations for another logon/registration attempt. A six-digit OTP, composed of numbers, 0–9 (e.g., 10), has a million combinations, ranging from 000000 to 999999. That's a lot of combinations to try. The white-hat hacker claims he put find the correct code at try 150 and instructed Burp to attempt a bunch of combinations if it would allow him to be successful.
