The attackers then put the victim's Google account into recovery mode and chose, out of the multiple options offered by the automated recovery, have Google the recovery account PIN to the victim's previously registered phone number. The victim did not recognize the incoming call from Google and let it go to voicemail. The AT&T had rerouted the victim's voicemail to a number under the hackers' control, and the hackers listened to the voice-mail from Google tech support and heard the Gmail recovery code.

The hackers used the Gmail recovery code to take over the victim's Gmail account. They then put the victim's CloudFlare account (their ultimate target) into recovery mode and had CloudFlare send the recovery code to the victim's Gmail account. The attackers then used CloudFlare recovery code to take over the victim's CloudFlare account.

The attackers then used the victim's CloudFlare account to make changes to victim's DNS records, which allowed the attacker to control the DNS address of the hacker's ultimate attack target. Whew! these admin abilities to

The intermediate victim, the chief executive of CloudFlare, was actively involved during the incident and fighting unsuccessfully against the hacker while his authentication was stolen. The attackers turned out to be hackivists who wanted to punish their ultimate target, 4Chan, for not self-policing well enough.

There is a fourth way to take over or change the alternate email account, but they are not common. The third way is if the recovery process contains a vulnerability that allows a knowable programmer to bypass the MFA requirement and update the recovery code option. This has happened a few times to MFA services in the past, although I could not find URLs to relevant articles to include in this chapter in time for publishing.

The fourth way is when an attacker compromises a victim's related device, which then allows MFA bypass on the target device. This can be done in some rare scenarios (and are more of "man-the-endpoint" attacks), and luckily, I don't know of any real-world malicious attacks.
