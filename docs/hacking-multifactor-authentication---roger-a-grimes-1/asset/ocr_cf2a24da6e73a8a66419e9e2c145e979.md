# Limit Number of Attempts

Most of the hackers in the attack demo talk about how many times the biometric device spoof failed failed or over successful attempts. Limiting the number of failed attempts before a successful authentication is a good way to reduce spoofing. Treat biometric attacks like a password and enable account lockout. On a related note, extend wait times between failed attempts so that each failed attempt begins to exert more of a cost on the hacker. Don't just let them over and really fast until they are eventually successful.

# Require MFA with Biometrics

Biometric authentication should always be paired with a second, non-biometric factor. The whole reason most going to MFA is because it requires two or more factors and makes it harder for a hacker to be successful against a potential victim. Biometrics should not change that. 1FA solutions are usually weak combinations. Biometrics don't change that and, in some cases, are weaker than simple passwords.

# Securely Protect Biometric Trust Databases

All developers need to ensure that their biometric trait databases, wherever they are, are strongly secure. They need to be secured with least privilege permissions and encrypted. For example, Windows Hello uses symmetric and asymmetric ciphers and protects the most critical keys with an additional private key stored on a Trusted Platform Module (TPM) chip on the motherboard and OS protected by Unified Extensible Firmware Secure Boot. It's a pretty great combination.

Fairly secure storage can be done without encryption and symmetric chips ensure the stored biometric patterns are obscured enough to be unrecognizable and outside the system from which it was stolen. I've heard of biometric systems where the data—fingerprints, prints patterns, or hand vein—is converted into a digital matrix that is unusable and unrecognizable outside of the system. As earlier, most pattern-recognition systems convert the images they see to inflection points, or like star constellations, and it is those "star constellations" that are stored and not the actual images.
