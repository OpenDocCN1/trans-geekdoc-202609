# Security Theater

There are also instances of security that are more theater and spectacle than true security (known as security theater by critics). The world is full of security theater. One of the best examples is signing checks or credit/ATM receipts for purchases in the United States. It has nearly zero security value in stopping fraud from being committed. With a check, you might possibly have a sales clerk compare the signature with your ID, but probably not. And if that clerk notes a difference, probably won't say anything to you and certainly won't stop your purchase. There is a chance a fraudulent signature can help your case in the event that fraud is noticed on the back later time, but it rarely stops the fraud from being committed in the first place.

For decades, I have signed all checks and credit cards with a known bad. I have signed thousands of my credit card receipts and checks with “Yogi Bear” and “ThisIsNotMyRealSignature” and I have never been stopped from purchasing something. I had a few people laugh, but none have stopped me from my purchase. I think, originally, most of us thought it important to make sure our signature was our legal, valid signature. We must have thought that there was a chance that someone was sitting in a background一来 comparing signatures and that person would notice the discrepancy and stop the transaction. It has never happened. We are legally required to provide that by law, supposedly for security purpose. But that review does not happen. Most of the time, we are wasting our time. It is not sure why we are a society even tolerates having signing signatures that do almost nothing. If signing took 20 seconds on average, I the requirement to use signatures would be changed.
