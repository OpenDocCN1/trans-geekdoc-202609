# Advanced: Pretexting

Pretexting is used in advanced phishing attacks. Pretexting is when the phisher initiates multiple contacts with the victim, with the first communication being something that is not asking the victim for anything suspicious. The phisher is “laying the groundwork” and establishing an ongoing relationship so that when they have a requested action in the future, the victim already feels comfortable with the phisher.

An example pretexting call might be a hacker calling a company's human resources number and talking to the accounts payable clerk and acting as they are now the new primary contact for an existing company that regularly sends invoices for payment. In the first case, the caller just says hello and communicates that they are the new contact, gives them new contact information, and tells the accounts payable clerk that all questions and requests related to the company should now go to them. They may even indicate that the change is happening as part of a “big update” and that they will be changing banks to a new bank that has more favorable terms, or something like that. The phisher waits a few days or weeks and asks the accounts payable clerk to update the payment information supposedly due to a new banking change. When the regular invoice from the real company comes into the accounts receivable, the clerk not knowing anything different, sends the regularly scheduled payment to the new bank and phisher. This are often not discovered for months.
