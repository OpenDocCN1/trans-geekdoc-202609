Prevention of the most likely threats is the most important defense you can have. It may sound funny that I have to write that sentence, but by many of your conventional computer defenses are based on an “assume breach” mentality, where you assume your environment is already breached or could be if someone concentrated into it. Assume essentially focuses on preventing internal spread and detection. All defenses definitely need a strong set of assume breach defenses. The primary focus has always been and still needs to be on stopping the badness from being successful in breaking in to an organization in the first place. Accordingly, I’m more of a STOP BREACH mentality.

Efficient threat detection is a matter of figuring out your most likely threats and determining whether you have the detection capability if they successfully occur against your environment? Take all your potential security logging and alert tools and figure out which ones map or don map to your likely threats. You’ll see a lot of overlap and some weaknesses. Plug the gaps. Recovery is damage control and figuring out what you did wrong in the first pillars, prevention and detection, so that it won't happen again.
