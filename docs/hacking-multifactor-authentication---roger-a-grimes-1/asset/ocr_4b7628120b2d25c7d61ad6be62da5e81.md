# We Don't Really Want the Best Security

Every computer security person understands the challenge of implementing good security controls without annoying users or making them more inefficient in their job or tasks. Make control too secure and you risk doing both. Computer security experts are always trying to improve security and lower cybersecurity risk, and sometimes (especially new people to the field) can get up in thinking that making security is the only goal that matters. That's from the case.

Any experienced security person will tell you that if you leave it to most ends they would want little to no security if it actually makes them a double adding thing that slows down in the slightest. Many people want the least amount of "friction" to the point that they would prefer to no security if it means slowing down from doing what they want to do. Well, they want everyone to have what's needed as security is needed, but they feel that they, themselves, do really need much "cumbersome and onerous" computer security controls. They feel they know what they are doing and that security only slows them down.

They reluctantly put up with what security controls management tells them that they have to. They want the bare controls that they perform for their job relatively safely and effectively, and they don't want those. What do they have because someone else higher up the chain said they have to. Until something goes very wrong, and then computer security gets blamed for not doing enough. For most computer security professionals, it's a double-edged sword with all of the responsibility and accountability without any authority to get the job done correctly.

It's not like computer security professionals can design secure solutions that prevent most to all cybersecurity incidents. Most of us can design computer security controls that would stop hackers and malware. It is designing them without an added burden to end users and keeping management's support that's the tricky part.
