# Unbreakable Oracle

In 2001, Oracle started a "unbreakable" campaign. It claimed that its products were essentially unhackable. It didn't take long for the first security bugs to be found. Over the next few years, over a hundred bugs were found in Oracle's core products. It was an embarrassment. There was none in the computer world who thought Oracle's claim would be found to be true for very long. Everyone was wondering why Oracle's CEO, Larry Ellison, even decided to go with the "unbreakable" marketing campaign was found to fail. But even, it was likely a good marketing campaign for Oracle. It put them in the news, and showed that Oracle was at least trying to make a better product, albeit just asking for trouble from hackers who had not previously contended on Oracle products.

# DJB

Daniel J. Bernstein (who is widely known as djb) is one of the leading cryptographers and secure teachers in the world (cr.yp.to/djb.html). According to what I remember at the time, he is so good at secure coding that students develop up to take his classes, even in some of his classes never failed. In one of his cases, he required that everyone find at least 10 previously undisclosed security bugs, and all the found were immediately released to the public without giving the generator any time to patch ahead of the public announcement. Students who didn't find 10 previously undisclosed vulnerabilities failed the class. Most students found only a few and ending his class. Still, the best programmers in the university found to take (and likely fail) his class. He was that of a teacher and that highly respected.

> It is possible that everyone failing his classes because they did not find and close 10 new vulnerabilities may have been a false rumor. But I'm pretty sure this is the truth or near the whole truth. I had some of his students write and confirm what I'm sharing here, djb himself, never responded to confirm my understanding.

Bernstein is a respected programmer who has written many respect, very secure source programs, including qmail and dbdjns (found here: crypto.to). His programs are known for not having very many security bugs, djb is a fierce critic of the huge number of security bugs found in software programs. He challenges developers and vendors for having many bugs, using too many lines of code, and not caring enough to aggressively minimize security bugs. He used to offer $1,000 for anyone finding a security bug in any product he could be exploited. Over a few years, at least two bugs were found. One was about in 2009 (www.csonline.com/article/263362/dbdjns-security-vulnerability-found.html). I believe he offers his $1000 reward anymore.
