# Solving Puzzles

A growing number of authentication solutions require that the user solve some sort of graphical, spatial, mathematical, or logical.

# Connect the Dots

The common types of puzzles involved in authentication include asking the user to "connect the dots" (see Figure 3.2). Dots are evenly placed in a tic-tac-toe grid, and the user selects or swipes dots in a predefined, previously agreed-upon, pattern.

Connect-the-dot puzzles suffer from many of the same problems as PINs, although probably the worst issue is how they figure out from shoulder surfing. It's easy for anyone watching it be done to re-create the pattern. I've seen many people, untrained for this type of skill in any way, re-create other people's patterns from a room with the "target's" device turned 180 degrees away, obscuring direct viewing. The shoulder surfing hacker is able to tell the target's hand movements and elbows what the target just did. I've seen this enough that I do not think connect-the-dot authentication is sufficient for anything beyond the merit suggestion of security.

# Graphical Swiping

Another type of authentication puzzle asks the user to select a picture or graphic, or supply their own, and determine a pattern that will be used for authentication (an example in Figure 3.3). Puzzle solutions like this allow the user to use a combination of gestures, such as "swiping," from one point to another without raising their finger, circles, or making other predefined gestures. These types of authentication solutions tend to be allowed on form factors larger than a few inches. On smaller form factors such as cell phones, it might be difficult to get enough movement area across the smaller representation of the figure to achieve the desired result.

The problems with these sorts of authentication solutions mimic the connect-the-dot puzzles, although not having stationary, fixed-positioned dots may prevent easy shoulder surfing. Still, research has shown that people using these types of puzzle solutions have predictable locations they choose and gestures and motions they make depending on the patterns and shapes in the underlying photo.
