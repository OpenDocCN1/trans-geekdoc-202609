# Bloomberg, L.P. and the Bloomberg Terminal

Bloomberg is the millionaire of New York City who ran for the 2020 presidential democratic nomination. He got billions of dollars from his Bloomberg, L.P. company and it has tweeted Bloomberg Terminal (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_Terminal) device, which is used by nearly every stock trader who can afford it. It first appeared in 1982 as a "dumb terminal" device. It quickly moved to the PC platform when PCs became dominant and, today, is essentially a mobile software program that remotely interfaces with the backend components. Most financial people and traders love Bloomberg Terminals. They are fast, relatively easy to use, and they access to tremendous amounts of information. For example, you can ask "show me the end-of-day stock price for IBM from every Friday it was listed on the stock market" and get an answer in one to two seconds. You want financial statements for the last 20 years? They can get them in seconds. You want to see the last 10 years of 10-Ks or the latest news? They have a ton of data and news about any public company, in all one place, very quickly retrieve. It is amazing to see. The speed and ability to retrieve almost any financial reason within is considered essential to high-end traders. Bloomberg Terminals are coveted. Many financial traders, before accepting a new job offer, will make sure that a Bloomberg Terminal is part of their new setup. If not, they won't take the job. If you think Apple phones were childish, you haven't talked to Bloomberg Terminal users. I used one; I understand.

The quality and speed of they deliver isn't identical. Bloomberg has committed strongly to offering information more quickly than any other company or product. It's their driving competitive advantage. If you're a computer scientist trying to help Bloomberg accomplishing something, if your solution slows down a Bloomberg information response by even a few milliseconds, you will have the job. Information and speed drive all decisions. But Bloomberg is pretty great in the computer security area.

Early on, you had to have a physical Bloomberg Terminal to get access. Later on, you could download Terminal software and register your device and a subscriber key and run the software on your computer. Today, a Bloomberg session is remotely accessible from other computers in what is called Bloomberg Anywhere (bba.bloomberg.net).

The Bloomberg experience doesn't come cheap. Because of that, there has always been a great incentive for crooks and cheats to get or free access to a terminal or the software that allows them to connect to remotely. Bloomberg, from the beginning, has always had cutting-edge physical security controls, dongles, and MFA, to control which one of their terminals.
