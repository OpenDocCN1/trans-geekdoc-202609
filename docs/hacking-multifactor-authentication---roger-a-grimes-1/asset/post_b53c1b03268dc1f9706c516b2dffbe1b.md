# Points Ranking

Many reviewers assign a point system to all the included requirements and desired features, and the MFA system with the most assigned points wins in the end. Suppose, for example, that all must-have-requirements are assigned a maximum value of 10. Every other individual requirement or feature is assigned a value of 3 to 1. If a vendor's solution meets a requirement or feature completely, it gets the full points for that item. If it partially meets a requirement or feature, it only gets points, however many the team feels adequate for the partial coverage. In the end, add up all the points, and the top point getter is selected as one of the pilot project contenders or the winner, depending on your haste and project stage.

# Research/Select Vendor Solutions

Now that you have a list of requirements and desired features, it's time to begin researching. In the perfect world you would want to review as many MFA solution candidates as possible that could come close to meeting all your needs. But there are thousands of possible MFA vendors, and most of them likely have never heard of and won't be able to find (without my help). What you want to do is narrow down all the possible MFA solution vendors to those who possibly meet your needs and not even have to bother with the rest.

# Read MFA Industry Reviews

A great way to narrow down MFA vendor lists is by checking the "official" product reports from the technology industry researchers, including Gartner (www.gartner.com), IDC (www.idc.com), and Forrester (go.forrester.com/research). Each of these research agencies has decades of experience in reviewing products and industries. Their annual industry reports are some of the most soughtafter reading. They usually cover the industry in general, summarize the major players, and pick winners and losers. An MFA vendor has to be for more than four years and have a stable customer base and revenue before they be listed in a report. The reports will let you know what customers think about each product and what the strengths and weaknesses of each are. They will cover the maturation of the industry overall and tell you where it is going. If I have limited time and want to get a quick overview of the top vendors, I'd read one of their reports.

> NOTE Many of these review services cater to larger enterprises and the concerns of large enterprises. may not cover cases that are important to small and medium-sized organizations.

# Conduct a Pilot Project

When you're doing your research, choose at least two or three MFA solutions for pilot projects. A good pilot project involves the whole team and user representatives across your organization. Participants should be involved in evaluating all selected MFA solutions so they can provide comparative feedback. You want to find out how easy the solution is to install, manage, and administer. How easy is to provision users? How well does the authentication work? Are there issues and problems? What's the estimated cost? Initial purchase, ongoing, maintenance?

When you call existing customers who are using the product, at the end of your conversation don't forget to ask them, "What would you change about the solution if you could?" You'd be amazed what one question opens up from customers who were previously giving you a review.

# Select a Winner

You've reached the end of the first part of your journey. If you are using points scoring, have everyone rank the solutions from best to worst and pick the winner that makes more people happy than all others. It is good etiquette to let the losing vendors know why they lost. That information can help them improve their solution.

# Deploy to Production

Now's time to roll out the solution. I'm a big believer in baby steps, if that is possible, don't make an implementation plan where the old authentication solution is all that can be done and the next one method can be used. It is best to do side-by-side runs if possible, opening the MFA solution to more people and departments as time goes on. A trickle-in methodology is possible, rarely will you regret it. I have regretted replacement, "go-live" projects where something went wrong was not detected in the pilot project and other testing a critical system down. Save yourself the heartache and frustration. Try to move from one authentication solution to stages, increasing the number of people and impacted time.

Be sure to update your policies and account for the new MFA solution. Provide training to IT, help support staff, train-trainers, trainers, and end users. Continue to keep your eyes and ears out for developing MFA trends. MFA is constantly improving.
