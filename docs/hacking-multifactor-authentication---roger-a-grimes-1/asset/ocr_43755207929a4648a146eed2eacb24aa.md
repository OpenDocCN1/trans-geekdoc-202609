When assigning potential likelihoods of risks and different attack components, sometimes you'll be taking with what technical folks call a "guess." That's okay. You just need to take your best guess, whatever it is, and put it on the attack tree so that risk calculations can be made. But even when you guess, think about the methods and what have been most successfully used against your organization or similar solutions in the past and are most likely to occur in the near future. Real attacks by malicious hackers mean that. Theoretical attacks that have never been accomplished against your solution or a solution like yours have a far lower likelihood of occurring.

For example, in today's world, social engineering and attacks against unpatched software are common, making up the vast majority of risk. We've also seen, as covered in this book, attacks against names, but those types of attacks are a minority in the MFA world. Most are theoretical, but not all. More popular are credential theft attacks and exploits due to incorrect permissions—those that are moderate, risks for most solutions.
