# Threat Modeling

Our voting scenario also gives us risk advantages. One, the fact that money isn't directly obtainable from hacking the voting cuts out 99 percent (or whatever the percentage is) of cybercriminals who want to hack it. Most hackers are trying to fill their pockets with money either directly (e.g., bank account fraud, ransomware) or indirectly (e.g., credit card fraud, seller fraud). Money could be made by hacking voting machines, but only as payment by those parties (politicians, nation-states, hacktists, corporate interests, etc.) that would pay for the private bug disclosure or for a hack moving votes to a designed outcome. And that is a smaller pool of potentially malicious actors than every petty criminal looking to line their pockets.

I see two to four main threats, and the most obvious, is maliciously changing the vote. Hacking an electronic voting method could end changing a lot of votes all at once or attack a collection point where individual votes are tallied and reported. Those are two different types of attacks. The first would need to target a substantial number of voters in a similar way nearly all at once. The second would need to target only the collection points, which would be a single location or at least fewer locations than the original number of votes/votes that would be impacted.

Another main threat would be a real denial-of-service (DoS) attack to interrupt or prevent legitimate votes. I think of this as two possible attacks. The first would be to truly interrupt a real vote. It could be against all voters or against voters who are inclined to vote the opposite way of the desired vote outcome. The other, and fourth objective, would interrupt the vote just enough or make the electorate think the vote was interrupted, no matter how much, to cause scintilla of doubt in the integrity of the overall voting process. The side that loses a vote is always looking for signs of illegal or impacted voting as a reason to declare the already taken election null and void. An attacker would not even have to actually do anything, except make it seem as if something malicious happened. It doesn't take much mischief to move our society to anger and violence.
