# Chapter 16 Attacks Against Biometrics 327

Biometrics are based on the core idea that many physical traits (and sometimes behaviors) are unique within the known world. Most were established during embryonic development and with us since birth. No one has our fingerprints. No one has our irises, retina, face, or voice. Or that's the theory, and so far, no one has ever demonstrated anything different for their particular traits. But it's not like we have actually taken everyone's biometric traits and done thorough search to conclusively prove that our biometric traits are unique in the world. We haven't taken every of the world's 7 billion people's fingerprints and compared them to one another, much less compared them to the over 100 billion people who have ever lived. We had to do that to get a definitive answer. Until then, we will have to agree that we've found any definitive matches in the people we have taken (beyond identical twins for some traits), and the likelihood of two (identical twins except) individuals sharing the same biometric traits is at least exceeding rare.

Even when identical twins, originating from the same egg and sperm, sharing the same DNA, have different fingerprints and often varying genetic material. Twins usually have genetic differences and varying environments that can and do shape some of their biometric traits differently. That's why even identical twins can be differentiated by humans (and computers), even though they can be very similar looking, especially the older they. Small genetic differences (which exist 80 percent of the time) can be detected using special tests. Read more on detecting the genetic variances in twins here: www.nytimes.com/2019/03/01/science/twins-dna-crime-paternity.html.

# Common Biometric Authentication Factors

Biometric authentication has become popular because our biometric traits are likely unique in the world; change slowly, if at all; and are carried on all the time without any additional effort. We can't easily lose them like we can a smartcard or USB token. This section explores the most popular biometric traits used in digital authentication systems.

# Fingerprints

Fingerprints are created by the naturally existing, uniquely shaped peaks and valleys (known as friction ridges) on a person's fingers (hands, feet, and toes, albeit named differently). A person touching nearly every surface that isn't thoroughly aqueous or a gas is leaving behind a fingerprint (at least partial) due to the natural oils (a mixture of water and fatty acids) or dirt located on the fingers or on the surface touched. Flat, solid surfaces are best, although fingerprints are routinely lifted from clothing, rubber, and even powders.
