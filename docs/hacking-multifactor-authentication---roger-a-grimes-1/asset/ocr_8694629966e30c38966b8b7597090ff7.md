# Identity Management

Most authentication systems depend on using an *identity management* (IDM) system, which handles all the possible identities throughout “*life cycle stages*” from creation (i.e., *provisioning*) to removal/ (*deprovisioning*). The user may create their own identity, or it may be assigned by another user (i.e., admin) or through an automated process. For example, in an automated process a new employee added to other computer systems. And if the employee has a separation-of-event, the employee's separation may result in the employee's accounts automatically becoming suspended, disabled, or removed. In fact, the process of automated, delegated user account creation and deletion is preferred by cybersecurity professionals.

In a secure environment, it is critical that the person or process (known as *access governance*) initiating a new subject identity account verify the subject to be needed in the IDM system as well as verify their real-life identity or before starting the new account or setup. Without the validity of these steps, an authenticated identity cannot be considered valid or assured at all.
