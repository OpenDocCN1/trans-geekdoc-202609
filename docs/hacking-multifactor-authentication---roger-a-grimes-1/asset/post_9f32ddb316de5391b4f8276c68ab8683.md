## Biometrics

In computer security, biometrics are personal authenticators linked to physical human body composition (i.e., physiological) or behaviors that can uniquely identify a person. To be used in biometric authentication, a biometric trait should meet the following requirements:

- It is universally unique to a specific person.
- It is permanent; it does not change except very slowly over many years, if at all.
- It is measurable in most people.
- It is easy to secure.
- It can be measured quickly (no longer than a few seconds).
- It is fast and easy to compare against stored biometric traits to confirm or deny an authentication attempt.

The common biometric traits used for authentication are fingerprint, face, or voice, but only slightly less popular is hand geometry, palm veins, iris, and retina. There is increasing interest in typing patterns and rhythms like keystroke dynamics, which essentially links biometric attributes to distinct, unique behavioral outcomes. We even obscure biometric projects based on traits like eye movements, walking gait, handwriting, brain activity, heart activity, and even scent/odor. One day I suppose we can get an immediate, very detailed DNA analysis, that may become the ultimate biometric trait for use for authentication (although it turns out fingerprints are far easier to use and are likely more unique).

> NOTE Biometrics are even being used to recognize individual animals (e.g., zebras, manta rays, dolphins) based on their physical characteristics to better track them.
