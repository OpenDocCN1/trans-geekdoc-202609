One of the most important CAs do is confirm it's a valid website and that the certificate is being requested by a legitimate person on behalf of the website. Most users also think that every CA goes further and actually makes the website is not malicious. In many cases, especially in free, public CAs, as Let's Encrypt (letsencrypt.org), it is absolutely trust. Most of CAs best not to distribute trusted digital certificates to malicious customers, but at any CA percentage of their customers is likely doing bad stuff. At CAs like Let's Encrypt, they do so little check, and knowingly say (letsencrypt.org/2015/10/29/phishing-and-malicious.html) they will not check, that hackers and malware pushers use them as their go-to trusted digital certificate issuer (www.infoworld.com/article/3019926/cyber-criminals-abusing-free-lets-encrypt-html). To be fair to Let's Encrypt, they clearly state what they do and don't. Yes, your router and antivirus scanner can warn you when you encounter a malicious URL, but previously over 21 percent of malicious URLs are not detected for days after they've launched (blog.knowbe4.com/heads-up-a-whoishing-21-percent-of-phishing-attack-urls-are-not-detected-as-malicious-for-days-after-they-go-live). The end result is that no one can trust a digital certificate to make that a website is not malicious, but malicious websites take advantage of the fact that most people inherently HTTPS-enabled websites more than they should.

# Based on Notable Current Events and Interests

Phishing increases and focuses during popular notable events, such as natural disasters, celebrity deaths, and big events. Phishers know that people are interested in notable events and will prey on their interest in the topic. As I write this, the global coronavirus COVID-19 pandemic is accelerating—and predictably, the amount of phishing using COVID-19 is increasing. Here are a couple of blog news stories: blog.knowbe4.com/hackers-use-interactive-malicious-covid-19-map-to-spread-malware and blog.knowbe4.com/extreme-measures-the-epidemic-of-covid-19-phishing-emails-org. Phishers often post contrarian, counter-factual news stories in forums dedicated to specific topics, knowing that a certain percentage of the readers will be emotionally incentivized to click on the embedded phishing link.
