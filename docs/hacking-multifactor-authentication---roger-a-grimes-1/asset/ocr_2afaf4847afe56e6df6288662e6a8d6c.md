# Hacking Multifactor Authentication

# Require an Acquaintance to Approve Recovery

Personal approval by a prereistered acquaintance, boss, coworker, or friend is one way to significantly cut down on rogue recoveries (although even acquaintances can be socially engineered). Early on, support technicians manually verified account recovery by personally recognizing the person's voice (or in-person request). On large systems not each tech support person can recognize every legitimate user. Instead, an authentication recovery system can ask any user to designate two or more acquaintances who can be used to verify the requester's account recovery. Any approval must come from at least one of the designated verifiers, who are instructed to confirm, by voice or in person, the legitimate party needs the account recovery. This type of control was used in Microsoft when I was there and worked well, although there were times when the designated acquaintances were unreachable and the recovery operation was delayed until they responded. Implementing a defense like this is easy to do in online, automated way, but it has been done, as in the case of Facebook's Trusted Contacts feature www.facebook.com/help/1198975144086.

# Use Advanced, Adaptive Personal-Knowledge Questions

This chapter singles personal-knowledge questions with specific criticism. The recommendation is that simple personal-knowledge questions should not be used, but there are many, far more secure implementations of advanced, adaptive, personal-knowledge questions that many of the major sites now use, such as the recovery process and questions used by Google and Microsoft. Anyone wanting a master's class in how to do personal-knowledge questions should study either of those two vendors do.

+   * First and foremost, they do not for store the kind of simple personal-knowledge questions listed earlier. They know how easy they are to hack and guess, and how hard they are for users to remember. They store the unique identity of the devices used by the user (e.g., computer, cell phone) and consider to be high risk any account recovery coming from a new location where the user has never before successfully authenticated from. When elevated risk is detected, the questions and processes require more to be successful.

They start by asking a more advanced series of questions that the site already knows the answer to the user having to. And these questions get progressively harder and detailed as the risk increases (due to incorrect answers or measured authentication traits). They simply asked me or more questions, such as my previous buying experience, more related, more detailed questions. This is the way to do detailed, adaptive, personal-knowledge questions.
