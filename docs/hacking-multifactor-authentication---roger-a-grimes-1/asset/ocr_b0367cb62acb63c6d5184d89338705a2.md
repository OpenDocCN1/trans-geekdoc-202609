On military bases and at higher security organizations, this sort of one-time verified trust in real life has been abused by trusted partners or to unleash unexpected, violent, murderous attacks against their followers.

Zero trust networks use the user or device come as a gateway device or software program, which authenticates the user or device initially but also continuously thereafter, during the subject's entire experience. Authentication is not checked or after a set period of time, but continuously, all the time, for every attempted access and movement.

If I compare zero trust with the real-world scenario I described earlier involving the military base, all soldiers would be constantly monitored. If a soldier, who was suddenly penned a loaded weapon and lots of ammunition, would be more carefully monitored or perhaps relieved of their weapon until evaluators determined if the soldier was carrying the weapon and ammunition for a legitimate reason. I grossly oversimplifying the problem and solution, but hopefully you get the idea. Everyone's actions and rewards are evaluated all the time.

Zero trust networks are likely the future of networking and authentication. According to this article (https://www.networkworld.com/article/3487270/the-vpn-is-dying-long-live-zero-trust.html) "Gartner predicts that by 2023, 60 percent of enterprises will phase out most of their VPNs in favor of zero trust network access, which can take the form of a gateway or broker that authenticates both device and user allowing role-based, context-aware access."

# Continuous, Adaptive, Risk-Based

Continuous, adaptive, risk-based authentication is absolutely the future of authentication, both on large authentication services (as they are partially deployed today) and what will eventually be deployed on our organizations' networks. MFA will still play a big part, but how the multifactor components are used and delivered will be the question.

Like passwords, today's traditional MFA solutions will be with us for a long time, a decade or longer. Moving from the traditional "one-time authentication and you're approved" will not happen overnight. Continuous, adaptive, risk-based authentication will essentially take everything we have learned so far in computer security, every tool we have created, and will put it all together in a real-time, constantly evaluating, automated way.
