I was sharing the news of my column with the CEO of KnowBe4, Stu Sjouwerman, the next morning when he wisely suggested I create a presentation on the topic and start giving it. Within a few days, I created a new presentation 12 Ways to Defeat Multi-Factor Authentication (Info .knowbe4/webinar-12-ways-to-defeat-mfa). As I did more research and thinking, I quickly came up with ways to hack MFA nearly every day.

I'm over 50 ways, all of which I share in this book. The presentation turned into a whitepaper. At KnowBe4, the average whitepaper is three to five pages long; mine was 20 pages. It was the longest whitepaper in the history of KnowBe4, and it quickly became a running joke around the office and I shared that I had originally created a rough draft double size and that the 20 pages were my trimmed-down version after their chiding. That then led it into becoming a short e-book (www.knowbe4.com/how-to-hack-multi-factor-authentication) at 40 pages.

I began my presentation around the world, including at the biggest computer security conferences, RSA and Black Hat. In both places I had standing room-only crowds and lines of attendees trying to come in some of the hack discussions. My 12 Ways to Multi-Factor Authentication presentation was so long that I have to choose fifth of the hacks I am going to share with audiences, although Kevin's original MFA hacking demo is still clearly a crowd favorite (and I asked the URL for it in this book).

Jim Minatel, my longtime friend and acquisitions editor at Wiley, came to see my presentation at RSA and saw the enthusiastic crowds. I was sick as a dog when I gave the presentation, In fact, I was hospitalized for the week day after the presentation with an acute, life-threatening illness. I felt like I had a terrible job to present managing. I would certainly love a favor do-over. But Jim saw the crowds and the energy the material generated and asked if I would write a book on the subject. I said yes over lunch, and this is true. The best part is that I've given hundreds and hundreds of pages to share everything I know on the subject. Even then I'm sure more books of the same size could be written on the subject. Multifactor authentication and its weaknesses are many. In truth, even this book is just scratching the surface. It's 500-plus pages of summary material. I hope all readers understand the strengths and weaknesses of multifactor authentication and that MFA developers will create better, more secure, solutions.

The ultimate objective of this book is to properly frame the security and weaknesses of all MFA solutions. If you only know the benefits and none of the risks, you're more likely to implement an MFA solution without the appropriate policies, controls, and education. This book is a push-back against the overwhelming marketing messages by some MFA vendors. MFA solutions can significantly reduce many faults of cybersecurity, but they aren't perfect panacea and does not mean we can throw away all the previous security lessons learned. If you come away with a suitable understanding of what MFA can and can't, and change your practices and controls appropriately, I've done my job.
