For example, a hacker might register vvatchguard.com hoping to trick users wishing to visit watchguard.com or register micosoft.com hoping to trick users wishing to visit microsoft.com. The latter is known as a “type squatting” domain, where the owner of the rogue domain is counting on accidentally missing the legitimate domain without realizing it and being redirected to the rogue site. If the rogue site looks decently legitimate, the misguided user may never realize their mistake and interact with the site as trusted site.

Figure 18.4 shows a real-world example of a phishing email sent to me by my previous roger_grimes@infoworld.com The sender sent an email claiming to be from Microsoft, with microsoftonline.com in the URL The hacker was hoping I was an unsuspecting victim who didn’t know how DNS addresses work. They hoped I would use the microsoftonline.com and assume it was the origin domain The real domain is devopsnw.com, which is not affiliated with Microsoft. Devopsnw.com was likely an innocent domain that was successfully hacked and for this phishing campaign, or a real domain registered by the phisher. (In the latter case, such domains are usually removed when enough complaints come into the DNS registrar, or they are listed on blacklisting services.)
