# Natural and Artificial Biometric Variances

The biggest impediment to more accurate biometrics is simply that biometrics are measuring living tissue in a changing world. There isn't a second when our bodies are exactly the same as before. We are constantly replacing and shedding off old cells for new cells. Our environment is changing us every second. The light, air, and dust we interact every second is changing us. Quantum mechanics means that billions of particles are entangling with our bodies every second of every day.

> **NOTE** Not to get too astophysics on you, but there are 65 billion particles called neutrinos passing at the speed of light through every square centimeter of our body every second. Look it up.

Every day our biometric traits are changing in some way. Most of those changes don't show up with enough volume or intermediacy to change our biometrics in a noticeable way that would be detected by most biometric authentication systems. But eventually they do accumulate in enough quantity to make a noticeable change. Our skin ages. We get wrinkles. Our irises and blood vessels change. One day I didn't notice varicose veins and then one I did. Our voices change. I distinctly remember my voice noticeably changing in just a few days as I entered puberty. My face is notably plumper than it was in high school. I've become far more gray over time.

# 342 Hacking Multifactor Authentication

Day-to-day changes occur due to my environment and my interactions with my environment. I get abrasions on my face and hands. I get dirty. Glue or caulk from some projects gets embedded on my fingers. I get cuts and scars. Colds and allergies change my voice. Some days I'm more swollen than others. cold skin is contracted. The few ridges on my fingers are a slightly different size and have slightly different drift and artifacts in them.

If face, hands, or fingerprint authentication systems were tuned too high accuracy was everything, it would end up causing many false rejections of minutiae that really isn't due to different faces, hands, or fingerprints. The reality is that biometric systems are intention “de-tuned” to be less accurate than otherwise could be. No one is going to use iTouch to unlock their cell phone if it works more than it. People want to work most of the time if not every day, even if it is not accurate as it could. And to do, these systems have false accuracy than the sensors and software are the information they collect. And this tuning increases the risk of false acceptance errors. Every single biometric system is a fight between too many false acceptance errors and too many false rejection errors. Vendors want to set an acceptable level of both that most customers will accept. Too many errors in either direction and potential customers using someone else's drift gets the rate high (for the customer). 

# Missing Biometric Traits

Not everyone has the biometric traits being measured. Our world is full of people missing digits, hands, and eyes. Every in 10,000 people are born many. Many people have glass eyes and are blind. Millions of people have eye injuries and changes that change their eyeball significantly in short periods of time. People with dermatoglyphia have no fingerprints. I have musician friends who played guitars long enough to have no on the main pads of their fingers. I had a friend who worked in a factory making sandpaper for life. His hands were permanently stained and looked dirty; he had to dip his hands into water before work to make them clean, and this process removed his fingerprints. Criminals intentionally modify their fingerprints (as an example: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fingerprint#media/File:Altered_Fingerprints_of_Alvin_karpis.jpg) or get face-altering plastic surgery.

Many reasons for that cannot be easily explained, some simply cannot be valid dated-to-day using the biometric trait as everyone else in the organization. Anyone who has a large biometric trait will tell you that there are some people who cannot use the selected biometric system day-to-day for one reason or another, often unknown. I know a woman whose fingerprints would not authenticate a second time when the fingerprints were taken, be it a day or just minutes later. Everyone looked at her fingerprints and they seemed to be as normal as any had ever seen. She didn't shake or move her fingers strangely when placing them on the fingerprint reader. There were no obvious defects or dirt. She just couldn successfully use our fingerprint authentication system, and so was frustrated as we. heard of similar measurement issues from other biometric system administrators. That doesn't happen with passwords, USB tokens, or most of the other MFA solution methods.

# Easy to Bypass Using Fake Traits

There isn't a biometric system that cannot be faked. Some are harder to fake than others, but they all can be bypassed using faked, simulated biometric traits. If someone is telling you that a biometric system can be faked, they are trying to sell it to you. There's often 16-year-old kid on YouTube showing who can fake it using materials. More on this later.

# Theft Causes Irreversible Damage

The majority of our commonly used biometrics are easily spread wherever we've traveled. Our fingerprints and DNA are everywhere. Capturing internal blood vessels with enough accuracy would be might the exception, although it probably wouldn't be hard to trick anyone submitting to a rogue biometric reader like gasoline pump/ATM criminals capture credit card details. With physical access, an adversary could steal "skimming" device at any biometric reader to record biometric traits. Far more are the wholesale thefts of entire databases (several examples in a moment) used by vendors and customers. Once your biometric trait is stolen, how can any system that relies on that be assured it is really you to log in? Compare to non-biometric authentication factors like passwords or tokens, where we all need to replace a stolen one is delete the old one and enable a new one. Very easy to do. We do it all the time. The damage to theft is not permanent, unless that led to your biometric traits.
