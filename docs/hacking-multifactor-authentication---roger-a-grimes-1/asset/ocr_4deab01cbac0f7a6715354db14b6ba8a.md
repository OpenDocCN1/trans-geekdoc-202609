# Unhackable Quantum Cryptography

My previous book, Cryptography Apocalypse (Wiley, 2019), is on quantum computers and how quantum computers will one day break traditional public key cryptography. It also covers how quantum computers and devices can be used to make hard-to-break quantum cryptography. According to quantum physicists, the very laws of physics prove that quantum cryptography can't be broken—that break quantum cryptography you have to break the laws of physics, and you are not doing. So, you will hear all these theoretical physicists state quantum cryptography is unbreakable. Of course, they are wrong.

Although quantum cryptography may be theoretically unhackable, practical application is far different. One, it really isn't unhackable in the first place. What they mean by "unhackable" is quantum information cannot be eavesdropped on without the sender and receiver knowing. That's not the same as "can't be eavesdropped on." It means that if it gets eavesdropped on, the legitimate parties will know it. If critical information was eavesdropped on, it will still be stolen—it's just that the legitimate parties know it happened.

Second, and more important, referring to the previous section, "Unhackable Fallacy," humans have never made anything that is unhackable. We've tried. We've thought we could. And in every case, we've been wrong. To be human is to be fallible. We make mistakes. And those lead to hacks.

Even if we were somehow able to make unhackable quantum cryptography, we would mess up implementing it in the real world. It's what happens. We take something that is perfect in theory and run it in practice. It's the way of the world. And I don't see quantum properties and quantum physics, which are inherently as complex as humans have messed with, suddenly being the thing we got right. Odds, are that quantum cryptography will be hacked just like everything else in the world we created.
