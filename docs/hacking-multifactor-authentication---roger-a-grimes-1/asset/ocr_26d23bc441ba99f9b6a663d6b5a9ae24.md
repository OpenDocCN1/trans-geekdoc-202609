# MFA Works

In a book dedicated to hacking MFA, some readers may come away with the idea that MFA is bad or not worth using. Let me be clear: MFA decreases cybersecurity risk in many use cases, especially general account takeover (ATO) scenarios. But there is a difference between decreasing risk in many scenarios and MFA not being hackable.
MFA stops hacking in many scenarios, especially phishing attacks that ask for the user's passwords and then uses those fraudulently obtained credentials to log on to the victim's legitimate sites and services. If you don't have a password to be stolen it will not work against you. MFA also stops many types of traditional attacks against sites and services that require MFA for login. Unless an intruder finds a way around the MFA requirement in those scenarios, MFA decreases risk.
