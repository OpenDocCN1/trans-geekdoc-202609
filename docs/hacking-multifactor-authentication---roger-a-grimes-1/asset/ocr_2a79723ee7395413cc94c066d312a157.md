**Chapter 3: Types of Authentication** Chapter 3 covers dozens of types of authentication, describes how they differ from one another, and examines the inherent strengths and weaknesses of each type of solution.

**Chapter 4: Usability, Security** Security is always a trade-off between user-friendliness and security. MFA is no exception. The most secure options often not be tolerated by end users. Chapter 4 covers the fundamental challenges of good security and when good security actually becomes so onerous that it becomes bad security. The best options are good trade-offs between usability and security. Find out when that line is crossed.

**Part II: Hacking MFA** This part of the book covers various ways to hack and attack various MFA solutions. Mitigations and defenses for each of the attacks are detailed in each chapter.

**Chapter 5: Hacking MFA in General** Chapter 5 begins by explaining the very high-level ways that MFA can be hacked, with some of the various techniques. Every MFA is susceptible to multiple hacking attacks and are covered in the rest of the chapter of this section.

**Chapter 6: Access Control Token Tricks** Chapter 6 starts by discussing, in detail, one of the most popular, decades-long, MFA hacking methods: of compromising the resulting access control token. Chapter 6 shows multiple ways in which access control tokens can be compromised.

**Chapter 7: Endpoint Attacks** A compromised device or computer can be attacked in hundreds of ways, including bypassing or hijacking MFA solutions. A compromised endpoint cannot be trusted. Chapter 7 discusses popular endpoint attacks.

**Chapter 8: SMS Attacks** Chapter 8 covers multiple Short Message Service (SMS) attacks, including subscriber identity module (SIM) hacks. For years now, the U.S. government has said that SMS should not be used for strong authentication and yet the most common MFA solutions on the Internet involve SMS. Learn why it shouldn't be the case.

**Chapter 9: One-Time Password Attacks** One-time password (OTP) solutions are among the most popular MFA solutions, and they are good but not perfect. Chapter 9 covers the various types of OTP attacks and how to hack them.

**Chapter 10: Subject Hijack Attacks** Unlike of the other MFA attacks described in this book, subject hijack attacks are not very popular. In fact, they have not been accomplished in a single public attack. Still, they can be done, and simply knowing about and how they can be accomplished is an important lesson. Chapter 10 covers one specific type of subject hijack attack on the world's most popular corporate authentication platform, in enough detail, that you will likely be worried about them forever.
