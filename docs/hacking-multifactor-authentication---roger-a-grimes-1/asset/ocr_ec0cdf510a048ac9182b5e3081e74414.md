# SIM Swap Methods

For over a decade, hackers have been obtaining a legitimate subscriber's SIM information and transferring it to a phone in their possession. This can be done in many ways:

+   - Social engineering of the user
- Social engineering of the network provider
- Insider attack at the network provider
- SIM information-stealing malware
- SMS network infrastructure exploitation

It is very common for SIM information to socially engineer a user, via email or phone, to get enough information so that the hacker can socially engineer the user's cell phone provider into switching the user's SIM information to the hacker's cell phone network. Hackers have also paid cell phone provider employees to perform SIM transfers.

No matter how it is accomplished, the SIM information is installed and active on the new cell phone, the old (original, legitimate) cell phone stops receiving calls and messages. Other than an "out of network" indicator, most victims realize for hours that their SIM information has been transferred. Usually the victim notices after a few hours then they realize they haven't received any calls or messages for hours. Usually only then they realize they do not have cellular service. The victim may think they are involved in a normal, temporary disruption. By the time they realize it's a temporary service disruption, they have to find out a way to call their cell phone provider's technical support. Usually at this point the victim is still unaware that they are victims of a crime and that their SIM information has been transferred to an attacker's phone.

Normally they call their cell phone provider's technical support and are told something like "You activated your new phone" or something like that by the technical support person. When the victim says they did not do that, the victim and the tech support proceed to get the SIM swap back to the original. It can take as long as days for the victim to get their cell phone service back. During all those hours the attacker is usually using their ill-gotten SMS access to their advantage and to the victim's disadvantage.

When a hacker does a malicious SIM swap, SMS-based MFA messages are sent to the hacker s cell phone instead of the victim. The hacker can then compromise your online account using the misrouted SMS-based MFA message. Usually whatever the hacker goes into the user's MFA-protected services (Gmail, Microsoft 365, etc.) and act like they cannot access the account without getting an SMS recovery message.
