And any good evaluator of MFA solutions needs to be aware that there are scenarios where MFA actually increase the risk of a successful attack. Many people are shocked to hear this. I, too, was taken aback the first time someone told me they went back to using names and passwords. After dozens of people told me the same, I understand that it isn't an uncommon reason and why they did so.

For example, if an MFA solution is SMS-based, it means that a SIM swap attack or social engineering my phone company's tech support folks may be enough to get around the MFA authentication defenses. SIM victims didn't do anything stupid. They relied on the MFA solution to provide them with what they were told "better" security than what they were previously using—and reliance ended up hurting them.

If they use SMS-based MFA, maybe they would use a regular name and password. If they use a unique name and password for each service, what are the odds that they will be phished over versus a SIM swapping happening? That risk comparison needs to be evaluated. Personally, I trust my ability not to be phished over my authentication to a phone company that hasn't so far (at least right now). So, I use a name and password only to protect some of my most valuable and important resources. And I don't regret it. It's absolutely the right security decision for me, and many in others, in many high-risk scenarios.

Of course, I think MFA can provide cybersecurity risk in many or most scenarios. The bigger problem I have is that no single MFA works in all my scenarios. That means I have to pick and choose scenarios I need to protect and decide among the available MFA solutions to protect them. When trying to pick the "best" MFA solution for me or one of my organizations, I use a proven process. It's one you can use, too.

# The Process for Selecting the Right MFA Solution

The process for selecting the “right” MFA solution for you and your organization consists of series of steps:

+   1. Create a project team.
2. Create a project plan.
3. Educate.
4. Determine what needs to be protected.
5. Choose required and desired details.
6. Research/select vendor solutions.
7. Conduct a pilot project.
8. Select a winner.
9. Deploy to production.
