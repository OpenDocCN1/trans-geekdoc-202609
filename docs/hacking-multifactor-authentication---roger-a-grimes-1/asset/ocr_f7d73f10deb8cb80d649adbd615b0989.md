All MFA solutions have trade-offs. Some are easier to use than others. Some are cheaper to purchase, install, and operate. Some integrate with more things. Some require separate devices be purchased. Some with authentication factors seem simply to integrate and be accepted into your organization better than others. Some companies provide external OTP tokens, like software-only solutions, and some really like biometric protection.

You may want to consider not using MFA at all may be the right solution for you at this time, either for or for particular sites and services. MFA doesn't work with everything, so what it means you have to make trade-offs.

MFA is not always needed. I don't want to authenticate when performing Internet queries or researching things on public, commonly available websites (like Google). I don't even like to authenticate to the various, one-off websites I use to buy things during the year. I don not necessarily need them to protect my personally identifiable information (PII). I do rather as anonymously as possible for the average person, although I don't go to the extremes of using one-use credit cards or using pseudo-anonymous identities. I like to use MFA where it makes sense for the values I'm trying to protect to prevent easy access by an unauthorized person.

We make similar risk decisions all the time in the real world. My door is locked at night my mailbox and external garage isn't. My garage has yard equipment I don't want to store, but in the absence of any criminal issues over the last 10 years in my neighborhood, I accept the risk that goes on with leaving it unlocked. I do have offsetting cameras that will alert me to activity in my yard.

I do lock my cars all time, it's apparent that many people in my county don't read the local crime reports. I'm always amazed by how many people with loaded guns in their car don't lock their cars at night. They obviously have different risk profiles compared to me. So, in general, in real and digital life, we choose what we do and not to strongly secure depending on the scenario. That's life.
