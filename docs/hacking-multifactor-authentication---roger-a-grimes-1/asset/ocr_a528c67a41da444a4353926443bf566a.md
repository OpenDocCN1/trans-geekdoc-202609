I can call from any phone claiming to be the president of the United States, using a second person acting as my "call setup" assistant, and with enough mimicry it might be tough for any receiver to prove that I'm not the president. Comic radio DJs do it all the time. When someone calls us claiming to be from our company bank, or phone company, rarely do we tell the caller to prove it in trust, irrefutable, and maybe.

Most phishers avoid real-life phone calls for a number of reasons, including many times the scammer is of a different nationality and may even speak the victim's native tongue well enough to pull off the scam or answer basic questions. Most phone callers receive any vendor's call, even if coming from someone sounding foreign, to be able to easily communicate in the customer's expected language and know basic details about the customer's referenced matter. Using a real phone number (versus the Internet) slightly increases the risk of being caught, but only slightly versa.

# Nonphysical

Direct person-to-person social engineering happens all the time, but criminals like the least of all possible methods. First, and foremost, the scammer can be more easily detected and arrested. Second, it involves appearing in person and answering any skeptical person's questions. Third, it is a very inefficient way of scam a large group of people. Most people do not fall for social engineering scams. This means scammers must try their phishing scam on a bunch of people for each unlucky victim who takes the bait.
