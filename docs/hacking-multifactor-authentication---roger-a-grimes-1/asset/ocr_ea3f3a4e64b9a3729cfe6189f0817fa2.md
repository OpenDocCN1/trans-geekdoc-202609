The newer reader types were created to explicitly defeat spoofled fingerprints printed out images or placed on flat surfaces, like glass or pictures. The newer liveness/realness-detection technologies possibly work because they require a 3D surface (i.e., the shape of a real fingerprint) and/or something conducts current like a real finger. The idea is that using these newer technologies could make it harder for forgers to spoof fingerprints. But as you will see, these particular anti-spoof technologies are not undetectable.

Fingerprint readers will ultimately record the entire fingerprint as a single image, map the traces and artifacts, or record the main points where the ridges change pattern or minutiae. In fingerprint science, there are nine generally recognized patterns known under the general classifications of whor, loop, and arch. See this article for detail on the different patterns: legalagle.com/7287158-nine-different-types-fingerprint.html. Minutiae is a term in fingerprint referring to artifacts smaller than a ridge with names like ridge ending, bifurcation, island and bridge.

Fingers have veins and arteries underneath the skin that can be mapped like fingerprints. Like fingerprints, finger vascular vessel shapes, patterns and patterns are thought to be universally unique per person. Finger vascular vessel biometrics is not as popular as fingerprints but have some potential advantages. Finger vein scanners use near-infrared light (700–900 nanometers) of the electromagnetic spectrum, where the hemoglobin of blood absorbs far more than the other components of the finger, allowing the scanner to get a very picture of the finger's vascular vessels. It is harder for a forger to secretly capture a victim's finger vascular vessels because patterns aren't lately around after a person has touched. To capture them, a forger would have to have the victim inately put their finger in a vein reader or steal the data from another system where it was already stored. There is more information on finger biometric authentication here: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finger_vein_recognition.
