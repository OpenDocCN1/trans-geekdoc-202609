# Disease Transmission

Lastly, shared biometric readers that require physical touching using a common device (e.g., fingerprint readers, handprints, geometry, finger vessel scanners, eyeball readers) can easily spread disease. If a company offers disinfectant solutions or wipes for users to avail themselves of, there is absolutely no assurance those quick remedies actually work. From discussion with health-officials on preventing the transmission of diseases between people recommend copious amounts of disinfectants and handover a minute or longer period of time—certainly more than just a quick, simple application of a gel or wipes. Not to mention airborne diseases that will collect in common waiting areas where shared biometric devices are forced to be used.

Taken as a whole, biometrics aren't the perfect or accurate authentication that many people make them out to be. This is not to say they can't be aren't a good solution in many application scenarios. People shouldn't think they are accurate and without flaws. If you use biometrics, you should do so with a clear idea of their strengths and weaknesses.
