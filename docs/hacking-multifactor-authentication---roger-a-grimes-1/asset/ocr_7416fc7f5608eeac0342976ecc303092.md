A site/service can also request access to your personal information, files, and data. Figure 19.5 shows Dropbox requesting access to see and download a subject's Gmail contact list. You should only allow the access if you trust the requesting site/service plans to do with that. Not-allowing the requested access cancels the requesting site/service's ability to use OAuth with that provider, if you don't allow the permission request, you can't use OAuth with that additional site/service.

> > Be aware that if site/service gets compromised, any attackers would have the same access to your personal information, give out any personal access to your data and information quickly and sparsely.

OAuth identity providers allow you to see a list of every previously allowed site/service (see Figure 19.6 for an example), and you can remove that site/service from your list of OAuth site/services you allow to interact with that particular provider on your behalf (see Figure 19.7 for an example).
