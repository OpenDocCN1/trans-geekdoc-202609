# 14.5 Conclusion

It is important to stress that cognitive vision does not exist in isolation as a mechanistic system that learns to detect what is where; this is in direct contrast to how vision is predominantly studied today. Since the seminal works of Held and Hein (1963), Stein and Meredith (1993), and Milner and Goodale (1995) and the stream of experiments on the visual coding of space and actions by Graziano, Yap, and Gross (1994), Graziano et al. (2002), and Fadiga et al. (2000), it has become evident that, apart from the very early processing stages, cognition operates through a unified representation wherein vision and other sensory modalities are dynamically merged through action.
