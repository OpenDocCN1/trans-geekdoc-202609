# 16.2 Techniques to Capture Human Information on Robot Manipulation

# 16.2.1 Surface Electromyography Signals

Ideally, to be used for robot manipulation, sEMG signals reflect human muscle activation and emb ed rich information about human joint motion, force, stiffness, and so on. Gener ally, sEMG signals can be processed into two divisions: finite class recognition serials and continuous control reference. The former usually refers to pattern recognition, such as hand posture recognition (Chu et al. 2006; Khezri and Jahed 2007), and such data serials are usually used for switch control, while the latter refers to extracting continuous force, stiffness, and even motion serials from sEMG signals, which reflect the variations of human limb kinematics and dynamics during limb movement or pose maintenance. Furthermore, the relationship between sEMG and stiffness, force, and motion is approximately linear, and thus biocontroller design tends to be simple in sEMG-based robot control systems.
