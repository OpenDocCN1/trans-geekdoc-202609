We argue rather than designing monetary policy independently, which is a challenging task in both practice and theory, efficient applications of DLT should tokenize stable financial instruments. These instruments could be denominated in fiat currencies, for instance, the US dollars. Later on, if these efforts are successful, one should tokenize more diverse asset pools. Such pools can counterbalance fiat currencies and be very useful for cross-border transfers.

In our estimation, and contrary to numerous unfunded claims, it is not currently possible to build a genuinely decentralized blockchain. This observation is partly because the determination of a crypto's coin price in terms of a fiat currency requires an oracle and automatically breaks decentralization. Such oracles are prone to manipulation, and more importantly, at odds with the coin's claimed decentralized nature. Ideally, one should be able to directly observe this price from the blockchain in a distributed fashion. Unfortunately, at present, it is not quite possible for both conceptual and technical reasons. Hence, any potentially successful stablecoin combine centralized and decentralized features. The degree of centralization vs. decentralization can vary, as explained below.

# 8.4.2 Stablecoins fully collateralized with fiat

Nicolas Copernicus, who, in addition to his mathematical and astronomical studies, consulted the Prussian Diet on monetary issues, articulated his requirements for sound money as follows; Taylor (1995): 1. “It must not be changed in value after right deliberation 2. One single coin must be chosen for the minting of the money, which must be in the name of the currency and not in the name of a single city.... 3. When a new currency is issued, the old currency must be demonetized and withdrawn from circulation.... 4. It is essential to have an inviolable and unchangeable rule to mint only 20 marks and no more from a pool of silver, deduct the quantity of silver necessary to cover the expenses of coinage.... 5. Too great a quantity of money must not be issued.... 6. All the kinds of coins should be issued at the same time....

It is truly astonishing to realise what extent of Copernicus's laws are valid today. For instance, the designers of the proposed cryptocurrency Libra miss the third postulate entirely. They do not understand and refuse to accept that Libra can create enormous inflationary pressures, especially in the emerging markets that it is supposed to help in the first place. However, such cavalier approach to creating new currencies is quite typical for most technologically innovative but financially naive straights.

Custodial stablecoins are relatively centralized. A single party performs the creation and destruction of such coins. However, once a coin is created, it can freely move on the corresponding blockchain before being eventually destroyed. This semi-centralized design, custodial coins are particularly prone to rogue influences, and, therefore, must be regulatory to survive. Several coins of this nature, including Tether (USDT), TrueUSD (TUSD), USD Coin (USDC), and Sila, already exist; several others are currently on the drawing board.
