# 2.2.8 Where does money come from?

After decades of bickering, the three schools of thought emerged: (A) credit theory of banking; (B) fractional reserve theory of banking; (C) financial intermediation theory of banking.

The creation theory of banking dominated the discourse in the 19th and early 20th centuries; several excellent books and papers, as MacLeod (1905): Mitchell-Innes (1914); Hahn (1920); Wicksell (1913); Werner (2005, 2014). Unfortunately, gradually lost its ground and was overtaken by the fractional reserve theory of banking; for example, Marshall (1887); Keynes (1930); Samu and Nordhaus (1995). The financial intermediation theory is the current champion; see Bernoulli and Binder

> ⁸Depreciation of the denarius had a lot of unintended consequences. In the Roman Empire, legionaries received their pay in worthless denarius (arius), a ration paid in kind (annona), and a bonus in gold coins and silver ingots. This was based on the accession of a new Emperor and five years of his reign (donatives); Elton (1998). In particular, the accession bonus was five gold coins and a pound of silver. As a result, the faded Roman legions became a pale shadow of their former selves, and the Empire collapsed. A cautionary tale for those who advocate bankers' bonuses or huge fund managers payouts.
