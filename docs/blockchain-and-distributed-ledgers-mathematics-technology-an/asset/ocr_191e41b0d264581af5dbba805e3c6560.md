# 4.13 Summary

In the chapter, which, by necessity, is somewhat technical, we introduce several important mathematical and technical concepts. In principle, a reader can go through the book without spending too much time on this chapter and taking for granted the existence of critical cryptographic primitives as elliptic curve cryptography, digital signature algorithm, and hash functions. However, for those who design new blockchains or pondering an adequate scheme for their project, this chapter equip them with the analysis framework. Moreover, individuals whose participation in the blockchain space is less technical will benefit from learning new concepts and appreciating the space's complexity. By its very nature, we show that cryptography is a natural mechanism for maintaining the integrity of computerized record keeping. This fact naturally makes the foundational tool for building financial and non-financial applications. While symmetric cryptography is essential for securely storing data in databases and other applications), it is secondary to transact over the Internet. At the same time, asymmetric cryptography is the primary instrument in this respect.

PKI, which includes public/secret key pair creation, distribution, and usage, provides a robust framework for building distributed applications, such as cryptocurrencies and blockchains.
