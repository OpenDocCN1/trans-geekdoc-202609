Let us turn our attention to the PM in alleviating climate change. The immense ramifications of climate change affect all aspects of society, we focus here on its profound financial cost. According to the Bank of England: "Climate change goes significant risks to the economy and to the financial system, and these risks may seem abstract and far away, they are in fact real, fast approaching, and in need of swift and immediate action to stymie their potential destruction." Yet, it is clear that protests alone are insufficient to convince people to make their behavior more environmentally friendly. Thus, we need to design suitable financial tools and incentives that push people into the right direction, of course. One possibility is the use of programmable green (GPM), first introduced by Pentland et al. (2020) as the environmental digital trade coin (EDTC). GPM is a special case of the Digital Trade Coin (DTC) discussed in Chapter 8: see Lipton et al. (2018b

We can structure GPM on a coalition loyalty program which aggregates single issuer loyalty programs that participants accrue and redeem loyalty points at various participating businesses.7 The GPM ecosystem consists of several components. At its core is an efficient, precise, and easy-to-use app that records participant participants' "positive" or environmentally-friendly actions and play the role of a "money printing press." It is used by individual participants fighting climate change and/or that are attracted to suitable financial initiatives. Their natural counterparts are corporate sponsors who are concerned about climate change and prepared to spend money to fight it. The ecosystem is held together by coalition members, such as users, stores, restaurants, gas and electric stations, etc., that accept GPM as partial payment for their goods and services.

The ability to program GPM is indispensable to making it possible to set an expiry date on the GPM earned by participants. Suitable circulation identifies "sources and sinks" for GPM. GMP is created at "source" when individuals perform environmentally-friendly actions and destroyed at "sink" when they redeem it as partial payment for goods and services.

However, a more general approach might be better. GPM can be earned by participants and rewarded by sponsors. In this scenario, end consumers receive and redeem GPM based on their ecofriendly actions.
