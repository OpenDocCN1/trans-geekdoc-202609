We use Arabic numerals to mark sequential information flows (IFs) and Roman numerals to mark cash flows (CFs). The transfer of money starts with the originator sending instructions to her Japanese bank, A. IF Bank A moves from the originator's own account, CF I. Bank A sends to Market Maker F, CF II, IF 2, who exchange yen into dollars and sends them to Bank B's account with an American bank C, CF III, IF 3. At the same time, Bank A sends SWIFT message MYT103, IF 4, Bank B, instructs the dollars to an American bank C, which is the corresponding bank of the Brazilian bank D, representing the beneficiary. Bank B transfers funds from Bank A to its account, CF IV, and moves dollars via Fedwick to Bank C, CF V, IF 5. Bank C moves the dollars to Bank D's account, CF VI. The dollars are exchanged for the real value Market Maker F, CF VII, IF 7, and sent to Bank D, CF VIII, IF 8. Bank C sends a SWIFT MT103 message to Bank D, Bank D moves the money to the beneficiary's account, CF IX, and informs that the money has arrived, IF 9. Regulators monitor the process in three jurisdictions — Japan, the US, and Brazil, IF 10. Given that most steps require payment and take a long time, the process is expensive and protracted.

A simple example of alternative money flows using cryptocurrencies, or ideally stablecoins, may illustrate how the process can be streamlined. Figure 12.4 (b) shows an elegant way of moving money from Japan to Brazil using cryptocurrencies, such as BTC, in place of USD. The transfer of money starts with the originator sending instructions to her Japanese bank A, IF 1. Bank A moves from the originator's account to its account, CF I. Next, Bank A sends to Market Maker F, CF II, who exchanges yen into BTC. At the same time, Bank A sends a SWIFT message to Bank D, IF 3. Bank B moves to Bank D's account, CF IV. At the same time, Bank A sends to Bank D's, IF 4. Bank D sends BTC to the same time, the second Market Maker F, CF V, who sends to Bank D, CF VI, IF 6. Finally, Bank D moves the money to the beneficiary's account, CF VII, and informs that the money has arrived, IF 7. Regulators monitor the process in two jurisdictions —

Japan and Brazil, IF 8. If necessary, they can do it in real-time. Provided that exchanges are regulatory-compliant, reliable, not prohibitively expensive (i.g., charge a reasonable bid-ask spread), and implement AML KYC methods, this method of moving money is far superior to its predecessor since no longer requires the participation of the two banks, Bank B and Bank C.

Of course, using yen- and reals-denominated stablecoins and an automated market maker to exchange them is an attractive alternative to BTC, which we leave to the interested reader to ponder. As we develop new cryptocurrency-based payment systems, we have a unique opportunity to make them faster, cheaper, and more global and customer friendly.

# 12.2.3 Progamable money

To start, we need to define the meaning of the term programmable money (PM). PM is represented by blockchain-based tokens on one side and value a suitable fiat currency or a basket they the other. In addition, PM possesses additional functionalities, such as conditional payments, outright payments, and payment commitments.
