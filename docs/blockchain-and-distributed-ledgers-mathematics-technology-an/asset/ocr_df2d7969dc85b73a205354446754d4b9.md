# 11.3.3 Coin mixing

Coin mixing is an alternative to establishing payment channels. The simplest centralized mixer is shown in Figure 11.6.

This figure shows four agents with Bitcoin addresses A, B, C, D, each sending 1 BTC to the mixing service. In turn, the mixer’s operator sends their BTC to new addresses A’, B’, C’, D’ (minus a small fee). Assuming that the operator is honest and does not steal from its clients, agents end up with BTC that are not directly linked to their addresses; instead, they are reconnected to the operator’s address. Given that all participants send the same amounts, their anonymity is further. External observer may see, who the owner of unspent transaction outputs (UTXOs) at A’, B’, C’, D’, although they can guess the original owner’s address belongs to the set {A, B, C, D}. Without merits, this approach is centralized and relies on honesty and discretion of the operator. Her honesty means she does not steal clients’ BTCCs, and discretion assumes that the actual mapping between the sets {A, B, C, D} and [A’, B’, C’, D’] to herself.
