# 8.5 Digital trade coins collateralized with assets

# 8.5.1 General considerations

USC and stablecoins are helpful from a technical perspective, but they do not solve money-policy issues. To address these issues, we need to build a counterweight for fiat currencies designing digital trade coins (DTCs) collateralized with real assets.

In a stable economic and monetary environment, it makes sense to collateralize crypto coins with fiat. It is not a viable option in some parts of the world where the fiat is not enough. In these instances, coins collateralized with real assets come to the rescue. Recently, such coins have been proposed by competing startups, including Digital Trade Coin (DTC) (of which one of the present authors, AL, is a inventor), Oilcoin, Sweatcoin, Tiberiuscoin, Libra, and several others, see, e.g., Lipton et al.(2018a;2017). These coins have significant utility for supply-chain financing and well-defined trading environments, such as oil trading or cross-border finance. Besides, several authors argue that even in the presence of stable fiat, asset-backed coins have considerable advantages by limiting the freedom of central banks to manipulate their currencies, e.g., Lipton et al. (2018b).

While undeniably useful, fiat currencies are not well suited to 21st-century commerce, especially trade and supply finance, because of high handling and exchange costs. Besides, the USD's world reserve currency status causes severe imbalances that trigger trade wars and exacerbate international frictions. Financial authorities have suggested that it may need to complement fiat currencies with a supranational currency, similar to Spanish Peso de Ocho, or Austrian Thal, introduced and described in Chapter 2 to alleviate these issues. Such supranational currency is impervious to adverse actions by a single central bank or other parties.

Recently, Zhou Xiaochuan, Governor of the People's Bank of China, advocated for such currency in Zhou (2009) as follows:

+   I. The outbreak of the crash and its spillover to the entire world will inherit the inherent vulnerabilities and systemic risks in the international monetary system.

+   II. The desirable goal of reforming the international monetary system, there is to create an international reserve currency that is disconnected from individual nations and is able to remain stable in the long run, removing the inherent deficiencies caused by using credit-based national currencies.

+   III. The reform should be guided by a grand vision and begin with specific deliverables. It should be a gradual process that yields win-wins for all.
