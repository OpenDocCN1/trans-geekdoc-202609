# 10.2 Can cryptrencies be viewed as money?

It is too early to say whether Bitcoin is a new form of money, but we think, at present, the answer is a “no.” Indeed, in the hard light of the day, Bitcoin does not satisfy most of the requirements listed in Chapter 2, which an instrument needs to fulfill to be money. Bitcoin is undoubtedly not a medium of exchange or a unit of account. However, one can view it as a form of treasure in the spirit of the asset classification developed by Greer, who divided assets into three “superclasses”: capital, consumable/transformable (C/T), and store of value (SOV) assets; see Greer (1997). SOV assets, such as gold, art, and other collectibles, do have value, though they are consumed and do not generate income. Time will tell if Bitcoin is an honorary member of the SOV club; see Szabo (2017).

If Bitcoin were to acquire some monetary features at a later stage of its evolution, in particular, we could experimentally study how money obtains and retains value.¹ We would be in a position to confirm or deny the following historical description from Mises of how commodity becomes money, see Mises (1998): “We will eventually arrive at a point in time when money was just an ordinary commodity whose demand and supply set its price. The commodity had... To put it simply, the day commodity becomes money, it already has an established purchasing power or price in terms of other goods. This purchasing power enables us to set the demand for that as money. In this turn, for a given supply, it sets its price on the day commodity starts to function. Once the price of money is fixed, it serves as input for the establishment of tomorrow’s price of money.”

At present, one can interpret BTC’s value if she thinks of the Bitcoin protocol as a game in a giant casino. As described in Section 5.5, BTCs have a fixed supply, are divisible, and technically relatively easy to acquire² Hence, they are ideal tokens, like casino chips. In addition to all other attributes, BTCs have entertainment value.

While one can argue about Bitcoin’s money credentials, altcoins, even the biggest ones, such as ETH and XRP, are undoubtedly not money, and more likely not, will never become one.
