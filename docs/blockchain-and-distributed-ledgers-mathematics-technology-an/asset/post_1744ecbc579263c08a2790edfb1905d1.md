## 5.7 Bitcoin pros and cons

There are heated debates between Bitcoin's supporters and its skeptics over its pros and cons; see, e.g., Bhutoria (2020). Skeptics argue that:

- (1) Bitcoin's volatility prevents it from being a store of value.
- (2) Bitcoin is not suitable as a means of payment.
- (3) Bitcoin's mining wastes energy on an immense scale.
- (4) Bitcoin's usage facilitates illicit activity.
- (5) Bitcoin has no backing.
- (6) A competitor will replace Bitcoin.

> ¹Mixing big-endian and little-endian formats in the Bitcoin protocol is likely a small design flaw. While standard hashes are big-endian, Bitcoin displays them in little-endian format because it considers hashes as little-endian integers rather than strings.

Supporters respond as follows:

- (1) Bitcoin's volatility will decrease in time due to its greater adoption as an asset class and the development of Bitcoin's derivatives.
- (2) Bitcoin deliberately optimizes its limited capacity and emphasizes the settlement of transactions outside of the financial system.
- (3) Bitcoin mining uses renewable energy; the energy consumption by the Bitcoin network is a legitimate use of natural resources.

![](img/d52d985b4b3970b396f9b7f6d2aa3d8e_246_2.png)

Surprisingly, the truth is somewhere between:

- (5) Bitcoin has no intrinsic value. Accordingly, it is better to think of it as chips in a giant casino. There is a fixed number of BTC and many potential players, which is enough to maintain their value in the long run. In addition to financial gains or losses, players also receive entertainment from trading and using their BTC. This should be compared and contrasted with conventional money, which, according to Aristotle, are derived from law rather than direct usefulness; see Section 2.4. Gold is a perfect example: it has survived for immorial despite being a depreciating asset for most industrial applications; see Chapter 2.
- (6) Nobody can be sure that Bitcoin will last a hundred years from now. Bitcoin has shown one thing in the last years: its value does lie in its scarcity. Although Ethereum goes further in many ways, Bitcoin is unequal in other ways, including its adoption rate, robustness, and trust. Therefore, it is unlikely that Bitcoin will be superseded by other technology for a long time. However, it is entirely possible, and even likely, that it will be superseded in the distant future.

## 5.8 Summary

After the publication of the seminal paper by Nakamoto (2008), Bitcoin took the world by storm. The fact that it has survived for more than a decade, and continues to thrive at present, speaks volumes. The Bitcoin protocol is a marvel of engineering, built in a very innovative way on top of excellent (if not most modern) cryptographic primitives. Bitcoin cleverly combines cryptography with game theory insights to make the protocol robust and mostly impervious to malicious interference.

However, the future of Bitcoin is uncertain. As shown in the chapter, it is not digital gold. The original promises are highly centralized in its implementation, mining and trading. Moreover, BTC ownership is exceedingly concentrated and profoundly undermines the democratic world it wishes to improve. Despite the above, we feel that Bitcoin has a future (if highly volatile) stock of value and can find exciting applications. For further discussion of BTC prices and related topics, see Chapter 10.

We hope that this book will help the reader make sense of Bitcoin’s merits and weaknesses. In our mind, it is a great success at present, but more likely than not, will be eclipsed by other protocols in the future.
