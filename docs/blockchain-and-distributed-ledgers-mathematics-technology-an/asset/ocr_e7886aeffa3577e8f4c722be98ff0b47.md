Given the properties of DLT outlined in Section 12.1, many academics and practitioners have proposed it as an essential tool for building IAM to the 21st century. By using DLT, we can design a decentralized, tamper-resistant, and user-controlled DID. Developing the blockchain-based DID is cost-efficient, compared to the existing technologies, making DIDs more inclusive, and achieving the UN’s goal mentioned at the beginning of this section. Accord to Dunphy and Peticolas (2018), all DLT-based schemes to date can be divided into two categories:

+   (1) **Self-Sovereign Identity**, owned and controlled by its owner, does not rely on any other administrative authority. It is created by a decentralized identity eco-system, recording and exchanging identity attributes and building trust among participating entities. As many suggest, no one can revoke such an identity.
(2) **Decentralized Trusted Identity**, provided by a centralized service, relies on trusted credentials, such as passports, birth certificates, and the likes. The centralized service records identity attestations on a DLT, which are subsequently validated by three parties.

The Core ID is particularly well-suited to implementing DLT based DID. Some best-known companies, such as Microsoft, and several startups, including Sovrin, uPort, OneName, Shoam, DID, ID.me, and IDChainZa, are developing complementary approaches to building DLT-based DID. Decentralized Trusted Identity is likely to be better suited than Self-Sovereign Identity to the real-world realities.
