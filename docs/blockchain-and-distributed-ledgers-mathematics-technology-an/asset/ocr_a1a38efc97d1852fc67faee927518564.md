Although none of these models are precise, we feel that a brief discussion of the stock-to-flow model is useful. As was mentioned earlier, BTC has no value. However, it does not mean that it has said zero price, as is occasionally claimed by its detractors. On the contrary, it can have any price (theoretically including zero and infinity).

It is worth articulating distinctions between BTC and conventional financial instruments, as stocks and bonds. The valuation of bonds is straightforward in theory, if not in practice. In the absence of credit risk, their price is equal to the discounted value of the coupon stream and the principal. In the presence of credit risk, the valuation is more nuanced and considers the principal and some of the coupons would not be repaid. The valuation of stocks depends on their current and future dividends, quarterly, and other financial metrics. It is not our intention to discuss the valuation of conventional financial instruments in detail. Instead, we want to mention what is expected from BTC as far as financial metrics are concerned. And this is its greatest strength! It cannot fail by definition, either on its own or compared to other instruments. Hence its price change without restrictions. However, external events can impact the price both positively and negatively. For instance, adoption by a major financial institution might result in a price increase, with the imposition of regulations might cause a price drop.

BTC scarcity, baked into the Bitcoin protocol from the start, makes it loosely similar to gold, diamonds, and art. The reader should not put too much credence into this similarity. Not all scarce acquire or retain their value; public tastes are fickle and can quickly move from one item of value to the next. Besides, the BTC cost of production depends on the number of miners involved in the process via the difficulty's dynamic adjustment. This distinction between BTC and gold or diamonds, whose production cost is mostly insensitive to the miners' activity as a group, is essential if selfemphasized.

The actual growth of the number of BTC in circulation is shown in Figure 5.19 (a), while the growth rate is shown in Figure 5.19 (b). We define the important stock-to-flow (S2F) ratio as follows:

```
S2F(t) = \frac{N_{BTC}(t)}{N_{BTC}(t) - N_{BTC}(t - \Delta t)}.
```

Here t is the calendar time in years, and Δt = 1. We define a backward-looking quantity since it reflects the actual production history, which was rather chaotic in the beginning. By now, BTC is produced at a relatively steady clip so that future values of S2F(t) can be calculated with a high degree of certainty. Given that block rewards are halved every four years, S2F is an increasing function of t.
