The most common are transactions with one input and two outputs, as depicted in Fig 5.10 (b). Typically, Alice pays Bob a certain amount of BTCCs, a transaction fee to miners, and sends the remainder to an address under her control. Transactions of type (a) are not uncommon and reflect moving all BTCCs to a different address. Transactions of type (b) describe the movement of BTCCs between one sender and several receivers, while transactions of type (d) depicts a complementary movement of BTCCs from several senders to several receivers.

A transaction redeem the outputs of previous transactions by signing them with the correct secret keys. Conceptually, a transaction script moves bitcoins from addresses $X_1, ..., X_i$ to addresses $Y_1, ..., Y_j$, with $I, J \ge 1$. However, these addresses are represented by the double hashes,

> $x_i = h_{RIPEMD160} \left(h_{SHA256} \left(Y_j\right)\right)\right)$

> $y_j = h_{RIPEMD160} \left(h_{SHA256} \left(Y_j\right)\right)$ (5.12)

Thus, the script moves bitcoin from addresses with hashes $x_i$ to addresses with hashes $y_j$.
