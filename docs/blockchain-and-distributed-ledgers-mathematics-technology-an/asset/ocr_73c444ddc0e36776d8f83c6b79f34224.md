Transfers contain the following information:

+   (1) nonce — for anti-replay;
(2) sender’s address (implicit);
(3) recipient’s address;
(4) signature’s of the sender;
(5) amount of ETH to transfer;
(6) STARTGAS value, which determines the maximum number of computational steps the originator of the transaction is willing to pay for;
(7) GASPRICE value representing the actual fee per computational step the sender is willing/able to pay;
(8) data field to specify an optimal smart contract method to call and its parameters.

The last two steps are crucial anti-denial of service measures and represent a unique feature of the Ethereum protocol. Gas pays for miners. Simply, gas is a unit measuring the computational effort required to perform operations and data store. Every successfully executed operation in Ethereum, be an elementary ETH transfer or a sophisticated SC, requires gas. Miners are paid in ETH equal to the total amount of gas needed for executing a request times the gas agreed upon by the transaction’s initiator. The amount of gas needed to support a simple fund is 21,000 gas. If Alice indicates her willingness to pay STARTGAS ≥21,000 gas; miners will process her transaction, of course, Alice has the requisite funds. If she only agrees to pay STARTGAS <21,000 gas for the transaction, the gas will be spent, but the transaction will not be completed and will not change the state of the Ethereum ecosystem. On the other hand, gas equal to the difference between STARTGAS and actual gas consumption will return to Alice. Therefore, there is a built-incentive to share a relatively high value of STARTGAS. However, if it becomes too high, the miners will not process Alice’s transaction to avoid clogging their blocks.
