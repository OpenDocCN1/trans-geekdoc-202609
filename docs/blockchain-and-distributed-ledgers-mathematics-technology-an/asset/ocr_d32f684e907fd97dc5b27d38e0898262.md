The most dramatic step in this direction is to conduct a modern analog of the “Great Survey” of England and Wales completed in 1086 by King William the Conqueror, which resulted in the creation of the Domesday Ledger. An essential aspect of the book is that the assessors’ reckoning of land possessions was dispositive and without appeal — hence the book’s name. Course, modern democracies be less inclined to inch such parcels as Medieval kings, so the blockchain-based land description can be created gradually — one parcel at a time, as part of the purchasing process, for example.

Next, the construction of RE can be facilitated by DLT, particularly in the rationalization of its supply chain management, as discussed in Section 12.5. Of course, the title for the finished property will be recorded on the blockchain.
LT facilitates the buying of newly constructed and existing properties on several levels. The property search can be harmonized and streamlined by replacing multiple listings with posting everything on a commonly accessible digital ledger. We already mentioned that DLT makes the conveyance process faster, cheaper, and more reliable than it is at. It is only natural to move the title of the newly acquired property to the underlying blockchain.

RE financing is also a promising target for DLT. PM, discussed in Section 12.2.3, is one direction to explore the DvP problem. Other revolutionary in spirit, is RE tokenization, which allows crowd financing of mortgages, and opens new opportunities for small investors to invest in RRE, CRE, and ARE as an asset class. As such, using cryptrencies for buying RE is an exciting dimension to explore.

Finally, maintaining of the existing properties, particularly rural and commercial ones, can be significantly facilitated by PM and related instruments by simplifying financial arrangements.

Of course, deployment of DLT in the RE ecosystem is not without issues:

+   (1) As discussed above, the line of ownership of the existing or newly-built RE has to be established with finality out of DLT.

+   (2) The level of awareness among decision-makers has to be raised make educated choices regarding advantages and disadvantages of DLT.

+   (3) Considerable technical obstacles need to be overcome since distributed ledgers pinning RE are extremely robust, backward compatible, and satisfy several additional requirements.

+   (4) Regulatory uncertainty must be resolved before the mass adoption of DLT in RE, which is highly regulated. DLT-based RE processing raises many legal and regulatory questions, such as privacy and data protection, system governance rules, tax and property law.
