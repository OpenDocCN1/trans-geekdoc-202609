# 4.5.7 Chaum's anonymous cash

David Chaum suggested using blind signatures, as the lines just outlined, to build the famous Dcash — anonymous electronic cash — which can be viewed as an early precursor of Bitcoin. To describe Chaum's framework, let Ben be a banker, Alice a depositor in his bank, and Bob a merchant. Alice wishes to send some money to Bob so that her bank cannot detect that is she who spends it at Bob's store. To end this, Alice sends $L$ blinded messages:

```
$\mu_j = k^e m_j \pmod n.$\quad (4.62)$
```

Every message contains the amount to be paid, say $A$, and a randomly chosen (very long) number $v_j$ so that for practical purposes, all $v_j$ are different. Ben asks Alice to blind $L-1$ randomly chosen messages and keep one message, say $\mu_j$ blinded. Provided that $L-1$ messages are signed in order, Ben signs the remaining message $\mu_j$ and sends the signed message back to Alice,

```
$sign(\mu_j) = \mu_j^d = k^{ed} m_j^d \pmod n.$\quad (4.63)$
```

Alice extracts the signed $m_j^d \pmod n$ and sends it to Bob with the unsigned message $m_j$. Bob checks the signature and the number $v_j$. If the number is not in its database of coins, Ben checks Bob's account (if it exists) or transfers funds to Bob's account at another bank. If the number is in use (Alice tries to double!), the transaction does not go through. The flowchart is shown in Figure 4.4.

These operations result as follows: Alice's balance at Ben's bank is reduced by $A$; Bob's account (or a different bank) is increased by the amount $A$ (minus fees); goods are shipped to Alice. The whole point is that Ben does not know who paid. In principle, unless Alice uses her physical address, Bob might not know her identity. The above algorithm is privacy-preserving. As such, it is naturally open to abuse, to use digital cash, the bank knows a payer's identity. However, if there are many users of this scheme, then a merchant might be paid by someone who requested another payment in the same amount, Alice's account will be credited to pay another merchant. Such scrambling hides identifies the identity of payers.
