To simplify the expression, we abstract details by assuming an AMM defined by the constant product rule. Let $\varepsilon$ be a percentage fee. Let $T_0$ and $T_1$ be two times. At time $T_0$, the price is $P_0$, and the pool's composition is $(x_0, y_0)$. At time $T_1$, the price is $P_1$ and we need to find the arbitrated' pool composition $(x_1, y_1)$. First, we assume that $P_1 > P_0$, so that, with zero transaction costs, it is beneficial to the arbitrageur to withdraw $TN_2$ and $TN_1$. With non-zero transaction costs the situation is more complicated. Eqs (10.78), (10.80), and (10.81) show that for nonzero transaction costs the actual composition of the pool is not only time-dependent, which is, of course, but surprisingly, path-dependent.

Let us study the profitability of a liquidity provider following a constant product rule. Clearly, profitability depends on the behavior of the price $P(t)$. We assume that $P(t)$ is mean-reverting and is driven by Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process:

```
$P(t) = \exp(p(t)),$
$dp(t) = -\kappa p(t)dt + \sigma dW(t), \quad p(0)=0. \quad (10.82)
```

Here $W(t)$ is the Wiener process driving variations of the log-price, $\kappa$ is mean-reversion rate, and $\sigma$ is volatility; $\kappa$ and $\sigma$ are measured in the units of $[1/\sqrt{T}]$, respectively. For small $\kappa$, the price is only weakly mean-reverting, while for large $\kappa$, it mean-reverts quickly. When $\sigma$ is small, the log-price is almost deterministic, when it is large stochastically. Since small price changes do not result in adjustments of portfolio composition, we can discretize the equation with a time step $\Delta t$, and rewrite it as:

```
$P(t) = \exp(p(t)),$
$P_{n+1} = (1-\tilde{\kappa})P_n + \tilde{\sigma}\eta_n, \quad P_0=0. \quad (10.83)
```

Here $\tilde{\kappa} = \kappa\Delta t, \tilde{\sigma} = \sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}$, and $\eta_n$ is the normal random variable. We assume that the pool's liquidity provider has a time horizon $T=N\Delta t$. The evolution of the system is driven by the following system of nonlinear equations:

```
$p_{n+1} = (1-\tilde{\kappa})p_n + \tilde{\sigma}\eta_n, \quad p_0=0,$
$x_{n+1}=f(p_{n+1},x_n), \quad x_0=1,$
$y_{n+1}=1/x_{n+1},$
$p_n=\exp(p_n). \quad (10.84)$
```

> ⁴Of course, since $\kappa$ and $\sigma$ are dimensional quantities, their size should be measured relative to the time period of interest, $T$, and their relative magnitude, $\kappa/\sigma^2$.
