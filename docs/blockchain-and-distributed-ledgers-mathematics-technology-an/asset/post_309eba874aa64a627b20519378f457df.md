Since the difficulty goes up and down depending on the miners' (or, more precisely, mining pools') combined hash power, bitcoin should not be viewed as digital gold, as often claimed. The reason is apparent. On the one hand, if some miners decide to start working, bitcoins' production costs go down. On the other, if some gold mine operators decide to shut down, other operators' production costs will not go. No mechanism limits the bitcoin price in dollars from below, while the gold price in dollar terms is limited by the production costs.

Based on the principle of proportional control, a centrifugal governor is a feedback system that regulates the flow of steam to an engine. In the 18th century, James Watt adopted one to regulate steam admission to his steam engine, controlling its rotational speed. A simple governor cannot maintain an exact speed; it needs a range instead. Mining difficulty adjustment, accommodating changes in the amount hash power used by miners, plays the same role as a governor regulating a steam engine's operation. The principle is simple: hashing — greater difficulty, less hashing — lower difficulty.

## 5.5 Pooling of resources

As described above, the simple economics of Bitcoin mining forces individual miners to coalesce into gigantic pools directed by managers. The reason is not hard to fathom — an individual miner faces a somewhat uncertain stream of income whose wins in the mining lottery are proportional to the hashing power she possesses. Hence, it is much safer to use resources with others. A pool's operator receives successful blocks, publishes them, and distributes rewards among the pool members. Participants communicate successful blocks and blocks that are almost successful, i.e., have hashes starting with several zeros.
