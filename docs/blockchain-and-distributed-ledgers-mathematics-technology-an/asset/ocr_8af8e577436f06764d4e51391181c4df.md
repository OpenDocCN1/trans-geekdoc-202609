The DAO, already mentioned in Chapter 1, is the best-known example of a (failed) DAO. The DAO was launched with grand fanfare in June 2016, with US$150 million war raised via crowdfunding. Unknown attackers immediately hacked the corresponding smart contract and transferred US$50 million worth of Ethereum to themselves. Vitalik Buterin and his entourage declared that they cannot tolerate this hack. They initiated an Ethereum fork, which restored the Ethereum blockchain to its state before.

# 6.10 Summary

This chapter covers some the most relevant aspects of the Ethereum protocol. In many respects, it is an extension of the seminal Bitcoin protocol discussed in Chapters 3 and 5, augmented with some additional striking capabilities. Because of that, Ethereum is more potent than its predecessor, but at the same time, due to its overarching ambitions, it has many weaknesses.

On the other side, Ethereum can do things best, moving the native token — ETH — from one address to the next. Moreover, because Ethereum is a transaction counting once concept, there is no need to spend the entire amount stored at a particular address every owner wants to pay in ETH. As was mentioned earlier, one can view Ethereum as a CaaS provider. The actual consensus provision relies on smart contracts. Thus, Ethereum is well-suited for building tokens on top of its underlying blockchain.

On the con side, Ethereum is frequently expensive to use, on occasion, unreliable. Given that smart contracts' verification requires a lot of effort from the miners, Ethereum must rely on an elaborate framework allowing users to pay for miners' services. Ethereum users to pay for each operation they perform in the price of gas constantly fluctuates and reacts how the network is at any given moment. This fee structure is Ethereum's Achilles heel — it is unfortunately rigid and very often, prohibitively expensive. The network capacity is relatively small, so the Ethereum blockchain is frequently slow and unreliable. The idea of the Ethereum blockchain being Turing complete world computer is not valid in practice.
