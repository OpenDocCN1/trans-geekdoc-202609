# # CHAPTER 10 Cryptocurrencies and Quantitative Finance

# ## 10.1 Introduction

In addition to the fascinating technological aspects of distributed ledgers, the associated cryptoassets are interesting to study in the context of quantitative finance. Given the rapid development of DeFi and its mostly unregulated nature, one can observe some exciting and unusual phenomena. We can view the crypto universe as a financial supernova, similar to a star that suddenly increases brightness due to a catastrophic ejecting most of its mass.

In this chapter, we briefly touch upon the very nature of cryptocurrencies and discuss what money-like features they possess, if any. We look at the elusive value of BTC and other cryptocurrencies. Given the highly controversial topic, the estimation of their actual value is in the eye of the beholder. We analyze historical returns of the three — as the total cryptocurrency index, which we denote by MRKT, and how to fit various popular distributions to these returns. In general, returns possess strongly non-Gaussian features and extremely heavy tails. Exchanges, such as CME, wishing to support crypto futures trading, need to appreciate this fact in. We use some techniques borrowed from epidemiology to analyze BTC dominance and we also analyze automated market makers (AMMs) and their profitability using some tried and tested methods of mathematical finance. Many results presented in this Chapter are original and have not been published before.

The chapter proceeds as follows. Section 10.2 analyzes money-like aspects of cryptocurrencies. We argue that in their current form, one cannot view them as money. Potentially, in the future, they can acquire some money-like features as part of their evolution. Section 10.3 presents our take on “fair” prices of crypto. In Sections 10.4–10.6, which are rather technical, we study statistical characteristics of historical returns of the three — BTC, ETH, and XRP, as well as the MRKT. In all cases, the generalized hyperbolic distribution provides the best fit to the empirical returns. We also calculate the Value-at-Risk (VAR) for BTC returns. quantities are of paramount importance for building a successful futures trading framework. In Section 10.7, we compare BTC, ETH, XRP, and MRKT among themselves, as well as against several currency pairs as EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, among others.
