In this figure, we show a particular implementation. All mappings are the actual mappings used during the war. Messages containing only letters (the sender had to spell the numbers out) are entered via a standard keyboard. A plugboard, reconfigured, is used for the initial interchange of letters, and then the rotors used in reverse. The plugboard performs the final interchange of letters. Finally, the encircled letter is lighted on the light board, by an operator, and sent via the Morse code by radio. Due to the presence of the reflector, the same machine can be used for decryption. However, there is a price to pay. The design is that a letter will never be encoded into itself. At the time, this was viewed as a major strength, yet to be a major weakness.
