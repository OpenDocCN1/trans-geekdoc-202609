An alternative model would be to elect a leader of the network participants and grant him the power to decide unilaterally on the transactions to approve or reject. In a distributed setup, becoming the leader should be achievable by any participant and should not be a permanent position in order to avoid recentral of the decision-making, and therefore the whole system. This approach only moves the needle to another challenge, that of the leader-election process itself. Selecting a leader is no easier than voting in favor of a specific transaction: one is still subject to Sybil attacks and therefore cannot naturally conclude on the legitimate leader.

Satoshi Nakamoto' consensus algorithm tackles the above challenge precisely. It establishes a new consensus for permissionless distributed ledgers, which makes Sybil attacks expensive and collaborative behaviors rewarding in order to set up a proper incentive system where network participants have a lot to gain by behaving properly, and a lot to lose by behaving improperly. This is the so-called Proof-of-Work (PoW) algorithm.

# 3.6.2 Digital work with proof-of-work

In a context where every network participant can inflate his perceived presence at will — for instance multiplying his Internet connections — and where participants have no reason to trust each other, how can one set up a process under which an network-wide agreement may be reached? Should there be a leader taking decisions, and if so, who the participants agree upon whom this leader should be? Be a leaderless, democracy model and if so, how to ensure that every participant only votes once?

It seems that Sybil attacks prevent any form of democratic model because of the inherent distrust between anonymous participants and their capacity to abuse a voting scheme at low cost and no penalty. To vote, Bitcoin must limit the ability of participants to inflate their power arbitrarily. Bitcoin must set up a framework where each and any participant may contribute, but voting is a costly enterprise. It must create an incentive system encouraging users to behave well and penalizing dissidents for misbehaving. Bitcoin must create an identity-less democratic system that does not allow fraud.

Satoshi Nakamoto sets the Bitcoin protocol so that the validation of a transaction block is conditioned on the preliminary resolution of a computationally expensive or work. The protocol guarantees that only blocks with a valid proof of resolution to the problem — or proof-of-work — are accepted as valid blocks; blocks without a valid proof-of-work are rejected by the network nodes. Satoshi properly designs the problem such that finding a solution is a computationally expensive, random challenge, while verifying the estimated amount of work invested by the solver of the problem. This approach still allows any participant to submit a new block of transactions to the network as long as this block contains a valid proof-of-work and therefore demonstrates to any observer that the work was achieved by the submitter — work which directly translates into an investment in time and money.
