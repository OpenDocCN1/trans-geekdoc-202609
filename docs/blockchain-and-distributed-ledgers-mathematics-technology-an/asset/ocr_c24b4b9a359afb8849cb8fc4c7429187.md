# 8.2.2 Possible designs of a narrow bank

An NB design is such that it cannot default except due to operational failures, which can
never be eliminated but only minimized by using state-of-the-art technology, thus
providing the safest payment system. By construction, the NB's mix includes solely
marketable low-risk securities and central bank cash in the amount exceeding its deposit
ase An NB does not need deposit insurance, eliminating its pervasive effects on the
system as a whole, not least the avoided moral hazards. In NBs we are as close
to actual currency as possible.

Depending on specific details, NBs keep all depositors' money as liquid cash deposited
at a central bank or government bonds, becoming thereby necessary stabilizers and
facilitators of the overall global financial system. There is no need for such stabilizers
after the GFC, which resulted in a massive concentration of assets in a handful of system-
important banks and, in the process, making the overall financial system even less robust
stable than it was before. In the addition to stabilizing the whole banking sector,
NBs naturally perform the record-keeping function in the economy while refraining from
issuing loans.

Since a narrow bank is as good as its assets, one needs to pay particular attention
the choice of these assets. There are several approaches for designing an NB; see, e.g.,
Pennacchi (2012); Dembo et al. (2018); Lipton et al. (2018a).
