Either by necessity or by design, these policies are vague and either unproven or proven by pre-crypto experiments to work. Centralized mechanisms, such as a centralized reserve, have to be maintained to ensure price stability in any case. Historically, all the live projects have suffered drops in value, just breaking their raison d'être. Besides so far, no one has been able to trust decentralized (price) oracles.

If the coin's value is going down (a more complicated case), then bond-like instruments are issued for coins, which are backed. As a result, the number of coins in circulation shrinks, and, in theory, their price increases. This method is the essence of the seigniorage; see Sams (2015).

When formulated as a cryptocurrency stabilization scheme, this sounds although. However, reframed in many financial terms, for instance, as a company trying to keep its stock price constant, is distilling its true colors. If the stock price increases, additional shares are issued, pushing the stock price down. When the price is falling, the company prescribes to sell bonds and use the corresponding proceeds to buy its stock, pushing its price up. This stability scheme represents another purely theoretical construct that cannot do work in practice and will collapse when coins do — not to mention selling bonds in the middle of a market panic might be an insurmountable obstacle.

## 8.5 Digital trade coins collateralized with assets

### 8.5.1 General considerations

USC and stablecoins are helpful from a technical perspective, but they do not solve money-policy issues. To address these issues, we need to build a counterweight for fiat currencies designing digital trade coins (DTCs) collateralized with real assets.

In a stable economic and monetary environment, it makes sense to collateralize crypto coins with fiat. It is not a viable option in some parts of the world where the fiat is not enough. In these instances, coins collateralized with real assets come to the rescue. Recently, such coins have been proposed by competing startups, including Digital Trade Coin (DTC) (of which one of the present authors, AL, is a inventor), Oilcoin, Sweatcoin, Tiberiuscoin, Libra, and several others, see, e.g., Lipton et al.(2018a;2017). These coins have significant utility for supply-chain financing and well-defined trading environments, such as oil trading or cross-border finance. Besides, several authors argue that even in the presence of stable fiat, asset-backed coins have considerable advantages by limiting the freedom of central banks to manipulate their currencies, e.g., Lipton et al. (2018b).

While undeniably useful, fiat currencies are not well suited to 21st-century commerce, especially trade and supply finance, because of high handling and exchange costs. Besides, the USD's world reserve currency status causes severe imbalances that trigger trade wars and exacerbate international frictions. Financial authorities have suggested that it may need to complement fiat currencies with a supranational currency, similar to Spanish Peso de Ocho, or Austrian Thal, introduced and described in Chapter 2 to alleviate these issues. Such supranational currency is impervious to adverse actions by a single central bank or other parties.

Recently, Zhou Xiaochuan, Governor of the People's Bank of China, advocated for such currency in Zhou (2009) as follows:

- I. The outbreak of the crash and its spillover to the entire world.
- II. The desirable goal of reforming the international system, there is to create.
- III. The reform should be guided by a grand vision and begin with.

The idea of anchoring the value of paper currency in commodities is old. Two approaches are common in: (a) redeemable currency, (b) commodity-standard.

### 8.5.2 How to build a DTC

We briefly describe how to build a DTC. The foundational layer of a DTC consists of real assets, such as oil, metals, crops, moorings rights, and other similar commodities and services of value:

- (1) The sponsors bring their commodities to the pool's administrator, who issues DTC in a one-to-one ratio.
- (2) The administrator sells DTCs to the public and deposits the corresponding fiat currencies with the affiliated BN.
- (3) The administrator passes the proceeds to sponsors.
- (4) As a result, the administrator possesses real assets. The sponsors end up with fiat currency that are always convertible into fiat at the current market price.

The price $P_{DTC}$ of DTC will be close to (but not strictly at) the market price of the corresponding asset pool, $P_{M}$. Indeed, if $P_{DTC}$ falls significantly below $P_{M}$, it becomes rational for economic agents to put DTC back to the administrator. In turn, the administrator has to sell a fraction of the pool's assets for cash and pass it to these agents.
