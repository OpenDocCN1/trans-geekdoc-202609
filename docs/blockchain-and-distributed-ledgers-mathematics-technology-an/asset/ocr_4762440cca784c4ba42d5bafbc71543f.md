To make chain analysis more difficult (but not impossible), one can contemplate the amount of information revealed by a particular transaction by keeping the sender's and recipient's public keys private. In addition, the transaction size can be kept private, too. However, the idea of making the global state of the blockchain completely private jeopardize is self-defeating. If the total number of coins in circulation were to be shielded, users could not be sure that there is no surprising issuance of additional coins.

In order to enforce user privacy, several methodologies have been developed. Below, we describe three of them, payment channels, coin mixing, and cryptographic obfuscation. The reader can find discussion and additional useful information in a recent survey of the blockchain privacy; see Peng et al. (2020).
