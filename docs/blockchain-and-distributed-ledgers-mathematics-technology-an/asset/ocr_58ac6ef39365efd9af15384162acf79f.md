Formally a simplistic blockchain data structure may be specified as follows:

+   (1) hash(message) is a cryptographic hash function. By construction, hash is a way that one cannot practically generate a message that yields a given output value not two different messages with the same output value (details in Chapter 4). Hash can be used to compute the digital equivalent to a fingerprint, such that any change to the original message, may be it, invalidates its hash.
(2) block_0; (transactions_0) is the genesis block of a blockchain, where transactions_0 is a list of transactions included in the block.
(3) block_i ; (hash(block_{i-1}),transactions_i) be the data structure of an descendent of the genesis block, where i > 0 and transactions is the list of transactions included in block.
(4) A blockchain is a sequence of blocks (block_i)_{i=0}^N where N is the index of the contemporary block.

By constructing the ledger with the above structure, one is able to create trust in the integrity of despite despite the unt environment where the ledger is often exposed and maintained. As the most recent block_N is known and agreed upon, the complete history is strictly defined and cannot be subject to any tampering without breaking the chain validity. In its recursive definition: block_N contains the hash of block_{N-1}, therefore block_{N-1} cannot be tampered with undetected, block_{N-1} contains the hash of block_{N-2}, therefore the same data integrity guarantee applies.
