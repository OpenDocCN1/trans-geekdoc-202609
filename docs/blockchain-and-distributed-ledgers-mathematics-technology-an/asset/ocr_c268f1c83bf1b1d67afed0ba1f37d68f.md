Monetary policies underpinning dynamically stabilized coins emanate from obsolete and outdated economic considerations. Either by necessity or by design, these policies are vague and either unproven or proven by pre-crypto experiments to work. Centralized mechanisms, such as a centralized reserve, have to be maintained to ensure price stability in any case. Historically, all the live projects have suffered drops in value, just breaking their raison d'être. Besides so far, no one has been able to trust decentralized (price) oracles.

Basis is a representative example of such a coin; see Al-Naji et al. (2017). The algorithm, denied of amenities, worth of the coin is going down (a relatively straightforward case), then, not surprisingly, new coins are issued and distributed among the holders on a pro-rata basis. If the coin's value is going down (a more complicated case), then bond-like instruments are issued for coins, which are backed. As a result, the number of coins in circulation shrinks, and, in theory, their price increases. This method is the essence of the seigniorage; see Sams (2015).

When formulated as a cryptocurrency stabilization scheme, this sounds although. However, reframed in many financial terms, for instance, as a company trying to keep its stock price constant, is distilling its true colors. If the stock price increases, additional shares are issued, pushing the stock price down. When the price is falling, the company prescribes to sell bonds and use the corresponding proceeds to buy its stock, pushing its price up. This stability scheme represents another purely theoretical construct that cannot do work in practice and will collapse when coins do — not to mention selling bonds in the middle of a market panic might be an insurmountable obstacle.

Algorithms of this type are not new. For example, Baron Munchausen is famous for using when pulling himself and his horse out by his hair. Many economists entertained similar ideas. They proposed a mechanism for fixing gold price by a government in terms of its fiat without any gold in reserve; see Black (1987). Even with all the coercive instruments at their disposal, no government could ever achieve such a feat. The probability of a crypto algorithm that lacks such exceeding ever. Without going too far in history, the recent crisis of the Argentine peso was accelerated by the increase of interest rates to 60%, which, according to the above theory, is supposed to arrest the currency farewell. A similar price dynamics for the Venezuelan government bonds comes to mind of anyone passing familiar with financial markets.
