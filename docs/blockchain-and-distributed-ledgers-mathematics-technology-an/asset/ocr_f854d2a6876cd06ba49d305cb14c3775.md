Figure 11.10 (a) demonstrates the process of splitting nodes into shards and allocating transactions to each shard. Of course, sharding requires solving two difficult problems: achieving consensus within a given shard and protecting it against possible attacks and handling cross-shard transactions efficiently and consistently.

Successfully implemented sharding results in a structure consisting of the main chain and several shard chains, which maintain their own subchains and commit their state to the main chain periodically. The main chain must be validated to ensure that cross-shard transactions are treated consistently. Zilliqa and several other protocols 11.10(b).
