## Abstract

Blockchain technology is a disruptive breakthrough built on information and computing technology that is still growing very quickly. This fundamental disruptive technology enables a shared digital history and record updating between parties who may not necessarily trust each other while avoiding the need for a central trusted agent for coordination. From its beginnings in cryptocurrency, blockchain is already finding use cases across a variety of industries, such as banking, law, education, healthcare, supply chains, and manufacturing. More than ever, marketers must learn how to use new technology and deploy them quickly.

However, despite the advent of blockchain technology more than ten years ago, academic marketing researchers have paid very little attention. Because of the distinctive characteristics of blockchain—transparency, decentralization, and immutability—it can foster a safer, customer-focused, and open marketplace for both individuals and companies.

This study’s primary goals are to increase knowledge and fill in knowledge gaps related to the creation and use of blockchains. It is meant to serve as a platform for academics and industry professionals to critically examine, investigate, and explain new models and forms that blockchain technology takes as well as the interaction between the technology and businesses, supply chains, and their stakeholders.

This study argues that blockchain is a technology that marketers should be well aware of and ready to use since it has the power to alter enterprises.

We seek to generate a discussion and future research on the impact of blockchain technology in five distinct areas: cryptocurrencies, digital platforms, supply chains, and online marketing.

## Introduction

Blockchain is a distributed, immutable database that simplifies the recording of transactions and the tracking of assets in a corporate network. Intangible assets can exist alongside tangible assets (such as a house, car, cash, or land) (intellectual property, patents, copyrights, and branding). A blockchain network can track and sell nearly anything of value, cutting risk and cost for all parties involved.

The lifeblood of a company is information. The sooner and more precisely it is received, the better. Blockchain is ideal for distributing that information because it provides immediate, shareable, and completely transparent data recorded on an immutable ledger that network members with permission may only read. A blockchain network, among other things, can track orders, payments, accounts, and output. Furthermore, because members have a unified view of the truth, you can see all aspects of a transaction from start to end, providing you with increased confidence as well as additional efficiencies and opportunities.

## Benefits of Blockchain

What must be changed: Repetitive record-keeping and third-party validations commonly waste time and effort in operations. Fraud and cyberattacks can take advantage of record-keeping systems. All of this slows business and reduces profits, emphasizing the need for a better solution. Enter blockchain technology.

### Increased Trust

As a member of a blockchain-enabled members-only network, you can be confident that you are receiving accurate and timely data, and that your sensitive blockchain records will be shared only with network members to whom you have explicitly granted access.

### Increased Security

Because confirmed transactions are permanently recorded, all network participants must agree on data veracity. No one, not even the system administrator, may erase a transaction.

### More Efficiencies

With a distributed ledger shared by network participants, time-consuming record reconciliations are avoided. A smart contract is a set of rules that may be written on the blockchain and implemented automatically to speed up transactions.

## How Blockchain Works

- 1. As each transaction occurs, it is logged as a "block" of data.
  These transactions show the movement of an asset, which could be either tangible (a product) or intangible (a service) (intellectual).
  The data block can record any type of information, such as who, what, when, where, how much, and even the condition — for example, the temperature of a food shipment.

- 2. Each block is connected to the ones before and behind it.
  These pieces establish a data chain as an asset moves from one location to another or ownership changes hands. The blocks confirm transaction timing and sequence, and they are securely linked together to prevent any block from being altered or introduced between two existing blocks.

- 3. A blockchain is an irreversible chain that connects transactions.
  Each subsequent block reinforces the verification of the previous block, and hence the entire blockchain. This renders the blockchain tamper-proof, giving it the key strength of immutability. This eliminates the possibility of hostile actor manipulation and creates a reliable record of transactions for you and other network users (Singh, 2021; Singh, Bansal and Sharma 2022).
