## Blockchain Technology in the Entertainment Industry

Through distributed ledger technology and smart contracts, blockchain technology has already been used to share material more fairly. As a result, the revenue can be automatically allocated under the predetermined contractual arrangement, which is advantageous to all parties. In 2021, funding for blockchain companies in the film and television industries increased by 713% year over year, totaling $25.2 billion. The only expected growth is in the north.

Decentralized media libraries powered by blockchain technology have also begun to emerge, and in recent years, many blockchain startups and enterprises have been bought by bigger corporations. For instance, the blockchain startup Media chain was purchased by Spotify. The gaming industry could benefit from more transparent competition and prize distribution thanks to blockchain technology. The use of cryptocurrency has increased in the betting industry as well. On the other hand, the development of NFT and Metaverse concepts as well as associated digital assets has been significantly aided by blockchain.

## Information and Communication Technology (ICT)

The introduction of ICT has altered all facets of business and society, as has been said countless times, but we feel compelled to repeat it. By the end of 2022, the ICT market is expected to reach a value of $5.5 trillion worldwide. Accelerated ICT adoption during the COVID-19 pandemic was observed in multiple sectors, and this helped to fuel trends like remote work that are now commonplace across many businesses. 5G, web 3.0, AI, and the metaverse have opened exciting new possibilities, but they have also prompted many concerns and issues. The roadmaps for digital transformation that businesses and government organizations have presented will decide how the economy and society advance and change over the next ten years.

### Current Concerns in the ICT Industry

The International Telecommunication Union reports that the rising carbon footprint of the ICT sector is a major issue now. Although recent technological advancements have flattened the emission curve, hardware and software intervention is still desperately needed. Additionally, there is a need to improve the efficiency of available resources as people and businesses demand ever-increasing processing and storage capacity. For instance, major corporations with extra ICT infrastructure capacity may grant specific access to other organizations or people. It would have a favorable effect on the environmental footprint generally as well as financially for the organization or business sharing the resource.

Other important issues facing the ICT sector are data privacy and security. With the introduction of IoT devices and technologies in more industries, it is more crucial than ever to solve security issues. The company offering ICT services is also responsible for protecting the privacy and user identity of regular users who are not technologically competent.

### Blockchain-Based Solutions for the ICT Industry

Most of the important questions that the ICT industry is currently dealing with can be answered by blockchain applications. Decentralized cloud solutions based on the blockchain can assist businesses or other entities in safely sharing their extra or idle resources with others in exchange for advantageous incentives. Either a public or a private ledger can be used to make it possible for devices in a network to share resources. A blockchain system could lead to more transparency, a wider range of services or resources, lower maintenance costs, less network congestion, less damage to the environment, and even the ability to reduce the risk of a single point of failure.

As with other ICT security questions, distributed ledger technology may help give a solution. The stored data cannot be managed by a single entity. Furthermore, since the data is distributed among the network’s nodes, it would be nearly hard for anyone to hack or alter the data. It also implies that no institution or authority could suppress any kind of information. In addition, by maximizing utilization and improving energy efficiency, blockchain has the potential to advance sustainability. As a result, it can help reduce the ICT sector’s carbon footprint. Blockchain use for tackling climate change has maintained a strong emphasis in the European Union’s digital agenda.

## Energy and Infrastructure

One of the major concerns of international governments today is access to affordable renewable energy. Low-carbon technologies receive close to 80% of public research and development funding, and this number is increasing. Global energy utilization decreased by 4.5 percent in 2020 because of the COVID-19 epidemic, but it increased by nearly 5% in 2021. According to the current investment trends, there is a shortfall in the infrastructure industry of over $15 trillion, or 0.55% of GDP. This divide is greater in some parts of the world than others. Beyond investing, there are substantial problems that need to be solved globally in the areas of infrastructure and energy.

### Problems That Need to Be Fixed in the Energy Sector

Systems for managing energy are changing. They are increasingly dispersed and computerized. Governments and businesses must expand their research into, consideration, and rapid deployment of dispersed technology to keep up with this trend. Emerging technologies based on ICT are already improving the energy markets, with ideas like prosumers, smart grids, smart meters, dynamic pricing, and microgrids gaining traction. This brings up the underlying issues that were discussed in the ICT industry part, as well as issues of control, trading practices, customer service, and resource sharing.

### Challenges That Need to be Addressed in the Infrastructure Sector

Despite several efforts by international governments and bodies, a significant funding shortfall remains to power the world's infrastructure.

The economy, poverty, the environment, and sustainability are all related to infrastructure. Proper attention must be paid to expanding power generation capacity, telecommunications, sanitation, rail and road infrastructure, and supply lines, especially for water.

### Marketing and Sales

Blockchain might be used in conjunction with AI and ML to help businesses better respond to consumer trends and personal preferences. At the same time, businesses can protect the privacy of specific consumers thanks to blockchain technology.

Today, the majority of firms undergo digital change, but manufacturing disruption is considerably more obvious. Industry 4.0 extends virtual data and processes and goes through significant advancements. The change has started, and it will likely have a big impact. Technical, economic, and social advancements are projected across the entire industrial ecosystem. Workers begin switching from nearshoring to offshore as a result. Numerous variables, including 3D printing, automation, IoT, and the digitization of supply chains, contribute to the Industry 4.0 revolution (Haleem et al., 2020; Teslya & Ryabchikov, 2017; Holland et al., 2018). The next technology that can assist raise the level of the digital revolution is blockchain. Blockchain can also benefit Industry 4.0 in situations where several partners in a business network must transact, exchange, and transmit data while maintaining a trustworthy environment.

Blockchain technology has the potential to be applied in a wide range of other industries, including smart meters that allow for safer management of building energy efficiency certificates or more efficient usage of electricity. It bases its working principles on permanence, record, and protection. Blockchain technology opens up a wide range of opportunities as an intelligent network in Industry 4.0. Due to the flexibility of the network, blockchain transactions promise a higher level of automation and reduced interparty frictions, which will lower costs and speed up processes (Khan et al., 2021; Leng & Jiang 2018; Zhang et al., 2017). It may be used in a variety of supply chain systems in a variety of ways to bring enormous benefits to all parties involved. Customers have a great deal of freedom, and businesses can become more competitive by real-time controlling supply and demand (Pinheiro et al., 2019; Kuhi et al., 2018; Saberi et al., 2019). The efficiency attained in business operations is demonstrated by the label features of immutability, traceability, safety, resilience, and openness.
