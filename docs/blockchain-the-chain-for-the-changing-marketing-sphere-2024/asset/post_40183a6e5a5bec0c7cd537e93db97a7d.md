
Government-related blockchain projects are predicted to boost India’s GDP by $5.1 billion by 2032.

- In India, various start-ups are experimenting only with technology to provide a variety of using it, especially in the banking, insurance, & financial services industries. Big corporations are attempting to integrate blockchain technology within their regular operations. The Indian government is also actively adopting technology and directing resources into use cases in areas like crop insurance, education, and land title registries, including electronic health management, among many others.

- Blockchain usage will increase, hastening India’s digital revolution. The government has been successful in laying the foundation for digital infrastructure for storing and transmitting data over the past few years through the deployment of technologies like “Aadhar, UPI, Digi-locker, and e-Sign as well as digitally enabled tax governance networks like the GSTN”.

- Despite being the second-biggest country in terms of population and the largest democracy in the world, India’s government faces several difficulties. Given the variety and complexity of administering public distribution services (PDS), blockchain presents special benefits and opportunities to support better governance. Because every step of the supply chain can be traced on the blockchain, it offers a mechanism to track and manage the commodities distributed to the beneficiaries. The information is easily traceable in real-time on the blockchain, ensuring that there are no leaks inside the PDS and that the allotted products and services are delivered to the final receiver.

- A growth in blockchain adoption will boost the economy right away by strengthening quality control, contract administration, and procurement, as well as decreasing leaks and wastage in supply chains.

- The private sector will be able to operate more effectively with the help of blockchain adoption as it grows, which will benefit SMEs. If the government permits “self-regulation,” the implementation of blockchain will significantly improve governance since entities will be less reliant on onerous regulatory scrutiny and compliance and more able to connect directly with one another on the distributed ledger through a trusted medium.

By incorporating the technology in the “National Agriculture Market (eNAM)”, an online trading platform for agricultural commodities in India, blockchain is also helpful for the country’s agricultural industry. The audit trails of all the farmer’s products are produced by logging the information flow on the blockchain, and the data cannot be tampered with because every record on the blockchain is cryptographically hashed. As a result, there is less mistrust between farmers and arhatyas (intermediaries in mandis) and transaction costs are decreased, enhancing trade in goods across India.

Blockchain also encourages farmers to produce high-quality goods by tying a financial incentive to their output in the form of certification of both the provenance as organic products, which demands a higher price on the export market.

The blockchain can also act as an aid in disaster management & assist in insurance claims after a natural tragedy by automatically releasing claims in real-time and retrieving the necessary data for claim management through smart contracts, saving the time and money involved in the process. Because blockchain makes it simpler to verify credentials and maintain records, the education sector benefits from it.

Similar to other revolutionary technologies like electricity, including the steam locomotive, the computer, as well as the internet, blockchain has the possibility of affecting social structures & economies.

Although these advantages of more blockchain use are said to be unparalleled, several reasons will significantly speed up its development, and education and understanding of the technology should be integrated into the educational system.

Cross-collaboration between organisations, industry, and academics, supported by the government, is essential to fully profit from the technology. Since the technology is decentralised. Also, it is highly suggested that the government work with other countries to build data and processing interoperability just on blockchain to facilitate easy application in supply chains, financial systems, healthcare records, etc.

## Suggestions

- When used in routine company operations, blockchain offers business efficiencies since, by its very nature, it eliminates middlemen and generates a decentralised database that the relevant parties can consult to make decisions.
- The digitalization process would benefit from increased efforts to link blockchain with diverse applications because India already has a strong basis for data collection.
- Businesses will operate more efficiently as a result of increased transparency and accountability since embedded smart contracts will significantly cut down on the time and expense required to grant permissions and give approvals, which will enhance the “Ease of Doing Business”.
- Blockchain has opened up a tonne of professional opportunities for Indians, especially since the technology requires expertise in software development, commercial acumen, and law. By rethinking the system’s present governance processes, the introduction of blockchain technology could also act as a catalyst to increase the system’s effectiveness and transparency.

## Conclusion

Blockchain technology has uses outside of the financial industry, including as in networking, digital advertising, cybersecurity, and forecasting. “Money transfers, supply chain management, data sharing, digital voter registration, real estate transactions”, and the transfer of vehicle titles are just a few of the operations that may be accomplished with blockchain technology. Additionally, it is capable of safeguarding royalties and copyrights. India is progressively embracing blockchain, therefore things are improving. With the size, variety, and complexity of providing public services as India’s administration currently faces, blockchain offers a unique option. The blockchain economy needs technological innovation. The development of the technology required for just a blockchain-based digital economy requires strong collaboration between the government and all levels of management. It must also convey the potential of this technology. If the government and organisations adopt blockchain deployment tools, the COVID-19-caused supply chain disruption as well as the current economic difficulties in India can be fixed. Blockchain technology can change India’s FinTech landscape and how digital transactions are carried out going forward. India can gain a lot by implementing blockchain technology.
