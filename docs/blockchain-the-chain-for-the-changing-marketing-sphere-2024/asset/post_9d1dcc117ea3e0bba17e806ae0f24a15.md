### Abstract

Blockchain is one of the emerging technologies for accessing and sharing transactional data across a vast peer-to-peer network, where non-trusting users can communicate with each other without an intermediary in a verifiable manner. The fundamentals of blockchain, its uses, kinds, and operation are reviewed in this essay. Security, privacy, and the Consensus processes of this technology are also significant and cause for concern, and they are the primary drivers behind this novel technique. Anonymity, integrity, scalability, and transparency are a few of the key benefits of blockchain technology. A variety of technical, social, economic, and regulatory hurdles make large-scale integration of blockchain complicated. The article describes blockchain technology, how it affects the Indian economy, and its role in shifting the economy to a digital economy.

Keywords: block chain, digital economy, digital transformation

### Introduction

“A blockchain is essentially a distributed database of records, or public ledger, of all executed transactions or digital events that have been transmitted among participating parties.” A majority of the system’s users must agree on every transaction before it can be recorded in the public ledger. Additionally, data that has been entered cannot ever be deleted. On the blockchain, every transaction that has ever been made is recorded in a precise and verifiable manner. In a straightforward analogy, it is simpler to steal a cookie from a jar that is hidden away than it is to steal it from one that is visible to hundreds of people in a market. Probably the most well-known use of blockchain technology is Bitcoin. As a major factor in the growth of an unregulated, multibillion-dollar global industry for anonymous commerce, it is also the most controversial. As a result, it has to deal with a wide range of regulatory issues that affect financial institutions and national governments. Blockchain technology, on the other hand, is not contested and has persistently operated flawlessly over time. It is successfully being used for both financial and non-financial applications in the real world.

Reliance on a recognised authority is the basis of the present digital economy. Every online transaction relies on a reliable source telling us the truth. This may be a social media site like Facebook alerting us that our posts have only been read by acquaintances, or it might be a bank ensuring us that our money has indeed been moved to our account in a trustworthy manner. The fact is that because we depend on a third party for said security and privacy of digital assets, we live in a dangerous environment in the digital world. Nonetheless, it is accurate that these additional sources could be controlled, hacked, or infiltrated. In this case, blockchain technology is useful. By offering a distributed consensus that will enable any future time for the verification of any past or present online transaction involving digital assets, it has the potential to completely revolutionise the digital world. It accomplishes this without jeopardising the confidentiality of the parties or the relevant digital assets. A key feature of blockchain technology is distributed consensus, as well as anonymity.

### Significance of the Study

The sharing economy notion has developed over the last ten years, and at the moment we use shared services like Ola, Uber, Oyo, etc. Blockchain technology is already revolutionising society in a few specific cases. Theoretically, buyers should be able to learn about a laptop’s location, current value, and condition if they register it on the Blockchain and choose to sell it on Olx. Imagine that a client purchases your laptop and asks for insurance. In such case, he can quickly share the blockchain data with the insurance company, including “what it is,” “how much it costs”, “what condition it’s in”, etc., and acquire insurance as a result. When items are kept around for a long period without being used, renting them out is less expensive than retaining them. In the coming years, blockchain will increase the efficiency and security of the sharing economy. Although there has been a slow uptake of blockchain technology, numerous countries have gradually adopted regulations that support it and virtual currencies. India is gradually embracing blockchain technology with the introduction of the new programme “National Digital Health Mission (NDHM)” as well as the adoption of a brand-new education policy. A database for quick deployment is what the government is attempting to give.

### Indian Economy and the Blockchain

The seventh-largest economy in the world right now is India. To combat corruption, the government abruptly announced a global demonetization on November 8, 2016, and outlawed all Rs. 500 and Rs. 1000 banknotes. Since then, the situation has already appeared bleak. A nation of 1.2 billion people had to switch overnight to cashless and digital transactions. Due to the difficult GST implementation, COVID lockdowns in 2020, and Demonetization, the Indian economy has developed slowly. A new UN study predicts that this coronavirus scenario would result in a 5.9% decline in India’s economy in 2020.

The tremendous expansion and also rise of digital or cashless modes of payments stand out even though there have only been a few bright spots, which have undoubtedly been eclipsed by challenging conditions and situations in Indian economy is currently facing. It’s seen that the fintech industry has experienced tremendous growth and has contributed in some way to economic expansion. According to the World Economic Forum (WEF), the government has greatly benefited from the explosion in digital transactions that has occurred in India as a result of the demonetization strategy. The capacity to track the movement of money inside the economy will be improved as a result, which is why it is. Additionally, the entry of blockchain technology and cryptocurrencies is a huge benefit. Even though the government abruptly decided to prohibit 80% of the country’s currency in 2018, the restriction on cryptocurrency transactions was met with a wave of fraud in the months that followed. Cryptocurrency exchanges challenged the ban in court, and in March 2020 they were granted a reprieve (Alaeddin and Rana, 2008).

The Indian government must find long-term answers to the problems preventing the spread of a smoother and fluid cashless economy, as will be the case for any government in the globe if it hopes to expand its cashless economy. Financial inclusion, high setup and transaction fees, plus protracted transaction delays are some of these difficulties. Fundamentally, blockchain is a good place to start because the government sorely needs an alternative to traditional financial solutions to manage a cashless economy. Blockchain technology virtually eliminates the need to be a part of a traditional financial structure to be monetarily included. By fostering transparency between government and private sector interfaces, it can also facilitate easier business collaboration and a better quality of life for Indian citizens. To tap into new sources of efficiency and value, Additionally, technology can completely transform current practices (Chillakuri and VS Prakash, 2022; Grover et. al., 2019).

India has improved dramatically since 2015, moving up 79 positions to be placed 63rd in the 2020 edition of the “Ease of Doing Business,” but it still performs appallingly poorly in several other areas that require immediate attention, including “enforcing contracts” (“ranking 163 out of 190 countries”), “property registration,” (“154 out of 190 countries”), and “opening a business” (“136 out of 190 countries”). The solution to these issues and the long-term saviour of the Indian economy could be the introduction of blockchain technology and Bitcoin transactions. In terms of the entire world, it is predicted that by 2030, Blockchain would have produced $3 trillion in annual economic value. 10% of the world’s GDP is expected to be held on blockchain by 2025, according to WEF predictions. Blockchain is also featured as one of the seven technologies that are anticipated to revolutionise a variety of facets of our life. Adopting and accepting cutting-edge technologies is the cornerstone of the sharing economy’s solution.
