# Solutions in the Form of Healthcare Blockchain

By 2025, blockchain technology may save the healthcare industry up to $100 billion per annum, according to a BIS Research study. These savings are the result of reduced costs associated with data infringement-related expenditures, IT costs, maintenance costs, costs for support functions and staff, frauds related to counterfeiting, and insurance frauds.

Pharmaceutical companies can trace pharmaceuticals across the whole supply chain thanks to blockchain technology, which makes it more difficult for thefts and enables quick discovery of inconsistencies. It makes sure that our data is more securely stored, offers informative transparency, and makes it possible for us to get real-time and current information. This makes it easier to spot false claims more quickly and reduces insurance fraud. Estonia was one of the first countries to lead the world in BC technology. It has a national electronic health records system that uses blockchains to integrate and store information from the healthcare sector and other fields. Data protection, integrity, and system access logs are ensured by blockchain capabilities, increasing security. The United Arab Emirates is another powerful nation. In line with UAE’s vision, the healthcare system will track licenses for health professionals by using blockchain technology.

# Insurance Sector

The insurance ecosystem is growing rapidly, yet with this expansion come new issues. Blockchain can be a great answer for the insurance industry, helping with fraud detection, reducing the amount of risk associated with underwriting, or improving cyber insurance plans. By 2023, the global insurance blockchain industry might reach $1.39 billion. By simplifying information sharing and reducing expenses by automating operations, blockchain technology can help reinsurers save up to $10 billion while lowering risk and costs. It makes sense that 65% of insurance executives thought that DLT adoption was necessary for their company to stay competitive.
