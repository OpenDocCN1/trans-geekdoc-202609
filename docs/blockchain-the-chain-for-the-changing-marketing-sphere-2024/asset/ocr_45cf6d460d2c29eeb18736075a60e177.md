# The Big Problem

RegTech emerged in the wake of the 2008 financial crisis. One effect of the universal financial crisis was the 492% rise in supervision laws (2008–2015). In parallel, there was a 45-fold rise in fines levied against the biggest banks between 2009 and 2014. Since then, financial institutions have been required to adhere to the control system's criteria, which target both user data security and criminal activity.

Financial penalties resulting from an incomplete adjustment are inescapable, despite the significant expense involved in implementing the new regulations. Since the 2008 global economic crisis, banking institutions have only been penalized more than $160 billion in the United States.

Compliance is estimated to cost banks over $70 billion yearly. RegTech can help reduce these expenses by improving effectiveness and efficiency.

# Advantages of Using RegTech

The best coordination of data interchange between the financial industry and regulatory agencies, increased data processing using AI tools or streamlined risk identification and analysis are the most significant advantages of deploying RegTech solutions.

Future developments might include technology-enabled process efficiency, data sharing and accumulation, real-time data supervising, anomaly detection, and blockchain-based compliance systems.

RegTech is a rapidly growing industry because of the rigorous regulations that apply to institutions in the financial sector. Utilizing modern technologies will lower the expenses of human mistakes, data storage, and regulatory adaptation, while also assisting in avoiding penalties and lowering fraud.

Blockchain is a great technology for tackling issues encountered by banks and other financial institutions because of its features including the Blockchain ledger, smart contracts, data tracking, and so forth.
