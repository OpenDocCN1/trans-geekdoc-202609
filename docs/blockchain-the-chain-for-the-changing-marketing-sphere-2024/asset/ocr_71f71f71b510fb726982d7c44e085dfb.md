As can be seen, certain sectors in these like Health care and financial services involve sensitive data-related issues involving possible overlapping of privacy and individual citizen rights which can be difficult to handle if not managed properly.

Developing Interfaces to manage the different sectoral requirements which are likely to be specific will be a major challenge and a modular approach to developing sector-specific solutions may perhaps be more possible rather than fully customizing the solution for a sector. This in turn will affect scaling and volumes, adoption of technologies by the concerned stakeholders in the sector concerned, and hence by default the acceptance of BC products and solutions. The situation is further compounded by the increasing role played by AI, Machine learning (ML), Deep Learning (DL) and Digital Technologies (DTL) have changed the way business models will work in the future, and the adoption of QR code, UPI and related technologies are changing the accounting, record keeping, and adoption by industries and hence are becoming crucial as combined with BC applications, these can leapfrog to other areas.

The danger however lies in the fact that the regulators who are to understand the implications of these changes happening are not likely to be aware of the issues and hence becomes more challenging as the incidents happening thereafter can quickly go out of hand. With the advent of fintech, regtech technologies BC has permeated the entire gamut of financial services and insurance services, which may lead to control issues and is likely to become a headache for the regulator of financial markets (RBI) and Capital Markets (SBI). In countries like India which are more oriented towards control at a central level, this is bound to be felt in the form of one legislation or another as applicable to BC in the future days to come.

The developments in sector-specific BC will happen if suitable interfaces are developed and suitably designed, incorporating the necessary controls needed, and herein lies the challenge for technology adoption. These will have to be designed based on feedback from market players, customers, suppliers, society, and possibly regulators too. The latter will certainly want to make sure that the level of comfort to be obtained by them before approval is of high order and with systemic risks arising and increasing market volatility, they are likely to delay the approval process or prescribe additional controls. The feedback loop is crucial to enable the BC applications to take shape in the right form and on time.
