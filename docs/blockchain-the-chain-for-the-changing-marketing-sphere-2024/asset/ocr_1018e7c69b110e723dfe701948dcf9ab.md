Government-related blockchain projects are predicted to boost India’s GDP by $5.1 billion by 2032.

+   In India, various start-ups are experimenting only with technology to provide a variety of using it, especially in the banking, insurance, & financial services industries. Big corporations are attempting to integrate blockchain technology within their regular operations. The Indian government is also actively adopting technology and directing resources into use cases in areas like crop insurance, education, and land title registries, including electronic health management, among many others.

+   Blockchain usage will increase, hastening India’s digital revolution. The government has been successful in laying the foundation for digital infrastructure for storing and transmitting data over the past few years through the deployment of technologies like “Aadhar, UPI, Digi-locker, and e-Sign as well as digitally enabled tax governance networks like the GSTN”.

+   Despite being the second-biggest country in terms of population and the largest democracy in the world, India’s government faces several difficulties. Given the variety and complexity of administering public distribution services (PDS), blockchain presents special benefits and opportunities to support better governance. Because every step of the supply chain can be traced on the blockchain, it offers a mechanism to track and manage the commodities distributed to the beneficiaries. The information is easily traceable in real-time on the blockchain, ensuring that there are no leaks inside the PDS and that the allotted products and services are delivered to the final receiver.

+   A growth in blockchain adoption will boost the economy right away by strengthening quality control, contract administration, and procurement, as well as decreasing leaks and wastage in supply chains.

+   The private sector will be able to operate more effectively with the help of blockchain adoption as it grows, which will benefit SMEs. If the government permits “self-regulation,” the implementation of blockchain will significantly improve governance since entities will be less reliant on onerous regulatory scrutiny and compliance and more able to connect directly with one another on the distributed ledger through a trusted medium.
