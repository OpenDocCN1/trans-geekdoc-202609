To coordinate food tracking throughout the agriculture supply chain, the proposed model (Casado-Vara et al., 2018) uses a MAS, a smart contract, and the blockchain. The export, the processor, and the wholesaler are located at this subsequent layer (Figure 15). The essential products that are received by the supply chain are processed in this middle layer. The retailer and the food service, which sell the products, comprise the final layer. The model shifts when blockchain is incorporated into the agriculture supply chain. Each member of the blockchain has access to critical data for its operations because the data is decentralized. There are five MAS layers. The producer agent is located in the producer layer. This agent coordinates the producer's entire operation. The processor agent is located in the processor layer. This agent coordinates all activities in this layer. The transport provider agent is located in the transport layer. This agent coordinates all transport between the other members of the supply chain. The retailer agent is located in the retailer layer. This agent works with the processor to buy and sell materials to the customer. The blockchain agent is situated on the blockchain layer. This agent is synchronized with the agents of the other layers to ensure that all of the transaction data is correctly stored in the blockchain (Singh, 2021; Singh, Bansal, and Sharma 2022).

## Challenges

### Performance and Security

A Blockchain is a novel approach to establishing trust in an open environment, storing information, carrying out functions, and executing transactions. From the data management perspective, a blockchain is a distributed database that organizes a list of changing transaction records into a hierarchical chain of blocks. The hash pointer (Chelladurai & Pandian, 2021) and the Merkle tree (Hu & Gharavi, 2013) are the two fundamental building blocks for using hash-chained storage to implement the blockchain in Bitcoin. Due to the different architectures and business processes used by other financial institutions and the use of manual processes, the reconciliation, clearing, and liquidation processes between financial institutions are prone to errors and inconsistencies between ledgers held by different financial institutions. It not only results in high transaction fees generated by clients and the background business side of financial institutions, but it also leads to high transaction fees. When online transactions are used for investment and asset management, diverse intermediaries manage equity, bonds, notes, income vouchers, warehouse receipts, and other assets. Not only does it increase transaction costs, but it also raises the possibility of certificates being intentionally forged or forged.

### Privacy

The ability of a blockchain to protect all data, including sensitive data, is referred to as the “data privacy” feature. Although the blockchain was initially designed as a global distributed ledger for the Bitcoin digital currency system, its potential uses go far beyond virtual currencies. Inference attacks, which link sensitive transaction data and pseudonyms to the true identity of the actual users, even though only a pseudonym is being used, have been the focus of data privacy research over the past few decades.

### Interoperability

Despite differences in, for instance, data structures, digital signature schemes, transmission protocols, verification mechanisms, consensus mechanisms, token issue mechanisms, and imaginative contract language, connecting blockchains proves to be a manageable strategy. In the medium term, interoperability between blockchains, other distributed ledger technologies, and enterprise systems is made possible by the Blockchain of Blockchains and Hybrid Connectors. Standards for blockchain interoperability are encouraged as a result of this.

### Energy Consumption

Blockchains are data structures or ledgers that are shared and distributed. They can safely store digital transactions without a central authority. According to energy sector decision-makers, blockchain technologies can significantly enhance the internal processes, customer services, and costs of energy enterprises and utility companies’ current operations. The development of distributed energy resources and information and communication technologies (ICT) has sparked a paradigm shift in energy systems. Blockchains can control and manage increasingly decentralized complex energy systems and microgrids thanks to their inherent nature. Integrating distributed generation, flexibility services, small-scale renewables, and consumer participation in the energy market are challenging. Customers’ and distributed generators’ billing can be automated using blockchains, smart contracts, and smart meters.

## Conclusion

Rules and system architectures for a blockchain network or system can vary based on the desired operation and use case. Network users and validators typically make up blockchain systems. User nodes hold a copy of the ledger and can initiate or receive transactions. Applications like Bitcoin represent a distributed ecosystem that is entirely secure and whose integrity requires a lot of money to keep safe. Validators are accountable for approving ledger modifications, achieving consensus across the network regarding the ledger’s valid state, and having read access privileges. Depending on the system’s configuration, it may apply access and validation rights, either wholly or partially. Any Internet user can join a public blockchain system. In contrast, access to private blockchains is restricted to authorized participants only. Because any network member can contribute to validating transactions, permissionless ledgers are entirely distributed and resistant to censorship.
