# Healthcare Technology

Contrary to what most businesses believe, the healthcare sector and emerging technology have a lot more in common. It should come as no surprise that the worldwide blockchain industry for healthcare might reach $5.61 billion by 2025. Implementing BC technology could help solve urgent problems in the healthcare sector that cost us huge amounts of money every year. In this section of the post, we'll discuss issues facing the healthcare sector and how blockchain technology may be able to help.

# Urgent Healthcare Issues

The absence of a global system for patient identification and record management is one of the most critical issues. Why is it essential? One of the causes of patient EHRs (Electronic Health Records) or digital personal medical records (DPMR) that don't match is this. When data is moved between medical systems, up to 50% of a patient's medical records are incorrect, according to statistics. Even within the same healthcare system, one out of every five patient records cannot be reliably linked. More than 176 million patient records are thought to have been compromised as a result of data security breaches between 2009 and 2018. It contains both genomic test results and banking information. The worrying development is that healthcare data breaches increased by 44.44% (monthly over a month) in October 2019. It indicates that in such breaches, over 600,000 healthcare records were reported as having been improperly released, exposed, or stolen. Although preserving records on paper might be safer, managing the daily volume of data generated is nearly impossible.

Insurance fraud is a significant issue that the healthcare sector must deal with. It involves unneeded medical treatment, prescriptions for drugs that are not legal, and unfounded allegations of corruption. Only in the USA, it is estimated that medical fraud costs roughly $68 billion annually. The serious problem of illegal drug sales affects the healthcare industry as well. The Health Research Funding Organization estimates that 10% to 30% of the medicines sold in developing nations are fake. An even bigger issue is that improper ingredient amounts are present in 16% and 17%, respectively, of counterfeit medications. Pharmaceutical businesses must therefore stress the security of the entire drug supply chain. Businesses may lose $200 billion a year because of fake medications.
