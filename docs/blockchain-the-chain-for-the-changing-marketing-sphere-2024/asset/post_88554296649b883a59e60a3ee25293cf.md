## The Big Problem

RegTech emerged in the wake of the 2008 financial crisis. One effect of the universal financial crisis was the 492% rise in supervision laws (2008–2015). In parallel, there was a 45-fold rise in fines levied against the biggest banks between 2009 and 2014. Since then, financial institutions have been required to adhere to the control system's criteria, which target both user data security and criminal activity.

Financial penalties resulting from an incomplete adjustment are inescapable, despite the significant expense involved in implementing the new regulations. Since the 2008 global economic crisis, banking institutions have only been penalized more than $160 billion in the United States.

Compliance is estimated to cost banks over $70 billion yearly. RegTech can help reduce these expenses by improving effectiveness and efficiency.

## Advantages of Using RegTech

The best coordination of data interchange between the financial industry and regulatory agencies, increased data processing using AI tools or streamlined risk identification and analysis are the most significant advantages of deploying RegTech solutions.

Future developments might include technology-enabled process efficiency, data sharing and accumulation, real-time data supervising, anomaly detection, and blockchain-based compliance systems.

RegTech is a rapidly growing industry because of the rigorous regulations that apply to institutions in the financial sector. Utilizing modern technologies will lower the expenses of human mistakes, data storage, and regulatory adaptation, while also assisting in avoiding penalties and lowering fraud.

Blockchain is a great technology for tackling issues encountered by banks and other financial institutions because of its features including the Blockchain ledger, smart contracts, data tracking, and so forth.

In this section of the post, we'll discuss issues facing the healthcare sector and how blockchain technology may be able to help.

## Urgent Healthcare Issues

The absence of a global system for patient identification and record management is one of the most critical issues. Why is it essential? One of the causes of patient EHRs (Electronic Health Records) or digital personal medical records (DPMR) that don't match is this. When data is moved between medical systems, up to 50% of a patient's medical records are incorrect, according to statistics. Even within the same healthcare system, one out of every five patient records cannot be reliably linked. More than 176 million patient records are thought to have been compromised as a result of data security breaches between 2009 and 2018. It contains both genomic test results and banking information. The worrying development is that healthcare data breaches increased by 44.44% (monthly over a month) in October 2019. It indicates that in such breaches, over 600,000 healthcare records were reported as having been improperly released, exposed, or stolen. Although preserving records on paper might be safer, managing the daily volume of data generated is nearly impossible.

Insurance fraud is a significant issue that the healthcare sector must deal with. It involves unneeded medical treatment, prescriptions for drugs that are not legal, and unfounded allegations of corruption. Only in the USA, it is estimated that medical fraud costs roughly $68 billion annually. The serious problem of illegal drug sales affects the healthcare industry as well. The Health Research Funding Organization estimates that 10% to 30% of the medicines sold in developing nations are fake. An even bigger issue is that improper ingredient amounts are present in 16% and 17%, respectively, of counterfeit medications. Pharmaceutical businesses must therefore stress the security of the entire drug supply chain. Businesses may lose $200 billion a year because of fake medications.

## Solutions in the Form of Healthcare Blockchain

By 2025, blockchain technology may save the healthcare industry up to $100 billion per annum, according to a BIS Research study. These savings are the result of reduced costs associated with data infringement-related expenditures, IT costs, maintenance costs, costs for support functions and staff, frauds related to counterfeiting, and insurance frauds.

Pharmaceutical companies can trace pharmaceuticals across the whole supply chain thanks to blockchain technology, which makes it more difficult for thefts and enables quick discovery of inconsistencies. It makes sure that our data is more securely stored, offers informative transparency, and makes it possible for us to get real-time and current information. This makes it easier to spot false claims more quickly and reduces insurance fraud. Estonia was one of the first countries to lead the world in BC technology. It has a national electronic health records system that uses blockchains to integrate and store information from the healthcare sector and other fields. Data protection, integrity, and system access logs are ensured by blockchain capabilities, increasing security. The United Arab Emirates is another powerful nation. In line with UAE’s vision, the healthcare system will track licenses for health professionals by using blockchain technology.
