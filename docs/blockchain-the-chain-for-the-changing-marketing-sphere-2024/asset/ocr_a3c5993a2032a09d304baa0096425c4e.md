It also had issues with safety, technological dangers, and risks from governmental regulations. Blockchain technology, according to Zhang et al., (2020), has certain flaws, including tamper-proofness, a poor fault tolerance rate, a low consensus efficiency, and a small data capacity, which limit the effectiveness of an enterprise’s digital transformation. According to Gong et al., (2021), blockchain technology is still in its developmental stage, with limited empirical research and data available. More research is needed to assess its impact on marketing and business administration, as well as to address challenges such as scalability, privacy and security concerns, software defects, cyber-attacks, high investment costs, and the absence of legal regulations. Given its relative youth and immaturity, many experts stress the importance of establishing an international technical standard for blockchain technology.

# Countermeasures

To increase the data transparency of SMEs in logistics, money flow, and information flow, Lu et al., (2021) proposed applying the blockchain-distributed ledger to all nodes of company production and operation and adopting “blockchain+ credit reference” technology. According to Yang et al., (2020), building an intellectual property data platform, a trading platform, and a valuation platform were all effective ways to boost the value of SMEs. These steps included determining the developmental stage of businesses using the “blockchain+ big data” model, assessing their needs for financing and financial stability, and identifying the SMEs’ developmental stage. Tang (2018) used the digital transformation of SMEs in Germany as an example to suggest that the nation should create a unified framework for digital transformation, develop a mechanism for Internet regulation, strengthen trust and security in data processing, place a premium on and increase investment in R&D, and offer opportunities for vocational training.
