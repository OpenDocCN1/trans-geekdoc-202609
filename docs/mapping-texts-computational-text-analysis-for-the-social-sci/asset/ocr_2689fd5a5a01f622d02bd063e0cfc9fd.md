# DEDUCITIVE ANALYSIS WITH PRETRAINED MODELS

In the preceding example, we trained our own models. Eventually, we’d use these models on data not used in the training process—not just “testing” data, but also unlabeled data. This is what is meant by using a *pretrained* model. As open science gains ground, pretrained models will proliferate. How do we go about using them to augment deductive methods?

Here, we'll focus on pretrained *word embeddings*. Recall pretrained models are typically made up of weights on features, and sometimes they include methods for matching inputs to the necessary features. Embeddings are typically matrices, where the rownames are the terms, and the columns are the *d* dimensions of the embedding space. Each unique word is represented by a vector of length *d*. To use these embeddings, we’ll match all the words in our documents to those in the embeddings.17
