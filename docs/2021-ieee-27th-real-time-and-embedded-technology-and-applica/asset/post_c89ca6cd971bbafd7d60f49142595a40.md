
## Work in Progress: Role-based Deep Reinforcement Learning with Information Sharing for Intelligent Unmanned Systems

1st Qingshuang Sun, 2nd Yuan Yao, 3rd Peng Yi, 4th Xingshe Zhou, 5th Gang Yang
School of Computer Science
Northwestern Polytechnical University
Xi’an, China
{1st sunqsh, 3rd yipeng}@mail.nwpu.edu.cn, {2nd yaoyuan, 4th zhousx, 5th yeungg}@nwpu.edu.cn

Abstract—Intelligent unmanned systems (IUSs) are distributed systems composed of multiple agents that share information or cooperate to accomplish specific complex tasks. Agents of the IUS are capable of perception, cognition, control, decision-making, and action. In some cases, the environmental situation and task objectives faced by the IUSs are constantly changing with time. Thus, IUSs are time-sensitive systems. To accelerate the task execution time and response speed, IUSs use artificial intelligence technology to increase the speed and quality of the “observation-orientation-decision-action” (OODA) cycle of task execution. IUSs will tend to decompose the system into different functional units in the future, and individuals take different task roles from the functional perspective of OODA. The system is evolving from a linear OODA cycle of individuals to a cooperative OODA (Co-OODA) with different node roles. At present, the reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm is the mainstream method to solve IUSs cooperation problems. However, it does not adapt to the Co-OODA with different roles, and cannot maximize the Co-OODA system’s potential. This paper introduces the role-based Co-OODA system. Furthermore, we propose and design a role-based deep reinforcement learning framework and its corresponding information sharing mechanism.

Index Terms—intelligent unmanned systems, OODA, role, reinforcement learning, information sharing

## I. INTRODUCTION

With the complexity and diversity of the environment and tasks, agents, such as Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), Unmanned Ground Vehicles (UGVs), and mobile robots, are constrained by the software and hardware conditions, which have some limitation in the complex tasks. For example, when a single agent performs an exploration task, it cannot achieve multi-dimensional and a wide range of coverage of the task area. To make up the limitation of individual resources, intelligent unmanned systems (IUSs) have become the mainstream trend, helping humans complete some dangerous or difficult missions, such as reconnaissance and surveillance in military tasks and search and rescue missions in civilian task. IUSs have some technical superiorities such as efficient information sharing, supporting the decomposition and collaboration of complex tasks, and individual low-cost. They have more widespread application prospects, which has been a hot spot of concern.

With the artificial intelligence (AI) development, intelligent cooperation control is the core technology of IUSs research and application. Intelligent cooperation control refers to selecting and coordinating multiple agents’ behavior in a centralized or distributed manner to achieve a common goal without human intervention. Reinforcement learning (RL) is an important method of intelligent control, where agents interact with a dynamic environment to learn an optimal policy to adapt to complex environmental problems.

IUS leverages RL to learn policies, each agent run the linear loop of ‘observation-orientation-decisions-action’ (OODA) at a regular time step to achieve the purpose of cooperation. This approach requires individuals to integrate sensors, mission computers, and task executors. The individual that aggregates multiple functions has two disadvantages. One is that the collision and drop of integrated functional agents will cause the loss of high-value assets when performing tasks. The second is that each agent needs to run a complete OODA cycle before entering the next OODA loop, which will extend the task completion time. However, IUS is a time-sensitive system. The environmental situation and task goals are constantly changing over time. When the original task plan does not necessarily meet the tasks, the system needs to analyze and decide about individual behavior within a limited time frame. For example, in the case of confrontation, if one party completes the cycle of OODA faster and enter the opponent’s decision-making cycle, the opponent will be forced to react to new situations, resulting in errors in decision-making and actions. The OODA loop should therefore be accelerated.

Today, the trend of IUS development is that different roles of agents are constructed according to task demands. As shown in Fig. 1, there are observation nodes, orientation nodes, decision nodes, and action nodes based on OODA in the system, allowing individuals to active one or more role functions. This pattern divides the complex system into multiple minimum units with a single function and good interoperability. Therefore, role agents’ cooperation can form multiple cooperative OODA (Co-OODA) sub-cycles in the functionally decomposed IUS. The independent individual OODA cycle of IUS becomes an integrated Co-OODA cycle jointly finished by agents.

RL enables agents to perform a complete OODA cycle to learn their global policy. However, this learning method cannot fully use this new Co-OODA system with various node roles. To tackle this problem, we first describe the role-based Co-OODA system. Secondly, we need to focus on preliminarily proposing a new role-based deep reinforcement learning framework for this pattern, where individuals learn their role policies at different levels and time abstraction scales, to make full use of this new role-based Co-OODA system and realize the cooperation control of the IUSs effectively. Finally, according to this system, it is also necessary to propose an information sharing mechanism based on different role tasks, so that information can be transferred between different role nodes and ensure the completion of a continuous flow Co-OODA cycle.

## II. RELATED WORK

Entities in the IUSs can be treated as agents with considerable autonomous capabilities. Therefore, the IUSs system can be regarded as a multi-agent system. In recent years RL has made extraordinary progress on many sequential decision-making problems, such as playing the game of [1], robotic control [2], and autonomous driving [3]. With the effective application of RL in some complex single-agent tasks, many researchers have transferred RL's research results to a multi-agent system.

The current research on multi-agent reinforcement learning can be divided into three groups, fully cooperative, fully competitive, and a mix of the two, which depend on the types of task settings [4]. In particular, in the cooperative setting, the cooperation process of agents has been divided into two stages: “information sharing” and “collaborative decision-making” [5]. In recent years, with the outstanding ability of deep learning (DL) in complex data-driven tasks, a learning paradigm – deep reinforcement learning (DRL) that combines the powerful feature representation of DL and efficient strategy search of RL is applied to the cooperation task of a multi-agent system.

To maximize the utility of the team, information sharing is the fundamental function of a multiagent system. Learning communication in multi-agent learning is an emerging sub-area in which agents can learn communication even from scratch under a distributed, partially observable environment. The agent’s communication protocols can determine when to communicate, what kind of message to send, and who to send them to. In [6], Each agent is modeled by a deep feed-forward network, and the agent modeled can be trained to extract hidden state h from the state to learn what message to send. [7] designs an attention unit that enables agents to learn when to communicate for the first time.

## III. ROLE-BASED CO-OODA SYSTEM

The OODA cycle theory was proposed by Air Force Col John R. Boyd’s in 1987. It describes the behavioral process of agents [8]. Agents gather information from the environment and then orients themselves to the environment to decide the action to be selected from the action set, and then act. If IUSs want to win in a hyper-competitive environment, these four steps must cycle quickly and continuously, completing decision-making and actions faster than the opponent. In August 2017, DARPA proposed the operational concept of mosaic warfare, which integrates the other ideas and operational concepts of the US military in recent years and has become the top-level core concept of the DARPA military combat system. Mosaic Warfare decomposes complex integrated systems into various functional units such as sensor systems, command and control systems, weapon platform systems. Through dynamic communication networks, various units are linked to form a highly distributed and flexible operational network. Mosaic warfare has lots of characteristics. One of the most important characteristics is that battle resources are abstracted into capability nodes that undertake different functions of OODA, which are connected by communication networks into a Co-OODA system. On the one hand, there is no critical node or small set of nodes whose loss will collapse the entire system. On the other hand, the OODA cycle has a high agility degree, flexibly combining various functional units according to available resources.

The idea of mosaic warfare has advantages in dealing with military tasks, and this idea can also be applied to non-military fields. With this trend, IUS in the future will perform tasks based on advancements in processing, computing, and networking, having the ability to seamlessly share information when and where needed, and use the most advanced communication network to achieve connecting of various functional roles in the mission area. Information flow between nodes through advanced data links. Observation nodes cooperate and provide multi-dimensional observations to orientation nodes. The orientation node aggregates the current observation information with the existing knowledge base, database, and historical experience information to form environmental state information.
