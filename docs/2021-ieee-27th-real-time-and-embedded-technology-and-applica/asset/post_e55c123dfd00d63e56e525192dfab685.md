## IV. DEEP REINFORCEMENT LEARNING FRAMEWORK AND INFORMATION SHARING MECHANISM IN ROLE-BASED CO-OODA SYSTEM

Role-based Co-OODA can speed up the OODA cycle from two aspects. On the one hand, Fig. 2 shows that IUSs are composed of multiple agents with different task roles to perform tasks. Instead of being sequential and linear, the OODA cycles are far more integrated including multiple interlinked and nested procedures. The observation node, the orientation node, the decision node, and the action node can perform their role tasks in parallel, thereby accelerating the OODA loop. In Fig. 3, assume that the time interval for the individual to perform the sub-steps of the OODA cycle is τ that can be a real number or an integer. Here we assume that τ is a unit time. In the role-based OODA system, the observation node observes the environment at time t and provides the observation information flow to the orientation node. At time t+2, the orientation node processes the received information. At this time, the observation node can enter the next OODA cycle’s observation phase at t+2 and continues to perceive the environment. Using the role-based Co-OODA cycle can speed up the execution of tasks, and the entire Co-OODA cycle is more integrated. On the other hand, appropriate artificial intelligence technology is applied to each role layer to accelerate the operation of the sub-elements of Co-OODA, which gives impetus to run the whole Co-OODA cycle.

The intelligence in the unmanned system is reflected in using artificial intelligence technology (AI) to accelerate the OODA loop’s speed in a complex environment, shorten the responding time for unknown threats. In role-based IUSs, agents undertake different role tasks according to the sub-steps of the OODA cycle so that several role nodes are required to cooperate to complete the Co-OODA cycle during task execution. Agents need to learn their policy from different temporal abstractions based on their roles and tasks. Multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MADRL) is one of the fundamental technologies to realize the intelligence of unmanned systems, and information sharing is the basis for the efficient exchange of information between different role nodes. Currently, MADRL cannot enable agents to learn role-related information sharing policy and behavioral decision-making policy at different times. Therefore, it is very significant to study the innovative learning framework and information sharing mechanism to make full use of this role-based system.

To make full use of the role-based Co-OODA system features, designing a role-based DRL framework is the basis for the learning and training on the role-based Co-OODA system. Role nodes need various AI to finish their tasks. Thus, different AI methods need to be added to the DRL framework. Upon this frame, each individual learns its role task policy. At present, the method of hierarchical reinforcement learning (HRL) can decompose the task into some subtasks at different levels of abstraction to learn each subtask’s policy and combine the policy of multiple subtasks to form an effective global policy. The learning process can abstract and expand the action set based on different temporal abstraction, and the agent realizes hierarchical control by learning on different temporal scale levels. The learning process can abstract and expand the action set based on different temporal abstraction, and the agent realizes hierarchical control by learning on different levels of the layer. The idea of HRL’s task layering can be applied to the new DRL framework of the role-based Co-OODA system. Different nodes describe their action sets and perform policy learning on different time scales according to their role tasks.

based Co-OODA loop as $\pi$. The policy $\pi$ can be defined as a set of sub-policies of role nodes: $\pi = \{ \pi_1, \pi_2, \dots, \pi_i \}$, where $\pi_i$ is the policy of each role layer. In the learning process, different role layers need different AI technologies to learn their target policies. The observation layer requires image recognition algorithms, natural language processing, and other technologies to process the perceived environmental information. The orientation layer needs to leverage big data analytics and knowledge graph technology to perform data analysis, knowledge extraction, knowledge fusion, and knowledge processing on multi-dimensional environmental observations to obtain aggregated information. The decision layer needs intelligent reasoning and planning algorithms to complete the mapping of aggregated information to actions. The action layer acts to the environment to obtain the reward that feedback to each layer node. Thus, IUSs need a new RL framework to use different AI to learn sub-policies and provide feedback to train policies. It is important to improve an effective role-based deep reinforcement learning algorithm in the subsequent work, which enables each role node to learn its task sub-policies separately by using various methods for AI so as to build a global policy that can complete the role-based Co-OODA system.

In the role-based Co-OODA system, there is apparent information dependency between each role layer. In Fig. 4, the dotted line indicates the flow of information between nodes. Nodes rely on advanced data links to transmit high-fidelity and high-quality information flow to guarantee a continuous OODA loop. Therefore, efficient information sharing between nodes is also an important factor in supporting and accelerating the Co-OODA cycle. The information sharing mechanism of the role-based Co-OODA system is also one of the issues that need to be solved urgently. At the information sharing level, the nodes of the same role and different roles need to formulate information communication protocols in different aspects.

We propose a preliminary information sharing mechanism. For the same role layer, nodes need to use a hierarchical communication topology to share information by way of enhancing complementary local information. Nodes are divided into different groups, and each group needs to elect a group leader dynamically. The same group nodes need to learn what message to send and when to communicate to the leader. For example, Fig. 4 shows that there are several observation nodes in the observation layer, and these nodes form different groups.

The sub-center node collects the information from other nodes and integrates the information to be transmitted between different layers. The sub-center node is the bridge for information transmission of different role layers. The information sharing in different role layers: After the observation node perceives the environmental state in a specific unit time, needs to learn to identify and distill important information and determine whether it needs to be sent to which orientation node. The orientation node needs to learn to interpret, sort, aggregate and evaluate the received information, transform information into aggregate information to obtain the multi-dimensional environmental perception that decision nodes can use. The decision node makes decisions based on the environmental situation information obtained by the orientation node and transmits the information command to the action node that needs to be activated to perform the task. Therefore, we need to optimize further an effective information sharing mechanism for the continuous and circular operation of the role-based Co-OODA system in subsequent research.

## V. CONCLUSION

This paper analyzes a development trend of intelligent unmanned systems and describes a role-based Co-OODA system. To better use intelligent algorithms to solve the cooperation problem of IUS, we preliminarily propose a role-based DRL framework and information sharing mechanism. They guarantee the learning of each role node and information flow to ensure the continuous cyclic execution of OODA.
