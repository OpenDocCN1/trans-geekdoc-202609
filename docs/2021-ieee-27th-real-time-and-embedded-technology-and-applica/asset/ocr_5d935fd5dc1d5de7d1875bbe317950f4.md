Abstract—Intelligent unmanned systems (IUSs) are distributed systems composed of multiple agents that share information or cooperate to accomplish specific complex tasks. Agents of the IUS are capable of perception, cognition, control, decision-making, and action. In some cases, the environmental situation and task objectives faced by the IUSs are constantly changing with time. Thus, IUSs are time-sensitive systems. To accelerate the task execution time and response speed, IUSs use artificial intelligence technology to increase the speed and quality of the “observation-orientation-decision-action” (OODA) cycle of task execution. IUSs will tend to decompose the system into different functional units in the future, and individuals take different task roles from the functional perspective of OODA. The system is evolving from a linear OODA cycle of individuals to a cooperative OODA (Co-OODA) with different node roles. At present, the reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm is the mainstream method to solve IUSs cooperation problems. However, it does not adapt to the Co-OODA with different roles, and cannot maximize the Co-OODA system’s potential. This paper introduces the role-based Co-OODA system. Furthermore, we propose and design a role-based deep reinforcement learning framework and its corresponding information sharing mechanism.

Index Terms—intelligent unmanned systems, OODA, role, reinforcement learning, information sharing

# I. INTRODUCTION

With the complexity and diversity of the environment and tasks, agents, such as Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), Unmanned Ground Vehicles (UGVs), and mobile robots, are constrained by the software and hardware conditions, which have some limitation in the complex tasks. For example, when a single agent performs an exploration task, it cannot achieve multi-dimensional and a wide range of coverage of the task area. To make up the limitation of individual resources, intelligent unmanned systems (IUSs) have become the mainstream trend, helping humans complete some dangerous or difficult missions, such as reconnaissance and surveillance in military tasks and search and rescue missions in civilian task. IUSs have some technical superiorities such as efficient information sharing, supporting the decomposition and collaboration of complex tasks, and individual low-cost. They have more widespread application prospects, which has been a hot spot of concern.

With the artificial intelligence (AI) development, intelligent cooperation control is the core technology of IUSs research and application. Intelligent cooperation control refers to selecting and coordinating multiple agents’ behavior in a centralized or distributed manner to achieve a common goal without human intervention. Reinforcement learning (RL) is an important method of intelligent control, where agents interact with a dynamic environment to learn an optimal policy to adapt to complex environmental problems.

IUS leverages RL to learn policies, each agent run the liner loop of ‘observation-orientation-decisions-action’ (OODA) at a regular time step to achieve the purpose of cooperation. This approach requires individuals to integrate sensors, mission computers, and task executors. The individual that aggregates multiple functions has two disadvantages. One is that the collision and drop of integrated functional agents will cause the loss of high-value assets when performing tasks. The second is that each agent needs to run a complete OODA cycle before entering the next OODA loop, which will extend the task completion time. However, IUS is a time-sensitive system. The environmental situation and task goals are constantly changing over time. When the original task plan does not necessarily meet the tasks, the system needs to analyze and decide about individual behavior within a limited time frame. For example, in the case of confrontation, if one party completes the cycle of OODA faster and enter the opponent’s decision-making cycle, the opponent will be forced to react to new situations, resulting in errors in decision-making and actions. The OODA loop should therefore be accelerated.

This pattern divides the complex system into multiple minimum units with a single function and good interoperability. Therefore, role agents’ cooperation can form multiple cooperative OODA (Co-OODA) sub-cycles in the functionally decomposed IUS.
