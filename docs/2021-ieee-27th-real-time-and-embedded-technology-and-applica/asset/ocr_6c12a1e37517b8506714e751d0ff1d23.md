In the learning process, different role layers need different AI technologies to learn their target policies. The observation layer requires image recognition algorithms, natural language processing, and other technologies to process the perceived environmental information. The orientation layer needs to leverage big data analytics and knowledge graph technology to perform data analysis, knowledge extraction, knowledge fusion, and knowledge processing on multi-dimensional environmental observations to obtain aggregated information. The decision layer needs intelligent reasoning and planning algorithms to complete the mapping of aggregated information to actions. The action layer act to the environment to obtain the reward that feedback to each layer node. Thus, IUS needs a new RL framework to use different AI to learn sub-policies and provide feedback to train policies. It is important to improve an effective role-based deep reinforcement learning algorithm in the subsequent work, which enables each role node to learn its task sub-policies separately by using various methods for AI so as to build a global policy that can complete the role-based Co-OODA system.

In the role-based Co-OODA system, there is apparent information dependency between each role layer. In Fig. 4, the dotted line indicates the flow of information between nodes. Nodes rely on advanced data links to transmit high-fidelity and high-quality information flow to guarantee a continuous OODA loop. Therefore, efficient information sharing between nodes is also an important factor in supporting and accelerating the Co-OODA cycle. The information sharing mechanism of the role-based Co-OODA system is also one of the issues that need to be solved urgently. At the information sharing level, the nodes of the same role and different roles need to formulate information communication protocols in different aspects.

We propose a preliminary information sharing mechanism. For the same role layer, nodes need to use a hierarchical communication topology to share information by way of enhancing complimentary local information. Nodes are divided into different groups, and each group needs to elect a group leader dynamically. The same group nodes need to learn what message to send and when to communicate to the leader. For example, Fig. 4 shows that there are several observation nodes in the observation layer, and these nodes form different groups.

The sub-center node collects the information from other nodes and integrates the information to be transmitted between different layers. The sub-center node is the bridge for information transmission of different role layers. The information sharing in different role layers, After the observation node perceives environmental state in a specific unit time, needs to learn to identify and distill important information and determine whether it needs to be sent to the which orientation node. The orientation node needs to learn to interpret, sort, aggregate and evaluate the received information, transform information into aggregate information to obtain the multi-dimensional environmental perception that decision nodes can use. The decision node makes decisions based on the environmental situation information obtained by the orientation node and transmits the information command to the action node that needs to be activated to perform the task. Therefore, we need to optimize further an effective information sharing mechanism for the continuous and circular operation of the role-based Co-OODA system in subsequent research.

# V. CONCLUSION

This paper analyzes a development trend of intelligent unmanned systems and describes a role-based Co-OODA system. To better use intelligent algorithms to solve the cooperation problem of IUS, we preliminary propose a role-based DRL framework and information sharing mechanism. They guarantee the learning of each role node and information flow to ensure the continuous cyclic execution of OODA.
