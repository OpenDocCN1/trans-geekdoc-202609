
# Data Exploration

Data exploration is a crucial step in data mining because it provides a comprehensive understanding of the data, focusing on parameters, characteristics, trends, and potential concerns. This understanding enables us to take appropriate actions to clean, prepare, and transform the data as needed. It also guides us in selecting the right machine learning methods and analytical configurations to develop a robust predictive model. During this step, we use visualization and descriptive statistics to explore and understand the data, the variables, and any potential issues, such as missing values or outliers. Visualization is a powerful tool for data exploration, and there are many visualization techniques available. I will demonstrate these tools with examples.

## Data Understanding

Before conducting any data analysis, it is essential to understand the data, its parameters, and its quality. The quality of the inputs directly influences the quality of the outputs—hence the saying, “Garbage in, garbage out.” This step is crucial for any type of data analysis, but it is particularly important for data mining, where we often deal with large, noisy datasets. When faced with a dataset that contains many variables and records, gaining a comprehensive understanding can be challenging due to the data's sheer size. Without a thorough understanding, we risk using invalid or irrelevant variables as predictors during modeling, or we may overlook important variables. Additionally, it can be difficult to select the appropriate machine learning method without understanding the data's characteristics and limitations. A poor model fit might also result from excessive missing values or outliers in the dataset.

To address these challenges, it is vital to thoroughly explore the data, focusing on the type of information available, the variables present, the measurement scales of those variables, and any emerging trends. As discussed in [Chapter 2](#chapter2.xhtml), the selection of a machine learning method depends on the data, the scale of the target variable, and the purpose of the project.

Data exploration is one of the most critical steps in data mining because it allows us to assess the data's structure and identify potential issues. Based on this understanding, we can determine the necessary strategies for data modification and transformation. A good predictive model requires quality inputs—the model is only as good as the data it's built upon. For example, imagine you are an airport executive responsible for predicting the likelihood of flight delays or cancellations at your airport on specific days or times. If your predictive model, based on poor inputs such as missing values or excessive noise, inaccurately predicts a low likelihood of delays (a false negative), you might decide not to take any preventive action. As a result, you could face significant chaos at the airport due to unexpected flight cancellations—an outcome that could have been mitigated with better data and a more accurate prediction. Although other factors can contribute to poor predictions, poor data quality is often a primary cause. Remember, “garbage in, garbage out.” [Table 4.1](#chapter4.xhtml#tbl4_1) highlights some key reasons why data exploration is vital in data mining.

There are two primary ways to explore data: visualization and statistical exploration. Visualization allows us to observe data trends and patterns through various types of graphs, making it easy to extract useful information. This method is user-friendly and can be incorporated into business intelligence dashboards for managers or executives. Patterns and trends can be quickly identified through visualization. Many visualization tools are available on the market, such as `Tableau`, `Power BI`, and `SAS Viya`. These applications enable the addition and customization of charts and graphs to meet specific needs. For big data, these tools can process and display data in real time, allowing users to monitor patterns of variables of interest over time. These dynamic visualization tools provide up-to-date information.

In this book, I focus primarily on the charts used to explore data for analysis purposes. These charts are deterministic, meaning they do not allow for interaction or real-time adjustments. Therefore, separate charts must be created for different variables and analysis purposes. Nevertheless, they provide essential information that helps us understand, process, and develop good predictive models. In this context, data visualization is considered a step in the data mining process. More extensive data visualization tools can be discussed in another book.

For data mining purposes, visualization alone may not be sufficient. Statistical exploration offers valuable quantitative information, such as descriptive statistics of variables, which help us understand the characteristics of the data. Additionally, statistical exploration provides preliminary insights into correlations between the target variable and predictors. By combining visualization with statistical exploration, we can leverage the strengths of both methods. In this book, I will demonstrate how to use both approaches to explore and understand data effectively.
