
Many streaming services, such as Netflix, Amazon Prime, and Hulu, collect data daily from users regarding their preferences for movies and TV shows, as well as their viewing habits. These companies use machine learning algorithms to uncover patterns from the data, showing the relationship between user preferences and demographic information. The streaming application can then predict what types of TV shows or movies a user may want to watch and suggest similar content. For example, if I am a fan of action movies and have watched numerous Tom Cruise and Will Smith films, the next time I log in to Netflix, I will see suggestions for other action movies featuring these actors. The home page may also show several TV shows and movies in the same genre. Additionally, Netflix uses this information to decide which kinds of shows or movies to invest in to increase user subscriptions. Examples of popular Netflix TV shows and movies include *Stranger Things*, *House of Cards*, *The Crown*, *The Irishman*, and *Red Notice*.

### Social Networks

![](images/ufig1_9.jpg)

Top social media platforms like Facebook and Twitter collect users’ demographic information, product or service searches, and posts, photos, and communications shared on social media. Algorithms are developed to predict user behavior and their relationship with demographic information. The application suggests relevant sites, shows ads based on user preferences, and recommends potential friends or posts the user may want to see. For instance, I searched for new posts about Lexus vehicles on Facebook, and soon after, I began seeing more ads for Lexus vehicles for sale. I also received friend suggestions based on my friend database, allowing me to reconnect with long-lost friends. These companies use the data mining output to adjust or change their applications to better meet user demands and attract more users.

### Online Shopping

![](images/ufig1_10.jpg)

Online shopping has gradually outperformed the traditional brick-and-mortar business model. Amazon is a dominant company in e-commerce and has achieved great success. Most traditional retailers have transitioned to e-commerce to improve profitability. Online retailers like Amazon, Best Buy, and Walmart use machine learning algorithms to predict customer shopping behavior. Their online platforms can suggest substitute products or new items based on customer demographics, past order history, or search history. For example, after searching for a smart outlet on Amazon, my Amazon app later suggested several options for smart outlets from different sellers. This convenience extends to predicting rising demand for specific products, allowing companies to prepare inventory to fulfill orders efficiently while reducing supply chain costs, inventory levels, and shipping times, thereby enhancing customer satisfaction.

### Airline Industry

![](images/ufig1_11.jpg)

The airline industry is one of the largest and most competitive sectors in the United States. Airline executives use data mining to improve passenger satisfaction and operational efficiency. Airlines collect passenger demographic information and travel behavior data to uncover relationships, allowing them to predict future travel demand and offer passengers promotional deals based on travel history. For example, I have flown frequently with American Airlines. The airline app records my travel history and occasionally sends recommendations for discounted tickets to similar destinations. Additionally, I receive offers for frequent flyers, such as bonus reward points for specific trips or promotions for airline credit cards. Airlines also analyze factors like onboard services, Wi-Fi, cleanliness, legroom, food quality, flight delays, and cancellations to understand passenger loyalty. This enables them to improve operations and services to retain and attract loyal customers.

### Banking and Finance

![](images/ufig1_12.jpg)

Banks and mortgage companies extensively use data mining to predict customer decisions and assess creditworthiness. Many of us have checking and savings accounts with banks, and we may decide to switch to another bank for various reasons. Banks use data mining to predict the likelihood of a customer closing an account, using demographics and banking history. Using these insights, bank managers can offer attractive deals to retain customers. Predictive modeling also helps banks offer appealing credit card deals or financing services. Additionally, mortgage companies use data mining to assess whether borrowers can repay their loans. They evaluate customer creditworthiness based on demographics and credit history to decide whether to lend, the loan amount, and the term. Data mining plays a key role in minimizing lenders’ risks.

### Healthcare

![](images/ufig1_13.jpg)

Data mining is vital in healthcare, helping providers improve service quality. Hospitals and doctors’ offices collect patients’ demographic information, health history, and treatment data. Predictive models can support patient treatment in several ways. For example, when a new patient arrives with certain symptoms, a doctor can enter the information into the system, which can suggest a preliminary diagnosis and treatment plan. The system can also predict the severity of the condition and suggest suitable treatments based on similar past cases. Furthermore, predictive modeling can determine if an issue is isolated or part of a larger pattern, enabling healthcare professionals to intervene and mitigate risks more effectively.

### Transportation

![](images/ufig1_14.jpg)

Traffic congestion is a significant issue in the United States and other developed countries, leading to delays, increased emissions, and wasted time. A large amount of traffic data is collected in real time, which can be used to predict the probability of traffic jams at specific locations and times. Authorities can use these predictions to reduce congestion and develop long-term transportation strategies, such as building new roads or promoting alternative transportation options. Accidents are also common and may lead to severe injuries or fatalities. Predictive models can be developed to anticipate accidents and their potential timing and location. Authorities can use these predictions to allocate resources effectively, ensuring that emergency medical technicians (EMTs) can reach the scene promptly. Remember, every second counts.

These are just a few examples of how data mining is being applied in practice to empower businesses and organizations to enhance operations, improve profitability, boost customer satisfaction, and ensure safety. There are numerous additional applications across domains such as education, training, manufacturing, engineering, aerospace, social science, and biology. Throughout this book, we will explore specific case studies across various sectors. Depending on your industry, you can determine how data mining can be applied. The reassuring aspect is that the same processes and algorithms can be used to analyze data; the difference lies in how you use the outcomes to make informed business decisions.
