## What Is the Regression Method?

Regression is a widely used method that estimates the relationship between a dependent variable (target variable) and multiple independent variables (predictors). The goal of regression is to develop a mathematical function or model that can predict the values of the target variable based on the predictors. In many statistics books, the terms "dependent variable" and "independent variable" are commonly used. However, for consistency, I will use the terms "target variable" and "predictors" throughout this book. It's important to remember that the target variable is the same as the dependent variable and predictors are the same as independent variables.

![](images/ufig7_4.jpg)

Let's consider an example: Suppose we want to predict whether customers will choose to dine at a restaurant. Here, the target variable is the dining decision (Yes or No), while predictors may include factors such as gender, race, age, education, income, diet preference, and health condition. The regression model built from this data identifies predictors that significantly influence the dining decision and estimates mathematical relationships between those predictors and the target variable. More specifically, the regression method calculates the coefficients (weights) of the predictors in the model to minimize the difference between predicted values and the actual values of the target variable.

The primary advantage of regression is its simplicity in terms of understanding, usage, and interpretation. Since its mathematical foundation is similar to what is taught in most statistics courses, there is an abundance of resources that explain the algorithm in detail. In fact, most statistics or quantitative analysis books provide comprehensive explanations of this method and how the regression equation is estimated. In this book, I will focus more on the practical application and purpose of regression rather than delving into the detailed mathematical foundations. However, I will explain key terminologies and important aspects of the data analysis process as needed.
