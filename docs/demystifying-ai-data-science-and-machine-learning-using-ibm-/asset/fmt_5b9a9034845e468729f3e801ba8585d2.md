
Another example of a population is all highway transportation incidents in Canada. By concentrating on this population, we can build predictive models to assess the risk of injuries in traffic incidents within that country. In this context, we avoid combining different modes of transportation, such as air, road, and water, because incidents across these modes have vastly different characteristics and contributing factors. Including such diverse data could introduce noise, leading to an invalid predictive model. By focusing solely on highway transportation, we can develop an effective and reliable model that accurately predicts highway incidents.

### Sampling Frame

Once we have determined the appropriate population for our study, the next step is to establish the sampling frame. It is neither practical nor recommended to survey or collect data from every individual within our population. One obvious reason is the difficulty in reaching every individual, and the fact that some may decline to participate, as participation is voluntary. Individuals have the right to refuse requests from data-collecting organizations.

Moreover, it is unnecessary to gather data from the entire population to draw valid conclusions. Statistically, studying a sample of the population, if done correctly, allows us to generalize the results to the entire population. The key is to ensure that the sample is representative and of adequate size. To achieve a representative sample, we must identify an appropriate sampling frame.

Simply put, “sampling frame” is the actual list of units or sources from which a sample is drawn. This frame should possess characteristics similar to the population to ensure representativeness. For example, in the airline recommendation project, where our population consists of U.S. adult residents who travel by air, we might collect data from residents in six states: New York, Minnesota, Florida, Texas, California, and Washington. These states serve as our sampling frame, representing 63 different regions—Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Southwest, and Northwest—thereby ensuring the sample's representativeness.

However, if we were to collect data only from residents in California, a state in the West, our sample would likely be underrepresented. This is because residents of California may have unique characteristics and behaviors specific to the Western region, making them unrepresentative of the entire U.S. population. Such a discrepancy between the sampling frame and the population would introduce sampling bias, leading to analysis results that do not accurately reflect the population. This is why it is crucial to carefully identify the sampling frame before proceeding with data sampling.

### Sample

“Sample” is a small subset of a population that we use for data analysis to make inferences about the entire population. For our conclusions about the population to be valid, the sample must be representative. To achieve this, we need a well-defined sampling frame and must draw the sample from that frame.

For example, in the airline recommendation project, we selected a sampling frame consisting of six states (New York, Minnesota, Florida, Texas, California, and Washington). We then collect data from qualified residents in those states. The qualifications require that residents must be 18 years or older and have used air transportation for travel in the past. This ensures that we focus on individuals who have used air travel and are capable of purchasing tickets. Their airline recommendations are valuable for predicting travel demand. A sample is considered representative if it shares the same attributes and characteristics as the population. Consequently, the analysis results based on this sample can be used to make inferences about the population.

### Sampling Methods

Even with a well-constructed sampling frame, we may still end up with a biased sample if we do not use an appropriate sampling strategy. “Sampling method” refers to the procedure we use to select participants and collect data. For instance, when an organization conducts a survey, they send invitations to residents within the sampling frame (the six states mentioned above) and request their responses. However, how they select those participants is crucial to ensuring a representative sample.

In data mining, if the full dataset has been collected by another organization, such as an airline or a government agency, data scientists must decide how to select individuals from that dataset for the sample. This process is known as the sampling method. Choosing the right sampling method is essential to ensuring the quality of the data. In the next section, I will describe common sampling methods in detail.
