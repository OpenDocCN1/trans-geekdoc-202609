## Rotation Methods

| Method | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Promax** | **Promax** is an oblique rotation method that extends the advantages of **Varimax** rotation while also accounting for possible correlations between the rotated components. It is considered a compromise between the simplicity of orthogonal rotation and the flexibility of oblique rotation. |

## Core Concepts

| Concept | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Dimensionality reduction** | Once the principal components have been identified, **PCA** can be used to transfer the high-dimensional data into a lower-dimensional data, while still retaining as much of the original variation as possible. The number of principal components retained determines the dimensionality of the new data. |
| **Reconstruction** | **PCA** allows the inverse transformation of the lower-dimensional data back into the original high-dimensional space. Although some information is lost during dimensionality reduction, the reconstruction can still be useful for interpretation or other downstream tasks. |

## Practical Applications of PCA in the Industry

![](images/ufig12_6.jpg)

**436.** **PCA** has a wide range of practical applications across various industries. Some key examples include:

*   **Image compression**: **PCA** can be used to compress images by reducing the dimensionality of the image data while preserving the essential features. For example, in facial recognition systems, **PCA** can be used to reduce the number of dimensions in the image data to improve recognition accuracy.
*   **Finance**: In finance, **PCA** can be used to identify the underlying factors that drive the correlation structure of asset returns. By decomposing the covariance matrix of asset returns into principal components, portfolio managers can better understand the sources of risk and return in their portfolios.
*   **Genetics**: In genetics, **PCA** can be used to identify the genetic variations that contribute to the differences between individuals. By analyzing the principal components of genetic data, researchers can identify groups of individuals with similar genetic profiles and gain insights into the genetic basis of diseases.
*   **Natural Language Processing**: In natural language processing, **PCA** can be used to reduce the dimensionality of text data by extracting the most informative features. For example, in sentiment analysis, **PCA** can be used to identify the keywords and phrases that are most strongly associated with positive or negative sentiment.
*   **Marketing**: In marketing, **PCA** can be used to identify the key factors that drive consumer preferences and behavior. By analyzing the principal components of customer data, marketers can identify the segments of customers that are most similar and tailor their marketing strategies accordingly.

These are just a few examples of the many applications of **PCA**. **PCA** is a versatile and widely used technique that can help simplify complex datasets and reveal the underlying structure and patterns in the data.

## Applications of PCA in Data Mining

![](images/ufig12_7.jpg)

In the context of data mining and predictive modeling, **PCA** serves two main purposes, as follows.

*   **In the Modify step of supervised learning**: **PCA** is used to transform a large number of predictors into a smaller number of predictors (principal components). These components are then used as new predictors in the Model step to develop predictive models for the target variable.
*   **In unsupervised learning**: **PCA** is employed to explore and detect underlying theoretical factors within the data. The principal components represent these factors, which can then be used to build a theoretical research model to examine the relationships among them.

## Advantages and Disadvantages

**PCA** is a powerful technique for reducing the dimensionality of datasets in machine learning. It has several advantages, but there are also some challenges associated with its use. Understanding these can help data scientists make informed decisions about whether **PCA** is suitable for their specific data mining projects.

**437. Advantages of PCA:**

*   **Reduces dimensionality**: **PCA** effectively reduces the number of variables in a dataset, making the data easier to analyze, visualize, and interpret.
*   **Retains maximum information**: **PCA** minimizes information loss by retaining the most important variance in the data, ensuring that the reduced dataset still captures most of the original dataset's essential information.
*   **Eliminates correlated features**: By transforming correlated variables into uncorrelated principal components, **PCA** eliminates redundancy and simplifies the structure of the data.
*   **Improves model accuracy**: By removing noise and irrelevant variables, **PCA** can improve the accuracy of machine learning models, as the most relevant data are highlighted.
*   **Speeds up computation**: Reducing the number of dimensions can significantly decrease the computational burden of machine learning algorithms, leading to faster processing and model building.

**Disadvantages of PCA:**

*   **Interpretability**: The new set of principal components may not have a clear or intuitive interpretation, making it difficult to understand the meaning of the transformed variables.
*   **Information loss**: While **PCA** aims to retain as much information as possible, reducing dimensions inevitably leads to some loss of detail. The more dimensions you remove, the more information may be lost.
*   **Sensitivity to outliers**: Outliers can distort the calculation of principal components, leading to skewed results and potentially undermining the analysis.
*   **Assumes linearity**: **PCA** works best when the relationships between variables are linear. It may not perform well on datasets with complex, non-linear relationships.
*   **Scalability**: **PCA** can struggle with very large datasets, as the computational and memory requirements increase with the size of the data, potentially limiting its scalability in some cases.

Balancing these advantages and disadvantages allows data scientists to decide whether **PCA** is the best dimensionality reduction technique for their specific needs.

## Steps of Principal Component Analysis

![](images/ufig12_9.jpg)

The general steps involved in performing **PCA** are outlined as follows:

1.  **Data standardization**: The first step in **PCA** is to standardize the dataset by centering the variables around zero and scaling them to have unit variance. This ensures that variables with larger variances do not dominate the analysis.
2.  **Compute the covariance matrix**: After standardizing the data, the covariance matrix is calculated to capture the relationships between the variables in the dataset.
3.  **Compute the eigenvectors and eigenvalues**: The next step is to compute the eigenvectors and eigenvalues of the covariance matrix. The eigenvectors represent the directions of maximum variability in the data, while the eigenvalues quantify the amount of variability each eigenvector explains.
4.  **438. Rank eigenvectors by eigenvalues**: The eigenvectors are ranked according to their eigenvalues. The eigenvector with the highest eigenvalue represents the first principal component, explaining the most variance.
5.  **Select the principal components**: In this step, the number of principal components to retain is determined. Typically, this is based on a threshold for the variance explained (e.g., 70% or higher) or using methods like the **Scree Plot** to help identify the optimal number of components.
6.  **Transform the data**: The selected principal components are then used to transform the data into a lower-dimensional space, resulting in a new set of variables that capture most of the original dataset's variability.
7.  **Interpret the results**: Finally, the transformed data can be analyzed to uncover patterns and structures in the reduced-dimensional space.
