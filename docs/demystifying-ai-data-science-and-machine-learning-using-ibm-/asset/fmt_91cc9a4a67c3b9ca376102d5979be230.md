# 8. There are several buzzwords related to data mining and predictive modeling that we hear or read every day: **Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning,** and **Deep Learning**. These terms are often overlapping and can be confusing, so I will briefly explain the differences among them. I will not go into great detail, as each is a field of study and a degree by itself. I will also explain the evolution of these fields of study over time ([Figure 1.3](#chapter1.xhtml#fig1_3)).

First, **Artificial Intelligence (AI)**. While AI is a growing trend in research, it is not a new term. The term AI was created back in the 1950s to refer to software that uses complex logic to automate operations. AI has evolved significantly over time with advances in computer science and robotics. But AI is not just about building robots. A more current definition of AI is “a system that simulates human intelligence for solving problems or making decisions.” AI can learn, predict, improve, and solve. Essentially, AI could be an application or solution that operates and acts with minimal human interference. Siri, Alexa, and Google Assistant are examples of AI virtual assistants that make our lives easier with smartphones, smart homes, and smart cars. Tesla's Full Self-Driving system is an AI that advances autonomous vehicles. ChatGPT is a generative AI that is capable of understanding natural language and performing various intelligent tasks based on users’ demands. These applications are just the beginning of the AI era. We will soon see more amazing AI tools in all industries. Of course, as machines get smarter, concerns regarding ethics and social impacts will arise. However, that is a topic for a different book.

**Machine learning** is a core component of AI, and it started in the 1980s. Machine learning involves algorithms that learn patterns from data without explicit instructions. The data mining we have been discussing uses machine learning algorithms. These algorithms are developed based on the foundations of mathematics, statistics, and computer science. In the next chapters, I will describe specific machine learning algorithms that we can use in our research. In the past, a strong background in mathematics and coding skills were essential for using machine learning due to the extensive math involved, and only certain software, like MATLAB^®, could handle the algorithms. Fortunately, in the last few years, more user-friendly applications have been created. As a result, **9. machine learning is no longer just for mathematicians or computer scientists, it can also be used for applied research.** While data scientists still use programming languages like Python or R for machine learning work, non-programmers now have more options to leverage this method for their research or businesses. Software applications such as SAS Enterprise Miner, IBM SPSS Modeler, Rapid Miner, KNIME, and Orange are easy-to-use data mining tools that allow us to analyze large datasets without writing codes.

Last but not least, **Deep Learning** is a buzzword we have started hearing more often in the past few years, even though it was introduced in the 2010s. Initially, it was not widely mentioned because only a few people understood the concept. Now, everyone talks about Deepfake, voice recognition, facial recognition on smartphones, and Tesla's Self-Driving Technology. In essence, deep learning is a complex neural network with many hidden layers. Some popular deep learning methods are convolutional neural networks and recurrent neural networks. I will not go into the technical details of this field, but it is a rapidly growing branch of machine learning. The technology that Apple uses for facial recognition and cameras is based on deep learning, as is Tesla's Self-Driving Technology. Deep learning is also used in natural language processing, art and music creation, and many other applications. You may have seen art and music created by AI that are indistinguishable from human creations. Deep learning remains an advanced subject, requiring a lot of data and complex algorithms to be performed correctly. Therefore, it is harder to find software that can handle deep learning without programming and coding.
