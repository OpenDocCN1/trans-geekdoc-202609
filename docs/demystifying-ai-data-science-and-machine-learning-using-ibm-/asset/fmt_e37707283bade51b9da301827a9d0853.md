# How to Write a Literature Review Section?

![](images/ufig11_13.jpg)

When writing a literature review section for a research paper that focuses on data mining, you need to review and summarize existing research and scholarly works specifically related to the topic, applications of data mining techniques in this area, and any advancements. Following are some tips to help you write an effective literature review section.

1.  **Define the scope**: Determine the scope and boundaries of your literature review. Use the research questions and study objectives to guide your review within the field of the focused topic and data mining. This helps focus your search and analysis on relevant literature.

2.  **Identify relevant sources**: Conduct a comprehensive search to identify relevant sources specifically related to the scope identified above. Use library databases, academic journals, conference proceedings, and reputable organizations that cover relevant topics. Include recent and seminal works to ensure the review is up-to-date and comprehensive.

3.  **Categorize the literature**: Organize the literature based on different categories or themes related to the topic of interest. If your paper involves advancements in data mining methods, include topics such as data preprocessing techniques, algorithm development, or relevant subfields within data mining.

4.  **Summarize key findings**: Summarize the key findings and contributions of each source within the relevant categories or themes. Provide concise summaries of each work, including the research objectives, methodologies, datasets, sample size, and main results or conclusions. Highlight any unique or innovative approaches, algorithms, or applications discussed. If you have a large number of studies to review, using a table to summarize key findings can make it easier to follow and save space.

5.  **Identify gaps and limitations**: Identify any gaps, limitations, or unresolved issues in the existing literature related to your topic. Discuss areas where further research is needed or where conflicting results or opinions exist. This highlights the need and rationale for your own research.

6.  **Compare and contrast**: For data mining literature, compare and contrast different approaches, algorithms, or techniques discussed. Analyze the strengths and weaknesses of each, considering factors such as accuracy, scalability, interpretability, or applicability. Identify trends, similarities, and differences in findings across reviewed studies.

7.  410. **Synthesize the literature**: Synthesize the information by integrating findings and conclusions across the reviewed sources. Look for common themes, patterns, or emerging trends. Provide a coherent narrative that connects the literature to your research objectives. This synthesis helps you identify the theoretical foundation for your study.

8.  **Describe the theoretical foundation for the study**: For theory-driven research, present the theoretical foundation or framework used to select variables for analysis. Even for data-driven, exploratory research like data mining studies, providing a theoretical basis for selecting variables is still important.

9.  **Discuss the novelty of this study**: Explain why your study is original and how it fills research gaps. Identify novel variables or approaches added to the study and describe their significance. This answers the "so what" question for the readers.

10.  **Write and revise**: Draft the literature review and incorporate the above points. Review and revise multiple times to ensure a logical flow, proper citation of sources, and a comprehensive overview of the literature.

The literature review section should provide an overview of current knowledge and advancements in your field. It should demonstrate your understanding of existing literature and how it informs and supports your research objectives. By critically analyzing and synthesizing the literature, you establish the context and rationale for your research within the field of data mining.

Since this section requires a thorough review of existing studies, I don't provide detailed examples specific to the airline recommendation study as it's hypothetical. However, by following the steps outlined, you should be able to write an effective literature review that informs readers of current knowledge in airline management, gaps in the literature, and the value this study adds.

Note that for technical and business reports, this section is usually much lighter and less focused on peer-reviewed literature. Instead, it emphasizes case studies, technical reports, or managerial reports published by organizations in the same field. These types of reports typically do not require extensive justification of the novelty of the study or its theoretical contributions. Instead, they focus more on the practical contributions and actionable insights derived from the findings. Therefore, depending on the type of report you are writing, you can adjust the literature review section accordingly to highlight relevant practical applications and insights.
