
## Domain Diversity

In some cases, domain-specific models or models trained on different datasets can be combined in an ensemble. This is particularly useful in transfer learning scenarios, where knowledge from one domain is leveraged to improve performance in another related domain.

By combining diverse base learners, ensemble models can collectively address different aspects of the data mining process and reduce individual model biases. Diversity is crucial for avoiding the problem of "groupthink," where multiple models end up making similar errors due to similar biases or limitations. Instead, a diverse ensemble fosters a more robust and accurate decision-making process, making ensemble modeling a powerful tool for tackling complex real-world challenges.

## Ensemble Methods

![](images/ufig10_5.jpg)

371. Now that we understand the purpose of ensemble modeling, how do we combine the predictions generated by multiple models? There are three primary methods for building ensemble models: `bagging`, `boosting`, and `stacking`.

### Bagging

Bagging stands for bootstrap aggregating. Bootstrapping is a sampling technique in which subsets of the dataset are created using a replacement procedure, and aggregation combines the predictions from each model to generate a final result. In bagging, multiple models are created by training each one on a different subset of the training data, which is generated by randomly sampling the data with replacement. The final prediction is the average of all these individual model predictions.

Bagging is especially useful for reducing variance. Since each model in the ensemble is trained on a different subset of the data, they may have different biases and variances. By combining their predictions, the ensemble model produces a prediction with lower variance than any single model. [Figure 10.1](#chapter10.xhtml#fig10_1) illustrates how bagging works.

### Boosting

**Boosting** is an ensemble technique that improves predictions by learning from previous predictor mistakes. In boosting, models are trained sequentially, with each model focusing on correcting the residual errors of the previous model. As a result, the errors made by earlier models are addressed by later ones. The predictions of all the models are then combined to produce the final output.

Boosting is effective for reducing bias. Since each model in the sequence is trained on the residuals of the previous model, it helps correct any biases present in earlier models. This technique is particularly useful for improving accuracy, especially in classification tasks. Boosting is illustrated in [Figure 10.2](#chapter10.xhtml#fig10_2).

### Stacking

**Stacking**, or stacked generalization, takes a different approach. In stacking, multiple models are trained, and their predictions serve as inputs to a meta-model. The meta-model then learns how to combine the predictions of the base models to produce a final, improved prediction.

Stacking is useful when the individual models in the ensemble have different strengths and weaknesses. By combining their predictions and allowing the meta-model to determine the optimal weights for each model's output, the ensemble produces a more accurate final prediction. ## Aggregation Methods

![](images/ufig10_6.jpg)

373. Ensemble modeling involves combining the predictions of multiple base learners to obtain a more accurate and robust final prediction. In this process, aggregation methods are used to calculate or determine the final ensemble prediction from individual models. Some common aggregation methods used in ensemble modeling include averaging, voting, weighted voting, and maximum.

### Averaging

*   In averaging, the predictions of individual base learners are combined by taking their arithmetic mean (for association models) or their weighted mean (for classification models).
*   For regression tasks, the ensemble prediction is the average of the individual predictions, resulting in a smoother and more stable final prediction.
*   In probabilistic classification, averaging the probabilities of the level (of the categorical target variable) from different base learners helps in obtaining a more confident estimate of the event probabilities.
*   It is particularly effective in reducing variance and improving model stability since the aggregated predictions tend to be less sensitive to outliers or noisy predictions from individual models.
