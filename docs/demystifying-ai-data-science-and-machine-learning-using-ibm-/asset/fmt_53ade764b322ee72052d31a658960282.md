
## Validity Evaluation of the Champion Model

The next step is to assess the validity of the champion model. For this, we focus on the model fit metrics for the validation sample as the final evaluation, as outlined in [Table 9.7](#chapter9.xhtml#tbl9_7). The observed prediction accuracy (ACC) falls below 80%, a threshold typically considered suboptimal. However, the sensitivity (true positive prediction) performs well at 0.906. The lower ACC is primarily due to the subpar specificity (true negative prediction) of 0.656, indicating a higher false positive rate.

**TABLE 9.7** Validity Evaluation of the Champion Model—MLP 3 [Return to text.⏎](#chapter9.xhtml#b9tbl9_7)

| Metrics | Validation | Evaluation | C&R Tree 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ACC | 78.71% | Not good | 79.5% |
| MR | 21.29% | Not good | 20.5% |

| Specificity or TNR | 0.656 | Not good | 0.623 |
| F1 | 0.817 | Acceptable | 0.83 |

The F1 score, which balances sensitivity and specificity, is within acceptable limits. The ROC index, at 0.85, is also considered acceptable, though an index of 0.9 or higher would indicate a better model fit. The Gini coefficient is quite good at 0.699. Additionally, the Lift chart reveals that with 20% of the dataset in use, the Lift value is 1.64, meaning the model captures 1.64 times more positive cases (32.8%) compared to a model-less scenario.

In summary, when we consider all metrics collectively, we can conclude that the model has marginally acceptable predictive power.

In conclusion, while certain fit metrics for this predictive model are deemed acceptable, the model may not meet the airline's expectations for predicting airline recommendations from passengers. Although the model performs well in predicting positive cases of airline recommendations, it falls short in accurately predicting non-recommendations, with a need for improvement in overall accuracy, particularly in reducing false positives. This mirrors the conclusions we reached in [Chapters 7](#chapter7.xhtml) and [9](#chapter9.xhtml).

As shown, the decision tree model has higher accuracy (ACC), lower MR, higher sensitivity, and a better F1 score. However, the `MLP 3` neural network model outperforms it in terms of specificity, Gini coefficient, and ROC index.
