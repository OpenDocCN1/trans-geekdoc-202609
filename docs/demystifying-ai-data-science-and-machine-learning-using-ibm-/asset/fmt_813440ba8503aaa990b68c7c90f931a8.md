Because of these differences in splitting algorithms, CART tends to produce smaller (narrower) but taller trees due to its binary splits, whereas CHAID typically results in larger but shorter trees because of its multi-split structure. Although CHAID may require more data than CART to train a model, it is generally less prone to overfitting. Some researchers suggest that pruning is unnecessary for CHAID, but I recommend pruning for both tree types to prevent overly large trees. We will explore examples of this in later sections.

291. Overall, each decision tree algorithm has its strengths and is suitable for different types of models and data. It is hard to speculate in advance which type of tree works better because they all have strengths and weaknesses. The choice of algorithm depends on the characteristics of the data and the specific requirements of the data mining. My recommendation is to build multiple trees using both types with different configurations and compare them to determine which tree works best for the data.
