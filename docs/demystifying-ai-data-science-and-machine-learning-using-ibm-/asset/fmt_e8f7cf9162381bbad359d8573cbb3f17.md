
# Reactive, Proactive, and Predictive Strategies in Aviation Safety

In order to use data mining effectively in businesses, it is essential to understand the primary purpose of data mining in predictive projects. I want to show you how the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) differentiates between reactive, proactive, and predictive strategies. While it is meant for aviation safety strategies, I find this classification represents very well different types of research projects and where predictive research fits in. The FAA's primary responsibility is to ensure safety in air transportation. In aviation safety, the FAA categorizes three types of safety strategies, which are fundamentally different: reactive, proactive, and predictive (Figure 1.5). Aviation organizations can follow those strategies to enhance their operational safety.

## Reactive Strategy

The first type is the **Reactive strategy**, which focuses on examining events that have already happened and identifying the root causes of a specific aviation incident or accident. Based on the identified root causes, aviation organizations can form appropriate strategies to mitigate the risk. This strategy requires experts to investigate the incident very thoroughly, examine the black box, interview involved parties, and examine the scene of the incident. Depending on the scope and severity of the incident/accident, the investigation may take a long time to complete. For example, an airline accident may take months or even years to complete an investigation and confirm the causes. This prolonged period is necessary to ensure that every aspect of the incident is carefully examined, and the root causes are accurately identified. Despite the time-intensive nature of this strategy, the outcomes of such investigations are invaluable. The detailed findings provide critical insights that can significantly enhance safety protocols and training programs within the aviation industry. By understanding what went wrong and why, aviation organizations can make informed decisions to prevent similar incidents in the future, thereby improving overall safety standards.

## Proactive Strategy

The second type is the **Proactive strategy**, which looks into the present. In other words, aviation organizations examine contributing factors to an incident/accident from various aspects of hazardous conditions and organizational processes and see how they are related to the incident or accident. This approach does not attempt to find the causality of incidents but rather investigates the correlations between various contributing factors and the occurrence of aviation incidents. By understanding these correlations, aviation organizations can implement preventive measures to enhance safety. Several common methods are employed in the Proactive strategy, including statistical analysis, scientific theories, aeronautical scientific models, and engineering mechanisms. These methods help in identifying patterns and correlations between different variables that could contribute to potential hazards. For instance, statistical analysis can reveal trends in data that indicate an increased risk of certain types of incidents under specific conditions. Using these insights, aviation organizations can develop strategies and action plans that target the identified contributing factors. This proactive approach allows for the implementation of measures that can mitigate risks before they escalate into serious incidents. Moreover, organizations can design applications and systems that continuously monitor various parameters to detect potential incidents in real time. With the integration of sensors and data collection technologies, real-time monitoring becomes a vital component of the Proactive strategy. These technologies enable the detection of anomalies and potential incidents as they occur, providing authorities with the necessary information to make timely decisions. Although it may not always be possible to prevent incidents from happening entirely, immediate detection allows for swift action to minimize injuries or damages.

## Predictive Strategy

The third type is **Predictive strategy**, which looks into the future. In other words, aviation organizations analyze a large amount of data to predict new potential safety problems in the airspace system in the future. By mining big data, they can develop a predictive model that predicts an aviation incident or accident before it occurs. While the Reactive and Proactive strategies focus on understanding and addressing past and present incidents, respectively, the Predictive strategy aims to prevent incidents by forecasting them. This forward-looking approach does not attempt to explain how an incident will happen beforehand; rather, it concentrates on predicting the occurrence based on data patterns. The detailed explanation of how an incident might unfold can be analyzed later, but the primary goal is to predict what, where, and when an incident might occur, along with its probability.

To achieve this, aviation organizations employ data mining techniques to uncover previously unknown patterns and correlations within large datasets. These patterns enable the construction of predictive models that can provide early warnings of potential safety issues. For example, by analyzing trends in flight data, maintenance records, and environmental factors, a predictive model can identify conditions that are likely to lead to an incident. This strategy marks a significant advancement in aviation safety, as it allows for the prevention of incidents rather than merely mitigating their effects. By predicting incidents before they happen, aviation organizations can implement measures to avoid them entirely, thus ensuring a higher level of safety. However, the success of the Predictive strategy relies heavily on the availability of a large amount of high-quality data. Accurate predictions require comprehensive and detailed datasets, which can be obtained from various sources such as flight operations, maintenance logs, weather reports, and air traffic control data. The concept of predictive modeling in aviation can be likened to scenarios depicted in the TV show *Person of Interest*, where data mining is used to predict and prevent crimes before they occur. Similarly, in aviation, data mining and predictive modeling serve as crucial tools for identifying and preventing potential incidents, thereby enhancing the overall safety of the airspace system.
