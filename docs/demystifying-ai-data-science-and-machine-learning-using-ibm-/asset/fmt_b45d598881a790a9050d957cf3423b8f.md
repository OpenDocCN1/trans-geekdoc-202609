## Introduction

![](images/ufig8_1.jpg)

This chapter focuses on the decision tree method, a popular machine learning technique that presents the hierarchical structure of variables in tree form using logical rules. The tree consists of nodes that split into two or more branches based on conditions that best separate individuals into classes. It is one of the most widely used machine learning methods due to its ease of implementation and interpretation. The tree structure visually demonstrates relationships among variables through nodes and branches, making it intuitive to understand. Additionally, decision trees are capable of detecting `nonlinear relationships`.

In this chapter, we will explore the advantages and disadvantages of decision trees, enabling us to compare them with other machine learning methods. The four common types of decision trees—CART (Classification and Regression Tree), CHAID (Chi-squared Automatic Interaction Detector), C5.0, and QUEST (Quick, Unbiased, Efficient, Statistical Tree)—will be discussed. We will examine their characteristics and differences, and I will demonstrate how to build these trees using SPSS Modeler, including the tree pruning process.

## What Is a Decision Tree? How Does it Work?

![](images/ufig8_2.jpg)

A `decision tree` is a highly popular machine learning method, frequently used in decision-making processes and data mining projects. It is a `non-parametric` and `nonlinear` method. Decision tree models offer the advantage of `conceptual simplicity`, making them easy to understand while also accommodating nonlinear associations between predictors and the target variable. The tree is represented as a `flowchart`, visually illustrating the relationships among variables, which makes it easy to comprehend and explain—even in complex processes. This is a significant advantage, as many other machine learning algorithms lack this level of interpretability.

Suppose we want to predict the likelihood of passengers purchasing an airline ticket for a trip, based on factors such as ticket price, family emergency, and hotel price. The flowchart captures the decision-making process or the rules that define the relationship between the passenger's decision (the target variable) and these factors (the predictors).
