# 7

# Random Processes

In this chapter, we turn our attention to random processes – collections of random variables $(X_t)_{t\in T}$, which may be dependent. In classical settings like Brownian motion, $t$ represents time, so $T \subset \mathbb{R}$. But in high-dimensional probability, $T$ can be any abstract set. A key example is the canonical Gaussian process

$$X_t = \langle g,t\rangle,\quad t\in T,$$

where $T \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ and $g \sim N(0,I_n)$. We introduce it in Section [7.1](7.1).

In Section [7.2](7.2), we explore powerful comparison inequalities for Gaussian processes – Slepian, Sudakov-Fernique, and Gordon – using a new trick: Gaussian interpolation. In Section [7.3](7.3), we use these tools to prove a sharp bound on the operator norm of an $m\times n$ Gaussian random matrices.

How does a Gaussian process $(X_t)_{t\in T}$ capture the geometry of $T$? In Section [7.4](7.4), we prove Sudakov inequality – a lower bound on the *Gaussian width*

$$w(T)=\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in T}\langle g,t\rangle$$

using covering numbers. (Upper bounds come later in Chapter [8](8).) Gaussian width is a key concept linking probability with metric geometry; we take a closer look at it in Section [7.5](7.5) and connect it to other ideas like effective dimension.

In Section [7.6](7.6), we compute the size of a random projection of any bounded set $T \subset \mathbb{R}^n$. The crucial quantity that determines it is the Gaussian width.

You will have plenty of chances to practice these ideas: prove symmetrization and contraction inequalities for random processes (Exercises [7.2–7.4](7.2–7.4), [7.8](7.8)), derive the important min-max inequality for Gaussian processes – Gordon inequality (Exercise [7.9](7.9)), get sharp bounds for Gaussian matrices (Exercises [7.11](7.11) and [7.13](7.13)), compute the Gaussian width for the $\ell^p$ balls (Exercise [7.17](7.17)), explore the nuclear norm (Exercises [7.18](7.18) and [7.19](7.19)), effective dimension (Exercises [7.21–7.22](7.21–7.22)), random projections of general sets (Exercises [7.25](7.25) and [7.26](7.26)), and matrix sketching (Exercise [7.27](7.27)).
