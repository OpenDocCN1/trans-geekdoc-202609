Bitcoin is a digital currency that first came out in 2009. Since it is a digital or virtual currency, it may be sent or exchanged through the internet. It is one of the first digital currencies to be decentralized, with its creation attributed to an individual known as Satoshi Nakamoto. Bitcoin today is considered to be the future of online currency, and it’s easy to see why.
What are the Advantages of Bitcoins?
As a type of online currency, Bitcoin offers a wide range of benefits. The following are some of its strongest advantages:
  • Fast, internet-based transaction . When you use bitcoins, you don’t have to make any bank transactions or employ a middleman for facilitation. Instead, you can perform any transaction through the Internet, making the process more efficient.
  • Reduced transaction fees . The nature of direct and straightforward transfers eliminates the need for expensive transaction payments, specifically as it is done over the Internet.
  • Global circulation . Bitcoins, unlike traditional currencies, are accepted across countries all over the world.
  • Growing market . Various businesses and brands, such as Microsoft and Paypal, accept bitcoins as currencies. The list of businesses that allow for purchases to be made through bitcoins is steadily growing.
  • Buyer ease . With their ease in making payments and the rising number of merchants that accept them as currency, bitcoins are considered as a favorable means of doing financial transactions online.
How Do Bitcoins Work?
Whether you have dollars, euros, or any other type of currency, you can exchange them for bitcoin. Digital wallets are necessary to store bitcoins properly, and these ‘wallets’ may be located on your own PC, mobile device, or via a third-party website. Once you have bitcoins, you can purchase anything in any currency without the lengthy transactions associated with banks and other financial websites.

THE QUESTION OF LEGITIMACY: IS BITCOIN LEGAL?
The questions on legality that surround Bitcoin are not surprising. Crucial to these questions is the fact that bitcoins are not subject to regulations. To those who are used to transacting only through fiat currency, bitcoin’s unregulated operations can be questionable.
Nonetheless, Bitcoin is far from being a counterfeit or illegal digital currency. First of all, it is not fake money. Businesses have been accepting bitcoins as payments just like they would accept any other existing currency. Secondly, the lack of regulations governing bitcoins does not necessarily point to its illegitimacy. Since bitcoins provide an entirely new way of making payments, no concrete rules have been made as of yet in order to regulate its use in a wider market.
Finally, bitcoins do not violate any laws; hence it is a mistake to refer to it as an illegal means of transacting financially. In fact, the IRS considers Bitcoin as properties similar to stocks and bonds. Thus, federal law mandates that the sale and purchase of bitcoins must be treated as the transactions of any other stock property.
In 2013, the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network of the U.S Treasury Department did not consider the purchase of well-natured products and services through bitcoin as an illegal activity. Mining bitcoins, however, may be subject to specific laws that regulate its operations and use. Bitcoin miners typically fall into the category of money transmitters. This means that miners must follow certain laws. Up to the present time, however, none of those laws have been employed as means to subvert the use of bitcoins.
Anonymity as a Feature of Criminal Activities
One of the events that led to speculations on bitcoins’ illegitimacy is Silk Road’s duplicitous activity. Silk Road used to be a digital marketplace for illegal goods, where bitcoins were considered as the only acceptable payment. The primary reason for using bitcoins is its anonymity since buyer identities will remain undisclosed during and after the sale. This makes it difficult for authorities to track down the persons involved in such illicit transactions.
Thus, authorities in the United States and other countries are concerned with the possibility that bitcoins will be used to facilitate more illegal transactions, such as money laundering and the exchange of illegal goods. In 2012, the FBI published a document that tackled the issues surrounding bitcoins and its distinction against other digital currencies, such as eGold and WebMoney. One major cause of concern raised by the FBI was the possible lack of regulations in offshore services and overseas exchanges. Tracking illicit activities beyond U.S. borders may be more taxing, if not near impossible.
While the FBI has already successfully shut down Silk Road in October 2013, especially after US Senator Charles Schumer’s called bitcoin a “surrogate currency,” the connection between this notorious site and bitcoin as its sole payment mode has nevertheless lingered. This has engendered an erroneous label on bitcoin as a dubious digital currency. In another case, the US Drug Enforcement Administration seized bitcoins from a US resident in June 2013 after finding out that the individual has used them in acquiring a controlled substance.
While its features of anonymity may render bitcoin as an attractive type of virtual currency for illicit transactions, this does not qualify bitcoin as an illegal currency. Countless illicit transactions all over the globe are being made through cash, gold, and bitcoin payments.
Nonetheless, the lack of proper regulations regarding the use of bitcoins makes it a complex currency to use, especially during exchanges and mining activities.
Digital Currency Laws across Countries
The use of bitcoins is viewed differently across countries all over the world. In Iceland, for example, bitcoin is automatically classified as illegal. Many countries have released statements classifying digital currencies, including bitcoin, as non-regulated currencies. However, bitcoin is generally considered a legitimate currency for legal transactions. As long as bitcoin is not used for illegal means or the exchange of illicit goods and services, it does not translate to any law violation.
If you are using bitcoins or planning to invest in bitcoins, it is important to consider your location. You need to understand which regulations govern bitcoins in your country. The laws regarding digital currencies, including bitcoins, continue to shift and change. Bitcoins will continue to grow in popularity and use. Given these factors, it is understandable how bitcoins will continue to generate questions in the months or years to come.

BITCOIN REGULATING AGENCIES
Different countries regulate bitcoins through various agencies. In the US, various national and state agencies serve as bitcoin regulators. The following are agencies which have already initiated regulations or have expressed intentions to draft regulations in the future:
  • FinCEN
FinCEN or the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, an agency within the US Treasury Department has published guidelines on virtual currencies and their use. Published on March 18, 2013, these guidelines aim to define the circumstances which will categorize virtual currency users as money transmitting businesses (MTBs) or money services businesses. MTBs must strictly follow the mandate on Anti-Money Laundering (AML), which prohibits financial laundering practices and Know Your Client (KYC) measures, which requires businesses to identify the individuals or other businesses they are transacting with.
  • CFTC
CFTC or the Commodity Futures Trading Commission takes care of financial derivatives in the U.S. Although they have not made any regulations as of present time, it has already announced that it would do so when necessary.
  • SEC
The SEC or the Securities and Exchange Commission has not issued any concrete regulation on virtual currencies. However, it has published pertinent investor alerts through its Office of Investor Education and Advocacy. These alerts have specifically focused on dishonest investment schemes, such as Ponzi schemes and other schemes that use bitcoin payments. These alerts were made after the case of Trendon T. Shavers (also known as ‘pirateat40’), a Texas resident who was criminally charged after founding and operating the Bitcoin Savings and Trust. This fraudulent company aimed to raise 70,000 bitcoins by promising up to 7% in weekly interest to investors.
According to the SEC, virtual currencies are considered as securities and should abide by guidelines governing securities.
  • THE LEGISLATIVE BRANCH
It was in August 2013 that the US Senate sent a letter of inquiry regarding the risks of virtual currencies to law enforcement agencies across the country. One of these agencies was the Department of Homeland Security. The letter included a request for policies and guidelines pertaining to the treatment of virtual currencies. One major concern was the lack of paper trail that could be used by regulating agencies to examine digital currency transactions.
By November 2013, the agencies have sent in their reply to the Senate. The Department of Homeland Security expressed concerns about the possibility of criminal activities instigated by the anonymity inherent in bitcoin use. Nonetheless, the Department of Justice and the Federal Reserve have acknowledged the legitimate use and value of virtual currencies.
Due to the case instigated by SEC, the legislative branch of the U.S. government has determined Bitcoin’s legal classification. In the Shaver case of Texas, the accused attempted to declassify bitcoin as money. Judge Amos Mazzant, however, responded to Shaver’s claims by issuing a memorandum classifying Bitcoin as legitimate money or currency.
  • U.S. STATES
Bitcoin follows specific regulations and guidelines that tend to vary from one state to another. In California and New York, efforts have been made to relentlessly pursue Bitcoin-related organizations. In New Mexico, South Carolina, and Montana, however, there are no specific regulations that govern MTBs or money transmitting businesses.
A brief history of state government approaches on bitcoin use began in May 2013 through a letter from the state financial regulator of California to the non-profit Bitcoin promotional entity, Bitcoin Foundation. The letter warned the organization that it may be operating as a non-licensed money transmission business and could face possible fines and imprisonment. In August 2013, the Department of Financial Services in New York did the same, although the letters were addressed to 22 bitcoin companies by means of subpoenas. The communication, however, was more conciliatory than accusatory in nature, as it simply contained a request to develop the necessary regulatory guidelines for digital currencies. This resulted in a more positive approach to the virtual currency industry. Specifically, this led to the consent for digital currency exchanges starting 2014, after the announcement of state Superintendent of Financial Services Mr. Benjamin M. Lasky in New York.
In June 2015, the New York State Department of Financial Services put up BitLicense, the first virtual licensing agency to address concerns on bitcoin and other digital currencies in the United States. The regulations found in BitLicense are different to the financial regulations on technology put up by other states such as Texas and Vermont, and the prior legislation amendments done by California.
  • PRIVATE SECTOR COMPANIES (BANKS)
Some banks have suspended the accounts of depositors who were found to have operated at bitcoin exchanges. In some of these cases, the reason for closing the accounts was the failure of the owners to open a money transmitting account instead.
Nonetheless, the US Senate has already answered these questions on banking and federal regulations regarding bitcoin exchanges. While there is no certainty for legislation as a result of these Senate hearings, agencies have released positive feedbacks from these hearings, specifically on the legitimacy of bitcoin as a digital currency.

WHAT THIS MAY MEAN TO YOU
In a nutshell, the legality of bitcoins depends on where you are located, who you are, and what you are doing with the coins. As a bitcoin stakeholder, you may fall into one or more than one categories. You must understand that each category comes with its own legal considerations.
  • Users . Users refer to individuals who use bitcoins whether for spending or hoarding. Per FinCEN rules, it is legal to use bitcoins as long as you simply do it for the purchase of goods and services. As a user, you will not be regulated under the category of a money transmitter.
  • Miners . If you obtain bitcoins through mining, you may be considered as a money transmitter and, therefore, subject to money transmitting rules. A money transmitter, according to FinCEN, is an individual that reproduces units of virtual money and sells those units to another entity for traditional currency.
    If you are a miner, you will be declassified from simple bitcoin user to money transmitter. As a result, you will have to abide by rules on money transmitting businesses. Nonetheless, bitcoin miners continue to request for clarificatory information from FinCEN regarding their status. As of present time, this issue has not yet reached any court process.
  • Exchangers . FinCEN defines exchangers or those that exchange their currencies for bitcoins, as money transmitting businesses. The agency furthermore defines an exchanger and money transmitter as someone who accepts bitcoins from one entity and transmits this virtual currency to another for the same purpose as transmitting any currency or funding.

IS BITCOIN SAFE?
Clearly, Bitcoin is beyond mainstream. In fact, it may be reasonable to expect that bitcoins will never be considered mainstream for a long time. However, to ignore the value of bitcoins may be a hasty route to take.
As of January 2017, $14.9 billion worth of bitcoins have already been circulating. Blockchain, the technology supporting bitcoin, has steadily grown in popularity. The blockchain infrastructure may eventually challenge certain processes of traditional trading, banking, and other types of financial transactions.
Still, the question remains: how safe are bitcoins?
Bitcoin as Risky Currency
As cryptocurrency, bitcoins cannot be without some considerable risk. In a report made by the Global Markets Research Department of Credit Suisse, the value of cryptocurrency is three times more volatile than the value of oil and eleven times more unstable than the exchange rate between the dollar and the British pound.
During transfers, bitcoin amounts are irreversible. Thus, there should be no room for errors when typing amounts during purchases or transfers. Also, the password that users employ to access their bitcoin accounts is private and non-resettable. In cases where the user forgets the private key, or someone else hacks into it, the bitcoins become irretrievable.
The Immune Structure of Blockchain
Despite the risky consequences of potential hacking, the blockchain technology supporting the exchanges of bitcoins is considered to be immune to hacking possibilities.
Instead of recording individual accounts, what the architecture does is to compile all past transactions done through bitcoins. During an individual transfer, all computers that run on bitcoin software will process the public signature of the sender. This signature will be verified along with the encoded transactions in the blockchain to identify whether it was the actual owner of the account that has sent the bitcoins.
There are other computers involved in the blockchain that validate the process further. Once the transaction is done, it is collected into a record of all previous transactions. Miners then start competing on a mathematical puzzle meant to authenticate the blockchain. Before a miner can be declared a winner, other miners will have to verify first the validity of all transactions. The winning miner gets newly-minted bitcoins as a reward for an accurately solved blockchain puzzle. This is a good way to increase the supply of bitcoin currency and to ensure the accuracy of each bitcoin exchange or transaction.
Hacking Probabilities
Nonetheless, questions on the probability of hacking still surround the bitcoin industry. Is it possible for a hacker to manipulate the blockchain and transfer money to his or her own account? The answer is yes, but only if the hacker possesses superior computing capabilities. This is because a current bitcoin exchange transaction can be validated only by viewing all past transactions. This means a computer will have to solve not only a present blockchain but also all blocks related to it. The blockchain is so complex and intricate that Credit Suisse has compared it to a geological layer, with the recent blocks as the soft soil and the previous blocks as hard, sedimented rocks. The deeper the blockchain goes, the more complex it becomes. This makes the blockchain more difficult to hack and manipulate.
The 51 Percent Attack
There is also considerable risk that occurs when bitcoin miners concentrate bitcoin value shares among themselves. There is the possibility of risking new transactions or deleting previous transactions in the event that a single party is able to gain control of more than 51 percent of the entire network. Credit Suisse estimates that even a 30 percent control of the network will allow a malicious miner to interfere with recent transactions. Thus, a 51 percent control attack could potentially result in the collapse of all bitcoin value. An attack of this scale will affect not only those of the malicious miners themselves but also the assets that they are hacking. It is possible, thus, for any malicious attacker to be a miner himself, to begin with, someone extensively familiar with the process needed to ensure the legitimacy and accuracy of the entire ledger.
Cryptocurrency Cyber Theft
There is no question that the unique design of the blockchain technology has the ability to deter hacking attempts. However, online exchanges and digital wallets can fall victim to cyber attacks.
On Bitfilnex exchange in August 2016, hackers compromised 119, 756 bitcoins with a total value of $82 million. One of the biggest instances of cyber attacks took place in 2014 through the Mt. Gox bitcoin exchange, which eventually filed for bankruptcy after hackers were able to steal 850,000 bitcoins valued at $590 million. Recovery efforts were made, but the number of reclaimed coins amounted only to 24 percent of the total sum. After various reports revealed the unscrupulous business practices from within the company, Japanese prosecutors charged Mt. Gox CEO Mark Karpeles with embezzlement.
In conclusion, blockchain technology is a highly secure architecture for digital currency trading and exchanges. However, it is when a malicious entity attempts to manipulate the structure that the system --- just like any other --- runs the risk of a compromise.

WAYS TО KЕЕР BІTСОІNЅ SAFE
Given the increasing value and growing popularity of bitcoins, one of the biggest concerns for investors would be where and how to keep them safe.
Bitcoins, unlike tangible coins, cannot be stored in a physical location. As a digital currency, bitcoins are stored and accessed through bitcoin addresses. These addresses need digital keys or passwords for access.
To fully secure your bitcoins, you need to make sure that these digital keys are protected at all times.
There are two keys for every Bitcoin address, and these are the public key and the private key. The Bitcoin address, which is visible to the public, is derived from the public keys. This address is similar to any email address that you will share with contacts. The private key, on the other hand, functions like your email password. You use it to access your digital wallet so you can send bitcoins for purchases or exchanges. To protect your bitcoins in the digital wallet, you must keep the private keys secure at all times.
When it comes to protecting your private keys, using a private password is just one step. You can also opt to keep your bitcoin access offline, such as in another database, in order to protect your bitcoins from Internet threats such as hackers.
You may also utilize multisignature addresses. These addresses allow several parties to hold a part of the address as a key. In order to access the bitcoins, you will have to get the approval of the other users of the address. In these multisignature addresses, it is possible to choose how many user signatures are needed. Also, it is possible to choose a variety of devices for multiple verifications, making the account more secure from hackers.
When choosing the best way to secure your bitcoins, it is best to evaluate each option according to their advantages and disadvantages. Accessibility is important, but you also have to make sure that your bitcoins are not accessible to malicious individuals and bad actors both online and offline

A CLOSER LOOK AT BITCOIN MINING
As digital currency, bitcoin money is not printed. Unlike traditional fiat money systems that allow governments around the world to print new money, bitcoin is discovered and mined. All over the globe, computers mine bitcoins by way of competition.

    When people send bitcoins over the network, these transactions are recorded into a ledger. This is done to keep track of payments, including the senders, receivers, and the kinds of transaction that took place. The transactions are collated into a list, also known as a block. When a transaction takes place, the miners verify and confirm the transactions and record them into a public ledger. This long list of blocks is called a blockchain.
    The blockchain is used to identify, evaluate, and confirm any transaction done between bitcoin addresses. A new list is added to the blockchain after a single transaction. However, the ledger is available only digitally. This raises the question of security and accuracy. To ensure that the blockchain is continually updated and verified, the miners involve themselves and subject each update into a process. With information derived from the block, the miners apply a mathematical formula to arrive at a hash. The hash is composed of randomly sequenced letters and numbers, which is compiled along with the block. The hash can be found at the end of a blockchain within a specific period.
  • What are other features of the hash?
    A hash is a unique combination of letters and numbers that play a role in keeping a blockchain intact. Although data from a block can be used to generate a hash, it will be impossible to identify the data used from which block just by simply looking at a hash. Each hash is unique so that altering any character in the bitcoin block will also alter the resulting hash.
    Furthermore, miners do not generate a hash simply by sifting through transactions in the blockchain. The hash of the last block stored in the blockchain is also needed in order to create a new hash. This makes the blockchain more secure and easier to identify if and when tampering occurs. A fake transaction may involve altering a stored block, which will then result in a change in the block’s hash. Once a hashing function is run, and the hash is found to be dissimilar to the one stored in the blockchain, the transaction will be classified as fake.
  • How do miners compete for coins?
    Using software specifically designed for mining blocks, miners compete among themselves to seal a block and create a correct hash. Once a miner is able to do this, he or she gets rewarded with 25 bitcoins. This has provided a solid incentive for mining to continually take place as well as for the blockchain to be constantly updated and secured.
    Nonetheless, computers may find it too easy to generate a hash from available block data. It is important for the bitcoin network to make the process more complex in order to prevent the generating of hashes in just a matter of seconds. To establish a sort of protocol, Bitcoin requires proof of work. The hash has to appear in a certain way, such as having the required number of zeroes. To create a different hash, miners have to use a nonce. The nonce is altered every time a hash fails to follow a specific format, and the hashing process recommences. It is important to find a nonce that generates a hash in the required format. Miners earn bitcoins once this correct hash is created.

INITIAL COIN OFFERINGS
Also known as Initial Coin Offering, ICO is a fund-raising venture meant to help cryptocurrency startups obtain capital. It is largely unregulated, which means that this process is typically done to evade the tough and heavily-regulated process of raising capital through banks and venture capitalists.
An ICO works through the same principle of an IPO (Initial Price Offering), which you will have to look into prior to buying stocks. In an ICO, you purchase cryptocurrency instead. Usually, you purchase these cryptocurrencies from a startup, which makes the promise that you will own some its assets relative to the investment value you have made into the company. In an ICO, you will own assets in the form of digital coins. In an IPO, buying stock can provide you with voting rights or even equity. When you purchase coins in an ICO, you do not have any equity rights. The value of the coins is heavily influenced by the value of the digital currency company issuing them.
In an ICO procedure, its developers will first declare its plans for the project or currency startup, and after which they will create a white paper to explain important details about the offering. They will also procure the support of advisors in the industry and decide on the tokens for offering. The ICO will then be advertised and held to interested entities.
Getting the Most Out of ICOs
Although many ICOs are legitimate, not all of them are worthy of your time or money. ICOs are unregulated, and this could open the process to scammers who do not intend to return any of your investment. Thus, it is important to exercise caution when planning to invest in an Initial Coin Offering.
An important guideline is to see whether the published white paper has clearly established the details of the offering. The agenda should be crisp and clear. It is also important for the identities of the developers to be readily available and accessible. One red flag is for the developers to hide under aliases or not have their location and contact details visible to interested parties.
Investing in ICOs comes with solid advantages such as being able to support worthwhile cryptocurrency projects. Also, ICOs do not require strict and rigorous paperwork, making it a relatively easy investment scheme compared to IPOs. Nonetheless, its unregulated environment can make it an attractive playground for scammers. There is also the worry that the investment is being made only through pure speculation. Network congestion could also result in failed transactions.
Nonetheless, ICOs continue to open new opportunities for innovative projects and valuable cryptocurrency technologies. Also, it provides incentives to those who have the skill and the resources to foster pioneering solutions to new and expected problems in cryptocurrency transactions. The important step in any investment, whether in an IPO or ICO, is to be cautious and to study each investment scheme as much as possible. This helps to track down ICO scams and to channel funds into projects that provide real and long-term digital currency benefits.
