**Abstract:** Source detection is a research field of growing interest due to environmental concerns about air quality in populated areas. We developed a data-driven approach based on a multilayer perceptron neural network and inspired by previous works on road traffic estimation. We then compared it to a classical variational method (3D-Var) on a one-dimensional and stationary model of pollution concentration. We tested source reconstruction with datasets of synthetic measurements generated by 12 and 50 sensors with and without noise. Both methods appear to be reasonably robust to measurement noise and the neural network performs slightly better compared to the classical 3D-Var method.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Today public policies are more and more interested in topics related to environmental risk assessment and air quality monitoring, where the detection of pollutant sources plays a key role. Source reconstruction has been widely used in the domain of civilian and military nuclear. For example, the Comprehensive Test Ban Treaty adopted in 1996 by the United Nations has encouraged researchers to develop methods of source detection using a global network of radionuclides sensors measurements to monitor compliance with this treaty. In this context, Pudyakiewicz (1998) has applied adjoint method to retrieve source parameters such as location, time and amount of release of radionuclides. Other works dealt with source rate estimation of the damaged Fukushima power plant during its accident in 2011. Winiarkek et al. (2014) used a merged Bayesian and variational estimation method to estimate with known uncertainty this source rate. On the other hand, there is a growing interest for the estimation of pollution sources in urban areas (e.g. Mons et al. (2017), Kumar et al. (2015)). In the context of a continuous point-wise source of pollution in urban environment, Kumar et al. (2015) used an adjoint method combined with a CFD (Computational Fluid Dynamics) model to retrieve source rate and source position in a stationary context. Ensemble methods are also cited in the literature. Mons et al. (2017) used an ensemble-based variational approach (EnVar) with an accurate but very computationally-expensive CFD model to estimate the diameter and position of a puddle of a volatile pollutant in a stationary and urban context.

To the best of our knowledge, machine learning has not been widely used for source detection so far. Recently,

a machine learning approach has been applied to solve an inverse problem dealing with road traffic estimation (Georges (2020)). This approach has inspired the present work, where a feed forward Neural Network (NN) is used with a few hidden layers. This data-driven method avoids any use of calculus of variations such as in variational methods and so, avoids to call a dispersion model inside an optimisation framework. Moreover, NNs are not computationally expensive once the training is performed, compared to variational methods, especially those based on complex CFD models. NN training is needed performed off-line, i.e. before the exploitation phase. This paper proposes a method to reconstruct a source of pollution in urban area in the case of a one-dimensional domain and stationary conditions to consider firstly pollutant dispersion in isolated streets. The machine learning approach is compared with a classical model-based variational approach (3D-Var) which is usually considered as a reference in terms of estimation accuracy.

The paper is now organized as follows: Section 2 presents the pollutant transport model and its finite-dimensional approximation. It also justifies the choices of wind velocity and diffusion coefficient used in the model. Section 3 details the proposed machine learning method and briefly presents the variational approach. Numerical simulation results are given in Section 4 whilst concluding remarks are discussed in Section 5.
