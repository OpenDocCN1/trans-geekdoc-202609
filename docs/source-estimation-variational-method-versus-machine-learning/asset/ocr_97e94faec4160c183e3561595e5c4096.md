# 4. CASE STUDY : SOURCE IDENTIFICATION IN A STREET

The here-proposed case study refers the detection of a polluting vehicle stopped somewhere on a boulevard with its motor turned on. It is assumed that stationary conditions of the pollution distribution are obtained under constant wind velocities. We could also consider a situation where another point-wise source such as a gas leak emit in a stationary regime.

# 4.1 Parameters

The training database is built by building the set of parameters,

{p_k = [A_{s,k}, x_{s,k}, σ_{disp k}^2, c_k] ; 1 ≤ k ≤ n_train} , (13)

with [A_s, x_s, σ_disp^2, c_k] ∈ [10^{-2},4] × [0.1,0.9] × [50,2000] × [-10^{-3},10^{-3}] generated by a Sobol sequence beginning from its 10001^{st} term, with units [g km^{-3} s^{-1}, km, km^{-2}, km s^{-1}] respectively. The validation set is created in the same manner with n_test samples, choosing [A_s, x_s, σ_disp^2, c] ∈ [5 × 10^{-2},3] × [0.1,0.9] × [70,1500] × [-10^{-3},10^{-3}], the sequence starts at its 101^{st} term. Source position is purposely generated so that x_s ∈ [0.1 km,0.9 km] to avoid to generate sources which would be located too close to the domain boundaries, that could lead to a non-physical solution of our dispersion model given by (1). Our database contains n_train = 15000 training samples, whilst validation is applied on n_test = 200 samples. As a metric of performance, the Relative Root-Square Error (RRSE) is chosen, defined by,

RRSE = ||q̂ - q_T||_2 / ||q_T||_2 , (14)

where (q_T, q̂) ∈ ℝ^{N_x}^2 are respectively the real source profile and its estimate. To facilitate the convergence of the variational method, the initial guess is given by

θ_k^b = [0, arg max_x(u_k^m), 0] , 1 ≤ k ≤ n_test; (15)

meaning that the initial guessed source position is located at the largest measured concentration. The initial guess is used both in the cost function and as initialization of the L-BFGS-B descent method.

The best neural network architecture has been obtained after some trials. Hence the chosen NN contains 2 fully-connected hidden layers of 50 neurons each, with the tanh activation function. The optimization is carried out using the L-BFGS-B algorithm. L_2 regularization is set at a low value of 10^{-12}. The machine learning approach is built using keras and tensorflow libraries under Python.

# 4.2 Comparison without noise

Experiment with 12 sensors: Simulations are performed without noise to determine how NN performs compared to the 3D-Var. Several very aberrant estimates returned by the NN (certainly due to non physical conditions in the training process) and bad estimates from 3D-Var (due to non convergence of the BFGS algorithm) are excluded: 16 out of 200, leading to the study of 184 estimates.

Fig. 2. Example of source reconstruction : left, with a neural network; right, with a variational method.

Based on this experiment, the neural network is able to estimate a source with an RRSE in the same order of magnitude than with the standard 3D-Var, with RRSE mean and standard deviation of μ_RRSE^NN = 0.22 and σ_RRSE^NN = 0.12 and μ_RRSE^var = 0.31 and σ_RRSE^var = 0.21 for NN and 3D-Var respectively. Overall the Neural Network performs slightly better. This is probably due to the fact that the variational approach is a non convex optimization problem. Therefore, some local minima may lead to near optimal solutions with significant differences in the parameter values.

The RRSE obtained with the machine learning and variational approaches follow a log-normal law confirmed by the use of a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test (p-value = 0.39 > 0.05 and 0.44 > 0.05 respectively). The distributions of RRSE are shown in Fig. 3.

The machine learning approach method is data-driven and thus does not contain any a priori information on the physical model whereas the variational approach relies on the knowledge of the physics and on a good guess as given by (15). We notice that the RRSE metric is sensitive to a slight error of estimated position. For example in the case of the 65^{th} sample of validation illustrated in Fig. 2, the neural network estimates well the magnitude and the dispersion, but a slight error of position leads to an RRSE^NN = 0.16 for the neural network against RRSE^var = 0.04 for the variational approach respectively.

Overall, without noise and using 12 sensors in total (10 regularly spaced in the domain and 2 that measure the boundary conditions), the machine learning approach performs slightly better than the variational approach with respect to the RRSE metric. The NN sometimes returns non-physical estimates such as negative sources or non-Gaussian sources. Fig. 4 shows that the performance of the two methods are similar.

Experiment with 50 sensors: Simulations with 50 sensors are carried out to observe a sensitivity of the estimations towards measurement density. Several non-physical estimates returned by the neural network or by the variational approach are excluded of the network: 9 out of 200, leading to the study of 191 estimates. Both methods return similar results as previously, with mean and standard deviation of μ_RRSE^NN = 0.13 and σ_RRSE^NN = 0.08 compared to μ_RRSE^var = 0.24 and σ_RRSE^var = 0.15 for NN and 3D-Var respectively.
