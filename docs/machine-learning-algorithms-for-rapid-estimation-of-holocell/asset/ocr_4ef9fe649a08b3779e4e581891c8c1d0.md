# ABSTRACT

In this study, regularization algorithms (RR, LR, and ENR), classical ML algorithms (SVR, DT, and RF), and advanced GBM algorithms (LightGBM, CatBoost, and XGBoost) were applied to build the holocellulose content predictive models of poplar based on features extracted from Raman spectra. Evaluation results of models indicate that classical ML algorithms show higher predictive accuracy than regularization algorithms, and the advanced GBM algorithms better than the classical ML algorithms. Furthermore, models built by CatBoost and XGBoost can estimate holocellulose content with high predictive accuracy of test R² above 0.93 and test RMSE less than 0.29%. It provides the heretofore best precision of holocellulose content predictive model based on Raman spectroscopy so far for our knowledge. Therefore, it is suggested that Raman spectroscopy coupled with ML algorithms is a promising tool for predicting the holocellulose content in poplar which can be applied in large-scale tree genetic and breeding programs.

# 1. Introduction

Holocellulose, the sum of cellulose and hemicellulose, is a water-insoluble carbohydrate fraction which can be extracted from wood materials through the chlorination method by removing the lignin (Basu, 2018). It is an important fraction because its hydrolyzed products were composed by various monomeric sugars, which are able to convert into useful compounds or to ethanol via fermentation (Isikgor & Becer, 2015; Ma et al., 2012). Due to the large differences in holocellulose content among different types of lignocellulosic biomass, species, or even genetic resources, the selection of resources with expected holocellulose content becomes an essential task. Meanwhile, the variation also provides a good opportunity to scheme a large-scale genetic breeding program for selecting and finding the suitable germplasm with the expected holocellulose content. However, for doing so, intensive labor and tedious testing are required when the traditional wet chemical methods (WCM) are used since there are plenty of samples that need to be measured. Besides that, because each genetic sample is prepared from small seedling or growth cone of living tree, the spendable quantity of the sample sometimes fails to meet the required replica when using

WCMs. Moreover, the WCMs are also criticized for the huge usage of toxic reagants during the determination. Therefore, a rapid, accurate, eco-friendly, and low sample quantities requirement method is needed.

Several spectroscopic methods, such as near-infrared (NIR) spectroscopy, middle-infrared (MIR) spectroscopy (Tucker et al., 2001) and Raman spectroscopy, combined with suitable algorithms have been successfully predicted the content of chemical components of lignocellulosic materials instead of traditional WCMs. NIR has been used to predict the lignin content in wood (Liang et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2019), lignin content in pears (Wu et al., 2021) and holocellulose content in wood (Liang et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2019). However, it is reported that both the position and intensity of water-related peaks of NIR spectra will be changed by the sample moisture and testing ambient humidity (Gordobil et al., 2021). So, it possibly results a disturbance on the quantitative results if the sample moisture and testing atmosphere is out of rigid control. Conversely, Raman spectroscopy, which is insensitive to the sample moisture, provides a great deal of spectral information with high chemical information content, which is characterized by a series of peaks related to specific chemical bonds (Jermyn et al., 2016).
