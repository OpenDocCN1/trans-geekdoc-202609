the selection result of optimal algorithm among advanced GBM algorithms was inconsistent in predicting holocellulose content and lignin content, due to the diverse characteristics of data for modeling. Therefore, it is suggested that the differences between data matrix cannot be ignored when selection of optimal algorithms in the group of GBM algorithms is executed.

Thus, it shows that more robust and precise models can be developed by Raman features combined with advanced GBM algorithms, which can be used to predict the holocellulose content of poplar wood. However, other lignocellulosic resources, including other angiosperm and gymnosperms, such as bamboos, rattans, and agricultural residuals with different chemical structure of holocellulose and different holocellulose content, are likely to be encountered, which have not been studied yet. But the general methodology was depicted in this study and the model need to be adjusted and modified when faced with different types of materials to adapt the different lignocellulosic resources, respectively.

# 4. Conclusion

All nine models built by regularization algorithms, classical ML algorithms, and advanced GBM algorithms have successfully predicted the holocellulose content. The advanced GBM algorithms outperformed all the models both in training and test process. In particular, the predictive accuracy of the CatBoost and XGBoost models was 0.93 for test R<sup>2</sup> and around 0.28% for test RMSE, which hold the first priority among all the nine algorithms. Therefore, it is evident that FT-Raman spectroscopy combined with ML algorithms is a promising way for predicting holocellulose content accurately and environmentally friendly without complex and tedious chemical experiments.
