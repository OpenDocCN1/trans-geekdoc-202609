But the popular increase in efficiency was accompanied by reducing the number of features and randomly dropping some instances with small gradients, which may marginally decline the prediction accuracy when the LightGBM model was developed (Ke et al., 2017). Because the number of datasets in this study is not very large compared with those were commonly used in ML tasks, the modeling efficiency is less important than prediction accuracy. Therefore, in this study, the algorithm of XGBoost, which obtained the higher prediction accuracy, holds the first priority due to the aforementioned advantages. Moreover, the predictive accuracy of HCP-Model built by CatBoost was close to that of XGBoost, while it is marginally lower than that of XGBoost in lignin content predictive model. It is possibly due to the different mathematical characteristics of the extracted features from the Raman spectra. Because the position and

intensity of holocellulose-related peaks are quite different from that of lignin-related ones, the extracted features related to the holocellulose were found to have larger number of the denoted input variables than that related to lignin. CatBoost algorithm is characteristic of the introduction of ordered boosting, which is skilled in avoiding overfitting caused by target leakage and then it will enhance the predictive accuracy of model consequently (Prokhorenkova et al., 2018). So, higher predictive accuracy of quantitative models would be contributed by this advantageous characteristic of CatBoost. Hence, it is concluded that XGBoost and CatBoost were superior to all other models in estimating holocellulose content based on Raman features. The selection of optimal algorithm in the groups of classical ML algorithms for building the corresponding predictive model didn't affected by the difference between two datasets, namely extracted Raman features related to lignin or related to holocellulose.
