# 3.1. Analysis of FT-Raman spectra

Raman spectra provides a great deal of spectral information with high chemical information content, which is characterized by a series of peaks related to specific chemical bonds (Jermyn et al., 2016). As shown in Fig. 1, both the signals at 1095 and 1122 cm–1 were attributed to the C—O and C—O stretching, symmetric and asymmetric stretching of COC, glycosidic, and ring breathing (Schenzel & Fischer, 2001; Wiley & Atalla, 1987). The peak located at 1149 cm–1 appeared to be related to HCC and HCO bending (Schenzel & Fischer, 2001; Wiley & Atalla, 1987), and 1462 cm–1 was assigned to the CH2 scissoring of carbohydrates (Agarwal & Atalla, 2010). At the signal of 1331 cm–1 and 1378 cm–1, HCC and HCO bending coordinates were most significant (Wiley & Atalla, 1987). The C—H stretching region from 2780 cm–1 to 3060 cm–1 includes contributions from lignin and carbohydrates (Wiley & Atalla, 1987). In detail, the peak at 2895 cm–1 was assigned to CH and CH2 stretching of the cellulose dominates (Wiley & Atalla, 1987), and the peak at 2945 cm–1 from the CH stretching of the methoxyl groups of the lignin was more pronounced (Gierlinger & Schwanninger, 2006; Wiley & Atalla, 1987), which have been proved to be a qualified internal standard in the task of predicting holocellulose content based on Raman spectroscopy (Gao, Shu, et al., 2022). After the spectral processes of smoothing, baseline correction, and deconvolution calculation, the input variables of HCP-Models were obtained, which were expressed as I1095, I1122, I1149, I1331, I1378, I1462, and I2895.

The predictive accuracy of all the quantitative models, namely, RR model, LR model, ENR model, SVR model, DT model, RF model, LightGBM model, CatBoost model, and XGBoost model, were estimated by the coefficient of determination (R2), root mean square error (RMSE), normalized root mean square error (NRMSE), mean absolute error (MAE), and mean absolute percentage error (MAPE). R2 is an index of goodness of fit, indicating the proportion of variance of output variable explained by the input variables. Theoretically, the larger the R2, the higher the model accuracy (Hosseinzadeh et al., 2022; Schubert & Kläusler, 2020). The RMSE is frequently used metric of errors between measured and predicted values, which serves to aggregate the magnitudes of the errors in predictions for various data points into a single measure of predictive power. The RMSE is a measure of accuracy, to compare forecasting errors of different models developed by RR, LR, ENR, SVR, DT, RF, LightGBM, CatBoost, and XGBoost. The NRMSE refers to the root mean square of RMSE normalized by the average of measured values. The lower the value of RMSE and NRMSE, the higher the predictive accuracy of quantitative model. The MAE is the average difference between the observations (measured values) and model output (predictions), which is a metric to measure the accuracy of a given model. MAPE is a simple average of absolute percentage errors, which used to define the accuracy of predictive model. The lower the value of MAE, the better a model is able to forecast values.
