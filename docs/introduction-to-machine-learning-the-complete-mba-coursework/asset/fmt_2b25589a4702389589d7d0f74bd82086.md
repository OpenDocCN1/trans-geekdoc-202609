## 1.2.2. Unsupervised Learning
This type of learning algorithm makes use of unlabelled data for training the models deployed for intelligent behavior. When the data at hand is unlabelled, the algorithm tries to find out similar patterns and places data with relevant features into one group. Unsupervised learning is a type of learning where no teacher is present, and the system learns without any supervision. Here the system learns based on experience. Clustering is an example of unsupervised learning. Examples of unsupervised learning algorithms include customer market segmentation, clustering, image restoration, drug discovery, etc.

## 1.2.3. Reinforcement Learning
Reinforcement learning was introduced when the researchers discovered that it would be nice to provide a reward as the system does the job in an intended way. In simple words, here, the system learns from the mistake. Reinforcement learning, often known as real AI, is utilized when we are not given enough training data. Lack of training data means the model has no expertise in the problem at hand and therefore learns gradually by experience. It tries random moves, in the beginning, to learn from its environment and then finds out what is the best strategy to solve the problem. There is an iterative feedback loop between the agent and its environment that helps it learn more interactively. Unlike the traditional machine learning algorithms, reinforcement learning algorithms like `SARSA` (State-Action-Reward-State-Action), Monte Carlo methods, and `Q-learning` are more dynamic in their behavior. The reinforcement learning algorithm works on the principle of learning patterns by reinforcing reward and punishment. The model tries to make the most suitable action in a particular situation that would maximize the cumulative reward and minimize punishment. If the step taken is right, it is awarded; otherwise, it is penalized. Reinforcement learning differs from the supervised learning approach where the training data already has an answer key so that the model is trained with the correct answer; in reinforcement learning, there is no answer/label, and the model decides to perform the given task in the light of its past experience. Thus in reinforcement learning, decision making is a sequential process, where we give labels to a sequence of decisions made earlier. One of the best examples of reinforcement learning is playing chess, where each of our moves on the board is rated as positive or negative given the history of previous moves. Some other typical reinforcement learning applications are seen in robotics for industrial automation, control theory, and game theory. In all these applications, there is a well-defined environment, actions, and rewards. A great example of game theory is the `AlphaGo` Reinforcement Learning model that was able to beat the world's best human player.

## 1.2.5. Deep Reinforcement Learning
Deep reinforcement learning incorporates artificial neural networks with the reinforcement learning architecture that sanctions software-defined agents to learn the best actions possible in a virtual environment to attain their goals, i.e., it unites function approximation and target optimization, mapping state-action pairs to expected rewards. Reinforcement algorithms that incorporate deep neural networks can beat human experts playing numerous Atari video games, Starcraft II and Dota-2, and the world champions of Go. While that may sound trivial to non-gamers, it is a vast improvement over reinforcement learning's previous accomplishments, and state of the art is progressing rapidly. One of the most intriguing areas of artificial intelligence is the concept of deep reinforcement learning, where machines can teach themselves based upon the results of their actions. It is one of the artificial intelligence areas that shows great promise, so let us look at what it is and explore real-world applications. Deep reinforcement learning is a category of artificial intelligence and machine learning where intelligent machines can learn from the results of their actions, similar to the way humans learn from experience. Inherent in this type of machine learning is that an agent is rewarded or penalized based on their actions. Actions that get them to the target outcome are rewarded (reinforced). A machine keeps learning through a series of trial and error, making this technology fitted for dynamic environments that keep evolving. Although reinforcement learning has been around for decades, it was recently combined with deep learning, which established phenomenal results. Here, the "deep" portion of reinforcement learning refers to multiple (deep) layers of artificial neural networks that replicate a human brain structure. Deep learning needs large amounts of training data and significant computing power. Now, over the last few years, the volumes of data have exploded while computing power costs have dramatically reduced, which has enabled the explosion of deep learning applications.
