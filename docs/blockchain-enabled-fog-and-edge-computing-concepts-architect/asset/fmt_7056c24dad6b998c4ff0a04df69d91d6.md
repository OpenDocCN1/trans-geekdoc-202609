
### 2.5.3 Formal definition of a Smart city

[Table 2.3](#12_Chapter02.xhtml#table2_3) enumerates several key Smart city IoT-based applications and correlates these applications to technical requirements and 5G support, as well as correlating the applications to broad security requirements and possible BCM applicability. [Figure 2.4](#12_Chapter02.xhtml#fig2_4) depicts graphically some key Smart city IoT-supported applications – note, however, that multiple distinct applications can coexist simultaneously in each edge network.

Typical Smart city services include, but are not limited to:
- Infrastructure and real estate management
- Physical security
- Power and other city-supporting utilities
- Electric and other utility manhole monitoring
- Traffic, transportation, and mobility
- Pollution monitoring
- Environmental monitoring
- Flood abatement
- Livability and smart services
- Logistics
- Smart city lighting

In the context of [Table 2.3](#12_Chapter02.xhtml#table2_3), the following are guidelines for bandwidth:
- **Low bandwidth**: 200 kbps or less
- **Medium bandwidth**: 200 kbps to 2 Mbps
- **High bandwidth**: more than 2 Mbps

The following are guidelines for latency:
- **Low latency**: real-time
- **Medium latency**: 1–5 seconds

The following are guidelines for reliability:
- **High reliability**: 99.999% availability
- **Medium reliability**: 99.99% availability

The following are guidelines for security:
- **High security**: impervious to infractions by state-sponsored hacking activities
- **Medium security**: impervious to infractions by professional 'black hat' hackers

The following are guidelines for BCM-mechanisms:
- **High usability**: a strong technical and financial advantage to the use of such technology
- **Medium usability**: a reasonable technical and financial advantage to the use of such technology
