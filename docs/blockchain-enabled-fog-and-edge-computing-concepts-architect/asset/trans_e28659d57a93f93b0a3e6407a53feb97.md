

## Literature Review

Over the past few years, due to the boom of the Bitcoin cryptocurrency, industry and researchers have begun to intensively investigate decentralized distributed ledger technology (i.e., blockchain). Researchers began to understand the paradigm of distributed transactions, which led to the formation of modern blockchains. Modern blockchain networks allow users to effectively mine and store their transaction records in a distributed manner on a decentralized ledger topology through consensus algorithms. The main reason for integrating blockchain lies in its security features, which enhance users' trust in the network; therefore, researchers are actively integrating blockchain into everyday life scenarios. In this section, we provide a detailed overview of the existing literature on blockchain, edge computing, and smart grid integration and their privacy possibilities from three different perspectives (see [Figure 9.1](#20_Chapter09.xhtml#fig9_1) and [Table 9.3](#20_Chapter09.xhtml#table9_3)).

### 9.3.1 Blockchain Works Considering Edge Computing and Its Privacy

Blockchain as a network provides security and immutability; however, common blockchain users (such as mobile phones, smart meters, and medical IoT devices) cannot perform heavy computation and mining functions. Therefore, there is always a need to introduce a new paradigm to enhance the computational capability of the network. To this end, researchers have integrated edge computing into this decentralized scenario, developing a framework that simultaneously possesses a secure blockchain and compute-rich edge computing capabilities.

Despite this integration, blockchain-based edge networks are not completely private and there exist significant privacy risks. Therefore, it is also crucial to take necessary measures to protect the privacy of such systems. In this subsection, we will discuss three major areas of work that have been carried out from the perspective of blockchain, edge computing, and their privacy issues.
