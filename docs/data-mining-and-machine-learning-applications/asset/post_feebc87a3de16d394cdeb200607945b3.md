## 2.5 Discussion

Learning individual models for each tenant could reveal the fundamental motivating factors at the individual level. Nevertheless, it may be common for different individuals to have different preferences and not to behave similarly. Subsequently, when extended to a community level, a clustering investigation might group inhabitants into a few patterns of trigger behavior. The most informative list of features, with their coefficients, is omitted from the output of the trained regression model. All the key-driven factors fall into two categories: time-related components including month, weekday/weekend, hour-of-day data, and condition-related variables including indoor temperature, relative humidity, indoor CO₂ concentration, and outdoor weather information. ## 2.6 Conclusion

In this chapter, we considered key aspects of data mining such as mining data streams, mining graph and network data, mining heterogeneous/multi-source data, mining high-dimensional data, mining imbalanced data, mining multimedia data, mining scientific data, mining sequential data, mining social networks, mining spatial and temporal data, and a data mining technique is proposed to examine occupant behavior in adjusting ventilation flow in a recently renovated community in the Netherlands. The goal is to uncover the hidden motivation driving tenants' behavior and to look for possible behavioral patterns among different individuals. An L1-regularized logistic regression classifier was developed and tuned to predict the occupant's possible response to a specific situation, during which it also numerically assesses the overall importance of each factor in the dynamic process.

In a wider context, the comparison between tenants showed three distinct inspiring patterns. Specifically, the environment-driven type, compared with tenants who are more sensitive to environmental variables, the time-driven type corresponds to occupants who have relatively set temporal tendencies, just like the occupants of the mixed type, whose behavior is far more unpredictable with no single tendency pattern that is clear across situational and temporal aspects.

The information-based approach to exploring tenants' behavior presented in this study provides additional opportunity to use BMS data. The insights drawn from the study could be used either to model individuals' behavior more explicitly in building simulation programs or to contribute to the development of intelligent buildings. Additionally, other than conventional approaches to exploring individuals' behavior by conducting surveys or interviews, the algorithmic technique is more robust with less artificial disturbance.
