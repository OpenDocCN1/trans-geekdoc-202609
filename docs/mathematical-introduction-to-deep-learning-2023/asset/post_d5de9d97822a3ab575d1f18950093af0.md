### 1.2.1 Multidimensional versions

To describe multidimensional activation functions, we frequently employ the concept of the multidimensional version of a function. This concept is the subject of the next notion.

**Definition 1.2.1** (Multidimensional versions of one-dimensional functions). Let $ T \in \mathbb{N} $, $ d_{1}, d_{2}, \ldots, d_{T} \in \mathbb{N} $ and let $ \psi: \mathbb{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{R} $ be a function. Then we denote by

$$ \mathfrak{M}_{\psi, d_{1}, d_{2}, \ldots, d_{T}}: \mathbb{R}^{d_{1} \times d_{2} \times \ldots \times d_{T}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{d_{1} \times d_{2} \times \ldots \times d_{T}} $$

the function which satisfies for all $ x = (x_{k_{1}, k_{2}, \ldots, k_{T}})_{(k_{1}, k_{2}, \ldots, k_{T}) \in(\prod_{i = 1}^{T}\{1, 2, \ldots, d_{i}\})} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{1} \times d_{2} \times \ldots \times d_{T}} $,

$ y = (y_{k_{1}, k_{2}, \ldots, k_{T}})_{(k_{1}, k_{2}, \ldots, k_{T}) \in(\prod_{i = 1}^{T}\{1, 2, \ldots, d_{i}\})} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{1} \times d_{2} \times \ldots \times d_{T}} $ with $\forall k_{1} \in\{1, 2, \ldots, d_{1}\}$, $k_{2} \in\{1, 2, \ldots, d_{2}\}$, $\ldots$, $k_{T} \in\{1, 2, \ldots, d_{T}\}$: $y_{k_{1}, k_{2}, \ldots, k_{T}} = \psi(x_{k_{1}, k_{2}, \ldots, k_{T}})$ that

$$ \mathfrak{M}_{\psi, d_{1}, d_{2}, \ldots, d_{T}}(x) = y $$

and we call $\mathfrak{M}_{\psi, d_{1}, d_{2}, \ldots, d_{T}}$ the $d_{1} \times d_{2} \times \ldots \times d_{T}$-dimensional version of $\psi$.

**Example 1.2.2** (Example for Definition 1.2.1). Let $A \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 1 \times 2}$ satisfy

$$ A = ((1, -1), (-2, 2), (3, -3)) $$

and let $\psi: \mathbb{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ that $\psi(x) = x^{2}$. Then

$$ \mathfrak{M}_{\psi, 3, 1, 2}(A) = ((1, 1), (4, 4), (9, 9)) $$

**Proof for Example 1.2.2**. Note that (1.18) establishes (1.20). The proof for Example 1.2.2 is thus complete.
