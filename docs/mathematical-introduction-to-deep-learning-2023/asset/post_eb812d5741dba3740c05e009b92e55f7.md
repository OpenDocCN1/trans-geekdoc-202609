
## Lemma 2.2.7 (Properties of fully-connected feedforward ReLU identity ANNs)

Let $d \in \mathbb{N}$. Then:

- (i) it holds that $\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{J}_d) = (d, 2d, d) \in \mathbb{N}^{3}$
- (ii) it holds that $\mathcal{R}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathfrak{J}_d) = \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}$

(cf. Definitions 1.3.1, 1.3.4, and 2.2.6).

### Proof of Lemma 2.2.7

Throughout this proof, let $L=2$, $l_{0}=1$, $l_{1}=2$, $l_{2}=1$. Note that (2.70) establishes that $\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{J}_1) = (1, 2, 1) = (l_0, l_1, l_2)$.

This, (2.71), and Proposition 2.2.4 prove that $\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{J}_d) = (d, 2d, d) \in \mathbb{N}^{3}$. This establishes item (i).

Next note that (2.70) assures that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ it holds that:
$(\mathcal{R}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathfrak{J}_1))(x) = \mathtt{r}(x)-\mathtt{r}(-x) = \max\{x,0\}-\max\{-x,0\} = x$.

Combining this and Proposition 2.2.3 demonstrates that for all $x=(x_1, \ldots, x_d) \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ it holds that $\mathcal{R}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathfrak{J}_d) \in C(\mathbb{R}^{d}, \mathbb{R}^{d})$ and:

\begin{align*}
(\mathcal{R}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathfrak{J}_d))(x) & = (\mathcal{R}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbf{P}_d(\mathfrak{J}_1, \mathfrak{J}_1, \ldots, \mathfrak{J}_1)))(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_d) \\
& = ((\mathcal{R}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathfrak{J}_1))(x_1), (\mathcal{R}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathfrak{J}_1))(x_2), \ldots, (\mathcal{R}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathfrak{J}_1))(x_d)) \\
& = (x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_d) = x
\end{align*}

(cf. Definition 2.2.1). This establishes item (ii). The proof of Lemma 2.2.7 is thus complete.

## 2.2.3 Extensions of fully-connected feedforward ANNs

### Definition 2.2.8 (Extensions of fully-connected feedforward ANNs)

Let $L \in \mathbb{N}$, $\mathbb{I} \in \mathbf{N}$ satisfy $\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{I}) = \mathcal{O}(\mathbb{I})$. Then we denote by:

$$
\mathcal{E}_{L, \mathbb{I}}: \{ \Phi \in \mathbf{N}: (\mathcal{L}(\Phi) \leq L \text{ and } \mathcal{O}(\Phi) = \mathcal{I}(\mathbb{I})) \} \rightarrow \mathbf{N}
$$

the function which satisfies for all $\Phi \in \mathbf{N}$ with $\mathcal{L}(\Phi) \leq L$ and $\mathcal{O}(\Phi) = \mathcal{I}(\mathbb{I})$ that:
$$
\mathcal{E}_{L, \mathbb{I}}(\Phi) = (\mathbb{I}^{* (L-\mathcal{L}(\Phi))}) \bullet \Phi
$$

(cf. Definitions 1.3.1, 2.1.1, and 2.1.6).

### Lemma 2.2.9 (Length of extensions of fully-connected feedforward ANNs)

Let $d, \mathbf{i} \in \mathbb{N}$, $\Psi \in \mathbb{N}$ satisfy $\mathcal{D}(\Psi) = (d, \mathbf{i}, d)$ (cf. Definition 1.3.1). Then:

- (i) it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ that $\mathcal{H}(\Psi^{*n}) = n$, $\mathcal{L}(\Psi^{*n}) = n+1$, $\mathcal{D}(\Psi^{*n}) \in \mathbb{N}^{n+2}$, and
$$
\mathcal{D}(\Psi^{*n}) = \begin{cases} 
(d, d) & : n = 0 \\ 
(d, \mathbf{i}, \mathbf{i}, \ldots, \mathbf{i}, d) & : n \in \mathbb{N} 
\end{cases} \tag{2.80}
$$
- (ii) it holds for all $\Phi \in \mathbb{N}$, $L \in \mathbb{N} \cap [\mathcal{L}(\Phi), \infty)$ with $\mathcal{O}(\Phi) = d$ that
$$
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{E}_{L, \Psi}(\Phi)) = L \tag{2.81}
$$

(cf. Definitions 2.1.6 and 2.2.8).

### Proof of Lemma 2.2.9

Throughout this proof, let $\Phi \in \mathbb{N}$ satisfy $\mathcal{O}(\Phi) = d$. Observe that Lemma 2.1.7 and the fact that $\mathcal{H}(\Psi) = 1$ show that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ it holds that:
$$
\mathcal{H}(\Psi^{*n}) = n\mathcal{H}(\Psi) = n \tag{2.82}
$$

(cf. Definition 2.1.6). Combining this with (1.78) and Lemma 1.3.3 ensures that:
$$
\mathcal{H}(\Psi^{*n}) = n, \quad \mathcal{L}(\Psi^{*n}) = n+1, \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{D}(\Psi^{*n}) \in \mathbb{N}^{n+2}. \tag{2.83}
$$

Next we claim that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ it holds that:
$$
\mathbb{N}^{n+2} \supseteq \mathcal{D}(\Psi^{*n}) = \begin{cases} 
(d, d) & : n = 0 \\ 
(d, \mathbf{i}, \mathbf{i}, \ldots, \mathbf{i}, d) & : n \in \mathbb{N}. 
\end{cases} \tag{2.84}
$$

We now prove (2.84) by induction on $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$. Note that the fact that:
$$
\Psi^{*0} = (\mathbf{I}_d, 0) \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d} \times \mathbb{R}^{d} \tag{2.85}
$$
establishes (2.84) in the base case $n = 0$ (cf. Definition 1.5.5).

For the induction step assume that there exists $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ which satisfies:
$$
\mathbb{N}^{n+2} \supseteq \mathcal{D}(\Psi^{*n}) = \begin{cases} 
(d, d) & : n = 0 \\ 
(d, \mathbf{i}, \mathbf{i}, \ldots, \mathbf{i}, d) & : n \in \mathbb{N}. 
\end{cases} \tag{2.86}
$$

Note that (2.86), (2.41), (2.83), item (i) in Proposition 2.1.2, and the fact that $\mathcal{D}(\Psi) = (d, \mathbf{i}, d) \in \mathbb{N}^{3}$ imply that:
$$
\mathcal{D}(\Psi^{*(n+1)}) = \mathcal{D}(\Psi \bullet (\Psi^{*n})) = (d, \mathbf{i}, \mathbf{i}, \ldots, \mathbf{i}, d) \in \mathbb{N}^{n+3} \tag{2.87}
$$

(cf. Definition 2.1.1). Induction therefore proves (2.84). This and (2.83) establish item (i).

Observe that (2.79), item (iii) in Proposition 2.1.2, (2.82), and the fact that $\mathcal{H}(\Phi) = \mathcal{L}(\Phi) - 1$ imply that for all $L \in \mathbb{N} \cap [\mathcal{L}(\Phi), \infty)$ it holds that:
$$
\begin{split}
\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{E}_{L, \Psi}(\Phi)) &= \mathcal{H}((\Psi^{\bullet (L - \mathcal{L}(\Phi))}} \bullet \Phi) = \mathcal{H}(\Psi^{\bullet (L - \mathcal{L}(\Phi))}}) + \mathcal{H}(\Phi) \\
&= (L - \mathcal{L}(\Phi)) + \mathcal{H}(\Phi) = L - 1.
\end{split} \tag{2.88}
$$

The fact that $\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{E}_{L, \Psi}(\Phi)) = \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{E}_{L, \Psi}(\Phi)) - 1$ hence proves that:
$$
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{E}_{L, \Psi}(\Phi)) = \mathcal{H}(\mathcal{E}_{L, \Psi}(\Phi)) + 1 = L. \tag{2.89}
$$

This establishes item (ii). The proof of Lemma 2.2.9 is thus complete.

### Lemma 2.2.10 (Realizations of extensions of fully-connected feedforward ANNs)

*Let* $a \in C(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R})$, $\mathbb{I} \in \mathbf{N}$ *satisfy* $\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I}) = \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{I}(1)}}$ *(cf. Definitions 1.3.1 and 1.3.4). Then:*

- (i) it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ that
$$
\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I}^{* n}) = \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{I}(1)}} \tag{2.90}
$$
- (ii) it holds for all $\Phi \in \mathbf{N}$, $L \in \mathbb{N} \cap [\mathcal{L}(\Phi), \infty)$ *with* $\mathcal{O}(\Phi) = \mathbb{I}(1)$ *that*
$$
\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathcal{E}_{L, \mathbb{I}}(\Phi)) = \mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\Phi) \tag{2.91}
$$

*(cf. Definitions 2.1.6 and 2.2.8).*

### Proof of Lemma 2.2.10

Throughout this proof, let $\Phi \in \mathbf{N}$, $L, d \in \mathbb{N}$ satisfy $\mathcal{L}(\Phi) \leq L$ and $\mathbb{I}(1) = \mathcal{O}(\Phi) = d$. We claim that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ it holds that:
$$
\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I}^{* n}) \in C(\mathbb{R}^{d}, \mathbb{R}^{d}) \quad \text{ and } \quad \forall x \in \mathbb{R}^{d}: \ (\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I}^{* n}))(x) = x. \tag{2.92}
$$

We now prove (2.92) by induction on $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$. Note that (2.41) and the fact that $\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{I}) = d$ demonstrate that $\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I}^{* 0}) \in C(\mathbb{R}^{d}, \mathbb{R}^{d})$ and $\forall x \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$: $(\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I}^{* 0}))(x) = x$. This establishes (2.92) in the base case $n = 0$.

For the induction step observe that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ with $\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I}^{* n}) \in C(\mathbb{R}^{d}, \mathbb{R}^{d})$ and $\forall x \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$: $(\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I}^{* n}))(x) = x$ it holds that:
$$
\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I}^{*(n+1)}) = \mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I} \bullet (\mathbb{I}^{* n})) = (\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I})) \circ (\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I}^{* n})) \in C(\mathbb{R}^{d}, \mathbb{R}^{d}) \tag{2.93}
$$
*and*
$$
\begin{split}
\forall x \in \mathbb{R}^{d}: \ (\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I}^{*(n+1))})(x) &= ((\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I})) \circ [\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I}^{* n})])(x) \\
&= (\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I})) (\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I}^{* n})(x)) = (\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{a}(\mathbb{I})) (x) = x. \tag{2.94}
\end{split}
$$
