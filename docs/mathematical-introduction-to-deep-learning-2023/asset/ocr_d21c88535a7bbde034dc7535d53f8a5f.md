Proof of Lemma 11.2.5. Throughout this proof, let $D = (D_1, \dots, D_n): E \to \mathbb{R}^n$ satisfy for all $x \in E$ that

$$D(x) = (D_1(x), D_2(x), \dots, D_n(x)) = (d(x, e_1), d(x, e_2), \dots, d(x, e_n)).$$

Note that (11.38) ensures that for all $x \in E$ it holds that

$$\begin{aligned} d(x, P(x)) &= d(x, \epsilon_{\min\{k \in \{1, \dots, n\}: d(x, e_k) = \min\{d(x, e_1), d(x, e_2), \dots, d(x, e_n)\}\})} \\ &= \min_{k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}} d(x, e_k). \end{aligned}$$

This establishes item (i). It thus remains to prove item (ii). For this observe that the fact that $d: E \times E \to [0, \infty)$ is continuous shows that $D: E \to \mathbb{R}^n$ is continuous. Hence, we obtain that $D: E \to \mathbb{R}^n$ is $\mathcal{B}(E)/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$-measurable. Furthermore, note that item (i) implies that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$, $x \in P^{-1}(\{e_k\})$ it holds that

$$d(x, e_k) = d(x, P(x)) = \min_{l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}} d(x, e_l).$$

Therefore, we obtain that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$, $x \in P^{-1}(\{e_k\})$ it holds that

$$k \geq \min\{l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}: d(x, e_l) = \min\{d(x, e_1), d(x, e_2), \dots, d(x, e_n)\}\}.$$

Moreover, observe that (11.38) demonstrates that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$, $x \in P^{-1}(\{e_k\})$ it holds that

$$\begin{aligned} \min\Big\{l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}: d(x, e_l) &= \min_{u \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}} d(x, e_u)\Big\} \\ \in \Big\{l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}: e_l = e_k\} \subseteq \{k, k+1, \dots, n\}. \end{aligned}$$

Hence, we obtain that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$, $x \in P^{-1}(\{e_k\})$ with $e_k \notin (\bigcup_{l \in \mathbb{N} \cap [0, k)} \{e_l\})$ it holds that

$$\min\Big\{l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}: d(x, e_l) = \min_{u \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}} d(x, e_u)\Big\} \geq k.$$

Combining this with (11.43) proves that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$, $x \in P^{-1}(\{e_k\})$ with $e_k \notin (\bigcup_{l \in \mathbb{N} \cap [0, k)} \{e_l\})$ it holds that

$$\min\Big\{l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}: d(x, e_l) = \min_{u \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}} d(x, e_u)\Big\} = k.$$

Therefore, we obtain that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$ with $e_k \notin (\bigcup_{l \in \mathbb{N} \cap [0, k)} \{e_l\})$ it holds that

$$P^{-1}(\{e_k\}) \subseteq \Big\{x \in E: \min\Big\{l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}: d(x, e_l) = \min_{u \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}} d(x, e_u)\Big\} = k\Big\}.$$
