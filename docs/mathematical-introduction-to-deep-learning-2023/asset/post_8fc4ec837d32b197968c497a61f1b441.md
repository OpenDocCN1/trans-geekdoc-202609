
This establishes for all $ n \in \mathbb{N}_{0} \cap [0, \tau) $ that

$$0 < \mathbb{L}(n) \leq \frac{2 \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2-2\alpha}}{\gamma} .$$

Combining this and (9.240) demonstrates for all $ n \in \mathbb{N}_{0} \cap [0, \tau - 1) $ that

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{\mathbb{L}(n)} - \frac{1}{\mathbb{L}(n+1)} & \leq \frac{1}{\mathbb{L}(n)} - \frac{1}{\mathbb{L}(n)(1-\frac{1}{2 \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2-2\alpha}} \mathbb{L}(n))} = \frac{\left( \frac{1-\frac{\gamma}{2 \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2-2\alpha}} \mathbb{L}(n) }{1} \right) - 1}{\mathbb{L}(n) \left( \frac{1-\frac{\gamma}{2 \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2-2\alpha}} \mathbb{L}(n) }{1} \right)} \\ & = \frac{\frac{-\frac{\gamma}{2 \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2-2\alpha}} \mathbb{L}(n)}{\left( 1-\frac{\gamma}{2 \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2-2\alpha}} \mathbb{L}(n) \right) } = \frac{1}{\left( \frac{2 \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2-2\alpha}}{\gamma} - \mathbb{L}(n) \right) } < \frac{\gamma}{2 \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2-2\alpha}} . \end{aligned}$$

Therefore, we get for all $ n \in \mathbb{N}_{0} \cap [0, \tau) $ that

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{\mathbb{L}(n)} & = \frac{1}{\mathbb{L}(0)} + \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \left[ \frac{1}{\mathbb{L}(k+1)} - \frac{1}{\mathbb{L}(k)} \right] > \frac{1}{\mathbb{L}(0)} + \frac{n\gamma}{2 \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2-2\alpha}} = \frac{1}{\mathfrak{c}} + \frac{n\gamma}{2 \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2-2\alpha}} . \end{aligned}$$

Hence, we obtain for all $ n \in \mathbb{N}_{0} \cap [0, \tau) $ that $ \mathbb{L}(n) < \frac{2 \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2-2\alpha}}{n\gamma + 2 \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2-2\alpha}} $. Combining this with the fact that for all $ n \in \mathbb{N}_{0} \cap [\tau, \infty) $ it holds that $ \mathbb{L}(n) = 0 $ shows that for all $ n \in \mathbb{N}_{0} $ we have that

$$\mathbb{L}(n) \leq \frac{2 \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2}}{\mathbb{I}_{\{0\}}(\epsilon) + \mathfrak{c}^{2\alpha}n\gamma + 2 \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}} .$$

This, (9.237), and the assumption that $ \mathscr{L} $ is continuous prove that

$$\mathscr{L}(\psi) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \mathscr{L}(\Theta_{n}) = \mathscr{L}(\vartheta) .$$

Combining this with (9.244) implies for all $ n \in \mathbb{N}_{0} $ that

$$0 \leq \mathscr{L}(\Theta_{n}) - \mathscr{L}(\psi) \leq \frac{2 \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2}}{\mathbb{I}_{\{0\}}(\epsilon) + \mathfrak{c}^{2\alpha}n\gamma + 2 \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}} .$$

Furthermore, observe that the fact that $ \mathbb{B} \ni \vartheta \mapsto \mathscr{G}(\vartheta) \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{g}} $ is continuous, the fact that $ \psi \in \mathbb{B} $, and (9.237) demonstrate that

$$\mathscr{G}(\psi) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \mathscr{G}(\Theta_{n}) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \gamma^{-1}(\Theta_{n} - \Theta_{n+1}) = 0 .$$

Next note that (9.244) and (9.232) ensure for all $ n \in \mathbb{N}_{0} $ that

$$\begin{aligned} \|\Theta_{n} - \psi\|_{2} & = \lim_{m \to \infty} \|\Theta_{n} - \Theta_{m}\|_{2} \leq \sum_{k=n}^{\infty} \|\Theta_{k+1} - \Theta_{k}\|_{2} \leq \frac{2 \mathfrak{C} \| \mathbb{L}(n) \|^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha} \\ & \leq \frac{2^{2-\alpha} \mathfrak{C}^{3-2\alpha} \mathfrak{c}^{2-2\alpha}}{\left( 1-\alpha \right) \left( \mathbb{I}_{\{0\}}(\epsilon) + \mathfrak{c}^{2\alpha}n\gamma + 2 \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c} \right)^{1-\alpha}} . \end{aligned}$$

Combining this with (9.245), (9.235), (9.247), and (9.246) establishes items (i), (ii), and (iii). The proof of Proposition 9.12.4 is thus complete.

# Corollary 9.12.5.

*Let* $\mathfrak{d} \in \mathbb{N}$, $\mathfrak{c} \in [0, 1]$, $\varepsilon, L, \mathfrak{C} \in (0, \infty)$, $\alpha \in (0, 1)$, $\gamma \in (0, L^{-1}]$, $\vartheta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$, *let* $\mathbb{B} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ *satisfy* $\mathbb{B} = \{\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} : \|\theta - \vartheta\|_2 \leq \varepsilon\}$, *let* $\mathcal{L} \in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}, \mathbb{R})$ *satisfy* $\mathcal{L}\|_{\mathbb{B}} \in \mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{B}, \mathbb{R})$, *let* $\mathcal{G}: \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} \to \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ *satisfy for all* $\theta \in \mathbb{B}$ *that* $\mathcal{G}(\theta) = (\nabla \mathcal{L})(\theta)$, *assume for all* $\theta_1, \theta_2 \in \mathbb{B}$ *that*

$$\|\mathcal{G}(\theta_1) - \mathcal{G}(\theta_2)\|_2 \leq L \|\theta_1 - \theta_2\|_2,$$ (9.249)

*let* $\Theta = (\Theta_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}_0}$, $\mathbb{N}_0 \to \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ *satisfy for all* $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ *that*

$$\Theta_{n+1} = \Theta_n - \gamma \mathcal{G}(\Theta_n),$$ (9.250)

*and assume for all* $\theta \in \mathbb{B}$ *that*

$$\begin{aligned} & \left[\mathcal{L}(\theta) - \mathcal{L}(\vartheta)\right]^\alpha \leq \mathfrak{C}\|\mathcal{G}(\theta)\|_2, \ \ \mathfrak{c} = \left|\mathcal{L}(\Theta_0) - \mathcal{L}(\vartheta)\right|, \ \ 2\mathfrak{C}(1-\alpha)^{-1} \mathfrak{c}^{1-\alpha} + \|\Theta_0 - \vartheta\|_2 \leq \frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma L}, \end{aligned}$$ (9.251)

*and* $\mathcal{L}(\theta) \geq \mathcal{L}(\vartheta)$. *Then there exists* $\psi \in \mathcal{L}^{-1}(\{\mathcal{L}(\vartheta)\}) \cap \mathcal{G}^{-1}(\{0\})$ *such that for all* $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ *it holds that* $\Theta_n \in \mathbb{B}$, $0 \leq \mathcal{L}(\Theta_n) - \mathcal{L}(\psi) \leq (2+\mathfrak{C}^{-2}\gamma n)^{-1}$, *and*

$$\|\Theta_n - \psi\|_2 \leq \sum_{k=n}^{\infty} \|\Theta_{k+1} - \Theta_k\|_2 \leq 2^{\alpha} \mathfrak{C}(1-\alpha)^{-1}(2+\mathfrak{C}^{-2}\gamma n)^{\alpha-1}.$$ (9.252)

*Proof of Corollary 9.12.5.*

*Observe that the fact that* $\mathcal{L}(\vartheta) = \inf_{\theta \in \mathbb{B}} \mathcal{L}(\theta)$ *ensures that* $\mathcal{G}(\vartheta) = (\nabla \mathcal{L})(\vartheta) = 0$ *and* $\inf_{n \in \{m \in \mathbb{N}_0: \ \forall k \in \mathbb{N}_0 \cap [0,m]: \ \Theta_k \in \mathbb{B}\}} \mathcal{L}(\Theta_n) \geq \mathcal{L}(\vartheta)$. *Combining this with Proposition 9.12.4 ensures that there exists* $\psi \in \mathcal{L}^{-1}(\{\mathcal{L}(\vartheta)\}) \cap \mathcal{G}^{-1}(\{0\})$ *such that*

- (I) *it holds for all* $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ *that* $\Theta_n \in \mathbb{B}$,
- (II) *it holds for all* $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ *that* $0 \leq \mathcal{L}(\Theta_n) - \mathcal{L}(\psi) \leq \frac{2\mathfrak{C}^2 \mathfrak{c}^2}{\mathbb{I}_{\{0\}}(\mathfrak{c})+\mathfrak{c}^{2\alpha}n\gamma+2\mathfrak{C}^2\mathfrak{c}}$, *and*
- (III) *it holds for all* $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ *that*

$$\begin{aligned} & \|\Theta_n - \psi\|_2 \leq \sum_{k=n}^{\infty} \|\Theta_{k+1} - \Theta_k\|_2 \leq \frac{2\mathfrak{C}|\mathcal{L}(\Theta_n) - \mathcal{L}(\psi)|^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\\ & \quad \leq \frac{2^{2-\alpha}\mathfrak{C}^{3-2\alpha}\mathfrak{c}^{2-2\alpha}}{(1-\alpha)(\mathbb{I}_{\{0\}}(\mathfrak{c})+\mathfrak{c}^{2\alpha}n\gamma+2\mathfrak{C}^2\mathfrak{c})^{1-\alpha}}. \end{aligned}$$ (9.253)

*Note that item (II) and the assumption that* $\mathfrak{c} \leq 1$ *establish for all* $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ *that*

$$0 \leq \mathcal{L}(\Theta_n) - \mathcal{L}(\psi) \leq 2\mathfrak{C}^2(\mathfrak{C}^2\mathbb{I}_{\{0\}}(\mathfrak{c})+ \mathfrak{c}^{-2}\mathfrak{C}^{2\alpha}n\gamma+2\mathfrak{c})^{-1} \leq (2+\mathfrak{C}^{-2}\gamma n)^{-1}.$$ (9.254)

*This and item (III) demonstrate for all* $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ *that*

$$\|\Theta_n - \psi\|_2 \leq \sum_{k=n}^{\infty} \|\Theta_{k+1} - \Theta_k\|_2 \leq \frac{2\mathfrak{C}|\mathcal{L}(\Theta_n) - \mathcal{L}(\psi)|^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha} \leq \left[\frac{2^{2-\alpha}\mathfrak{C}}{1-\alpha}\right] (2+\mathfrak{C}^{-2}\gamma n)^{\alpha-1}.$$ (9.255)

*The proof of Corollary 9.12.5 is thus complete.*

## 9.13 On the analyticity of realization functions of ANNs

### Proposition 9.13.1 (Compositions of analytic functions)

Let $l, m, n \in \mathbb{N}$, let $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{l}$ and $V \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{m}$ be open, let $f: U \to \mathbb{R}^{m}$ and $g: V \to \mathbb{R}^{n}$ be analytic, and assume $f(U) \subseteq V$ (cf. Definition 9.7.1). Then

$$U \ni u \mapsto g(f(u)) \in \mathbb{R}^{n} \tag{9.260}$$

is analytic.

Proof of Proposition 9.13.1. Observe that Faà di Bruno’s formula (cf., for instance, Fraenkel [134]) establishes that $f \circ g$ is analytic (cf. also, for example, Krantz & Parks [254, Proposition 2.8]). The proof of Proposition 9.13.1 is thus complete. $\square$

### Lemma 9.13.2

Let $\mathbf{0}_{1}, \mathbf{0}_{2}, l_{1}, l_{2} \in \mathbb{N}$, for every $k \in \{1, 2\}$ let $F_{k}: \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{0}_{k}} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_{k}}$ be analytic, and let $f: \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{0}_{1}} \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{0}_{2}} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_{1}} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_{2}}$ satisfy for all $x_{1} \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{0}_{1}}$, $x_{2} \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{0}_{2}}$ that

$$f(x_{1}, x_{2}) = (F_{1}(x_{1}), F_{2}(x_{2})) \tag{9.261}$$

(cf. Definition 9.7.1). Then $f$ is analytic.
