
(cf. Definition 2.2.1). Furthermore, note that item (i) in Lemma 2.4.2 demonstrates that

$$
\mathcal{D} \big( \mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{O}(\Phi_m), n-m+1} \big) = \big( (n-m+1) \mathcal{O}(\Phi_m), \mathcal{O}(\Phi_m) \big)
\tag{2.157}
$$

(cf. Definition 2.4.1). This, (2.156), and item (i) in Proposition 2.1.2 show that

$$
\begin{split}
\mathcal{D} \big( \mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{O}(\Phi_m), n-m+1} \bullet \big[ \mathbb{P}_{n-m+1}(\Phi_m, \Phi_{m+1}, \dots, \Phi_n) \big] \big) 
&= \bigg( (n-m+1) \mathcal{I}(\Phi_m), \sum_{k=m}^{n} \mathbb{D}_1(\Phi_k), \sum_{k=m}^{n} \mathbb{D}_2(\Phi_k), \dots, \sum_{k=m}^{n} \mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{I}(\Phi_m)-1}(\Phi_k), \mathcal{O}(\Phi_m) \bigg).
\end{split}
\tag{2.158}
$$

Moreover, observe that item (i) in Lemma 2.4.7 establishes that

$$
\mathcal{D} \big( \mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{I}(\Phi_m), n-m+1} \big) = \big( \mathcal{I}(\Phi_m), (n-m+1) \mathcal{I}(\Phi_m) \big)
\tag{2.159}
$$

(cf. Definitions 2.1.1 and 2.4.6). Combining this, (2.158), and item (i) in Proposition 2.1.2 ensures that

$$
\begin{split}
\mathcal{D} \bigg( \bigoplus_{k=m}^{n} \Phi_k \bigg)
&= \mathcal{D} \bigg( \mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{O}(\Phi_m), (n-m+1)} \bullet \big[ \mathbb{P}_{n-m+1}(\Phi_m, \Phi_{m+1}, \dots, \Phi_n) \big] \bullet \mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{I}(\Phi_m), (n-m+1)} \bigg) \\
&= \bigg( \mathcal{I}(\Phi_m), \sum_{k=m}^{n} \mathbb{D}_1(\Phi_k), \sum_{k=m}^{n} \mathbb{D}_2(\Phi_k), \dots, \sum_{k=m}^{n} \mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{I}(\Phi_m)-1}(\Phi_k), \mathcal{O}(\Phi_m) \bigg)
\end{split}
\tag{2.160}
$$

(cf. Definition 2.4.10). This proves items (i) and (ii). Note that Lemma 2.4.9 and (2.156) imply that for all $a \in C(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R})$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}(\Phi_m)}$ it holds that

$$
\mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbf{N}} \big( \big[ \mathbb{P}_{n-m+1}(\Phi_m, \Phi_{m+1}, \dots, \Phi_n) \big] \bullet \mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{I}(\Phi_m), n-m+1} \big) \in C( \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{I}(\Phi_m)}, \mathbb{R}^{(n-m+1)\mathcal{O}(\Phi_m)} )
\tag{2.161}
$$

and

$$
\begin{split}
\big( \mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbf{N}} \big( \big[ \mathbb{P}_{n-m+1}(\Phi_m, \Phi_{m+1}, \dots, \Phi_n) \big] \bullet \mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{I}(\Phi_m), n-m+1} \big) \big)(x)
&= \big( \mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbf{N}} \big( \big[ \mathbb{P}_{n-m+1}(\Phi_m, \Phi_{m+1}, \dots, \Phi_n) \big] \big) (x, x, \dots, x)
\end{split}
\tag{2.162}
$$

(cf. Definition 1.3.4). Combining this with item (ii) in Proposition 2.2.3 demonstrates that for all $a \in C(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R})$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}(\Phi_m)}$ it holds that

$$
\begin{split}
\big( \mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbf{N}} \big( \big[ \mathbb{P}_{n-m+1}(\Phi_m, \Phi_{m+1}, \dots, \Phi_n) \big] \bullet \mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{I}(\Phi_m), n-m+1} \big) \big)(x)
&= \big( (\mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbf{N}} (\Phi_m))(x), (\mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbf{N}} (\Phi_{m+1}))(x), \dots, (\mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbf{N}} (\Phi_n))(x) \big) \in \mathbb{R}^{(n-m+1)\mathcal{O}(\Phi_m)}.
\end{split}
\tag{2.163}
$$

Lemma 2.4.3, (2.157), and Corollary 2.1.5 hence show that for all $a \in C(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R})$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}(\Phi_m)}$ it holds that $\mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbf{N}} \big( \bigoplus_{k=m}^{n} \Phi_k \big) \in C(\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{I}(\Phi_m)}, \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{O}(\Phi_m)})$ and

$$
\begin{split}
\bigg( \mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbf{N}} \bigg( \bigoplus_{k=m}^{n} \Phi_k \bigg) \bigg)(x)
&= \big( \mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbf{N}} \big( \mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{O}(\Phi_m), n-m+1} \bullet \big[ \mathbb{P}_{n-m+1}(\Phi_m, \Phi_{m+1}, \dots, \Phi_n) \big] \bullet \mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{I}(\Phi_m), n-m+1} \big) \big)(x) \\
&= \sum_{k=m}^{n} (\mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbf{N}} (\Phi_k))(x).
\end{split}
\tag{2.164}
$$

This establishes item (iii). The proof of Lemma 2.4.11 is thus complete.

## 3.1 Linear interpolation of one-dimensional functions

### 3.1.1 On the modulus of continuity

**Definition 3.1.1 (Modulus of continuity).** Let $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be a set and let $f \colon A \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be a function. *Then we denote by $w_f \colon [0,\infty[ \rightarrow [0,\infty[$ the function which satisfies for all $h \in [0,\infty[$ that*

$$
\begin{align*}
	w_f(h) = \operatorname{sup} \big( \{ | f(x) - f(y) | : (x,y) \in A \text{ with } | x - y | \leq h \} \cup \{ 0 \} \big) \notag \\
	\phantom{w_f(h) =} & \operatorname{sup} \big( \{ r \in \mathbb{R} : (\exists x \in A, \; y \in A \cap [x-h,x+h]: \; r = | f(x) - f(y) | \} \cup \{ 0 \} \big) \tag{3.1}
\end{align*}
$$

*and we call $w_f$ the modulus of continuity of $f$.*

**Lemma 3.1.2** (Elementary properties of moduli of continuity). *Let $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be a set and let $f : A \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be a function. Then*

- *(i)* it holds that $w_f$ is non-decreasing,
- *(ii)* it holds that $f$ is uniformly continuous if and only if $\lim_{h\searrow 0} w_f(h) = 0$,
- *(iii)* it holds that $f$ is globally bounded if and only if $w_f(\infty) < \infty$, and
- *(iv)* it holds for all $x, y \in A$ that $|f(x) - f(y)| \leq w_f(|x - y|)$

*(cf. Definition 3.1.1).*

*Proof of Lemma 3.1.2.* Observe that (3.1) proves items (i), (ii), (iii), and (iv). The proof of Lemma 3.1.2 is thus complete. $\Box$

**Lemma 3.1.3** (Subadditivity of moduli of continuity). *Let $a \in [-\infty, \infty]$, $b \in [a, \infty]$, let $f : ([a, b] \cap \mathbb{R}) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be a function, and let $h, \mathfrak{h} \in [0, \infty]$. Then*

$$w_f(h + \mathfrak{h}) \leq w_f(h) + w_f(\mathfrak{h}) \tag{3.2}$$

*(cf. Definition 3.1.1).*

*Proof of Lemma 3.1.3.* Throughout this proof, assume without loss of generality that $\mathfrak{h} \leq h < \infty$. Note that the fact that for all $x, y \in [a, b] \cap \mathbb{R}$ with $|x - y| \leq h + \mathfrak{h}$ it holds that $[x - h, x + h] \cap [y - \mathfrak{h}, y + \mathfrak{h}] \cap [a, b] \neq \emptyset$ ensures that for all $x, y \in [a, b] \cap \mathbb{R}$ with $|x - y| \leq h + \mathfrak{h}$ there exists $z \in [a, b] \cap \mathbb{R}$ such that

$$|x - z| \leq h \quad \text{and} \quad |y - z| \leq \mathfrak{h}. \tag{3.3}$$

Items (i) and (iv) in Lemma 3.1.2 therefore imply that for all $x, y \in [a, b] \cap \mathbb{R}$ with $|x - y| \leq h + \mathfrak{h}$ there exists $z \in [a, b] \cap \mathbb{R}$ such that

$$
\begin{align*}
|f(x) - f(y)| &\leq |f(x) - f(z)| + |f(y) - f(z)| \\
&\leq w_f(|x - z|) + w_f(|y - z|) \leq w_f(h) + w_f(\mathfrak{h})
\end{align*} \tag{3.4}
$$

*(cf. Definition 3.1.1). Combining this with (3.1) demonstrates that*

$$w_f(h + \mathfrak{h}) \leq w_f(h) + w_f(\mathfrak{h}). \tag{3.5}$$

The proof of Lemma 3.1.3 is thus complete. $\Box$

**Lemma 3.1.4** (Properties of moduli of continuity of Lipschitz continuous functions). *Let $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, $L \in [0, \infty)$, let $f : A \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $x, y \in A$ that*

$$|f(x) - f(y)| \leq L|x - y|, \tag{3.6}$$

*and let $h \in [0, \infty)$. Then*

$$w_f(h) \leq Lh \tag{3.7}$$

*(cf. Definition 3.1.1).*
