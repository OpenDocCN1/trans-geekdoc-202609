
Combining this with $(9.301)$ proves item **(iii)**. Observe that items **(ii)** and **(iii)** ensure that for all open $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{n}$ and all $x \in U$ with $\mathcal{L}\vert_{U} \in C^{1}(U, \mathbb{R})$ it holds that

$\{\nabla \mathcal{L}(x)\} = (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(x) \subseteq (\mathcal{Q}\mathcal{L})(x).$ $(9.304)$

In addition, note that for all open $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{3}$, all $x \in U$, $y \in \mathbb{R}^{3}$ and all $z = (z_{1}, z_{2}) : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}^{3} \times \mathbb{R}^{3}$ with $\lim \sup_{k \to \infty}(\|z_{1}(k) - x\|_{2} + \|z_{2}(k) - y\|_{2}) = 0$ and $\forall k \in \mathbb{N} : z_{2}(k) \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(z_{1}(k))$ there exists $K \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for all $k \in \mathbb{N} \cap [K, \infty)$ it holds that

$z_{1}(k) \in U.$ $(9.305)$

Combining this with item **(iii)** shows that for all open $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{3}$, all $x \in U$, $y \in \mathbb{R}^{3}$ and all $z = (z_{1}, z_{2}) : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}^{3} \times \mathbb{R}^{3}$ with $\mathcal{L}\vert_{U} \in C^{1}(U, \mathbb{R})$, $\lim \sup_{k \to \infty}(\|z_{1}(k) - x\|_{2} + \|z_{2}(k) - y\|_{2}) = 0$ and $\forall k \in \mathbb{N} : z_{2}(k) \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(z_{1}(k))$ there exists $K \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $\forall k \in \mathbb{N} \cap [K, \infty) : z_{1}(k) \in U$ and

$\begin{aligned} \lim \sup_{\substack{k \in \mathbb{N} \cap [K, \infty) \to \infty}} & (\|z_{1}(k) - x\|_{2} + \|\nabla \mathcal{L})(z_{1}(k)) - y\|_{2}) \\ \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad & = \lim \sup_{k \to \infty}(\|z_{1}(k) - x\|_{2} + \|z_{2}(k) - y\|_{2}) = 0. \end{aligned}$ $(9.306)$

This and item **(i)** imply that for all open $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{3}$ and all $x \in U$, $y \in (\mathcal{Q}\mathcal{L})(x)$ with $\mathcal{L}\vert_{U} \in C^{1}(U, \mathbb{R})$ it holds that

$y = (\nabla \mathcal{L})(x).$ $(9.307)$

Combining this with $(9.304)$ establishes item **(iv)**. Observe that $(9.284)$ demonstrates that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^{3}$ it holds that

$\mathbb{R}^{3} \backslash ((\mathcal{Q}\mathcal{L})(x)) = \bigcup_{\varepsilon \in (0, \infty)} \big(\mathbb{R}^{3} \backslash \overline{\{y \in \mathbb{R}^{3} : \|x - y\|_{2} < \varepsilon\} (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(y)}\big)$ $(9.308)$

Therefore, we obtain for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^{3}$ that $\mathbb{R}^{3} \backslash ((\mathcal{Q}\mathcal{L})(x))$ is open. This proves item **(v)**. The proof of Lemma $9.15.3$ is thus complete. $\square$

## **Lemma 9.15.4** (Fréchet subgradients for maxima). Let $c \in \mathbb{R}$ and let $\mathcal{L} : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ that $\mathcal{L}(x) = \max\{x, c\}$. Then

-   (i) it holds for all $x \in (-\infty, c)$ that $(\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(x) = \{0\}$,
-   (ii) it holds for all $x \in (c, \infty)$ that $(\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(x) = \{1\}$, and
-   (iii) it holds that $(\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(c) = [0, 1]$ $\quad \quad \quad \quad$ (cf. Definition $9.15.1$).

Proof of Lemma $9.15.4$. Note that item **(iii)** in Lemma $9.15.3$ establishes items **(i)** and **(ii)**. Observe that Lemma $9.15.2$ establishes

$[0, 1] \subseteq (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(c).$ $(9.309)$

Furthermore, note that the assumption that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ it holds that $\mathscr{L}(x) = \max\{x, c\}$ ensures that for all $a \in (1, \infty)$, $h \in (0, \infty)$ it holds that

$$\frac{\mathscr{L}(c+h)-\mathscr{L}(c)-ah}{|h|} = \frac{(c+h)-c-ah}{h} = 1-a<0. \qquad \qquad (9.310)$$

Moreover, observe that the assumption that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ it holds that $\mathscr{L}(x) = \max\{x, c\}$ shows that for all $a, h \in (-\infty, 0)$, it holds that

$$\frac{\mathscr{L}(c+h)-\mathscr{L}(c)-ah}{|h|} = \frac{c-c-ah}{-h} = a<0. \qquad \qquad (9.311)$$

Combining this with $(9.310)$ demonstrates that

$$(\mathcal{D}\mathscr{L})(c) \subseteq [0,1]. \qquad \qquad (9.312)$$

This and $(9.309)$ establish item (iii). The proof of Lemma $9.15.4$ is thus complete. $\square$

## Lemma 9.15.5 (Limits of limiting Fréchet subgradients). Let $\mathbf{0} \in \mathbb{N}$, $\mathscr{L} \in C(\mathbb{R}^9, \mathbb{R})$, let $(x_k)_{k \in \mathbb{N}_0} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^9$ and $(y_k)_{k \in \mathbb{N}_0} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^9$ satisfy

$$\lim \sup_{k \rightarrow \infty} (\|x_k-x_0\|_2 + \|y_k-y_0\|_2) = 0, \qquad \qquad (9.313)$$

and assume for all $k \in \mathbb{N}$ that $y_k \in (\mathcal{D}\mathscr{L})(x_k)$ (cf. Definitions 9.3.4 and 9.15.1). Then $y_0 \in (\mathcal{D}\mathscr{L})(x_0)$.

Proof of Lemma $9.15.5$. Note that item (i) in Lemma 9.15.3 and the fact that for all $k \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that $y_k \in (\mathcal{D}\mathscr{L})(x_k)$ imply that for every $k \in \mathbb{N}$ there exists $z^{(k)} = (z^{(k)}_1, z^{(k)}_2)$: $\mathbb{N} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^9 \times \mathbb{R}^9$ which satisfies for all $v \in \mathbb{N}$ that

$$z^{(k)}_2(v) \in (\mathcal{D}\mathscr{L})(z^{(k)}_1(v)) \text{ and } \lim \sup_{w \rightarrow \infty} \left(\|z^{(k)}_1(w) - x_k\|_2 + \|z^{(k)}_2(w) - y_k\|_2\right) = 0. \quad (9.314)$$

Observe that $(9.314)$ demonstrates that there exists $v = (v_k)_{k \in \mathbb{N}}: \mathbb{N} \rightarrow \mathbb{N}$ which satisfies for all $k \in \mathbb{N}$ that

$$\|z^{(k)}_1(v_k) - x_k\|_2 + \|z^{(k)}_2(v_k) - y_k\|_2 \leq 2^{-k}. \qquad \qquad (9.315)$$

Next let $Z = (Z_1, Z_2): \mathbb{N} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^9 \times \mathbb{R}^9$ satisfy for all $j \in \{1, 2\}$, $k \in \mathbb{N}$ that

$$Z_j(k) = z^{(k)}_j(v_k). \qquad \qquad (9.316)$$

Note that $(9.314)$, $(9.315)$, $(9.316)$, and the assumption that $\lim \sup_{k \rightarrow \infty}(\|x_k-x_0\|_2 + \|y_k - y_0\|_2) = 0$ prove that

$$\begin{aligned} \lim \sup_{k \rightarrow \infty} \left(\|Z_1(k)-x_0\|_2 + \|Z_2(k)-y_0\|_2\right) \\ \leq \left[\lim \sup_{k \rightarrow \infty} \left(\|Z_1(k)-x_k\|_2 + \|Z_2(k)-y_k\|_2\right)\right] \\ \quad + \left[\lim \sup_{k \rightarrow \infty} \left(\|x_k-x_0\|_2 + \|y_k-y_0\|_2\right)\right] \\ = \lim \sup_{k \rightarrow \infty} \left(\|Z_1(k)-x_k\|_2 + \|Z_2(k)-y_k\|_2\right) \\ = \lim \sup_{k \rightarrow \infty} \left(\|z^{(k)}_1(v_k)-x_k\|_2 + \|z^{(k)}_2(v_k)-y_k\|_2\right) \\ \leq \lim \sup_{k \rightarrow \infty} 2^{-k} = 0.\end{aligned} \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad (9.317)$$

Furthermore, observe that (9.314) and (9.316) establish that for all $k \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that $Z_2(k) \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(Z_1(k))$. Combining this and (9.317) with item (i) in Lemma 9.15.3 proves that $y_0 \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(x_0)$. The proof of Lemma 9.15.5 is thus complete. $\square$

## 9.16 Non-smooth slope

**Definition 9.16.1 (Non-smooth slope).** *Let $\mathfrak{d} \in \mathbb{N}$, $\mathcal{L} \in C(\mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}, \mathbb{R})$. Then we denote by* $\mathbb{S}_\mathcal{L}: \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d} \to [0, \infty]$ *the function which satisfies for all* $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$ *that*

$$\mathbb{S}_\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \inf \{\lvert r \rvert : r \in \mathbb{R}: (\exists h \in (\mathfrak{D}\mathcal{L})(\theta): r = \lvert h \rvert_2) \} \cup \{\infty\} \quad (9.319)$$

*and we call* $\mathbb{S}_\mathcal{L}$ *the non-smooth slope of* $\mathcal{L}$ *(cf. Definitions 3.3.4 and 9.15.1).*

## 9.17 Generalized KL functions

**Definition 9.17.1 (Generalized KL inequalities).** *Let* $\mathfrak{d} \in \mathbb{N}$, *$c \in \mathbb{R}$, $\alpha \in (0, \infty)$, $\mathcal{L} \in C(\mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}, \mathbb{R})$, let* $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$ *be a set, and let* $\theta \in U$. *Then we say that* $\mathcal{L}$ *satisfies the generalized* **KL** *inequality at* $\theta$ *on* $U$ *with exponent* $\alpha$ *and constant* $c$ *(we say that* $\mathcal{L}$ *satisfies the generalized* **KL** *inequality at* $\theta$*) if and only if for all* $\vartheta \in U$ *it holds that*

$$\lvert \mathcal{L}(\theta) - \mathcal{L}(\vartheta) \rvert^\alpha \leq c \lvert \mathbb{S}_\mathcal{L}(\vartheta) \rvert \quad (9.320)$$

*(cf. Definition 9.16.1).*
