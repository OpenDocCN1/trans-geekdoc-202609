
### Lemma 1.2.3 (Fully-connected feedforward ANN with one hidden layer)

Let $\mathcal{I}, \mathcal{H} \in \mathbb{N}$, $\theta = (\theta_1, \theta_2, \dots, \theta_{\mathcal{H}\mathcal{I}+2\mathcal{H}+1}) \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{H}\mathcal{I}+2\mathcal{H}+1}$, $x = (x_1, x_2, \dots, x_\mathcal{I}) \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{I}}$ and let $\psi : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a function. Then

$$
\mathcal{N}^{\theta, \mathcal{I}}_{\mathfrak{M}_{\psi, \mathcal{H}, \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{I}}}}(x) = \left[ \sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{H}} \theta_{\mathcal{H}\mathcal{I}+k+k} \psi \left( \sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}} x_i \theta_{(k-1)\mathcal{I}+i} + \theta_{\mathcal{H}\mathcal{I}+k} \right) \right] + \theta_{\mathcal{H}\mathcal{I}+2\mathcal{H}+1}. \tag{1.24}
$$

*(cf. Definitions 1.1.1, 1.1.3, and 1.2.1).*

**Proof of Lemma 1.2.3.** Observe that (1.5) and (1.18) show that

$$
\begin{align}
&\mathcal{N}^{\theta, \mathcal{I}}_{\mathfrak{M}_{\psi, \mathcal{H}, \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{I}}}}(x) \\
&= \left( \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}} \circ \mathcal{A}^{0, \mathcal{H}\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{H}}_{1, \mathcal{H}} \circ \mathfrak{M}_{\psi, \mathcal{H}} \circ \mathcal{A}^{\theta, 0}_{\mathcal{H}, \mathcal{I}} \right)(x) \\
&= \mathcal{A}^{0, \mathcal{H}\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{H}}_{1, \mathcal{H}} \left( \mathfrak{M}_{\psi, \mathcal{H}} \left( \mathcal{A}^{\theta, 0}_{\mathcal{H}, \mathcal{I}}(x) \right) \right) \\
&= \left[ \sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{H}} \theta_{\mathcal{H}\mathcal{I}+k+k} \psi \left( \sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}} x_i \theta_{(k-1)\mathcal{I}+i} + \theta_{\mathcal{H}\mathcal{I}+k} \right) \right] + \theta_{\mathcal{H}\mathcal{I}+2\mathcal{H}+1}. \tag{1.25}
\end{align}
$$

The proof of Lemma 1.2.3 is thus complete. $\Box$

## 1.2.3 Rectified linear unit (ReLU) activation

In this subsection we formulate the ReLU function which is one of the most frequently used activation functions in deep learning applications (cf., for example, LeCun et al. [263]).

**Definition 1.2.4 (ReLU activation function).** We denote by $\mathfrak{r} : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ the function which satisfies for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ that

$$
\mathfrak{r}(x) = \max \{x, 0\} \tag{1.26}
$$

and we call $\mathfrak{r}$ the ReLU activation function (we call $\mathfrak{r}$ the rectifier function).

**Definition 1.2.5 (Multidimensional ReLU activation functions).** Let $d \in \mathbb{N}$. Then we denote by $\Re_d: \mathbb{R}^d \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$ the function given by

$$
\Re_d = \mathfrak{M}_{\epsilon_d} \tag{1.27}
$$

and we call $\Re_d$ the $d$-dimensional ReLU activation function (we call $\Re_d$ the $d$-dimensional rectifier function) (cf. Definitions 1.2.1 and 1.2.4).

**Lemma 1.2.6** (An **ANN** with the **ReLU** activation function as the activation function). Let $W_1 = w_1 = 1$, $W_2 = w_2 = -1$, $b_1 = b_2 = B = 0$. Then it holds for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ that

$$
x = W_1 \max\{w_1 x + b_1, 0\} + W_2 \max\{w_2 x + b_2, 0\} + B. \tag{1.28}
$$

**Proof of Lemma 1.2.6.** Observe that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ it holds that

$$
\begin{split}
W_1&\max\{w_1 x + b_1, 0\} + W_2 \max\{w_2 x + b_2, 0\} + B \\
&= \max\{w_1 x + b_1, 0\} - \max\{w_2 x + b_2, 0\} = \max\{x, 0\} - \max\{-x, 0\} \\
&= \max\{x, 0\} + \min\{x, 0\} = x. \tag{1.29}
\end{split}
$$

The proof of Lemma 1.2.6 is thus complete. $\Box$
