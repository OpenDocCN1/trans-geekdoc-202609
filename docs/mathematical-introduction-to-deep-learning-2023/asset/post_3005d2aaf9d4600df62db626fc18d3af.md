
Hence, we obtain that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $t \in [0, \min \{\tau_{n+1}, n\})$ it holds that

$$\liminf_{s \nearrow \min \{\tau_{n+1}, n\}} \big[\big\| \Theta_{s}^{(n+1)} \big\| + \tfrac{1}{n-s} \big] = \infty \tag{5.163}$$

and

$$\Theta_{t}^{(n+1)} = \xi + \int_{0}^{t} \mathscr{G} \big( \Theta_{s}^{(n+1)} \big) \, \mathrm{d} s. \tag{5.164}$$

Combining this with (5.161) demonstrates that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that

$$\tau_{n} = \min \{\tau_{n+1}, n\} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \Theta^{(n)} = \Theta^{(n+1)} \big|_{[0, \min \{\tau_{n+1}, n\}]}. \tag{5.165}$$

Therefore, we obtain that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that

$$\tau_{n} \leq \tau_{n+1} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \Theta^{(n)} = \Theta^{(n+1)} \big|_{[0, \tau_{n}]}. \tag{5.166}$$

Next let $\mathbf{t} \in (0, \infty]$ be the extended real number given by

$$\mathbf{t} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \tau_{n} \tag{5.167}$$

and let $\Theta \colon [0, \mathbf{t}) \to \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{g}}$ satisfy for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $t \in [0, \tau_{n})$ that

$$\Theta_{t} = \Theta_{t}^{(n)}. \tag{5.168}$$

Observe that for all $t \in [0, \mathbf{t})$ there exists $n \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $t \in [0, \tau_{n})$. This, (5.161), and (5.166) assure that for all $t \in [0, \mathbf{t})$ it holds that $\Theta \in C([0, \mathbf{t}), \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{g}})$ and

$$\Theta_{t} = \xi + \int_{0}^{t} \mathscr{G} ( \Theta_{s} ) \, \mathrm{d} s. \tag{5.169}$$

In addition, note that (5.165) ensures that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $k \in \mathbb{N} \cap [n, \infty)$ it holds that

$$\min \{\tau_{k+1}, n\} = \min \{\tau_{k+1}, k, n\} = \min \{\min \{\tau_{k+1}, k\}, n\} = \min \{\tau_{k}, n\}. \tag{5.170}$$

This shows that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $k \in \mathbb{N} \cap (n, \infty)$ it holds that $\min \{\tau_{k}, n\} = \min \{\tau_{k-1}, n\}$. Hence, we obtain that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $k \in \mathbb{N} \cap (n, \infty)$ it holds that

$$\min \{\tau_{k}, n\} = \min \{\tau_{k-1}, n\} = \dots = \min \{\tau_{n+1}, n\} = \min \{\tau_{n}, n\} = \tau_{n}. \tag{5.171}$$

Combining this with the fact that $(\tau_{n})_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq [0, \infty)$ is a non-decreasing sequence implies that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that

$$\min \{\mathbf{t}, n\} = \min \bigg\{\lim_{k \to \infty} \tau_{k}, n \bigg\} = \lim_{k \to \infty} \big( \min \{\tau_{k}, n\} \big) = \lim_{k \to \infty} \tau_{n} = \tau_{n}. \tag{5.172}$$

Therefore, we obtain that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ with $\mathbf{t} < n$ it holds that

$$\tau_{n} = \min \{\mathbf{t}, n\} = \mathbf{t}. \tag{5.173}$$

This, (5.161), and (5.168) demonstrate that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ with $\mathbf{t} < n$ it holds that

$$
\begin{align*}
\liminf_{s \uparrow \tau_n} \|\Theta_s \| &= \liminf_{s \uparrow \tau_n} \|\Theta_s \| = \liminf_{s \uparrow \tau_n} \| \Theta^{(n)}_s \| \\
&= -\frac{1}{n-\tau_n} + \liminf_{s \uparrow \tau_n} \left[ \left\| \Theta^{(n)}_s \right\| + \frac{1}{n-\tau_n} \right] \\
&= -\frac{1}{n-\tau_n} + \liminf_{s \uparrow \tau_n} \left[ \left\| \Theta^{(n)}_s \right\| + \frac{1}{n-s} \right] = \infty.\tag{5.174}
\end{align*}
$$

Therefore, we obtain that

$$\liminf_{s \uparrow t} \|\hat{\Theta}_s + s \| = \infty.\tag{5.175}$$

Next note that for all $i \in (0, \infty)$, $\hat{\Theta} \in C([0, i], \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta})$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $t \in [0, \min\{\hat{t}, n\})$ with $\liminf_{s \uparrow t} \|\hat{\Theta}_s + s \| = \infty$ and $\forall s \in [0, i)$: $\hat{\Theta}_s = \xi + \int_0^s \mathcal{D}(\hat{\Theta}_u) \, du$ it holds that

$$
\begin{align*}
\liminf_{s \uparrow \min\{\hat{t}, n\}} \left[ \left\| \hat{\Theta}_s \right\| + \frac{1}{n-s} \right] &= \infty \quad \text{and} \quad \hat{\Theta}_t = \xi + \int_0^t \mathcal{D}(\hat{\Theta}_s) \, \mathrm{d}s.\tag{5.176}
\end{align*}
$$

This and (5.161) prove that for all $i \in (0, \infty)$, $\hat{\Theta} \in C([0, i], \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta})$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$ with $\liminf_{s \uparrow t} \|\hat{t}_t + t \| = \infty$ and $\forall t \in [0, i)$: $\hat{\Theta}_t = \xi + \int_0^t \mathcal{D}(\hat{\Theta}_s) \, ds$ it holds that

$$
\begin{align*}
\min\{\hat{t}, n\} &= \tau_n \quad \text{and} \quad \hat{\Theta}_{[0, \tau_n)} = \Theta^{(n)}.\tag{5.177}
\end{align*}
$$

Combining (5.169) and (5.175) hence assures that for all $i \in (0, \infty)$, $\hat{\Theta} \in C([0, i], \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta})$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$ with $\liminf_{s \uparrow t} \|\hat{t}_t + t \| = \infty$ and $\forall t \in [0, i)$: $\hat{\Theta}_t = \xi + \int_0^t \mathcal{D}(\hat{\Theta}_s) \, ds$ it holds that

$$
\begin{align*}
\min\{\hat{t}, n\} = \tau_n &= \min\{\hat{t}, n\} \quad \text{and} \quad \hat{\Theta}_{[0, \tau_n)} = \Theta^{(n)} = \Theta|_{[0, \tau_n)}.\tag{5.178}
\end{align*}
$$

This and (5.167) show that for all $i \in (0, \infty)$, $\hat{\Theta} \in C([0, i], \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta})$ with $\liminf_{s \uparrow t} \|\hat{t}_t + t \| = \infty$ and $\forall t \in [0, i)$: $\hat{\Theta}_t = \xi + \int_0^t \mathcal{D}(\hat{\Theta}_s) \, ds$ it holds that

$$
\begin{align*}
\hat{t} &= t \quad \text{and} \quad \hat{\Theta} = \Theta.\tag{5.179}
\end{align*}
$$

Combining this, (5.169), and (5.175) completes the proof of Lemma 5.7.3.

## 5.7.3 Approximation of local minimum points through GFs revisited

**Theorem 5.7.4** (Approximation of local minimum points through GFs revisited). *Let $\vartheta \in \mathbb{N}$, $c \in (0, \infty)$, $r \in (0, \infty]$, $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta}$, $\mathbb{B} = \{w \in \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta}: \|w - \theta\|_2 \leq r\}$, $\xi \in \mathbb{B}$, $\mathcal{L} \in C^2(\mathbb{R}^{\vartheta}, \mathbb{R})$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{B}$ that*

$$(\theta - \theta, \langle \nabla \mathcal{L} \rangle(\theta)) \geq c \| \theta - \theta \|^2_2\tag{5.180}$$

*(cf. Definitions 1.4.7 and 3.3.4). Then*

- (i) there exists a unique continuous function $\Theta: [0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta}$ such that for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that
  $$\Theta_t = \xi - \int_0^t (\nabla \mathcal{L}) (\Theta_s) \, \mathrm{d}s,$$
  (5.181)
- (ii) it holds that $\{\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta} : \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \inf_{w \in \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta}} \mathcal{L}(w)\} = \{\theta\}$,
- (iii) it holds for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ that $\|\Theta_t - \theta\|_2 \leq e^{-ct} \|\xi - \theta\|_2$, and
- (iv) it holds for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ that
  $$0 \leq \frac{c}{2} \|\Theta_t - \theta\|_2^2 \leq \mathcal{L}(\Theta_t) - \mathcal{L}(\theta).$$
  (5.182)

Proof of Theorem 5.7.4. First, observe that the assumption that $\mathcal{L} \in C^2(\mathbb{R}^{\vartheta}, \mathbb{R})$ ensures that

$$\mathbb{R}^{\vartheta} \ni \theta \mapsto -(\nabla \mathcal{L})(\theta) \in \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta}$$
(5.183)

is continuously differentiable. The fundamental theorem of calculus hence implies that

$$\mathbb{R}^{\vartheta} \ni \theta \mapsto -(\nabla \mathcal{L})(\theta) \in \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta}$$
(5.184)

is locally Lipschitz continuous. Combining this with Lemma 5.7.3 (applied with $\mathfrak{G} \sim (\mathbb{R}^{\vartheta} \ni \theta \mapsto -(\nabla \mathcal{L})(\theta) \in \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta})$ in the notation of Lemma 5.7.3) proves that there exists a unique extended real number $\tau \in (0, \infty]$ and a unique continuous function $\Theta: [0, \tau) \to \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta}$ such that for all $t \in [0, \tau)$ it holds that

$$\liminf_{s \uparrow \tau} \|\Theta_s \|_2 + s] = \infty \quad\text{and}\quad \Theta_t = \xi - \int_0^t (\nabla \mathcal{L}) (\Theta_s) \, \mathrm{d}s.$$
(5.185)

Next observe that Proposition 5.7.1 proves that for all $t \in [0, \tau)$ it holds that

$$\|\Theta_t - \theta\|_2 \leq e^{-ct} \|\xi - \theta\|_2.$$
(5.186)

This implies that

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\liminf_{s \uparrow \tau} \|\Theta_s \|_2 \leq \left[\liminf_{s \uparrow \tau} \|\Theta_s - \theta\|_2\right] + \|\theta\|_2 \\
&\quad \leq \left[\liminf_{s \uparrow \tau} e^{-cs} \|\xi - \theta\|_2\right] + \|\theta\|_2 \leq \|\xi - \theta\|_2 + \|\theta\|_2 < \infty .
\end{aligned}
$$
(5.187)

This and (5.185) demonstrate that

$$\tau = \infty.$$
(5.188)

This and (5.185) prove item (i). Moreover, note that Proposition 5.7.1 and item (i) establish items (ii), (iii), and (iv). The proof of Theorem 5.7.4 is thus complete. $\square$
