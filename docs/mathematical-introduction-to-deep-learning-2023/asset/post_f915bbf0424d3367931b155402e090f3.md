## Chapter 8

## Backpropagation

In Chapters 6 and 7 we reviewed common deterministic and stochastic GD-type optimization methods used for the training of ANNs. The specific implementation of such methods requires efficient explicit computations of gradients. The most popular and somehow most natural method to explicitly compute such gradients in the case of the training of ANNs is the backpropagation method. In this chapter we derive and present this method in detail.

Further material on the backpropagation method can, for example, be found in the books and overview articles [176], [4, Section 11.7], [60, Section 6.2.3], [63, Section 3.2.3], [97, Section 5.6], and [373, Section 20.6].

### 8.1 Backpropagation for parametric functions

**Proposition 8.1.1** (Backpropagation for parametric functions). Let $L \in \mathbb{N}, l_0, l_1, \ldots, l_L, \mathfrak{d}_1$, $\mathfrak{d}_2, \ldots, \mathfrak{d}_L \in \mathbb{N}$, *for every* $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ *let* $F_k = (F_k(\theta_k, x_{k-1}))_{(\theta_k, x_{k-1}) \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_k} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}}: \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_k} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_k}$ *be differentiable*, *for every* $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ *let* $f_k = (f_k(\theta_k, \theta_{k+1}, \ldots, \theta_L$, $x_{k-1}))_{(\theta_k, \theta_{k+1}, \ldots, \theta_L, x_{k-1}) \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_k} \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_{k+1}} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_L} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}}: \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_k} \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_{k+1}} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_L} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_L}$ *satisfy for all* $\vartheta = (\theta_1, \theta_{k+1}, \ldots, \theta_L) \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_1} \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_2} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_L}, x_{k-1} \in \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}}$ *that*

$$ f_k(\theta, x_{k-1}) = (F_L(\theta_L, \cdot) \circ F_{L-1}(\theta_{L-1}, \cdot) \circ \cdots \circ F_k(\theta_k, \cdot))(x_{k-1}), \quad (8.1) $$

*let* $\vartheta = (\vartheta_1, \vartheta_2, \ldots, \vartheta_L) \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_1} \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_2} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_L}, \mathfrak{f}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_0}$, $\mathfrak{f}_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_1}, \ldots, \mathfrak{f}_L \in \mathbb{R}^{l_L}$ *satisfy for all* $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ *that*

$$ \mathfrak{f}_k = F_k(\vartheta_k, \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}), \quad (8.2) $$

*and let* $D_k \in \mathbb{R}^{l_k \times l_{k-1}}$, $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L+1\}$, *satisfy for all* $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ *that* $D_{L+1} = I_{l_L}$ *and*

$$ D_k = D_{k+1} \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial F_k}{\partial x_{k-1}} (\vartheta_k, \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}) \end{bmatrix}, \quad (8.3) $$

(cf. Definition 1.5.5). Then

-   (i) it holds for all $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ that $f_k \colon \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta_k} \times \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta_{k+1}} \times \ldots \times \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta_L} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{l_L}$ is differentiable,

-   (ii) it holds for all $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ that

    $$ D_k = \begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{\partial f_k}{\partial x_{k-1}} \end{pmatrix} \left( (\vartheta_k, \vartheta_{k+1}, \ldots, \vartheta_L, \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}) \right), \quad (8.4) $$

    and

-   (iii) it holds for all $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ that

    $$ \begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{\partial f_1}{\partial \vartheta_k} \end{pmatrix} (\vartheta, \mathfrak{f}_0) = D_{k+1} \left[ \begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{\partial F_k}{\partial \vartheta_k} \end{pmatrix} (\vartheta_k, \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}) \right]. \quad (8.5) $$

Proof of Proposition 8.1.1. Note that (8.1), the fact that for all $k \in \mathbb{N} \cap (0, L)$, $(\vartheta_k, \vartheta_{k+1}, \ldots, \vartheta_L) \in \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta_k} \times \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta_{k+1}} \times \ldots \times \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta_L}$, $x_{k-1} \in \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}}$ it holds that

$$ f_k((\vartheta_k, \vartheta_{k+1}, \ldots, \vartheta_L), x_{k-1}) = (f_{k+1}((\vartheta_{k+1}, \vartheta_{k+2}, \ldots, \vartheta_L), \circ F_k(\vartheta_k, \cdot)))(x_{k-1}), \quad (8.6) $$

the assumption that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ it holds that $F_k \colon \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta_k} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{l_k}$ is differentiable, Lemma 5.3.2, and induction imply that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ it holds that

$$ f_k \colon \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta_k} \times \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta_{k+1}} \times \ldots \times \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta_L} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{l_L} \quad (8.7) $$

is differentiable. This proves item (i). Next we prove (8.4) by induction on $k \in \{L, L-1, \ldots, 1\}$. Note that (8.3), the assumption that $D_{L+1} = \mathbf{I}_{l_L}$, and the fact that $f_L = F_L$ assure that

$$ D_L = D_{L+1} \left[ \begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{\partial F_L}{\partial x_{L-1}} \end{pmatrix} (\vartheta_L, \mathfrak{f}_{L-1}) \right] = \begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{\partial f_L}{\partial x_{L-1}} \end{pmatrix} (\vartheta_L, \mathfrak{f}_{L-1}). \quad (8.8) $$

This establishes (8.4) in the base case $k = L$. For the induction step note that (8.3), the chain rule, and the fact that for all $k \in \mathbb{N} \cap (0, L)$, $x_{k-1} \in \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}}$ it holds that

$$ f_k((\vartheta_k, \vartheta_{k+1}, \ldots, \vartheta_L), x_{k-1}) = f_{k+1}((\vartheta_{k+1}, \vartheta_{k+2}, \ldots, \vartheta_L), F_k(\vartheta_k, x_{k-1})) \quad (8.9) $$

imply that for all $k \in \mathbb{N} \cap (0, L)$ with $D_{k+1} = \left( \frac{\partial f_{k+1}}{\partial x_k} \right)( (\vartheta_{k+1}, \vartheta_{k+2}, \ldots, \vartheta_L), \mathfrak{f}_k )$ it holds that

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\left( \frac{\partial f_k}{\partial x_{k-1}} \right)( (\vartheta_k, \vartheta_{k+1}, \ldots, \vartheta_L), \mathfrak{f}_{k-1} ) \\
&= \left( \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}} \ni x_{k-1} \mapsto f_k ( (\vartheta_k, \vartheta_{k+1}, \ldots, \vartheta_L), x_{k-1} ) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_k} \right) ^{\prime} ( \mathfrak{f}_{k-1} ) \\
&= \left( \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}} \ni x_{k-1} \mapsto f_{k+1} ( (\vartheta_{k+1}, \vartheta_{k+2}, \ldots, \vartheta_L), F_k(\vartheta_k, x_{k-1}) ) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_{k+1}} \right) ^{\prime} ( \mathfrak{f}_{k-1} ) \\
&= \left[ \left( \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}} \ni x_{k-1} \mapsto f_{k+1} ( (\vartheta_{k+1}, \vartheta_{k+2}, \ldots, \vartheta_L), x_k ) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_{k+1}} \right) ^{\prime} ( F_k(\vartheta_k, \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}) ) \right] \\
&= \left[ \left( \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}} \ni x_{k-1} \mapsto F_k(\vartheta_k, x_{k-1}) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_k} \right) ^{\prime} ( \mathfrak{f}_{k-1} ) \right] \\
&= \left[ \left( \frac{\partial f_{k+1}}{\partial x_k} \right)( (\vartheta_{k+1}, \vartheta_{k+2}, \ldots, \vartheta_L), \mathfrak{f}_k ) \right] \left[ \left( \frac{\partial F_k}{\partial x_{k-1}} \right)(\vartheta_k, \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}) \right] \\
&= D_{k+1} \left[ \left( \frac{\partial F_k}{\partial x_{k-1}} \right)(\vartheta_k, \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}) \right] = D_k .
\end{aligned}
$$

Induction thus proves **(8.4)**. This establishes item **(ii)**. Moreover, observe that **(8.1)** and **(8.2)** assure that for all $k \in \mathbb{N} \cap (0, L)$, $\theta_k \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_k}$ it holds that

$$
\begin{aligned}
&f_1 ( (\vartheta_1, \ldots, \vartheta_{k-1}, \theta_k, \vartheta_{k+1}, \ldots, \vartheta_L), \mathfrak{f}_0 ) \\
&= ( F_L(\vartheta_L, \cdot) \circ \ldots \circ F_{k+1}(\vartheta_{k+1}, \cdot) \circ F_k(\theta_k, \cdot) \circ F_{k-1}(\vartheta_{k-1}, \cdot) \circ \ldots \circ F_1(\vartheta_1, \cdot) )(\mathfrak{f}_0) \\
&= ( f_{k+1} ( (\vartheta_{k+1}, \vartheta_{k+2}, \ldots, \vartheta_L), F_k(\theta_k, \cdot) ) ) ( (F_{k-1}(\vartheta_{k-1}, \cdot) \circ \ldots \circ F_1(\vartheta_1, \cdot) )(\mathfrak{f}_0) ) \\
&= f_{k+1} ( (\vartheta_{k+1}, \vartheta_{k+2}, \ldots, \vartheta_L), F_k(\theta_k, \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}) ).
\end{aligned}
$$

Combining this with the chain rule, **(8.2)**, and **(8.4)** demonstrates that for all $k \in \mathbb{N} \cap (0, L)$ it holds that

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\left( \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial \theta_k} \right)(\vartheta, \mathfrak{f}_0) = \left[ \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_k} \ni \theta_k \mapsto f_{k+1} ( (\vartheta_{k+1}, \vartheta_{k+2}, \ldots, \vartheta_L), F_k(\theta_k, \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}) ) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_{k+1}} \right]^{\prime}(\vartheta_k) \\
&= \left[ \left( \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_k} \ni \theta_k \mapsto f_{k+1} ( (\vartheta_{k+1}, \vartheta_{k+2}, \ldots, \vartheta_L), x_k ) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_{k+1}} \right) ^{\prime} ( F_k(\theta_k, \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}) ) \right] \\
&\left[ \left( \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_k} \ni \theta_k \mapsto F_k(\theta_k, \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_k} \right) ^{\prime} ( \vartheta_k ) \right] \\
&= \left[ \left( \frac{\partial f_{k+1}}{\partial x_k} \right)( (\vartheta_{k+1}, \vartheta_{k+2}, \ldots, \vartheta_L), \mathfrak{f}_k ) \right] \left[ \left( \frac{\partial F_k}{\partial \theta_k} \right)(\vartheta_k, \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}) \right] \\
&= D_{k+1} \left[ \left( \frac{\partial F_k}{\partial \theta_k} \right)(\vartheta_k, \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}) \right] .
\end{aligned}
$$
