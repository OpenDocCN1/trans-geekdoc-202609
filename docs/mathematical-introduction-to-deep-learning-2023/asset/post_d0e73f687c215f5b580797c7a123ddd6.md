
Proof of Corollary 6.1.13. Note that item (ii) in Proposition 6.1.9 and the assumption that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that $\gamma_n \in \llbracket 0, \frac{c}{L^2} \rrbracket$ ensure that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that

$$0 \leq 1 - 2c\gamma_n + (\gamma_n)^2L^2 \leq 1 - 2c\gamma_n + \gamma_n \left[ \frac{c}{L^2} \right] L^2 = 1 - 2c\gamma_n + \gamma_n c = 1 - c\gamma_n \leq 1. \tag{6.101}$$

This proves item (ii). Moreover, note that (6.101) and Proposition 6.1.9 establish items (i), (iii), and (iv). The proof of Corollary 6.1.13 is thus complete. $\square$

In the next result, Corollary 6.1.14 below, we, roughly speaking, specialize Corollary 6.1.13 above to the case where the learning rates $(\gamma_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq \llbracket 0, \frac{c}{L^2} \rrbracket$ are a constant sequence.

Corollary 6.1.14 (Error estimates in the case of small and constant learning rates). *Let* $\emptyset \in \mathbb{N}$, $c$, $L \in (0, \infty)$, $r \in (0, \infty)$, $\gamma \in (0, \frac{c}{L^2}]$, $\vartheta \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{D}$, $\mathbb{B} = \left\{ w \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{D} : \|w - \vartheta\|_2 \leq r \right\}$, $\xi \in \mathbb{B}$, $\mathcal{L} \in C^1(\mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{D}, \mathbb{R})$ *satisfy for all* $\theta \in \mathbb{B}$ *that*

$$(\theta - \vartheta, \langle \nabla \mathcal{L} \rangle(\theta)) \geq c \|\theta - \vartheta\|_2^2 \quad \text{and} \quad \|\langle \nabla \mathcal{L} \rangle(\theta)\|_2 \leq L \|\theta - \vartheta\|_2, \tag{6.102}$$

and let $\Theta: \mathbb{N}_0 \to \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{D}$ *satisfy for all* $n \in \mathbb{N}$ *that*

$$\Theta_0 = \xi \quad \text{and} \quad \Theta_n = \Theta_{n - 1} - \gamma \langle \nabla \mathcal{L} \rangle(\Theta_{n - 1}) \tag{6.103}$$

*(cf. Definitions 1.4.7 and 3.3.4). Then*

- (i) it holds that $\left\{ \theta \in \mathbb{B} : \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \inf_{w \in \mathbb{B}} \mathcal{L}(w) \right\} = \left\{ \vartheta \right\}$,
- (ii) it holds that $0 \leq 1 - c\gamma < 1$,
- (iii) it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ that $\|\Theta_n - \vartheta\|_2 \leq (1 - c\gamma)^{n/2}\|\xi - \vartheta\|_2$, and
- (iv) it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ that $0 \leq \mathcal{L}(\Theta_n) - \mathcal{L}(\vartheta) \leq \frac{L}{2}(1 - c\gamma)^n \|\xi - \vartheta\|_2^2.$

Proof of Corollary 6.1.14. Corollary 6.1.14 is an immediate consequence of Corollary 6.1.13. The proof of Corollary 6.1.14 is thus complete. $\square$

### 6.1.4.5 On the spectrum of the Hessian of the objective function at a local minimum point

A crucial ingredient in our error analysis for the GD optimization method in Sections 6.1.4.1, 6.1.4.2, 6.1.4.3, and 6.1.4.4 above is to employ the growth and the coercivity-type hypotheses, for instance, in (6.47) in Proposition 6.1.9 above. In this subsection we disclose in Proposition 6.1.16 below suitable conditions on the Hessians of the objective function of the considered optimization problem which are sufficient to ensure that (6.47) is satisfied so that we are in the position to apply the error analysis in Sections 6.1.4.1, 6.1.4.2, 6.1.4.3, and 6.1.4.4 above (cf. Corollary 6.1.17 below). Our proof of Proposition 6.1.16 employs the following classical result (see Lemma 6.1.15 below) for symmetric matrices with real entries.

**Lemma 6.1.15** (Properties of the spectrum of real symmetric matrices). Let $\mathfrak{n} \in \mathbb{N}$, let $A \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{n} \times \mathfrak{n}}$ be a symmetric matrix, and let

$$\mathcal{S}=\left\{\lambda \in \mathbb{C}: (\exists v \in \mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{n}} \backslash\{0\}: A v=\lambda v)\right\}. \tag{6.104}$$

Then

- (i) it holds that $\mathcal{S}=\left\{\lambda \in \mathbb{R}: (\exists v \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{n}} \backslash\{0\}: A v=\lambda v)\right\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$,
- (ii) it holds that

$$\sup _{v \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{n}} \backslash\{0\}}\left[\frac{\|A v\|_{2}}{\|v\|_{2}}\right]=\max _{\lambda \in \mathcal{S}}|\lambda|, \tag{6.105}$$

and
- (iii) it holds for all $v \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{n}}$ that

$$\min (\mathcal{S})\|v\|_{2}^{2} \leq\langle v, A v\rangle \leq \max (\mathcal{S})\|v\|_{2}^{2} \tag{6.106}$$

(cf. Definitions 1.4.7 and 3.3.4).

Proof of Lemma 6.1.15. Throughout this proof, let $e_{1}, e_{2}, \ldots, e_{\mathfrak{n}} \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{n}}$ be the vectors given by

$$e_{1}=(1,0, \ldots, 0), \quad e_{2}=(0,1,0, \ldots, 0), \quad \ldots, \quad e_{\mathfrak{n}}=(0, \ldots, 0,1). \tag{6.107}$$

Observe that the spectral theorem for symmetric matrices (see, for example, Petersen [331, Theorem 4.3.4]) proves that there exist $(\mathfrak{n} \times \mathfrak{n})$-matrices $\Lambda=\left(\Lambda_{i, j}\right)_{(i, j) \in\{1,2, \ldots, \mathfrak{n}\}^{2}}, O=\left(O_{i, j}\right)_{(i, j) \in\{1,2, \ldots, \mathfrak{n}\}^{2}} \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{n} \times \mathfrak{n}}$ such that $\mathcal{S}=\left\{\Lambda_{1,1}, \Lambda_{2,2}, \ldots, \Lambda_{\mathfrak{n}, \mathfrak{n}}\right\}, O^{*} O=O O^{*}=\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{n}}, A=O \Lambda O^{*}$, and

$$\Lambda=\left(\begin{array}{ccc} \Lambda_{1,1} & 0 & \\ & \ddots & \\ 0 & & \Lambda_{\mathfrak{n}, \mathfrak{n}} \end{array}\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{n} \times \mathfrak{n}} \tag{6.108}$$

(cf. Definition 1.5.5). Hence, we obtain that $\mathcal{S} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$. Next note that the assumption that $\mathcal{S}=\left\{\lambda \in \mathbb{C}:(\exists v \in \mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{n}} \backslash\{0\}: A v=\lambda v)\right\}$ ensures that for every $\lambda \in \mathcal{S}$ there exists $v \in \mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{n}} \backslash\{0\}$ such that

$$A \Re(e v)+\mathrm{i} A \Im(e v)=A v=\lambda v=\lambda \Re(e v)+\mathrm{i} \lambda \Im(e v). \tag{6.109}$$

The fact that $\mathcal{S} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ therefore demonstrates that for every $\lambda \in \mathcal{S}$ there exists $v \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{n}} \backslash\{0\}$ such that $A v=\lambda v$. This and the fact that $\mathcal{S} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ ensure that $\mathcal{S} \subseteq\left\{\lambda \in \mathbb{R}:(\exists v \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{n}} \backslash\{0\}: A v=\lambda v)\right\}$. Combining this and the fact that $\left\{\lambda \in \mathbb{R}:(\exists v \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{n}} \backslash\{0\}: A v=\lambda v)\right\} \subseteq \mathcal{S}$ proves item **(i)**. Furthermore, note that **(6.108)** assures that for all $v = (v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_\mathfrak{n}) \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{n}$ it holds that

$$\begin{aligned} \|\Lambda v\|_2 = \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{\mathfrak{n}} |\Lambda_{i,i}v_i|^2 \right]^{1/2} &\leq \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{\mathfrak{n}} \max\left\{ |\Lambda_{1,1}|^2, \ldots, |\Lambda_{\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}}|^2 \right\} |v_i|^2 \right]^{1/2} \\ &= \left[ \max\left\{ |\Lambda_{1,1}|, \ldots, |\Lambda_{\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}}| \right\}^2 \|v\|_2^2 \right]^{1/2} \tag{6.110} \\ &= \max\left\{ |\Lambda_{1,1}|, \ldots, |\Lambda_{\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}}| \right\} \|v\|_2 \\ &= \left( \max_{\lambda \in \mathcal{S}} |\lambda| \right) \|v\|_2 \end{aligned}$$

(cf. Definition **3.3.4**). The fact that $O$ is an orthogonal matrix and the fact that $A = O \Lambda O^*$ therefore imply that for all $v \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{n}$ it holds that

$$\begin{aligned} \|Av\|_2 = \|O \Lambda O^*v\|_2 &= \|\Lambda O^*v\|_2 \\ &\leq \left( \max_{\lambda \in \mathcal{S}} |\lambda| \right) \|O^*v\|_2 \tag{6.111} \\ &= \left( \max_{\lambda \in \mathcal{S}} |\lambda| \right) \|v\|_2. \end{aligned}$$

This implies that

$$\sup_{v \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{n} \setminus \{0\}} \left[ \frac{\|Av\|_2}{\|v\|_2} \right] \leq \sup_{v \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{n} \setminus \{0\}} \left[ \frac{\left( \max_{\lambda \in \mathcal{S}} |\lambda| \right) \|v\|_2}{\|v\|_2} \right] = \max_{\lambda \in \mathcal{S}} |\lambda|. \tag{6.112}$$

In addition, note that the fact that $\mathcal{S} = \left\{ \Lambda_{1,1}, \Lambda_{2,2}, \ldots, \Lambda_{\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}} \right\}$ ensures that there exists $j \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathfrak{n}\}$ such that

$$|\Lambda_{j,j}| = \max_{\lambda \in \mathcal{S}} |\lambda|. \tag{6.113}$$

Next observe that the fact that $A = O \Lambda O^*$, the fact that $O$ is an orthogonal matrix, and **(6.113)** imply that

$$\begin{aligned} \sup_{v \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{n} \setminus \{0\}} \left[ \frac{\|Av\|_2}{\|v\|_2} \right] &\geq \left[ \frac{\|AOe_j\|_2}{\|Oe_j\|_2} \right] = \|O \Lambda O^* O e_j\|_2 = \|O \Lambda e_j\|_2 \tag{6.114} \\ &= \|\Lambda e_j\|_2 = \|\Lambda_{j,j} e_j\|_2 = |\Lambda_{j,j}| = \max_{\lambda \in \mathcal{S}} |\lambda|. \end{aligned}$$

Combining this and **(6.112)** establishes item **(ii)**. It thus remains to prove item **(iii)**. For this note that **(6.108)** ensures that for all $v = (v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_\mathfrak{n}) \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{n}$ it holds that

$$\begin{aligned} \langle v, \Lambda v \rangle &= \sum_{i=1}^{\mathfrak{n}} \Lambda_{i,i} |v_i|^2 \leq \sum_{i=1}^{\mathfrak{n}} \max\{\Lambda_{1,1}, \ldots, \Lambda_{\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}}\} |v_i|^2 \tag{6.115} \\ &= \max\{\Lambda_{1,1}, \ldots, \Lambda_{\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}}\} \|v\|_2^2 = \max(\mathcal{S}) \|v\|_2^2 \end{aligned}$$
