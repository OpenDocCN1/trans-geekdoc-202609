
The proof of Proposition 14.2.1 is thus complete. □

## Chapter 15
### Composed error estimates

In Part **II** we have established several estimates for the approximation error, in Part **III** we have established several estimates for the optimization error, and in Part **IV** we have established several estimates for the generalization error. In this chapter we employ the error decomposition from Chapter **14** as well as parts of Parts **II, III**, and **IV** (see Proposition **4.4.12** and Corollaries **11.3.9** and **13.3.3**) to establish estimates for the overall error in the training of **ANNs** in the specific situation of **GD**-type optimization methods with many independent random initializations.

In the literature such overall error analyses can, for instance, be found in [25, 226, 230]. The material in this chapter consists of slightly modified extracts from Jentzen & Welti [230, Sections 6.2 and 6.3].

### 15.1 Full strong error analysis for the training of ANNs

**Lemma 15.1.1.** *Let* $d, \mathbf{d}, \mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{N}$, $\mathbf{l} = (\mathbf{l}_0, \mathbf{l}_1, \dots, \mathbf{l}_{\mathbf{L}}) \in \mathbb{N}^{\mathbf{L}+1}$, $u \in [-\infty, \infty)$, $v \in (u, \infty]$, *let* $D \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$, *assume*

$$
\mathbf{l}_0 = d, \qquad \mathbf{l}_{\mathbf{L}} = 1, \qquad \text{and} \qquad \mathbf{d} \ge \sum_{i=1}^{\mathbf{L}} \mathbf{l}_i (\mathbf{l}_{i-1} + 1), \tag{15.1}
$$

*let* $\mathcal{E} \colon D \to \mathbb{R}$ *be* $\mathcal{B}(D) / \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$-measurable, *let* $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ *be a probability space*, *and let* $X \colon \Omega \to D$, $\mathbf{k} \colon \Omega \to (\mathbb{N}_0)^2$, *and* $\Theta_{k,n} \colon \Omega \to \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$, $k, n \in \mathbb{N}_0$, *be random variables. Then*

- (i) *it holds that* $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}} \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}} \ni (\theta, x) \mapsto \mathcal{N}_{u,v}^{\Theta, \mathbf{l}}(x) \in \mathbb{R}$ *is* $(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}) \otimes \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})) / \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$-measurable,

- (ii) *it holds for all* $\omega \in \Omega$ *that* $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}} \ni x \mapsto \mathcal{N}_{u,v}^{\Theta_{k,n}(\omega), \mathbf{l}}(x) \in \mathbb{R}$ *is* $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}) / \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$-measurable, *and*

- (iii) *it holds for all* $p \in [0, \infty)$ *that*

$$
\Omega \ni \omega \mapsto \int_D |\mathcal{N}_{u,v}^{\Theta_{k,n}(\omega), \mathbf{l}}(x) - \mathcal{E}(x)|^p \, \mathbb{P}_X(\mathrm{d}x) \in [0, \infty[ \tag{15.2}
$$

*is $\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{B}([0, \infty])$-measurable*

*(cf. Definition 4.4.1).*

*Proof of Lemma 15.1.1*. Throughout this proof let $\Xi: \Omega \to \mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d}$ satisfy for all $\omega \in \Omega$ that

$$
\Xi(\omega) = \Theta_{\mathbf{k}(\omega)}(\omega).
$$

Observe that the assumption that $\Theta_{k,n}: \Omega \to \mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d}$, $k, n \in \mathbb{N}_0$, and $\mathbf{k}: \Omega \to (\mathbb{N}_0)^2$ are random variables implies that for all $U \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d})$ it holds that

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Xi^{-1}(U) = \{\omega \in \Omega: \Xi(\omega) \in U\} = \{\omega \in \Omega: \Theta_{\mathbf{k}(\omega)}(\omega) \in U\} \\
&\quad= \{\omega \in \Omega: [\exists k, n \in \mathbb{N}_0: (\Theta_{k,n}(\omega) \in U \wedge \mathbf{k}(\omega) = (k, n))]\} \\
&\quad= \bigcup_{k=0}^\infty \bigcup_{n=0}^\infty \{\omega \in \Omega: \Theta_{k,n}(\omega) \in U\} \cap \{\omega \in \Omega: \mathbf{k}(\omega) = (k, n)\} \\
&\quad= \bigcup_{k=0}^\infty \bigcup_{n=0}^\infty ([ (\Theta_{k,n})^{-1}(U)] \cap [\mathbf{k}^{-1}(\{(k, n)\})]) \in \mathcal{F}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This proves that

$$
\Omega \ni \omega \mapsto \Theta_{\mathbf{k}(\omega)}(\omega) \in \mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d}
$$

is $\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d})$-measurable. Furthermore, note that Corollary 11.3.7 (applied with $a \curvearrowleft \|x\|_\infty$, $b \curvearrowleft \|x\|_\infty$, $u \curvearrowleft u$, $v \curvearrowleft v$, $d \curvearrowleft \mathbf{d}$, $L \curvearrowleft \mathbf{L}$, $l \curvearrowleft 1$ for $x \in \mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d}$ in the notation of Corollary 11.3.7) ensures that for all $\theta, \vartheta \in \mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d}$ it holds that

$$
\begin{aligned}
&|\mathcal{N}_{u,v}^{\theta,1}(x) - \mathcal{N}_{u,v}^{\vartheta,1}(x)| \le \sup_{y \in [-\|x\|_\infty, \|x\|_\infty]} |0| |\mathcal{N}_{u,v}^{\theta,1}(y) - \mathcal{N}_{u,v}^{\vartheta,1}(y)| \\
&\le \mathbf{L}\max\{1, \|x\|_\infty\} (\|\|\|_\infty + 1)\mathbf{L}\left(\max\{1, \|\theta\|_\infty, \|\vartheta\|_\infty\} \mathbf{L}^{-1}\|\theta - \vartheta\|_\infty\right)
\end{aligned}
$$

(cf. Definitions 3.3.4 and 4.4.1). This shows for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d}$ that

$$
\mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d} \ni \theta \mapsto \mathcal{N}_{u,v}^{\theta,1}(x) \in \mathbb{R}
$$

is continuous. Moreover, observe that the fact that for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d}$ it holds that $\mathcal{N}_{u,v}^{\theta,1} \in C(\mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d}, \mathbb{R})$ establishes that for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d}$ it holds that $\mathcal{N}_{u,v}^{\theta,1}(x) \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d})/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$-measurable. This, (15.7), the fact that $(\mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d}, \|\cdot\|_\infty|_{\mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d}})$ is a separable normed $\mathbb{R}$-vector space, and Lemma 11.2.6 prove item (i). Note that item (i) and (15.5) demonstrate that

$$
\Omega \times \mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d} \ni (\omega, x) \mapsto \mathcal{N}_{u,v}^{\Theta_{\mathbf{k}(\omega)}(\omega),1}(x) \in \mathbb{R}
$$

is $(\mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d}))/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$-measurable. This implies item (ii). Observe that item (ii) and the assumption that $\mathcal{E}: D \to \mathbb{R}$ is $\mathcal{B}(D)/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$-measurable ensure that for all $p \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that

$$
\Omega \times D \ni (\omega, x) \mapsto (\mathcal{N}_{u,v}^{\Theta_{\mathbf{k}(\omega)}(\omega),1}(x) - \mathcal{E}(x))^p \in [0, \infty)
$$

is $(\mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{B}(D))/\mathcal{B}([0, \infty))$-measurable. Tonelli's theorem hence establishes item (iii). The proof of Lemma 15.1.1 is thus complete. \qquad \qquad \qquad \square

$$

$$
