*(iii)* it holds for all $n \in \{1, 2, \dots, \mathcal{L}(\Phi)\}$ that

$\mathcal{W}_{n,\Phi} \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{D}_{n}(\Phi) \times \mathbb{D}_{n-1}(\Phi)} \quad \text{ and } \quad \mathcal{B}_{n,\Phi} \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{D}_{n}(\Phi)}.$

*Proof of Lemma 1.3.3.* Note that the assumption that

$\Phi \in \mathbf{N} = \bigcup_{L \in \mathbf{N}} (\cup_{(l_{0}, l_{1}, \dots, l_{L}) \in \mathbf{N}^{L+1}} (\textbf{\bigotimes}_{k=1}^{L} (\mathbb{R}^{l_{k} \times l_{k-1}} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_{k}})))$

ensures that there exist $L \in \mathbb{N}$, $l_{0}, l_{1}, \dots, l_{L} \in \mathbb{N}$ which satisfy that

$\Phi \in \textbf{\bigotimes}_{k=1}^{L} (\mathbb{R}^{l_{k} \times l_{k-1}} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_{k}}).$

Observe that (1.85), (1.78), and (1.79) imply that

$\mathcal{L}(\Phi) = L, \quad \mathcal{I}(\Phi) = l_{0} = \mathbb{D}_{0}(\Phi), \quad \text{ and } \quad \mathcal{O}(\Phi) = l_{L} = \mathbb{D}_{L}(\Phi).$

This shows that

$\mathcal{D}(\Phi) = (l_{0}, l_{1}, \dots, l_{L}) \in \mathbf{N}^{L+1} = \mathbf{N}^{\mathcal{L}(\Phi)+1}.$

Next note that (1.85), (1.79), and (1.81) ensure that for all $n \in \{1, 2, \dots, \mathcal{L}(\Phi)\}$ it holds that

$\mathcal{W}_{n,\Phi} \in \mathbb{R}^{l_{n} \times l_{n-1}} = \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{D}_{n}(\Phi) \times \mathbb{D}_{n-1}(\Phi)} \quad \text{ and } \quad \mathcal{B}_{n,\Phi} \in \mathbb{R}^{l_{n}} = \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{D}_{n}(\Phi)}.$

The proof of Lemma 1.3.3 is thus complete.
