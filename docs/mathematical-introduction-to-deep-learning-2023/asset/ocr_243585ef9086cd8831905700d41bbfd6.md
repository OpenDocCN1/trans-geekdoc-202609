*Proof of Corollary 6.1.5.* Throughout this proof, let $\mathcal{G}: \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d} \to \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$ that

$$\mathcal{G}(\theta) = - (\nabla\mathcal{L})(\theta).$$ (6.20)

Note that the fact that for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that $\Theta_t = \Theta_0 + \int_0^t \mathcal{G}(\Theta_s) \, \mathrm{d}s$, the fact that $\theta = \Theta_T + \gamma \mathcal{G}(\Theta_T)$, the fact that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$ it holds that $\|\mathcal{G}(x)\|_2 \leq c$, the fact that for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$ it holds that $\|\mathcal{G}'(x) y\|_2 \leq c \|\mathcal{G}(y)\|_2$, and Lemma 6.1.4 imply that $\|\Theta_{T+\gamma} - \theta\|_2 \leq c^2 \gamma^2$. The proof of Corollary 6.1.5 is thus complete.

# **6.1.3 Lyapunov-type stability for GD optimization**

Corollary 5.6.3 in Section 5.6.2 and Corollary 5.6.5 in Section 5.6.3 in Chapter 5 above, in particular, illustrate how Lyapunov-type functions can be employed to establish convergence properties for GFBs. Roughly speaking, the next two results, Proposition 6.1.6 and Corollary 6.1.7 below, are the time-discrete analogs of Corollary 5.6.3 and Corollary 5.6.5, respectively.

**Proposition 6.1.6** (*Lyapunov-type stability for discrete-time dynamical systems*). Let $\mathfrak{n} \in \mathbb{N}$, $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$, $c \in (0, \infty)$, $(\gamma_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subset [0, c]$, let $V\colon \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d} \to \mathbb{R}$, $\Phi\colon \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d} \times [0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$, and $\varepsilon\colon [0, c] \to [0, \infty)$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$, $t \in [0, c]$ that

$$V(\Phi(\theta, t)) \leq \varepsilon(t) V(\theta),$$ (6.21)

and let $\Theta\colon \mathbb{N}_0 \to \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$ satisfy for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ that

$$\Theta_0 = \xi \quad \text{and} \quad \Theta_n = \Phi(\Theta_{n-1}, \gamma_n).$$ (6.22)

Then it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ that

$$V(\Theta_n) \leq \biggl[\prod_{k=1}^{n} \varepsilon(\gamma_k)\biggr] V(\xi).$$ (6.23)

*Proof of Proposition 6.1.6.* We prove (6.23) by induction on $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$. For the base case $n=0$ note that the assumption that $\Theta_0 = \xi$ ensures that $V(\Theta_0) = V(\xi)$. This establishes (6.23) in the base case $n=0$. For the induction step observe that (6.22) and (6.21) ensure that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ with $V(\Theta_n) \leq (\prod_{k=1}^{n} \varepsilon(\gamma_k)) V(\xi)$ it holds that

$$egin{align*} V(\Theta_{n+1}) &= V(\Phi(\Theta_n, \gamma_{n+1})) \leq \varepsilon(\gamma_{n+1}) V(\Theta_n) \\ &\leq \varepsilon(\gamma_{n+1}) \biggl[\prod_{k=1}^{n} \varepsilon(\gamma_k)\biggr] V(\xi) = \biggl[\prod_{k=1}^{n+1} \varepsilon(\gamma_k)\biggr] V(\xi). \end{align*}$ (6.24)

Induction thus establishes (6.23). The proof of Proposition 6.1.6 is thus complete.
