
and let $\pmb{\mathfrak{L}}: C^{1,2}([0,T] \times \mathbb{R}^{d}, \mathbb{R}) \rightarrow [0, \infty)$ satisfy for all $v = (v(t,x))_{(t,x) \in [0,T] \times \mathbb{R}^{d}} \in C^{1,2}([0,T] \times \mathbb{R}^{d}, \mathbb{R})$ that

$$\pmb{\mathfrak{L}}(v) = \mathbb{E}[|v(0, \mathcal{X}) - g(\mathcal{X})|^{2} + |\big(\tfrac{\partial v}{\partial t}\big)(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{X}) - (\Delta_{x}v)(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{X}) - f(v(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{X}))|^{2}]. \tag{16.12}$$

Observe that Theorem 16.1.1 assures that the unknown function $u$ satisfies

$$\pmb{\mathfrak{L}}(u) = 0 \tag{16.13}$$

and is thus a minimizer of the optimization problem associated to (16.12). Motivated by this, we consider aim to find approximations of $u$ by computing approximate minimizers of the function $\pmb{\mathfrak{L}}: C^{1,2}([0,T] \times \mathbb{R}^{d}, \mathbb{R}) \rightarrow [0, \infty]$. Due to its infinite dimensionality this optimization problem is however not yet amenable to numerical computations.

For this reason, in the second step, we reduce this finite dimensional stochastic optimization problem to a finite dimensional stochastic optimization problem involving *ANNs*. Specifically, let $a: \mathbb{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be differentiable, let $h \in \mathbb{N}$, $l_{1}, l_{2}, \ldots, l_{h}$, $\mathfrak{d} \in \mathbb{N}$ satisfy $\mathfrak{d} = l_{1}(d+2) + [\sum_{k=2}^{h}l_{k}(l_{k-1} + 1)] + l_{h} + 1$, and let $\mathcal{L}: \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} \rightarrow [0, \infty)$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ that

$$\begin{align*} \mathcal{L}(\theta) &= \pmb{\mathfrak{L}}\big(\mathcal{N}^{\theta, d+1}_{\mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{1}}, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{2}}, \ldots, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{h}}, \operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}}}\big) \\ &= \mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\mathcal{N}^{\theta, d+1}_{\mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{1}}, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{2}}, \ldots, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{h}}, \operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}}}(0, \mathcal{X}) - g(\mathcal{X})\big|^{2} \\ &\quad+ \big(\tfrac{\partial \mathcal{N}^{\theta, d+1}_{\mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{1}}, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{2}}, \ldots, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{h}}, \operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}}}}{\partial t}(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{X}) - (\Delta_{x}\mathcal{N}^{\theta, d+1}_{\mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{1}}, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{2}}, \ldots, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{h}}, \operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}}})(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{X}) \ &\quad- f\big(\mathcal{N}^{\theta, d+1}_{\mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{1}}, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{2}}, \ldots, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{h}}, \operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{X})\big)\big)^{2}\Big] \tag{16.14} \end{align*}$$

(cf. Definitions 1.1.3 and 1.2.1). We can now compute an approximate minimizer of the function $\pmb{\mathfrak{L}}$ by computing an approximate minimizer $\vartheta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ of the function $\mathcal{L}$ and employing the realization $\mathcal{N}^{\vartheta, d+1}_{\mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{1}}, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{2}}, \ldots, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{h}}, \operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}}}$ of the *ANN* associated to this approximate minimizer as an approximate minimizer of $\pmb{\mathfrak{L}}$.

The third and last step of this derivation is to approximately compute such an approximate minimizer of $\mathcal{L}$ by means of *SGD*-type optimization methods. We now sketch this in the case of the plain-vanilla *SGD* optimization method (cf. Definition 7.2.1). Let $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$, $J \in \mathbb{N}$, $(\gamma_{n})_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq [0, \infty)$, for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $j \in \{1,2,\ldots,J\}$ let $\mathfrak{T}_{n,j}: \Omega \rightarrow [0,T]$ and $\mathfrak{X}_{n,j}: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{d}$ be random variables, assume for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $j \in \{1,2,\ldots,J\}$, $A \in \mathcal{B}([0,T])$, $B \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$ that

$$\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{T} \in A) = \mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{T}_{n,j} \in A) \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{X} \in B) = \mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{X}_{n,j} \in B), \tag{16.15}$$

let $\mathscr{L}: \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} \times [0, T] \times \mathbb{R}^{d} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$, $t \in [0, T]$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ that

$$\mathscr{L}(\theta, t, x)=\left|\mathcal{N}^{\theta, d+1}_{\mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{1}}, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{2}}, \ldots, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{h}}, \operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}}}(0, x)-g(x)\right|^{2} +\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{N}^{\theta, d+1}_{\mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{1}}, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{2}}, \ldots, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{h}}, \operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}}}}{\partial t}(t, x)-\left(\Delta_{x} \mathcal{N}^{\theta, d+1}_{\mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{1}}, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{2}}, \ldots, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{h}}, \operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}}}(t, x) -f\left(\mathcal{N}^{\theta, d+1}_{\mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{1}}, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{2}}, \ldots, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{h}}, \operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}}}(t, x)\right)\right)^{2}, \tag{16.16}$$

and let $\Theta=(\Theta_{n})_{n \in \mathbb{N}_{0}}: \mathbb{N}_{0} \times \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ satisfy for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ that

$$\Theta_{0}=\zeta \quad \text{and} \quad \Theta_{n}=\Theta_{n-1}-\gamma_{n}\left[\frac{1}{J} \sum_{j=1}^{J}\left(\nabla_{\theta} \mathscr{L}\right)\left(\Theta_{n-1}, \mathfrak{T}_{n, j}, \mathfrak{X}_{n, j}\right)\right]. \tag{16.17}$$

Finally, the idea of PINNs and DGMs is then to choose for large enough $n \in \mathbb{N}$ the realization $\mathcal{N}^{\Theta_{n}, d+1}_{\mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{1}}, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{2}}, \ldots, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{h}}, \operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}}}$ as an approximation

$$\mathcal{N}^{\Theta_{n}, d+1}_{\mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{1}}, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{2}}, \ldots, \mathfrak{A}_{a, l_{h}}, \operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}}} \approx u \quad(16.18)$$

of the unknown solution $u$ of the PDE in (16.10).

The ideas and the resulting schemes in the above derivation were first introduced as PINNs in Raissi et al. [347] and as DGMs in Sirignano & Spiliopoulos [379]. Very roughly speaking, PINNs and DGMs in their original form differ in the way the joint distribution of the random variables $(\mathfrak{T}_{n, j}, \mathfrak{X}_{n, j})_{(n, j) \in \mathbb{N} \times\{1,2, \ldots, J\}}$ would be chosen. Loosely speaking, in the case of PINNs the originally proposed distribution for $(\mathfrak{T}_{n, j}, \mathfrak{X}_{n, j})_{(n, j) \in \mathbb{N} \times\{1,2, \ldots, J\}}$ would be based on drawing a finite number of samples of the random variable $(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{X})$ and then having the random variable $(\mathfrak{T}_{n, j}, \mathfrak{X}_{n, j})_{(n, j) \in \mathbb{N} \times\{1,2, \ldots, J\}}$ be randomly chosen among those samples. In the case of DGMs the original proposition would be to choose $(\mathfrak{T}_{n, j}, \mathfrak{X}_{n, j})_{(n, j) \in \mathbb{N} \times\{1,2, \ldots, J\}}$ independent and identically distributed. Implementations of PINNs and DGMs that employ more sophisticated optimization methods, such as the Adam SGD optimization method, can be found in the next section.

## 16.3 Implementation of PINs

In Source code 16.1 below we present a simple implementation of the PINN method, as explained in Section 16.2 above, for finding an approximation of a solution $u \in C^{1, 2}([0, 3] \times \mathbb{R}^{2})$ of the two-dimensional Allen-Cahn-type semilinear heat equation

$$\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\right)(t, x)=\frac{1}{200}(\Delta_{x} u)(t, x)+u(t, x)-\left[u(t, x)\right]^{3} \quad(16.19)$$

with $u(0, x)=\sin \left(\|x\|_{2}^{2}\right)$ for $t \in[0,3], x \in \mathbb{R}^{2}$. This implementation follows the original proposal in Raissi et al. [347] in that it first chooses 20000 realizations of the random variable

$(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{X})$, where $\mathcal{T}$ is continuous uniformly distributed on [0,3] and where $\mathcal{X}$ is normally distributed on $\mathbb{R}^{2}$ with mean $0 \in \mathbb{R}^{2}$ and covariance $4 I_{2} \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$ (cf. Definition 1.5.5). It then trains a fully connected feed-forward ANN with 4 hidden layers (with 50 neurons on each hidden layer) and using the swish activation function with parameter 1 (cf. Section 1.2.8). The training uses batches of size 256 with each batch chosen from the 20000 realizations of the random variable $(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{X})$ which were picked beforehand. The training is performed using the Adam SGD optimization method (cf. Section 7.9). A plot of the resulting approximation of the solution $u$ after 20000 training steps is shown in Figure 16.1.
