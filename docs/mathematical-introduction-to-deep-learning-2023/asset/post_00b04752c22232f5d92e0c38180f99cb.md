
(iii) it holds for all $\Xi \in C^1([0, \infty), \mathbb{R}^9)$, $\tau \in (0, \infty)$ with $\Xi(\tau) = \Theta(\tau)$ and $\|\Xi'(\tau)\|_2 = \|\Theta'(\tau)\|_2$ that

$$(\mathcal{L} \circ \Theta)^\top(\tau) \le (\mathcal{L} \circ \Xi)^\top(\tau) \quad (5.20)$$

(cf. Definition 3.3.4).

Proof of Corollary 5.2.4. Observe that Lemma 5.2.3 and the fundamental theorem of calculus imply item (i) and item (ii). Note that Lemma 5.2.2 shows that for all $\Xi \in C^1([0, \infty), \mathbb{R}^9)$, $t \in (0, \infty)$ it holds that

$$(\mathcal{L} \circ \Xi)^\top(t) = [\mathcal{L}'(\Xi(t))](\Xi'(t))$$
$$\ge \inf_{v \in \{i \in \mathbb{R}^9 : \|i\|_2 = \|\Xi'(i)\|_2\}} [\mathcal{L}'(\Xi(t))](v)$$
$$= -\|\Xi'(t)\|_2 \|(\nabla \mathcal{L})(\Xi(t))\|_2 \quad (5.21)$$

(cf. Definition 3.3.4). Lemma 5.2.3 therefore ensures that for all $\Xi \in C^1([0, \infty), \mathbb{R}^9)$, $\tau \in (0, \infty)$ with $\Xi(\tau) = \Theta(\tau)$ and $\|\Xi'(\tau)\|_2 = \|\Theta'(\tau)\|_2$ it holds that

$$(\mathcal{L} \circ \Xi)^\top(\tau) \ge -\|\Xi'(\tau)\|_2 \|(\nabla \mathcal{L})(\Xi(\tau))\|_2 \ge -\|\Theta'(\tau)\|_2 \|(\nabla \mathcal{L})(\Theta(\tau))\|_2$$
$$= -\| (\nabla \mathcal{L})(\Theta(\tau)) \|_2^2 = (\mathcal{L} \circ \Theta)^\top(\tau). \quad (5.22)$$

This and item (ii) establish item (iii). The proof of Corollary 5.2.4 is thus complete. $\square$

## 5.3 Regularity properties for ANNs

### 5.3.1 On the differentiability of compositions of parametric functions

**Lemma 5.3.1.** Let $\mathbf{0}_1, \mathbf{0}_2, l_1, l_2 \in \mathbb{N}$, let $A_1 : \mathbb{R}^{l_1} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_1} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_2}$ and $A_2 : \mathbb{R}^{l_2} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_1} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_2}$ satisfy for all $x_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_1}$, $x_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_2}$ that $A_1(x_1) = (x_1, \mathbf{0})$ and $A_2(x_2) = (\mathbf{0}, x_2)$, for every $k \in \{1, 2\}$ let $B_k : \mathbb{R}^{l_1} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_2} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_k}$ satisfy for all $x_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_1}$, $x_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_2}$ that $B_k(x_1, x_2) = x_k$, for every $k \in \{1, 2\}$ let $F_k : \mathbb{R}^{l_k} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_k}$ be differentiable, and let $f : \mathbb{R}^{l_1} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_2} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_1} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_2}$ satisfy for all $x_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_1}$, $x_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_2}$ that

$$f(x_1, x_2) = (F_1(x_1), F_2(x_2)).\tag{5.23}$$

*Then*
- (i) it holds that $f = A_1 \circ F_1 \circ B_1 + A_2 \circ F_2 \circ B_2$ and
- (ii) it holds that $f$ is differentiable.

*Proof of Lemma 5.3.1.* Observe that (5.23) implies that for all $x_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_1}$, $x_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_2}$ it holds that

$$(A_1 \circ F_1 \circ B_1 + A_2 \circ F_2 \circ B_2)(x_1, x_2) = (A_1 \circ F_1)(x_1) + (A_2 \circ F_2)(x_2)$$
$$= (F_1(x_1), \mathbf{0}) + (\mathbf{0}, F_2(x_2))\tag{5.24}$$
$$= (F_1(x_1), F_2(x_2)).$$

Combining this and the fact that $A_1$, $A_2$, $F_1$, $F_2$, $B_1$, and $B_2$ are differentiable with the chain rule establishes that $f$ is differentiable. The proof of Lemma 5.3.1 is thus complete. $\square$

**Lemma 5.3.2.** Let $\mathbf{0}_1, \mathbf{0}_2, l_0, l_1, l_2 \in \mathbb{N}$, let $A : \mathbb{R}^{l_1} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_2} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_0} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_2} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_1+l_0}$ and $B : \mathbb{R}^{l_2} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_1+l_0} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_2} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_1}$ satisfy for all $\theta_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_1}$, $\theta_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_2}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^{l_0}$ that

$$A(\theta_1, \theta_2, x) = (\theta_2, (\theta_1, x)) \quad\text{and}\quad B(\theta_2, (\theta_1, x)) = (\theta_2, F_1(\theta_1, x)),\tag{5.25}$$

for every $k \in \{1, 2\}$ let $F_k : \mathbb{R}^{l_k} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_k-1} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_k}$ be differentiable, and let $f : \mathbb{R}^{l_1} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_2} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_0} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_2}$ satisfy for all $\theta_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_1}$, $\theta_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_2}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^{l_0}$ that

$$f(\theta_1, \theta_2, x) = (F_2(\theta_2, \cdot) \circ F_1(\theta_1, \cdot))(x).\tag{5.26}$$

*Then*
- (i) it holds that $f = F_2 \circ B \circ A$ and
- (ii) it holds that $f$ is differentiable.

*Proof of Lemma 5.3.2.* Note that (5.25) and (5.26) ensure that for all $\theta_{1} \in \mathbb{R}^{l_{1}}$, $\theta_{2} \in \mathbb{R}^{l_{2}}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^{l_{0}}$ it holds that

$$f(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},x)=F_{2}(\theta_{2},F_{1}(\theta_{1},x))=F_{2}(B(\theta_{2},\theta_{1},x))=F_{2}(B(A(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},x))). \qquad (5.27)$$

Observe that Lemma 5.3.1 (applied with $l_{1} \mapsto l_{2}$, $l_{2} \mapsto l_{1}+l_{0}$, $F_{1} \mapsto (\mathbb{R}^{l_{2}} \ni \theta_{2} \mapsto \theta_{2} \in \mathbb{R}^{l_{2}})$, $F_{2} \mapsto (\mathbb{R}^{l_{1}+l_{0}} \ni (\theta_{1},x) \mapsto F_{1}(\theta_{1},x) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_{1}})$ in the notation of Lemma 5.3.1) implies that $B$ is differentiable. Combining this, the fact that $A$ is differentiable, the fact that $F_{2}$ is differentiable, and (5.27) with the chain rule assures that $f$ is differentiable. The proof of Lemma 5.3.2 is thus complete.
