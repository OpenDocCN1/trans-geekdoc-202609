## 5.6 Lyapunov-type functions for GFs

### 5.6.1 Gronwall differential inequalities

The following lemma, Lemma 5.6.1 below, is referred to as a Gronwall inequality in the literature (cf., for instance, Henry [194, Chapter 7]). Gronwall inequalities are powerful tools to study dynamical systems and, especially, solutions of ODEs.

**Lemma 5.6.1** (Gronwall inequality). Let \( T \in (0, \infty) \), \( \alpha \in \mathbb{R} \), \( \epsilon \in C^{1}([0, T], \mathbb{R}) \), \( \beta \in C([0, T], \mathbb{R}) \) satisfy for all \( t \in [0, T] \) that

\[ \epsilon(t) \leq \alpha \epsilon(t) + \beta(t). \tag{5.92} \]

Then it holds for all \( t \in [0, T] \) that

\[ \epsilon(t) \leq e^{\alpha t}\epsilon(0) + \int_{0}^{t} e^{\alpha(t-s)} \beta(s) \, \mathrm{d}s. \tag{5.93} \]

*Proof of Lemma 5.6.1.* Throughout this proof, let \( v: [0, T] \rightarrow \mathbb{R} \) satisfy for all \( t \in [0, T] \) that

\[ v(t) = e^{\alpha t}\left[ \int_{0}^{t} e^{-\alpha s} \beta(s) \, \mathrm{d}s \right] \tag{5.94} \]

and let \( u: [0, T] \rightarrow \mathbb{R} \) satisfy for all \( t \in [0, T] \) that

\[ u(t) = [\epsilon(t) - v(t)] e^{-\alpha t}. \tag{5.95} \]

Note that the product rule and the fundamental theorem of calculus demonstrate that for all \( t \in [0, T] \) it holds that \( v \in C^{1}([0, T], \mathbb{R}) \) and

\[ \begin{aligned} v^{\prime}(t) & =\left[ \int_{0}^{t} \alpha e^{\alpha(t-s)} \beta(s) \, \mathrm{d}s \right]+\beta(t) = \alpha\left[ \int_{0}^{t} e^{\alpha(t-s)} \beta(s) \, \mathrm{d}s \right]+\beta(t) = \alpha v(t) + \beta(t). \end{aligned} \tag{5.96} \]

The assumption that \( \epsilon \in C^{1}([0, T], \mathbb{R}) \) and the product rule therefore ensure that for all \( t \in [0, T] \) it holds that \( u \in C^{1}([0, T], \mathbb{R}) \) and

\[ \begin{aligned} u^{\prime}(t) & =[\epsilon^{\prime}(t)-v^{\prime}(t)] e^{-\alpha t}-[\epsilon(t)-v(t)] \alpha e^{-\alpha t} \\ & =[\epsilon^{\prime}(t)-v^{\prime}(t)-\alpha \epsilon(t)+\alpha v(t)] e^{-\alpha t} \\ & =[\epsilon^{\prime}(t)-\alpha v(t)-\beta(t)-\alpha \epsilon(t)+\alpha v(t)] e^{-\alpha t} \\ & =[\epsilon^{\prime}(t)-\beta(t)-\alpha \epsilon(t)] e^{-\alpha t}. \end{aligned} \tag{5.97} \]

Combining this with the assumption that for all \( t \in [0, T] \) it holds that \( \epsilon^{\prime}(t) \leq \alpha \epsilon(t)+\beta(t) \) proves that for all \( t \in [0, T] \) it holds that

\[ u^{\prime}(t) \leq[\alpha \epsilon(t)+\beta(t)-\beta(t)-\alpha \epsilon(t)] e^{-\alpha t} = 0. \tag{5.98} \]

This and the fundamental theorem of calculus imply that for all $t \in [0, T]$ it holds that

$u(t) = u(0) + \int_0^t u'(s) \, \mathrm{d}s \le u(0) + \int_0^t 0 \, \mathrm{d}s = u(0) = \epsilon(0). \tag{5.99}$

Combining this, (5.94), and (5.95) shows that for all $t \in [0, T]$ it holds that

$\epsilon(t) = e^{\alpha t}u(t) + v(t) \le e^{\alpha t}\epsilon(0) + v(t) \le e^{\alpha t}\epsilon(0) + \int_0^t e^{\alpha(t-s)}\beta(s) \, \mathrm{d}s$. \tag{5.100}

The proof of Lemma 5.6.1 is thus complete.

### 5.6.2 Lyapunov-type functions for ODEs

**Proposition 5.6.2** (Lyapunov-type functions for ODEs). Let $\varnothing \in \mathbb{N}$, $T \in (0, \infty)$, $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$, let $O \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{\varnothing}$ be open, let $\beta \in C(O, \mathbb{R})$, $\mathscr{F} \in C(O, \mathbb{R}^{\varnothing})$, $V \in C^1(O, \mathbb{R})$ satisfy for all $\theta \in O$ that

$V'(\theta)\mathscr{F}(\theta) = \langle (\nabla V)(\theta), \mathscr{F}(\theta) \rangle \le \alpha V(\theta) + \beta(\theta)$, \tag{5.101}

and let $\Theta \in C([0, T], O)$ satisfy for all $t \in [0, T]$ that $\Theta_t = \Theta_0 + \int_0^t \mathscr{F}(\Theta_s) \, \mathrm{d}s$ (cf. Definition 1.4.7). Then it holds for all $t \in [0, T]$ that

$V(\Theta_t) \le e^{\alpha t}V(\Theta_0) + \int_0^t e^{\alpha(t-s)}\beta(\Theta_s) \, \mathrm{d}s$. \tag{5.102}

**Proof of Proposition 5.6.2.** Throughout this proof, let $\epsilon, b \in C([0, T], \mathbb{R})$ satisfy for all $t \in [0, T]$ that

$\epsilon(t) = V(\Theta_t) \quad \text{ and } \quad b(t) = \beta(\Theta_t)$. \tag{5.103}

Observe that (5.101), (5.103), the fundamental theorem of calculus, and the chain rule ensure that for all $t \in [0, T]$ it holds that

$\epsilon'(t) = \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(V(\Theta_t)) = V'(\Theta_t)(\dot{\Theta}_t) = V'(\Theta_t)\mathscr{F}(\Theta_t) \le \alpha V(\Theta_t) + \beta(\Theta_t) = \alpha \epsilon(t) + b(t)$. \tag{5.104}

Lemma 5.6.1 and (5.103) hence demonstrate that for all $t \in [0, T]$ it holds that

$V(\Theta_t) = \epsilon(t) \le \epsilon(0)e^{\alpha t} + \int_0^t e^{\alpha(t-s)}b(s) \, \mathrm{d}s = V(\Theta_0)e^{\alpha t} + \int_0^t e^{\alpha(t-s)}\beta(\Theta_s) \, \mathrm{d}s$. \tag{5.105}

The proof of Proposition 5.6.2 is thus complete.

**Corollary 5.6.3.** Let $\varnothing \in \mathbb{N}$, $T \in (0, \infty)$, $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$, let $O \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{\varnothing}$ be open, let $\mathscr{F} \in C(O, \mathbb{R}^{\varnothing})$, $V \in C^1(O, \mathbb{R})$ satisfy for all $\theta \in O$ that

$V'(\theta)\mathscr{F}(\theta) = \langle (\nabla V)(\theta), \mathscr{F}(\theta) \rangle \le \alpha V(\theta)$, \tag{5.106}

and let $\Theta \in C([0, T], O)$ satisfy for all $t \in [0, T]$ that $\Theta_t = \Theta_0 + \int_0^t \mathscr{F}(\Theta_s) \, \mathrm{d}s$ (cf. Definition 1.4.7). Then it holds for all $t \in [0, T]$ that

$V(\Theta_t) \le e^{\alpha t}V(\Theta_0)$. \tag{5.107}
