
*Proof of Proposition 9.2.1.* Throughout this proof, let $V: O \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ and $U: O \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $\theta \in O$ that

$$
V(\theta) = -|\mathscr{L}(\theta) - \mathscr{L}(\vartheta)|^{-1} \quad \text{and} \quad U(\theta) = |\mathscr{L}(\theta) - \mathscr{L}(\vartheta)|^{1-\alpha}. \quad (9.8)
$$

Observe that the assumption that for all $\theta \in O$ it holds that $|\mathscr{L}(\theta)-\mathscr{L}(\vartheta)|^{\alpha} \leq \mathfrak{C}\| (\nabla\mathscr{L})(\theta)\|_{2}$ shows that for all $\theta \in O$ it holds that

$$
\| (\nabla\mathscr{L})(\theta)\|_{2}^{2} \geq \mathfrak{C}^{-2} |\mathscr{L}(\theta)-\mathscr{L}(\vartheta)|^{2\alpha}. \quad (9.9)
$$

Furthermore, note that (9.8) ensures that for all $\theta \in O$ it holds that $V \in C^{1}(O, \mathbb{R})$ and

$$
(\nabla V)(\theta)=|\mathscr{L}(\theta)-\mathscr{L}(\vartheta)|^{-2} (\nabla\mathscr{L})(\theta). \quad (9.10)
$$

Combining this with (9.9) implies that for all $\theta \in O$ it holds that

$$
\langle (\nabla V)(\theta), -(\nabla \mathscr{L})(\theta)\rangle = - |\mathscr{L}(\theta)-\mathscr{L}(\vartheta)|^{-2} \| (\nabla\mathscr{L})(\theta)\|_{2}^{2} \leq -\mathfrak{C}^{-2} |\mathscr{L}(\theta)-\mathscr{L}(\vartheta)|^{2\alpha-2} \leq -\mathfrak{C}^{-1}. \quad (9.11)
$$

The assumption that for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that $\Theta_{t} \in O$, the assumption that for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that $\Theta_{t}=\Theta_{0}-\int_{0}^{t} (\nabla\mathscr{L})(\Theta_{s}) \, \mathrm{d}s$, and Proposition 5.6.2 therefore establish that for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that

$$
-|\mathscr{L}(\Theta_{t})-\mathscr{L}(\vartheta)|^{-1} = V(\Theta_{t}) \leq V(\Theta_{0}) + \int_{0}^{t} -\mathfrak{C}^{-1} \, \mathrm{d}s = V(\Theta_{0}) - \mathfrak{C}^{-1} t = -|\mathscr{L}(\Theta_{0})-\mathscr{L}(\vartheta)|^{-1} - \mathfrak{C}^{-1} t. \quad (9.12)
$$

Hence, we obtain for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ that

$$
0 \leq \mathscr{L}(\Theta_{t})-\mathscr{L}(\vartheta) \leq \left( |\mathscr{L}(\Theta_{0})-\mathscr{L}(\vartheta)|^{-1} + \mathfrak{C}^{-1}t \right)^{-1}. \quad (9.13)
$$

Moreover, observe that (9.8) ensures that for all $\theta \in O$ it holds that $U \in C^{1}(O, \mathbb{R})$ and

$$
(\nabla U)(\theta)=(1-\alpha) |\mathscr{L}(\theta)-\mathscr{L}(\vartheta)|^{-\alpha} (\nabla\mathscr{L})(\theta). \quad (9.14)
$$

The assumption that for all $\theta \in O$ it holds that $|\mathscr{L}(\theta)-\mathscr{L}(\vartheta)|^{\alpha} \leq \mathfrak{C}\| (\nabla\mathscr{L})(\theta)\|_{2}$ therefore demonstrates that for all $\theta \in O$ it holds that

$$
\langle (\nabla U)(\theta), -(\nabla\mathscr{L})(\theta)\rangle = -(1-\alpha) |\mathscr{L}(\theta)-\mathscr{L}(\vartheta)|^{-\alpha} \| (\nabla\mathscr{L})(\theta)\|_{2}^{2} \leq -\mathfrak{C}^{-1} (1-\alpha) \| (\nabla\mathscr{L})(\theta)\|_{2}. \quad (9.15)
$$

Combining this, the assumption that for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that $\Theta_{t} \in O$, the fact that for all $s, t \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that

$$
\Theta_{s+t} = \Theta_{s} - \int_{0}^{t} (\nabla\mathscr{L})(\Theta_{s+u}) \, \mathrm{d}u, \quad (9.16)
$$

and Proposition 5.6.2 (applied for every $s \in [0, \infty)$, $t \in (s, \infty)$ with $\vartheta \mapsto \vartheta,\, \Theta \mapsto t - s$, $O \mapsto O,\, \alpha \mapsto 0,\, \beta \mapsto (O \ni \theta \mapsto -\mathfrak{C}^{-1}(1-\alpha) \|(\nabla\mathscr{L})(\theta)\|_2 \in \mathbb{R}$, $\mathscr{G} \mapsto (\nabla\mathscr{L})$, $\Theta \mapsto \{[0, t-s] \ni u \mapsto \Theta_{s+u} \in O\}$) in the notation of Proposition 5.6.2 ensures that for all $s, t \in [0, \infty)$ with $s < t$ it holds that

$$
\begin{split}
0 \leq & \left|\mathscr{L}\left(\Theta_t\right)-\mathscr{L}\left(\vartheta\right)\right|^{1-\alpha}=U\left(\Theta_t\right) \\
\leq & U\left(\Theta_s\right)+\int_s^t-\mathfrak{C}^{-1}(1-\alpha)\|\left(\nabla\mathscr{L}\right)\left(\Theta_u\right)\|_2\,\mathrm{d}u \\
= & \left|\mathscr{L}\left(\Theta_s\right)-\mathscr{L}\left(\vartheta\right)\right|^{1-\alpha}-\mathfrak{C}^{-1}(1-\alpha)\left[\int_s^t\|\left(\nabla\mathscr{L}\right)\left(\Theta_u\right)\|_2\,\mathrm{d}u\right].
\end{split} \quad (9.17)
$$

This implies that for all $s, t \in [0, \infty)$ with $s < t$ it holds that

$$
\int_s^t\|\left(\nabla\mathscr{L}\right)\left(\Theta_u\right)\|_2\,\mathrm{d}u\leq\mathfrak{C}(1-\alpha)^{-1}\left|\mathscr{L}\left(\Theta_s\right)-\mathscr{L}\left(\vartheta\right)\right|^{1-\alpha}. \quad (9.18)
$$

Hence, we obtain that

$$
\int_0^{\infty}\|\left(\nabla\mathscr{L}\right)\left(\Theta_s\right)\|_2\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\mathfrak{C}(1-\alpha)^{-1}\left|\mathscr{L}\left(\Theta_0\right)-\mathscr{L}\left(\vartheta\right)\right|^{1-\alpha}<\infty \quad (9.19)
$$

This demonstrates that

$$
\limsup_{r\to\infty}\int_r^{\infty}\|\left(\nabla\mathscr{L}\right)\left(\Theta_s\right)\|_2\,\mathrm{d}s=0. \quad (9.20)
$$

In addition, note that the fundamental theorem of calculus and the assumption that for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that $\Theta_t = \Theta_0 - \int_0^t (\nabla\mathscr{L})(\Theta_s)\,\mathrm{d}s$ establish that for all $r, s, t \in [0, \infty)$ with $r \leq s \leq t$ it holds that

$$
\left\|\Theta_t-\Theta_s\right\|_2=\left\|\int_s^t\left(\nabla\mathscr{L}\right)\left(\Theta_u\right)\,\mathrm{d}u\right\|_2\leq\int_s^t\left\|\left(\nabla\mathscr{L}\right)\left(\Theta_u\right)\right\|_2\,\mathrm{d}u\leq\int_r^{\infty}\left\|\left(\nabla\mathscr{L}\right)\left(\Theta_u\right)\right\|_2\,\mathrm{d}u. \quad (9.21)
$$

This and (9.20) prove that there exists $\psi \in \mathbb{R}^{q}$ which satisfies

$$
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\left\|\Theta_t-\psi\right\|_2=0. \quad (9.22)
$$

Combining this and the assumption that $\mathscr{L}$ is continuous with (9.13) demonstrates that

$$
\mathscr{L}\left(\psi\right)=\mathscr{L}\left(\lim_{t\to\infty}\Theta_t\right)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathscr{L}\left(\Theta_t\right)=\mathscr{L}\left(\vartheta\right). \quad (9.23)
$$

Next observe that (9.22), (9.18), and (9.21) show that for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that

$$
\begin{split}
\left\|\Theta_t-\psi\right\|_2=\left\|\Theta_t-\left[\lim_{s\to\infty}\Theta_s\right]\right\|_2 &=\lim_{s\to\infty}\left\|\Theta_t-\Theta_s\right\|_2 \\
&\leq\int_t^{\infty}\left\|\left(\nabla\mathscr{L}\right)\left(\Theta_u\right)\right\|_2\,\mathrm{d}u \\
&\leq\mathfrak{C}(1-\alpha)^{-1}\left|\mathscr{L}\left(\Theta_t\right)-\mathscr{L}\left(\vartheta\right)\right|^{1-\alpha}.
\end{split} \quad (9.24)
$$

Combining this with 9.13 and 9.23 establishes items (i), (ii), and (iii). The proof of Proposition 9.2.1 is thus complete.

## 9.3 Standard KL inequalities for monomials

**Lemma 9.3.1** (Standard **KL** inequalities for monomials). Let $\mathfrak{d}\in \mathbb{N}$, $p\in (1,\infty)$, $\varepsilon,c,\alpha\in (0,\infty)$ satisfy $c\geq p^{-1}\varepsilon^{p(\alpha-1)+1}$ and $\alpha\geq 1-\frac{1}{p}$ and let $\mathscr{L}:\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}\to \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $\vartheta\in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ that

$$
\mathscr{L}(\vartheta)=\|\vartheta\|_{2}^{p}.
$$

Then

- (i) it holds that $\mathscr{L}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}},\mathbb{R})$ and
- (ii) it holds for all $\vartheta\in \{v\in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}:\|v\|_{2}\leq \varepsilon\}$ that

$$
|\mathscr{L}(0)-\mathscr{L}(\vartheta)|^{\alpha}\leq c\|(\nabla \mathscr{L})(\vartheta)\|_{2}.
$$

Proof of Lemma 9.3.1. First, note that the fact that for all $\vartheta\in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ it holds that

$$
\mathscr{L}(\vartheta)=\left(\|\vartheta\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{p/2}
$$

implies that for all $\vartheta\in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ it holds that $\mathscr{L}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}},\mathbb{R})$ and

$$
\|(\nabla \mathscr{L})(\vartheta)\|_{2}=p\|\vartheta\|_{2}^{p-1}.
$$

Furthermore, observe that the assumption that $\alpha\geq 1-\frac{1}{p}$ ensures that $p(\alpha-1)+1\geq 0$. The assumption that $c\geq p^{-1}\varepsilon^{p(\alpha-1)+1}$ therefore demonstrates that for all $\vartheta\in \{v\in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}:\|v\|_{2}\leq \varepsilon\}$ it holds that

$$
\|\vartheta\|_{2}^{p\alpha}\|\vartheta\|_{2}^{p(p-1)}=\|\vartheta\|_{2}^{p(\alpha-1)+1}\leq \varepsilon^{p(\alpha-1)+1}\leq c p.
$$

Combining 9.28 and 9.29 ensures that for all $\vartheta\in \{v\in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}:\|v\|_{2}\leq \varepsilon\}$ it holds that

$$
|\mathscr{L}(0)-\mathscr{L}(\vartheta)|^{\alpha}=\|\vartheta\|_{2}^{p\alpha}\leq c p\|\vartheta\|_{2}^{p-1}=c\|(\nabla \mathscr{L})(\vartheta)\|_{2}.
$$

This completes the proof of Lemma 9.3.1.

## 9.4 Standard KL inequalities around non-critical points

**Lemma 9.4.1** (Standard **KL** inequality around non-critical points). Let $\mathfrak{d}\in \mathbb{N}$, let $U\subseteq \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ be open, and let $\mathscr{L}\in C^{1}(U,\mathbb{R})$, $\theta\in U$, $c\in (0,\infty)$ satisfy for all $\vartheta\in U$ that

$$
\max \{ |\mathscr{L}(\theta)-\mathscr{L}(\vartheta)|^{\alpha}, c\|(\nabla \mathscr{L})(\theta)-(\nabla \mathscr{L})(\vartheta)\|_{2} \} \leq \frac{c\|(\nabla \mathscr{L})(\vartheta)\|_{2}}{2}
$$

(cf. Definition 3.3.4). Then it holds for all $\vartheta\in U$ that

$$
|\mathscr{L}(\theta)-\mathscr{L}(\vartheta)|^{\alpha}\leq c\|(\nabla \mathscr{L})(\vartheta)\|_{2}.
$$
