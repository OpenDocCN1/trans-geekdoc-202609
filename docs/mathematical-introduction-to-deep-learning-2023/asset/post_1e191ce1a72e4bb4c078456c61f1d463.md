### Lemma 4.4.4

Let $a\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})$, $L\in\mathbb{N}$, $\mathrm{l}_0, \mathrm{l}_1, \ldots, \mathrm{l}_L$, $l_0, \mathrm{l}_1, \ldots, l_L\in\mathbb{N}$ satisfy for all $k\in\{1,2,\ldots,L\}$ that

$$
\mathrm{l}_0 = l_0, \quad \mathrm{l}_L = l_L, \quad \text{and} \quad \mathrm{l}_k \geq l_k \quad (4.144)
$$

and let $\Phi\in\mathbb{N}$ satisfy $\mathcal{D}(\Phi) = (l_0, l_1, \ldots, l_L)$ (cf. Definition 1.3.1). Then there exists $\Psi\in\mathbb{N}$ such that

$$
\mathcal{D}(\Psi) = (\mathrm{l}_0, \mathrm{l}_1, \ldots, \mathrm{l}_L), \quad \|\mathcal{T}(\Psi)\|_\infty = \|\mathcal{T}(\Phi)\|_\infty, \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{R}_{a}^{\mathrm{N}}(\Psi) = \mathcal{R}_{a}^{\mathrm{N}}(\Phi) \quad (4.145)
$$

(cf. Definitions 1.3.4, 1.3.5, and 3.3.4).

**Proof of Lemma 4.4.4.** Throughout this proof, let $B_k = (B_{k,i})_{i\in\{1,2,\ldots,l_k\}}\in\mathbb{R}^{l_k}$, $k\in\{1,2,\ldots,L\}$, and $W_k = (W_{k,i,j})_{(i,j)\in\{1,2,\ldots,l_k\}\times\{1,2,\ldots,l_{k-1}\}}\in\mathbb{R}^{l_k\times l_{k-1}}$, $k\in\{1,2,\ldots,L\}$, satisfy

$$
\Phi = ((W_1, B_1), (W_2, B_2), \ldots, (W_L, B_L)) \quad (4.146)
$$

and let $\mathfrak{M}_k = (\mathfrak{M}_{k,i,j})_{(i,j)\in\{1,2,\ldots,l_k\}\times\{1,2,\ldots,l_{k-1}\}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{l}_k\times\mathrm{l}_{k-1}}$, $k\in\{1,2,\ldots,L\}$, and $\mathfrak{B}_k = (\mathfrak{B}_{k,i})_{i\in\{1,2,\ldots,\mathrm{l}_k\}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{l}_k}$, $k\in\{1,2,\ldots,L\}$, satisfy for all $k\in\{1,2,\ldots,L\}$, $i\in\{1,2,\ldots,\mathrm{l}_k\}$, $j\in\{1,2,\ldots,\mathrm{l}_{k-1}\}$ that

$$
\mathfrak{M}_{k,i,j} = \begin{cases} 
W_{k,i,j} & : i \leq l_k \land (j \leq l_{k-1}) 
\end{cases}
\quad \text{and} \quad 
\mathfrak{B}_{k,i} = \begin{cases} 
B_{k,i} & : i \leq l_k \\ 
0 & : i > l_k 
\end{cases}
\quad (4.147)
$$

Note that (1.77) establishes that $((\mathfrak{M}_1, \mathfrak{B}_1), (\mathfrak{M}_2, \mathfrak{B}_2), \ldots, (\mathfrak{M}_L, \mathfrak{B}_L)) \in (\prod_{i=1}^{L}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{l}_i\times\mathrm{l}_{i-1}} \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{l}_i})) \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ and

$$
\mathcal{D}(((\mathfrak{M}_1, \mathfrak{B}_1), (\mathfrak{M}_2, \mathfrak{B}_2), \ldots, (\mathfrak{M}_L, \mathfrak{B}_L))) = (\mathrm{l}_0, \mathrm{l}_1, \ldots, \mathrm{l}_L). \quad (4.148)
$$

Furthermore, observe that Lemma 1.3.8 and (4.147) demonstrate that

$$
\|\boldsymbol{T}\left(\left(\mathfrak{W}_{1}, \mathfrak{B}_{1}\right),\left(\mathfrak{W}_{2}, \mathfrak{B}_{2}\right), \ldots,\left(\mathfrak{W}_{L}, \mathfrak{B}_{L}\right)\right)\|_{\infty}=\|\boldsymbol{T}(\Phi)\|_{\infty} \quad (4.149)
$$

(cf. Definitions 1.3.5 and 3.3.4). Moreover, note that Lemma 4.4.3 proves that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{R}_{a}^{\mathbf{N}}(\Phi) &=\mathcal{R}_{a}^{\mathbf{N}}\left(\left(\left(W_{1}, B_{1}\right),\left(W_{2}, B_{2}\right), \ldots,\left(W_{L}, B_{L}\right)\right)\right) \\
&=\mathcal{R}_{a}^{\mathbf{N}}\left(\left(\mathfrak{W}_{1}, \mathfrak{B}_{1}\right),\left(\mathfrak{W}_{2}, \mathfrak{B}_{2}\right), \ldots,\left(\mathfrak{W}_{L}, \mathfrak{B}_{L}\right)\right)
\end{aligned} \quad (4.150)
$$

(cf. Definition 1.3.4). The proof of Lemma 4.4.4 is thus complete. $\square$

### Lemma 4.4.5

Let $L, \mathfrak{L} \in \mathbb{N}, l_{0}, l_{1}, \ldots, l_{L}, l_{0}, l_{1}, \ldots, l_{\mathfrak{L}} \in \mathbb{N}, \Phi_{1} = \left(\left(W_{1}, B_{1}\right),\left(W_{2}, B_{2}\right), \ldots,\left(W_{L}, B_{L}\right)\right) \in \left(\prod_{k=1}^{L}\left(\mathbb{R}^{l_{k} \times l_{k-1}} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_{k}}\right)\right), \Phi_{2} = \left(\left(\mathfrak{W}_{1}, \mathfrak{B}_{1}\right),\left(\mathfrak{W}_{2}, \mathfrak{B}_{2}\right), \ldots,\left(\mathfrak{W}_{\mathfrak{L}}, \mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{L}}\right)\right) \in \left(\prod_{k=1}^{\mathfrak{L}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{l_{k} \times l_{k-1}} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_{k}}\right)\right)$. Then

$$
\left\|\boldsymbol{T}\left(\Phi_{1} \bullet \Phi_{2}\right)\right\|_{\infty} \leq \max \left\{\left\|\boldsymbol{T}\left(\Phi_{1}\right)\right\|_{\infty},\left\|\boldsymbol{T}\left(\Phi_{2}\right)\right\|_{\infty},\left\|\boldsymbol{T}\left(\left(\left(W_{1} \mathfrak{W}_{2}, W_{1} \mathfrak{B}_{2}+B_{1}\right)\right)\right)\right\|_{\infty}\right\} \quad (4.151)
$$

(cf. Definitions 1.3.5, 2.1.1, and 3.3.4).

**Proof of Lemma 4.4.5.** Observe that (2.2) and Lemma 1.3.8 establish (4.151). The proof of Lemma 4.4.5 is thus complete. $\square$

$$

$$

(cf. Definitions 1.3.5, 2.2.6, 2.2.8, and 3.3.4).

**Proof of Lemma 4.4.6.** Throughout this proof, assume without loss of generality that $L > \mathcal{L}(\Phi)$ and let $l_{0}, l_{1}, \ldots, l_{L-\mathcal{L}(\Phi)+1} \in \mathbb{N}$ satisfy

$$
