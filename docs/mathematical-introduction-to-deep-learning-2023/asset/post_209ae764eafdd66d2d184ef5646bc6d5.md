
Therefore, we obtain for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ with $|x - \xi| \leq \frac{1}{2} \left[ \sum_{n=m}^{N} \frac{|\beta_n n!|}{|\beta_m m!|} \right]^{-1}$ that

$$
\begin{aligned}
|p'(x)| &\geq |x - \xi|^{m-1} \left( |\beta_m m!| - |x - \xi| \left[ \sum_{n=m+1}^{N} |\beta_n n!| \right] \right) \\
&\geq |x - \xi|^{m-1} \left( |\beta_m m!| - \frac{|\beta_m m!|}{2} \left( |x - \xi| \left[ \sum_{n=m}^{N} \frac{2|\beta_n n!|}{|\beta_m m!|} \right] \right) \right) \\
&\geq |x - \xi|^{m-1} \left( |\beta_m m!| - \frac{|\beta_m m!|}{2} \right) = \frac{1}{2}|x - \xi|^{m-1} |\beta_m m!|.
\end{aligned}
$$

Combining this with **(9.54)** demonstrates that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ with $|x - \xi| \leq \frac{1}{2} \left[ \sum_{n=m}^{N} \frac{|\beta_n n!|}{|\beta_m m!|} \right]^{-1}$ it holds that

$$|p(x) - p(\xi)|^{\alpha} \leq \frac{\xi}{2}|x - \xi|^{m-1} |\beta_m m!| \leq c |p'(x)|.$$

This establishes **(9.48)**. The proof of Corollary **9.6.2** is thus complete.

**Corollary 9.6.3** (Quantitative standard **KL** inequalities for general one-dimensional polynomials). *Let* $\xi \in \mathbb{R}$, $N \in \mathbb{N}$, *p* $\in \mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R})$ *satisfy for all* $x \in \mathbb{R}$ *that* $p^{(N+1)}(x) = 0$, *let* $\beta_0, \beta_1, \dots, \beta_N \in \mathbb{R}$ *satisfy for all* $n \in \{0, 1, \dots, N\}$ *that* $\beta_n = \frac{p^{(n)}(\xi)}{n!}$, *let* $\rho \in \mathbb{R}$ *satisfy* $\rho = \mathbb{1}_{\{0\}}(\sum_{n=1}^{N} |\beta_n n!|) + \min(\{|\sum_{n=1}^{N} |\beta_n n!|\} \setminus \{0\}\} \cup \{\sum_{n=1}^{N} |\beta_n n!|\}$, *and let* $\alpha \in (0, 1]$, *c, \varepsilon \in* $[0, \infty)$ *satisfy*

$$\alpha \geq 1 - N^{-1}, \quad c \geq 2\rho^{-1} \left[ \sum_{n=1}^{N} |\beta_n|^\alpha \right], \quad \varepsilon \leq \rho \mathbb{1}_{\{0\}}(\sum_{n=1}^{N} |\beta_n|) + 2(\sum_{n=1}^{N} |\beta_n n!|)^{-1}.$$

*Then it holds for all* $x \in [\xi - \varepsilon, \xi + \varepsilon]$ *that*

$$|p(x) - p(\xi)|^{\alpha} \leq c |p'(x)|.$$

*Proof of Corollary 9.6.3.* Throughout this proof, assume without loss of generality that

$$\sup_{x \in \mathbb{R}} |p(x) - p(\xi)| > 0.$$

Note that Corollary **9.6.1** and **(9.60)** ensure that $\sum_{n=1}^{N} |\beta_n| > 0$. Hence, we obtain that there exists $m \in \{1, 2, \dots, N\}$ which satisfies

$$|\beta_m| > 0 = \sum_{n=1}^{m-1} |\beta_n|.$$

Observe that **(9.61)**, the fact that $\alpha \geq 1 - N^{-1}$, and Corollary **9.6.2** ensure that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ with $|x - \xi| \leq \frac{1}{2} \left[ \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{|\beta_n n!|}{|\beta_m m!|} \right]^{-1}$ it holds that

$$|p(x) - p(\xi)|^{\alpha} \leq \left[ \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{2|\beta_n|^{\alpha}}{|\beta_m m!|} \right] |p'(x)| \leq \left[ \frac{2}{\rho} \left( \sum_{n=1}^{N} |\beta_n|^{\alpha} \right) \right] |p'(x)| \leq c |p'(x)|.$$

This establishes **(9.59)**. The proof of Corollary **9.6.3** is thus complete.

**Corollary 9.6.4** (Qualitative standard **KL** inequalities for general one-dimensional polynomials). *Let* $\xi \in \mathbb{R}$, $N \in \mathbb{N}$, $p \in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R})$ *satisfy for all* $x \in \mathbb{R}$ *that* $p^{(N)}(x) = 0$. *Then there exist* $\varepsilon, c \in (0, \infty)$, $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ *such that for all* $x \in [\xi - \varepsilon, \xi + \varepsilon]$ *it holds that*

$$|p(x) - p(\xi)|^{\alpha} \leq c|p'(x)|. \tag{9.63}$$

*Proof of Corollary 9.6.4*. *Note that Corollary 9.6.3 establishes (9.63). The proof of Corollary 9.6.4 is thus complete.* $\Box$

**Corollary 9.6.5**. *Let* $\mathscr{L} : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ *be a polynomial. Then* $\mathscr{L}$ *is a standard* **KL** *function* (cf. Definition 9.1.2).

*Proof of Corollary 9.6.5*. *Observe that (9.2) and Corollary 9.6.4 establish that* $\mathscr{L}$ *is a standard* **KL** *function* (cf. Definition 9.1.2). *The proof of Corollary 9.6.5 is thus complete.* $\Box$

## 9.7 Power series and analytic functions

**Definition 9.7.1** (Analytic functions). *Let* $m, n \in \mathbb{N}$, *let* $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$ *be open, and let* $f : U \to \mathbb{R}^n$ *be a function. Then we say that* $f$ *is analytic if and only if for all* $x \in U$ *there exists* $\varepsilon \in (0, \infty)$ *such that for all* $y \in \{u \in U : \|x - u\|_2 < \varepsilon\}$ *it holds that* $f \in C^{\infty}(U, \mathbb{R}^n)$ *and*

$$\lim \sup_{K \to \infty} \Big|\Big|f(y) - \sum_{k=0}^{K} \frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(x)(y - x, y - x, \dots, y - x)\Big|\Big|_2 = 0 \tag{9.64}$$

(cf. Definition 3.3.4).

**Proposition 9.7.2** (Power series). *Let* $m, n \in \mathbb{N}$, $\varepsilon \in (0, \infty)$, *let* $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$ *satisfy* $U = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^m : \|x\|_2 \leq \varepsilon\}$, *for every* $k \in \mathbb{N}$ *let* $A_k : (\mathbb{R}^m)^k \to \mathbb{R}^n$ *be* $k$-linear *and symmetric, and let* $f : U \to \mathbb{R}^n$ *satisfy for all* $x \in U$ *that*

$$\lim \sup_{k \to \infty} \Big|\Big|f(x) - f(0) - \sum_{k=1}^{K} A_k(x, x, \dots, x)\Big|\Big|_2 = 0 \tag{9.65}$$

(cf. Definition 3.3.4). *Then*

-   *it holds for all* $x \in \{u \in U : \|u\|_2 < \varepsilon\}$ *that* $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \|A_k(x, x, \dots, x)\|_2 < \infty$ *and*
    $$f(x) = f(0) + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} A_k(x, x, \dots, x), \tag{9.66}$$
-   *it holds that* $f|_{\{u \in U : \|u\|_2 < \varepsilon\}}$ *is infinitely often differentiable,*
-   it holds for all $x \in \{u \in U : \|u\|_2 < \varepsilon\}$, $l \in \mathbb{N}$, $v_1, v_2, \dots, v_l \in \mathbb{R}^m$ that
    $$\sum_{k=l}^{\infty} \left( \left[ \frac{k!}{(k-l)!} \right] \| A_k(v_1, v_2, \dots, v_l, x, x, \dots, x) \|_2 \right) < \infty \tag{9.67}$$
    and
    $$f^{(l)}(x)(v_1, \dots, v_l) = \sum_{k=l}^{\infty} \left( \left[ \frac{k!}{(k-l)!} \right] A_k(v_1, v_2, \dots, v_l, x, x, \dots, x) \right) \tag{9.68}$$
-   it holds for all $k \in \mathbb{N}$ that $f^{(k)}(0) = k!A_k$.

**Proof of Proposition 9.7.2.** Throughout this proof, for every $K \in \mathbb{N}_0$ let $F_K : \mathbb{R}^m \to \mathbb{R}^n$ satisfy for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^m$ that

$$F_K(x) = f(0) + \sum_{k=1}^{K} A_k(x, x, \dots, x). \tag{9.69}$$

Note that (9.65) ensures that for all $x \in U$ it holds that

$$\lim \sup_{K \to \infty} \| f(x) - F_K(x) \|_2 = 0. \tag{9.70}$$

Therefore, we obtain for all $x \in U$ that

$$\lim \sup_{K \to \infty} \| F_{K+1}(x) - F_K(x) \|_2 = 0. \tag{9.71}$$

This proves for all $x \in U$ that

$$\sup_{k \in \mathbb{N}} \| A_k(x, x, \dots, x) \|_2 = \sup_{K \in \mathbb{N}_0} \| F_{K+1}(x) - F_K(x) \|_2 < \infty. \tag{9.72}$$

Hence, we obtain for all $x \in \{u \in U : \|u\|_2 < \varepsilon\} \setminus \{0\}$ that

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \| A_k(x, x, \dots, x) \|_2 = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \left( \left[ \frac{\|x\|_2}{\varepsilon} \right]^k \left\| A_k \left( \frac{\varepsilon x}{\|x\|_2}, \frac{\varepsilon x}{\|x\|_2}, \dots, \frac{\varepsilon x}{\|x\|_2} \right) \right\|_2 \right) \\
&\hspace{5cm} \le \left[ \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \left[ \frac{\|x\|_2}{\varepsilon} \right]^k \right] \left[ \sup_{k \in \mathbb{N}} \left\| A_k \left( \frac{\varepsilon x}{\|x\|_2}, \frac{\varepsilon x}{\|x\|_2}, \dots, \frac{\varepsilon x}{\|x\|_2} \right) \right\|_2 \right] < \infty.
\end{aligned} \tag{9.73}
$$

This shows that for all $x \in \{u \in U : \|u\|_2 < \varepsilon\}$ it holds that

$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \| A_k(x, x, \dots, x) \|_2 < \infty. \tag{9.74}$$

Combining this with (9.65) establishes item (i). Observe that, for instance, Krantz & Parks [254, Proposition 2.2.3] implies items (ii) and (iii). Note that (9.68) implies item (iv). The proof of Proposition 9.7.2 is thus complete.

**Proposition 9.7.3** (Characterization for analytic functions). *Let* $m,n \in \mathbb{N}$, *let* $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{m}$ *be open, and let* $f \in C^{\infty}(U,\mathbb{R}^{n})$. *Then the following three statements are equivalent:*

-   *It holds that $f$ is analytic (cf. Definition 9.7.1).*
-   *It holds for all $x \in U$ that there exists $\varepsilon \in (0, \infty)$ such that for all $y \in \{ u \in U : \lVert x-u \rVert_2 < \varepsilon\}$ it holds that $\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k!} \lVert f^{(k)}(x)(y-x, y-x, \dots, y-x) \rVert_2 < \infty$ and*
    $$f(y) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k!} f^{(k)}(x)(y-x, y-x, \dots, y-x). \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad (9.75)$$
-   *It holds for all compact $\mathfrak{C} \subseteq U$ that there exists $c \in \mathbb{R}$ such that for all $x \in \mathfrak{C}$, $k \in \mathbb{N}$, $v \in \mathbb{R}^{m}$ it holds that*
    $$\lVert f^{(k)}(x)(v, v, \dots, v) \rVert_2 \leqslant k! \, c^{k} \lVert v \rVert_2^{k}. \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad (9.76)$$

*Proof of Proposition 9.7.3.* The equivalence is a direct consequence from Proposition 9.7.2. The proof of Proposition 9.7.3 is thus complete. $\Box$

## 9.8 Standard KL inequalities for one-dimensional analytic functions

In Section 9.6 above we have seen that one-dimensional polynomials are standard KL functions (see Corollary 9.6.5). In this section we verify that one-dimensional analytic functions are also standard KL functions (see Corollary 9.8.6 below). The main arguments for this statement are presented in the proof of Lemma 9.8.2 and are inspired by [129].

**Lemma 9.8.1.** *Let* $\varepsilon \in (0, \infty)$, *let* $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ *satisfy* $U = \{x \in \mathbb{R} : \lvert x \rvert \leqslant \varepsilon\}$, *let* $(a_k)_{k \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, *and let* $f \colon U \to \mathbb{R}$ *satisfy for all* $x \in U$ *that*

$$\lim_{K \to \infty} \sup \left| f(x) - f(0) - \sum_{k=1}^{K} a_k x^{k} \right| = 0. \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad (9.77)$$

*Then*

-   *it holds for all $x \in \{y \in U : \lvert y \rvert < \varepsilon\}$ that $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \lvert a_k \rvert \lvert x \rvert^{k} < \infty$ and*
    $$f(x) = f(0) + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} a_k x^{k}, \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad (9.78)$$
-   *it holds that $f \lvert_{\{y \in U : \lvert y \rvert < \varepsilon\}}$ is infinitely often differentiable,*
