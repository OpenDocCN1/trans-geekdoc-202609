
Proof of Lemma 6.1.4. Note that the fundamental theorem of calculus, the hypothesis that $\mathscr{G} \in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{9}, \mathbb{R}^{9})$, and (6.10) assure that for all $t \in (0, \infty)$ it holds that $\Theta \in C^{1}([0, \infty), \mathbb{R}^{9})$ and

$$\dot{\Theta}_{t} = \mathscr{G}(\Theta_{t}).\tag{6.13}$$

Combining this with the hypothesis that $\mathscr{G} \in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{9}, \mathbb{R}^{9})$ and the chain rule ensures that for all $t \in (0, \infty)$ it holds that $\Theta \in C^{2}([0, \infty), \mathbb{R}^{9})$ and

$$\ddot{\Theta}_{t} = \mathscr{G}^{\prime}(\Theta_{t})\dot{\Theta}_{t} = \mathscr{G}^{\prime}(\Theta_{t})\mathscr{G}(\Theta_{t}).\tag{6.14}$$

Theorem 6.1.3 and (6.13) therefore imply that

$$\begin{aligned} \Theta_{T+\gamma} &= \Theta_{T}+\gamma\dot{\Theta}_{T}+\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)\gamma^{2}\ddot{\Theta}_{T+r\gamma}\mathrm{d}r \\ &= \Theta_{T}+\gamma\mathscr{G}(\Theta_{T})+\gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)\mathscr{G}^{\prime}(\Theta_{T+r\gamma})\mathscr{G}(\Theta_{T+r\gamma})\mathrm{d}r. \end{aligned}\tag{6.15}$$

This and (6.10) demonstrate that

$$\begin{aligned} &\left\|\Theta_{T+\gamma}-\theta\right\|_{2} \\ &=\left\|\Theta_{T}+\gamma\mathscr{G}(\Theta_{T})+\gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)\mathscr{G}^{\prime}(\Theta_{T+r\gamma})\mathscr{G}(\Theta_{T+r\gamma})\mathrm{d}r-\left(\Theta_{T}+\gamma\mathscr{G}(\Theta_{T})\right)\right\|_{2} \\ &\leq \gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)\|\mathscr{G}^{\prime}(\Theta_{T+r\gamma})\mathscr{G}(\Theta_{T+r\gamma})\|_{2}\mathrm{d}r \\ &\leq c^{2}\gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{1} r\mathrm{d}r = \frac{c^{2}\gamma^{2}}{2} \leq c^{2}\gamma^{2}. \end{aligned}\tag{6.16}$$

The proof of Lemma 6.1.4 is thus complete.

□

**Corollary 6.1.5** (Local error of the Euler method for **GF ODEs**). Let $\mathfrak{d} \in \mathbb{N}$, $T, \gamma, c \in [0, \infty)$, $\mathscr{L} \in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{9}, \mathbb{R})$, $\Theta \in C([0, \infty), \mathbb{R}^{9})$, $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{9}$ *satisfy for all* $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^{9}$, $t \in [0, \infty)$ *that*

$$\Theta_{t} = \Theta_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}(\nabla\mathscr{L})(\Theta_{s})\mathrm{d}s, \quad \theta = \Theta_{T}-\gamma(\nabla\mathscr{L})(\Theta_{T}),\tag{6.17}$$

$$\| (\nabla\mathscr{L})(x)\|_{2} \leq c, \quad \text{and} \quad \|\text{Hess}\, \mathscr{L}(x) y\|_{2} \leq c\|y\|_{2}\tag{6.18}$$

(cf. Definition 3.3.4). *Then*

$$\|\Theta_{T+\gamma}-\theta\|_{2} \leq c^{2}\gamma^{2}.\tag{6.19}$$

*Proof of Corollary 6.1.5.* Throughout this proof, let $\mathcal{G}: \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d} \to \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$ that

$$\mathcal{G}(\theta) = - (\nabla\mathcal{L})(\theta).$$ (6.20)

Note that the fact that for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that $\Theta_t = \Theta_0 + \int_0^t \mathcal{G}(\Theta_s) \, \mathrm{d}s$, the fact that $\theta = \Theta_T + \gamma \mathcal{G}(\Theta_T)$, the fact that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$ it holds that $\|\mathcal{G}(x)\|_2 \leq c$, the fact that for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$ it holds that $\|\mathcal{G}'(x) y\|_2 \leq c \|\mathcal{G}(y)\|_2$, and Lemma 6.1.4 imply that $\|\Theta_{T+\gamma} - \theta\|_2 \leq c^2 \gamma^2$. The proof of Corollary 6.1.5 is thus complete.

## 6.1.3 Lyapunov-type stability for GD optimization

Corollary 5.6.3 in Section 5.6.2 and Corollary 5.6.5 in Section 5.6.3 in Chapter 5 above, in particular, illustrate how Lyapunov-type functions can be employed to establish convergence properties for GFBs. Roughly speaking, the next two results, Proposition 6.1.6 and Corollary 6.1.7 below, are the time-discrete analogs of Corollary 5.6.3 and Corollary 5.6.5, respectively.

**Proposition 6.1.6** (*Lyapunov-type stability for discrete-time dynamical systems*). Let $\mathfrak{n} \in \mathbb{N}$, $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$, $c \in (0, \infty)$, $(\gamma_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subset [0, c]$, let $V\colon \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d} \to \mathbb{R}$, $\Phi\colon \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d} \times [0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$, and $\varepsilon\colon [0, c] \to [0, \infty)$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$, $t \in [0, c]$ that

$$V(\Phi(\theta, t)) \leq \varepsilon(t) V(\theta),$$ (6.21)

and let $\Theta\colon \mathbb{N}_0 \to \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$ satisfy for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ that

$$\Theta_0 = \xi \quad \text{and} \quad \Theta_n = \Phi(\Theta_{n-1}, \gamma_n).$$ (6.22)

Then it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ that

$$V(\Theta_n) \leq \biggl[\prod_{k=1}^{n} \varepsilon(\gamma_k)\biggr] V(\xi).$$ (6.23)

*Proof of Proposition 6.1.6.* We prove (6.23) by induction on $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$. For the base case $n=0$ note that the assumption that $\Theta_0 = \xi$ ensures that $V(\Theta_0) = V(\xi)$. This establishes (6.23) in the base case $n=0$. For the induction step observe that (6.22) and (6.21) ensure that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ with $V(\Theta_n) \leq (\prod_{k=1}^{n} \varepsilon(\gamma_k)) V(\xi)$ it holds that

$$\begin{align*} V(\Theta_{n+1}) &= V(\Phi(\Theta_n, \gamma_{n+1})) \leq \varepsilon(\gamma_{n+1}) V(\Theta_n) \\ &\leq \varepsilon(\gamma_{n+1}) \biggl[\prod_{k=1}^{n} \varepsilon(\gamma_k)\biggr] V(\xi) = \biggl[\prod_{k=1}^{n+1} \varepsilon(\gamma_k)\biggr] V(\xi). \end{align*}$$ (6.24)

Induction thus establishes (6.23). The proof of Proposition 6.1.6 is thus complete.

**Corollary 6.1.7** (On quadratic Lyapunov-type functions for the GD optimization method). Let $\mathfrak{d} \in \mathbb{N}$, $\vartheta, \xi \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$, $c \in (0, \infty)$, $(\gamma_{n})_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq [0, c]$, $\mathscr{L} \in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}, \mathbb{R})$, let $\|\cdot\| : \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} \rightarrow [0, \infty)$ be a norm, let $\varepsilon : [0, c] \rightarrow [0, \infty)$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$, $t \in [0, c]$ that

$$\|\theta - t(\nabla \mathscr{L})(\theta) - \vartheta\|^{2} \leq \varepsilon(t)\|\theta - \vartheta\|^{2}, \quad(6.25)$$

and let $\Theta: \mathbb{N}_{0} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ satisfy for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ that

$$\Theta_{0} = \xi \quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\Theta_{n} = \Theta_{n - 1} - \gamma_{n}(\nabla \mathscr{L})(\Theta_{n - 1}). \quad(6.26)$$

Then it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_{0}$ that

$$\|\Theta_{n} - \vartheta\| \leq \bigg[\prod_{k = 1}^{n}\varepsilon(\gamma_{k})\bigg]^{1/2}\|\xi - \vartheta\|. \quad(6.27)$$

*Proof of Corollary 6.1.7.* Throughout this proof, let $V : \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ and $\Phi : \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} \times [0, \infty) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$, $t \in [0, \infty)$ that

$$V(\theta) = \|\theta - \vartheta\|^{2}\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\Phi(\theta, t) = \theta - t(\nabla \mathscr{L})(\theta). \quad(6.28)$$

Observe that Proposition 6.1.6 (applied with $V \curvearrowright V$, $\Phi \curvearrowright \Phi$ in the notation of Proposition 6.1.6) and (6.28) imply that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_{0}$ it holds that

$$\|\Theta_{n} - \vartheta\|^{2} = V(\Theta_{n}) \leq \bigg[\prod_{k = 1}^{n}\varepsilon(\gamma_{k})\bigg]V(\xi) = \bigg[\prod_{k = 1}^{n}\varepsilon(\gamma_{k})\bigg]\|\xi - \vartheta\|^{2}. \quad(6.29)$$

This establishes (6.27). The proof of Corollary 6.1.7 is thus complete.

Corollary 6.1.7, in particular, illustrates that the one-step Lyapunov stability assumption in (6.25) may provide us suitable estimates for the approximation errors associated to the GD optimization method; see (6.27) above. The next result, Lemma 6.1.8 below, now provides us sufficient conditions which ensure that the one-step Lyapunov stability condition in (6.25) is satisfied so that we are in the position to apply Corollary 6.1.7 above to obtain estimates for the approximation errors associated to the GD optimization method. Lemma 6.1.8 employs the growth condition and the coercivity-type condition in (5.189) in Corollary 5.7.5 above. Results similar to Lemma 6.1.8 can, for example, be found in [103, Remark 2.1] and [221, Lemma 2.1]. We will employ the statement of Lemma 6.1.8 in our error analysis for the GD optimization method in Section 6.1.4 below.

**Lemma 6.1.8** (Sufficient conditions for a one-step Lyapunov-type stability condition). Let $\mathfrak{d} \in \mathbb{N}$, let $\langle\cdot, \cdot\rangle : \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be a scalar product, let $\|\cdot\| : \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $v \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ that $\|v\| = \sqrt{\langle v, v\rangle}$, and let $c, L \in (0, \infty)$, $r \in (0, \infty]$, $\vartheta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$, $\mathbb{B} = \{w \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} : \|w - \vartheta\| \leq r\}$, $\mathscr{L} \in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}, \mathbb{R})$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{B}$ that

$$\langle\theta - \vartheta, (\nabla \mathscr{L})(\theta)\rangle \geq c\|\theta - \vartheta\|^{2}\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\|(\nabla \mathscr{L})(\theta)\| \leq L\|\theta - \vartheta\|. \quad(6.30)$$

Then
