## **Lemma 4.4.7.**

Let $L, \mathfrak{L} \in \mathbb{N}$, $l_0, l_1, \dots, l_L, \mathfrak{l}_0, \mathfrak{l}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{l}_\mathfrak{L} \in \mathbb{N}$ satisfy

\mathfrak{L} \ge L, \qquad l_0 = \mathfrak{l}_0, \qquad \text{and} \qquad \mathfrak{l}_\mathfrak{L} = l_L,

assume for all $i \in \mathbb{N} \cap [0, L)$ that $l_i \ge l_\mathfrak{i}$, assume for all $i \in \mathbb{N} \cap (L-1, \mathfrak{L})$ that $\mathfrak{l}_i \ge 2 l_L$, and let $\Phi \in \mathbb{N}$ satisfy $\mathcal{D}(\Phi) = (l_0, l_1, \dots, l_L)$ (cf. Definition 1.3.1). Then there exists $\Psi \in \mathbb{N}$ such that

$$
\mathcal{D}(\Psi) = (l_0, \mathfrak{l}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{l}_\mathfrak{L}), \quad \left\| \mathcal{T}(\Psi) \right\|_{\infty} \le \max \{1, \left\| \mathcal{T}(\Phi) \right\|_{\infty} \}, \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{R}_i^{\mathbb{N}}(\Psi) = \mathcal{R}_i^{\mathbb{N}}(\Phi)
$$

(cf. Definitions 1.2.4, 1.3.4, 1.3.5, and 3.3.4).

Proof of Lemma 4.4.7. Throughout this proof, let $\Xi \in \mathbf{N}$ satisfy $\Xi = \mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{I}_L}(\Phi)$ (cf. Definitions 2.2.6 and 2.2.8). Note that item (i) in Lemma 2.2.7 establishes that $\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{I}_{L}\mathfrak{c}) = (L, 2L, L_L) \in \mathbb{N}^{3}$.
