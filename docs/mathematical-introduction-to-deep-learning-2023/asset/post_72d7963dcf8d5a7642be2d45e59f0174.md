
## Lemma 6.3.3 (An explicit representation of momentum terms)

Let $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{N}$, $(\alpha_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, $(a_{n,k})_{(n,k) \in (\mathbb{N}_0)^2} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, $(\mathscr{G}_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}_0} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d}$, $(\mathbf{m}_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}_0} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^\mathbf{d}$ satisfy for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $\mathbf{k} \in \{0, 1, \dots, n-1\}$ that

$$
\mathbf{m}_0 = \mathbf{0}, \quad \mathbf{m}_n = \alpha_n \mathbf{m}_{n-1} + (1 - \alpha_n)\mathscr{G}_{n-1}, \quad \text{and} \quad a_{n,k} = (1 - \alpha_{k+1}) \left[ \prod_{l=k+2}^{n} \alpha_l \right] \tag{6.196}
$$

Then

- (i) it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ that

$$
\mathbf{m}_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} a_{n,k} \mathscr{G}_k \tag{6.197}
$$

- and
- (ii) it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ that

$$
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} a_{n,k} = 1 - \prod_{k=1}^{n} \alpha_k. \tag{6.198}
$$

### Proof of Lemma 6.3.3

Throughout this proof, let $(m_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}_0} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ that

$$
m_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} a_{n,k}. \tag{6.199}
$$

We now prove item (i) by induction on $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$. For the base case $n=0$ note that (6.196) ensures that

$$
\mathbf{m}_0 = \mathbf{0} = \sum_{k=0}^{-1} a_{n,k} \mathscr{G}_k. \tag{6.200}
$$

This establishes item (i) in the base case $n=0$. For the induction step note that (6.196) assures that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ with $\mathbf{m}_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} a_{n,k} \mathscr{G}_k$ it holds that

$$
\begin{split}
\mathbf{m}_{n+1} &= \alpha_{n+1} \mathbf{m}_n + (1 - \alpha_{n+1}) \mathscr{G}_n \\
&= \left[ \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \alpha_{n+1} a_{n,k} \mathscr{G}_k \right] + (1 - \alpha_{n+1}) \mathscr{G}_n \\
&= \left[ \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \alpha_{n+1} (1 - \alpha_{k+1}) \left[ \prod_{l=k+2}^{n} \alpha_l \right] \mathscr{G}_k \right] + (1 - \alpha_{n+1}) \mathscr{G}_n \\
&= \left[ \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (1 - \alpha_{k+1}) \left[ \prod_{l=k+2}^{n+1} \alpha_l \right] \mathscr{G}_k \right] + (1 - \alpha_{n+1}) \mathscr{G}_n \\
&= \sum_{k=0}^{n} (1 - \alpha_{k+1}) \left[ \prod_{l=k+2}^{n+1} \alpha_l \right] \mathscr{G}_k = \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_{n+1,k} \mathscr{G}_k.
\end{split} \tag{6.201}
$$

Induction thus proves item **(i)**. Furthermore, observe that (6.196) and (6.199) demonstrate that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that $m_0 = 0$ and

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{n} &= \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} a_{n,k} = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (1-\alpha_{k+1}) \left[ \prod_{l=k+2}^{n} \alpha_{l} \right] \\
&= 1 - \alpha_{n} + \sum_{k=0}^{n-2} (1-\alpha_{k+1}) \left[ \prod_{l=k+2}^{n} \alpha_{l} \right] \\
&= 1-\alpha_{n} + \sum_{k=0}^{n-2} (1-\alpha_{k+1}) \left[ \prod_{l=k+2}^{n} \alpha_{l} \right] \\
&= 1-\alpha_{n} + \alpha_{n} \sum_{k=0}^{n-2} a_{n-1,k} = 1-\alpha_{n} + \alpha_{n} m_{n-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Combining this with Lemma 6.3.2 implies that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_{0}$ it holds that

$$
m_{n} = 1 - \prod_{k=1}^{n} \alpha_{k}.
$$

This establishes item **(ii)**. The proof of Lemma 6.3.3 is thus complete.

## Corollary 6.3.4 (On a representation of the momentum GD optimization method)

Let $d \in \mathbb{N}$, $(\gamma_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq [0, \infty)$, $(a_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq [0, 1]$, $(a_{n,k})_{(n,k) \in \mathbb{N}_0^2} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^d$ satisfy for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $k \in \{0, 1, \ldots, n-1\}$ that

$$
a_{n,k} = (1-\alpha_{k+1}) \left[ \prod_{l=k+2}^{n} \alpha_{l} \right] \tag{6.204}
$$

let $\mathcal{L}: \mathbb{R}^d \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ and $\mathcal{G}: \mathbb{R}^d \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$ satisfy for all $U \in \{V \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d: V \text{ is open}\}$, $\theta \in U$ with $\mathcal{L}|_U \in C^1(U, \mathbb{R}^d)$ that

$$
\mathcal{G}(\theta) = (\nabla \mathcal{L})(\theta) \tag{6.205}
$$

and let $\Theta$ be the momentum GD process for the objective function $\mathcal{L}$ with generalized gradient $\mathcal{G}$, learning rates $(\gamma_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, momentum decay factors $(\alpha_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, and initial value $\xi$ (cf. Definition 6.3.1). Then

- (i) it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $k \in \{0, 1, \ldots, n-1\}$ that $0 \leq a_{n,k} \leq 1$,
- (ii) it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ that

$$
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} a_{n,k} = 1 - \prod_{k=1}^{n} \alpha_{k} \tag{6.206}
$$

- and
- (iii) it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ that

$$
\Theta_{n} = \Theta_{n-1} - \gamma_{n} \left[ \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} a_{n,k} \mathcal{G}(\Theta_{k}) \right]. \tag{6.207}
$$

### Proof of Corollary 6.3.4

Throughout this proof, let $\mathbf{m}\colon\mathbb{N}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ satisfy for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$ that

$$
\mathbf{m}_{0}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{m}_{n}=\alpha_{n}\mathbf{m}_{n-1}+(1-\alpha_{n})\mathcal{G}(\Theta_{n-1}). \tag{6.208}
$$

Note that (6.204) implies item (i). Observe that (6.204), (6.208), and Lemma 6.3.3 assure that for all $n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}$ it holds that

$$
\mathbf{m}_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{n,k}\mathcal{G}(\Theta_{k})\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{n,k}=1-\prod_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}. \tag{6.209}
$$

This proves item (ii). Note that (6.189), (6.190), (6.191), (6.208), and (6.209) demonstrate that for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$ it holds that

$$
\Theta_{n}=\Theta_{n-1}-\gamma_{n}\mathbf{m}_{n}=\Theta_{n-1}-\gamma_{n}\Big[\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{n,k}\mathcal{G}(\Theta_{k})\Big]. \tag{6.210}
$$

This establishes item (iii). The proof of Corollary 6.3.4 is thus complete. $\square$

## 6.3.2 Bias-adjusted GD optimization with momentum

### Definition 6.3.5 (Bias-adjusted momentum GD optimization method)

Let $\mathfrak{d}\in\mathbb{N}$, $(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq[0,\infty)$, $(\alpha_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq[0,1]$, $\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ satisfy $\alpha_{1}<1$ and let $\mathcal{L}\colon\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}\to\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathcal{G}\colon\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ satisfy for all $U\in\{V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}:V\text{ is open}\}$, $\theta\in U$ with $\mathcal{L}\big|_{U}\in C^{1}(U,\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}})$ that

$$
\mathcal{G}(\theta)=(\nabla\mathcal{L})(\theta). \tag{6.211}
$$

Then we say that $\Theta$ is the bias-adjusted momentum GD process for the objective function $\mathcal{L}$ with generalized gradient $\mathcal{G}$, learning rates $(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$, momentum decay factors $(\alpha_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$, and initial value $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ (we say that $\Theta$ is the bias-adjusted momentum GD process for the objective function $\mathcal{L}$ with learning rates $(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$, momentum decay factors $(\alpha_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$, and initial value $\boldsymbol{\xi}$) if and only if it holds that $\Theta\colon\mathbb{N}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ is the function from $\mathbb{N}_{0}$ to $\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ which satisfies that there exists $\mathbf{m}\colon\mathbb{N}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ such that for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$ it holds that

$$
\Theta_{0}=\boldsymbol{\xi},\quad\quad\mathbf{m}_{0}=0, \tag{6.212}
$$

$$
\mathbf{m}_{n}=\alpha_{n}\mathbf{m}_{n-1}+(1-\alpha_{n})\mathcal{G}(\Theta_{n-1}), \tag{6.213}
$$

and

$$
\Theta_{n}=\Theta_{n-1}-\frac{\gamma_{n}\mathbf{m}_{n}}{1-\prod_{l=1}^{n}\alpha_{l}}. \tag{6.214}
$$

### Corollary 6.3.6 (On a representation of the bias-adjusted momentum GD optimization method)

Let $\mathfrak{d}\in\mathbb{N}$, $(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq[0,\infty)$, $(\alpha_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq[0,1]$, $\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$, $(a_{n,k})_{(n,k)\in(\mathbb{N}_{0})^{2}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$, $k\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}$ that $\alpha_{1}<1$ and

$$
a_{n,k}=\frac{(1-\alpha_{k+1})\prod_{l=k+2}^{n}\alpha_{l}}{1-\prod_{l=1}^{n}\alpha_{l}}, \tag{6.215}
$$

let $\mathcal{L}: \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ and $\mathscr{G}: \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ satisfy for all $U \in \{V \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}: V\text{ is open}\}$, $\theta \in U$ with $\mathcal{L}\vert_U \in C^1(U, \mathbb{R})$ that

$$
\mathscr{G}(\theta) = (\nabla\mathcal{L})(\theta),
$$

and let $\Theta$ be the bias-adjusted momentum GD process for the objective function $\mathcal{L}$ with generalized gradient $\mathscr{G}$, learning rates $(\gamma_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, momentum decay factors $(\alpha_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, and initial value $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ (cf. Definition 6.3.5). Then

- (i) it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $k \in \{0, 1, \ldots, n-1\}$ that $0 \leq a_{n,k} \leq 1$,
- (ii) it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ that

$$
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{n,k} = 1, \tag{6.217}
$$

- and
- (iii) it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ that

$$
\Theta_n = \Theta_{n-1} - \gamma_n\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{n,k}\mathscr{G}(\Theta_k)\right]. \tag{6.218}
$$

### Proof of Corollary 6.3.6

Throughout this proof, let $\mathbf{m}: \mathbb{N}_0 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ satisfy for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ that

$$
\mathbf{m}_0 = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbf{m}_n = \alpha_n\mathbf{m}_{n-1} + (1-\alpha_n)\mathscr{G}(\Theta_{n-1}) \tag{6.219}
$$

and let $(b_{n,k})_{(n,k) \in (\mathbb{N}_0)^2} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $k \in \{0, 1, \ldots, n-1\}$ that

$$
b_{n,k} = (1-\alpha_{k+1})\left[\prod_{l=k+2}^n\alpha_l\right], \tag{6.220}
$$

Observe that (6.215) implies item (i). Note that (6.215), (6.219), (6.220), and Lemma 6.3.3 assure that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that

$$
\mathbf{m}_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}b_{n,k}\mathscr{G}(\Theta_k) \quad \text{and} \quad \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{n,k} = \frac{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}b_{n,k}}{1-\prod_{l=1}^n\alpha_l} = \frac{1-\prod_{l=1}^n\alpha_l}{1-\prod_{l=1}^n\alpha_l} = 1. \tag{6.221}
$$

This proves item (ii). Observe that (6.212), (6.213), (6.214), (6.219), and (6.221) demonstrate that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that

$$
\Theta_n = \Theta_{n-1} - \frac{\gamma_n\mathbf{m}_n}{1-\prod_{l=1}^n\alpha_l} = \Theta_{n-1} - \gamma_n\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{b_{n,k}}{1-\prod_{l=1}^k\alpha_l}\mathscr{G}(\Theta_k)\right]
$$

$$
= \Theta_{n-1} - \gamma_n\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{n,k}\mathscr{G}(\Theta_k)\right]. \tag{6.222}
$$

This establishes item (iii). The proof of Corollary 6.3.6 is thus complete.
