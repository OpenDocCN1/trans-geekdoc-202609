
proves that for all $\mathbf{z} = (z_1, \dots, z_d) \in [0, \infty)^d$ with $\sum_{i=1}^d z_i = \sum_{i=1}^d y_i$ and $\forall i \in J: z_i = y_i$ it holds that $\mathbf{z} = \mathbf{y}$. The assumption that $\sum_{i=1}^d x_i = \sum_{i=1}^d y_i$ and $\mathbf{x} \neq \mathbf{y}$ therefore ensures that there exists $i \in J$ such that $x_i \neq y_i > 0$. Combining this with (5.60) shows that for all $h \in [0, 1)$ it holds that

$$g''(h) > 0. \tag{5.62}$$

The fundamental theorem of calculus hence implies that for all $h \in (0, 1)$ it holds that

$$g'(h) = g'(0) + \int_0^h g''(b) \, \mathrm{d}b > g'(0). \tag{5.63}$$

In addition, note that (5.59) and the assumption that $\sum_{i=1}^d x_i = \sum_{i=1}^d y_i$ demonstrate that

$\begin{align*} g'(0) &= -\sum_{i \in J} \frac{y_i (x_i - y_i)}{y_i} = \sum_{i \in J} (y_i - x_i) = \left[ \sum_{i \in J} y_i \right] - \left[ \sum_{i \in J} x_i \right] \\ &= \left[ \sum_{i \in \{1,2,\dots,d\}} y_i \right] - \left[ \sum_{i \in J} x_i \right] = \left[ \sum_{i \in \{1,2,\dots,d\}} x_i \right] - \left[ \sum_{i \in J} x_i \right] = \left[ \sum_{i \in \{1,2,\dots,d\} \setminus J} x_i \right] \ge 0. \end{align*} \tag{5.64}

Combining this and (5.63) establishes that for all $h \in (0, 1)$ it holds that

$$g'(h) > 0. \tag{5.65}$$

Therefore, we obtain that $g$ is strictly increasing. This and (5.55) prove that $f|_{[0,1]}$ is strictly decreasing. Next observe that (5.55) and (5.58) ensure that for all $h \in (0, 1]$ it holds that

$$f(h) = -\sum_{i \in J} \ln(y_i + (1-h)(x_i - y_i)) y_i = -\sum_{i \in J} \ln(x_i + h(y_i - x_i)) y_i \in \mathbb{R}. \tag{5.66}$$

Furthermore, note that items (i) and (ii) in Lemma 5.4.6 show that

$$\left[ f(0) = \infty \right] \lor \left[ f(0) = -\sum_{i \in J} \ln(x_i + 0(y_i - x_i)) y_i \in \mathbb{R} \right]. \tag{5.67}$$

Combining this with (5.66) implies that

$$\left[ f(0) = \infty \right] \lor \left[ \forall h \in [0, 1]: f(h) \in \mathbb{R} \right] \land \left( [0, 1] \ni h \mapsto f(h) \in \mathbb{R} \right) \in C([0, 1], \mathbb{R}]). \tag{5.68}$$

This and the fact that $f|_{[0,1]}$ is strictly decreasing demonstrate that $f$ is strictly decreasing. The proof of Lemma 5.4.7 is thus complete.

**Corollary 5.4.8.** *Let $d \in \mathbb{N} \setminus \{1\}$, let $A = \{ \mathbf{x} = (x_1, \dots, x_d) \in [0, 1]^d: \sum_{i=1}^d x_i = 1 \}$, let $\mathbf{L}$ be the $d$-dimensional cross-entropy loss function, and let $\mathbf{y} \in A$ (cf. Definition 5.4.5). Then*

*(i)* it holds that

$\{x \in A: \mathbf{L}(x, y) = \inf_{z \in A} \mathbf{L}(z, y)\} = \{y\}$

and

*(ii)* it holds that

$\inf_{z \in A} \mathbf{L}(z, y) = \mathbf{L}(y, y) = -\sum_{i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, d\}, \; y_i \neq 0} \ln(y_i)y_i.$

Proof of Corollary 5.4.8. Observe that Lemma 5.4.7 shows that for all $x \in A \setminus \{y\}$ it holds that

$\mathbf{L}(x, y) = \mathbf{L}(x + 0(y - x), y) > \mathbf{L}(x + 1(y - x), y) = \mathbf{L}(y, y).$

This and item (ii) in Lemma 5.4.6 establish items (i) and (ii). The proof of Corollary 5.4.8 is thus complete. $\square$

# 5.4.5 Kullback–Leibler divergence loss

Lemma 5.4.9. Let $z \in (0, \infty)$. Then

*(i)* it holds that

$\liminf_{x \downarrow 0} [\ln(x)x] = 0 = \limsup_{x \downarrow 0} [\ln(x)x]$

and

*(ii)* it holds for all $y \in [0, \infty)$ that

$\liminf_{x \uparrow y} [\ln(\frac{x}{y})x] = \begin{cases} 0 & : y = 0 \\ \ln(\frac{y}{y})y & : y > 0 \end{cases} = \limsup_{x \uparrow y} [\ln(\frac{x}{y})x].$

Proof of Lemma 5.4.9. Throughout this proof, let $f: (0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ and $g: (0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $x \in (0, \infty)$ that

$f(x) = \ln(x^{-1}) \quad \text{and} \quad g(x) = x.$

Note that the chain rule proves that for all $x \in (0, \infty)$ it holds that $f$ is differentiable and

$f'(x) = -x^{-2}(x^{-1})^{-1} = -x^{-1}.$

Combining this, the fact that $\lim_{x \to \infty} |f(x)| = \infty = \lim_{x \to 0} |g(x)|$, the fact that $g$ is differentiable, the fact that for all $x \in (0, \infty)$ it holds that $g'(x) = 1 \neq 0$, and the fact that $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{-x^{-1}}{1} = 0$ with l'Hôpital's rule ensures that

$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = 0 = \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)}.$

This shows that

$$\lim_{{x \searrow 0}} \inf \frac{{f({x^{ - 1}})}}{{g({x^{ - 1}})}} = 0 = \lim_{{x \searrow 0}} \sup \frac{{f({x^{ - 1}})}}{{g({x^{ - 1}})}} .\qquad (5.77)$$

The fact that for all $x \in (0, \infty)$ it holds that $\frac{{f({x^{ - 1}})}}{{g({x^{ - 1}})}} = \ln (x)x$ hence establishes item **(i)**. Observe that item **(i)** and the fact that for all $x \in (0, \infty)$ it holds that $\ln (\frac{z}{x})x = \ln (z)x - \ln (x)x$ prove item **(ii)**. The proof of Lemma 5.4.9 is thus complete. $\square$

**Definition 5.4.10.** *Let* $d \in \mathbb{N}\backslash\{1\}$*. We say that* $\mathbf{L}$ *is the* $d$*-dimensional Kullback-Leibler divergence loss function if and only if it holds that* $\mathbf{L}\colon [0, \infty)^d \times [0, \infty)^d \rightarrow ( - \infty, \infty]$ *is the function from* $[0, \infty)^d \times [0, \infty)^d$ *to* $( - \infty, \infty]$ *which satisfies for all* $x = (x_1, \ldots, x_d)$, $y = (y_1, \ldots, y_d) \in [0, \infty)^d$ *that*

$$\mathbf{L}(x, y) = - \sum_{i = 1}^{d}\lim_{z \searrow x_i}\lim_{w \searrow y_i}\ln (\frac{z}{w})u\qquad (5.78)$$

(cf. Lemma 5.4.9).

**Lemma 5.4.11.** Let $d \in \mathbb{N} \setminus \{1\}$, let $\mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}$ be the $d$-dimensional cross-entropy loss function, and let $\mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{KLD}}$ be the $d$-dimensional Kullback-Leibler divergence loss function (cf. Definitions 5.4.5 and 5.4.10). Then it holds for all $x,y \in [0,\infty)^d$ that

$\mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(x, y) = \mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{KLD}}(x, y) + \mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(y, y).$ (5.79)

**Proof of Lemma 5.4.11.** Note that Lemma 5.4.9 implies that for all $a,b \in [0,\infty)$ it holds that

$\begin{aligned} \lim_{z\searrow a} \lim_{u\searrow b} \left[\ln \left(\frac{z}{u}\right) u\right] &= \lim_{z\searrow a} \lim_{u\searrow b} [\ln(z)u - \ln(u)u] \\
&= \lim_{z\searrow a} \left[\ln(z)b - \lim_{u\searrow b}[\ln(u)u]\right] \\
&= \left(\lim_{z\searrow a}[\ln(z)b]\right) - \left(\lim_{u\searrow b}[\ln(u)u]\right). \end{aligned}$ (5.80)

This and (5.78) demonstrate that for all $x = (x_1,\dots,x_d)$, $y = (y_1,\dots,y_d) \in [0,\infty)^d$ it holds that

$\begin{aligned} \mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{KLD}}(x,y) &= -\sum_{i=1}^{d} \lim_{z\searrow x_i} \lim_{u\searrow y_i} \left[\ln \left(\frac{z}{u}\right) u\right] \\
&= -\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d} \lim_{z\searrow x_i} [\ln(z)y_i]\right) + \left(\sum_{i=1}^{d} \lim_{u\searrow y_i} [\ln(u)u]\right). \end{aligned}$ (5.81)

Furthermore, observe that Lemma 5.4.9 ensures that for all \( b \in [0, \infty) \) it holds that

$$\lim_{u \searrow b} [\ln(u)b] = \begin{cases} 0 & : b = 0 \\ \ln(b) & : b > 0 \end{cases} . \tag{5.82}$$

Combining this with (5.81) shows that for all \( x = (x_1, \ldots, x_d) \), \( y = (y_1, \ldots, y_d) \in [0, \infty)^d \) it holds that

$$\mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{KLD}}(x, y) = -\left( \sum_{i=1}^{d} \lim_{z \searrow x_i} [\ln(z)x_i] \right) + \left( \sum_{i=1}^{d} \lim_{u \searrow y_i} [\ln(u)y_i] \right) = \mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(x, y) - \mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(y, y). \tag{5.83}$$

Therefore, we obtain (5.79). The proof of Lemma 5.4.11 is thus complete.

**Lemma 5.4.12.** Let \( d \in \mathbb{N} \setminus \{1\} \), let \( \mathbf{L} \) be the $d$-dimensional Kullback–Leibler loss function, let \( x = (x_1, \ldots, x_d) \), \( y = (y_1, \ldots, y_d) \in [0, \infty)^d \) satisfy \( \sum_{i=1}^{d} x_i = \sum_{i=1}^{d} y_i \) and \( x \neq y \), and let \( f \) : \([0, 1] \rightarrow (-\infty, \infty] \) satisfy for all \( h \in [0, 1] \) that

$$f(h) = \mathbf{L}(x + h(y - x), y) \tag{5.84}$$

(cf. Definition 5.4.10). Then \( f \) is strictly decreasing.

Proof of Lemma 5.4.12. Note that Lemma 5.4.7 and Lemma 5.4.11 establish (5.84). The proof of Lemma 5.4.12 is thus complete.

**Corollary 5.4.13.** Let \( d \in \mathbb{N} \setminus \{1\} \), let \( A = \{x = (x_1, \ldots, x_d) \in [0, 1]^d \) : \( \sum_{i=1}^{d} x_i = 1 \}\), let \( \mathbf{L} \) be the $d$-dimensional Kullback–Leibler divergence loss function, and let \( y \in A \) (cf. Definition 5.4.10). Then

- (i) it holds that

- and
- (ii) it holds that \( \inf_{z \in A} \mathbf{L}(z, y) = \mathbf{L}(y, y) = 0 \).

Proof of Corollary 5.4.13. Observe that Corollary 5.4.8 and Lemma 5.4.11 prove items (i) and (ii). The proof of Corollary 5.4.13 is thus complete.
