
## 6.3.3 Error analysis for GD optimization with momentum

In this subsection we provide in Section 6.3.3.2 below an error analysis for the momentum GD optimization method in the case of a class of quadratic objective functions (cf. Proposition 6.3.11 in Section 6.3.3.2 for the precise statement). In this specific case we also provide in Section 6.3.3.3 below a comparison of the convergence speeds of the plain-vanilla GD optimization method and the momentum GD optimization method. In particular, we prove, roughly speaking, that the momentum GD optimization method outperforms the plain-vanilla GD optimization method in the case of the considered class of quadratic objective functions; see Corollary 6.3.13 in Section 6.3.3.3 for the precise statement. For this comparison between the plain-vanilla GD optimization method and the momentum GD optimization method we employ a refined error analysis of the plain-vanilla GD optimization method for the considered class of quadratic objective functions. This refined error analysis is the subject of the next section (Section 6.3.3.1 below).

In the literature similar error analyses for the momentum GD optimization method can, for instance, be found in [48, Section 7.1] and [337].

### 6.3.3.1 Error analysis for GD optimization in the case of quadratic objective functions

**Lemma 6.3.7** (Error analysis for the GD optimization method in the case of quadratic objective functions). Let $d \in \mathbb{N}$, $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^d$, $v = (v_1, \dots, v_d) \in \mathbb{R}^d$, $\kappa, K, \lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots, \lambda_d \in (0, \infty)$ satisfy $\kappa = \min\{\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots, \lambda_d\}$ and $K = \max\{\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots, \lambda_d\}$, let $\mathcal{L}: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $\theta = (\theta_1, \dots, \theta_d) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ that

$$
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^d \lambda_i |\theta_i - v_i|^2 \right], \quad (6.223)
$$

and let $\Theta: \mathbb{N}_0 \to \mathbb{R}^d$ satisfy for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ that

$$
\Theta_0 = \xi \quad \text{and} \quad \Theta_n = \Theta_{n-1} - \frac{2}{(\kappa+K)} (\nabla \mathcal{L})(\Theta_{n-1}). \quad (6.224)
$$

Then it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ that

$$
\|\Theta_n - v\|_2 \leq \left[ \frac{K-\kappa}{K+\kappa} \right]^n \|\xi - v\|_2 \quad (6.225)
$$

(cf. Definition 3.3.4).

**Proof of Lemma 6.3.7.** Throughout this proof, let $\Theta^{(1)}, \Theta^{(2)}, \dots, \Theta^{(d)}: \mathbb{N}_0 \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ that $\Theta_n = (\Theta_n^{(1)}, \Theta_n^{(2)}, \dots, \Theta_n^{(d)})$. Note that (6.223) implies that for all $\theta = (\theta_1, \theta_2, \dots, \theta_d) \in \mathbb{R}^d$, $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, d\}$ it holds that

$$
\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta_i}\right)(\theta) = \lambda_i(\theta_i - v_i). \quad (6.226)
$$

Combining this and (6.224) ensures that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, d\}$ it holds that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Theta_n^{(i)} - v_i &= \Theta_{n-1}^{(i)} - \frac{2}{(\kappa+K)} \left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial v_i}\right)_{n-1}(\Theta_{n-1}) - v_i \\
&= \Theta_{n-1}^{(i)} - v_i - \frac{2}{(\kappa+K)} \left[\lambda_i(\Theta_{n-1}^{(i)} - v_i)\right] \\
&= \left(1 - \frac{2\lambda_i}{(\kappa+K)}\right)(\Theta_{n-1}^{(i)} - v_i). \quad (6.227)
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, we obtain that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|\Theta_n - v\|_2^2 &= \sum_{i=1}^d \|\Theta_n^{(i)} - v_i\|^2 \\
&= \sum_{i=1}^d \left[1 - \frac{2\lambda_i}{(\kappa+K)}\right]^2 \left[\|\Theta_{n-1}^{(i)} - v_i\|^2\right] \\
&\leq \left[\max\left\{\left[1 - \frac{2\lambda_1}{(\kappa+K)}\right]^2, \dots, \left[1 - \frac{2\lambda_d}{(\kappa+K)}\right]^2\right\}\right] \left[\sum_{i=1}^d \|\Theta_{n-1}^{(i)} - v_i\|^2\right] \\
&= \left[\max\left\{\left|1 - \frac{2\lambda_1}{(\kappa+K)}\right|, \dots, \left|1 - \frac{2\lambda_d}{(\kappa+K)}\right|\right\}\right]^2 \|\Theta_{n-1} - v\|_2^2 \quad (6.228)
\end{aligned}
$$

(cf. Definition 3.3.4). Moreover, note that the fact that for all $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, d\}$ it holds that $\lambda_i \geq \kappa$ implies that for all $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, d\}$ it holds that

$$
1 - \frac{2\lambda_i}{(\kappa+K)} \leq 1 - \frac{2\kappa}{(\kappa+K)} = \frac{\kappa+K-2\kappa}{\kappa+K} = \frac{K-\kappa}{\kappa+K} \geq 0. \quad (6.229)
$$

In addition, observe that the fact that for all $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, d\}$ it holds that $\lambda_i \leq K$ implies that for all $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, d\}$ it holds that

$$
1 - \frac{2\lambda_i}{(\kappa+K)} \geq 1 - \frac{2K}{(\kappa+K)} = \frac{\kappa+K-2K}{\kappa+K} = -\left[\frac{K-\kappa}{\kappa+K}\right] \leq 0. \quad (6.230)
$$

This and (6.229) ensure that for all $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, d\}$ it holds that

$$
\left|1 - \frac{2\lambda_i}{\kappa+K}\right| \leq \frac{K-\kappa}{\kappa+K}. \quad (6.231)
$$

Combining this with (6.228) demonstrates that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|\Theta_n - v\|_2 &\leq \left[\max\left\{\left|1 - \frac{2\lambda_1}{\kappa+K}\right|, \dots, \left|1 - \frac{2\lambda_d}{\kappa+K}\right|\right\}\right] \|\Theta_{n-1} - v\|_2 \\
&\leq \left[\frac{K-\kappa}{\kappa+K}\right] \|\Theta_{n-1} - v\|_2. \quad (6.232)
\end{aligned}
$$

Induction therefore establishes that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ it holds that

$$
\|\Theta_n - v\|_2 \leq \left[\frac{K-\kappa}{\kappa+K}\right]^n \|\Theta_0 - v\|_2 = \left[\frac{K-\kappa}{\kappa+K}\right]^n \|\xi - v\|_2. \quad (6.233)
$$

The proof of Lemma 6.3.7 is thus complete. $\square$

Lemma 6.3.7 above establishes, roughly speaking, the convergence rate $\frac{K-\kappa}{K+\kappa}$ (see (6.225) above for the precise statement) for the GD optimization method in the case of the objective function in (6.223). The next result, Lemma 6.3.8 below, essentially proves in the situation of Lemma 6.3.7 that this convergence rate cannot be improved by means of a different choice of the learning rate.

**Lemma 6.3.8** (Lower bound for the convergence rate of GD for quadratic objective functions). Let $d \in \mathbb{N}$, $\xi = (\xi_1, \xi_2, \ldots, \xi_d) \in \mathbb{R}^d$, $v = (v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_d) \in \mathbb{R}^d$, $\gamma, \kappa, K, \lambda_1, \lambda_2, \ldots, \lambda_d \in (0, \infty)$ satisfy $\kappa = \min\{\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \ldots, \lambda_d\}$ and $K = \max\{\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \ldots, \lambda_d\}$, let $\mathcal{L}: \mathbb{R}^d \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $\theta = (\theta_1, \theta_2, \ldots, \theta_d) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ that

$$
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^d \lambda_i |\theta_i - v_i|^2 \right], \quad (6.234)
$$

and let $\Theta: \mathbb{N}_0 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$ satisfy for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ that

$$
\Theta_0 = \xi \quad \text{and} \quad \Theta_n = \Theta_{n-1} - \gamma \left( \nabla \mathcal{L} \right)(\Theta_{n-1}). \quad (6.235)
$$

Then it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\| \Theta_n - v \|_2 &\geq \left[ \max\{\gamma K - 1, 1 - \gamma \kappa\} \right]^n \left[ \min\{ |\xi_1 - v_1|, \ldots, |\xi_d - v_d| \} \right] \\
&\geq \left[ \frac{K-\kappa}{K+\kappa} \right]^n \left[ \min\{ |\xi_1 - v_1|, \ldots, |\xi_d - v_d| \} \right] \quad (6.236)
\end{aligned}
$$

(cf. Definition 3.3.4).

**Proof of Lemma 6.3.8.** Throughout this proof, let $\Theta^{(1)}, \Theta^{(2)}, \ldots, \Theta^{(d)}: \mathbb{N}_0 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ that $\Theta_n = (\Theta_n^{(1)}, \Theta_n^{(2)}, \ldots, \Theta_n^{(d)})$ and let $\iota, \mathcal{I} \in \{1, 2, \ldots, d\}$ satisfy $\lambda_{\iota} = \kappa$ and $\lambda_{\mathcal{I}} = K$. Observe that (6.234) implies that for all $\theta = (\theta_1, \theta_2, \ldots, \theta_d) \in \mathbb{R}^d$, $i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, d\}$ it holds that

$$
\left( \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta_i} \right)(\theta) = \lambda_i (\theta_i - v_i). \quad (6.237)
$$

Combining this with (6.235) implies that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, d\}$ it holds that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Theta_n^{(i)} - v_i &= \Theta_{n-1}^{(i)} - \gamma \left( \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta_i} \right)(\Theta_{n-1}) - v_i \\
&= \Theta_{n-1}^{(i)} - v_i - \gamma \lambda_i (\Theta_{n-1}^{(i)} - v_i) \\
&= (1 - \gamma \lambda_i)(\Theta_{n-1}^{(i)} - v_i). \quad (6.238)
\end{aligned}
$$

Induction hence proves that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$, $i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, d\}$ it holds that

$$
\Theta_n^{(i)} - v_i = (1 - \gamma \lambda_i)^n (\Theta_0^{(i)} - v_i) = (1 - \gamma \lambda_i)^n (\xi_i - v_i). \quad (6.239)
$$
