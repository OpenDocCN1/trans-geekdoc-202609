In our proof of (5.146) we distinguish between the case \(\varepsilon(0)=0\) and the case \(\varepsilon(0)>0\). We first prove (5.146) in the case

\(\varepsilon(0)=0\). (5.147)

Observe that (5.147), the assumption that \(r \in(0, \infty]\), and the fact that \(\epsilon:[0, T] \rightarrow[0, \infty)\) is a continuous function show that

\(\tau=\inf(\{t \in[0, T]: \epsilon(t)>r^{2}\} \cup\{T\})>0\). (5.148)

This establishes (5.146) in the case \(\varepsilon(0)=0\). In the next step we prove (5.146) in the case

\(\varepsilon(0)>0\). (5.149)

Note that (5.143) and the assumption that \(c \in(0, \infty)\) assure that for all \(t \in[0, \tau]\) with \(\epsilon(t)>0\) it holds that

\(\epsilon^{\prime}(t) \leq-2 c \epsilon(t)<0\). (5.150)

Combining this with (5.149) shows that

\(\epsilon^{\prime}(0)<0\). (5.151)

The fact that \(\epsilon^{\prime}:[0, T] \rightarrow[0, \infty)\) is a continuous function and the assumption that \(T \in(0, \infty)\) therefore demonstrate that

\(\inf(\{t \in[0, T]: \epsilon^{\prime}(t)>0\} \cup\{T\})>0\). (5.152)

Next note that the fundamental theorem of calculus and the assumption that \(\xi \in \mathbb{B}\) imply that for all \(s \in[0, T]\) with \(s<\inf(\{t \in[0, T]: \epsilon^{\prime}(t)>0\} \cup\{T\})\) it holds that

\(\epsilon(s)=\epsilon(0)+\int_{0}^{s} \epsilon^{\prime}(u) d u \leq \epsilon(0)=\|\xi-\vartheta\|_{2}^{2} \leq r^{2}\). (5.153)

Combining this with (5.152) proves that

\(\tau=\inf(\{s \in[0, T]: \epsilon(s)>r^{2}\} \cup\{T\})>0\). (5.154)

This establishes (5.146) in the case \(\varepsilon(0)>0\). Observe that (5.145), (5.146), and the assumption that \(c \in(0, \infty)\) demonstrate that

\(\left\|\Theta_{\tau}-\vartheta\right\|_{2} \leq\|\xi-\vartheta\|_{2} e^{-c \tau}<r\). (5.155)

The fact that \(\epsilon:[0, T] \rightarrow[0, \infty)\) is a continuous function, (5.140), and (5.146) hence assure that \(\tau=T\). Combining this with (5.145) proves that for all \(t \in[0, T]\) it holds that

\(\left\|\Theta_{t}-\vartheta\right\|_{2} \leq\|\xi-\vartheta\|_{2} e^{-c t}\). (5.156)
