*Proof of Lemma 9.15.3.* Throughout this proof, for every $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^9$ let $Z^{x, y} = (Z_1^{x, y}$,
$Z_2^{x, y}) : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}^9 \times \mathbb{R}^9$ satisfy for all $k \in \mathbb{N}$ that

$Z_1^{x, y}(k) = x \quad \text{and} \quad Z_2^{x, y}(k) = y$. (9.289)

Note that (9.284) proves that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^9$, $y \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(x)$, $\varepsilon \in (0, \infty)$ it holds that

$y \in \overline{\bigcup_{v \in \{w \in \mathbb{R}^9: \|x-w\|_2 < \varepsilon\}} (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(v)}$. (9.290)

This implies that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^9$, $y \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(x)$ and all $\varepsilon, \delta \in (0, \infty)$ there exists $Y \in
(\bigcup_{v \in \{w \in \mathbb{R}^9: \|x-w\|_2 \leq \varepsilon\}} (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(v))$ such that

$\|y-Y\|_2 < \delta$. (9.291)

Hence, we obtain that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^9$, $y \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(x)$, $\varepsilon, \delta \in (0, \infty)$ there exist $v \in \{w \in
\mathbb{R}^9 : \|x-w\|_2 < \varepsilon\}$, $Y \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(v)$ such that $\|y-Y\|_2 < \delta$. This demonstrates that for all
$x \in \mathbb{R}^9$, $y \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(x)$, $\varepsilon, \delta \in (0, \infty)$ there exist $X \in \mathbb{R}^9$, $Y \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(X)$ such that

$\|x-X\|_2 < \varepsilon \quad \text{and} \quad \|y-Y\|_2 < \delta$. (9.292)

Therefore, we obtain that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^9$, $y \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(x)$, $k \in \mathbb{N}$ there exist $z_1, z_2 \in \mathbb{R}^9$ such
that

$z_2 \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(z_1) \quad \text{and} \quad \|z_1-x\|_2 + \|z_2-y\|_2 < \frac{1}{k}$. (9.293)

Furthermore, observe that for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^9$, $\varepsilon \in (0, \infty)$ and all $z = (z_1, z_2) : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}^9 \times \mathbb{R}^9$
with $\operatorname{lim}\sup_{k \to \infty}(\|z_1(k)-x\|_2 + \|z_2(k)-y\|_2) = 0$ and $\forall k \in \mathbb{N} : z_2(k) \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(z_1(k))$ there
exist $X, Y \in \mathbb{R}^9$ such that

$Y \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(X) \quad \text{and} \quad \|X-x\|_2 + \|Y-y\|_2 < \varepsilon$. (9.294)

Hence, we obtain that for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^9$, $\varepsilon, \delta \in (0, \infty)$ and all $z = (z_1, z_2) : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}^9 \times \mathbb{R}^9$
with $\operatorname{lim}\sup_{k \to \infty}(\|z_1(k)-x\|_2 + \|z_2(k)-y\|_2) = 0$ and $\forall k \in \mathbb{N} : z_2(k) \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(z_1(k))$ there
exist $X, Y \in \mathbb{R}^9$ such that

$Y \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(X), \quad \|x-X\|_2 < \varepsilon, \quad \text{and} \quad \|y-Y\|_2 < \delta$. (9.295)

This ensures that for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^9$, $\varepsilon, \delta \in (0, \infty)$ and all $z = (z_1, z_2) : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}^9 \times \mathbb{R}^9$
with $\operatorname{lim}\sup_{k \to \infty}(\|z_1(k)-x\|_2 + \|z_2(k)-y\|_2) = 0$ and $\forall k \in \mathbb{N} : z_2(k) \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(z_1(k))$ there
exist $v \in \mathbb{R}^9 : \|x-v\|_2 < \varepsilon\}$, $Y \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(v)$ such that $\|y-Y\|_2 < \delta$. Therefore,
we obtain that for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^9$, $\varepsilon, \delta \in (0, \infty)$ and all $z = (z_1, z_2) : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}^9 \times \mathbb{R}^9$ with
$\operatorname{lim}\sup_{k \to \infty}(\|z_1(k)-x\|_2 + \|z_2(k)-y\|_2) = 0$ and $\forall k \in \mathbb{N} : z_2(k) \in (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(z_1(k))$ there exists
$Y \in \overline{\bigcup_{v \in \{w \in \mathbb{R}^9 : \|x-w\|_2 \leq \varepsilon\}} (\mathcal{D}\mathcal{L})(v)}$ such that

$\|y-Y\|_2 < \delta$. (9.296)
