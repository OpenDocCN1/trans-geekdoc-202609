Moreover, note that $(6.249)$ and the multiplicative property of the determinant (see, for example, Petersen [331], (1) in Proposition 5.5.2) imply that for all $x \in \mathbb{C}$ it holds that

$$\det\left(\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D_x \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} D_x & 0 \\ -C & I_{\mathfrak D} \end{pmatrix}\right)=\det\left(\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D_x \end{pmatrix}\right)\det\left(\begin{pmatrix} D_x & 0 \\ -C & I_{\mathfrak D} \end{pmatrix}\right)$$

$\begin{aligned} &=\det\left(\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D_x \end{pmatrix}\right)\det(D_x)\det(I_\mathfrak D)\tag{6.251} \\ &=\det\left(\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D_x \end{pmatrix}\right)\det(D_x). \end{aligned}$

Combining this and $(6.250)$ demonstrates that for all $x \in \mathbb{C}$ it holds that

$$\det\left(\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D_x \end{pmatrix}\right)\det(D_x)=\det(AD_x-BC)\det(D_x).\tag{6.252}$$

Hence, we obtain for all $x \in \mathbb{C}$ that

$$\left(\det\left(\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D_x \end{pmatrix}\right)-\det(AD_x-BC)\right)\det(D_x)=0.\tag{6.253}$$

This implies that for all $x \in \mathbb{C}$ with $\det(D_x) \neq 0$ it holds that

$$\det\left(\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D_x \end{pmatrix}\right)-\det(AD_x-BC)=0.\tag{6.254}$$

Moreover, note that the fact that $\mathbb{C} \ni x \mapsto \det(D-xI_\mathfrak{X}) \in \mathbb{C}$ is a polynomial function of degree $\mathfrak d$ ensures that $\{x \in \mathbb{C} : \det(D_x) = 0\} = \{x \in \mathbb{C} : \det(D-xI_\mathfrak{X}) = 0\}$ is a finite set. Combining this and $(6.254)$ with the fact that the function

$$\mathbb{C} \ni x \mapsto \det\left(\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D_x \end{pmatrix}\right)-\det(AD_x-BC) \in \mathbb{C}\tag{6.255}$$

is continuous shows that for all $x \in \mathbb{C}$ it holds that

$$\det\left(\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D_x \end{pmatrix}\right)-\det(AD_x-BC)=0.\tag{6.256}$$

Hence, we obtain for all $x \in \mathbb{C}$ that

$$\det\left(\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D_x \end{pmatrix}\right)=\det(AD_x-BC).\tag{6.257}$$

This establishes that

$$\det\left(\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix}\right)=\det\left(\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D_0 \end{pmatrix}\right)=\det(AD_0-BC)=\det(AD_0-BC).\tag{6.258}$$

The proof of Lemma 6.3.10 is thus completed.
