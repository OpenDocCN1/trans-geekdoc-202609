
(ii) it holds that $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{H})=(1,1,1)\in\mathbb{N}^{3}$,

(iii) it holds that $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbf{H})\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})$, and

(iv) it holds for all $x\in\mathbb{R}$ that $(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbf{H})) (x)=h\,\max\{\alpha x+\beta,0\}$

(cf. Definitions 1.2.4 and 1.3.4).

Proof of Lemma 3.2.4. Observe that Lemma 2.3.2 ensures that

$$\boldsymbol{\mathsf{A}}_{\alpha,\beta}=(\alpha,\beta), \qquad \boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{A}}_{\alpha,\beta})=(1,1)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{A}}_{\alpha,\beta})\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}), \tag{3.46}$$

and $\forall x\in\mathbb{R}:(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{A}}_{\alpha,\beta})(x)=\alpha x+\beta$ (cf. Definitions 1.2.4 and 1.3.4). Proposition 2.1.2, Lemma 3.2.2, Lemma 3.2.3, (1.26), (1.91), and (2.2) hence imply that

$$\mathbf{i}_{1}\bullet\boldsymbol{\mathsf{A}}_{\alpha,\beta}=((\alpha,\beta),(1,0)),\ \boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{i}_{1}\bullet\boldsymbol{\mathsf{A}}_{\alpha,\beta})=(1,1,1)\in\mathbb{N}^{3},\ \boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbf{i}_{1}\bullet\boldsymbol{\mathsf{A}}_{\alpha,\beta})\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}), \tag{3.47}$$

and $$\forall x\in\mathbb{R}:(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbf{i}_{1}\bullet\boldsymbol{\mathsf{A}}_{\alpha,\beta}))(x)=\mathsf{r}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{A}}_{\alpha,\beta})(x))=h\,\max\{\alpha x+\beta,0\}. \tag{3.48}$$

This, Lemma 2.3.5, and (2.127) demonstrate that

$$\mathbf{H}=h\otimes(\mathbf{i}_{1}\bullet\boldsymbol{\mathsf{A}}_{\alpha,\beta})=((\alpha,\beta),(h,0)),\qquad \boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{H})=(1,1,1),\qquad \boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbf{H})\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}), \tag{3.49}$$

and $$(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbf{H})(x)=h(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbf{i}_{1}\bullet\boldsymbol{\mathsf{A}}_{\alpha,\beta}))(x))=h\,\max\{\alpha x+\beta,0\}. \tag{3.50}$$

This establishes items (i), (ii), (iii), and (iv). The proof of Lemma 3.2.4 is thus complete.

## 3.2.3 ReLU ANN representations for linear interpolations

Proposition 3.2.5 (ReLU ANN representations for linear interpolations). Let $K\in\mathbb{N}$, $f_{0}$, $f_{1},\ldots,f_{K}$, $\mathtt{f}_{0}$, $\mathtt{f}_{1},\ldots,\mathtt{f}_{K}\in\mathbb{R}$ satisfy $f_{0}<f_{1}<\ldots<f_{K}$ and let $\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{N}$ satisfy

$$\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{A}_{1,f_{0}}\bullet\left(\bigoplus_{k=0}^{K}\left(\frac{(f_{\min\{k+1,K\}}-f_{k})}{(f_{\min\{k+1,K\}}-f_{\min\{k,K-1\}})}-\frac{(f_{k}-f_{\max\{k-1,0\})}}{(f_{\max\{k,1\}}-f_{\max\{k-1,0\})}}\right)\otimes(\mathbf{i}_{1}\bullet\boldsymbol{\mathsf{A}}_{1-f_{k}})\right) \tag{3.51}$$

(cf. Definitions 1.3.1, 2.1.1, 2.3.1, 2.3.4, 2.4.10, and 3.2.1). Then

(i) it holds that $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{F})=(1,K+1,1)\in\mathbb{N}^{3}$,

(ii) it holds that $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathtt{r}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbf{F})=\mathcal{L}^{f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{K}}$, and

(iii) it holds that $\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{F})=3K+4$

(cf. Definitions 1.2.4, 1.3.4, and 3.1.5).

*Proof of Proposition 3.2.5.* Throughout this proof, let $c_{0}, c_{1}, \dots, c_{K} \in \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $k \in \{0, 1, \dots, K\}$ that

$$c_{k} = \frac{f_{\min\{k+1,K\}} - f_{k}}{\mathfrak{f}_{\min\{k+1,K\}} - \mathfrak{f}_{\min\{k,K-1\}}} - \frac{(f_{k} - f_{\max\{k-1,0\}})}{(\mathfrak{f}_{\max\{k,1\}} - \mathfrak{f}_{\max\{k-1,0\}})} \tag{3.52}$$

and let $\Phi_{0}, \Phi_{1}, \dots, \Phi_{K} \in ((\mathbb{R}^{1 \times 1} \times \mathbb{R}^{1}) \times (\mathbb{R}^{1 \times 1} \times \mathbb{R}^{1})) \subseteq \mathbf{N}$ satisfy for all $k \in \{0, 1, \dots, K\}$ that

$$\Phi_{k} = c_{k} \otimes (\mathbf{i}_{1} \bullet \mathbf{A}_{1,-\mathfrak{f}_{k}}). \tag{3.53}$$

Note that Lemma 3.2.4 shows that for all $k \in \{0, 1, \dots, K\}$ it holds that

$$\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{\mathfrak{c}_{k}}(\Phi_{k}) \in C(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R}), \qquad \mathcal{D}(\Phi_{k}) = (1,1,1) \in \mathbb{N}^{3}, \tag{3.54}$$

and

$$\forall \, x \in \mathbb{R} : (\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{\mathfrak{c}_{k}}(\Phi_{k}))(x) = c_{k}\max\{x - \mathfrak{f}_{k}, 0\}. \tag{3.55}$$

(cf. Definitions 1.2.4 and 1.3.4). This, Lemma 2.3.3, Lemma 2.4.11, and (3.51) prove that

$$\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{F}) = (1, K+1, 1) \in \mathbb{N}^{3} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{\mathfrak{c}}(\mathbf{F}) \in C(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R}). \tag{3.56}$$

This establishes item (i). Observe that item (i) and (1.78) ensure that

$$\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{F}) = 2(K+1) + (K+2) = 3K + 4. \tag{3.57}$$

This implies item (iii). Note that (3.52), (3.55), Lemma 2.3.3, and Lemma 2.4.11 demonstrate that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ it holds that

$$(\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{\mathfrak{c}}(\mathbf{F}))(x) = f_{0} + \sum_{k=0}^{K}(\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{\mathfrak{c}_{k}}(\Phi_{k}))(x) = f_{0} + \sum_{k=0}^{K} c_{k}\max\{x - \mathfrak{f}_{k}, 0\}. \tag{3.58}$$

This and the fact that for all $k \in \{0, 1, \dots, K\}$ it holds that $\mathfrak{f}_{0} \le \mathfrak{f}_{k}$ show that for all $x \in (-\infty, \mathfrak{f}_{0}]$ it holds that

$$(\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{\mathfrak{c}}(\mathbf{F}))(x) = f_{0} + 0 = f_{0}. \tag{3.59}$$

Next we claim that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, K\}$ it holds that

$$\sum_{n=0}^{k-1} c_{n} = \frac{f_{k} - f_{k-1}}{\mathfrak{f}_{k} - \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}}. \tag{3.60}$$

We now prove (3.60) by induction on $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, K\}$. For the base case $k=1$ observe that (3.52) proves that

$$\sum_{n=0}^{0} c_{n} = c_{0} = \frac{f_{1} - f_{0}}{\mathfrak{f}_{1} - \mathfrak{f}_{0}}. \tag{3.61}$$

This establishes (3.60) in the base case $k = 1$. For the induction step observe that (3.52) ensures that for all $k \in \mathbb{N} \cap (1, \infty) \cap (0, K]$ with $\sum_{n=0}^{k-2} c_n = \frac{f_{k-1} - f_{k-2}}{\mathfrak{f}_{k-1} - \mathfrak{f}_{k-2}}$ it holds that

\begin{align*}
\sum_{n=0}^{k-1} c_n &= c_{k-1} + \sum_{n=0}^{k-2} c_n = \frac{f_k - f_{k-1}}{\mathfrak{f}_k - \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}} - \frac{f_{k-1} - f_{k-2}}{\mathfrak{f}_{k-1} - \mathfrak{f}_{k-2}} + \frac{f_{k-1} - f_{k-2}}{\mathfrak{f}_{k-1} - \mathfrak{f}_{k-2}} = \frac{f_k - f_{k-1}}{\mathfrak{f}_k - \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}}. \quad (3.62)
\end{align*}

Induction thus implies (3.60). Furthermore, note that (3.58), (3.60), and the fact that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, K\}$ it holds that $\mathfrak{f}_{k-1} < \mathfrak{f}_k$ demonstrate that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, K\}$, $x \in [\mathfrak{f}_{k-1}, \mathfrak{f}_k]$ it holds that

\begin{align*}
(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{f}}^{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbf{F}))(x) - (\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{f}}^{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbf{F}))(\mathfrak{f}_{k-1}) &= \sum_{n=0}^{K} c_n (\max\{x - \mathfrak{f}_n, 0\} - \max\{\mathfrak{f}_{k-1} - \mathfrak{f}_n, 0\}) \\
&= \sum_{n=0}^{k-1} c_n [(x - \mathfrak{f}_n) - (\mathfrak{f}_{k-1} - \mathfrak{f}_n)] = \sum_{n=0}^{k-1} c_n (x - \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}) \\
&= \left(\frac{f_k - f_{k-1}}{\mathfrak{f}_k - \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}}\right)(x - \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}). \quad (3.63)
\end{align*}

Next we claim that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, K\}$, $x \in [\mathfrak{f}_{k-1}, \mathfrak{f}_k]$ it holds that

$$(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{f}}^{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbf{F}))(x) = f_{k-1} + \left(\frac{f_k - f_{k-1}}{\mathfrak{f}_k - \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}}\right)(x - \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}). \quad (3.64)$$

We now prove (3.64) by induction on $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, K\}$. For the base case $k = 1$ observe that (3.59) and (3.63) show that for all $x \in [\mathfrak{f}_0, \mathfrak{f}_1]$ it holds that

$$(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{f}}^{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbf{F}))(x) = (\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{f}}^{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbf{F}))(\mathfrak{f}_0) + (\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{f}}^{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbf{F}))(x) - (\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{f}}^{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbf{F}))(\mathfrak{f}_0) = f_0 + \left(\frac{f_1 - f_0}{\mathfrak{f}_1 - \mathfrak{f}_0}\right)(x - \mathfrak{f}_0). \quad (3.65)$$

This proves (3.64) in the base case $k = 1$. For the induction step note that (3.63) establishes that for all $k \in \mathbb{N} \cap (1, \infty) \cap [1, K]$, $x \in [\mathfrak{f}_{k-1}, \mathfrak{f}_k]$ with $\forall y \in [\mathfrak{f}_{k-2}, \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}]: (\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{f}}^{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbf{F}))(y) = f_{k-2} + \frac{f_{k-1} - f_{k-2}}{\mathfrak{f}_{k-1} - \mathfrak{f}_{k-2}}(y - \mathfrak{f}_{k-2})$ it holds that

\begin{align*}
(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{f}}^{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbf{F}))(x) &= (\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{f}}^{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbf{F}))(\mathfrak{f}_{k-1}) + (\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{f}}^{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbf{F}))(x) - (\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{f}}^{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbf{F}))(\mathfrak{f}_{k-1}) \\
&= f_{k-2} + \left(\frac{f_{k-1} - f_{k-2}}{\mathfrak{f}_{k-1} - \mathfrak{f}_{k-2}}\right)(\mathfrak{f}_{k-1} - \mathfrak{f}_{k-2}) + \left(\frac{f_k - f_{k-1}}{\mathfrak{f}_k - \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}}\right)(x - \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}) \\
&= f_{k-1} + \left(\frac{f_k - f_{k-1}}{\mathfrak{f}_k - \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}}\right)(x - \mathfrak{f}_{k-1}). \quad (3.66)
\end{align*}

Induction thus ensures (3.64). Moreover, observe that (3.52) and (3.60) imply that

$$\sum_{n=0}^{K} c_n = c_K + \sum_{n=0}^{K-1} c_n = -\frac{f_K - f_{K-1}}{\mathfrak{f}_K - \mathfrak{f}_{K-1}} + \frac{f_K - f_{K-1}}{\mathfrak{f}_K - \mathfrak{f}_{K-1}} = 0. \quad (3.67)$$
