and let $\mathcal{X}_n: E \times \Omega \to \mathcal{E}$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, satisfy for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $x \in E$, $\omega \in \Omega$ that

$$\mathcal{X}_n(x, \omega)=X\left(P_{n}(x), \omega\right). \quad(11.54)$$

Note that (11.54) shows that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $B \in \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})$ it holds that

$\begin{align*}\left(\mathcal{X}_{n}\right)^{-1}(B) & =\{\{x, \omega\} \in E \times \Omega: \mathcal{X}_{n}(x, \omega) \in B\} \\ & =\bigcup_{y \in \operatorname{Im}\left(P_{n}\right)}\left(\left[\left(X_{n}\right)^{-1}(B)\right] \cap\left(\left(P_{n}\right)^{-1}(\{y\}) \times \Omega\right)\right) \\ & =\bigcup_{y \in \operatorname{Im}\left(P_{n}\right)}\left\{\{x, \omega\} \in E \times \Omega: \left[\mathcal{X}_{n}(x, \omega) \in B \text { and } x \in\left(P_{n}\right)^{-1}\left(\{y\}\right)\right]\right\} \\ & =\bigcup_{y \in \operatorname{Im}\left(P_{n}\right)}\left\{\{x, \omega\} \in E \times \Omega: \left[X\left(P_{n}(x), \omega\right) \in B \text { and } x \in\left(P_{n}\right)^{-1}\left(\{y\}\right)\right]\right\}.\end{align*}\tag{11.55}

Item (ii) in Lemma 11.2.5 therefore implies that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $B \in \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})$ it holds that

$\begin{align*}\left(\mathcal{X}_{n}\right)^{-1}(B) & =\bigcup_{y \in \operatorname{Im}\left(P_{n}\right)}\left\{\{x, \omega\} \in E \times \Omega: \left[X\left(y, \omega\right) \in B \text { and } x \in\left(P_{n}\right)^{-1}\left(\{y\}\right)\right]\right\} \\ & =\bigcup_{y \in \operatorname{Im}\left(P_{n}\right)}\left(\left\{\{x, \omega\} \in E \times \Omega: X\left(y, \omega\right) \in B\right\} \cap\left(\left(P_{n}\right)^{-1}\left(\{y\}\right) \times \Omega\right)\right) \\ & =\bigcup_{y \in \operatorname{Im}\left(P_{n}\right)}\left(\underbrace{\left[E \times\left(X(y, \cdot)^{-1}(B)\right)\right]}_{\in \mathcal{B}(E) \otimes \mathbb{F}} \cap \underbrace{\left[\left(P_{n}\right)^{-1}\left(\{y\}\right) \times \Omega\right]}_{\in \mathcal{B}(E) \otimes \mathbb{F}}\right) \in\left(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}) \otimes \mathbb{F}\right).\end{align*}\tag{11.56}

This demonstrates that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that $\mathcal{X}_{n}$ is $(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}) \otimes \mathbb{F}) / \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})$-measurable. Furthermore, observe that item (i) in Lemma 11.2.5 and the assumption that for all $\omega \in \Omega$ it holds that $E \ni x \mapsto X(x, \omega) \in \mathcal{E}$ is continuous ensure that for all $x \in E$, $\omega \in \Omega$ it holds that

$$\operatorname*{lim}_{n \rightarrow \infty} \mathcal{X}_{n}(x, \omega)=\operatorname*{lim}_{n \rightarrow \infty} X\left(P_{n}(x), \omega\right)=X(x, \omega). \quad(11.57)$$

Combining this with the fact that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that $\mathcal{X}_{n}: E \times \Omega \to \mathcal{E}$ is $(\mathcal{B}(E) \otimes \mathbb{F}) / \mathcal{B}(E)$-measurable establishes that $X: E \times \Omega \to \mathcal{E}$ is $(\mathcal{B}(E) \otimes \mathbb{F}) / \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})$-measurable. The proof of Lemma 11.2.6 is thus complete. $\square$
