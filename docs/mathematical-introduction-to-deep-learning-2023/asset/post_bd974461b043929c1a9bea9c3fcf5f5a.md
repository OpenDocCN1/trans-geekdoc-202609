
Next observe that Lemma 11.2.2 demonstrates that for all $x \in (0, \infty)$, $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ it holds that

$$
\begin{align*} (\max\{x + \alpha - 1, 0\})^\alpha &= (x + \alpha)^\alpha \left( \frac{\max\{x + \alpha - 1, 0\}}{x + \alpha} \right)^\alpha \\ &= (x + \alpha)^\alpha \left( \max \left\{1 - \frac{1}{x + \alpha}, 0\right\} \right)^\alpha \\ &\leq (x + \alpha)^\alpha \left( 1 - \frac{\alpha}{x + \alpha} \right) = (x + \alpha)^\alpha \left( \frac{x}{x + \alpha} \right) \\ &= \frac{x}{(x + \alpha)^{1 - \alpha}}. \end{align*}
$$

This and (11.27) prove item (iii). Furthermore, note that induction, item (i) in Lemma 11.2.1, the fact that $\forall \alpha \in [0, \infty): \alpha - \lfloor \alpha \rfloor \in [0, 1)$, and item (iii) ensure that for all $x \in (0, \infty)$, $\alpha \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that

$$
\begin{align*} \frac{\Gamma(x + \alpha)}{\Gamma(x)} &= \left[ \prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor \alpha \rfloor} (x + \alpha - i) \right] \frac{\Gamma(x + \alpha - \lfloor \alpha \rfloor)}{\Gamma(x)} \leq \left[ \prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor \alpha \rfloor} (x + \alpha - i) \right] x^{\alpha - \lfloor \alpha \rfloor} \\ &\leq (x + \alpha - 1)^{\lfloor \alpha \rfloor} x^{\alpha - \lfloor \alpha \rfloor} \\ &\leq (x + \max\{\alpha - 1, 0\})^{\lfloor \alpha \rfloor} (x + \max\{\alpha - 1, 0\})^{\alpha - \lfloor \alpha \rfloor} \\ &= (x + \max\{\alpha - 1, 0\})^\alpha. \end{align*}
$$

Moreover, observe that the fact that $\forall \alpha \in [0, \infty): \alpha - \lfloor \alpha \rfloor \in [0, 1)$, item (iii), induction, and item (i) in Lemma 11.2.1 show that for all $x \in (0, \infty)$, $\alpha \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that

$$
\begin{align*} \frac{\Gamma(x + \alpha)}{\Gamma(x)} &= \frac{\Gamma(x + \lfloor \alpha \rfloor + \alpha - \lfloor \alpha \rfloor)}{\Gamma(x)} \\ &\geq (\max\{x + \lfloor \alpha \rfloor + \alpha - \lfloor \alpha \rfloor - 1, 0\})^{\alpha - \lfloor \alpha \rfloor} \left[ \frac{\Gamma(x + \lfloor \alpha \rfloor)}{\Gamma(x)} \right] \\ &= (\max\{x + \alpha - 1, 0\})^{\alpha - \lfloor \alpha \rfloor} \left[ \prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor \alpha \rfloor} (x + \lfloor \alpha \rfloor - i) \right] \frac{\Gamma(x)}{\Gamma(x)} \\ &\geq (\max\{x + \alpha - 1, 0\})^{\alpha - \lfloor \alpha \rfloor} x^{\lfloor \alpha \rfloor} \\ &= (\max\{x + \alpha - 1, 0\})^{\alpha - \lfloor \alpha \rfloor} (\max\{x, 0\})^{\lfloor \alpha \rfloor} \\ &\geq (\max\{x + \min\{\alpha - 1, 0\}, 0\})^{\alpha - \lfloor \alpha \rfloor} (\max\{x + \min\{\alpha - 1, 0\}, 0\})^{\lfloor \alpha \rfloor} \\ &= (\max\{x + \min\{\alpha - 1, 0\}, 0\})^\alpha. \end{align*}
$$

Combining this with (11.32) establishes item (iv). The proof of Proposition 11.2.3 is thus complete.

**Corollary 11.2.4**. Let $\mathbb{B}: (0, \infty)^2 \rightarrow (0, \infty)$ satisfy for all $x, y \in (0, \infty)$ that $\mathbb{B}(x, y) = \int_{0}^{1} t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1} dt$ and let $\Gamma: (0, \infty) \rightarrow (0, \infty)$ satisfy for all $x \in (0, \infty)$ that $\Gamma(x) = \int_{0}^{\infty} t^{x-1} e^{-t} dt$. Then it holds for all $x, y \in (0, \infty)$ with $x + y > 1$ that

$$ \frac{\Gamma(x)}{(y + \max\{x - 1, 0\})^{x}} \leq \mathbb{B}(x, y) \leq \frac{\Gamma(x)}{(y + \min\{x - 1, 0\})^{x}} \leq \frac{\max\{1, x^{x}\}}{x(y + \min\{x - 1, 0\})^{x}}. \quad (11.34) $$

*Proof of Corollary 11.2.4*. Note that item (iii) in Lemma 11.2.1 implies that for all $x, y \in (0, \infty)$ it holds that

$$ \mathbb{B}(x, y) = \frac{\Gamma(x)\Gamma(y)}{\Gamma(y + x)}. \quad (11.35) $$

Furthermore, observe that the fact that for all $x, y \in (0, \infty)$ with $x + y > 1$ it holds that $y + \min\{x - 1, 0\} > 0$ and item (iv) in Proposition 11.2.3 demonstrate that for all $x, y \in (0, \infty)$ with $x + y > 1$ it holds that

$$ 0 < (y + \min\{x - 1, 0\})^{x} \leq \frac{\Gamma(y + x)}{\Gamma(y)} \leq (y + \max\{x - 1, 0\})^{x}. \quad (11.36) $$

Combining this with (11.35) and item (ii) in Proposition 11.2.3 proves that for all $x, y \in (0, \infty)$ with $x + y > 1$ it holds that

$$ \frac{\Gamma(x)}{(y + \max\{x - 1, 0\})^{x}} \leq \mathbb{B}(x, y) \leq \frac{\Gamma(x)}{(y + \min\{x - 1, 0\})^{x}} \leq \frac{\max\{1, x^{x}\}}{x(y + \min\{x - 1, 0\})^{x}}. \quad (11.37) $$

The proof of Corollary 11.2.4 is thus complete. $\square$

## 11.2.2 Product measurability of continuous random fields

**Lemma 11.2.5** (Projections in metric spaces). Let $(E, d)$ be a metric space, let $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $e_{1}, e_{2}, \ldots, e_{n} \in E$, and let $P: E \rightarrow E$ satisfy for all $x \in E$ that

$$ P(x) = e_{\min\{k \in \{1, \ldots, n\}: d(x, e_{k}) = \min(d(x, e_{1}), d(x, e_{2}), \ldots, d(x, e_{n}))\}}. \quad (11.38) $$

*Then*

- (i) it holds for all $x \in E$ that

$$ d(x, P(x)) = \min_{k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}} d(x, e_{k}) \quad (11.39) $$

*and*

- (ii) it holds for all $A \subseteq E$ that $P^{-1}(A) \in \mathcal{B}(E)$.

Proof of Lemma 11.2.5. Throughout this proof, let $D = (D_1, \dots, D_n): E \to \mathbb{R}^n$ satisfy for all $x \in E$ that

$$ D(x) = (D_1(x), D_2(x), \dots, D_n(x)) = (d(x, e_1), d(x, e_2), \dots, d(x, e_n)). $$

Note that (11.38) ensures that for all $x \in E$ it holds that

$$
\begin{aligned}
d(x, P(x)) &= d(x, e_{\min\{k \in \{1, \dots, n\}: d(x, e_k) = \min\{d(x, e_1), d(x, e_2), \dots, d(x, e_n)\}\}}) \\
&= \min_{k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}} d(x, e_k).
\end{aligned}
$$

This establishes item (i). It thus remains to prove item (ii). For this observe that the fact that $d: E \times E \to [0, \infty)$ is continuous shows that $D: E \to \mathbb{R}^n$ is continuous. Hence, we obtain that $D: E \to \mathbb{R}^n$ is $\mathcal{B}(E)/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$-measurable. Furthermore, note that item (i) implies that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$, $x \in P^{-1}(\{e_k\})$ it holds that

$$ d(x, e_k) = d(x, P(x)) = \min_{l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}} d(x, e_l). $$

Therefore, we obtain that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$, $x \in P^{-1}(\{e_k\})$ it holds that

$$ k \geq \min\{l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}: d(x, e_l) = \min\{d(x, e_1), d(x, e_2), \dots, d(x, e_n)\}\}. $$

Moreover, observe that (11.38) demonstrates that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$, $x \in P^{-1}(\{e_k\})$ it holds that

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\min\Big\{l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}: d(x, e_l) = \min_{u \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}} d(x, e_u)\Big\} \\
&\in \Big\{l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}: e_l = e_k\} \subseteq \{k, k+1, \dots, n\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, we obtain that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$, $x \in P^{-1}(\{e_k\})$ with $e_k \notin (\bigcup_{l \in \mathbb{N} \cap [0, k)} \{e_l\})$ it holds that

$$ \min\Big\{l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}: d(x, e_l) = \min_{u \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}} d(x, e_u)\Big\} \geq k. $$

Combining this with (11.43) proves that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$, $x \in P^{-1}(\{e_k\})$ with $e_k \notin (\bigcup_{l \in \mathbb{N} \cap [0, k)} \{e_l\})$ it holds that

$$ \min\Big\{l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}: d(x, e_l) = \min_{u \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}} d(x, e_u)\Big\} = k. $$

Therefore, we obtain that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$ with $e_k \notin (\bigcup_{l \in \mathbb{N} \cap [0, k)} \{e_l\})$ it holds that

$$ P^{-1}(\{e_k\}) \subseteq \Big\{x \in E: \min\Big\{l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}: d(x, e_l) = \min_{u \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}} d(x, e_u)\Big\} = k\Big\}. $$

This and (11.38) ensure that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$ with $e_k \notin \bigcup_{i \in \mathbb{N} \setminus \{0, k\}} \{e_i\}$ it holds that

$$ P^{-1}(\{e_k\}) = \left\{x \in E: \min \left\{l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}: d(x, e_l) = \min_{u \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}} d(x, e_u) \right\} = k\right\}. \quad (11.40) $$

Combining (11.40) with the fact that $D: E \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^n$ is $\mathcal{B}(E)/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$-measurable hence establishes that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$ with $e_k \notin \bigcup_{i \in \mathbb{N} \setminus \{0, k\}} \{e_i\}$ it holds that

$$
\begin{aligned}
P^{-1}(\{e_k\}) &= \left\{x \in E: \min \left\{l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}: d(x, e_l) = \min_{u \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}} d(x, e_u) \right\} = k\right\} \\
&= \left\{x \in E: \min \left\{l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}: D_l(x) = \min_{u \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}} D_u(x) \right\} = k\right\} \\
&= \left\{x \in E: \begin{array}{c} \forall l \in \mathbb{N} \cap \{0, k\}: D_k(x) < D_l(x) \text{ and} \\ \forall l \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}: D_k(x) \leq D_l(x) \end{array} \right\} \\
&= \left[ \underset{\substack{l=1 \\ \cup \mathcal{B}(E)}}{\bigcap_{l=1}^{k-1}} \left\{x \in E: D_k(x) < D_l(x)\right\} \right] \cap \left[ \underset{\substack{l=1 \\ \cup \mathcal{B}(E)}}{\bigcap_{l=1}^{n}} \left\{x \in E: D_k(x) \leq D_l(x)\right\} \right] \in \mathcal{B}(E).
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we obtain that for all $f \in \{e_1, e_2, \dots, e_n\}$ it holds that

$$ P^{-1}(\{f\}) \in \mathcal{B}(E). $$

Hence, we obtain that for all $A \subseteq E$ it holds that

$$ P^{-1}(A) = P^{-1}(A \cap \{e_1, e_2, \dots, e_n\}) = \bigcup_{f \in A \cap \{e_1, e_2, \dots, e_n\}} P^{-1}(\{f\}) \in \mathcal{B}(E). $$

This proves item (ii). The proof of Lemma 11.2.5 is thus complete.
