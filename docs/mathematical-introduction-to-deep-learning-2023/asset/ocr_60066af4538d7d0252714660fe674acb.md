*Proof of Lemma 5.3.2.* Note that (5.25) and (5.26) ensure that for all $\theta_{1} \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_{1}}$, $\theta_{2} \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_{2}}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_{k}}$ it holds that

$$f(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},x)=F_{2}(\theta_{2},F_{1}(\theta_{1},x))=F_{2}(B(\theta_{2},\theta_{1},x))=F_{2}(B(A(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},x))). \qquad (5.27)$$

Observe that Lemma 5.3.1 (applied with $\mathbf{d}_{1} \curvearrowleft \mathbf{d}_{2}$, $\mathbf{d}_{2} \curvearrowleft \mathbf{d}_{1}+\mathbf{l}_{1}$, $\mathbf{l}_{1} \curvearrowleft \mathbf{d}_{2}$, $\mathbf{l}_{2} \curvearrowleft \mathbf{l}_{1}$, $F_{1} \curvearrowleft (\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_{2}} \ni \theta_{2} \mapsto \theta_{2} \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_{2}})$, $F_{2} \curvearrowleft (\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_{1}+\mathbf{l}_{1}} \ni (\theta_{1},x) \mapsto F_{1}(\theta_{1},x) \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{l}_{1}})$ in the notation of Lemma 5.3.1) implies that *B* is differentiable. Combining this, the fact that *A* is differentiable, the fact that *F*₂ is differentiable, and (5.27) with the chain rule assures that *f* is differentiable. The proof of Lemma 5.3.2 is thus complete.
