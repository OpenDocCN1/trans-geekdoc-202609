
Combining this with (12.139) implies for all \(N \in \mathbb{N}\), \(i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, N\}\), \(x \in [a + \frac{(i-1)(b-a)}{N}, a + \frac{i(b-a)}{N}]\) that \(|x - g_{N,i}| \leq \frac{b-a}{2N}\). This proves that for every \(N \in \mathbb{N}\), \(x \in [a, b]\) there exists \(y \in \{g_{N,1}, g_{N,2}, \ldots, g_{N,N}\}\) such that

\(|x - y| \leq \frac{b-a}{2N}\). (12.141)

This shows that for every \(x = (x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_d) \in [a, b]^d\) there exists \(y = (y_1, y_2, \ldots, y_d) \in A\) such that

\(\delta(x, y) = \|x - y\|_\infty = \max_{i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, d\}} |x_i - y_i| \leq \frac{b-a}{2N} \leq \frac{(b-a)2r}{2(b-a)} = r\). (12.142)

Combining this with (4.82), (12.138), (12.136), and the fact that \(\forall x \in [0, \infty) : |x| \leq \mathbb{I}_{(0,r]}(x)r + 2x\mathbb{I}_{(r, \infty)}(x)\) demonstrates that

\(\mathcal{C}^{([a,b]^d,\delta), r} \leq |A| = \left|\mathbb{N}^d = \left(\frac{b-a}{2r}\right)^d\right| \leq \mathbb{I}_{(0,r]}\left(\frac{b-a}{2}\right) + \left(\frac{b-a}{r}\right)^d \mathbb{I}_{(r, \infty)}\left(\frac{b-a}{2}\right)\). (12.143)

(cf. Definition 4.3.2). The proof of Proposition 12.2.24 is thus complete.

## 12.3 Empirical risk minimization

### 12.3.1 Concentration inequalities for random fields

**Lemma 12.3.1.** Let \((E, d)\) be a separable metric space and let \(F \subseteq E\) be a set. Then

\((F, d|_{F \times F})\). (12.144)

is a separable metric space.

*Proof of Lemma 12.3.1.* Throughout this proof, assume without loss of generality that \(F \neq \emptyset\), let \(e = (e_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} : \mathbb{N} \to E\) be a sequence of elements in \(E\) such that \(\{e_n \in E : n \in \mathbb{N}\}\) is dense in \(E\), and let \(f = (f_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} : \mathbb{N} \to F\) be a sequence of elements in \(F\) such that for all \(n \in \mathbb{N}\) it holds that

\(d(f_n, e_n) \leq \left\{\begin{array}{ll}0 & : e_n \in F \\ \inf_{x \in F} d(x, e_n) + \frac{1}{2^n} & : e_n \notin F.\end{array}\right.\). (12.145)

Observe that for all \(v \in F \setminus \{e_m \in E : m \in \mathbb{N}\}\), \(n \in \mathbb{N}\) it holds that

\(\inf_{m \in \mathbb{N}} d(v, f_m) \leq \inf_{m \in \mathbb{N}[n, \infty)} d(v, f_m)\)

\(\leq \inf_{m \in \mathbb{N}[n, \infty)} \left[ d(v, e_m) + d(e_m, f_m) \right]\)

\(\leq \inf_{m \in \mathbb{N}[n, \infty)} \left[ d(v, e_m) + \inf_{x \in F} d(x, e_m) \right] + \frac{1}{2^n}\)

\(\leq \inf_{m \in \mathbb{N}[n, \infty)} \left[ 2 d(v, e_m) + \frac{1}{2^n} \right]\)

\(\leq 2 \left[ \inf_{m \in \mathbb{N}[n, \infty)} d(v, e_m) \right] + \frac{1}{2^n} = \frac{1}{2^n}\). (12.146)

Combining this with the fact that for all $v \in F \cap \{e_m \in E: m \in \mathbb{N}\}$ it holds that $\inf_{m \in \mathbb{N}} d(v, f_m) = 0$ ensures that the set $\{f_n \in F: n \in \mathbb{N}\}$ is dense in $F$. The proof of Lemma **12.3.1** is thus complete.

**Lemma 12.3.2.** Let $(E, \mathfrak{E})$ be a topological space, assume $E \neq \emptyset$, let $\mathbf{E} \subseteq E$ be an at most countable set, assume that $\mathbf{E}$ is dense in $E$, let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$ be a measurable space, for every $x \in E$ let $f_x: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be $\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$-measurable, assume for all $\omega \in \Omega$ that $E \ni x \mapsto f_x(\omega) \in \mathbb{R}$ is continuous, and let $F: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}$ satisfy for all $\omega \in \Omega$ that

$$F(\omega) = \sup_{x \in E} f_x(\omega). \tag{12.147}$$

Then

-   (i) it holds for all $\omega \in \Omega$ that $F(\omega) = \sup_{x \in \mathbf{E}} f_x(\omega)$ and
-   (ii) it holds that $F$ is $\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\})$-measurable.

*Proof of Lemma 12.3.2.* Observe that the assumption that $\mathbf{E}$ is dense in $E$ shows that for all $g \in C(E, \mathbb{R})$ it holds that

$$\sup_{x \in E} g(x) = \sup_{x \in \mathbf{E}} g(x). \tag{12.148}$$

This and the assumption that for all $\omega \in \Omega$ it holds that $E \ni x \mapsto f_x(\omega) \in \mathbb{R}$ is continuous demonstrate that for all $\omega \in \Omega$ it holds that

$$F(\omega) = \sup_{x \in E} f_x(\omega) = \sup_{x \in \mathbf{E}} f_x(\omega). \tag{12.149}$$

This proves item **(i)**. Furthermore, note that item **(i)** and the assumption that for all $x \in E$ it holds that $f_x: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is $\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$-measurable establish item **(ii)**. The proof of Lemma **12.3.2** is thus complete.

**Lemma 12.3.3.** Let $(E, \delta)$ be a separable metric space, let $\varepsilon, L \in \mathbb{R}$, $N \in \mathbb{N}$, $z_1, z_2, \ldots, z_N \in E$ satisfy $E \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\{x \in E: 2 L \delta(x, z_i) \leq \varepsilon\}$, let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ be a probability space, and let $Z_x: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$, $x \in E$, be random variables which satisfy for all $x, y \in E$ that $|Z_x - Z_y| \leq L \delta(x, y)$. Then

$$\mathbb{P}(\sup_{x \in E}|Z_x| \geq \varepsilon) \leq \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbb{P}(|Z_{z_i}| \geq \tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}) \tag{12.150}$$

(*cf.* Lemma **12.3.2**).

*Proof of Lemma 12.3.3.* Throughout this proof, let $B_1, B_2, \ldots, B_N \subseteq E$ satisfy for all $i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, N\}$ that $B_i = \{x \in E: 2 L \delta(x, z_i) \leq \varepsilon\}$. Observe that the triangle inequality and the assumption that for all $x, y \in E$ it holds that $|Z_x - Z_y| \le L \delta(x, y)$ show that for all $i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, N\}$, $x \in B_i$ it holds that

$|Z_x| = |Z_x - Z_{z_i} + Z_{z_i}| \le |Z_x - Z_{z_i}| + |Z_{z_i}| \le L \delta(x, z_i) + |Z_{z_i}| \le \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + |Z_{z_i}|.$ (12.151)

Combining this with Lemma **12.3.2** and Lemma **12.3.1** proves that for all $i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, N\}$ it holds that

$\mathbb{P}\big(\sup_{x \in B_i}|Z_x| \ge \varepsilon\big) \le \mathbb{P}\big(\frac{\varepsilon}{2} + |Z_{z_i}| \ge \varepsilon\big) = \mathbb{P}\big(|Z_{z_i}| \ge \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\big).$ (12.152)

This, Lemma **12.3.2**, and Lemma **12.3.1** establish that

$\mathbb{P}\big(\sup_{x \in E}|Z_x| \ge \varepsilon\big) = \mathbb{P}\bigg(\sup_{x \in \bigcup_{i=1}^N B_i}|Z_x| \ge \varepsilon\bigg) = \mathbb{P}\bigg(\bigcup_{i=1}^N \big\{\sup_{x \in B_i}|Z_x| \ge \varepsilon\big\}\bigg)$

$\le \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{P}\big(\sup_{x \in B_i}|Z_x| \ge \varepsilon\big) \le \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{P}\big(|Z_{z_i}| \ge \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\big).$ (12.153)

This completes the proof of Lemma **12.3.3**.$\quad \square$

**Lemma 12.3.4.** Let $(E, \delta)$ be a separable metric space, assume $E \neq \emptyset$, let $\varepsilon, L \in (0, \infty)$, let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ be a probability space, and let $Z_x: \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$, $x \in E$, be random variables which satisfy for all $x, y \in E$ that $|Z_x - Z_y| \le L \delta(x, y)$. Then

$\big[\mathcal{C}(E, \delta, \frac{\varepsilon}{2L})\big]^{-1}\mathbb{P}\big(\sup_{x \in E}|Z_x| \ge \varepsilon\big) \le \sup_{x \in E}\mathbb{P}\big(|Z_x| \ge \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\big).$ (12.154)

*(cf. Definition 4.3.2 and Lemma 12.3.2).*

*Proof of Lemma **12.3.4**. Throughout this proof, let $N \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$ satisfy $N = \mathcal{C}(E, \delta, \frac{\varepsilon}{2L})$, assume without loss of generality that $N < \infty$, and let $z_1, z_2, \ldots, z_N \in E$ satisfy $E \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^N \{x \in E: \delta(x, z_i) \le \frac{\varepsilon}{2L}\}$ (cf. Definition **4.3.2**). Observe that Lemma **12.3.2** and Lemma **12.3.3** establish that*

$\mathbb{P}\big(\sup_{x \in E}|Z_x| \ge \varepsilon\big) \le \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{P}\big(|Z_{z_i}| \ge \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\big) \le N\big[\sup_{x \in E}\mathbb{P}\big(|Z_x| \ge \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\big)\big].$ (12.155)

This completes the proof of Lemma **12.3.4**.$\quad \square$
