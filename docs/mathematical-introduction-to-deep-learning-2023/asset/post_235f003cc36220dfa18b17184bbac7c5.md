# Chapter 17

## Deep Kolmogorov methods (DKMs)

The **PINNs** and the **DGMs** presented in Chapter 16 do, on the one hand, not exploit a lot of structure of the underlying *PDE* in the process of setting up the associated stochastic optimization problems and have as such the key advantage to be very widely applicable deep learning methods for *PDE*s. On the other hand, deep learning methods for *PDE*s that in some way exploit the specific structure of the considered *PDE* problem often result in more accurate approximations (cf., for example, Beck et al. [24] and the references therein). In particular, there are several deep learning approximation methods in the literature which exploit in the process of setting up stochastic optimization problems that the *PDE* itself admits a stochastic representation. In the literature there are a lot of deep learning methods which are based on such stochastic formulations of *PDE*s and therefore have a strong link to stochastic analysis and formulas of the Feynman–Kac-type (cf., for instance, [20, 119, 145, 187, 207, 336] and the references therein).

The schemes in Beck et al. [19], which we refer to as **DKMs**, belong to the simplest of such deep learning methods for *PDE*s. In this chapter we present in Sections 17.1, 17.2, 17.3, and 17.4 theoretical considerations leading to a reformulation of heat *PDE* problems as stochastic optimization problems (see Proposition 17.4.1 below), we use these theoretical considerations to derive **DKMs** in the specific case of heat equations in Section 17.5, and we present an implementation of **DKMs** in the case of a simple two-dimensional heat equation in Section 17.6.

Sections 17.1 and 17.2 are slightly modified extracts from Beck et al. [18], Section 17.3 is inspired by Beck et al. [23, Section 2], and Sections 17.4 and 17.5 are inspired by Beck et al. [18].

## 17.1 Stochastic optimization problems for expectations of random variables

Lemma 17.1.1. Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ be a probability space and let $X: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be a random variable with $\mathbb{P}[|X|^{2}] < \infty$. Then

(i) it holds for all $y \in \mathbb{R}$ that
$\mathbb{P}[X - y]^{2} = \mathbb{P}[X - \mathbb{P}[X]^{2}] + |\mathbb{P}[X] - y|^{2},$ (17.1)

(ii) there exists a unique $z \in \mathbb{R}$ such that
$\mathbb{P}[X - z]^{2} = \inf_{y \in \mathbb{R}} \mathbb{P}[X - y]^{2},$ (17.2)

and

(iii) it holds that
$\mathbb{P}[X - \mathbb{P}[X]^{2}] = \inf_{y \in \mathbb{R}} \mathbb{P}[X - y]^{2}.$ (17.3)

Proof of Lemma 17.1.1. Note that Lemma 7.2.3 establishes item (i). Observe that item (i) proves items (ii) and (iii). The proof of Lemma 17.1.1 is thus complete.

## 17.2 Stochastic optimization problems for expectations of random fields

Proposition 17.2.1. Let $d \in \mathbb{N}$, $a \in \mathbb{R}$, $b \in (a, \infty)$, let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ be a probability space, let $X = (X_{x})_{x \in [a, b]^{d}}: [a, b]^{d} \times \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be $(\mathcal{B}([a, b]^{d}) \otimes \mathcal{F}) / \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$-measurable, assume for every $x \in [a, b]^{d}$ that $\mathbb{P}[|X_{x}|^{2}] < \infty$, and assume that $[a, b]^{d} \ni x \mapsto \mathbb{P}[X_{x}] \in \mathbb{R}$ is continuous. Then

(i) there exists a unique $u \in C([a, b]^{d}, \mathbb{R})$ such that
$\int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{P}[|X_{x} - u(x)|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x = \inf_{v \in C([a, b]^{d}, \mathbb{R})} \left( \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{P}[|X_{x} - v(x)|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x \right)$ (17.4)

and

(ii) it holds for all $x \in [a, b]^{d}$ that $u(x) = \mathbb{P}[X_{x}]$.

Proof of Proposition 17.2.1. Note that item (i) in Lemma 17.1.1 and the assumption that for all $x \in [a, b]^{d}$ it holds that $\mathbb{P}[|X_{x}|^{2}] < \infty$ demonstrate that for every function $u: [a, b]^{d} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ and every $x \in [a, b]^{d}$ it holds that

$\mathbb{P}[|X_{x} - u(x)|^{2}] = \mathbb{P}[X_{x} - \mathbb{P}[X_{x}]^{2}] + |\mathbb{P}[X_{x}] - u(x)|^{2}.$ (17.5)

Fubini's theorem (see, for example, Klenke [248, Theorem 14.16]) hence implies that for all $u \in C([a, b]^{d}, \mathbb{R})$ it holds that

$$ \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - u(x)|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x = \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - \mathbb{E}[X_{x}]|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x + \int_{[a, b]^{d}} |\mathbb{E}[X_{x}] - u(x)|^{2} \, \mathrm{d}x . \tag{17.6} $$

This ensures that

$$ \begin{split} \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - \mathbb{E}[X_{x}]|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x \\ \geq \inf_{v \in C([a, b]^{d}, \mathbb{R})} \left( \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - v(x)|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x \right) \\ = \inf_{v \in C([a, b]^{d}, \mathbb{R})} \left( \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - \mathbb{E}[X_{x}]|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x + \int_{[a, b]^{d}} |\mathbb{E}[X_{x}] - v(x)|^{2} \, \mathrm{d}x \right) \end{split} \tag{17.7} $$

The assumption that $[a, b]^{d} \ni x \mapsto \mathbb{E}[X_{x}] \in \mathbb{R}$ is continuous therefore shows that

$$ \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - \mathbb{E}[X_{x}]|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x \geq \inf_{v \in C([a, b]^{d}, \mathbb{R})} \left( \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - \mathbb{E}[X_{x}]|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x \right) $$

$$ \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - \mathbb{E}[X_{x}]|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x \geq \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - \mathbb{E}[X_{x}]|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x . \tag{17.8} $$

Hence, we obtain that

$$ \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - \mathbb{E}[X_{x}]|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x = \inf_{v \in C([a, b]^{d}, \mathbb{R})} \left( \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - v(x)|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x \right) . \tag{17.9} $$

The fact that the function $[a, b]^{d} \ni x \mapsto \mathbb{E}[X_{x}] \in \mathbb{R}$ is continuous therefore establishes that there exists $u \in C([a, b]^{d}, \mathbb{R})$ such that

$$ \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - u(x)|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x = \inf_{v \in C([a, b]^{d}, \mathbb{R})} \left( \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - v(x)|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x \right) . \tag{17.10} $$

Furthermore, observe that (17.6) and (17.9) prove that for all $u \in C([a, b]^{d}, \mathbb{R})$ with

$$ \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - u(x)|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x = \inf_{v \in C([a, b]^{d}, \mathbb{R})} \left( \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - v(x)|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x \right) \tag{17.11} $$

it holds that

$$ \begin{split} \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - \mathbb{E}[X_{x}]|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x \\ = \inf_{v \in C([a, b]^{d}, \mathbb{R})} \left( \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - v(x)|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x \right) = \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - u(x)|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x \\ = \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}[|X_{x} - \mathbb{E}[X_{x}]|^{2}] \, \mathrm{d}x + \int_{[a, b]^{d}} |\mathbb{E}[X_{x}] - u(x)|^{2} \, \mathrm{d}x . \end{split} \tag{17.12} $$

Hence, we obtain that for all $u \in C([a, b]^{d}, \mathbb{R})$ with

$$ \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}\left[ \left| X_{x} - u(x) \right|^{2} \right] \, \mathrm{d}x = \inf_{v \in C([a, b]^{d}, \mathbb{R})} \left( \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}\left[ \left| X_{x} - v(x) \right|^{2} \right] \, \mathrm{d}x \right) \tag{17.13} $$

it holds that

$$ \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \left| \mathbb{E}\left[ X_{x} \right] - u(x) \right|^{2} \, \mathrm{d}x = 0. \tag{17.14} $$

This and the assumption that $[a, b]^{d} \ni x \mapsto \mathbb{E}[X_{x}] \in \mathbb{R}$ is continuous demonstrate that for all $y \in [a, b]^{d}$, $u \in C([a, b]^{d}, \mathbb{R})$ with

$$ \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}\left[ \left| X_{x} - u(x) \right|^{2} \right] \, \mathrm{d}x = \inf_{v \in C([a, b]^{d}, \mathbb{R})} \left( \int_{[a, b]^{d}} \mathbb{E}\left[ \left| X_{x} - v(x) \right|^{2} \right] \, \mathrm{d}x \right) \tag{17.15} $$

it holds that $u(y) = \mathbb{E}[X_{y}]$. Combining this with (17.10) establishes items (i) and (ii). The proof of Proposition 17.2.1 is thus complete.

## 17.3 Feynman–Kac formulas

### 17.3.1 Feynman–Kac formulas providing existence of solutions

**Lemma 17.3.1** *(A variant of Lebesgue’s theorem on dominated convergence). Let* $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ *be a probability space, for every* $n \in \mathbb{N}_{0}$ *let* $X_{n}: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ *be a random variable, assume for all* $\varepsilon \in (0, \infty)$ *that*

$$ \limsup_{n \rightarrow \infty} \mathbb{P}\left( \left| X_{n} - X_{0} \right| > \varepsilon \right) = 0, \tag{17.16} $$

*let* $Y: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ *be a random variable with* $\mathbb{E}\left[ \left| Y \right| \right] < \infty$, *and assume for all* $n \in \mathbb{N}$ *that* $\mathbb{P}\left( \left| X_{n} \right| \leq Y \right) = 1$. *Then*

- *(i) it holds that* $\limsup_{n \rightarrow \infty} \mathbb{E}\left[ \left| X_{n} - X_{0} \right| \right] = 0$,
- *(ii) it holds that* $\mathbb{E}\left[ \left| X_{0} \right| \right] < \infty$, *and*
- *(iii) it holds that* $\limsup_{n \rightarrow \infty} \mathbb{E}\left[ \left| X_{n} \right| \right] = 0$.

Proof of Lemma 17.3.1. Note that, for instance, the variant of Lebesgue’s theorem on dominated convergence in Klenke [248, Corollary 6.26] proves items (i), (ii), and (iii). The proof of Lemma 17.3.1 is thus complete.
