Furthermore, observe that the assumption that $X_k$, $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, K\}$, are i.i.d. random variables establishes that for all $\varepsilon \in (0, \infty)$ it holds that

$\mathbb{P}(\min_{k \in \{1, \dots, K\}} [\delta(X_k, x)]^p > \varepsilon) = \mathbb{P}(\forall k \in \{1, 2, \dots, K\} : [\delta(X_k, x)]^p > \varepsilon)$
$= \prod_{k=1}^K \mathbb{P}([\delta(X_k, x)]^p > \varepsilon) = [\mathbb{P}([\delta(X_1, x)]^p > \varepsilon)]^K = [\mathbb{P}(\delta(X_1, x) > \varepsilon^{1/p})]^K.$ (11.8)

Combining this with (11.7) proves (11.6). The proof of Lemma 11.1.2 is thus complete. $\square$

# **11.2.1 Properties of the gamma and the beta function**

**Lemma 11.2.1.** *Let* $\Gamma: (0, \infty) \to (0, \infty)$ *and* $\mathbb{B}: (0, \infty)^2 \to (0, \infty)$ *satisfy for all* $x, y \in (0, \infty)$ *that* $\Gamma(x) = \int_0^{\infty} t^{x-1} e^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t$ *and* $\mathbb{B}(x, y) = \int_0^1 t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}\,\mathrm{d}t$. *Then*

+   (*i*) *it holds for all* $x \in (0, \infty)$ *that* $\Gamma(x+1) = x\,\Gamma(x)$,

+   (*ii*) *it holds that* $\Gamma(1) = \Gamma(2) = 1$, *and*

+   (*iii*) *it holds for all* $x, y \in (0, \infty)$ *that* $\mathbb{B}(x, y) = \frac{\Gamma(x)\Gamma(y)}{\Gamma(x+y)}$.

*Proof of Lemma 11.2.1.* Throughout this proof, let $x, y \in (0, \infty)$, let $\Phi: (0, \infty) \times (0, 1) \to (0, \infty)^2$ satisfy for all $u \in (0, \infty)$, $v \in (0, 1)$ that

$\Phi(u, v) = (u(1-v), uv)$, (11.9)

and let $f: (0, \infty)^2 \to (0, \infty)$ satisfy for all $s, t \in (0, \infty)$ that

$f(s, t) = s^{(x-1)}\, t^{(y-1)}\, e^{-(s+t)}$. (11.10)

Note that the integration by parts formula proves that for all $x \in (0, \infty)$ it holds that

$\Gamma(x+1) = \int_0^{\infty} t^{(x+1)-1} e^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t = -\int_0^{\infty} t^x [ -e^{-t} ]\,\mathrm{d}t$
$= -\left( \left[ t^x e^{-t} \right]_{t=0}^{t=\infty} - x\int_0^{\infty} t^{(x-1)} e^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t \right) = x\int_0^{\infty} t^{(x-1)} e^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t = x \cdot \Gamma(x)$. (11.11)

This establishes item (i). Furthermore, observe that

$\Gamma(1) = \int_0^{\infty} t^0 e^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t = [ -e^{-t} ]_{t=0}^{t=\infty} = 1$. (11.12)
