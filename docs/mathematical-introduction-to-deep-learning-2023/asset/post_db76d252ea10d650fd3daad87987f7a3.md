Proof of Lemma 4.4.8. Throughout this proof, let $\eta_1, \eta_2, \dots, \eta_d \in \mathbb{R}$ satisfy

$$\pmb{\theta} = (\eta_1, \eta_2, \dots, \eta_d) \tag{4.173}$$

and let $\pmb{\Phi} \in \big(\bigtimes_{i=1}^{L} \mathbb{R}^{l_i \times l_{i-1}} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_L}\big)$ satisfy

$$\boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}(\pmb{\Phi}) = \big(\eta_1, \eta_2, \dots, \eta_{\mathcal{P}(\Phi)}\big) \tag{4.174}$$

(cf. Definitions 1.3.1 and 1.3.5). Note that Lemma 4.4.7 establishes that there exists $\Psi \in \mathbf{N}$ which satisfies

$$\mathcal{D}(\Psi) = (\mathfrak{l}_0, \mathfrak{l}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{l}_{\ulcorner}), \; \big\| \boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}(\Psi) \big\|_{\infty} \leq \max \big\{ 1, \big\| \boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}(\pmb{\Phi}) \big\|_{\infty} \big\}, \; \text{and} \; \mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{\mathfrak{T}}(\Psi) = \mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{\mathfrak{T}}(\pmb{\Phi}) \tag{4.175}$$

(cf. Definitions 1.2.4, 1.3.4, and 3.3.4). Next let $\vartheta = (\vartheta_1, \vartheta_2, \dots, \vartheta_{\mathfrak{l}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{l}}$ satisfy

$$\big(\vartheta_1, \vartheta_2, \dots, \vartheta_{\mathcal{P}(\Psi)}\big) = \boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}(\Psi) \quad \text{and} \quad \forall \, i \in \mathbb{N} \cap \big( \mathcal{P}(\Psi), \pmb{0} + 1 \big): \vartheta_i = 0. \tag{4.176}$$

Observe that (4.173), (4.174), (4.175), and (4.176) show that

$$\big\| \vartheta \big\|_{\infty} = \big\| \boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}(\Psi) \big\|_{\infty} \leq \max \big\{ 1, \big\| \boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}(\pmb{\Phi}) \big\|_{\infty} \big\} \leq \max \big\{ 1, \big\| \pmb{\theta} \big\|_{\infty} \big\}. \tag{4.177}$$

Furthermore, note that Lemma 4.4.2 and (4.174) ensure that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^{l_0}$ it holds that

$$\big( \mathcal{L}^{\theta, (l_0, l_1, \dots, l_L)}_{\infty, \infty} \big)(x) = \big( \mathcal{L}^{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}(\Psi), \mathcal{D}(\Psi)}_{\infty, \infty} \big)(x) = \big( \mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{\mathfrak{T}}(\pmb{\Phi}) \big)(x) \tag{4.178}$$

(cf. Definition 4.4.1). Moreover, observe that Lemma 4.4.2, (4.175), and (4.176) imply that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^{l_0}$ it holds that

$$\big( \mathcal{L}^{\vartheta, (l_0, l_1, \dots, l_{\ulcorner})}_{\infty, \infty} \big)(x) = \big( \mathcal{L}^{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}(\Psi), \mathcal{D}(\Psi)}_{\infty, \infty} \big)(x) = \big( \mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{N}}_{\mathfrak{T}}(\Psi) \big)(x). \tag{4.179}$$

Combining this and (4.178) with (4.175) and the assumption that $\mathfrak{l}_0 = l_0$ and $\mathfrak{l}_{\ulcorner} = l_L$ demonstrates that

$$\mathcal{L}^{\theta, (l_0, l_1, \dots, l_L)}_{\infty, \infty} = \mathcal{L}^{\vartheta, (l_0, l_1, \dots, l_{\ulcorner})}_{\infty, \infty}. \tag{4.180}$$

Therefore, we obtain that

$$\mathcal{L}^{\theta, (l_0, l_1, \dots, l_L)}_{u, v} = \mathcal{C}_{u, v, l_L} \circ \mathcal{L}^{\theta, (l_0, l_1, \dots, l_L)}_{\infty, \infty} = \mathcal{C}_{u, v, l_{\ulcorner}} \circ \mathcal{L}^{\vartheta, (l_0, l_1, \dots, l_{\ulcorner})}_{\infty, \infty} = \mathcal{L}^{\vartheta, (l_0, l_1, \dots, l_{\ulcorner})}_{u, v} \tag{4.181}$$

(cf. Definition 1.2.10). This and (4.177) prove (4.172). The proof of Lemma 4.4.8 is thus complete.

## 4.4.3 Approximation through ANNs with variable architectures

Corollary 4.4.9. Let $d, K, \mathbf{d}, \mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{N}$, $\mathbf{l} = (\mathbf{l_0}, \mathbf{l_1}, \dots, \mathbf{l_K}) \in \mathbb{N}^{K+1}$, $L \in [0, \infty)$ satisfy that

$$\mathbf{L} \geq [\log_2(K)] + 2, \quad \mathbf{l_0} = d, \quad \mathbf{l_K} = 1, \quad \mathbf{l_1} \geq 2dK, \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbf{d} \geq \sum_{i=1}^\mathbf{L} \mathbf{l_i} (\mathbf{l_{i-1}} + 1), \tag{4.182}$$

assume for all $i \in \mathbb{N} \cap (1, \mathbf{L})$ that $\mathbf{l_i} \geq 3 [\frac{K^i}{2^{i-1}}]$, let $E \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ be a set, let $\mathbf{r_1}, \mathbf{r_2}, \dots, \mathbf{r_K} \in E$, and let $f: E \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $x, y \in E$ that $|f(x) - f(y)| \leq L \|x - y\|_1$ (cf. Definitions 3.3.4 and 4.2.6). Then there exists $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^q$ such that

$$\|\theta\|_\infty \leq \max\{1, L, \max_{\mathbf{k} \in \{1,2,\dots,K\}} \|\mathbf{r_k}\|_\infty, 2 \max_{\mathbf{k} \in \{1,2,\dots,K\}} |f(\mathbf{r_k})|\} \tag{4.183}$$

and

$$\sup_{x \in E} |f(x) - \mathcal{A}_{\infty,\infty}^{\theta,1}(x)| \leq 2L [\sup_{x \in E} (\inf_{\mathbf{k} \in \{1,2,\dots,K\}} \|x - \mathbf{r_k}\|_1)] \tag{4.184}$$

(cf. Definition 4.4.1).

Proof of Corollary 4.4.9. Throughout this proof, let $\boldsymbol{\eta} \in \mathbb{R}^K$, $\boldsymbol{\Phi} \in \mathbb{N}$ satisfy $\boldsymbol{\eta} = (f(\mathbf{r_1}), f(\mathbf{r_2}), \dots, f(\mathbf{r_K}))$ and

$$\boldsymbol{\Phi} = \mathbb{M}_K \bullet \mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{L} - 1, \mathbf{L}_K} \bullet \mathbf{P}_K (\underline{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathbf{d}} \bullet \mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{l_d}, -\mathbf{r_1}}, \underline{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathbf{d}} \bullet \mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{l_d}, -\mathbf{r_2}}, \dots, \underline{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathbf{d}} \bullet \mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{l_d}, -\mathbf{r_K}}) \bullet \mathbb{T}_{\mathbf{d}, K} \tag{4.185}$$

(cf. Definitions 1.3.1, 1.5.5, 2.1.1, 2.2.1, 2.3.1, 2.4.6, 4.2.1, and 4.2.5). Note that Lemma 4.2.9 and Proposition 4.3.1 establish that

- (I) it holds that $\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}) = [\log_2(K)] + 2$,
- (II) it holds that $\mathcal{I}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}) = d$,
- (III) it holds that $\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}) = 1$,
- (IV) it holds that $\mathbb{D}_1(\boldsymbol{\Phi}) = 2dK$,
- (V) it holds for all $i \in \{2, 3, \dots, \mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}) - 1\}$ that $\mathbb{D}_i(\boldsymbol{\Phi}) \leq 3 [\frac{K^i}{2^{i-1}}]$,
- (VI) it holds that $\|\mathcal{T}(\boldsymbol{\Phi})\|_\infty \leq \max\{1, L, \max_{\mathbf{k} \in \{1,2,\dots,K\}} \|\mathbf{r_k}\|_\infty, 2 \max_{\mathbf{k} \in \{1,2,\dots,K\}} |f(\mathbf{r_k})|\}$, and
- (VII) it holds that $\sup_{x \in E} |f(x) - (\mathcal{R}_\mathcal{K}^{\mathbf{N}}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}))(x)| \leq 2L [\sup_{x \in E} (\inf_{\mathbf{k} \in \{1,2,\dots,K\}} \|x - \mathbf{r_k}\|_1)]$

(cf. Definitions 1.2.4, 1.3.4, and 1.3.5). Furthermore, observe that the fact that $\mathbf{L} \geq [\log_2(K)] + 2 = \mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\Phi})$, the fact that $\mathbf{l_0} = d = \mathbb{D}_0(\boldsymbol{\Phi})$, the fact that $\mathbf{l_1} \geq 2dK = \mathbb{D}_1(\boldsymbol{\Phi})$, the fact that for all $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, \mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}) - 1\} \setminus \{1\}$ it holds that $\mathbf{l_i} \geq 3 [\frac{K^i}{2^{i-1}}] \geq \mathbb{D}_i(\boldsymbol{\Phi})$, the fact that for all $i \in \mathbb{N} \cap (\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}) - 1, \mathbf{L})$ it holds that $\mathbf{l_i} \geq 3 [\frac{K^i}{2^{i-1}}] \geq 2 = 2\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\Phi})}(\boldsymbol{\Phi})$, the fact that $\mathbf{l_K} = 1 = \mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\Phi})}(\boldsymbol{\Phi})$, and Lemma 4.4.8 show that there exists $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^q$ which satisfies that

$$\|\theta\|_\infty \leq \max\{1, \|\mathcal{T}(\boldsymbol{\Phi})\|_\infty\} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{A}_{\infty,\infty}^{\theta, (\mathbf{l_0},1,\dots,1,\mathbf{l}_\mathbf{L})} = \mathcal{A}_{\infty,\infty}^{\mathcal{T}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}), \mathcal{D}(\boldsymbol{\Phi})}. \tag{4.186}$$

This and item (VI) ensure that

$$\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_{\infty} \leq \max\{1, L, \max_{\boldsymbol{k} \in \{1,2,\dots,K\}} \|\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\|_{\infty}, 2\max_{\boldsymbol{k} \in \{1,2,\dots,K\}} |f(\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{k}})|\} \tag{4.187}.$$

Moreover, note that (4.186), Lemma 4.4.2, and item (VII) imply that

$$\begin{aligned} \sup_{x \in E} |f(x) - \mathscr{A}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}, (\boldsymbol{l}_0, 1, \dots, \boldsymbol{l}_{\boldsymbol{L}})}_{\infty, \infty}(x)| &= \sup_{x \in E} |f(x) - \mathscr{A}^{T(\boldsymbol{\Phi}), \mathscr{D}(\boldsymbol{\Phi})}_{\infty, \infty}(x)| \\
&= \sup_{x \in E} |f(x) - (\mathscr{R}_{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\boldsymbol{N}}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}))(x)| \\
&\leq 2L \left[\sup_{x \in E} \left(\text{inf}_{\boldsymbol{k} \in \{1,2,\dots,K\}} \|x - \boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\|_1\right)\right] \tag{4.188} \end{aligned}$$

(cf. Definition 4.4.1). The proof of Corollary 4.4.9 is thus complete.

Corollary 4.4.10. Let $\boldsymbol{d}, \boldsymbol{K}, \boldsymbol{L} \in \mathbb{N}$, $\boldsymbol{l} = (\boldsymbol{l}_0, 1, \dots, \boldsymbol{l}_{\boldsymbol{L}}) \in \mathbb{N}^{\boldsymbol{L}+1}$, $L \in [0, \infty)$, $\boldsymbol{u} \in [-\infty, \infty)$, $v \in (u, \infty]$ satisfy that

$$\boldsymbol{L} \geq [\log_2 K] + 2, \quad \boldsymbol{l}_0 = \boldsymbol{d}, \quad \boldsymbol{l}_{\boldsymbol{L}} = 1, \quad \boldsymbol{l}_1 \geq 2\boldsymbol{d}K, \quad \text{and} \quad \boldsymbol{d} \geq \sum_{i=1}^{\boldsymbol{L}} \boldsymbol{l}_i(\boldsymbol{l}_{i-1}+1), \tag{4.189}$$

assume for all $i \in \mathbb{N} \cap \{1, \boldsymbol{L}\}$ that $\boldsymbol{l}_i \geq 3[\frac{K^i}{2^{i-1}}]$, let $E \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ be a set, let $\boldsymbol{f}_1, \boldsymbol{f}_2, \dots, \boldsymbol{f}_K \in E$, and let $f: E \to ([u, v] \cap \mathbb{R})$ satisfy for all $x, y \in E$ that $|f(x) - f(y)| \leq L\|x - y\|_1$ (cf. Definitions 3.3.4 and 4.2.6). Then there exists $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^\boldsymbol{d}$ such that

$$\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_{\infty} \leq \max\{1, L, \max_{\boldsymbol{k} \in \{1,2,\dots,K\}} \|\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\|_{\infty}, 2\max_{\boldsymbol{k} \in \{1,2,\dots,K\}} |f(\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{k}})|\} \tag{4.190}$$

and

$$\sup_{x \in E} |f(x) - \mathscr{A}_{u, v}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}, 1}(x)| \leq 2L \left[\sup_{x \in E} \left(\text{inf}_{\boldsymbol{k} \in \{1,2,\dots,K\}} \|x - \boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\|_1\right)\right]. \tag{4.191}$$

(cf. Definition 4.4.1).

Proof of Corollary 4.4.10. Observe that Corollary 4.4.9 demonstrates that there exists $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^\boldsymbol{d}$ such that

$$\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_{\infty} \leq \max\{1, L, \max_{\boldsymbol{k} \in \{1,2,\dots,K\}} \|\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\|_{\infty}, 2\max_{\boldsymbol{k} \in \{1,2,\dots,K\}} |f(\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{k}})|\} \tag{4.192}$$

and

$$\sup_{x \in E} |f(x) - \mathscr{A}_{u, v}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}, 1}(x)| \leq 2L \left[\sup_{x \in E} \left(\text{inf}_{\boldsymbol{k} \in \{1,2,\dots,K\}} \|x - \boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\|_1\right)\right]. \tag{4.193}$$

Furthermore, note that the assumption that $f(E) \subseteq [u, v]$ proves that for all $x \in E$ it holds that

$$f(x) = \boldsymbol{c}_{u, v}(f(x)) \tag{4.194}$$

(cf. Definitions 1.2.9 and 4.4.1). The fact that for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$ it holds that $|\boldsymbol{c}_{u, v}(x) - \boldsymbol{c}_{u, v}(y)| \leq |x - y|$ and (4.193) hence establish that

$$\begin{aligned} \sup_{x \in E} & |f(x) - \mathscr{A}_{u, v}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}, 1}(x)| = \sup_{x \in E} |\boldsymbol{c}_{u, v}(f(x)) - \boldsymbol{c}_{u, v}(\mathscr{A}_{\infty, \infty}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}, 1}(x))| \\
&\leq \sup_{x \in E} |f(x) - \mathscr{A}_{\infty, \infty}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}, 1}(x)| \leq 2L \left[\sup_{x \in E} \left(\text{inf}_{\boldsymbol{k} \in \{1,2,\dots,K\}} \|x - \boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\|_1\right)\right]. \tag{4.195} \end{aligned}$$

The proof of Corollary 4.4.10 is thus complete.
