Proof of Corollary 9.11.7. Note that Theorem 9.9.1 shows that for all $\vartheta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{o}}$ there exist $\varepsilon, \mathfrak{C} \in (0, \infty)$, $\alpha \in (0,1)$ such that for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{o}}$ with $\|\theta - \vartheta\|_2 < \varepsilon$ it holds that

$|\mathcal{L}(\theta) - \mathcal{L}(\vartheta)|^{\alpha} \leq \mathfrak{C} \| (\nabla \mathcal{L})(\theta) \|_2$. (9.202)

Corollary 9.11.6 therefore establishes (9.201). The proof of Corollary 9.11.7 is thus complete. $\square$

# ### 9.12.1 One-step descent property for GD processes

**Lemma 9.12.1.** *Let $\mathfrak{o} \in \mathbb{N}$, $L \in \mathbb{R}$, let $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{o}}$ be open and convex, let $\mathcal{L} \in C^1(U, \mathbb{R})$, and assume for all $x, y \in U$ that*

$\| (\nabla \mathcal{L})(x) - (\nabla \mathcal{L})(y) \|_2 \leq L \| x - y \|_2$ (9.204)

*(cf. Definition 3.3.4). Then it holds for all $x, y \in U$ that*

$\mathcal{L}(y) \leq \mathcal{L}(x) + \langle (\nabla \mathcal{L})(x), y - x \rangle + \frac{L}{2} \| x - y \|_2^2$ (9.205)

*(cf. Definition 1.4.7).*

**Proof of Lemma 9.12.1.** *Observe that the fundamental theorem of calculus, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and (9.204) prove that for all $x, y \in U$ we have that*

$\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal{L}(y) - \mathcal{L}(x) \\
&= \big[ \mathcal{L}(x + r(y-x)) \big]_{r=0}^{r=1} = \int_0^1 \langle (\nabla \mathcal{L})(x + r(y-x)), y - x \rangle \, \mathrm{d}r \\
&= \langle (\nabla \mathcal{L})(x), y - x \rangle + \int_0^1 \langle (\nabla \mathcal{L})(x + r(y-x)) - (\nabla \mathcal{L})(x), y - x \rangle \, \mathrm{d}r \\
&\leq \langle (\nabla \mathcal{L})(x), y - x \rangle + \int_0^1 \big| \langle (\nabla \mathcal{L})(x + r(y-x)) - (\nabla \mathcal{L})(x), y - x \rangle \big| \, \mathrm{d}r \\
&\leq \langle (\nabla \mathcal{L})(x), y - x \rangle + \left[ \int_0^1 \| (\nabla \mathcal{L})(x + r(y-x)) - (\nabla \mathcal{L})(x) \|_2 \, \mathrm{d}r \right] \| y - x \|_2 \\
&\leq \langle (\nabla \mathcal{L})(x), y - x \rangle + L \| y - x \|_2 \left[ \int_0^1 \| r(y - x) \|_2 \, \mathrm{d}r \right] \\
&= \langle (\nabla \mathcal{L})(x), y - x \rangle + \frac{L}{2} \| x - y \|_2^2
\end{aligned}$ (9.206)
