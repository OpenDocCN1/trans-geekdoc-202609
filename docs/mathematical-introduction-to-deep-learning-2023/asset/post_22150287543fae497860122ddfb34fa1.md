
## 9.11. Convergence analysis for solutions of GF ODEs

The assumption that for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that $\Theta_t = \Theta_0 - \int_0^t \mathscr{F}(\Theta_s) \, ds$ shows that for all $r, s, t \in [0, \infty)$ with $r \leq s \leq t$ it holds that
$$
\|\Theta_t - \Theta_s\|_2 = \left\|\int_s^t \mathscr{F}(\Theta_u) \, du\right\|_2 \leq \int_s^t \|\mathscr{F}(\Theta_u)\|_2 \, du \leq \int_r^\infty \|\mathscr{F}(\Theta_u)\|_2 \, du = \mathscr{G}_r. \tag{9.146}
$$

Next note that (9.144) and (9.145) imply that $\infty > \mathscr{G}_0 \geq \lim\sup_{r \to \infty} \mathscr{G}_r = 0$. Combining this with (9.146) demonstrates that there exist $\psi \in \mathbb{R}^9$ which satisfies
$$
\lim\sup_{t \to \infty} \|\Theta_t - \psi\|_2 = 0. \tag{9.147}
$$

In the next step of our proof of items (i), (ii), (iii), and (iv) we show that $\mathcal{L}(\Theta_t), \ t \in [0, \infty), \) converges to $\mathcal{L}(\psi)$ with convergence order 1. We accomplish this by bringing a suitable differential inequality for the reciprocal of the function $\mathbf{L}$ in (9.128) into play (see (9.150) below for details). More specifically, observe that (9.135), (9.145), (9.130), and (9.126) ensure that for almost all $t \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that
$$
\mathbf{L}'(t) = -\|\mathscr{F}(\Theta_t)\|_2^2 \leq -\mathbf{c}^{-2}[\mathbf{L}(t)]^{2\alpha}. \tag{9.148}
$$

Hence, we obtain that $\mathbf{L}$ is non-increasing. This proves that for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that $\mathbf{L}(t) \leq \mathbf{L}(0)$. This and the fact that for all $t \in [0, \tau)$ it holds that $\mathbf{L}(t) > 0$ establish that for almost all $t \in [0, \tau)$ it holds that
$$
\mathbf{L}'(t) \leq -\mathbf{c}^{-2}[\mathbf{L}(t)]^{(2\alpha - 2)}[\mathbf{L}(t)]^2 \leq -\mathbf{c}^{-2}[\mathbf{L}(0)]^{(2\alpha - 2)}[\mathbf{L}(t)]^2 = -\mathfrak{D}^{-1}[\mathbf{L}(t)]^2. \tag{9.149}
$$

Therefore, we obtain that for almost all $t \in [0, \tau)$ it holds that
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial t} \left( \frac{\mathfrak{D}}{\mathbf{L}(t)} \right) = - \left( \frac{\mathfrak{D}\, \mathbf{L}(t)}{[\mathbf{L}(t)]^2} \right)^{-1} \geq 1. \tag{9.150}
$$

Furthermore, note that the fact that for all $t \in [0, \tau)$ it holds that $[0, t] \ni s \mapsto \mathbf{L}(s) \in (0, \infty)$ is absolutely continuous shows that for all $t \in [0, \tau)$ it holds that $[0, t] \ni s \mapsto \mathfrak{D}\mathbf{L}(s)^{-1} \in (0, \infty)$ is absolutely continuous. This and (9.150) imply that for all $t \in [0, \tau)$ it holds that
$$
\frac{\mathfrak{D}}{\mathbf{L}(t)} - \frac{\mathfrak{D}}{\mathbf{L}(0)} \geq t. \tag{9.151}
$$

Hence, we obtain that for all $t \in [0, \tau)$ it holds that
$$
\frac{\mathfrak{D}}{\mathbf{L}(t)} \geq \frac{\mathfrak{D}}{\mathbf{L}(0)} + t. \tag{9.152}
$$

Therefore, we obtain that for all $t \in [0, \tau)$ it holds that
$$
\mathfrak{D}\left( \frac{\mathfrak{D}}{\mathbf{L}(0)} + t \right)^{-1} \geq \mathbf{L}(t). \tag{9.153}
$$

This demonstrates that for all $t \in [0, \tau)$ it holds that
$$
\mathbf{L}(t) \leq \mathfrak{D}\left[\mathbf{L}\left(t_{0}\right)^{-1}+t\right]^{-1}=\mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2-2 \alpha}\left(\mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{1-2 \alpha}+t\right)^{-1}=\mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2}\left(\mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}+\mathfrak{c}^{2 \alpha} t\right)^{-1} . \quad (9.154)
$$

The fact that for all $t \in[\tau, \infty)$ it holds that $\mathbf{L}(t)=0$ and (9.131) hence ensure that for all $t \in[0, \infty)$ it holds that
$$
0 \leq \mathbf{L}(t) \leq \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2}\left(\mathbf{L}(0)(\epsilon)+\mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}+\mathfrak{c}^{2 \alpha} t\right)^{-1} . \quad(9.155)
$$

Moreover, observe that (9.147) and the assumption that $\mathcal{L} \in C\left(\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{D}}, \mathbb{R}\right)$ prove that $\limsup _{t \rightarrow \infty}\left|\mathcal{L}\left(\Theta_{t}\right)-\mathcal{L}(\vartheta)\right| =0$. Combining this with (9.155) establishes that $\mathcal{L}\left(\psi\right)=\mathcal{L}(\vartheta)$. This and (9.155) show that for all $t \in[0, \infty)$ it holds that
$$
0 \leq \mathcal{L}\left(\Theta_{t}\right)-\mathcal{L}(\psi) \leq \mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2}\left(\mathbf{L}(0)(\epsilon)+\mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}+\mathfrak{c}^{2 \alpha} t\right)^{-1} . \quad(9.156)
$$

In the final step of our proof of items (i), (ii), (iii), and (iv) we establish convergence rates for the real numbers $\|\Theta_{t}-\psi\|_{2}$, $t \in[0, \infty)$. Note that (9.147), (9.146), and (9.142) imply that for all $t \in[0, \infty)$ it holds that
$$
\left\|\Theta_{t}-\psi\right\|_{2}=\left\|\Theta_{t}-\left[\lim _{s \rightarrow \infty} \Theta_{s}\right]\right\|_{2}=\lim _{s \rightarrow \infty}\left\|\Theta_{t}-\Theta_{s}\right\|_{2} \leq \mathscr{G}_{t} \leq \mathfrak{C}(1-\alpha)^{-1}[\mathbf{L}(t)]^{-1-\alpha} . \quad(9.157)
$$

This and (9.156) demonstrate that for all $t \in[0, \infty)$ it holds that
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \left\|\Theta_{t}-\psi\right\|_{2} \leq \mathscr{G}_{t} \leq \mathfrak{C}(1-\alpha)^{-1}\left[\mathcal{L}\left(\Theta_{t}\right)-\mathcal{L}(\psi)\right]^{-1} \alpha \\
& \quad \leq \mathfrak{C}(1-\alpha)^{-1}\left[\mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}^{2}\left(\mathbf{L}(0)(\epsilon)+\mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}+\mathfrak{c}^{2 \alpha} t\right)\right]^{-1-\alpha} \\
& \quad =\mathfrak{C}^{3-2 \alpha} \mathfrak{c}^{2-2 \alpha}\left(1-\alpha\right)^{-1}\left(\mathbf{L}(0)(\epsilon)+\mathfrak{C}^{2} \mathfrak{c}+\mathfrak{c}^{2 \alpha} t\right) \alpha^{-1} . \quad(9.158)
\end{aligned}
$$

Combining this with (9.132) and (9.156) proves items (i), (ii), (iii), and (iv). The proof of Proposition 9.11.2 is thus complete.

### Corollary 9.11.3

Let $\mathfrak{D} \in \mathbb{N}, \vartheta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{D}}, \mathfrak{c} \in[0,1], \mathcal{C}, \epsilon \in(0, \infty), \alpha \in(0,1)$, $\Theta \in C\left([0, \infty), \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{D}}\right)$, $\mathcal{L} \in C\left(\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{D}}, \mathbb{R}\right)$, let $\mathscr{G}: \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{D}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{D}}$ be $\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{D}}\right) / \mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{D}}\right)$-measurable, assume for all $t \in[0, \infty)$ that
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathcal{L}\left(\Theta_{t}\right)=\mathcal{L}\left(\Theta_{0}\right)-\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathscr{G}\left(\Theta_{s}\right)\right\|_{2}^{2} d s \quad \text { and } \quad \Theta_{t}=\Theta_{0}-\int_{0}^{t} \mathscr{G}\left(\Theta_{s}\right) d s, \quad(9.159)
\end{aligned}
$$
and assume for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{D}}$ with $\|\theta-\vartheta\|_{2}<\epsilon$ that
$$
\left|\mathcal{L}(\theta)-\mathcal{L}(\vartheta)\right|^{\alpha} \leq \mathfrak{C}\|\mathscr{G}(\theta)\|_{2}, \quad \mathfrak{c}=\left|\mathcal{L}\left(\Theta_{0}\right)-\mathcal{L}(\vartheta)\right|, \quad \mathfrak{C}(1-\alpha)^{-1} \mathfrak{c}^{1-\alpha}+\|\Theta_{0}-\vartheta\|_{2}<\epsilon, \quad (9.160)
$$
and
$$
\inf _{\{t \in[0, \infty) ; \vartheta \in[0, s] ;\|\Theta_{s}-\vartheta\|_{2} \leq \epsilon\}} \mathcal{L}\left(\Theta_{t}\right) \geq \mathcal{L}(\vartheta) \text { (cf. Definition } 3.3.4) \text {. }
$$
Then there exists $\psi \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{D}}$ such that for all $t \in[0, \infty)$ it holds that $\mathcal{L}(\psi)=\mathcal{L}(\vartheta)$, $\|\Theta_{t}-\psi\|_{2}<\epsilon$, $0 \leq$
$$
\mathcal{L}\left(\Theta_{t}\right)-\mathcal{L}(\psi) \leq\left(1+\mathfrak{C}^{-2} t\right)^{-1}, \text { and }
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|\Theta_t - \psi\|_2 &\le \int_t^\infty \|\mathscr{G}(\mathfrak{S}_s)\|_2 \, \mathrm{d}s \\
&\le \mathfrak{C}(1-\alpha)^{-1}[\mathcal{L}(\Theta_t) - \mathcal{L}(\psi)]^{1-\alpha} \\
&\le \mathfrak{C}^{3-2\alpha}\mathfrak{C}^{2-2\alpha}(1-\alpha)^{-1}(\mathbf{L}(0)(\mathfrak{c}) + \mathfrak{C}^{2}\mathfrak{c} + \mathfrak{C}^{2\alpha}t)^{\alpha-1}.
\end{aligned}
\quad (9.162)
$$

Proof of Corollary 9.11.3. Observe that Proposition 9.11.2 ensures that there exists $\psi \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ which satisfies that
- (i) it holds that $\mathcal{L}(\psi) = \mathcal{L}(\vartheta)$,
- (ii) it holds for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ that $\|\Theta_t - \psi\|_2 < \varepsilon$,
- (iii) it holds for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ that $0 \le \mathcal{L}(\Theta_t) - \mathcal{L}(\psi) \le \mathfrak{C}^{2}\mathfrak{C}^{2}(\mathbf{L}(0)(\mathfrak{c}) + \mathfrak{C}^{2}\mathfrak{c} + \mathfrak{C}^{2\alpha}t)^{-1}$, and
- (iv) it holds for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ that
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|\Theta_t - \psi\|_2 &\le \int_t^\infty \|\mathscr{G}(\mathfrak{S}_s)\|_2 \, \mathrm{d}s \\
&\le \mathfrak{C}(1-\alpha)^{-1}[\mathcal{L}(\Theta_t) - \mathcal{L}(\psi)]^{1-\alpha} \\
&\le \mathfrak{C}^{3-2\alpha}\mathfrak{C}^{2-2\alpha}(1-\alpha)^{-1}(\mathbf{L}(0)(\mathfrak{c}) + \mathfrak{C}^{2}\mathfrak{c} + \mathfrak{C}^{2\alpha}t)^{\alpha-1}.
\end{aligned}
\quad (9.162)
$$

Note that item (iii) and the assumption that $\mathfrak{c} \le 1$ establish that for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that
$$
0 \le \mathcal{L}(\Theta_t) - \mathcal{L}(\psi) \n\le \mathfrak{C}^{2}(\mathfrak{C}^{2}\mathbf{L}(0)(\mathfrak{c}) + \mathfrak{c} + \mathfrak{C}^{-2}\mathfrak{C}^{2\alpha}t)^{-1} \n\le (1+\mathfrak{C}^{-2}t)^{-1}.
\quad (9.163)
$$

This and item (iv) show that for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ it holds that
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|\Theta_t - \psi\|_2 &\le \int_t^\infty \|\mathscr{G}(\mathfrak{S}_s)\|_2 \, \mathrm{d}s \\
&\le \mathfrak{C}(1-\alpha)^{-1}[\mathcal{L}(\Theta_t) - \mathcal{L}(\psi)]^{1-\alpha} \\
&\le \mathfrak{C}(1-\alpha)^{-1}(1+\mathfrak{C}^{-2}t)^{\alpha-1}.
\end{aligned}
\quad (9.164)
$$

Combining this with item (i), item (ii), and (9.163) proves (9.161). The proof of Corollary 9.11.3 is thus complete.

## 9.11.2 Abstract global convergence results for GF processes

Proposition 9.11.4. Let $\mathfrak{d} \in \mathbb{N}$, $\Theta \in C([0, \infty), \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}})$, $\mathcal{L} \in C(\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}, \mathbb{R})$, let $\mathscr{G} : \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} \to \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ be $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}})/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}})$-measurable, assume that for all $\vartheta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ there exist $\varepsilon, \mathfrak{C} \in (0, \infty)$, $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ such that for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ with $\|\theta - \vartheta\|_2 < \varepsilon$ it holds that
$$
|\mathcal{L}(\theta) - \mathcal{L}(\vartheta)|^{\alpha} \n\le \mathfrak{C}\|\mathscr{G}(\theta)\|_2,
\quad (9.165)
$$
assume for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ that
$$
\mathcal{L}(\Theta_t) = \mathcal{L}(\Theta_0) - \int_0^t \|\mathscr{G}(\mathfrak{S}_s)\|_2^2 \, \mathrm{d}s
\quad \text{and} \quad
\Theta_t = \Theta_0 - \int_0^t \mathscr{G}(\mathfrak{S}_s) \, \mathrm{d}s,
\quad (9.166)
$$
and assume $\lim_{t\to\infty}\inf \|\Theta_t\|_2 < \infty$. Then there exist $\vartheta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$, $\mathfrak{C}, \tau, \beta \in (0, \infty)$ such that for all $t \in [\tau, \infty)$ it holds that
$$
\|\Theta_t - \vartheta\|_2 \n\le (1+\mathfrak{C}(t-\tau))^{-\beta}
\quad \text{and} \quad
0 \le \mathcal{L}(\Theta_t) - \mathcal{L}(\vartheta) \n\le (1+\mathfrak{C}(t-\tau))^{-1}.
\quad (9.167)
$$
