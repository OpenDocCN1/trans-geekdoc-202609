
## 12.2.2 Inequalities for packing entropy quantities in metric spaces

### 12.2.2.1 Lower bounds for packing radii based on lower bounds for packing numbers

**Lemma 12.2.8** (Lower bounds for packing radii) Let $(X, d)$ be a metric space and let $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $r \in [0, \infty]$ satisfy $n \le \mathcal{P}_{(X, d), r}$ (cf. Definition 12.2.7). Then $r \le \mathcal{P}_{(X, d), n}$ (cf. Definition 12.2.6).

*Proof of Lemma 12.2.8.* Note that (12.77) ensures that there exist $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_{n+1} \in X$ such that

$$ \left[ \min_{i,j \in \{1,2,\ldots,n+1\}, i \ne j} d(x_i, x_j) \right] > 2r. \tag{12.78} $$

This implies that $\mathcal{P}_{(X, d), n} \ge r$ (cf. Definition 12.2.6). The proof of Lemma 12.2.8 is thus complete.

### 12.2.2.2 Upper bounds for packing numbers based on upper bounds for packing radii

**Lemma 12.2.9.** Let $(X, d)$ be a metric space and let $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $r \in [0, \infty]$ satisfy $\mathcal{P}_{(X, d), n} < r$ (cf. Definition 12.2.6). Then $\mathcal{P}_{(X, d), r} < n$ (cf. Definition 12.2.7).

*Proof of Lemma 12.2.9.* Observe that Lemma 12.2.8 establishes that $\mathcal{P}_{(X, d), r} < n$ (cf. Definition 12.2.7). The proof of Lemma 12.2.9 is thus complete.

### 12.2.2.3 Upper bounds for packing radii based on upper bounds for covering radii

**Lemma 12.2.10.** Let $(X, d)$ be a metric space and let $n \in \mathbb{N}$. Then $\mathcal{P}_{(X, d), n} \le \mathcal{C}_{(X, d), n}$ (cf. Definitions 12.2.1 and 12.2.6).

*Proof of Lemma 12.2.10.* Throughout this proof, assume without loss of generality that $\mathcal{C}_{(X, d), n} < \infty$ and $\mathcal{P}_{(X, d), n} > 0$, let $r \in [0, \infty)$, $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n \in X$ satisfy

$$ X \subseteq \left[ \bigcup_{m=1}^n \{ v \in X : d(x_m, v) \le r \} \right], \tag{12.79} $$

let $\mathbf{r} \in [0, \infty)$, $\mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{x}_{n+1} \in X$ satisfy

$$ \left[ \min_{i,j \in \{1,2,\ldots,n+1\}, i \ne j} d(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j) \right] > 2\mathbf{r}, \tag{12.80} $$

and let $\varphi : X \rightarrow \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$ satisfy for all $v \in X$ that

$$ \varphi(v) = \min\{ m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n\} : v \in \{ w \in X : d(x_m, w) \le r \} \} \tag{12.81} $$

(cf. Definitions 12.2.1 and 12.2.6 and Lemma 12.2.5). Observe that (12.81) shows that for all $v \in X$ it holds that

$$v \in \{w \in X : d(x_{\varphi(v)}, w) \leq r\}.$$

Hence, we obtain that for all $v \in X$ it holds that

$$d(v, x_{\varphi(v)}) \leq r$$

Moreover, note that the fact that $\varphi(\mathbf{x}_1), \varphi(\mathbf{x}_2), \ldots, \varphi(\mathbf{x}_{n+1}) \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$ ensures that there exist $i, j \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n+1\}$ which satisfy

$$i \neq j \quad \text{and} \quad \varphi(\mathbf{x}_i) = \varphi(\mathbf{x}_j).$$

The triangle inequality, (12.80), and (12.83) hence show that

$$2r < d(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j) \leq d(\mathbf{x}_i, x_{\varphi(\mathbf{x}_i)}) + d(x_{\varphi(\mathbf{x}_i)}, \mathbf{x}_j) = d(\mathbf{x}_i, x_{\varphi(\mathbf{x}_i)}) + d(\mathbf{x}_j, x_{\varphi(\mathbf{x}_j)}) \leq 2r.$$

This implies that $\mathbf{r} < r$. The proof of Lemma 12.2.10 is thus complete.

### 12.2.2.4 Upper bounds for packing radii in balls of metric spaces

**Lemma 12.2.11.** Let $(X, d)$ be a metric space, let $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $x \in X$, $r \in (0, \infty]$, and let $S = \{v \in X : d(x, v) \leq r\}$. Then $\mathcal{P}_{(S, d | S), n} \leq r$ (cf. Definition 12.2.6).

*Proof of Lemma 12.2.11.* Throughout this proof, assume without loss of generality that $\mathcal{P}_{(S, d | S), n} > 0$ (cf. Definition 12.2.6). Observe that for all $\mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{x}_{n+1} \in S$, $i, j \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n+1\}$ it holds that

$$d(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j) \leq d(\mathbf{x}_i, x) + d(x, \mathbf{x}_j) \leq 2r.$$

Hence, we obtain that for all $\mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{x}_{n+1} \in S$ it holds that

$$\min_{i, j \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n+1\}, i \neq j} d(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j) \leq 2r.$$

Moreover, note that (12.75) ensures that for all $\rho \in [0, \mathcal{P}_{(S, d | S), n})$ there exist $\mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{x}_{n+1} \in S$ such that

$$\min_{i, j \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n+1\}, i \neq j} d(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j) > 2\rho.$$

This and (12.87) demonstrate that for all $\rho \in [0, \mathcal{P}_{(S, d | S), n})$ it holds that $2\rho < 2r$. The proof of Lemma 12.2.11 is thus complete.

## 12.2.3 Inequalities for covering entropy quantities in metric spaces

### 12.2.3.1 Upper bounds for covering numbers based on upper bounds for covering radii

**Lemma 12.2.12.** Let $(X, d)$ be a metric space and let $r \in [0, \infty]$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$ satisfy $\mathcal{C}_{(X,d),n} < r$ (cf. Definition 12.2.1). Then $\mathcal{C}^{(X,d),r} \leq n$ (cf. Definition 4.3.2).

*Proof of Lemma 12.2.12.* Observe that the assumption that $\mathcal{C}_{(X,d),n} < r$ ensures that there exists $A \subseteq X$ such that $|A| \leq n$ and

$$X \subseteq \left[\bigcup_{a \in A} \left\{v \in X : d(a, v) \leq r \right\}\right]. \tag{12.89}$$

This establishes that $\mathcal{C}^{(X,d),r} \leq n$ (cf. Definition 4.3.2). The proof of Lemma 12.2.12 is thus complete.

**Lemma 12.2.13.** Let $(X, d)$ be a compact metric space and let $r \in [0, \infty]$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, satisfy $\mathcal{C}_{(X,d),n} \leq r$ (cf. Definition 12.2.1). Then $\mathcal{C}^{(X,d),r} \leq n$ (cf. Definition 4.3.2).

*Proof of Lemma 12.2.13.* Throughout this proof, assume without loss of generality that $X \neq \emptyset$ and let $x_{k,m} \in X$, $m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$, $k \in \mathbb{N}$, satisfy for all $k \in \mathbb{N}$ that

$$X \subseteq \left[\bigcup_{m=1}^{n} \left\{v \in X : d(x_{k,m}, v) \leq r + \frac{1}{k} \right\}\right] \tag{12.90}$$

(cf. Lemma 12.2.4). Note that the assumption that $(X, d)$ is a compact metric space demonstrates that there exist $\mathfrak{r} = (\mathfrak{r}_m)_{m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}} : \{1, 2, \ldots, n\} \rightarrow X$ and $k = (k_i)_{i \in \mathbb{N}} : \mathbb{N} \rightarrow \mathbb{N}$ which satisfy that

$$\lim \sup_{l \to \infty} \max_{m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}} d(\mathfrak{r}_m, x_{k_l,m}) = 0 \quad \text{ and } \quad \lim \sup_{l \to \infty} k_l = \infty. \tag{12.91}$$

Next, observe that the assumption that $d$ is a metric ensures that for all $v \in X$, $m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$, $l \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that

$$d(v, \mathfrak{r}_m) \leq d(v, x_{k_l,m}) + d(x_{k_l,m}, \mathfrak{r}_m). \tag{12.92}$$

This and (12.90) prove that for all $v \in X$, $l \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that

$$\begin{aligned}
\min_{m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}} d(v, \mathfrak{r}_m) &\leq \min_{m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}} \left[d(v, x_{k_l,m}) + d(x_{k_l,m}, \mathfrak{r}_m)\right] \\
&\leq \left[\min_{m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}} d(v, x_{k_l,m})\right] + \left[\max_{m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}} d(x_{k_l,m}, \mathfrak{r}_m)\right] \tag{12.93} \\
&\leq \left[r + \frac{1}{k_l}\right] + \left[\max_{m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}} d(x_{k_l,m}, \mathfrak{r}_m)\right].
\end{aligned}$$

Hence, we obtain for all $v \in X$ that

$$\min_{m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}} d(v, \mathfrak{r}_m) \leq \lim \sup_{l \to \infty} \left(\left[r + \frac{1}{k_l}\right] + \left[\max_{m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}} d(x_{k_l,m}, \mathfrak{r}_m)\right]\right) = r. \tag{12.94}$$

This establishes that $\mathcal{C}^{(X,d)} \leq n$ (cf. Definition 4.3.2). The proof of Lemma 12.2.13 is thus complete.

### 12.2.3.2 Upper bounds for covering radii based on upper bounds for covering numbers

**Lemma 12.2.14.** Let $(X, d)$ be a metric space and let $r \in [0, \infty]$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$ satisfy $\mathcal{C}^{(X, d), r} \leq n$ (cf. Definition 4.3.2). Then $\mathcal{C}_{(X, d), n} \leq r$ (cf. Definition 12.2.1).

*Proof of Lemma 12.2.14.* Observe that the assumption that $\mathcal{C}^{(X, d), r} \leq n$ ensures that there exists $A \subseteq X$ such that $|A| \leq n$ and

$$X \subseteq \bigcup_{a \in A} \{v \in X : d(a, v) \leq r\} \,. \tag{12.95}$$

This establishes that $\mathcal{C}_{(X, d), n} \leq r$ (cf. Definition 12.2.1). The proof of Lemma 12.2.14 is thus complete.

### 12.2.3.3 Upper bounds for covering radii based on upper bounds for packing radii

**Lemma 12.2.15.** Let $(X, d)$ be a metric space and let $n \in \mathbb{N}$. Then $\mathcal{C}_{(X, d), n} \leq 2 \mathcal{P}_{(X, d), n}$ (cf. Definitions 12.2.1 and 12.2.6).

*Proof of Lemma 12.2.15.* Throughout this proof, assume w.l.o.g. that $X \neq \emptyset$, assume without loss of generality that $\mathcal{P}_{(X, d), n} < \infty$, let $r \in [0, \infty]$ satisfy $r > \mathcal{P}_{(X, d), n}$, and let $N \in \mathbb{N}_0 \cup \{\infty\}$ satisfy $N = \mathcal{P}^{(X, d), r}$ (cf. Definitions 12.2.6 and 12.2.7). Observe that Lemma 12.2.9 ensures that

$$N = \mathcal{P}^{(X, d), r} < n. \tag{12.96}$$

Moreover, note that the fact that $N = \mathcal{P}^{(X, d), r}$ and (12.77) demonstrate that for all $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_{N+1}, x_{N+2} \in X$ it holds that

$$\min_{i, j \in \{1, \dots, N+2\}, i \neq j} d(x_i, x_j) \leq 2r. \tag{12.97}$$

In addition, observe that the fact that $N = \mathcal{P}^{(X, d), r}$ and (12.77) imply that there exist $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_{N+1} \in X$ which satisfy that

$$\min \left( \{d(x_i, x_j) : i, j \in \{1, 2, \dots, N + 1\}, \ i \neq j \} \cup \{\infty\} \right) > 2r. \tag{12.98}$$

Combining this with (12.97) establishes that for all $v \in X$ it holds that

$$\min_{i \in \{1, 2, \dots, N\}} d(x_i, v) \leq 2r. \tag{12.99}$$

Hence, we obtain that for all $w \in X$ it holds that

$$w \in \left[ \bigcup_{m = 1}^{n} \{v \in X : d(x_i, v) \leq 2r\} \right]. \tag{12.100}$$
