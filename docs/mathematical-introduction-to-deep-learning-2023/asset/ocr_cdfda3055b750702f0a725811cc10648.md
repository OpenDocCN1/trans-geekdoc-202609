# 4.4.4 Refined convergence rates for the approximation error

**Lemma 4.4.11.** Let $d, \mathbf{d}, \mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{N}$, $L, a \in \mathbb{R}$, $b \in (a, \infty)$, $u \in [-\infty, \infty)$, $v \in (u, \infty)$, $\mathbf{l} = (l_0, l_1, \dots, l_{\mathbf{L}}) \in \mathbb{N}^{\mathbf{L}+1}$, assume $l_0 = d$, $\mathbf{l}_{\mathbf{L}} = 1$, and $\mathbf{d} \ge \sum_{i=1}^{\mathbf{L}} \mathbf{l}_i(\mathbf{l}_{i-1}+1)$, and let $f: [a, b]^d \to ([u, v] \cap \mathbb{R})$ satisfy for all $x, y \in [a, b]^d$ that $|f(x) - f(y)| \le L \|x - y\|_1$ (cf. Definition 3.9.4). Then there exists $\vartheta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ such that $\|\vartheta\|_\infty \le \sup_{x \in [a, b]^d} |f(x)|$ and

$$\sup_{x \in [a, b]^d} \left| \mathcal{A}_{a, v}^{\vartheta, \mathbf{l}}(x) - f(x) \right| \le \frac{dL(b-a)}{2} \tag{4.196}$$

(cf. Definition 4.4.1).

*Proof of Lemma 4.4.11.* Throughout this proof, let $\mathbf{d} = \sum_{i=1}^{\mathbf{L}} \mathbf{l}_i(\mathbf{l}_{i-1}+1)$, let $\mathbf{m} = (\mathbf{m}_1, \mathbf{m}_2$, $\dots, \mathbf{m}_{\mathbf{d}}) \in [a, b]^{\mathbf{d}}$ satisfy for all $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, d\}$ that

$$\mathbf{m}_i = \frac{a+b}{2}, \tag{4.197}$$

and let $\vartheta = (\vartheta_1, \vartheta_2, \dots, \vartheta_{\mathbf{d}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ satisfy for all $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, \mathbf{d}\} \setminus \{\mathbf{d}\}$ that $\vartheta_i = 0$ and $\vartheta_{\mathbf{d}} = f(\mathbf{m})$. Observe that the assumption that $\mathbf{l}_{\mathbf{L}} = 1$ and the fact that $\forall i \in \{1, 2, \dots, \mathbf{d} - 1\} : \vartheta_i = 0$ show that for all $x = (x_1, x_2, \dots, x_{\mathbf{l}_{\mathbf{L}-1}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{l}_{\mathbf{L}-1}}$ it holds that

$$\begin{align*} \mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{l}_1, \mathbf{l}_{\mathbf{L}-1}}^{\vartheta, \sum_{i=1}^{\mathbf{L}-1} \mathbf{l}_i(\mathbf{l}_{i-1}+1)}(x) &= \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{\mathbf{l}_{\mathbf{L}-1}} \vartheta \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{\mathbf{L}-1} \mathbf{l}_i(\mathbf{l}_{i-1}+1) + i \right] x_i \right] + \vartheta_{\sum_{i=1}^{\mathbf{L}-1} \mathbf{l}_i(\mathbf{l}_{i-1}+1) + \mathbf{l}_{\mathbf{L}-1} + 1} \\\ &= \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{\mathbf{l}_{\mathbf{L}-1}} \vartheta_{\left[ \sum_{i=1}^{\mathbf{L}-1} \mathbf{l}_i(\mathbf{l}_{i-1}+1) - (\mathbf{l}_{\mathbf{L}-1} - i + 1) \right] x_i} \right] + \vartheta_{\sum_{i=1}^{\mathbf{L}-1} \mathbf{l}_i(\mathbf{l}_{i-1}+1)} \\ &= \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{\mathbf{l}_{\mathbf{L}-1}} \vartheta_{\mathbf{d} - (\mathbf{l}_{\mathbf{L}-1} - i + 1)} x_i \right] + \vartheta_{\mathbf{d}} = \vartheta_{\mathbf{d}} = f(\mathbf{m}) \tag{4.198} \end{align*}$$

(cf. Definition 1.1.1). Combining this with the fact that $f(\mathbf{m}) \in \overline{[u, v]}$ ensures that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{l}_{\mathbf{L}-1}}$ it holds that

$$\begin{align*} \left( \mathfrak{C}_{u, v, \mathbf{L}} \circ \mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{l}_1, \mathbf{l}_{\mathbf{L}-1}}^{\vartheta, \sum_{i=1}^{\mathbf{L}-1} \mathbf{l}_i(\mathbf{l}_{i-1}+1)} \right)(x) &= \left( \mathfrak{C}_{u, v, 1} \circ \mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{l}_1, \mathbf{l}_{\mathbf{L}-1}}^{\vartheta, \sum_{i=1}^{\mathbf{L}-1} \mathbf{l}_i(\mathbf{l}_{i-1}+1)} \right)(x) \\ &= \mathfrak{C}_{u, v, \mathfrak{f}}(f(\mathbf{m})) = \max\{u, \min\{\mathfrak{f}(\mathbf{m}), v\}\} \\ &= \max\{u, f(\mathbf{m})\} = f(\mathbf{m}) \tag{4.199} \end{align*}$$

(cf. Definitions 1.2.9 and 1.2.10). This implies for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$ that

$$\mathcal{A}_{a, v}^{\vartheta, \mathbf{l}}(x) = f(\mathbf{m}). \tag{4.200}$$

Furthermore, note that (4.197) demonstrates that for all $x \in [a, \mathbf{m}_1]$, $\mathfrak{f} \in [\mathbf{m}_1, b]$ it holds that

$$\begin{align*} |\mathbf{m}_1 - x| &= \mathbf{m}_1 - x = \frac{(a+b)}{2} - x \le \frac{(a+b)}{2} - a = \frac{(b-a)}{2} \\ \text{and} \quad |\mathbf{m}_1 - \mathfrak{f}| &= \mathfrak{f} - \mathbf{m}_1 = \mathfrak{f} - \frac{(a+b)}{2} \le b - \frac{(a+b)}{2} = \frac{(b-a)}{2}. \tag{4.201} \end{align*}$$
