# 2.2.4 Parallelizations of fully-connected feedforward ANNs with different lengths

Definition 2.2.12 (Parallelization of fully-connected feedforward ANNs with different length). Let \( n \in \mathbb{N} \), \( \Psi = (\Psi_1, \dots, \Psi_n) \in \mathbb{N}^n \) satisfy for all \( j \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\} \) that

\[ \mathcal{H}(\Psi_j) = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{I}(\Psi_j) = \mathcal{O}(\Psi_j) \tag{2.104} \]

(cf. Definition 1.3.1). Then we denote by

\[ \mathrm{P}_{n, \Psi}: \left\{ \Phi = (\Phi_1, \dots, \Phi_n) \in \mathbb{N}^n: \; \forall j \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}: \mathcal{O}(\Phi_j) = \mathcal{I}(\Psi_j) \right\} \to \mathbb{N} \tag{2.105} \]

the function which satisfies for all \( \Phi = (\Phi_1, \dots, \Phi_n) \in \mathbb{N}^n \) with \( \forall j \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\} \): \( \mathcal{O}(\Phi_j) = \mathcal{I}(\Psi_j) \) that

\[ \mathrm{P}_{n, \Psi}(\Phi) = \mathrm{P}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{max}_{k \in \{1, \dots, n\}} \mathcal{L}(\Psi_k)}, \Psi_1(\Phi_1), \dots, \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{max}_{k \in \{1, \dots, n\}} \mathcal{L}(\Psi_k)}, \Psi_n(\Phi_n)\big) \tag{2.106} \]

(cf. Definitions 2.2.1 and 2.2.8 and Lemma 2.2.9).

Lemma 2.2.13 (Realizations for parallelizations of fully-connected feedforward ANNs with different length). Let \( a \in C(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R}) \), \( n \in \mathbb{N} \), \( \mathbb{I} = (\mathbb{I}_1, \dots, \mathbb{I}_n) \), \( \Phi = (\Phi_1, \dots, \Phi_n) \in \mathbb{N}^n \) satisfy for all \( j \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\} \), \( x \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{O}(\Phi_j)} \) that \( \mathcal{H}(\mathbb{I}_j) = 1 \), \( \mathcal{I}(\mathbb{I}_j) = \mathcal{O}(\mathbb{I}_j) = \mathcal{O}(\Phi_j) \), and \( (\mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbb{I}_j))(x) = x \) (cf. Definitions 1.3.1 and 1.3.4). Then

+   (i) it holds that
\[ \mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbb{N}}\big(\mathrm{P}_{n, \mathbb{I}}(\Phi)\big) \in C\big(\mathbb{R}^{[\sum_{j=1}^n \mathcal{I}(\Phi_j)]}, \mathbb{R}^{[\sum_{j=1}^n \mathcal{O}(\Phi_j)]}\big) \tag{2.107} \]
and

+   (ii) it holds for all \( x_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{I}(\Phi_1)} \), \( x_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{I}(\Phi_2)} \), \( \dots \), \( x_n \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{I}(\Phi_n)} \) that
\[
\begin{aligned}
& \big(\mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathrm{P}_{n, \mathbb{I}}(\Phi))\big)(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n) \\
& \qquad = \big((\mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbb{N}}(\Phi_1))(x_1), (\mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbb{N}}(\Phi_2))(x_2), \dots, (\mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbb{N}}(\Phi_n))(x_n)\big) \in \mathbb{R}^{[\sum_{j=1}^n \mathcal{O}(\Phi_j)]}
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.108}
\]

(cf. Definition 2.2.12).

Proof of Lemma 2.2.13. Throughout this proof, let \( L \in \mathbb{N} \) satisfy \( L = \mathrm{max}_{j \in \{1, \dots, n\}} \mathcal{L}(\Phi_j) \). Note that item (ii) in Lemma 2.2.9, the assumption that for all \( j \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\} \) it holds that \( \mathcal{H}(\mathbb{I}_j) = 1 \), (2.79), (2.4), and item (ii) in Lemma 2.2.10 demonstrate

+   (I) that for all \( j \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\} \) it holds that \( \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{E}_{L, \mathbb{I}_j}(\Phi_j)) = L \) and \( \mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathcal{E}_{L, \mathbb{I}_j}(\Phi_j)) \in C(\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{I}(\Phi_j)}, \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{O}(\Phi_j)}) \) and

+   (II) that for all \( j \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\} \), \( x \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{I}(\Phi_j)} \) it holds that
\[
\big(\mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathcal{E}_{L, \mathbb{I}_j}(\Phi_j))\big)(x) = \big(\mathcal{R}_a^{\mathbb{N}}(\Phi_j)\big)(x)
\tag{2.109}
\]
