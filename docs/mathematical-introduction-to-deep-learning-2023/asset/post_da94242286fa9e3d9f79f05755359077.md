
Proof of Lemma 9.13.2. Throughout this proof, let $A_1: \mathbb{R}^{l_1} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_1} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_2}$ and $A_2: \mathbb{R}^{l_2} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_1} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_2}$ satisfy for all $x_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_1}, x_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_2}$ that

$$ A_1(x_1) = (x_1, 0) \quad \text{and} \quad A_2(x_2) = (0, x_2) \quad\quad\quad (9.262) $$

and for every $k \in \{1, 2\}$ let $B_k: \mathbb{R}^{l_1} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_2} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_k}$ satisfy for all $x_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_1}, x_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{l_2}$ that

$$ B_k(x_1, x_2) = x_k. \quad\quad\quad (9.263) $$

Note that item **(i)** in Lemma 5.3.1 shows that

$$ f = A_1 \circ F_1 \circ B_1 + A_2 \circ F_2 \circ B_2. \quad\quad\quad (9.264) $$

This, the fact that $A_1$, $A_2$, $F_1$, $F_2$, $B_1$, and $B_2$ are analytic, and Proposition 9.13.1 establishes that $f$ is differentiable. The proof of Lemma 9.13.2 is thus complete. $\square$

**Lemma 9.13.3.** *Let $\mathfrak{d}_1, \mathfrak{d}_2, l_0, l_1, l_2 \in \mathbb{N}$, for every $k \in \{1, 2\}$ let $F_k: \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_k} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_k - 1} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_k}$ be analytic, and let $f: \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_1} \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_2} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_0} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_2}$ satisfy for all $\theta_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_1}, \theta_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_2}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^{l_0}$ that

$$ f(\theta_1, \theta_2, x) = \big(F_2(\theta_2, \cdot) \circ F_1(\theta_1, \cdot)\big)(x) \quad\quad\quad (9.265) $$

(cf. Definition 9.7.1). Then $f$ is analytic.*

Proof of Lemma 9.13.3. Throughout this proof, let $A: \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_1} \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_2} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_0} \to \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_2} \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_1 + l_0}$ and $B: \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_2} \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_1 + l_0} \to \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_2} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_1}$ satisfy for all $\theta_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_1}$, $\theta_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_2}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^{l_0}$ that

$$ A(\theta_1, \theta_2, x) = (\theta_2, (\theta_1, x)) \quad \text{and} \quad B(\theta_2, (\theta_1, x)) = (\theta_2, F_1(\theta_1, x)), \quad\quad\quad (9.266) $$

Observe that item **(i)** in Lemma 5.3.2 proves that

$$ f = F_2 \circ B \circ A. \quad\quad\quad (9.267) $$

Furthermore, note that Lemma 9.13.2 (with $\mathfrak{d}_1 \frown \mathfrak{d}_2$, $\mathfrak{d}_2 \frown \mathfrak{d}_1 + l_1$, $l_1 \frown \mathfrak{d}_2$, $l_2 \frown l_1$, $F_1 \frown (\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_2} \ni \theta_2 \mapsto \theta_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_2})$, $F_2 \frown (\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}_1 + l_1} \ni (\theta_1, x) \mapsto F_1(\theta_1, x) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_1})$ in the notation of Lemma 9.13.2) implies that $B$ is analytic. Combining this, the fact that $A$ is analytic, the fact that $F_2$ is analytic, and (9.267) with Proposition 9.13.1 demonstrates that $f$ is analytic. The proof of Lemma 9.13.3 is thus complete. $\square$

**Corollary 9.13.4** (Analyticity of realization functions of ANNs). *Let $L \in \mathbb{N}$, $l_0, l_1, \ldots, l_L \in \mathbb{N}$ and for every $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ let $\Psi_k: \mathbb{R}^{l_k} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_k}$ be analytic (cf. Definition 9.7.1). Then

$$ \mathbb{R}^{\sum_{k=1}^{L} l_k(l_k - 1) + 1} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_0} \ni (\theta, x) \mapsto \big(\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{d}_0}_{\Psi_1, \Psi_2, \ldots, \Psi_L}\big)(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_L} \quad\quad\quad (9.268) $$

is analytic (cf. Definition 1.1.3).*

Proof of Corollary 9.13.4. Throughout this proof, for every $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ let $\mathbf{d}_k = l_k(l_{k-1} + 1)$ and for every $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ let $F_k\colon \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}_k} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_k}$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}_k}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}}$ that

$$ F_k(\theta, x) = \Psi_k\bigl(\mathcal{A}^{\theta,0}_{l_k,l_{k-1}}(x)\bigr) \tag{9.269} $$

(cf. Definition 1.1.1). Observe that item (i) in Lemma 5.3.3 demonstrates that for all $\theta_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}_1}$, $\theta_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}_2}$, $\ldots$, $\theta_L \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}_L}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^{l_0}$ it holds that

$$ \bigl(\mathcal{N}^{\theta_1,\theta_2,\ldots,\theta_L}_{\psi_1,\psi_2,\ldots,\psi_L}^{0}\bigr)(x) = \bigl(F_L(\theta_L, \cdot) \circ F_{L-1}(\theta_{L-1}, \cdot) \circ \cdots \circ F_1(\theta_1, \cdot)\bigr)(x) \tag{9.270} $$

(cf. Definition 1.1.3). Note that the assumption that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ it holds that $\Psi_k$ is analytic, the fact that for all $m$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{m(n+1)}$ it holds that $\mathbb{R}^{m(n+1)} \times \mathbb{R}^n \ni (\theta, x) \mapsto \mathcal{A}^{\theta,0}_{m,n}(x) \in \mathbb{R}^m$ is analytic, and Proposition 9.13.1 ensure that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ it holds that $F_k$ is analytic. Lemma 5.3.2 and induction hence ensure that

$$ \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}_1} \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}_2} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}_L} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_0} \ni (\theta_1, \theta_2, \ldots, \theta_L, x) $$
$$ \mapsto \bigl(F_L(\theta_L, \cdot) \circ F_{L-1}(\theta_{L-1}, \cdot) \circ \cdots \circ F_1(\theta_1, \cdot)\bigr)(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_L} $$

is analytic. This and (9.270) establish that

$$ \mathbb{R}^{\sum_{k=1}^{L} l_k(l_{k-1}+1)} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_0} \ni (\theta, x) \mapsto \mathcal{N}^{\theta,l_0}_{\psi_1,\psi_2,\ldots,\psi_L}(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_L} \tag{9.272} $$

is analytic. The proof of Corollary 9.13.4 is thus complete. $\Box$

**Corollary 9.13.5 (Analyticity of the empirical risk function).** Let $L, \mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}$, $M$, $l_0$, $l_1$, $\ldots$, $l_L \in \mathbb{N}$, $x_1, x_2$, $\ldots$, $x_M \in \mathbb{R}^{l_0}$, $y_1, y_2$, $\ldots$, $y_M \in \mathbb{R}^{l_L}$ satisfy $\mathbf{d} = \sum_{k=1}^{L} l_k(l_{k-1} + 1)$, let $a\colon \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbf{L}\colon \mathbb{R}^{l_L} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_L} \to \mathbb{R}$ be analytic, let $\mathscr{L}\colon \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}} \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ that

$$ \mathscr{L}(\theta) = \frac{1}{M} \left[\sum_{m=1}^{M} \mathbf{L}\bigl(\bigl(\mathcal{N}^{\theta,l_0}_{\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_1},\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_2},\ldots,\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_{L-1}},\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{l_L}}}\bigr)(x_m), y_m\bigr)\right] \tag{9.273} $$

(cf. Definitions 1.1.3, 1.2.1, and 9.7.1). Then $\mathscr{L}$ is analytic.

Proof of Corollary 9.13.5. Observe that the assumption that $a$ is analytic, Lemma 9.13.2, and induction show that for all $m \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that $\mathfrak{M}_{a,m}$ is analytic. This, Corollary 9.13.4 and Lemma 9.13.2 (applied with $\mathbf{d}_1 \sim \mathbf{d} + l_0$, $l_1 \sim l_0$, $l_2 \sim l_L$, $l_3 \sim l_L$, $\tilde{F}_1 \sim (\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_0} \ni (\theta, x) \mapsto \bigl(\mathcal{N}^{\theta,l_0}_{\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_1},\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_2},\ldots,\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_{L-1}},\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{l_L}}}\bigr)(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_L}$), $F_2 \sim \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{l_L}}$ in the notation of Lemma 9.13.2) ensure that

$$ \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_0} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_L} \ni (\theta, x, y) \mapsto \bigl(\bigl(\mathcal{N}^{\theta,l_0}_{\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_1},\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_2},\ldots,\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_{L-1}},\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{l_L}}}\bigr)(x), y\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_L} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_L} \tag{9.274} $$

is analytic. The assumption that $\mathbf{L}$ is differentiable and the chain rule therefore establish that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^{l_0}$, $y \in \mathbb{R}^{l_L}$ it holds that

$$ \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}} \ni \theta \mapsto \mathbf{L}\bigl(\bigl(\mathcal{N}^{\theta,l_0}_{\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_1},\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_2},\ldots,\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_{L-1}},\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{l_L}}}\bigr)(x_m), y_m\bigr) \in \mathbb{R} \tag{9.275} $$

is analytic. This proves (9.273). The proof of Corollary 9.13.5 is thus complete. $\Box$
