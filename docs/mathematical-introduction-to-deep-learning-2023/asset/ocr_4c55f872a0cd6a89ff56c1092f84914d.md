# One-dimensional ANN approximation results

In learning problems $\mathbf{ANNs}$ are heavily used with the aim to approximate certain target functions. In this chapter we review basic \ref{Relu ANN} approximation results for a class of one-dimensional target functions (see Section \ref{sec:1D-ReLU-approx}). \ref{ANN} approximation results for multi-dimensional target functions are treated in Chapter \ref{chap:5} below.

In the scientific literature the capacity of $\mathbf{ANNs}$ to approximate certain classes of target functions has been thoroughly studied; cf., for instance, \cite{cit1,cit2,cit3,cit4} for early universal $\ref{ANN}$ approximation results, cf., for example, \cite{cit5,cit6,cit7,cit8,cit9,cit10} and the references therein for more recent $\ref{ANN}$ approximation results establishing rates in the approximation of different classes of target functions, and cf., for instance, \cite{cit11,cit12,cit13,cit14} (and the references therein for approximation capacities of $\mathbf{ANNs}$ related to solutions of $\ref{PDEs}$ (cf. also Chapters \ref{chap:16} and \ref{chap:17} in Part \ref{part:VI} of these lecture notes for machine learning methods for $\ref{PDEs}$). This chapter is based on Ackermann et al. \cite{ackermann2018complexity, Section 4.2} (cf., for example, also Hutzenhaler et al. \cite{hutzenhaler2019uniform, Section 3.4}).

# 3.1.1  On the modulus of continuity

**Definition 3.1.1 (Modulus of continuity).** Let $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be a set and let $f \colon A \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be a function. *Then we denote by $w_f \colon [0,\infty[ \rightarrow [0,\infty[$ the function which satisfies for all $h \in [0,\infty[$ that*

```
$$
\begin{align*}
	w_f(h) = \operatorname{sup} \big( \{ | f(x) - f(y) | : (x,y) \in A \text{ with } | x - y | \leq h \} \cup \{ 0 \} \big) \notag \
	\phantom{w_f(h) =} & \operatorname{sup} \big( \{ r \in \mathbb{R} : (\exists x \in A, \; y \in A \cap [x-h,x+h]: \; r = | f(x) - f(y) | \} \cup \{ 0 \} \big) \tag{3.1}
\end{align*}
$$
```

*and we call $w_f$ the modulus of continuity of $f$.*
