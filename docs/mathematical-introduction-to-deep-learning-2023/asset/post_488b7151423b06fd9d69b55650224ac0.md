## 7.4.1 Bias-adjusted SGD optimization with classical momentum

**Definition 7.4.2** (Bias-adjusted momentum SGD optimization method). Let $d \in \mathbb{N}$, $(\gamma_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}} \subseteq [0,\infty)$, $(J_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}} \subseteq \mathbb{N}$, $(\alpha_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}} \subseteq [0,1]$ satisfy $\alpha_1 < 1$, let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ be a probability space, let $(S, \mathcal{S})$ be a measurable space, let $\xi: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{d}$ be a random variable, for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $j \in \{1, 2, \dots, J_n\}$ let $X_{n,j}: \Omega \rightarrow S$ be a random variable, and let $\mathcal{L} = (\mathcal{L}(\theta, x))_{(\theta, x) \in \mathbb{R}^{d} \times S}: \mathbb{R}^{d} \times S \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ and $\boldsymbol{g} = (\boldsymbol{g}_1, \dots, \boldsymbol{g}_d): \mathbb{R}^{d} \times S \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{d}$ satisfy for all $U \in \{V \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{d}: V \text{ is open}\}$, $x \in S$, $\theta \in U$ with $(U \supseteq \varnothing \mapsto \mathcal{L}(\theta, x) \in \mathbb{R} \text{ is } C^1(U, \mathbb{R}))$ that

$$ \boldsymbol{g}(\theta, x) = (\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L})(\theta, x). \quad (7.112) $$

Then we say that $\Theta$ is the bias-adjusted momentum **SGD** process on $((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}), (S, \mathcal{S}))$ for the loss function $\mathcal{L}$ with generalized gradient $\boldsymbol{g}$, learning rates $(\gamma_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, batch sizes $(J_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, momentum decay factors $(\alpha_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, initial value $\xi$, and data $(X_{n,j})_{(n,j) \in \{(k,i) \in \mathbb{N}_0^2: i \le J_k\}}$ (we say that $\Theta$ is the bias-adjusted momentum **SGD** process for the loss function $\mathcal{L}$ with learning rates $(\gamma_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, batch sizes $(J_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, momentum decay factors $(\alpha_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, initial value $\xi$, and data $(X_{n,j})_{(n,j) \in \{(k,i) \in \mathbb{N}_0^2: i \le J_k\}}$ if and only if $\Phi: \mathbb{N}_0 \rightarrow \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{d}$ is the function from $\mathbb{N}_0 \times \Omega$ to $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ which satisfies that there exists $\boldsymbol{m}: \mathbb{N}_0 \times \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{d}$ such that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that

$$ \Theta_0 = \xi, \quad \boldsymbol{m}_0 = 0, \quad (7.113) $$
$$ \boldsymbol{m}_n = \alpha_n \boldsymbol{m}_{n-1} + (1-\alpha_n) \left[ \frac{1}{J_n} \sum_{j=1}^{J_n} \boldsymbol{g}(\Theta_{n-1}, X_{n,j}) \right], \quad (7.114) $$
$$ \text{and} \quad \Theta_n = \Theta_{n-1} - \frac{\gamma_n \boldsymbol{m}_n}{1 - \prod_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i}. \quad (7.115) $$

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import numpy as np

net = nn.Sequential(
    nn.Linear(1, 200), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(200, 1)
)

M = 1000

X = torch.rand((M, 1)) * 4 * np.pi - 2 * np.pi
Y = torch.sin(X)

J = 64

N = 150000

loss = nn.MSELoss()
lr = 0.01
alpha = 0.99
adj = 1

momentum = [p.clone().detach().zero_() for p in net.parameters()]

for n in range(N):
    indices = torch.randint(0, M, (J,))

    x = X[indices]
    y = Y[indices]

    net.zero_grad()

    loss_val = loss(net(x), y)
    loss_val.backward()

    adj *= alpha

    with torch.no_grad():
        for m, p in zip(momentum, net.parameters()):
            m.mul_(alpha)
            m.add_((1-alpha) * p.grad)
            p.sub_(lr * m / (1 - adj))

    if n % 1000 == 0:
        with torch.no_grad():
            x = torch.rand((1000, 1)) * 4 * np.pi - 2 * np.pi
            y = torch.sin(x)
            loss_val = loss(net(x), y)
            print(f"Iteration: {n+1}, Loss: {loss_val}")
```

In this section we introduce the stochastic version of the Nesterov accelerated GD optimization method from Section 6.4 (cf. [302, 387]).

**Definition 7.5.1** (Nesterov accelerated SGD optimization method). Let $d \in \mathbb{N}$, $(\gamma_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq [0, \infty)$, $(J_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq \mathbb{N}$, $(\alpha_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq [0, 1]$, let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ be a probability space, let $(S, \mathcal{S})$ be a measurable space, let $\xi : \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{d}$ be a random variable, for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $j \in \{1, 2, \ldots, J_n\}$ let $X_{n, j} : \Omega \rightarrow S$ be a random variable, and let $\ell = (\ell(\theta, x))_{(\theta, x) \in \mathbb{R}^{d} \times S} : \mathbb{R}^{d} \times S \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ and $\boldsymbol{g} = (\boldsymbol{g}_{1}, \ldots, \boldsymbol{g}_{d}) : \mathbb{R}^{d} \times S \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{d}$ satisfy for all $U \in \{V \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{d} : V \text{ is open}\}$, $x \in S$, $\theta \in U$ with $(U \ni \psi \mapsto \ell(\psi, x) \in \mathbb{R}) \in C^{1}(U, \mathbb{R})$ that

$$ \boldsymbol{g}(\theta, x)=(\nabla_{\theta} \ell)(\theta, x). \quad (7.116) $$

Then we say that $\Theta$ is the Nesterov accelerated SGD process on $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}), (S, \mathcal{S})$ for the loss function $\ell$ with generalized gradient $\boldsymbol{g}$, learning rates $(\gamma_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, batch sizes $(J_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, momentum decay factors $(\alpha_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, initial value $\xi$, and data $(X_{n, j})_{(n, j) \in \{(k, i) \in \mathbb{N}_0^2 : i \leq J_k\}}$ if and only if $\Theta : \mathbb{N}_{0} \times \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{d}$ is the function from $\mathbb{N}_{0} \times \Omega$ to $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ that satisfies that there exists $\mathbf{m} : \mathbb{N}_{0} \times \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{d}$ such that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that

$$ \Theta_{0}=\xi,\quad\mathbf{m}_{0}=0, \quad (7.117) $$
$$ \mathbf{m}_{n}=\alpha_{n} \mathbf{m}_{n-1}+\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left[\frac{1}{J_{n}} \sum_{j=1}^{J_{n}} \boldsymbol{g}\left(\Theta_{n-1}-\gamma_{n} \alpha_{n} \mathbf{m}_{n-1}, X_{n, j}\right)\right], \quad (7.118) $$
$$ \text{and} \quad \Theta_{n}=\Theta_{n-1}-\gamma_{n} \mathbf{m}_{n} . \quad (7.119) $$
