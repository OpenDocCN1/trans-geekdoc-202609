
and the assumption that the function $\Phi : \mathbb{X} \times \mathbb{Y} \rightarrow [0, \infty]$ is $(\mathcal{X} \otimes \mathcal{Y})/\mathcal{B}([0, \infty])$-measurable show that the function

$$
\forall Y \ni y \mapsto \phi(y) = \mathbb{E}[\Phi(X, y)] = \int_\Omega \Phi(X(\omega), y) \mathbb{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega) \in [0, \infty] \tag{7.34}
$$

is $\mathcal{Y}/\mathcal{B}([0, \infty])$-measurable. This proves item **(i)**. Observe that the integral transformation theorem, the fact that $(X, Y)(\mathbb{P}) = (X(\mathbb{P})) \otimes (Y(\mathbb{P}))$, and Fubini's theorem establish that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}\big[\Phi(X, Y)\big] &= \int_\Omega \Phi(X(\omega), Y(\omega)) \mathbb{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\\
&= \int_{\mathbb{X} \times \mathbb{Y}} \Phi(x, y) \big((X, Y)(\mathbb{P})\big)(\mathrm{d}x, \mathrm{d}y)\\
&= \int_\mathbb{Y} \Big[\int_\mathbb{X} \Phi(x, y) \big(X(\mathbb{P})\big)(\mathrm{d}x) \Big] \big(Y(\mathbb{P})\big)(\mathrm{d}y)\\
&= \int_\mathbb{Y} \mathbb{E}\big[\Phi(X, y)\big] \big(Y(\mathbb{P})\big)(\mathrm{d}y)\\
&= \int_\mathbb{Y} \phi(y) \big(Y(\mathbb{P})\big)(\mathrm{d}y) = \mathbb{E}\big[\phi(Y)\big].
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.35}
$$

This proves item **(ii)**. The proof of Lemma 7.2.7 is thus complete. $\square$

### Lemma 7.2.8
Let $\mathfrak{d} \in \mathbb{N}$, let $(S, \mathcal{S})$ be a measurable space, let $\ell = (\ell(\theta, x))_{(\theta, x) \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d} \times S} : \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d} \times S \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be $(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}) \otimes \mathcal{S})/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$-measurable, and assume for every $x \in S$ that the function $\mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d} \ni \theta \mapsto \ell(\theta, x) \in \mathbb{R}$ is differentiable. Then the function

$$
\mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d} \times S \ni (\theta, x) \mapsto \big(\nabla_\theta \ell\big)(\theta, x) \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d} \tag{7.36}
$$

is $(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}) \otimes \mathcal{S})/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d})$-measurable.

**Proof of Lemma 7.2.8.** Throughout this proof, let $\boldsymbol{g} = (\boldsymbol{g}_1, \ldots, \boldsymbol{g}_\mathfrak{d}) : \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d} \times S \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$, $x \in S$ that

$$
\boldsymbol{g}(\theta, x) = \big(\nabla_\theta \ell\big)(\theta, x). \tag{7.37}
$$

The assumption that the function $\ell : \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d} \times S \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is $(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}) \otimes \mathcal{S})/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$-measurable implies that for all $i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathfrak{d}\}$, $h \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ it holds that the function

$$
\mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d} \times S \ni (\theta, x) = \big((\theta_1, \ldots, \theta_\mathfrak{d}), x\big) \mapsto \bigg(\frac{\ell((\theta_1, \ldots, \theta_{i-1}, \theta_i + h, \theta_{i+1}, \ldots, \theta_\mathfrak{d}), x) - \ell(\theta, x)}{h} \bigg) \in \mathbb{R} \tag{7.38}
$$

is $(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}) \otimes \mathcal{S})/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$-measurable. The fact that for all $i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathfrak{d}\}$, $\theta = (\theta_1, \ldots, \theta_\mathfrak{d}) \in \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}$, $x \in S$ it holds that

$$
\boldsymbol{g}_i(\theta, x) = \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} \bigg( \frac{\ell((\theta_1, \ldots, \theta_{i-1}, \theta_i + 2^{-n}, \theta_{i+1}, \ldots, \theta_\mathfrak{d}), x) - \ell(\theta, x)}{2^{-n}} \bigg) \tag{7.39}
$$

hence demonstrates that for all $i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathfrak{d}\}$ it holds that the function $\boldsymbol{g}_i : \mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d} \times S \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is $(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}) \otimes \mathcal{S})/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$-measurable. This ensures that $\boldsymbol{g}$ is $(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d}) \otimes \mathcal{S})/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^\mathfrak{d})$-measurable. The proof of Lemma 7.2.8 is thus complete. $\square$

### Lemma 7.2.9
Let $d \in \mathbb{N}$, $\{\gamma_n\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq (0, \infty)$, $\{J_n\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq \mathbb{N}$, let $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle : \mathbb{R}^d \times \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}$ be a scalar product, let $\| \cdot \| : \mathbb{R}^d \to [0, \infty)$ satisfy for all $v \in \mathbb{R}^d$ that

$$
\|v\| = \sqrt{\langle v, v \rangle}, \tag{7.40}
$$

let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ be a probability space, let $\xi : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}^d$ be a random variable, let $(S, \mathcal{S})$ be a measurable space, let $X_{n,j} : \Omega \to S$, $j \in \{1, 2, \dots, J_n\}$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, be i.i.d. random variables, assume that $\xi$ and $\{X_{n,j}\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}, j \in \{1, \dots, J_n\}}$ are independent, let $\ell = (\ell(\theta, x))_{(\theta, x) \in \mathbb{R}^d \times S} : \mathbb{R}^d \times S \to \mathbb{R}$ be $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^d) \otimes \mathcal{S} / \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$-measurable, assume for all $x \in S$ that $(\mathbb{R}^d \ni \theta \mapsto \ell(\theta, x)) \in C^1(\mathbb{R}^d, \mathbb{R})$, assume for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^d$ that $\mathbb{E}[\| (\nabla_\theta \ell)(\theta, X_{1,1}) \|] < \infty$ (cf. Lemma 7.2.8), let $\mathcal{V} : \mathbb{R}^d \to [0, \infty]$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^d$ that

$$
\mathcal{V}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\big[\| (\nabla_\theta \ell)(\theta, X_{1,1}) - \mathbb{E}[ (\nabla_\theta \ell)(\theta, X_{1,1}) ] \|^2\big], \tag{7.41}
$$

and let $\Theta : \mathbb{N}_0 \times \Omega \to \mathbb{R}^d$ be the stochastic process which satisfies for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ that

$$
\Theta_0 = \xi \quad \text{and} \quad \Theta_n = \Theta_{n-1} - \gamma_n \left[ \frac{1}{J_n} \sum_{j=1}^{J_n} (\nabla_\theta \ell)(\Theta_{n-1}, X_{n,j}) \right]. \tag{7.42}
$$

Then it holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $v \in \mathbb{R}^d$ that

$$
(\mathbb{E}[\| \Theta_n - v \|^2])^{1/2} \geq \left[ \frac{\gamma_n}{(J_n)^{1/2}} \right] (\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{V}(\Theta_{n-1})])^{1/2}. \tag{7.43}
$$

**Proof of Lemma 7.2.9.** Throughout this proof, for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$ let $\phi_n : \mathbb{R}^d \to [0, \infty]$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^d$ that

$$
\phi_n(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\left[ \left\| \theta - \frac{\gamma_n}{J_n} \left[ \sum_{j=1}^{J_n} (\nabla_\theta \ell)(\theta, X_{n,j}) \right] - v \right\|^2 \right]. \tag{7.44}
$$

Note that Lemma 7.2.3 shows that for all $v \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and all random variables $Z : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}^d$ with $\mathbb{E}[\| Z \|] < \infty$ it holds that

$$
\mathbb{E}[\| Z - v \|^2] = \mathbb{E}[\| Z - \mathbb{E}[Z] \|^2] + \| \mathbb{E}[Z] - v \|^2 \geq \mathbb{E}[\| Z - \mathbb{E}[Z] \|^2]. \tag{7.45}
$$

Therefore, we obtain for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^d$ that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\phi_n(\theta) &= \mathbb{E}\left[ \left\| \frac{\gamma_n}{J_n} \left[ \sum_{j=1}^{J_n} (\nabla_\theta \ell)(\theta, X_{n,j}) \right] - (\theta - v) \right\|^2 \right] \\
&= \mathbb{E}\left[ \left\| \frac{\gamma_n}{J_n} \left[ \sum_{j=1}^{J_n} (\nabla_\theta \ell)(\theta, X_{n,j}) \right] - \mathbb{E}\left[ \frac{\gamma_n}{J_n} \left[ \sum_{j=1}^{J_n} (\nabla_\theta \ell)(\theta, X_{n,j}) \right] \right\|^2 \right] \\
&= \frac{(\gamma_n)^2}{(J_n)^2} \mathbb{E}\left[ \left\| \sum_{j=1}^{J_n} \left( (\nabla_\theta \ell)(\theta, X_{n,j}) - \mathbb{E}[ (\nabla_\theta \ell)(\theta, X_{n,j}) ] \right) \right\|^2 \right].
\end{aligned} \tag{7.46}
$$

Lemma 7.2.6, the fact that $X_{n,j}: \Omega \to S$, $j \in \{1, 2, \ldots, J_n\}$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, are i.i.d. random variables, and the fact that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $j \in \{1, 2, \ldots, J_n\}$, $\vartheta \in \mathbb{R}^d$ it holds that

$$
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\| (\nabla_\vartheta \ell)(\theta, X_{n,j}) \|\bigr] = \mathbb{E}\bigl[\| (\nabla_\vartheta \ell)(\theta, X_{1,1}) \|\bigr] < \infty \qquad\qquad (7.47)
$$

hence establish that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $\vartheta \in \mathbb{R}^d$ it holds that

$$
\begin{gathered}
\phi_n(\vartheta) \geq \left(\frac{\gamma_n}{J_n}\right)^2 \sum_{j=1}^{J_n} \mathbb{E}\left[\left\|(\nabla_\vartheta\ell)(\theta, X_{n,j}) - \mathbb{E}\left[(\nabla_\vartheta\ell)(\theta, X_{n,j})\right] \right\|^2\right] \\
= \left(\frac{\gamma_n}{J_n}\right)^2 \sum_{j=1}^{J_n} \mathbb{E}\left[\left\|(\nabla_\vartheta\ell)(\theta, X_{1,1}) - \mathbb{E}\left[(\nabla_\vartheta\ell)(\theta, X_{1,1})\right] \right\|^2\right] \\
= \left(\frac{\gamma_n}{J_n}\right)^2 \sum_{j=1}^{J_n} \mathcal{V}(\theta) = \left(\frac{\gamma_n}{J_n}\right)^2 \left[J_n \mathcal{V}(\theta)\right] = \left(\frac{\gamma_n}{J_n}\right)^2 \mathcal{V}(\theta).
\end{gathered} \qquad (7.48)
$$

Furthermore, observe that **(7.42)**, **(7.44)**, the fact that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that $\Theta_{n-1}$ and $(X_{n,j})_{j \in \{1,2,\ldots,J_n\}}$ are independent random variables, and Lemma **7.2.7** prove that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $\vartheta \in \mathbb{R}^d$ it holds that

$$
\begin{gathered}
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\| \Theta_n - \vartheta \|^2\bigr] = \mathbb{E}\left[\left\| \Theta_{n-1} - \frac{\gamma_n}{J_n}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{J_n} (\nabla_\vartheta\ell)(\Theta_{n-1}, X_{n,j})\right] - \vartheta \right\|^2\right] \\
= \mathbb{E}\bigl[\phi_n(\Theta_{n-1})\bigr].
\end{gathered} \qquad (7.49)
$$

Combining this with **(7.48)** implies that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $\vartheta \in \mathbb{R}^d$ it holds that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\| \Theta_n - \vartheta \|^2\bigr] &\geq \mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{\gamma_n}{J_n}\right)^2 \mathcal{V}(\Theta_{n-1})\right] = \left(\frac{\gamma_n}{J_n}\right)^2 \mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathcal{V}(\Theta_{n-1})\bigr].
\end{aligned} \qquad (7.50)
$$

This establishes **(7.43)**. The proof of Lemma **7.2.9** is thus complete.
