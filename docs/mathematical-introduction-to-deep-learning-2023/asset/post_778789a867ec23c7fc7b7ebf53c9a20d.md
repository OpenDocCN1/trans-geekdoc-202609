
## 5.6.5 On a linear growth condition

### Lemma 5.6.9 (On a linear growth condition)

Let $\mathfrak{d} \in \mathbb{N}$, $L \in \mathbb{R}$, $r \in (0, \infty)$, $\vartheta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$, $\mathbb{B} = \{w \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} : \|w - \vartheta\|_2 \leq r\}$, $\mathscr{L} \in C^1(\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}, \mathbb{R})$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{B}$ that

$$
\| (\nabla \mathscr{L})(\theta) \|_2 \leq L \| \theta - \vartheta \|_2 \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad (5.135)
$$

(cf. Definition 3.3.4). Then it holds for all $\theta \in \mathbb{B}$ that

$$
\mathscr{L}(\theta) - \mathscr{L}(\vartheta) \leq \frac{L}{2} \| \theta - \vartheta \|_2^2. \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad (5.136)
$$

**Proof of Lemma 5.6.9.** Observe that (5.135), the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and the fundamental theorem of calculus ensure that for all $\theta \in \mathbb{B}$ it holds that

$$
\begin{array}{rcl} 
\mathscr{L}(\theta) - \mathscr{L}(\vartheta) & = & [\mathscr{L}(\vartheta + t(\theta - \vartheta))]_{t=0}^{t=1} \\ 
& = & \int_0^1 \mathscr{L}'(\vartheta + t(\theta - \vartheta))(\theta - \vartheta) \, \mathrm{d}t \\ 
& = & \int_0^1 \langle (\nabla \mathscr{L})(\vartheta + t(\theta - \vartheta)), \theta - \vartheta \rangle \, \mathrm{d}t \\ 
& \leq & \int_0^1 \| (\nabla \mathscr{L})(\vartheta + t(\theta - \vartheta)) \|_2 \| \theta - \vartheta \|_2 \, \mathrm{d}t \qquad \qquad (5.137) \\ 
& \leq & \int_0^1 L \| \vartheta + t(\theta - \vartheta) - \vartheta \|_2 \| \theta - \vartheta \|_2 \, \mathrm{d}t \\ 
& = & L \| \theta - \vartheta \|_2^2 \left[ \int_0^1 t \, \mathrm{d}t \right] = \frac{L}{2} \| \theta - \vartheta \|_2^2 
\end{array}
$$

(cf. Definition 1.4.7). The proof of Lemma 5.6.9 is thus complete. $\square$

## 5.7 Optimization through flows of ODEs

### 5.7.1 Approximation of local minimum points through GFs

**Proposition 5.7.1 (Approximation of local minimum points through GFs).** Let $\mathfrak{d} \in \mathbb{N}$, $c, T \in (0, \infty)$, $r \in (0, \infty]$, $\vartheta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$, $\mathbb{B} = \{w \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} : \|w - \vartheta\|_2 \leq r\}$, $\xi \in \mathbb{B}$, $\mathscr{L} \in C^1(\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}, \mathbb{R})$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{B}$ that

$$
\langle \theta - \vartheta, (\nabla \mathscr{L})(\theta) \rangle \geq c \| \theta - \vartheta \|_2^2, \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad (5.138)
$$

and let $\Theta \in C([0, T], \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}})$ satisfy for all $t \in [0, T]$ that $\Theta_t = \xi - \int_0^t (\nabla \mathscr{L})(\Theta_s) \, \mathrm{d}s$ (cf. Definitions 1.4.7 and 3.3.4). Then

- (i) it holds that $\{\theta \in \mathbb{B} : \mathscr{L}(\theta) = \inf_{w \in \mathbb{B}} \mathscr{L}(w)\} = \{\vartheta\}$,
- (ii) it holds for all $t \in [0, T]$ that $\|\Theta_t - \vartheta\|_{2} \leq e^{-ct}\|\xi - \vartheta\|_2$, and
- (iii) it holds for all $t \in [0, T]$ that

$$
0 \leq \frac{c}{2}\|\Theta_t - \vartheta\|_2^2 \leq \mathscr{L}(\Theta_t) - \mathscr{L}(\vartheta). \quad (5.139)
$$

**Proof of Proposition 5.7.1.** Throughout this proof, let $V: \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} \to [0, \infty)$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ that $V(\theta)=\|\theta - \vartheta\|_{2}^{2}$, let $\epsilon: [0, T] \to [0, \infty)$ satisfy for all $t \in [0, T]$ that $\epsilon(t)=\|\Theta_t - \vartheta\|_{2}=\sqrt{V(\Theta_t)}$, and let $\tau \in [0, T]$ be the real number given by

$$
\tau=\inf(\{t \in [0, T]: \Theta_t \notin \mathbb{B}\} \cup \{T\})=\inf(\{t \in [0, T]: \epsilon(t)>r^{2}\} \cup \{T\}). \quad (5.140)
$$

Note that (5.138) and item (ii) in Lemma 5.6.8 establish item (i). Next observe that Lemma 5.6.4 implies that for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ it holds that $V \in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}, [0, \infty))$ and

$$
(\nabla V)(\theta)=2(\theta - \vartheta). \quad (5.141)
$$

Moreover, observe that the fundamental theorem of calculus (see, for example, Coleman [85, Theorem 3.9]) and the fact that $\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} \ni v \mapsto (\nabla \mathscr{L})(v) \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ and $\Theta: [0, T] \to \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ are continuous functions ensure that for all $t \in [0, T]$ it holds that $\Theta \in C^{1}([0, T], \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}})$ and

$$
\frac{d}{d t}(\Theta_t)=- (\nabla \mathscr{L})(\Theta_t). \quad (5.142)
$$

Combining (5.138) and (5.141) hence demonstrates that for all $t \in [0, \tau]$ it holds that $\epsilon \in C^{1}([0, T], [0, \infty))$ and

$$
\begin{aligned}
\epsilon'(t)= & \frac{d}{d t}(V(\Theta_t))=V^{\prime}(\Theta_t)(\frac{d}{d t}(\Theta_t)) \\
= & \langle(\nabla V)(\Theta_t), \frac{d}{d t}(\Theta_t)\rangle \\
= & \langle 2(\Theta_t - \vartheta), - (\nabla \mathscr{L})(\Theta_t)\rangle \\
= & -2\langle(\Theta_t - \vartheta), (\nabla \mathscr{L})(\Theta_t)\rangle \\
\leq & -2c\|\Theta_t - \vartheta\|_2^2=-2c\epsilon(t).
\end{aligned} \quad (5.143)
$$

The Gronwall inequality, for instance, in Lemma 5.6.1 therefore implies that for all $t \in [0, \tau]$ it holds that

$$
\epsilon(t) \leq \epsilon(0)e^{-2ct}. \quad (5.144)
$$

Hence, we obtain for all $t \in [0, \tau]$ that

$$
\|\Theta_t - \vartheta\|_2=\sqrt{\epsilon(t)} \leq \sqrt{\epsilon(0)e^{-2ct}}=\|\Theta_0 - \vartheta\|_2e^{-ct}=\|\xi - \vartheta\|_2e^{-ct}. \quad (5.145)
$$

In the next step we prove that

$$
\tau>0. \quad (5.146)
$$

In our proof of (5.146) we distinguish between the case $\epsilon(0)=0$ and the case $\epsilon(0)>0$. We first prove (5.146) in the case

$$
\epsilon(0)=0. \quad (5.147)
$$

Observe that (5.147), the assumption that $r \in(0, \infty]$, and the fact that $\epsilon:[0, T] \rightarrow[0, \infty)$ is a continuous function show that

$$
\tau=\inf(\{t \in[0, T]: \epsilon(t)>r^{2}\} \cup\{T\})>0. \quad (5.148)
$$

This establishes (5.146) in the case $\epsilon(0)=0$. In the next step we prove (5.146) in the case

$$
\epsilon(0)>0. \quad (5.149)
$$

Note that (5.143) and the assumption that $c \in(0, \infty)$ assure that for all $t \in[0, \tau]$ with $\epsilon(t)>0$ it holds that

$$
\epsilon^{\prime}(t) \leq-2 c \epsilon(t)<0. \quad (5.150)
$$

Combining this with (5.149) shows that

$$
\epsilon^{\prime}(0)<0. \quad (5.151)
$$

The fact that $\epsilon^{\prime}:[0, T] \rightarrow\mathbb{R}$ is a continuous function and the assumption that $T \in(0, \infty)$ therefore demonstrate that

$$
\inf(\{t \in[0, T]: \epsilon^{\prime}(t)>0\} \cup\{T\})>0. \quad (5.152)
$$

Next note that the fundamental theorem of calculus and the assumption that $\xi \in \mathbb{B}$ imply that for all $s \in[0, T]$ with $s<\inf(\{t \in[0, T]: \epsilon^{\prime}(t)>0\} \cup\{T\})$ it holds that

$$
\epsilon(s)=\epsilon(0)+\int_{0}^{s} \epsilon^{\prime}(u) d u \leq \epsilon(0)=\|\xi-\vartheta\|_{2}^{2} \leq r^{2}. \quad (5.153)
$$

Combining this with (5.152) proves that

$$
\tau=\inf(\{s \in[0, T]: \epsilon(s)>r^{2}\} \cup\{T\})>0. \quad (5.154)
$$

This establishes (5.146) in the case $\epsilon(0)>0$. Observe that (5.145), (5.146), and the assumption that $c \in(0, \infty)$ demonstrate that

$$
\left\|\Theta_{\tau}-\vartheta\right\|_{2} \leq\|\xi-\vartheta\|_{2} e^{-c \tau}<r. \quad (5.155)
$$

The fact that $\epsilon:[0, T] \rightarrow[0, \infty)$ is a continuous function, (5.140), and (5.146) hence assure that $\tau=T$. Combining this with (5.145) proves that for all $t \in[0, T]$ it holds that

$$
\left\|\Theta_{t}-\vartheta\right\|_{2} \leq\|\xi-\vartheta\|_{2} e^{-c t}. \quad (5.156)
$$

This establishes item (ii). It thus remains to prove item (iii). For this observe that (5.138) and item (i) in Lemma 5.6.8 demonstrate that for all $\theta \in \mathbb{B}$ it holds that

$$
0 \leq \frac{c}{2}\|\theta - \vartheta\|_2^2 \leq \mathscr{L}(\theta) - \mathscr{L}(\vartheta). \quad (5.157)
$$

Combining this and item (ii) implies that for all $t \in [0, T]$ it holds that

$$
0 \leq \frac{c}{2}\|\Theta_t - \vartheta\|_2^2 \leq \mathscr{L}(\Theta_t) - \mathscr{L}(\vartheta). \quad (5.158)
$$

This establishes item (iii). The proof of Proposition 5.7.1 is thus complete.

### 5.7.2 Existence and uniqueness of solutions of ODEs

**Lemma 5.7.2 (Local existence of maximal solution of ODEs).** Let $\varrho \in \mathbb{N}$, $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^\varrho$, $T \in (0, \infty)$, let $\|\|\cdot\|\|: \mathbb{R}^\varrho \to [0, \infty)$ be a norm, and let $\mathscr{G}: \mathbb{R}^\varrho \to \mathbb{R}^\varrho$ be locally Lipschitz continuous. Then there exist a unique real number $\tau \in (0, T]$ and a unique continuous function $\Theta: [0, \tau) \to \mathbb{R}^\varrho$ such that for all $t \in [0, \tau)$ it holds that

$$
\liminf_{s \nearrow \tau}\Big[\|\|\Theta_s\|\| + \frac{1}{(T-s)}\Big] = \infty \quad\text{and}\quad \Theta_t = \xi + \int_0^t \mathscr{G}(\Theta_s)\,\mathrm{d}s. \quad (5.159)
$$

**Proof of Lemma 5.7.2.** Note that, for example, Teschl [394, Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.16] implies (5.159) (cf., for instance, [5, Theorem 7.6] and [222, Theorem 1.1]). The proof of Lemma 5.7.2 is thus complete.

**Lemma 5.7.3 (Local existence of maximal solution of ODEs on an infinite time interval).** Let $\varrho \in \mathbb{N}$, $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^\varrho$, let $\|\|\cdot\|\|: \mathbb{R}^\varrho \to [0, \infty)$ be a norm, and let $\mathscr{G}: \mathbb{R}^\varrho \to \mathbb{R}^\varrho$ be locally Lipschitz continuous. Then there exist a unique extended real number $\tau \in (0, \infty]$ and a unique continuous function $\Theta: [0, \tau) \to \mathbb{R}^\varrho$ such that for all $t \in [0, \tau)$ it holds that

$$
\liminf_{s \nearrow \tau}\big[\|\|\Theta_s\|\| + s\big] = \infty \quad\text{and}\quad \Theta_t = \xi + \int_0^t \mathscr{G}(\Theta_s)\,\mathrm{d}s. \quad (5.160)
$$

**Proof of Lemma 5.7.3.** First, observe that Lemma 5.7.2 implies that there exist unique real numbers $\tau_n \in (0, n]$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, and unique continuous functions $\Theta^{(n)}: [0, \tau_n) \to \mathbb{R}^\varrho$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, such that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $t \in [0, \tau_n)$ it holds that

$$
\liminf_{s \nearrow \tau_n}\Big[\|\|\Theta^{(n)}_s\|\| + \frac{1}{(n-s)}\Big] = \infty \quad\text{and}\quad \Theta^{(n)}_t = \xi + \int_0^t \mathscr{G}(\Theta^{(n)}_s)\,\mathrm{d}s. \quad (5.161)
$$

This shows that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $t \in [0, \min\{\tau_{n+1}, n\})$ it holds that

$$
\liminf_{s \nearrow \tau_{n+1}}\Big[\|\|\Theta^{(n+1)}_s\|\| + \frac{1}{(n+1-s)}\Big] = \infty \quad\text{and}\quad \Theta^{(n+1)}_t = \xi + \int_0^t \mathscr{G}(\Theta^{(n+1)}_s)\,\mathrm{d}s. \quad (5.162)
$$
