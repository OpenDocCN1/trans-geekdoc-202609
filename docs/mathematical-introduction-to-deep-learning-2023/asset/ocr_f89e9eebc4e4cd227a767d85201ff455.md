let $\mathscr{L}: \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta} \times [0, T] \times \mathbb{R}^{d} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta}$, $t \in [0, T]$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ that

> $\begin{array}{c} \mathscr{L}(\theta, t, x)=|\mathscr{N}_{\mathfrak{m}_{n, \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}}}^{\theta}, d+1} \overline{\mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{1}}}, \overline{\mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{2}}}, \ldots, \overline{\mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{h}}}(0, x)-g(x)|^{2} \\ +\left(\frac{\partial \mathscr{N}_{\mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{1}}}, \overline{\mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{2}}}, \ldots, \overline{\mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{h}}, \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}}}^{\theta, d+1}}{\partial t}(t, x)-\left\langle\Delta_{x} \mathscr{N}_{\mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{1}}}, \overline{\mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{2}}}, \ldots, \overline{\mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{h}}, \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}}}^{\theta, d+1}(t, x) \right. \\ \left.-f\left(\mathscr{N}_{\overline{\mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{1}}}, \mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{2}}}, \ldots, \overline{\mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{h}}, \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}}}^{\theta, d+1}(t, x))\right)\right)^{2}, \end{array} \quad(16.16)$

and let $\Theta=(\Theta_{n})_{n \in \mathbb{N}_{0}}: \mathbb{N}_{0} \times \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\vartheta}$ satisfy for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ that

> $\Theta_{0}=\zeta \quad \text { and } \quad \Theta_{n}=\Theta_{n-1}-\gamma_{n}\left[\frac{1}{J} \sum_{j=1}^{J}\left(\nabla_{\theta} \mathscr{L}\right)\left(\Theta_{n-1}, \Xi_{n, j}, \mathbf{X}_{n, j}\right)\right] . \quad(16.17)$

Finally, the idea of PINNs and DGMs is then to choose for large enough $n \in \mathbb{N}$ the realization $\mathscr{N}_{\mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{1}}}, \mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{2}}, \ldots, \overline{\mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{h}}, \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}}}^{\Theta_{n}, d+1}$ as an approximation

> $\mathscr{N}_{\overline{\mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{1}}}, \mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{2}}}, \ldots, \overline{\mathfrak{m}_{n, l_{h}}, \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}}}^{\Theta_{n}, d+1} \approx u \quad(16.18)$

of the unknown solution $u$ of the PDE in (16.10).

The ideas and the resulting schemes in the above derivation were first introduced as PINNs in Raissi et al. [347] and as DGMs in Sirignano & Spiliopoulos [379]. Very roughly speaking, PINNs and DGMs in their original form differ in the way the joint distribution of the random variables $(\Xi_{n, j}, \mathbf{X}_{n, j})_{(n, j) \in \mathbb{N} \times\{1,2, \ldots, J\}}$ would be chosen. Loosely speaking, in the case of PINNs the originally proposed distribution for $(\Xi_{n, j}, \mathbf{X}_{n, j})_{(n, j) \in \mathbb{N} \times\{1,2, \ldots, J\}}$ would be based on drawing a finite number of samples of the random variable $(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{X})$ and then having the random variable $(\Xi_{n, j}, \mathbf{X}_{n, j})_{(n, j) \in \mathbb{N} \times\{1,2, \ldots, J\}}$ be randomly chosen among those samples. In the case of DGMs the original proposition would be to choose $(\Xi_{n, j}, \mathbf{X}_{n, j})_{(n, j) \in \mathbb{N} \times\{1,2, \ldots, J\}}$ independent and identically distributed. Implementations of PINNs and DGMs that employ more sophisticated optimization methods, such as the Adam SGD optimization method, can be found in the next section.
