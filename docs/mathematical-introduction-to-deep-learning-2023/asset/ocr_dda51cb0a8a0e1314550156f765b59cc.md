(cf. Definitions 1.3.5 and 3.3.4). Next note that (4.156) ensures that for all $k \in \mathbb{N}$, $\mathfrak{W} = (w_{i,j})_{(i,j) \in \{1,2,\dots,d\} \times \{1,2,\dots,k\}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$ it holds that

```
$W_1 \mathfrak{W} = \begin{pmatrix} w_{1,1} & w_{1,2} & \dots & w_{1,k} \\ -w_{1,1} & -w_{1,2} & \dots & -w_{1,k} \\ w_{2,1} & w_{2,2} & \dots & w_{2,k} \\ -w_{2,1} & -w_{2,2} & \dots & -w_{2,k} \\ \ddots & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots \\ w_{d,1} & w_{d,2} & \dots & w_{d,k} \\ -w_{d,1} & -w_{d,2} & \dots & -w_{d,k} \end{pmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{(2d) \times k}.$
```

Furthermore, observe that (4.156) and (4.158) imply that for all $\mathfrak{B} = (b_1, b_2, \dots, b_d) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ it holds that

```
$W_1 \mathfrak{B} + B_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & \dots & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & \dots & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & \dots & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & \dots & 0 \\ \ddots & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots \\ 0 & 0 & \dots & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & \dots & -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \\ \ddots \\ b_d \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} b_1 \\ -b_1 \\ b_2 \\ -b_2 \\ \ddots \\ b_d \\ -b_d \end{pmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{2d}.$
```

Combining this with (4.160) demonstrates that for all $k \in \mathbb{N}$, $\mathfrak{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$, $\mathfrak{B} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ it holds that

```
$\left\| \mathcal{T}(((W_1 \mathfrak{W}, W_1 \mathfrak{B} + B_1))) \right\|_{\infty} = \left\| \mathcal{T}((\mathfrak{W}, \mathfrak{B})) \right\|_{\infty}.$
```

This, Lemma 4.4.5, and (4.159) prove that

```
$\left\| \mathcal{T}(\mathcal{E}_{L,J_d}(\Phi)) \right\|_{\infty} = \left\| \mathcal{T}(((J_d)^{\bullet(L-\mathcal{L}(\Phi))} \bullet \Phi)) \right\|_{\infty}$

$\le \max \left\{ \left\| \mathcal{T}(J_d)^{\bullet(L-\mathcal{L}(\Phi))} \right\|_{\infty}, \left\| \mathcal{T}(\Phi) \right\|_{\infty} \right\} = \max \{1, \left\| \mathcal{T}(\Phi) \right\|_{\infty} \}$
```

(cf. Definition 2.2.8). The proof of Lemma 4.4.6 is thus complete.

# **Lemma 4.4.7.**

Let $L, \mathfrak{L} \in \mathbb{N}$, $l_0, l_1, \dots, l_L, \mathfrak{l}_0, \mathfrak{l}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{l}_\mathfrak{L} \in \mathbb{N}$ satisfy

```
$\mathfrak{L} \ge L, \qquad l_0 = \mathfrak{l}_0, \qquad \text{and} \qquad \mathfrak{l}_\mathfrak{L} = l_L,$
```

assume for all $i \in \mathbb{N} \cap [0, L)$ that $l_i \ge l_\mathfrak{i}$, assume for all $i \in \mathbb{N} \cap (L-1, \mathfrak{L})$ that $\mathfrak{l}_i \ge 2 l_L$, and let $\Phi \in \mathbb{N}$ satisfy $\mathcal{D}(\Phi) = (l_0, l_1, \dots, l_L)$ (cf. Definition 1.3.1). Then there exists $\Psi \in \mathbb{N}$ such that

```
$\mathcal{D}(\Psi) = (l_0, \mathfrak{l}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{l}_\mathfrak{L}), \quad \left\| \mathcal{T}(\Psi) \right\|_{\infty} \le \max \{1, \left\| \mathcal{T}(\Phi) \right\|_{\infty} \}, \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{R}_i^{\mathbb{N}}(\Psi) = \mathcal{R}_i^{\mathbb{N}}(\Phi)$
```

(cf. Definitions 1.2.4, 1.3.4, 1.3.5, and 3.3.4).
