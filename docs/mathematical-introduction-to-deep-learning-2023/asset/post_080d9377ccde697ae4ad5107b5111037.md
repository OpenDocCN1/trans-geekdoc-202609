
**Corollary 9.8.4.** Let $\varepsilon \in (0, \infty)$, $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, $\vartheta \in U$ satisfy $U = \{ \theta \in \mathbb{R} : |\theta - \vartheta| \le \varepsilon \}$ and let $\mathscr{L} : U \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $\theta \in U$ that

$$\limsup_{K \to \infty} \left| \mathscr{L} (\theta) - \mathscr{L} (\vartheta) - \sum_{k=1}^{K} a_{k} (\theta - \vartheta)^{k} \right| = 0. \tag{9.99}$$

Then there exist $\delta \in (0, \varepsilon)$, $c \in (0, \infty)$, $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ such that for all $\theta \in \{ \xi \in \mathbb{R} : |\xi - \vartheta| < \delta \}$ it holds that

$$|\mathscr{L} (\theta) - \mathscr{L} (\vartheta)|^{\alpha} \le c \, |\mathscr{L}^{\prime} (\vartheta)|. \tag{9.100}$$

*Proof of Corollary 9.8.4.* Throughout this proof, let $V \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ satisfy $V = \{ \theta \in \mathbb{R} : |\theta| \le \varepsilon \}$ and let $\mathcal{M} : V \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $\theta \in V$ that $\mathcal{M}(\theta) = \mathscr{L} (\theta + \vartheta)$. Observe that (9.99) and the fact that for all $\theta \in V$ it holds that $\theta + \vartheta \in U$ ensures that for all $\theta \in V$ it holds that

$$\begin{aligned} &\limsup_{K \to \infty} \left| \mathcal{M} (\theta) - \mathcal{M}(0) - \sum_{k=1}^{K} a_{k} \theta^{k} \right| \\ &= \limsup_{K \to \infty} \left| \mathscr{L}(\theta + \vartheta) - \mathscr{L} (\vartheta) - \sum_{k=1}^{K} a_{k} ((\theta + \vartheta) - \vartheta)^{k} \right| = 0. \end{aligned} \tag{9.101}$$

Corollary 9.8.3 hence establishes that there exist $\delta \in (0, \varepsilon)$, $c \in (0, \infty)$, $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ which satisfy that for all $\theta \in \{ \xi \in \mathbb{R} : |\xi| < \delta \}$ it holds that

$$|\mathcal{M} (\theta) - \mathcal{M}(0)|^{\alpha} \le c \, |\mathcal{M}^{\prime} (0)|. \tag{9.102}$$

Therefore, we obtain for all $\theta \in \{ \xi \in \mathbb{R} : |\xi| < \delta \}$ that

$$|\mathscr{L}(\theta + \vartheta) - \mathscr{L} (\vartheta)|^{\alpha} \le c \, |\mathscr{L}^{\prime} (\vartheta)|. \tag{9.103}$$

This implies (9.100). The proof of Corollary 9.8.4 is thus complete.

**Corollary 9.8.5.** Let $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be open, let $\mathscr{L} : U \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be analytic, and let $\vartheta \in U$ (*cf.* Definition 9.7.1). Then there exist $\varepsilon, c \in (0, \infty)$, $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ such that for all $\theta \in \{ \xi \in U : |\vartheta - \xi| < \varepsilon \}$ it holds that

$$|\mathscr{L} (\vartheta) - \mathscr{L} (\theta)|^{\alpha} \le c \, | (\nabla \mathscr{L}) (\vartheta)|. \tag{9.104}$$

*Proof of Corollary 9.8.5.* Note that Corollary 9.8.4 establishes (9.104). The proof of Corollary 9.8.5 is thus complete.

**Corollary 9.8.6.** Let $\mathscr{L} : \mathbb{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be analytic (*cf.* Definition 9.7.1). Then $\mathscr{L}$ is a standard *KL* function (*cf.* Definition 9.1.2).

*Proof of Corollary 9.8.6.* Observe that (9.2) and Corollary 9.8.5 establish that $\mathscr{L}$ is a standard *KL* function (*cf.* Definition 9.1.2). The proof of Corollary 9.8.6 is thus complete.

## 9.9 Standard KL inequalities for analytic functions

**Theorem 9.9.1 (Standard KL inequalities for analytic functions).** *Let* $\mathfrak{d} \in \mathbb{N}$, *let* $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ *be open, let* $\mathcal{L}: U \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ *be analytic, and let* $\vartheta \in U$ *(cf. Definition* 9.7.1*).* Then there exist $\varepsilon, c \in (0, \infty)$, $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ such that for all $\theta \in \{u \in U: \|\vartheta-u\|_2 < \varepsilon\}$ it holds that

$\left|\mathcal{L}(\vartheta)-\mathcal{L}(\theta)\right|^{\alpha} \leq c \|\nabla \mathcal{L}(\theta)\|_2\tag{9.105}$

*(cf. Definition* 3.3.4*).

*Proof of* Theorem 9.9.1. *Note that Lojasiewicz* [281, Proposition 1] *demonstrates* (9.105) *(cf., for example, also Biernstone & Milman* [38, Proposition 6.8]*). The proof of* Theorem 9.9.1 *is thus complete.*

**Corollary 9.9.2.** *Let* $\mathfrak{d} \in \mathbb{N}$ *and let* $\mathcal{L}: \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ *be analytic (cf. Definition* 9.7.1*). Then* $\mathcal{L}$ *is a standard* **KL** *function (cf. Definition* 9.1.2*).

*Proof of Corollary* 9.9.2. *Observe that* (9.2) *and* Theorem 9.9.1 *establish that* $\mathcal{L}$ *is a standard* **KL** *function (cf. Definition* 9.1.2*). The proof of Corollary* 9.9.2 *is thus complete.*

## 9.10 Counterexamples

**Example 9.10.1 (Example of a smooth function that is not a standard KL function).** *Let* $\mathcal{L}: \mathbb{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ *satisfy for all* $x \in \mathbb{R}$ *that*

$\mathcal{L}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}{ll} \exp \left(-x^{-1}\right) & : x>0 \\ 0 & : x \leq 0 . \end{array}\right.\tag{9.106}$

*Then*
- * $(i)$ *it holds that* $\mathcal{L} \in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R})$,
- * $(ii)$ *it holds for all* $x \in (0, \infty)$ *that* $\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(x)=x^{-2} \exp \left(-x^{-1}\right)$,
- * $(iii)$ *it holds for all* $\alpha \in (0,1)$, $\varepsilon \in (0, \infty)$ *that*

$\sup_{x \in(0, \varepsilon)}\left(\frac{\left|\mathcal{L}(x)-\mathcal{L}(0)\right|^{\alpha}}{\left|\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(x)\right|}\right)=\infty,\tag{9.107}$

*and*
- * $(iv)$ *it holds that* $\mathcal{L}$ *is not a standard* **KL** *function*

*(cf. Definition* 9.1.2*).

*Proof for Example 9.10.1*. Throughout this proof, let

$P = \{f \in C((0, \infty), \mathbb{R}): f \text{ is a polynomial}\}$ (9.108)

and for every $f \in C((0, \infty), \mathbb{R})$ let $G_f: (0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $x \in (0, \infty)$ that

$G_f(x) = f(x^{-1}) \exp(-x^{-1}).$ (9.109)

Note that the chain rule and the product rule ensure that for all $f \in C^1((0, \infty), \mathbb{R})$, $x \in (0, \infty)$ it holds that $G_f \in C^1((0, \infty), \mathbb{R})$ and

$\begin{aligned} (G_f)^{\prime}(x) = &-f^{\prime}(x^{-1})x^{-2} \exp(-x^{-1}) + f(x^{-1})x^{-2} \exp(-x^{-1}) \\ & = (f(x^{-1}) - f^{\prime}(x^{-1}))x^{-2} \exp(-x^{-1}). \end{aligned}$ (9.110)

Hence, we obtain for all $p \in P$ that there exists $q \in P$ such that

$(G_p)^{\prime} = G_q,$ (9.111)

Combining this and (9.110) with induction ensures that for all $p \in P$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that

$G_p \in C^{\infty}((0, \infty), \mathbb{R}) \quad \text{ and } \quad (\exists q \in P: (G_p)^{(n)} = G_q).$ (9.112)

This and the fact that for all $p \in P$ it holds that $\lim_{x \searrow 0} G_p(x) = 0$ establish that for all $p \in P$ it holds that

$\lim_{x \searrow 0} (G_p)^{(n)}(x) = 0,$ (9.113)

The fact that $\mathscr{L}|_{(0, \infty)} = G_{|_{(0, \infty) \ni x \mapsto 1 \in \mathbb{R}}}$ and (9.110) therefore establish item (i) and item (iii). Observe that (9.106) and the fact that for all $y \in (0, \infty)$ it holds that

$\exp(y) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{y^k}{k!} \geq \frac{y^3}{3!} = \frac{y^3}{6}$ (9.114)

ensure that for all $\alpha \in (0, 1)$, $\varepsilon \in (0, \infty)$, $x \in (0, \varepsilon)$ it holds that

$\begin{aligned} \frac{|\mathscr{L}(x) - \mathscr{L}(0)|^{\alpha}}{|\mathscr{L}^{\prime}(x)|} & = \frac{|\mathscr{L}(x)|^{\alpha}}{|\mathscr{L}^{\prime}(x)|} = \frac{x^2 |\mathscr{L}(x)|^{\alpha}}{|\mathscr{L}(x)|} = x^2 |\mathscr{L}(x)|^{\alpha-1} \\ & = x^2 \exp\left( \frac{(1-\alpha)}{x} \right) \geq \frac{x^2(1-\alpha)^3}{6x^3} = \frac{(1-\alpha)^3}{6x}. \end{aligned}$ (9.115)

Hence, we obtain for all $\alpha \in (0, 1)$, $\varepsilon \in (0, \infty)$ that

$\sup_{x \in (0, \varepsilon)} \left( \frac{|\mathscr{L}(x) - \mathscr{L}(0)|^{\alpha}}{|\mathscr{L}^{\prime}(x)|} \right) \geq \sup_{x \in (0, \varepsilon)} \left( \frac{(1-\alpha)^3}{6x} \right) = \infty.$ (9.116)

The proof for Example 9.10.1 is thus complete. □

**Example 9.10.2** (Example of a differentiable function that fails to satisfy the standard **KL** inequality). *Let* \(\mathcal{L} : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) *satisfy for all* \(x \in \mathbb{R}\) *that*

\[ \mathcal{L}(x)= \int_{0}^{\max \{x, 0\}} y|\sin(y^{-1})| \, dy. \tag{9.117} \]

*Then*
- (i) it holds that \(\mathcal{L} \in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R})\),
- (ii) it holds for all \(c \in \mathbb{R}\), \(\alpha, \varepsilon \in (0, \infty)\) that *there exist* \(x \in (0, \varepsilon)\) *such that*

\[ |\mathcal{L}(x)-\mathcal{L}(0)|^{\alpha} > c|\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(x)|, \tag{9.118} \]

- (iii) it holds for all \(c \in \mathbb{R}\), \(\alpha, \varepsilon \in (0, \infty)\) that *we do not have that* \(\mathcal{L}\) *satisfies the standard* **KL** *inequality at* \(0\) *on* \([0, \varepsilon)\) *with exponent* \(\alpha\) *and constant* \(c\)

*(cf. Definition 9.1.1).*

**Proof for Example 9.10.2.** Throughout this proof, let \(\mathcal{G}: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) satisfy for all \(x \in \mathbb{R}\) that

\[ \mathcal{G}(x)= \begin{cases} x|\sin(x^{-1})| & : x > 0 \\[2pt] 0 & : x \leq 0. \end{cases} \tag{9.119} \]

Note that (9.119) proves that for all \(k \in \mathbb{N}\) it holds that

\[ \mathcal{G}((k \pi)^{-1})= (k \pi)^{-1}|\sin(k \pi)|= 0. \tag{9.120} \]

Furthermore, observe that (9.119) shows for all \(x \in (0, \infty)\) that

\[ |\mathcal{G}(x)-\mathcal{G}(0)|= |x\sin(x^{-1})| \leq |x|. \tag{9.121} \]

Therefore, we obtain that \(\mathcal{G}\) is continuous. This, (9.117), and the fundamental theorem of calculus ensure that \(\mathcal{L}\) is continuously differentiable with

\[ \mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\mathcal{G}. \tag{9.122} \]

Combining this with (9.120) demonstrates that for all \(c \in \mathbb{R}\), \(\alpha \in (0, \infty)\), \(k \in \mathbb{N}\) it holds that

\[ |\mathcal{L}((k \pi)^{-1})-\mathcal{L}(0)|^{\alpha}= |\mathcal{L}((k \pi)^{-1})|^{\alpha} > 0= c|\mathcal{G}((k \pi)^{-1})|= c|\mathcal{L}^{\prime}((k \pi)^{-1})|. \tag{9.123} \]

The proof for Example 9.10.2 is thus complete. \(\quad\) \(\square\)
