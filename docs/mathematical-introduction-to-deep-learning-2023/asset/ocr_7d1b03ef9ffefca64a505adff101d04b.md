Exercise 1.2.6 (Two-dimensional maximum). Prove or disprove the following statement: There exist $\boldsymbol{d}$, $H \in \mathbb{N}$, $l_1, l_2, \dots, l_H \in \mathbb{N}$, $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^d$ with $\boldsymbol{d} \geq 3l_1 + [\sum_{k=2}^{H} l_k(l_{k-1} + 1)] + l_H + 1$ such that for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$ it holds that

    $$\big(\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{d, 2}}_{\mathfrak{N}_{l_1}, \mathfrak{N}_{l_2}, \dots, \mathfrak{N}_H, \text{id}_\mathbb{R}}\big)(x, y) = \max\{x, y\} \tag{1.34}$$

(cf. Definitions 1.1.3 and 1.2.5).

Exercise 1.2.7 (Real identity with two hidden layers). Prove or disprove the following statement: There exist $\boldsymbol{d}$, $l_1, l_2 \in \mathbb{N}$, $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^d$ with $\boldsymbol{d} \geq 2l_1 + l_1 l_2 + 2l_2 + 1$ such that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ it holds that

    $$\big(\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{d, 1}}_{\mathfrak{N}_{l_1}, \mathfrak{N}_{l_2}, \text{id}_\mathbb{R}}\big)(x) = x \tag{1.35}$$

(cf. Definitions 1.1.3 and 1.2.5).

The statement of the next lemma, Lemma 1.2.8, provides a partial answer to Exercise 1.2.7. The proof of Lemma 1.2.8 is left as an exercise.

# Lemma 1.2.8 (Real identity with two hidden layers). Let $\theta = (1, -1, 0, 0, 1, -1, -1, 1, 0, 0, 1, -1, 0) \in \mathbb{R}^{13}$. Then it holds for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ that

    $$\big(\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{d, 1}}_{\mathfrak{N}_{2}, \mathfrak{N}_{2}, \text{id}_\mathbb{R}}\big)(x) = x \tag{1.36}$$

(cf. Definitions 1.1.3 and 1.2.5).

Exercise 1.2.8 (Three-dimensional maximum). Prove or disprove the following statement: There exist $\boldsymbol{d}$, $H \in \mathbb{N}$, $l_1, l_2, \dots, l_H \in \mathbb{N}$, $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^d$ with $\boldsymbol{d} \geq 4l_1 + [\sum_{k=2}^{H} l_k(l_{k-1} + 1)] + l_H + 1$ such that for all $x, y, z \in \mathbb{R}$ it holds that

    $$\big(\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{d, 3}}_{\mathfrak{N}_{l_1}, \mathfrak{N}_{l_2}, \dots, \mathfrak{N}_H, \text{id}_\mathbb{R}}\big)(x, y, z) = \max\{x, y, z\} \tag{1.37}$$

(cf. Definitions 1.1.3 and 1.2.5).

Exercise 1.2.9 (Multidimensional maxima). Prove or disprove the following statement: For every $k \in \mathbb{N}$ there exist $\boldsymbol{d}$, $H \in \mathbb{N}$, $l_1, l_2, \dots, l_H \in \mathbb{N}$, $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^d$ with $\boldsymbol{d} \geq (k+1)l_1 + [\sum_{k=2}^{H} l_k(l_{k-1} + 1)] + l_H + 1$ such that for all $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_k \in \mathbb{R}$ it holds that

    $$\big(\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{d, k}}_{\mathfrak{N}_{l_1}, \mathfrak{N}_{l_2}, \dots, \mathfrak{N}_H, \text{id}_\mathbb{R}}\big)(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_k) = \max\{x_1, x_2, \dots, x_k\} \tag{1.38}$$

(cf. Definitions 1.1.3 and 1.2.5).

Exercise 1.2.10. Prove or disprove the following statement: There exist $\boldsymbol{d}$, $H \in \mathbb{N}$, $l_1, l_2, \dots, l_H \in \mathbb{N}$, $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^d$ with $\boldsymbol{d} \geq 2l_1 + [\sum_{k=2}^{H} l_k(l_{k-1} + 1)] + (l_H + 1) + 1$ such that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ it holds that

    $$\big(\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{d, 1}}_{\mathfrak{N}_{l_1}, \mathfrak{N}_{l_2}, \dots, \mathfrak{N}_H, \text{id}_\mathbb{R}}\big)(x) = \max\{x, \frac{x}{2}\} \tag{1.39}$$

(cf. Definitions 1.1.3 and 1.2.5).
