Proof of Corollary 9.13.4. Throughout this proof, for every $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ let $\mathbf{d}_k = l_k(l_{k-1} + 1)$ and for every $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ let $F_k\colon \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}_k} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}} \to \mathbb{R}^{l_k}$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}_k}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^{l_{k-1}}$ that

F_k(\theta, x) = \Psi_k\bigl(\mathcal{A}^{\theta,0}_{l_k,l_{k-1}}(x)\bigr)\tag{9.269}

(cf. Definition 1.1.1). Observe that item (i) in Lemma 5.3.3 demonstrates that for all $\theta_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}_1}$, $\theta_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}_2}$, $\ldots$, $\theta_L \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}_L}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^{l_0}$ it holds that

\bigl(\mathcal{N}^{\theta_1,\theta_2,\ldots,\theta_L}_{\psi_1,\psi_2,\ldots,\psi_L}^{0}\bigr)(x) = \bigl(F_L(\theta_L, \cdot) \circ F_{L-1}(\theta_{L-1}, \cdot) \circ \cdots \circ F_1(\theta_1, \cdot)\bigr)(x)\tag{9.270}

(cf. Definition 1.1.3). Note that the assumption that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ it holds that $\Psi_k$ is analytic, the fact that for all $m$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{m(n+1)}$ it holds that $\mathbb{R}^{m(n+1)} \times \mathbb{R}^n \ni (\theta, x) \mapsto \mathcal{A}^{\theta,0}_{m,n}(x) \in \mathbb{R}^m$ is analytic, and Proposition 9.13.1 ensure that for all $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ it holds that $F_k$ is analytic. Lemma 5.3.2 and induction hence ensure that

\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}_1} \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}_2} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}_L} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_0} \ni (\theta_1, \theta_2, \ldots, \theta_L, x)\tag{9.271}\nonumber\nonumber
\mapsto \bigl(F_L(\theta_L, \cdot) \circ F_{L-1}(\theta_{L-1}, \cdot) \circ \cdots \circ F_1(\theta_1, \cdot)\bigr)(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_L}

is analytic. This and (9.270) establish that

\mathbb{R}^{\sum_{k=1}^{L} l_k(l_{k-1}+1)} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_0} \ni (\theta, x) \mapsto \mathcal{N}^{\theta,l_0}_{\psi_1,\psi_2,\ldots,\psi_L}(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_L}\tag{9.272}

is analytic. The proof of Corollary 9.13.4 is thus complete. $\Box$

**Corollary 9.13.5 (Analyticity of the empirical risk function).** Let $L, \mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}$, $M$, $l_0$, $l_1$, $\ldots$, $l_L \in \mathbb{N}$, $x_1, x_2$, $\ldots$, $x_M \in \mathbb{R}^{l_0}$, $y_1, y_2$, $\ldots$, $y_M \in \mathbb{R}^{l_L}$ satisfy $\mathbf{d} = \sum_{k=1}^{L} l_k(l_{k-1} + 1)$, let $a\colon \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbf{L}\colon \mathbb{R}^{l_L} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_L} \to \mathbb{R}$ be analytic, let $\mathscr{L}\colon \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}} \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ that

\mathscr{L}(\theta) = \frac{1}{M} \left[\sum_{m=1}^{M} \mathbf{L}\bigl(\bigl(\mathcal{N}^{\theta,l_0}_{\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_1},\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_2},\ldots,\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_{L-1}},\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{l_L}}}\bigr)(x_m), y_m\bigr)\right]\tag{9.273}

(cf. Definitions 1.1.3, 1.2.1, and 9.7.1). Then $\mathscr{L}$ is analytic.

Proof of Corollary 9.13.5. Observe that the assumption that $a$ is analytic, Lemma 9.13.2, and induction show that for all $m \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that $\mathfrak{M}_{a,m}$ is analytic. This, Corollary 9.13.4 and Lemma 9.13.2 (applied with $\mathbf{d}_1 \sim \mathbf{d} + l_0$, $l_1 \sim l_0$, $l_1 \sim l_L$, $l_2 \sim l_L$, $\tilde{F}_1 \sim (\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_0} \ni (\theta, x) \mapsto \bigl(\mathcal{N}^{\theta,l_0}_{\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_1},\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_2},\ldots,\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_{L-1}},\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{l_L}}}\bigr)(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_L}$), $F_2 \sim \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{l_L}}$ in the notation of Lemma 9.13.2) ensure that

\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_0} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_L} \ni (\theta, x, y) \mapsto \bigl(\bigl(\mathcal{N}^{\theta,l_0}_{\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_1},\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_2},\ldots,\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_{L-1}},\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{l_L}}}\bigr)(x), y\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_L} \times \mathbb{R}^{l_L}\tag{9.274}

is analytic. The assumption that $\mathbf{L}$ is differentiable and the chain rule therefore establish that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^{l_0}$, $y \in \mathbb{R}^{l_L}$ it holds that

\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}} \ni \theta \mapsto \mathbf{L}\bigl(\bigl(\mathcal{N}^{\theta,l_0}_{\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_1},\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_2},\ldots,\mathfrak{M}_{a,l_{L-1}},\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{l_L}}}\bigr)(x_m), y_m\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}\tag{9.275}

is analytic. This proves (9.273). The proof of Corollary 9.13.5 is thus complete. $\Box$
