
## 6.4 GD optimization with Nesterov momentum

In this section we review the Nesterov accelerated GD optimization method, which was first introduced in Nesterov [302] (cf., for instance, Sutskever et al. [387]). The Nesterov accelerated GD optimization method can be viewed as building on the momentum GD optimization method (see Definition 6.3.1) by attempting to provide some kind of foresight to the scheme. A similar perspective is to see the Nesterov accelerated GD optimization method as a combination of the momentum GD optimization method (see Definition 6.3.1) and the explicit midpoint GD optimization method (see Section 6.2).

**Definition 6.4.1** (Nesterov accelerated GD optimization method). Let $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{N}$, $(\gamma_{n})_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq [0, \infty)$, $(\alpha_{n})_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq [0, 1]$, $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ and let $\mathscr{L}: \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ and $\mathscr{G}: \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ satisfy for all $U \in\{V \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}: V$ is open$\}$, $\theta \in U$ with $\mathscr{L}|_{U} \in C^{1}(U, \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})$ that

$$\mathscr{G}(\theta)=(\nabla \mathscr{L})(\theta). \tag{6.328}$$

Then we say that $\Theta$ is the Nesterov accelerated GD process for the objective function $\mathscr{L}$ with generalized gradient $\mathscr{G}$, learning rates $(\gamma_{n})_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, momentum decay factors $(\alpha_{n})_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, and initial value $\xi$ (we say that $\Theta$ is the Nesterov accelerated GD process for the objective function $\mathscr{L}$ with learning rates $(\gamma_{n})_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, momentum decay factors $(\alpha_{n})_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, and initial value $\xi$) if and only if it holds that $\Theta: \mathbb{N}_{0} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ is the function from $\mathbb{N}_{0}$ to $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ which satisfies that there exists $\mathbf{m}: \mathbb{N}_{0} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ such that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ it holds that

$$\begin{gathered} \Theta_{0}=\xi, \quad \mathbf{m}_{0}=0, \\ \mathbf{m}_{n}=\alpha_{n} \mathbf{m}_{n-1}+\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right) \mathscr{G}\left(\Theta_{n-1}-\gamma_{n} \alpha_{n} \mathbf{m}_{n-1}\right), \tag{6.329} \\ \text { and } \quad \Theta_{n}=\Theta_{n-1}-\gamma_{n} \mathbf{m}_{n} . \tag{6.330} \end{gathered}$$

## 6.5 Adagrad GD optimization (Adagrad)

In this section we review the Adagrad GD optimization method. Roughly speaking, the idea of the Adagrad GD optimization method is to modify the plain-vanilla GD optimization method by adapting the learning rates separately for every component of the optimization process. The name Adagrad is derived from *adaptive subgradient method* and was first presented in Duchi et al. [117] in the context of stochastic optimization. For pedagogical purposes we present in this section a deterministic version of Adagrad optimization and we refer to Section 7.6 below for the original stochastic version of Adagrad optimization.

**Definition 6.5.1** (Adagrad GD optimization method). Let $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{N}$, $(\gamma_{n})_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq [0, \infty)$, $\varepsilon \in (0, \infty)$, $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ and let $\mathscr{L}: \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ and $\mathscr{G}=\left(\mathscr{G}_{1}, \ldots, \mathscr{G}_{\mathbf{d}}\right): \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ satisfy for all $U \in\{V \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}: V$ is open$\}$, $\theta \in U$ with $\mathscr{L}|_{U} \in C^{1}(U, \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})$ that

$$\mathscr{G}(\theta)=(\nabla \mathscr{L})(\theta). \tag{6.332}$$

Then we say that $\Theta$ is the Adagrad GD process for the objective function $\mathcal{L}$ with generalized gradient $\mathcal{G}$, learning rates $\left(\gamma_{n}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, regularizing factor $\varepsilon$, and initial value $\xi$ (we say that $\Theta$ is the Adagrad GD process for the objective function $\mathcal{L}$ with learning rates $\left(\gamma_{n}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, regularizing factor $\varepsilon$, and initial value $\xi$) if and only if it holds that $\Theta=\left(\Theta^{(1)}, \ldots, \Theta^{(\mathbf{d})}\right): \mathbb{N}_{0} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ is the function from $\mathbb{N}_{0}$ to $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ which satisfies for all $n \in \mathbb{N}, i \in\{1,2, \ldots, \mathbf{d}\}$ that

$$\Theta_{0}=\xi \quad \text { and } \quad \Theta_{n}^{(i)}=\Theta_{n-1}^{(i)}-\gamma_{n}\left[\varepsilon+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left|\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(\Theta_{k}\right)\right|^{2}\right]^{-1 / 2} \mathcal{G}_{i}\left(\Theta_{n-1}\right).$$

## 6.6 Root mean square propagation GD optimization (RMSprop)

In this section we review the RMSprop GD optimization method. Roughly speaking, the RMSprop GD optimization method is a modification of the Adagrad GD optimization method where the sum over the squares of previous partial derivatives of the objective function (cf. (6.333) in Definition 6.5.1) is replaced by an exponentially decaying average over the squares of previous partial derivatives of the objective function (cf. (6.335) and (6.336) in Definition 6.6.1). RMSprop optimization was introduced by Geoffrey Hinton in his courtesy class on Neural Networks for Machine Learning (see Hinton et al. [199]) in the context of stochastic optimization. As in the case of Adagrad optimization, we present for pedagogical purposes first a deterministic version of RMSprop optimization in this section and we refer to Section 7.7 below for the original stochastic version of RMSprop optimization.

**Definition 6.6.1** (RMSprop GD optimization method). Let $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{N},\left(\gamma_{n}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq[0, \infty),\left(\beta_{n}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq[0,1], \varepsilon \in(0, \infty), \xi \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ and let $\mathcal{L}: \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ and $\mathcal{G}=\left(\mathcal{G}_{1}, \ldots, \mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{d}}\right): \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ satisfy for all $U \in\left\{V \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}: V \text { is open }\right\}, \theta \in U$ with $\mathcal{L}\left._{V} \in C^{1}\left(U, \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}\right)$ that

$$\mathcal{G}(\theta)=(\nabla \mathcal{L})(\theta) .$$

Then we say that $\Theta$ is the RMSprop GD process for the objective function $\mathcal{L}$ with generalized gradient $\mathcal{G}$, learning rates $\left(\gamma_{n}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, second moment decay factors $\left(\beta_{n}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, regularizing factor $\varepsilon$, and initial value $\xi$ (we say that $\Theta$ is the RMSprop GD process for the objective function $\mathcal{L}$ with learning rates $\left(\gamma_{n}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, second moment decay factors $\left(\beta_{n}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, regularizing factor $\varepsilon$, and initial value $\xi$) if and only if it holds that $\Theta=\left(\Theta^{(1)}, \ldots, \Theta^{(\mathbf{d})}\right): \mathbb{N}_{0} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ is the function from $\mathbb{N}_{0}$ to $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ which satisfies that there exists $\mathrm{M}:=\left(\mathrm{M}^{(1)}, \ldots, \mathrm{M}^{(\mathbf{d})}\right): \mathbb{N}_{0} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}$ such that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}, i \in\{1,2, \ldots, \mathbf{d}\}$ it holds that

$$\Theta_{0}=\xi, \quad \mathrm{M}_{0}=0, \quad \mathrm{M}_{n}^{(i)}=\beta_{n} \mathrm{M}_{n-1}^{(i)}+\left(1-\beta_{n}\right)\left|\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(\Theta_{n-1}\right)\right|^{2}, \text { and } \Theta_{n}^{(i)}=\Theta_{n-1}^{(i)}-\gamma_{n}\left[\varepsilon+\mathrm{M}_{n}^{(i)}\right]^{-1 / 2} \mathcal{G}_{i}\left(\Theta_{n-1}\right) .$$

### 6.6.1 Representations of the mean square terms in RMSprop

**Lemma 6.6.2** (On a representation of the second order terms in RMSprop). *Let* $\mathfrak{d} \in \mathbb{N}$, $(\gamma_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq [0, \infty)$, $(\beta_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq [0, 1)$, $(b_{n,k})_{(n,k) \in (\mathbb{N}_0)^2} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, $\varepsilon \in (0, \infty)$, $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ *satisfy for all* $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $k \in \{0, 1, \ldots, n-1\}$ *that*

$$b_{n,k} = \left(1 - \beta_{k+1}\right) \left[\prod_{l=k+2}^{n} \beta_l\right], \tag{6.337}$$

*let* $\mathcal{L}: \mathbb{R}^{d} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ *and* $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{G}_1, \ldots, \mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{d}}): \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ *satisfy for all* $U \in \{V \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}: V \text{ is open}\}$, $\theta \in U$ *with* $\mathcal{L}|_U \in C^1(U, \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}})$ *that*

$$\mathcal{G}(\theta) = (\nabla \mathcal{L})(\theta), \tag{6.338}$$

*and let* $\Theta = (\Theta^{(1)}, \ldots, \Theta^{(\mathfrak{d})}): \mathbb{N}_0 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ *be the RMSprop GD process for the objective function* $\mathcal{L}$ *with generalized gradient* $\mathcal{G}$, *learning rates* $(\gamma_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, *second moment decay factors* $(\beta_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, *regularizing factor* $\varepsilon$, *and initial value* $\xi$ *(cf. Definition 6.6.1)*. *Then*

- *(i) it holds for all* $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $k \in \{0, 1, \ldots, n-1\}$ *that* $0 \le b_{n,k} \le 1$,
- *(ii) it holds for all* $n \in \mathbb{N}$ *that*

$$\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} b_{n,k} = 1 - \prod_{k=1}^{n} \beta_k, \tag{6.339}$$

*and*

- *(iii) it holds for all* $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathfrak{d}\}$ *that*

$$\Theta_n^{(i)} = \Theta_{n-1}^{(i)} - \gamma_n \left[\varepsilon + \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} b_{n,k} |\mathcal{G}_i(\Theta_k)|^2\right]^{-1/2} \mathcal{G}_i(\Theta_{n-1}). \tag{6.340}$$

*Proof of Lemma 6.6.2*. Throughout this proof, let $\mathbb{M} = (\mathbb{M}^{(1)}, \ldots, \mathbb{M}^{(\mathfrak{d})}): \mathbb{N}_0 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{d}}$ *satisfy for all* $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathfrak{d}\}$ *that* $\mathbb{M}_0^{(i)} = 0$ *and*

$$\mathbb{M}_n^{(i)} = \beta_n \mathbb{M}_{n-1}^{(i)} + (1 - \beta_n) |\mathcal{G}_i(\Theta_{n-1})|^2. \tag{6.341}$$

Note that (6.337) implies item (i). Furthermore, observe that (6.337), (6.341), and Lemma 6.3.3 assure that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathfrak{d}\}$ it holds that

$$\mathbb{M}_n^{(i)} = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} b_{n,k} |\mathcal{G}_i(\Theta_k)|^2 \quad \text{ and } \quad \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} b_{n,k} = 1 - \prod_{k=1}^{n} \beta_k. \tag{6.342}$$
