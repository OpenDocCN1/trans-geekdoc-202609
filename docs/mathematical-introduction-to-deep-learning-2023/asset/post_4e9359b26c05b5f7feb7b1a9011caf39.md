## 4.1 Approximations through supremal convolutions

**Definition 4.1.1 (Metric).** *We say that* $\delta$ *is a metric on* $E$ *if and only if it holds that* $\delta: E \times E \rightarrow [0, \infty)$ *is a function from* $E \times E$ *to* $[0, \infty)$ *which satisfies that*

- (i) *it holds that*

$$ \{ (x, y) \in E^2: \delta(x, y) = 0\} = \{ (x, x) \}_{x \in E} \quad \quad (4.1) $$

*(positive definiteness),*

- (ii) *it holds for all* $x, y \in E$ *that*

$$ \delta(x, y) = \delta(y, x) \quad \quad (4.2) $$

*(symmetry), and*

- (iii) *it holds for all* $x, y, z \in E$ *that*

$$ \delta(x, z) \leq \delta(x, y) + \delta(y, z) \quad \quad (4.3) $$

*(triangle inequality).*

**Definition 4.1.2** (Metric space). *We say that* $\mathcal{E}$ *is a metric space if and only if there exist a set* $E$ *and a metric* $\delta$ *on* $E$ *such that*

$$\mathcal{E}=(E,\delta)\tag{4.4}$$

*(cf. Definition 4.1.1).*

**Proposition 4.1.3** (Approximations through supremal convolutions). *Let* $(E, \delta)$ *be a metric space, let* $L \in [0, \infty)$*,* $D \subseteq E$*,* $\mathcal{M} \subseteq D$ *satisfy* $\mathcal{M} \neq \emptyset$*, let* $f: D \to \mathbb{R}$ *satisfy for all* $x \in D$*,* $y \in \mathcal{M}$ *that* $|f(x) - f(y)| \leq L \delta(x, y)$*, and let* $F: E \to \mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}$ *satisfy for all* $x \in E$ *that*

$$F(x)=\sup_{y \in \mathcal{M}} [f(y) - L \delta(x, y)]\tag{4.5}$$

*(cf. Definition 4.1.2). Then*

- (i) *it holds for all* $x \in \mathcal{M}$ *that* $F(x) = f(x)$*,*
- (ii) *it holds for all* $x \in D$ *that* $F(x) \leq f(x)$*,*
- (iii) *it holds for all* $x \in E$ *that* $F(x) < \infty$*,*
- (iv) *it holds for all* $x, y \in E$ *that* $|F(x) - F(y)| \leq L \delta(x, y)$*, and*
- (v) *it holds for all* $x \in D$ *that*

$$|F(x) - f(x)| \leq 2L \Big[\inf_{y \in \mathcal{M}} \delta(x, y)\Big].\tag{4.6}$$

*Proof of Proposition 4.1.3.* First, observe that the assumption that for all $x \in D$, $y \in \mathcal{M}$ it holds that $|f(x) - f(y)| \leq L \delta(x, y)$ ensures that for all $x \in D$, $y \in \mathcal{M}$ it holds that

$$f(y) + L \delta(x, y) \geq f(x) \geq f(y) - L \delta(x, y).\tag{4.7}$$

Hence, we obtain that for all $x \in D$ it holds that

$$f(x) \geq \sup_{y \in \mathcal{M}} [f(y) - L \delta(x, y)] = F(x).\tag{4.8}$$

This establishes item (ii). Moreover, note that (4.5) implies that for all $x \in \mathcal{M}$ it holds that

$$F(x) \geq f(x) - L \delta(x, x) = f(x).\tag{4.9}$$

This and (4.8) establish item (i). Note that (4.7) (applied for every $y, z \in \mathcal{M}$ with $x \sim y$, $y \sim z$ in the notation of (4.7)) and the triangle inequality ensure that for all $x \in E$, $y, z \in \mathcal{M}$ it holds that

$$f(y) - L \delta(x, y) \leq f(z) + L \delta(y, z) - L \delta(x, y) \leq f(z) + L \delta(x, z).\tag{4.10}$$

Hence, we obtain that for all $x \in E$, $z \in \mathcal{M}$ it holds that

$$\begin{gathered} F(x) = \sup_{y \in \mathcal{M}} [ f(y) - L \delta(x, y) ] \leq f(z) + L \delta(x, z) < \infty. \end{gathered}\tag{4.11}$$

This and the assumption that $\mathcal{M} \neq \emptyset$ prove item **(iii)**. Note that item **(iii)**, **(4.5)**, and the triangle inequality show that for all $x, y \in E$ it holds that

$$\begin{gathered} F(x) - F(y) = \left[ \sup_{v \in \mathcal{M}} (f(v) - L \delta(x, v)) \right] - \left[ \sup_{w \in \mathcal{M}} (f(w) - L \delta(y, w)) \right] \\ = \sup_{v \in \mathcal{M}} \left[ f(v) - L \delta(x, v) - \sup_{w \in \mathcal{M}} (f(w) - L \delta(y, w)) \right] \\ \leq \sup_{v \in \mathcal{M}} \left[ f(v) - L \delta(x, v) - (f(v) - L \delta(y, v)) \right] \tag{4.12} \\ = \sup_{v \in \mathcal{M}} (L \delta(y, v) - L \delta(x, v)) \\ \leq \sup_{v \in \mathcal{M}} (L \delta(y, x) + L \delta(x, v) - L \delta(x, v)) = L \delta(x, y). \end{gathered}$$

This and the fact that for all $x, y \in E$ it holds that $\delta(x, y) = \delta(y, x)$ establish item **(iv)**. Observe that items **(i)** and **(iv)**, the triangle inequality, and the assumption that $\forall x \in D, y \in \mathcal{M}$: $|f(x) - f(y)| \leq L \delta(x, y)$ ensure that for all $x \in D$ it holds that

$$\begin{gathered} |F(x) - f(x)| = \inf_{y \in \mathcal{M}} | F(x) - F(y) + f(y) - f(x) | \\ \leq \inf_{y \in \mathcal{M}} ( |F(x) - F(y)| + |f(y) - f(x)| ) \tag{4.13} \\ \leq \inf_{y \in \mathcal{M}} (2L \delta(x, y)) = 2L \left[ \inf_{y \in \mathcal{M}} \delta(x, y) \right]. \end{gathered}$$

This establishes item **(v)**. The proof of Proposition **4.1.3** is thus complete. $\square$

**Corollary 4.1.4** *(Approximations through supremum convolutions.)* *Let $(E, \delta)$ be a metric space*, *let $L \in [0, \infty)$*, *$\mathcal{M} \subseteq E$ satisfy* $\mathcal{M} \neq \emptyset$*, let $f \colon E \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfy for all $x \in E$, $y \in \mathcal{M}$ that $|f(x) - f(y)| \leq L \delta(x, y)$, and let $F \colon E \to \mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}$ satisfy for all $x \in E$ that

$$\begin{gathered} F(x) = \sup_{y \in \mathcal{M}} [ f(y) - L \delta(x, y) ] \end{gathered}\tag{4.14}$$

Then

- (i) it holds for all $x \in \mathcal{M}$ that $F(x) = f(x)$,
- (ii) it holds for all $x \in E$ that $F(x) \leq f(x)$,
- (iii) it holds for all $x, y \in E$ that $|F(x) - F(y)| \leq L \delta(x, y)$, and
- (iv) it holds for all $x \in E$ that

$$\| F(x) - f(x) \| \le 2L \left[ \inf_{y \in \mathcal{M}} \delta(x, y) \right]. \quad (4.15)$$

*Proof of Corollary 4.1.4*. Note that Proposition **4.1.3** establishes items **(i)**, **(ii)**, **(iii)**, and **(iv)**. The proof of Corollary **4.1.4** is thus complete. $\square$

## 4.2 ANN representations

### 4.2.1 ANN representations for the 1-norm

**Definition 4.2.1** (1-norm ANN representations). *We denote by* $(\mathbb{L}_d)_{d \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq \mathbf{N}$ *the fully-connected feedforward ANN*s *which satisfy that*

(i) it holds that

$$\mathbb{L}_1 = \left( \left( \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} \right), \left( \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \right), \left( \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \right) \right) \in (\mathbb{R}^{2 \times 1} \times \mathbb{R}^{2}) \times (\mathbb{R}^{1 \times 2} \times \mathbb{R}^1) \quad (4.17)$$

and

(ii) it holds for all $d \in \{2, 3, 4, \ldots\}$ that $\mathbb{L}_d = \mathbb{S}_{1, d} \bullet \mathbf{P}_d(\mathbb{L}_1, \mathbb{L}_1, \ldots, \mathbb{L}_1)$

(*cf. Definitions* **1.3.1**, **2.1.1**, **2.2.1**, *and* **2.4.1** *).*

**Proposition 4.2.2** (Properties of fully-connected feedforward 1-norm ANNs). *Let* $d \in \mathbb{N}$. *Then*

(i) it holds that $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{L}_d) = (d, 2d, 1)$,

(ii) it holds that $\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{r}}^{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbb{L}_d) \in C(\mathbb{R}^d, \mathbb{R})$, *and*

(iii) it holds for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$ that $(\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{r}}^{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbb{L}_d))(x) = \|x\|_1$

(*cf. Definitions* **1.2.4**, **1.3.1**, **1.3.4**, **3.3.4**, *and* **4.2.1** *).*

Proof of Proposition 4.2.2. Observe that the fact that $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{L}_{1}) = (1, 2, 1)$ and Lemma 2.2.2 show that

$\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{P}_{d}(\mathbb{L}_{1}, \mathbb{L}_{1}, \ldots, \mathbb{L}_{1})) = (d, 2d, d)$

(cf. Definitions 1.3.1, 2.2.1, and 4.2.1). Combining this, Proposition 2.1.2, and Lemma 2.3.2 ensures that

$\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{L}_{d}) = \mathcal{D}(\mathbb{S}_{1,d} \bullet \mathbf{P}_{d}(\mathbb{L}_{1}, \mathbb{L}_{1}, \ldots, \mathbb{L}_{1})) = (d, 2d, 1)$

(cf. Definitions 2.1.1 and 2.4.1). This establishes item (i). Note that (4.17) assures that for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ it holds that

$(\mathcal{R}_{\tau}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbb{L}_{1}))(x) = \tau(x) + \tau(-x) = \max\{x, 0\} + \max\{-x, 0\} = |x| = \|x\|_{1}$

(cf. Definitions 1.2.4, 1.3.4, and 3.3.4). Combining this and Proposition 2.2.3 shows that for all $x = (x_{1}, \ldots, x_{d}) \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ it holds that

$(\mathcal{R}_{\tau}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbf{P}_{d}(\mathbb{L}_{1}, \mathbb{L}_{1}, \ldots, \mathbb{L}_{1})))(x) = (|x_{1}|, |x_{2}|, \ldots, |x_{d}|).$

This and Lemma 2.4.2 demonstrate that for all $x = (x_{1}, \ldots, x_{d}) \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ it holds that

$(\mathcal{R}_{\tau}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbb{L}_{d}))(x) = (\mathcal{R}_{\tau}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbb{S}_{1,d} \bullet \mathbf{P}_{d}(\mathbb{L}_{1}, \mathbb{L}_{1}, \ldots, \mathbb{L}_{1})))(x)$
$= (\mathcal{R}_{\tau}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbb{S}_{1,d}))(|x_{1}|, |x_{2}|, \ldots, |x_{d}|) = \sum_{k=1}^{d} |x_{k}| = \|x\|_{1}$.

This establishes items (ii) and (iii). The proof of Proposition 4.2.2 is thus complete.
