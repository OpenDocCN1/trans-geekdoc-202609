### Lemma 12.3.6

Let $(E, \delta)$ be a separable metric space, assume $E \neq \emptyset$, let $\varepsilon, L \in(0, \infty)$, let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ be a probability space, and let $Z_{x} : \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}, x \in E$, be random variables which satisfy for all $x, y \in E$ that $\mathbb{E}\left[\left|Z_{x}\right|\right]<\infty$ and $\left|Z_{x}-Z_{y}\right| \leq L \delta(x, y)$. Then

$
\left[\mathcal{C}^{(E, \delta), \frac{x}{\delta}}\right]^{-1} \mathbb{P}\left(\sup _{x \in E}\left|Z_{x}-\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{x}\right]\right| \geq \varepsilon\right) \leq \sup _{x \in E} \mathbb{P}\left(\left|Z_{x}-\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{x}\right]\right| \geq \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right).
$

(cf. Definition 4.3.2 and Lemma 12.3.5).

Proof of Lemma 12.3.6. Throughout this proof, let $Y_{x} : \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}, x \in E$, satisfy for all $x \in E, \eta \in \Omega$ that $Y_{x}(\eta)=Z_{x}(\eta)-\mathbb{E}[Z_{x}]$. Observe that Lemma 12.3.5 ensures that for all $x, y \in E$ it holds that

$
\left|Y_{x}-Y_{y}\right| \leq 2 L \delta(x, y).
$

This and Lemma 12.3.4 (applied with $(E, \delta) \curvearrowright (E, \delta), \varepsilon \curvearrowright \varepsilon, L \curvearrowright 2 L, (\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}) \curvearrowright (\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}),\left(Z_{x}\right)_{x \in E} \curvearrowright\left(Y_{x}\right)_{x \in E}$ in the notation of Lemma 12.3.4) establish (12.158). The proof of Lemma 12.3.6 is thus complete.

### Lemma 12.3.7

Let $(E, \delta)$ be a separable metric space, assume $E \neq \emptyset$, let $M \in \mathbb{N}$, $\varepsilon, L, D \in(0, \infty)$, let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ be a probability space, for every $x \in E$ let $Y_{x, 1}, Y_{x, 2}, \ldots, Y_{x, M} : \Omega \rightarrow[0, D]$ be independent random variables, assume for all $x, y \in E, m \in\{1, 2, \ldots, M\}$ that $\left|Y_{x, m}-Y_{y, m}\right| \leq L \delta(x, y)$, and let $Z_{x} : \Omega \rightarrow[0, \infty), x \in E$, satisfy for all $x \in E$ that

$
Z_{x}=\frac{1}{M}\left[\sum_{m=1}^{M} Y_{x, m}\right].
$

Then

- (i) it holds for all $x \in E$ that $\mathbb{E}[|Z_{x}|] \leq D < \infty$,
- (ii) it holds that $\Omega \ni \eta \mapsto \sup_{x \in E}|Z_{x}(\eta) - \mathbb{E}[Z_{x}]| \in [0, \infty]$ is $\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{B}([0, \infty])$-measurable, and
- (iii) it holds that
  $\mathbb{E}(\sup_{x \in E}|Z_{x} - \mathbb{E}[Z_{x}]| \geq \varepsilon) \leq 2\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{E}(\delta, \varepsilon/\sqrt{\mathcal{D}})} \exp(-\varepsilon^{2}M/2D^{2})$  (12.161)

(cf. Definition 4.3.2).

**Proof of Lemma 12.3.7.** First, observe that the triangle inequality and the assumption that for all $x, y \in E, m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, M\}$ it holds that $|Y_{x, m} - Y_{y, m}| \leq L \delta(x, y)$ imply that for all $x, y \in E$ it holds that

$
\begin{aligned}
|Z_{x} - Z_{y}| &= \left| \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M Y_{x, m} - \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M Y_{y, m} \right| = \frac{1}{M} \left| \sum_{m=1}^M (Y_{x, m} - Y_{y, m}) \right| \\
&\leq \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M |Y_{x, m} - Y_{y, m}| \leq L \delta(x, y). \quad (12.162)
\end{aligned}
$

Next note that the assumption that for all $x \in E, m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, M\}, \omega \in \Omega$ it holds that $|Y_{x, m}(\omega)| \in [0, D]$ ensures that for all $x \in E$ it holds that

$
\mathbb{E}[|Z_{x}|] = \mathbb{E}\left[ \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M |Y_{x, m}| \right] = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M \mathbb{E}[|Y_{x, m}|] \leq D < \infty. \quad (12.163)
$

This proves item (i). Furthermore, note that item (i), (12.162), and Lemma 12.3.5 establish item (ii). Next observe that (12.160) shows that for all $x \in E$ it holds that

$
|Z_{x} - \mathbb{E}[Z_{x}]| = \left| \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M Y_{x, m} - \mathbb{E}\left[ \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M Y_{x, m} \right] \right| = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M |Y_{x, m} - \mathbb{E}[Y_{x, m}]| \quad (12.164)
$

Combining this with Corollary 12.1.21 (applied with $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}) \sim (\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$, $N \sim M$, $\varepsilon \sim \varepsilon/\sqrt{\mathcal{D}}$, $(a_{1}, a_{2}, \ldots, a_{N}) \sim (0, 0, \ldots, 0)$, $(b_{1}, b_{2}, \ldots, b_{N}) \sim (D, D, \ldots, D)$, $(X_{n})_{n \in\{1,2, \ldots, N\}} \sim (Y_{x, m})_{m \in\{1,2, \ldots, M\}}$ for $x \in E$ in the notation of Corollary 12.1.21) ensures that for all $x \in E$ it holds that

$
\mathbb{P}(|Z_{x} - \mathbb{E}[Z_{x}]| \geq \varepsilon/\sqrt{\mathcal{D}}) \leq 2 \exp\left(-2[\varepsilon/\sqrt{\mathcal{D}}]^{2}M^{2} / MD^{2}\right) = 2 \exp\left(-\varepsilon^{2}M / 2D^{2}\right). \quad (12.165)
$

Combining this, (12.162), and (12.163) with Lemma 12.3.6 establishes item (iii). The proof of Lemma 12.3.7 is thus complete.

### 12.3.2 Uniform estimates for the statistical learning error

### Lemma 12.3.8

Let $(E, \delta)$ be a separable metric space, assume $E \neq \emptyset$, let $M \in \mathbb{N}$, $L, D \in(0, \infty)$, let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ be a probability space, let $X_{x, m}: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}, x \in E, m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, M\}$, and $Y_{m}: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}, m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, M\}$, be functions, assume for all $x \in E$ that $(X_{x, m}, Y_{m})$, $m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, M\}$, are i.i.d. random variables, assume for all $x, y \in E, m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, M\}$ that $|X_{x, m} - X_{y, m}| \leq L \delta(x, y)$ and $|X_{x, m} - Y_{m}| \leq D$, let $\mathfrak{E}_{x}: \Omega \rightarrow[0, \infty), x \in E$, satisfy for all $x \in E$ that

$
\mathfrak{E}_{x} = \frac{1}{M}\left[\sum_{m=1}^M |X_{x, m} - Y_{m}|^2\right], \quad (12.166)
$

and let $\underline{\mathfrak{E}}_{x} \in[0, \infty), x \in E$, satisfy for all $x \in E$ that $\underline{\mathfrak{E}}_{x} = \mathbb{E}[|X_{x, 1} - Y_{1}|^2]$. Then $\Omega \ni \omega \mapsto \sup_{x \in E}|\mathfrak{E}_{x}(\omega) - \underline{\mathfrak{E}}_{x}| \in [0, \infty]$ is $\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{B}([0, \infty))$-measurable and

$
\mathbb{P}(\sup_{x \in E}|\mathfrak{E}_{x} - \underline{\mathfrak{E}}_{x}| \ge \varepsilon) \le 2 \mathcal{C}^{(E, \delta), \frac{2 L D}{\varepsilon}} \exp\left(\frac{-\varepsilon^2 M}{2 D^4}\right) \quad (12.167)
$

(cf. Definition 4.3.2).

Proof of Lemma 12.3.8. Throughout this proof, let $\mathfrak{E}_{x, m}: \Omega \rightarrow[0, D^{2}], x \in E, m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, M\}$, satisfy for all $x \in E, m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, M\}$ that

$
\mathfrak{E}_{x, m} = |X_{x, m} - Y_{m}|^2. \quad (12.168)
$

Observe that the fact that for all $x_{1}, x_{2}, y \in \mathbb{R}$ it holds that $(x_{1} - y)^{2} - (x_{2} - y)^{2} = (x_{1} - x_{2})((x_{1} - y) + (x_{2} - y))$, the assumption that for all $x \in E, m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, M\}$ it holds that $|X_{x, m} - Y_{m}| \leq D$, and the assumption that for all $x, y \in E, m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, M\}$ it holds that $|X_{x, m} - X_{y, m}| \leq L \delta(x, y)$ imply that for all $x, y \in E, m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, M\}$ it holds that

$
\begin{aligned}
|\mathfrak{E}_{x, m} - \mathfrak{E}_{y, m}| &= \left|(X_{x, m} - Y_{m})^2 - (X_{y, m} - Y_{m})^2\right| \\
&= |X_{x, m} - X_{y, m}||(|X_{x, m} - Y_{m}| + |X_{y, m} - Y_{m}|) \\
&\le |X_{x, m} - X_{y, m}|(|X_{x, m} - Y_{m}| + |X_{y, m} - Y_{m}|) \\
&\le 2D|X_{x, m} - X_{y, m}| \le 2LD\delta(x, y). \quad (12.169)
\end{aligned}
$

In addition, note that (12.166) and the assumption that for all $x \in E$ it holds that $(X_{x, m}, Y_{m})$, $m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, M\}$, are i.i.d. random variables show that for all $x \in E$ it holds that

$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}[\mathfrak{E}_{x}] &= \frac{1}{M}\left[\sum_{m=1}^M \mathbb{E}[|X_{x, m} - Y_{m}|^2]\right] = \frac{1}{M}\left[\sum_{m=1}^M \mathbb{E}[|X_{x, 1} - Y_{1}|^2]\right] = \frac{1}{M}\left[\sum_{m=1}^M \underline{\mathfrak{E}}_{x}\right] = \underline{\mathfrak{E}}_{x}, \quad (12.170)
\end{aligned}
$

Furthermore, observe that the assumption that for all $x \in E$ it holds that $(X_{x, m}, Y_{m})$, $m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, M\}$, are i.i.d. random variables ensures that for all $x \in E$ it holds that $\mathfrak{E}_{x, m}$, $m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, M\}$, are i.i.d. random variables. Combining this, (12.169), and (12.170) with Lemma 12.3.7 (applied with $(E, \delta) \sim (E, \delta)$, $M \sim M$, $\varepsilon \sim \varepsilon$, $L \sim 2LD$, $D \sim D^{2}$, $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}) \sim (\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$, $(Y_{x, m})_{x \in E, m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, M\}} \sim (\mathfrak{E}_{x, m})_{x \in E, m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, M\}}$, $(Z_{x})_{x \in E} \sim (\mathfrak{E}_{x})_{x \in E}$ in the notation of Lemma 12.3.7) establishes (12.167). The proof of Lemma 12.3.8 is thus complete.

### Proposition 12.3.9

Let $d, \mathcal{M} \in \mathbb{N}$, $R, L, \mathcal{R}, \varepsilon \in(0, \infty)$, let $D \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{d}$ be a compact set, let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ be a probability space, let $X_{m}: \Omega \to D$, $m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathcal{M}\}$, and $Y_{m}: \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$, $m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathcal{M}\}$, be functions, assume that $(X_{m}, Y_{m})$, $m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathcal{M}\}$, are i.i.d. random variables, let $H = (H_{\theta})_{\theta \in [-R, R]^{d}}: [-R, R]^{d} \to C(D, \mathbb{R})$ satisfy for all $\theta, \vartheta \in [-R, R]^{d}$, $x \in D$ that $|H_{\theta}(x) - H_{\vartheta}(x)| \leq L \| \theta - \vartheta \|_{\infty}$, assume for all $\theta \in [-R, R]^{d}$, $m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathcal{M}\}$ that $|H_{\theta}(X_{m}) - Y_{m}| \leq \mathcal{R}$ and $\mathbb{E} | Y_{m} |^{2} < \infty$, let $\mathcal{E}: C(D, \mathbb{R}) \to [0, \infty)$ satisfy for all $f \in C(D, \mathbb{R})$ that $\mathcal{E}(f) = \mathbb{E} | f(X_{1}) - Y_{1} |^{2}$, and let $\mathfrak{E}: [-R, R]^{d} \times \Omega \to [0, \infty)$ satisfy for all $\theta \in [-R, R]^{d}$, $\omega \in \Omega$ that

$
\mathfrak{E}(\theta, \omega) = \frac{1}{\mathcal{M}} \left[ \sum_{m=1}^{\mathcal{M}} | H_{\theta}(X_{m}(\omega)) - Y_{m}(\omega) |^{2} \right].
$

(cf. Definition 3.9.4). Then $\Omega \ni \omega \mapsto \sup_{\theta \in [-R, R]^{d}} | \mathfrak{E}(\theta, \omega) - \mathcal{E}(H_{\theta}) | \in [0, \infty]$ is $\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{B}([0, \infty])$-measurable and

$
\mathbb{P}( \sup_{\theta \in [-R, R]^{d}} | \mathfrak{E}(\theta) - \mathcal{E}(H_{\theta}) | \ge \varepsilon ) \le 2 \max \left\{ 1, \left[ \frac{16 L \mathcal{R}^{2}}{\varepsilon} \right]^{d} \right\} \exp \left( \frac{-\varepsilon^{2} \mathcal{M}}{2 \mathcal{R}^{4}} \right).
$

**Proof of Proposition 12.3.9.** Throughout this proof, let $B \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{d}$ satisfy $B = [-R, R]^{d} = \{ \theta \in \mathbb{R}^{d}: \| \theta \|_{\infty} \leq R \}$ and let $\delta: B \times B \to [0, \infty)$ satisfy for all $\theta, \vartheta \in B$ that

$
\delta(\theta, \vartheta) = \| \theta - \vartheta \|_{\infty}.
$

Observe that the assumption that $(X_{m}, Y_{m})$, $m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathcal{M}\}$, are i.i.d. random variables and the assumption that for all $\theta \in [-R, R]^{d}$ it holds that $H_{\theta}$ is continuous imply that for all $\theta \in B$ it holds that $(H_{\theta}(X_{m}), Y_{m})$, $m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathcal{M}\}$, are i.i.d. random variables. Combining this, the assumption that for all $\theta, \vartheta \in B$, $x \in D$ it holds that $|H_{\theta}(x) - H_{\vartheta}(x)| \leq L \| \theta - \vartheta \|_{\infty}$, and the assumption that for all $\theta \in B$, $m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathcal{M}\}$ it holds that $|H_{\theta}(X_{m}) - Y_{m}| \leq \mathcal{R}$ with Lemma 12.3.8 (applied with $(E, \delta) \sim (B, \delta)$, $M \sim \mathcal{M}$, $\varepsilon \sim \varepsilon$, $L \sim L$, $D \sim \mathcal{R}$, $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}) \sim (\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$, $(X_{x, m})_{x \in E, m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathcal{M}\}} \sim (H_{\theta}(X_{m}))_{\theta \in B, m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathcal{M}\}}$, $(Y_{m})_{m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathcal{M}\}} \sim (Y_{m})_{m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \mathcal{M}\}}$, $(\mathfrak{E}_{x})_{x \in E} \sim (\mathcal{E}(H_{\theta}))_{\theta \in B}$ in the notation of Lemma 12.3.8) establishes that $\Omega \ni \omega \mapsto \sup_{\theta \in B} | \mathfrak{E}(\theta, \omega) - \mathcal{E}(H_{\theta}) | \in [0, \infty]$ is $\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{B}([0, \infty])$-measurable and

$
\mathbb{P}( \sup_{\theta \in B} | \mathfrak{E}(\theta) - \mathcal{E}(H_{\theta}) | \ge \varepsilon ) \le 2 \mathcal{C}^{(B, \delta), \frac{16 L \mathcal{R}^{2}}{\varepsilon}} \exp \left( \frac{-\varepsilon^{2} \mathcal{M}}{2 \mathcal{R}^{4}} \right).
$
