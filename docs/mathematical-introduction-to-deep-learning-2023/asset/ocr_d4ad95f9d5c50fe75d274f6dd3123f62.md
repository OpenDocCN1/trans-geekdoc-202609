This establishes item (ii). It thus remains to prove item (iii). For this observe that (5.138) and item (i) in Lemma 5.6.8 demonstrate that for all $\theta \in \mathbb{B}$ it holds that
$$0 \leq \frac{\xi}{2}\|\theta - v\|_2^2 \leq \mathscr{L}(\theta) - \mathscr{L}(v). \tag{5.157}$$
Combining this and item (ii) implies that for all $t \in [0, T]$ it holds that
$$0 \leq \frac{c}{2}\|\Theta_t - v\|_2^2 \leq \mathscr{L}(\Theta_t) - \mathscr{L}(v) \tag{5.158}$$
This establishes item (iii). The proof of Proposition 5.7.1 is thus complete.

# **5.7.2** Existence and uniqueness of solutions of ODEs

**Lemma 5.7.2** (Local existence of maximal solution of ODEs). Let $\varrho \in \mathbb{N}$, $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^\varrho$, $T \in (0, \infty)$, let $\|\|\cdot\|\|: \mathbb{R}^\varrho \to [0, \infty)$ be a norm, and let $\mathscr{G}: \mathbb{R}^\varrho \to \mathbb{R}^\varrho$ be locally Lipschitz continuous. Then there exist a unique real number $\tau \in (0, T]$ and a unique continuous function $\Theta: [0, \tau) \to \mathbb{R}^\varrho$ such that for all $t \in [0, \tau)$ it holds that
$$\liminf_{s \nearrow \tau}\Big[\|\Theta_s\|\| + \frac{1}{(T-s)}\Big] = \infty \quad\text{and}\quad \Theta_t = \xi + \int_0^t \mathscr{G}(\Theta_s)\,\mathrm{d}s. \tag{5.159}$$

*Proof of Lemma 5.7.2.* Note that, for example, Teschl [394, Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.16] implies (5.159) (cf., for instance, [5, Theorem 7.6] and [222, Theorem 1.1]). The proof of Lemma 5.7.2 is thus complete.

**Lemma 5.7.3** (Local existence of maximal solution of ODEs on an infinite time interval). Let $\varrho \in \mathbb{N}$, $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^\varrho$, let $\|\|\cdot\|\|: \mathbb{R}^\varrho \to [0, \infty)$ be a norm, and let $\mathscr{G}: \mathbb{R}^\varrho \to \mathbb{R}^\varrho$ be locally Lipschitz continuous. Then there exist a unique extended real number $\tau \in (0, \infty]$ and a unique continuous function $\Theta: [0, \tau) \to \mathbb{R}^\varrho$ such that for all $t \in [0, \tau)$ it holds that
$$\liminf_{s \nearrow \tau}\big[\|\|\Theta_s\|\| + s\big] = \infty \quad\text{and}\quad \Theta_t = \xi + \int_0^t \mathscr{G}(\Theta_s)\,\mathrm{d}s. \tag{5.160}$$

*Proof of Lemma 5.7.3.* First, observe that Lemma 5.7.2 implies that there exist unique real numbers $\tau_n \in (0, n]$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, and unique continuous functions $\Theta^{(n)}: [0, \tau_n) \to \mathbb{R}^\varrho$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, such that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $t \in [0, \tau_n)$ it holds that
$$\liminf_{s \nearrow \tau_n}\Big[\|\|\Theta^{(n)}_s\|\| + \frac{1}{(n-s)}\Big] = \infty \quad\text{and}\quad \Theta^{(n)}_t = \xi + \int_0^t \mathscr{G}(\Theta^{(n)}_s)\,\mathrm{d}s. \tag{5.161}$$
This shows that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $t \in [0, \min\{\tau_{n+1}, n\})$ it holds that
$$\liminf_{s \nearrow \tau_{n+1}}\Big[\|\|\Theta^{(n+1)}_s\|\| + \frac{1}{(n+1-s)}\Big] = \infty \quad\text{and}\quad \Theta^{(n+1)}_t = \xi + \int_0^t \mathscr{G}(\Theta^{(n+1)}_s)\,\mathrm{d}s. \tag{5.162}$$
