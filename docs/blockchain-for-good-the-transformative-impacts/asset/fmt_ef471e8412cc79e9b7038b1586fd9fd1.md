## 1. Introduction

Planetary boundaries define the levels at which humans and other species can safely operate without risking major global human-induced environmental change ([Rockström et al., 2009](#ch11.xhtml#ref11_57); [Richardson et al., 2023](#ch11.xhtml#ref11_55)). Most planetary boundaries are reached by the deposit of waste back into the environment ([Richardson and Bai, 2023](#ch11.xhtml#ref11_55)): climate change, novel entities (i.e. anthropogenic introductions such as synthetic chemicals and substances, genetically modified organisms, and anthropogenically mobilised radioactive materials), stratospheric ozone depletion, atmospheric aerosol loading, ocean acidification, and biochemical flows ([Rockström et al., 2009](#ch11.xhtml#ref11_57); [Richardson et al., 2023](#ch11.xhtml#ref11_55)).[^(26)](#fn26)
