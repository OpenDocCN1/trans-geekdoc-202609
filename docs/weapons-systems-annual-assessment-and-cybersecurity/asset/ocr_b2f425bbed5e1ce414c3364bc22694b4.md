In the past, consideration of cybersecurity was not a focus of the key processes governing the development of weapon systems. It was not a focus of key acquisition and requirements policies nor was it a focus of key documents that inform decision-making. For example, until a few years ago, DOD’s main requirements policy did not call for programs to factor cyber survivability into their key performance parameters.30 Key performance parameters are the most important system capabilities, called “requirements,” that must be met when developing weapon systems. They are established early on in an acquisition program and drive system design decisions. They are also used as a benchmark to measure program performance and are reviewed during acquisition decisions and other oversight processes. Because cybersecurity key performance parameters were not required, Joint Staff officials and some program officials said that many current weapon systems had no high-level cybersecurity requirements when they began, which in turn limited emphasis on cybersecurity during weapon system design, development, and oversight.31 In addition, Joint Staff officials said that, historically, cybersecurity was not a factor in analyses of alternatives.
