For nodes, twelve elements available in the periodic table were considered, but only combination of four features (group, period, metal number, and ionization potential) was used to predict the adsorption energy of 5209 values (Fig. 2C). The addition of extra features caused undeniably decreased the mean absolute error. In Fig. 2 and Table 5, the ML test of each element (O, OH, HH, and Co) is shown with a MAE ranging from 0.06 to 0.14 eV, probably due to the narrower range of adsorption energies. The MAE of the OH adsorption was achieved at 0.29 eV at a low level, due to the smallest size of the dataset. The total MAE was 0.16 eV after 5-fold cross-validation, significantly better than that in our previous report due to the increased dataset size [37]. No overfitting was observed during ML training, as shown in the first in of Fig. 2E.
