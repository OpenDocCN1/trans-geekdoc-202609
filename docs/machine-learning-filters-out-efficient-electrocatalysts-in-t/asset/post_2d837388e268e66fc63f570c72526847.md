# ABSTRACT

Despite their potential, multi-component materials have not been actively considered as catalysts due to the massive compositional complexity. However, targeting electrocatalysts for fuel cells, we present a machine learning (ML)-driven catalyst screening protocol to identify structures of structural stability, selectivity, performance, and cost-effectiveness. We tested 17 candidates out of 30 elements through three test materials in the alloy coreshell{X(Y)Z}Fe for each cathode and anode of fuel cells. The candidates are potentially stable, lower-cost and higher-performance than a Pt film. CuAuPt, one of the final candidates, outperformed a Pt film by an approximately 2-fold increase in current density with the 2.7-fold reduction in Pt usage. This demonstration demonstrates that our ML-driven strategy can be highly useful for exploring multicomponent systems and catalysis design.

# 1. Introduction

Platinum (Pt), a highly scarce and expensive element, is used as an indispensable ingredient in fuel cells. For the last decade, extensive efforts have been made to develop less expensive catalysts that maintain catalytic activity comparable to that of [1–4]; however, the others are either poor in Pt or merely a Pt-based material [5–9].

For the cathode where oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) occurs, Pt(with Pt skin) is the state of the art; on the anode where hydrogen reaction (HOR) occurs, Ni is used due to its high activity to avoid the severe CO issues but is not durable [10]. It is difficult to find novel and lower-cost materials for both the cathode and anode without sacrificing the performance.
