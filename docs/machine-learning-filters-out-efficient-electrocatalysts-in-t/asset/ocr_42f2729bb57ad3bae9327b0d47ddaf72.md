# Results and discussion

# 3. Adsorption energy as a descriptor for the fuel cell performance

The first step is to identify the descriptors for ORR and HER performance. For the ORR, where O2 molecules are reduced to H2O via a four-electron pathway (Fig. 1A), the rate determining step (RDS) is the largest uphill step from O to H2O at the electrochemical potential (1.23 V vs RHE). ORR over-low scaling relations suggest that one of the following mechanisms is limiting: (1 + +E) - O* (O2* - OH*) or (1.23) - OH* (H2O - OH*) or (1.45) - OH* (H2O - OH*) (Fig. 1B).

On the other hand, the HER is a simpler reaction (H2+) involving a two-electron pathway with intermediate steps (H*). H2 molecules are adsorbed on the surface of the metal, and the H2 adsorbed on the catalytic surfaces dissociate into hydrogen atoms (H*), finally recombining to evolve as H2. Here, a potential difference much smaller than 0.24 eV (at Pt(111)) is desired. Therefore, CO poisoning on the catalytic surfaces is not a problem for the anode; however, CO poisoning is the bottleneck [56,57]. Therefore, the promising zone for the HER is defined by Fig. 1D with diagrams of ΔG_H of 0.24 eV and -1.4 eV (both values corresponding to pure Pt).
