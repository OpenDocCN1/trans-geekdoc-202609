## 3.5. Experimental setup of novel ORR catalysts

The promising catalysts suggested from the above design protocol were needed to be experimentally verified. Since the sluggish ORR kinetics at the cathode are the key bottleneck for fuel cell performance, we performed electrochemical measurements for each material. Specifically, CuAu@Pt films showed higher activity (0.72 V) than pure Pt (0.56 V) but also exhibited improved effects (Fig. 4). In addition, previous experiments reported better CuAu-based alloys, showing the potential versatility of the CuAu-shell system.

In fact, ORR activity was higher than that of nanoparticles (NP) to capture the catalytic performance between pure Pt and our CuAu@Pt thin film. For core-shell synthesis by wet chemical methods, it is difficult to control the size and shape of the cores. By controlling experimental parameters (e.g., varying ratios between catalyst, ion, and solvents), we fabricated CuAu@Pt thin films using direct-current magnetron sputtering (DC-MS) and showed that catalytic properties can be enhanced by the CuAu thin film (alloy core part).

The 3:1 stoichiometric CuAu alloy was obtained by sputtering on a 3-inch substrate by increasing the number of Au atoms, the thickness of the CuAu thin film increased from 7.7 nm to 29.4 nm (Fig. S2). Using energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and transmission electron microscopy (TEM), we confirmed that the CuAu alloy film (thickness 80 shots) was uniform.

The next step is deposition of Pt thin film on CuAu surfaces using DC-MS (Fig. S3). DC-MS is a type of physical vapor deposition (PVD) which is directly evaporated with high-energy plasma in a vacuum. Unlike pulsed-based PVD methods, the advantage can be found by tuning the pulse width with a short duration (<16 ms). The deposition process can achieve accurate results as Pt thickness increases (Fig. S10). As such, for less thickness, the Pt film was only partially covered (non-monolayer). For the case of 80 shots, the Pt thickness was approximately 1.6 nm, and the Pt distribution was uniform for others 120 and 200 shots.

## 3.6. ORR performance of CuAu@Pt

As shown in Fig. 4, the ORR characteristics of CuAu@Pt films with various shell thicknesses on Ni electrode through the liquid half-cell. Test samples included CuAu@Pt films with varying shell thicknesses (2.4 nm to 4.4 nm). The shell with thickness identical to the Pt film (4.4 nm) showed improved effects. We can finally compare the core-shell effects of CuAu versus pure Pt thin film in an electrochemical test. For electrochemical measurements, we performed tests in an alkaline electrolyte (0.1 M KOH) and Tafel plots (Fig. S8) show the kinetic current density (J) as shown in Fig. 4 g, b, and c. The ORR activity of CuAu@Pt film with 1.6 nm Pt shell (60 shots) was higher than the other cases. In terms of 2.4 nm, 2.5 nm (200 shots) and 4.4 nm (240 shots) Pt shells, the shells were significantly less effective. The core-shell system performed better.

The kinetic current density was calculated at 0.85 V/cm², which is 1.7 times greater than that of the pure Pt film (0.67 V/cm²) (Fig. S9). This comparison shows that CuAu and Pt shell have significant ORR activity, consistent with our DFT and ML results. For the case of the non-monolayer (50 shots) Pt shells, the film surface was not fully covered as shown in SEM. The substrate was not fully covered (1.09 mA/cm²) which was less than for 1.6 nm case (surface Pt film).

For the 2.5 nm and 4.4 nm Pt shells, the shells were significantly thicker, and the ORR activity decreased. For the 1.6 nm case, the Pt loading was 3.4 μg/cm², which is about 2 times larger than 2.4 nm and 1.8 times smaller than the loading of 9.4 nm (5.2 μg/cm²). The CuAu@Pt 1.6 nm thick Pt film exhibited the kinetic density twice times higher than films with much thicker Pt shells (4.4 nm and 29.4 nm). In important factors in practice, we evaluated the electrochemical stability of CuAu@Pt thin film in the alkaline electrolyte for 5000 seconds.
