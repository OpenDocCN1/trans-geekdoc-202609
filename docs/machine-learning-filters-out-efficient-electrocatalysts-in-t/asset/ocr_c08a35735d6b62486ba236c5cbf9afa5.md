For the element (Z), we selected the electrochemically stable elements based on the Pourbaix diagrams that did not dissolve under various conditions such as OR and Au, Ir, Pd, Ag, Rh, Ba, Tc, Ce and W for the HOR. On the other hand, for the core (Z), we estimated its thermodynamic stability (Ef < 0.1 eV). We used the predicted GCNN model (MAE of only 0.04 eV) to predict the formation energies of these alloys. Note that the threshold was 0.1 eV for any promising candidate materials.

The second criterion was the mechanical stability between the core shell, which was verified by its lattice mismatch [58]. The cases with lattice parameter mismatch than 5 % were not selected at this stage. The last criterion is the catalytic performance, and the predicted GCNN model was used. For the OR, materials with U (pure Pd) were passed. For the HOR, materials with $|\Delta G_{HOR}|<0.24$ eV and $\Delta G_{HOR}>-1.4$ eV (the values of pure Pd) passed. In addition we excluded materials that received electroactive elements (alkali metals and alkaline earth metals) such as Li, Na, Mg, K, Ca, and trace elements (Hg, Ti, Pb), and the very expensive element Re. These filtering processes resulted in only 55 and 94 promising candidates for the OR and HOR, respectively, which are potentially stable and may outperform each other experimentally.

# 3.4. DFT of filtered-out candidates

The GCNN and screening models involve some errors in their predictions for materials (not part of our training dataset), and DFT-verifications are expected to reduce these errors. First, for the OR, the main checkpoint of DFT verification is whether or not U, (limiting potential) is greater than 0.56 V (the value of pure Pd) passed.
