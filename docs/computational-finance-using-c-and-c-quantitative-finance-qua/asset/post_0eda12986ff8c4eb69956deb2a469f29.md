## Appendix F: Black–Scholes finite-difference schemes

If the eigenvalues of $T_l$ are $\lambda_k, k = 1, \dots, n_s - 2$, the eigenvalues of $T_l^{-1}$ are $\lambda_k^{-1}, k = 1, \dots, n_s - 2$. This means that the system is stable if all the eigenvalues of $T_l$ have a modulus greater than one. This result can be proved by considering the eigenvalues with the smallest modulus, $\lambda_{\min}$. If $|\lambda_{\min}| > 1$ then the result is proved.

Now the eigenvalues of $T_l$, see Smith (1985), are given by:

$$\lambda_k = 1 + \Delta t\left(r + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right) + 2\sqrt{AC} \cos\left(\frac{k\pi}{n_s - 2 + 1}\right) \quad (F.2.8)$$
$$k = 1, \dots, n_s - 2,$$

where the term

$$2\sqrt{AC} = \sqrt{\frac{\Delta t^2(\sigma^4 - b^2\Delta Z^2)}{\Delta Z^4}} \quad (F.2.9)$$

It can be seen that if $b^2\Delta Z^2 > \sigma^4$ then eigenvalues are complex and if $\sigma^4 > b^2\Delta Z^2$ then eigenvalues are real. We will consider each of these cases in turn.

### Complex eigenvalues: $b^2\Delta Z^2 > \sigma^4$

We represent the $k$th complex eigenvalue as:

$$\lambda_k = R + iY$$

where the real part is

$$R = 1 + \Delta t\left(r + \frac{\sigma^2}{\Delta Z^2}\right)$$

and the imaginary part is

$$Y = 2\sqrt{AC} \cos\left(\frac{k\pi}{n_s - 2 + 1}\right)$$

Since

$$|\lambda_k| > |R| + |Y| \quad \text{and} \quad |R| > 1,$$

we conclude that

$$|\lambda_{\min}| > 1$$

### Real eigenvalues: $\sigma^4 \geqslant b^2\Delta Z^2$

In this case the $k$th eigenvalue is real, and from Eq. (F.2.8) we have:

$$\lambda_k = 1 + \Delta t\left(r + \frac{\sigma^2}{\Delta Z^2}\right) - 2\sqrt{AC}$$

Since $b^2\Delta Z^2 > 0$ from Eq. (F.2.9) we have

$$2\sqrt{AC} < \sqrt{\frac{\sigma^4 \Delta t^2}{\Delta Z^4}}$$

or

$$|2\sqrt{AC}| < \frac{\sigma^2 \Delta t}{\Delta Z^2}$$

So

$$\lambda_{\min} > 1 + \Delta t \left(r + \frac{\sigma^2}{\Delta Z^2}\right) - \frac{\sigma^2 \Delta t}{\Delta Z^2}$$

Therefore we have

$$|\lambda_{\min}| > 1 + r\Delta t$$

and, since $r > 0$, we have:

$$|\lambda_{\min}| > 1$$

## Appendix G: The Brownian bridge: alternative derivation

We provide an alternative derivation of the Brownian bridge equation given in Chapter 2.
Let a Brownian process have values $W_{t_0}$ at time $t_0$ and $W_{t_1}$ at time $t_1$. We want to find the conditional distribution of $W_t$, where $t_0 < t < t_1$. This distribution will be denoted by $P(W_t|W_{t_0}, W_{t_1})$, to indicate that $W_t$ is conditional on the end values $W_{t_0}$ and $W_{t_1}$.
We have:

$$P(W_t|W_{t_0}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t-t_0)}} \exp\left{\frac{(W_t - W_{t_0})^2}{2(t-t_0)}\right\}$$

The joint distribution of $W_t$ and $W_{t_1}$ given $W_{t_0}$ is:

$$P(\{W_t, W_{t_1}\})P(W_{t_1}|W_{t_0}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t-t_0)(t_1-t_0)}} \exp\left{\frac{(W_t - W_{t_0})^2}{2(t-t_0)}, \frac{(W_{t_1} - W_{t_0})^2}{2(t_1-t_0)}\right\} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t-t_0)(t_1-t)}} \exp\left{\frac{1}{2}\frac{(W_t - W_{t_0})^2}{2(t-t_0)} + \frac{(W_{t_1} - W_{t_0})^2}{2(t_1-t)}\right\}$$

Similarly

$$P(W_{t_1}|W_{t_0}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t_1-t_0)}} \exp\left{\frac{(W_{t_1}-W_{t_0})^2}{2(t_1-t_0)}\right\}$$

Now we have:

$$P(W_t|W_{t_0}, W_{t_1}) = \frac{P(\{W_t, W_{t_1}\}|W_{t_0})}{P(W_{t_1}|W_{t_0})} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\frac{t_1-t}{t-t_0}}} \times \exp\left{\frac{1}{2}\frac{(W_t-W_{t_0})^2}{2(t-t_0)} - \frac{(W_{t_1}-W_{t_0})^2}{2(t_1-t)}\right\}$$

For ease of reference we will write the above equation as:

$$P(W_t|W_{t_0}, W_{t_1}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\frac{t_1-t}{t-t_0}}} \exp\{A\}$$

We now consider the terms in exponent.

$$A = \frac{1}{2} \frac{(W_t - W_{t_0})^2}{2(t-t_0)} - \frac{(W_{t_1} - W_{t_0})^2}{2(t_1-t)}$$

Dividing top and bottom of the above expression for $A$ by $(t_1 - t_0)$ we obtain:

$$A = \frac{1}{2V} \left( (W_t^2 + W_{t_0}^2 - 2W_tW_{t_0}) \frac{t_1-t}{t-t_0} \dots \right)$$

where $V = \frac{(t-t_0)(t_1-t)}{t_1-t_0}$

$$A = \frac{1}{2V} \left( W_t^2 \frac{t_1-t}{t-t_0} + W_{t_1}^2 \frac{t}{t_1} \dots (W_{t_1}^2 + W_{t_0}^2 - 2W_{t_1}W_t) \frac{t-t_0}{(t_1-t)^2} \right)$$

We show that $A$ can be expressed as quadratic form:

$$B = \frac{1}{2V}(W_t - \mu)^2 = \frac{1}{2V}(W_t^2 + \mu^2 - 2\mu W_t)$$

where

$$V = \frac{(t-t_0)(t_1-t)}{t_1-t_0} \quad \text{and} \quad \mu = W_{t_0} \frac{t_1-t}{t_1-t_0} + W_{t_1} \frac{t-t_0}{t_1-t_0}$$

Therefore we have:

$$B = \frac{1}{2V} \left( W_t^2 + W_{t_0}^2 \frac{t_1-t}{t_1-t_0} + W_{t_1}^2 \frac{t-t_0}{t_1-t_0} - 2W_tW_{t_0} \frac{t_1-t}{t_1-t_0} - 2W_tW_{t_1} \frac{t-t_0}{t_1-t_0} \right)$$

Expanding and gathering terms we obtain:

$$B = -\frac{1}{2V} \left( W_t^2 + W_{t_0}^2 \frac{(t_1-t)^2}{(t_1-t_0)^2} + W_{t_1}^2 \frac{(t-t_0)^2}{(t_1-t_0)^2} \right) + 2W_t W_{t_1} \frac{(t_1-t)(t-t_0)}{(t_1-t_0)^2} - 2W_t W_{t_0} \frac{t_1-t}{t_1-t_0} - 2W_t W_{t_1} \frac{t-t_0}{t_1-t_0}$$

Comparing coefficients of $A$ and $B$ we have:

### Coefficients for $W_{t}^2$

$$A: -\frac{1}{2V} \left( \frac{t_1-t}{t_1-t_0} \right), \quad B: -\frac{1}{2V}$$

### Coefficients for $W_{t_0}^2$

$$A: -\frac{1}{2V} \left( \frac{(t_1-t)^2}{(t_1-t_0)^2} \right), \quad B: -\frac{1}{2V}$$

### Coefficients for $W_{t_1}^2$

$$A: -\frac{1}{2V} \left( \frac{(t-t_0)^2}{(t_1-t_0)^2} \right), \quad B: -\frac{1}{2V}$$

The remaining coefficients in $A$ and $B$ for $W_t W_{t_1}$, $W_t W_{t_0}$ and $W_{t_0} W_t$ are identical.

We have thus shown that:

$$P(W_t|W_{t_0}, W_{t_1}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \sqrt{\frac{t_1-t_0}{(t-t_0)(t_1-t)}} \exp\left\{-\frac{(W_t-\mu)^2}{2V}\right\}$$

Thus the conditional distribution of $W_t$ is a Gaussian with mean

$$\mu = W_{t_0} \frac{t_1-t}{t_1-t_0} + W_{t_1} \frac{t-t_0}{t_1-t_0}$$

and variance

$$V = \frac{(t-t_0)(t_1-t)}{t_1-t_0}$$

and we can obtain a variable $\widetilde{W}_t$ from this distribution by:

$$\widetilde{W}_t = W_{t_0} \frac{t_1-t}{t_1-t_0} + W_{t_1} \frac{t-t_0}{t_1-t_0} + \sqrt{\frac{(t-t_0)(t_1-t)}{t_1-t_0}}Z, \quad \text{where } Z \sim N(0,1)$$

## Appendix H: Brownian motion: more results

### H.1 Some results concerning Brownian motion

Here we will prove some facts concerning Brownian motion If the Brownian motion has zero drift then:

$$dX_t = \sigma \sqrt{dt} dZ_t, \quad dZ_t \sim N(0,1) \quad (H.1.1)$$

and

$$P(m_t^x \le b, X_t \ge x) = N\left(\frac{2b-x}{\sqrt{t}}\right) \quad (H.1.2)$$

where $m_t^X$ denotes the minimum value of $X_t$ over the time interval $[0, t]$, $b \le 0$, and $x \ge 0$.

When the Brownian motion has nonzero drift

$$d\bar{X}_t = v dt + \sigma \sqrt{dt} dZ_t, \quad d\bar{Z}_t \sim N(0,1) \quad (H.1.3)$$

and the following equations are satisfied by $m_t^{\bar{X}}$ and $\bar{X}_t:$

$$P(\bar{X}_t \le K) = N\left(\frac{K-vt}{\sqrt{t}}\right) \quad (H.1.4)$$

$$P(\bar{X}_t \ge K) = N\left(\frac{vt-K}{\sqrt{t}}\right) \quad (H.1.5)$$

$$P(m_t^{\bar{X}} \le b, \bar{X}_t \ge x) = \exp\left(\frac{2vb}{\sigma^2}\right)N\left(\frac{2b-x+vt}{\sqrt{t}}\right) \quad (H.1.6)$$

$$P(m_t^{\bar{X}} \ge b, \bar{X}_t \ge x) = N\left(\frac{vt-x}{\sqrt{t}}\right) - \exp\left(\frac{2vb}{\sigma^2}\right)N\left(\frac{2b-x+vt}{\sqrt{t}}\right) \quad (H.1.7)$$

$$P(m_t^{\bar{X}} \le b) = N\left(\frac{b-vt}{\sqrt{t}}\right) + \exp\left(\frac{2vb}{\sigma^2}\right)N\left(\frac{b+vt}{\sqrt{t}}\right) \quad (H.1.8)$$

$$P(m_t^{\bar{X}} \ge b) = N\left(\frac{vt-b}{\sqrt{t}}\right) - \exp\left(\frac{2vb}{\sigma^2}\right)N\left(\frac{b+vt}{\sqrt{t}}\right) \quad (H.1.9)$$

where $K$ is a constant, $\bar{X} \ge 0$, $b \le 0$, and **P(condition)** denotes the probability associated with the appropriate condition, i.e., $\bar{X}_t \ge K, m_t^{\bar{X}} \ge b, \bar{X}_t \ge x$, etc.
