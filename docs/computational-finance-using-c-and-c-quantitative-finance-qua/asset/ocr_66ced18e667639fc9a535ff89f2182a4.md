# 7.5.2 Black–Scholes quantos

The Black–Scholes equation can also be used to price equity quanto options (Reiner (1992)). We have the following processes:

```
$dS^f = \mu_s X_s^f dt + \sigma_s X_s^f dW_s^P$
$dX_s^f = \mu_x X_s^f dt + \sigma_x X_s^f dW_x^P$
$dB^f = r^f B^f dt$
$dB^d = r^d B^d dt$ (7.5.10)
```

$S$ is the price (in foreign currency) of the foreign stock. $B^d$ is the domestic money market account where money grows at the (constant) risk free rate $r^d$. $B^f$ is the foreign money account where money grows at the (constant) risk free interest rate $r^f$. $X_s^f$ is the foreign exchange rate, that is the value of one unit of foreign currency in units of domestic currency.

The tradables for the domestic investor are the foreign money market account priced in domestic currency units (that is, $X_s^f B^f$) and the foreign stock priced in domestic currency units, $X_s^f B^f$.

We know from Eq. (4.4.30) that there is a probability measure (the risk neutral measure) $\mathbb{Q}$ under which the relative price of tradables such as equities are martingales. We also established that under $\mathbb{Q}$ the process followed by these tradables is GBM with constant drift $r^d$. So the process for the domestic equity $S^d$ is:

```
$dS^d = S^d r^d dt + \sigma S^d dW^{\mathbb{Q}}$ (7.5.11)
```
