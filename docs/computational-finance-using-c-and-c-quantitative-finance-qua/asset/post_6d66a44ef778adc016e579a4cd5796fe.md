
## Other financial derivatives

### Quanto swaplet

A quanto (also known as diff or differential) swaplet is an agreement in which one party makes floating rate payments on the foreign forward rate while the other makes fixed or floating payments based on domestic interest rates.

Here we consider quanto swaplets in which the received floating leg coupons (in domestic currency) are computed using foreign forward rates.

The value of a standard swaplet (in which all the coupons are domestic) can be found using Eqs. (7.20) and (7.274) to write:

Caplet$(t_0)$ - Floorlet$(t_0)$

$= P\tau D^F(t_0, t_k) E^Q[max(F_d^F - K, 0) - max(K - F_d^F, 0)]$

$= P\tau D^F(t_0, t_k) E^Q[F_d^F - K]$

$= P\tau D^F(t_0, t_k) \{E^Q[F_d^F] - K\}$

$= P\tau D^F(t_0, t_k) \{F^F(t_0, t_{k-1}, t_k) - K\}$ (7.2.94)

From Eq. (7.294) we can see that Quantolet$(t_0)$ - Floorlet$(t_0)$ is the value of a swaplet in which the owner pays the fixed rate K and receives the floating rate $F_d^F(t_0, t_{k-1}, t_k)$.

The value of the floating leg payment can be found by setting $K = 0$ in Eq. (7.294), and is $P\tau D^F(t_0, t_k) F_d^F(t_0, t_{k-1}, t_k)$.

The value of the floating legs in a quanto swap can be found in a similar manner:

Quantolet$(t_0)$ - Floorlet$(t_0)$

$= P\tau D^F(t_0, t_k) E^Q[max(F_f^F - K, 0) - max(K - F_f^F, 0)]$

Substituting from Eqs. (7.279) and (7.293):

Quantolet$(t_0)$ - Floorlet$(t_0)$

$= P\tau D^F(t_0, t_k) \{F^F(t_0, t_{k-1}, t_k) exp(\alpha(t_{k-1} - t_0))N_1(d_1) - K N_1(d_2)$

$- F^F(t_0, t_{k-1}, t_k)N_1(-d_1) + K N_1(-d_2)\}$

When $K=0$ we have $d_1 = \infty, N_1(d_1) = 1$ and $N_1(-d_1) = 0$, which means that the value of the floating leg payment is:

Floatlet$(t_0)$

$= P\tau D^F(t_0, t_k) F^F(t_0, t_{k-1}, t_k) exp(\alpha(t_{k-1} - t_0))$ (7.2.95)

where as before $\alpha = -\sigma_f \rho_{s,f}$.

Using Eq. (7.295) the value of a quanto swap with (pay) fixed domestic rate and (receive) foreign rate is:

$Qswaplet$(t_0) = P\tau D^F(t_0, t_k) \{F^F(t_0, t_{k-1}, t_k) exp(\alpha(t_{k-1} - t_0)) - K\}$

## 7.3 Foreign exchange derivatives

We consider derivatives based on the exchange rate between a domestic currency and a foreign currency. We will use the convention that quantities relating to the domestic currency will have the superscript $d$, while for the foreign currency will have the superscript $f$. The notation for the various exchange rates is as follows:

- $X_d^f(t)$ is the spot value of one unit of foreign currency in domestic currency at time $t$
- $X_b^f(t)$ is the spot value of one unit of foreign currency in base currency at time $t$
- $X_b^d(t)$ is the spot value of one unit of domestic currency in base currency at time $t$
- $X_d^f(t, T)$ is the (time $t$) forward value of one unit of foreign currency in domestic currency at time $T$.

### Covered interest arbitrage

If the current spot exchange rate $X_d^f(t)$, is known then using covered interest arbitrage it is possible to obtain a value for the future spot exchange rate—we denote this forward exchange rate by $X_d^f(t, T)$, where $T > t$.
Let us consider the following two scenarios:

### Scenario A

At time $t$ an investor deposits one unit of foreign currency which grows at the (constant) foreign free interest rate $r^f$. By time $T$ the initial amount will have increased $1/DF^f(t, T)$ units of foreign currency, where $DF^f(t, T) = \exp(-r^f(T-t))$. The foreign currency is converted into domestic currency at the time $T$ forward exchange rate $X_d^f(t, T)$, and thus yields $X_d^f(t, T)/DF^f(t, T)$ units of domestic currency.

### Scenario B

At time $t$ an investor deposits $X_d^f(t)$ units of domestic currency (the sum is equivalent to one unit of foreign currency), which grows at the (constant) domestic interest rate $r^d$. At time $T$ the initial sum will have increased to $X_d^f(t)/DF^d(t, T)$ units of domestic currency, where $DF^d(t, T) = \exp(-r^d(T-t))$. For no arbitrage to occur the final amount of domestic currency in both scenarios must be the same—we assumed there is no charge in converting one currency into another. We have thus:

$\frac{X_d^f(t)}{DF^f(t, T)} = \frac{X_d^f(T)}{DF^d(t, T)}$

which means that the forward exchange rate, at time $T$ is

$X_d^f(t, T) = X_d^f(t) \frac{DF^f(t, T)}{DF^d(t, T)}$ (7.3.1)

### 7.3.1 FX forward

An FX forward is a contract to exchange an amount of domestic currency for an agreed amount of foreign currency at a future time $T$. If $P^f$ is the amount (number of units) of foreign currency, and $P^d$ is the amount (number of units) of domestic currency, then the value (in domestic currency) of the FX contract at time $T$ is:

$FX_d(T) = P^f X_d^f(T) - P^d$

The value of the contract at time $t$ is:

$FX_d(t) = \{P^f X_d^f(t) - P^d\}DF^d(t, T)$

where $t < T$. Substituting for $X_d(t, T)$ from Eq. (7.3.1) gives:

$FX_d(t) = \left\{P^f X_d^f(t) \frac{DF^f(t, T)}{DF^d(t, T)} - P^d\right\}DF^d(t, T)$

which can be re-expressed

$FX_b(t) = P^f X_d^f(t)DF^f(t, T) - P^d DF^d(t)$ (7.3.2)

The value of this FX forward contract in base currency is:

$FX_b(t) = \{P^f X_d^f(t)DF^f(t, T) - P^d DF^d(t, T)\}X_b^d$

That is,

$FX_b(t) = P^f X_d^f(t)DF^f(t, T) - P^d DF^d(t, T)X_b^d$ (7.3.3)

where we used the fact that $X_d^f(t) = X_d^b(t)$.

An alternative way of expressing Eq. (7.3.3) is as:

$FX_b(t) = P^f \{X_d^f(t)DF^f(t, T) - KDF^d(t, T)X_d^b(t)\}$ (7.3.4)

where $K = P^d/P^f$. In the next section we will see that $K$, the agreed rate for one unit of foreign currency in units of domestic currency, corresponds to the strike of an FX call option.

### 7.3.2 European FX option

Foreign exchange options can be priced using the Black–Scholes formula (Garman and Kohlhagen, 1983). There are three processes involved in foreign exchange options and, in the real-world probability measure $\mathbb{P}$, they are:

$dB^f = r^f dB^f$
$dX^f = X_d^f \mu dt + X_d^f \sigma dW^P, \quad dW^P \sim N(0, dt)$
$dB^d = r^d dB^d$ (7.3.5)

Where $X_d^f$ is the value of one unit of foreign currency in domestic currency, where money grows at the (constant) risk free rate $r^d$, $B^f$ is the foreign money account where money grows at the (constant) risk free rate $r^f$, and $\sigma$ is the volatility of $X_d^f$. From a domestic point of view there are only two assets—the money market account $B^d$, and the value of the foreign market account in domestic currency, $B^f X_d^f$.

From Ito's rule in Chapter 2, using Eq. (2.6.3) with $X_1 = X_d^f$ and $X_2 = B^d$, we have:

$d(X_d^f/B^d) = X_d^f(r^f + \mu)dt + X_d^f \sigma dW^P$

We now consider $B^d$ as the numaire and obtain the process for $P^f X_d^f/B^d$ using Ito rule given in Chapter 2. Substituting $X_1 = P^f X_d^f$ and $X_2 = B^d$ in Eq. (2.6.8) we obtain:

$d(X_d^f/B^d) = \frac{P^f X_d^f(t)}{B^d}[(r^f + \mu)dt] + \frac{P^f X_d^f(t)}{B^d} \sigma dW^P$

If we choose probability measure $Q$ such that:

$dW^P = dW^Q - \frac{r^f + \mu}{\sigma}dt$ (7.3.6)

then $(B^f X_d^f/B^d)$ is a martingale since

$d(\frac{B^f X_d^f}{B^d}) = \frac{B^f X_d^f}{B^d} \sigma dW^Q$

Substituting for $dW^P$ in Eq. (7.3.5) yields:

$dX_d^f = X_d^f \mu dt + X_d^f \sigma \{dW^Q - \frac{r^f + \mu}{\sigma}dt\}$
$dX_d^f = X_d^f(r^f)dt + X_d^f \sigma dW^Q$ (7.3.7)

It can be seen that Eq. (7.3.7) is identical to Eq. (4.53) using the following mapping:

$S \rightarrow X_d^f, \quad r \rightarrow r^d, \quad q \rightarrow r^f$ (7.3.8)

This means that the above mapping allows us to price European puts and calls with the Black-Scholes formulae given in Eqs. (4.55) and (4.56).

### FX call

The time $t$ value (in domestic currency) of an FX call to buy one unit of foreign currency can be found from Eq. (4.55) and the substitutions given in (7.3.8). We have:

$d_1 = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{T-t}}\left\{log(\frac{X_d^f(t)}{K}\right) + (r^d - r^f)(T-t) + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2(T-t)\right\}$ (7.3.10)
$d_2 = d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{T-t}$ (7.3.11)

Where $K$ is the strike rate that has been agreed to pay for one unit of foreign currency in units of domestic currency, and $\sigma$ is the implied foreign/domestic currency exchange rate volatility, which may depend on effects such as time to maturity, volatility, and scale.

In practice the following, modified version of Eq. (7.3.9) is usually used:

$C_d(t) = P^f\{X_d^f(t)\exp(-r^f(T-t))N(d_1) - K\exp(-r^d(T-t))N(d_2)\}$ (7.3.12)

Where $P^f$ is the number of units of foreign currency and all other symbols have their previous meanings.
The value $C_b(t)$ of the call option in base currency can be found by using $C_b(t) = X_b^f C_d(t)$. From Eq. (7.3.12) we have:

$C_b(t) = P^f\{X_b^f(t)N(d_1)DF^f(t, T) - KX_b^f(t)N(d_2)DF^d(t, T)\}$ (7.3.13)

where we used the fact that $X_d^f(t) = X_b^f(t), DF^f(t, T) = \exp(-r^f(T-t))$ and $DF^d(t, T) = \exp(-r^d(T-t))$. We can also re-express the values for $d_1$ in Eq. (7.3.10) as:

$d_1 = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{T-t}}\left\{log\left(\frac{X_b^f(t)}{KX_d^f(t)}\right) +log\left(\frac{DF^f(t, T)}{DF^d(t, T)}\right) + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2(T-t)\right\}$ (7.3.14)

where we used the fact that

$log(DF^f(t, T)) = -r^f(T-t)$,

and

$log\left(\frac{X_d^f(t)}{K}\right) =log\left(\frac{X_b^f(t)X_d^f(t)}{KX_d^f(t)}\right) =log\left(\frac{X_b^f(t)}{KX_d^f(t)}\right)$
