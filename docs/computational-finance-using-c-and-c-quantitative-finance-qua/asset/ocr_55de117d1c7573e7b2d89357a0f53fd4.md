# 1 Overview of financial derivatives

A financial derivative is a contract between two counterparties (here referred to as A and B) which derives its value from the state of underlying financial quantities. We can further derivatives into those that carry a future obligation and those that don't. In the financial world a derivative which gives the owner the right but not the obligation to participate in a given financial contract is called an option. We will now illustrate this using both a Foreign Exchange Forward contract and a Foreign Exchange option.

# Foreign Exchange Forward—a contract with an obligation

In a Foreign Exchange Forward contract a certain amount of foreign currency will be bought (or sold) at a future date using a prearranged foreign exchange rate.

For instance, counterparty A may own a Foreign Exchange Forward which, in one year's time, contractually obliges A to purchase from B the sum of $200 for £100. At the end of one year several things may have happened.

+   - The value of the pound may have decreased with respect to the dollar
- The value of the pound may have increased with respect to the dollar
- Counterparty B may refuse to honor the contract—B may have gone bust, etc.
- Counterparty A may refuse to honor the contract—A may have gone bust, etc.

We will now consider events (i)–(iv) from A's perspective.
Firstly, if (i) occurs then A will be able to obtain $200 for the current market rate, say £120. In this case $200 can be bought for £100 and immediately sold for £120, giving a profit of £20. This profit can only be realized if B honors the contract—that is, event (iii) does not happen.
Secondly, if (ii) occurs then A is obliged to purchase $200 for more than the current market rate, say £90. In this case $200 are bought for £100 but could have been bought for £90, giving a loss of £10.
The probability of events (iii) and (iv) occur are related to the Credit Risk associated with counterparty B. The value of the contract is not affected by (iv), although A may be sued if both (ii) and (iv) occur.
