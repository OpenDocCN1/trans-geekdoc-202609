d) Splitting is the reverse of stacking. Splitting will always keep the resulting tensors the same number of dimensions as the input tensor. Figure 2.3 illustrates examples of split operations.

e) When specifying the second argument of the split methods (e.g. `num_or_size_splits`) as integers, `np.split` and the subsequent alias methods `np.vsplit`, `np.hsplit`, `np.dsplit`, as well as the corresponding Tensorflow method `tf.split`, will divide the tensor into equal sized pieces along a certain dimension; they will therefore throw an error if the tensor is not evenly divisible. `np.array_split` is more general and is equivalent to `torch.split`, which both allow a tensor to be divided into unequal pieces.

f) These additional split operations can also be achieved by simply using `split` (which may be why Tensorflow does not implement them natively).

+   * vsplit always splits the tensors along the first dimension, equivalent to `split (..., axis=0)`, and can be used as the reverse of `vstack`.
+   * hsplit always splits the tensors along the second dimension, equivalent to `split (..., axis=1)`, and can be used as the reverse of `hstack`.
+   * dsplit always splits the tensors along the third dimension, equivalent to `split (..., axis=2)`, and can be used as the reverse of `dstack`.
