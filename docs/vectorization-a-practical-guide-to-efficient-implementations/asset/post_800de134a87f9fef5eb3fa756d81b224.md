Scammers can pretend to be the victim's friends and family who are in an emergency and ask for financial assistance, making it more difficult for the victims to recognize the deception [Butts, 2024].

- **Ethical and societal considerations**: Ethical concerns encompass a broad range of issues, from the potential misuse of AI for deceptive purposes to the ethical implications of AI-generated art and literature. Societal considerations involve the broader impact of AI on society, including its influence on employment, privacy, and social dynamics. Most notably, data sources used to train the large-scale models are neither “scraped” from the internet, without first asking for permission from the creators, nor would the model provide appropriate credit to the creators. Whether or not using scraped content to train AI models that can regenerate such content constitutes fair use is still under debate. More recently, the New York Times has sued OpenAI over using copyrighted materials to train LLM [Grynbaum and Mac, 2023].

- **Risk mitigation**: Addressing these challenges involves comprehensive risk mitigation strategies. This includes developing robust algorithms that can detect and counteract biases, implementing strict guidelines for data handling and model training, and continuously monitoring AI systems for unintended behaviors. Major players of the landscape including Meta [Touvron et al., 2023b], Google [Chowdhery et al., 2022], and OpenAI [OpenAI et al., 2023] are investing heavily in the research of this topic. Their efforts focus not only on further enhancing the technical capabilities of generative models but also on ensuring the downstream applications of the models are carefully evaluated to align with AI safety standards.

- **Policy and regulation**: The role of policy and regulation is paramount in shaping the safe development and deployment of AI technologies. Governments and regulatory bodies are increasingly focusing on developing frameworks to ensure that AI advances in a manner that is safe, ethical, and beneficial for society. This includes regulations around data privacy, AI transparency, and accountability. Government entities across the world, including the European Union [European Parliament, 2023], the United States [iapp, 2024; Klobuchar, 2024], China [Costigan, 2024], and Japan [AP, 2024] are actively developing new legislation and guidelines regarding AI safety and regulation.

Addressing these issues is critical to ensure the new AI technologies are safe and beneficial for society. As the technology continues to innovate, we will see more and more collaborations between researchers, industry leaders, and policymakers to navigate the complex landscape of AI safety and alignment.
