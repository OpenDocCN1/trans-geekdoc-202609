We want to minimize \(J\). By setting the gradients as zero, we have

\(\frac{\partial J}{\partial u_i} = 2 \sum_{j \in \Psi_i} (r_{ij} - \vec{u}_i \vec{w}_j^T)(-\vec{w}_j) = 0\)

\(\sum_{j \in \Psi_i} r_j(-\vec{w}_j) - \sum_{j \in \Psi_i} (\vec{u}_i \vec{w}_j^T)(-\vec{w}_j) = 0\)

\(\sum_{j \in \Psi_i} (\vec{u}_i \vec{w}_j^T)\vec{w}_j = \sum_{j \in \Psi_i} r_j \vec{w}_j\)

\(\sum_{j \in \Psi_i} \vec{u}_i (\vec{w}_j^T \vec{w}_j) = \sum_{j \in \Psi_i} r_j \vec{w}_j\)

\(\vec{u}_i \left( \sum_{j \in \Psi_i} \vec{w}_j^T \vec{w}_j \right) = \sum_{j \in \Psi_i} r_j \vec{w}_j\)

Notice that \(\vec{w}_j^T \vec{w}_j\) is an outer product. Also, notice that the last step is in the form of \(AX = b\) (if \(X\) is column vector, or \(XA = b\) is \(X\) is a row vector).

We can invert the matrix \(A = \sum_{j \in \Psi_i} \vec{w}_j^T \vec{w}_j\) part to solve for \(\vec{u}_i\), yielding

\(\vec{u}_i = \sum_{j \in \Psi_i} r_{ij} \vec{w}_j \left( \sum_{j \in \Psi_i} \vec{w}_j^T \vec{w}_j \right)^{-1}\)

Numerically, we can take advantage of numpy's `np.linalg.solve(A, b)` function to solve for \(\vec{u}_i\).

To solve for \(w_j\), we yield similar results

\(\vec{w}_j = \sum_{i \in \Omega_j} r_{ij} \vec{u}_i \left( \sum_{i \in \Omega_j} \vec{u}_i^T \vec{u}_i \right)^{-1}\)

We apply the isolated \(u_i\) and \(w_j\) updates iteratively to minimize the loss function \(J\). This is called **alternating least squares (ALS)**. We first initialize \(u_i\) and \(w_j\) randomly, then update according to the equations we obtained.
