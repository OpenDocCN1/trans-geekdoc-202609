So, in one of our projects, we were trying to find a way to correct the effect of head movement during the scan [Cui et al., 2018]. But, how can we track the head position in a dark environment inside the MRI machine? A smart engineer from our team thought of a simple way: we printed a pair of dark squares on a piece of white paper and fixed these fiducial markers on a pair of swimming goggles. Then, the patient undergoing the scan wore these goggles while an infrared camera took pictures of the fiducial markers. After collecting the data, we initially thought of using simple image processing steps like thresholding and segmentation to find the centroids of the pair of fiducial markers, and the mean position of the two centroids over time would form a time series of head positions relative to the starting position. However, due to lighting and patient head orientations, the image quality of the data collected was not always the best (see Figure 4.5, e.g. where images taken could be skewed and affected by lighting artifacts). Still, if a human being looked at the images, they could pinpoint the mean position of the fiducial marker very well. Nowadays, image segmentation and object tracking problems are mostly solved by deep-learning-based solutions. But, back in 2013, this was a pretty difficult problem to solve at scale. Probably due to my skill level at the time, despite trying many tools in MATLAB’s image processing library and Python’s OpenCV and Scipy, none of them helped us consistently track the positions of the markers (which we verified through visual inspections after processing the images through the algorithm).

Therefore, I was tasked to determine a robust, alternate algorithm that would allow us to automate the process of accurately determining the mean position of the markers. My thought at the time was that we only needed to extract part of the image that would match the template just well enough, so that the lighting artifact or any noise in the image would be filtered out during the template-matching process.
