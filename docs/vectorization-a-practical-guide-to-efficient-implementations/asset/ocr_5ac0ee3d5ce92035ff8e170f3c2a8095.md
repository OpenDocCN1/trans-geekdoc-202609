# 2.10 Reshaping

Reshaping operations keep the underlying data of the tensor identical to the inputs but represent the tensor in a different format. Being able to trace the evolution of the tensor shapes through a computational graph is probably one of the most important skills to master before being able to successfully implement a deep learning algorithm. Oftentimes, the outputs of a previous operation may not be directly usable in the subsequent operations. For example, a feed-forward layer may output an array of 9 elements, but the subsequent operation requires us to take an average of 3 groups of 3 elements. a) Concatenation is sometimes similar to stacking. However, concatenation will always combine the tensors along existing axes and will throw an error if the axis does not exist. In contrast, stacking will combine the tensors along either an existing or a new axis and will change the dimensions of the tensors sometimes. When operating on 1D vectors, the horizontal stack operation (hstack) is identical to the concatenation (concatenate). Figure 2.1 illustrates the concatenation and stacking operations on 1D vectors. When operating on higher dimensions matrices or tensors, stacking can act similarly to concatenation. For example, vstack is equivalent to concatenating along the 1st axis (concatenate(..., axis=0)) while hstack is equivalent to concatenating along the second axis (concatenate(..., axis=1)) dstack will stack along the third dimension (and will create it if not already existing). b) row_stack or vstack always stacks the tensors along the first dimension, equivalent to stack(..., axis=0); column_stack or hstack always stacks the tensors along the second dimension, equivalent to stack(..., axis=1); dstack always stacks the tensors along the third dimension, equivalent to stack(..., axis=2).
