However, many researchers still found that they need to carefully tune the learning rate for their specific tasks. Techniques used in the transformer model such as normalization and skip connections are known to address the problem of either an exploding or vanishing gradient, which is crucial for model training stability and convergence, yet these problems still exist. Hence, Xiong et al., 2020 proposed an alternative arrangement of the layers within the transformer block [Xiong et al., 2020]. They call it Pre-LN architecture, to distinguish it from the original architecture which they call Post-LN. A comparison of the two architectures is illustrated in Figure 11.2. The key difference between the two architectures is that the original Post-LN architecture performs attention and feedforward operations and then normalizes their outputs, whereas Pre-LN architecture starts with layer normalization on the inputs. With this change, Xiong et al., 2020 demonstrated that the transformer can be trained more stably without the use of a learning-rate schedule with a warm-up period, hence significantly improving the training efficiency of the transformer model by allowing faster convergence. I have seen many domains of study still use Post-LN with a learning-rate schedule in their neural architecture design. If trained successfully, the Post-LN architecture almost always outperforms that of Pre-LN architecture, especially in certain areas such as text-to-speech.
