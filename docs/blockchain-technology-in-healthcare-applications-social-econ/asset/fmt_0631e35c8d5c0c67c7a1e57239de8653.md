## 11.1 Introduction

In recent years, the coming out of the Internet of Things (IoT) has taken a significant impact on science and technology [[1]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_1). The IoT is defined as a network of identifiable and unique elements that communicate without human interaction using IP connectivity [[1]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_1). These items can perceive, control, analyze, and make decisions independently, as well as in collaboration with other objects [[2]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_2). At the end of 2019, there were around 9.5 billion connected IoT devices, according to IoT Analytics estimates [[3]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_3). The concept of the IoT is evolving exponentially and covering more and more domains every day [[4]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_4). It will change the way we live and work by making different aspects of life smart. Through the development of new applications in fields such as smart homes, smart health, smart cities, industry 4.0, Wireless Sensor Networks (WSN), smart agriculture, and others, IoT has the potential to introduce and establish a smart world [[5]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_5). The interested reader is referred to [[1]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_1), [[2]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_2), [[3]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_3), [[4]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_4), [[5]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_5), [[6]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_6), [[7]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_7), [[8]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_8), [[9]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_9), [[10]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_10), [[11]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_11), [[12]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_12), [[13]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_13), [[14]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_14), [[15]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_15), [[16]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_16), [[17]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_17), [[18]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_18), [[19]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_19), [[20]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_20) for a deeper understanding of the IoT.

Healthcare 4.0 is a combination between the IoT and modern Information and Communication Technologies [[6]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_6). The Smart health plays an important role in health applications by integrating sensors and actuators into patients’ bodies for monitoring and follow-up. The global IoT medical device market is expected to grow at a compound annual growth rate of 4.5%, reaching $409.5 billion in 2025 [[7]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_7). The IoT is used in healthcare to monitor the physiological status of patients. On-board sensors can collect information directly from the patient’s body and transmit it to the physician. These collected information can be stored, processed, and make it available to doctors to give a consultation at any time and from any devices that are connected to the Internet (e.g., Smartphone or Tablet) [[8]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_8), [[9]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_9), [[10]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_10), [[11]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_11), [[12]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_12), [[13]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_13), [[14]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_14), [[15]](#C_016_c11.xhtml#r11_15). Further, the doctor is alerted in real time of any sudden change concerning the condition of his patient, as well as take actions like advising patients, and interrogate the sensors to have the current values. This technology can fully isolate the patient from the hospital’s centralized system while yet allowing them to communicate with their physician.
