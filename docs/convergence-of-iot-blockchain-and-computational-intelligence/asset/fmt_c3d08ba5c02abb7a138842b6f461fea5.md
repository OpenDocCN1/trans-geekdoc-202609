
The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) is a US-based law enacted in 1996 for data security regulation. HIPAA helps define what constitutes health data or "Protected Health Information" (PHI). HIPAA makes use of 18 PHI identifiers: names, zip codes, medical records, social security numbers, etc. Securing privacy can foster more effective communication between physician and patient, which is crucial for quality of care, enhancing autonomy, and averting economic harm, embarrassment, and discrimination. The patient suffering from a health issue should be assured of confidentiality as a pillar of medicine [[29](#chapter10.xhtml#ref10_29)]. Securing the personal details of a patient is not just a matter of moral respect; it is necessary for maintaining the essential bond of trust between doctor and patient. Upholding privacy and confidentiality helps protect the patient from potential harms, including psychological harm (embarrassment or distress); social harm (loss of employment or damage to financial standing); and criminal and civil liability [[30](#chapter10.xhtml#ref10_30)].

[Figure 10.15](#chapter10.xhtml#fig10_15) shows that data governance in healthcare is concerned with how to protect, secure, and accurately gather each piece of data [[31](#chapter10.xhtml#ref10_31)]. Data governance in the healthcare system is an important process and system to maintain data for making decisions about patient care. Data governance helps secure against unauthorized access to patients' private health information [[32](#chapter10.xhtml#ref10_32)]. Personal Health Information (PHI) was clearly defined by data governance to set strategies that safeguard health data and keep it safe.
