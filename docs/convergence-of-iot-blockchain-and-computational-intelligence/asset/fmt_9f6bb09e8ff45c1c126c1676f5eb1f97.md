## 2.5.6 IoT in Sustainability and Climate Change

IoT frameworks operate in various sustainability and climate change projects worldwide. These projects have increased the usage of recycling products and weather control on earth using the IoT framework. Smart sensors are ruling the world under the IoT framework for the automation and optimization of machines used for real-world applications. Weather control reports are generated every second using the IoT framework, and futuristic problems like earthquakes, storms, and floods will be identified accurately. From a sustainability point of view, all recycled items in the real world have to be centralized and controlled using IoT. Renewable energy and water conservation concepts are improved with the help of IoT smart sensors and are used to monitor ([Taha et al., 2018](#chapter2.xhtml#ref2_42)) anywhere from a remote connection. The controlling process of all devices and machines under IoT became very easy when IoT handled its features. Moreover, the amount of data generation is also very high but handled as big data and stored in a repository for the recycling process. So, the extraction and backup of all information about the entire process is under control at real-time execution. ## 2.5.7 IoT with Blockchain

IoT framework data transmission between machines and sensors is more secure with the help of encryption and decryption concepts. For providing secure transactions, every time the data has to change or be updated from one form to another during transmission. Due to this, if a hacker interrupts the network, the data has not been leaked or hacked at that time. Blockchain is the new technology used to provide secured transactions over IoT, and all data used for processes are encrypted with algorithms. Though encryption is secured, it may cause latency problems during transmission in network-related places. To overcome this, blockchains are used as snippet coding transactions by encryption techniques, making every process secure. ## 2.5.8 Services of Cloud and Edge Computing in IoT

There are several machines and sensors connected in the IoT framework, and connectivity between different platforms or networks creates conflicts during the carrying of digital data. The platforms create security issues on their edges in the local nodes which are connected internally. So, the nodes transmit data from one place to another using two different edge technologies: cloud and edge computing ([Wen et al., 2018](#chapter2.xhtml#ref2_44)). This overcomes the problem of resource management and energy conservation issues at end-to-end transactions. Here, cloud computing stores the data generated from all outside sources, whereas edge computing connects the nodes and then the transaction of data occurs. So, if any hackers enter any one of the nodes, their backup has been taken and stored in their repository; moreover, it provides encrypted data to hackers ([Rana and Kumar, 2019](#chapter2.xhtml#ref2_20)). ## 2.5.9 IoT Devices – Sensors Increasingly Becoming Commodity

The devices and sensors connected in the IoT framework have increased rapidly due to real-world applications. However, the commodity service for those sensors and devices is increasing day by day for their features. Trillions of devices are working continuously and generating data randomly all over the world. The main purpose of the commodity sensor is to implement its capability and caliber. So, rapid changes and updates in all sensors used in IoT will improve the efficiency of applications.

## 2.5.10 LPWAN and 5G Technologies for IoT

Most countries like China, South Korea, Japan, and European countries have tried Low Power Wide Area Networks (LPWAN) for their automation purposes and have succeeded with good results. It leads to reduced energy consumption and low latency timings for secured transmission at a certain distance. Moreover, 5G is also introduced with high-speed bandwidth and frequency for secured transmission of data over the IoT framework. When discussing these scenarios concerning the time and speed of data transmission, they provide good results in real-world applications. But other factors like the size of data, security, and authentication issues give a poor percentage success rate because it works at a certain distance only. However, for IoT framework regions in smart city and smart home automation, etc., it reveals a good environment for controlling and monitoring the system without a human role at a 75% success rate.
