A key element of optimising operational efficiency is in understanding the actual cost of a process or system. Effective financial performance management is a critical component of this exercise (covered earlier) but with technology increasingly being a feature in process costing, the need to understand the IT cost that ‘attaches’ to that process is a key enabler to understanding.

# IT Cost Transparency

To have a clear idea on operational costings, and how to optimise them, banks are normally keen to understand how IT costs are allocated to the various parts of the business. The key components of transparency in the cost of banking technology comprise:

+   - A ‘catalogue-based menu’ of costing allocation, which means the provision of a single, consistent source of services and agreed functions, which simplifies product management, service charging and assurance.
+   - Consumption measurement, which can be based on either agreed hourly or functional rates, and is payable upon usage.
+   - Technology chargeback and internal invoicing, which comprises internal cost allocation mechanisms.
+   - Driver-based planning, which is an approach to management that identifies the key ‘drivers’ of a business, and ‘creates a series of business plans that mathematically model how those things most necessary for the organisation’s success would be affected by different variables.’²⁴

# Branch Performance Management

Perhaps the first question to be asked is whether the branch has a foreseeable future, especially in the world of advanced analytics and AI. As a starting point, it’s important to understand what makes a branch effective. First, we need to think about how we measure the success or profitability of a branch, and how this in itself is measured. We call this ‘branch performance management’. Branch performance management takes into account the use of data and analytics to measure current and past performance, and help in the planning process. Effective branch performance management takes into account the mix of customers who hold accounts at the branch, their product type and how to upsell and cross-sell to ensure maximum profitability.

Beyond this, it allows a bank to understand whether a particular branch is meeting its key objectives, which are normally sales or service oriented, and to what degree these are aligned to the wider strategy of the bank. This normally is related to growth and customer retention targets but there may be other ‘softer’ measures such as customer satisfaction.
