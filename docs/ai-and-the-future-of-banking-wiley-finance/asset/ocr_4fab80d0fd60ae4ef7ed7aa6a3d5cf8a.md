# FIVE SCENARIOS FOR THE ‘BANK OF THE FUTURE’

It’s both challenging and risky to set out a single view of the Bank of the Future, so a number of different scenarios are offered. One of these may prevail but perhaps the final outcome will be some sort of hybrid. Earlier the idea of ambidexterity of strategy was considered and it’s feasible that two or more scenarios might co-exist within the same organisation.

In this case, a single major assumption has been made and that is one of AI infusing the end result. The Bank of the Future will be an ‘AI-enabled’ organisation. Maybe the bank itself will not be designed by humans but by the technology itself in some form of ‘self-learning’ evolutionary process. Advanced analytical technology has already recently gone through a sea change, transforming itself from being a ‘knowledge-based system’ (that is, a system which is capable of making decisions based on the information it is storing and then applying context and an interface engine), into a ‘machine-learning’ capability with the ability to partly or fully automate using RPA (robotic process automation) techniques.

Put another way, knowledge-based systems work according to rules created by humans, whereas with a machine learning system it is the machine itself that creates the rules (or algorithms). The problem, according to an MIT report, is fundamentally one of scale, with so much data that so many rules are required and there is a need to manually create codes. The solution, apparently, might be to allow the machines to sort out the rules themselves.²²
