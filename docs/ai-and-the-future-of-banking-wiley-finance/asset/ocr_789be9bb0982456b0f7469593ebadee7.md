Their telecoms study ‘Navigating the road to 2020,’¹² based on interviews with 40 operators and other industry stakeholders, also revealed that ‘61% of industry leaders saw “OTTs” (Over the Top Services) as the industry entity most likely to alter customer demand scenarios in the years to come’.

In cutting aside any tech jargon but inadvertently slipping into marketing jargon, this seems to be an issue of creating ‘added value’ services, which in itself creates ‘customer delight’.

2. **Lack of regulatory certainty on new market structures**: Beyond this, they recognised that, like banking, they were operating in a changing regulatory environment, which was affecting their investment strategy, especially as regulatory frameworks were shifting as a result of convergence, and traditional models and definitions were also changing.

EY’s view was that telecoms providers should ‘engage proactively with regulators to help shape the digital society’. The implication here is that telecoms providers should not be passive and reactive but rather be on the front foot with respect to regulatory impact, especially because they are key stakeholders. There is a lesson here too for the financial services sector.

3. **Ignoring new imperatives in privacy and security**: Cybersecurity remains a mainstream concern to all industries, including telecoms and banking, especially because the customer relationship is based on trust and the need for privacy and security will increase as we increasingly adopt the behaviour of a digital age.

Beyond this, there are regulatory impacts with respect to ownership and protection of customer data.

4. **Failure to improve organisational agility**: In effect, the challenges of coping with change within the organisation, in terms of responding to shorter product development cycles and meeting customer demands being driven by other players such as the ‘web giants’.

EY specifically recommend transformation of people, processes and increased collaboration within the enterprise ‘to create more customer centric organisations’.

5. **Lack of data integrity to drive growth and efficiency**: Telecoms companies need to recognise that effective use of data and analytics is one of the key solutions to enable them to gain a 360-degree view of the customer, and in doing so, to effectively upsell and cross sell their products.

6. **Insufficient performance measurement to drive execution**. Whilst the measurement of performance remains important, one challenge for telecoms companies is to understand and ultimately measure the right metrics for the digital age.

They suggest that the ‘fast-changing marketplace requires new concepts of performance measurement that marry appropriate objectives and incentives with better internal and external metrics.’

7. **Failure to understand what customers value**: This goes beyond merely customer personalisation but to keep pace with ‘customers attitudes’ leading to more advanced (and conversely simple) propositions, and the creation of new roles such as ‘Customer Experience Officer’.
