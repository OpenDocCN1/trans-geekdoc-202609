+   2. Brands will become ‘ultra-gamified’ by embedding their brand into the virtual world. This approach will especially appeal to Gen Z with as many as 68% of Gen Z males actively gaming.
+   3. Brands will start to ‘self-generate’ based on algorithms that detect patterns of consumer need and self-fill them quickly.
+   4. There will be an increase in brand fluidity, which constantly changes due to market circumstances and with consumers themselves being co-branders.
+   5. Actualisation of brands will create a deeper and more meaningful relationship with customers. This will replace the bombardment of customers with messages of a much deeper level of engagement requiring transparency, honesty and including an emotional connection.
+   6. Ethical spending will increase in importance. Ethically minded consumers will look for more cues and directions as to how to buy, and ethical decisions will start to edge out purchasing decisions based on value and consumption alone.
+   7. Crowds will define brands using different methods such as social media to identify brand ‘gaps’ and consumers themselves will act to fill those gaps. In this scenario, consumers view the ‘brand as a friend’.
+   8. Brand relationships will reduce in importance with ‘breadth’ replacing ‘depth’ of brand. The brand mantra will be ‘wide and often’ with multiple touchpoints, and with a much simpler brand engagement.
+   9. Post-retail branding will emerge. By 2030 online shopping will have become so convenient that the brand will somehow have to encourage the consumer to make purchasing decisions outside the normal retailing cycle, and retailers will use strong brand identify to assist that process.
+   10. Brand ecosystems will emerge such as the connected home, with significant discounts available to cross-purchasers (in return for being ‘locked into’ a brand partnership).

The above list, pointing to a possible world in a decade’s time, offers some important clues for banking and especially in the context of the branding. As with data and analytics, leadership in the context of customer service and consumer engagement has come from the retail sector, so banks would be well advised to look to that sector for the next wave of innovation in branding. Possible future opportunities to consider might include:

+   - The stretching of the bank’s own brand across a much wider ecosystem than currently exists, and adding value to the customer experience at every opportunity
+   - The impact of ethical investment by the banks to meet the particular ethical aspirations of their customers
+   - A strengthening of the link between banking and gamification, which may occur in different ways. Perhaps more financially orientated games might be created, including the sponsoring of gaming by financial services organisations or the embedding of banking brands.
+   - A more fluid and flexible AI-driven approach to brand identity which is more closely aligned to the individual brand expectations of the consumer. What this implies is that the larger banner ads may reduce in importance, especially in the digital age.
