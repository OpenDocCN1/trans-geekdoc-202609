Rather, it recognises that in banking marketplaces where mergers and acquisitions are likely to feature, either between banks or other business (such as technology companies for example), then brand is important in terms of the market valuation of a potential ‘target’.

Studies by companies such as Brand Finance, a leading brand valuation consultancy, seek to identify the world’s biggest banks by their brand value.^6^ They calculate value through three contributing factors:

+   1. Enterprise value, which they define as ‘The value of the entire enterprise, made up of multiple branded businesses’
+   2. Branded business value, which they define as ‘The value of a single branded business operating under the subject brand’
+   3. Brand contribution, which they define as ‘The overall uplift in shareholder value that the business derives from owning the brand rather than operating a generic brand’

Brand Finance differentiate between ‘brand value’ and ‘brand strength’. The latter is defined by ‘the efficacy (or value) of a brand’s performance on intangible measures, relative to its competitors’, measured by marketing investment, stakeholder equity and the impact of those on business performance.

In their 2018 report, Brand Finance also draw specific attention to non-finance competitors in the broader financial services sector such as Apple, Facebook, Google and Amazon. They make the point that these tech giants have either already created consumer-facing financial services or are expected to do so soon. As such they ‘represent new challenges to traditional banking services, as they are more aligned to modern customer expectations around instant multi-channel and multiplatform services’.
