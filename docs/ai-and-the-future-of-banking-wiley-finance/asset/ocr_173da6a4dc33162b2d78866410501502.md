# Cyber Risk Management Apps

There is an increasing focus on the use of Fintech to address cyber risk issues. The Willis Towers Watson Quarterly Insuretec briefing of Q4, 2018¹⁷ particularly focused on the management of cyber risk, referring to:

+   - ‘Cyence Risk Analytics’ from Guidewire, which models the economic effect of cybercrime
+   - ‘Corax’, which build analytics tools to support the design of new cyber insurance products
+   - ‘Paladin’, which creates cyber-risk detection
+   - ‘Zeguro’, which builds tools to reduce cyber risk
+   - ‘Refirm Labs’, which creates methods to vet and validate hardware security

From an insurance point of view, the cyber problem is increasingly being described as ‘Silent Cyber’, referring to insurance liabilities and obligations arising from cyber-related losses. These ‘silent’ losses stem from traditional property and liability insurance policies that were not specifically designed to cover cyber risk but where such losses aren’t specifically excluded, nor are they priced for by underwriters. It’s becoming more topical as an issue. In 2017 the NotPetya cyberattack was to become one of the costliest in history, attacking a range of businesses, from shipping companies to law firms, and demanding a bitcoin ransom. The attack was thought to have cost shipping giant Merck and also FedEx over US$300 million each.¹⁸,¹⁹

In mid-2017 the UK-based Prudential Regulatory Authority (PRA) said that it ‘expects firms to be able to identify, quantify and manage cyber insurance underwriting risk’. But it’s a challenge. As John Merchant, director of customer solutions at Guidewire Cyence Risk Analytics, puts it, ‘Five-years-old is ancient in terms of cyber’.²⁰

# Broader Cyber Issues for Banking

Whilst cyber security is a worry at a personal level, there are broader issues that need to be considered. In part, these are increasingly leading to a new category of cybercrime insurance, which in many cases has yet to be tested in the courts of law. According to global insurers AXA, there are at least four key areas that need to be addressed.²¹

+   - **Infiltration of industrial systems**: That is, the ability of cybercriminals to infiltrate industrial controls (In 2014, cybercriminals increased the speed of machinery in a German steel mill until it burst into flames.)
+   - **Ransom risk**: The concept of ‘cyber extortion’, where a system is infiltrated and a ransom is demanded, usually payable through some form of crypto-currency
+   - **Reputational risk**: Infiltration leading to loss of brand trust and the public losing faith in an organisation they have trusted with their personal information
+   - **Professional liability risk**: Where organisations (such as lawyers) that are less concerned with data loss realise that they are holding sensitive information, the loss of which would create insights that are both actionable and potentially detrimental to the owners
