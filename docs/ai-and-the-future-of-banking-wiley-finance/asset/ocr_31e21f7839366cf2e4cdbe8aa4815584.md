API management is a critical component in the transformation of banks to a digital format. APIs, or Application Programming Interfaces, are a set of functions and procedures for building software. They are also viewed as critical in the Open Banking implementation. In effect, they are a set of programming instructions for developers to allow them to access web-based systems¹¹ and to allow banks and other organisations to:

+   - Accelerate application creation, allowing the speedy and effective implementation of mobile, omnichannel and IoT strategies.
+   - Improve the management of a partner ecosystem, ensuring scalable integration with global security standards, which will be critical as banks seek to extend their brand influence and universality.
+   - Create effective environments for innovation, experimentation, hackathons and new business models.
+   - Allow the bringing together of startup and innovation ecosystems.
+   - Provide clear separation ensuring security, agility and governance.

‘Cloud’ computing also needs to be considered. One good and simple description of cloud computing, often just referred to as simply ‘the cloud’, is the delivery of on-demand computing resources. This includes everything from applications to data centres. These resources may be on a pay-for-use basis, often accessible through wireless or increasingly at no cost to the consumer as an added-value offering. There’s a growing trend for High Street branches of banks to offer complimentary Wi-Fi to visiting customers and also, incidentally, to passers-by as an attempt to encourage them to visit the premises.

For the record, the concept of ‘the cloud’ is not a process in the sky or somewhere in the ether but rather is an expression to reflect a capability. Users should not be misled by the fact that there are usually no cables or physical connectivity involved. As with the other processes described above, the technology is too complex to be considered in detail and cloud computing as a topic is in itself worthy of its own book. Cloud computing also provides another example of how a paradigm shift in the thinking of the banking industry needs to take place.

The entire concept opens the door to new thinking and those who do not have an open mind will be disadvantaged. In their 2014 document ‘Predicts 2015: Cloud Computing Goes Beyond IT Into Digital Business’,¹² Gartner indicate that business leaders will need to ‘constantly adapt their strategies to leverage increasing cloud capabilities’. The use of a ‘private cloud’ is also a critical element in terms of the adoption of a digital strategy. A ‘private cloud’ is one accessible only within the business enterprise.
