Marketing for financial services is increasingly considered to be a specialist area requiring in-depth knowledge not only about products and services but also about consumer sentiment and what is most likely to resonate with current and future customers. Brand positioning for many financial services seems to be relatively intangible when considered against the bank itself. Financial services often use brand identities associated with sports, animals or both, and then aim to reinforce messages of innovation, teamwork and trustworthiness. In a more sophisticated world of AI and imagery being presented on a small mobile screen, almost certainly new brand techniques will need to start to emerge such as sound, voices and other sensory attributes. AI methods will in time start to customise how an individual consumer is best attracted by a particular element of branding.

As the concept of universal or full-service banks develop, the challenge for marketers will be that of ensuring the brand message is communicated by all elements of the banking ecosystem in a consistent way. The weakest link of the supply ecosystem will become the weakest link of the banking universe. More effective governance of the supply chain will become increasingly important, and the role of the supply chain manager will be obliged to reflect this.

# BRAND VALUE AND EQUITY IN BANKING

Brand isn’t just an exercise in attracting customers but has a strong and relatively clear financial value as well. In *Managing Brand Equity: Capitalizing on the Value of a Brand Name*,⁵ David Aaker – who is an American organisational theorist, consultant and professor emeritus at the University of California – considered the concept of ‘brand equity’ or ‘brand value’, describing it as a ‘complex combination of brand awareness, perceived quality, brand loyalty and brand associations’.

Brand value is measured in three key ways:

+   - Cost-based brand valuation
+   - Market-based brand valuation
+   - Income-approach brand valuation

Experts also recognise the importance of value and how to financially assess it, typically understanding the significance of:

+   1. Value derived out of customer perception, social media, and so on, as a relative measure against an unbranded/common brand
+   2. Value derived in terms of growth in market share and revenue through perceived brand equity over a time period
