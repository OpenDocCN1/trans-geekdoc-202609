# Finding Capital to Innovate: One Example

For many banks, the pressure is on to get beyond their normal walls of business and have the ability to compete with newer, more flexible competitors. Unless specific funds have been put aside for development, there is the inevitable challenge of where funding might be found. Despite recognising that in many cases it becomes a ‘mission critical’ exercise to transform, at the same time there are often budgetary challenges.

This particular example is included to flag up the potential for looking more carefully into an existing business to identify where development funding might be obtained. In this situation, the use case was that of developing an omnichannel solution across online and social media environments, with funding obtained through better management of their telecom costs.

By way of background to this innovation example, omnichannel banking is described as a ‘seamless banking experience’ that a customer enjoys however he or she interacts with the bank, whether mobile, retail or over the internet. (See Figure 10.26.) It reflects the desire of consumers that the bank ‘speaks with one voice’. According to Global and Finance Banking site, by adopting an effective omnichannel strategy, Wells Fargo bank in the US have reported an ‘increase in purchase rate by 1.9 times, increase in customer retention by 20ppt and increased cross-selling per house-hold by 6 products among others.’17
