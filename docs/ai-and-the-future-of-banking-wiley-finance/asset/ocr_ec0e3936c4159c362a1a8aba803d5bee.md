# INTRODUCTION

Analytics is already heavily used in banking and is a key enabler to many quite diverse parts of the organisation. This section will review the current uses of analytics in the banking industry, but this is far from a comprehensive picture as each separate section merits detailed investigation by the reader.

The essence of all analytical analysis in banking is that of the management of finances and the operations of the Office of Finance. This is the ‘engine room’ of the banking organisation. Clear understanding of the profitability of divisions, channels and products rests at the heart of bank profitability. Beyond this are considered issues of customer insight, followed then by risk management, the latter driving into more detail in relation to operational risk, credit risk and market risk, by way of example. The diversity of the industry lends itself to multiple types of risk management and not all can be considered in sufficient depth within a single volume, but will perhaps provide a taster as to the issue and complexities involved.

Additionally, matters such as operational analytics (for example, branch performance management) are included. Whilst the concept of branches as we normally understand them is, if not in doubt, then certainly under discussion, it is useful to understand how the success of branches is currently measured. Later on, in Chapter 8, the way that branches might develop in the future will be explored, including the concept of ‘debranching’.

Even taking all the above into account, the use of analytics in banking is a broad church, and one that will continue not only to broaden but also to deepen. It is an integral part of the AI journey and the route towards the Bank of the Future.

# OFFICE OF FINANCE MANAGEMENT

It is in the nature of some readers to think that analytics in the office of finance need not be industry specific or indeed banking specific, to think that all finance officers and accountants are more or less the same, and that they undertake the same functions. To some degree they are right in that accounting practices across all industries by necessity have a degree of standardisation to enable organisations to be benchmarked against one another. Equally, there is an argument that financial analytics are too complex to be understood and managed by other than professionally trained accountants.

On the other hand, many industries do necessarily have distinct characteristics, and banking falls within that grouping. Banking has many functions, including the need to manage structured uncertainty in multiple lines of business and product sets, and manage that risk with a view to creating measurable benefit from that risk, and ultimately turn a profit.
