
The operations and IT team are a crucial part of the banking business yet are often seen as a cost to the organisation rather than a profit centre. Being a ‘cost’ to the business places pressure on optimising, usually reducing, the cost of manpower. This has also led to the use of contractors, an approach that has been prevalent in the sector, and one that allows operational flexibility in a volatile marketplace, even though it carries the risks of inconsistency and occasional bad practices.

Some IT and operational centres have also been used as a ‘shared service’ between different banks in a form of quasi-outsourced basis, although there are invariably issues regarding risk, security and privacy to contend with.²³

The criticality of security and especially ‘cyber security’ should not be overlooked within this section. If, as suggested later, there is a need for banks to extend their influence into the wider marketplace (the concept of the universal or full-service bank), then as a result there will also be major security issues to contend with. These are by their nature particularly specialist in character and will become increasingly so as the complexity of cybercrime increases.

## Channel Management

This term is used to express the different ways that a bank (or other business) can bring their services or products before their existing and prospective customers. Experts suggest that increasing the effectiveness of channel management is becoming a CSF (critical success factor) for the retail banking sector, especially as banks recognise and respond to the shift in customer behaviour and new buying patterns.

> “To drive greater financial value,” says Jim Burson of Cornerstone, who are channel management consultants, “bankers must shake up their branch-centric perspective and embrace a more integrated view of delivery channel performance management.”²⁴ This means that banks must be able to create a clear understanding not only of the value of a particular ‘channel’ but rather they must also understand the ROC (‘Return on Capital’) of each channel to be able to gauge its success or otherwise. Effective use of financial performance management (see Chapter 4) is a key enabler to that process.

## Product Development

Product development is viewed as being one of the key engines of banking growth requiring not only innovation but especially also the ability to implement. Expert Dan Moutrie of Saylent Technologies suggests²⁵ that new banking products require six key tactics:

1. Clarity of purpose: the bank needs to understand why it is introducing new products
2. Simplicity of products: products that can be clearly explained and understood
3. Speed of introduction: a methodology that allows the bank to take the opportunity of changing market conditions
4. Security: the capability to ensure competitive advantage and differentiation from peer banks or new entrants
5. Standardisation: a methodical approach to internal product development processes
6. Surprise: intrinsically, having the ability to bring a new product to market quickly.

A useful article by Ron Shevlin (also of Cornerstone) suggests²⁶ that the banking sector has a dilemma:

> The larger banks (who may have new product development resources) are too big to turn the ship in a timely fashion, and the smaller institutions (who are probably more nimble) have no one who knows how to turn the wheel.

He also says that the ‘disruptive’ nature of technology, economic climate and outlook means that there’s no better time than now for banks to focus on product development.

## Sales

As with the ‘acquisition’ function above, understanding of what we mean by the ‘sales function’ can differ between types of bank. In a retail environment, the sales function may comprise the process of selling products and services such as credit cards and health insurances, which includes cross-selling. The sales function is often commission-driven, which can lead to bad practices emerging. Increasingly sales performance management software is being used to manage direct salesforce and indirect (agency) sellers.

In an investment bank, the expression ‘sales function’ can also relate to the ‘sales and trading function’, which is one of the most important operations of the bank. In investment banking, sales involves the buying and selling of ‘securities’ (such as tradable financial assets) and other financial instruments. The expression ‘market making’ is also used where financial products are bought and sold principally to stimulate investment by clients. Underpinning the sales and trading function is the need to have detailed analytics, which help create successful trading strategies. ## Marketing

Promotion and marketing are important parts of a financial organisation (and usually most non-financial ones) as they play an important part in creating and maintaining a reputation. The consistent use of logos and colour, for example, is important both in analogue and digital forms of marketing, and both are aligned to campaign management, slogans and branding. Effective use of analytics is a critical part of the marketing process, both in a B2C and B2B environment.

With an increasingly cynical and ethically aware customer base, the way in which a bank portrays itself is especially important. Experts already recognise that certain tools and methodologies resonate at a deeper psychological level, including the use of colour in the brand projection.

For example, organisations that use the colours blue and yellow do not do this by accident. Blue is associated with trust, whilst yellow is associated with happiness. There are also international perspectives that need to be considered as well, especially for global players. For example, red has quite different meanings in the West when compared to China. Black, as used by Société Générale, represents both austerity and power – perhaps very timely attributes to reflect the economic uncertainty of current times. Strong imagery also has an increasing part to play, not only from a symbolic point of view (the lion of ING, for example) but also as the industry increasingly moves to the digital channel, where prompt recognition on a small cellphone screen becomes critical.

## Customer Support and Service

For many retail customers, the level and quality of customer support and service can be a key differentiator, especially when there are queries about the technology, products or simply questions to be asked and answers to be found. A 2014 JD Power Survey about U.S. Retail Banking Satisfaction found that poor customer service was the number one reason customers switched banks.

Customer, product and system knowledge are all critical in the customer support function, but overall this part of the operation is seen as an expensive way to service the customer. As a result banks are increasingly driving towards ‘self-service’ even in the physical branch. Possibly the most effective customer support is one that combines accuracy and practical help with good interpersonal skills. Consequently, the retail banking industry is leaning towards ‘augmented AI’ where the activities of the contact centre agent are enhanced by technology, chat bots and eventually robotics, all of which will be analytically infused.

The prevalence of ATMs as a principal part of the bank’s interaction with the customer is especially recognised as a lost opportunity for improving the quality and depth of the relationship. The use of the ATM presupposes that cash will still have a part to play, but the concept of cardless eATMs is gaining attention. With ATMs and eATMs doing 60% of the function of the traditional teller, the future for ATMs to replace the traditional bank branch is especially likely, with one probable modification that future access need no longer be by plastic card but rather by iris scan or equivalent.
