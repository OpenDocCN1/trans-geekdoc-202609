# BANKS WILL USE AI TO BECOME LIFESTYLE MANAGERS

The convergence of brand and lifestyle has long been recognised. Both consumer goods suppliers and banks/wealth managers focus on the same market, albeit from different directions, so there is increasingly greater opportunity for convergence of thinking and shared technologies:

+   - Banks have a potential role in providing a catalyst for consumer spending.
+   - Consumer goods suppliers have a potential role in helping banks not only provide available lines of credit, but also wealth management advice.

Both might use advanced analytics and AI not only to identify potential customers but also to ensure that the right products, services and lines of credit are placed before them in a timely way. One particular challenge is that of ensuring customers do not over extend themselves in terms of their financial decisions.

In considering the future of banking, the most sophisticated financial institutions will not only provide modeling for the present, taking into account different risk appetites, but also help the consumer plan effectively for the future, taking into account current and anticipated assets, current and expected commitments, lifestyle and life expectancy. Key branded organisations will ultimately become ‘partners for life’, a sort of quasi-financial marriage using advanced analytics and AI as a form of catalyst. We will conceivably become ‘married’ to the financial brand. Integral to that long-term relationship will be key issues such as consumption and credit smoothing.

# Consumption and Credit Smoothing

The concept of ‘consumption smoothing’ is a process by which consumers manage their personal lifestyles to ensure that they have adequate savings to maintain their lifestyle, including during retirement. It’s a balance between sensibility and frugality. Those who overspend during their working life and fail to save will suffer the consequences later on in life when they have inadequate savings and need to adopt a more frugal existence. Those who ‘oversave’ whilst working and lead a frugal existence at that time will (hopefully) reap the benefits in later life.

It suggests that some element of ‘frugality’ will need to occur sooner or later, and that it’s a delicate balance to plan. From an economic perspective, ‘consumption smoothing’ is all about:

+   - Stability of consumption, for example, the management of spending patterns or purchasing behaviour at different stages of the consumer lifecycle
+   - Reduction of risk and uncertainty, for example, where there is an unexpected loss of income or life event
+   - The ability to predict what might be needed in terms of financial reserves
