> I think cryptocurrencies could very well serve as a viable settlement instrument. Of particular application is that of remittances or money transfers. The World Bank estimate that migrants across the world send home an estimated US $574 billion, which is about double the amount they were sending a decade ago. Over $138 billion was transferred from the US to other countries as remittances in 2016 alone, and migrants from Latin America and the Caribbean sent record amounts home. According to Pew Research, these remittances are more than just a family lifeline in many cases but a major contributor to some local economies especially in developing areas. Yet these payments struggle because of the need to involve intermediaries for this process, adding time, cost and complexity to the arrangement. The use of a Blockchain solution is said to provide for clearing and settlement in near-real time, and also to allow funds to be transferred at a lower cost than traditional correspondent banking.

The essence of the IBM World Wire solution is that it provides a ‘private and permissioned’ Blockchain protocol for the banking sector, as opposed to one that is more ‘public and permissioned’. The differentiator is that this is a banking-specific solution that meets the broader criteria of the Stellar protocol, which calls for ‘thousands of transactions per second’. According to Lund, ‘This network really has the potential to radically transform foreign exchange entirely’.

From a customer point of view, there are implications of speed, reduced cost and greater efficiency. For the banking industry, this is yet another indication of the use of technology reducing (or even removing) traditional jobs in the banking workplace.

Foreign exchange is described as a ‘market that dwarfs the stock market’.¹⁰ Principally there are two types of foreign exchange market, Interbank Market and Foreign Exchange (or ‘Forex’).

# Interbank Market

The Interbank market is one where, although there are few banks involved, the scale is enormous and sufficient to open the doors to manipulation, especially in the Forex business environment, where there have been previous accusations of rate fixing. Interbank transactions can be between 10 million and 100 million per day. Increased automation has a part to play in the governance of these deals and in helping detect anomalies.
