The report calls for international standardisation, which is no small undertaking in the absence of a unifying body. In their conclusion, the report comments:

This might sound rather a condescending approach to the matter. Perhaps were it not for the Chinese example it could be a valid conclusion, but money and political control have often remained significantly hand in hand. Advanced analytics and AI have already been established as the foundations of disruptive technology, regardless of what industry or profession is being considered. The technological impacts of Blockchain and cryptocurrencies fall into that category. The importance of Blockchain on the future of banking is critical in the impact that it will have not only on process but also on governance.

There will be a technological cost to be met but also a human cost as well. As with greater automation, robotics and AI, these complementary technologies will inevitably transform the banking industry.

# Blockchain and Open Banking in Africa

In the longer run, the ability of consumers to look across different banking systems looks quite appealing. Already the insurance and utilities industries operate forms of cost comparison sites. It’s difficult to see why the financial services industry should not provide a similar setup.

Elsewhere beyond Europe, the need for change goes beyond simply consumerism. The demand to find the best banking deal for SMEs might prove to be the difference between their survival and collapse. The degree of ‘under banking’ (where people don’t have access to mainstream banks and therefore resort to alternative financing such as loan sharks) in emerging territories also encourages the use of a form of Open Banking.

Already countries are considering not how these new technologies will change the face of banking but rather how they will change the face of a continent. Considering Africa, for example, the annual Blockchain Africa Conference brings together key experts who are focusing on the use of Blockchain in that region. Since 2015 all the key big banks in South Africa have been actively researching its use, and according to Ross A. Mauri, general manager of IBM’s Z Systems, ‘All South Africa’s leading financial institutions have some proof-of-concept or early stage technology related to the Blockchain’. This isn’t only a South African initiative but has extended Africa-wide.
