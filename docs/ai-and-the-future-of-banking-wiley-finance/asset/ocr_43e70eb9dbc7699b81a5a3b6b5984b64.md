+   - **Open Banking:** This allows third parties not only to have access to bank data to build applications that create a connected network of financial institutions and third-party providers, but also connectivity between different banking systems.
+   - **Insurtech:** This is the insurance equivalent to Fintech and is perhaps especially interesting to banks in the context of bancassurance and the Universal Bank.
+   - **RegTech (or Regtec):** This provides analytical support to help banks cope with the myriad of regulations and is especially critical in helping global enterprises.
+   - **Robotic Advisors:** These chatbots are part of a bank's functionality to allow consumers to ‘self-service’ without human intervention.
+   - **Cybersecurity:** Critical especially given the IoT, and the connectivity and vulnerability of emerging ecosystems.
+   - **Smart Contracts:** These utilise computer programs to automatically execute contracts between buyers and sellers. The key elements in Smart Contracts are autonomy, decentralisation and auto-sufficiency. *Autonomy* means that, once the deal is initiated, the originator does not need to be further involved. *Decentralisation* means that the system isn't reliant on one network or computer. *Auto-sufficiency* means that as they execute the contracts, automated systems are able to collect and distribute money and even to spend funds to increase their computing or storage power.
+   - **Services to the unbanked/underbanked:** This is one of those undercovered elements of tech development yet is especially important. These services aim to serve disadvantaged or low-income individuals who are ignored or underserved by traditional banks. It's an area of interest aimed at those who prefer to transact money using cash or limit their financial management to basic use of banking facilities. According to PayPal this constitutes over 24 million households globally and is viewed as a key area for growth of the banking sector.

The Financial Stability Board (FSB), an international body that monitors and makes recommendations about the global financial system, defines Fintech as ‘technology-enabled innovation in financial services that could result in new business models, applications, processes or products with an associated material effect on the provision of financial services’.²² Fintechs have the potential to:

+   - Increase market access
+   - Increase market products
+   - Provide greater customer convenience
+   - Lower costs
