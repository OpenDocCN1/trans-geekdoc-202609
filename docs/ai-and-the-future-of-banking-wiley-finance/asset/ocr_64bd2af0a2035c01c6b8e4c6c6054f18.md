In an AI context, this might be a difficult pill to swallow. Technological transformation usually depends on the robust return on investment, and this in turn often depends on headcount reductions. Innovation in AI development is no different to any other form of automation. ‘Heads’ are at risk and employees livelihoods are likely to be threatened. This is almost certainly going to impact their approach to change. Beyond this is a slightly irrational approach to AI, more aligned to sci-fi movies than reality but in some cases contributing to the ‘fear factor’. Effective communication is a key part in reducing that particular worry.

# CONCLUSION

Advances in technology can only be effectively monetised when they are effectively implemented. It is increasingly recognised that failed implementations can be due as much to cultural, organisational or behavioural issues as to failures in the technology itself.

This chapter has considered some of the issues relative both to innovation and implementation, first by asking what is meant by innovation and how can this be done, and then second by considering some implementation issues, which include hackathons and the use of external sources such as Fintech hubs. Finally, it considers potential blockages to change.

One of the major ones is that of organisations being unable to deliver on promised ROI, either because the original plan was overly optimistic or inadequate controls were in place to measure progress. To that degree, it appears essential that relatively straightforward BI reporting tools are in place in the office of finance at the outset of an implementation programme, to avoid disagreements at a later stage about the accuracy of achieved benefits.

Some ‘legacy’ financial reporting solutions are already being rebadged as being critical components of AI implementation. Whilst this might arguably be considered jumping on the proverbial AI bandwagon, nevertheless they make an important point and offer a timely reminder that the analytical ‘basics’ need to be in place before companies launch into relatively futuristic AI solutions.
