+   - Next two characters: the location/city code

As with Stellar.org, SWIFT is non-profit-making. In this case it comprises a cooperative model owned by members who pay a subscription to take part, plus support fees dependent on the category of use. SWIFT’s particular challenge is in respect to the number and complexity of transactions, and the increasing degree of automation that is occurring. Whilst SWIFT appears to have a dominant position at the moment, there’s a real call to action to invite SWIFT to respond to changing times or suffer the consequences. After all, in this banking industry, how many capabilities created over three decades ago will carry the test of time without changing in the future?

In more recent times, bearing in mind the focus on analytics and AI, it is recognised that ‘SWIFT has recently introduced dashboards and reporting utilities which enable the clients to get a dynamic, real-time view of monitoring the messages, activity, trade flow, and reporting. The reports enable filtering based on region, country, message types, and related parameters.’¹¹

When compared to the forward thinking approach of Blockchain, SWIFT might be argued as holding a ‘defensive position’ concerning issues of scalability, reference data and compliance. These by themselves are not impenetrable positions and it will be interesting to see how SWIFT responds to, or complements with, the Blockchain proposition. Perhaps one test is to consider whether a new entrant to the banking sector would prefer a SWIFT solution or a Blockchain-type solution. Probably both in the interim, but which of these options is likely to be more sustainable?

# Forex Market

The second type of foreign exchange is what is called the Forex or ‘over-the-counter’ market. This market isn't as highly regulated and can be subject to substantial opportunism. It's very much around ‘spot trading’ at the time of exchange or at some time in the future. Every day currency is ‘fixed’ against benchmark foreign currency rates in London which are determined by the buy and sell traders 60 seconds either side of 4 pm, and the benchmark rates of 21 currencies are determined within that one-minute window. The industry has been found to be subject to abuse through rate manipulation and allegations have been made against traders colluding by sharing information about orders prior to the 4 pm ‘fix’, and by what is known as ‘banging the close’ – that is, aggressive buying or selling of pending orders prior to the 4 pm deadline.¹²

There’s no need at this point to go into detail about the pros and cons of these trading approaches other than to consider the impact of more detailed analytics and automation. Perhaps more than the human workforce aspect, the use of advanced analytics is important both to help in the decision-making process and also to detect anomalies.
