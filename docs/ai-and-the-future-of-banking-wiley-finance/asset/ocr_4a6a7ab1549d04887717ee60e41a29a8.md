An obvious question to ask perhaps at this stage is that of ‘How does AI actually work?’ In the spirit of trying to give a simple answer to a complex question, there appear to be two main approaches to AI:

+   (a) The first is an ‘algorithm-based’ approach, which comprises a series of capabilities found in advanced analytics, such as behaviour trees and statistical approaches, which are in effect stitched together to solve a particular problem or achieve a desired effect. This approach is often found in computational models designed to respond to a specific issue such as voice or text analysis.
+   (b) The second is that of a machine learning approach (artificial neural networks) which mimic the way that the human brain works.

In its broadest sense, one view of an AI ‘ecosystem’ is that it comprises four key elements:

+   1. The ability to apply advanced data mining techniques
+   2. The ability to apply expert systems
+   3. The ability to ‘reverse engineer’ the human brain, and in doing so replicate how it works
+   4. The ability to satisfy the so-called ‘Turing Test’, which is used to establish whether a user is interacting with a human or a machine.

At this stage in the development of systems, there is evidence of cognitive systems but limited ‘true’ experience of pure AI. That is, where end users interface with a system in an intelligent way but fail to recognise that it is a machine. However, that time will definitely come within a generation and most probably sooner. The distinction between cognitive analytics and AI is best measured by the degree of human intervention or ratification of the process. Where an intelligent system has a 90% success rate in its decision-making capability but still requires human intervention to manage the 10% balance, this is often called ‘weak AI’.

The fact that there appear to be so many AI-described apps emerging from so many start-ups is arguably a feat of ‘marketing over mastery’, in that new entrants are invariably tempted to exaggerate their AI capability in order to gain attention and investment.
