# Regulatory Compliance

With such focus by banks on compliance, the use of advanced analytics and AI in this critical area is an inevitable area of focus. Known as Regtech, this falls broadly into the area of operational risk (which comprises a failure to comply with regulations, as well as other issues of compliance such as internal requirements).

The particular challenge for global banks is that of complying with diverse local requirements even if there are similar underlying requirements such as customer interaction and capital management.

In the area of customer management for example, the use of AI-infused recording and analysis can be used to monitor the nature and quality of advice given during a customer conversation, and ultimately to improve it. With the consent of the customer, AI can be used to break down a conversation into relevant key parts, such as whether the discussion was specifically financial advice or just general ‘chitchat’. From this, the financial advice element can be interrogated to establish whether the information given was within the accepted parameters. The AI element is a key component of how insight can be obtained, and a process by which the system constantly increases an understanding both of the nature and quality of interactions, and also the context of the conversation.

At central bank level, AI is increasingly likely to play a part especially in the management of capital adequacy, in particular in the area of stress testing. The volatility of the economic climate lends itself to greater automation. The likelihood is that as banks enter the next decade, they will enter more into fully digitised processes. The impact of this is that they will move, probably quite quickly, from paper to fully digitised formats, which lend themselves more readily to modern approaches such as machine learning, natural learning and ultimately to AI.

# Risk Management and AI

The principles concerned here are:

+   - Management of counterparty risk
+   - Management of credit risk
+   - Management of compliance

With specific reference to the use of risk management, the issue is one of whether AI can provide some sort of automation, or whether it is just another element of the risk management ‘toolbox’. Even relatively robust solutions currently require human talent from data scientists and industry analysts. Beyond this, the data in itself may not be sufficient even when coupled with appropriate algorithms. Ultimately there must be the potential for AI to:

+   - Combine different volatility models (a process called ensemble learning)
+   - Choose and combine between different backtesting models, where strategies or models are applied to historical data
+   - Make forecast volatility models learn from errors (reinforcement learning)
+   - Create early warning systems for potential crises (neural networks)
