Despite this, assessing the firms against a cyber readiness model:

+   - Only 1 in 10 (10%) achieved expert status.
+   - More worryingly, nearly three-quarters (74%) ranked as ‘novices’, with a sharp drop in German and US firms achieving expert status.

Failures in the supply chain are also particularly noticeable, with two-thirds of firms experiencing cyber-related issues in 2018. The results are especially noticeable in the technology, media/telecoms and transportation sectors.

There’s a temptation to rely on government action and regulation to become the saviour, but Constance Bommel aer, Senior Director, Global Internet Policy at the ‘Internet Society’ (ISOC), suggests that the challenge of cyber is too strong for this to be resisted in what some might call traditional institutional ways. She blames the governmental and institutional approach that is fundamentally based around ‘policy making’ and is reactive rather than proactive. The answer, it appears, is not entirely in the world of ‘politics’ even if (according to Hiscox), the introduction of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) does appear to have had an impact, with 84% of continental (and 80% of UK) firms having made some changes.

The potential sales market for cyber security vendors is significant. According to market experts, the size of the cyber security market will be nearly US $250 billion by 2023, having shown double-digit CAGR since 2018. The US are leading the way in expenditure in terms of the global picture, with financial markets being sector leaders.28

For the purposes of market categorisation, they segment the cyber sector into three key elements:

+   - IAM (identity and access management), encryption, governance, risk and compliance
+   - UTM (unified threat management)
+   - SIEM (security information and event management)

Of these they view the IAM sector, especially encryption, as being one of the areas of focus, especially due to the amount of information being held on the cloud and on personal devices. They believe that encryption will become more topical as there is greater interest in biometric capabilities, such as facial and voice recognition. In such a competitive marketplace, technical vendors inevitably are keen to position themselves favourably. Some of the major players such as Symantec Corporation, Check Point Technology Ltd., Paloalto Networks, Fortinet and FireEye, amongst others, are vying for position against the likes of broader players like SAS and IBM.

In such a large and wide market, especially in a culture of startups and Fintech, there’s always room for new entrants who perhaps can offer a different viewpoint, as well as technical capability. One company that comes to attention is UK-based ‘Darktrace’, founded in 2013 by mathematicians and intelligence experts from the University of Cambridge, MI5, GCHQ and the CIA. Based in Cambridge and San Francisco, their capability ‘Enterprise Immune System’ uses AI to identify potential cyber threats, allowing security teams time to act.
