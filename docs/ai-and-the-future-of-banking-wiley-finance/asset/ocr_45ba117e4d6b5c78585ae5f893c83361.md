The management of currencies and transactions looks a little like a game but there are trillions of dollars at stake held by investors and pension funds. ‘Gaming theory’ ideas may be worth considering in the context of these issues, especially as automation of some or all of the rules might be on the horizon. Two particular gaming ideas come to mind, in the context of an interconnected economic environment and on which much has been written and analysed:

+   - The ‘prisoner’s dilemma’ is an example of a game where two rational participants fail to cooperate or to trust each other even though it is in both their interests to do so. It’s a situation often seen in the climate change discussion.
+   - In ‘grim trigger’, both sides will initially cooperate, but if one of the participants defects once, then the other will defect (or cheat) all the time.

# Investment Banks and Blockchain

In their 2017 report¹³ Accenture, together with benchmarking firm McLagan, indicated the use of Blockchain could enable investment banks to cut their infrastructure costs by between US $8.0 billion and $12.0 billion by 2025. In their survey of eight of the ten largest investment banks in the world, they concluded that they could ‘reduce infrastructure costs by an average of 30 percent, helped by better data quality and transparency’. Beyond this, it’s suggested that ‘costs associated with compliance, business operations such as trade support and centralized operations such as know-your-customer checks, could fall by up to 50 percent’. These savings do not allow for implementation costs. Others are more sceptical, suggesting that this is just another attempt to jump on the ‘Blockchain bandwagon’.

The decision rests at the door of the regulator, who, it is suggested, might be reluctant to upset the applecart and introduce new solutions and technologies in the clearing and settlement infrastructure without being absolutely certain about the safety, security and resilience of these new approaches.

Other countries appear to be more bullish about the use of this technology. In his article ‘The Bank of China Sees Future in Blockchain’,¹⁴ writer Anujit Mukhopadhyay reminds us that the Bank of China – one of the largest in China and a state-owned enterprise – are looking to transform their business not only through the use of data and AI but also through the use of Blockchain. The bank already uses Blockchain in as many as 12 projects (as of 2018), including cross border payment, digital currencies and data sharing, amongst others. By 2018 they had already filed 11 patents for Blockchain technologies, making them the largest Blockchain innovator in the Chinese financial services sector. According to Mukhopadhyay, one particular patent is that of compressing data in the blocks, which gives scalability. BoC’s goal is not only to digitise its entire operation but also to transfer all its operations to a digital platform and remove the need for hard currency.
