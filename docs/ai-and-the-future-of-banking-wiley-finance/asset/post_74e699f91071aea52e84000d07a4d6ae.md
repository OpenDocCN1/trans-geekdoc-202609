
## CONCLUSION

Brand is an integral part of the Future of Banking, not only as a mechanism for loyalty and customer acquisition in an era of Open Banking, but as part of a way to generate value for the organisation. Brand is not soft or fluffy but rather has hard financial dynamics attached that justify executive focus.

The service experience – which is what we experience in our interaction with the bank, including through the use of apps – will increasingly become the ‘brand experience’. When all else is the same in the banking industry as systems are powered by more or less the same technologies, the only thing that might differentiate one bank from another will be its brand.

The concept of brand will also need to change, from being some form of ubiquitous logo or slogan to something deeper and more relevant to the individual consumer. Brand might also take up new attributes such as sounds or emotional engagement, in a way that is somehow consistent across the channels but unique and hyper-personal to the individual. In the same way that there is ongoing reinvention of the concept of banking, including the traditional branch network, it is likely that the entire approach to branding will need to be reviewed, with marketers considering the topic by viewing it through an AI lens.

### INTRODUCTION

In an earlier book by this author² that looks at the future of work especially in the context of AI, the concept of work was considered and why it is so important. If we understand the notion of working, then it’s possible to better understand the notion of leadership.

Nearly all of us want to be or imagine ourselves to be ‘leaders’. To do otherwise characterises us as ‘followers’ or commercial ‘serfs’ whose roles are to dutifully fulfil the tasks allocated to us by others, even if we have some degree of control over our affairs. The expression ‘serf’ has its origins in the notion of slavery, which in turn is derived from the Roman for slave (servus). The conditions for a slave in Roman times depended entirely on the disposition of the master, who might be cruel, kind or indifferent. The Roman writer Columella wrote about the treatment of slaves, recommending the stick as well as the carrot, and amongst Romans at that time there was a general consensus about the virtues and financial benefits of a balanced approach to servitude.

The flip side of slavery is that of ‘freedom’, which the ancient Greeks recognised as they consulted the Pythia, who was the priestess at the temple known as the Oracle in Delphi. On the walls of the temple were defined the four elements of freedom:

- 1. Representation in legal matters
- 2. Freedom from arrest and seizure
- 3. The right to do as one wished
- 4. The right to go where one wished

Two thousand years later, US President Franklin D Roosevelt spoke in 1941 of a world founded on four freedoms:

- 1. Freedom of speech and expression
- 2. Freedom of worship
- 3. Freedom from want
- 4. Freedom from fear

The notions of slavery and freedom might seem to depend on our place in what is a hierarchical structure, whereas Marx suggested in his Communist Manifesto that it is somehow linked to the struggle between classes and hierarchy. Issues of ‘power’ also play a part in that equation.

What are the alignments between power, mastery, and leadership in the modern and future worlds? We define:

- ‘Power’ as ‘the ability or capacity to do something or act in a particular way’ or ‘the capacity or ability to direct or influence the behaviour of others or the course of events’.³
- ‘Mastery’ as ‘control or superiority over someone or something’⁴
- ‘Leadership’ as ‘the action of leading a group of people or an organization’ from ‘to lead’, which is to ‘be in charge or command of’, or to ‘organise and direct’, or ‘set (a process) in motion’.⁵

It all feels quite nuanced. Here are three words all with subtle characteristics or meanings. They all seem to link back to five or perhaps six traits,⁶ which are in some areas interlinked:

- 1. Knowledge where individuals are ‘knowledge masters’ and have insights that others do not
- 2. Charismatic influence, which comprises the power of charm and personality
- 3. Money or financial control, which allows an individual to direct (or withhold) funds and therefore facilitate or block certain activities
