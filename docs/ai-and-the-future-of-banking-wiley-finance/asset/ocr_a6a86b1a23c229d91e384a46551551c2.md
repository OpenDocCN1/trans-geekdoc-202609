And if that wasn’t sufficient, consider that moving on from analogue to digital thinking isn’t good enough either, and the name of the game isn’t about digitalising existing processes and ideas but rather it’s an entirely new and different ball game. What does that new ‘ballgame’ look like? Thinking about it starts to open the door to new styles of leadership, a form that might be described as being either ‘augmented’ by technology or ‘AI-infused’.

‘Augmented leadership’ indicates that the style of leadership is principally augmented by technology. When we start to look for examples, one temptation is to look at some modern leaders whose period of office is infused by and augmented by the use of social media.

The message appears to be that, increasingly, leadership and technology cannot be separated.

In business, effective operational leaders need to be absolutely engaged with appropriate technologies. This need not necessarily extend to being active users but they (or their team) must have complete awareness of the value (and risks) of these new capabilities. Normally such leaders aren’t interested, or don’t have the time to be interested in the ‘numbers’. They need quick visualisations that enable them to understand at a glance where the issues and pressure points are. Increased emphasis on visualisations by business intelligence companies that converge analytics with location intelligence opens the door for senior executives to be able to understand and to digest heavyweight analytical interpretations.

Effective visualisations are a key extension of the digital environment as complex information becomes more easily digested. What might an AI-infused environment with equivalent complexity represent for our business leaders? Will they be threatened, confused or disbelieving? Or enabled, encouraged and empowered? After all, if the truth is ‘in the data’, then how can any leader not want to use that data and insight to make effective decisions?

Increasingly, data integrity and realistic insight will gain in importance. Few leaders will be prepared to ‘buck the trend’ and make decisions that are not supported by the output of analytical insight. Those who back their own intuition rather than the data might find themselves vulnerable in terms of corporate governance.

Stakeholders who are also dependent on their ‘institutional leaders’ acting on the data are likely to be extremely critical were those leaders to reject insight from high-powered analytical interrogation.
