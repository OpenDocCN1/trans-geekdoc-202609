The source of this report, from the World Economic Forum, places the UK above the US and Canada, which some might find slightly surprising. The report explains, ‘Although the United States is a world leader in new technologies, it does not feature in the top 10. This is because the technological-readiness measurement assesses how well economies adopt existing technologies to enhance the productivity of their industries, not whether the technologies were developed in those countries.’ Perhaps technological readiness isn’t a good proxy for behavioural usage? It’s also difficult to overlook China and Asia generally in this equation, with China especially becoming very digital in nature.⁷

A report by Millennial Marketing suggests that Millennials are not necessarily ‘tech savvy’ but rather that they are ‘tech dependent’, and that there is a wide range of tech capability. In a 2007 survey based on 27 variables, the report concedes that ‘web-use skill is not randomly distributed among a group of young adults who have grown up with digital media.’ They suggest that the level of diversity may be linked to family background, socioeconomic status, and what they call the ‘context of use’.⁸

Beyond this, an Economist article suggested, ‘Only a small fraction of students may count as true digital natives. The rest are no better or worse at using technology than the rest of the population.’⁹

# Virtual Reality: Banking and Gaming Converge

Virtual reality is defined as ‘a realistic simulation of an environment, including three-dimensional graphics, by a computer system using interactive software and hardware’¹⁰ It lends itself to the banking and financial services market and especially to the Gen Z community, providing:

+   - An ability to revisit the customer experience, which can be tested and checked out rather than taken for granted
+   - The potential for ‘pure segmentation’ rather than the broad-brush approach of multi- and omni-channel
+   - A capability to differentiate beyond the relatively generic High Street presence of traditional banks
+   - An approach more specifically aligned to the Gen Z customer
+   - A less expensive distribution model than traditional ‘bricks and mortar’
+   - The potential to create new revenue streams from interested and curious stakeholders
+   - A possible modelling of income streams and financial outcomes before individual customers commit to making a decision
