# SAFEGUARDING THE BANK OF THE FUTURE:
NEW CYBER SECURITY THREATS

In considering the future of banking in an AI-context, the future of cyber threat also needs to be addressed. In his report ‘Future cyber threats and challenges: Are you ready for what’s coming?’22 information technology expert Michael Nadeau challenges the suggestion that the world is changing and the internet is changing as a result. Rather, he suggests, the internet is changing and the world is changing as a result. He looks though the other end of the telescope. If his thinking is extended, it’s possible to recognise that as the internet changes and evolves, then banking will also change as a result.

He also refers to a group called ‘The Internet Society’ (ISOC), founded in 1992 and still going strong. Their 2017 report ‘Paths to Our Digital Future’ reinforces their view that ‘the future of the internet is ours to shape for the next generation. Humanity must be at the centre of tomorrow’s Internet’.

Elsewhere Nadeau also writes about the ‘State of Cybercrime 2017: Security Events Decline, but not the Impact’. In it, he refers to the US State of Cybercrime Survey, conducted annually. Of the 510 respondents of these major respondees, the average IT security budget is US $11 million, yet the majority of respondents (61%) saw cyber security as an IT issue rather than one of corporate governance.

Beyond this, Symantec’s 2019 report23 also records multiple cyber intrusions:

+   1. ‘Formjacking’ attacks skyrocketed, with an average of 4,800 websites compromised each month. (Formjacking is an approach where hackers insert a small piece of code into the checkout page of an online supplier and from this are able to steal credit card details of the purchaser.)
+   2. A reduction in ‘cryptojacking’, which comprises the secret use of your device to ‘mine’ or access crypto currency.
+   3. ‘Ransomware’ criminals who are shifting targets from consumers to enterprises, where infections rose 12%.
+   4. More than 70 million records were stolen from poorly configured third-party supply chains, put down to rapid cloud adoption. Supply chains remain a soft target, with attacks ballooning by 78%. This especially presents a challenge to the concept of the extended universal bank, considered earlier.
+   5. Internet of Things (IoT) and devices were a key entry point for targeted attacks, and that most IoT devices are vulnerable. The same issues arise as in item 4 above, in terms of the extended universal bank trying to add value but in doing so adding vulnerability.

Items 4 and 5 are particularly important as society increasingly considers itself as being ‘connected’ (at least at a technological level). Symantec suggest that the weakest links are both in the supply chain and in the devices that will be more readily used. The concept of the ‘Connected Car’ sits alongside the ‘Connected Home’ and the ‘Connected Factory’ where the IoT indicates that associated devices are the weakest link in the chain.
