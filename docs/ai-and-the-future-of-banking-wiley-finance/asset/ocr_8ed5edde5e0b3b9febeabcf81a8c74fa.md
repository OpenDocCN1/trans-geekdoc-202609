# The Bank of the Future

# SUMMARY

> > ‘In the future, the (banking) interface will not be a branch, a computer or even a phone’.
> >
> > —Jim Marous

Some of the concepts for the Bank of the Future will be considered in this chapter, including their feasibility and practicality. The first issue is whether there is still a demand for traditional financial services and, if so, what it looks like. Will the digital bank require a High Street presence in the future, especially recognising that some of the challenger banks are growing successfully without the need to incur these costs?

We will examine different potential banking models and consider whether traditional banking is dead and what might then replace it. Responses will be coloured by considering the impact of advanced analytics and artificial intelligence in the overall mix of thinking and implementation.

The chapter offers different scenarios for the ‘Bank of the Future’, such as disaggregation, conversational banking and collaborative banking, suggesting that perhaps no single one of these will prevail but that the Bank of the Future may prove ultimately to be an amalgam.

Finally, we will explore some of the issues affecting investment banking and where transformation may happen.
