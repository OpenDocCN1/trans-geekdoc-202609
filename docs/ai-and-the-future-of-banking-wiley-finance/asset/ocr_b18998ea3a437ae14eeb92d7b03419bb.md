> ‘Only three things happen naturally in organizations’ says Drucker, ‘friction, confusion, and underperformance. Everything else requires leadership’.

The essence, therefore, is how do banks reorganise themselves and their business models not only to meet current imperatives but also those in the future, and how will this reorganisation take into account the use of advanced analytics and AI? According to Jim Marous:31

Most financial institutions understand the major trends that are impacting the banking industry as well as what needs to be done to respond to those trends.

The challenge for legacy banking organizations is prioritizing and deploying resources in alignment with these realizations.

The question is whether organizations are ready to embrace change, take risks and disrupt themselves.

Earlier in this chapter we considered issues of leadership and how it was important not only that leaders were able to win and lose but also that empowerment of the team is critical.

Stephen Balzac identified 10 key points:32

+   1. Clarification of aims and objectives
+   2. Provision of information at the point of delivery
+   3. Encouragement of constructive discussion
+   4. Understanding of what success and failure look like
+   5. Ease of providing feedback
+   6. Minimisation of disruption to routines
+   7. Establishment of key checkpoints to monitor progress
+   8. Analysis, evaluation and adjustment – and no penalising
+   9. Revision of the vision as progress is made
+   10. Relinquish power and delegate

It’s tempting to try and create some illustrative templates for the Bank of the Future. What is recognised is that the future bank is a probable combination of:

+   - Greater digitalisation
+   - New technologies working alongside legacy core technologies
+   - Greater focus on web and mobile technologies
+   - Centres of innovation and testing
+   - More APIs
+   - Personalisation of the customer experience

One very interesting example of transformation is that of Bank of America, whose chairman and CEO Bryan Moynihan described it as being ‘a technology company, wrapped around a great bank, and that’s going to be the future of what we do’, he says ‘And that’s because our customers demand it’.

Like other large banks such as Barclay, BoA have innovation hubs that support and mentor fintechs and are constantly on the lookout for technical allies.
