The process is that it ranks consumers according to their creditworthiness, sets the price of the proposed loan and allocates the capital from investors.

Despite the level of investment, margins are said to be relatively low because Zopa in effect acts as the middleman. A fee is paid both by the lender and the consumer when the loan is set up. In the event of a default in payment it is the lender (rather than Zopa) that bears the risk. According to Jaidev Janardana, Zopa’s boss, Zopa are now looking at becoming a bank ‘because the loans it writes using its own capital will be more profitable than those arranged over the peer to peer platform’.15

There are other peer-to-peer lenders. Funding Circle, the market’s biggest player and recently subject to an IPO in 2019, was the first to use a peer-to-peer lending platform for businesses rather than consumers. Since then it has facilitated £5.0 billion in loans to 50,000 small and medium-sized businesses.16 It operates in the UK, the US, Germany, and the Netherlands. Lender funds are auto-allocated, with an average rate of return for lenders of 6.5% (2017), which is well in excess of High Street saving rates. Their backers include the leading firms behind Facebook, Twitter, Skype and Betfair. Investors include local councils, universities and the British Government. ‘Funding Circle has secured an extra £40m from the Government to lend to small businesses, providing fresh capital at a time when business borrowing is facing a squeeze’ (Telegraph, January 2017).

There seems to be an element of gamesmanship in the lending process. According to one user of the bank, in the capacity of a lender of money:

> Although I’ve lost money, the average interest rate has been good. The trick is to lend the smallest amount you can to the most borrowers, so when investing, take your time allocating funds.17

Other peer-to-peer lenders include Ratesetter, Lending Club, Upstart and Prosper Marketplace. The disappointing outcome of the Funding Circle IPO in 2019 may well have put off some of the other P2P companies from adopting a similar IPO strategy, at least for the moment, but analysts suggest that this may have had more to do with market nerves than the business model itself.

P2Ps are attracting the attention of global regulators with the UK regulator FSA having created a framework that:

+   - Provides additional protection for all the customers
+   - Promotes healthy competition within the P2P industry
+   - Ensures that the platforms provide correct information and have clearly defined procedures for handling funds from the clients
+   - Guarantees that the companies deal with customers appropriately during financial difficulties18
