# Responding to Cyber Attack

In addition, the NCSC recommend the following advice²⁶ in the event that a business is subject to cyberattack:

+   - **Secure the network:** By locking down the network this will restrict the hackers from having access to data but at a cost of disrupting the business’s own operation.
+   - **Activate the ‘Response Team’**: NCSC recommend that there is a trained response team in place representing key parts of the business (IT, HR, Legal), or there is an external arrangement with a certified cyber security incident provider (such as from the ‘Council of Registered Ethical Security Testers’).
+   - **Report the incident**: In Europe, for example, the GDPR rules require a business to notify the Information Commissioners Office (ICO) within 72 hours. Cases of fraud or extortion should be reported to the police. (In 2018 the ICO fined Uber £385,000 under the Data Protection Act for a 2016 breach when the firm admitted to paying hackers $100,000 to destroy data on 50 million users and keep quiet about it.)
+   - **Review and learn**: Before returning to business as usual, it is recommended there is a complete audit of the business’s cyber strategy, and it is adjusted accordingly.

Cyber security experts Analecta, a US-based company focusing on small and medium-sized business, suggest that susceptibility to cybercrime can be reduced by 75% with adequate training, which covers issues such as legal compliance, motivation and engagement, and improved responses to attack, and develops appropriate competences.²⁷

The top five challenges to the adoption of cyber awareness programmes for businesses appear to be:

+   1. Adequate resources
+   2. Adoption or embracing of the awareness programme
+   3. Support from management
+   4. Support from end users
+   5. Finding time to take part

Whilst a banking organisation may have both policies and capabilities to manage cyber risk within the walls of its own enterprise, the problem is that its business partners may not. Constance Bommel aer, Senior Director, Global Internet Policy at ISOC, who adds that collaboration among all the actors within an ecosystem is required to promote complete security, puts it this way:

> It may be the ecosystem of banking services that your business depends on, your ISP, or the legal framework in your country that puts your business at a security disadvantage.
