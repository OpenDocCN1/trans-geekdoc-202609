Effectively managing the key business and performance drivers translates into effectively managing performance. Studying past performance can help banks understand the key reasons for success and from this, to allow key performance indicators to be prepared. In banking specifically, some of the key drivers are customer assets and liabilities, and their corresponding interest margins and profitability. It’s then possible to use techniques such as matched-funds transfer pricing to drive growth in particular areas. (Matched-fund transfer is an approach to understand how specific funds are adding to the profitability of the bank, and can assist in product development, branch optimisation and judging the efficiency of processes.)^6

+   2. **Use of Rolling Forecasts:** A rolling forecast is a financial model which, unlike static budgeting models, manages the performance of a business over a continuous period. It operates on an add/drop process where months are added/dropped as the year progresses, and has the main benefit of helping businesses operating in a fluid and sometimes volatile business environment. For example, moving from a traditional balance sheet approach to a rolling forecast can help banks swiftly and effectively understand the impact of a change in central bank rates, and what effect this might have on the business plan.

+   3. **Reporting and Visualisation:** Effective use of dashboarding and visualisation as a method of communicating data-driven information and insights, together with drilldown capability, helps users rapidly understand what is happening at a product, branch and distribution level. The integration of bi-directional location analytics also helps banks (and others) understand what is happening with a geographical perspective.

+   4. **Democratisation of Information:** This expression implies that unambiguous ‘trusted’ information is shared across the enterprise, with appropriate permissions and levels of authority, so that business users have timely access to relevant information. In this way, quicker and better decisions can be made.

+   5. **Planning tools:** The most effective planning tools and processes enable effective linkage between departments, lines of business and the enterprise. This eliminates independently initiated manual operations, and helps provide integration between development and execution of strategy, business planning and, ultimately, business execution. Relative to banking, in particular, calculations such as consolidation of business units, transfer pricing between business units, calculation of funds transfer pricing and interest margins can be both automated and driver-based. This eliminates manual errors and time-consuming reconciliation processes.
+   
+   Effective planning tools also include workflow management to aid the planning administrator in managing deadlines, especially where there is a high-participation planning process.

+   6. **Effective Analytical Tools:** These analytical processes and tools help explain variance against plans (such as Variance Analysis, aka ‘ANOVA’); tools that test ‘what if’ hypotheses and ‘recover’ scenarios; and others like predictive analytics – all of which ultimately improve decision making.^7
