# IMPLEMENTATION

When the issue of implementation is considered, what in effect is being considered is the ‘operationalisation’ of the innovated product, service or solution. This issue relates not only to organically designed (that is, internally innovated) products and services, but also to those that are created in association with an external partner, which may be a Fintech start-up. Implementation should not focus on the technology, no matter what the temptation, but should also consider issues such as how it affects the current (and perhaps future) business models, what is the value proposition from a customer viewpoint and how the innovation might be brought to market.

In the specific context of implementing an AI programme, there is a series of key components that might be described as a hierarchy, in that they range from foundational requirements, such as hardware and storage, to the ability to deliver solutions at scale and that meet particular business requirements. (See Figure 10.25.)

Cultural issues are also an important contributor to change. In their 2019 report ‘Building the AI Powered Organisation’, McKinsey propose that organisations guard against a ‘plug-and-play’ approach to AI by suggesting that adopting a number of point solutions to individual problems is not the best way forward, but rather that organisations need to adopt an enterprise-wide view of AI transformation, focusing, for example, on the entire customer journey. They say, ‘At most businesses that aren’t born digital, traditional mindsets and ways of working run counter to those needed for AI.’
