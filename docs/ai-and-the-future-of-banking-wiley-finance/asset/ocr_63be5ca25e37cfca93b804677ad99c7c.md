# INTRODUCTION

We think we know all about branding and brand awareness. After all, we live with it every day, are surrounded by it and often make our buying decisions based on it. But what is it really other than (some might say) just another tool in the back pocket of marketing professionals? According to the American Marketing Association:^2

> A brand is a name, term, design, symbol, or any other feature that identifies one seller’s good or service as distinct from those of other sellers.

It’s the image that people have in mind when considering the physical nature of the good or service, and their emotional engagement with it. In their book *Marketing Management*, Kotler and Keller say, ‘Branding is endowing products and services with the power of a brand’, which seems fairly straightforward, and there’s no shortage of information about the topic given its importance.^3

Branding is achieved in multiple ways, such as:

+   - Advertising
+   - Communication strategy
+   - Product and packaging design
+   - Customer experience
+   - Sponsoring and partnerships
+   - Visual identity of the brand (logo, website and colours).

But why does brand resonate so strongly with us, how does it create such a differentiating effect, why is it such a key component to loyalty and why will it be even more important going forward? As we understand these aspects more, what will be the impact of the digital age and the AI-era?

In his paper ‘The Importance of Brand Awareness in Consumers Buying Decisions and Perceived Risk Assessment’^4 Associate Professor Olividiou Moisescu carried out research into the relationship between retail banks and consumers, and his findings reinforce the importance of brand awareness and recognition in the decisioning process.

Moisescu equates branding in the eye of the consumer with issues of risk, as familiarity with the brand and its characteristics provides confidence in the purchasing decision.
