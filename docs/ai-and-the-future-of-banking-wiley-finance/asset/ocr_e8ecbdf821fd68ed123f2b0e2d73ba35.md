The placing of High Street branches is usually based on potential footfall and proximity to other retailers, but conveniently ignores those relatively geographically remote locations where high-net-worth customers are invariably based, or where perhaps banks (as they leverage their brand extension) might want to revisit.

Some might argue that the concept of branch performance management is relatively archaic in that it is a set of metrics reflecting a banking period of the past. It’s a fair criticism, especially in light of online banking and increasing centralisation. We should, however, assume that there is the potential for some sort of banking organisation footprint in the community, and that such a footprint needs to be measured.

Elsewhere we have drawn analogies to the banking and the retail sectors. With the current increase in online purchases relegating shops to becoming no more than ‘showrooms’, we have to ask the question of retail banks as to how far they can (or might) go with respect to replicating the retail model.

Perhaps banking will increasingly become ‘an experience’, which is especially relevant given the relatively transient nature of money as explained in the opening chapter. In that chapter we said that, in effect, money doesn’t exist and that the entire banking infrastructure and ecosystem is based on promises and trust. (The correlation between brand, brand values and trust is covered in Chapter 6.)

So perhaps we should momentarily cast our attention to the ‘banking experience’. We see this in three forms:

+   - The online experience, which might relate to the quality – or clunkiness – of the banking apps being used
+   - The call centre experience, covered later
+   - The physical branch experience, which we’ll look at in a little more detail at a later point

With regards to the ‘branch experience’, in her article ‘9 Keys to Branch Sales and Service’,^{25} Meredith Dean of FMSI (Financial Management Solutions Inc) suggests that at least in the front office, three key things matter:

+   - Waiting time: *How long do we have to wait before someone comes and asks what we want?*
+   - Assist time: *How long does the customer interact with the service representative?*
+   - Service to Product ratio: *In other words, how long does it take to upsell/cross-sell?* Ideally, according to FMSI, product interactions ranged from 69% of customer contacts at top-performing branches to 30% on the low end.

FMSI also suggests a 9-point plan for effective operations in the front office, comprising:

+   1. More ‘self-directed technology’ such as ATMs
+   2. Use of a real-time monitoring system that ensures customers aren’t waiting too long to be seen
+   3. More effective management of lobby ‘traffic’
