The banking industry is in a key transitional stage, which will be analytically and ultimately AI-fuelled and there will be important questions, the answers to which will underpin their strategy:

+   1. How quickly will AI become not only a significant part of the banking agenda but a cornerstone of a bank’s strategy going forward?
+   2. When will we see reference to AI in the annual reports not only of the major banks but of the intermediary and smaller ones as well?
+   3. When will we see banks flip over from being financial services providers using technology, to becoming technology companies with banking knowledge?

# CONCLUSION

This chapter is only meant to be an introduction or ‘prologue’ to the main topic, which is that of analytics, AI and the ‘Future of Banking’.

In an earlier book by this author, consideration was given as to how AI would impact the ‘Future of Work’, relating to all professions and industries.33 To allow that issue to be fully considered effectively required an understanding of what is meant by ‘work’. Adopting the same approach, it’s helpful to start to understand what is mean both by ‘money’ and by ‘banking’, although both are big topics and only the surface has been scratched here. Others, such as Galbraith and Fergusson, have written about these subjects in much greater depth. Galbraith’s book Money: Whence It Came, Where It Went34 was written in 1975 and is of its time, failing to mention the digital economy, which was at that time not even on the radar. Fergusson’s book The Ascent of Money,35 republished in 2009, only gives passing comment in his original version to the impact of digitalisation, which is a reflection of how much has changed within a decade or so.

Fergusson also toyed with the idea of calling his book, with due homage to Charles Darwin, ‘The Descent of Finance’. He recognised finance as a topic that was – and continues to be – evolutionary. His argument, like Darwin’s, was that until we understand the fundamental truth about money, and inter alia, the fundamental truth about banking, then we can never really understand the future. In the financial evolutionary process, we need to absolutely recognise that the purpose and functions of banks is evolving through analytics and AI. Artificial intelligence might be to the banking industry what a meteor strike was to the dinosaur population. Through this process of ‘evolution’, banks will continually need to create new and sometimes radical processes and financing models, not only to maximise their future profits and maintain their existing profitability, but also to survive in a recognisable form. As Darwin discovered, it will not be the biggest and the strongest that survive, but rather the most agile.

‘We will never understand the fundamental truth about money’, says Fergusson. But he recognises that money and financial markets are, in some way, ‘a mirror of mankind, revealing every hour of every working day the way in which we value ourselves and the resources of the world around us’. If money is the mirror, then aren’t banks the frame that contains the mirror?
