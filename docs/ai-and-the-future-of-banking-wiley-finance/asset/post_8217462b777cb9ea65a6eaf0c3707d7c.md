**Head of Innovation:** This role seeks to ensure that all ideas are managed with innovation in mind, which is usually the coordination and management of internal brainstorming and of ‘new thinking’. This is especially important with a complex organisation, which might operate in silos, or an international organisation where different territories might have different ideas. Once an idea has reached a certain level of maturity – which might involve it having been beta-tested – then it may be taken back to product leaders and designers for further input and refinement.

**Head of Product:** Having agreed on the innovation, the duty of the product manager is in effect to set it up and get it off the ground. This may involve a mixture of marketing, training, clarifying the value proposition and other various support services.

There are other job roles, such as brand and innovation manager (cultivating some new thinking that will enable investment to flow), innovation consultant, chief innovation officer, business model designer, cluster director of operational innovation, digital and innovation practice strategist and others. These roles not only serve to provide focus within the organisation for innovation but help create methodologies for implementing self-grown ideas.

## Bootcamps, Hackathons, Innovation Labs, and Other Devices

Some of the ‘mechanisms’ through which innovation happens, especially within the organisation, include techniques such as bootcamps and hackathons.

**Innovation Bootcamps:** In essence these comprise teams formed from different areas of the business. The bootcamps are usually done off site and kick off with presentations around a particular theme so that the innovation teams are educated. Part of this education is to ensure that the team are aware of market conditions so that any innovations are timely and appropriate to customer need, which may vary from geography to geography. Each team then is allocated an executive sponsor and are tasked to go away and come up with an individual innovation, which may comprise a product, solution or service. At the end of an agreed period, normally 4 or 6 weeks, each team makes a presentation and the most successful is developed into a fully fledged solution.

**24 Hour Hackathons:** Much the same as the above but much more intense, especially because the participants get little or no sleep.

**Device-Centric Innovation:** Whilst much of the focus of innovation appears to be technologically led, new ideas may not in their own right be confined just to new technological thinking. They might also relate to how different but complementary capabilities and offerings might be bundled together. With much of the thinking being concentrated on the IoT and interconnected devices, their impact on business models needs to be considered, as well as issues such as risk, liabilities and cyber security.

## IMPLEMENTATION

When the issue of implementation is considered, what in effect is being considered is the ‘operationalisation’ of the innovated product, service or solution. This issue relates not only to organically designed (that is, internally innovated) products and services, but also to those that are created in association with an external partner, which may be a Fintech start-up. Implementation should not focus on the technology, no matter what the temptation, but should also consider issues such as how it affects the current (and perhaps future) business models, what is the value proposition from a customer viewpoint and how the innovation might be brought to market.

In the specific context of implementing an AI programme, there is a series of key components that might be described as a hierarchy, in that they range from foundational requirements, such as hardware and storage, to the ability to deliver solutions at scale and that meet particular business requirements. Cultural issues are also an important contributor to change. In their 2019 report ‘Building the AI Powered Organisation’, McKinsey propose that organisations guard against a ‘plug-and-play’ approach to AI by suggesting that adopting a number of point solutions to individual problems is not the best way forward, but rather that organisations need to adopt an enterprise-wide view of AI transformation, focusing, for example, on the entire customer journey. They say, ‘At most businesses that aren’t born digital, traditional mindsets and ways of working run counter to those needed for AI.’

## Innovation or Adaptation?

For many organisations, whilst creating an enterprise-wide programme of AI change might be desirable, in practical terms this may be more difficult. The expense and risk of change is such that only incremental steps might be possible, and only then after proven demonstration of key deliverables. To overcome internal barriers such as cultural resistance, some organisations still choose to effect a quasi-innovative approach by integrating new technology into their current workflows. This avoids radical change but gradually introduces new ideas and learning in a safer way.

In many ways this goes against the mantra of new technology simply digitising existing analogue processes but at the same time it’s important that a more conservative option is also on the table for organisations less able to act radically. Business transformation specialists Futurion say that successful implementation can be done by:

- Using outside resources to reduce the implementation cycle
- Minimising approval cycles and make them both agile and speedy
- Employing agile process development methods
- Letting C-level executives and the board connect directly with technology staff and vendors
- Going off-the-shelf even if it means limiting your choices
- Getting consumer feedback but not too much or output will be clouded
- Forgetting top-down ideation but rather encouraging bottom-up innovation⁸˒⁹

Futurion’s 2017 ‘Innovation Speed to Market Report: How Financial Sector Firms Manage Innovative Projects’¹⁰ is based on a survey of 28 industry leaders, and asked three questions:

- 1. How long do innovative projects take on average?
- 2. How are projects structured?
- 3. What is the learning?

Perhaps unsurprisingly they record that projects differ considerably in duration, with the typical project length being 24 months. Undertaking development in manageable chunks is critical, they say. According to one executive, ‘Sometimes when a company’s development process is too long, you don’t even launch what you first built. . . you just scrap it before you finish it’.

However, many financial services firms appear able to move more quickly ‘if they have to’ – for example, if there are hard deadlines that must be met. The need to comply with regulation is a prime example. It is not surprising that innovators create ‘burning platforms’ if there are none already present in order to energise the organisation. On the other hand, some burning platforms originate naturally.

The most successful approaches to innovation appear to replicate what occurs in software companies, rather than behaving like traditional financial services. In terms of how these projects are structured, successful financial services businesses described their innovation process implementation as tending to be one of three different options:

**Waterfall:** A waterfall development is a linear, sequential approach to development that symbolises a logical progress of steps. Typically, this comprises identifying the requirement, analysis, design, coding (where needed), implementation, testing, operation and ultimately maintenance. This approach is considered to require meticulous record keeping.¹¹

**Agile:** This is a form of implementation where development occurs in incremental, rapid releases, with each release building on the previous functionality. Unlike the waterfall model, very little planning is needed to start the project. On the down side, key issues are that it is difficult to anticipate timing and resources needed. It’s an approach that is mainly collaborative in nature and focuses on principles rather than process.¹²

**Agile plus Waterfall:** This approach combines both the waterfall and agile approaches. It allows developers to plan out the end results of the final project to allow time/money to be forecasted while having a decent documentation of the work (Waterfall), and also to allow breaking down the project into defined phases.
