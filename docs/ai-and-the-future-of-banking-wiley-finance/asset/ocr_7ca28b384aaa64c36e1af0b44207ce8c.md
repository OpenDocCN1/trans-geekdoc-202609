In terms of how the computer analyses these, there are four broad approaches, although these can be further broken down into more granular analysis, as per Figure 5.13:

+   1. **Simple pattern matching**: This is where each word is recognised in its entirety so that the system recognises the pattern of speech. This is a very basic approach and one that is familiar in the use of call centres, where the automated phone system invites us to say our ‘selected service’ or to read out our bank account details. It usually only comprises the ability to recognise ten numbers and the computation is relatively not complex.
+   2. **Pattern and feature analysis**. This is where each word is broken into bits and each bit (such as a vowel) is recognised. In this process, the speech (in its analogue form) is digitised, converted into a graphical form and into acoustic frames each lasting 1/25th or 1/50th of a second. The output is then compared to a phonetic dictionary and the system identifies what has probably been said. Many of these systems have a form of correction process so that there is a degree of continuous learning by the system.
