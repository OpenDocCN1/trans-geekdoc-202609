In the banking sector, most of the major banks already have apps that allow their retail customers to manage their accounts and make payments, for instance. In 2019, UK award-winning National Westminster’s app boasted the following capabilities:⁵

+   - Android fingerprint login, touch ID and iPhone X FaceID
+   - Checking of account balance
+   - Viewing of transaction details
+   - Personalisation of accounts with nicknames
+   - Payment of bills to friends or someone new up to £500 (limits apply)
+   - Making future dated payments
+   - Registration of travel plans
+   - Management of 24/7 account alerts
+   - Messaging

The use of outstanding apps are one way that so-called challenger banks are especially growing their market share. By definition a challenger bank is a ‘relatively small retail bank set up with the intention of competing for business with the larger, long-established high-street banks’.

The Money Whisperer website suggest a series of key capabilities that are often found with challenger banks, and that are especially attractive to Millennials and those born later, such as:

+   - The ability to ‘lock the card’ through the app and then to unlock it if needed. Rather than needing to call the bank to freeze it (and in doing so to endure the contact centre experience), self-service ‘locking’ places the customer in control.
+   - The ability to set up an account through the app by scanning passports or other agreed ID. The system then requires the user to read a script through a video link, thus establishing identity and biometric checks.
+   - Deeper information provided in a visualised way that identifies spending by category, real time data and strong graphics.
+   - Better budgeting, including the ability to set a budget and track against it. It’s a capability that seems to resonate well with Gen Z, who believe they’re in control not only of their life but also their finances.
+   - Paying friends by sending a link rather than payment details.
+   - Creating ‘pots’ so that customers can save for special events or purchases. It’s the electronic way of what used to be done at home with a ‘piggy bank’.⁶

Typical challenger banks in the UK include Aldermore, Atom Bank, Metro Bank, Monzo, Revolut, Starling Bank, Tandem, Tesco Bank and Virgin Money, all of which have already received PRA authorisation to act as banks in the UK. Similarly, there is no shortage of challenger banks in the US, including organisations such as ‘BankMobile’, which describes itself as a digital bank created by an established US-based financial services player, Customers Bancorp.
