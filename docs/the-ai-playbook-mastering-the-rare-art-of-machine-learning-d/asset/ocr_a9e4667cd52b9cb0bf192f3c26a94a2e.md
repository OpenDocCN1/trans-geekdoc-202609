# It's about Time: Input Variables

> Time is the wisest counselor of all.
> —Pericles

Preparing the training data is a real ordeal because time matters. You're setting the stage to train a model, which later, when put to work, will input what is known at the time and output the odds on what's to come. This means the values you place in the input columns must reflect what was known at an earlier point in time than when the output column became known.

For example, let's say you pull from company logs that a user who's now in tenth grade responded to an ad one year ago. That serves as a learning case, but you've got to roll back the clock: This row of training data should show the grade as ninth, since that's what grade they were in when they responded to the ad.

It's a similar story when targeting marketing. Behavioral variables meant to serve as model inputs need the same kind of TLC. Consider a row of training data corresponding to a customer who was sent a marketing brochure last October 18 and at the time we knew he was male, lived in California, and had made ten purchases so far, as well as other factors. By two months later, on December 18, we knew that the outcome was positive—he had purchased the product.
