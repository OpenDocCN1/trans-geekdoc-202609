These mere mortals can only examine a small fraction, so inevitably they employ ad hoc heuristics to focus their endless search. Targeting auditor activities with a model would add some science to this ad hoc method. It would formalize and improve the choice of which transactions auditors spend time on.

To deploy the model, management must accept a loss of control. The model takes the reins, determining a slew of transactions that will never be audited: those scored as improbable by the model. Any fraud therein will go undetected. And yet, if management forgoes the model, succumbing to the temptation to check out those unflagged cases, they're only compromising the benefit of model deployment. The whole point of the model is to flag cases worth auditing.
