# Train the Model

Machine learning algorithms constitute the single most powerful generally applicable technology. They're also the coolest. By learning from data, they derive models that work—the models are capable of making predictions for new, unique cases. When training a model, the computer is essentially programming itself.

If you've excitedly jumped right to this chapter, then you're in good company—and yet you're exactly the person for whom I wrote the chapters that come before this one. You need to pay your business-side dues before you get to revel in this sexy rocket science. We all must fight our natural propensity to exalt the advanced tech in lieu of sufficiently obsessing over its launch—and in lieu of executing the preceding four project steps needed to make that launch possible. But if you've read all the pages before this one, you've earned the right to revel—enjoy!

This chapter delivers an accessible crash course for newcomers and business professionals to ramp up on ML algorithms. After all, these core methods have great ramifications: They will drive your large-scale operations. We'll keep it relevant by diving into the nifty principles of internal combustion, not how to change a spark plug.
