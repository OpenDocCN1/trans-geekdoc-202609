The complete chapter has been summarized in the following points:

- Unsupervised learning is a methodology for machine learning, where the model does not need to be supervised.
- Almost all types of patterns can be identified through unsupervised machine learning.
- Association and clustering are the two types of unsupervised machine learning.
- Exclusive, agglomerative, overlapping, and probabilistic are the four methods of clustering.
- Hierarchical clustering, k-means clustering, and the Gaussian Mixture Model are some important types of clustering.
- For a large dataset, association rules allow us to create associations between data objects.
- Labeled data is used to train the model in supervised learning, whereas in unsupervised learning unlabeled data is used to train the model.
- In a dataset that is valuable for detecting suspicious transactions, anomaly detection may uncover essential data points.
- The greatest downside to unsupervised learning is that detailed knowledge about data sorting cannot be accessed.

# Chapter 4: Introduction to Statistical Learning Theory

Statistical learning was introduced in the 1960s, and during that time it was a purely theoretical concept of analyzing various problems using available datasets. In the 1990s, a new type of learning algorithm was introduced using the theoretical concept of statistical learning, and it became popular with the name **Support Vector Machine**. This new technique gave a new direction to statistical learning, and since then statistical learning is not only a theoretical approach for analysis but also a tool for creating algorithms for handling and estimating multidimensional functions. Statistical learning has a major role in different areas such as industry, medical science, and other finance-related industries. Now we will discuss some examples of statistical learning problems:

- Predicting if a patient hospitalized due to a heart attack will get a second heart attack or not. This prediction is based on the patient's age, gender, past clinical history, diet, etc.
- Predicting the stock market price in six months from now according to the past performance and economic status of the company.
- Determining the numbers in a handwritten ZIP code from a digitized image.
- Estimation of the glucose level in the blood of a patient.
- Detection of the risk of prostate cancer, brain tumor, and lung cancer according to the clinical reports of the patient.
