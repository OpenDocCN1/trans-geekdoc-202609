# From Biological to Artificial Neurons

Surprisingly, ANNs have been around for quite a while: they were first introduced back in 1943 by the neurophysologist Warren McCulloch and the mathematician Walter Pitts. In their landmark paper, "A Logical Calculus of Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity," McCulloch and Pitts presented a simplified computational model of how biological neurons might work together in animal brains to perform complex computations using propositional logic. This was the first neural network architecture. Since many other architectures have been invented, we will see.

The early successes of ANNs until the 1960s led to the widespread belief that we would soon be conversing with truly intelligent machines. When it became clear that this promise went unfilled (at least for a while), finding everywhere ANNs entered a long winter. In the 1980s there was a revival of interest in connectionism (the study of neural networks), as new architectures were invented and better training techniques were developed. But progress was slow, and by the 1990s other powerful Machine Learning techniques were invented, such as Support Vector Machines (Chapter 5). These techniques seemed to offer better results and theoretical foundations than ANNs, once again the study of neural networks entered a long winter.
