# Acknowledgments

Never in my wildest dreams did I imagine the first edition of this book would get such a large audience. I received so many messages from readers, many asking questions, some kindly pointing out errata, and most sending me encouraging words. I cannot express how grateful I am to all these readers for their tremendous support. Thank you all so much! Please do not hesitate to file issues on github if you find errors in the code (or just to ask questions), to submit errata if you find errors in the text. Some readers also shared how this book helped them solve a concrete problem they were working on: If you find such feedback motivating I would love it if you could share your story with me, either privately (e.g., via LinkedIn) or publicly (e.g., in an Amazon review).

I am also incredibly thankful to all the amazing people who took time out of their lives to review my book with such care. In particular, I would like to thank François Chollet for the review of the chapters based on Keras and TensorFlow, and giving me some great, in-depth feedback. Since Keras is one of the main additions to my 2nd edition, having his author review was invaluable. I highly recommend François's excellent book Deep Learning with Python': it has the conciseness, clarity and depth of the Keras library itself. Big thanks as well as to Anwar Patel, who reviewed every chapter of this 2nd edition and gave me excellent feedback.

This book also benefited from plenty of help from members of the TensorFlow team, in particular Martin Wicke, who tirelessly answered dozens of my questions and dispatched the patch to restore the rethink, including Alexander Passons, Alain Lavoie, André Susano Pinto, Anna Reviaux, Anthony Platonios, Clemens Mewald, Dan Moldoldo, Daniel Dobson, Dustin Tran, Ed Wilder-Wildmer, Goldie Gadde, Jiri Simsa, Karim Mellison, Nick Fe, Paige Bailey, Pete Warden (who also reviewed the 1st edition), Ryan Sepassi, Sandeep Gupta, Sean Morgan, Todd Wang, Tom O'Malley, William Chang, and Yuefeng Zhou, all of whom were tremendously helpful. A huge thank to all of you, and to other members of the TensorFlow team. Not just for your help, but also for making such a great library.

Big thanks to Haesun Park, who gave me plenty of excellent feedback and caught errors while he was writing the Korean translation of the 1st edition of this book. He also translated the Jupyter notebooks to Korean, not to mention TensorFlow’s documentation. I do not speak Korean, but judging by the quality of his feedback, all his translations must be truly excellent! Moreover, he kindly contributed some of the solutions to the exercises in this book.
