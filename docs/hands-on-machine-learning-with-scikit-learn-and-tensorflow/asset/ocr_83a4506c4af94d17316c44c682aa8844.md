```
keras.layers.Dense(10, activation="relu", kernel_initializer="he_normal")
```


# Nonsaturating Activation Functions

One of the insights in the 2010 paper by Glorot and Bengio was the vanishing/
exploding problems were due to a poor choice of activation function.
Until most people had assumed that Mother Nature had chosen to use
roughly activation functions in biological neurons, they would be an excellent
choice. It turns out that other activation functions behave much better in
neural networks, in particular the ReLU activation function, mostly because it does
ot saturate for positive values (and also is quite fast to compute).

To solve this problem, you might want to use one of the ReLU function, such as
the leaky ReLU. This function is defined as LeakyReLU(z) = max(az, z) (see
Figure 11-2). The hyperparameter defines how the function "leaks": it is
slope of the function with z < 0, and is typically set to 0.1. This small opens
that leaky ReLU never dies; they can go into a coma, but they have a chance to
never wake up. A 2015 paper compared several variants of the ReLU activation
function and one of its conclusions was that the leaky always outperformed
the strict ReLU activation function. In fact, setting α = 0.2 (small leaky) seemed to
result in better performance than α = 0.01 (small leaky). They evaluated the
randomized leaky ReLU (RReLU), where α is picked randomly in a range during
training, and it is fixed to an average value during testing. It performed fairly well
 seemed to act as a regularizer (reducing the risk of overfitting the training set).
