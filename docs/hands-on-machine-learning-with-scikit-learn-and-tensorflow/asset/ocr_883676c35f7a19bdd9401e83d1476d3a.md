In this example, we create a DenseFeatures layer with two columns, and we have some data, in the form of a dictionary of features. In this case, the bucketed_income column relies on the median_income_column, the dictionary must include the "median_income" key, and similarly the ocean_proximity_embedded column is based on the ocean_proximity column, the dictionary must include the "ocean_proximity" key. Columns are handled in alphabetical order, so first we look at the bucketed income column (its name is the same as the median_income column name, plus "bucketed"). The incomes 3, 7 and 1 get mapped respectively to category_02 (for incomes between 1.5 and 3), category_0 (for incomes below 1.5), and category_4 (for incomes greater than 6). These category IDs get one-hot encoded: category 2 gets encoded as [0., 0., 1., 0.] and so on (note that buckets get one-hot encoded by default, use indicator_column()). Now on the ocean_proximity_embedded column. The "NEAR OCEAN" and "INLAND" categories mapped to their respective embeddings (which were initialized randomly). The resulting tensor is the concatenation of the one-vectors and their embeddings.
