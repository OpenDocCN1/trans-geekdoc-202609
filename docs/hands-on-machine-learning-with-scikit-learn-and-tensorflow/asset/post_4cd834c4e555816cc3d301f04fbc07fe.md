So far we have considered random sampling methods. But what if your dataset is large enough (relative to the number of attributes), but if it is not, you run the risk of introducing a significant sampling bias. When a survey decides to call 1000 people to ask them a few questions, they don't just pick 1000 people randomly in a phone book. They try to ensure that these 1000 are representative of the whole population. For example, the US population is composed of 51.3% female and 48.7% male, so a well-conducted survey in the US would try to maintain this ratio in the same: 513 female and 487 male. This is called stratified sampling: the population is divided into homogeneous subgroups called each stratum, to guarantee that the test set is representative of the overall population. If they used purely random sampling, there would be about 12% chance of sampling a skewed test set with less than 49% female or more than 54% female. Either way, the results would be significantly biased.
