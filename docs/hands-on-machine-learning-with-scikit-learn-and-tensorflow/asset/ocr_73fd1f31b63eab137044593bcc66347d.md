# Mean Average Precision (mAP)

A very common metric used in object detection is the Mean Average Precision (mAP), 'Mean Average' sounds a bit redundant, doesn't it? To understand this, let's go back to two classification metrics we discussed in Chapter 3: precision and recall. Remember the tradeoff: the higher the recall, the higher the precision. You can visualize this in a Precision/Recall curve (see Figure 3-5). To summarize this curve into a single number, we could compute its Area Under the Curve (AUC). But note that the Precision/Recall curve may contain a few sections where precision actually goes up when recall increases, especially at low recall values (you can see this at the top left of Figure 3-5). This is one of the motivations for the mAP metric.

Suppose the classifier has 90% precision at 10% recall, but 96% precision at 20% recall; there makes more sense to use the classifier at 20% recall rather than at 10%, as you get both higher recall and higher precision. Instead of looking at the precision at 10% recall, we really be looking at the maximum precision that the classifier can offer with at least 10% recall. It would be 96%, not 90%. So how do we get a fair idea of the model's performance to compute the maximum precision you can get with at 0% recall, then 10%, 20%, and so on to 100%, and then calculate the mean of these maximum precisions. This is called the Average Precision (AP) metric. When there are more 2 classes, we can compute the AP for each class, and then compute the mean (mAP). That's it!

However, in an object detection systems, there is an additional level of complexity: what if the system detects the correct class, but at the wrong location (i.e., the bounding box is completely off?) Surely we should not count this as a positive detection. One approach is to define an IOU threshold: for example, we may define that a prediction is correct only if the IOU is greater than, 0.5, and the prediction class is correct. The corresponding mAP is generally noted mAP@0.5(50%, or sometimes just AP_{50}. In competitions (such as the COCO competition), the mAP is computed for different IOU thresholds (0.50, 0.55, 0.60, ..., 0.95), and the final metric is all of these mAPs (not AP@[.50:95] or AP@[.50:95]). Yes, that's a mean average.
