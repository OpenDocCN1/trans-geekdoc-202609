So a simple approach for tuning the learning rate is to start with a large value that makes the training algorithm diverge, divide this by 3 and try again, and repeat until the training algorithm stops diverging. At that point, you won't be too far from the optimal learning rate. That said, it is sometimes useful to reduce the learning rate during tuning: we discuss this in Chapter 11.

+   - Choosing a better optimizer than Mini-batch Gradient Descent (and its hyperparameters) is also quite important. We will discuss this in Chapter 11.

+   - The batch size can also have a significant impact on your model's performance and the training time. In general the optimal batch size will be lower than 32 (in April 2018, Yann Leccun tweeted "Friends don't use mini-batches larger than 32\). A small batch size ensures that each training iteration is very fast, and a large batch size will give a more precise estimate of the gradients, in practice this does not matter since the optimization landscape is complex and the direction of the true gradients do not precisely in the direction of the optimum. However, having a batch size greater than 10 helps multiplications, so it will speed up training. Moreover, if you use Batch Normalization (see Chapter 11), the batch size should not be too small (in general less than 20).

+   - We discussed the choice of activation function earlier in this chapter: in general, ReLU activation function will be a good default for all hidden layers. For the output layer, it really depends on your task.

+   - In most cases, the number of training iterations does not actually need to be tweaked: use early stopping instead.
