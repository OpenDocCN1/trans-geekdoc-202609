

# Pattern Analysis in Distributed Systems

In distributed systems, pattern analysis techniques usually have a wider range of applications. The main goal of pattern analysis is to find structurally similar patterns in databases. Retail enterprises utilize consumer purchasing patterns to update inventory levels as a carefully designed key decision-making mechanism to gain an advantage over competitors. Enterprises adopt modeling techniques to understand customer behavior. For example, customer purchasing trends for specific services or goods, price and timing preferences, and the effectiveness of post-analysis decisions. This analysis helps retail enterprises provide better customer service, thereby increasing revenue. Retail organizations and enterprises need cutting-edge technology and need to continuously search for data to maintain market dynamics. This raises the question: Can data be transferred within the enterprise using multiple databases and combined in a common location? Maintaining databases for the number of points of sale is extremely difficult, if not impossible. Mobile wireless point-of-sale (POS) devices are also commonly used in stores that utilize real-time data to complete sales transactions. The exchange of data sources is often hindered by obstacles. Pattern analysis techniques require multiple types of data. For example, in product recommendation systems, the enterprise or owner has records (product information and user purchase data) but lacks information specifying payment habits and purchasing power. Existing databases exist in the form of small silos in most industries. This is the result of fierce market competition, customer privacy concerns, challenging administrative processes, and even resistance to data integration within departments. Therefore, combining different data from various institutions and agencies across the country is economically impractical, if not impossible [[13](#ch13.xhtml#ref13_13)].

## 2.5 Blockchain Data Security

Due to the increase in incidents involving data and user privacy, companies attach great importance to data security issues. Data breaches not only damage corporate reputation but also attract public and government attention. For example, the recent Facebook data hacking incident triggered various complaints. Therefore, some US states have re-examined their data protection legislation. The development and use of `GDPR` is the best example of data enforcement. The law grants users the legal guarantee to request erasure or withdrawal of their data. Companies found violating the law will face severe fines. Both China and the US have taken similar measures. The `Cybersecurity Law` and `General Principles of Civil Law` mandate data security. To prevent data breach issues when conducting data transactions with other parties, the law also enforces contracts (legally binding and protective). They ensure contracts comply with legal data protection requirements. These legal obligations and the adoption of new legislation create a secure data society. However, applying popular pattern analysis techniques to data without disclosing it raises a new problem [[14](#ch13.xhtml#ref13_14)].

The transaction models used in pattern analysis models usually contain stages such as data collection, data transmission to the server, data cleaning, and implementation of fusion requirements by all parties. Then, depending on the application, the cleaned data used by third parties is used for integration, construction, and extraction of patterns, helping to create models suitable for making judgments. Usually, they provide the model as a service to other enterprises. Due to the legal binding force of data protection laws, the following new data regulations and legislation now face problems. Traditional methods violate laws such as `GDPR` because users do not know how the model will be used in the future [[15](#ch13.xhtml#ref13_15)].

To create a smart home system, appliance manufacturers are committed to obtaining consumer feedback to improve their products and services. We created a Federated Learning (`FL`) system using a reputation mechanism to help appliance manufacturers train machine learning models based on consumer data, assisting manufacturers in developing smart home systems. Manufacturers can then predict future consumer demands and consumption patterns. System operation is divided into two parts. First, users use mobile devices and Mobile Edge Computing (`MEC`) servers to train the manufacturer's initial model. Customers use smartphones to collect data from various home appliances, subsequently downloading and training the base model using local data. After obtaining the signed models from regional customers, they transmit them to the blockchain. If the user or manufacturer behaves maliciously, we use the blockchain to replace the centralized aggregator of the standard `FL` system. The immutability of blockchain records makes it possible to track the behavior of dishonest buyers or producers. In the second phase, the manufacturer selects specific individuals or groups as miners, who will use the models received from customers to compute the average model. At the end of the crowdsourcing work, one of the miners elected as the temporary leader uploads the model to the blockchain. We impose Differential Privacy (`DP`) on the retrieved features and provide a novel normalization method to protect customer privacy while enhancing test accuracy. We demonstrate through experiments that when `DP` protection is applied to features, our normalization method outperforms Batch Normalization. In addition, we created an incentive system to reward participants to encourage more users to participate in crowdsourcing `FL` work [[16](#ch13.xhtml#ref13_16)].

# 3. Federated Learning

Federated Learning is very similar to distributed learning, and the two are closely related. Traditional distributed systems consist of distributed computing and distributed storage. In some respects, distributed computing is similar to the first model update `FL` proposed for Android clients. Although `FL` strongly emphasizes privacy protection, the latest research in distributed machine learning also pays great attention to privacy-preserving distributed systems. Using communication networks, multiple computers at different locations can be connected and managed by a central server to perform distributed processing, where each computer completes various components of a single task. Therefore, `FL` focuses on developing collaborative models without leaking privacy, while distributed processing mainly focuses on accelerating processing steps.
