
# Federated Learning Algorithms

## 1. Federated Stochastic Gradient Descent (FedSGD)

Traditional SGD computes gradients using a subset of data samples from the complete sample set known as mini-batches. These mini-batches may be compared with multiple clients that each have local data in a federated environment. Clients can access the primary model using FedSGD, and they can independently generate the gradients using local data. To determine the gradient descent step after receiving these gradients, the central server aggregates them depending on the number of samples on each client.

## 2. Federated Averaging (FedAvg)

The FedSGD algorithm has been extended by federated averaging. Multiple local gradient descent updates can be run by clients. The local model’s adjusted weights, not its gradients, are shared with the essential global system. The weights (model parameters) from the clients are then averaged on the global server. Federated Averaging is a refinement of FedSGD, where aggregating the weights is the same as aggregating a gradient if all clients start from the same baseline.

## 3. Federated Learning with Dynamic Regularization (FedDyn)

By adding a penalty term to the loss function, regularization in traditional machine learning approaches aims to improve generalization. In federated learning, the local losses from many devices must be combined to determine the overall loss. Because of the heterogeneity of clients, minimizing global loss is different from minimizing local loss. The FedDyn technique responds to data properties like data volume or transmission cost to create the regularization term for local losses. Dynamic regularization modifies local losses, allowing them to converge to the global loss. HeteroFL was developed as a countermeasure to well-known Federated Learning approaches that assume the architectures of the local models correspond to those of the global model. However, that doesn’t happen very often. HeteroFL can give a single global model for inference after training over a multitude of local models.

This strategy eliminates the possibility of a single point of failure, but the network architecture of the edge devices is completely responsible for the model’s accuracy. ## Key Technologies for Cloud-Edge Collaborative FL

An important technology for integrating federated learning into interactive cloud-edge design.

### Communication

Cross-device FL necessitates clients to execute several communication rounds in order for the model to converge, even though clients can connect with edge servers more effectively. Clients may have to spend resources on training for this, and the resulting communication delay is undesirable [[14]](#ch7.xhtml#ref7_14). For the implementation of federated learning in cloud-edge collaborative design, it is therefore generally acknowledged that ensuring successful communication is a vital technology. Our study has led us to four successful strategies for achieving successful interaction:

*   **Edge Computing**
*   **Aggregation Control**
*   **Model Compression**
*   **Privacy and Security**

The two most common assumptions in most cloud-based FL frameworks are: (i) The clients and edge servers used in FL are all trustworthy devices. They completely adhere to the FL manager’s demands. (ii) No individual has a copy of other clients’ data. It is not possible to build a trustworthy FL structure in the cloud-edge collaborative design without considering these two assumptions, which may lead to privacy leakage and a failure to protect against malicious attacks, upsetting the social system and having a negative effect on clients. The cloud-edge cooperative paradigm may not fully trust the edge servers and clients, and malicious attackers can easily participate in FL. Private and confidential solutions are crucial for the successful deployment of FL.

### Personalization

Clients opt to engage in FL with the overall objective of improving the model when the local dataset is too small to train a model with great accuracy. This puts a strain on privacy and consumes communication and computing resources. However, in some instances, such as when an individual contributing to cross-silo FL has a large dataset, the accuracy of the locally generated model may be better than that of the combined model [[18]](#ch7.xhtml#ref7_18). Model heterogeneity means the potential for various clients to have diverse requirements for the model. In a word prediction task, for example, multiple individuals typing the same "I like..." would undoubtedly expect various prediction outcomes. Basic customization strategies can help to some extent with model heterogeneity issues. ### Federated Learning Deployment Difficulties and Prospective Study Goals in Interactive Cloud-Edge Architecture

#### Outlier

In edge federated learning, a collection of edge devices is selected for every training session. The client selection approach was used to develop an exclusive model for every client. However, each of these methods assumes that every client can continue to connect to the edge servers. Since these devices require little energy and their connectivity surroundings are constantly changing, edge devices are prone to becoming disconnected during the training process. These disengaged clients are commonly referred to as outliers. A great deal of the previously completed research on handling outliers focuses on sustaining training while there are just a few, but they are unable to handle large numbers of outliers, and the efficacy of federated learning will be significantly reduced.

#### Aggregation

Model aggregation, one of the most significant FL operations, has a direct impact on training quality. The aggregation, which is frequently simplistic in FL environments, is unable to manage complex cloud-edge collaborative architecture. Further investigation into robust FL aggregation is still required, including consideration of accessibility collection along with additional safeguard accumulation.
