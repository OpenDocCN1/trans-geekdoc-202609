
The works of [\[15\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0075) and [\[14\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0070) have proposed FBO algorithms. The work of [\[15\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0075) has introduced the first FBO algorithm, named `federated Thompson sampling` (FTS), which addressed several important challenges in FBO. Specifically, the FTS algorithm is free from the requirement to transmit the observations of BO, requires a small number of parameters to be exchanged, and is theoretically guaranteed to be no-regret despite agent heterogeneity [\[15\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0075). The more recent work of [\[14\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0070) has extended the FTS algorithm of [\[15\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0075) to incorporate a rigorous privacy guarantee and to further improve its practical performance via the method of distributed exploration. Regarding FRL, the recent work of [\[26\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0130) has introduced the first FRL framework with a theoretically guaranteed convergence. Specifically, building on the classic policy gradient algorithm, [\[26\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0130) only requires the agents to exchange their policy gradients instead of their raw trajectories and has achieved low sample complexity by leveraging variance-reduced optimization techniques. Moreover, [\[26\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0130) has also achieved theoretically guaranteed robustness against Byzantine agents (i.e., caused by random failures or adversarial attacks), which is another important consideration in FL. These recent works on FBO [\[14\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0070), [\[15\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0075) and FRL [\[26\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0130) have been shown to be both theoretically grounded and practically effective, and we will discuss them in more detail in Sections [14.2](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#s0015) and [14.3](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#s0045), respectively. In addition to FBO and FRL, another line of recent works has extended `multi-armed bandits` [\[40\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0200), which is another classic sequential decision-making problem, to the federated setting and hence introduced federated bandits [\[16\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0080), [\[22\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0110), [\[66\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0330). These recent works on federated bandits mostly focus on the theoretical perspective, and we will discuss them in Section [14.4.3](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#s0090).

In the remainder of this chapter, we will separately discuss FBO (Section [14.2](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#s0015)) and FRL (Section [14.3](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#s0045)), including their background, representative existing works, algorithms/frameworks, as well as theoretical and empirical results. Subsequently, in Section [14.4](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#s0075), we will briefly review other recent related works on FBO (Section [14.4.1](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#s0080)), FRL (Section [14.4.2](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#s0085)) and federated bandits (Section [14.4.3](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#s0090)). Lastly, we will discuss open problems and potential future directions in the area of federated sequential decision-making (Section [14.5](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#s0095)).

### 14.2.1 Background on Bayesian optimization

`Bayesian optimization` (BO) aims to use sequential queries to maximize an objective function `f : X → R`, i.e., to find `x* ∈ arg max[x ∈ X] f(x)`, in which `X ⊂ R^d` is a discrete subset of the `d`-dimensional domain.

A GP `GP(μ(·), k(·, ·))` is characterized by its mean function `μ` and kernel function `k`. We assume without loss of generality that `μ(x) = 0` and `k(x, x') ≤ 1, ∀x, x' ∈ X`, and we mainly focus on the widely used `squared exponential` (SE) kernel here [\[14\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0070), [\[15\]](#Chp014_257-279_B9780443190377000235.xhtml#br0075). In iteration `t + 1` of BO, given the first `t` observed input–output pairs, the GP posterior is given by `GP(μ_t(·), σ_t²(·, ·))` where

```
μ_t(x) := k_t(x)^⊤(K_t + λI)^(−1)y_t,
σ_t²(x, x') := k(x, x') − k_t(x)^⊤(K_t + λI)^(−1)k_t(x')
```
