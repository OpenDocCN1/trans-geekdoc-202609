
where `w` is shared global parameter, `θ = (θ[1], …, θ[n])` are personalized models, `g_m(w, θ_m) = (1/|D_m|) ∑_{i=1}^{|D_m|} ℓ_m((w, θ_m); (x_m^(i), c_m^(i)))` is the local loss function of the *m*th client, and `0 ≤ d[w]`, `d[θ] ≤ d`. The formulation [(7.2)](#Chp007_125-141_B9780443190377000107.xhtml#deq3) covers most of the existing convex and nonconvex pFL approaches by carefully constructing the local loss `g_m(w, θ_m)` [[17]](#Chp007_125-141_B9780443190377000107.xhtml#br0085) and traditional FL objective [(7.1)](#Chp007_125-141_B9780443190377000107.xhtml#deq1) when `d[θ] = 0` and `d[w] = d`. Consequently, training different pFL models is equivalent to solving a particular instance of problem [(7.2)](#Chp007_125-141_B9780443190377000107.xhtml#deq3) and enables obtaining of a wide range of novel pFL formulations as a special case. In the cases of linear regression, logistic regression, or SVM, the general pFL's problem [(7.2)](#Chp007_125-141_B9780443190377000107.xhtml#deq3) is a convex problem. More complicated cases arise in the context of deep neural networks (DNN), convolutional neural networks (CNN), or recurrent neural networks (RNN) where a nonlinear mapping is used rather than the linear mapping `x^(T)w`. DNN, CNN, or RNN make predictions through a set of layers with a nonconvex function of the feature vector `x` by using nonlinear activation functions such as tanh, ReLu, Softmax. Then, Eq. [(7.2)](#Chp007_125-141_B9780443190377000107.xhtml#deq3) is nonconvex.

## 7.3 Review of personalized FL approaches

In this section, we provide a systematic review of existing pFL approaches.

### 7.3.1 Mixing models

The general idea for mixing model context is to learn a personalized model for each client that is the optimal mixture of optimal local and global models. Common works using mixing technique are APFL [[7]](#Chp007_125-141_B9780443190377000107.xhtml#br0035), L2GD [[16]](#Chp007_125-141_B9780443190377000107.xhtml#br0080), and model interpolation [[27]](#Chp007_125-141_B9780443190377000107.xhtml#br0135). In the APFL algorithm, a mixing parameter `α[m]` for each client *m* is adaptively learned during the pFL training process to control the mixing weights between the global and local models. At each global communication round *t*, each client maintains three different models: local version of the global `w[m]^((t))`, its own local model `θ[m]^((t))`, and the mixed personalized model `\overline{θ}_m^(t) = α_m θ_m^(t) + (1 - α_m) w_m^(t)`. The weighting factor `α[m]` can be found by solving `α[m]^(*) = arg min_{α[m] ∈ [0, 1]} g_m(α[m] θ + (1 - α[m])) w`. The value of `α[m]` is expected to be smaller if the local and global data distributions are close, and vice versa. A similar approach with APFL involving the mixing of local and global models has also been proposed in [[27]](#Chp007_125-141_B9780443190377000107.xhtml#br0135).

### 7.3.2 Model-based approaches: meta-learning

Meta-learning is known as a “learning to learn” method that can learn new and heterogeneous tasks quickly and effectively [[14](#Chp007_125-141_B9780443190377000107.xhtml#br0070), [30]](#Chp007_125-141_B9780443190377000107.xhtml#br0150). MAML [[14]](#Chp007_125-141_B9780443190377000107.xhtml#br0070) and Reptile [[30]](#Chp007_125-141_B9780443190377000107.xhtml#br0150) are two well-known meta-learning algorithms and are widely used by their ability to fast adapt to new heterogeneous tasks. A common meta-learning process includes meta-training and meta-testing steps. The meta-training step in MAML can be mapped to the FL global model training process, while the meta-testing step can be mapped to the FL personalization process. Inspired by the idea of applying meta-learning to find the meta global model which can quickly adapt to the client's data to improve the performance of FL over non-i.i.d. data, the authors of [[13]](#Chp007_125-141_B9780443190377000107.xhtml#br0065) and [[9]](#Chp007_125-141_B9780443190377000107.xhtml#br0045) came up with Per-FedAvg and pFedMe algorithms for pFL.
