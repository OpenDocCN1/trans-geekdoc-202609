## 20.1.4 Differential privacy and federated speech assistant

After understanding the foundation of federated speech model design, we will connect to the privacy-preserving federated speech modeling that could incorporate the new data regulation requirements providing measurable privacy loss through a reliable federated system. More specifically, we focus on differential privacy [[1](#Chp020_353-368_B9780443190377000302.xhtml#br0005),[18]](#Chp020_353-368_B9780443190377000302.xhtml#br0090) (DP) preserving federated learning framework, where federated learning with DP aims at offering a quantitative privacy measure. DP satisfies some of the requirements specified in the GDPR guidelines because of its advantages in protecting both data privacy (e.g., user identity) and model parameters (e.g., weights and gradient information). DP algorithms [[1](#Chp020_353-368_B9780443190377000302.xhtml#br0005),[20]](#Chp020_353-368_B9780443190377000302.xhtml#br0100) is aiming to provide quantitative guarantees and further prevent identity (e.g., accent) inference. We define a mathematical formation of differential privacy and investigate potential impacts on speech processing in the following discussion, where differential privacy mechanism [[18]](#Chp020_353-368_B9780443190377000302.xhtml#br0090) has been recognized as an established standard to deploy algorithms with a target privacy guarantee.

We will introduce some recent developments on extending and developing the Renyi divergence [[42](#Chp020_353-368_B9780443190377000302.xhtml#br0210),[62]](#Chp020_353-368_B9780443190377000302.xhtml#br0310) measurement from the definition of local differential privacy to the context of speech processing and provide a theoretical justification on some advantages of combining an ensemble learning framework for DP-preserving speech processing.

We first elaborate `ε`-DP for supervised speech recognition, where `ε` denotes a privacy budget forming from a random noise mechanism deploying on the training speech dataset. A smaller `ε` indicates a large noisy distortion in a space with `ℓ[p]`-norm bounded (`p` ≥ 1) distance [[64]](#Chp020_353-368_B9780443190377000302.xhtml#br0320). We define a mathematical formation of differential privacy and investigate potential impacts on speech processing in the following discussion, where differential privacy mechanism [[18]](#Chp020_353-368_B9780443190377000302.xhtml#br0090) has been recognized as an established standard to deploy algorithms with a target privacy guarantee.

### Definition 20.1

A randomized algorithm `ℳ` with domain `𝒟` and range `ℛ` is (`ε`, `δ`)-differentially private if for any two neighboring inputs (e.g., speech data) `d`, `d'` ∈ `𝒟` and for any subset of output predictions (e.g., labels) `S` ⊆ `ℛ`, the following stands:

`Pr[ℳ(d) ∈ S] ≤ e^(ε)Pr [ℳ(d')∈S] + δ`.

(20.1)

The definition above produces a notion of privacy that can be explained as a measure of the probabilistic difference of a specific outcome by a multiplicative factor, `e^(ε)`, and an additive amount, `δ`. The `DP` mechanism with post-processing [[1]](#Chp020_353-368_B9780443190377000302.xhtml#br0005) (e.g., batch-wise training) is under a general Renyi-divergence [[42]](#Chp020_353-368_B9780443190377000302.xhtml#br0210) measurement with order `α` ∈ (1, ∞), called `RDP[α]`, for all neighboring data sets `d`, `d'`:

```
RDP_α(M(d)||M(d')) = 1/(α - 1) log S_θ ~ M(d')[(p_M(d)(θ)/p_M(d')(θ))^α] ≤ ε.
```
