
We want to estimate this gradient from individual transitions data. So we express each of the 3 terms forming the product in the gradient expression above as expectations of functions of individual transitions $s\overset{\pi}{\rightarrow}(r,s^{\prime})$. Denoting `r + γ ⋅ ϕ(s′)^(T) ⋅ w − ϕ(s)^(T) ⋅ w` as `δ`, we get:

$$
\begin{array}{r} {\mathbf{\Phi}^{T} \cdot \mathbf{D} \cdot \mathbf{\delta}_{\mathbf{w}} = \mathbb{E}[\delta \cdot \phi(s)]} \\ \end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{r} {\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}{(\mathbf{\Phi}^{T} \cdot \mathbf{D} \cdot \mathbf{\delta}_{\mathbf{w}})}^{T} = \mathbb{E}[(\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\delta) \cdot \phi{(s)}^{T}] = \mathbb{E}[(\gamma \cdot \phi(s^{\prime}) - \phi(s)) \cdot \phi{(s)}^{T}]} \\ \end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{r} {\mathbf{\Phi}^{T} \cdot \mathbf{D} \cdot \mathbf{\Phi} = \mathbb{E}[\phi(s) \cdot \phi{(s)}^{T}]} \\ \end{array}
$$

Substituting, we get:

$$
\begin{array}{r} {\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}) = 2 \cdot \mathbb{E}[(\gamma \cdot \phi(s^{\prime}) - \phi(s)) \cdot \phi{(s)}^{T}] \cdot {(\mathbb{E}[\phi(s) \cdot \phi{(s)}^{T}])}^{- 1} \cdot \mathbb{E}[\delta \cdot \phi(s)]} \\ \end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{r} {\Delta\mathbf{w} = - \alpha \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w})} \\ \end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{r} {= \alpha \cdot \mathbb{E}[(\phi(s) - \gamma \cdot \phi(s^{\prime})) \cdot \phi{(s)}^{T}] \cdot {(\mathbb{E}[\phi(s) \cdot \phi{(s)}^{T}])}^{- 1} \cdot \mathbb{E}[\delta \cdot \phi(s)]} \\ \end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{r} {= \alpha \cdot (\mathbb{E}[\phi(s) \cdot \phi{(s)}^{T}] - \gamma \cdot \mathbb{E}[\phi(s^{\prime}) \cdot \phi{(s)}^{T}] \cdot {(\mathbb{E}[\phi(s) \cdot \phi{(s)}^{T}])}^{- 1} \cdot \mathbb{E}[\delta \cdot \phi(s)]} \\ \end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{r} {= \alpha \cdot (\mathbb{E}[\delta \cdot \phi(s)] - \gamma \cdot \mathbb{E}[\phi(s^{\prime}) \cdot \phi{(s)}^{T}] \cdot {(\mathbb{E}[\phi(s) \cdot \phi{(s)}^{T}])}^{- 1} \cdot \mathbb{E}[\delta \cdot \phi(s)])} \\ \end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{r} {= \alpha \cdot (\mathbb{E}[\delta \cdot \phi(s)] - \gamma \cdot \mathbb{E}[\phi(s^{\prime}) \cdot \phi{(s)}^{T}] \cdot \mathbf{\theta})} \\ \end{array}
$$

where `θ = (𝔼[ϕ(s) ⋅ ϕ(s)^(T)])^(−1) ⋅ 𝔼[δ ⋅ ϕ(s)]` is the solution to the weighted least-squares linear regression of `B^(π) ⋅ V − V` against `Φ`, with weights as `μ[π]`.

We can perform this gradient descent with a technique known as `Cascade Learning`, which involves simultaneously updating both `w` and `θ` (with `θ` converging faster). The updates are as follows:

$$
\begin{array}{r} {\Delta\mathbf{w} = \alpha \cdot \delta \cdot \phi(s) - \alpha \cdot \gamma \cdot \phi(s^{\prime}) \cdot (\phi{(s)}^{T} \cdot \mathbf{\theta})} \\ \end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{r} {\Delta\mathbf{\theta} = \beta \cdot (\delta - \phi{(s)}^{T} \cdot \mathbf{\theta}) \cdot \phi(s)} \\ \end{array}
$$

where `β` is the learning rate for `θ`. Note that `ϕ(s)^(T) ⋅ θ` operates as an estimate of the TD error `δ` for current state `s`.

Repeating what we had said in [Chapter 12](#chapter12.xhtml), Gradient TD converges reliably for the Prediction problem even when we are faced with the Deadly Triad of `[Bootstrapping, Off-Policy, Non-Linear Function Approximation]`. The picture is less rosy for Control. Gradient Q-Learning (Gradient TD for Off-Policy Control) converges reliably for both on-policy and off-policy linear function approximations, but there are divergence issues for non-linear function approximations. For Control problems with non-linear function approximations (especially, neural network approximations with off-policy learning), one can leverage the approach of the DQN algorithm (Experience Replay with fixed Target Network helps overcome the Deadly Triad).

## 13.9 KEY TAKEAWAYS FROM THIS CHAPTER

*   Batch RL makes efficient use of data.

*   DQN uses Experience-Replay and fixed Q-learning targets, avoiding the pitfalls of time-correlation and varying TD Target.

*   LSTD is a direct (gradient-free) solution of Batch TD Prediction.

*   LSPI is an Off-Policy, Experience-Replay Control Algorithm using LSTDQ for Policy Evaluation.

*   Optimal Exercise of American Options can be tackled with LSPI and Deep Q-Learning algorithms.

*   For Prediction, the 4 Value Function vectors of interest in the `Φ` subspace are `w[π]`, `w[BE]`, `w[TDE]`, `w[PBE]` with `w[PBE]` as the key sought-after function approximation for Value Function `V^(π)`.

*   For Prediction, Gradient TD solves for `w[PBE]` efficiently and robustly in the case of non-linear function approximation and in the case of Off-Policy.

It's time to take stock of what we have learnt so far to set up context for this chapter. So far, we have covered a range of RL Control algorithms, all of which are based on Generalized Policy Iteration (GPI). All of these algorithms perform GPI by learning the Q-Value Function and improving the policy by identifying the action that fetches the best Q-Value (i.e., action value) for each state. Notice that the way we implemented this `best action identification` is by sweeping through all the actions for each state. This works well only if the set of actions for each state is reasonably small. But if the action space is large/continuous, we have to resort to some sort of optimization method to identify the best action for each state, which is potentially complicated and expensive.
