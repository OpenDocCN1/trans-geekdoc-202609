
$$\\begin{array}{r} {{\\int\_{t}^{T}e^{- \\rho(s - t)}} \\cdot \\frac{c\_{s}^{1 - \\gamma}}{1 - \\gamma} \\cdot ds + \\frac{e^{- \\rho(T - t)} \\cdot \\epsilon^{\\gamma} \\cdot W\_{T}^{1 - \\gamma}}{1 - \\gamma}} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

Our goal is to find the `Policy` :(`t`,`W`[*t*])→(`π`[*t*],`c`[*t*]) that maximizes the `Expected Return`. Note the important constraint that `c`[*t*] ≥ 0, but `π`[*t*] is unconstrained.

Our first step is to write out the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) Equation (the analog of the Bellman Optimality Equation in continuous-time). We denote the Optimal Value Function as `V`^(\*) such that the Optimal Value for wealth `W`[*t*] at time `t` is `V`^(\*)(`t`, `W`[*t*]). Note that unlike [Section 5.13](#chapter5.xhtml#sec5_13) in [Chapter 5](#chapter5.xhtml) where we denoted the Optimal Value Function as a time-indexed sequence `V`[*t*]^(\*)( ⋅ ), here we make `t` an explicit functional argument of `V`^(\*). This is because in the continuous-time setting, we are interested in the time-differential of the Optimal Value Function. [Appendix D](#appD.xhtml#appD) provides the derivation of the general HJB formulation (Equation (D.1) in [Appendix D](#appD.xhtml#appD))—this general HJB Equation specializes here to the following:

$$\\begin{array}{r} {\\max\_{\\pi\_{t},c\_{t}}\\left\\{ \\mathbb{E}\_{t}\\lbrack dV^{\*}(t,W\_{t}) + \\frac{c\_{t}^{1 - \\gamma}}{1 - \\gamma} \\cdot dt \\right\\} = \\rho \\cdot V^{\*}(t,W\_{t}) \\cdot dt} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(8.2)

Now use Ito's Lemma on `dV`^(\*), remove the `dz`[*t*] term since it's a martingale, and divide throughout by `dt` to produce the HJB Equation in partial-differential form for any 0 ≤ `t` < `T`, as follows (the general form of this transformation appears as Equation (D.2) in [Appendix D](#appD.xhtml#appD)):

$$\\begin{array}{r} {\\max\_{\\pi\_{t},c\_{t}}\\left\\{ \\frac{\\partial V^{\*}}{\\partial t} + \\frac{\\partial V^{\*}}{\\partial W\_{t}} \\cdot ((\\pi\_{t}(\\mu - r) + r)W\_{t} - c\_{t}) + \\frac{\\partial^{2}V^{\*}}{\\partial W\_{t}{}^{2}} \\cdot \\frac{\\pi\_{t}^{2} \\cdot \\sigma^{2} \\cdot W\_{t}^{2}}{2} + \\frac{c\_{t}^{1 - \\gamma}}{1 - \\gamma} \\right\\} = \\rho \\cdot V^{\*}(t,W\_{t})} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(8.3)

This HJB Equation is subject to the terminal condition:

$$\\begin{array}{r} {V^{\*}(T,W\_{T}) = \\epsilon^{\\gamma} \\cdot \\frac{W\_{T}^{1 - \\gamma}}{1 - \\gamma}} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

Let us write Equation (8.3) more succinctly as:

$$\\begin{array}{r} {\\max\_{\\pi\_{t},c\_{t}}\\Phi(t,W\_{t};\\pi\_{t},c\_{t}) = \\rho \\cdot V^{\*}(t,W\_{t})} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(8.4)

It pays to emphasize again that we are working with the constraints `W`[*t*] > 0, `c`[*t*] ≥ 0 for 0 ≤ `t` < `T`

To find optimal `π`[*t*]^(\*), `c`[*t*]^(\*), we take the partial derivatives of `Φ`(`t`, `W`[*t*]; `π`[*t*], `c`[*t*]) with respect to `π`[*t*] and `c`[*t*], and equate to 0 (first-order conditions for Φ). The partial derivative of Φ with respect to `π`[*t*] is:

$$\\begin{array}{r} {(\\mu - r) \\cdot \\frac{\\partial V^{\*}}{\\partial W\_{t}} + \\frac{\\partial^{2}V^{\*}}{\\partial W\_{t}{}^{2}} \\cdot \\pi\_{t} \\cdot \\sigma^{2} \\cdot W\_{t} = 0} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

$$\\begin{array}{r} \\left. \\Rightarrow\\pi\_{t}^{\*} = \\frac{- \\frac{\\partial V^{\*}}{\\partial W\_{t}} \\cdot (\\mu - r)}{\\frac{\\partial^{2}V^{\*}}{\\partial W\_{t}{}^{2}} \\cdot \\sigma^{2} \\cdot W\_{t}} \\right. \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(8.5)

The partial derivative of Φ with respect to `c`[*t*] is:

$$\\begin{array}{r} {- \\frac{\\partial V^{\*}}{\\partial W\_{t}} + {(c\_{t}^{\*})}^{- \\gamma} = 0} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

$$\\begin{array}{r} \\left. \\Rightarrow c\_{t}^{\*} = {(\\frac{\\partial V^{\*}}{\\partial W\_{t}})}^{\\frac{- 1}{\\gamma}} \\right. \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(8.6)

Now substitute `π`[*t*]^(\*) (from Equation (8.5))and `c`[*t*]^(\*) (from Equation (8.6)) in `Φ`(`t`, `W`[*t*]; `π`[*t*], `c`[*t*]) (in Equation (8.3)) and equate to `ρ` ⋅ `V`^(\*)(`t`, `W`[*t*]). This gives us the Optimal Value Function Partial Differential Equation (PDE):

$$\\begin{array}{r} {\\frac{\\partial V^{\*}}{\\partial t} - \\frac{{(\\mu - r)}^{2}}{2\\sigma^{2}} \\cdot \\frac{{(\\frac{\\partial V^{\*}}{\\partial W\_{t}})}^{2}}{\\frac{\\partial^{2}V^{\*}}{\\partial W\_{t}{}^{2}}} + \\frac{\\partial V^{\*}}{\\partial W\_{t}} \\cdot r \\cdot W\_{t} + \\frac{\\gamma}{1 - \\gamma} \\cdot {(\\frac{\\partial V^{\*}}{\\partial W\_{t}})}^{\\frac{\\gamma - 1}{\\gamma}} = \\rho \\cdot V^{\*}(t,W\_{t})} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(8.7)

The boundary condition for this PDE is:

$$\\begin{array}{r} {V^{\*}(T,W\_{T}) = \\epsilon^{\\gamma} \\cdot \\frac{W\_{T}^{1 - \\gamma}}{1 - \\gamma}} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

The second-order conditions for Φ are satisfied under the assumptions: $c\_{t}^{\*} > 0,W\_{t} > 0,\\frac{\\partial^{2}V^{\*}}{\\partial W\_{t}{}^{2}} < 0$ for all 0 ≤ `t` < `T` (we will later show that these are all satisfied in the solution we derive), and for concave `U`( ⋅ ), i.e., `γ` > 0

Next, we want to reduce the PDE (8.7) to an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) so we can solve the (simpler) ODE. Towards this goal, we surmise with a guess solution in terms of a deterministic function (`f`) of time:

$$\\begin{array}{r} {V^{\*}(t,W\_{t}) = f{(t)}^{\\gamma} \\cdot \\frac{W\_{t}^{1 - \\gamma}}{1 - \\gamma}} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(8.8)

Then,

$$\\begin{array}{r} {\\frac{\\partial V^{\*}}{\\partial t} = \\gamma \\cdot f{(t)}^{\\gamma - 1} \\cdot f^{\\prime}(t) \\cdot \\frac{W\_{t}^{1 - \\gamma}}{1 - \\gamma}} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(8.9)

$$\\begin{array}{r} {\\frac{\\partial V^{\*}}{\\partial W\_{t}} = f{(t)}^{\\gamma} \\cdot W\_{t}^{- \\gamma}} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(8.10)

$$\\begin{array}{r} {\\frac{\\partial^{2}V^{\*}}{\\partial W\_{t}{}^{2}} = - f{(t)}^{\\gamma} \\cdot \\gamma \\cdot W\_{t}^{- \\gamma - 1}} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(8.11)

Substituting the guess solution in the PDE, we get the simple ODE:

$$\\begin{array}{r} {f^{\\prime}(t) = \\nu \\cdot f(t) - 1} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(8.12)

where

$$\\begin{array}{r} {\\nu = \\frac{\\rho - (1 - \\gamma) \\cdot (\\frac{{(\\mu - r)}^{2}}{2\\sigma^{2}\\gamma} + r)}{\\gamma}} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

We note that the bequest function `B`(`T`) = *ϵ*^(`γ`) proves to be convenient in order to fit the guess solution for `t` = `T`. This means the boundary condition for this ODE is: `f`(`T`) = *ϵ*. Consequently, this ODE together with this boundary condition has a simple enough solution, as follows:

$$\\begin{array}{l} {f(t) = \\left\\{ \\begin{array}{ll} \\frac{1 + (\\nu\\epsilon - 1) \\cdot e^{- \\nu(T - t)}}{\\nu} & {\\text{for~}\\nu \\neq 0} \\\\ {T - t + \\epsilon} & {\\text{for~}\\nu = 0} \\\\ \\end{array}~ \\right.} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(8.13)

Substituting `V`^(\*) (from Equation (8.8)) and its partial derivatives (from Equations (8.9), (8.10) and (8.11)) in Equations (8.5) and (8.6), we get:

$$\\begin{array}{r} {\\pi^{\*}(t,W\_{t}) = \\frac{\\mu - r}{\\sigma^{2}\\gamma}} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(8.14)

$$\\begin{array}{l} {c^{\*}(t,W\_{t}) = \\frac{W\_{t}}{f(t)} = \\left\\{ \\begin{array}{ll} \\frac{\\nu \\cdot W\_{t}}{1 + (\\nu\\epsilon - 1) \\cdot e^{- \\nu(T - t)}} & {\\text{for~}\\nu \\neq 0} \\\\ \\frac{W\_{t}}{T - t + \\epsilon} & {\\text{for~}\\nu = 0} \\\\ \\end{array}~ \\right.} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(8.15)

Finally, substituting the solution for `f`(`t`) (Equation (8.13)) in Equation (8.8), we get:

$$\\begin{array}{l} {V^{\*}(t,W\_{t}) = \\left\\{ \\begin{array}{ll} {\\frac{{(1 + (\\nu\\epsilon - 1) \\cdot e^{- \\nu(T - t)})}^{\\gamma}}{\\nu^{\\gamma}} \\cdot \\frac{W\_{t}^{1 - \\gamma}}{1 - \\gamma}} & {\\text{for~}\\nu \\neq 0} \\\\ \\frac{{(T - t + \\epsilon)}^{\\gamma} \\cdot W\_{t}^{1 - \\gamma}}{1 - \\gamma} & {\\text{for~}\\nu = 0} \\\\ \\end{array}~ \\right.} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(8.16)
