
These results for the simple case of no-market-making-after-time-*t* serve as approximations for our problem of optimal market-making. Think of *Q*[*t*]^((*m*)) as a *pseudo mid price* for the market-maker, an adjustment to the OB mid price *S*[*t*] that takes into account the magnitude and sign of *I*[*t*]. If the market-maker is long inventory (*I*[*t*] > 0), then *Q*[*t*]^((*m*)) < *S*[*t*], which makes intuitive sense since the market-maker is interested in reducing her risk of inventory buildup and so, would be be more inclined to sell than buy, leading her to show bid and ask prices whose average is lower than the OB mid price *S*[*t*]. Likewise, if the market-maker is short inventory (*I*[*t*] < 0), then *Q*[*t*]^((*m*)) > *S*[*t*] indicating inclination to buy rather than sell.

Armed with this intuition, we come back to optimal market-making, observing from Equations (10.16) and (10.17):

$$\\begin{array}{r} {P\_{t}^{(b)}{}^{\*} < Q\_{t}^{(b)} < Q\_{t}^{(m)} < Q\_{t}^{(a)} < P\_{t}^{(a)}{}^{\*}} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

Visualize this ascending sequence of prices \[*P*[*t*]^((*b*))^(\*), *Q*[*t*]^((*b*)), *Q*[*t*]^((*m*)), *Q*[*t*]^((*a*)), *P*[*t*]^((*a*))^(\*)\] as jointly sliding up/down (relative to OB mid price *S*[*t*]) as a function of the inventory *I*[*t*]'s magnitude and sign, and perceive *P*[*t*]^((*b*))^(\*), *P*[*t*]^((*a*))^(\*) in terms of their spreads to the *pseudo mid price* *Q*[*t*]^((*m*)):

$$\\begin{array}{r} {Q\_{t}^{(b)} - P\_{t}^{(m)}{}^{\*} = \\frac{Q\_{t}^{(b)} + Q\_{t}^{(a)}}{2} + \\frac{1}{\\gamma} \\cdot \\log(1 - \\gamma \\cdot \\frac{f^{(b)}(\\delta\_{t}^{(b)}{}^{\*})}{\\frac{\\partial f^{(b)}}{\\partial\\delta\_{t}^{(b)}}()(\\delta\_{t}^{(b)}{}^{\*})})} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

$$\\begin{array}{r} {P\_{t}^{(a)}{}^{\*} - Q\_{t}^{(m)} = \\frac{Q\_{t}^{(b)} + Q\_{t}^{(a)}}{2} + \\frac{1}{\\gamma} \\cdot \\log(1 - \\gamma \\cdot \\frac{f^{(a)}(\\delta\_{t}^{(a)}{}^{\*})}{\\frac{\\partial f^{(a)}}{\\partial\\delta\_{t}^{(a)}}()(\\delta\_{t}^{(a)}{}^{\*})})} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

### 10.3.3 Analytical Approximation to the Solution to Avellaneda-Stoikov Formulation

The PDE (10.18) we derived above for *θ* and the associated implicit Equations (10.16) and (10.17) for *δ*[*t*]^((*b*))^(\*), *δ*[*t*]^((*a*))^(\*) are messy. So we make some assumptions, simplify, and derive analytical approximations(as presented in the Avellaneda-Stoikov paper). We start by assuming a fairly standard functional form for *f*^((*b*)) and *f*^((*a*)):

$$\\begin{array}{r} {f^{(b)}(\\delta) = f^{(a)}(\\delta) = c \\cdot e^{- k \\cdot \\delta}} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

This reduces Equations (10.16) and (10.17) to:

$$\\begin{array}{r} {\\delta\_{t}^{(b)}{}^{\*} = S\_{t} - Q\_{t}^{(b)} + \\frac{1}{\\gamma} \\cdot \\log(1 + \\frac{\\gamma}{k})} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(10.19)

$$\\begin{array}{r} {\\delta\_{t}^{(a)}{}^{\*} = Q\_{t}^{(a)} - S\_{t} + \\frac{1}{\\gamma} \\cdot \\log(1 + \\frac{\\gamma}{k})} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(10.20)

which means *P*[*t*]^((*b*))^(\*) and *P*[*t*]^((*a*))^(\*) are equidistant from *Q*[*t*]^((*m*)). Substituting these simplified *δ*[*t*]^((*b*))^(\*), *δ*[*t*]^((*a*))^(\*) in Equation (10.18) reduces the PDE to:

$$\\begin{aligned} & {\\frac{\\partial\\theta}{\\partial t} + \\frac{\\sigma^{2}}{2} \\cdot (\\frac{\\partial^{2}\\theta}{\\partial S\_{t}{}^{2}} - \\gamma \\cdot {(\\frac{\\partial\\theta}{\\partial S\_{t}})}^{2}) + \\frac{c}{k + \\gamma} \\cdot (e^{- k \\cdot \\delta\_{t}^{(b)}{}^{\*}} + e^{- k \\cdot \\delta\_{t}^{(a)}{}^{\*}}) = 0} \\\\ & {\\text{~with~boundary~condition~}\\theta(T,S\_{T},I\_{T}) = I\_{T} \\cdot S\_{T}} \\\\ \\end{aligned}$$  

(10.21)

Note that this PDE (10.21) involves *δ*[*t*]^((*b*))^(\*) and *δ*[*t*]^((*a*))^(\*). However, Equations (10.19), (10.20), (10.14) and (10.15) enable expressing *δ*[*t*]^((*b*))^(\*) and *δ*[*t*]^((*a*))^(\*) in terms of *θ*(*t*, *S*[*t*], *I*[*t*] − 1), *θ*(*t*, *S*[*t*], *I*[*t*]) and *θ*(*t*, *S*[*t*], *I*[*t*] + 1). This gives us a PDE just in terms of *θ*. Solving that PDE for *θ* would give us not only *V*^(\*)(*t*, *S*[*t*], *W*[*t*], *I*[*t*]) but also *δ*[*t*]^((*b*))^(\*) and *δ*[*t*]^((*a*))^(\*) (using Equations (10.19), (10.20), (10.14) and (10.15)). To solve the PDE, we need to make a couple of approximations.

First, we make a linear approximation for *e*^( − *k* ⋅ *δ*[*t*]^((*b*))^(\*)) and *e*^( − *k* ⋅ *δ*[*t*]^((*a*))^(\*)) in PDE (10.21) as follows:

$$\\begin{array}{r} {\\frac{\\partial\\theta}{\\partial t} + \\frac{\\sigma^{2}}{2} \\cdot (\\frac{\\partial^{2}\\theta}{\\partial S\_{t}{}^{2}} - \\gamma \\cdot {(\\frac{\\partial\\theta}{\\partial S\_{t}})}^{2}) + \\frac{c}{k + \\gamma} \\cdot (1 - k \\cdot \\delta\_{t}^{(b)}{}^{\*} + 1 - k \\cdot \\delta\_{t}^{(a)}{}^{\*}) = 0} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(10.22)

Combining the Equations (10.19), (10.20), (10.14) and (10.15) gives us:

$$\\begin{array}{r} {\\delta\_{t}^{(b)}{}^{\*} + \\delta\_{t}^{(a)}{}^{\*} = \\frac{2}{\\gamma} \\cdot \\log(1 + \\frac{\\gamma}{k}) + 2\\theta(t,S\_{t},I\_{t}) - \\theta(t,S\_{t},I\_{t} + 1) - \\theta(t,S\_{t},I\_{t} - 1)} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

With this expression for *δ*[*t*]^((*b*))^(\*) + *δ*[*t*]^((*a*))^(\*), PDE (10.22) takes the form:

$$\\begin{aligned} {\\frac{\\partial\\theta}{\\partial t} + \\frac{\\sigma^{2}}{2} \\cdot} & {(\\frac{\\partial^{2}\\theta}{\\partial S\_{t}{}^{2}} - \\gamma \\cdot {(\\frac{\\partial\\theta}{\\partial S\_{t}})}^{2}) + \\frac{c}{k + \\gamma} \\cdot (2 - \\frac{2k}{\\gamma} \\cdot \\log(1 + \\frac{\\gamma}{k})} \\\\ & {- k \\cdot (2\\theta(t,S\_{t},I\_{t}) - \\theta(t,S\_{t},I\_{t} + 1) - \\theta(t,S\_{t},I\_{t} - 1))) = 0} \\\\ \\end{aligned}$$  

(10.23)

To solve PDE (10.23), we consider the following asymptotic expansion of *θ* in *I*[*t*]:

$$\\begin{array}{r} {\\theta(t,S\_{t},I\_{t}) = {\\sum\\limits\_{n = 0}^{\\infty}\\frac{I\_{t}^{n}}{n!}} \\cdot \\theta^{(n)}(t,S\_{t})} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

So we need to determine the functions *θ*^((*n*))(*t*, *S*[*t*]) for all *n* = 0, 1, 2, …

For tractability, we approximate this expansion to the first 3 terms:

$$\\begin{array}{r} {\\theta(t,S\_{t},I\_{t}) \\approx \\theta^{(0)}(t,S\_{t}) + I\_{t} \\cdot \\theta^{(1)}(t,S\_{t}) + \\frac{I\_{t}^{2}}{2} \\cdot \\theta^{(2)}(t,S\_{t})} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

We note that the Optimal Value Function *V*^(\*) can depend on *S*[*t*] only through the current *Value of the Inventory* (i.e., through *I*[*t*] ⋅ *S*[*t*]), i.e., it cannot depend on *S*[*t*] in any other way. This means *V*^(\*)(*t*, *S*[*t*], *W*[*t*], 0) =  − *e*^( − *γ*(*W*[*t*] + *θ*^((0))(*t*, *S*[*t*]))) is independent of *S*[*t*]. This means *θ*^((0))(*t*, *S*[*t*]) is independent of *S*[*t*]. So, we can write it as simply *θ*^((0))(*t*), meaning $\\frac{\\partial\\theta^{(0)}}{\\partial S\_{t}}$ and $\\frac{\\partial^{2}\\theta^{(0)}}{\\partial S\_{t}{}^{2}}$ are equal to 0. Therefore, we can write the approximate expansion for *θ*(*t*, *S*[*t*], *I*[*t*]) as:

$$\\begin{array}{r} {\\theta(t,S\_{t},I\_{t}) = \\theta^{(0)}(t) + I\_{t} \\cdot \\theta^{(1)}(t,S\_{t}) + \\frac{I\_{t}^{2}}{2} \\cdot \\theta^{(2)}(t,S\_{t})} \\\\ \\end{array}$$  

(10.24)
