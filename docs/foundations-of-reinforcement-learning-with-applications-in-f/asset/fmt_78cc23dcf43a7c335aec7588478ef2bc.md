
Note the dependency of feature vector `η(s, a)` on `θ`.

$$
\begin{array}{r} {\eta(s,a) = \frac{(a - \phi{(s)}^{T} \cdot \theta) \cdot \phi(s)}{\sigma^{2}}\text{~for~all~}s \in \mathcal{N}\text{~for~all~}a \in \mathcal{A}} \\ \end{array}
$$  

Note the dependency of feature vector `η(s, a)` on `θ`.

We note that any compatible linear function approximation `Q(s, a; w)` serves as an approximation of the advantage function because:

$$
\begin{array}{r} {{\sum\limits_{a \in \mathcal{A}}\pi}(s,a;\theta) \cdot Q(s,a;w) = {\sum\limits_{a \in \mathcal{A}}\pi}(s,a;\theta) \cdot ({\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{m}\frac{\partial\log\pi(s,a;\theta)}{\partial\theta_{i}}} \cdot w_{i})} \\ \end{array}

$$
\begin{array}{r} {= {\sum\limits_{a \in \mathcal{A}}{({\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{m}\frac{\partial\pi(s,a;\theta)}{\partial\theta_{i}}} \cdot w_{i})}} = {\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{m}{({\sum\limits_{a \in \mathcal{A}}\frac{\partial\pi(s,a;\theta)}{\partial\theta_{i}}})}} \cdot w_{i}} \\ \end{array}
$$  

$$

Denoting `∇[θ]log π(s, a; θ)` as the score column vector `SC(s, a; θ)` and assuming compatible linear-approximation critic:

$$
\begin{aligned} {\nabla_{\theta}J(\theta)} & {= {\sum\limits_{s \in \mathcal{N}}\rho^{\pi}}(s) \cdot {\sum\limits_{a \in \mathcal{A}}\pi}(s,a;\theta) \cdot SC(s,a;\theta) \cdot (SC{(s,a;\theta)}^{T} \cdot w_{\theta}^{*})} \\ & {= {\sum\limits_{s \in \mathcal{N}}\rho^{\pi}}(s) \cdot {\sum\limits_{a \in \mathcal{A}}\pi}(s,a;\theta) \cdot (SC(s,a;\theta) \cdot SC{(s,a;\theta)}^{T}) \cdot w_{\theta}^{*}} \\ & {= \mathbb{E}_{s\sim\rho^{\pi},a\sim\pi}[SC(s,a;\theta) \cdot SC{(s,a;\theta)}^{T}] \cdot w_{\theta}^{*}} \\ \end{aligned}
$$  

Note that `𝔼[s ∼ ρ^(π), a ∼ π][SC(s, a; θ) ⋅ SC(s, a; θ)^(T)]` is the Fisher Information Matrix `FIM[ρ^(π), π](θ)` with respect to `s ∼ ρ^(π), a ∼ π`. Therefore, we can write `∇[θ]J(θ)` more succinctly as:

$$
\begin{array}{r} {\nabla_{\theta}J(\theta) = FIM_{\rho^{\pi},\pi}(\theta) \cdot w_{\theta}^{*}} \\ \end{array}
$$

Thus, we can update `θ` after each atomic experience at time step `t` by calculating the gradient of `J(θ)` for the atomic experience as the outer product of `SC(S[t], A[t]; θ)` with itself (which gives a `m × m` matrix), then multiply this matrix with the vector `w`, and then scale by `γ^(t)`, i.e.

$$
\begin{array}{r} {\Delta\theta = \alpha_{\theta} \cdot \gamma^{t} \cdot SC(S_{t},A_{t};w) \cdot SC{(S_{t},A_{t};w)}^{T} \cdot w} \\ \end{array}
$$  

The update for `w` after each atomic experience is the usual Q-Value Function Approximation update with Q-Value loss function gradient for the atomic experience calculated as:

$$
\begin{array}{r} {\Delta w = \alpha_{w} \cdot (R_{t + 1} + \gamma \cdot SC{(S_{t + 1},A_{t + 1};\theta)}^{T} \cdot w - SC{(S_{t},A_{t};\theta)}^{T} \cdot w) \cdot SC(S_{t},A_{t};\theta)} \\ \end{array}
$$

## 14.8 Policy Gradient Methods in Practice

### 14.8.1 Natural Policy Gradient

Natural Policy Gradient (abbreviated NPG) is due to a paper by Kakade ([Kakade 2001](#bib01.xhtml#refbib_34)) that utilizes the idea of Natural Gradient first introduced by Amari ([Amari 1998](#bib01.xhtml#refbib_2)). We won't cover the theory of Natural Gradient in detail here, and refer you to the above two papers instead. Here we give a high-level overview of the concepts, and describe the algorithm.

The core motivation for Natural Gradient is that when the parameters space has a certain underlying structure (as is the case with the parameters space of `θ` in the context of maximizing `J(θ)`), the usual gradient does not represent its steepest descent direction, but the Natural Gradient does. The steepest descent direction of an arbitrary function `f(θ)` to be minimized is defined as the vector `Δθ` that minimizes `f(θ + Δθ)` under the constraint that the length `|Δθ|` is a constant. In general, the length `|Δθ|` is defined with respect to some positive-definite matrix `G(θ)` governed by the underlying structure of the `θ` parameters space, i.e.,

$$
\begin{array}{r} {|\Delta\theta|^{2} = {(\Delta\theta)}^{T} \cdot G(\theta) \cdot \Delta\theta} \\ \end{array}
$$  

We can show that under the length metric defined by the matrix `G`, the steepest descent direction is:

$$
\begin{array}{r} {\nabla_{\theta}^{nat}f(\theta) = G^{- 1}(\theta) \cdot \nabla_{\theta}f(\theta)} \\ \end{array}
$$  

We refer to this steepest descent direction `∇[θ]^(nat)f(θ)` as the Natural Gradient. We can update the parameters `θ` in this Natural Gradient direction in order to achieve steepest descent (according to the matrix `G`), as follows:

$$
\begin{array}{r} {\Delta\theta = - \alpha \cdot \nabla_{\theta}^{nat}f(\theta)} \\ \end{array}
$$  

Amari showed that for a supervised learning problem of estimating the conditional probability distribution of `y|x` with a function approximation (i.e., where the loss function is defined as the KL divergence between the data distribution and the model distribution), the matrix `G` is the Fisher Information Matrix for `y|x`.

Kakade specialized this idea of Natural Gradient to the case of Policy Gradient (naming it Natural Policy Gradient) with the objective function `f(θ)` equal to the negative of the Expected Returns `J(θ)`. This gives the Natural Policy Gradient `∇[θ]^(nat)J(θ)` defined as:
