Observe that for each state `s` ∈ `𝒩` and each action `a` ∈ `𝒜`,

\begin{array}{r}
{\left\{ \mathcal{R}(s,a) + \gamma{\sum\limits_{s^{\prime} \in \mathcal{N}}\mathcal{P}}(s,a,s^{\prime}) \cdot \mathbf{\mathbf{X}}(s^{\prime}) \right\} - \left\{ \mathcal{R}(s,a) + \gamma{\sum\limits_{s^{\prime} \in \mathcal{N}}\mathcal{P}}(s,a,s^{\prime}) \cdot \mathbf{\mathbf{Y}}(s^{\prime}) \right\}}
\\
\end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{= \gamma{\sum\limits_{s^{\prime} \in \mathcal{N}}\mathcal{P}}(s,a,s^{\prime}) \cdot (\mathbf{\mathbf{X}}(s^{\prime}) - \mathbf{\mathbf{Y}}(s^{\prime})) \geq 0}
\\
\end{array}
$$

Therefore for each state `s` ∈ `𝒩`,

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{\mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{\*}(\mathbf{\mathbf{X}})(s) - \mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{\*}(\mathbf{\mathbf{Y}})(s)}
\\
\end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{= \max\_{a \in \mathcal{A}}\left\{ \mathcal{R}(s,a) + \gamma{\sum\limits_{s^{\prime} \in \mathcal{N}}\mathcal{P}}(s,a,s^{\prime}) \cdot \mathbf{\mathbf{X}}(s^{\prime}) \right\} - \max\_{a \in \mathcal{A}}\left\{ \mathcal{R}(s,a) + \gamma{\sum\limits_{s^{\prime} \in \mathcal{N}}\mathcal{P}}(s,a,s^{\prime}) \cdot \mathbf{\mathbf{Y}}(s^{\prime}) \right\} \geq 0}

### 2. Constant Shift Property

i.e., for all `X` ∈ `ℝ`^`(m)`, `c` ∈ `ℝ`,

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{\mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{\*}(\mathbf{\mathbf{X}} + c)(s) = \mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{\*}(\mathbf{\mathbf{X}})(s) + \gamma c\text{~for~all~}s \in \mathcal{N}}
\\
\end{array}
$$

In the above statement, adding a constant (∈ `ℝ`) to a Value Function (∈ `ℝ`^`(m)`) adds the constant point-wise to all states of the Value Function (to all dimensions of the vector representing the Value Function). In other words, a constant ∈ `ℝ` might as well be treated as a Value Function with the same (constant) value for all states. Therefore,

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{\mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{\*}(\mathbf{\mathbf{X}} + c)(s) = \max\_{a \in \mathcal{A}}\left\{ \mathcal{R}(s,a) + \gamma{\sum\limits_{s^{\prime} \in \mathcal{N}}\mathcal{P}}(s,a,s^{\prime}) \cdot (\mathbf{\mathbf{X}}(s^{\prime}) + c) \right\}}
\\
\end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{= \max\_{a \in \mathcal{A}}\left\{ \mathcal{R}(s,a) + \gamma{\sum\limits_{s^{\prime} \in \mathcal{N}}\mathcal{P}}(s,a,s^{\prime}) \cdot \mathbf{\mathbf{X}}(s^{\prime}) \right\} + \gamma c = \mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{\*}(\mathbf{\mathbf{X}})(s) + \gamma c}
\\
\end{array}
$$

With these two properties of `B`^`(*)` in place, let's prove that `B`^`(*)` is a contraction function. For given `X`, `Y` ∈ `ℝ`^`(m)`, assume:

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{\max\_{s \in \mathcal{N}}|(\mathbf{\mathbf{X}} - \mathbf{\mathbf{Y}})(s)| = c}
\\
\end{array}
$$

We can rewrite this as:

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{\mathbf{\mathbf{X}}(s) - c \leq \mathbf{\mathbf{Y}}(s) \leq \mathbf{\mathbf{X}}(s) + c\text{~for~all~}s \in \mathcal{N}}
\\
\end{array}
$$

Since `B`^`(*)` has the monotonicity property, we can apply `B`^`(*)` throughout the above double-inequality.

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{\mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{\*}(\mathbf{\mathbf{X}} - c)(s) \leq \mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{\*}(\mathbf{\mathbf{Y}})(s) \leq \mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{\*}(\mathbf{\mathbf{X}} + c)(s)\text{~for~all~}s \in \mathcal{N}}
\\
\end{array}
$$

Since `B`^`(*)` has the constant shift property,

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{\mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{\*}(\mathbf{\mathbf{X}})(s) - \gamma c \leq \mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{\*}(\mathbf{\mathbf{Y}})(s) \leq \mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{\*}(\mathbf{\mathbf{X}})(s) + \gamma c\text{~for~all~}s \in \mathcal{N}}
\\
\end{array}
$$

In other words,

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{\max\_{s \in \mathcal{N}}|(\mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{\*}(\mathbf{\mathbf{X}}) - \mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{\*}(\mathbf{\mathbf{Y}}))(s)| \leq \gamma c = \gamma \cdot \max\_{s \in \mathcal{N}}|(\mathbf{\mathbf{X}} - \mathbf{\mathbf{Y}})(s)|}
\\
\end{array}
$$

This gives us the following iterative algorithm, known as the `Value Iteration` algorithm, due to Richard Bellman ([Bellman 1957a](#bib01.xhtml#refbib_8)):

*   Start with any Value Function `V`[`0`] ∈ `ℝ`^`(m)`
*   Iterating over `i` = 0, 1, 2, …, calculate in each iteration:

    $$
    \begin{array}{r}
    {\mathbf{\mathbf{V}}\_{\mathbf{\mathbf{i}} + \mathbf{1}}(s) = \mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{\*}(\mathbf{\mathbf{V}}\_{\mathbf{\mathbf{i}}})(s)\text{~for~all~}s \in \mathcal{N}}
    \\
    \end{array}
    $$

*   Stop the algorithm when `d`(`V`[`i`], `V`[`i` + `1`]) = max[`s` ∈ `𝒩`]|(`V`[`i`] − `V`[`i` + `1`])(`s`)| is adequately small.

It pays to emphasize that Banach Fixed-Point Theorem not only assures convergence to the unique solution `V`^`(*)` (no matter what Value Function `V`[`0`] we start the algorithm with), it also assures a reasonable speed of convergence (dependent on the choice of starting Value Function `V`[`0`] and the choice of `γ`).

## 5.9 Optimal Policy from Optimal Value Function

Note that the Policy Iteration algorithm produces a policy together with a Value Function in each iteration. So, in the end, when we converge to the Optimal Value Function `V`[`j`] = `V`^`(*)` in iteration `j`, the Policy Iteration algorithm has a deterministic policy `π`[`j`] associated with `V`[`j`] such that:

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{\mathbf{\mathbf{V}}\_{\mathbf{\mathbf{j}}} = \mathbf{\mathbf{V}}^{\pi\_{j}} = \mathbf{\mathbf{V}}^{\*}}
\\
\end{array}
$$

and we refer to `π`[`j`] as the Optimal Policy `π`^`(*)`, one that yields the Optimal Value Function `V`^`(*)`, i.e.,

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{\mathbf{\mathbf{V}}^{\pi^{\*}} = \mathbf{\mathbf{V}}^{\*}}
\\
\end{array}
$$

But Value Iteration has no such policy associated with it since the entire algorithm is devoid of a policy representation and operates only with Value Functions. So now the question is: when Value Iteration converges to the Optimal Value Function `V`[`i`] = `V`^`(*)` in iteration `i`, how do we get hold of an Optimal Policy `π`^`(*)` such that:

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{\mathbf{\mathbf{V}}^{\pi^{\*}} = \mathbf{\mathbf{V}}\_{\mathbf{\mathbf{i}}} = \mathbf{\mathbf{V}}^{\*}}
\\
\end{array}
$$

The answer lies in the Greedy Policy function `G`. Equation (5.7) told us that:

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{\mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{G(\mathbf{\mathbf{V}})}(\mathbf{\mathbf{V}}) = \mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{\*}(\mathbf{\mathbf{V}})\text{~for~all~}\mathbf{\mathbf{V}} \in {\\mathbb{R}}^{m}}
\\
\end{array}
$$

Specializing `V` to be `V`^`(*)`, we get:

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{\mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{G(\mathbf{\mathbf{V}}^{\*})}(\mathbf{\mathbf{V}}^{\*}) = \mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{\*}(\mathbf{\mathbf{V}}^{\*})}
\\
\end{array}
$$

But we know that `V`^`(*)` is the Fixed-Point of the Bellman Optimality Operator `B`^`(*)`, i.e., `B`^`(*)`(`V`^`(*)`) = `V`^`(*)`. Therefore,

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{\mathbf{\mathbf{B}}^{G(\mathbf{\mathbf{V}}^{\*})}(\mathbf{\mathbf{V}}^{\*}) = \mathbf{\mathbf{V}}^{\*}}
\\
\end{array}
$$

The above equation says `V`^`(*)` is the Fixed-Point of the Bellman Policy Operator `B`^`(*G(`**`V`**`^`(*)`))`. However, we know that `B`^`(*G(`**`V`**`^`(*)`))` has a unique Fixed-Point equal to `V`^`(*G(`**`V`**`^`(*)`))`. Therefore,

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{\mathbf{\mathbf{V}}^{G(\mathbf{\mathbf{V}}^{\*})} = \mathbf{\mathbf{V}}^{\*}}
\\
\end{array}
