
# Bitcoin History (2020-2023)

## 2020

Bitcoin recovered to $7,000 in April, only one month before its third and final historical event - halving. After three months of stabilization, Bitcoin broke over the $10,000 barrier in October. Then, within two months, as investors were still discussing the prospect of Bitcoin falling lower, it surmounted obstacles of $12K, $14K, $16K, and ultimately $19K. In 2020, institutional investors and government sectors will undoubtedly benefit from Bitcoin's growth. JPMorgan Chase was the catalyst for the adoption. And this was the impetus for the 2021 cryptocurrency bull run.

## 2021

The bull run that began in 2020 lasted far into 2021, with experts and fans issuing lucrative price forecasts for major cryptocurrencies. Elon Musk's support at the start of the year accelerated the pace. All of the optimism surrounding Bitcoin propelled it to new highs and positively influenced virtually all the altcoins.

However, a hurdle occurred in May when Elon Musk changed his stance and said that Tesla would no longer take Bitcoin because it would jeopardize the environment. To make matters worse, a series of crackdown operations against crypto mining was conducted in China in May, the country hosting most of the world's crypto mining. This affected virtually all cryptocurrencies, and it seemed as if the so-called bubble had burst.

On the other side, half the Earth away from China, the year 2021 will be remembered as another South American nation formally accepts the first time Bitcoin. El Salvador has recognized Bitcoin as legal money after Congress received a request from President Nayib Bukele to embrace the cryptocurrency and eliminate all related capital gains taxes. Additionally, the President pledged to harness the country's geothermal energy by instructing a state-owned utility to construct geothermal facilities that harness the power from volcanoes for Bitcoin mining. The height for BTC in 2021 reached $68,000, another record high reached in October 2021, but closed the year below $40,000.

## 2022

As we moved from 2021 to 2022, inflation, the energy crisis, and geopolitical upheaval owing to Russia's aggressiveness toward Ukraine lowered BTC and other top cryptocurrencies' values. Prepare for the 2022 Crypto Winter and shifting Bitcoin values. From January to May 2022, Bitcoin's price plummets. BTC dropped below $30,000 for the first time since July 2021 in May. Crypto winters are less defined than bear markets. Like a bear market, this period sees the cryptocurrency markets decrease steadily and sometimes sharply. Cryptocurrencies fell in Q2. Stablecoins like TerraUSD and Terra Luna contributed to the crypto price drop. Bitcoin traded around $20,700 as cryptocurrency hedge fund Three Arrows Capital defaulted on a bitcoin loan and was ordered into liquidation by a court in the British Virgin Islands. In November 2022, FTX, a major crypto exchange, unexpectedly collapsed, further depressing crypto values. The conversation went bankrupt after its CEO, Sam Blankman Fried, was accused of misappropriating funds. FTX's financial difficulties and possible fraud prevented Binance from bailing it out.

The shocking collapse of FTX sent shockwaves across the financial industry, generating a domino effect in the cryptocurrency market and leaving investors uneasy. Inflation fears and rising interest rates impacted the market. As 2022 ended, Bitcoin's price dropped below $20,000, indicating the cryptocurrency market's health.

## 2023

The first half of the year saw BTC in the $20s. We are now experiencing the gradual yet promising rise of Bitcoin's value, reaching approximately $30,000 when writing this chapter and igniting a glimmer of hope that the crypto winter is finally ending. Q1 had seen other cryptocurrencies following suit with similar price trends.

Experience the new heights of Bitcoin mining as we draw closer to the end of May. The recent surge in miner interest has caught traders' attention, who view it as an excellent bullish signal. While inflation remains a concern in the broader markets, there is growing optimism that the measures implemented by the Fed could lead to a gentle landing.

# Chapter 2: The Bitcoin Phenomenon

## The Story Of Bitcoin's Birth

On November 1, 2008, an anonymous entity named Satoshi Nakamoto wrote in an email to a cryptography mailing list with a nine-page white paper containing the first-ever mention of Bitcoin, calling it a "peer-to-peer electronic cash system."

"I've been working on a new electronic cash system that's fully peer-to-peer, with no trusted third party. The paper is available at `Bitcoin.org/Bitcoin.pdf`." Nakomoto said. The original paper detailing the math and specifics of Bitcoin is still available on the website.

As Nakamoto proposed, the system's main properties would be that the electronic transactions would be peer-to-peer and would not need to involve a financial institution. The plan was to be completely decentralized, meaning that the currency users would not need to rest their trust in a central authority, such as traditional central banks.

Satoshi also expressed dissatisfaction that banks have repeatedly breached the trust of the people who deposit money with them by lending them money in credit bubbles while keeping minimal reserves. "The root problem with conventional currency is all the trust required to make it work. The central bank must be trusted not to debase the currency, but the history of fiat currencies is full of breaches of that trust," Satoshi wrote.

After publishing his revolutionary paper, Nakamoto released Bitcoin's first software and partnered with developers and coders online to improve it.

The first lines of code were posted to the Bitcoin blockchain on January 3, 2009, a few months after the original whitepaper surfaced. These lines of code, known as the "genesis block," are credited to the person or persons known as Satoshi Nakamoto.

On January 12, Nakamoto sent ten Bitcoins to Hal Finney, giving birth to a new finance counterculture, taking impetus away from banks and back to the people. At this point, Bitcoin's monetary value was negligible. Initially, users rewarded each other with Bitcoins for good comments in forums. The first "real" transaction occurred on May 22, 2010, when Laszlo Hanyecz famously bought two pizzas for 10,000 Bitcoin, which was about $30. This transaction would be worth more than $38 million at current prices.

The collaborative environment between Nakomoto and other developers continued until 2011, when, without warning, Nakamoto vanished, or did he?

Many attempts have been made to discover Satoshi Nakamoto's true identity, but with each name suggested, each turned out to be something other than the creator. However, details about the creator, such as Nationality and origins being obscure, made the currency trustworthy and free of any inherent biases. Bitcoin was invented amidst the aftermath of the 2008 financial crisis, and the crisis was an apparent motivating factor that led to its creation.

Many banks and other financial institutions failed worldwide and had to be bailed out by governments at the expense of taxpayers' money. This highlighted the fragility of the modern financial system, where the health of the monetary system was heavily reliant on banks and other financial institutions. We are forced to trust these institutions to make wise and prudent decisions with the money we give them. Too often, they need to carry out this fundamental responsibility adequately.

For most of its history, Bitcoin was associated with three overlapping communities: the small community of original investors, blockchain technology enthusiasts, and speculators just here to make money off this new financial instrument.
