
# NFT Overview

When a person purchases art using an NFT, the artist or the original owner encrypts digital information and metadata onto the blockchain. This data is used to prove the ownership of the NFT. Further, since blockchains have timestamps, one could even trace back the right of the digital asset to see when and how many times it has changed hands over time.

NFTs can be traded like any other digital assets; there are markets for NFTs where one can go and purchase the token they like. Any fixed market force does not set the price for NFTs; much like art, their price is determined by what the buyer is willing to pay for the asset. NFTs can even be programmed so that the owner is paid a certain amount every time the NFT changes hands, so in case the price of the NFT skyrockets, the original owner is still compensated. NFTs use distributed ledger technology such as blockchain, which means there is no need for an intermediary or an exchange for the transfer of NFTs, bringing down the transaction costs.

NFTs have little to no regulations in the global markets, and their future is very much connected with cryptocurrencies and blockchain technology. For example, if cryptocurrency trade is banned in a particular country, then the sale and purchase of NFTs will also be restricted there.

While NFTs have become increasingly popular, their criticisms have also increased. One of the significant criticism associated with NFTs is their carbon footprint. The world is currently dealing with a climatic disaster partly fuelled by using non-renewable and polluting energy sources. We know that blockchain is a very energy-intensive industry. It takes electricity to run the servers responsible for mining on the blockchains. NFTs, to be created also need similar mining requirements, causing pollution and climate change. Some artists have pulled out of making their NFTs after getting to know about the environmental impact of NFTs.

# Properties of Non-fungible Tokens

Non-fungible tokens have certain features which help it function the way it does. Some of these features are:

## Unique
Every NFT is unique, i.e., an NFT cannot be copied. Unlike other cryptocurrencies, one NFT does not equate to another NFT, even if they are of the same value. If one trades NFTs, they have something different in their hand and not just a separate copy of the same thing.

## Indivisible
NFTs are also indivisible, which means that NFTs cannot be broken down into smaller parts of lesser value and then sold, collected again, and reassembled. An NFT is always traded as a whole.

## Indestructible
The data for an NFT is stored on blockchains. Most of the NFTs are stored on the Ethereum blockchain. Since we know that the data on blockchains are tough to destroy or alter, especially on public blockchains, the NFTs cannot be destroyed.

## Absolute ownership
When a person owns an NFT, they carry total ownership over the digital asset; it is not like purchasing the license to use a particular image, song, or gif. This means that the owner has unrestricted access to these NFTs.

## Verifiable
Since the data regarding the ownership of an NFT is stored on blockchains and such data is always timestamped and, at the same time, assured of authenticity, it is easy to verify and trace the ownership of an NFT.

## Limited
NFTs are limited in number; they are scarce. Most NFTs are only produced singularly; however, an artist may have a limited number of similar products, such as 10 or 50, much like little edition clothes or cars by real-life manufacturers.

## Transferable
NFTs are digital assets or certificates that pronounce the ownership of digital assets; hence like any other digital asset, NFTs can be transferred from one person to another for a price.

## Permanent
The data stored on blockchains are endless; once entered, it is always there. Unlike real-life paintings or art forms that might get spoilt over time, the NFTs will be permanent.

# History of Non-fungible Tokens

The idea behind non-fungible tokens started with something called “colored coins” in 2012, which were being offered on the Bitcoin blockchain. These colored coins denote the real-life ownership of cars, metals, real estate, etc.

Non-fungible Tokens were first minted in the year 2014 by Kevin Mckoy. He coined the first non-fungible token called “quantum” before using NFTs extensively in crypto art. Quantum is a pixelated image of an octagonal object that changes shapes and colors.

The NFTs started catching public attention after the introduction of CryptoKitties on the Ethereum blockchain and that of crypto punks. Crypto kitties are non-fungible tokens depicting cats, each with unique age, size, and color features. It means that none of the cats are the same; they are thus traded as collectibles among people, much like sports cards or Pokémon cards in real life. Cryptopunks, conversely, are avatars or profile pictures that have been converted into NFTs, which people can use for various social media platforms. Again, each cyberpunk avatar differs from the others and has its value. The NFTs were growing in the crypto community, but they entered a mainstream boom in 2021.

# The NFT Revolution in 2021

In the year 2021, the interest in NFTs ballooned to unprecedented heights. While this was in line with the bull market that saw the prices of cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin, Ethereum, and even dogecoin during the first quarter of the year, it was observed that despite the fall in the prices of these cryptocurrencies from their peaks in the second quarter of the year there was continued growth in the sale and purchase of non-fungible tokens during the same period.

The NFT market has grown by leaps and bounds in 2021. In the first half of 2021, compared to the 13.7 million dollars in sales in the first half of 2020, the NFT sales had surged to an almost unbelievable 2.5 billion dollars. Within this, we saw an increase in Quarter 1 to Quarter 2 of about 114.6%. In this same period, there was a substantial increase in the number of wallets that store NFTs, at about 151.89%. NFT trading platforms such as Open Sea have also increased, with Open Sea currently worth around 1.5 billion dollars. Sales of NFTs have moved on from being only on trading platforms to being made by traditional auction houses such as Sotheby’s and Christie’s.

It is not just the quantum of NFT sales that has increased in 2021. The value of the NFTs also increased substantially, with a majority of the most expensive NFT sales ever made being in 2021. In February, we saw digital art by the musician Grimes being sold for an amount nearing 6 million dollars, while the GIF for Nyan cat fetched a price of almost half a million dollars or 300 ethers. In March, the artwork by Beeple, pseudonym of the digital artist Michael Winkelmann, called “Every Day: The first 5000 Days” was listed by the auction house Christie’s and sold for a record price of 69.3 million dollars. The same month, Jack Dorsey, Twitter's founder, and CEO, sold his first-ever tweet after converting it into an NFT. This tweet was sold for a price of 2.9 million dollars. Similarly, the cost of various avatars by Cyberpunks has continuously been sold for prices ranging from a few hundred thousand dollars to 7.6 million dollars or 4200 Ether for CryptoPunks #7804.
