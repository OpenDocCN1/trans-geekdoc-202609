# Properties Of Cryptos

In the middle of the 2008 Financial Crisis, an unknown person or group of people known as Satoshi Nakamoto published a whitepaper titled "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System." At the time, very few individuals were aware of this game-changing breakthrough in money.

Cryptocurrency has become an alternate trade means due to the development of encryption and `blockchain` technology. Since its inception in 2009, `Bitcoin` has grown in popularity across the globe. It has garnered so much attention as a result of its unique features.

To properly comprehend the cryptocurrency revolution, it's critical to understand the basic features of cryptocurrencies and `blockchain` technology that contribute to its revolutionary and game-changing status. While there are many cryptocurrencies in the modern world, each with its distinct features, cryptocurrency, at its core, has three essential traits.

## Trustless

`Bitcoin` is trustless because it was built to require no one to trust anybody else to operate. Before `Bitcoin`, every form of money needed you to charge a central authority to use it. In each of these instances, the foremost authority becomes the primary weakness that ultimately results in the currency's downfall. With `Bitcoin`, each ecosystem component verifies what the others are saying without relying on anybody. When a `Bitcoin` transaction is broadcast, all nodes receive it and verify that the signatures are genuine. If the signatures are invalid, the transaction is discarded.

Because everyone on the network has a copy of the ledger, we no longer need to rely on a single entity, organization, or third party, as there is no need to trust when you can check against this ledger using your copy. The `blockchain` is the decentralized ledger, and Individual network players being rewarded through a `PoW` (proof-of-work) consensus mechanism is one of the most revolutionary concepts in contemporary economics.

The incentive may assist nodes in maintaining their integrity. If a greedy attacker can build more CPU proof-of-workers than all the honest nodes, he must choose between defrauding people by reclaiming their money and generating new currencies.

He should consider it more lucrative to follow the rules, which reward him with more new coins than everyone else combined, than to undermine the system and the legitimacy of his riches. While we are still discovering how and why we utilize cryptocurrencies, they are here to stay. Apart from the other advantages of cryptocurrencies and `blockchain` technology, resolving the centralized trust problem alone is a significant breakthrough that will ensure the long-term viability of crypto.

## Unchangeable/Immutable

In the most straightforward meaning, "immutable" implies "unable to be undone." Immutability should be based on three concepts about `blockchain` and cryptocurrency: Rewriting history should be extremely unlikely or difficult. Anyone other than the owner of a `private key` should be unable to transfer money. The `blockchain` records all transactions to ensure the two principles above.

We consult our bank's transaction history to see how our money has been spent from our bank accounts. We entrust our banks with the responsibility of not fabricating transactions or manipulating our money, just as we entrust them with the responsibility of delivering our transactions to receivers. If fraudulent transactions occur, the bank must also be trusted to reverse them and rectify the problem.

As previously shown, a cryptocurrency needs more centralization and trust; there is no longer a third party in whom we can place our confidence to perform these functions. As a result, transaction records are made publicly accessible and irreversible (immutable). Although changing the transaction ledger is not impossible, cryptographic security makes it very difficult. It necessitates the compromising of the whole `Bitcoin` network.

## Distributed

Because "decentralization" is such a hot topic in the crypto world, it's critical to describe it precisely. It may have a variety of connotations: "`Blockchains` are decentralized politically (no one controls them) and architecturally (there is no single point of failure in the infrastructure), but they are centralized conceptually (there is a single generally accepted state, and the system acts like a single computer)." — Vitalik Buterin.

**Fault Tolerance**: since decentralized systems rely on networks of independent components, they are less likely to fail unintentionally.

**Attack Resistance**: Because decentralized systems lack susceptible central points that may be attacked at a far lower cost than the surrounding system, they are more costly to attack, destroy, or control.

**Collusion Resistance**: individuals of decentralized systems find it more difficult to behave in ways that benefit themselves at the cost of others. On the other hand, businesses and governments constantly cooperate in ways that enrich them while harming others.

Besides central banks and governments, banks regulate the supply and production of money via mints and interest rates. Users of fiat currencies are at the whims of central banks regarding money creation.

## Scarcity and Limited Supply

Fiat currencies have an infinite supply because central banks can issue and print as many as they wish. Central banks often influence the value of their nations' money as part of their economic strategies. The inflationary nature of fiat currencies implies a gradual decline in the currency's value over time. On the other hand, most cryptocurrencies have a finite and predetermined supply programmed into their underlying algorithm when they are established.

However, with cryptocurrencies, a person or consortia can only control or exercise substantial influence over the money supply with the majority's consent. The most popular cryptocurrencies have limited tokens or an endless pool with pre-defined manufacturing criteria. Like `Bitcoin`, `Litecoin`, and `Dash`, numerous popular other cryptocurrencies have a finite supply, making them naturally deflationary. Any rise in demand or acceptance of the cryptocurrency will result in a price increase.
