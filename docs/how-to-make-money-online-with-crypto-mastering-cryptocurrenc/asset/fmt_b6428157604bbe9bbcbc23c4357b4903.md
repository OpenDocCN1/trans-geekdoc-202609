
# Cryptocurrency Investment Guide

## Key Evaluation Criteria

- One should look at the coin's total market capitalization and cannot access its supply and circulation. It helps you understand the cryptocurrency's size and offers a hint towards the stability it might offer.
- High fluctuations in the price of a cryptocurrency are not usually seen as a good sign. One should avoid coins that only climb in value because of one person or company endorsing them.
- It is crucial that before buying a cryptocurrency, you study the persons and companies that have issued said cryptocurrency. The kind of business the company is in, and the person's reputation can impact the coin's future value.
- The white paper is the plan forward. It is created for every coin, and it contains the purpose of the particular cryptocurrency and the vision for the same. The investor should look at the white paper and compare what has been accomplished to what was promised.
- **Technology**: The underlying technology on which the cryptocurrency is based is also essential; disruptive technologies with a brighter future provide better value in coins, such as the innovative contract technology that Ethereum initiated.

## Portfolio Diversification

Further, it must be understood that investing in just a single cryptocurrency is not the wisest decision. Hence, it is necessary to build a portfolio of various cryptocurrencies based on your needs and what they offer to investors. Ideally, an investor should have a healthy balance between the riskier but high-yielding coins and the stable but low-yielding coins. Initially, having your portfolio tilted more toward safer options is considered a better option. When one becomes more experienced, one can start investing in riskier coins.

### Portfolio Allocation Segments

A good portfolio would have the following segments:

- **Store of Value**: Bitcoin, Ethereum
- **Digital Currency**: Litecoin, XRP, XLM, Dash, etc.
- **Distributed Applications**: Polkadot, Cardano, Tron, Waves, etc.
- **New Tech**: ADA, Tezos, Nano
- **Private Coins**: Monero, Verge, etc.
- **Exchange Tokens**: Binance Coin, Uniswap, etc.
- **Stablecoins**: Tether, DAI, USD Coin

A healthy mix of the above shall give the investor an excellent risk-to-yield ratio, which shall be suitable for a long-term investment.

## Placing the Order

After the investor has decided what cryptocurrency combination they want to invest in, the next step is placing the order on the crypto exchange for said coin. However, you must first have money to place the order. Depending on the platform, the crypto exchange or the holder shall allow you to transfer money into the account through cards, wire transfers, or PayPal.

In some cases, you will need to use other cryptocurrencies to make purchases, in which case you will have to deposit them into your account. Sometimes it can take a while for the money to be credited into your account, so you must wait before purchasing your choice of cryptocurrencies.

Once the investor has the money in their account, they can place the orders. For placing the order, the person must choose the coin by clicking on its ticker (depending on the user interface), for example, `BTC` for Bitcoin, and then select the quantity they want to purchase. Most crypto exchanges even allow you to buy a fraction of a cryptocurrency, such as Bitcoin, which is too expensive to buy whole. After completing this, the cryptocurrency is deposited into the holder's account, and the corresponding money is debited. The exchanges and brokers charge a commission or fee for every transaction, depending on the quantum of the trade.

## Storing Your Crypto Investment

The last step is safely storing the cryptocurrency you have purchased since it is a precious investment. Since most exchanges do not provide much protection for the coins, they can be highly susceptible to hacks and thefts. There have been thefts worth millions of dollars in cryptocurrencies in the past.

For this purpose, one needs to store their crypto investments in crypto wallets. Crypto wallets can be of different types, such as wallets linked to exchanges, software wallets, hardware wallets, cold wallets, and hot wallets. Depending on their needs, one can choose what kind of wallet they want, as different wallets have different features, security, and costs.
