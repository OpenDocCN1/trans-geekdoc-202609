
# Bitcoin: Price History and Impact on Banking

## Price Volatility (2021–2022)

Bitcoin prices shot up 6.24% to a new high of over $63,000 in anticipation of Coinbase IPO. But soon, the crypto gets crushed under the weight of a reversal by Tesla to cease accepting payment in Bitcoin because of the environmental impact. Bitcoin sinks down 13%. Things got worse as Bitcoin went through another lousy patch as China banned financial institutions from offering any crypto-related services, sending prices down over 14%.

Bitcoin's price, which started at around $60,000 in May, dropped to about $30,000; almost every other cryptocurrency also took a nosedive. Bitcoin was still in freefall after ending the week down 25% due to global regulatory obstacles. By May, Bitcoin was trading at $38,400. News from El Salvador sent prices galloping up as it could make the legal currency tender and embrace the digital token in an unexpected move that could dramatically shift the currents of the international crypto marketplace.

At Bitcoin 2021, El Salvador President Nayib Bukele announced that his country was officially adopting Bitcoin as a legal tender. Once implemented, it would be the first country in the world to do so and mark a significant milestone in the history of international monetary policy. It would also mean no capital gains tax for Bitcoin in El Salvador. Meanwhile, as China intensified its crypto mining crackdown and banned most mining operations, Bitcoin plummeted to its lowest price since January.

After a year of record highs and parabolic gains, crypto markets are going through a turbulent time that doesn't look to be ending any time soon. Bitcoin looks to be making a comeback, with prices popping to nearly $43k in August 2021 as Germany moves towards greater crypto adoption, allowing institutional funds to hold up to 20% in crypto assets for the first time.

Inflation, the energy crisis, and geopolitical upheaval owing to Russia's aggressiveness toward Ukraine lowered BTC and other top cryptocurrencies' values in 2022. BTC dropped below $30,000 for the first time since July 2021 in May 2022. This period sees the cryptocurrency markets decrease steadily and sometimes sharply. Stablecoins like TerraUSD and Terra Luna's failure contributed to the crypto price drop. Bitcoin traded around $20,700 as cryptocurrency hedge fund Three Arrows Capital defaulted on a bitcoin loan and was ordered into liquidation by a court in the British Virgin Islands. The FTX exchange went bankrupt after its CEO, Sam Blankman Fried, was accused of misappropriating funds. FTX's financial difficulties and possible fraud prevented Binance from bailing it out. By the end of 2022, Bitcoin's price dropped below $20,000, indicating the cryptocurrency market's health.

## Bitcoin vs. Banks

Bitcoin was created to decentralize the financial system. In other terms, it was designed to replace the centralized banking system. To date, it has not officially been able to capture the banks, but the efforts are still intact. So, the dominance of Bitcoin over banks would decentralize the banking system. Thus, we need to concentrate on the characteristics and features of decentralized and centralized strategies to understand if Bitcoin can take over the current banking system.

### Decentralized vs. Centralized Systems

Before the introduction of Bitcoin in the market, we were exposed only to the working of the traditional banking system. Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies work by storing the transactions on a decentralized public ledger. The ledger uses cryptographic algorithms to ensure their records are on point and the user's identity is encrypted.

Bitcoin owners have digital wallets where they can store their coins. And the work of the ledger is to ensure that a proper spendable balance is maintained in the wallet. Moreover, it tracks the transaction history to ensure the owner spends from their wallets.

Unlike traditional currencies, Bitcoin is not created by printing notes and coins. But are made through a process called ‘cryptocurrency mining.’ Mining solves complex mathematical puzzles to confirm and add transactions to the ledger (block). Now that you know the basic working, we can understand its implications on banks.

### Implications for Banks

In recent years, a drastic change has occurred in how people look at businesses and make transactions. One can exchange currency outside traditional banks with a few clicks on a mobile phone.

The significant point is that people need not physically go to a traditional bank if they require financing. With the development of the peer-to-peer network, as in the case of Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies, it is getting much more popular and familiar, and people are turning away from banks and looking forward to more decentralized systems like Bitcoin.

### Bitcoin as a Threat to Traditional Banks?

In short, yes. Cryptocurrency enthusiasts have long ago identified that Bitcoin and its technology threaten the banking industry. Not just them, but banks too have strong perceptions on the replacement of working of banks with blockchain technology. For instance, BNP Paribas, a French banking giant, released a report regarding their discussion on the technology behind Bitcoin and how it could make traditional banks redundant.

Furthermore, an analyst for the bank compared the software behind Bitcoin with an invention like the steam and combustion engine and stated that it has the potential to transform the world of finance. A UK banking report concluded that Bitcoin and the following cryptocurrencies are a menace to traditional banks, especially if they need to consider people's new preferences and behaviors regarding how they wish to transact with money.

### Potential After Effects

To understand Bitcoin's power, let us assume a scenario where Bitcoin has taken over the financial system. Here are the effects banks can have with the entry of Bitcoin in the world of finance.

#### Demise of the Banking System

Banks cannot create reserves and perform fractional reserve lending with Bitcoin. There is no means through which it can control Bitcoin, as Bitcoin has no representative and is fully decentralized. Banks can no longer inflate or deflate away the purchasing power of users’ saving accounts. Banks cannot do bailouts with Bitcoin if cryptocurrencies get adopted worldwide.

Banks cannot generate revenue from remittance businesses, which levy 10–30 percent in cross-border transactions.

Banks act as an intermediary for every transaction that is made. But, with Bitcoin in the market, banks can become inexistent as one of the main features of Bitcoin is the absence of middlemen. These possibilities could emerge if Bitcoin came into the mainstream business. And thus, banks consider Bitcoin as a potential threat to themselves. Moreover, some believe that Bitcoin’s impact on banks and the current technology will be beyond one’s imagination. And the financial elite who control the politicians and the media will do everything they can to stop that from happening.

### Advantages of Bitcoin Over Traditional Banking

Bitcoin, a decentralized system, has several benefits over the centralized system like banks, mainly because of the capability to operate without any intermediary and no case of failure at any point.

#### Transaction Processing

Local transactions in the banking system are settled quickly. However, when it comes to international fund transfers, banks take several days to process the transaction. On the other hand, Bitcoin, a peer-to-peer payment system, can process over $1 billion worth of transactions on average. Thus, transactions are processed within a few minutes, irrespective of the sender and receiver location. Banks work for five business days; typically, no transactions are settled during the weekend or holidays. Meanwhile, Bitcoin is open daily and has no concept of holidays and weekends.
