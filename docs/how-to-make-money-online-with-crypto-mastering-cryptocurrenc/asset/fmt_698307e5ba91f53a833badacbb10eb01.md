## Market Analysis

The value of Bitcoin soared by more than 500% in 2020-21 at its peak. One Bitcoin is worth twenty times as much as an ounce of gold. For example, some governments like the Central African Republic and El Salvador have recently legalized Bitcoin as a payment method, increasing the usage of Bitcoin bought with current currencies such as the dollar. This meteoric rise in the value of this cryptocurrency has generated substantial public interest and an increase in its perceived value. It has also led many speculators and gamblers to rush to invest in this currency to achieve future exponential returns. Again, this has played the role of a significant catalyst in the rise of its perceived value and made the currency extremely volatile. The unique features of keeping the digital footprint of those who transact in Bitcoin anonymous and not being controlled by any centralized organization have made some traders perceive Bitcoins as an attractive investment asset. Bitcoin's finite supply concept has also made some traders compare it to traditional assets such as gold and silver. However, the constantly fluctuating value of Bitcoins also makes its valuation extremely volatile.

The lack of centralized control or regulatory body makes it prone to changing compliance issues and vulnerable to money laundering. These aspects have also made many people wonder whether Bitcoin is a bubble that will eventually burst.

Bitcoin has strength by design to make it a viable currency that has elevated its status over the years, more notably its fixed supply. Bitcoin will be mined with diminishing returns every four years until the maximum number of Bitcoins is reached: 21 million. This aspect of Bitcoin is essential for its value. Due to the limited amount of Bitcoins, there will never be an overabundance of Bitcoin. This makes Bitcoin a good asset for investors to put their wealth into, as it generally does not lose value due to inflation. However, as with most cryptocurrencies, the price can fluctuate wildly based on many other external factors.

Cryptocurrency is uniquely positioned as a forerunner in transforming technology to long-standing financial systems. It can fill gaps in current financial technologies by its very nature. Bitcoin's technology allows individuals to exchange currency without needing a trusted third party, like a bank, to oversee the transaction. All that is required to use Bitcoin is a mobile phone, which many people can access. Due to Bitcoin's peer-to-peer networking capability, users can trade Bitcoin with each other by scanning QR codes displayed on their wallet application. This is a genuinely innovative solution to a problem that has existed for many years. The transactions will invariably increase as the user base grows, so the demand for a better cryptocurrency network and applications will come to the forefront.
