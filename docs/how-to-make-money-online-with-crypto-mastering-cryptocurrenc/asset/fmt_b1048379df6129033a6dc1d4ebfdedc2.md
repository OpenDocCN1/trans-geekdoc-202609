
# NFTs vs Cryptocurrencies: Key Differences

## Cost
The cost or price of NFTs and cryptocurrencies differs to a great extent. When we talk about cryptocurrencies, the most popular and expensive cryptocurrency is `Bitcoin`. `Bitcoin`, during the bull market of 2021, reached a peak price of just above 64,000 dollars. This, however, pales in comparison to the kind of prices that people have paid for some NFTs. People regularly pay upwards of a million dollars for NFTs, with the most expensive NFT netting around 69 million dollars during an auction in March 2021.

## Divisibility
Another difference between NFTs and cryptocurrencies is their ability to be divided into smaller pieces. Cryptocurrencies can be broken down into pieces, which can further be broken down into parts. This is essential for expensive cryptocurrencies like `Bitcoin` so people can purchase them in smaller quantities. NFTs always exist as a whole, and they are like art pieces. They can't be broken down, traded, collected, and reassembled.

## Blockchain Used
The blockchains used for the two also differ. While for cryptocurrencies, almost every cryptocurrency runs on its own blockchain, which stores the transactions of said currency. For example, the trades of `Ether` are recorded on `Ethereum`. Whereas NFTs are more often than not running on the `Ethereum` blockchain; some of them are also on the `Binance Smart Chain`. These are the two most commonly used blockchains for NFTs.

## Purchase Platforms
The platforms at which the two are traded are also different. At the same time, cryptocurrencies are traded on crypto exchanges, such as `Binance` and `Coinbase`. These crypto exchanges can be both centralized or decentralized in nature. NFTs are bought and sold on NFT platforms like `OpenSea` and `Nifty Gateway`. At the same time, NFTs are also being sold by auction houses such as `Sotheby's` and `Christie's`.

## Currencies Accepted
The money that can be paid for the purchase of the two is also different. When we talk about cryptocurrencies, one can pay for them in fiat currencies or other cryptocurrencies, depending on their crypto exchange. Thus we see that cryptocurrencies are readily convertible to real-world money. On the other hand, to purchase NFTs, one has to pay only in cryptocurrencies. If the NFT is based on the `Ethereum` blockchain, it is paid for in `Ethers (ETH)`, and if it is on the `Binance Smart Chain`, then it is paid for in `Binance coins (BNB)`.

## Timeline
The timeline of the emergence of the two is also very different. Cryptocurrencies were first created in 2008, the first being `Bitcoin`. Cryptocurrencies came into the mainstream in the middle to late 2010s, with the first bull market in 2017 and the second in early 2021. On the other hand, the first NFT was introduced in 2016; it became slightly famous in 2017 due to `CryptoKitties` and `CryptoPunks`, but it only entered the mainstream in 2021 when the NFT market ultimately ballooned.

# Workings of NFTs

## Creating an NFT
Creating an NFT is relatively easy; various platforms allow people to make an NFT. To make a non-fungible token, one needs two things: the digital art or asset that will be converted into a non-fungible token and the payment for doing the minting. Recently, much stuff has been converted into non-fungible tokens, including digital art, songs, videos, and even tweets; anything with a digital form or signature can be converted into an NFT. Once created, the NFTs can be viewed by the owner on wallets, just like cryptocurrencies. These NFTs similarly exist on addresses on the blockchain, which can be accessed by the person holding the key.

## NFT Standards
During the minting process of an NFT, the owner or creator of the NFT executes code that conforms to different NFT standards. NFTs use standards and protocols such as the following:

### `ERC-721`
`ERC-721` was the original standard developed to create NFTs in 2017. It was written in `Solidity` on the `Ethereum` blockchain. This standard allows minting new NFTs easier, faster, and non-repetitive. `ERC-721` is the most commonly used standard; anyone can create their own NFT.

### `ERC-1155`
`ERC-1155` is another standard on the `Ethereum` blockchain. It was developed after `ERC-721` and is considered an upgrade over the previous one. The standard has smart contracts executed in a way that makes new non-fungible tokens. One of the significant advantages that `ERC-1155` has over `ERC-721` is that one can create multiple NFTs on a single smart contract. On an `ERC-721`, one had to run the smart contract for a new NFT every time. The `ERC-1155` is more efficient and takes less storage space, but it is also more diverse since it can make fungible tokens and non-fungible tokens.

### Hedera Token Service
This is a non-`Ethereum`-based standard that `Hedera` offers. This is much cheaper than the `Ethereum` standards and also faster. `Infinite by SUKU` is one company that has shifted to `Hedera` from `Ethereum`.

## Fractional NFTs
Currently, we see that NFTs are proprietary and have single owners. There are plans to introduce fractional NFT standards shortly, allowing multiple users to own a fraction of a single NFT. This would align with the decentralizing and multiple-node nature of blockchains in general.

# NFT Use Cases

NFTs can be used in various sectors. Some of these use cases are:

## Gaming
Non-fungible tokens, primarily online gaming, are extensively used in the gaming sector. Some of the first NFTs, such as `CryptoKitties`, were just games set up on blockchains. NFTs can be used in gaming in two broad ways: either the collection and storing of the NFTs is in itself a game, and people are trying to get NFTs, convert them into something else within the game, and then try to win a reward, as is the case in `CryptoKitties` or `Alchemy Toys`. Conversely, it is also possible that the NFTs are being used as appendages to other games. These can be cosmetic (skins, avatars, helmets, etc.) and utile (weapons, pets, etc.).

In Massively Multiplayer Online Role-Playing games, people can use NFTs to own certain in-game items, giving them the exclusive right to use these. The gaming industry is one of the fastest-growing industries in the world. With the increase in mobile-based games and internet penetration in previously untapped regions, it is set to expand even further in the next few years. The gaming industry will be worth over 300 billion dollars by 2023. This provides NFTs with a large market base and potential growth in this sector.

## Digital Assets
The digital asset marketplace is also one of these tokens' most critical use cases. It is possible to purchase assets that only exist in the digital world. `Decentraland` is among the most common names when buying digital assets as NFTs. `Decentraland` is a virtual world with a certain amount of finite and scarce land. A prospective buyer can purchase this land from others or even the developers. The price of the land is dependent, much like in the real world, on the location of this 'plot.' Some of the game's prime location digital real estate is being sold for thousands of dollars. Companies are planning to set up their digital stores in these spaces. `Cryptovoxels` is another project where people can purchase and sell digital assets.

## Art
Currently, Art is the sector where NFTs are used most. Digital art for years was plagued with the problem of being easily copied, and thus with no scarcity for the said product, the price of digital art could not be higher. This completely changed after people started converting their digital art to NFTs. When Art was combined with NFT, it became a lot easier for the owners of the said pieces to authenticate their ownership. This created a limited supply of digital art, which caused it to shoot up in value. Over the last couple of years, we have seen some digital paintings being sold for astronomical prices, such as *Everydays: The First 5000 Days*, which was sold at 69 million dollars.
