
# Bitcoin vs Traditional Banking

Financial institutions like JP Morgan, which dominates the offshore banking industry, have links with large banks, making it possible to clear billions of money very efficiently and securely. However, a downside is that the transfer of billions of dollars requires considerable manual labor, including transaction verification, Anti-money Laundering checks, and payment clearing. But being completely decentralized, Bitcoin does not require any manual work, as mentioned. Moreover, settlement and offshore banking are provided by Bitcoin with significantly lesser costs and a much more robust infrastructure.

## Can Bitcoin Take Over the Banking System?

Can Bitcoin take over the Banking system? Yes and no. With the incredible benefits Bitcoin offers, there is a possibility of Bitcoin ruling over the banking system. Since the traditional banking system is mature, people rely upon and believe in this system. At the same time, Bitcoin does have downsides in terms of volatility and regulation. Therefore, it cannot be said with certainty that Bitcoin will replace the existing centralized system, even though it can do so.

# Bitcoin's Fundamental & Technical Analysis

## Fundamental Analysis

Fundamental analysis determines an asset's value by looking at its financial performance, prospects, market value, and use cases before investing. We will now look at the fundamental analysis of Bitcoin by studying essential metrics such as market cap, liquidity, and the Bitcoin whitepaper, among other things.

### Market Capitalization

Market capitalization, commonly called a market cap, is the total market value of an asset. Market capitalization is equal to the current market price of the currency multiplied by the number of coins in circulation. The market cap of Bitcoin tells us about its proliferation in the market and the extent of its adoption. It can also be used to predict future growth potential. A coin with a low market cap is likelier to see high growth than one with a very high market capitalization, whereas a coin with a high market cap tends to be more established and stable.

Bitcoin has the largest market cap of all cryptocurrencies. It is a well-established coin and comparatively a safe investment bet. The prominent market cap reflects the trust market participants have in the currency.

### Liquidity

Liquidity measures how easily an asset can be bought or sold. Good liquidity is a sign of consistent supply and demand. Liquidity is also directly linked to trading volumes. High liquidity ensures an asset can be quickly and easily traded and exchanged relatively.

Analyzing Bitcoin's liquidity shows us an average daily trading volume of 2 - 4 billion dollars, meaning Bitcoin is a very liquid asset representing buyers and sellers readily available. The high trading volumes also indicate the market interest in the coin.

### Supply Characteristics

The nature of a coin's supply is a fundamental characteristic of a cryptocurrency. Bitcoin has a fixed collection of 21 million coins; the mining rate becomes half every four years. This is done to preserve the coin's value and counter inflation. A limited supply is almost always advantageous to a currency as it ensures rarity and counters inflation. This makes Bitcoin superior to many other assets due to the limited supply that gives it anti-inflationary properties. Meaning a Bitcoin investment can counter inflation.

### Transaction Activity

Information such as the rate and number of transactions happening in the Bitcoin blockchain gives us insights into the activity taking place in the network. Transaction value can be used to understand the movement of money in a network as it shows how much matter was transferred in a time. Bitcoin has a very high transaction count and weight, as it is the top cryptocurrency traded currently.

### Hash Rate

Blockchains run on different algorithms, and the hash rate can be used to assess the health of a blockchain network. Bitcoin's healthy hash rate indicates a high interest surrounding the currency or increased mining rates. In simple terms, the hash rate can be said as the rate of mining of a coin. In combination with other metrics, the hash rate can be used to predict trends in a currency.

### Active Addresses

Active addresses are the blockchain addresses that remain active in a given period. Methods of calculating this metric vary, but it is also a good indicator of the dynamic market participants in the Bitcoin ecosystem. Several unique addresses can also be studied to see how the addition of new users is progressing in the network. Bitcoin has more than half a million addresses active at any given time, hinting at its popularity and rate of adoption.

### Whitepaper

The whitepaper is a technical document that gives us an overview of the cryptocurrency project. Reading a project's whitepaper before investing in it is a good idea. A good whitepaper should define the network's objectives and give us an insight into the technology used, use cases, and the roadmap ahead of the coin. Bitcoin has solid and well-defined documentation backing its blockchain technology; only minimal modifications and additions were made from the original paper published by Satoshi Nakamoto in 2009.

### Real-World Use Cases

A cryptocurrency project is only valuable as long as it stays relevant. It should always have different or well-established applications or some unique, irreplaceable aspect that keeps it appropriate in the crypto universe. Hence while studying crypto, one must pay close attention to the network's integration into real-world use cases. Bitcoin is the most widespread and proliferative. Cryptocurrency enjoys an almost monopoly over transaction volumes and has become a standard index currency in crypto, signifying its relevance.

### Combining Fundamental and Technical Analysis

Using fundamental analysis, Bitcoin traders can understand the cryptocurrency's value and decide whether it is an excellent time to buy or sell. However, relying too much on fundamental analysis without also using technical analysis can prevent a trader from buying or selling at the wrong time.

To limit this risk, traders usually combine fundamental analysis with technical analysis. For instance, a fundamental analyst might look at several demand indicators, conclude that Bitcoin is underbought, and then implement a technical analysis by reading charts to find the optimal entry point.

Alternatively, a trader might use technical indicators to determine an excellent time to sell and then use fundamental analysis techniques to confirm this view by looking at key demand drivers.

# Technical Analysis of Bitcoin

Technical analysis is an entirely different ballgame from fundamental analysis. In technical analysis, we take a statistical approach to study an asset by analyzing key metrics and studying charts using mathematical tools and functions. Here we will briefly discuss some essential methods and concepts that fall under technical analysis.

## Support and Resistance

A support level is a price level where the desire to buy is so strong that it can withstand the pressure of sellers. As a result, the fall is suspended, and prices start increasing again. Usually, a support level can be determined in advance, according to the trends established by the previous decline. Likewise, a resistance level is opposite to a support level and represents a level above the market, where sellers' pressure exceeds buyers' influence. The prices tend to increase if the asset takes support at a support level, but it is likely to decline further if it breaks the support level and moves downwards. Similarly, if an asset breaks a resistance level, it is expected to keep moving up but is likely to move down if it does not cross the level.

## Candlestick Charts

Unlike the traditional linear chart that shows the current and historical price data, Japanese candlesticks and bars use as many as four data points for a given time instead of one:

1. The opening price when the time began
2. The highest point the price reached during the time
3. The lowest point the price reached in the period
4. The closing price when the period ended
