
# Bitcoin's Decade-Long Rise

## Introduction

In its decade-long rise, Bitcoin has changed how global markets work and made many people super-rich. The world's most popular cryptocurrency started on January 3, 2009, by mining the genesis block (Block 0), which created 50 Bitcoins. On January 9, Version 0.1 of Bitcoin was officially released, including the generation system that limits Bitcoin's total circulation to 21 million by 2040.

We now walk through Bitcoin's eventful journey to the top and analyze some of the significant milestones hit by the coin along the way.

## 2009 - 2011

After its inception in January 2009, Bitcoin remained more or less confined to the developers' circles and didn't catch much attention outside of it. But as October of 2009 came around, a pro-Crypto website named New Liberty Standard, which an original Bitcoin developer ran, displayed the first BTC-USD exchange rate. At the time, they calculated the exchange rate based on the cost of electricity required to mine one Bitcoin, which was around $0.0007 on October 5, 2009. Less than a month later, Bitcoin 0.2 was released with Linux support and new features that upgraded the blockchain capabilities. In May of 2010, the first Bitcoin purchase occurred when Programmer Laszlo Hanyecz bought two Papa John's pizzas for 10,000 Bitcoins (worth about $30) through an exchange on the Bitcointalk chat forum. By late 2010, Bitcoin's popularity was increasing beyond the original media, and it was gaining momentum; this led to Bitcoin's total market cap hitting the $1 million mark by November 2010. One Bitcoin was worth about $0.5 at this point.

2011 was a significant year for Bitcoin as it breached the $1 mark for the first time and made global headlines, further boosting its popularity. The infamous Silk Road website was also launched around this time by Mr. Ross Ulbricht, who wanted to create a lawless dark web marketplace where people could buy and sell whatever they wanted without a trace. Bitcoin is an obvious choice of currency as it is anonymous and untraceable. However, with the momentum gained from media attention, Bitcoin continued to rise, going beyond $10 to over $30 by June 2011. As this happened, the original creator, Satoshi Nakamoto, disappeared from the internet.

After rallying from $1 in February to highs of $31 on June 8, the dream run of Bitcoin suddenly reversed, the Bitcoin bubble as it was known - burst - and prices tumbled back down to single digits by June 12.

The sudden climb gave crypto fans massive hope in the new digital currency, with some expecting the price to reach highs beyond $1,000 and enter mainstream usage by the end of the year. But the sudden crash brought as much despair as the climb had given hope, and soon the existence of black market applications like Silk Road had people questioning the future of Bitcoin. Bitcoin version 0.5 was in operation around this time.

## 2012-2017

Bitcoin, being ever-volatile, had been slowly rising again over the past few months after the price peaked back in June 2011, climbing back above $9 for the first time in almost a year. Following this, multiple events of Bitcoin exchanges getting hacked and Bitcoins stolen were reported. Bitcoin continued its bull run, breaking the all-important $100 barrier for the first time, jumping from $96.15 on March 31, 2012, to close at $104.70 the next day.

However, the price was over 70% in two days as a panic sell-off ensued, falling from $228.94 to $68.09 in just six days, leaving the currency back where it had started for the month. The secret online black market called Silk Road that uses Bitcoin for shady transactions was seized by the FBI in October 2013, and Bitcoin prices plunged by over 18% from $127.30 to $103.85 as a result, but the price rebounded the very next day. By November, Bitcoin surged almost ten times in value reaching $1000. China's foreign minister Wang Yi takes a positive stand and announces that people in China are "free to participate in the Bitcoin market" and promises to "adopt a long-term perspective" on the currency, bringing much optimism to the market participants.

By 2015, Bitcoin was now accepted by over 100,000 retailers worldwide: including Microsoft, Dell, Wikipedia, Twitch, Greenpeace, Expedia, and PayPal. The widespread merchant adoption reassured people that Bitcoin has real value as a currency, not just as a digital investment. By late 2017, Bitcoin was trading around the $1000 mark as stray hacking incidents continued.

In a gigantic jump, Bitcoin went from under $1,000 on January 1, 2017, to $10,840 on December 1, making many people very rich. It went on to top a record-breaking high of $20,089 and closed the day at an all-time high of $19,783.06.

## 2018-2020

Prices sank 3.60% after Twitter announced a ban on cryptocurrency advertisements joining the likes of tech giants Google and Facebook to protect investors from fraud.

The crackdowns came as global regulators started to notice the potential risks in Initial Coin Offerings (ICOs) and other misuse and frauds related to cryptocurrency use. The price dropped from $8,518.64 to $7,471.18 during the week. The seventeenth generation of Bitcoin's original software client, Bitcoin Core 0.17.0, became available with significant upgrades by 2018 as prices kept diminishing. As 2019 came around, the price climbed above $10k, hitting $13,880 on June 26 before sliding back.

The plague struck in 2020, Covid-19 crashed into our lives, and markets worldwide plunged. Bitcoin doesn't escape either, falling by over 50% in less than a week as the world moves toward a total lockdown. On March 12, Bitcoin dropped from almost $8,000 to below $5,000, losing 39% daily. Market liquidity froze up, and investors fled into cash and commodity segments. Amazingly, however, the price was back up to pre-pandemic levels by May as Bitcoin traders got back into the game quickly.

By November 2020, Bitcoin was up 100% on the year at $15,770; the bullish momentum continued, with prices increasing to a daily high of $19,864 on November 30 before closing the month at $19,700. Soon enough, on December 16, the cryptocurrency blows past the $20,000 barrier before charging on up to reach a record-breaking high of $21,576 – a yearly gain of over 180%. The currency kept climbing, passing $24,000 on December 21 and $26,000 on December 26.

## 2021-present

The bull run is sustained as Bitcoin bowls into a record-breaking 2021 by smashing through the $30,000 barrier on January 2 and smashing past $40,000 less than a week later before reaching a high of $42,000 on January 8, resulting in a 40% gain in the first week of the year. It's been very volatile since Bitcoin topped $40,000 at the start of January, and the currency has displayed immense volatility.

It's lost a quarter of its value since then, dipping briefly below $30,000 on January 22 and again on January 27. In yet another example of the massive influence Elon Musk can wield online, he adds the #Bitcoin hashtag to his Twitter profile, and the price skyrockets, gaining almost 11% in a single day and continuing with a further 10% rise the following day.

Following this on Monday, January 8, Tesla announced in an SEC filing that it had invested $1.5 billion into Bitcoin as part of a new investment policy, sending prices skyward. It's been an excellent year for Bitcoin, and by February 2021, the price bounced through $50,000 for the first time. In a significant milestone, Bitcoin's price reflected 9% on February 19- taking its total market cap past $1 trillion for the first time, climbing above $53,000. Shortly after, Elon Musk announced over Twitter that after buying $1.5 billion worth of Bitcoin last month, Tesla will now accept Bitcoins as payment for its cars - integrating the cryptocurrency further into the financial system.
