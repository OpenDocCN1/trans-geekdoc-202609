# Chapter 1: The Cryptocurrency Revolution

Understanding The Basics Of Cryptocurrency

Electronic virtual currencies originated way before the inception of the first-ever scalable crypto - Bitcoin. However, these currencies weren’t termed cryptos because they do not have the cryptography tech embedded in them. But these are the very first attempts that eventually led to the invention of the currently used cryptocurrencies.   

The revolution of digital currencies started in the Netherlands in the 1980s.  A group of developers invented an electronic card system to avoid the thefts in Gas Stations that happen at night. Whoever wants to access Gas can use those cards, so the use of real money is eliminated.

The second attempt towards virtual currencies is made by ‘David Chaum.’ He is an American cryptographer and the inventor of Blinded Cash. The USP of this currency is that it is untraceable. That is, transactions made using this virtual currency cannot be tracked. Chaum used complex encryption techniques to achieve this feat. However, this crypto has failed because of various scalability issues.    

We must know that eCash, B-Money, Bit Gold, and Hashcash are notable failed attempts in this space. eCash was the first conceptualized digital token currency to transfer between individuals. B Money was portrayed as an electronic cash system that is anonymous and secured at the same time. Bit Gold is considered the first digital asset with complete decentralized functionality. Bit Gold was pegged to the value of gold while moving away from centralization.

HashCash was one of the most successful digital currencies, getting everything in place except for scalability. It functions almost like Bitcoin and has a similar consensus algorithm too. This digital currency was truly ahead of its time, which could be one reason for its failure. Finally, in 2008, the whitepaper of Bitcoin was released, while in early 2009, the first ever Bitcoin block was mined, and the rest is history.

Cryptocurrency is a kind of digital money that exists only online. There is only an actual coin or bill if you utilize a service that enables you to exchange cryptocurrencies for physical tokens. Typically, you trade cryptocurrencies with another person online, through your phone or computer, without a third party's assistance, like a bank. Cryptocurrencies are unrelated to valuable things – they are irrelevant to anything of actual worth in the real world, which causes their value to fluctuate wildly, as you have probably seen.

In contrast to equities, bonds, artwork, real estate, or precious metals, cryptocurrencies have no intrinsic value or use. For instance, gold is a critical component of electronics and jewelry — and therefore has worth in addition to its scarcity. On the other hand, cryptocurrency costs money solely because someone else owns it and needs money to deliver it to you.

Cryptocurrencies address the following critical issues associated with the use of cash. There is a need for a centralized authority to control these objects' value, manufacturing, and authenticity. Possibility of fraudulent duplicating. To understand this better, one should get a good understanding of some of these basics.

Cryptography

Cryptocurrency uses cryptography — the art of concealing and disclosing information — to guarantee the security of user data and the integrity of transactions. As the name implies, a cryptocurrency is digital or virtual money protected by encryption, making it hard to counterfeit or double-spend.

Blockchain

A blockchain is a subset of Distributed Ledger Technology (DLT), a distributed database distributed among many operators (nodes, computing devices, etc.) Numerous cryptocurrencies are decentralized networks powered by blockchain technology—a distributed ledger maintained by a distributed network of computers.

This is the underlying technology that drives cryptocurrency. It is a digital ledger that validates transactions, accounts, and balances. Blockchain may be used for various applications outside financial transactions, including supply chain management, monitoring art ownership, and even digital collectibles.

A node is a word associated with blockchain technology that will be used throughout this essay. A node is a discrete component of a larger data structure known as a blockchain. Without nodes, the system would implode. Cryptography and blockchain technology assist cryptocurrencies in the creation of new coins, the enforcement of legal transactions, and the establishment of a secure system.

Decentralized

A distinguishing characteristic of cryptocurrencies is that they are not issued by any central authority, which supposedly protects them against government intervention or manipulation. As shown by Bitcoin, decentralization implies that all authoritative source is spread evenly across all peers on a network, and there is no single point of failure. For example, to "hack" Bitcoin, someone must access at least 51% of the vast network of computers that operate Bitcoin, which is considered impossible.

Peer-to-Peer

Without a broker, cryptocurrency may be transferred directly between two individuals. These transactions are made with minimal processing costs that benefit the network, allowing customers to avoid the high transaction fees associated with more conventional money transfer services.

There are many cryptocurrencies in existence. You've almost certainly heard of some of the most prominent cryptocurrencies, like Bitcoin, Litecoin, and Ethereum. Cryptocurrencies are becoming an increasingly popular alternative to traditional online payment methods. Before you convert actual dollars, euros, pounds, or other conventional currencies to cryptocurrencies, it's essential to understand what they are, the dangers associated with their use, and how to safeguard your investment.

A cryptocurrency is a digital money that was developed using encryption techniques. It is a type of alternative payment. Trustless

Bitcoin is trustless because it was built to require no one to trust anybody else to operate. Before Bitcoin, every form of money needed you to charge a central authority to use it. In each of these instances, the foremost authority becomes the primary weakness that ultimately results in the currency's downfall. With Bitcoin, each ecosystem component verifies what the others are saying without relying on anybody. When a Bitcoin transaction is broadcast, all nodes receive it and verify that the signatures are genuine. If the signatures are invalid, the transaction is discarded.

Because everyone on the network has a copy of the ledger, we no longer need to rely on a single entity, organization, or third party, as there is no need to trust when you can check against this ledger using your copy. The blockchain is the decentralized ledger, and Individual network players being rewarded through a proof-of-work (PoW) consensus mechanism is one of the most revolutionary concepts in contemporary economics.

Unchangeable/Immutable

In the most straightforward meaning, "immutable" implies "unable to be undone." Immutability should be based on three concepts about blockchain and cryptocurrency: Rewriting history should be extremely unlikely or difficult. Anyone other than the owner of a private key should be unable to transfer money. The blockchain records all transactions to ensure the two principles above.

We entrust our banks with the responsibility of not fabricating transactions or manipulating our money, just as we entrust them with the responsibility of delivering our transactions to receivers. As previously shown, a cryptocurrency needs more centralization and trust; there is no longer a third party in whom we can place our confidence to perform these functions. Distributed

Because "decentralization" is such a hot topic in the crypto world, it's critical to describe it precisely. It may have a variety of connotations "Blockchains are decentralized politically (no one controls them) and architecturally (there is no single point of failure in the infrastructure), but they are centralized conceptually (there is a single generally accepted state, and the system acts like a single computer)." — Vitalik Buterin.

Fault Tolerance: since decentralized systems rely on networks of independent components, they are less likely to fail unintentionally.

Attack Resistance: Because decentralized systems lack susceptible central points that may be attacked at a far lower cost than the surrounding system, they are more costly to attack, destroy, or control.

Collusion Resistance: individuals of decentralized systems find it more difficult to behave in ways that benefit themselves at the cost of others. On the other hand, businesses and governments constantly cooperate in ways that enrich them while harming others.

Besides central banks and governments, banks regulate the supply and production of money via mints and interest rates. Users of fiat currencies are at the whims of central banks regarding money creation.

Like Bitcoin, Litecoin, and Dash, numerous popular other cryptocurrencies have a finite supply, making them naturally deflationary. The Story Of Bitcoin's Birth

On November 1, 2008, an anonymous entity named Satoshi Nakamoto wrote in an email to a cryptography mailing list with a nine-page white paper containing the first-ever mention of Bitcoin, calling it a "peer-to-peer electronic cash system."

"I've been working on a new electronic cash system that's fully peer-to-peer, with no trusted third party. The paper is available at Bitcoin.org/Bitcoin.pdf." Nakomoto said. The original paper detailing the math and specifics of Bitcoin is still available on the website.

As Nakamoto proposed, the system's main properties would be that the electronic transactions would be peer-to-peer and would not need to involve a financial institution. The plan was to be completely decentralized, meaning that the currency users would not need to rest their trust in a central authority, such as traditional central banks. 

Satoshi also expressed dissatisfaction that banks have repeatedly breached the trust of the people who deposit money with them by lending them money in credit bubbles while keeping minimal reserves. "The root problem with conventional currency is all the trust required to make it work. After publishing his revolutionary paper, Nakamoto released Bitcoin's first software and partnered with developers and coders online to improve it.

How Does Bitcoin Work?

Digital currencies like Bitcoin are a relatively modern concept, and there are crucial differences between digital currencies and the currently circulating government-issued currencies. Digital currency is similar to real currency, but they are not issued by central banks nor financially backed by the state as digital money. Cryptocurrency issuing is decentralized and is not decided by politicians but by technical aspects that are usually well-defined.

For instance, Bitcoins use a cryptographic algorithm to generate the currency made by the network users and not by a centralized governing body. To open a conventional bank account, one might require several documents, such as a valid ID and proof of residence, whereas to register to a digital currency, a valid IP address will suffice. Individuals can use Bitcoins to pay others or merchants without involving any third party.

When someone talks about Bitcoin or the Bitcoin blockchain, they usually refer to the Bitcoin network. There are two types of participants in the network: wallets and nodes. Bitcoin users typically own wallets with which they can send and receive payments. Nodes are the computers that validate and verify Bitcoin transactions and maintain a history of past transactions. A node can also act as a wallet. Some Bitcoin network nodes are also known as miners.

Anyone can join the network as a wallet or node and then start using Bitcoin on equal terms with everyone else. Although every network participant can send and receive Bitcoin payments, only nodes keep track of the entire history of transactions in real time. This log of transactions maintained by the nodes is called the Bitcoin blockchain. The word blockchain comes from adding new transactions in segments called blocks. 

A block is a permanent record of recent transactions. The blocks of recorded data build upon each other to form the blockchain, dating back to the first Bitcoin transaction. The transparency established by the blockchain is essential in securing the validation process as it allows the community to monitor and self-police transaction activity. It also allows for verification of both the spender and the recipient and ensures that double-spending a Bitcoin is impossible. In the case of Bitcoin, new blocks are added roughly once every 10 minutes. Nodes validate every new transaction and ensure that only legitimate transactions get added to the blockchain.

A node can operate autonomously and requires no maintenance; it only requires an internet connection. Thus anyone can set up a node without much effort. Nodes communicate with each other in a peer-to-peer fashion. Peer-to-peer is like a network where each node relays information to a few other nodes, and eventually, the message gets to all the nodes in the network. If a node tries to cheat the network by marking an invalid transaction as valid, it would be rejected by all other nodes on the web. Therefore, nodes collectively guarantee the network's transparency, availability, decentralization, and security. The nodes are spread worldwide, and with over 10,000 Bitcoin nodes powering the network at any given time, cheating on such a network is nearly impossible.

How Do I Store Bitcoins and Make Transactions? 

Individuals must set up a digital wallet to safely store and transact Bitcoins. A wallet is a file that holds all the unique keys (passwords) attributed to an owner's Bitcoins. These keys will be recorded and synchronized when the system sends or receives payments. Wallets usually come in two forms: hot and cold. Hot wallets allow instantaneous transfers online through the Blockchain; they are connected to the internet. Cold wallets are offline wallets on a user's computer or device. Cold wallets offer greater security as they are offline, but the transaction process is longer and more cumbersome than a hot wallet. 

To receive Bitcoins, you provide the sender with your wallet address. A wallet address is a long sequence of numbers and letters. It is the unique identifier used to send payments to you. New addresses can be generated for each transaction to maintain greater financial privacy. 

Bitcoin technology significantly improves over traditional payment systems when utilizing the currency for both micro and large-scale payments. Micropayments can be made in increments as small as a fraction of a penny (0.00000001). The lowest possible unit of digital money, known as Bitcoin, is called a satoshi. It takes its name from Satoshi Nakamoto, credited with creating Bitcoin. The exchange rate for satoshi to bitcoin is currently set at 100 million satoshis to one bitcoin. For example, if you bought a $100 item with one bitcoin, your charge might ring up as .00341583 BTC (if BTC equaled $29,275.50). However, it's easier to understand 341,583 Satoshi. Conversely, the system can make large-scale payments without worrying about daily transaction limits or slow deposit processes standard at many traditional financial institutions.

Technical Analysis of Bitcoin

Technical analysis is an entirely different ballgame from fundamental analysis. In technical analysis, we take a statistical approach to study an asset by analyzing key metrics and studying charts using mathematical tools and functions. Here we will briefly discuss some essential methods and concepts that fall under technical analysis.

Support and Resistance

A support level is a price level where the desire to buy is so strong that it can withstand the pressure of sellers. As a result, the fall is suspended, and prices start increasing again. Usually, a support level can be determined in advance, according to the trends established by the previous decline. Likewise, a resistance level is opposite to a support level and represents a level above the market, where sellers' pressure exceeds buyers' influence. The prices tend to increase if the asset takes support at a support level, but it is likely to decline further if it breaks the support level and moves downwards. Similarly, if an asset breaks a resistance level, it is expected to keep moving up but is likely to move down if it does not cross the level.

Candlestick Charts

Unlike the traditional linear chart that shows the current and historical price data, Japanese candlesticks and bars use as many as four data points for a given time instead of one: 

1\. The opening price when the time began 

2\. The highest point the price reached during the time 

3\. The lowest point the price reached in the period 

4\. The closing price when the period ended 

These four points increase the information value of the chart, which means they can immediately indicate what is happening in the market. This is why almost always candlestick charts are preferred over regular line charts.

Indicator-Based Trading

Technical indicators are a great way to predict future movement and make better trading decisions; here, we will discuss a couple of technical indicators that traders can use to predict the future moment of Bitcoins market price.

Simple Moving Average (SMA) is the most common trend indicator. It gives an average price of a traded instrument over a particular time and identifies the primary trend. It looks like a smooth line on the chart. Moving averages are of 3 types generally - SMA, EMA, and WMA. All the moving averages need to catch up because we average the cost. If the price on your chart goes up or down sharply, this indicator will react, but you will receive this signal with a delay. 

When the price exceeds the moving average, it's a buy signal. When it crosses below, it's a sell signal. So we buy when the closing price is above MA and sell when the closing price is below MA.

Relative strength index (RSI) is a widely used technical indicator. It was developed by American engineer Welles Wilder in 1978. RSI shows how much the price changes in the direction of its movement. It transforms the cost into a percentage, indicating overbought zones (below 30%) and oversold zones (above 70%). 

Thus, RSI values of 30% or below indicate an oversold condition, while RSI values of 70% or above indicate that security is becoming overbought. When the price from the overbought zone enters the neutral zone, it is a sell signal; when the cost from the oversold zone enters the neutral zone, it's a buy signal.

Although technical indicators are developed using robust methods of math and statistics, they are not 100% accurate all of the time. Why is Risk Management Crucial?

Trading is a game of probabilities. In other words, a trading market is a place that is never certain. Thus, it comes with default risk. As a trader, the primary should be to reduce and manage risk in the best way possible. Professional traders believe understanding risk management is more crucial than having a good strategy.

Risk refers to the probability of an event happening against the intended outcome. Trading involves making predictions between two choices. And risk in this domain is simply the percentage of the money you are ready to lose. For instance, if a trader's account balance is $1000 and says he is risking 2% on a trade, the trader is betting 2% of his account balance, which is $20. So, if the business goes against the trader, he would be at most $20.

Risk Management Techniques

A trader who can manage their risk effectively can automatically maximize their profits. A professional trader’s approach is to control their losses rather than running after profits. Here are some risk management strategies that every trader should know mandatorily.

1\. Stop Loss and Take Profit

The first and foremost rule in trading is to have a stop loss and take profit for every trade. Stop-loss is an executable order that cuts the positions at a specified price when the market goes in the intended direction. On the other hand, take profit is also an executable order that liquidates the parts when the market reaches a specified price in the trader's favor. A stop-loss will keep a trader safe from further losses, and taking profit will lock in the money even if the market goes against the desired direction later.

2\. Position Sizing

Position sizing is the number of units of security invested by a trader. This is not a random number derived using the account size and risk tolerance.

Firstly, account size is the account balance in a trading account. For example, if a trader deposits $1,000 into his trading account, the account size will be $1,000.

Account risk is the percentage of the account size a trader is ready to lose on a trade. For example, if you set your account risk to 1%, you will risk 1% of your account balance on the work.

Trade risk is simply the stop loss for a trade. It is the difference between the entry price and the stop-loss price. For example, if the entry price for a buy is $210, and the stop loss is $160, the trade risk would be $50 per unit traded.

Position size

Using the above two parameters, the position size for a trade can be determined. Mathematically, the position size is obtained by dividing the account risk by the trade risk. For example, if the account risk on a trade is $100 and the trade risk is $2 per unit, the position size would be $100/$2 = 50 units.

3\. Risk/Reward Ratio

Another critical risk management tool is the risk/reward ratio. This ratio compares actual risk to the potential gains in a trade. The risk/reward tells you whether a business is worth taking. For example, there could be situations when your stop loss has to be higher than the take profit. In such trades, you would be risking more than gaining.

Thus, this ratio is used to stay cautious with such scenarios. For instance, if a trader is risking $100 on a trade and the risk: reward is 1:1, he is betting $100 to make $100. Similarly, if the ratio were 1:2, he would risk $100 to make $200. Therefore, you should refrain from trading under 1:1 to safeguard your capital.

Key Features of DeFi

Immutability

Use Cases of DeFi 

DeFi has taken the world by a whirlwind with its efficacy, resilience, immutability, and transparency in financial operations. The use cases of this system are already growing at an unprecedented rate and are expected to follow an ascending graph line. A few prominent use cases have been delineated below. 

Borrowing and Lending

With DeFi's influence on systems of finance and transactions increasing, the loopholes in the traditional finance system are becoming all the more evident to the users. Traditional financial markets have proliferation in revenue generation by the interest they pay the lenders and the interest they charge from the borrowers. Such a system often discouraged the borrowers from approaching these markets due to rigid protocols for qualifying for a loan, including viable collateral and a hefty amount of interest charged. The digital ledger technology bolstered by DeFi opens opportunities for borrowers who need a loan and fail to procure it due to rigid requirements. With smart contracts in place, lending/borrowing loans and maintaining the terms of conditions outlined in the smart contracts smoothens the dual lending and borrowing processes. The dynamics structuring DeFi support peer-to-peer lending and borrowing. This use case is finding widespread application in the form of an increase in DeFi projects about this. 

For instance, Compound is a DeFi protocol that stands out because it has an autonomous interest basis. Compound welcomes lenders to earn interest on various cryptocurrencies. It provides the lenders with tokens, which are then siphoned off to the borrowers at algorithmically decided autonomous interest rates. The compound is highly efficient because the intelligent contract identifies the lenders and borrowers with concurring interests and lays out interest rates following the borrowed and lent assets ratio in the discussion. Another prominent example of such a project is ‘Pool Together,’ which uses akin mechanisms in lending and borrowing money.

Tokenization

One of the significant use cases of the DeFi system is in the context of tokenization. Tokenization refers to curating, issuing, regulating, and transferring digital assets on a blockchain network. Tokenization is highly pertinent because any form of investment can be converted into tokens for their storage on the blockchain. Ethereum blockchain successfully utilizes tokenization and has granted the world a secure and easy means of trading in the form of Ethereum-based tokens. Out of the many advantages conferred by the usage of tickets, the main benefits emerge as fractional aspects of these tokens and available options for their customization. Thus tokenization is one of the central features of the booming DeFi ecosystem due to its digital alternatives and efficacy in complementing trade.   

Efficacy of Stablecoins

One of the significant downsides of cryptocurrency is the profound volatility that is widespread in the crypto ecosystem. Such fluctuations of prices that experience frequent ascent and descent in their value result in the reduction of the reliability of cryptocurrency. Stablecoins pose as a savior in this context. Stablecoin can be understood as a type of unique cryptocurrency because it is tied with an or bulk of them. These assets can be anything ranging from fiat to gold or even any other kind of cryptocurrency. Their consolidation with acquisitions offers stability in contrast to the volatility in the crypto market. They also provide the blockchain greater credibility as a reliable payment method. Stablecoins are now used for borrowing, lending, etc., in the DeFi ecosystem. 

Process of Asset Management 

DeFi allows users to exercise greater control over their assets. With no central authority for regulation, Decentralization helps the users gain absolute control of their assets. It will enable the users to manage their assets according to their volition. It can involve buying, selling, lending, or transferring digital assets. By removing an intermediary, only the users can access and manage their assets in any way they deem perfect. For example, in many DeFi projects, including Gnosis Safe, Argent facilitates the users' complete control of their information and ensures safe data storage with the user only. 

Application in the Insurance Industry

Another major use case of DeFi is in the insurance industry. DeFi is revolutionizing the insurance industry by eliminating the significant limitations posed by the traditional handling of insurance systems. Such rules include unfathomable amounts of paperwork, the flawed claim handling process, time-consuming, etc. The insurance industry also experiences setbacks due to the fraudulent practices of false claims. Blockchain's decentralized technology can verify the presented documents, ownership, policy registration-related details, and claim authenticity. With smart contracts in place, the process of financial exchange as a result of insurance becomes easier for both the seeker and the insurance company.  

Compliance Supported by the KYT mechanism 

Know-your-customers (KYC) protocols majorly guided compliance in countering-the-financing-of-terrorism and anti-money laundering (AML). However, KYC protocols often interfered with DeFi's premise of privacy. It also did involve the external authority at one step or another contradicting the decentralization aspect of DeFi. Therefore, Defi has introduced a relatively evolved and better Know-Your-Transaction (KYT) mechanism. This mechanism lays the ground for the focus to shift from the identity of the user to digital addresses and the transaction behavior of the users. Such a mechanism assesses the live behavior of transactions and maintains the users' privacy. This mechanism helps assess risk or crisis proliferating in the system, early detection of which can combat eventual large-scale frauds and siphoning of hefty amounts.  

Decentralized Exchanges

Decentralized exchanges (abbreviated as DEXs) refer to the transactions of cryptocurrency that occur P2P (peer-to-peer) without the involvement of any central authority, thus supporting the basic premise of DeFi. The assets in such exchanges are non-custodial to any external exchange platform. It allows users to interact, transact and maintain control of their assets. The significant advantage conferred is in terms of mitigation of risks. Since the assets are not under the direct supervision of any external authority or platform, it reduces the chances of hacking, fraud, and price manipulation. Currently, many DEXs are used in the user-friendly and easily accessible DeFi space. The most popular ones are Liquality, AirSwap, Mesa, etc.  

Bolstered Digital Identity

Digital identity systems based on blockchain are coming to focus as viable and efficient identity systems. Mainly when amalgamated with the DeFi protocols, these identity systems can experience a boost in their efficiency in terms of immediate processing when required to avail of financial services. These systems are also changing the indicators of creditworthiness. However, the digital identity reinforced by DeFi and blockchain protocols authenticates the seekers by their transactional behavior, financial activities, online reputation, or any other attributes that deem practical and necessary. Significant Considerations of DeFi

Even though the DeFi systems offer a vast array of advantageous facets due to the culmination of finance and blockchain networks on DLT, it is still fraught with many risks and limitations, which the users should be aware of before venturing into the DeFi ecosystem. With the increasing applications industries, DeFi has also become home to cyber perpetrators on these platforms. Reports published by CipherTrace (a firm involved in crypto analytics) brought to light threatening figures of crypto crimes. According to this report, DeFi thefts in 2020 alone were about $100 million. In the latter half of 2020, about half of the cryptocurrency thefts were due to DeFi protocols. These staggering figures indicate the risks associated with DeFi. In addition to these risks, this system has many downsides, which will be explained in the sections below. 

Before delineating the cons of this system, it is pertinent to note a few things. To begin with, the more significant part of the downsides of DeFi is because of the technology upon which they are built. The challenges of the base technology, in turn, propels the minor downsides of DeFi to become profound in their impact. If the blockchain network or technology that DeFi apps are built on suffers from issues of instability. If the base technology they are built upon is stable, the subsequently developed protocols will also be safe. Additionally, some of the risks associated with DeFi can be mitigated and managed if the users know them.  

Risks Arising Due to Smart Contracts 

The first and foremost disadvantage branches from the essential components of this system: smart contracts. Although intelligent contracts have eased the process of exchanging financial services, ensuring high reliability, even then, it is fraught with a few technical limitations. Smart contracts, at their outset, are at the disposal of humans, as the lines of the code are written by an individual only before its public upload on the blockchain. This introduces the first instance of the possibility of human error. No matter how strict a smart contract is, it can still have many loopholes. The availability of these smart contracts on the public blockchain offers the content multiple sets of eyes to skim through the contract for the slightest loopholes. While just a few individuals form an intelligent agreement, it is available for audit to the global database accessing it. Such math opens up opportunities for exploitation of the loopholes present in the smart contract. Even a single loophole can be misused to drain millions from the smart contract.

Since smart contracts are available on the public blockchain, prospective investors can review them themselves or often outsource the review to security firms for auditing. This brings to light the second possibility of losses due to human error. Humans can miss out on loopholes in the contract, which then leads to exploitation in the future and financial losses eventually.  

For example, Decentralization Autonomous Organization (DAO) was built on an open-source code allowing absolute autonomy and supplementing Decentralization. Launched in April 2016, it was a venture capital fund directed by investors. It saw exponential growth, which soon made this platform one of the most significant crowdfunding ventures, collecting around $120 million. By June 2016, however, an array of hackers emerged who identified a few flaws and vulnerabilities in the smart contract. These vulnerabilities helped the hackers in siphoning off about $50 million. The siphoned amount made it impossible for investors to access their funds for weeks. This became the biggest hack in the history of crowdfunding.

Efforts are being made to prevent the exploitation of smart contracts. For instance, Nexus Mutual is a team focused on structuring aspects of decentralized insurance that will help ensure the interests and security of the users in case they are faced with scams and frauds by cyber perpetrators on these systems. 

Hacking and Frauds

The proliferation of fraud occurs in a way that often goes unnoticed at the outset and comes to the spotlight in retrospect. There have been reports of funds laundering on DeFi platforms like Kyber Network, Uniswap, etc. As explained in the previous sections, another source for fraud to occur is hacking by identifying the loopholes in the smart contract that are available on the blockchain network. Such activities put shade on the security of the funds on the DeFi platforms, raising concerns in the context of the trust quotient. While this may lead to users losing their money, on the one hand, it discourages non-existing users from switching from traditional financial systems to DeFi. 

Problems with the Scalability of the Host Platforms 

The DeFi protocols are well-suited for ensuring the inclusion of more people to cater to their financial services irrespective of geographic impediments. However, blockchain-based projects need to improve the scalability of these host platforms. DeFi suffers as the transactions take longer to process after confirmation. This can be attributed to the fact that these DeFi systems can process a definite set number of transactions at a single moment. For instance, the DeFi-based platform - Ethereum, even at its total capacity, can only process about 13 transactions per second. At the same time, traditional centralized financial systems can allow many transactions in the same period without any error. Therefore, despite the ease offered by DeFi in initiating a transaction, it needs substantial waiting periods for the confirmation of the transaction. Additionally, transaction costs increase at times of such congestion. 

The basic premise of DeFi systems is bringing Decentralization to the table and replacing the conventional financial plans with the central authority as an impediment. This Decentralization provides transparency, flexibility, and accessibility, increasing the trustworthiness of DeFi projects and reducing the possibilities of fraud and scandals. However, despite the claims, not all services of DeFi systems are decentralized. For example, a critical use case of these systems in the form of stablecoins introduces the role of central authority either directly in the case of fiat currency-based stablecoins or indirectly in the case of commodity-backed stablecoins. 

Apart from the abovementioned issues, many other problems inflect the DeFi systems. To begin with, the anonymity DeFi systems provide can often accelerate the chances of scams and fraud. With the public space open to all users around the globe, the risk factor becomes prominent. Since the DeFi platform does not require KYC from its users, it often includes the bad actors on the forum who indulge in malpractices. Although the verification systems used by DeFi are efficient, robust, and less time-consuming, they can often be misleading. Since authentication is facilitated by tracking online behavior, transactional activities, and other such practical attributes, these attributes can be manipulated, thus tricking the authentication system. 

Secondly, insurance is given requisite attention in the case of centralized finance due to its necessity in ensuring the customers' trust and protecting them from events of hacking or fraud which might lead the customers to incur substantial financial losses. However, the aspect of insurance in the case of DeFi is rarely given due importance, if ever. Also, such insurances demand a place, especially when the users have to take responsibility solely.

Even if DeFi has no associated risks or issues, the responsibility for everything will be on the user's shoulders. With the complexity of the operation, room for mistakes is always there, especially for users with less experience with operating technology or those who have just joined DeFi platforms. Their likelihood of losing their funds due to a tiny mistake causes them significant financial problems. # Chapter 6: Non-Fungible Tokens

What are NFTs?

Non-Fungible Tokens or NFTs, as the name suggests, are tokens, much like other crypto tokens such as Bitcoin or ether; however, the critical difference is that they are non-fungible, meaning they cannot be copied or replicated. Each of the tokens is unique by itself. E.g., when you buy a cryptocurrency, such as a Bitcoin, and then you exchange it for another Bitcoin, in the end, you have the same thing; there is no way to differentiate between the Bitcoin that you were holding earlier and the Bitcoin that you are holding now, this means that they are fungible.

Non-Fungible tokens are unique because if you exchange one NFT of similar value with another NFT, then what you have in your hand is something different. The fungibility of currencies and cryptocurrencies usually helps us use them as a medium of exchange since everyone has an agreed value common to all the units of the said currency. NFTs, on the other hand, can be compared to collectibles such as unique sports cards or art pieces.

NFTs use blockchain technology to register the transaction, typically, it is the Ethereum blockchain, but other blockchains are also used, which keeps track of the ownership of the NFT. NFTs are created on these blockchains using the ERC-721 and ERC-1155 frameworks. More often than not, NFTs are artwork such as images, GIFs, videos, and music. What happens is that the person purchasing that original piece of art has proof of their ownership, the transaction, stored on the blockchain. Since we know that blockchain has immutable and permanent data, the transaction cannot be altered and is thus a set proof of the fact that the person owns that particular asset.

The value of most things is directly proportional to the scarcity of the said thing. When we say that gold and diamonds are expensive, they are only available in a limited amount, and we cannot create any more natural gold or diamonds. Similarly, art pieces such as those by Picasso, Monet, Banksy, or Basquiat carry astronomical values during auctions and other sales because they are only a limited number of such pieces. Further, each piece is unique from the other. Before the advent of blockchain technology and NFTs, there was no such scarcity of digital art forms, one could search for a song or a photograph and they would get thousands of copies of the same. With NFTs, however, there is a newfound scarcity in the ownership of digital assets, which increases the value of these digital assets. 

NFTs work as financial assets; they can be compared to physical art, except they are digital art forms. Just like physical art has no intrinsic value, and most people purchase it either for the bragging rights of having the ownership of the said piece of art or for using it as an investment which will give them returns when they sell it off at a higher value in the future, NFTs are similarly either owned to be flipped for profit in the future or for the fact that one holds the original version of a digital asset.

What one needs to understand, however, is the fact that just because they have ownership of an NFT doesn’t mean that the same digital art can’t be reproduced by anyone else. Think of it as a painting, such as The Starry Night by Vincent Van Gogh; At the same time, various painters made multiple copies of The Starry Night, and it is even available as posters or drawing aids; the ownership of the original Starry Night lies in the hands of a single person, or this case, the Rijks museum. Similarly, the same gif or image will be available online on various web pages or search engines. Still, the original art lies in the hands of the owner of the non-fungible token.

When a person purchases art using an NFT, the artist or the original owner encrypts digital information and metadata onto the blockchain. This data is used to prove the ownership of the NFT. Further, since blockchains have timestamps, one could even trace back the right of the digital asset to see when and how many times it has changed hands over time. 

NFTs can be traded like any other digital assets; there are markets for NFTs where one can go and purchase the token they like. Any fixed market force does not set the price for NFTs; much like art, their price is determined by what the buyer is willing to pay for the asset. NFTs can even be programmed so that the owner is paid a certain amount every time the NFT changes hands, so in case the price of the NFT skyrockets, the original owner is still compensated. NFTs use distributed ledger technology such as blockchain, which means there is no need for an intermediary or an exchange for the transfer of NFTs, bringing down the transaction costs.

NFTs have little to no regulations in the global markets, and their future is very much connected with cryptocurrencies and blockchain technology. For example, if cryptocurrency trade is banned in a particular country, then the sale and purchase of NFTs will also be restricted there.  

While NFTs have become increasingly popular, their criticisms have also increased. One of the significant criticism associated with NFTs is their carbon footprint. The world is currently dealing with a climatic disaster partly fuelled by using non-renewable and polluting energy sources. We know that blockchain is a very energy-intensive industry. It takes electricity to run the servers responsible for mining on the blockchains. NFTs, to be created also need similar mining requirements, causing pollution and climate change. Some artists have pulled out of making their NFTs after getting to know about the environmental impact of NFTs.

Properties of Non-fungible Tokens

Non-fungible tokens have certain features which help it function the way it does. Some of these features are:

Unique: Every NFT is unique, i.e., an NFT cannot be copied. Unlike other cryptocurrencies, one NFT does not equate to another NFT, even if they are of the same value. If one trades NFTs, they have something different in their hand and not just a separate copy of the same thing. 

Indivisible: NFTs are also indivisible, which means that NFTs cannot be broken down into smaller parts of lesser value and then sold, collected again, and reassembled. An NFT is always traded as a whole. 

Indestructible: The data for an NFT is stored on blockchains. Most of the NFTs are stored on the Ethereum blockchain. Since we know that the data on blockchains are tough to destroy or alter, especially on public blockchains, the NFTs cannot be destroyed. 

Absolute ownership: When a person owns an NFT, they carry total ownership over the digital asset; it is not like purchasing the license to use a particular image, song, or gif. This means that the owner has unrestricted access to these NFTs.

Verifiable: Since the data regarding the ownership of an NFT is stored on blockchains and such data is always timestamped and, at the same time, assured of authenticity, it is easy to verify and trace the ownership of an NFT. 

Limited: NFTs are limited in number; they are scarce. Most NFTs are only produced singularly; however, an artist may have a limited number of similar products, such as 10 or 50, much like little edition clothes or cars by real-life manufacturers.

Transferable: NFTs are digital assets or certificates that pronounce the ownership of digital assets; hence like any other digital asset, NFTs can be transferred from one person to another for a price.

Permanent: The data stored on blockchains are endless; once entered, it is always there. Unlike real-life paintings or art forms that might get spoilt over time, the NFTs will be permanent.

Differences Between NFTs and Typical Cryptocurrencies

Before we look at how non-fungible tokens differ from the otherwise mainstream or typical cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin, Ether, or Cardano, we must first understand their similarities and what connects them. NFTs and cryptocurrencies are, in essence, digital assets that can be sold or purchased on online platforms with little to no inherent or intrinsic value. Both NFTs and cryptocurrencies are based on blockchain or distributed ledger technologies. The transaction history in both cases is stored on blockchains and is permanent and immutable. NFTs, much like cryptocurrencies, are also in a grey area when it comes to regulation in most countries.

Nature: The first point of difference that we see between NFTs and cryptocurrencies is their essential nature. When we talk about cryptocurrencies, we consider them, as the name suggests, as currencies, i.e., they are to be used as tools or money-like instruments that will be used for purchasing other things or assets. However, in some places and by some people, they are treated as investments that might grow in value and then be sold off. On the other hand, when we talk about NFTs, as earlier discussed, they are not currencies despite being tokens. NFTs are like assets in nature. They are not to be used for purchasing other stuff but rather as collectibles. While cryptocurrencies can be compared to money in real life, NFTs can be compared to works of Art and Luxury.  

Fungibility: The most crucial characteristic of NFTs that differentiates them from typical cryptocurrencies is the one that is in their name, i.e., fungibility. Fungibility is the ability of similar goods or assets to be interchangeable or replaced by an identical item. Fungibility is an essential feature of currency. It ensures that people can freely use cash anywhere, and there is no difference between one unit of money with another. Cryptocurrencies like fiat currencies are fungible. If you trade one ether for another, there is no difference between the two; you essentially have the same thing in hand. On the other hand, NFTs are not fungible; each one is unique and thus cannot be copied. If you exchange one NFT for another, you have something different now than you had earlier.

Value determinant: The way NFTs are determined is also different from how the value of cryptocurrencies is determined. Since cryptocurrencies have a very high volume of sales and exchange hands very frequently, thus the price of the cryptocurrencies is determined by the prevailing market forces, i.e., demand and supply. NFTs are unique, and each carries a different value; further, the price of the NFT is determined by what the buyer is willing to pay for the said product. In the case of NFTs, the market's supply side is constant. 

Cost: The cost or price of NFTs and cryptocurrencies also differ to a great extent. When we talk about cryptocurrencies, the most popular and expensive cryptocurrency is Bitcoin. Bitcoin, during the bull market of 2021, reached a peak price of just above 64,000 dollars. This, however, completes pales in comparison to the kind of prices that people have paid for some NFTs. People regularly pay upwards of a million dollars for NFTs, with the most expensive NFT netting around 69 million dollars during an auction in March 2021.

Divisibility: Another difference between NFTs and cryptocurrencies is their ability to be divided into smaller pieces. Cryptocurrencies can be broken down into pieces, which can further be broken down into parts. This is essential for expensive cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin so people can purchase them in smaller quantities. NFTs always exist as a whole, and they are like art pieces. They can't be broken down, traded, collected, and reassembled.  

Blockchain used: The blockchains that are used for the two also differ. While for cryptocurrencies, almost every cryptocurrency runs on its blockchain, which stores the transactions of the said currency. For example, the trades of Ether are recorded on Ethereum. Whereas NFTs are more often than not running on the ether blockchain, some of them are also on the Binance bright chain. These are the two most commonly used blockchains for NFTs.

Purchase platforms: The platforms at which the two are traded are also different. At the same time, Cryptocurrencies are traded on crypto exchanges, such as Binance and Coinbase. These crypto exchanges can be both centralized or decentralized in nature. NFTs are bought and sold on NFT platforms like Open Sea and Nifty Gateway. At the same time, NFTs are also being sold by auction houses such as Sotheby’s and Christie’s.

Currencies Accepted: The money that can be paid for the purchase of the two is also different. When we talk about cryptocurrencies, one can pay for them in fiat currencies or other cryptocurrencies, depending on their crypto exchange. Thus we see that cryptocurrencies are readily convertible to real-world money. On the other hand, to purchase NFTs, one has to pay only in cryptocurrencies. If the NFT is based on the Ethereum blockchain, it is paid for in Ethers (ETH), and if it is on the Binance Smart Chain, then it is paid for in Binance coins (BNB)

Timeline: The timeline of the emergence of the two is also very different. Cryptocurrencies were first created in 2008, the first being Bitcoin. Cryptocurrencies came into the mainstream in the middle to late 2010s, with the first bull market in 2017 and the second in early 2021. On the other hand, the first NFT was introduced in 2016, it became slightly famous in 2017 due to crypto kitties and punks, but it only entered the mainstream in 2021 when the NFT market ultimately ballooned. 

Workings of NFTs

Creating an NFT is relatively easy; various platforms allow people to make an NFT. To make a non-fungible token, one needs two things, the digital art or asset that will be converted into a non-fungible permit and the payment for doing the minting. Recently, much stuff has been converted into non-fungible tokens, including digital art, songs, videos, and even tweets; anything with a digital form or signature can be converted into an NFT. Once created, the NFTs can be viewed by the owner on wallets, just like cryptocurrencies. These NFTs similarly exist on addresses on the blockchain, which can be accessed by the person holding the key. 

During the minting process of an NFT, the owner or creator of the NFT executes code that conforms to different NFT standards. NFTs use standards and protocols such as the following:

ERC - 721: ERC - 721 was the original standard developed to create NFTs in 2017. It was written in Solidity on the Ethereum blockchain. This standard allows minting new NFTs easier, faster, and non-repetitive. ERC-721 is the most commonly used standard; anyone can create their own NFT.

ERC - 1155: ERC - 1155 is another standard on the Ethereum blockchain. It was developed after ERC-721 and is considered an upgrade over the previous one. The bar has smart contracts executed in a way that makes new non-fungible tokens. One of the significant advantages that ERC - 1155 has over ERC - 721 is that one can create multiple NFTs on a single, smart contract. On an ERC-721, one had to run the intelligent contract for a new NFT every time. The ERC-1155 is more efficient and takes less storage space, but it is also more diverse since it can make fungible tokens and non-fungible receipts. 

Hedera Token Service - This is a non-Ethereum-based standard that Hedera offers. This is much cheaper than the Ethereum standards and also faster. Infinite by SUKU is one company that has shifted to Hedera from Ethereum.

Currently, we see that NFTs are proprietary and have single owners. There are plans to introduce fractional NFT standards shortly, allowing multiple users to own a fraction of a single NFT. This would align with the decentralizing and multiple-node nature of blockchains in general.

What is a Blockchain?

Blockchain is often confused with the first-ever cryptocurrency - Bitcoin. This is because the term blockchain became popular only after the inception of Bitcoin. In reality, blockchain is the primary technology that enables the working of cryptos. If not for blockchain, we won’t be seeing any of the cryptocurrencies that are in circulation today. It is essential to know that cryptocurrency is just one of the various potential applications of blockchain technology.

In simple terms, a blockchain is a distributed electronic ledger built around a Peer 2 Peer system. This ledger can be shared among infinite users for their reference. The transactions entered into the blockchain are tamper-proof and time-stamped individually. A set of valid transactions makes a block complete. Each verified block is linked to the previous block to form a chain of blocks. Hence the name - Blockchain!  

The working of the Blockchain is different from that of centralized systems. It is a distributed ledger and works as a consensus-based network, thus, making it decentralized. Following is the step-by-step working of a transaction on the Blockchain.

1\. A node, a computer in the blockchain network, receives a transaction from the sender. It initiates that transaction on the web by creating it. Later, the cryptography technique gets the transaction digitally signed with a private key.

2\. The transaction information is passed from that node to all the other nodes on the network to validate the transaction based on some criteria preset by the Blockchain. This process is called the Gossip protocol. In technical terms, it is called the Proof of Work, the standard consensus algorithm of Blockchain. According to the algorithm, the nodes must solve a complex mathematical puzzle to authenticate a transaction. Usually, two or more nodes are required to validate the transaction.

3\. Once the nodes validate the transaction entirely, it is included inside a block and is propagated toward the leading blockchain network. This is when the trade gets its first confirmation.

4\. Later, when a new block is created for the same transaction, it goes through the same process above and gets linked to the first block, referred to as the “genesis block.” The league gets its first confirmation, and the transaction reaches its second confirmation.

5\. the same process is repeatedly reconfirmed for every new block created. Typically, about five-six confirmations are required for the transaction to be officially complete. And finally, the ownership of the cryptocurrency/digital asset is moved to the receiver.

More importantly, these contracts are cost-effective as we can eliminate fees to typical intermediaries such as authorizers, document creators, etc.

Types of Blockchains

Critical Components of a Blockchain

To understand how a blockchain works, one must first know what a blockchain is made of. The components of a blockchain are:

Blocks: The blocks are the most basic units of storing information on a blockchain. After each block is full of data, it is connected to the previous block, thereby creating a chain of data that we call a blockchain. Blocks themselves consist of multiple parts. These parts are:

Data: This data has been stored on the block for safekeeping.

Nonce: A 32-bit whole number is generated for every block when creating a new partnership. This nonce then creates the hash.

Hash: The hash is a 256-bit number unique to every block. This hash encrypts the data on a block and connects one block to another in a given blockchain.

Block header: The block header provides us with the metadata related to the block, such as the hash of the previous block, the time stamp, and the nonce. 

Transactions: The block contains all of the transactions that have been stored on the block.

Nodes: Nodes refer to the people or servers with whom the blockchain has been shared. A blockchain has no central authority, and data is stored on distributed ledgers. Each of these ledgers has its node. The more the number of nodes in a blockchain, the more secure it is. Since the same data is secured on every node, the hacker will have to access each node and then change their data to hack into it or make a change. 

Miners: Miners are the people who are responsible for adding new blocks to a blockchain. To add a new block to a blockchain, one needs to solve a complex mathematical problem; this problem can only be solved by trial and error and hence requires significant computing power. A nonce is a 32-bit number, and the hash is a 256-bit number, so about 4 billion nonce-bit combinations are possible. The process of finding the correct combination is called mining. For undergoing this process, the miners are rewarded in cryptocurrencies.

Principles of Blockchain

Blockchain technology works based on certain principles; these principles are:

Distributed Database: The most basic principle behind blockchains is that the database in a blockchain is not stored in a single location and is spread across various nodes and servers. It means that for a transaction to be entered into a blockchain, it must be entered in every node, making it a very secure form of data storage, albeit a bit slower.

Peer-to-peer Transactions: The transactions and communication in a blockchain happen directly between the peers and nodes and not via a moderator. This ensures a certain amount of trust and transparency between blockchain network users.

Transparency: The transactions stored in a public blockchain are transparent. Anyone can access these transactions, making it harder for people to cheat or defraud others by creating false entries.

Irreversibility: The transaction, once stored on the blockchain, is marked with a timestamp, and it is stored as such. The data, once entered, cannot be changed after that. The entire chain must be mined again to make even a small change on one of the blockchain blocks. This would require massive computing power and would be nearly impossible.

Computational Logic: The data is stored on the blocks in a logical format, so the users don’t have to add it manually. They can create algorithms to enter the transactions by themselves. 

Cryptography in a Blockchain

A blockchain uses two basic kinds of cryptography: the asymmetric key and the hash function. These two are used at the same time and have different processes.

The asymmetric key means two types of keys are needed to access the blockchain. For every blockchain, there is a public key and a private key. The public key is available to everyone on the network, and it is the same for everyone. However, the private key is unique to every node and is only available to the node owner. In this kind of cryptography, the public encrypts the plain data into cipher text. The node owner then uses the private key to decipher the reader to plain text. Combining the private and public keys creates a signature unique to the node owner. 

The hash function is the cryptography used for chaining the blocks to each other. Every block has its hash; at the same time, it also carries the previous block's hash. E.g., block number 21 in a blockchain will have the hash of block number 20, which will chain it to the last block. Meanwhile, block number 22 will have the hash of block number 21; this is how they connect and create a chain. It ensures no new person can create and attach random data to a blockchain.  The block’s hash is made via the Merkel tree, where every transaction has its hash, which keeps combining until we get a hash for the whole block.

Fundamental Properties of Blockchain

Some of the fundamental properties of a blockchain are:

Distributed: One of the most essential features of blockchains is that they have a distributed ledger. They are also called distributed ledger systems for the same reason. It means a blockchain has multiple nodal centers instead of having a single central authority or a central node from where the data is disseminated. The data is hence stored on all these servers at the same time. These various nodes ensure the security of the data since it is nearly impossible to mutate the data on all nodes simultaneously. This also reduces the cost of the database since there is no need to have large servers on which the data is stored, thereby saving the cost of purchasing these servers and the rent of physically holding all these servers in a place.

Immutable: The data in a blockchain is immutable, i.e., the data is permanent in nature, and it can’t be mutated. This is so because once the various nodes verify the transaction on a blockchain, it is stored on a block, which is then attached to the chain. To change the data on a block, one needs to have the correct nonce and hash combination, which is one out of four billion and can only be found through the hit and trial method. Further, each block has its hash in the next block, so to change one block, one needs to change the data in every one of the blocks in the chain. 

Timestamped: One of the defining features of blockchain technology is that all of the transactions stored on the platform are time-stamped. This means the time and date are also simultaneously attached as soon as the transaction is entered on the block. This ensures that no one can backdate and change the values. It provides us with a record in chronological order. This feature allows us to keep track of all cryptocurrency transactions and ensure that the cryptocurrency is not doubly sold or bought.

Unanimous: The data stored on the blockchain platform is unanimous because everyone on the forum is verified and accepted through all nodes. One of the nodes may carry a different value or transaction, and the other takes an additional weight or a trade. Due to this feature, everyone has access to the same data, reducing the risks of discrepancies and errors in communication.

Anonymous: The transactions recorded on the blockchains do not carry the name or identity of the people involved. Most of these transactions are either anonymous or pseudonymous. This allows people to carry on transactions without associating themselves with it and, to a certain extent, pass various laws and regulations. The transactions cannot be traced back to the people.

Secure: The data history on a blockchain is considered incredibly fast. Since the data is not stored on a single server and the same data is stored on multiple nodes across the network, it is tough for someone to hack into the blockchain network and change the data at every node. In a traditional database, central servers are easy to break into; however, this is not so in a blockchain. Hence, blockchains are much more secure than the usual servers.

Programmable: Blockchain is a programmable data-storing service. This means the data must be manually put into the servers only sometimes. One can set up mathematical and logical algorithms that verify the transactions themselves. It also allows for setting up intelligent contracts over blockchain technology, a feature now increasingly used by blockchain companies such as Ethereum.

Crypto Investing Strategies

Investing is not just a game of luck, as many people have started to believe now. Investing takes a lot of analytics, decision-making, and asking the right questions. As Ben Graham, the father of value investing, as he is often called, once said, "The individual investor should act consistently as an investor and not as a speculator." So just putting money in a particular coin or currency because it is trendy is not smart investing. To become a better investor, you need to learn the game and start investing with specific ideas and goals; this means you must follow a particular set of strategies. There is, of course, still much leeway within these strategies and individual decisions to make, but they provide you with a guiding path you can follow.

Cost Averaging

This is one of the most commonly used investing strategies. Depending on the currency used, it is sometimes called Dollar Cost Averaging. The main idea behind this strategy is to average the cost of your investment over a more extended period, primarily when investing in a highly volatile market or where the price of the said investment is high.

Under the cost averaging method, the investor does, instead of purchasing all of the investment in one go at a single point, the purchases are spread over time. They can be done every week or every month at the same time. Thereby it reduces the risk of the investment's price falling drastically over a short time, and one is left with cryptocurrencies that were bought at a high price, reducing the possible profits. 

For example, If investor A wishes to purchase a cryptocurrency worth 100 dollars and the current price is 10 dollars, then he can buy ten such coins at a go; however, if he invests 40 dollars at first and gets four coins. Then next week, when the price is 11 dollars, he gets 2.7 coins for 30 dollars; the week after that, when the price is 8 dollars, he gets 3.75 coins for 30 dollars. So by the end of three weeks, he has 10.45 coins at 9.56 dollars per coin. So we see that, on average, despite the fluctuations, the cost of each currency is lower than what would have happened had invested all of them in one go. If the price rises significantly, the investor can hold back on the subsequent investments if he wishes to.

HODL

HODL Means HODLing, which is a deliberate misspelling of the word holding, and it was created when an investor on a Bitcoin forum saw the fall in the price of Bitcoin and said, "I am HOLDING." It is easy to understand the concept behind this strategy. This is one of the most straightforward strategies, and most beginners try to use it. It is a way to invest the money you know you won't need for a few years to earn a sleeping income. One should follow the golden rule: Only invest the money you can afford to lose.

The point is to invest in blue-chip cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin or Ethereum and then leave the investment for at least a few years. This strategy is, in fact, not only limited to just cryptocurrencies; it is generally accepted that holding on to blue-chip stocks gives better long-term results than other volatile riskier investments. The strategy is based on the fact that Blockchain and cryptocurrency are expected to be the next big thing and are still nascent. Even though the market might dip a bit right now, it will only grow over the coming years. 

HODLing also lets you earn passive income through yields, which are cryptocurrency dividends. If you stake your cryptocurrencies, you can profit about 5-15% annually. Of course, it includes a certain amount of risk since not all hard wallets allow for staking.

Balanced Portfolio Diversification

Diversification is one of the oldest strategies in the world of investing, and it is equally applicable to crypto investing. As mentioned earlier, portfolio diversification is hedging your risks onto several different cryptocurrencies. If even one fails, you still have profited from other cryptocurrencies to balance the losses. Of course, the reward in this system is slightly lower, but so is the risk.

In a balanced portfolio, the investor puts an equal amount of money into all of his investments; e.g., if he has 500 dollars and is investing in 5 cryptocurrencies, it will be as follows:

Bitcoin - 100 dollars (20%)

Ethereum - 100 dollars (20%)

Polkadot - 100 dollars (20%)

Other ALT - 100 dollars (20%)

Tether or other Stable Coin- 100 dollars (20%)

The exact amount is being invested in the different cryptocurrencies. The value being invested is not dependent on the market of the crypto, the prospects of a single currency, or any other kind of research. A beginner can adopt this strategy if he needs to learn more about crypto. This means that if you do not know which cryptocurrency will give you the best gains or which is the riskiest, you invest equally in all of them. 

Unbalanced Portfolio Diversification

The strategy of unbalanced portfolio diversification is, in fact, just a slightly more advanced way of diversification. While beginners maintain balanced portfolios, most of the more experienced crypto investors have unbalanced portfolios, which allows them to be much more flexible with their money. 

For example, if a person has 500 dollars and he wants to invest in four different coins, then depending on their prospects, he may support as follows:

Bitcoin - 300 dollars (60%) 

Ethereum - 100 dollars (20%)

Tether or other Stable Coin - 75 dollars (15%)

Other Alt Coin - 25 dollars (5%)

In such cases, we usually see that a more significant portion of the portfolio is invested in the more meaningful,  more common, and slightly more stable cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin. This allows the person to have a fixed investment that he can hold and also allows them to play with the market on the more volatile coins to earn faster gains. 

Profit Re-Investing

It is generally agreed that one should only invest the money they do not need into crypto, but that might mean that a person who wants to earn more is low on capital. For this purpose, one can re-invest the profit made from previous investments. For example, if A bought three coins at the cost of 50 dollars and then sold them at the price of 450 dollars, then the total profit earned by A is 1200 dollars (400 dollars per coin). Instead of using that money as an income, A can invest the 1200 dollars earned as profit back into crypto. This provides A with additional capital without digging back into its resources. 

Profit re-investing might mean limited immediate gains since the money is being returned to the market. Still, it also means that the long-term progress of the investor can grow exponentially. 

Golden Cross/Death Cross

The golden cross/death cross strategy is slightly technical and uses statistics. The fundamental values that we need in this strategy are the 50-day moving average and the 200-day moving average of the value of the cryptocurrency over a long time frame. When we juxtapose the two charts over one another, we see two kinds of points, the convergence point, or the golden cross, and the divergence point, or the death cross. 

In the convergence point, we see that the 50-day moving average crosses the 200-day moving average, which means there is a buyer's market, and the price in the short term is moving above a long time. Hence one should buy cryptocurrency. In the divergence point, the 50-day moving average goes below the 200-day moving average, which shows that it is a seller's market and the prices are falling below the long-time average, and one should sell his coin. 

This method works best in the more volatile markets. Of course, these are not full-proof methods, but they give you a general momentum of the price and whether you should purchase or sell the cryptocurrencies.

These are the various strategies an investor can use to make better investments. The designs are not mutually exclusive, and a person can use multiple systems simultaneously to maximize the gains they can earn over a more extended period.

What are Crypto Wallets?

Crypto wallets are software or devices where you can securely keep your cryptocurrencies. You can easily track, send and receive cryptocurrencies using crypto wallets. Just like you need a bank account for your fiat currency, you need a crypto wallet for your cryptocurrencies. 

Technically speaking, the crypto wallets don't store the cryptocurrency by themselves. Instead, they keep the private and the public keys that enable one to access the blockchains on which the cryptocurrencies are. These keys are essential, and the loss of them can mean the loss of your entire investment. 

A public key is part of the wallet that is visible to the public and the other parties of a transaction, and it can be seen as something like your account number or email address. This is what the other party puts in to send the cryptocurrency to you. On the other hand, the private key provides access to the account and is known only to the user or the wallet owner. It is an encrypted alphanumeric code that unlocks the Blockchain. 

To make it easier to understand, think of the public key as a box with a slit on it; people can put their suggestions and letters of donations inside the box. However, only the person with the key, or the private key, can open the box to retrieve these objects.

Crypto wallets can also provide other features such as crypto conversion, a list of your crypto transaction, and your current balance of cryptocurrencies, along with storing your public and private keys.

Types of Crypto Wallets

Several different types of wallets are available in the market. Some of the major types are:

*   Hardware Wallet: A hardware wallet is also called a cold or offline wallet because it is not connected to the internet. Due to its offline nature, it is usually considered a much more secure crypto wallet. Hardware wallets can exist as USB sticks or hard drives attached to the computer. Once you connect them to your industry, you can use the private key to access your cryptocurrencies. Due to their more secure nature, they are generally pricier than their software counterparts. Ledger and Trezor are two of the most common hardware wallets.
    
*   Software Wallet: A Software wallet is also called a hot wallet. They don’t physically exist. These can further be of different types, such as:
    
*   Desktop Wallet: A desktop wallet is an application or software on your laptop or desktop. They can be compatible with different operating systems like Mac or Windows. Some desktop wallets can also be accessed when not connected to the internet. They have a slightly more technical user experience than more straightforward mobile wallets. Exodus and Electrum are considered the leading desktop wallets.
    
*   Mobile Wallet: The wallets can be downloaded on your mobile as an application. Different mobiles have different compatible mobile wallets. A mobile wallet allows you to connect to cryptocurrencies through your mobile phone and is thus considered more mobile than desktop wallets. Mobile wallets are also usually easier to use. Exodus mobile app and Atomic mobile app are two commonly used mobile wallets.
    
*   Online Wallet: Online wallets exist only on the internet or the cloud. This makes it easier to access them from no matter any computer, which means higher mobility. However, since the private key for the wallet is stored online and secured by a third, these online wallets need to be more secure and can be hacked into. They are only recommended if the person is risk-averse and cannot afford a better, more secure wallet. 
    
*   Paper Wallets: Paper wallets are nothing but, as the name suggests, just papers with public and private keys printed. The keys can be published in the form of a QR code. They are considered a type of cold wallet since someone cannot access them online. Paper wallets are controversial since they are both secure, as only the paper holder can have the keys, but at the same time, if the paper is itself lost, then the keys can be compromised. Also, if the form is damaged, it can cause many issues. Bitaddress.org, Verge paper wallet generator, and Walletgenerator.net are the frequently used paper wallet generators that one can use.  
    

How to Choose a Crypto Wallet?

As we have seen, there are many different types of crypto wallets, and further, each type has wallets being offered by many other companies. This cannot be very clear to a person. So to better choose the correct crypto wallet, one should look at the following factors:

Price: The price or the cost of the crypt wallet is the first factor to look into. Many online wallets are available on the web for free, but they are less secure; one could get them if one cannot afford a better wallet. Meanwhile, the hardware wallets from companies such as Ledger can cost a bit more, but they are very secure and should be used with some with an extensive portfolio.

Security: Different crypto wallets offer varying amounts of protection. The entire reason behind getting a crypto wallet is to provide a secure gateway to your crypto assets. One should carefully assess the risks associated with the different wallets before selecting one. Hardware wallets generally offer more security than hot wallets; however, some are more secure than others, even between software wallets. If a person is risk-averse, they can purchase a software wallet.

User interface: The user interface is integral to the investor's experience. A beginner usually starts with an easier-to-use crypto wallet before getting more technical. Mobile wallets, just mobile applications, offer the most easy-to-use platforms. Paper wallets are also considered easy to use, especially for those not keen on using digital software. 

Flexibility and Compatibility: The compatibility and mobility offered by crypto wallets are also important factors. If one wants to use the wallet on the fly and access it from any computer or device, then an online wallet or even a mobile wallet is recommended. Desktop and hardware wallets are less mobile since the person can only carry them sometimes. A wallet is considered good if it can be used on multiple devices such as phones, desktops, laptops, and operating software like Windows, Mac, Linux, iOS, Android, etc.

Currencies offered: The number of cryptocurrencies the wallet supports must also be considered. If a person plans on having a portfolio containing many different cryptocurrencies, it is best to get a wallet supporting multiple currencies. However, if a person only invests in a single currency, they may get a dedicated wallet only made for that cryptocurrency.

Backup: The backup to a wallet is necessary. There have been instances where one loses their purse due to its corruption and cannot access their crypto assets. This can be a cause of significant concern. Even in the case of backups, there are multiple prices that the wallet creators may charge. 

Multisig: Multisig is a new function available on specific crypto wallets. Multisig means that multiple people hold private keys to a particular crypto wallet.  To make a transaction, all of the private keys are required. This makes the wallet much more secure since even if one of the private keys gets leaked or hacked, the transaction will still not go through. This is a feature that is ideal for family members and business partners.

Transparency: A wallet provider must be as transparent as the technology to secure the crypto wallets. A transparent open-source-based wallet provider is usually the safest option. 

Privacy: The user’s privacy is of utmost importance; when choosing the wallet, one should see all the data being asked, especially during the KYC process, and ensure that the crypto wallets do not leak such data.

These are the significant factors that one must consider; however, a person may decide according to their choice depending on their constraints and what they do or do not want in their crypto wallet.

It is also possible to not have a specific cryptocurrency wallet for oneself. Most crypto exchange platforms have their wallets attached to the account; however, they are significantly more risky than a proper cryptocurrency wallet.
