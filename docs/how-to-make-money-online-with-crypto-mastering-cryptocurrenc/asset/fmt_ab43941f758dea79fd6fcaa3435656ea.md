
# DeFi Protocols: Challenges and Limitations

The DeFi protocols are well-suited for ensuring the inclusion of more people to cater to their financial services irrespective of geographic impediments. However, blockchain-based projects need to improve the scalability of these host platforms. DeFi suffers as the transactions take longer to process after confirmation. This can be attributed to the fact that these DeFi systems can process a definite set number of transactions at a single moment. For instance, the DeFi-based platform Ethereum, even at its total capacity, can only process about 13 transactions per second. At the same time, traditional centralized financial systems can allow many transactions in the same period without any error. Therefore, despite the ease offered by DeFi in initiating a transaction, it needs substantial waiting periods for the confirmation of the transaction. Additionally, transaction costs increase at times of such congestion.

The basic premise of DeFi systems is bringing Decentralization to the table and replacing the conventional financial plans with the central authority as an impediment. This Decentralization provides transparency, flexibility, and accessibility, increasing the trustworthiness of DeFi projects and reducing the possibilities of fraud and scandals. However, despite the claims, not all services of DeFi systems are decentralized. For example, a critical use case of these systems in the form of stablecoins introduces the role of central authority either directly in the case of fiat currency-based stablecoins or indirectly in the case of commodity-backed stablecoins.

Apart from the abovementioned issues, many other problems inflect the DeFi systems. To begin with, the anonymity DeFi systems provide can often accelerate the chances of scams and fraud. With the public space open to all users around the globe, the risk factor becomes prominent. Since the DeFi platform does not require KYC from its users, it often includes the bad actors on the forum who indulge in malpractices. Although the verification systems used by DeFi are efficient, robust, and less time-consuming, they can often be misleading. Since authentication is facilitated by tracking online behavior, transactional activities, and other such practical attributes, these attributes can be manipulated, thus tricking the authentication system.

Secondly, insurance is given requisite attention in the case of centralized finance due to its necessity in ensuring the customers' trust and protecting them from events of hacking or fraud which might lead the customers to incur substantial financial losses. However, the aspect of insurance in the case of DeFi is rarely given due importance, if ever. Also, such insurances demand a place, especially when the users have to take responsibility solely.

Even if DeFi has no associated risks or issues, the responsibility for everything will be on the user's shoulders. With the complexity of the operation, room for mistakes is always there, especially for users with less experience with operating technology or those who have just joined DeFi platforms. Their likelihood of losing their funds due to a tiny mistake causes them significant financial problems. Thus, more work is required in the DeFi space to make the platform user-friendly and create tools that minimize the risks of potential human errors.

Despite the risks and limitations of DeFi, it is still booming as it continues to offer advantages to various industries in the form of its successful and efficient applications. Especially after 2020, these systems must focus due to the increasing number of locked assets. There has also been an increase in the use cases of DeFi, which has yielded more recognition of the system. Even though the technology used by DeFi requires refinement in terms of the necessary tools to reduce the associated risks, nonetheless, it is expected to reign in the financial world due to its interoperability and innovativeness, amongst other factors.

# Chapter 6: Non-Fungible Tokens

## What are NFTs?

Non-Fungible Tokens or NFTs, as the name suggests, are tokens, much like other crypto tokens such as Bitcoin or ether; however, the critical difference is that they are non-fungible, meaning they cannot be copied or replicated. Each of the tokens is unique by itself. E.g., when you buy a cryptocurrency, such as a Bitcoin, and then you exchange it for another Bitcoin, in the end, you have the same thing; there is no way to differentiate between the Bitcoin that you were holding earlier and the Bitcoin that you are holding now, this means that they are fungible.

Non-Fungible tokens are unique because if you exchange one NFT of similar value with another NFT, then what you have in your hand is something different. The fungibility of currencies and cryptocurrencies usually helps us use them as a medium of exchange since everyone has an agreed value common to all the units of the said currency. NFTs, on the other hand, can be compared to collectibles such as unique sports cards or art pieces.

NFTs use blockchain technology to register the transaction, typically, it is the Ethereum blockchain, but other blockchains are also used, which keeps track of the ownership of the NFT. NFTs are created on these blockchains using the `ERC-721` and `ERC-1155` frameworks. More often than not, NFTs are artwork such as images, GIFs, videos, and music. What happens is that the person purchasing that original piece of art has proof of their ownership, the transaction, stored on the blockchain. Since we know that blockchain has immutable and permanent data, the transaction cannot be altered and is thus a set proof of the fact that the person owns that particular asset.

The value of most things is directly proportional to the scarcity of the said thing. When we say that gold and diamonds are expensive, they are only available in a limited amount, and we cannot create any more natural gold or diamonds. Similarly, art pieces such as those by Picasso, Monet, Banksy, or Basquiat carry astronomical values during auctions and other sales because they are only a limited number of such pieces. Further, each piece is unique from the other. Before the advent of blockchain technology and NFTs, there was no such scarcity of digital art forms, one could search for a song or a photograph and they would get thousands of copies of the same. With NFTs, however, there is a newfound scarcity in the ownership of digital assets, which increases the value of these digital assets.

NFTs work as financial assets; they can be compared to physical art, except they are digital art forms. Just like physical art has no intrinsic value, and most people purchase it either for the bragging rights of having the ownership of the said piece of art or for using it as an investment which will give them returns when they sell it off at a higher value in the future, NFTs are similarly either owned to be flipped for profit in the future or for the fact that one holds the original version of a digital asset.

What one needs to understand, however, is the fact that just because they have ownership of an NFT doesn't mean that the same digital art can't be reproduced by anyone else. Think of it as a painting, such as The Starry Night by Vincent Van Gogh; At the same time, various painters made multiple copies of The Starry Night, and it is even available as posters or drawing aids; the ownership of the original Starry Night lies in the hands of a single person, or this case, the Rijks museum. Similarly, the same gif or image will be available online on various web pages or search engines. Still, the original art lies in the hands of the owner of the non-fungible token.
