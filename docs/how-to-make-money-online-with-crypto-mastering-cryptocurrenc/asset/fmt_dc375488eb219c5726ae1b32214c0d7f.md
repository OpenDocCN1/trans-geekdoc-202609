
# Cryptocurrency Investment Strategies and Storage

## Unbalanced Portfolio Diversification

The strategy of unbalanced portfolio diversification is, in fact, just a slightly more advanced way of diversification. While beginners maintain balanced portfolios, most of the more experienced crypto investors have unbalanced portfolios, which allows them to be much more flexible with their money.

For example, if a person has 500 dollars and he wants to invest in four different coins, then depending on their prospects, he may allocate funds as follows:

- **Bitcoin**: 300 dollars (60%)
- **Ethereum**: 100 dollars (20%)
- **Tether or other Stable Coin**: 75 dollars (15%)
- **Other Alt Coin**: 25 dollars (5%)

In such cases, we usually see that a more significant portion of the portfolio is invested in the more meaningful, more common, and slightly more stable cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin. This allows the person to have a fixed investment that he can hold and also allows them to play with the market on the more volatile coins to earn faster gains.

## Profit Re-Investing

It is generally agreed that one should only invest the money they do not need into crypto, but that might mean that a person who wants to earn more is low on capital. For this purpose, one can re-invest the profit made from previous investments.

For example, if A bought three coins at the cost of 50 dollars and then sold them at the price of 450 dollars, then the total profit earned by A is 1200 dollars (400 dollars per coin). Instead of using that money as income, A can invest the 1200 dollars earned as profit back into crypto. This provides A with additional capital without digging back into their resources.

Profit re-investing might mean limited immediate gains since the money is being returned to the market. Still, it also means that the long-term progress of the investor can grow exponentially.

## Golden Cross/Death Cross

The golden cross/death cross strategy is slightly technical and uses statistics. The fundamental values that we need in this strategy are the 50-day moving average and the 200-day moving average of the value of the cryptocurrency over a long time frame. When we juxtapose the two charts over one another, we see two kinds of points: the convergence point (or the golden cross) and the divergence point (or the death cross).

At the convergence point, we see that the 50-day moving average crosses the 200-day moving average, which means there is a buyer's market, and the price in the short term is moving above the long-term average. Hence, one should buy cryptocurrency. At the divergence point, the 50-day moving average goes below the 200-day moving average, which shows that it is a seller's market and the prices are falling below the long-time average; one should sell their coin.

This method works best in more volatile markets. Of course, these are not foolproof methods, but they give you a general momentum of the price and whether you should purchase or sell the cryptocurrencies.

These are the various strategies an investor can use to make better investments. The strategies are not mutually exclusive, and a person can use multiple systems simultaneously to maximize the gains they can earn over a longer period.

## How To Safely Store Your Cryptocurrencies?

Cryptocurrencies are precious assets, and people can have portfolios worth millions of dollars in cryptocurrencies. Hence, like any other item of value, one must take care of the security of their cryptocurrencies. Cryptocurrencies are susceptible to thefts, fraud, and hacks; one of the largest hacks occurred when around 850,000 Bitcoins, valued at about 45 million then, were stolen from Mt. Gox, a Japan-based crypto exchange. At the time, Mt. Gox was the most significant Bitcoin intermediary. Hence, it becomes necessary that instead of trusting crypto exchanges unthinkingly, one takes care of the security of their assets by themselves.

When you purchase cryptocurrency on a crypto exchange, you get the coins or tokens; however, they are still on the exchange, which means that if somehow the exchange was hacked or, say, your account was deleted by the business, you could lose all of your investment in the blink of an eye. Hence, it becomes necessary that instead of trusting the crypto exchanges unthinkingly, one takes care of the security of their assets by themselves. For this purpose, we have Crypto wallets.

## What are Crypto Wallets?

Crypto wallets are software or devices where you can securely keep your cryptocurrencies. You can easily track, send, and receive cryptocurrencies using crypto wallets. Just like you need a bank account for your fiat currency, you need a crypto wallet for your cryptocurrencies.

Technically speaking, crypto wallets don't store the cryptocurrency by themselves. Instead, they keep the private and the public keys that enable one to access the blockchains on which the cryptocurrencies reside. These keys are essential, and the loss of them can mean the loss of your entire investment.

A **public key** is part of the wallet that is visible to the public and the other parties of a transaction, and it can be seen as something like your account number or email address. This is what the other party uses to send the cryptocurrency to you. On the other hand, the **private key** provides access to the account and is known only to the user or the wallet owner. It is an encrypted alphanumeric code that unlocks the Blockchain.

To make it easier to understand, think of the public key as a box with a slit on it; people can put their suggestions and letters of donations inside the box. However, only the person with the key (the private key) can open the box to retrieve these objects.

Crypto wallets can also provide other features such as crypto conversion, a list of your crypto transactions, and your current balance of cryptocurrencies, along with storing your public and private keys.

## Types of Crypto Wallets

Several different types of wallets are available in the market. Some of the major types are:

### Hardware Wallet

A hardware wallet is also called a cold or offline wallet because it is not connected to the internet. Due to its offline nature, it is usually considered a much more secure crypto wallet. Hardware wallets can exist as USB sticks or hard drives attached to the computer. Once you connect them to your device, you can use the private key to access your cryptocurrencies. Due to their more secure nature, they are generally pricier than their software counterparts. Ledger and Trezor are two of the most common hardware wallets.

### Software Wallet

A software wallet is also called a hot wallet. They don't physically exist. These can further be of different types:

#### Desktop Wallet

A desktop wallet is an application or software on your laptop or desktop. They can be compatible with different operating systems like Mac or Windows. Some desktop wallets can also be accessed when not connected to the internet. They have a slightly more technical user experience than more straightforward mobile wallets. Exodus and Electrum are considered the leading desktop wallets.

#### Mobile Wallet

The wallets can be downloaded on your mobile as an application. Different mobiles have different compatible mobile wallets. A mobile wallet allows you to connect to cryptocurrencies through your mobile phone and is thus considered more portable than desktop wallets. Mobile wallets are also usually easier to use. Exodus mobile app and Atomic mobile app are two commonly used mobile wallets.

#### Online Wallet

Online wallets exist only on the internet or the cloud. This makes it easier to access them from any computer, which means higher mobility. However, since the private key for the wallet is stored online and secured by a third party, these online wallets are less secure and can be hacked. They are only recommended if the person is risk-tolerant and cannot afford a better, more secure wallet.
