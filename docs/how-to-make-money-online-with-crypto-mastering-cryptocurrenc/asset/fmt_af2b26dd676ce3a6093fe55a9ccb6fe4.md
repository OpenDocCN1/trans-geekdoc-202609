
These four points increase the information value of the chart, which means they can immediately indicate what is happening in the market. This is why almost always candlestick charts are preferred over regular line charts.

## Indicator-Based Trading

Technical indicators are a great way to predict future movement and make better trading decisions; here, we will discuss a couple of technical indicators that traders can use to predict the future moment of Bitcoins market price.

### Simple Moving Average (SMA)

Simple Moving Average (SMA) is the most common trend indicator. It gives an average price of a traded instrument over a particular time and identifies the primary trend. It looks like a smooth line on the chart. Moving averages are of 3 types generally - SMA, EMA, and WMA. All the moving averages need to catch up because we average the cost. If the price on your chart goes up or down sharply, this indicator will react, but you will receive this signal with a delay.

When the price exceeds the moving average, it's a buy signal. When it crosses below, it's a sell signal. So we buy when the closing price is above MA and sell when the closing price is below MA.

### Relative Strength Index (RSI)

Relative strength index (RSI) is a widely used technical indicator. It was developed by American engineer Welles Wilder in 1978. RSI shows how much the price changes in the direction of its movement. It transforms the cost into a percentage, indicating overbought zones (below 30%) and oversold zones (above 70%).

Thus, RSI values of 30% or below indicate an oversold condition, while RSI values of 70% or above indicate that security is becoming overbought. When the price from the overbought zone enters the neutral zone, it is a sell signal; when the cost from the oversold zone enters the neutral zone, it's a buy signal.

Although technical indicators are developed using robust methods of math and statistics, they are not 100% accurate all of the time. Experienced traders use more than one technical indicator and other crucial inputs such as market sentiment, financial news, and fundamental analysis techniques before trading. In addition to diverse information, seasoned traders have sound risk and money management strategies to ensure optimal outcomes.

## Why is Risk Management Crucial?

Trading is a game of probabilities. In other words, a trading market is a place that is never certain. Thus, it comes with default risk. As a trader, the primary should be to reduce and manage risk in the best way possible. Professional traders believe understanding risk management is more crucial than having a good strategy.

Risk refers to the probability of an event happening against the intended outcome. Trading involves making predictions between two choices. And risk in this domain is simply the percentage of the money you are ready to lose. For instance, if a trader's account balance is $1000 and says he is risking 2% on a trade, the trader is betting 2% of his account balance, which is $20. So, if the business goes against the trader, he would be at most $20.

## Risk Management Techniques

A trader who can manage their risk effectively can automatically maximize their profits. A professional trader's approach is to control their losses rather than running after profits. Here are some risk management strategies that every trader should know mandatorily.

### Stop Loss and Take Profit

The first and foremost rule in trading is to have a stop loss and take profit for every trade. Stop-loss is an executable order that cuts the positions at a specified price when the market goes in the intended direction. On the other hand, take profit is also an executable order that liquidates the parts when the market reaches a specified price in the trader's favor. A stop-loss will keep a trader safe from further losses, and taking profit will lock in the money even if the market goes against the desired direction later.

### Position Sizing

Position sizing is the number of units of security invested by a trader. This is not a random number derived using the account size and risk tolerance.

Firstly, account size is the account balance in a trading account. For example, if a trader deposits $1,000 into his trading account, the account size will be $1,000.

Account risk is the percentage of the account size a trader is ready to lose on a trade. For example, if you set your account risk to 1%, you will risk 1% of your account balance on the work.

Trade risk is simply the stop loss for a trade. It is the difference between the entry price and the stop-loss price. For example, if the entry price for a buy is $210, and the stop loss is $160, the trade risk would be $50 per unit traded.

Using the above two parameters, the position size for a trade can be determined. Mathematically, the position size is obtained by dividing the account risk by the trade risk. For example, if the account risk on a trade is $100 and the trade risk is $2 per unit, the position size would be $100/$2 = 50 units.

### Risk/Reward Ratio

Another critical risk management tool is the risk/reward ratio. This ratio compares actual risk to the potential gains in a trade. The risk/reward tells you whether a business is worth taking. For example, there could be situations when your stop loss has to be higher than the take profit. In such trades, you would be risking more than gaining.

Thus, this ratio is used to stay cautious with such scenarios. For instance, if a trader is risking $100 on a trade and the risk: reward is 1:1, he is betting $100 to make $100. Similarly, if the ratio were 1:2, he would risk $100 to make $200. Therefore, you should refrain from trading under 1:1 to safeguard your capital.
