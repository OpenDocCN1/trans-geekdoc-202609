
# Blockchain Technology Applications Across Sectors

## Real Estate Sector

The Real estate sector will get a boost after introducing blockchain technology in its record-keeping and payment processes. In real estate, the trust between the buyer and seller is imperative; the buyer must be assured that the seller is the actual owner of the property and does not have any incumbent interest in it, and the seller must be guaranteed that the buyer is a genuine buyer who will pay him wholly for the said property. This trust is enhanced with blockchain technology's decentralized, transparent data-keeping methods. Further, through safer and faster payment methods, the cost of transferring the property will be significantly reduced, benefiting both parties. Companies like Safewire use blockchain technology to minimize wire fraud prevalent in real estate transactions.

## Pharmaceutical Sector

The pharmaceutical sector has fake medicines, expired patents, and long testing periods. Blockchain technology can help resolve most of these issues. Firstly, having an open and transparent ledger would make it easier for consumers to check whether the medicines sold are authentic and have not expired. Further, the high cost of most drugs is because of the patents that have been claimed on them by pharmaceutical companies through blockchains; one could easily keep track of which all patents are expiring soon enough and thus provide a generic version of the same medicine to the consumers at lower prices and consequently save many lives. The testing phase can also be bettered with blockchains as if and when there are errors, they can be caught quickly and then be traced back to the same batch of medicine.

## Education Sector

In education, people often provide fake degrees, diplomas, and certificates. This can be checked by ensuring all certificates are on a public blockchain platform. The educational institutions can confirm and verify that the proper authority has issued the provided certificates and belong to the right person. Further, credentials can be lost or damaged, and even carrying them from one place to another is an issue; by having them on a secure and authenticated platform, the students and degree holders can only take them physically, sometimes for authentication. Blockchains would also help the working of libraries, as it helps keep better track of the books being lent and who has which book, allowing even the set up of peer-to-peer libraries.

## Stock Trading Sector

Much like how transactions regarding cryptocurrencies are stored on blockchain technology, the whole asset trading sector can completely shift to blockchain technology. This sector is the closest to the crypto industry, for which blockchain technology was primarily created in the first place. Using the transactions that are time-stamped, it is straightforward to find who is the owner of a particular share and if he has already sold it to another person or not. Further, if trading starts through blockchains, the trades will be settled much faster, and the seller can be credited with his money instantly. Through the use of algorithms and smart contracts, it would also become more accessible for stock traders to execute trades by themselves.

## Energy Sector

The energy sector has in the past been one of the most centralized sectors, where energy collection and distribution is done through power corporations, private or public, depending on the countries and territories. With blockchain, this could completely change with people moving on from these centralized power producers to acquiring power from decentralized sources, that is, peer-to-peer purchase of power, which reduces the intermediaries and, thus, the cost of energy and production. Also, using this technology, one could find the source of the point they are using, i.e., whether the energy they consume is clean or made with non-renewable sources. Some blockchain companies use energy certificates, which may be sold from one person to another and then redeemed for power. Another way that blockchains can impact the energy sector is the introduction of cryptocurrencies to pay energy bills.

## Food and Beverage Sector

Food and beverages typically have a concise shelf life and, if spoilt, can cause many diseases, such as salmonella or diarrhea. Via blockchain, one can track how old a specific food product is, and the consumer can then make an informed decision. The food supply chain can easily be monitored via blockchains. By using smart contracts, companies can also automate the orders for more products, thereby increasing efficiency and decreasing the workload. Also, if an issue arises, it is easier to trace where the raw material came from and where the problem might have occurred.

## Election Sector

Elections all over the world, and especially in developing countries, are marred by allegations of voter fraud. Elections would become much more secure once blockchains store the votes. This would happen in various ways. Firstly, when the voters' data is stored on a blockchain, it is transparent so everyone can see who are the voters in a particular area, how many of them have cast their votes. Further, verifying and tracing the vote means one can prove that the voices are not being hacked and changed to help a particular party or person. Also, blockchain companies are working on ways to start casting votes via blockchains themselves. Do we need to remind anyone of the chaos of the 2020 US presidential election?

## Auditing Sector

The auditing process will become a lot easier once the data and information of the companies and firms are uploaded on the blockchains. This would make it difficult for the companies to falsify their accounts, and it would show an accurate picture of the monetary state of the companies. Further, it makes it easier to keep track of the purchases and sales made over the year by a firm, and with the time-stamped transactions, it becomes even harder to hide such expenses.

## Mining Sector

The mining industry has started adopting blockchains as well. In the mining sector, there are multiple intermediaries. All of them can be brought on the same page quite quickly using this technology, which makes getting the mined product from the mines to the end consumers much more accessible. Further, the consumers can trace their products back to the mines from where they are claimed to be. This ensures that a certain amount of value is attached to the origin of the stone or metal. De Beers, the diamond company, has developed a blockchain solution called Tracr, which traces the source of the diamonds from the mine to the buyer.

## Public Assistance

Public assistance, with the application of blockchain, could reduce wastage significantly. This way, the Non-Governmental Organisations and other inter-government bodies can keep track of their assistance funds and verify that the aid's end receiver is the person for whom the support was intended or not. The World Food Programme of the united nations has already adopted blockchain technology for directly sending assistance to needy people by using blockchain authentication. In some countries such as Jordan, World Food Programme has attached the people's biometrics to their blockchain-based accounts; once the person's identity is confirmed, the money received via aid in the said account is automatically detected, thus allowing instant settlement at the grocery stores.

## Gambling Industry

The online gambling sector has grown by leaps and bounds; however, the biggest issue is the lack of transparency. This is something that the introduction of blockchain platforms can easily handle, as this would ensure transparency and inculcate trust between the business and the consumers. It removes the adage of "the house always wins" as the ledger stores all the data and is open. Decentralizing betting through blockchain also allows for anonymity which is necessary for numerous gamblers who do not want to disclose their gambling habits.
