
# Bitcoin: Market Potential, Energy Concerns, and Expert Predictions

## Market Potential and Advantages

There is a large, untapped market for developers to create these applications, as this technology could be adopted by any industry that relies on a third-party clearing system. Bitcoin's progression into becoming a transformative technology lies in its ability to solve long-standing problems in combination with an active and growing community of users and developers.

Businesses are also beginning to see the benefits of using cryptocurrencies for international transactions. Using conventional methods, the trade can be hit with any unexplained fees as it crosses borders, making sending the correct amount to another business complex. Cryptocurrency has a significant advantage over traditional currencies thanks to its agility in fast peer-to-peer transactions, especially in international business-to-business scenarios.

Internet marketplaces have been thriving and are genuine contenders to conventional brick-and-mortar stores. Amazon.com has grown to a degree that seems almost unexpected. Online shopping is flourishing, and Bitcoin is poised to extend its reach with efficient and easy payments for vendors and customers.

One of Bitcoin's most significant opportunities is that it can act as a commodity, similar to gold. The value of gold can shoot up considerably whenever an event threatens the balance of global markets, as we have seen with the COVID-19 pandemic. The commodity market is a widely accepted and well-established form of trade, and cryptocurrency has seemingly begun to mimic the characteristics of gold. Gold has been a long-standing holder of value based on the universal acceptance and trust of its value. Cryptocurrencies could become a significant player in the commodity market. They have the unique property of being able to be purchased through a direct online mechanism, which creates an easy on-ramp for buyers. If Bitcoin remains a valid refuge for inflating fiat currencies, it will gain investors' confidence and move closer to becoming more mainstream.

## Energy Consumption Concerns

It is well known that the "mining" of Bitcoins is a very energy-intensive process. Recent studies have shown that a single transaction in the Bitcoin network consumes 800 kilowatts of energy. By contrast, 500,000 Visa transactions can be carried out using the same amount. In comparison to other payment methods, like cash or fiat money, Bitcoin is highly energy-intensive. Bitcoin's energy consumption is not unfavorable at its current price. Still, global warming and climate change have raised concerns over non-renewable energy sources used in Bitcoin mining.

## Current Market Status

We are now experiencing the rise of Bitcoin, which may be the beginning of the next bull run in full steam in 2024.

## Expert Price Predictions

Bitcoin has stagnated over the latter half of 2021, falling by more than half from its peak of around $65,000 set in April.

However, the Bitcoin price is still significantly higher than when the massive rally began in October 2020, a bull run that sent the combined crypto market to a staggering $2.5 trillion before pulling back. Predicting the future of cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin is challenging, and the results could be more accurate to a high degree. Bitcoin being one of the most volatile assets out there makes future valuations very difficult to get right. That being said, let's overview what cryptocurrency experts and financial analysts say about Bitcoin's future.

### Tim Draper

Bitcoin bull Tim Draper had one of the most optimistic calls on Bitcoin, predicting the token would be worth $250,000 by the end of the year.

### Shervin Pishevar

Shervin Pishevar, a venture capitalist and angel investor who co-founded Hyperloop One and Sherpa Capital, tweeted that Bitcoin will hit $100k. The prediction came soon after meeting with MicroStrategy CEO Michael Saylor, whose company invested more than $1 billion in Bitcoin.

### Thomas Fitzpatrick

Citibank's global head of their market insights product, CitiFX Technicals, Thomas Fitzpatrick, made headlines for his Bitcoin valuation of $318,000, which came up after a report was leaked online. His analysis drew similarities between the gold market of the 1970s and Bitcoin's price action and pointed out the acceleration in money-printing by central banks since the emergence of COVID-19, which may fuel the Bitcoin run.

### Winklevoss Twins

Winklevoss twins – the famous Bitcoin billionaires – have come out and said Bitcoin has the potential to hit a price of $500,000 by 2030. This would put its market cap on par with gold at around $9 trillion.

### Wences Casares

Wences Casares is the CEO and founder of Xapo, a Bitcoin wallet startup, and a board member at PayPal, among other roles. Casares said that Bitcoin will hit $1 million sometime before 2027 at the Consensus 2017 conference in New York.

### Chamath Palihapitiya

Chamath Palihapitiya is the Founder of Social Capital and Co-Owner of the Golden State Warriors basketball team. He started his Bitcoin investment journey with an investment back in 2012, and by 2013 he had included Bitcoin in all of his portfolios. At one point, he owned 5% of all Bitcoin in circulation.

Palihapitiya has claimed that Bitcoin price will reach $100,000 in the next 3-4 years, adding that it will reach $1 million by 2037. He said, "This thing has the potential to be comparable to the value of gold…This is a fantastic hedge and store of value against autocratic regimes and banking infrastructure that we know is corrosive to how the world needs to work properly."

### Jeremy Liew

Jeremy Liew is a partner at Lightspeed Venture Partners. He was one of the first investors in the social media app Snapchat. In an interview with Business Insider, Liew said that the Bitcoin price could "realistically" reach $500,000 by 2030. Following this, Liew's prediction was backed by Peter Smith, the CEO and co-founder of Blockchain — the world's most popular Bitcoin wallet.

### Mark Yusko

Mark Yusko is a billionaire investor and Founder of Morgan Creek Capital. He predicts Bitcoin to be worth over $400,000 in the long term, a statement that made headlines because of its grandiose scale.

"It's just math. It is gold equivalent; there is about 20 million Bitcoin available today. Gold today is about $8 trillion. That gives you $400,000. And that doesn't include use cases related to currency," Yusko said. He further alluded to the fact that cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin strike fear into the hearts of bankers because Bitcoin altogether eliminates the need for banks.

### Kay Van-Petersen

Kay Van-Petersen is an analyst at Saxo Bank, an investment bank specializing exclusively in online trading and investment.

According to a feature by CNBC News, Van-Petersen believes that Bitcoin will rise to $100,000 per unit by 2027. He successfully predicted Bitcoin's flow up to $2,000 by 2017. Although optimistic, his most recent estimate suggests that Bitcoin would account for 10% of the global foreign exchange market volume.

### Andy Edstrom

Wealth manager for a California-based investment advisory firm called WESCAP, Andy Edstrom is an investor and an avid Twitter user, constantly tweeting about the topic and even having written a book titled "Why Buy Bitcoin."

In February 2020, Edstrom predicted that Bitcoin would reach an $8 trillion market cap by 2030. This would put the price of a single coin at around $400,000 with a market cap around that of gold. Edstrom believes Bitcoin could become the world's default currency.
