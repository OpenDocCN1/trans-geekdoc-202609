# Chapter 4: The Altcoin Market

## What are Altcoins?

Since its inception in 2009, Bitcoin has become the first and most popular cryptocurrency globally. It has paved the way for creating many other alternative cryptocurrencies (or Altcoins) looking to outperform each other in a dynamic and fast-moving financial market. Bitcoin has also been the most popular currency for users of Dark Net markets due to its semi-anonymous qualities, lack of regulation, and effectiveness in laundering the profits from online contraband sales. However, users have criticized Bitcoin as lacking true anonymity and have been accused of providing a trail of evidence for law enforcement to trace payments via its blockchain due to its design limitations. This led to an increase in the development of alternative cryptocurrencies (Altcoins) in which anonymity and security are prioritized to make tracing payments and laundering of proceeds almost impossible. But all Altcoins are not geared towards anonymity; any cryptocurrency, not Bitcoin, is termed an Altcoin.

With enhanced security features, an Altcoin can see its price increasing dramatically due to demand. Still, it may be the currency of choice for the wider majority. Each Altcoin project is unique and has its own target niche, objectives, and blockchain technologies it was built upon.

## Bitcoin vs. Altcoins

Altcoins work in different ways than Bitcoin does. For example, while Bitcoin produces only new Bitcoins every 10 minutes, altcoins such as Litecoin create new coins every 2.5 minutes. This means Litecoin can process payments faster. Moreover, Litecoin's total supply is capped at around 84 million Litecoin, whereas Bitcoin's total is about 21 million.

Litecoin also uses a different set of rules for mining than Bitcoin. Bitcoin mining requires costly hardware, whereas Litecoin can be mined with standard computer hardware.

Litecoin is just one of the thousands of altcoins available today. However, some altcoins stand out as popular alternatives to Bitcoin; although they don't reach Bitcoin's $100 billion market cap, most have some advantage over conventional Bitcoin. A few examples of such altcoins include:

- Ethereum - ETH
- Ripple – XRP
- Cardano – ADA
- Solona – SOL
- Polygon – MATIC
- Polkadot - DOT
- Litecoin – LTC
- Avalanche - AVAX

Generally, these altcoins were built and released to improve on Bitcoin's flaws by providing faster transactions, faster mining rates, more security, or lower network fees.

However, these altcoins may need more stability, liquidity, and a large market cap that Bitcoin offers.

## Early Examples of Altcoins

One of the earliest notable Altcoins, Litecoin, released in October 2011, was marketed as the "silver to Bitcoin's gold." While fundamentally similar in code and functionality to Bitcoin, Litecoin diverges from Bitcoin in several ways. Litecoin allows transactions to be approved more frequently. It also provides for a total supply of 84 million coins to be created, which is four times Bitcoin's total supply. Litecoin is still among the most popular altcoins; some even think it could be a better investment than Bitcoin.

## Types of Altcoins

Depending on their functionalities and blockchain mechanisms, altcoins come in many varieties and categories. Here's a rundown of some of the more important ones:

### Mining-Based

As their name indicates, mining-based altcoins are generated through mining. Most altcoins that fall into this category use Proof-of-Work (PoW). Proof-of-work is how computers generate new coins by solving complex problems to create blocks. Examples of mining-based altcoins are Litecoin, Zcash, and Monero. Most of the top altcoins in early 2020 were mining-based coins. Pre-mined coins are the alternative to mining-based altcoins. Pre-mined coins are not produced through an algorithm but distributed before being listed in a cryptocurrency exchange. An example of a pre-mined coin is XRP, also known as Ripple.

### Stablecoins

Cryptocurrencies, since their launch, have been very volatile by nature. Stablecoins aim to reduce this overall volatility by clinching their value to various goods or commodities, such as precious metals, fiat currencies, or other cryptocurrencies. This ensures that investors can redeem their money if the cryptocurrency fails or faces problems. Price fluctuations for stablecoins don't usually go beyond a narrow range.

Social media giant Facebook's Diem is the most famous example of a stablecoin. It is a dollar-backed coin, meaning Diem's value is linked to the US dollar. Other examples of stablecoins include USDC and MakerDAO.

### Security Tokens

Security tokens are comparable to securities traded in stock markets, except they have a digital origin. Security tokens are similar to traditional stocks, and they typically promise equity in the form of ownership or a dividend payout to holders. The possibility of price appreciation for such tokens is a major draw for investors to buy and hold these coins. Security tokens are generally offered to investors through ICOs or initial coin offerings from major crypto exchanges.

### Utility Tokens

Utility tokens are used to provide and avail services within a network. For example, they might be used to redeem rewards or purchase services. Utility tokens, unlike security tokens, do not pay dividends. Filecoin, used to buy cloud storage space on a network, is an example of a utility token.

## Are Altcoins Good Investments?

The market for altcoins is still very new. And in recent times, the number of altcoins listed in cryptocurrency markets has rapidly multiplied. It has attracted crowds of retail investors, relentlessly betting on their price movements to book quick profits. But retail investors need more capital to generate sufficient market liquidity.

Shallow markets and an absence of regulation produce immense volatility in altcoin markets and valuations.

There is money to be made buying and selling altcoins, but there's also money to be lost. Even though this is true with almost any investment, altcoins come with unique risks.

Altcoin prices are incredibly volatile, and most individual investors cannot manage this volatility. Plus, there needs to be more regulation in place around altcoins. Widespread fraud also happens in the cryptocurrency market when scammers lure investors into buying fake cryptocurrencies.
