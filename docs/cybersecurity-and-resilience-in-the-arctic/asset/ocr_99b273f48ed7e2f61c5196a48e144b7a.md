# 3.1.3. Cyber-Resilience of Ships

The tight integration of Information and Communication Technologies (ICT) within\ship systems brings new cyber risks and makes the maritime industry a potential target\for various kinds of cyber attacks. Moreover, traditional safety and risk analysis
methods, such as Fault-Tree Analysis (FTA), Failure Mode Effect Analysis (FMEA),
HazOP, do not work well with the emerging cyber-physical systems (Leveson
2011). These models are best suited for analysis of the faults by a single
component in the systems, but do not describe failures in complex
interconnected systems.

As Information and Communication Technologies (ICT) have advanced,
t shipping industry has started relying on ICT. This has ushered in a new era in the
maritime industry. The bridge and engine components of the ship such as the Global\Navigation Satellite System (GNSS), Electronic Chart Display Systems (ECDIS),
Automatic Identification System (AIS), propulsion control, and propeller are
interconnected with the on-board communication network in order to control the.
tight coupling between the ICT and physical systems on ships have
introduced a lot of risks related to cyber attacks, faults, and failure. Moreover, systems
are becoming increasingly complex and difficult to manage. The maritime industry has
come more vulnerable to cyber attacks because of its adoption of ICT. For instance,
t 2018 attack on Maersk is a well-known example of a cyber attack that spreads to the
shipping industry (Safety4Sea 2018). Therefore, it is important to make sure that
security and safety requirements are identified in ship systems and adequately
addressed.

The CyberShip (Sahay & Sepúl Estrada 2018) is a proposed framework
comprising of different components, on which System-Theoretical Process Analysis
(STPA) method for assessing cyber risks is applied. STPA identifies and enforces
required constraints to eliminate or reduce the damage caused by risk. It can be used for
treat modeling and vulnerability analysis for cyber attacks against cyber-physical
systems on ships. Figure 1 shows two main controllers in the cyber-physical infrastructure of a ship: the Integrated Bridge (IBC) and the Engine IBC provides a centralized interface to visualize all the information from bridge devices such as AIS,ISIS, radar, etc. The engine controller is
