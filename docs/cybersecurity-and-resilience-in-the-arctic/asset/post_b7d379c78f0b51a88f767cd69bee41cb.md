## 1. Introduction

Modern societies are rapidly becoming more dependent on internet technology and communication, with digital solutions the degree that they have created a digital dependency. At a conference on 5G and security in Prague in May 2019 (Czech Republic) the chairman made that cyber security is not only a technical issue. Adequate national strategies, comprehensive legal frameworks, and dedicated personnel, trained and educated appropriately, are needed as well for meeting the requirements of safe, secure, and resilient infrastructures. As example, the number of scientific papers on cyber security has increased exponentially in recent years, more than doubling every two-year period¹. Cyber security has become of critical importance.

The identification of which services are considered critical has changed over the years. In the Directive on Security of Network and Information Systems (NIS-directive), the European Union defined the following services as energy, transport, banking, financial market infrastructures, drinking water and distribution, and digital infrastructure. The description in this chapter is based on documented references as well as discussions with some of the people involved in the development of Internet usage in the Arctic. As much of this usage is currently undergoing rapid changes the overview given here is bound to incomplete.
