The nature of cyber attacks allows adversaries to target locations in different geographic areas without the need for a physical presence near the objects of attack. Hackers use freely available tools and existing vulnerabilities in software rather than previously unknown weaknesses, making any attribution more difficult. As a motivation for the discussions of this chapter, a number of recent cyber incidents related to northern and Arctic regions are discussed below and summarized in Table 1.

It was feared that industrial secrets and information about contract negotiations were stolen but threat actors may have been conducting surveillance to plan later destructive activities. The attackers gained access to networks using customized emails with viruses; these emails did not trigger malware detection systems (BBC, 18 November 2011).
