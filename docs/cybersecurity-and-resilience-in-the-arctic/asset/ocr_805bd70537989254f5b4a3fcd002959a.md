Failure of one or more assets (due to disruptive events, realised risk etc.) has a cascading impact on the mission of related business processes, services, and the organisation can impede assurance of assured services and can translate into achieving organisational goals and objectives. A “Cyber Resilience Review” (CRR) can be performed to understand an organisation’s operational resilience and ability to manage risk by its critical services during normal operations and as times of operational stress and crisis. CRR is a questionnaire to help understand how organisations depl its assets (people, technology, and facilities) in support of specific operational missions (i.e., critical services)¹

Allen [1] suggests a set of ten strategic metrics for managing operational resilience derived from high-level objectives of the operational resilience management system defined in the CERT-RMM. These measures relate to computing percentages of resilience aspects such as activities, assets, services, controls among others. Allen et al [2] then outline cases in how the CERT-RMM has been applied and the analytical methodologies used to understand the effects of applying resilience to real-world organisations, using a comparative methodology, a predictive methodology with little data, a predictive methodology with much data and a probability example. The European Union Agency for Networks and Information Security (ENISA) has a program on Networks and Resilience and Critical Infrastructure Protection (CIIP)⁴. They have two works [19,20] towards a uniform assessment of resilience and measurement frameworks for public communications networks. Examples of the metrics [20] include: mean time to incident, mean time to reach, patch management coverage, vulnerability scan among others. They reported here a lack of a standard framework or practices for a durability measurement of resilience, and a lack of knowledge and awareness of resilience issues.

Goldman et al. [27] presents a series of proactive and reactive approaches to improving resilience and described an application scenario. The authors argue that organisations have to prepare “fighting through” cyber-attacks to ensure success in a degraded or contested environment. The paper has a technical focus, describing at a high level the actuated architectural and operational recommendations to address the advanced cyber threat and enable mission assurance for critical operations. The proactive approaches range from containment of emerging resilience to diverting attackers (among others). The reactive approaches range from dynamic reconfiguration to deception, to costly reconstruction and seeking alternative operations (among others). All techniques will not work to all systems: to begin building resilience in existing or emerging systems, designers analyse which strategies are most effective for the environments and missions.

Bodeau & Graffiti [8] discusses issues pertaining cyber resilience goals, objectives, practices, and costs. The work serves to motivate and characterise cyber resilience metrics. Their framework is intended to evolve the discipline of cyber resilience engineering measures.
