The applicability of the international law of the sea to the heart of the Arctic, the Arctic Ocean, means that there is already a widely accepted regulatory framework, the international law of the sea, for international governance efforts of a large part of the Arctic. In addition, the Arctic States have themselves created in the Arctic Council an international forum which implies wide respect and has proven effective in creating soft law as well as influencing the binding rules of international treaties which have been negotiated under its auspices. International law therefore is likely to remain an important tool for governance in the Arctic. This importance of a rules-based order in the Arctic is derived from the legitimacy this approach enjoys not only in terms of the inputs (involving not only states but also non-state actors, such as indigenous peoples) but also in terms of the outputs this competitive system has produced so far, most notably but by no means only, in the context of the Arctic Council. As new problems emerge (e.g., cybersecurity) which require significant technical expertise and the design of effective governance approaches, the existing frameworks for cooperation are an important part of facilitating such approaches and ideas.
