# 5. A Brief Overview of the Arctic Council

The main organ of the Arctic Council is the secretariat, which rotates every two years, with each chair determining the goals and objectives of the council and a plan through which they can be achieved. This is the highest level at which interaction within the council occurs. This presents a problem, as goals can change every few years. In response to this challenge, Norway, Sweden and Denmark made an agreement on common objectives and priorities and shared the chair for six years (2007-2013), helping to ensure and promote Arctic protection through the continuation of programmes, producing greater stability and progress in achieving objectives.

Between biennial 'ministerial meetings', the work of the Arctic Council is primarily carried out by working groups and Programs and Plans. This work is directed by Senior Arctic Officials (SAOs) – representatives appointed by each Arctic state to manage its interests in the Arctic Council. These officials oversee the work of the Arctic council subsidiary bodies, including the working groups. The six working groups cover emission and pollution (ACAP), the scientific monitoring of Arctic environment, ecosystems and human populations (AMAP), conserving Arctic biodiversity (CAFF), protecting the Arctic environment from the impact of accidental pollution/radionuclide release (EPPR), protection and promotion of sustainable use of the Arctic marine environment (PAME), and advancing sustainable development (SDWG). The Programs and Action plans include the Arctic Biodiversity Assessment, the Circular Biodiversity Monitoring Program (CBMP), the Arctic Climate Impact Assessment, and the Arctic Human Development.

The council may additionally create Task forces and expert groups to carry out more specific work; most recently under Finnish chairmanship from 2017-19, the task forces were the Task Force on Marine Cooperation (TFAMC), and the Task Force on Improved Connectivity in the Arctic (TFICA).

> “Rules of procedure” exist to detail in which meetings are run and the mechanism for which decisions should be taken across all Arctic council bodies. They cover general provisions of Arctic Council meetings, ministerial meetings, meetings of Senior Arctic Officials, the implementation of cooperative activities, and other matters. These rules were revised in 2013 and can be found as published by the Arctic Council [41]. They apply to all bodies of the council and represent an agreed upon framework for international cooperation between all parties.
