The manager minimizes the number of countermeasures to deploy by selecting shared countermeasures e.g. those that affect attacks in distinct chains. The focus on shared countermeasures dramatically reduces the number of countermeasures to deploy that, in our experience, is never larger than 5% of the current vulnerabilities.

However, the focus on countermeasures is one of the innovative features of the *manager*, another one is its iterative behavior. The tool assumes an intelligent adversary that reacts to countermeasures by selecting and implementing a distinct chain to a goal. For this reason, the *manager* loops it over, after selecting a set of countermeasures, it updates the system model to describe its deployment and executes a new experiment to discover how the agents react to countermeasures it has selected. If the experiment shows that the attackers can implement some distinct chains, the *manager* selects a set of countermeasures that stop both the previous chains and those the experiment has just discovered. Then, it runs a new experiment and so on. In this way, the tool sequentially evaluates sets of countermeasures to discover that no agent can reach a goal despite its reactions. At the end, the manager returns a database that describes the updated version of the system.

The Harpex methodology minimizes the number of countermeasures to deploy and favors a solution that prevents the attackers from reaching their goals by minimizing the lowest number of vulnerabilities. Hence, it accepts some vulnerabilities in the modules without forcing a patch-all solution to remove any vulnerability.

## 4. A Case Study

We describe briefly a risk assessment of the ICT + OT infrastructure of a 4.0 ship that is currently under development by a main international shipbuilder. The assessment has been executed as a step of the design to minimize the overall risk. Some of the modules to be used were already available. These modules have been scanned to discover the corresponding vulnerabilities. Other modules were not available yet and their vulnerabilities have been acquired either through a vulnerability database or other sources. Both system scanners and web ones have been used.

The whole infrastructure includes about one hundred computers that are partitioned into two subnets and run more than OS instances.

The attack surface of the overall ICT + OT infrastructure includes any system that is connected to the Internet as well as USB ports that enable external operators to connect their devices. The attacker goals include the navigation system, the power control as well as the fire system. The latter enables DOS as well as attacks with a high impact on the people on board.

The assessment has considered a scenario with a number of attackers adopting a distinct strategy so that the assessment covers any of the attacker parameters.

The shipbuilder required a high confidence level that Harpex has reached through experiments with more than a million runs. Only model-based approach can implement this using simulations because it is unrealistic to run these experiments on the target system, even when it already exists.

The Harpex has revealed more than one thousand distinct attack chains that the attackers can implement to their goals. There is an order of magnitude difference among the lengths of these chains. This is due to the paths that count distinct subsystems to reach their goals. We have also simulated attacks implemented by malware hidden in a software module. Lastly, we have assumed an attacker knows a 0-day attack.

We can stop all the chains discovered by deploying countermeasures for less than 5% of the ship vulnerabilities. The execution of risk assessment and management in the design step, before building and deploying the interconnected system, guarantees that these countermeasures have a negligible cost in both absolute and relative terms.

## 5. Resilience

We briefly discuss strategies to increase the resilience of a4.0 ship [14, 15]. By resilience, we mean the ability to prepare for, and adapt to, changing conditions and withstand and recover from disruptions. Resilience includes the ability to withstand and recover from disturbances of the deliberate attack types, or naturally occurring threats or incidents. In principle, a resilience-based design differs from one based on risk management and assessment because it ensures continuity in functions and services when little information is available on attackers. Instead, a risk management approach minimizes risk from known attackers by identifying and hardening the system modules they use. Lack of information on the attacker and its goal prevents the detection of critical modules. A solution that effectively hardens or protects all the modules against any attacker is too complex to be.

An intermediate solution solves the output of a risk management process after introducing some uncertainty in the input of adversarial simulation. In this way, the system design can exploit available information on attack TTPs but it adds some uncertainty to prevent premature optimization. We can introduce noise to information on the attack TTPs to anticipate future changes. Suppose, as example, that threat intelligence selects low noise to minimize the probability the system detects its attack chain. An assessment may add noise by assuming there is a non-null probability that it selects attacks with noise larger than the lowest one. Furthermore, a what-approach may be adopted that adds unknown vulnerabilities in modules of the onboard network. This will result in the adoption of further countermeasures to harden a larger number of modules. Further noise may be added to system parameters with an interval that includes the original value. This replaces a parameter with an interval that includes as attack success probability. One of the interval models an attacker more expert than the one the assessment has assumed, the other a less experienced one. The larger the interval, the larger the noise that is added. The improvement of resilience when deploying the countermeasures with the noise in the parameters that risk assessment and management adopt. Furthermore, an assessment may assume the attackers can access some information on the design or that one is attacking the system. All the previous strategies increase the number of simulations Monte Carlo method needs to reach the confidence levels of interest.

By adding uncertainty to risk assessment and management, we do not fully increase the information we have available to minimize the costs of countermeasures. This preserves some redundancy in the system and avoids premature optimization. Highly optimized systems can resist attackers but may become highly fragile with respect to unknown ones.

## 6. Conclusion

The topic of cyber risk in the maritime sector is becoming more and more critical. Cyberattacks against 4.0 ships and shipping companies may have a devastating impact on worldwide commerce. We have briefly discussed the features of a methodology to assess and manage cyber risk for safety on a vessel and presented a possible solution with its supporting tools. More experience in this field is required to evaluate alternative solutions and choose the most promising ones.
