The challenge in implementing KPIs lies in identifying which are the most appropriate sensors to deploy any system's resilience concerns: we are monitoring the mission, the assets or both? Are we looking at threat actors? Are these questions that need to be prioritised. Prioritisation is particularly important in an environment with finite resources. Our concerns include: climate and weather affect the quality of data sampled, distances affect the density of data samples far apart can have adverse effects on the interpretation of data or limit our ability to physically go to a location in time, before a serious incident. Another challenge lies in understanding how network-level activities relate to the real-world. This is an aspect not discussed in the resilience literature. There is a significant body of work that examines asset-to-mission dependencies, as discussed by Heinhbook et al. [33].

Misuse detection system can help identify any rules have been broken [48]. These rules however rely on expert systems to be in place, i.e someone has to define what rules should exist. Anomaly detection helps to identify something of the ordinary. Anomaly detection can be broken down into three subclasses of methods, these are: 1) statistical methods (univariate and multivariate analysis) [17,57,24,11,26]; requiring prior knowledge about normal behaviour. A disadvantage of statistical methods is that if attackers are sufficiently capable they may be able to avoid detection. 2) knowledge-based systems (e.g. making use of a finite-state, heuristics or rule sets) [24]; encode an understanding about a system's normal activities to detection. Knowledge-based systems may have incomplete insight about a system and may be difficult to update. 3) machine learning methods; normal behaviour using automated approaches with supervised or unsupervised learning [38,24,55,111]. Minimising false positives and false negatives remains a major challenge in both misuse detection and anomaly detection. Glass-Vanderlan et al. [26] provide a more to date discussion on the state of host-based intrusion detection.

Misuse and anomaly detection, while traditionally used for network defences, can also be used for monitoring other types of system behaviour, e.g. examining performance KPIs, and alert when sensors are reporting data outside expected threshold. It is possible to envisage monitoring of weather, wear and tear and correlate this with physical conditions of vessels or sensors in the ocean.

# 3.4. Common Pitfalls and Limitations

Any analytics or approach of choice should be the one that is most appropriate for the task, user and data in mind. Many pitfalls and limitations exist [47], especially those with visualization in mind, including:

+   **Underestimating the problem domain.** It is necessary to understand the complexity of the data we are attempting to investigate. Instance, how many dimensions are there to data?; what correlations may be of interest?; I have classified my data correctly? are just some of the questions that may be relevant to ask. Prior to any analysis, it is important to clearly identify the questions that answering and how to best answer.

+   **Underestimating the volume of data.** The sheer amount of data to process may be too much for some tools, this may have a detrimental effect on the tool's performance. Ensure the hardware on the project is capable of the visualizations in the project.
