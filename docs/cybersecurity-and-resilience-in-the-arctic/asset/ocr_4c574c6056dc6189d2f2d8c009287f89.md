The Arctic region's inhospitable weather and other environmental challenges have limited human activity and settlement. This is the reason that there are no permanent settlements above 78° north latitude [1]. The Arctic region is one of the least populated areas in the world and there are only a few large cities. About half of the Arctic population centers around the Arctic Circle in Russia: Murmansk (population around 300,000), Norilsk (over 100,000), and Vorkuta (around 60,000). Tromso, Norway has 71,000 inhabitants, and Reykjavik, Iceland has more than 100,000.

Most of the four million inhabitants live on lands bordering the Arctic Ocean. The population is a mix of Caucasians and several major groups of indigenous people, who lived in the north polar lands for centuries. Caucasians make up sizable portions of Siberia and Greenland's populations, and a near-majority in Iceland.
