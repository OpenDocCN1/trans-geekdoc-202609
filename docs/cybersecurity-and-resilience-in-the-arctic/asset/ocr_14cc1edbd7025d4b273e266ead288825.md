The Agreement on Cooperation on Aeronautical and Maritime Search and Rescue in the Arctic (2011): The Agreement on Cooperation on Aeronautical and Maritime Search and Rescue in the Arctic, signed at the Nuuk Ministerial in 2011, builds on existing international and regional search (SAR) frameworks and is the first legally-binding agreement negotiated under the auspices of the Arctic Council (Sydnes 2017). The policy process began in 2009 at the Arctic Council Ministerial in Norway, where the Ministers mandated the creation of a Task Force to develop an international instrument for cooperation on SAR operations in the Arctic. The Agreement, which came into force in January 2013, facilitates international SAR cooperation in the Arctic by detailing cooperation procedures including for SAR assistance, border-crossing, information sharing, and hosting regular meetings (Arctic Council, 2011). The Agreement allows for coordination of the military, coast guard, police and transport services of Arctic States for Greaves and Pomerants, 2017.

Governments have an ongoing function of carrying out exercises under the SAR Agreement, which-to-date has included series of tabletop and full-scale exercises involving the coast guards and armed forces of participating nations to test communications, equipment, and procedures (Sydnes 2017). Participating governments have produced reports on the successes and gaps in the exercises to adapt to regional operational needs and improve the effective implementation of the Agreement. Joint exercises are a crucial function of international relations because they encourage effective cooperation, the gradual development of working relations between participating agencies, and the strengthening of the overall international relationship between stakeholders (Sydnes 2017).

The Agreement on Cooperation on Marine Oil Pollution Preparedness and Response in the Arctic (2013): The Agreement on Cooperation on Marine Oil Pollution Preparedness and Response in the Arctic is the second legally binding agreement negotiated under the auspices of the Arctic Council, signed at Kiruna, Sweden, and entering into force in March 2016. The two-year negotiation process, began with the establishment of an interdisciplinary task force at the May 2011 Nuuk Declaration of the Arctic Council Ministerial Meeting, involved five meetings over a one-year period from October 2011 to October 2012 (Trigatti, Bjerkemo and Evertit 2014). The Task Force included a number of Arctic stakeholders in the negotiation process including Arctic States, Arctic Council Permanent Participants, observers, industry representatives, and invited subject matter experts; decision-making was based on consensus among stakeholders (Trigatti, Bjerkemo and Evertit 2014). The Agreement priorities shared among Arctic States, including those relating to the promotion of social and economic development and environmental protection, and provides a vehicle for international cooperation on these issues.
