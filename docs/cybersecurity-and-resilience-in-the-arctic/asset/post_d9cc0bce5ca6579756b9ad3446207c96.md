## 2.2 Coding Scheme

Upon acquiring the relevant set of 52 supply chain resilience modeling papers, the authors coded each piece across 5 domain areas, including “Resilience Characteristics,” “Supply Chain Network Representation,” “Transportation Network Representation,” “Command and Control Representation,” and “Disruptions Representation.” Within each domain, the coding scheme was used to assess papers based upon how they tackle a number of issues:

- 1. “Resilience Characteristics” classifies how the paper characterizes resilience. Papers were coded to which of the National Academy of Sciences phases of resilience were included in their methods and if they used any metrics to measure resilience, separate from base network characteristics. The National Academy of Sciences phases are “Plan,” “Absorb,” “Recover,” and “Adapt” (National Research Council 2012). “Calculate Resilience Metric” was used to indicate the usage of a numeric measure to identify a supply chain’s resilience, so long as that measure was not a simple characteristic of network geometry, such as betweenness, or length path. This restriction was put in place to ensure that resilience is being calculated for a working system, rather than a complex abstract.

- 2. The “Supply Chain Network Representation” characterizes the type of graphical model used in the papers. The Supply Chain Network Representation describes the steps of acquisition, transformation, and distribution that materials take to support organizational goals. The classifiers are “Linear,” “Branching,” “Graph,” and “Other.” Linear supply chains are the simplest model, where the choice of where to move goods is entirely constrained as one node exists for each stage of production. A branching supply chain is more complex, with multiple options for each stage of production available. Branching supply chains, however, constrained directionally, as stages of production must be strictly unidirectional. A graph supply chain is the most complex of the three general models and describes any supply chain that allows outputs of later stages of production to be used as inputs of earlier stages of production. Examples of these three types of supply chains can be seen in the other models used in the first three categories to be classified separately, but not common enough to get their own category. An example would be making use of economic input-output (EIO) models. While EIO models are mathematical, their conceptual underpinnings are quite different from those that attempt to model a physical description of supply chains.

- 3. The “Transportation Network Representation” describes how materials move among nodes in the Supply Chain Network. Any supply chain not existing in one location will be dependent upon a transportation network and how this dependency is handled is important. The possible values are “None,” “Same as SC,” “Independent Links,” “Graph,” and “Other.” A value of “None” means that the model ignores the possibility of disruptions while goods are moving among supply chain nodes. A value of “Same as SC” means that the model allows for disruption of goods while moving among supply chain nodes, but has no model of re-routing might occur to mitigate disruptions. A value of “Independent Links” means that routing decisions between individual supply chain nodes are modeled, but the routes between each supply chain node are entirely independent from one another. This is illustrated in Figure 4A. A value of “Other” means that the transportation network is modeled differently enough from the other models used in the first four categories to be classified separately.

- 4. The “Command and Control Representation” describes how decisions are made. This can include decisions on what to produce etc. The possible values are “None/Predetermined,” “If-Then / Heuristic,” “Agent Based,” “Optimization.” A value of “None/Predetermined” means that supply chain decisions will not change in response to events. A value of “If-Then / Heuristic” means that the model applies simple and deterministic rules at each supply chain node. A value of “Agent Based” means that multiple autonomous agents, potentially independent, follow probabilistic rules to make decisions. A value of “Optimization” means that the whole supply chain runs on a global optimization algorithm. A value of “other” means that the decision-making framework is modeled differently enough from the other models used in the first four categories to be classified separately.

- 5. “Disruptions Representation” describes the set of disturbances that a supply chain will be simulated as facing. The possible values are “Case Study,” “Set List,” “Monte Carlo,” “Targeted,” and “None/Other.” A value of “Case Study” shows that the circumstances andor disturbances of the model will be based upon contemporary or historical situations. A value of “Set List” means that the model is subjected to a finite and predefined list of disturbances, which are not explicitly derived from contemporary or historic situations. A value of “Monte Carlo” means that disturbances will be modeled with random variable(s). A value of “Targeted” means that disturbances are being generated to include a mathematical model meant to maximize supply chain disruption. It was decided to include a value before the initial review, due to specific sponsor interest. A value of “None/Other” shows that either no disturbances were modeled, or that the method was used differently enough from the other models used in the first four categories to be classified separately.
