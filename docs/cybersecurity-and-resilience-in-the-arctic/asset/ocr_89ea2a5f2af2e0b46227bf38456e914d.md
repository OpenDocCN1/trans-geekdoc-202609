Nevertheless, the rapidly changing and globalized economy threatens several elements of Arctic life, on a much larger scale and shorter timeframe than ever before. As residents in a location with a variety of industries, from resource extraction to fishing and fur trade, from commercial whaling to “extinction tourism” (McKie, 2016), indigenous communities have been faced with the introduction of new infrastructure and migration, with the successfulness of the integration varying.

Accordingly, activities in the Arctic can either enhance or harm the ability for indigenous communities to be resilient. Resilient communities can be disrupted by many of the structures and processes that nurture an indigenous community’s capacity to adapt; in some cases, people have been limited in their ability to move, had mandatory schooling imposed, and had hunter-gathering activities restricted. Alternatively, flexibility and support can allow resilience (Zem et al., 2019). One example is the Dempster Highway, built in Canada in the 1960s (Padilla and Kofinas, 2014); Bali and Kofinas, of this construction transformed local communities’ ability to hunt carving, fearing the caribou population would decrease as a result of the highway; policies were adapted which included local people managing in the herd through co-management and information in governance, (Arctic Council, 2016). Indigenous knowledge was used to integrate differing interests and information in governance, resulting in a scenario where all were mutually benefited. 

Acting proactively to recognize and mitigate the negative impacts of development in the Arctic should be a key concern for the authors of any proposal in the High North, and developments should therefore be considered with respect to bettering a system’s identity, function, and structure. Sole using risk assessment and management techniques when evaluating impacts on indigenous communities in the Arctic is inadequate; there is a need to ensure sustainable development while limiting negative impacts, and this cannot be easily achieved when impacts are unknown and are hard to predict.
