The Chinese white paper covers many policy goals; however, they can be briefly summarized as:

+   1. Deepening the exploration and understanding of the Arctic
2. Protecting the eco-environment of the Arctic and addressing climate change
3. Utilizing Arctic resources in a Lawful and rational manner:
    * Including participation in the development of Arctic shipping routes
    * Participation in oil, gas, mineral and other non-living resource extraction
    * Participation in conservation and utilization of fisheries and other living resources
    * Participation in developing tourism resources
4. Participating in Arctic governance and international cooperation
5. Promoting a peaceful, secure and stable Arctic order.

Furthermore, China also declares that its policy on the Arctic are to understand, develop and participate in the governance of the Arctic, so as to safeguard the common interests of all countries and the international community in the Arctic and promote sustainable development of the Arctic. The white paper goes on to identify four key areas for achieving these policy, in “cooperation”, “win-win result”, and “sustainability”. They aim to promote international law, respect sovereign rights, and work with Arctic and sub-Arctic states in producing sustainable development in the Arctic region.

The white paper suggests China has a strong commitment to upholding the institutional and legal frameworks for Arctic governance and simultaneously acknowledging the sovereign rights of the Arctic states and the Indigenous communities. However, the paper also asserts China’s right as a non-Arctic state to be involved and participate in Arctic affairs under International law.

# 7.2. Japan

Japan’s interest and claims in the Arctic stem largely from similar concerns that China has, the irreversible impacts of Climate Change on global sea level, the increased frequency of extreme weather, and its impact on ecosystems. Furthermore, Japan specifically outlines its interests in commercial shipping routes, that are now increasingly accessible to the extent to which sea ice in the Arctic has melted.

Japan released a report in 2015, outlining the country’s policy in the Arctic [59]. First, the report outlines areas of topic and issues that Japan believes need to be increasingly focused upon in the Arctic; these include:

+   1. Global environmental issues
2. Indigenous peoples of the Arctic
3. Science and Technology
4. Ensuring the rule of law and promoting international cooperation
5. The Arctic Sea Route
