According to Frankl (1992), the constitutive characteristic of humans to extend one's purely human existence to something or beyond oneself to which he attaches, gives the individual a cause for living. This self-transcendence is necessary in order to make sense of suffering or conditions existing which cannot be willed away. Self-transcendence allows one to see situations from perspectives beyond one's vantage point. Personal actions including those relating to adaptive behaviors as those of the idsjel of Norway. Personal strategies, which are perceived to bring positive results, become preferred behaviors (Cskszentintihalyi, 1990, 1996; Cskszentintihalyi, Rathnade, & Whalen, 1997). When accompanied by an optimism that these experiences can be replicated to some extent in the future (Cskszentintihalyi, Rathnade, & Whalen, 1997), the personal adaptive processes become hardwired, and can be applied in creative ways to transform effects of disruptive regime changes into sustainable resilience (Arctic Council, 2016).

Transformative agency is capacity which allows individuals to create a new system when the current system (ecological, political, social, economic) is untenable. Socio-ecological system resilience requires some level of learning, reorganization, innovation and transformation for the part of the individual as an agent of change (Folke, 2006), which is noted earlier, are employed in reflexive adaptation. The adaptation, learning, and revising-knowledge function of the individual as a complex system gets inextricably linked to the resilience capacity of the SES by informing agency (Rocha, Peterson, & Biggs, 2015). There are differences between people in which these functions ultimately play out; and the kinds of outcomes come as a result. A significant attribute relates to the application of individual creative talent in generating pathways to adaptive solutions.

# 3.2. Creativity

Agency mobilizes creative processes that are particularly important in complex situations. Creativity, reflective of diverse talents, is critical to survival of both individual and society. That dependency can be expressed in terms of four categories (Tannenbaum, 1983): a) Scarcity-talents in short supply but needed by society and all people; b) Burplus-talents-designed by society but not essential for survival; c) Anomalous talents-felt between scarcity and surplus; d) Exceptional exceptions, with practical use.

Extraordinary creativity is particularly important in the face of substantial uncertainty. The human functional system of adaptation is a complex, integrated hierarchical formation, related to experiential history. Vygotsky's (1978) theoretical framework on behavioral psychology offers insight into the complex and nuanced dynamics of functional systems in response to internal and external stimuli, in varying contexts, as required under adaptive conditions. Viewing humans as highly plastic, and malleable organizations situated in cultural environments that will eventually change, Vygotsky emphasizes "the interactions between social change and the biological sub-status of behavior" (p.123).
