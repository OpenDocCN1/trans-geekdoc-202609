Such a situation calls for exploration of creative solutions from the bottom up. Those who want to help from the top or outside could be most effective if they empower and reinforce organic growth rather than simply pursuing well-intentioned objectives. Bottom-up resilience has been studied in its context, but less often in relation to its contributions to the bigger picture; moreover, it is not prominent in policies, or initiatives that implement. This chapter provides a framework that relates principles of individual, collective, and place-based adaptation that can guide top-down action. Individuals ultimately are the agents of action. They are inspired by hope and meaning; informed by processes that temper individuality with creative feedback; and enabled through the particular nature of resources, support could be most valuable to the degree that it fosters dynamics.

The above cases illustrate how individuals and communities use creativity and dialogue to shape local initiatives, each instance, the communities reject the expectation of external direction and the aspiration to emulate urban models. In the instance of Alaska, used art to inspire participation, and also as a channel to solicit external support. Notably, some of the earliest and most sustaining external support for relocation effort has been through grassroots innovation. Community art and events are effective catalysts for reflection but the availability of media often constrain their external agencies—especially those focused on the power of art—can provide not only community centers, online presence, but can provide inspiration like minded people, eradication efforts—even a global collaboration to redress ("Reinvent the Toilet," n.d.). In this environment, borders as readily as changes. The challenge shifts from navigating government portfolios to the ability to distill and convey key steps and their impacts.

Arctic communities will find the formula for their own resilience, but need external help. As the Stockholm Resilience Center suggests (Arctic Council, 2016), the new paradigm should foster local context and self-organization. The most useful assistance may come in the form of resources that catalyze community-based collaboration, information sharing capabilities, and mentoring from entities with common interests. In essence, the dynamic must shift from directive programs toward entrepreneurial development in a newly empowered world.
