# 6.8. Swedish Arctic strategy

Sweden's Arctic Policy and strategy dates back to May 2011, when the Swedish Government adopted “Sweden’s Strategy for the Arctic Region” [50].

Before the publication of this strategy, the Swedish government had been largely quiet on northern affairs. The country has been active in international Arctic cooperation from the beginning, as it is after all member of the Arctic Council and a co-founder. While Swedish territory goes beyond the Arctic Circle, ties to the Arctic demographically also strong; Saami groups have lived in Sweden for centuries, and Sweden has greatly contributed to Arctic polar research [43].

Sweden are particularly concerned with three key areas, which they define in the strategy as their priorities. These areas are:

+   1. Climate and the environment;
2. Economic growth and growing business field and interests;
3. The human dimension

The document states several reasons why there’s a growing interest in the Arctic, and why a strategy for the region is needed. Among these are climate change and global warming, the challenges facing the living conditions of indigenous peoples and the struggles for natural resources.

The main priorities of the document, and a list of objectives in order to achieve these goals in the near future are as follows:

+   1. Climate change and the environment; outlined here are sub-priorities, each of which relate to climate, environmental protection, biodiversity and scientific research. Sweden aims to reduce greenhouse emissions, whilst also emphas the impacts of climate change as highlighted in line with international climate agreements and negotiations. Moreover, investing in scientific research is key to becoming a leader in climate and environmental change, as well the impacts they will have on society.
