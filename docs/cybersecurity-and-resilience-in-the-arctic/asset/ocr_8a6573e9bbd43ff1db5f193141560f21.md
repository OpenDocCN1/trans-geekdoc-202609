# 310 E.P. Yakabovic / Cyber Resilience Measurements and Metrics

Metrics also assist with tracking specific objectives and goals in meeting compliance and regulatory matters, or other major operational drivers that impact the business greatly. Regardless, metrics measurement must be normalized to assist in the resiliency conversation and repeatable so that key stakeholders and decision makers understand the importance and impact that has to the business. Second, to be meaningful, metrics must eliminate over complexity and jargon, making it easier for decision makers to understand the value to operations and why the measurement is important. Last, the group or person creating the metric must understand the importance of the why, what, and how a metric will be presented and address cyber resilience to the business otherwise they will lose their opportunity to reflect the importance of the measurement to the business or the metric will become a rubber stamp with no value.

For metrics to be beneficial they must be described clearly; otherwise they will be confusing with no value, and waste budget funding resources. Resiliency metrics should be captured regularly on a consistence and normalized schedule. This help demonstrate the resilient return (ROI), overall maturity, and add value by feeding into the overall business resiliency metrics. Resiliency metrics should also be agreed upon by both the ICT professional and the business leadership so both understand the goal and the data being reported.

Metric identification is difficult, especially for resiliency the concept is new and changing every year. Having a metric does not represent the value to the business unless it reflects connection to sustainable, scalable, and meeting business objectives. Making informed decisions on any business investment and application of resources to improve and enhance technology is a challenge, thus the tracking of KPIs through metrics can be used to track multiple objectives and goals. Although easy to say, this is the most challenging to the ICT professional; managing resiliency and business objectives. Most important, the value of resiliency can be shown within the metrics tracking and reporting through relative and appropriate indicators within business language based on current management and controls already present and used in the orig. Yet another challenge to the ICT professional in creating the metrics to properly measure resiliency within practical and easy to understand artifacts based on standards, practices, and processes used today. The next section will review a common general method to represent resiliency and the data necessary to create and use the visual for metrics.
