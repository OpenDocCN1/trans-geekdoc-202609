Krotofil & Cardenas [37] provide an empirical analysis of the Tennessee Eastman process control challenge to gain insights into the behavior of a physical process confronted with fighter-cyber-risk attacks. The paper highlights countless process-aware cyber-risk assessments in discovering the weaknesses of process design in the presence of cyber-attacks. The authors highlight that (general) low availability of time and resources to perform such an analysis means that work needs to be addressed from a process-engineering standpoint.

Linkov et al. [41,42] provide a cyber-resilience matrix, which provides a means to consider how cyber systems can "plan and prepare for", "absorb", "recover from" and "adapt to" in four stages of the event management cycle. The authors highlight how metrics can provide the quantitative or qualitative means of recovering an attribute (e.g. system performance during disruption). Linkov et al. [40] also outline how benchmarks and thresholds for risk analysis are built into the regulations and policies of organisations and nations. They suggest early integration of resilience is needed to address the emerging issues associated with complexity and uncertainty of the world.

Eis et al. [18] assert that resilience metrics in the literature often fail due to existing concepts that reduce their use, including conflict of risk and resilience, and the necessity of reconciling engineering and ecological resilience definitions and objectives.

Bjork et al. [7] aims to define cyber resilience and compares the concept to cyber security. The authors consider five keys in their comparison between the two systems: objective, intention, approach, architecture and scope. They conclude that resilience is centered on business continuity management despite adverse cyber events.

Liu et al. [43] focus on resilience management, going from a "reactive" paradigm to a proactive one through the use of situational awareness of internal network factors and external elements of complex, dynamic and heterogeneous network environment. Their situational awareness covers three main network resilience issues: perception (metrics to measure resilience of network and external factors from environment), comprehension (pattern identification) and anticipation (threat analysis)
