Furthermore, the Arctic is unique in that it is an area of great economic value; not only is it more value will be in the form of physical and transient infrastructure, but also more in the value of people and systems are expected to become vulnerable to the dangers and risks associated with the High North.

These Arctic systems constitute complex sociotechnical systems (Mayntz and Hughes, 1988); technological infrastructure (including weather stations, scientific instruments, communications, satellites, computer systems etc.) and the material elements in the Arctic are as reliant as their southern counterparts (the organizations, operational knowledge, standards and protocols, and communities of practice) that are all necessary for the system to function properly. The intertwining of these systems presents an even more significant challenge – one where both systems have fundamental components hardened through strategies of risk analysis and risk management, and as systems as a whole are developed in a resilient manner.
