# ## INTRODUCTION

Global forces are driving profound changes in the Far North (Figure 1), resulting in unexpected consequences for people and nature. The Far North’s environment is characterized by low precipitation, long and cold winters, and short summers. In response to climate change, Arctic temperatures are warming at least twice faster than the rest of the planet. The past five years’ air temperatures exceeded all records since 1900 [1]. This rapid warming compounds multiple social, ecological, and security transformations also manifesting in the region. Managing this uncertain change requires a convergence of expertise from multiple domains. A complex systems framework can organize diverse sources of knowledge, illuminate potential risks and opportunities to people and nature of the Far North, and ultimately bolster resilience.
