Fourth, the Circumpolar North is experiencing increased militarization.[76] After the end of the Cold War, the Arctic transitioned from a militarized area (e.g., USA and western Europe versus Soviet Union) to a global zone of peace and cooperation. In recent decades, shifting geopolitics have increased the danger of arctic military confrontations between arctic coastal states and non-arctic states.[77] These difficult challenges require careful consideration so that old and new actors can address them in ways that maintain a peaceful political environment in the Circumpolar North. Importantly, because militarization falls outside the scope of the Arctic Council, actors in the region lack explicit norms and principles of engagement.
