# 3.3. Enhancing Resilience Based on Potential and Predicted Disruptions

This chapter discussed some of the disruptions most likely to affect USN operations in the Arctic in the near future. The following sections examine some of the methods proposed in the literature to improve resilience in the face of these disruptions, challenges, and threats. It is important to keep in mind, however, that this is only addressing known or predictable disruptions. To be resilient, Navy vessels should be prepared to respond to, absorb, recover and adapt to unexpected and unknown disruptions. This is a significant challenge, and it will take insubstantial resources to tackle. Nevertheless, it is important if the USN is to maintain its competitive advantage. It is somewhat challenging, but no less important, for the USN to fund further research and incorporate into improving the resilience of USN systems to predicted disruptions.

# 3.3.1. Environmental disruptions

The main (and known) environmental disruptions to USN operations in the Arctic will result from climate change and its effects. The most likely challenges will be for navigation due to melting sea ice, wind and waves, unpredictable currents, and inaccurate, obsolete maps and to infrastructure built on permafrost or on the coast. As, if the Navy emphasizes navigating and infrastructural resilience, it may be able to improve its overall resilience efficiently. A number of ideas have been proposed to enhance the resilience of these systems; this section will outline a few of them. More research would be, particularly with regards to how climate change in the Arctic can be prepared for and Arctic Navy systems must remain resilient in the face of the harsh and unpredictable environment.

As regards, the biannual ice Exercise (ICEEX) is an excellent start for increasing the USN's preparedness, broad utility, and recovery ability. ICEEX provides the opportunity to carry out research and development, to evaluate new systems or designs, and practice combined forces interoperability [173]. Unfortunately, ICEEX alone will not be enough to build sufficiently resilient USN systems and maintain the Navy's strategic edge in the Arctic.
