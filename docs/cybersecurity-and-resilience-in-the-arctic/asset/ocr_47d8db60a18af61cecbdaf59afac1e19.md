When developing future infrastructure and information communication systems in the Arctic regions there is also a need to go through use cases from which we would define the requirements for infrastructure, services, and information and communication. Use cases from oil and gas companies and mining companies are also needed in the development of Arctic regions. From all of these cases we can define the requirements for the system, and from them the dependencies, and the dependencies we can define risks. When we have the use cases, we can define the different types of architectures required for different services. These architectures include: an Arctic with infrastructure architectures, integrations architectures, security and cybersecurity architectures, and also smart device architectures. Once we have determined the defined risks to these systems, we can conduct risk analyses. At the same time, we must try to identify the vulnerabilities of our current systems and services so that we can define threats and cyber threats and threat analysis from them.

In the development of future infrastructure in the Arctic region, care must be taken to follow EU directives and guidelines as well as international agreements and laws. This information shows designers and developers the right directions for developments. Because the Arctic region is very difficult to build in a large area, a further question is of expense: what is reasonable and what is not.
