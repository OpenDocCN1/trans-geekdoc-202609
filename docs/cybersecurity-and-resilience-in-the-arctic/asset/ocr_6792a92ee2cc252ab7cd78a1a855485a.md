# # Digital Resilience: Navigating in the New Normal

Jordanka Tomkova, PhD
INNOVABRIGE Foundation, Switzerland

**Abstract.** This paper seeks to expand the scope of conceptualizing social resilience in the digital era. It examines emerging trends in how groups or communities, formally or informally informed, use the digital space in the recovery stages of disruptive events. While disruptive events or collective disasters include health epidemics, security or economic crises, the focus here is on collective social responses to hybrid threats. Hybrid threats combine with offline destabilizing tactics that attack targets' virtual or (physical) infrastructure, information manipulation, political subversion and social polarization. The paper argues that the anonymous character of hybrid threats seeks new responses and enhanced digital social resilience means and how it manifests practiced in reality. Following a review of literature this paper identifies how communities use digital tools to respond to hybrid threats: i) collective mobilization, crowdsourcing and toolmaking; ii) emotive solidarity, and iii) restoration of morality quests. The paper postulates that these observed forms of collective behavior characterize and define merging of social resilience in the contemporary online environment.

**Keywords.** Digital social resilience, social networks, digital coping strategies, hybrid threats

# ## INTRODUCTION

As new information and communication technologies rapidly permeate our realities, the offline and online spaces increasingly collide in unprecedented ways forming our new normal. As part of this reality, cyberattacks, online anonymity, the darkweb, deployment of malware and subversive social media bots, disinformation, identity thefts, trolling, astroturfing, doxxing, swatting are emerging neotypes of socially erosive behavior. In combination with hybrid threats these have been observed to deepen cognitive tribalism, exploit geopolitical manipulation, political subversion, social polarization or malicious civic disobedience threatening the social fabric of our societies. They seek to expose targets' vulnerabilities, to instill a state of permanence and to destabilize the socio-political, security and often economic order as we know it.[11]

In response to these threats what hat or ethical hacking, hashtag activism, fact-checking, debunking and unmasking are emerging as the online antidotes and implicitly, what this paper considers measures of social resilience. Reviewing pioneering research and illustrative case studies, this paper seeks to place a magnifying glass on examining social resilience from the online or digital perspective.
