+   - Collect evidence

Based on the plan defined in the previous step, we can collect evidence from each identified source. However the following three points should be considered:

+   - Documentation (log file) of all systems accessed and all actions taken during evidence collection. The file itself should be safely stored following the same guidelines as the evidence itself. The information collected should include time, source of the evidence, acquisition method and the involved investigator(s).
- Capturing of the evidence itself. This is the part where we capture network packets, copy system and application logs, etc.
- Storage/Transport of the collected evidence. Here we should maintain the Chain of Custody, it “showing the seizure, custody, control, transfer, analysis, and disposition of evidence, physical or electronic” (Bononi et al., 2018).

Since adversity, of network-based evidence, is not clear-cut, we should take careful notes and consult legal counsel when needed. With evidence, proper care must be taken to preserve evidence integrity while documenting usage and disposition throughout its life cycle.

Tips for evidence collection include fast and lawful acquisition, creation of cryptographically verifiable copies, analysis only on the cases, usage of reputable and reliable tools, and final deconstruction whenever we do.

+   - Conduct analysis

During this step we recover material using a number of different methodologies and tools. According to Brian D. Carrier (2006), “After the obvious evidence has been found, more exhaustive searches are conducted to start filling in the holes.” This suggests that the selected method for analysis depends on the case and what leads are already present. As a result, several iterations of examination and analysis are foreseen to support a theory. During the analysis process the following should be considered as essential:

+   - Correlation of data from different sources.
- Timeline of activities, allowing us to understand who did what, when, and how is the basis for any theory of the case.
- Isolation of Events,
- Correlation of evidence through multiple sources to the low fidelity of data and the “false positives.”
- Recovery of additional evidence, since all previous steps could increase the evidence collected and analysed.
- Interpretation (educated assessments) of the meaning of the collected evidence, aiming to help investigators identify additional sources of evidence, and construct a theory of the events that were likely to have occurred. Attention must be paid in differentiating the interpretations of the evidence from the fact, since interpretations may be proved or disproved.
