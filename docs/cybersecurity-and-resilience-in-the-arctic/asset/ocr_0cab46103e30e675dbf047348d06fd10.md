# **Why Cybersecurity Matters for the Arctic**

Many of the emerging risk concerns in the Arctic are predicated upon the growing reliance upon complex digital technologies. With other areas in the world, industrial and military activity in the Arctic is increasingly predicated upon the use of complex digital technologies and information systems. From GPS navigation, to mine, such technologies are intended to allow a small number of decision makers to monitor and govern complex system processes nearly instantaneously. These technologies have greatly expanded stakeholder capabilities, and have reduced the number of personnel needed to construct and manage a facility or vessel within a remote and climatologically harsh Arctic.

However, such digital technologies have critical vulnerabilities, that if exploited or disrupted, can trigger extensive and long-lasting system failures. Such disruptions can arrive in various forms, from cyberattacks and hacking events, to the unanticipated degradation or failure of digital systems due to ambient activity (solar storms, violent climatic events, etc.). More concerning is the unpredictability of such events, where cyberattacks or environmental disruptions will likely arise in unexpected scenarios, and trigger cascading failures that are beyond existing countermeasures or plans of action.
