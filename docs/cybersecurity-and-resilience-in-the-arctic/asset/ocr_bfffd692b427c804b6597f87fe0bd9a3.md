Another element of the Arctic where regime shifts are taking place is in the tundra region. The tundra is covered by permafrost - permanently frozen ground that occurs from several inches below the surface to depths of more than 330 meters. Increases in temperatures will are already having adverse effects on permafrost; thawing is expected to disrupt infrastructure of several types – bogging up roads to destabilizing the ground under which buildings stand, the sinking beneath oil pipelines. Hjort et al. [21] estimate that nearly million people and 70% of infrastructure in the permafrost domain have high potential for thaw. Development will require new devices, and construction and maintenance costs are likely to increase. Moreover, thawed in permafrost and cryosphere are greenhouse gases, and melting releases them into the atmosphere. Some estimates put the thawing of East Siberia (the northern Russian Arctic) to come with an average global price of $60 trillion alone, in the absence of mitigating action

Lastly, ice sheets and glaciers in the Arctic are another important feature of the Arctic system. The Greenland ice sheet covers over 82% of Greenland, and if melted is expected to cause a rise of over seven meters in sea level [23]. The ice sheet, however, has long been viewed to be resistant to climate change, as it is typical for thousands of years before large sheets report changing conditions. Recent observations suggest that major changes in the dynamics of parts of the ice sheet are occurring in the time scale of just a few years [1]. Ice has thinning due to temperature increases, especially during the summer, and feedback mechanisms that play a role in stabilizing the ice sheet (ice-albedo feedback and meltwarming) are now playing a destabilizing role within the ice sheet.

# 2.2. Arctic Ecosystems

The Arctic is a home to a diverse range of flora and fauna. Organizations have adapted to a region that is often cold, exposed to prolonged periods of daylight and darkness in alternating seasons, and from a range of habitats in wetlands and deserts - from ponds to open ocean. Some of these organisms, as the polar bear are icons representing the Arctic, yet others are lesser known and it is likely that some are as uncovered. The range of biodiversity is faced by an ever increasing speed of change, with a potential for some of these change to be irreversible.

According to the Arctic Council’s CAFF working group (Conservation on Arctic Flora and Fauna), ecosystems can be defined as “a dynamic complex of plant, animal and micro-organism communities and their non-living environment interacting as a functional unit” The Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) states biodiversity as “the variability among living organisms from all sources, including inter alia, terrestrial, marine and aquatic ecosystems, the ecological complexes of which they are a part”

Visible effects of climate change are proving damaging impacts on Arctic ecosystems. Today, polar bear population is estimated to be between 22,000-31,000 [24].
