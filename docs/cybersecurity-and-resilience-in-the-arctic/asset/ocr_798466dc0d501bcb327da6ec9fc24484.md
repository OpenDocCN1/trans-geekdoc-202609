Maritime shipping has comprehensive international laws and regulations, particularly through the EEZs which demarcate each nation's economic zones (see Figure 11). However, there is no single point of authority for governance in the cruise ship industry—oversight and accountability are decentralized and spread over a multitude of individuals and organizations. While cruise ships have provided local communities with much needed business and economic wealth, these benefits come with uncertainty, risks, and consequences to both people and the environment.[46], [47] Recent efforts from national and supranational stakeholders have focused on the importance of sustainability in Arctic tourism.[44] Ultimately, cruise ship tourism will remain a modestly growing field due to the Arctic's unpredictable environmental and weather conditions, strict regulations and policies, and large upfront development costs.
