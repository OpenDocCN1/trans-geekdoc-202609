Domain 4: Transformations of Far North ecosystems and permafrost
If the North is to secure, resilient, and desirable place for humans and human activities, it is imperative to understand and manage ecosystem processes. During our 2018 workshops, community Elders from Huslia and Nulato tribes emphasized that the local ecosystem was rapidly transforming, with thawing permafrost to blame. They increasingly treacherous navigation routes and changes in the nutritional content (e.g., fatback) of moose and seals due to changes in vegetation. Both are tied to changes in permafrost stability and hydrology. Indeed, ecological processes provide continuity for human activities ranging from subsistence to infrastructure stability [28]. An example of this includes the need to account for environmental disruptions, like less frozen landscapes interfering with access to hunting and fishing.

The study of nutrient and carbon flows (biogeochemistry) is one way to understand how changed elements alter social and infrastructural subsystems in the Far North. In particular, thawing permafrost is a fundamental change in Arctic geochemistry with uncertain repercussions for people, ecosystems, and the built environment.
