## 3. Natural threats, accidental threats and cyber threats

Infrastructure related threats

Quite infrastructure management and control systems, like electric power stations, big buildings, and homes, are connected to the Internet. This kind of management and control system forms its own service network in the Internet, but security protections there may be inadequate in protection technology and this gives hackers and cyber-attackers ample opportunities to attack it. Many of the service operators of the real estate automation systems use the Internet to control their management systems and the real estate automation systems remotely, which are their responsibility. Vulnerabilities are more likely to help attackers to penetrate systems and thus cause harm to the systems which are used every day.

Long-distance electric power transmission lines are controlled by radio systems, which also involve security challenges. These control systems are also quite easy to disturb and provide an easy way for hackers to get inside the power supply management system to interfere with functions or to block the operation altogether.

### Satellite System Threats

Satellite system uses different radio frequencies from the ground station to the satellite and from the satellite to the ground station, Figures 19 and 20. These frequencies are quite easy to disturb and even hinder the operation of satellite. Satellite location information can be changed to the wrong coordinates, which causes a great deal of harm to the sea. We need some form of shipping service (Vessel Traffic Service = VTS) to help ships in such situations, and also if the systems are down for one reason or another.

### Communications System Threats

The communication systems in the Arctic are interconnected in one way or another in an international communications network, and this also allows connections to the continent's communications. These connections have been made; the Arctic communication systems are also targeted by hackers and cyber-attackers in a similar way to the rest of the world. Mobile technologies like 2G/3G/4G are used in the Arctic.

### The Threats Associated with Submarine Optical Cable Systems in the Arctic Region

In addition to the technical design criteria of the submarine optical cable systems, we also need to take into account the different types of threats that can occur, such as natural threats, accidental threats, and cyber or malicious threats. In this context, we are looking mainly for threats against the submarine optical cable systems. These threats can contribute to prolonging cable routes or partial routes or even altering the original routes.

Natural threats include threats such as sharks, earthquakes, landslides, volcanoes, eruptions, tsunamis, icebergs, sea currents, storm winds, and so on. Accidental threats can be caused by everyday work at sea, such as fishing, dragging the anchor, dredging, etc., and can damage submarine optical cables, threatening the performance of submarine optical cable systems. We also need to look at potential adverse effects as the submarine cable routes are long and submerged. There are many countries that have the ability to join (“tapping”) fiber cables to collect the information transmitted therein. In every situation, we must look for opportunities for malicious actors to hack or make cyber-attacks on submarine cables, whether those are tapping operations or other methods such as side channel attacks or side channel spying. We must also implement encryption systems for layers 1, 2 and 3 that we can protect our communications systems against hacking and cyber-attacks.

The undersea cable types to be used depend on the depth of the sea and the vicinity to the coast in the areas where the above-mentioned threats occur, and where the threats are realized. If we consider the attackers’ opportunities to join the optical cable, it would be easiest to penetrate the cable exactly where the armor layers are thin. In practice, only a few large states have the capabilities to do so. When we look at this planned undersea cable system with its length of 18,000 km, distances between optical amplifiers of around 50 km, attackers will be able to connect to underwater optical cables after each optical amplifier.
