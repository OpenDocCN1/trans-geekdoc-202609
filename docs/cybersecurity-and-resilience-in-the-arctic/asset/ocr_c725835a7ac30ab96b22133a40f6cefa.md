Through this framework, the “Wave Analogy framework for cyber-resilience”, Guerra & Sepulveda (Guerra & Sepulveda Estay, 2018) describes an arrangement of these themes with the occurrence of a cyber-event, a dynamic (time-related) approach for the relative “positions” of each these along a timeline. The relative position of each is related to the time when each element’s presence is more relevant, and this relevance is characterized by 1) in the prevention of risk, and recovery from cyber events, and 2) short, medium, or long-term effects. As a result, the themes located farther away from the time of the event are more strategic in nature, and with a more immediate effect on operations. This is reflected in Figure 2.

The anomaly is extended by Guerra & Sepulveda by using to explain the effects of a cyber-event before and after its occurrence. What is located to the left of the cyber-event, also called the “point of impact”, is the reflection of the “defensive mechanisms” that would fail for a cyber-event to materialize.

This approach has similarities to causal models like the Swiss cheese model of risk, and substantial differences with them. The Swiss cheese model of accidental causation is an analysis that suggests that despite any organization may have many layers of defense between hazards and accidents, flaws in each layer and the alignment of these layers may allow the accident to occur.
