Pajic et al (2017) explored methods to increase the resilience of cyber-physical systems (CPSs) in the face of attacks on control systems (with a particular focus on attacks that compromise sensors) [163]. Cyber-physical systems are systems in which the software and physical components are tightly-coupled; as such, they are especially vulnerable to disruptions. Attackers may infiltrate a system and provide inaccurate or delayed readings, or they can disrupt the operation of the system.

Pajic et al (2017) note that "even high-assurance military systems" can be pregnable and that preparation for intrusions must be in the initial control-design phase [163]. A number of recent studies have suggested ways to increase the resilience of vessels operating specifically in relation to other vessels. For example, Wahlström (2019) proposes a method for improving the resilience of vessels navigating between and other vessels by incorporating the perspectives of shipshanders [164]. O'Dea et al (2009) invented a vertical damping system which "counteracts the effect of roll motion on water vessels, enabling ship-to-ship functions such as cargo loading and the like"" [165].

Han et al. (2012) focus on the inherent vulnerabilities of systems-of-systems given that failures can easily propagate through the entire system-of-systems [166]. Using Bayesian networks, they develop a method to determine the resilience of a system-of-systems. This determines each individual system's contribution to the resilience of the system-of-systems as a whole, as well as the total resilience pattern, which demonstrates the functionality of system-of-systems as components fail [166].
