# Cyber Security and Resilience of Smart Grid Infrastructure in the Arctic

Inna SKARGA-BANDUROVA^{a,b,1} and Igor KOTSIUBA^b

^V. Dahl East Ukrainian Institute for Modeling in Energy Engineering NAS of Ukraine

**Abstract.** Smart Grids are an emerging technology promising significant changes in the economy and the social sphere all over world. Arctic region transformation in its energy sector from a consumer of electricity to producing, sharing, and storing deploying smart grid infrastructure. With that, due to often repeat and extreme conditions, cybersecurity is one of many challenges in leveraging energy grids in the Arctic. Recent smart hackers' attacks on energy grids and taking into account the distributed structure of a crime figure becomes more difficult or impossible. This traditional means of connection and the search for a crime paper summarizes our previous work and the findings from a working group at a NATO Advanced Workshop on Governance for Cybersecurity and Resilience in the Arctic as it pertains to critical infrastructure, held in Romania from 27-30 January 2019. It aims to introduce some areas of smart grid security and forensics, discuss the open issues in the topic. The several problems that may arise during the forensics process in smart grids and practical recommendations for their resolving also discussed. Following recommendations of the UK National Cyber Security Center, we follow a procedure to analyze architectures and highlight issues related to Chain of Custody (CoC) process. We also discuss challenges for forensic in smart grids in connection with a blockchain and propose a decentralized transaction platform based on blockchain tailored to the energy sector with all the latest technology such as metering infrastructure, distribution generation, etc. Some aspects of developing a cyberforensic framework for cyber-crime investigation based on the smart grid network data are also discussed.

**Keywords.** Cyber security, smart grid, infrastructure, forensics, blockchain.
