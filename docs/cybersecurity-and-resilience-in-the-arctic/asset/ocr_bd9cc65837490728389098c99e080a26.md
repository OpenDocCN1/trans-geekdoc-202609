This includes accounting for risk-based challenges and outcomes of many permanent stakeholders (indigenous communities, industry, government/military, environmental organizations, etc.). Within this vein, submissions by Pechorin as Skarga-Bandurov et al indicate data securitization possibilities through emerging technologies. Likewise, Sepulveda-Esteban and Guerra review resilience challenges to sharing information from the perspective of systems operations – a growing trend in Arctic waters. Mathematically, Yakabowicz discusses how cyber resilience can be operationalized, measured, and assessed for a variety of systems and processes. Morsky et al. unpack and explore rising challenges in the management of complex supply chains in the Arctic and beyond, resilience is required to ensure supply chain disruptions do not substantially degrade or destroy the ability of an organization to function. Lappy describes support requirements and capabilities for Arctic facilities and operations.

Referring to social challenges and consequences of cybersecurity disruption in the Arctic, Klas et al. provide a comprehensive overview of societal and governance challenges in the Arctic and sub-Arctic. Their work is complemented by Kirchner et al., Zvaritch, Roege and Roege, who each touch upon local, and/or governance challenges with developing, managing, and operating large infrastructure projects in the Arctic domain. Tomkov reviews the social governance challenges and needs associated with digital technologies and communication approaches, while Brig et al unpack how resilience may be applied in a structured framework for a multi-systems approach to address the environmental, infrastructural, social, and indigenous needs, and perspectives in the Arctic and sub-Arctic.

This collective work represents a small but important subset of challenges and opportunities facing the Arctic today. Collectively, they have not to serve as the first word of what engineering and governance decisions must be executed to assist Arctic stakeholders, to open the door for discussion about what a safe and sustainable Arctic may look in the years to come. Where actors from various backgrounds are turning to complex and interconnected digital technologies to promote their safety and standard of living within an otherwise forbidding Arctic environment, rigorous assessments of cybersecurity and safety (including the implications and outcomes of further development) also take place to prepare for the threatened threats and consequences we have already begun to face.
