# ## 1. Introduction

Modern societies are rapidly becoming more and more dependent on internet technology and communication, with digital solutions the corners of such societies. At a conference on 5G and security in Prague in May 2019 (Czech Republic Government, 2019) the chairman made that cyber security is not only a technical issue. Adequate national strategies, comprehensive legal frameworks, and dedicated personnel, trained and educated appropriately, are needed as well for meeting the requirements of safe, secure, and resilient infrastructures. Following following the state of the art and having sufficient competence to do such for any country. As example, the number of scientific papers on cyber security has increased exponentially in recent years, more than doubling every two-year period¹. Modern societies base the growth and improvement of services on digital solutions to the degree that they have created a digital dependency. A failure in digital systems can seriously affect the ability of societies to provide vital services. Cyber security has become of critical importance. It is also growing in complexity, as can be seen in the ENISA (2018) report on R&D priorities in cybersecurity including the educational challenge, artificial intelligence, quantum technology, cascade effect, supply chain, and cybercrime. The identification of which services are considered critical has changed over the years. In the Directive on Security of Network and Information Systems (NIS-directive), the European Union defined the following services as energy, transport, banking, financial market infrastructures, drinking water and distribution, and digital infrastructure (EU, 2016). The description in this chapter is based on documented references as well as discussions with some of the people involved in the development of Internet usage in the Arctic. As much of this usage is currently undergoing rapid changes the overview given here is bound to incomplete.
