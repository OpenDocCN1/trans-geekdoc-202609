The Global Cyber Security Capacity Centre has a Cybersecurity Capacity Maturity
Model (CMM) for Nations that aims to help nations increase their security capacity.
While cybersecurity maturity is not equivalent to resilience, there is enough overlap to
consider cybersecurity maturity, in particular, relates to resilience. The
model considers cybersecurity maturity as being comprised of five dimensions (at five
levels of maturity): 1) Cybersecurity policy and strategy, 2) Cyber culture and society,
3) Cyber security education, training and skills, 4) Legal and regulatory frameworks, and
5) Standards, organisations, and technologies. The CMM includes factors

Hult & Svanberg [34] explore the feasibility of a paradigm shift in cyber resilience,
from going information security capacity to confronting adversarial cyber
security. This is done in the effort to communicate attributes of a cyber-resilient
organisation. The article provides examples and to illustrate what good cyber resilience looks like today.

Vurgeon & Theoretical [56] describe a resilience assessment methodology that combines
qualitative analysis techniques with performance-based metrics. The former enables
identification of features that limit resilience (identification of weaknesses),
while quantitative data can be used to confirm the effectiveness of mitigation options.
a case study is presented to demonstrate how the approach could be applied to a
hothetical Human Resources system and the ramifications of those cyber systems
being affected. In the example, the system is disrupted due to human error by an
employee of the company. The framework is used to identify and evaluate options
improving the resilience of the system.
