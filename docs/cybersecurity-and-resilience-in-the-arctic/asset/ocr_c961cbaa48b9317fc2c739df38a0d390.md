As many systems remain ad hoc in approach to resilience, there is a great demand for methods and tools that address resilience concerns more dynamically, efficiently and effectively. Specifically, these tools and techniques should allow for enhanced measurement, assessment, improvement and communication of resilience of systems. An approach which requires a much wider perspective than considering security and risk concerns alone. Prior work has been able to show progress towards some high-level theoretical and methodological objectives, but is still lacking in progress towards empirical evidence and tools development.

# 4.1. Recommendations

It remains to be whether the extent of the utility of analytics tools can sufficiently support analysts, risk-owners and decision-makers. Below follows an outline of recommendations for investigating resilience in the arctic.

+   Setting Objectives for the Topic of Resilience. The central aim of any possible pathways would be to develop pragmatic, usable solutions to improve proactive and reactive system resilience has a strong foundation in academic and scientific research. To meet this objective, fundamental breakthroughs should be made with both theoretical and tool-implementation/practical challenges. Some objectives may include:

+   Empirical Objectives. Identify which sensors and KPIs are best suited for analytics in the arctic landscape, and how to deploy them to create useful empirical evidence for decision making. It will be necessary to collect and evaluate insight about how real-world systems of different types operate in a heterogeneous space, how they both dissimilar and similar in their resilience needs, and identify critical factors that affect performance, including usability and functional relationships.

+   Theoretical Objectives. Establish a sound, universally-accepted theory of resilience (from the empirical). We need to establish a sound theoretical foundation for the development of methods to support resilience research. This can be achieved by demonstrating how lack of resilience affect real-world systems, and how new methods can effectively measure, assess, improve and communicate resilience. A sound theory can facilitate informed decision-making, and in the long term, help best-practices. Any such approach ought to be data-driven from the ground up.

+   Methodological Objectives. Establish the means to test hypotheses and identify potential risks (from the theory). We need to develop a suite of simulation, red team exercises, threat modelling, analytics tools and policy creations based on sound theory of order for decision makers to try new ideas and simulate concerns to identify whether any hypothesis has any reason to cause genuine concern.
