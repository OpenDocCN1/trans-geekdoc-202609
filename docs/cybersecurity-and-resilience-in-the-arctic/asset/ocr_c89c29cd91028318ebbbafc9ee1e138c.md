The land and beach areas of submarine optical cables are the easiest for attackers to penetrate. When using large capacity systems in the undersea environment, and new types of modulation technology in those systems, the possible cable tapping points for cyber-attacks are after every optical amplifier in the deep underwater area. This offers many opportunities to obtain large amount of information from different companies, organizations, and governments. In this situation cyber-attacks and hackers can obtain IP addresses from these companies, organizations, and governments, and either make DDoS (Distributed Denial of Service) attacks against them or use different types of ransomware or malware attacks. Ransomware attacks are typically carried out by a Trojan, entering a system, for example, a vulnerability in a network service. One possible cyber-attack model is of an Advanced Persistent Threat (APT), which is a targeted cyber-attack in which an intruder gains access to a network and remains undetected for a long time. APT attacks typically target organizations such as national defense, manufacturing, and the financial industry, as well as companies that deal with high-value information, military plans, and other data from government and enterprise organizations.
