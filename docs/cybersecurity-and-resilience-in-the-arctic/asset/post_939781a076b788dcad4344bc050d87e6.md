To understand and develop resilient counter-measures to imminent threats, rigorous interdisciplinary approaches borrow from information and communication sciences, sociology, psychological science, anthropology and crisis management studies will also need to be employed.[60] Dynamic interaction and complementarity of offline and online environments as well as time dimensions of online tools and spaces are used in short, medium and long-term perspectives will need to be considered.

This paper aims to contribute to the exploration of the new normal where the offline-online spatial intersects offer many opportunities for making collective responses to imminent threats more efficient and effective. Organic responses with signs of resilience to emerging threats, as outlined in this paper may develop anti-bodies that protect itself, organic reactions can be developed for adaptive stages. With increased emphasis on addressing cyber security risks, future policies need to address the latter's social ramifications and measures for enhanced digital social resilience. To this effect, communication via conventional websites is no longer sufficient in times of crisis. Resilience to complex hybrid threats will require agile, and in many cases, new types. When effectively utilized, social media networks such as Facebook, Twitter, YouTube; real-time data analytics and crowdsourcing platforms provide many opportunities for speed, immediacy and improved targeting in communication. As we navigate the rapid innovation era where decentralized ledgers, big data and artificial intelligence will strengthen the social environment – investing into deeper understanding of these phenomena and their implications for social resilience will be progressively essential.
