# 2.3.2.1. Developments of Russian Federation Defense Capabilities in the Arctic

In 2003, Russian polar explorer and politician Artur Chilarov proclaimed, “This is our Arctic, the Russian Arctic, and the Russian flag should be here” [132]. He was further than hundred miles from the North Pole. Four years later, Chilarov was part of a Russian expedition that planted a flag on the seabed at the geographic Pole [136]. The event was televised to the Russian public. Said Chilarov, “The High North is Russian. We will not give it to anybody!” [136]. Then head of combat training for the Russian General Forces, Vladimir Shamanov, stated, “after the reaction to Russia’s territorial claims in the Arctic, my division immediately started planning the training for troops that could be engaged in Arctic combat missions” [136], [137].

Moscow has steadily increasing its ability to make good on Chilarov’s promise. In 2007, Putin instituted regular air patrols over the Arctic Ocean, including strategic bombers (Tu-95, Tu-160) and long-range anti-submarine cruise aircraft (Tu-143); Russian planes would go on into the North American Aerospace Defense Command (NORAD) airspace times that year [136].

In 2008, the Russian Security Council adopted “The Fundamentals of State Policy in the Arctic and Beyond (“Policy in the Arctic to 2020),” a document which lays forth several elements of Moscow’s strategy which would worry NATO. The desires are to: 1) keep the NSR under Russian jurisdiction and to use “an advanced operational regime in the Arctic zone of the Russian groups, as well as “maintaining the capacity of Russian Armed Forces, as of other military, military formations and in the region 3 utilize mass media to highlight answers to “connected with the national interests of the Russian Federation in the Arctic... with the goal of forming a positive image of Russia 4 transform the Russian Arctic into the “leading strategic resource base of the Russian Federation” 5 “maintain the role of a leading Arctic power” and 6 “realize a complex build of the competitive advantages of the Arctic zone of the Russian Federation with the aim of strengthening the Russian position in the Arctic” [138].
