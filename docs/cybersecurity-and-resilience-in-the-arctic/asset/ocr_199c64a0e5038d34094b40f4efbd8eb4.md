# 2.1.3. A Geopolitical Assessment

The Arctic becomes a symbol for climate and environmental change, but it also brings strong relationships among nations, raises questions about sovereignty and jurisdiction, and faces resource expansion. The north is a rapidly changing region, with the potential to have enormous economic value limited only to the eight Arctic states but also the rest of the world. The Arctic has created a new challenge for governments and policy makers; never before such conditions existed.

Combined with the lack of oversight, legal frameworks and global risks, the Arctic stands out as an area where precious access and rising oil, gas and mineral prices will spark twenty-first-century gold rushes, challenging the political will and governance of governments who, for decades, have largely ignored the Arctic. Trial and error what states are forced to turn to, yet error could lead to catastrophic damage affecting an extremely endangered and fragile environment, as well as the rest of the world who may have feel impacts such as rising seas and changing climate. The geopolitical situation in the Arctic is a rare one, but advancements have been made.
