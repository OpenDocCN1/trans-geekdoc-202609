# 2.2. The Economic Environment

Shrinking sea ice has made the Arctic more accessible for economic exploitation.[35] The Arctic is estimated to hold over 20 percent of the world's undiscovered oil and gas reserves, amounting to 13% of the world's undiscovered oil and 30% of natural gas (see Figure 7).[36], [37] Offshore oil and gas resources are more easily available, especially on the continental shelves of Arctic coastal states (i.e., Canada, United States, Russia, Norway, Denmark).[14] The majority of these resources are located on Russian territory (see Figure 8). However, not all of these resources are economically viable. For example, Canada has oil sands in Alberta that are untapped due to poor infrastructure, its remote location, and the poor quality of the petroleum.[38] Untapped fishing grounds that are not included in territorial exclusive economic zones (EEZ) are opening up. New shipping routes (northeastern and northwestern Arctic routes) are readily available for both trade and tourism (see Figure 9).
