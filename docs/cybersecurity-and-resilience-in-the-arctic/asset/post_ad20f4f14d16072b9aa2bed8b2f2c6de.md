Future infrastructure and information communication systems need to be designed and adapt to this challenging environment. A large amount of communication capacity and new contact points will be needed in the future in order to satisfy the data transfer needs of users, businesses organizations, and governments in these areas. The regional operational of these northern areas require reliable communication connections as those provided by submarine optic communications systems and satellite systems. These new connections provide people in the area with real-time access to digital services in their home country as well as the ability to communicate with friends locally or elsewhere in the world.

One of the good options would be satellite communications, which would allow for the flexibility of services required in the Arctic. However, there are some challenges to implementing satellite communication in the Arctic region. One of the challenges is to cover the Polar area above 75°N and the area between 66°N and 75°N. These areas are outside the geographical coverage area of the Arctic. These areas are further divided into a European part, a Russian part, and a North American part. When developing future infrastructure and information communication systems in the Arctic regions there is also a need to go through use cases from which we would define the requirements for infrastructure, services, and information and communication. Use cases from oil and gas companies and mining companies are also needed in the development of Arctic regions. From all of these cases we can define the requirements for the system, and from them the dependencies, and the dependencies we can define risks. When we have the use cases, we can define the different types of architectures required for different services. These architectures include: an Arctic with infrastructure architectures, integrations architectures, security and cybersecurity architectures, and also smart device architectures. Once we have determined the defined risks to these systems, we can conduct risk analyses. At the same time, we must try to identify the vulnerabilities of our current systems and services so that we can define threats and cyber threats and threat analysis from them.

In the development of future infrastructure in the Arctic region, care must be taken to follow EU directives and guidelines as well as international agreements and laws. This information shows designers and developers the right directions for developments.
