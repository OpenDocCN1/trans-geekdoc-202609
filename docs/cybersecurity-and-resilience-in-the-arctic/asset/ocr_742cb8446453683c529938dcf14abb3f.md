+   2. The “Supply Chain Network Representation” characterizes the type of graphical model used in the papers. The Supply Chain Network Representation describes the steps of acquisition, transformation, and distribution that materials take to support organizational goals. The classifiers are “Linear,” “Branching,” “Graph,” and “Other.” Linear supply chains are the simplest model, where the choice of where to move goods is entirely constrained as one node exists for each stage of production. A branching supply chain is more complex, with multiple options for each stage of production available. Branching supply chains, however, constrained directionally, as stages of production must be strictly unidirectional. A graph supply chain is the most complex of the three general models and describes any supply chain that allows outputs of later stages of production to be used as inputs of earlier stages of production. Examples of these three types of supply chains can be seen in the other models used in the first three categories to be classified separately, but not common enough to get their own category. An example would be making use of economic input-output (EIO) models. While EIO models are mathematical, their conceptual underpinnings are quite different from those that attempt to model a physical description of supply chains.

+   3. The “Transportation Network Representation” describes how materials move among nodes in the Supply Chain Network. Any supply chain not existing in one location will be dependent upon a transportation network and how this dependency is handled is important. The possible values are “None,” “Same as SC,” “Independent Links,” “Graph,” and “Other.” A value of “None” means that the model ignores the possibility of disruptions while goods are moving among supply chain nodes. A value of “Same as SC” means that the model allows for disruption of goods while moving among supply chain nodes, but has no model of re-routing might occur to mitigate disruptions. A value of “Independent Links” means that routing decisions between individual supply chain nodes are modeled, but the routes between each supply chain node are entirely independent from one another. A value of “Other” means that the transportation network is modeled differently enough from the other models used in the first four categories to be classified separately, but not common enough to get its own category.
