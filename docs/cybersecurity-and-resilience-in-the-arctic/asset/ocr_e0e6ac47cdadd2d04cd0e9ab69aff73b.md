Moreover, reflexivity is central to collective processes of communication and meaning-making. "Forethoughtful, as well as generative and reflective capabilities are therefore vital for survival and human progress" (Bandura, 2001, p. 3). Recurring individual reflexive behaviors, learned to personal adaptation along with subsequent effortless communication with others helps nurture collective agency, thereby enhancing community resilience (Otsuki, et al., 2018, p.156).

Historically, within indigenous societies, reflections were spread amongst and villages by word-of-mouth, the results of which were not immediate. Over time, the person-to-person acquisition of information has been transformed—to an exceptional degree today, resulted in the disintegration of digital communication and rapid technological advancements (Otsuki, et al., 2018). An evolving hegemonic culture change, impacted egalitarian learning for transformative social change, resulted in power relations between diverse regions and communities, and dominated discourse and interactions (Aubert & Soler, 2007). Indigenous people struggled to "define and assert their own representations" (Sefai, De & Doyle-Wood, 2007, p. 658) of history and culture relation to the mental constructs, the ways in which they make sense of their world, based on values and beliefs spawned by their experiential learning. Today, there is a growing trend to acknowledge the interplay between cultures and communities as a pedagogical framework which has potential to increase our understanding of the world through diverse forms of information-gathering, bringing benefits to decision-making processes in the face of challenges (Sefai, et al., 2007).

As SESs are met with external threats in the current climate of swift regime change and information-transfer, it is imperative that "re-construction of the stories of the people who have taken adaptive actions and reflected on them," are appreciated (Kapaffier, 2006).
