# Cyber Security Challenges to Arctic Critical Infrastructures

Heimir THORISSON^a, Fabrizio BAIARDI^b, Mirva SALMINEN^c, Rozelien Van DERDEGHEM^d, Rishikesh SAHAY^e, Bo PAQUIN^f, Charlee HEATH^g, Inna SKARGA-BANDUROVA^h, Mathieu BRANLAT^a, Aarne HUMMELHOLM^i, James. H. LAMBERT^c, Igor INKOV^l, Benjamin D. TRUMP

*a* University of Virginia, *b* University of Pisa, *c* University of Lapland, *d* MAN Energy Solutions, *e* Global Affairs Canada/Canadian International Arctic Centre, Oslo, *f* University of Ottawa, *g* Sintef, *h* University of Yväskälä, *i* US Army Corps of Engineers

Abstract. The Arctic regions have seen increase in human activity not seen before in modern times. Reducing ice and climate change have contributed to the opening of new sea routes, opportunities in international shipping and tourism. Increased accessibility has enabled the extraction of natural gas and oil, and metals. The cold climate provides natural cooling for data centers and other computational facilities. Economic activities are coupled with the expansion of military and civilian infrastructure, including for telecommunications, scientific installations, ports, and other internal transport facilities. Information technology promotes efficiency and technologies such as fiber optic cables, satellite communications, roads, and others enables access to these infrastructures from locations outside the Arctic. However, the reliance on information and communication technology and the connectedness of most critical infrastructure (communications, financial information and governmental systems, etc.) result in new vulnerabilities exposed by natural disasters or environmental accidents and adversarial agents that can exploit. Cyber security and resilience play a central role in ensuring the safety and security of communities in this age of interconnectedness and digital world. Due to their remote and extreme conditions, Arctic regions face unique challenges of cyber security and critical infrastructure. This chapter summarizes discussions and lessons learned from working a NATO Advanced Research Group for Cyber Security and Resilience in the Arctic as it pertains to critical infrastructure, held in Romania, Finland on 27-30 January 2019. It aims to provide perspectives on cyber security in the context of Arctic infrastructure from multiple disciplines, including engineering and computer science, natural sciences, law and governance. Each perspective identifies challenges and opportunities in cyber security and resilience, in particular ones characteristic to Arctic regions. This includes documenting available theory and methods, including analogous methods from other fields, describing data availabilities and needs. Lessons derived from past and ongoing scenarios incidents and methods for forecasting emerging and future scenarios are reviewed. For research and practice to increase the cyber security and resilience of infrastructure are provided.

**Keywords.** Cyber security, infrastructure, resilience.
