In order the Arctic region as a whole to function and to be able to provide citizens with digital services, the information systems in the various service sectors need to be able to work together and to exchange information with each other so that the services of the region can be implemented flexibly and efficiently. The information systems used by different service sectors or smaller entities within them are, however, in different phases of their life cycles.

We must also address the geopolitical issues in the Arctic region. Geopolitics is the study of the effects of geography on politics and international relations. At the level of international relations, geopolitics is a method of finding policy to understand, explain, and predict international behavior through geographical variables. These variables include the Arctic region studies, climate, topography, demographic, and natural resources. The international competition over oil and gas resources has been one of the main aspects of geopolitics since the 1950s. There is also increased military activity in the Arctic, in this chapter, we are not looking at the Arctic from a military perspective.

When dealing with technology and segments in the Arctic, we divide the geographical area into three parts: the mainland, the sea, and coastal areas. On the continent, we are dealing with smart cities and the connections between them. In coastal and marine areas, we are dealing with submarine optic cable systems, coastal landing stations, and Data Centers.
