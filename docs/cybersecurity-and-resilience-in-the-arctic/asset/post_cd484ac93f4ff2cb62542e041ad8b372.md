## 2. Background

The Arctic region consists of the area of the Arctic Ocean and surrounding lands. Eight countries have sovereign land within the Arctic Circle and membership in the Arctic Council, an organization promoting cooperation and interactions in the regions and addressing issues of its populations: Canada, Denmark (through Greenland), Iceland, Norway, Sweden, Finland, Russia, and the United States. The Arctic has been a home to people for years. Yet, because of harsh weather conditions and other environmental challenges, it is one of the sparsely inhabited areas of the world. It is estimated that approximately four million people live in the Arctic; the proportion of Indigenous peoples is estimated to be ten percent (Arctic Centre 2019). The unique climate, sparse population, and distances between population centers in the Arctic create challenges specific to the region as well as highlighting global challenges related to cyber security of critical infrastructure (Trump et al., 2018).

Cyber security is the convergence of "security" and "cyber." Rothschild states that security has meant different things in contexts, continually changing over time (1995). Definitions of security are abundant in the literature. Security includes the protection of people from injury, harm, death and the protection of objects and property from damage and destruction by adversarial agents (many, Including Kitou 2014). Security has also been viewed as a quality defined by absence, whether objectively the absence of threats to some values or subjectively the absence of fear on those values (Bourne 2014). It can refer to the desired end state and the means used to strive toward that end state (Wolfers 1952; Baldwin 1997). Baldwin (1997) states that security in its broadest sense be conceptualized by two questions: “Security for whom?” and “Security for which values?” The prefix “cyber-” in modern terms understood today the use of computers is still linked to the systemic thinking which is present in the traditional meaning of cybernetics as the theory of communication and control in living things and machines (Dann Cavnett 2007). In the context of this chapter, we view cyber security in a broad sense, information security, network security, cloud security, and others.
