**Abstract.** Ensuring a reliable and efficient supply chain is important to an organization's mission. Disruptions to the supply chain can harm organizational performance, and improving resilience can minimize such harm. This paper conducts a bibliometric and thematic review of supply chain resilience focused on modeling; supply chains where they operate. Resilience is defined based on system evolution (plan, absorb, adapt) in response to changes, effects on components of the supply chain sub-systems (production, transportation, command and control). The results show that most papers investigated either all phases of resilience for incomplete supply chains or components of resilience for whole chains, but not both. This paper argues that improving supply chain resilience requires looking more than a set of locations and spots in production, but also requires a system representation of the multiple networks and decision tools that determine how goods travels between locations.

# ## 1. Introduction

As globalization increases, the performance of supply chains becomes more critical to businesses, organizations, and communication routes opening in the Arctic and sub-Arctic further. New trade catalyze globalization, allowing organizations to increase the distance between suppliers and the number of suppliers for the goods and services they require to function. This turns up the supply chain to additional vulnerabilities. Within Arctic latitudes, such disruptions can include, among others, environmental and climatological disruption, cyberattack, and infrastructure strain and collapse (Verny 2015; Milaković 2014), the use of Arctic and sub-Arctic adds complexity to the existing surrounding supply chain management and development, forcing planners and decision makers to proactively address threats and vulnerabilities to their systems.

A risk-based approach to protecting supply chains is no longer effective or effective. It is commonly understood that a resilience-based approach is necessary to ensure supply chain management best practices. Unfortunately, this report shows that there are significant gaps in the literature regarding supply chain resilience, mostly with respect to a failure to apply a systems-based approach to quantifying, analyzing, and improving supply-chain resilience.
