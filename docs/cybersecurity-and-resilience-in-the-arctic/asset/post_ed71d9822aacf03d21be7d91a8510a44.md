# Analytics for Decision Support in the Arctic

Jassim Happa Royal Holloway, University of London

**Abstract.** Threats such as attackers, accidents, wear and tear, and natural forces make analytics for resilience a challenging task. The Arctic region presents distinct resilience challenges compared to other regions as well as their purpose is to provide a discussion on analytics and decision-making for resilience. Specifically, this paper outlines a number of academic works for situational awareness and decision-making. I discuss how these works might be applied in the arctic, as well as the challenges in doing so.

**Keywords.** Resilience, Cybersecurity, Analytics, Threat Detection, Decision Support

# 1. Introduction

Two key aims of analytics tools and techniques is to improve situational awareness and decision-making. It is difficult to predict, detect and discern real-world consequences of threats. There are many reasons for this, some include: we may not have enough data to assess a situation; we may not have the most relevant data available; we may not have the capabilities or capacities to assess the data; or we may not trust the data we have (it may be out of date, from an untrusted source or it may be noisy data). Well-informed decision making can make systems more resilient and robust. Improving resilience in systems is not only about improving cybersecurity it also concerns how systems relate to the natural world, technical, social and societal factors. Resilience is here defined as a system's ability to deliver its intended output at expected times while being exposed to threats.
