# 6.3. Canadian Arctic Policy

Canadian Arctic Strategy and Policy detailed by “Our North, Our Heritage, Our Future” released in 2009 [47] and a follow up “Statement on Canada’s Arctic Foreign Policy” [48] can be viewed as a single entity and analysed as such.

As much of Canadian Arctic policy denotes not international policy and strategy but also the goals being sought deomestically the government’s domestically. The main concern for the Canadian government is establishing and exercising sovereignty. Along the mainland, Canada also lays claim to a large extent of continental shelf and the waters between the islands of the Archipelago. The status of this water is contested most notably by the United States, who consider these waters to be international. Moreover, Canada also shared a territorial dispute over Hans Island with the Kingdom of Denmark, as both claim the island as being in part within their jurisdictions.

In the Arctic, nations-states often resort to contesting sovereignty through performances of effective occupation in a overt manner than they would otherwise do with their territorial regions. Wood-Dolly in their 2018 book Performing Arctic Sovereignty,"Imagine, if the Canadian Prime Minister was making similar statements regarding establishing sovereignty over Ottawa’s province of Ontario, as it does over Arctic Islands! It’s clear that sovereignty over Arctic territories and waters is an incredibly important priority for Canada, despite much of these disputes taking the form of words rather than actions.

The Strategy identifies four areas of priority, of which the statement expands out and fully promotes:

+   - "Exercising Arctic sovereignty"; achieving this through good governance and responsible stewardship.
- "Promoting social and economic development"; by taking steps to facilitate other states in implementing sustainable development, seeking economic prospects that benefit all Canadians, and encourage a better understanding of the human component within the Arctic.
- "Protecting our environmental heritage"; by promoting an ecosystem-based management approach amongst actors in the Arctic.
