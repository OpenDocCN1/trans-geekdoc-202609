# 3.2.1. Role of International Relations in the Arctic

The vulnerabilities affecting Arctic societies are multi-jurisdictional in nature and are not contained within the confines of national borders. Thus, an effective approach to addressing these vulnerabilities will be multi-jurisdictional as well. International cooperation to address vulnerabilities in the Arctic from all governance levels and must provide space for interactions between scientists and policy-makers (Smieszek, Stepien & Kankaanpa, 2016). The national Arctic strategies of the eight Arctic states, as well as the Arctic Council Ministerial Declarations, have all recognized the indispensable role of international cooperation in addressing Arctic challenges (Arctic Council, 2016).

The Arctic is a low-conflict region characterized by international cooperation (Hossain et al. 2017). Cooperation in the Arctic occurs at the community, local, subnational, Indigenous and national level, as well as by Arctic-specific platforms and other international bodies, at the bilateral, regional, and international levels. The result is a complex framework of overlapping and linked stakeholders and processes that make up Arctic governance. While actors and stakeholders involved in Arctic governance may have varying Arctic, international, or domestic interests, they all play a role in building societal resilience in the Arctic (Arctic Council, 2016). Thus "the resilience of local communities is a function of decisions made at many levels" (Arctic Council, 2016).

International relations and diplomacy are tools that allow states to align incentives and allow for meaningful cooperation towards shared priorities, for example, safety and security, environmental protection, and supporting Arctic communities (Trump et al. 2016). These tools are useful for fostering stability in the region because they promote trust and confidence-building between stakeholders. Where there may be tension in the context of the general bilateral relationship between states, cooperation on shared Arctic interests is a channel for maintaining an open dialogue and constructive cooperation. Mr. Pertti Salolainen, Member of Parliament of the Republic of Finland, highlights how the Arctic Council has this to effect, "as an example of Arctic resilience: in spite of the generally negative trend in triste relations, the Arctic Council has managed to strengthen regional stability and even expand the area of constructive co-operation" (House of Commons, 2018). Thus, governance organizations can play a role in enhancing or eroding societal resilience in the Arctic (Arctic Council, 2016).

International cooperation based on trust and confidence is essential for Arctic governance organizations to be effective at strengthening societal resilience. To facilitate international cooperation, specifically with regard to cyber security and protecting critical infrastructure, stakeholders in the Arctic require a "coherent and cohesive framework governance prevention and response to threats" (Cassotta & Sidortsov, 2019). Through this legal framework, governance organizations play a role in improving the resilience capacity of systems in the Arctic before, during, and after a shock or stress affects the system (Trump et al. 2016). Governance organizations must engage with each other on an international level to negotiate and adopt legal frameworks through which they can promote societal resilience, anticipate various shocks and stresses, and take appropriate steps to fund systems that build societal resilience (Trump et al. 2016).
