In an era of disinformation, a guide of calls for restorative ethics online is also netiquette. Netiquette refers to a rather contemporary practice calling netizens to restore ethical conduct and standards in their online behavior. This different effect netiquette manifests clientele online providing ICT and Internet users a code and tips for ethical behavior under precepts as ‘Remember the Human; Be Ethical; know what you are in cyberspace; Adhere to the standards of behavior online that you play in real life, Respect other people' time and bandwidth, share expert knowledge, keep waves under control, respect other peoples' privacy, don't abuse your power, be forgiving of others' mistakes’ [66] Debunking and fact-checking practices in combatting recent misinformation abuses are hence another illustrative practice initiated by online communities with the aim to guide users' ethical conduct and restorative morality in the digital public space.

# Conclusion

Much study has been devoted to the study and understanding of social resilience in the offline context, but less so in the online environment. With the pervasive use of ICT and the emergence of new hybrid threats, this is an oversight. Using an interdisciplinary literature review and examples from practice this paper examined how online communities respond and cope with emerging threats in contemporary times. Three distinct behavioral patterns were identified among collective responses: 1) self-organization, crowd-sourcing, mobilization and digital toolkit, ii effort solidarity quests, and iii restorative of morality. The paper argued that the observed online patterns generate positive social capital characteristic of digital resilience. As rapid innovation, pervasive use of ICT and hybrid forms of threats are likely to remain in understanding and useful takeaways from these resilient practices is essential for future policy making and for our preparedness to mitigate against future threats.

Yet, as the new media provides tools and spaces for facilitating communities' efforts to self-organize and crowdsource restorative social capital, online spaces are equally prone to harmful practices. Cautionary caves in the use of new technologies for enhance social capital need to be considered and further researched. Malicious hacking, use of subversive bots and rampant misinformation or hate speech can compromise restorative social capital by inciting distrust, cognitive bias and socio-political polarization. This exploitation of subversive vulnerabilities can be particularly damaging targeting fragile populations or in countries with social media but low levels of digital literacy. Sustainability of pertinent ICT usage and slackivism is another concern. [67]
