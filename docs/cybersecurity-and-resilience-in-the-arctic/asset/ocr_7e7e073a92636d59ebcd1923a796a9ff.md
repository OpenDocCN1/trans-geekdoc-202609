# 1.2.1. Transit Traffic

The purpose of transit traffic is for ships to navigate the Arctic for the sole effort of linking Southern markets with one another. Increases in operations costs by the complexities of Arctic routes porte the potential to offset the advantages that would otherwise be gained from the raw savings in distance. Bulk shipping – that of ores, grains, and petroleum products loaded into cargo holds and tanks – is based on the demand of a specific amount from point A to point B. These shipments are managed as on-off operations, making them more suitable for seasonal shipments [16]. Liner shipping, in contrast, would likely be impacted by the scale of changes in the Arctic, alongside uncertainty transit times, disrupting just-in-time supply-chains which might require greater degrees of reliability.

# 1.2.2. Resources-Based Traffic

Resources-Based traffic is traffic bound specifically in this context for the Arctic in order to load or unload cargo and from the region. The Arctic contains vast of mineral resources, including lead and nickel. Both lead and zinc have been shipped every summer from the mid-70s to the 2000s, nickel has been shipped year-round [17]. For these shipments, no alternative routing is available; the cargo must be shipped from Arctic locations to demanding it across the rest of the world. Hence, large technical requirements are needed to decide what routes are most viable. The rapidly changing climate and environment of the Arctic are having a direct impact on costs; ice-free summer windows have allowed for non-ice-strengthened ships to be used, requiring no ice-breaker fleets either. Furthermore, the number of resources that can be shipped from the vast expanses in the Arctic are currently limited by the number of ships that can traverse through Arctic conditions; changing conditions are likely to only increase the number of ships that can be used to transport these resources but also increase the length of the shipping season, allowing for more profitability.

# 1.2.3. Re-supply traffic

The need for re-supply traffic is derived from that of local communities. Food and lighter goods can be brought by aircraft, but building materials, vehicles and heavier equipment are only be transported via ships. This traffic is also mitigated by the availability of local ports, and furthermore the overall demand for these resources, which will likely be shaped more by the socio-economic development of Arctic communities under changing climatic conditions.

# 1.3. Energy – Oil and Gas

A key question surrounding the Arctic and its large resource stores is whether development in the Arctic will be driven by oil and gas production. By understanding the combined effect of environmental and geological variables (those below ground) and the political and political/economic variables (those above ground), a more nuanced picture of the oil and gas development in the Arctic can be created allowing the environment to be better understood.
