From the 20th century, immigrants to the Arctic region because there were job opportunities attracted them. For most part they did work that relates to the natural resources in the region one way or another. This large influx of immigrants has dramatically changed the balance of non-indigenous and indigenous peoples in many areas.

This migration has also affected the infrastructure, towns, and villages, throughout the whole of the Arctic region, including the available services and the entire ecosystem. There are already companies in the region that take advantage of oil and gas resources. The Arctic region contains a wide range of landscapes including plains, mountains, some large rivers and lakes, rolling hills, huge spans of tundra and the largest biome in the Taiga. The ice at Arctic sea is largely formed from the frozen sea. It contains a part of the world's multi-year sea ice, which is 3-4 m thick on average with even thicker ridges. Greenland holds the largest glacier in the Arctic. When a part of the Greenland breaks off, it forms an iceberg from the frozen-glacier. Most of the iceberg is located underwater, but it is not quite as dense as the water, about one-nin of it remains above the surface.
