# A. Hummelholm / Threat Characterization for Various Layers of Infrastructure

In order for the Arctic regions as a whole to function properly and to be able to provide citizens with the necessary digital services, the information systems in the various service sectors to be able to work together and be able to exchange information with each other not just horizontally but also vertically so that the services of the Arctic regions can be implemented flexibly and efficiently. The information systems used by different sectors or the smaller entities within them, are often in different phases of their life cycle. In the future Arctic region can be seen as one entity, in which cooperative efforts between different countries and user groups should be made due to a difficult and sometimes critical living environment. This means that everyone should be able to access the same services and be able to use them in a critical situation or to obtain information in some way from other countries in order to obtain assistance in certain cases. This means that we need both horizontal and vertical communications opportunities between different user groups. This also means that we must perform cyber analyses both horizontally and vertically on those infrastructures, communications systems, and services. Consequently, the integration between information systems may not succeed for many technical reasons. Data models, operating systems, management systems, and application interfaces may not work well enough. Their coordination and the success of the data exchange through integration environments are very challenging. The system solutions in place can also be delivered, by different vendors and may have their own facto standards that are incompatible with the equipment and systems from other vendors. This may result in the use of certain of platform solutions to guarantee the functionality of some particular system until renewal is worthwhile or until it becomes timely with the end of its lifecycle. The security solutions for systems and services may also be lead to practical challenges; for example, it may be impossible to combine the various systems (make federations) and consequently to make different services compatible. Each entity may also contain information at different security levels, which must be properly protected and controlled. As a result of the ongoing developments in the Arctic region, people and systems will produce huge amounts of information in the future that need to be processed and stored. However, not all of the technical solutions of the new service environments may be in accordance with international standards, such as interfaces with telecommunication and service networks. At the same time, services based on technically outdated solutions and services using new technology are also in use. Future information and communication systems must be designed and adapted to work in this challenging business environment where security threats and cyber threats are present.
