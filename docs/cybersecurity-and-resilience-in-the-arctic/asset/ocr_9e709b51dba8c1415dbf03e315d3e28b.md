# 3.2. Organizational Challenges

Cyber resilience with regard to critical infrastructure in the Arctic region must be built
a a foundation of resilient Arctic societies. Policies, established
throughgovernmental efforts, are key drivers of strengthening societal resilience
in the Arctic. Societal resilience is defined here as "the ability of individuals and
communities to adapt, reorganize and improve in the midst of shocks and
stresses" (Trump, et al. 2016). Examining cyber resilience in the Arctic through the\lens of societal resilience is useful to understand the level of international governance
relations in addressing cyber security concerns and building cyber resilience.

Societal resilience must be considered in terms of the capacity of a society to
respond to vulnerabilities. Societal vulnerabilities stem from inequalities in access to
resources and information and political marginalization within and between
communities (Cutter et al. 2008). These vulnerabilities account for differences in
preparedness, impact, and response to shocks and stresses across a society (Cutter et al.
2008). Arctic resilience is threatened by the compounding impacts of a number
ofulnerabilities, including climate change, vast changes and a sparse
population, cold pollutants and contaminants, increases in resource
development and tourism, environmental degradation, rapid economic development,
industrialization, integration into global markets, erosion of cultural traditions, limited
pitical participation of local and Indigenous communities, disputes over
autonomous, conflicts over land use, and access to digital connectivity and
broadband (Salimen & Hossain, 2018 and Hossain, Joer, Grever, Roncero and
Sheenan, 2017). The Arctic is comprised of complex socio-economic, ecological,
and infrastructure systems. Arctic systems are especially intertwined; therefore, threats to one
system have a profound effect on the security of another. For example, environmental and
health\u201ntreats to the socio-ecological systems in the Arctic caused by oil spills affect
critical infrastructure and cyber systems as well. Thus, a holistic, societal resilience
approach to Arctic security is useful to understand how building resilient Arctic
societies strengthens cyber resilience.
