All definitions are roughly overlapping, although with some gaps for particular stages. For example, ERS and Neches (2011) do not specifically mention an absorb stage [154], [155], while Alexeev et al. (2017) fails to mention adaptation as important [153]. It is possible to map all the definitions to each other, by rearranging the order of the stages (see Table 1). By doing this, we can define the broadest or most extensive definition of resilience for military systems

# Table 1. Various Definitions of Resilience in the Military Context

Combining the definitions and descriptions of stages of resilience as given in the literature results in a broad five-stage definition of resilience in the military context:

+   1. **Broad Utility: Prepare** to be able to effectively perform even during disruptions
2. **Absorb** the disruption, maintaining as much functionality as possible while repelling it
3. **Recover** to pre-disruption levels of functionality
