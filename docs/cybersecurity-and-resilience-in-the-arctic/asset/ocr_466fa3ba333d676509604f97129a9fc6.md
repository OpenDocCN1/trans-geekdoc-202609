# Introduction

The seasonal melting of sea ice within the Arctic is a widely reported and well-documented occurrence, becoming a flashpoint for discussions speculating the renewal of a ‘cold war’ for the control of the Arctic’s extensive natural resources and implications of emerging sea routes. Activity in the Arctic is on the rise, with private enterprises seeking to exploit shipping routes and invest in infrastructure projects, such as oil and gas exploitation, telecommunications development (including the laying of subsea cables), and fishing ever now. State activity is also on the rise, with the expansion of military and defense capabilities in what has previously been a relatively uncontested and strategically unimportant region. This section aims to outline information about each of these ventures and give a brief overview of some of the issues and challenges that each of them face.

# 1. The Need for Resilience

The growing diversity of human in the Arctic and sub-Arctic is predicated upon complex interconnected systems. On a micro level, this could include the integration of heating, telecommunications, water/plumbing, and power for a given facility or building. At a macro level, this includes complex transport and supply chains, that if interrupted or broken, are difficult to repair or replace in an expensive manner. Disruption to these systems can lead to considerable harm to humans, animals, and the environment, making it essential to develop capabilities to minimize disruptions and risk management.
