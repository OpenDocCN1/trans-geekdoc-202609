# 3.2. Cyber-resilience

Resilience as a descriptor in a plethora of fields. Colloquially, resilience has multiple meanings from elasticity to toughness. Resilience is both a philosophy and a methodological practice. The National Academy of Sciences defines resilience as the ability to prepare for, absorb, recover from, and adapt to events. While modern applications of resilience focus on analyzing how systems bounce back from disruptions.The emphasis is on the role of recovery after a disruption to a system, as well as absorption of a threat to ensure survival.Not all threats are preventable. Most are mitigable. Despite comprehensive preparation and planning, it is impossible to prevent all threats. An unexpected disruption will always occur. Therefore, resilience is a way to handle systems that are unknown or have uncertainties. Resilience can complement traditional risk-based approaches. Methodologically, it can assess how a system performs and functions in various scenarios regardless of type of threat.[97] Resilience analysis is built upon traditional risk assessment but minimizes the long-term damage of external shocks to system efficiency and functionality.[97] Moreover, resilience allows actors to manage system, cascading effects where one disruption can trigger connected systems, as well as, probability threats with catastrophic consequences.[97, 159] Resilience tries to provide the foundation for a ‘soft landing’ in order to reduce both harshly and quickly efficiently improve system rebound to full functionality.[97] Thus, system resilience is an adaptive process where system functions are constantly adjusting to deal with threats.
