# 2. A framework to understand resilience

An answer to this question was posed by our team (Guerra & Sepulveda Estay, 2018) in 2018. We performed a structured literature review (SLR), a documented process to collect and store existing knowledge to answer the question of How cyber-risks are managed along the supply chain? Their work initially considered supply chains as the unit of analysis, and here the framework is to reflect its application to the shipping industry.

The twelve most recurrent themes found in the analysis (Guerra & Sepulveda Estay, 2018) when applied to shipping operations, are the following:

+   1. **Compliance**, understood as the identification of, and conformance of shipping operations, to legislation affecting cyber risks and to the standards that have been developed for cyber-risk prevention, mitigation, and response.

+   2. **Situational Awareness**, which involves the identification of cyber threats, vulnerabilities, and risks in shipping operations, and the assessment of the probability and impacts of occurrences of cyber-risk events.

+   3. **Governance**, which defines who, where, and how decisions affecting IT are made. (Ali, Mahfouz & Arisha, 2017).

+   4. **Pre-Event Knowledge Management**, understood as making the best use of the knowledge available in order to achieve resilience (response to disruptions) and the improvement of this knowledge, through practices such as training, and the creation of a resilience/risk management centre.

+   5. **Cyber-Security**, which refers to the protection of the assets and systems (physical or digital) in the storing and processing of information in digital format, and involving proactive measures and techniques, focusing on the protection of the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information (Boyes, 2015).

+   6. **Agility**, defined as "the ability to respond to unpredictable changes in demand or supply" (Petit, Croxton & Fiksel, 2013), through visibility and velocity. Visibility refers to awareness about the current status of operational assets and environment (Patnayakuni & Patnayakuni, 2014; Christopher & Peck, 2004), as to be able to detect cyber-risk events.

+   7. **Velocity** refers to the speed of the reaction when cyber-events happen (Petit et al., 2013).

+   8. **Adapt**, understood as the ability to manage critical resources and shipping operations, and to adjust them to challenges and opportunities (Patnayakuni & Patnayakuni, 2014), through flexibility and redundancy. Flexibility is the capability of doing and using resources in different ways, according to the specific situation. Redundancy is building and maintaining excess capacity as a mechanism to adapt to disruptive events.

+   9. **Recovery Management**, which considers identifying vulnerabilities and risks that are critical, developing contingency plans, for the availability of resources needed during post-disruption periods, and executing those when needed.

+   10. **Market Position and Financial Strength**, which refers to the status of an organization/or in its products market, and the capacity of the shipping business to absorb variations in cash flow (Christopher & Peck, 2004), required for recovery over operational disruptions (Patnayakuni & Patnayakuni, 2014).
