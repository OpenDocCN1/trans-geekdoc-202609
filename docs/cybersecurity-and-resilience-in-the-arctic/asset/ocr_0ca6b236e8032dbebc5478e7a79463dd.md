# # The Wave Analogy of Cyber-Resilience as Applied to Shipping Operations

Daniel SEPULVEDA ESTAY*1, Pablo GUERRA†

*Dept. of Technology Management and Economics, Management Science Division, Technical University of Denmark, DK-2800 Kgs., Lyngby, Denmark

**Abstract.** The nature of cyber is distinct in several dimensions from other risks in shipping and transport. This article explores these differences and describes the results of a research process to categorize published literature about cyber-risks in supply chain and shipping, through a framework called the Wave Analogy of Resilience. By using this analogy, this article describes a way of organizing this categorization, and describes how it can be used to understand how a shipping system failure results in a cyber-attack, and how a cyber-attack increasingly affects areas in the shipping system, from operational to strategic, until the attack is stopped.

**Keywords.** Ship systems, Resilience, Decision Frameworks

# ## 1. The new risks

The presence of IT systems in shipping operations is ubiquitous and increasingly relevant. This follows a trend as the fourth industrial revolution or Industry 4.0, which marks a radical shift in the way industry operates, by digitizing and the Internet to most parts of the value chain in shipping operations. From this perspective, ship is understood as a Cyber-Physical System (CPS) built-up from the composition of software, physical production and human operators, integrating elements like higher automation, Internet of Things (IoT), data sharing, and computing with other ships and operations over the internet (Boyes, 2015), Jayaram, 2016), (Manners-Bell, 2017).

Competitive pressures require that companies make use of advances in information technologies and adapt their supply chain in a global economy (Boyes, 2015), (Khan & Sepul Estay, 2015). As a result, companies are required to have the ability to identify and respond faster to changes in the external environment (i.e. customer preferences), through automating processes using cyber-physical systems (CPS), implementing digitally-based management systems, and reducing inventory such container positions of just-in-time-production-to-order systems (Tjahjono, Eplugues, Ares, & Palaez, 2017), (Boyes, 2015).
