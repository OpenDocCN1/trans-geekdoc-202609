# A. Hummelholm/Threat Characterization for Various Layers of Infrastructure

Energy Efficiency solutions also affect small buildings as homes, outbuildings, and summer cabins (at least in Finland), etc. The reasons for this are the long distances from electricity distribution points, the reliability of supply, and the cost. An example of a small unit suitable for Arctic regions is an energy system working with solar power, wind, and thermal energy, working together as a hybrid energy system, as seen in Figure 14. This hybrid energy system, described in Figure 14, has been tested in Finland for years now near the Arctic region. Solar panels are installed in the building, depending on whether the sunlight hits them, considering the sun's direction at different times of the day and at different times of the year in the Arctic. The situation also changes depending on the Arctic region latitude. Solar panels can be installed on all sides of the building's roof, to provide the maximum benefit throughout the day.

It is also possible to build parts of small buildings like home under the ground level, so in winter time or cold weather will not have a direct impact on the temperature inside the building. This kind of concept gives opportunities to develop more energy efficient solutions.
