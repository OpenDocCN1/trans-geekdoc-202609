# Emotional Solidarity Quests

Parallel to the search for resources, emotional venting and emotional sharing is a composite element of social coping strategies in the recovery stages of post-traumatic events. Collective emotional episodes are special in that they elicit emotional feedback loops where collective participation, enhanced social belonging, values, and prosocial attitudes correlate with higher expressions of solidarity, and indicate the level of social resilience in a community.[50] In disaster-preparedness models bonding, bridging, and linking is a primary asset within resilient communities[51] where coordinated activities, feelings of connectedness and shared identities represent key contributions to social resilience.[51]. Emotional responses and expressions of solidarity are consistent with offline studies of social behavior among disaster survivors where the provision of coordinated instrumental social support is a key mediator and predictor of collective efficacy.[55] Computer and Internet mediated communication caters to the facilitation of emotional sharing through textual and visual content – e.g. emojis, images and videos. # Conclusion

Much study has been devoted to the study and understanding of social resilience in the offline context, but less so in the online environment. With the pervasive use of ICT and the emergence of new hybrid threats, this is an oversight. Using an interdisciplinary literature review and examples from practice this paper examined how online communities respond and cope with emerging threats in contemporary times. Three distinct behavioral patterns were identified among collective responses:
1) self-organization, crowd-sourcing, mobilization and digital toolkit
2) effort solidarity quests
3) restorative of morality

The paper argued that the observed online patterns generate positive social capital characteristic of digital resilience. As rapid innovation, pervasive use of ICT and hybrid forms of threats are likely to remain in understanding and useful takeaways from these resilient practices is essential for future policy making and for our preparedness to mitigate against future threats.

Yet, as the new media provides tools and spaces for facilitating communities' efforts to self-organize and crowdsource social capital, online spaces are equally prone to harmful practices. Cautionary caves in the use of new technologies for enhance social capital need to be considered and further researched. Malicious hacking, use of subversive bots and rampant misinformation or hate speech can compromise restorative social capital by inciting distrust, cognitive bias and socio-political polarization. This exploitation of subversive vulnerabilities can be particularly damaging targeting fragile populations or in countries with social media but low levels of digital literacy. Sustainability of pertinent ICT usage and slackivism is another concern.[67].
