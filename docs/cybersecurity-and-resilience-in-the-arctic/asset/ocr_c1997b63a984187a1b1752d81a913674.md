# 6.2. Russian Arctic Policy

Russian Arctic policy is detailed in “Fundamentals of State policy of the Russian Federation in the Period up to 2020 and Beyond: adopted by President Medvedev in 2009 [45]. Here, a shortened translation of the document, which was published in the official government newspaper, Rossiyskaya Gazeta on the 30th of March in 2009, is referenced.

The strategic priorities of the State Policy of the Russian Federation in the Arctic (up to 2020 and beyond) are to:

+   1. Create a uniform Arctic search and rescue regime and prevention of man-caused accidents;
2. Ensure a uniform Arctic SAR regime in an attempt to prevent accidents caused by human error;
3. Strengthen bilateral relationships within the framework of regional organizations, such as the Arctic Council and the Barents Euro-Arctic Council;
4. Assist in the organization, management, and effective use of cross-polar air routes and the North Sea Route for international navigation;
5. Participate and contribute within Arctic forums through the Russian-European partnerships;
6. Outline regions in the Arctic Ocean, ensuring a mutually advantageous presence of Russia in the Spitsbergen archipelago;
7. Improve state management, developing the Arctic economically and socially, and increase support for scientific research and monitoring;
8. Improve the quality of life for indigenous peoples and their social and economic activities;
9. Develop the Arctic resource base using new technological advancements;
10. Modernize and develop the Arctic infrastructure, including that of transport and fisheries in the Russian Arctic

Russia’s policy objectives in the Arctic are:

+   1. Resource Development: An expansion of the resource base, in order to satisfy Russia’s needs in hydrocarbon resources, hydro-biological resources, and other types of strategically important resources;
2. Military Security; ensure the upkeep and maintenance of the required combat potential of Russia’s military groupings, maintaining favorable operative regimes in the Russian Arctic zone;
3. Environmental security: work to preserve and support the Arctic environment;
4. IT and Communication: work towards the formation of a uniform information area;
5. Science and Technology: enabling a sufficient level of scientific research and creation of scientific bases and management, as well as implementing the development of means for the solution of defense and security problems, in addition to functioning systems of life support and industrial activity under environmental and climatic conditions.
6. International Cooperation: working bilaterally and multilaterally with Arctic and sub-States on the basis of international treaties and agreements which Russia is part, ensuring mutually advantageous outcomes.
