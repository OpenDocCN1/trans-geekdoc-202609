# Technology – Public and Private services in The Arctic region

When regulating and designing laws, designers of networks and services as well as the authorities must take into account the changes affecting society as shown in Figures 9, 10, 11 and 12, as well as the massive amounts of data generated by them. Data types include "Big Data," "Open Data," "MyData," and "Private Data."

These are further associated with other developing aspects such as real-time, digitalization, environmental changes, changes in service provision, international cooperation and competition, conflicts of interest (international, regional, national, local), changes in lifestyles, increased pressure on service provision, infrastructure, urbanization, environmental pressures, political climate, interest groups, refugee problems, climate change, etc.

The challenge in this future environment is we need to be that the data of citizens are properly protected in accordance with the regulations in the registers. The registry holders have a responsibility to protect the citizens' information that is kept in their registers. They should demonstrate that the data remains protected in accordance with laws and, if necessary, revised in practice across European areas [29][30][31].

The EU-GDPR directive in brief [29]: The General Data Protection Regulation, obliges the organization to identify risks when defining technical and organizational measures to ensure the protection of personal data. Technical and organizational measures include, for example, instructions given to staff to implement data protection, self-control through access control, information system, data encryption, and other security measures. Risk assessment is a continuous activity: the adequacy of the measures in relation to the risk involved in the treatment must be continuously evaluated and updated as necessary. The controller, in this case the healthcare organization, has a duty to demonstrate a risk-based approach. The EU Data Protection introduces risk management and reporting obligations (Jenni Siermala, SoteDigi, 2019).

The EU-NIS directive in brief [30]: “Network and information security (NIS) directive is created to ensure a high level of security of network and information systems across the Union. According to the Directive, service providers for example health and care digital service providers are required to manage comprehensive network and information security risks and report to the responsible authorities any incidents that happen or threaten the continuity of operations” (Jenni Siermala, SoteDigi, 2019).

The EU-MDR directive in brief [31]: “Medical device regulation (MDR) requires using only devices defined by the regulation for treatment, diagnose and measure the patient. The responsibility of the medical device manufacturer covers the entire product lifecycle from exit to market. The manufacturer is responsible for the entire lifecycle of the medical device, the application, interfaces and security as well as unqualified standards. The device manufacturer must monitor the product on the feedback, the matter must be corrected immediately. However according to the MDR, the Manufacturers shall set minimum requirements for hardware, network features, and security measures, as required to use the software” (Jenni Siermala, SoteDigi, 2019).

The content of these Directives is reflected in the national legislation of the European Union.
