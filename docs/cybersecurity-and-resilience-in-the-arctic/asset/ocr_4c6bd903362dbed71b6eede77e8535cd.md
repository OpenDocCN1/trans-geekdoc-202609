+   9. Market Position and Financial Strength: Customers and public image were affected by this attack. Maersk estimates the costs brought by NotPety at US$300 million. Nonetheless, its ability to survive this event can be at least partly explained by its sheer size. In 2017, Maersk made a net profit of US$57 million. Its activity accounts for 17% of the world share in container transportation, making it the biggest freight provider in the world.

+   10. Post-Event Knowledge Management: The mechanics of the attack became clear, as it was found that NotPety used two system vulnerabilities, both known at the time of the attack. After the NotPety attack, Maersk CEO indicated that cyber-security is viewed by the company as a competitive advantage. IT makeover is still underway. For example, some servers in the company are still running the unsupported operating system Windows 2000. Incentives are not all aligned either. For example, IT makeover has not been carried out completely due to insufficient incentives, for example, as a makeover has not been aligned to IT executive's pay.

+   11. Social Capital: Customers and public image were affected by this attack. Some containers became lost in the system for 3 months due to the attack and subsequent recovery process. The Maersk resilience and status monitoring system for customers was for several days, and ports only recovered an acceptable level of operation more than a week after the attack. Maersk reportedly tried to reinforce customer relationships by covering at least part of the additional costs accrued by their disrupted business (McQuade, 2018). However, not all stakeholders were treated equally, as collateral damage from Maersk's disrupted operations spared nearly.

As a result of this analysis, example of parallel action plans can be undertaken by a company as Maersk, resulting from the structured analysis of an attack as Non-Pety:

+   1. Strategic Preventive: Review the appropriate norms and regulations for cyber resilience (Compliance)
2. Strategic Preventive: Implement a process to update patches in Software and Systems (Situational Awareness)
3. Operational Preventive: Install a fallback procedure and identification of Critical Data and Systems (Governance)
4. Operational Preventive: Consider non-digital backup of IT structures for reconstruction (Cyber-security)
5. Operational Reactive: Lay out Analog alternatives to critical operations (Ability to adapt)
6. Strategic Reactive: Create and Train emergency customer management processes (Market Position)
