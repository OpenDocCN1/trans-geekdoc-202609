Both socio-ecological and social theory consider social resilience to be a dynamic process and a normative concept rather than a state (ecology) or a trait (socialpsychology) that is subject to change over time. Moreover, as pointed out by Obris[13], when studying resilience, it is essential to ask what threat or risk is examined? Resilience to what? Is it individual or to community-based threat, is it a short-term or a long-term threat, a single hazard or to multiple hazards, recurring, chronic, or seasonal threats, to slow-onset or rapid-onset risks? This paper does not touch all these dimensions but they are important for the framing of social resilience in future research.[14]

# Uniqueness of the online space

While the noted ‘creative literature’ provides pertinent theoretical frames for conceptualizing social resilience in offline settings – it makes references to what social resilience means in the contemporary online context. As a point of departure in conceptualizing digital social resilience, it is important to identify the characteristics and functionalities of the online or digital environment and the ‘social’. By digital or online we make the use of computer or Internet information and communication technologies (ICT) and data including, email social media (Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, Reddit, YouTube), mobile and software applications, digital platforms and dashboards. Digital spaces are functionally unique in that they grant self-administered 24/7 access to sets, enabling synchronous (in real time e.g. chatrooms) but also asynchronous (with time lag – e.g. e-mails) one-to-one, but also one-to-many-many communication hence enhancing the convenience, signification[15] and speed (virtual), interaction and cost-effectiveness of disseminating information.

The online environment’s unique discursive practices and psychosocial aspects add structural, representational, and interactive dimensions to the online public sphere.[16] Unlike traditional print and television media which are unidirectional intermediaries in mass communication and mutuality among users[17], enabling users to easily move between the roles of audience and content producers. In the virtual realm, people interact and engage in diverse forms of information exchange by posting and reposting content. Iconic features of social media is the ability to provide immediate and quantifiable social feedback in response to other people’s content (e.g. likes, shares, retweet) amplifies users’ propensity to express moral emotions in response to moradores-relevant content.[18] The feedback delivery system of social media further amplifies norm conformity our group identities become strongly highly and hyper-latent.[19]
