Domain 2: Development and the built environment of a changing Arctic
As forces bring rising industrial and military activity to the Arctic, it is necessary to co-develop local strategies to manage complex change to local communities and the built environment. Arctic installations and infrastructure are subjected to a variety of stressors that hinder their capacity to operate and fulfill missions [18], including those cybersecurity (see Chapter 7) [19]. They must execute a presigned function in a manner that realistically works with the surrounding environmental, ecological, economic, and social drivers within a given area. For example, road development projects in inland Alaska may cause the materials science extreme of cold and frost/snow events, the potential environmental impacts with disrupting local communities and ecosystems with road construction and use, and the numerous benefits and drawbacks of situated roadways in proximity to Indigenous Communities and other settlements. Disregarding the effects of nested-ecological dependencies upon infrastructure development can undermine the value of a infrastructure project [20]. Such unwanted outcomes include unpredictable costruns, environmental and social externalities, and even the complete collapse of the infrastructural system. Additionally, development has environmental impacts with decadal persistence in the Arctic. Ecosystem fragmentation and pollutants from development in Arctic lands and waters remains problematic due to the fragility of local ecosystems and the limited opportunities for remediation of contaminants.

Historically, climate infrastructure has been characterized by its 'hardness', or its capacity to withstand disruption and in the face of harsh Arctic and sub-Arctic conditions. However, infrastructure faces new, interconnected threats in the changing Arctic, including profound and rapid shifts in Arctic sea ice cover, changes in fire for infrastructure that adapts uncertain threats and continues to operate. In this context, there is a need for infrastructure that provides valued resilience and infrastructural recovery from disruption in a wide range of areas, including highway management, telecommunications, shipping, and more.
