This paper adds to the field of literature by presenting a literature review of supply chain modeling and quantification from a simulation of multiple-dependent networks, system-approach, using the NAS definition of resilience.

This paper argues that awareness of these multi-network interactions and explicit accounting for how they are all modeled or simulated is vital to understanding supply chain resilience. This paper reviews the current supply chain resilience literature that incorporates both a systems perspective and at least some aspects of modeling and simulation. This paper provides supply chain definitions (Beamon 1998)'s definition of supply chain as processes, as discrete processes lend themselves to simulation. This paper starts with a bibliometric review, to identify the literature space and trends. It then moves to a thematic review, to describe the ways in which the current literature models the interlaced networks that drive a supply chain's functionality. It comparatively reviews the strengths and weaknesses of the different approaches as well as some of the scenarios and purposes for which these approaches are more valid. It identifies the gaps in current research and how addressing these gaps may improve understanding of supply chain resilience.

# 2. Methods

# 2.1 Review Process and Inclusion Criteria

The biometric review process included two literature search phases. The first phase was a keyword search of all the Web of Science databases. Literature review papers found in this search were used in a “Forward Backward Search,” whereby the papers that they cited and papers that cited them were added to the search phase. In both phases a filtering process was used to filter out low quality, irrelevant, or unavailable papers.

The first search used the Web of Science (WOS) “All Databases” collection for the literature search. Papers were limited to those published in English between 2007 and 2017. Peer-reviewed papers were identified in the search and available through USACO and CMU library subscription access, university repositories or other public sources were included in the review. Proceedings and other non-reviewed papers as well as duplicate publications were excluded. A Web of Science search for the intersection of “supply chain” and “resilience” resulted in 673 papers. Twenty of these papers were restricted access and counted for under 3% of the total results the majority from Francis and Taylor publications. Abstracts from excluded papers were reviewed by reviewers and found not to differ significantly in distribution of content from reviewed papers. The authors believe the inclusion of these papers would not significantly change the results.
