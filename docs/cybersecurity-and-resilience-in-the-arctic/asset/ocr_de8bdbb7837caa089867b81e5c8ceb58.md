# 7. Sub-Arctic states and their policies in the Arctic

Those concerned with what happens in the Arctic aren solely limited to those who hold territorial claims within the Arctic circle. An issue of concern for many states globally is the disruptive impact Climate Change is extremely likely to have on coastlines. McGranan, Balk and Anderson [57] estimated at the time that the world 634 million people live in Low Elevation Coastal Zones (LECZ), areas that are less than 10 meters above sea level. In island nations, such as the UK and Japan, sea level rise poses a large human, environmental and economic threat. Furthermore, in China, where a trend of urbanization is driving more and more people to coastal regions every year, the risk of disaster is increasing. These nations each hold observer status on the Arctic Council and have outlined policies towards the Arctic.

# 7.1. China

China produced a white paper outlining its official Arctic policy in January 2018 [58], describing themselves as a “near-Arctic state” – being one within the Arctic circle but still close to it, and each stakeholder in Arctic affairs. In the past, China has made this connection on the basis of the impacts of climate change, especially those in the Arctic that will impact the climate system for the rest of the world. Furthermore, this will impact China’s environment, ecosystem, agriculture, and flooding threat. More recently, however, China has clarified its assertion into Arctic affairs, claiming that China holds “rights and interests” in the Arctic.
