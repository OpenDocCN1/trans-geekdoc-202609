### Technology – The Arctic region and energy

The most important segment in Arctic society is energy, Figure 11. All forms of functionality in the Arctic region and in any smart society and smart city are very much dependent on the supply of electricity. It is possible that smart grid, heating, cooling, and energy distribution systems collapse due to internal faults, but the failure can also be caused by a cyber-attack against these systems. Cyber-attacks have also been made against power stations, meaning that electricity-based systems in the distribution area of the power station are not working at any day, Figure 11. We use different fuels for homes, cars, planes, etc. But it is not possible to pump fuel into a tank without electricity in many cases. ### Technology – The infrastructure of the Arctic region

The Arctic region has cities, villages, research stations, and satellite earth stations that need various services; these infrastructures must be connected to the communications systems in one or another. This infrastructure also requires electricity, water, smart lighting systems, waste systems, trains, buses, and ships. Figure 11. One of the large systems is logistics, which is needed on both ground regions and in the sea. There are many different types of services which are available to citizens in this kind of environment that they need in their everyday lives. However, when these communication systems and services are used it also means that there are opportunities for attackers and hackers to target these systems and disrupt their use.

### Technology – The infrastructure of the Arctic region - Buildings and Homes

Buildings and homes are dealing with energy efficiency issues better than ever. We are now talking about houses and buildings in which the newest energy efficient solutions are used [19][20][21][22]. However, this also means that many devices and sensors integrated into buildings are quite vulnerable, considering the potential for cyber attackers have already carried out many attacks. Figure 13 shows the future office building with its infrastructure services and communications systems. Most of the IoT devices and sensor systems are connected to the communications access node. Inside these buildings there are local networks to which all IoT sensor devices are connected through either wireless or fixed systems. People who are working inside the buildings are using the same connections and systems of those IoT devices and sensors at the same time. When we look for security issues impacting these types of systems there are many security and cyber challenges. In those challenges are not only a question of the size of the building, but it is also coming to everybody's home, where there are new types of IoT devices and sensors installed. When people use medical devices in their home, there are many opportunities for interference between those devices and the home systems because they are using the same frequencies. Nowadays, many patients are sent home with various sensors, so that healthcare staff can track their conditions. There are many different types of vulnerabilities in these systems, such that hackers and cyber attackers can come to make attacks into different systems in the surrounding cities and society. One challenge will be in the future energy buildings, because mobile communications systems do not work properly from outside to inside and versa. If we consider the Arctic regions, this situation is even more challenging because the connections are not as good and a patient's vital information must be seen by the hospital database in real time. The same situation pertains to different types of critical systems in the Arctic region. Under the ground level we can build corridors and channels for cables, electric supply systems logistics, water pipes, waste systems, heating pipes. The underground spaces of the building offer opportunities to develop efficient solutions for smart city infrastructures.
