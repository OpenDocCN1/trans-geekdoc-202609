The wave analogy goes to represent the effect of a cyber-event after its occurrence. This is represented by what lies to the right of the point of impact, where the mitigation mechanisms are identified. The strength of the attack's impact is represented as the extent to which a “wave” from the point of impact can advance or propagate. # 3. The wave analogy for shipping resilience

The usefulness of this framework is now explored for shipping operations by using it to identify elements present before and after a cyber-attack. In particular, this article analyzes the NotPetya attack, which affected the shipping company AP Moller-Maersk (Maersk in 2017 (Van Niekerk, 2018). This attack, despite having the initial appearance of being Ransomware, turned out to be a Wiper (Furnell & Emma, 2017), in which data recovery was possible, even if the required Bitcoin payment had been carried out. The Wave Analogy framework, applied to the facts of the NotPetya attack, resulting in the following analysis, supported by journalistic data (McQuade, 2018).
