# 2.1.2. An increase in activity – An Overview

With more viable conditions, activity in the Arctic as has been increasing, and military operations have been no different. Russia has been reopening and strengthening cold war bases on the Kola Peninsula in the far-west of the country; Norway has also been increasing its military presence in the high Arctic [59]. In October 2018, NATO staged a Trident Jun with 50,000 troops - the biggest military exercise in Norway in more than a decade. Throughout this GPS exercise failed with Finnish Prime Minister stating that it was because of these issues [60]. A month earlier Britain announced a new ‘Defence Arctic Strategy’ and promised a 10-year deployment of 800 commands to Norway and four RAF Typhoons to polar Iceland. The US is also sending more marines to the region on long-term rotations and has threatened to send naval vessels through Arctic shipping lanes for the first time.
