# 2.3.2. Russia

As compared to China, Russia likely presents a more acute and near-threat in the
Arctic [130], [131]. Over 10,000 miles, Russia’s border with the Arctic is the longest
of any state — and represents one-third of the total length of Russia’s national borders
[132]. Much of Russian territory is also within the Arctic Circle. Currently, Russia views
control over the Arctic (which it defines rather broadly, as she shall be) as
integral to its national security — and has so for centuries [132]. It was security
cerns made apparent by the 1905 Russo-Japanese War, for instance, first
prompted Russia to begin developing the NSR [133]. Even in the economically troubled
tumuous years after the downfall of the Soviet Union, Russian security, military,
and government leaders were forced to come by the Northern
Strategic bastion doctrine and massive Northern Fleet naval exercises [132, 134].
Indeed, though the Yeltsin era’s Northern Fleet was always provided with\adequate funds to maintain its nuclear submarines and infrastructure — even when other
defense spending fell by the wayside [136].
