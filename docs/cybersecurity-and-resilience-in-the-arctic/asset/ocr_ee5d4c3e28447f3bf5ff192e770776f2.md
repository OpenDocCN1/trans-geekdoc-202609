# J. Hall et al. / Framing the Arctic Part II: Complex Systems and the Need for Resilience

Resilience-based approaches are essential for Arctic threats, where the complex and high-value critical infrastructure components held by Arctic organizations and countries are increasingly under threat of disruption – with a rising potential for cascading systemic risk through cybersecurity concerns. A resilience approach would empower Arctic infrastructure and relevant stakeholders to better understand (i) where vulnerabilities and risks may arise within interconnected Arctic systems, (ii) how cyberattack or intrusion could cause risks to the safety and operation of Arctic sites, and (iii) identify strategies to secure critical infrastructure against cyber threats, particularly against exposed or brittle infrastructural targets.

In the remainder of this chapter, we highlight the growing complexity and consequences associated with economic, industrial, transport, and defense activities in the Arctic. While it is not the purpose of this chapter to identify the exact resilience strategy for each actor or initiative, we present an argument that systemic complexity and heightened hazard environment make resilience an essential requirement of risk-informed planning, development, and Activity within that region. As noted in Chapters 1 and 2, the fact that digital systems serve the backbone of many critical functions within Arctic infrastructural and transport systems is a concerning one, where safeguards must be developed to ensure that deliberate disruption (i.e., hacking or attack) or random disruptions (i.e., severe weather or material stress) do not trigger cascading failure into many other dependent sub-systems.
