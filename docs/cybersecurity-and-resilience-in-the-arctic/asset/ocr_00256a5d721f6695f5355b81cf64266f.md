# 3.3.2. Cyber disruptions

The USN is highly vulnerable to cyber attacks. Some of the most vulnerable systems and tools have been delineated above, including GPS, ECDIS, AIS, VSAT, and Inmarsat. GPS can be spoofed, ECDIS can be modified, data gleaned from an AIS can be used to determine cargo loads of vessels or misreport their positions, VSAT can provide attackers a means of access to the local network, and through its gateways, Inmarsat satellite communications can be intercepted. In addition, Arctic critical infrastructure, especially the submarine fiber optic cable, is vulnerable.

Rather than combatting these vulnerabilities after the fact, the Navy could engineer software that highly reduces these vulnerabilities and adopts resilience principles [180]; [1881]. It is impossible to harden all Navy systems against all possible cyber intrusions, given the number and complexity of these systems and the rapid pace at which threats develop and mutate. Therefore, the USN might choose to view system hardness, i.e. risk prevention, as just one element in its toolkit for the fight against cyber threats. The full toolkit, as offered by resilience theory, includes risk prevention, but also quick detection of intrusions, restoration of necessary measures as swiftly as possible, and adaptation to future cyber threats [188]. If the Navy focuses on ensuring the resilience of its systems, cyber attacks could cease to be such critical threats. When an attack occurs, resilient areas affected by the attempt utilize redundancies or other techniques to restore system function.

The Navy has already implemented some elements of resilience-based thinking. For example, celestial navigation has recently been reintroduced to the service member curriculum. Services are now being taught to navigate with sextants and paper maps as a backup in the case of GPS or other system failure [182]. In creating redundancy -- both humans and GPS capable of navigation -- the Navy is ensuring that if one of these systems (like GPS) fails to an attack, the other (humans) can still navigate the ship, rapidly absorbing the disruption and maintaining normally functioning.

As one of the most promising steps toward implementing resilient systems, the Navy has contracted with River Analytics Inc., to design a cyber-resilient framework for Navy physical systems. This framework, the Embedded Architecture for Cyber-Resilience (EAC), allows Navy systems to maintain functionality in the face of disruptions, fulfilling the second "absorb" stage of resilience. However, the funding that this project represents only a small fraction of the USN budget; additionally, the project does not focus on cyber physical systems capable of all stages of resilience.
