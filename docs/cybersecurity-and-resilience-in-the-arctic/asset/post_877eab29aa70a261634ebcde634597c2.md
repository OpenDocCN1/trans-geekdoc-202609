## 7.3. The United Kingdom (UK)

The UK is also another state that has great interest in what occurs in the Arctic, on the claim that it is the Arctic's nearest neighbour; the UK released its first Arctic policy in 2013 and then updated it in a following document 2018. The goal of the UK is to work towards the objective of ensuring a safe and secure Arctic; a region that is well-governed in respect to indigenous populations and in line with international law. It believes that the process of policy development should be on the sound basis of scientific research, in full adherence to environmental protection where responsible development is permitted. The UK acknowledges that the scale of Arctic challenges is immense and rapidly changing; this is why the Government has committed itself to ensuring a comprehensive set of measures outlined within its 2013 framework. It is to set an example, ensuring cooperation with Arctic states and the wider international community in order to facilitate a sustainable Arctic in years to come.

In 2013, the Foreign and Commonwealth Office issued an Arctic policy titled "Adapting to Change" [60], setting out their overall approach to the Arctic. It outlines that the UK respects of the sovereign rights of the Arctic states and presents a three-pronged approach to the Arctic.

First, the UK aims to respect respect; for the sovereign rights of Arctic states to exercise jurisdiction over their territory; for the views and interests of those living and working in the Arctic, to whom it is a home; and for the environment, its fragility and its central importance to the global climate system.

Second, the UK priorities leadership; fundamentally the UK recognizes that good stewardship rests in the hands of the eight Arctic states and their populations, with direct interest and experience in living, working and operating the Arctic –

however, it would be wrong to suggest that the UK should not actively contribute leadership on issues affecting the Arctic. Climate change and its consequences are currently being felt in the circumpolar north, with the UK's capacity as a global leader in pushing for reduced greenhouse emissions and understanding the underlying causes of rapid climate, the UK scientific community, industries and NGOs all have and continue playing an active role in tackling these problems, underpinning policy, stable governance and responsible commerce. Moreover, the UK also seeks to be an active leader in increasing the awareness and understanding of Arctic environments, and their inherent value.

Lastly, the UK seeks to promote cooperation with the eight Arctic states, indigenous populations and others close to issues facing the Arctic. Recognizing the mix of actors, interests and expertise in the Arctic is key to producing a dialogue and effective cooperation at the heart of Arctic governance.

In 2018, the UK updated its policy, releasing a document entitled “Beyond the Ice”. In this document, the UK acknowledges that the rate of change in the Arctic has increased since 2013, which updates are necessary to the previous existing Arctic policy. The document states that this was an intention and while the core principles of respect, leadership and cooperation are the close approach, further ambitions are necessary to ensure the UK remains in a position of importance and influence regarding Arctic affairs and developments.

"Beyond the Ice" identifies actions and priorities for the UK to be across three key areas: Firstly, protecting global influence is an important aspect of promoting a global Britain that is engaged in international activity, advancing prosperity and security around the world. The UK pinpoints the Arctic as a region important to the UK, of which research, science and innovation are helping to promote global understanding of climate changes in the Arctic, and the solutions it poses.

Second on the UK's 2018 Arctic policy agenda is ensuring the protection of the Arctic's fragile environment in line with the goals set by the UN for Global Sustainable Development in 2015. Urgent action is necessary in combating climate change, and the UK is fully committed to conservation and sustainability of resources and life on land.

Lastly, the UK wishes to continue to promote prosperity, utilizing the region where economic and commercial development can take place in a manner that prioritises sustainability and responsibility. These developments should be for the benefit and prosperity of local regions and people, and the UK supports communities investing in the Arctic, ensuring they understand and have an awareness of issues but also opportunities.
