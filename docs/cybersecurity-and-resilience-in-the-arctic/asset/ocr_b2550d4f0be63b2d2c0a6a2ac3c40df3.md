There are geologically active areas below sea level in the Arctic, which can limit the various forms of construction and development in the region. Geologists have found that there are underwater volcanoes melting the Arctic ice [4]. The Gakkel ridge is a gigantic chain of underwater volcanoes spanning 1,800 kilometers beneath the Arctic Ocean from the northern tip of Greenland to Siberia. When we are designing infrastructures for the Arctic region, we thus also need to take into consideration these underwater volcanoes and how they might impact our planned construction, **Figure 3.**

Powerful geothermal heat forces deep in the Arctic Ocean are melting the ice, these underwater volcanoes may also erupt at some point in the time and cause great damage to the Arctic region and infrastructures. However, the Arctic region also contains a large amount of natural resources, especially oil and gas, **Figure 4**, which are of interest to several states [6]. The exploitation of these resources under these conditions is a challenge and it is necessary to make these extreme conditions into account in all situations [7].

The United States Geological Survey estimates that over 87% of the Arctic's oil and natural gas resources (about 300 billion barrels oil equivalent) are located in seven Arctic basin provinces: the Eurasian Basin, Arctic Alaska Basin, East Barents Basin, East Greenland Rift Basin, Greenland-East Canada Basin, West Siberian Basin, and the Yenisei-Katanga Basin, **Figure 4.** The Arctic region is between different countries, **Figure 5.**
