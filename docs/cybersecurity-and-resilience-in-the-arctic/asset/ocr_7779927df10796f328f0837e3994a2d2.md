# 4.2. General Network Forensics Methodology

In order to ensure the results of the network forensic task are reproducible and accurate, forensic investigators should perform their activities within a methodological framework. To date, there are two approaches that can guide investigators through their analysis. As example, we consider the OSCAR methodology. The acronym OSCAR stands for Information Acquisition, Strategy, Collect evidence, Analyze, and Report (ENISA, 2016).

+   - **Obtaining information** involves collecting data about the following components:

+   - the incident itself, such as the date and when an incident was discovered, persons and systems involved, who has initiated, actions taken since discovery, who is incident manager, etc. The time frame for investigation/recovery/resolution should be defined and written down;

+   - the environment where it took place, including business model, legal issues, network topology (network map), available sources of network evidence, organizational structure (organizational chart), incident response management process/procedures, etc.

+   - **Strategy generation

This step deals with the planning of the investigation. Due to the volatility of the network-based evidence, their acquisition should be prioritized, considering their potential value to the investigation and the effort needed to obtain them.
