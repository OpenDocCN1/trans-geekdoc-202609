## 3. The wave analogy for shipping resilience

The usefulness of this framework is now explored for shipping operations by using it to identify elements present before and after a cyber-attack. In particular, this article analyzes the NotPetya attack, which affected the shipping company AP Moller-Maersk (Maersk in 2017 (Van Niekerk, 2018)). This attack, despite having the initial appearance of being Ransomware, turned out to be a Wiper (Furnell & Emma, 2017), in which data recovery was possible, even if the required Bitcoin payment had been carried out. The Wave Analogy framework, applied to the facts of the NotPetya attack, resulting in the following analysis, supported by journalistic data (McQuade, 2018).

- 1. Compliance: Norms and regulations in the shipping industry include global norms like ISO 27000 (Disterer, 2013), NIST SP-800-53 (D. Budeau & Graubert, 2013), industry-related norms like the Danish Cyber and Information Security Strategy for the Maritime Sector, the BIMCO Guidelines on cybersecurity on board ships, the IMO Guidelines on maritime cyber management as well as any internal guidelines of the company.

- 3. Governance: Maersk had a Chief Information Security Officer since the year 2015, and it was one of the first major shipping companies to do this, on its digitalization strategy. Maersk therefore had a hierarchy for the decision-making process of its IT infrastructure, located in England and overseeing the digital needs of the almost 80,000 employees at the company. Therefore, even though Maersk had resources to manage this governance structure, situational awareness was apparently insufficient, it did not trigger enough actions to prevent and mitigate the risk of a global IT meltdown in the company.
