# 3. Analytics

Next, we review broad capabilities of analytics and how we may use these moving forward. Analysts, risk owners and decision makers can employ analytics in the interest of well-informed decisions. Effective decision making is predicated on receiving the most useful data available at the fastest possible time in order to get good situational awareness. Analytics helps us discover, interpret and communicate data and information (data insight). Some of this could be machine-driven through automation. Other tasks ought to be investigated by the human visual system – particularly the human observer excels at detecting certain types of patterns and correlations which might take longer to identify. This can come from sensors that measure or operate in the real world (e.g. weather-related, GPS data, radar or similar).

Correct use of analytics tools is not straightforward however, decision making from data not always rely on having 'enough' data, but rather making good use of data that is available. Filtering amounts of noisy data. We also need data that is accurate, precise, relevant (to the mission), actionable (ability to act on the data either or in context) and has intrinsic value. The intrinsic value of data might be determined by a number of factors, such as freshness (how recent is the data?), provenance (origin of data), timeliness (data may be fresh, but it needs to be used when needed), competence (is the source capable?), or corroboration (multiple sources agreeing?).

This is not an exhaustive list of factors, and it is assumed other factors are also important to consider. While statistics is used to identify anomalies and measurements of significant statistical merit, analytics is broader as practice. Analytics can come in many forms: which relates logical deduction or inference based on data or models.

# 3.1. Uses of Analytics

Any analytical capabilities, need to address each of these concerns, in order to support decision-making.

+   - Physical and situational awareness. We require sensors and tools that provide data about our surroundings – both as they are and how we experience them. This data requires such sensors as on vessels, and ability to query any far and nearby objects, including positioning, directional, temperature, colour and other, spatially relevant. Different sensors will deliver different types of data - some pertaining to weather, living beings or other vessels in the vicinity. Such data only capture a snippet in time, but with enough background and mission appropriate selection of sensors and technologies made available, experienced analysts ought to be able to make use of their physical (or) surroundings better.

+   - Predictive analytics make use of historical data to anticipate the future. However, prediction often make mistakes.
