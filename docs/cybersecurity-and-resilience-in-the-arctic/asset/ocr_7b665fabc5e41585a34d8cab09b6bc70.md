+   4. Pre-Event Knowledge: EternalBlue and Mimikatz were used, but the first one was relatively recent, and a combined use of both was not widely observed before. Because of this, there was no initial knowledge on how to prevent or deal with this specific threat until it was reverse-engineered some times afterwards.

Cyber-Security: it might have been possible to prevent or reduce the impact of NotPety from attack, Maersk had in place elements such as more software patching policies, up-to-date operating systems, and network segmentation (McQuade, 2018). Nonetheless, the lack of awareness regarding this specific threat made it highly arguably difficult to prepare for it. For example, Maersk reported around 150 domain controllers (servers that store and communicate the rules which users can access systems) before NotPety, which was secured up to act as a backup for each other. However, domain controllers failed to foresee the scenario in which all they were taken simultaneously (McQuade, 2018).

+   6. Agility: It took two hours to disconnect Maersk's systems from the network. The recovery team at Maidenhead, using clean equipment, searched for backup images of servers and domain controllers. The only unaltered (affected by NotPety) copy of this domain controller was found in a server in Ghana, due to a fortuitous power outage. This fortunate discovery was regarded as a key piece in the relatively quick reconstruction of Maersk IT infrastructure.

Moreover, many ships could not continue operations as the cyber-event severely impacted and disrupted Maersk's operations.
