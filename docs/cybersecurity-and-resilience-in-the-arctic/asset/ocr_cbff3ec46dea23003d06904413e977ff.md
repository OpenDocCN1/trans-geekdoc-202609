# 6.7. Norwegian Arctic Policy

Since the turn of the millennia Norway was the first state to produce an Arctic strategy and policy; 2003 was met with an expert report outlining the strategic interests of the Arctic. This report was followed in 2006 with “The Norwegian Government’s High North Strategy” [54], which defined the High North as an area of interest for the government, placing explicit attention on the environment, indigenous populations, business, scientific knowledge and foreign policy. Furthermore, in 2009 Norway produced another follow-strategy. This was again presenting a series of Arctic areas which support and focus on the main prioritized priorities set out in the 2003 report [55], 2014 saw the publication of Nordkloden, yet another government strategy prioritizing outlying areas. The most recent report was published in 2017, “Between geopolitics and social development”[56], serving as another update to Norway’s Arctic strategy.

The 2017 report identifies a vision or the Arctic, and presents strategies for helping build peace, innovation and sustainability in this region, encompasses both foreign and domestic policy. The four overarching goals of the report are:

+   1. Peace, stability and predictability
2. Integrated, ecosystem-based management
3. International cooperation and the international legal order
4. A stronger basis for employment, value creation and welfare

The five priorities in the 2017 report, and separated into sections, are as follows:

+   1. Cooperation with specific regard to Arctic states and the EU, using existing formed in the Arctic, promoting security and respect to indigenous communities.

Providing sustainability whilst utilizing the resources of the Arctic, supporting research-based value creation and supporting greater connection between business development and the research community.
