# 4. Highlights of Analytical Approaches

# 4.1 Introduction

Used as a concept to qualify and quantify "things, forces, circumstances or hazards that endanger people or what they value" (National Research Council (NRC), 1996), various forms of risk assessment have been around for centuries, and each has its own hazards of special concern. Though risk is not an inherently quantifiable characteristic, methods of risk analysis have subsequently evolved to assume that risk can be estimated, measured and expressed in numerical terms. Using historical data, analysis can be conducted from statistical significance of the loss. This is the type of approach used in cases where data is widespread and readily available, such as the analysis of car accidents, car deaths, or the frequency and impacts of disasters. In cases where historical data is scarce and losses are not logged, the approach is to use risk analysis models, where potential risk and loss is predicted. Risk analysis attempts to measure the extent and significance of a loss, and the negatives associated with this a complex system. Moreover, analysis is typically conducted in three ways: quantitatively, qualitatively, or a mixture of the two. Each of these ways has different strengths, weaknesses and purposes.

The security of a system, by the application of methods used in an attempt to deter or prevent undesirable events from occurring, should be in proportion to its risks. There is a tradeoff between security and cost, and for decision makers to identify what controls are appropriate and cost-effective, this process is often a complicated subjective matter. Risk analysis is, therefore, a methodical process for establishing uncertainties and their impacts that could be encountered in a venture or a system. It aims to establish an objective measure for threats, vulnerabilities and consequences, establishing a foundationally empirical basis for which to make decisions upon (Merad & Trump 2020).

# 4.2 Classical Risk Assessment Constructs

Risk assessment tools and strategies are used to identify vulnerabilities and weaknesses in a system and identify potential harm that could result from exposure to a hazard or other initiating event. Throughout different stages in a system's life cycle, strategies can be used to analyse the hazards being faced, and their potential adverse consequences.
