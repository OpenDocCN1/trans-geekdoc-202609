#  2. Defining Resiliency

Before reviewing the structure in creating resiliency metrics, the basic resiliency need will be discussed for a better understanding of what will be measured. Since term resiliency has taken on many meanings and definitions over the last several years, the discussion will align common meanings to understand the differences and to reduce the confusion of aligning a common meaning or approach for a resiliency metric. The intent is not to define resiliency, but to provide common meanings and practices to then allow the creation of resiliency metrics to meet the reader’s needs.

Although the term is greatly used in many books and other sources is still not defined in many industries; there is no agreement on methods for measuring and applying resiliency in a meaningful and practical method. As with term cybersecurity, the cybersecurity resiliency metric and meaning are different based on the many factors to country, language, industry, and regulatory guidelines. Although a common lexicon appears to be forming, the industry in general has to agree on one term or meaning, the intent is clear to provide something that is resilient to change or disruption with proper recovery. Metrics and measurement of resiliency also appears to be scattered and unique to specific factors. Similar issues of defining the meaning of resiliency and how that is applied to organization appear to have many input vectors. This paper will review some common definitions for resiliency and represent resiliency through metrics.

To understand the complexity and what define ‘resiliency’, the United States (US) National Institute of Systems and Technology (NIST) standards to specify NIST special publication (SP) 800-161 Vol 2 will be discussed. To define resiliency, NIST uses the context of critical infrastructure as the highest level of discussion to address the need for resiliency to a larger set of national systems. Those are broken down into sectors, sub-sectors, or may be called industries. These further down the term ‘resilience’ into several critical infrastructure components that are defined with wording that quantifies ‘resilience’ to each sector:

> “the critical infrastructure’ means systems and assets, whether physical or virtual, so that to the United States is the incapacity or disruption of such systems and assets would have a devastating impact on security, national economic security, national public health or safety, or any combination of those matters” [2].
