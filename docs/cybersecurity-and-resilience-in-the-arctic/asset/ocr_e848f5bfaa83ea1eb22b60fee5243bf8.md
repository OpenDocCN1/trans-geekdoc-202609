# Conclusion

Using blockchain technology to implement and maintain integrity in distributed peer-to-peer systems consisting of an unknown number of participants with an unknown level of reliability and trust will provide:

+   - The trust of the parties. The register is not changed and is only open for addition - records can be added only to this database and never be deleted or changed. It can be updated only by consensus or agreement on the status of data among peers.
- Decentralization and transparency. The blockchain database is distributed between several computers (nodes) that store full or partial copies of this. This eliminates the monopoly and a single point of failure. Provides trust between parties
- Security and fraud prevention. Entries on the blockchain are protected by cryptography. Each transaction is signed with a digital signature. If the record is changed, the signature becomes invalid. A peer-to-peer network immediately learns something that has happened. The blockchain does not have a single point of failure and cannot be changed by one computer. Such an approach can facilitate more collaborative measures of governance, while reducing concerns of system validity or corruptibility due to the use of an impartial arabic over contract management and governance [10].
