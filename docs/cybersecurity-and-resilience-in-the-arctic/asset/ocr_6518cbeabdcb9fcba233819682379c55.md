# Section 5. Conclusions and future work

The system to be built in the Arctic region is technically very complicated and many new technical solutions will be needed to meet the required transmission rates, the usability, quality requirements, and criteria. This places considerable demands on the management and control of the system as well as the organization of its maintenance. The long lifecycle of submarine optical cables, about 25 years, should be taken into account when developing submarine optical cable systems. Changes in social structures, which take very quickly also affect the implementations and operating models these changes must also be consider of developing submarine optical cable systems. The current powerful digitalization trend increases the range of services offered and facilitate their use. These developments also have a strong impact on the service chains for the provided services, including the subcontractors with their subcontracting chains, the hardware providers, the service providers, and the operating models in every part of the service chain on every continent.

And in the future, modern communications will connect data centers and different continents, enabling real-time communication throughout the world. This type of communications is made possible by undersea optical cable and satellite systems, which we use for daily communications. Because submarine cables systems have a large strategic impact on our society, they are also a very tempting for hackers, cyber-attackers, terrorists, and malevolent state actors. They seek to gain access to the information that is transmitted through the networks on the continents that are connected to each other by undersea cables.
