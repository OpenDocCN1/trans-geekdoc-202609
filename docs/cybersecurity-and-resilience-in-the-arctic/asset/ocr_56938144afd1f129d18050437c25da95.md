# J. Tomkova/ Digital Social Resilience: Navigating in the New Normal

Literature on **social resilience** in the digital context, however, is inchoate and fragmented. Among the voluminous streams explores the role and effects of social media on citizens’ self-resilience relative to information gathering, dissemination, collaborative problem-solving, and coping in the management of crises such as disasters.[226] Recent findings show that during crises, people seek immediate and in-depth information.[227] Reliance on the word of mouth acquisition and spread of information through the use of social media networks rather than digital seeking on official organizations or web blogs to be more common. This is because formal websites lag in the real-time provision of information and offer only limited interactive engagement in knowledge sharing, reflection and the exchange of views when compared to social media sites.[228] Discourse and text analytics of deliberative content on social media platforms in different contexts is another domain of research offering new insights into social behavior of individuals and groups online.[229] In the advent of big data, the accessibility and operational use of data and analytical tools for the empowerment of communities facing disruptive is newly emerging.[230]

In the context, social system resilience is defined here as individuals’, groups or organizations’ ability to maintain, change, recover, adapt to recover their social capacity mobilization, sustenance of social cohesion and collective effort, dignity - using ICT and the online space to do so. Systems comprises individuals (private citizens, micro-bloggers identified online activists), groups or communities (video sharing, content-driven communities) or formally associated (professional networks, media outlets), use the digital space(s) and tools (social media platforms - Facebook, Twitter, Reddit, Instagram, YouTube etc.). In other words, digital social resilience comprises computer and enhanced, social adaptive processes to withstand external shocks and cyber threats. Being socially resilient in online environments means individuals, groups, communities and organizations (at all levels) are well-placed to adopt new systems and processes in mobilizing their social capacity, ensuring their continued capacity to resist, protect and restore public, social good in whatever social contexts they function or the crises they cause. Enabling accessibility, interchangeability, operational quality and timeliness of data and response tools is a growing element of digital resilience.[231] While others contend that media and digital literacy - technical, cognitive, social and creative capacities that allow a citizen to access, have a critical understanding of the media and interact with it[232] - is a constitutive indicator of digital social resilience
