## 4.1. The forensics process in Smart Grid

The forensics process comprises of the following phases (Kent et al., 2006):

- **Collection.** This is where all data related to a specific event is identified, labelled, and collected, while ensuring that its integrity is preserved. In this phase the Chain of Custody process is initiated.
- **Examination.** In this phase, we use a scientifically process to collect data while forensic tools and techniques appropriate to the types of data collected are executed to carve out the relevant information from the collected data while protecting its integrity. During this phase, the results of the investigation process are recorded and noted in the Chain of Custody, including the disposition of any evidence used in the examination and how it was used.
- **Analysis.** At this phase, analysis of the examination results is performed, using justifiable methods and techniques aiming to derive useful information that addresses the questions posed in the investigation.
- **Reporting.** This final phase includes the documentation of the analysis, the findings of evidence, and recommendations for improvement.

## 4.2. General Forensics Methodology

In order to ensure the results of investigation are reproducible and accurate, investigators follow the OSCAR methodology: Acquisition, Strategy, Collect, Analyze, and Report (ENISA, 2016).

- **Obtaining information** This involves collecting the following components:
    - The environment where it took place.
    - The time of discovery.
    - The involved persons.
    - The actions taken.
- **Strategy** This step deals with the planning of the investigation. Due to the volatility of evidence, sources should be prioritized as shown in the table:

| Source of evidence | Potential Value | Effort | Volatility |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Firewall logs | High | Medium | 2 |
| Web proxy cache | High | Low | Medium |
| ARP tables | Low | Low | 3 |

- **Collect evidence** Based on the plan defined in the previous step, we can collect data from each source. However, the following points should be considered:
    - The documentation of all actions.
    - The source of evidence.
    - The acquisition method.
- **Capturing of evidence** This is the part where we capture:
    - Network packets.
    - System and application logs.
- **Storage/Transport** Here we should maintain the Chain of Custody, showing the seizure, custody, transfer, analysis, and disposition of evidence (Bononi et al., 2018).
- **Conduct analysis** During this step we recover material using a number of different methodologies and tools. According to Brian D. Carrier (2006), “After the obvious evidence has been found, more exhaustive searches are conducted to start filling in the holes.” During the analysis process the following should be considered essential:
    - Correlation of data from different sources.
    - Timeline of activities.
    - Isolation of Events.
    - Correlation of evidence through multiple sources.
