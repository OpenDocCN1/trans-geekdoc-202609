# Background

As is evident from the above, smart grids have become a vector of the energy policy of many countries. The main objectives in the implementation of smart grids are energy security, economic growth, and environmental sustainability. It is characterized by way communications architecture between service providers and their customers, enabling customers to more effectively manage their power consumption. When talking about Smart Grid, we always assume infrastructure (see Fig. 1). A smart grid is an advanced system for generating, transmitting, distributing, and consuming electricity that provides seamless integration of computing and power supply for improved and sustainable general-purpose operations. It consolidates traditional power plants with virtual power plants (VPP).

VPP is a structure that contains three elements of types:

+   - distributed generators (solar panels, wind turbines, photovoltaic stations, minis and micro-CHP, etc);
- consumers load regulators (household and industrial);
- energy storage systems.

Household consumers (washing machines, refrigerators, televisions, microwave ovens, air conditioning systems, heating elements, etc.) are the most easily controlled loads. The management of a load of industrial users largely depends on the flexibility of the users' technological processes. For some operations, flexibility increases to to energy storage systems.
