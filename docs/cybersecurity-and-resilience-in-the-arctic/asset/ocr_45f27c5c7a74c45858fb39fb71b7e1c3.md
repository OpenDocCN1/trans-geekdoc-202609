# 134 M. Pollock et al. / U.S. Navy Resilience in the Arctic

Complex systems and system-of-systems can consist of networks operating in multiple different domains at once. Accordingly, a failure in one component of a complex, tightly-coupled system can have ramifications across different domains and areas of operation:

> ...increasing interdependence... means that disruptions from natural (e.g., hurricanes, earthquakes, tsunamis) and man-made (e.g., terrorist attacks, civil wars, financial fraud) can quickly propagate through a complex web of supply-chain, transportation, and communication networks to cause unpredictable failures in parts of complex, interconnected networks (Rinaldi et al. 2001; Chang 2009; Vespignani 2010). Under such conditions, risk analysis and management alone are insufficient. A better understanding of design for resilient, coupled, complex systems must be emphasized. [161]

This is especially case for a highly complex system-of-systems like the Navy, which transportation systems (i.e. ships), supply chain, logistical systems, operational supply systems, weapons systems, medical treatments systems, and so forth, are all connected and highly dependent upon one another. Disruption and failure of an individual system or system components in this case will happen: the only question is how the Navy is prepared to cope with failure once it. Resilience offers the toolkit to minimize the impact and consequences of disruptions, whether natural or man-made, whether accidental or malicious, whether known or unknown.

# 3.2. Enhancing Resilience in Different Navy Theaters

The following sections will explore specific ways in which the Navy can incorporate resilience-based design in some of the theaters in it operates. The future is on surface and underwater operations; while the USN also operates in the air and on land, its fundamental domain is the sea. The strategies for enhancing resilience outlined below can be further investigated and researched by the USN and potentially implemented; they can also serve as jumping off points for brainstorming other ideas and strategies. Additionally, these strategies are applicable not just for the USN but also for NATO's marine operations as a whole.

# 3.2.1. Surface

Most Navy operations take place on the surface of the sea; as such, resilience is especially important for this theater. Nemeth et al. (2015) engaged in a project to incorporate human-centered design into the acquisition of support systems [162]. Using cognitive systems engineering methods, they assessed and analyzed the needs of the two most complex of the Navy Expeditionary Combat Command groups [162]. The team then developed a Command and Control (C2) system and display prototype [162].
