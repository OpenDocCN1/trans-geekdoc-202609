+   * Hurricane Katrina (USA) – The costliest storm in US history at the time (Gibbons, 2019), Hurricane Katrina overwhelmed flood management structures around New Orleans, Louisiana in August 2005, leading to widespread inundation of communities, property destruction, and loss of lives. Some 1,200 people died and 400,000 were permanently displaced by flooding. As the disaster unfolded, large numbers of citizens with recreational boats recognized the need and opportunity to help fellow citizens, approximately 600,000 of whom had not chosen to evacuate before the storm. Between 350-400 people responded to former state senator Nick Gatreaux's request over radio and television to help the effort, the Cajun Navy matched the property (approximately $125b USD) of Hurricane Katrina. The group once again reached 8,000-10,000 people (Dept of Homeland Security, 2018)

+   * Northern Ghana – Otsuki, Jasaw, and Lolig (2018) share four stories of farmers in northern Ghana who created climate adaptation strategies in the context of their communities. In the face of land and water scarcity, poorly developed markets, and especially established constraints, farmers used experiential learning techniques including work (in other regions) as migrant farm labor; planting different or diverse crops that favor various conditions; offering products such as butter to different markets and challenging expectations—especially associated with gender. Each farmer learned from his/her experiences and built upon early success to improve community or diversified capabilities. Interactions with others in their families invoked skepticism and reinforcement; eventually, the effort informed new local practices and cultivated more resilient socio-economic ecosystems of farmers, families, suppliers, et cetera.

+   * Caggiano, Italy – In 2011, in this small community near Salerno began a series of place-based projects that increased community cohesion, self-reliance, and prosperity. Salvia and Quarantine (2017) report that the initiatives precipitated from a shared dissatisfaction with the tactic of relying upon “top-down” governments, so thespeople took into their own hands. Being with a project to promote the use of locally grown foods into the school menu, community partnerships developed that evolved to community water distribution and food processing projects, and ultimately promoted prominent restaurants, food festivals, and a winery. The researchers found that the efforts not only promoted self-reliance, but led to streamlining of resilience-related factors such as sense of community, trust, and education.
