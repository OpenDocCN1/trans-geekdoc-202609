+   * Studies to human factors have shown that cognitive biases, born of hierarchical power structures present in the airline cockpit, can result in airline disasters by compromised communication and lack of action. During the approach of Air flight 6560, the relatively inexperienced first officer noticed that the aircraft was veering off-course. The captain—who, unlike the first officer, had flown many times over the Arctic region—believed the instruments were adversely impacted by the proximity of the north magnetic pole. Assuming it was the first officer’s lack of experience affecting his perception of the situation, the captain ignored the repeated warnings and suggestions to go around. The mistake proved fatal to the captain, the first officer and 10 passengers when the plane crashed. The higher one perceives oneself to be in the hierarchy, the less negative feedback individual will accept. Moreover, the greater power imbalance between the captain and the first officer, the higher the risk of false-consensus bias (as an authoritative position based on belief that approval is automatically granted by the less experienced pilot) in the cockpit. Communication that validates and assesses personal perspectives to a given situation is critical to informed decision-making in any context (Fabre, 2018).
* Historically, economic development policy neglects the assets within communities which offer multiple opportunities to build upon local lands and resources. When people reside there that they have no voice in decisions which affect them either directly or indirectly, this intervention is viewed with distrust. As highlighted above, the rural township of Cigliano, was frustrated by a centralized policy process, which imposed a primary sector dominance. Local authorities have the power and funding to prevent misuse of resources that are crucial to rural development (Johnson, 2001). Because no two communities share the same profiles, efforts to capture the unique qualities of place help avoid the opportunity cost of relying upon top-down or one-size-fits-all policy implementation.
