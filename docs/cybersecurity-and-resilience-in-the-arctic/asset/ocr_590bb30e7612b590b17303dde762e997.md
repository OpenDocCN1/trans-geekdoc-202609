# 2.1. Classical Constructs

The concept of resilience brings another disaster of cybersecurity infrastructure in Arctic, demographic risks, environmental risk and climate risks. Existing disaster risks are increased by climate change and geopolitical changes – as well as climate change causing slow-onset impacts such as temperature increase, sea-level rise and therefore, environmental changes generally. Geopolitical changes are unpredictable things.

Infrastructure decisions must be informed by an understanding of the likely frequency and magnitude of climate events, geopolitical events and how they are likely to change in the future. It is necessary to develop a few scenarios of the changes. It is important to understand what the possible future changes may be that unpredictable disasters, slow onset impacts or recurring seasonal events. This requires an understanding of climate and disaster risk, and an understanding of current weather patterns, because special equipment for Arctic cybersecurity should develop.

Climate change models predict significant shifts worldwide in temperature, storm surges, icing, increases in sudden and catastrophic weather events, and in sea level rise. These changes impact a particular place upon weather systems and topology. This will result in different decisions for the design, operation, maintenance and upgrading of Arctic cybersecurity infrastructure in different locations.

Disasters are never solely natural. They are the product of how incidents interact with such as a lack of preparedness, poor capacity and adaptation to Arctic environment, weak resilience, as well as exposure and vulnerability to hazards. These are, to a great extent, dependent upon development decisions and subsequent human activity [1,2].

# 2.2. Formalized presentation of information security risk level

The complex risk security assessment includes risks associated with the losses of confidentiality, integrity and availability. Therefore the complex security assessment R is some combination of prime risks: boundary risks of confidially $r_c$, boundary risk of integrity $r_i$ and boundary risk of availability $r_A$. If risks $r_c$, $r_i$, $r_A$ are independent.
