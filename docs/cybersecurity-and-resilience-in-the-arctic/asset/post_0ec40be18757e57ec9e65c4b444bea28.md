The concept of resilience brings another disaster of cybersecurity infrastructure in Arctic, demographic risks, environmental risk and climate risks. Existing disaster risks are increased by climate change and geopolitical changes – as well as climate change causing slow-onset impacts such as temperature increase, sea-level rise and therefore, environmental changes generally. Geopolitical changes are unpredictable things.

Infrastructure decisions must be informed by an understanding of the likely frequency and magnitude of climate events, geopolitical events and how they are likely to change in the future. It is necessary to develop a few scenarios of the changes. It is important to understand what the possible future changes may be that unpredictable disasters, slow-onset impacts or recurring seasonal events. This requires an understanding of climate and disaster risk, and an understanding of current weather patterns, because special equipment for Arctic cybersecurity should develop.

Climate change models predict significant shifts worldwide in temperature, storm surges, icing, increases in sudden and catastrophic weather events, and in sea level rise. These changes impact a particular place upon weather systems and topology. This will result in different decisions for the design, operation, maintenance and upgrading of Arctic cybersecurity infrastructure in different locations.

Disasters are never solely natural. These are, to a great extent, dependent upon development decisions and subsequent human activity [1,2].

### 2.2. Formalized presentation of information security risk level

The complex risk security assessment includes risks associated with the losses of confidentiality, integrity and availability. Therefore the complex security assessment R is some combination of prime risks: boundary risks of confidentiality $r_c$, boundary risk of integrity $r_i$ and boundary risk of availability $r_A$. If risks $r_c$, $r_i$, $r_A$ are independent.

### 2.3. Linear random processes in resilience Arctic applications

The effective decision of problem from "fail safe" to "safe to fail" design of cybersecurity system is necessary to elaborate complex models of Arctic resilience.

There are many mathematical models for Arctic resilience may be elaborated. We have considered some of them. For example, mathematical model called black-box models. Linear random process is used for elaboration of the model [6].

A linear random process (LRP) is a functional of the following

> $\zeta(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi(\tau,t)d\eta(\tau), \quad t \in (-\infty,\infty),\quad (4)$

where $\varphi(\tau,t) \in L_2(-\infty,\infty)$ with respect to $\tau$ for all $t$ is a non-stochastic real function; $\eta(\tau)$, $\eta(0) \in (-\infty,\infty)$ is a stochastically continuous real process with independent increments that is often called a generating process. While solving many problems including statistical simulation it is convenient to consider the LRP as a response of a linear filter with the impulse transient function $\varphi(\tau,t)$ on the action of the white noise $\eta'(\tau)$. It is understood that the $\eta'(\tau)$ is a generalized derivative of the corresponding process with independent increments.
