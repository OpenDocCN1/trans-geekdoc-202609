# 1. Introduction

Nowadays there is a problem of trust and transparency in any project with a large number of participants. In cryptology, there is the problem of the Byzantine generals: The interaction of remote servers who received orders from one center. Some subscribers, including the center, can be intruders. It was solved as a general case using the blockchain in the bitcoin network when the number of generals (network nodes) is unlimited and can be dynamically changed.

# 2. Blockchain and Modern Decision Making Challenges

The example of solving the problem of the Byzantine generals, we see that the goal is not to defeat the enemy, but of reaching consensus [1] in the network (either attack or withdraw together). The key decision we are observing in the cryptocurrency market is to achieve agreement and interaction between unfamiliar participants in the network at a distance. Lampert L. [2] proposed a solution algorithm for a special case when the number of generals is limited and cannot dynamically change. The algorithm reduces the problem of *m traitors among n generals* for the case of *m-1* traitor.

An example of a case to solve the problem of Byzantine generals.

The Byzantine army consists of n legions, each of which is commanded by its own general. The army is controlled by the Commander-in-chief, to whom the generals obey. Communication is through messages. Any of the n generals or the commander in chief may be a m traitor.
