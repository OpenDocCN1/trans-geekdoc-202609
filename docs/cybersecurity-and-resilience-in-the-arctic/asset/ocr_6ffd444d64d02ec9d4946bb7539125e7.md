**Abstract:** Arctic regions are gaining external attention as climate change and technology advancements expand opportunities for economic exploitation in such domains as information, technologies, and mining. Media coverage and political conflict whilst highlight ecological change such as melting glaciers and permafrost, shifting precipitation, and related ecological interactions. International and national efforts are urgent to define and implement policies and to mobilize resources to mitigate impacts, but Arctic citizens themselves represent the key to resilience in the face of such irreversible change to natural and socio-economic landscapes. This chapter transcends the predominant analytical domain of competences and decision analysis to explore how people live and interact within their communities to sense, recover, and adapt to their changing world; the essence of resilience. We show how important concepts of creativity, hope, meaning, and reflexivity allow people to interact and synthesize effective place-based, resilient capacity.

**Keywords:** Agency, arctic, community, creativity, hope, meaning, place-based, reflexivity, rural, Sisu

# 1. Introduction

Sisu . . "strength of will, determination, perseverance, and acting rationally in the face of adversity of Finnish people" (Finnish, n.d.) is a culturally-rich term that cannot be translated simply into other languages. Online translators suggest "driving force", "enthusiast", or "dedicated person" as English alternatives for the word ildjsjel (Wiktionary, 2017); but the literal translation, "souls of fire" believes the mental and emotional sense of positive positivity, community contribution, and leadership that the term imports to Norwegians, especially in small villages (Magis, 2010). Every language comes with underlying cultural implications, but these simple examples indicate the richness of meaning es among rural cultures, and Arctic communities in particular. Moreover, they are the tip of the iceberg in terms of how deeply resilience is rooted in the hearts and cohorts of citizens.
