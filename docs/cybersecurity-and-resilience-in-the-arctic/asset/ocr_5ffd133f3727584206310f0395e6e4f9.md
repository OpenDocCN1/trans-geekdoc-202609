282 A.C. Mersky et al. / Quantitative Modeling of Supply Chain Resilience: Literature Review

Many investigators have reviewed resilience in the Supply Chain context, either in focus or in part. Table 1 summarizes 12 review papers on the state of supply chain resilience. It notes whether work uses the National Academy of Sciences (NAS) or a similar definition of resilience as the basis for analysis; whether the work addresses the modeling/simulation of supply chains; whether the paper addresses supply chain dependence on other networks, such as Command and Control or transportation networks; and whether the paper addresses the usage of metrics to measure supply chain resilience. A smaller subset of literature review is focused on representation of the supply chain as part of a larger system that includes supply nodes, locations, delivery paths, or transactions. This review indicates that the majority of reviewed supply chain resilience literature tends to ignore furthermore supply chain system and network views or to implicitly integrate some portion of other networks into supply chain components and models. This despite the fact that supply chain networks or graphs is common practice and is recommended by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (Boyens et al. 2015). Explicit acknowledgement of these networks is conceptually accounted for in more qualitative supply chain resilience literature, but these papers do not enable objective quantitative measurement of resilience. Without proper modeling of the supply chain itself, supply chain management and resilience modeling of the supply chain cannot be achieved.
