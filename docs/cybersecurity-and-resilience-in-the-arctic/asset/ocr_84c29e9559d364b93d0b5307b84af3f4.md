The twelve principles go on to further support and outline ways in to achieve these
objectives, emphasizing the priority for securing Iceland's position as an Arctic
costal state, while also improving the wellbeing of Arctic populations and advancing
the awareness and knowledge available in relation to northern issues. These twelve
principles can be summarised into the following:

+   1. Strengthening international cooperation, particularly with the Faroe Islands and
Greenland in order to promote and strengthen the Arctic Council as a
mechanism for resolving issues, primarily using UNCLOS as a legal foundation
in this otherwise largely soft-law regime.
2. Promoting Iceland as a coastal state in the Arctic region, alongside
increasing an understanding that the Arctic region extends to parts of the
Northern Atlantic Ocean
3. To promote and enhance Icelandic knowledge of Arctic and the global\importance of the Arctic region, with aims to utilize Iceland as a venue for
international meetings, conferences and dialogues related to the Arctic.
4. Doing whatever possible to prevent and reduce the impacts and effects of
human induced climate change, in attempt to improve social wellbeing,
preserve cultures and languages, life, and rights of northern populations.
5. Safeguarding against militarization of the Arctic, and instead promoting
security interest through civilian means and international cooperation.

The key takeaway from both the report and parliamentary resolution is Iceland's focus
and promotion of multilateral, international cooperation with neighbouring countries,
using the Arctic Council as a mechanism for this, but also excluding the Barents
Euro-Arctic Council. Moreover, the report illuminates that Icelandic foreign policy is
well aware of Arctic and northern issues, making it one of its key areas moving
forward.
