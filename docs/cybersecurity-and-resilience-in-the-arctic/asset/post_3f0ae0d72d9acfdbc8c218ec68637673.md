There are many factors affecting the future of the Arctic region and its ecosystem. The political issues of the Arctic region, climate issues, and various conflicts of interest between the various groups are types of factors. Figure 9 represents the rapid change affecting Arctic structures, the lives of the people, the culture and environment of the Arctic region. We need to consider these factors of change and their effects on various activities in more detail, so that we can accurately plan the structures, service needs, and developments of the Arctic region's ecosystem.

In order to provide services to the right place and at the right time over such a large area, it is useful to group the Arctic region's services into different segments in which the current structures and services can be located. Arctic region's structures can be divided into different service sectors, as shown in Figure 10: infrastructure, energy, buildings and homes, mobility, telecommunications, and private and public services. Each set of operations has its own service and communication needs depending on the user group. Groups include, for example, the indigenous peoples, immigrants, peoples living in a particular area, design and maintenance personnel, financial staff, telecom and service operator staff, virtual service providers and operators, state actors, administrative actors,

In order for the Arctic regions as a whole to function properly and to be able to provide citizens with the necessary digital services, the information systems in the various service sectors to be able to work together and be able to exchange information with each other not just horizontally but also vertically so that the services of the Arctic regions can be implemented flexibly and efficiently. The information systems used by different sectors or the smaller entities within them, are often in different phases of their life cycle. In the future Arctic region can be seen as one entity, in which cooperative efforts between different countries and user groups should be made due to a difficult and sometimes critical living environment. This means that everyone should be able to access the same services and be able to use them in a critical situation or to obtain information in some way from other countries in order to obtain assistance in certain cases. This means that we need both horizontal and vertical communications opportunities between different user groups. This also means that we must perform cyber analyses both horizontally and vertically on those infrastructures, communications systems, and services. Consequently, the integration between information systems may not succeed for many technical reasons. Data models, operating systems, management systems, and application interfaces may not work well enough. Their coordination and the success of the data exchange through integration environments are very challenging. The system solutions in place can also be delivered, by different vendors and may have their own facto standards that are incompatible with the equipment and systems from other vendors. This may result in the use of certain of platform solutions to guarantee the functionality of some particular system until renewal is worthwhile or until it becomes timely with the end of its lifecycle. The security solutions for systems and services may also be lead to practical challenges; for example, it may be impossible to combine the various systems (make federations) and consequently to make different services compatible. Each entity may also contain information at different security levels, which must be properly protected and controlled. In the future development of Arctic infrastructure, services and governance, we must also look at green values like energy efficient solutions, there for international laws and regulations, EU directives and regulations, national laws and regulations, us requirements, dependencies, and so on, as shown in Figure 11. As a result of the ongoing developments in the Arctic region, people and systems will produce huge amounts of information in the future that need to be processed and stored. However, not all of the technical solutions of the new service environments may be in accordance with international standards, such as interfaces with telecommunication and service networks. At the same time, services based on technically outdated solutions and services using new technology are also in use. Future information and communication systems must be designed and adapted to work in this challenging business environment where security threats and cyber threats are present.
