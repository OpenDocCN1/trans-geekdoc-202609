# Some Mathematical Models Which Could Be Applied to Arctic Cybersecurity Systems

Valerij ZVARITCH a
ᵃ National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine (NASU)

Abstract. Some historical information and photographs about Arctic exploration are presented. Formalized presentation of information security risk levels is considered. Linear random processes that can be used to construct a cybersecurity system in the Arctic and some of their properties are presented Definitions and some properties of linear ARMA fields are also shown.

Keywords. Resilience models, information security risks, linear random processes, linear ARMA fields.

# ## Introduction

The Arctic is a very unique region. Despite similarities in both weather conditions and the conditions for human habitation, it is ultimately not very similar to the Antarctic.
The Arctic refers to the frozen ocean with unique properties of underwater flows, ice drifts, and hummocking processes. Countries that border the Arctic take a great interest in it, as do other countries, for example China. Interest in the Arctic is associated with the huge deposits of minerals underneath the sea floor and is also connected with its geopolitical position.

Resilience - climate, environmental, and natural disaster risks - is considered a goal of proactive action regarding and existing Arctic cybersecurity infrastructure. This includes the design of infrastructure to ensure resilience and continued operation during conflicts, though it is noted that some Arctic cybersecurity infrastructure can withstand targeted attacks [18].

Arctic research has no history. For more than 50 years. A 50-year-old trip that was presented by a participant of the Workshop, D. V. Zvaritch, showed a certain specificity of the expeditions. These expeditions had no GPS, computers, Internet, and of course, there had no cybersecurity systems (see Figures 1, 2, 3, 4).

But challenges of the expedition are taking place now. Special staff of tough, logistic, medical service, communication systems, ice humming, ecology (first of all there are polar bears) evaluation methods of staff - relevant now.

Arctic cybersecurity infrastructure will be affected by environmental and climate risks, including disasters. When to plan develop Arctic cybersecurity infrastructure needs to be understood over the full lifespan of the infrastructure.
