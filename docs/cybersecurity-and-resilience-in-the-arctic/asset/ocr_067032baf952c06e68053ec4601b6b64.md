Researchers mostly use optimization for their "Command and Control Representation" and overwhelmingly use either case studies or prior defined "Set Lists" of events for "Disruptions Representation." While "Transportation Network Representation" is more balanced than "Command and Control Representation" the graph method was still significantly less common than the other three methods. This stands in contrast to "Command and Control Representation" and "Supply Chain Network Representation," where the more complicated optimization and graph methods are most common, "Resilience Characteristics" also shows a trend of increasing in the later stages of resilience, with adaptation being investigated in half of the reviewed papers. The classifications of each individual paper are included in the Appendix. The Greek review resulted in similar trends and confirmed the review of all published research.

# 3.2 Defining and Characterizing Resilience

Many papers fail to explicitly define resilience and there is not a broad consensus among those that do. Almost all authors who explicitly define resilience agree that at least the first three phases of resilience (Plan, Absorb, and Recover (National Research Council 2012)) are important, but most focus on modeling only some of the phases of resilience for a component of the system. The "Adapt" is most often used in the context of the Recovery phase, but several papers explicitly acknowledge adaptation in terms of a process.
