For the first filtering stage, the papers were filtered for prominence in the field based on a combination of citation numbers and year of publication, with older papers requiring more citations to be included. This was done to filter for papers which continued to perform well (i.e., maintain relevance) over time. The minimum number of citations needed to be included in the final literature review, by year of citation, is summarized in Table 2.

For the second filtering step, literature reviews were set aside for the backward search, and not included in the general results. Duplicates were excluded and defined as sharing the majority of authors, published in the same year, at multiple conferences, or appearing annually for multiple years. The authors vetted these duplicates to determine if they effectively covered the same material or substantially different different findings. For duplicate groups only one paper was included in the final literature review; with journal articles retained over conference proceedings, which were in turn kept over chapters; otherwise the first published paper was selected.

The remaining papers were screened for relevance. Only papers focused on supply chains and resilience modeling and simulation were included. For example, papers about organization characteristics that affect resilience that did not include a supply chain model were excluded. These papers often utilized surveys of managers and executive officers over aspects of their organizations and used these to correlate with resilience, without incorporating models or simulations. Resilience screening was done by two of the authors, who coordinated with each other. Overlap in reviewed papers was used to ensure consistency in relevancy designations.

In phase 2, additional papers were added through forward backward search. This process, as described by (Levy and Ellis 2006), improves the rigor of the initial search by revealing the search space as the keywords evolve and continue, rather than a single moment in time. The existence of significant differences in relevant papers found in this phase may also help identify inadequacies in the initial search. Literature reviews that passed phase 1 criteria were not reviewed themselves, but instead used for backward search. Literature reviews were excluded to focus on papers presenting original research and supply chain modeling methodology.
