## 3.2 Defining and Characterizing Resilience

Many papers fail to explicitly define resilience and there is not a broad consensus among those that do. Almost all authors who explicitly define resilience agree that at least the first three phases of resilience (Plan, Absorb, and Recover (National Research Council 2012)) are important, but most focus on modeling only some of the phases of resilience for a component of the system. The "Adapt" is most often used in the context of the Recovery phase, but several papers explicitly acknowledge adaptation in terms of a process.

## 3.3 Supply Chain Network Representation

The Supply Chain Network Representation is the method in which the physical networks compromising the supply chain are represented. This shows the locations of the goods at each step from representation, to transportation, and finally to distribution. Each of these phases, extraction, transformation, and distribution may take multiple steps. This can be modeled as a linear process, where goods must go through steps, with no option, as shown in Figure 3A; a design process with multiple options for some portion of steps, as shown in Figure 3B; or a complex graph where outputs from later phases may be components in earlier stages, as shown in Figure 3C. In the reviewed literature, Modeling Supply Chains as complex graphs was the most common approach, with 20 of the 52 reviewed papers utilizing them, followed by branching networks, at 16 reviewed papers. Linear supply chain networks, while not uncommon, were the least of the main groups with 11 reviewed papers modeling them.

Complex and complete real-world supply chains are complex graphs, as raw material extraction does require finish goods. Modeling this, however, can be computationally expensive. Branching models of supply chains may be useful when modeling the resilience of any component of the supply chain that a single actor can control, over which a control may be more in its own processes than those of others. A strictly linear graph is of limited use when measuring resilience as any disruption completely completes performance. Supply chain graphs have also been generated from Economic Input-Output (EIO) models. EIO uses national-level data to connect monetary flows among industries (BEA). These can be used to generate supply chain graphs for modeling.
