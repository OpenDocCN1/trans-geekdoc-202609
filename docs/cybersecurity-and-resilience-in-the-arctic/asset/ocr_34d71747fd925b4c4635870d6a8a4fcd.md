# M. Pollock et al. / Analytical Strategies to Operationalize Arctic Infrastructure 201

In the modern era, data are more important than ever. The twenty-first century has seen a massive expansion and adoption of complex interconnected systems that simplified the management of processes and created efficiencies across vast expanses. But development of these systems has brought with looming challenges; simplifications for users and operators have inadvertently exacerbated vulnerabilities, where targeted disruptions of critical susceptibilities in these technologies can significantly affect function, even cause system failures. In an environment as extreme as the Arctic, these types of disturbances could reduce economic output while also dictating life or death.

Comprehensive and accurate knowledge of climatic conditions, incoming threats, and available resources is critical to any mission in the Circumpolar North - preservation of collected data to these topics is especially vital. Interim deterioration or faults could miss an incoming attack, or potentially create an exploitable vulnerability allowing systems to be compromised. There is hence an identification for the coexistence of risk management and resilience-based strategies, where the components of systems are hardened to ensure resilience to vulnerabilities exploited through natural processes or a malicious manner, but also where the system as a whole is designed using an approach that is predicated upon the ideals of rapid and sustainable recovery from partial or total system failure.

This chapter explores six themes that have addressed the group discussions:

+   * What does "Arctic resilience" mean, and how is it validated?
* Responding to Arctic incidents and disasters poses a substantial challenge that could be eased by enhancing predictive capabilities for Arctic installations. What technologies could improve the ability to foresee hazards for Arctic facilities due to weather, adversarial attack, or human error?
* What types of information Arctic planners need to ensure they do not undertake a significantly disruptive or dangerous mission?
* "Arctic resilience" be predicted in the laboratory? What modifications to current equipment testing methods are warranted to account for Arctic conditions (e.g., cold)?
* One of the most effective methods for recovering from a cyber breach is to have saved copies of information as backup. In the Arctic, where resources are already limited, storing on-site backups of all information may be infeasible or impractical. What steps could be taken to increase Arctic data storage potential, or where could data be stored to expedite resuming operations after a breach?
* As expanding commercial and military operations to the Arctic becomes more profitable, competition for Arctic projects is anticipated to intensify. How can different approaches to the same mission be compared to determine which provides the best value? What criteria are most important and relevant for Arctic operations?

In considering these themes, we seek to identify relevant information and research purposes as well as key data gaps, prospective methods for evaluating resilience in the context of the Arctic, and propose further research in order to fill the gaps.
