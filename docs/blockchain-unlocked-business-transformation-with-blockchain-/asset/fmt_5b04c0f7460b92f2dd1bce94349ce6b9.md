
# Thirdly: Fraud in Real Estate

As a real estate investor, you have to deal with the rampant fraud that exists in the industry. Agents and other players often misrepresent or fail to disclose material facts about the assets they are selling or facilitating a lease. After all, their motivation is to increase the size of their takings. Indeed, the details about a single piece of an asset can change as many times as the number of potential buyers or lessees. Also, many cases of scammers compromising land title registries and misleading investors to send money to wrong accounts have been reported. In one case in Washington DC, US, a couple was tricked into wiring US$1.57 million to con artists. It turned out that the criminals had accessed the title registry servers and launched what appeared like a genuine transaction^[1](#ch16.xhtml#ch16fn1). According to one report by the CIA, Americans lose at least US$150 million annually to fraud in the real estate sector^[2](#ch16.xhtml#ch16fn2).

# Fourth: Asset Liquidity

Being able to turn an asset quickly into cash is an essential consideration for many investors. At the moment, real estate is a low liquidity market. According to Zillow, an American online real estate database company, it takes an average of about three months before a listed house is sold. Many investors keep away from the market because of this reality^[3](#ch16.xhtml#ch16fn3).

# Fifth: Asset Management Challenges

As an investor, it is really challenging to manage a real estate asset after you have acquired it. This is especially so if you do not have the necessary experience or are busy with other engagements. Finding the right agent to manage the asset on your behalf is a complicated process, especially to avoid being a victim of fraud. Of course, the management not only includes the physical care of the asset, but also has to cater for the needs of those who utilise it and pay the appropriate fees and rates to the authorities.

Blockchain technology can fix most of these issues, making the real estate market-friendly, cheaper, safer, and convenient to everyone. It could significantly transform the industry in a way that benefits all. And blockchain startups and projects are already building various kinds of solutions for the market.

## Blockchain Benefits for Real Estate

What exactly can blockchain technology do to the real estate market? The blockchain offers the following six benefits to the sector: (1) a better title system, (2) a better way of listing assets in the market, (3) tools to facilitate peer-to-peer trading, (4) tools for managing asset portfolios, (5) better mortgage financing options, and (6) more efficient but less costly escrow services.

Let’s go through each of these six points in detail.

### Better Title System

The title (deed) is a critical document as it proves ownership of a piece of real estate. For centuries, a title was a simple piece of paper that carried the real estate number, the name of the owner, and the signatures of witnesses and notaries who observed and supervised the transfer. It also contained the seal of the official who oversaw the transfer, whether through sale or inheritance.

As sophisticated printing capabilities became more accessible, the paper alone ceased being authoritative enough to prove ownership. A registry with the records on ownership became a critical part of the title system. The files are usually in the custody of a third-party authority (usually a public office), which would confirm the authenticity of the certificate and prove that its bearer is indeed who owns the piece of real estate in question.

The land registries have undergone major transformation over the years. And the most significant one recently is digitisation. Information technology has made it faster and easier to access and query real estate registries from anywhere in the globe. However, like other information and communications technology (ICT) systems, digital land registries remain susceptible to certain vulnerabilities. In particular, they come with numerous single points of failure that can easily be compromised. Hackers have been able to break into systems easily, making changes that end up scamming unsuspecting investors. The records are not safe even from those with the responsibility to protect them. With some level of inducement, the staff at the registries, especially in developing countries, often tamper with the records in a way that benefits those who pay them and that disenfranchises rightful owners of real estate assets. These are ills explored in the earlier chapters of this book.

Considering all the necessary and critical processes to buy or sell a piece of real estate, digital land title systems have not made investing in the sector efficient enough. The systems still need improvement, especially through a technology that offers efficiency, more security, and even interoperability.

We have already seen what the blockchain can do for ICT systems in other areas. What it offers land title systems is similar. First, blockchain makes the systems secure. In particular, it makes it hard for a hacker to attack the system, given that they have to be successful at taking control of many independent computers in a peer-to-peer network, which is near impossible. It also makes it a lot easier to prove ownership without having to go through a third party. On the blockchain, the title acquires some level of sovereignty (existing free of any particular authority) along with high provability. The title also becomes immutable. No one can arbitrarily alter its details.

Besides, titles on blockchain become even more potent when combined with other applications possible through this technology, such as smart contracts—a blockchain application explored extensively in this book. This combination makes trading and managing real estate a lot more convenient.

Startups and projects are already building title systems on the blockchain. One of those is Bitland, a startup founded in 2013. In Ghana, where Bitland is headquartered, close to 80 percent of the land is without title and is community-owned. This exposes those who live on it to possible dispossession. Using blockchain, the company plans to help communities in west Africa register and get titles for their lands for the first time.

In the United States, several projects have been set up to put real estate titles on the blockchain. In late 2018, Overstock.com, Inc., through its land management subsidiary known as Medici Land Governance, signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with Teton County, Wyoming, US, to design and build a blockchain-based real estate title system. Earlier in the same year, Propy, Inc., a California-based blockchain startup, announced a pilot phase of a similar project carried out in collaboration with the City of South Burlington, in Vermont.

### Better Way of Listing Assets in the Market

When it comes to real estate listing, the technology fixes many of the challenges experienced on centralised listing platforms. Listing is a critical component in the real estate markets, without which purchasers would not know of the existence of properties on sale and sellers would not have a channel to reach possible buyers. In the centralised systems, however, a listing is often disjointed from the other systems that facilitate the sale and lease transactions, such as financiers, escrow, and the title system. This often leads to poor presentation or a misrepresentation of the listed real estate assets.
