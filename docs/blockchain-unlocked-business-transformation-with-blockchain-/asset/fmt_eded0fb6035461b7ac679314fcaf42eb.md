
# The blockchain can fix some of the challenges that service providers like Airbnb are facing

For example, the host and the guest exchanging keys is always a challenging part of an Airbnb transaction. Where the host is not around to receive the guest, in many instances they are forced to leave the key under the doormat or with the neighbour. That is a huge gap in this otherwise very convenient service.

Slock.it, a German blockchain startup, has designed a blockchain solution that could fix this problem. It comes in the form of a smart lock that you open using a private key on the Ethereum blockchain. With the lock installed on the door, the host does not need to exchange a physical key. A smart contract guides the transaction on the blockchain; once the guest pays, the smart contract provides them with a key in the form of a passkey (a digital key), which works only for the duration of the stay. Once the agreed time of the visit elapses, the key ceases to function. The host no longer needs to worry about guests who might overstay or having to be present at check out. This is just one way the technology could help with managing real estate.

Some developers are already designing and building platforms on the blockchain that could give hosts and guests a better experience than what they get on Airbnb. One such blockchain-based home-sharing platform is Bee Token, which raised about US$15 million in an ICO in January 2018. The decentralised platform facilitates transactions in cryptocurrency.

## Better mortgage financing options

The majority of homebuyers cannot pay cash; homes are often a costly investment. For this reason, mortgages are a huge business. Financial institutions pay the entire cost of the real estate asset to the seller on behalf of the buyer. The buyer then commits in a contract to repay the financial institution in instalments that are often spread out in an extended period, sometimes decades. In return, the mortgage company makes a massive return in the form of interests and transaction fees.

The blockchain technology is offering new ways of mortgage financing that is not only cheaper for the person taking the loan, but also opens up the market for the small investors to put in their money and generate revenue. Today, only banks and financial institutions can fund a mortgage. Through decentralised financial (DeFi) applications, the technology makes it possible to get mortgage funding from ordinary people who want to invest in such an instrument.

On the blockchain, the mortgage can be split into tokens, which are then sold to those who want to finance a mortgage with the hope of getting returns over a long period. The entire transaction is set on a smart contract so that every time a repayment goes out, the holders of the mortgage tokens receive what is equal to their part of the mortgage.

## More efficient escrow services

Finally, how does the blockchain improve real estate transactions, in particular escrow services? As seen in the finance and banking sector (in [Chapter 9](#ch09.xhtml)), the escrow service is very critical in real estate transactions. It protects buyers, sellers, and the financiers from possibly being defrauded. This service, however, does not come cheap. On average, the escrow service can cost up to five percent of the value of the transacted asset. Through smart contract applications, the blockchain has made possible escrow that is sovereign and free to use. Two individuals can trade in a peer-to-peer transaction without involving a third party for the escrow service. The smart contract can hold both the title and the agreed payment and swap them when the counterparties meet the pre-agreed conditions.

Projects are already working towards providing blockchain escrow services to the real estate industry. One such project is Reasi by a startup based in California. The platform would facilitate end-to-end real estate transactions and eliminate the need to rely on third-party service providers, in particular escrow companies.

## Challenges in using blockchain

Blockchain has a few obstacles to overcome so that it can achieve its potential in the real estate sector. The first challenge is for regulations around the world to be aligned to the possible blockchain technical capacities. Without the recognition of law, transactions carried out on the blockchain would be null and void, such as decentralised mortgage arrangements and tokenised assets on the blockchain. Lack of recognition would slow real estate from embracing blockchain. Another challenge is the lack of interoperability. The title system has to talk to the listing platform, and both have to be linked to the trading exchange as well as the escrow service. Moreover, different financing applications and centralised real estate systems need to communicate with the other platforms. Without extensive interoperability, real estate transactions will remain slow and inefficient.

Of course, the real estate application of the blockchain has to deal with the scalability issues that we have talked about in the other chapters. Given the size of the global real estate market, the number of transactions on the blockchain could become massive.

Last but not least, all stakeholders, including investors, developers, regulators, and others, have to have a change of mindset so that they can embrace the systems in the new technology. In particular, those who already benefit from the current system and are in the position to influence policy have to be won over.

In summary, the real estate sector stands to gain a lot from blockchain technology, not least in efficiency, lowered costs, more security, and increased transparency. The technology can provide a superior platform at every stage of real estate-related transactions. Indeed, for almost every aspect of real estate, startups are building blockchain solutions to change it^([5](#ch16.xhtml#ch16fn5)).
