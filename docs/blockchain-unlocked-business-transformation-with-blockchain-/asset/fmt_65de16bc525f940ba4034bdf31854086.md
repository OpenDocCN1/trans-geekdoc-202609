## Beyond 2020

In this chapter, we are going to look at some of the trends we should expect going forward. It is important to point out, however, that no one can accurately predict what exactly the technology is going to do or how it is going to turn out in the next five or ten years. Trying to do that is like predicting in the early 1990s that the Internet would bring us social media like Facebook, video platforms like YouTube, and video calling apps like Skype. Likely not even the experts who worked on the foundational infrastructure of the Internet could foresee what we have today. The technology just snowballed, and we discovered and invented things as we went along. In the same way, blockchain most likely will deliver services and solutions in the future that no one today can comprehend.

It is not impossible, however, to foresee particular trends, especially those that are likely to happen in the near future. We can also figure the general direction the technology might take in the long term though without clarity on the specifics.

### Bitcoin will be used everywhere

First on my list is the widespread adoption of the blockchain technology. The hype around cryptos seems to have cooled down, but serious research by governments, mainstream technology companies, and even small to medium enterprises is underway. Those who ignored or dismissed cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin in the early days are now giving the underlying technology a serious look. They are launching projects, companies, and consortiums to help them find out the value in it for their businesses, systems, and in processes. Of particular interest to them is the security, privacy, and automation that the technology can provide. It is also attractive to them that blockchain can create new possibilities and also facilitate the adoption of other emerging technologies such as AI and IoT. These multifaceted efforts are going to translate to a massive movement with regard to the adoption of the technology in the decade that starts in 2020^([1](#bm01.xhtml#ch19fn1)).

### Blockchain will be at the heart of global finance

The technology is moving into the centre of the global financial system. Blockchain offers the tools necessary to build currencies fit for the 21st century. Interestingly, the year 2020 began with the global coronavirus (Covid-19) pandemic. One of the situations cited as contributing to the spread of the diseases was physical cash. The World Health Organisation made a point of encouraging governments around the world to find a way to minimise the use of coins and paper bills, as one person with a deadly virus could easily infect many others by touching and passing along physical cash that could theoretically be contaminated. This is just another reason for the push towards digital currencies. And because of its qualities, Blockchain is hard to ignore in the building of a new financial system that relies less on physical currencies. Some countries, including China, which is one of the largest economies in the world, have put actions in place to see the implementation of a government-backed digital currency^([2](#bm01.xhtml#ch19fn2)).

Nevertheless, blockchain is also going to continue challenging the idea that only the state can issue currency. Beyond 2020, more and more people are going to appreciate that private currency, or currency not issued through the power of the state, can be as efficient and even better in some circumstances. In other words, we stand to see the use of both the state and non-state issued currency in almost the same measure going forward, and that is thanks to the tools made available by the blockchain to society.

### Increasing capacity of blockchain

In the short-term period coming after 2020, we are going to see a lot of effort go into scaling the capacity of blockchains. It is true that while it is revolutionary, the blockchain has exhibited weaknesses, one of which is low capacity and can be classified as critical. For example, Bitcoin has struggled to process more than ten transactions per second, which is very low, especially compared to payment services it seeks to replace. In late 2017, the Ethereum blockchain clogged because of increased activities from Cryptokitties, a gaming application launched on top of it. Blockchain developers have already begun focusing on this problem and are working on various ways of scaling the blockchain networks. Some of the solutions being designed to help with the scaling problem include Lightning Network, Raiden network, and Sidechains. The implementations of these scaling solutions are in the early stages as at the time of writing this book. Their full potential is most likely going to be realised in the decade after 2020. It turns out that it could well coincide with the increased adoption of this technology around the globe.

### More energy efficiency

Fourthly, we should watch out for the development and deployment of consensus protocols that are less energy-intensive. We have already seen in the previous chapters that the initial blockchain consensus protocols, in particular, the Proof of Work that Bitcoin and Ethereum use consumes a lot of energy. This makes them harmful to the environment due to their greenhouse gas emissions. The newer blockchains are designed to use less energy-intensive protocols, and this trend is going to grow over time. Indeed, Ethereum plans to shift to Proof of Stake by 2022. The Bitcoin community might likely resort to doing a hard fork and adopting a more environmentally friendly consensus protocol sometime in the future.
