# 6. Heatmaps

Heatmaps use color intensity to represent the magnitude of values in a matrix format. They are commonly used to visualize correlations between variables, where the color of each cell corresponds to the strength of the correlation between the corresponding variables.

# 7. Treemaps

Treemaps are used to visualize hierarchical data structures. They represent nested categories as a set of rectangles, where the size of each rectangle is proportional to a specified dimension (e.g., value or frequency). Treemaps are effective in displaying large amounts of hierarchical data in a compact and intuitive manner.

# 8. Bubble Charts

Bubble charts enhance scatter plots by introducing a third dimension through varying bubble sizes. This additional dimension can reveal insights about a third variable, such as the frequency or importance of data points.

# 9. Area Charts

Area charts are similar to line plots but with the area under the line filled. They are useful for visualizing cumulative data patterns, such as the total sales over time or the growth of a variable.

# 10. Pie Charts

Pie charts are used to visualize the proportional sizes of different categories. They are divided into slices, with each slice representing a category. While pie charts are commonly used, they are limited in their ability to display precise values and can be less effective when comparing many categories
