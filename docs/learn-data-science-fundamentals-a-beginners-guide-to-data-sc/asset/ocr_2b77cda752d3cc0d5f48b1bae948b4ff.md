# 1. Measures of Central Tendency

Measures of central tendency describe the center or typical value of a dataset. The most common measures are:

+   - **Mean:** The average of all values in the dataset, calculated by summing all the values and dividing by the number of observations. The mean is sensitive to outliers, which can skew the result.
- **Median:** The middle value when the dataset is ordered from least to greatest. If there is an even number of observations, the median is the average of the two middle values. The median is less affected by outliers and provides a better measure of central tendency for skewed distributions.
- **Mode:** The value that appears most frequently in the dataset. A dataset can have no mode, one mode, or multiple modes (bimodal or multimodal) if multiple values occur with the same highest frequency.

# 2. Measures of Variability

Measures of variability provide insights into the spread or dispersion of the data. They help to understand how much the values differ from the central tendency. Key measures include:

+   - **Range:** The difference between the maximum and minimum values in the dataset. The range provides a simple measure of variability but does not account for how data points are distributed between the extremes.
- **Variance:** The average of the squared deviations from the mean. Variance quantifies the degree of spread in the dataset. A higher variance indicates that the data points are more spread out from the mean.
