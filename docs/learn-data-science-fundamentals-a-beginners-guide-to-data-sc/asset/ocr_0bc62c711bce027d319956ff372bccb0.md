# Types of Missing Data

Understanding the nature of missing data is essential for selecting appropriate handling techniques. There are three primary types of missingness:

+   1. Missing Completely at Random (MCAR):** The missingness of data points is entirely random, and the probability of missing data is not related to any observed or unobserved data. In this case, the analysis remains unbiased, and standard statistical methods can be applied.
2. Missing at Random (MAR):** The probability of missing data is related to observed data but not to the missing data itself. For instance, if older individuals are less likely to respond to a survey, the missingness is related to age, which is observed. Techniques that utilize the observed data can help adjust for the missing values.
3. Missing Not at Random (MNAR):** The missing data is related to the missing values themselves. For example, individuals with higher incomes may be less likely to report their income. This type of missingness is the most challenging to handle, as it can introduce bias that cannot be corrected using observed data alone.
