# Exploring Data Distributions in Practice

To explore the distribution of a variable in a dataset, analysts typically follow these steps:

+   1. Create a histogram or density plot to visualize the overall shape and characteristics of the distribution.

+   2. Calculate summary statistics, such as the mean, median, mode, standard deviation, and interquartile range, to quantify the central tendency and spread of the data.

+   3. Assess the symmetry of the distribution by comparing the mean and median. If they are significantly different, the distribution is likely skewed.

+   4. Identify the presence of multiple modes in the distribution, which may suggest the existence of distinct subgroups within the data.

+   5. Detect potential outliers by examining the tails of the distribution or using techniques like box plots.

+   6. Compare the distribution to a known theoretical distribution, such as the normal distribution, to assess whether the data follows a specific pattern.

# 3.4 Dimensionality Reduction Techniques

Dimensionality reduction is a critical process in data analysis and machine learning that involves reducing the number of features (or dimensions) in a dataset while retaining as relevant information as possible. This technique is essential for improving model performance, reducing computational costs, and facilitating data visualization. Dimensionality reduction can be broadly categorized into two main approaches: feature selection and feature extraction. Each approach employs different techniques to achieve its goals.
