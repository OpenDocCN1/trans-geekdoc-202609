## 6.10 FUTURE FOR BIG DATA IN HEALTHCARE

Similarly, as officials in trade and manufacturing sectors announce that their big data initiatives have been effective and transformational, the outlook for healthcare is much more exciting. The following are a few areas where big data is likely to change healthcare [33].

Precision medicine, as imagined by the National Institutes of Health (NIH), tries to select one million individuals to volunteer their health data in the All of Us research program. This program is a part of the NIH Precision Medicine Initiative. According to the NIH, the initiative expects to see how a person's genetics, environment, and lifestyle can help decide the best way to prevent or treat illness. The long-term objectives of the Precision Medicine Initiative focus on bringing precision medicine to all areas of health and healthcare on a vast scale.

Wearable devices and IoT sensors, officially noted earlier, can potentially change healthcare for some patient populations and to enable individuals to stay healthy. A wearable device or sensor may one day provide an immediate, constant feed to a patient's electronic health records, which enables medical staff to monitor and then consult with the patient, either face to face or remotely [34].

Machine learning, a part of artificial intelligence, and one that relies upon big data are already helping doctors to enhance patient care. IBM with its Watson Health computer system has already partnered with Mayo Clinic, CVS Health, Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center, and others. Machine learning, together with healthcare, extends data analysis and duplicate guardians' capacity to improve patient care.
