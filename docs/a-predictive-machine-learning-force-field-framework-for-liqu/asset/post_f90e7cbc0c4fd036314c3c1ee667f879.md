## Architecture of GEL layers

In Fig. 2c, the GEL architecture used in BAMBOO draws inspiration mainly from the TorchM-NET. Beginning with the atom blocks $[Z_i]$ and the positions between atoms $[\mathbf{r}_{ij}]$, we first initialize the scalar representation $x_i^0$ and the vector representation $v_i^0$, as well as the scalar edge blocks. We feed $x_i^0$ and $v_i^0$ into the GEL layers to be transformed iteratively (c). As indicated in Fig. 1d, each GEL layer transforms each scalar block within a cut-off radius ($r=5$ Å). This figure illustrates how information is exchanged between nodes and edges. After the transformer block, on the scalar side, we update with $x_i^l$; the vector representation $v_i^l$ is transformed into a scalar $u_i^l$. The transformation from vector to scalar is achieved through the inner-product operation to maintain rotational invariance. Finally, we combine $x_i^l$ and $u_i^l$ to obtain the next-layer scalar representation $x_i^{l+1}$, and $v_i^l$ and $u_i^l$ to obtain the next-layer vector representation $v_i^{l+1}$. ## Ensemble distillation and density alignment

In Fig. 6, we show that density alignment effectively reduces density errors from 0.005 g/cm³ to 0.001 g/cm³. Importantly, the reduction in error achieved, using only 13 experimental data points included in the alignment, transfers to not only the tested molecules but also those with higher salt concentrations (Supplementary Figs 10a and 12a). For example, the density of molecules with hydrogen bonding is significantly improved.

To address this issue we aim to mitigate the discrepancy among MD simulations by using ensemble of MLFFs to predict the energy and forces of MD trajectories. We aggregate the mean and use this mean value to optimize the MLFFs. In Fig. 2 and Supplementary Table 7, we demonstrate the standard deviation of density predictions from five models by more than 50%, from 0.030 g/cm³ to 0.014 g/cm³. In particular, ensemble knowledge distillation does not require new DFT bodies.
