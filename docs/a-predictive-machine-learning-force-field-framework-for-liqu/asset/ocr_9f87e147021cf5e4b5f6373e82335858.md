# Ensemble distillation and density alignment

In Fig. 2, we illustrate the concept of ensemble knowledge-distillation in machine learning can introduce various challenges into MD simulations. With particular, if we use electrolytes that GNNs trained with different datasets report, the density results vary, yielding divergent macroscopic properties. This density fluctuation is an inherent process which, in particular, an MD simulation in liquids, can be treated as fluctuations of density around 0.004 g/cm³ across different random seeds of the BAMBO MLFFs.

In Fig. 6, we show that density alignment effectively reduces density errors from 0.005 g/cm³ to 0.001 g/cm³. Importantly, the reduction in error achieved, using only 13 experimental data points included in the alignment, transfers to not only the tested molecules but also those with higher salt concentrations (Supplementary Figs 10a and 12a). For example, the density of molecules with hydrogen bonding characteristics is significantly improved.

To address this issue we aim to mitigate the discrepancy among MD simulations by using ensemble of MLFFs to predict the energy and forces of MD trajectories. We aggregate the mean and use this mean value to optimize the MLFFs. In Fig. 2 and Supplementary Table 7, we demonstrate the standard deviation of density predictions from five models by more than 50%, from 0.030 g/cm³ to 0.014 g/cm³. In particular, ensemble knowledge distillation does not require new DFT bodies. By BAMBO and electrolytes used, this concept can be applied to any MLFF for systems. Furthermore, in Fig. 7, we illustrate that density alignment decreases the errors of predicted properties such as viscosity and ionic conductivity. Through theoretical analysis, the requirements of density alignment, and the relationship between density, viscosity and ionic conductivity are demonstrated in the Methods. Our results show that BAMBO represents an average error of 0.001 g/cm³, viscosity reduction of 27% and ionic conductivity deviation of 26% across a wide range of molecular liquids and solutions with varying salt concentrations. The level of error achieved by BAMBO represents the state-of-the-art in this field (Supplementary Materials 8).
