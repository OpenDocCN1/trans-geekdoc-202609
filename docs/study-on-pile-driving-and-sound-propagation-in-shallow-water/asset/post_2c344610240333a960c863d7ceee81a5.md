$B_{zero}(u, w, x) = 0 \text{ on } \partial\Omega$ (22a)

$B_{non-zero}(u, w, x) = C \text{ on } \partial\Omega$ (22b)

where $\partial\Omega$ is the domain boundary, and $C$ is the value on non-zero boundaries.

In inverse problems of pile driving, the data of the exact solution is given to solve some unknown parameters, for example, geometric parameters, material parameters, and the natural frequency of the pile. By anchoring and matching values of some points, there are also extra losses added to the total losses to be minimized.

After restricting the neural network to satisfy the physical law, we start to train the model by minimizing the loss based on stochastic gradient descent (SGD). The loss function involves several parts, such as the PDE module, boundary conditions module, and so on. Notice that loss functions of non-zero boundary conditions are also defined automatically. The total loss function can be universally defined as

$L_{total} = w_f L_f + w_{B_{zero}} L_{B_{zero}} + w_{B_{non-zero}} L_{B_{non-zero}}$ (23)

where

$L_f = \frac{1}{|T_f|} \sum_{x \in T_f} \left\| f \left( x; u, \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}, u, \frac{\partial w}{\partial x}; \dots \right) \right\|_2^2$ (24a)

$L_{B_{zero}} = \frac{1}{|T_{B_{zero}}|} \sum_{x \in T_{B_{zero}}} \| B_{zero}(u, w, x) \|_2^2$ (24b)

$L_{B_{non-zero}} = \frac{1}{|T_{B_{non-zero}}|} \sum_{x \in T_{B_{non-zero}}} \| B_{non-zero}(u, w, x) \|_2^2$ (24c)

and $w_f, w_{B_{zero}}$ and $w_{B_{non-zero}}$ are weights of each part.

Optimizer "Adam" is chosen to minimize the loss function by gradient descent. Also, optimizer "L-BFGS" can be used then to help train the model further. The test metric of the pile driving model and the sound propagation model is as follows

$metric = \frac{u_{true} - \widehat{u}}{u_{true}} + \frac{w_{true} - \widehat{w}}{w_{true}}$ (25a)

$metric = \frac{p_{true} - \widehat{p}}{p_{true}}$ (25b)

where $\widehat{u}, \widehat{w}$ and $\widehat{p}$ are the expected values of the axial displacement of the pile, the radial displacement of the pile, and the underwater sound pressure.

Compared with common numerical techniques, such as the finite element method and the finite difference method, which both involve numerical discretization, we can find that the PINN framework implements the solving procedure through a neural network. The discretization of those methods is decided by the highest frequency of interest, which restricts their application. However, the PINN method is a mesh-free and frequency-independent method, and it can address physical problems with a suitable framework and proper hyperparameters.

### 3.2. Additional measure

Boundary conditions in solving dynamic and vibrational problems of structures are widely applied. For example, common boundary conditions of vibration of a pile (just taking the beam theory as an example) include the free boundary condition, the simply supported boundary condition, and the clamped boundary condition. Those traditional boundary conditions work well in theoretical derivation and mathematical solving, unfortunately not in deep learning sometimes. For some partial differential equations, such as free vibration problems of beams, plates, and shells without any externally coupled term, it is discovered that those neural networks tend to become lazy, so we call this phenomenon "lazy learning". In these cases, the neural network is much less accurate than the exact solutions. To be specific, the objective functions to be trained, usually displacement functions, are apt to be functions of zero, which also satisfy the partial differential equations. Having pointed out this problem, the way to avoid lazy learning is proposed in this study by setting additional synthetic non-zero boundary conditions as shown in Table 3.

The values of $D_1, D_2, D_3$ and $D_4$ in Table 3 are obtained from the exact solution of model shape functions, respectively. In this way, the neural network has to satisfy those non-zero boundary conditions in the procedure of minimizing the loss. We can also tune a neural network by setting the loss weights of boundary conditions and other parts. Note that not all of the non-zero boundary conditions should be adopted based on consideration of training speed and convergence in specific situations. Non-zero boundary conditions are used in our work to help maximize the training effect in the next section.

### 4.1. Fine-tuning

The initial work of solving physical equations by using PINN is to try to find a good network structure with specific parameters. To build a reliable, robust, and efficient deep learning neural network, neither overfitting nor underfitting, we first construct a moderate one, and then try different network structures to identify the sensitivity of hyperparameters to the PINN framework. After analyzing the effects of the hyperparameters on the PINN framework, we choose a reasonable PINN framework to learn the dynamic behavior of pile driving and sound propagation in shallow water.

Among all network frameworks and parameters of the PINN algorithm, some, built on the consensus with other researchers, can be adopted without any hesitation. For instance, the fully connected neural network is proved suitable for similar questions; "Adam" is one of the most popular optimization algorithms and can be used as the optimizer, which is proved appropriate and effective in general. Let us consider a common PINN framework of pile driving and sound propagation in shallow water, and its parameters are listed in Table 4. Notice that the same parameters are used for the following tuning procedure.

When it comes to the structure of the network in the PINN framework, neurons, layers, and activations are the most important hyperparameters to be tuned prudently. The number of neurons and layers is not always better to be more. On the one hand, the increase in the number of neurons and layers will lead to an increase in the computational quantity. On the other hand, it may lead to overfitting and other problems. In addition, an activation function essentially renders the neural network nonlinear, which is also crucial to the approximation of the exact solutions. Our goal is to build a simple, practical, and adaptable neural network. Therefore, we set a series of tests to help us find one neural network with good comprehensive coordination of hyperparameters.

Due to the symmetry of the pile driving model, the input variable is the spatial coordinate, namely the axial coordinate, and the outputs are the axial displacement and radial displacement of the pile. The experimental results of different neurons, layers, and activations are shown in Fig. 3, Fig. 4, and Fig. 5, respectively. It is apparent that there is barely a significant difference in the mean squared error of different neurons in Fig. 3. Even if only 40 neurons are applied, the loss of train and test can be reduced to a satisfactory degree, which shows the great ability of the neural network to fit. A slight difference can be found in Fig. 4, that is, with the increasing of layers, the loss and oscillations of computation can be further reduced. As we expected, there are huge differences in diverse activations. Because of the mathematical form of the solution, the sine function exactly works and performs best, while other activations do not learn so well, especially the activation of the relu function. Moreover, for any sine-shaped solution, like the Fourier expansion, the sine function with its derivative is the best choice as an activation, while a PINN algorithm with other activations has to tune at cost, more or less.

Also studying the effect of the learning rate on the learning process is vital because the learning rate can heavily affect the results according to other studies (Lee et al., 2021). We choose 0.001 (a conservative learning rate used in many algorithms), 0.002, 0.003, and 0.004 as the learning rate with the same other parameters to investigate the effect of the learning rate for the PINN framework. As can be seen from Fig. 6, there is a big difference between these four cases. As the learning rate increases, convergence and numerical stability are badly affected. When the learning rate reaches 0.003, the losses of the train and test are no longer convergent, and the predicted values are far from the exact solutions. So, a steady strategy is recommended with a smaller learning rate and longer iterations.

### 4.3. Advantages and outlook

There are many characteristics and advantages of the proposed PINN method in solving problems of pile driving. Firstly, the proposed deep learning method is quite innovative with its revolutionary concept of numerical computation. It uses a specifically designed neural network with physical knowledge for training and learning. Thanks to the mighty strength of approximation, it works well with the pile driving model. Secondly, compared with FEM and FDM, the proposed PINN method is almost independent with mesh, frequency, and geometrical domain, which means it cost less time in high-frequency ranges and a long-range distance than other methods. Thirdly, the proposed method is very solid to solve partial differential equations and learn from actual data in contrast with deriving and computing complex solutions in inverse problems.

According to our research, we can optimistically believe that various vibroacoustic problems of structures in ocean engineering are supposed and expected to be addressed by using PINN, and we will focus on those problems as much as possible and devote ourselves to the optimization of the algorithm in our further research. One of the most interesting and exciting research territories to be explored is underwater noise radiating from pile driving using PINN with real measured data, which will greatly help us to investigate the fluid-pile-soil coupled dynamic model and underwater acoustic model. Based on deep learning and practical engineering, our ultimate goals of the preservation of marine life and the ocean environment can be achieved in the future.
