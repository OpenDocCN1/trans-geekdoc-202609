## ABSTRACT

A novel, effective, and precise physics-informed neural network (PINN) is presented in this paper to learn and investigate pile driving and sound propagation in shallow water. The validation of the proposed PINN framework is proved by comparing analytical and predicted displacements of the pile and underwater sound pressure radiated from pile driving. The optimization of the PINN algorithms is studied in detail by tuning the hyper-parameters. And then, a fast computing PINN framework with good inverse-solving abilities is constructed. A suggestion to help the neural network learn partial differential equations better by adding non-zero boundary conditions is put forward. It is shown that the neural network made up of a small number of neurons, a shallow network with a few layers and the activation of the sine function is reliable and satisfying. The identification of unknown parameters in the PINN framework is carried out and discussed via various tests, and the PINN method accurately predicts the solution of motion of pile driving and approximates the underwater sound pressure in shallow water for a wide range of frequencies and propagation distance. The advantage of the proposed PINN method is that it is a powerful tool to deal with inverse problems and is independent of the frequency and the distance.

## 1. Introduction

In coastal and offshore engineering, the pile is one of the most important structures as foundations and support structures. There are roughly two kinds of piles in terms of cross-section, solid pile and pipe pile. The pipe pile is widely used because of its good mechanical properties and lower cost. The installation of piles is an essential practice in offshore engineering. However, intense impulsive underwater noise due to pile driving can affect aquatic life, for example, fishes, sea turtles, and marine mammals (Henninger and Watson, 2005; Madsen et al., 2006; Popper and Hastings, 2009; Bailey et al., 2010; Knaap Inge et al., 2022; 2011; MacGillivray, 2013; Marshall, 2013; Ryan Wilkes et al., 2014; Lippert and Otto von Estorff, 2014; Amaral et al., 2022), the vibro-acoustic physical model of pile driving has not been established on common ground (Lippert et al., 2016). There are several common problems when these theoretical and numerical models are used and compared to each other, for instance, the absence of an analytical solution for the problem, the lack of available measurement data, and the high computational costs. One of the toughest problems of all these numerical methods and theoretical models is the uncertain values or unknown parameters in these proposed physical models according to measured data or experimental results which are usually sparse, limited, and expensive.

According to the universal approximation theorem, a potential and powerful way to address the above problem is to use the artificial neural network (ANN), an advancing and intelligent tool, to learn from certain data and invert the significant parameters of those physical models. Many studies of deep learning have been carried out recently to solve physical problems, such as partial differential equations, including the Poisson equation, Laplace equation, Helmholtz equation, Burgers

One of the mightiest tools is developed as a so-called Physics-informed neural network (PINN) to solve PDEs (Raissi et al., 2019). PINN has been studied, used, and optimized in many physical fields, for example, in fluid mechanics and vibration (Raissi et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2021), structural durability (Xue et al., 2022), wave propagation (Alkhdhar and Mohamed, 2021). Different from the finite element method (FEM), using PINN to approximate the solution is a mesh-free method, which means the computational efficiency is irrelevant to the frequency of models (Karniadakis et al., 2021; Sacchetti et al., 2022). And more importantly, by data-driven modeling techniques, PINN has the extraordinary ability to solve unknown parameters at very little cost (Tartakovsky et al., 2020; He et al., 2020; Lu et al, 2021). Once Feed-forward-Neural Network (FNN) is built and the physical law is embedded as additional knowledge, the inverse problems, with minimal coding, can be easily solved based only on very few data.

The main work of this paper includes two parts, the dynamic and acoustic studies based on PINN frameworks. The first section is the dynamic behavior study of offshore pipe piles in water using a physics-informed neural network, including forward problems and inverse problems. In this part, the accuracy, efficiency, and convenience of the proposed PINN method to predict some unknown parameters of coupled pile driving model are demonstrated. The other is the study on acoustic propagation in shallow water based on the normal mode theory, in which the significant advantage of the PINN method is found as a frequency-independent and distance-independent method.

## 2. Mathematical formulations

### 2.1. Pile driving

An offshore pipe pile can be modeled as a vertical, thin, and elastic cylindrical shell, and the loss factor is usually so small that can be neglected. The toe of the pipe pile is in the seabed and its top end is impacted by external force. There are two typical kinds of pile driving, impact pile driving and vibratory pile driving. Impact pile driving is rapid and broadband, while vibratory pile driving is with a narrower frequency band and lower underwater sound pressure level. No matter which way to drive a pile into the soil, the study of the vibrational characteristics of the pile is primary. In the model of pile driving, a thin elastic cylindrical pile pipe is considered to investigate its dynamic behavior (Junger and Feit, 1986). And the sound propagation of pile driving in shallow water is studied using the normal mode theory (Ellis and Chapman, 1985). The main concern of our work is to investigate the ability of PINN to study the dynamic behaviors and underwater sound pressure radiated from the pile driving in shallow water. So, the effects of the seabed on the pile are considered but replaced by a reasonable approximation.

Define $x$ and $r$ as the axial coordinate and radial coordinate of the pile, $u$ and $w$ as the axial displacement and radial displacement of the pile, respectively. Also, there are some geometric and material symbols as follows: $\rho$, the density of the pile, $E$, Young's modulus of elasticity, $\mu$, Poisson ratio, $L$, the length of the pile, $R$, the radius of the pile, $h$, the wall thickness of the pile. In the dynamic analysis of a thin cylindrical shell in water, such assumptions are made in advance:

- The azimuthal vibration is independent among three types of vibration;
- There is no variation with radius in partial differential equations;
- Only sound pressure is considered as an external force;
- Torsion and bending involving fourth order terms are neglected.

Therefore, the equations of motion can be simplified to

\[
\frac{\partial^2 u(x,t)}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\mu}{R} \frac{\partial w(x,t)}{\partial x} = \frac{\rho(1-\mu^2)}{E} \frac{\partial^2 u(x,t)}{\partial t^2} \tag{1a}
\]

\[
\frac{\mu}{R} \frac{\partial u(x,t)}{\partial x} + \frac{w(x,t)}{R^2} = -\frac{\rho(1-\mu^2)}{E} \left( \frac{P_a}{\rho h} + \frac{\partial^2 w(x,t)}{\partial t^2} \right) \tag{1b}
\]

where $P_a$ is the fluid radiation pressure and acts normally to the wall of the pipe pile. Via a coupled fluid-structure interaction (FSI), its expression can be given as

\[
P_a(x,t) = Z(x,t) \frac{\partial w(x,t)}{\partial t} \tag{2}
\]
