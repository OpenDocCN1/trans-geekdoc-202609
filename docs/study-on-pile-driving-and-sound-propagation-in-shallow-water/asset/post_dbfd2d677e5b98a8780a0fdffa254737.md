The hyperparameter sensitivity of the proposed PINN method is analyzed in detail. It is found that: 1) A small number of neurons in the neural network can get a satisfying result; 2) A flat and shallow neural network is good enough to fit, but the increasing of layers can make the loss of training and testing reduce steadily; 3) As nonlinear operators, activations play an important role in mapping inputs to outputs, and the sine function is the best choice for pile driving problems in our research; 4) A small learning rate is recommended because of its good stability. Furthermore, we suggest using a new technique, namely non-zero boundary conditions, to help the neural network avoid lazy learning.

After building and optimizing the PINN framework, we test its ability to learn from partial differential equations and real data for solving forward and inverse problems of pile driving. In our research, the ability of PINN to solve unknown parameters is quite good based on an excellent feed-forward network. Lastly, to distinguish the proposed PINN method from other methods and to explore the possibility of the proposed PINN method, we mainly carry out some numerical experiments on the frequency domain and geometrical domain. The results of displacements and underwater sound pressure given by the PINN method agree very well with the exact values over a wide frequency and distance range.
