# ABSTRACT

A novel, effective, and precise physical-informed neural network (PINN) is presented in this paper to learn and investigate pile driving and sound propagation in shallow water. The validation of the proposed PINN framework is proved by comparing analytical and predicted displacements of the pile and underwater sound pressure radiated from pile driving. The optimization of the PINN algorithms is studied in detail by tuning the hyper-parameters. And then, a fast computing PINN framework with good inverse-solving abilities is constructed. A suggestion to help the neural network learn partial differential equations better by adding non-zero boundary conditions is put forward. It is shown that the neural network made up of a small number of neurons, a shallow network with a few layers and the activation of the sine function is reliable and satisfying. The identification of unknown parameters in the PINN framework is carried out and discussed via various tests, and the PINN method accurately predicts the solution of motion of pile driving and approximates the underwater sound pressure in shallow water for a wide range of frequencies and propagation distance. The advantage of the proposed PINN method is that it is a powerful tool to deal with inverse problems and is independent of the frequency and the distance.

# 1. Introduction

In coastal and offshore engineering, the pile is one of the most important structures as foundations and support structures. There are roughly two kinds of piles in terms of cross-section, solid pile and pipe pile. The pipe pile is widely used because of its good mechanical properties and lower cost. The installation of piles is an essential practice in offshore engineering. However, intense impulsive underwater noise due to pile driving can affect aquatic life, for example, fishes, sea turtles, and marine mammals (Henninger and Watson, 2005; Madsen et al., 2006; Popper and Hastings, 2009; Bailey et al., 2010; Knaap Inge et al., 2022; 2011; MacGillivray, 2013; Marshall, 2013; Ryan Wilkes et al., 2014; Lippert and Otto von Estorff, 2014; Amaral et al., 2022), the vibro-acoustic physical model of pile driving has not been established on common ground (Lippert et al., 2016). There are several common problems when these theoretical and numerical models are used and compared to each other, for instance, the absence of an analytical solution for the problem, the lack of available measurement data, and the high computational costs. One of the toughest problems of all these numerical methods and theoretical models is the uncertain values or unknown parameters in these proposed physical models according to measured data or experimental results which are usually sparse, limited, and expensive.

According to the universal approximation theorem, a potential and powerful way to address the above problem is to use the artificial neural network (ANN), an advancing and intelligent tool, to learn from certain data and invert the significant parameters of those physical models.
