**ABSTRACT** Heterogeneous networks (HetNCs) offer a promising solution to cope with the current cellular network crunch. Due to the large transport power disparity, while favoring maximum power (MPR) association scheme, a higher number of users are associated with macro-cell BS (MBS) than small-cell BS (SBS). Hence, an imbalance allocation takes places across the HetNCs. Using cell range expansion-based association, we can balance the load across the HetNCs. However, using MPR association scheme, users leads to two challenges: 1) macro-cell interference, in which the MBS interfers with the overloaded users, and 2) decoupled downlink-uplink association, where a random user associates with a single tier's base station (BS) in uplink (UL) and downlink (DL) directions. This paper addresses these problems while considering a two-tier scenario. We employ a reverse frequency allocation (RFA) scheme. Besides Coupled DL-UL association (Co-DU), this paper also highlights the notion of decoupled DL-UL association. In De-DU, a random user associates with two different tiers BS, with one tier's BS in DL and the other tier's BS in UL. Our results illustrate that, in comparison with the Co-DU, De-DU with RFA employment achieves a better coverage performance.

# I. INTRODUCTION

To meet the current wireless data deluge, cellular networks enter into a new paradigm where the low power small-cells, i.e., pico-cells and femto-cells, utilize the existing infrastructure of high power macro-cells in heterogeneous cellular networks (HetNCs) [1]. Different types of BS differ from each other in their transmit power, BS densities, user densities associated and cell selection offsets. Due to the higher transmit power, coverage of macro-cells are larger compared with low power small-cells. Fewer number of users are associated with small-cells.
