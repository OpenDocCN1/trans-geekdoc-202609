# Don’t Talk To Strangers

The critical line of code is this one:

```
$user->payOrder($order);
```

In one sweep we eliminated all of that extra knowledge that the action method shouldn't have had in the first place. Even though I've left you to imagine how this method acquires the $user and $order instances in the first place, the simple act of passing the $order instance to the user's $payForOrder() method would have served the pre-credit account era just well as the post-credit account online.

In other words, knowledge of the inner workings of the order payment process has been removed from where it doesn't. Our action method no longer has any kind of idea as to what happens to Order instance on the other side of the $payForOrder() invocation.

Now, the only knowledge it has of process is that if an exception is thrown, it has to deal with it, which is exactly as it should.

> If you've already encountered the Law of Demeter previously, you might have noticed how there are some distinct parallels between the example that we've been working here and the subject of a paper by David Beck entitled The Paperboy, The Wallet and The Law of Demeter in which he describes the process of paying the paperboy not by having the customer hand over his wallet, but by having the paperboy request payment and allowing the customer to organise how the bill gets settled. In other words, the paperboy has no need of ever seeing the wallet or even to know it exists in the first place. I heartily recommend reading this paper. It's short and beautifully illustrates the point.
