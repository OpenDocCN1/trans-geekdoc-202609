# Who this book is for.

In a nutshell, I'm pitching this book at good developers who are keen to shine, to develop an almost supernatural ability to foresee and avoid future bug scenarios, to be awesome.

This isn't a beginner's guide to PHP, nor is it intended to be a book for mid-level developers either. The intended audience that I'm writing for is one that mainly comprises developers at the Senior end of the scale, though I will freely admit that the term Senior Developer means wildly things to different people and across different organisations.

As such then I'm attempting to gear the content towards an anticipated reader who is already familiar with the language and has been using it in an object oriented fashion for a number of years. How many years? Again, this is an exceedingly difficult to quantify. One developer who spent five years only installing WordPress and has not engaged in any personal study would not be as far along the curve as another developer who has spent the last six months dedicating themselves to accreting the mastery and art of managing components via Composer.

In any event the reader, that is, you, will have a certain amount of commercial or commercial-like experience already under your belt. Quite likely, you'll have tried and tested a few of the MVC frameworks that litter the PHP landscape, perhaps even settled on one as being your personal favourite.
