# Summary

For our first chapter, I've concentrated on the idea of grouping application's objects into two distinct camps: the knowers and the doers. This is very much the key that I would like to introduce at this point. In a very general sense, our knowers are likely to be the principle citizens that reside in our model layer. They hold and represent the data that lies at the heart of our application. These are the users, the products, the orders and the invoices. They present an interface which is designed to set, retrieve, manipulate or transform the individual elements of data that are responsible for.

Then are the doers. The objects within our system that cause things to happen and whose interfaces comprise methods that we can call in order to trigger those things. These doers might mask the simplest of processes, such as the hashing of a password, or they might be a facade unto much more complex procedures, governing the various stages of user registration for example.

Clearly then, I'm really quite keen on this idea. Largely because I've witnessed the positive effects that it can have. It's not so much the idea in itself per see, more that the end results speak for themselves.
