# The Four Central Tenets

If there's one thing that developers seem to get a bit lax about when they oriented attain the senior developer status, it's the basics. The simplest bits of object oriented PHP development. I don't know why this would be. If you take your average Joe Senior and ask him to explain things like encapsulation or inheritance, the responses you'll get tend to sound like blog post titles. And why not? They are after all, the basics of object oriented development.

Encapsulation? Yeah, you can do data hiding with encapsulation. Abstraction? Great for reusing common bits of code by moving them into a parent class.

A core part of the problem here is that nobody really thinks about the four central tenets of object oriented development once they've gotten the beginner stuff. They read it. They got it. They tried the code examples. Bang. Done. What's next?

This, of course, is entirely reasonable. Why should they? With a constantly evolving landscape and really exciting new tech stuff just around the corner it's not exactly easy to persuade your average developer to give up that article on PSR-7 and the implications that it brings for HTTP middlewares in favour of reading encapsulation all over again.

Indeed, I know that there's quite a hurdle ahead of me programming topics that are decades old. Nevertheless, I'm motivated to turn my readers into winners because it's the developers who do strengthen their understanding of the basics - those guys, you, guys, you are the ones that will win in the end. You might feel tempted to go thundering ahead without doing a pre-race check but I can assure you, those wheels are going to come off before you know.

Instead, the developer who takes his time over the fundamentals and the basics is the developer who will find the middle and end stages of the race, the crucial stages, much smoother and easier. He or she will be the one to go cruising past and on the finishing line, whilst the other guys? They'll be the ones bogged in the pit lanes of bug fixing and maintenance.
