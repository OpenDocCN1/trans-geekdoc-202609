Is our work complete then? Not quite. There's a very clear and obvious problem here but before we get on working our way through it, we should take a moment to consider what we have achieved.

By divorcing the family of decorators from the principle subject to be decorated, we have successfully dispensed with the rather odd notion that an oak shield or a chainmail shirt is-a of Warrior in the same way that it seems to suggest that an olive topping is-a of pizza.

To put it another way, we have set our decorators free and improved their reusability.

The problem that we've introduced here is a pretty obvious one. If we were to send our Warrior instance onto the battlefield we would not be able to do anything except take damage and report its current, composite health status. Pretty rubbish as far as warriors go then.

The reason for this is down to the fact that we created the instance purely for the methods of interest, of which there are just two. By decorating the warrior with armour we have effectively shut off to the warrior's other methods. Methods we presumably included providing the ability to fight back and hopefully defeat enemies.

How should we fix this situation?

We could proceed to extend the required interface so that it also included all of the Warrior class' combat methods that would be messy. We would end up with a ChainMailHauber that somehow knew how to fight, which is clearly wrong.

Instead, we would need to approach the construction of a Warrior instance with a a completely different strategy. There's that word again. Strategy.

Since our armour decorators are all defence related, our best approach is to employ the Strategy Pattern in order to create defence strategies, as StandAndFight, HideAndSnipe or RunAway, which we can then proceed to decorate with the armour as desired.

The same approach could then also be applied to the notion of creating a fight strategy, with their corresponding decorators;Flaming? Or PoisonTipped? Both of these options could equally be applied to instances of Sword, Dagger or Arrow.
