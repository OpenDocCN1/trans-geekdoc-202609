## 11.2 Fundamentals of quantum computing

Modern cell phones are significantly more powerful in terms of computations than the large computers (mainframes) that were used in the past for critical tasks like guiding spacecraft to the moon [4]. The mainframes were very large and expensive computers that were used for complex tasks before the rise of personal computers.

Researchers say that we are soon going to enter the era of quantum advances, where quantum computers will be used to increase the efficiency of classical computers, just like GPUs.

As shown in Figure 11.1, in quantum computing, there are five fundamental topics:

- 1) Superposition
- 2) Gates
- 3) Measurement
- 4) Interference
- 5) Entanglement

Let's first talk about bits. Classical computers use bits, which are like switches that can be either 0 or 1. This type of computation has served us well, such that almost all modern computers work in the same way. However, this type of approach doesn't solve all the problems we have today, just as problems that can grow exponentially and would take a classical computer decades or more to solve [4].

Other types of difficult problems can include optimization, chemistry simulation, and ML.

Unlike classical computers, quantum computers do not use simple 0 and 1 bits, as shown in Figure 11.2.

Instead, they use qubits. Any linear combination of the two bits, 0 and 1, can be these qubits. Superposition refers to this range of states [2][4].

We integrate qubits using an called gates, which may alter the qubits' states within the circuits, just like we accomplish in conventional computers.

For example,
The first possible state of a qubit could be zero. Following that, we will employ the Hadamard gate in quantum computing. The Hadamard gate is a logical gate that combines 0 and 1 to produce a superposition of both states. Through this method, we are enabled to have a circuit with several qubits interlaced with many gates.

A qubit collapses into a simple 0 or 1 when measured, losing its superposition.
This means that a needle is pointing anywhere between 0 and 1 has a fifty percent chance of measuring 1, instead of measuring 0.5. This explains why it is mentioned that a qubit can be 0 and a 1 at the same time. This means that much of the data will be defined by only a few qubits.

Quantum computers outperform classical computers because of parallel computation, where the operations are applied simultaneously to 0 and 1 states. Interference ensures the correct answer is amplified while incorrect ones get canceled. The states of entangled qubits exhibit a significant correlation. That is, altering one qubit's state has the power to alter another qubit's state [4]. For instance, two qubits may be made to entangle so that, in their respective states, there is a 50% probability of measuring a 00 and a 50% chance of measuring would an 11, but never a 01 or a 10. Here, changing one's condition alone would cause the other to alter as well [3].

Therefore, quantum computers can tackle problems that a conventional computer is just unable to handle because of their combined capabilities of superposition, interference, gates, and entanglement.

## 11.3 Fundamentals of machine learning

ML is the niche point of artificial intelligence as shown in Figure 11.3 in which an algorithm analyzes the data and produces results with no human involvement or programming. Traditional programming involves instructing the computer on and every step of the way. ML refers to the ability of the computer to perform the steps itself by analyzing the data.

![](img/003d10cd18c2ce02e1f86a5992a8292b_190_0.png)

There are three types of ML, which are based on the type of data that is being used during training [5]:

- 1. **Supervised learning:** It requires labeled data (data with known outputs). In this, the algorithm learns the relationship between inputs and outputs and uses it to make predictions for new data points [5]. Examples: Spam filtering, image recognition, and weather forecasting.
- 2. **Unsupervised learning:** It uses unlabeled data (data without predefined categories). Analyzing the provided data, identifies trends and reveals hidden patterns [5]. Examples: Customer segmentation, market research, and anomaly detection.
- 3. **Reinforcement learning:** This type of ML involves an agent that interacts with the environment and learns through trial and error. If the behavior is good, the agent is rewarded, and over time, agents learn how to optimize rewards [5]. Examples: Training a robot to navigate its surroundings, playing games like chess or Go.

To convert raw data into useful insights, ML algorithms follow a specific process. This section outlines the crucial stages in building and implementing a successful ML model:

- 1. **Data gathering:** Data plays a central role in AI systems and serves as a core component. Step is essentially the large-scale collection of data linked to all the goals of the task at the highest level. The information provided should be extensive, general, and have the capability to allow the prediction or classification process to accurately reflect real-life patterns.
- 2. **Data preparation:** Often, the raw data is untidy and may have inconsistencies, incorrect values, or missing values. Data cleaning, formatting, or transformation are performed to prepare the data.
