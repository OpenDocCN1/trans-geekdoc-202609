# 4.3 Evolution, context, and key technologies of quantum computing

In this section, we delve into the essential technologies that drive the development of modern quantum computing systems [10]. These quantum computing-ologies are systematically organized across different domains, including hardware structure, control processor plane, quantum data plane, host processor, quantum control and measurement plane, and qubit technologies, as seen in Table 4.2.

Table 4.2: Roadmap of quantum computing.

| Year | Milestone | Technology used | Findings and significance | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|---|--|
| 1981 | Richard Feynman proposed the concept of quantum computing | N/A | Laid the foundation for quantum computing theory | Potential for exponentially fast computation | Theoretical concept, no practical implementation |
| 1994 | Peter Shor developed Shor's algorithm for factoring large numbers | Shor's algorithm for factoring numbers | Quantum algorithms | Demstrated quantum computers' ability to break widely encryption methods | Threat to classical cryptography | No practical quantum computer at the time |
| 1996 | Lov Grover Grover's algorithm for searching unsorted databases | Quantum algorithms | Showed quantum advantage in searching unstructured data | Faster database searches | Limited to search-related tasks |
| 1998 | Isaac Chuang's group at IBM performed the first NMR quantum computation | NMR-based group at IBM computing | Experimental demonstration of quantum principles | First practical quantum computation | Limited scalability and error-prone |
| 2000 | The first functioning two-qubit quantum computer was built at IBM | Superconducting qubits | Practical quantum hardware development | Step toward scalable quantum computers | Still a small number of qubits |
| 2011 | D-Wave Systems introduced its quantum annealing processor | Quantum annealing | Commercial quantum computing system | Access to quantum computing for specific optimization problems | Controversy over the quantumness of D-Wave's device
