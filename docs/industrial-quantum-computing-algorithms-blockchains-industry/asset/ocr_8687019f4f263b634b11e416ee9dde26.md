# 9.2.1 Quantum neural networks (QNN)

QNN is a computational ML framework that blends artificial neural networks with quantum technology to build more efficient algorithms. QNNs follow the theory of quantum mechanics. “Subhash Kash and Ron Cris published the first concepts on quantum neural network in 1995. They discussed the theory of QNN, which claims quantum effects have an impact on cognitive functioning of models.

In recent research, QNN involves combining classical artificial network models with the benefits of quantum computations. It's challenging to train classical models on large amounts of data. However, quantum computing features, such as quantum parallelism, interference, and entanglement, are potential resources for improving neural network efficiency. Most of the QNN models remain theoretical proofs, awaiting proper implementation in actual experiments [2].

**Table 9.2:** Differences between classical neural networks (CNNs) and quantum neural networks (QNNs).

| Aspect | Classical neural network (CNN) | Quantum neural network (QNN) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basis of computation | Classical computing | Quantum computing |
| Representation of neurons | Neurons are represented by classical nodes and activations | Neurons are represented by quantum states and operations |
| Neuron activation function | Typically uses classical activation functions (e.g., sigmoid and ReLU) | May use quantum-inspired activation functions (e.g., quantum gates) or classical activation functions |
| Training methodology | Backpropagation algorithm for gradient descent optimization | Variational quantum circuit optimization or other quantum algorithms |
| Quantum advantage | Not applicable | Potential for exponential speedup in certain tasks |
| Handling nonlinear data | Limited by classical architecture | Inherent capability to process quantum data and learn nonlinear mappings |
| Complexity | Polynomial time complexity (for training and inference) | Complexity depends on the quantum algorithms and hardware resources |
| Scalability | Scales well for large datasets and complex architectures | Limited scalability due to current limitations in quantum hardware |
| Noise sensitivity | Robust to noise and errors in classical computing environments | Susceptible to noise and errors in quantum computation |
| Realization on quantum hardware | Not applicable | Realization requires quantum hardware and algorithms |
