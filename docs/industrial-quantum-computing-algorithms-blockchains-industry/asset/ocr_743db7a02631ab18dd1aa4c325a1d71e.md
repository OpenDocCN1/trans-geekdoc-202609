Grover's algorithm is one of the quantum algorithms, as explained in Figure 13.4, which makes finding items in an unordered database crucially faster than classical algorithms. This algorithm was introduced in 1996 by Lov Grover. It is the most well-known algorithm in quantum computing.
The Grover quantum search algorithm scans N items in a database in just about $\sqrt N$ steps, whereas the classical equivalents need N/2 steps on average [15]. Therefore, the quadratic acceleration realized through the implementation of quantum superposition and interference to boost the chances of getting to the true answer perfectly is the mechanism for this quantum gain. This portrays the advantage that quantum machines outline in a database search for unsorted data, a task that is exponentially faster than classical algorithms [4].
