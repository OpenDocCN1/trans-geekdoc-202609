## 1.3.6 Cryptography

Because of its ability to crack existing encryption systems, quantum computing might have far-reaching consequences for the field of cryptography. Integer factorization and discrete logarithms are fundamental to the security of many cryptographic techniques; quantum computers might solve both difficulties. As a result, quantum-resistant encryption has emerged, and quantum key distribution has become the go-to method for foolproof communication [31].

## 1.3.7 Pharmaceutical and drug industries

Because quantum computers can more faithfully mimic the behavior of complicated molecules than conventional computers, they open up new avenues for pharmacological research and medication development. This skill can create novel medications and treatments by streamlining and shorting the drug discovery process [32].

## 1.3.8 Computer programs that use AI and ML

Because quantum computing allows for quicker optimization and more accurate pattern recognition, it has the potential to greatly improve AI and machine learning systems. In several contexts, including autonomous cars and medical diagnostics, this can enhance AI models, accelerate data processing, and improve decision-making [33].

## 1.3.9 Environment and climate models

By simulating complex natural systems more accurately, quantum computing has the potential to radically alter climate modeling and environmental studies. Researchers can create more effective solutions to environmental problems and planet preservation if they have a deeper knowledge of the elements that cause climate change and how our ecosystems function [34, 35].

## 1.3.10 Streamlining logistics and the supply chain

Outperforming conventional computers in solving complicated optimization issues, quantum computing may aid in optimizing supply chains and logistical operations. Businesses and industries may see cost reduction, improved resource allocation, and improved efficiency [35].

## 1.4 Societal impacts

There has been a proactive effort to build legal, regulatory, ethical, and cybersecurity initiatives to handle the digital trust issues that quantum computing brings, even if it is expected to have far-reaching beneficial effects on society, the economy, and several businesses [36]. Quantum computing's rapid evolution is revealing its societal effects. Quantum computing may transform how we access and handle massive volumes of data, whether for cybersecurity or traffic optimization. Quantum computing might revolutionize our lives, but it carries severe moral and ethical issues. Quantum computing experts worry about people abusing quantum computing [37, 38].

Quantum computing may revolutionize data storage, analysis, and use. Traditional computing issues are now easier to solve using this method. Despite this, many experts feel this technology might radically change privacy rules. Quantum computing may make data storage safe. Classical computing stores data centrally, subjecting it to hacking and other attacks. Quantum computing can secure data in several places, making access it almost difficult. This may preserve privacy and greatly prevent data breaches [38, 39].

Quantum computing may also improve encryption. These algorithms may secure sensitive data like medical or financial information. This may help protect users from unauthorized parties. Quantum computing has the potential to provide novel authentication methods. These tactics will enable us to restrict access to sensitive information to authorized users. Quantum computing has the potential to profoundly transform privacy legislation. This technology enables enhanced authentication, encryption, and data storage capabilities. These features may make data more secure and less easily accessible [39, 40].

## 1.5 Ethical considerations

The emergence of quantum computing has sparked ethical concerns about the preservation of data security, the assurance of privacy, and the promotion of responsible utilization of developing technologies [40]. Quantum technology development and use give rise to moral challenges, namely fairness, respect for individuals, inclusivity, environmental, social, and governance considerations, human rights, and other associated concerns.

Establishing moral principles and methods to govern its usage is imperative. The emergence of quantum computers raises concerns about equitable access, appropriate use, and possible misuse. Establishing regulations and structures to deal with these issues and maintaining equity and social principles is necessary. The ability of quantum computing to bypass current encryption methods raises privacy and data security issues. To safeguard sensitive information in the era of quantum computing, it is crucial to create and secure communication protocols and encryption techniques that are immune to the capabilities of quantum computers [41].

With the progress and development of quantum computing, it is crucial to carefully contemplate its ethical ramifications. Although it can resolve intricate issues and transform the computing field, substantial ethical quandaries must be addressed. Important issue is the potential for quantum computing to be used as a means of exploiting security vulnerabilities. Quantum computers have significant computational capabilities that might be used to decipher passwords and gain unauthorized access to confidential information, presenting a grave risk to the security of sensitive data, including financial and personal particulars [42, 43].

### 1.5.1 Cyber

Both cyberattacks and cyber defenses are possible with quantum computing. An ideal zero-trust cybersecurity system or an encryption-decryption key might be constructed using quantum computing. The zero-trust principle states that no data should ever be sent or stored on any device, network, or user unless explicitly stated otherwise [43].

### 1.5.2 Governance

Public policy, crisis management, voting, and other intricate simulations and models might all benefit from quantum computing [40, 41].

### 1.5.3 Citizenship

Strong digital identities for people worldwide can be built on quantum computing, cutting-edge biometric methods, and distributed ledger technologies like blockchain [37–39].

## 1.6 Challenges and limitations

This section covers the challenges and limitations of quantum computing research.

### 1.6.1 Quantum computing challenges

Our planet is poised for a quantum computing revolution. Many sectors of the economy, academia, society, and the natural world stand to benefit from the answers that quantum computers may provide. Quantum computing has garnered much interest recently because of its enormous breakthrough potential, leading to more investments, partnerships, and faster overall advancements in the industry. Advances in quantum computing are expected to progress exponentially in the years ahead. To fully realize its promise, however, this technology needs further study and development since it is still in its early phases. The area of quantum computing is being confronted with several significant obstacles [40, 41]:

- **Scalability**: The capacity to scale is one of the most difficult aspects. Finding ways to increase the number of qubits and make them coherent is a priority for scientists if we want to make greater use of quantum computers. Even the most advanced quantum computers lack the necessary number of qubits a few hundred to address pressing issues. Thousands of qubits, or far bigger quantum computers, are required to get a genuine quantum advantage: the capacity to solve problems more quickly than classical computers.
- **Error correction**: Quantum computing also faces the problem of error correction. Quantum computers may produce inaccurate results due to the sensitivity of qubits to noise and errors, which can cause them to lose their quantum state.
