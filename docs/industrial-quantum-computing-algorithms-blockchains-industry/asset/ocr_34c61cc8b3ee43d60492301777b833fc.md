# 5.1.1 A connected world: overview from 1G to 5G

The progression of mobile communication from the first generation (1G) to the fifth generation (5G) has led to profound global changes that shape how billions of people interact, work, and live [21]. Today's smartphone is an inseparable part of our lives as it links us with friends and the world. Nevertheless, the way it arrived represents a remarkable evolution starting from the past five generations (1G to 5G) and witnessing a large growth in speed, reach, and functionality [4]. Let us embark on a journey through this global story of connectivity, with statistics that illustrate the impact of each generation:

# 5.1.1.1 1G (first generation): pioneering steps (1980s)

+   - Global phone subscribers: 30 million (1985)
- Data transmission: Nonexistent

The 1G network was introduced in the 1980s and can be used for basic voice calls through analog cellular systems. By the early 1990s, the 1G network had planned to cover more than 25% of the world's population, which brought new ways of mobility to the world. Imagine the outage of old, chunky phones, poor call quality, and no Internet access. The mobile revolution was the reality of 1G, but it was the foundation of it. The limited range and functionality of the service meant that it was mostly a Public Relations statement for the concept of portable communication.

# 5.1.1.2 2G (second generation): after all the voice calls (1990s)

+   - Global phone subscribers: 1 billion (1997)
- Data speeds: As much as 14 kbps

In turn, the digital technology chain of arrival means a quantum leap. 2G had better call quality, text messaging became possible, and the evolution of mobile data, although annoyingly at slow speeds. For this generation, the world of the Internet was taken personally to the next cellular level as the first email on the go, and perhaps was so that early access to mobile Internet was made available.
