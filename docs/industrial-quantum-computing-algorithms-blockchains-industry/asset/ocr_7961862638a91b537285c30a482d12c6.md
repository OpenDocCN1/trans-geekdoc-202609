# 7.5.4 Hardware fabrication and design

Design and fabrication of quantum hardware components require an understanding of materials, fabrication, and device geometries. It is important to ensure consistency and repeatability in quantum devices to ensure performance and reliability. However, the fabrication of quantum hardware issues relates to material purity, engineering defects, and lithographic resolution.

A study conducted by Shulman et al. (2014) highlighted the importance of fabrication techniques such as electron beam lithography and atomic layer deposition in the successful fabrication of defective qubits. Yet scaling quantum fabrication processes to large-scale production is a daunting task that requires interdisciplinary collaboration and technological innovation.

In short, the limitations of quantum hardware are a major challenge for the realization of quantum computing in industrial automation and other fields. Solving these challenges will require collaboration among the scientific community, industry stakeholders, and policymakers to improve quantum hardware design, technology development output, error correction strategies, and scalability solutions. Overcoming these limitations is key to unlocking the transformative potential of quantum computing and ushering in a new era of computing.

# 7.5.6 Scalability issues

While quantum computers have been shown to be capable of solving certain problems, scaling these systems to solve larger and more complex business applications remains a challenge. To keep quantum computing more powerful than traditional computing, we need to increase the number of quantum bits (qubits) and their connections. But this is usually a huge task. It implies we must address issues related to error correction, integration time, and material science when we are building and maintaining large quantum systems.
