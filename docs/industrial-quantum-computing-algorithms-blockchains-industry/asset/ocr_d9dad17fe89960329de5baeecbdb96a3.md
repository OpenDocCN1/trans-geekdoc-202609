In addition, more fundamental innovation in technology, computation, and application could represent a catalyst in many areas and, in many more, represent the beginning of a trail to entirely ways to think about the solution to important problems. Quantum computing has arrived. It is the beginning of an extreme era where new ways to do things will reveal radical new approaches to some of society’s most important problems. We owe it to ourselves to learn about these and understand them. We need to put them into the mix. But we need to call out the problems that will precipitate so they can be properly ameliorated, and we must ensure a more equitable and fair distribution of benefits. There are many promises that come with quantum computing, and over time, using “Pave the cow path” or “Plan for,” our world would be entirely different.

How does it matter that we do not know how long it might take before we have a scalable quantum computer, or what kind of packages will eventually arrive? The definition, as well as the attitude toward quantum computing, has already shifted dramatically. It is no longer a mystery, no longer exotic. Quantum measurement and entangled states are just two of the many things that become easier to grasp in the context of quantum data processing. It is hard to imagine what concrete consequences such deep conceptual understanding of the nature of the physical world in which we live might have. But they are sure to have starting direct effects in the intellectual and technological world in the coming decades.
