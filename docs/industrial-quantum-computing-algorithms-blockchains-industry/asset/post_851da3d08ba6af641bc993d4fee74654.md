#### 7.5.5.1 Qubit stability and coherence

Qubits, or quantum objects, are the basis of quantum information processing. Unlike classical objects, which have a constant state of 0 or 1, qubits exist in a superposition of these states, allowing for parallelism. However, qubits are intrinsically delicate and susceptible to external noise and environmental perturbations. Stable and consistent qubit control (the ability to alter quantum states over time) is a challenge in quantum hardware design. According to Poggiial et al. (2018), the qubit's temporal integration, which determines the duration of a quantum operation, can be efficient due to its minimal contact with the environment. Thermal fluctuations and electromagnetic interference can create decoherence in qubits, jeopardizing the accuracy of computation.

#### 7.5.5.2 Bug fixes and errors

Qubit failures render quantum systems that are error-prone. Reducing these errors is critical to obtaining consistent quantum computing. Correction technologies, such as quantum error-correcting codes and fault-tolerant circuits, aid in the detection and correction of errors. Implementing error correction into quantum hardware requires extra qubits and computational overhead [7]. Additionally, quantum architectures require nonlinear gates and error-correcting codes.
