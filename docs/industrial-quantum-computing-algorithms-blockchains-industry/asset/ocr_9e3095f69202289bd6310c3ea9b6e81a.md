# 15.7.1 Privacy and security in quantum computing for chemical and pharmaceutical research

Since quantum computing could improve pharmaceutical and chemical research, ethical considerations must be thoroughly investigated. Quantum computing's privacy and security implications for chemical and pharmaceutical research are examined here. We will identify the biggest issues, their causes, and solutions. People worry about privacy and security because of quantum computing' enormous processing power. Quantum computers outperform classical computers in integer factorization and discrete logarithm. This could replace encryption. Patented chemical structures, intellectual property, and medical records are at risk. Grover's and Shor's quantum algorithms can crack RSA and ECC, threatening storage and communication networks. Researchers are creating quantum-resistant cryptographic systems like hash- and lattice-based encryption to reduce this risk.

Quantum databases and AI algorithms raise concerns about personal data processing speed and security. Avoiding data breaches requires protecting genetic, pharmaceutical, and chemical databases. Quantum-resistant protocols and post-quantum cryptography protect private data. QKD and quantum-resistant encryption protect communication and data in the age of quantum computing. Quantum computing in chemical and pharmaceutical research requires technical advances, regulatory frameworks, and ethical norms for privacy and security. We need quantum-resistant cryptographic approaches, quantum R&D transparency and accountability, and better data encryption and access controls to reduce this risk.
