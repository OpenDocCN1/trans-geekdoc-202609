# 1.2.1 Quantum computing elements

The key elements of quantum computing are as follows (Figure 1.1):

![](bbox=[0.105, 0.187, 0.563, 0.355])

**Figure 1.1:** Key elements of quantum computing.

+   **Quantum bits** are the basic building blocks of quantum computing, often known as qubits. In contrast to traditional bits, qubits can only take on the value 0 or 1, qubits can hold onto both states simultaneously. Quantum computers may increase their processing capacity exponentially by processing huge volumes of data in parallel, thanks to their special characteristics [14].
**Quantum superposition** allows qubits to exist in different states simultaneously. It is a principle of quantum physics. This effect gives quantum computers a significant advantage over conventional computers for solving problems, as they may perform computations on all conceivable combinations of qubits concurrently [15].
**Quantum entanglement** is an crucial feature of quantum physics. It occurs when two physically distant qubits' states become interdependent. The instantaneous communication between qubits made by their interconnection considerably increases the power and efficiency of quantum computing [16].
