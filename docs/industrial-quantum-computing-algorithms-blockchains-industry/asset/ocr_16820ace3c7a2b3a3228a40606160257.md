**d) Quantum reinforcement learning (QRL):** These algorithms develop the best rules and behavior through reinforcement learning using quantum agents and quantum surroundings. Utilizing quantum resources and feedback, QRL blends quantum theory with reinforcement learning to improve information and incentives for quantum agents over traditional agents [16]. Although QRL has not yet been empirically tested it has demonstrated improved performance in simulations. Quantum parallelism and the superposition of quantum states serve as the foundation for QRL. Through simulated trials, it seeks to expedite learning and accomplish a balanced approach between exploration and exploitation in reinforcement learning.

The aim of QRL is to create quantum principles that enhance decision-making in dynamic situations, which often include a trade-off between exploration and exploitation. Reinforcement learning focuses on an agent interacting with an environment to maximize the overall rewards obtained over time [9]. This interaction is often shown as a Markov decision process, including a tuple $(S_t, A_t, P_t, R_t)$, where

+   - $S_t$ depicts the possible states of the environment,
- $A_t$ is the collection of actions available to the agent,
- $P_t$ is the transition function that represents the likelihood of moving from one state to another based on a certain activity, and
- $R_t$ is the reward function that gives instant feedback to the agent according to its behaviors.

e) Quantum strengthening, quantum algorithms are used in learning to improve the effectiveness and efficiency of learning and decision-making processes. To do this, states, actions, and policies must be represented by quantum states and operations, and the state-action space must be efficiently traversed by utilizing quantum parallelism and interference. The agent can explore several possibilities simultaneously representing the quantum state as a superposition of classical states.
