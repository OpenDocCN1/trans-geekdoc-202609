As mentioned earlier, medical imaging generates the largest volumes of data in the medical system, which also means a high percentage of errors in image recording, especially X-rays, with their percentage reaching up to 25% [11].

Researchers at the University of Washington, Seattle, have developed an application dedicated to errors in X-ray scans that manage to reduce the percentage of error from 20.6% to 6.9%. This is being done automatically, and the application manages to replace about 7 h of work required by a specialist to complete the same activity [12].

This shows us that big data can no longer be selected, analyzed and processed to provide useful results for humans without the help of computers and digital applications.

In order for digital data to be used and the information contained to be used to its full value, it is necessary that it is stored certain elements to save optimization, classification of data according to predetermined criteria, creation of structured databases, insertion of contextual elements that can help perform analysis and processing, and the use of globally standardized codes so that data can be comparable.

# 6.4 How is health information used?

The evolution of health systems is a major one, with changes related to both existing infrastructure and facilities, as well as new models of treatment for diseases data collected at the level of populations or individuals.

Applying analytics to data collected in health can save lives by analyzing the evolution or prevention of pandemics, revealing trends or even optimizing health costs.

At the individual level, the existence of a digital health record, which includes the patient's medical history over several years, can easily show what future health trends are, with the possibility of detecting a disease at an early stage or even anticipating measures to prevent it.

Screening activities can detect cases of illness early, and their treatment is easier when detecting advanced forms of the disease. Both activities fulfill at least two positive aspects: maintaining a high level of health and at the same time reducing health expenses. The costs of treating a disease or preventing it are significantly lower than those detected at an advanced stage, where, most of the time, advanced medical procedures or state-of-the-art treatments are needed [13].

The importance of data processed in health is given by the results obtained, which can predict future developments and even solve problems by anticipating trends in health, rapid evaluation of treatments and methods used, efficient cost management by optimizing expenses, monitoring the health status of patients, and engaging them in activities beneficial to health.

The amount of data obtained by healthcare workers is increasing, and the underlying issue being not only its quantity but also the quality of the information stored, as well as how compatible the different data sources are with each other.
