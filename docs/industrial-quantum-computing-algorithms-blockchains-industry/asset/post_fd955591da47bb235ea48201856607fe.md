## 2.4 Key applications

Because it operates in a binary domain, classical computing is limited in the amount of data it can analyze and the quality of the judgments it can generate. Another name for this is serial processing. However, multidimensional processing is what QCMs employ. To get the right answer, serial processing evaluates each combination ofata by one by. In contrast to the bits-based method of serial processing, multidimensional processing is stacked. This improves the variety of the findings, speeds up the supply of accurate calculations, and more. Quantum computing, in a nutshell, can provide higher quality outcomes more quickly, by Flöter [36]. Figure 2.2 represents the key application of QML.

Some future uses of quantum computing include the following:

- AI and ML technologies: AI-ML may considerably benefit from the ability to calculate solutions to problems in parallel rather than sequentially. Organizations nowadays depend on AI and ML to discover new approaches for optimizing processes and automating repetitive tasks. Optimization becomes much more effective and adaptable when dealing with large, complex, or unorganized datasets combined with quantum technologies by Rokade and Sharma [37].
- Economic forecasting: Financial companies might benefit from quantum computing modeling capabilities for large-scale investment and security behavior modeling. The optimization of large-scale portfolios, the reduction of risk, and the implementation of financial institutions’ understanding of the patterns and movements of the global financial industry might all be achieved using QML by Lilhore [38].
- Protecting data online: The future of encryption and personal data protection may be affected by quantum computing. Data encryption while in use, both at rest and in transit, may be aided by QMCs in the cybersecurity scenario [39].
- Optimal routing and traffic: An efficiently functioning supply chain depends on well-designed transportation routes. QMCs analyze the data instantaneously and concurrently optimize travel plans of a whole fleet of vehicles.
- The production process: Better, more lifelike testing and prototyping can be done using QMCs. In the industrial sector, this could potentially improve designs with less testing and lower cost [40].
- Studying chemicals and drugs: With the help of QMCs, we may improve our knowledge of molecular structure and interactions with other elements. Future development, evolution, and interactions of chemical compounds and pharmaceuticals might be predicted by the predictive capabilities of QCs.
- Energy storage devices: With quantum computing, producers may better understand how to integrate novel materials into existing goods like semiconductors and batteries. This might uncover more information on how to make batteries last longer and use less power. The chemistry of batteries and lithium compounds may be better understood by quantum computing.
