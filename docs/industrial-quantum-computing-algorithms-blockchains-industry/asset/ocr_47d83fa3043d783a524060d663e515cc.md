Quantum computing uses quantum mechanics to process information faster than traditional computers, which have huge healthcare potential. In this study, we examine quantum physics, quantum technologies, and the significant implications for healthcare applications [1–2]. Qubits' extraordinary features have propelled quantum computing to the forefront of scientific inquiry and innovation. Superposition and entanglement allow qubits to exist in various states, unlike classical bits that can only represent 0 or 1. This unique quantum characteristic lets quantum computers perform calculated calculations much quicker than classical computers. The healthcare sector is about to use this quantum power to solve its biggest problems. We begin this examination by describing how qubits function and how quantum gates control them compute. Readers of any background will understand quantum computing's healthcare potential with this core understanding. After comprehending quantum fundamentals, we examine the existing technologies. IBM, Google, and companies like Rigetti and IonQ are creating and deploying quantum technology quickly. The hardware, software, and advances in solving the problems that have hindered quantum computing's practical implementation will be evaluated. We will examine quantum computing, cloud platforms, and programming languages that academics and healthcare practitioners may use to improve the area. As we study quantum computing, we will discover its enormous and interesting healthcare applications. Quantum computing will simulate chemical interactions with unparalleled accuracy and speed, revolutionizing drug research. It might revolutionize genomics by providing rapid analysis of genetic datasets for tailored therapy. Quantum computing can improve medical imaging for faster, more accurate diagnoses and better treatment strategies by building logical processes. We will examine each application in depth to show how quantum computing will change healthcare. However, quantum computing in healthcare presents ethical and practical challenges before it can reach its full potential. Security, error risk, and quantum-resistant encryption are important challenges.
