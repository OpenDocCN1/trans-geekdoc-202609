# 5.6 Supply chain spectre

The multilayer global supply chains for manufacturing and distributing IoT devices offer attackers several tactics, which provide an opportunity to insert malicious code or hardware backdoors at various stages:

+   1. **Hidden backdoors:** Meanwhile, malicious actors can put stealthy backdoors in the firmware or hardware when manufacturing items, which may give them access to devices after the supply chain. As reported by the National Intelligence and Security Center in 2020, supply chain attacks are among the major threats to national security by 80% of organizations experiencing at least one such.
2. **Counterfeit components:** Instead of the original, incorporating malware into counterfeit components can open doors to system-wide vulnerabilities. A study by the International Trade Commission in 2021 shows that the amount of counterfeit electronics was estimated at $230 billion worldwide, pinpointing this problem at a high level.
3. **Software tampering:** Malicious entities could modify software updates delivered during distribution, introducing vulnerabilities and compromising numerous devices as if a virus. The 2021 SolarWinds attack is a good example of the negative consequences for a company that has been made to update its software using compromised methods.
4. **Edge computing exposure:** 6G's great focus on edge computing, which means that data is processed close to the devices [15], presents a new set of security challenges.
5. **Distributed attack surface:** The greater number of entrance endpoints increases the security, making it harder to protect and monitor every entry point. Sources indicate that 6G could produce 1 trillion connected devices by 2030, which would increase the number of vulnerabilities by a large factor [17].
6. **Limited resource constraints:** Edge devices are usually affected by resource-constrained attacks because these devices have limited processing power and memory. Research carried out by the University of Oxford in 2022 has shown that resource depletion attacks may make compromising edge devices used in smart homes possible, which could result in gaining unauthorized access or manipulating data.
7. **Data privacy concerns:** The edge computing environment causes concern as to the safety of data and how it could be misused by unauthorized access. Pew Research Center finds that 72% of Americans worry about companies collecting their data without permission. This shows that data privacy regulation is crucial for edge computing.
