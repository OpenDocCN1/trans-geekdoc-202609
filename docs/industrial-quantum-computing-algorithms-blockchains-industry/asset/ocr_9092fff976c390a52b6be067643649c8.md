QNNs consist of layers, including input encoding, where classical data is transformed into quantum data; quantum gate layers, where operations are performed on qubits; entanglement layers for creating complex relationships between qubits; parameterized gates for adjustable operations; measurement layers for obtaining classical output; and error correction layers for mitigating noise and de-coherence. By utilizing the features of quantum mechanics, QNNs hold promise for revolutionizing various fields of ML and computational science.

# 9.2.2 Architecture of QNN

The QNNs are developed as feed-forward networks, where each layer evaluates input from the previous layer and passes the output to the next layer. The layers can have different widths (varying numbers of qubits) and are trained similarly to classical neural networks.
