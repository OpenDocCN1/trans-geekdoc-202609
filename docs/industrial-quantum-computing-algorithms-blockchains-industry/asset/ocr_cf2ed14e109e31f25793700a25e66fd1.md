# 5.2 Literature review

This section comprehensively reviews the existing literature on IoT networks' security and privacy challenges. It synthesizes insights from previous research, establishing a foundation for understanding the unique considerations of integrating 6G technologies.

Rappaport et al. [1] provided an extensive overview of future wireless systems above 100 GHz, likely part of 6G, discussing regulatory frameworks and showcasing potential applications. It emphasizes the significance of THz frequencies for new capabilities like cognition, imaging, and communication, highlighting challenges and the need for further research in such as antenna technology, propagation measurements, and biological effects of THz radiation.

Ziegler et al. [2] described the journey toward 6G connectivity with the successful initiation of architectural research, while commercial 5G deployment is ongoing worldwide. Over the next 8 years, the evolution of 5G architecture will continue, paving the way for exciting opportunities in real-time communication and synchronization between the physical, digital, and biological worlds. This will be facilitated by a hot cloud platform, convergent RAN-CORE, new data and information architecture, specialization of the network, and intent-based automation, laying the foundation for diverse applications across consumer and vertical industries.

Akyildiz et al. [3] surveyed key techniques for future wireless networks, emphasizing wireless ubiquity as a driving force for 6G and beyond. They highlight essential technologies, operational nuances, and challenges, aiming to offer insights and inspire contributions toward achieving ubiquitous wireless access. Minghao Wang et al. [4] discussed the transition from 5G to 6G networks, highlighting the anticipation for advancements in network services. They provide a comprehensive investigation into security and privacy concerns, from the historical evolution of mobile networks to potential applications of 6G, aiming to stimulate further research and interest in addressing these issues.

Liu et al. [5] discussed the rapid advancement of ICDT properly toward digital twin integration, with envisioned applications such as synesthesia Internet and super-transportation. Key network performance requirements for 6G, including peak rates and ultra-high-precision positioning, are discussed alongside proposed features like on-demand fulfillment and AI, aimed at addressing lessons learned from 5G's development.
