# 15.3 Quantum chemistry models

Computational chemists use molecular structure simulation to understand molecular activity, predict chemical properties, and speed drug development. Quantum computing and molecular simulation are extensively covered in this article. This application's methods, capabilities, and outcomes are covered. The main issues with classical molecular dynamics simulations are the inadequacy of classical force fields in modeling quantum events and the exponential growth of computing needs with system size. By using quantum computers, we can simulate quantum mechanical phenomena exponentially better. This enables attribute modeling and molecular interaction. Modeling molecular architectures uses quantum eigenvectors and quantum subspace expansion. These technologies accurately calculate molecular energies, electrical structures, and spectroscopic properties using quantum computers. We also consider quantum algorithms to molecular dynamics simulations and quantum chemistry calculations to create a hybrid strategy that incorporates the best of both worlds. Quantum error correction, efficient quantum algorithms, noise and decoherence mitigation, complicated molecular systems, and other molecular structure research topics are covered. We also examine how quantum computing could improve molecular biology, rational materials, and drug development. Quantum computing may assist computational chemistry and molecular structure comprehension, as shown by this lengthy study.
