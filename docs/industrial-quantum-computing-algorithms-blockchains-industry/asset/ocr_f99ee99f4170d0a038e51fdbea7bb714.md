# 10.2.1 Theoretical background

The rapid and widespread advancement of automation and AI is transforming business, industry, and service operations in unprecedented ways. A large portion of the discussion following the introduction of these innovative technologies has centered on their potential effects on jobs and the likelihood of people losing their jobs. Nevertheless, another effect that is not often talked about but is just as important as its influence on wealth inequality. This literature review will examine the shifts in inequality in developed and developing countries in recent decades, and how automation and AI have impacted and will continue to impact this trend.

Income inequality refers to the degree of economic disparity among individuals within a society, as determined by their personal income and wealth [14]. Economic inequality has been a consistent aspect of life in the majority of nations. Recently, this thus has again captured national interest. During extended economic stagnation and increasing profits, lower-income households have seen stagnant or decreasing salaries, while higher-income households have experienced a growth in their incomes [15]. This significant change in dynamics has led to a rise in income inequality, both in theory and in practice, by developed and developing countries [16]. From 1945 to 1975, the developed world saw major improvements in equality due to widespread economic growth and redistributive policies. This was partially a result of increased government regulation of labor markets, higher taxes on the wealth, and social welfare programs [17]. Nevertheless, starting from 1975, inequality started to increase consistently due to shifts in politics, salaries, and taxation, leading to a growing disparity in wealth between the affluent and the marginalized sectors of society [18].
