# 12.3.1 Legacy systems and outdated infrastructure

Many healthcare institutions still depend on obsolete infrastructure with no modern security features or patch management. Such systems, therefore, become easy for attackers to exploit by capitalizing on the known vulnerabilities that are not fixed immediately or are not feasible to fix due to compatibility issues.

# 12.3.2 Fragmented ecosystem

The healthcare sector is a network of intertwined stakeholders such as hospitals, clinics, insurers, and pharmacies. This fragmented ecosystem provides many infiltration points for attackers, increasing the potential attack surface.

# 12.3.3 Human error

Even though there is progress in technology, the human factor is still a weak link in cybersecurity. Phishing attacks and insider threats are still a major concern. Healthcare professionals can be fooled into giving out confidential information by phishing emails, and dissatisfied employees may purposefully damage systems or data.

# 12.3.4 Data privacy concerns

With healthcare data becoming increasingly valuable, containing private details such as medical records, financial information, and even genetic information, breaches can have terrible outcomes. With such data, identity theft, financial fraud, and discrimination are possible.

# 12.3.5 Evolving attack landscape

Cybercriminals keep devising novel and smart attack techniques. The increase in ransomware incidents has affected healthcare, obstructed services, and led to financial losses. Additionally, targeted attacks against organizations or individuals are on the rise.
