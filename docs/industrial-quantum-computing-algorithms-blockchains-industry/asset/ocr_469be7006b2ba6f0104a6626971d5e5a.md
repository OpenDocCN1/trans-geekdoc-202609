# 1.2 Quantum computing

In quantum computing, which is grounded in the principles of quantum physics, data is represented and processed using qubits, which are quantum bits, rather than conventional bits. Because of this, quantum computers can factor in huge numbers and other specific kinds of computations far more quickly than conventional computers. Number theory and cryptography are two areas of mathematics that stand benefit greatly from the advent of quantum computing [12].

To illustrate the potency of quantum technology in cryptography, consider the possibility that quantum computers might decipher a number of the encryption techniques already used to safeguard online communications. Furthermore, chemistry, materials research, and medication development might benefit from quantum computing's ability to model quantum systems. Complex quantum systems might be modeled via these simulations, which are infeasible or difficult to describe with conventional computers. Quantum theory has far-reaching consequences in this field [13].
