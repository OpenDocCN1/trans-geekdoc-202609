# 2.7 Conclusion and future direction

QML is an area brimming with dangers and opportunities. To fully exploit the capabilities of this technology, researchers must surround challenges as hardware limitations, algorithmic advancement, and data encoding. Conversely, QML exhibits auspicious potential in terms of heightened velocity, improved precision, and innovative implementations in numerous fields. The pharmaceutical industry is increasingly adopting ML techniques, whereas QCMs have only recently entered this market. It is a daunting task to integrate the two technologies practically; therefore, multidisciplinary research teams with a broad spectrum of expertise are required. This is our inaugural attempt at assessing QML algorithms in the context of pharmaceutical discovery.

By analyzing paragraphs and articles from the last 5 years, this study sought to derive information regarding trends in drug development using QML models, the phases of the drug discovery pipeline where these models were implemented, and the challenges associated with this endeavor. When each case is optimal, the costs of classical and QML methods are equivalent, it is unclear which is more efficient. In the preponderance of cases, conventional and quantum computing are combined. Regarding drug development, QML inevitably its efforts on the initial stages of the drug discovery pipeline. Small datasets, large molecules, quantum embedding, reduced dimensionality, baseline dataset, and near-term QCM are the seven obstacles that have been surmounted.

Due to constrained qubits, the researchers' primary concern was the prospective pharmaceutical industry applications of near-term QCM. As a result of the substantial body of literature and the participation of scholars from academia and industry, the forthcoming research will focus on undertaking a systematic review of this specific field of inquiry. In the coming years, monitoring the rate of drug discovery advances due to QML will be critical.
