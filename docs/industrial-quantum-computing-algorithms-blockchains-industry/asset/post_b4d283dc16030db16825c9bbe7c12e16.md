## 10.4 Results

### 10.4.1 Use of AI in BRICS

#### 10.4.1.1 Use of AI and automation in industry

A World Bank report states that around 10% of the global workforce works in the banking sector, where tasks are increasingly being handled by automated machines and AI technologies. According to Paul [61], Fintech companies are utilizing AI technology and more to achieve multiple goals such as risk management, risk assessment, fraud detection, and safeguarding consumer interests. Other commonly utilized fields encompass credit evaluation, virtual assistants, efficient use of resources, and examination of market impact, trade indicators, and regulatory technology solutions. Factors such as risk have left vulnerable populations like women, youths, and small businesses as smallholder farmers to left out of the traditional banking sector. Due to the difficulty in measuring and evaluating risk within these vulnerable populations, many of them were seen as high-risk individuals. However, the growing use of algorithms to automate risk detection, management, and oversight is changing the landscape of financial inclusion. AI is helping marginalized communities to utilize digital resources as smartphones and provide access to financial services and connect to devices point-of-sale terminals [63, 64]. A mobile device equipped could enable individuals to perform various financial tasks such as deposit funds, withdraw cash, transfer money, payments, access credit, and save money [65]. This allows individuals with low income to access services that have been out of reach through the traditional banking system. In addition, AI technology was utilized to complete the process of digital account sign-ups, with approximately 17 million accounts being registered in Kenya in the initial stages of 2012, and 7 accounts registered in Tanzania in 2016 [66].

Banks are currently adopting AI-driven customer support systems and help desks, which are significantly improving efficiency and reducing costs associated with customer support. Moreover, AI can be utilized by financial institutions for personalized banking through chatbots and AI offering an electronic virtual assistant (EVA). AI assistants, offering tailored financial advice and quick, self-service client support using natural language processing [67, 68]. Moreover, AI is utilized as a relationship manager, as banks are integrating chatbots to provide financial advice and assistance to vulnerable rural households that may not receive it when interacting with humans. The HDFC Bank in India has already placed a chatbot in place to act as a relationship manager. The utilization of AI and automation is quickly growing in the BRICS nations (Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa) as technology progresses in developed and emerging economies. AI and automation are being used to increase efficiency, streamline manual tasks, and enhance customer interactions. Nevertheless, these advancements have implications for disparity in earnings because automation may impact lower-paying jobs more and AI has the capability to supplant human judgment.

AI and automation are being employed in China to promote increased efficiency in the industrial and service industries, resulting in increased productivity and profit margins. However, automation has resulted in job losses in both the manufacturing and service industries, increasing economic inequality. Research from the Chinese Academy of Sciences shows that automation in China led to the loss of around 13 million manufacturing jobs from 1995 to 2015 resulting in significant salary reductions for the remaining workers. Automation has decreased and janitorial services, reducing the need for low-paying jobs in the service sector, resulting in a rise in income inequality [69]. AI and automation are being utilized in India's banking industry to enhance operational efficiency, reduce manual labor expenses, and enhance customer satisfaction. Yet, AI and automation are being utilized in the retail industry to replace low-paying positions, resulting in increased inequality [70]. Research from the Indira Gandhi Institute of Development shows that automation in the retail industry in India led to a rise in income inequality by 11% from 2007 to 2013.

AI and automation are being used in Brazil to enhance agricultural efficiency, reducing labor expenses and boosting agricultural production [71]. However, automation has resulted in the reduction of poorly paid jobs in the industry, leading to a growth in income inequality. A study from Universidade Federal de Sergi found the use of AI in Brazil's agriculture industry led to reduced employment and earnings, with a 6.8% rise in income inequality from 2002 to 2015. AI and automation are being utilized in the manufacturing sector in Russia, leading to improved effectiveness and reduced costs [72]. With the replacement of low-skilled and low-paid manufacturing jobs by automation, there has been a rise in income inequality [73]. Research from the National Research University Higher School of Economics suggests that automation in manufacturing led to a 0.33% rise in income inequality between 1992 and 2006 [74]. AI and automation are more prevalent in the financial industry in South Africa, leading to higher efficiency and improved customer satisfaction. Nonetheless, automation in this industry has been linked to an increase in economic inequality as it leads to the elimination of jobs that require little skill and pay low wages [60]. Research from the University of Johannesburg found that the implementation of AI and automation in the financial services sector of South Africa led to a 0.12% rise in income inequality from 1989 to 2004. In general, the utilization of AI and automation in the BRICS countries is resulting in higher efficiency and reduced costs. Nevertheless, it is also causing a rise in income inequality, with automation replacing jobs that require lower wages and skills, while AI takes the place of human decision-making.

## 10.4.2 Use of AI and automation in agriculture

Poverty, as mentioned before, comes in many forms. It appears as a deficiency in earnings, schooling, and sometimes a shortage of social support, along with concerns about access to food (by DAIA Medium 2020). This mainly impacts rural regions, where most of the impoverished population resides. The World Bank states that in locations where poverty is widespread, agriculture serves as the main source of income. AI applications, per Strusani and Houbongnon (2019), have the ability to tackle various challenges faced by individuals in the lowest bracket, specifically the bottom 40%. Although low-income individuals are unable to purchase AI-enabled technology, there is a suggestion that they may still benefit from AI through utilizing service solutions on their smartphones. The machine learning system used in BRICS countries agriculture is an illustration of practicality. This app was used for farmers to detect leaf damage in photos captured by farmers and shared with authorities to monitor the presence of a pest. Another aspect of AI is the ability to link data from mobile devices with a farmer's financial status, level of education, and state of health. This allows apps to offer microloans, personalized education, healthcare assessments, and mediation. Using speech recognition and speech-to-text features to help the most marginalized groups overcome literacy challenges, especially when using text-based apps, is a crucial way of AI technology. In many rural areas, AI image recognition can be used to assess farmer insurance claims. These AI tools used by farmers enhance efficiency, resulting in higher profits. Mhlanga [57] states that AI and robotics are increasingly important in combating global hunger. Companies like Google and Stanford University's sustainability and AI lab are making progress. These organizations are creating AI programs for use in agriculture to improve farming by detection of diseases, forecasting crop yields, and identifying areas at risk of shortages. A demonstration of AI is in the FarmView application created by researchers at Carnegie Mellon University to help farmers increase production using the same amount of crops [57]. Therefore, AI is assisting African agriculture in achieving greater sustainability, ultimately aiding in feeding the population.
