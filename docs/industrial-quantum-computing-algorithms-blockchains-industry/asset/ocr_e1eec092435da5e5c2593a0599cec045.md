# 5.8.7 Continuous security monitoring and compliance

Regular supervision of infrastructure elements and adherence to industry regulations to achieve the desired level of security is a must. A study from IBM revealed that the average data breach cost would be $4.2 million, which shows significant continuous monitoring regarding security in detection and timely response to incidents. The 6G IoT network protection employs a strategic approach that considers cryptography, authentication, secure design principles, intrusion detection, ZTA, automation, threat intelligence awareness, and continuous monitoring. By integrating these approaches with global statistics and industry best practices, organizations can manage risks and IoT deployments from emerging threats.

# 5.8.8 Mitigating risks through quantum-secure solutions

While looking at the edge of the quantum computer, classic cryptographic techniques are being challenged by the growing necessity. Currently, quantum computers will probably render the cryptographic protocols useless, which threaten to be the new norm that will be the focal point of security of the 6G networks for the IoT-connected future. Confronting this looming danger, quantum-protected systems have become essential for preserving cryptography even stronger against quantum-controlled attacks [24, 39].

Quantum security solutions constitute a paradigm change of a purely technical approach to encryption algorithms and utilizing the elements of physics to make them secure and withstand quantum attacks. Among several prospective approaches, latticebased cryptography is probably the most promising method based on the probable intractability of some mathematical problems in higher dimensional space. Lattice-based schemes are feasible as they bring strong security guarantees, unlike classical and quantum hazards, as they are potential candidates for providing security for the 6G IoT.

Code-based cryptography builds up error-correcting codes as foundations for cryptographic primitives that are inhumanly attack-proof. Without quantum computing, code-based cryptography, in which errors in quantum operations are detectable and correctable, is the best way to protect data from hostile quantum environments. Hash function-based cryptography is another promising technology that uses cryptographic hash functions for authentication and verification. More checksums than the numbers set by quantum computing could stop it from being cracked. Although hash-based methods are not unruff by quantum hackers, they can be added with quantum-safe processes to develop their security.
