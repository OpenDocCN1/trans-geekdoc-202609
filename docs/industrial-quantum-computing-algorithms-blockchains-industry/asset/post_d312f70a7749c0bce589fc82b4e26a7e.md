Inequality has grown on a worldwide scale in the past few decades, creating concerns about the welfare of people and communities in numerous areas of the globe. Promoting entrepreneurship is a suggested strategy for increasing economic opportunities and reducing inequality. Studies have demonstrated that entrepreneurship has diverse impacts on income inequality, and the existing research is crucial for policymakers seeking to address these disparities. Multiple research papers have indicated that entrepreneurship can help individuals escape poverty. For instance, Si and Ahlstrom [38] found that individuals who lost their own businesses were in comparison to individuals who remained employed or unemployed. Like-Waziriiri and Chivandi [39] explored a link between starting new businesses and decreasing poverty, showing that people can earn more money than before even with limited resources to establish a company. Entrepreneurship plays a vital role in reducing disparities among gender and social classes. Research shows that people from diverse backgrounds encounter many obstacles when to establish a business, including issues like inadequate funding and limited social connections [38]. Research shows that female entrepreneurs, can profit from entrepreneurship and make higher salaries than in conventional job markets [40]. Moreover, entrepreneurship contribute to decreasing overall income inequality by promoting the redistribution of wealth across various economic levels. Similarly, Morris and Santos [41] stated that increased entrepreneurial activity has narrowed the income gap, benefiting individuals with lower income levels.

Entrepreneurs managed to utilize resources, acquire expertise, and tap into new markets, enabling them to increase their wealth. Several research studies have also discovered variations in the effect of entrepreneurship on income inequality based on the type of entrepreneur. For instance, Bruton and Sutter [42] discovered that younger entrepreneurs were more inclined to increase their wealth compared to older entrepreneurs due to their improved access to resources and networks. When considering policy implications, it is important to emphasize the need to empower entrepreneurs with necessary skills and resources to enhance the impact of entrepreneurship on reducing income inequality. Academics have recommended that the government play a more proactive role in fostering entrepreneurial activities by expanding financial opportunities, improving the availability of government information, and supporting post-law research, development, and market entry [46]. ## 10.2.3 Impact of women's empowerment on income inequality

In the current economy, the problem of income inequality is increasingly noticeable and significant. Finkelstein and Harding [48] have linked income inequality to increased poverty rates, persistent racial and gender discrimination, deteriorating mental health, decreased overall well-being, and reduced economic growth. Women are equally affected by income inequality, facing challenges like gender disparity, poverty levels, and systematic discrimination in the workplace [49]. Empowering women has become an important strategy in addressing economic inequality [50]. The empowerment of women involves various aspects with the goal of enhancing economic growth, social progress, and political stability by ensuring equal opportunities for both men and women according to their capabilities and requirements. Valeriani and Sarajevo Vukovic [51] define women's empowerment as a strategy that includes six dimensions: economic security, education access, legal rights, decision-making power, political engagement, and women's welfare. Every one of these attributes has been demonstrated to impact women's financial stability, thereby helping decrease income inequality. Encouraging income-generating activities and increasing financial autonomy are vital strategies for empowering women to mitigate economic disparities. Research shows that providing women access to microfinance directly impacts their capacity to generate income, enhance earnings from current businesses, and improve economic stability [52]. This holds particularly true for females in developing countries, who have limited opportunities for education, resources, and credit [53].

Empowering women can also reduce income inequality by increasing their educational opportunities. Education plays a crucial role in an individual's overall welfare and has the potential to greatly influence their earnings. Research has demonstrated that education enhances women's outcomes in the labor market, less poverty, and stimulates economic growth [54]. Women's ability to obtain education, especially at secondary and postgraduate levels, increases their chances of securing well-paying jobs and narrowing the wage disparity between men and women. Ultimately, the empowerment of women contributes to achieving social and economic equity by reducing gender discrimination in the workforce. Research suggests that providing women with equal opportunities in the labor force and eliminating gender discrimination can positively affect income levels and decrease income inequality [55]. In addition, providing women with economic empowerment can result in improved economic opportunities for all women, ultimately enhancing household welfare [56].
