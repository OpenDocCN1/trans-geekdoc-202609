Implementing a secure-by-design principle for IoT devices and infrastructure development minimizes vulnerabilities [23]. ### 5.8.8 Mitigating risks through quantum-secure solutions

While looking at the edge of the quantum computer, classic cryptographic techniques are being challenged by the growing necessity. Currently, quantum computers will probably render the cryptographic protocols useless, which threaten to be the new norm that will be the focal point of security of the 6G networks. Confronting this looming danger, quantum-protected systems have become essential for preserving cryptography even stronger quantum attacks [24, 39].

Quantum security solutions constitute a paradigm change of a purely technical approach to encryption algorithms and utilizing the elements of physics to make them secure and withstand quantum attacks. Among several prospective approaches, latticebased cryptography is probably the most promising method based on the probable intractability of some mathematical problems in higher dimensional space. Lattice-based schemes are feasible as they bring strong security guarantees, unlike classical and quantum hazards, as they are potential candidates for providing security for the 6G IoT.

Code-based cryptography builds up error-correcting codes as foundations for cryptographic primitives that are inhumanly attack-proof. Without quantum computing, code-based cryptography, in which errors in quantum operations are detectable and correctable, is the best way to protect data from hostile quantum environments. Hash function-based cryptography is another promising technology that uses cryptographic hash functions for authentication and verification. Although hash-based methods are not unruff by quantum hackers, they can be added with quantum-safe processes to develop their security.
