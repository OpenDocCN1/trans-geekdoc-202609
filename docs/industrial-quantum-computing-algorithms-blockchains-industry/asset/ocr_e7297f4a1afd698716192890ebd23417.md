# 5.1.1.3 3G (third generation): the mobile Internet age (2000s)

+   - Global phone subscribers: During 2007, the number of people equal 2.5 billion (2.5).
- Data speeds: Up to 2,222.2 kbps.

The introduction of 3G networking in the early 2000s, the most crucial technological innovation of time, allowed for rapid data transmission and, for, Internet access from mobile phones and multimedia messaging. In 2010, 3G networks covered nearly 90% of the global population, driving the proliferation of mobile data services. The rates rocket, making it possible to hold video calls, stream music, or browse the Internet. This age has ushered in the years of smartphones and apps, making it impossible for us to consume information in the same way and interact with the digital world in the same manner. This generation fuels the explosion of global mobile data, using it like no other generation ever has.

# 5.1.1.4 4G (fourth generation): the era of the speed revolution (2010s)

+   - Global phone subscribers: 5.4 billion (2017)
- Data speeds: Up to 100 Mbps

The 2000s rolled out 4G and showed a revolutionary change in the network with high-speed broadband or ultrafast speed, low latency, and increased efficiency. The upgrade from 3G to 4G was a big jump that changed the Internet. This generation away with the legacy of slow mobile Internet access, which was severely restrictive compared to what was possible before, allowing for HD content streaming, audio-video communications, and bandwidth-hungry applications. The magnitude went beyond the individuals, manifesting itself in the operation and progress of the various enterprises that depend on real-time data transmission. The mobile phone became a common device; thus, mobile Internet became users' top connectivity choice.
