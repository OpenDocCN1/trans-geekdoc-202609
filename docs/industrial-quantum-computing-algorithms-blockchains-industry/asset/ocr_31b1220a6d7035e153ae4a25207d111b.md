Quantum computing's capacity to handle large datasets and complicated data dimensions can help us comprehend disease genetics in genomics and biomarker detection. Quantum-enhanced imaging might improve medical imaging by detecting diseases early and more precisely. Quantum computing may improve supply optimization, patient scheduling, and medicine dose, improving healthcare delivery. However, healthcare quantum integration issues must be acknowledged. Hardware limits, mistake correction, and ethics are these obstacles. These concerns show that quantum computing in healthcare is still young [7–9]. Current research and development are tackling these restrictions. Quantum computing entrepreneurs, healthcare institutions, and regulatory agencies must collaborate to realize its full potential. To protect data privacy, security, and ethical use, ethics must guide this shift. Future quantum computing in healthcare promises more precise diagnosis, tailored therapies, and better outcomes. This transformative technology underscores the need for sustained research and medical implementation, ushering in a new era where quantum computing is essential to medical science and global healthcare.
