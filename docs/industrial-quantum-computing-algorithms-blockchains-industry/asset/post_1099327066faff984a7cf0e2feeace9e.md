## 6.1 Introduction

Health systems are a basic component of society, which must provide the necessary medical services to maintain the best possible health of citizens, their performance ensuring the support and development of society. From an economic perspective, a well-performing healthcare system can influence citizens' productivity, and on the other, generate income for healthcare workers through jobs and business opportunities.

The functions of health systems are multiple, their role being not only to provide healthcare services to the population but also to limit citizens' exposure to healthcare costs. There is also a social component, which shows their complexity.

If, at all the medical field developed through the need to solve specific health problems or accidental discoveries by dedicated researchers who, through their research, revolutionized medicine, now we can talk about moving to another stage, in which discoveries and innovations in the field influence the quality of life by improving health and the way we live.

Modern medicine is inconceivable without medical equipment, achieved because of the technological revolution in the field, which provides specialists with increasingly sophisticated and advanced tools to support the provision of the highest quality medical services for the benefit of patients.

Nowadays, the use of computed tomography (CT), nuclear magnetic resonance magnetic imaging (MRI), positron emission tomography, angiography, various medical robots for diagnosis, treatment, and surgery, as well as the use of a multitude of personal mobile devices dedicated to health monitoring, has become commonplace due to an accelerated evolution in the medical field.

The era of patient medical records with hundreds of yellowed pages and handwritten medical documents is becoming history, which is replaced by digital files that can be easily stored and transmitted for analysis by specialists.

All this has been made possible by the perfection and widespread use of personal computers and dedicated applications that have evolved over time and are interconnected in networks across the globe.

But what do they all have in common? Big Data, which consists of information stored in electronic format, with exponential growth over time, and their storage, analysis and processing is a real challenge for the most advanced computing systems.

The level of stored electronic data has reached such a high volume and level of complexity that it is impossible for humans to analyze and process it without the help of specialized applications installed on equipment with increasing computing power.

This has reached the stage where, many times, current technology is no longer efficient at such a high volume of data, and the solutions from quantum computers that can process information and solve problems at breakneck speeds.

In healthcare, several clear directions are already outlined where quantum computers can make a difference: in pharmaceuticals to accelerate the discovery of new drugs and vaccines, DNA sequencing, molecular simulations, diagnostic assistance, radiotherapy, predictions, and health simulations.

These are just a few examples where quantum computers would show their supremacy in terms of information processing power, but the possibilities are endless if we talk about the use of artificial intelligence (AI) or quantum cloud.

This chapter aims to analyze the medical fields in which quantum computers have been used, as well as the development possibilities and applications in which they could revolutionize health systems.

On the other hand, the question arises as to how prepared health systems are to adopt quantum technology, given the challenges related to its implementation and maintenance, and especially to data and information security.

In an era of digital technology, where electronically stored data is multiplying exponentially, with increasing cyber threats in the healthcare sector, protecting it will be of paramount importance.

## 6.2 Modern health systems

Health systems, regardless of their form of organization, are extremely complex and dynamically interconnected with other systems of society, and the evolution of these systems and the results of medical activity can influence society.

The proper functioning of a health system is based on certain key functions that can generate a difference in their performance: the level of funding, system administration, provision of medical services, resources attracted to the system include staff, facilities developed, or knowledge acquired [1].

On the other hand, among the basic components of health services provided to the population should include [2]:

- monitoring services
- Diagnosis and treatment of diseases
- Medical education and prevention services
- Development of policies and plans, respectively, their adaptation to dynamic health challenges
- Connecting citizens to health systems for healthcare
- Analysis of effectiveness, accessibility, and quality of care provided
- Research aimed at finding innovative solutions to health problems

It can be noted that most of these components are based on information, without which health systems cannot be properly managed, becoming central tools, both in planning and administering health services, as well as in research or training [3].
