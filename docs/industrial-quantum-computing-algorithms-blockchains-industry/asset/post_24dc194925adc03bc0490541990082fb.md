## 3.3 Quantum machine learning

Machine learning has made a wide range of cutting-edge applications possible in social and scientific domains, especially in deep networks that use the propagation method. Speech recognition and handwriting recognition together with sophisticated applications in scientific research are widely used successful examples [9]. The theory and practice of machine learning training algorithms have advanced significantly, but they still need a lot of computing power. We must prepare for the possibility that development may slow down in the future as Moore’s law losing traction. New and intriguing opportunities are emerging with the impending release of quantum computing devices, which use the principles of quantum physics to circumvent the constraints of conventional computation. Research employing quantum computing systems is in its early stages regarding quantum machine learning (QML). Quantum applications include quantum measurement, adaptive quantum computing, and complex system modeling may benefit from traditional machine learning. Conventional machine learning processes can be accelerated by quantum algorithms. Learning difficulties may be solved using quantum data utilizing quantum computing devices [13]. QML blends machine learning methods with quantum algorithms to boost the effectiveness and speed of learning from data as represented in Figure 3.3.

### 3.3.1 Types of quantum machine learning

There are different types of QML.

**a) Quantum neural networks (QNNs):** In both science and industry, neural networks have achieved great success. With the latest developments in quantum technology, the goal is to build QNNs that can complete entire quantum learning tasks [13]. More sophisticated than standard artificial neural network (ANN), QNN combines ANNs with quantum computing [12]. The basic building blocks of quantum information, qubits and quantum logic gates, are used by these neural networks to operate. **Quantum phenomena like superposition and entanglement are used by QNNs, enabling qubits to exhibit correlation with one another and exist in several states simultaneously.** When compared to conventional neural networks, QNNs have faster and more efficient learning [9]. Large dataset management, function approximation, and computer gaming are applications of QNN. Among other things, QNN algorithms are utilized to simulate automated control systems, associative memory devices, and social networks. Especially noteworthy is the discovery of a QNN made of four-level qubits that commute inside each layer.

**c) Quantum support vector machines (QSVMs):** These are support vector machines (SVMs) that identify the best hyperplane for grouping data points into discrete categories using quantum techniques [9]. QSVMs enable quantum computers to solve complex problems than traditional computers by executing several operations concurrently using quantum speedup and quantum parallelism. The goal of QML is to use machine learning algorithms on quantum computers, leveraging concepts like entanglement and superposition to solve issues more quickly. SVM is a machine learning methodology that performs well in classification, even if the SVM kernel method may occasionally be sluggish and error. Using proper quantum feature mapping, this technique seeks to compare the speed and accuracy of QSVMs with those of classical SVM classification [14]. The following steps are commonly involved in the QSVM algorithm [9][15]:

**Step 1:** Using quantum feature maps, quantum data encoding converts classical data points into quantum states. Within a high-dimensional quantum space, the feature maps transform conventional data into quantum states.

**Step 2:** Quantum kernel calculation: To determine how similar two data points are to one another in the quantum feature space, a quantum kernel function is used in the analysis of quantum states of data points. In many cases, quantum circuits designed for quantum processes compute the quantum kernel function.

**Step 3:** Optimization and training: Using the quantum kernel matrix, a quadratic optimization issue is created, which is similar to how traditional SVMs operate. The hyperplane that splits differences in the feature space is found using quantum algorithms, or a hybrid of quantum and traditional techniques.

**Step 4:** Additional data points can be mapped into the feature space and positioned in accordance with the optimal hyperplane to help the QSVM simplify it has been trained.

**d) Quantum reinforcement learning (QRL):** These algorithms develop the best rules and behavior through reinforcement learning using quantum agents and quantum surroundings. Utilizing quantum resources and feedback, QRL blends quantum theory with reinforcement learning to improve information and incentives for quantum agents over traditional agents [16]. Although QRL has not yet been empirically tested it demonstrated improved performance in simulations. Quantum parallelism and the superposition of quantum states serve as the foundation for QRL. Through simulated trials, it seeks to expedite learning and accomplish a balanced approach between exploration and exploitation in reinforcement learning.

The aim of QRL is to create quantum principles that enhance decision-making in dynamic situations, which often include a trade-off between exploration and exploitation. Reinforcement learning focuses on an agent interacting with an environment to maximize the overall rewards over time [9]. This interaction is often shown as a Markov decision process, including a tuple $(S_t, A_t, P_t, R_t)$, where

- $S_t$ depicts the possible states of the environment,
- $A_t$ is the collection of actions available to the agent,
- $P_t$ is the transition function that represents the likelihood of moving from one state to another based on a certain activity, and
- $R_t$ is the reward function that gives instant feedback to the agent according to its behaviors.

e) Quantum strengthening, quantum algorithms are used in learning to improve the effectiveness and efficiency of learning and decision-making processes. To do this, states, actions, and policies must be represented by quantum states and operations, and the state-action space must be efficiently traversed by utilizing quantum parallelism and interference. The agent can explore several possibilities simultaneously representing the quantum state as a superposition of classical states.
