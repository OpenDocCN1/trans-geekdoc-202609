# Chapter 7 Industrial automation and quantum computing

#  7.5.7 Algorithmic development

We are still in the early stages of making custom quantum calculations for mechanization. To create these calculations for things like optimization, reenactment, and ML, we require a group effort from quantum physicists, computer researchers, and mechanical engineers. To turn conventional optimization issues into quantum calculations and test how well they work in real-world circumstances, we require a profound understanding of both quantum computing models and trade processes.

#  7.5.8 Interdisciplinary collaboration

To make quantum computing work for commercial automation, individuals from distinctive regions need to work together. Quantum physicists must work with computer researchers and mechanical engineers to bridge the hole between quantum hypotheses and gadget plan. This partnership is essential to identify and overcome specific challenges arising from the integration of quantum technologies into the existing automation systems.

#  7.5.9 Cost and accessibility

Quantum gear is costly to build and maintain. The high expense of developing and running quantum computers, along with the need for specialized infrastructure, limits their use to a small number of well-known universities and technological businesses. This circumstance raises concerns regarding quantum technologies' independence in the corporate sphere, since small businesses may encounter access barriers owing to budgetary restraints [8].

#  7.5.10 Quantum security concerns

Integrating quantum technologies into industrial systems raises additional security problems. While quantum computing has the potential to defeat present encryption systems, there is still time to develop quantum-resistant encryption techniques.
