The advantages of telemedicine are countless and are related to reducing costs or access to medical services, and monitoring and consulting patients can be performed regardless of the location of the doctor or patient.

Electronic health records are implemented at a very high rate in the USA, while the EU plans to increase the percentage of implementation. The biggest advantage of medical records is that medical staff and patients have at their disposal, at any time, the medical history in digital format, including the evolution over time of the health status or alerts on performing analyses or administering treatments. It is extremely important to establish the level of access to medical data and the security of the collected information or the possibility of modifying this data.

By analyzing data at the community level, patterns can be made on trends and evolution of certain pathologies, especially related to the possibility of emergence or management of pandemics. Thus, with the help of data, health monitoring authorities can prevent or limit the evolution of diseases with high impact on the population, through real-time alerts or information on the evolution of diseases. The best example of this is the COVID-19 pandemic, when health experts relied on data to track the evolution in real time and were able to limit the increase in the number of diseases in some cases.

Among the real-time prevention and alerting solutions are also portable medical devices, smartwatches, or bracelets that collect data and transmit to the cloud so can be analyzed by doctors, and measures can be taken if needed, including personalized treatment.

Risk analyses based on population-level data from specific demographic groups can be useful to determine whether medical services are available for certain types of conditions in the geographical areas analyzed. Thus, specialists in the field can take measures to adapt the offer of medical services according to the needs of a certain area, meeting the needs of the population. Setting levels, combined with prevention, can reduce hospital admissions and reduce expenses, with available resources being allocated to cases of greatest need.

Data collected on treatment regimes and disease progression following treatments can be used to improve outcomes by developing new therapies.

Today's medicine is inconceivable without the existence of medical imaging, which has evolved tremendously since the discovery of X-rays in 1895 by professor Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen, who are still widely used today. However, keeping radiographic films is a logistical challenge for any institution, besides large storage spaces, storage conditions are specific, given their sensitivity to environmental conditions, with an impact on the expenses incurred.

The transition to digitization in the medical imaging area has enormous advantages, not only in terms of cost reduction but also in terms of the possibility of accessing and interpreting stored data with the help of specialized platforms that use various digital algorithms to analyze thousands of images and provide results to radiologists.

Maintaining and developing health systems around the world is extremely expensive. From treatments and provision of medical services to the purchase of new-generation equipment or innovative medicines, the digitalization of the system helps to eliminate avoidable expenses that do not necessarily provide benefits to patients, eliminating fraud in the field.

Obviously, there are multiple challenges and obstacles, including in the area of big data, most of them are related to patient data security, regulations in the field being different from state to state, or the availability of cooperation between entities. Those who have invested enormously in collecting and analyzing data are not necessarily interested in sharing the conclusions obtained from the collected information.

There are also challenges related to the incompatibility of collected data, in the absence of regulated universal models of data collection, allowing the interconnection of data stored by different entities.

# Can quantum computers revolutionize health systems?

Even if the number of quantum computers is not extremely high now, they already manage to find their applications in many areas, including health systems. Thus, we can already see a number of important areas within the healthcare system, where quantum computers are beginning to act, including molecular simulation and drug design, personalized treatment optimization, algorithm optimization and diagnostic assistance, advanced simulations for medical research, health logistics optimization, quantum cloud in health, quantum encryption for health data security (Figure 6.3).

The major advantage of quantum computers at amazing speeds, compared with current systems, is to perform calculations which obtains results in an extremely short time and can be applied on large databases.

It is important to note that at the beginning of 2023, a huge step was taken regarding the use of quantum computers in health. By providing the first quantum computer to the private sector, IBM has delivered to the Cleveland Clinic a quantum computer that will be fully dedicated to medical discoveries.
