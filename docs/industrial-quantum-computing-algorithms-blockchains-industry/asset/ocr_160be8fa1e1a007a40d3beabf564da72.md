# 12.2 Related survey

Abdullah Baz et al. [1] recommended making Industry 5.0 more human-centric, secure, and sustainable. It addresses healthcare security risks, proposes a symrical solution roadmap, and introduces a validated security risk assessment framework, SRVFI5.0, offering a novel concept for impact analysis and enhanced security indexing.

Attaal et al. [2] proposed a fuzzy AHP-TOPSIS-integrated approach for assessing security risks, ranking healthcare web applications based on their security methods. The findings highlight the effectiveness of mitigating risks during the design phase, encouraging the establishment of security standards, and prioritizing security attributes for enhanced web application service.

Abirami Raja Santhi et al. [3] highlighted the current state of the fourth industrial revolution (Industry 4.0) and anticipated the need for a more human-centric and sustainable approach in the upcoming phase, tentatively termed "Industry 4.0.S." They emphasized the potential drawbacks of Industry 4.0, such as job loss and environmental impact, and stressed the importance of humans in digitalization. This phase aims for inclusivity and eco-friendliness, challenging the premature notion of Industry 5.0.

Amr Adel et al. [4] discussed defining Industry 5.0 from industrial and academic viewpoints, exploring applications to grasp its features. Enabling technologies are discussed, emphasizing the concept's aim to optimize human-machine efficiency. Challenges are presented for addressing Industry 5.0 issues, and the paper outlines directions for its effective implementation.

Alexeis Garcia-Perez et al. [5] explored the digital transformation of healthcare, emphasizing the importance of cybersecurity and resilience. Analysis of perspectives from UK healthcare executives indicates the significance of these constructs. The researchers prescribed a balanced approach involving cybersecurity knowledge, uncertainty management, and consideration for systemic interdependence. Poor performance in these aspects may lead to increased vulnerability and risks, highlighting the necessity for a comprehensive understanding of digital transformation's challenges and opportunities in the healthcare sector.
