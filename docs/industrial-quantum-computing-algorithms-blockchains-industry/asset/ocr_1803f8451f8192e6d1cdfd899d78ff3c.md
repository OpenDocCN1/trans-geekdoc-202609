Quantum computing promises a disruptive technological innovation in a range of industries, including encryption, drug discovery, optimization, artificial intelligence, and others. For instance, quantum computers may render well-known cryptographic software, necessitating modifications to programming code to bypass this. Quantum algorithms might revolutionize drug discovery by calculating molecular interactions with unmatched precision, opening the doors to producing new medicines and materials [9]. Besides being very good at solving optimization problems like route planning and resource allocation, which are widely used in logistics and finance, quantum computers can also factor numbers and simulate complex chemical processes through their ability to easily replicate quantum states.

Figure 13.2 explains the assembly of one qubit used in quantum computing. This is an explanation of its parts.

+   - Enclosure: A metal enclosure provides protection for the entire unit. The fragile components are shielded from outside disturbances by this cover.
- Standardized connection: Top assembly connector that is standardized. This might make it easier to transmit electricity to and from other sources or to communicate.
- Pump for refrigerant: The existence of a refrigerant pump suggests that the ideal temperature must be maintained. To minimize noise and anomalies, quantum systems often function at extremely low temperatures.
- Super vacuum that is always closed: The quantum bit, or qubit, is kept in a cavity within a super vacuum that is always closed. The impacts of external quantum noise are suppressed by its isolation.
- Q-storage cavity: Q-bits are in the assembly's central part. The essential data of quantum mechanics are called quantum bits.
- Electrical input and output connectors: You can see the electrical connections below. These allow the q-bit to receive input signals (like commands) and output signals (like measurement data).
