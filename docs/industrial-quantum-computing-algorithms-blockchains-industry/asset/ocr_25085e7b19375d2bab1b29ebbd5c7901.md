# 10.2.2 Automation and AI's impact on income inequality

The emergence and implementation of automation and AI have caused a great deal of concern about their consequences for income inequality. Automation is the process of employing machines or computers to accomplish tasks that were previously performed by humans, whereas AI is the development of computer systems capable of completing tasks that would ordinarily need human intelligence [22]. In the face of these quickly changing aspects of society, there is substantial disagreement about whether they will result in greater inequality or wage pressures [23]. According to one of thought, the rise of automation and AI would increase inequality by altering the labor market and leaving limited options for lower-skilled employees. This is because technologies lower the demand for physical labor while increasing the demand for highly qualified individuals [23]. Such an effect has already been observed in the case of industrial robots, which has contributed to job loss, wage reductions, and a widening of the wage disparity between low- and high-skilled workers [24]. It might also be argued that these new technologies will strengthen the wealthy' bargaining and market power, allowing them to reap the greatest profits while lesser-skilled laborers are increasingly sidelined. Another school of thought holds that the rise of automation and AI will not always result in levels of inequality. This is because, while technologies diminish low-skilled employment opportunities, they also increase economic activity by opening new markets and improving productivity [22]. Furthermore, it is feasible that automation would boost lower-skilled employees' access to capital and labor markets, as well as their ability to supply to wealthier owners [27].

In theory, higher rates of economic growth could contribute to more equity in society, especially if rewards are allocated more progressively [28]. Automation and AI have a significant impact on the global economy, rendering certain processes and jobs obsolete and limiting the pay of lower-income people [29]. Employees who are particularly susceptible to workplace challenges as racial and gender bias are at the highest risk of being replaced by robotic or AI alternatives [30].
