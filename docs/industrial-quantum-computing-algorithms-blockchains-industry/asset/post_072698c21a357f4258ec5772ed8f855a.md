## 11.7 Challenges in quantum machine learning

QML offers the potential to alter many industries, but before it becomes widely applied, there are nonetheless a variety of challenges to be solved.

### 11.7.1 Hardware restrictions

**Noisy qubits:** Quantum become faulty as they cease to be the original classical bits (0 or 1) and instead remain in a state of superposition. These errors can occur during the check process, potentially creating errors in the result and producing incorrect outcomes [4]. QML algorithms should be designed with error suppression as a major function and should include fault tolerance or an error correction approach to minimize the effect of these errors. Techniques as color codes and topological theorem yawning sign move in the direction of this area of study a dynamic look.

**Limited qubit stability:** Current quantum computers have a limited range of qubits (generally less than 100). This restricts the complexity of problems they can tackle. Scaling up qubits is a substantial engineering task, requiring advancements in qubit manipulation and error correction. Additionally, QML algorithms need to be designed with scalability in mind, specializing in efficient resource usage and breaking down large problems into smaller, conceivable subproblems.

### 11.7.2 Algorithm making

**The challenge of quantum advantage:** Although QML produces considerable improvement in usability in comparison to contemporary device mastery approaches, it has a key disadvantage. The matter of investigation concentrates upon problem fields where QML algorithms surpass traditional methods by virtue of speed or accuracy. It might involve some mechanisms that are explicitly based on quantum mechanics with singular optimization tasks. In addition, QML could uniquely work high-order-type problems, which are generally hard for conventional algorithms to solve.

**Quantum-optimal algorithm design:** An upgraded classical approach can help find algorithms that could be effective in the quantum domain. The traditional algorithm uses the basic operations of linear algebra; however, a mirror image is not foreseeable on quantum gates. Research must be done to build new QML, particularly including the use of superposition and entanglement of quantum mechanics, to sort of deal with issues nearby or beyond construction.
