# 9.2 Quantum machine learning (QML)

QML is an enhanced version of ML that acquires some properties of quantum computing. In recent years, researchers have explored how quantum technology can enhance ML algorithms. The detailed mechanism of QML is shown in Figure 9.1.

![](bbox=[0.176, 0.296, 0.905, 0.477])

**Figure 9.1:** Steps of quantum machine learning.

In the first step, users need to convert classical data into quantum data or a group of qubits. In order to make classical data fit into quantum computing, this process involves quantum feature mapping, which changes classical data into an infinite-dimensional space, i.e., Hilbert space. Choosing the right quantum gate and arranging them in order is very important for designing a quantum circuit that can process data and provide the desired output. A quantum circuit is useful for specific ML tasks such as clustering, regression, and classification. The complexity and structure of the circuit differs from problem to problem and depends on the desired output. To increase the efficiency of the model, quantum algorithms should be minimize cost functions, which can be calculated as the difference between the prediction by the model and the actual value. Further, quantum algorithms use different techniques, where changes can be made to the quantum circuit based on feedback provided by quantum machines. Once the quantum circuit has processed the input data, results can be retrieved through the measurements of qubits. The classical output is decoded and interpreted to provide insights or predictions concerning the original problem.
