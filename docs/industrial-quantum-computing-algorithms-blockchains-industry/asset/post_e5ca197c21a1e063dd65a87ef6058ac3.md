## 10.2 Literature review

### 10.2.1 Theoretical background

The rapid and widespread advancement of automation and AI is transforming business, industry, and service operations in unprecedented ways. A large portion of the discussion following the introduction of these innovative technologies has centered on their potential effects on jobs and the likelihood of people losing their jobs. Nevertheless, another effect that is not often talked about but is just as important as its influence on wealth inequality. This literature review will examine the shifts in inequality in developed and developing countries in recent decades, and how automation and AI have impacted and will continue to impact this trend.

Income inequality refers to the degree of economic disparity among individuals within a society, as determined by their personal income and wealth [14]. Economic inequality has been a consistent aspect of life in the majority of nations. Recently, this thus has again captured national interest. During extended economic stagnation and increasing profits, lower-income households have seen stagnant or decreasing salaries, while higher-income households have experienced a growth in their incomes [15]. This significant change in dynamics has led to a rise in income inequality, both in theory and in practice, by developed and developing countries [16]. From 1945 to 1975, the developed world saw major improvements in equality due to widespread economic growth and redistributive policies. This was partially a result of increased government regulation of labor markets, higher taxes on the wealth, and social welfare programs [17]. Nevertheless, starting from 1975, inequality started to increase consistently due to shifts in politics, salaries, and taxation, leading to a growing disparity in wealth between the affluent and the marginalized sectors of society [18]. Aside from the brief period after the 2008 Global Financial Crisis when in inequality slightly decreased due to government action, there has been a steady rise in inequality in the developing world recent decades [19]. Inequality in the developing world significantly increased in the late twentieth century following a period of economic stabilization, and higher real wages [20]. In many developing countries, the gap between rich and poor has been consistently growing over the past decades, with inequality levels typically higher than those seen in developing countries [20]. The gap has increased due to the extensive effects of globalization, leading to a significant difference in where economic activities are located and the opportunities for financial success [21].

### 10.2.2 Automation and AI's impact on income inequality

The emergence and implementation of automation and AI have caused a great deal of concern about their consequences for income inequality. Automation is the process of employing machines or computers to accomplish tasks that were previously performed by humans, whereas AI is the development of computer systems capable of completing tasks that would ordinarily need human intelligence [22]. In the face of these quickly changing aspects of society, there is substantial disagreement about whether they will result in greater inequality or wage pressures [23]. According to one of thought, the rise of automation and AI would increase inequality by altering the labor market and leaving limited options for lower-skilled employees. This is because technologies lower the demand for physical labor while increasing the demand for highly qualified individuals [23]. Such an effect has already been observed in the case of industrial robots, which has contributed to job loss, wage reductions, and a widening of the wage disparity between low- and high-skilled workers [24]. It might also be argued that these new technologies will strengthen the wealthy' bargaining and market power, allowing them to reap the greatest profits while lesser-skilled laborers are increasingly sidelined. Another school of thought holds that the rise of automation and AI will not always result in levels of inequality. This is because, while technologies diminish low-skilled employment opportunities, they also increase economic activity by opening new markets and improving productivity [22]. Furthermore, it is feasible that automation would boost lower-skilled employees' access to capital and labor markets, as well as their ability to supply to wealthier owners [27].

In theory, higher rates of economic growth could contribute to more equity in society, especially if rewards are allocated more progressively [28]. Automation and AI have a significant impact on the global economy, rendering certain processes and jobs obsolete and limiting the pay of lower-income people [29]. Employees who are particularly susceptible to workplace challenges as racial and gender bias are at the highest risk of being replaced by robotic or AI alternatives [30].
