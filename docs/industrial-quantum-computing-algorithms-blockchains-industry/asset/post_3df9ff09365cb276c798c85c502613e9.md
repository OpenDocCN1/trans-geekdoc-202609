### 9.3.3.3 Optimization

The main aim of optimization is to find the optimal parameters for a quantum circuit to minimize a cost function that represents the classification error. In general, descent or various classical optimization algorithms are used for optimization. The QSVM finds the ideal hyperplane that ideally separates the classes by minimizing this cost function. The cost function measures the difference between the predicted values by the QSVM and the actual output of the training data. To minimize the cost function and enhance the QSVM's classification performance, the optimization algorithm adjusts the parameters. Iterative updates of the quantum circuit's parameters are part of the training process. The predictions of the QSVM are computed in each iteration using the quantum kernel matrix $k$ and the current parameters.

### 9.3.3.4 Convergence

The training process repeats until the convergence criteria are met, which is usually determined by reaching an established threshold for the cost function or the parameters. The QSVM is ready for classification and prediction tasks once convergence is reached. The training complexity of the model is based on various aspects such as the size of the data, the design of the quantum circuit, and the algorithm used for optimization.

### 9.3.4 Testing and evaluation

After training the model, the performance of QSVM can be determined by testing the data. The efficiency of the model in the areas of classification and prediction can be evaluated by factors such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. The selected framework provides documentation and resources that cover all the information for implementing QSVMs.

## 9.5 Challenges in QML

With the combination of ML and quantum computing, this field is making progress that requires robust and scalable hardware. This progress is also facing some issues like size data, quality errors caused by de-coherence, scalability issues. Hence, it is very important to make powerful computers that can handle complex algorithms despite noisy environments [13]. Here, we have discussed some common issues that need attention:

- **i. Poor quality of data:** The quality of data plays a big role in the success rate of any ML model. Whatever you feed to your model during training has a great impact on the outcome. Data that has noise, is incomplete, and is not clean can result in inaccurate predictions. For this reason, preprocessing steps, i.e., handling missing values, cleaning of data, and feature selection, are essential to enhance data quality before model training.

- **Under-fitting of training data:** Under-fitting occurs when a model is too simple to capture the underlying patterns in the data. Strategies to address under-fitting include:
    - Extending training time, which allows the model to learn more complex relationships.
    - Increasing model complexity by using more sophisticated algorithms or adding additional features that can enhance performance.
    - Adjusting regularization parameters to prevent excessive simplification of the model.

- **Overfitting of training data:** Overfitting arises when a model learns noise or biases present in the training data, resulting in poor generalization to unseen data. Overfitting can be mitigated using the following approaches:
    - Applying regularization techniques like L1 and L2 to regularize or penalize overly complex models.
    - Employing cross-validation to evaluate model performance on different data subsets and detect overfitting.
    - Diversifying the training data to expose the model to a broader range of examples and reduce reliance on specific patterns.

**iv. Scalability and efficiency:** When working with complex datasets, selecting optimized parameters, and efficiency of the model is very important. Here we have some solutions to achieve scalability and accuracy:

    - Developing efficient data processing and storage mechanisms to manage large volumes of data effectively.
    - Optimizing model training algorithms to minimize computation time and resource utilization.
    - Leveraging parallelization and distributed computing frameworks to distribute computational tasks across multiple processors or machines, thereby accelerating training and inference.

**v. Algorithm development:** To develop new quantum algorithms, one requires a deep understanding of both quantum mechanics and computational theory. Researchers are making progress in this field by creating small prototypes. The primary aim of all this research is to make quantum models that are more accurate and fast.

**vi. Quantum communication:** Another big challenge is to design secure and reliable quantum communication channels. Entanglement and superposition are two main factors that have a major impact on quantum communication. These quantum communication channels can be affected by decoherence and loss over long distances. Therefore, to overcome this problem, enhanced quantum repeaters and satellite-based quantum communication are required to establish a global quantum network [13].
