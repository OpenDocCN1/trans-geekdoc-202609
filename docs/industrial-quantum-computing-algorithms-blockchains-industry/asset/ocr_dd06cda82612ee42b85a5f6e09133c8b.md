# 15.6.1 Quantum hardware in chemistry: Potential and recent advances

Quantum computing could transform drug discovery, chemical property prediction, and molecular system modeling in chemical research. This research examines quantum hardware for chemistry's future possibilities, challenges, and possible solutions. We begin with a comprehensive introduction of quantum hardware technologies, focusing on their use in chemical simulations and their revolutionary changes. We then explore the future of quantum hardware in chemistry and the trends that will shape it. Advances in material research and production should boost qubit coherence times and reduce noise. New materials and qubit designs may produce quantum hardware for simulations that is more stable and reliable. Error correction and fault-tolerant architectures must develop for quantum simulations to be more reliable quantum computation accuracy. We require error-correcting codes and qubits for chemical processes to improve quantum computation accuracy. More connection and qubit count in scalable quantum computers will enable simulation of more sophisticated and large-scale chemical systems. Distributed quantum computing architectures will become possible for collaborative chemical simulations as quantum networking and communication technologies progress.

Exploring techniques to connect quantum hardware with classical computing will enable hybrid quantum-classical algorithms for chemical simulations. Hybrid approaches combine the advantages of both paradigms for chemical process simulations with conventional computational capabilities for data processing and interpretation. Advances in quantum hardware will improve drug discovery, material design, and catalysis, spurring the creation of new treatments and materials features. To forecast material properties with remarkable precision, optimize chemical structures, and speed up drug development, quantum simulations are essential.
