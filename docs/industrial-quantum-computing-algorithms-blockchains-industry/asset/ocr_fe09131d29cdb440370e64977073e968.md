# 15.3.1 Quantum algorithms to revolutionize electrical structure computations

Electronic structure computations are needed to understand chemical reactions, materials, and molecules. Computer chemistry and materials science use them. Quantum algorithms for calculating electronic structures are described in this article, along with their benefits and revolutionary potential. Classical structure approaches have issues with standard basis sets not describing electronic correlations and the high processing cost of solving Schrödinger equation. We examine how quantum algorithms can uniquely simulate molecular and material structures on quantum computers. Quantum subspace estimation (QSE), quantum phase estimation (QPE), and variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) are methods for computing electronic structures [38]. These approaches efficiently and accurately determine molecular energies, electronic wavefunctions, and characteristics using quantum computers. Along with DFT and wavefunction-based electronic approaches, we consider quantum algorithms. This allows a hybrid technique that combines classical and quantum computing. Quantum algorithms for electronic structure calculations are also examined for their pros and cons. This includes developing efficient quantum algorithms for large molecular systems, cleaning up quantum hardware, and comparing quantum conclusions to experimental data.
