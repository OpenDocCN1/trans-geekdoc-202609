Modern health systems are increasingly dependent on technology and information systems that are used to increase the quality and effectiveness of care [6], with some authors believing adopting electronic health records can improve the healthcare system [7].

The resulting analyses are used to improve the quality of medical services offered, as well as to better manage the costs related to carrying out the activity.

# 6.3 Big data in health

Every second, health systems collect huge amounts of data, and the term “big data” in health is a very real one with multiple challenges regarding storage, analysis, interpretation, and use.

In the past, data of gigabytes or terabytes were considered high, today we can say that petabytes have become basic units of measurement for health data, registering in just a few years astronomical to the level of exabytes.

According to studies, in 2013, data totaling 153 exabytes were produced at the level of health systems (1 exabyte = 1 billion gigabytes), and in 2020, the estimated value increased exponentially by about 48%, to 2,314 exabytes [9].

The increase in global health systems is driven not only by new devices, which are able to store information electronically, but also by the increased number of details recorded. At this point, medical equipment can provide highly detailed information, which automatically translates into larger amounts of data. Also, at the patient level, the volume of stored data increases with each presentation to the doctor, hospitalization, or treatment performed with electronic medical records recording personal history.

Medical imaging produces the highest percentage of the volume of data generated by medical equipment, which can be explained by the increase in the use of MRI and CT machines that are becoming more efficient, providing extremely detailed images, with 3D and 4D images becoming benchmarks in the industry. With the increase in the number of digital scanners, hospitals store hundreds of millions of digital images, which are used for a short period of time to establish diagnosis and treatment, and then only kept on different storage media.
