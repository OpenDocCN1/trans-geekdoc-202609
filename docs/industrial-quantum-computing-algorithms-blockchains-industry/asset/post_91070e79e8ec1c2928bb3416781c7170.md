## 4.4 Crafting quantum algorithms tailored to healthcare applications

The rapid advancements in quantum computing may not have a positive impact on drug development for healthcare unless there are well-designed computer programs to support it [18–23]. Contemporary quantum programs often integrate traditional and quantum computing. Developing hybrid programs for healthcare is critically important because they can leverage powerful quantum hardware [24]. Even if they do not utilize all of its capabilities, they can enhance conventional computer programs. Hybrid and quantum-inspired approaches are important steps toward creating quantum programming. A quantum algorithm that classifies CT images quickly with quantum machine learning has been developed for COVID-19 patient data processing. In another case, a team created a software to manage healthcare chores with tight constraints, knowing the serious ramifications for patients. Healthcare programs are being developed in large-scale initiatives. The UK is heavily funding a quantum computer for medical research. Pharmaceutical businesses are working with quantum computing drug development organizations. Quantum computing for healthcare is being researched in several ways [25–31]. Some use quantum approaches to identify early illnesses, make three-dimensional medical imaging, or handle data. Even SARS-CoV-2 is detected using quantum technologies. Blood sugar measurement to smart e-healthcare security improvements are among the new concepts. This section discusses healthcare quantum computing. Quantum encryption, digital signatures, quantum walk-based cryptography, and blockchain hybrid systems are covered. It also emphasizes system performance assessment and quantum computing knowledge for healthcare practitioners. The paragraph stresses the need for healthcare security and quantum cryptography for secure communication. One data transfer system is quantum key distribution (QKD) [31].

This section discusses QKD and its importance in healthcare security. QKD uses intrusion detection to stop unwanted access. BB84, invented by Gilles Brassard, is a popular quantum computing approach. Shor and colleagues developed BB84's security by tying it to entanglement purification and quantum error correction. QKD may also be used for remote health monitoring, as Devi and Kalaivani [2] present an improved BB84 protocol to secure data transmission in wireless body sensor networks for patient supervision. Perpetual and colleagues [32] developed an enhanced QKD method to secure a variety of medical equipment [33–36]. This invention secures communication between authorized parties, protecting healthcare data. D-level quantum devices, which can convey data quicker, are being discussed for their potential in enhanced medical sensors. These devices are used in brain-machine interfaces to efficiently transport large amounts of neural data across hundreds of monitoring electrodes in the brain's layers. QKD protocol difficulties, including quantum computing defensive techniques, Authentication, interception, substitution, and other security risks must be addressed.
