# 6.6.4 Advanced simulations for medical research

In the research area, quantum computers have huge potential, from which researchers in the field of biology or medicine can benefit, whether they are applications dedicated to advanced simulation or for solving complex problems in medical research.

The volume of huge medical data existing now and its exponential growth makes processing with the help of classical technology almost impossible or inefficient, for amounts of data and time required for complex medical research.

Quantum computing can revolutionize the pharmaceutical industry in developing new medicines, from the research phase to preclinical and clinical studies, as well as support in managing supply chains.

Through quantum simulations and calculations, researchers can discover new chemical compounds that can be used in drug creation and possess the desired properties and efficacy.

Before moving on to clinical trials, quantum computers can simulate the efficacy and safety of new drugs using advanced algorithms designed for complex simulations that can predict how drugs will behave in biological systems.

The possibility of predicting drug side effects is essential in drug development, and by using quantum computers, these processes can be accelerated and simulated so as to limit the reactions of new drugs to biological systems, saving resources and time in the production of new drugs.

Clinical trials can be optimized by analyzing large volume of data collected from patients by correlating demographic or genetic factors, which can influence outcomes or establish dedicated treatment protocols.

Recent research in the field has focused on creating quantum algorithms that can help understand protein folding, an extremely important area in the development of new drugs and toward which much quantum research is heading.

Chemical reactions within cells or population dynamics can be explored through quantum simulations, helping to understand the molecular mechanisms involved in diseases and strategies needed for intervention.
