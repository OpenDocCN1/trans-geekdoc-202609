# 9.1.1 Quantum basics

Quantum computing is based on the principles of quantum mechanics to process data and perform calculations. While regular computations use bits as the basic unit of information (0 or 1), quantum computing employs qubits or qubits. In quantum mechanics, states like photon polarization, electron spin, or states of atoms can be seen as qubits, which are the foundation of quantum computing. These qubits represent and manipulate information in 0 and 1 states simultaneously, a phenomenon superposition. One significant difference between classical and quantum systems is how their parts combine. In classical systems, each part's state defines the system's state. However, in quantum systems, most states cannot be explained by just looking at the parts. These are called entangled states, where qubits are dependent on each other despite their distance. This result in better performance, where quantum computers can perform faster in situations like analyzing and running simulations.

Quantum measurement gives specific outcomes, even though there are many possible states. After measurement, the system ends up in one of these possible outcomes, chosen probabilistically, and changing the states, which means you cannot measure an unknown state without affecting it. Also, you cannot perfectly copy an unknown quantum state due to a principle called the no-cloning principle. The various elements of quantum computing are discussed in Table 9.1.

Table 9.1: Elements of quantum computing.

| Keyword | Description |
| --- | --- |
| State space | Includes all possible states of a physical system |
| Qubit | The basic unit of information in quantum computing; it is represented by complex vector spaces |
| Entanglement | Phenomenon where states of multiple qubits become interconnected, leading to entangled states |
| Quantum measurement | Provides discrete outcomes from a continuum of possible states, with outcomes determined probabilistically |
| No-cloning | Can't exactly copy an unknown quantum state |
| Superposition | Property of qubits allowing them to be in a mix of their basic states |
| Quantum algorithm design | Involves finding efficient sequences of operations to solve problems in quantum computing |
