# 10.4.2 Use of AI and automation in agriculture

Poverty, as mentioned before, comes in many forms. It appears as a deficiency in earnings, schooling, and sometimes a shortage of social support, along with concerns about access to food (by DAIA Medium 2020). This mainly impacts rural regions, where most of the impoverished population resides. The World Bank states that in locations where poverty is widespread, agriculture serves as the main source of income. AI applications, per Strusani and Houbongnon (2019), have the ability to tackle various challenges faced by individuals in the lowest income bracket, specifically the bottom 40%. Although low-income individuals are unable to purchase AI-enabled technology, there is a suggestion that they may still benefit from AI through utilizing service solutions on their smartphones. The machine learning system used in BRICS countries agriculture sector is an illustration of practicality. This app was used for farmers to detect leaf damage in photos captured by farmers and shared with authorities to monitor the presence of a troublesome pest impacting earning farmers. Another aspect of AI is the ability to link data from mobile devices with a farmer's financial status, level of education, and state of health. This allows apps to offer microloans, personalized education, healthcare assessments, and mediation. Using speech recognition and speech-to-text features to help the most marginalized groups overcome literacy challenges, especially when using text-based apps, is a crucial way of AI technology. In many rural areas, AI image recognition can be used to assess farmer microinsurance claims from cities. These AI tools used by farmers enhance efficiency, resulting in higher profits. Mhlanga [57] states that AI and robotics are increasingly important in combating global hunger.

Companies like Google and Stanford University's sustainability and AI lab are making progress. These organizations are creating AI programs for use in agriculture to improve farming by ai detection of diseases, forecasting crop yields, and identifying areas at risk of shortages. A demonstration of AI is in the FarmView application created by researchers at Carnegie Mellon University to help farmers increase production using the same amount of crops [57]. This effort is being made to quickly address the important issue of population growth. It is expected that the global population will reach approximately 9.8 billion by 2050, leading to a rise in food scarcity, highlighting the significance of this issue. Nevertheless, the presence of AI technology will help enhance agricultural methods, including the use of sensing and robotics for crop management. Farmers find it challenging to examine and oversee each crop. The availability of AI has enabled the use of machine learning to enhance disease and pest control efficiency. Utilizing thousands of categorized cassava photos, a machine learning model developed and transformed into an application for farmers to detect and manage crop diseases by using images for analysis [76]. Therefore, AI is assisting African agriculture in achieving greater sustainability, ultimately aiding in feeding the population
