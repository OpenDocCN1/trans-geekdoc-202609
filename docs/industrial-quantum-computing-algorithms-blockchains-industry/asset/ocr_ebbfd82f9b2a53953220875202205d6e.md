# 3.1.2 Superposition and entanglement

Superposition is the capacity of a quantum system to exist in many states concurrently; in other words, a system can exist in "here" and "there," or "up" and "down" at the same time [1]. A quantum mechanical notion known as superposition allows a qubit simultaneously to be represented in the states "0" and "1". The n qubits are essentially in a superposition state with 2n states [17].

Entanglement, on the other hand, describes a strong connection between two particles. The linked qubit state will instantly reflect the transition in the first qubit state. This contributes to quantum computers operating at higher speeds [1].

# 3.1.3 Qubit technology

The methodical study of algorithmic methods and techniques that define and reform information is referred to as computing in science. Utilizing the foundations of quantum physics, quantum computing addresses difficult computational issues. By using the storage and transport capabilities of small items, such as ions, electrons, or photons, it offers novel computing possibilities. Qubits, or quantum bits, are used in quantum computing and are similar to bits, or 0 and 1, in the way many concurrent processes do times quicker than a regular computer in applications, such as quantum simulation and prime factorization.
