# 11.6 Applications of quantum machine learning

The higher the volume of the data we generate daily, the more it belongs to unlabeled data. These include analog and digital artifacts such as images, videos, financial documents, social media comments, medical scans, and sensor readings, to mention a few. Data labeling is a time-consuming task that involves attaching relevant tags to data with the help of experts who are aware of the topic. This is the reason unsupervised ML is also critical. The aim is to operate with unlabeled data to build models and algorithms capable of distinguishing information and patterns. Scientists manually analyze the data in order to identify certain features related to place and time, which may potentially be hidden in datasets due to unsupervised approaches. The primary task would be design approaches that would portray unlabeled information in such a way that would illuminate different aspects. It is the ability to extract various patterns from unsupervised algorithms that makes these techniques advantageous. These methods could be used alongside more traditional classifiers and regressors in an integrated manner to achieve better overall performance, thus leading to a significant reduction in the need for labeled data.

Generative models are widely used in unsupervised learning, as they have the ability to “hear” what the data is saying [4]. These models may be able to estimate the probability of distinctive variables (marginal distributions) and represent them, as well as how they affect each other (conditional distributions), and of course, analyze all the variables in general (joint probability distribution).
