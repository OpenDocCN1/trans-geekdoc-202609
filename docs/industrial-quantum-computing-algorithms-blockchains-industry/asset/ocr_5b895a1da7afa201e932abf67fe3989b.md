Thus, the risk of unauthorized access to medical data is significantly reduced, and its security is greatly improved.

Quantum computers can solve the problem of data transmission by distributing a quantum key distribution (QKD) between communicating parties using the quantum properties of light particles, which makes any attempt to intercept transmitted data according to the quantum principle of disruptive measurement.

Through this procedure, medical data is encrypted using quantum keys, and data confidentiality is ensured.

The solution of quantum encryption is becoming increasingly important with the advancement of quantum encryption algorithms that pose a threat to classical encryption systems.

Although many steps have been taken in terms of data transmission through quantum encryption, in 2004, the first bank transfer was made, in 2008 the world's first computer network was secured by QKD technology, and in 2016 the first secure video transmission was carried out at 7,500 km. The technology is in its infancy and there are still many problems to be solved.

# 6.6.8 Quantum computers and artificial intelligence

Some authors believe collaboration between AI and quantum computers would not generate positive results [19], given that AI is in its infancy and the technology is not necessarily ready for it.

It is obvious that research in the field is evolving much faster than we imagine and at some point, AI will be able to take advantage of the fantastic computing power of quantum computers.

In the medical field, AI has been able to demonstrate its capabilities, especially in the pharmaceutical field, where a team from the University of Toronto and AI Ins silico Medicine took 21 days to create 30,000 models for certain molecules. The researchers synthesized six molecules and tested them in the laboratory, with the entire process taking only 46 days, which is extremely little [20].

In terms of AI, the processes of training machine learning models could be accelerated by the extremely high processing speed of quantum computers, calculations being performed much faster than classical computers.

The possibility of parallel processing of possible solutions through simultaneous simulations could be useful in the processes of optimizing learning algorithms, and the most efficient configurations of AI models can be quickly identified.

Through their fantastic data processing speed, quantum computers can analyze extremely large volume of data needed to train AI algorithms.
