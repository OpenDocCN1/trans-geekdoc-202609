In quantum mechanics, each physical system can be in a superposition of physical states represented by mutually orthogonal basis of the state vector in complex Hilbert space. The entire state space is $2^N$-dimensional, where N is the number of particles of the object under investigation. The unit vectors in Hilbert spaces are denoted by 'ket' and are represented by |ψ⟩ [13, 14]

Quantum mechanics is a theoretical paradigm that is considered one of the two theoretical corners of modern physics [15]. Quantum mechanics deals with the descriptions of the nature and behavior of matter and energy at an atomic and subatomic scale [16]. It provides a mathematical description of much of the dual particle- and wave-like behavior as well as the interactions of matter and energy [17]. The entities, i.e., atoms, molecules behave according to the quantum formalism that is significantly different from classical physics. Quantum mechanics provides an alternative to Statistical and Classical mechanics in situations where the molecular scale of matter and energy is the most relevant scales. [11]

Within the context of the healthcare industry, this chapter considers both the benefits and the constraints that are brought about by the use of quantum computing technology.

## 14.2 Applications of quantum computing in healthcare

Quantum computers are faster at data analysis than current models. Hence, the timely identification of population-level solutions reduce the overall need for hospital-based care during the pandemic. For different healthcare supplies coordination such as the transfer of plasma from recovered COVID-19 patients to comprehensive and well-funded diagnostic tests, some equilibrium conditions and control strategies are developed using data science and agent-based testing [18].

Moreover, a large-scale, multifunctional, interconnected quantum computer can be used to understand convalescent plasma molecular composition and find effective treatments. Specifically, quantum technology can quickly optimize the composition in a real-time feedback loop and generate convalescent blood in desired pharmacological limits, including safety properties. [19]

Regarding detection of new infectious diseases, it empowers to identify and in real time assess epidemiologically linked contacts of positive cases (i.e. the identification and tracking of people who have been exposed to individuals testing for a communicable disease) which is an essential strategy for infectious disease control [16]. For instance, an accurate and continuous local assessment and response with strategically scheduled tightening of movement controls in hospitals and other traffic, proximal, and well-mixed spaces. Quantum computing can also significantly enhance care planning and medical services coordination. For example, it enables developing an agent-based network model to share clinical resources in general hospitals and community healthcare centres to help alleviate the emergency room strain. [20]

Quantum computers can break cryptographic codes that traditional computers cannot [21]. However, it can revolutionize health care too. It can generate new simulated materials in the form of drugs, vaccines, molecules for genetics, brain-machine interfaces, and many others could potentially reduce drug discovery costs and development timelines [22]. Seeing quantum computing is powerful processing platform holds potential in myriad of fields like disease control, detection of new infectious diseases, healthcare supplies coordination.

### 14.2.1 Application of quantum computing in healthcare

When it comes to revolutionizing the field of healthcare, tremendous potential has been shown. By utilizing the unique properties of quantum mechanics, scientists and researchers have discovered various applications that can significantly enhance diagnostics, treatment, and overall patient care. Quantum computing has advanced drug development. Quantum computers can efficiently examine large datasets and find potential medicine candidates performing complex calculations and simulations. This could speed up medication development and improve disease treatment. Quantum computing can improve personalized treatment. According to [23], Figure 14.3 shows quantum computing in healthcare.

### 14.2.2 Drug discovery DCQC

A third type of DCQC is based on Quantum Machine Learning [QML] methodologies. Quantum computers can intrinsically model data by implementing a many-body state according to the information of the input space. Then, they can be used to determine state classification boundaries by imposing some desirable quantum operations. The only task remaining, at the moment, is to realize quantum neural networks deeply enough to effectively encode the information and later reproduce the task. [26, 27]

A second type of DCQC is based on the quantum variational eigensolver, where the molecular state energy is computed by a hybrid quantum-classical algorithm [28]. A molecular Hamiltonian is implemented by the quantum computer, and classical algorithms optimize the molecular parameters to minimize the energy.

A specific application that is getting traction nowadays is drug discovery using Quantum Computing [DCQC]. One type of such algorithm is based on the quantum Fourier Transform, where the molecular structure is both chemically and geographically mapped onto a molecule tensor, and the Fourier Transform is applied. A better performance of the quantum approach, in terms of computational effort, is expected than traditional algorithms since the quantum properties are mapped directly from the quantum. [29]

Applying Quantum Computing to the healthcare setting is one of the best applications of quantum technologies under current development, enabling new possibilities both in the core healthcare activities, like drug discovery and development, and in providing care [17]. ## 14.3 Challenges and limitations

Quantum computing can be a potential remedy in Health Industry problems involving Big Data, Patient Care, and Drug Discovery and re-purposing. But there are also various barriers and challenges for its successful implementation [17].

- **Security Risk:** Due to the power of QC, the biggest urgent requirements are for Quantum-Resistant Post quantum Cryptography techniques which can still protect sensitive data even when a fully deployed Universal Quantum Computer [UQC] becomes available.
- **Not Sufficient Human Capital:** The human resources for another quantum computing are very few who know how to develop quantum algorithms, quantum gates etc. To overcome this, there should be more funding for implementing online courses and offline courses so that more researchers and professionals develop in this field. [30, 31]
