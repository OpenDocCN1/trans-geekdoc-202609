AI systems make independent crop management conceivable. By examining soil moisture content and climate data, algorithms can determine the ideal crop water requirements in real time when used along with Internet of Things (IoT) sensors. The goal of an autonomous crop irrigation system is to support sustainable farming methods while preserving water. Artificial intelligence plays a crucial role in identifying leaks in irrigation systems by leveraging algorithms that can discern patterns and anomalies in data indicative of potential leaks. Machine learning (ML) models can be trained to recognize specific signatures associated with leaks, such as deviations in water pressure or flow, enhancing the efficacy and accuracy of leak detection in irrigation systems. Initial identification is made conceivable by real-time monitoring and study, which helps to avoid both possible harm to crops and water loss.

For detecting areas with elevated water consumption, artificial intelligence (AI) integrates climate data with crop water requirements, enhancing water efficiency by automating leak detection and issuing timely alerts, thereby assisting farmers in conserving resources. The health and growth of crops can be significantly impacted by the incorrect combination of nutrients in the soil. With AI, farmers can quickly recognize these nutrients and determine how they affect crop yield allowing for suitable modifications to be made. While the accuracy of human assessment is limited, computer vision models can collect precise data by monitoring soil conditions. Then, using these data from plant science, yield predictions and crop health are made, along with any specific problems being highlighted. In actuality, AI has demonstrated a level of speed and accuracy unmatched by humans in tracking the stages of wheat growth and tomato ripeness.

In addition to measuring yield of crops and soil condition, computer vision could classify diseases and pests. This operates by perusing photos meant for insects, rot, mold, and other hazards to crop health utilizing artificial intelligence. When used in connection with alert mechanisms, this enables farmers to take prompt action to eradicate pests or isolate crops to stop the spread of illness. Apple black rot has been identified by AI with a more than 90% accuracy rate. It is equally accurate at looking at other kinds of insects such as flies, bees, moths, etc. Researchers first gathered pictures of the pests to generate the training dataset that was required for the algorithm to be trained effectively.
