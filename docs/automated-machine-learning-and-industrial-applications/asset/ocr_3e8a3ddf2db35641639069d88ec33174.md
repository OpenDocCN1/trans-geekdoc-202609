# 10.1.1 Obstacles in the Agricultural Sector

# 10.1.1.1 Soil Erosion

The natural method of soil erosion occurs when water or wind carry soil particles from one location to another. Farmers face severe issues when this occurs on a large scale. Many factors contribute to soil erosion, such as excessive soil tillage that degrades the soil's condition and reduces water retention. The top soil layers are swiftly removed during flooding. To use less water in irrigation, protecting soil from wind and water erosion with mulch and crop covers, and limiting livestock grazing excessively, are all ways to avert soil erosion.

# 10.1.1.2 Absence of High-Quality Seeds

To increase the production of crops, farmers must use high-quality seeds. Achieving steady growth in agricultural output is also dependent on it. The distribution of superior seeds holds equal significance to that of seed production. Most farmers, especially marginal and small farmers, usually cannot afford seeds of good quality because of their prohibitively expensive prices.

# 10.1.1.3 Lack of Contemporary Farming Machinery

The absence of proper farm equipment is one of the biggest issues farmers face, and it can make it difficult for them to adjust to the demands of contemporary farming methods. Farmers can have major improvements in their lives when they are trained to use the equipment. It is crucial to use the aforementioned equipment.

# 10.1.2 Challenges of AI in Agriculture

Many believe that AI is unrelated for farming tasks and only applies to the digital world. In general, this assumption stems to an insufficient amount of knowledge about AI tools. The general public, particularly those in non-tech fields, is not well informed about artificial intelligence (AI), which is causing the agricultural industry to adopt AI more slowly. Despite numerous progressions in agriculture during its long history, various farmers are still more familiar with using conventional systems.
