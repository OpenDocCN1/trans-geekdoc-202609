## Democratizing Machine Learning: The Rise of Automated Machine Learning (AutoML)

**Debarati Dutta and Priya G.***

### Abstract

The current paradigm of artificial intelligence (AI) is centered on developing algorithms that acquire extraordinary outcomes by learning directly from data, particularly in supervised deep learning. This has streamlined the machine learning systems by systematizing much of the learning process. In many different areas of our everyday life, machine learning (ML) is essential. However, developing effective ML applications typically requires skilled data scientists and subject matter specialists. Using automated machine learning (AutoML), domain experts may develop machine learning features without requiring extensive statistics or machine learning training. The area of AutoML is expanding quickly, with the goal of automating machine learning model development. By reducing tedious steps in the machine learning process—from selecting and fine-tuning algorithms to preparing data—it seeks to increase efficiency and productivity. This article outlines a review of AutoML methods and provides detailed explanations. It provides an indication of AutoML’s history and current state of this revolutionary technology. It starts by outlining the notion of AutoML, outlining the fundamental stages of every machine learning method, specifying the issues it aims to solve, and outlining the three main mechanisms of AutoML techniques. AutoML’s versatility and ongoing development applications and future scopes are also highlighted here. Overall, this comprehensive overview serves as an invaluable tool for machine learning scholars and practitioners, laying the groundwork for future advancements in AutoML.

### 14.1 Introduction

Machine learning (ML) has revolutionized various fields such as image classification, disease diagnosis, language translation, weather forecasting, and many more. The breakthrough act of AlexNet [1] in the 2012 ImageNet challenge led to the development of deeper networks like ResNet-50 [2], which has 50 layers and 25.6 million parameters. However, its success deeply depends on specialists who plan complex machine learning pipelines, including data preprocessors and training methods. Choosing the right mechanisms and optimizing their hyperparameters is an inspiring task that requires experience, experimentation, and resources. This challenge has driven the rise of Automated Machine Learning (AutoML), which intends to streamline and automate the creation of high-performance pipelines [3]. Current AutoML systems mainly target automated model building, which involves selecting the pipeline structure, its stages, and its hyperparameters.

When automating data science, four essential procedures are often taken into consideration as follows: 1. Collection and exploration of data. 2. Data engineering to clean and eliminate outliers from a dataset, data augmentation, and feature engineering to make it for feeding into a machine learning pipeline. 3. Choosing a model-building algorithm. 4. Hyperparameter optimization. 5. Training the model. 6. Data exploitation for decision-making. Among them, the main goal of modern AutoML systems is automatic model creation for an exact dataset.

John Rice proposed the algorithm selection problem in 1976 placing the groundwork for present AutoML systems [4]. In the 1990s, commercial keys started offering automatic hyperparameter optimization (HPO) via grid search. By 1995, versions of grid search using a greedy best-first approach occurred. The true potential of AutoML was understood with the introduction of the algorithm selection and optimization (CASH) problem. The formation of Auto-WEKA, by Thornton et al., in 2013 [5], efficiently illustrated this by using this idea to select models from a search space of conventional ML methods and related hyperparameters. Neural architecture search (NAS), a field whose emphasis on autonomously creating a particular type of machine learning model—neural networks—has also received a lot of attention in recent AutoML research [6].

AutoML research has recently made important strides in both academics and industry leading to a flow in research actions. Academic advancements include systems like Auto-sklearn [7], AutoKeras [8], and TPOT [9]. Inspired by these, industrial platforms, such as Google Cloud AutoML [10], Oracle AutoML [11], and Amazon SageMaker Autopilot [12], have been developed for large-scale use. These systems enable the creation of high-performance machine learning models and pipelines with minimal user effort.

This study offers a thorough indication of the methodologies employed in AutoML research to assist novice researchers and practitioners. It covers state-of-the-art AutoML schemes for both ML and deep neural networks and future research directions. This study is divided into three primary subtasks by the survey: feature engineering, data augmentation, and pre-processing. Our review covers all areas of AutoML methods beyond only NAS techniques, whereas others focus on the comparison of AutoML with other methods. This chapter gives a brief introduction to AutoML techniques, based on the State-of-the-Art (SoTA) pipeline. It also categorized the AutoML approaches into three components: (i) search space, (ii) search approach, and (iii) performance assessment. The primary goal is to provide beginners with a comprehensive introduction to the arena of automatic machine learning (AutoML). The remaining segments are organized in the following ways: Section 14.2 outlines the fundamental steps of AutoML system and pipeline structures. In Section 14.3, we describe the AutoML technology and framework in another component. Then, Section 14.4 gives some idea about the AutoML applications. Section 14.5 delves into potential future trajectories, while Section 14.6 brings the entire paper to a close.
