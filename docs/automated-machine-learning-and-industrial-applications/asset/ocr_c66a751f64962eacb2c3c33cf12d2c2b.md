Farmers are well aware by now that there is opportunity to optimize the use of pesticides. Unfortunately, there are significant limits to both programmed and manual application procedures. While manually applying chemical fertilizers can be labor intensive and time consuming, it allows for greater precision when focusing on particular areas. Although automated pesticide spraying is faster and requires less labor, it frequently lacks accuracy, which can contaminate the environment. Drones driven by AI combine the best features of each strategy without any of its disadvantages. Drones that utilize computer vision can calculate how much pesticide should be sprayed on each area. Even though this technology is still in its infancy, it is advancing quickly in precision. Yield mapping analyzes big datasets instantly using machine learning algorithms. Better planning is made possible for farmers as a result of their increased understanding of the patterns and traits of their crops. Farmers can forecast soil harvests for particular crops by combining approaches like 3D mapping with information from sensors and drones. Multiple drone flights provide data collection for increasingly accurate analysis through the application of algorithms.

With the aid of these methods, farmers are able to accurately forecast upcoming yields for particular crops assisting them in seeding locations and timing as well as resource allocation for optimal return on investment. Just as computer vision can classify diseases and pests, it can also be employed to recognize weeds and invasive plant species in agricultural settings. Computer vision examines the shape, color, and size of leaves to identify weeds from crops when paired with machine learning. These kinds of remedies can be employed to program machines for projects like automatic weeding that are part of robotic process automation (RPA). Indeed, a robot just like this has been put to good use already. As these innovations become more widely available, smart bots may eventually perform both cultivation and weeding tasks completely.

Apple black rot has been identified by AI with a 90% precision rate. It is equally accurate at identifying other insects, like flies, bees, moths, etc. It was up to the researchers to gather pictures of the pests to compile the training dataset that the algorithm needed. This chapter’s main focus is on the important artificial intelligence (AI) strategies applied to agricultural problems. This study explores the application of artificial intelligence techniques across various aspects of agriculture to monitor and assess the progress of agro-intelligent systems.
