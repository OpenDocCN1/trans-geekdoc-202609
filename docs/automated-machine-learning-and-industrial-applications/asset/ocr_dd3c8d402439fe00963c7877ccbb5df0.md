**Challenges and Future Directions**: Despite the advancements, the literature also acknowledges the challenges faced in implementing predictive maintenance solutions. These include the need for extensive and high-quality data [10, 11], the complexity of model development and deployment [12, 13], and the integration of these systems into existing industrial infrastructures [14, 15].

**Innovative Approaches and Emerging Technologies**: Innovative approaches, like the use of Gaussian processes [15], quantile regression and kernel density estimation [17], and clustering for machine status monitoring [18], highlight the ongoing evolution of predictive maintenance strategies. The emergence of technologies, such as transfer learning [16, 20] and mixed-instance learning [22], further underscores the dynamic nature of this field.

**Expanding Into New Domains and Utilities**: The study by Lokman et al. [21] explores predictive maintenance in the context of smart toilets demonstrating the versatility of predictive maintenance applications beyond traditional industrial settings. Similarly, Rahman et al. [19] focus on medical device failure predictions highlighting the critical role of predictive maintenance in healthcare.

**Machine Learning and AI enhancements**: The works of Bakdi et al. [22], Pezze et al. [23], and Kung and Liao [24] showcase the incorporation of advanced machine learning techniques such as multiple instance learning, deep learning, and optimization methods. These studies emphasize the continual evolution of machine learning algorithms in improving predictive maintenance strategies.

**Robustness and Noise Management**: Suawa et al. [25] address the challenge of noise in machine learning models, a significant factor in industrial environments. Their focus on noise-robust models underlines the importance of accuracy and reliability in predictive maintenance algorithms.

**Specialized Industry Applications**: Abdelli et al. [26] present a machine learning-based framework for the semiconductor industry demonstrating the specificity required in predictive maintenance applications for different industry sectors. This trend is also visible in Thurlbeck and Cao's work [27], which deals with power electronic converter lifetimes in turbine array generation systems.
