[4] Chatbots try to give accurate responses to user queries instead of presenting the user with a list of relevant documents, as in the case of search engines. To offer tailored responses to every user, thereby improving chatbot performance, personalization approaches are incorporated into chatbot design. The authors have proposed a personalized chatbot using sentiment analysis techniques to provide customized responses to each user. The proposed methodology involves five primary components: question processing, personalization, dialogue management, information extraction, and answer generation. Each component of the proposed methodology is associated with a set of well-defined tasks forming a robust framework that can offer relevant and customized responses to user inquiries. Whereas traditional search engines typically only return a list of linked documents, chatbots and question-answering systems seek to deliver exact replies to user requests. Personalization approaches are utilized to enhance the chatbot’s functionality by providing tailored information to individual users according to their preferences. Furthermore, sentiment analysis methods are applied to gain a deeper comprehension of the user’s questions and emotional condition. To provide the necessary information, it is intended to advance a deeper understanding of the user and his needs. In this study, we present a sentiment analysis-enhanced personalized chatbot that offers each user customized and relevant information. The primary elements of the system and the methods used to handle user inquiries are described in depth in the paper. We have deployed a proof-of-concept version of our technology as a customer support chatbot in e-commerce applications to assess its efficacy. Based on user feedback, the trial results show a noteworthy development in user satisfaction with both the overall experience and the customized replies. Overall, this system shows how sentiment analysis and personalization strategies can be used to improve the functionality and efficiency of chatbots and question-answering systems.

To determine whether users were satisfied with a chatbot’s performance, a controlled experiment was conducted. After choosing a representative sample of 60 individuals, an A/B test series was conducted. During the experiments, participants were instructed to engage with two versions of the chatbot system (Version A: via Sentiment Analysis Customization, and Version B: lacking Sentiment Analysis Customization). Following every session, participants were asked to deliver feedback on various parts of the system and score their degree of satisfaction on a 0–10 scale. [5] Depression is a predominant and debilitating mental health condition that moves millions of entities globally. Standard therapy techniques, including cognitive–behavioral therapy (CBT) and medication, are helpful for many people, but not everyone can access or afford them. Chatbots, conversational machines designed to replace human discourse, are a potential replacement for depression therapy. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of a chatbot-based depression therapy in a randomized controlled experiment. The participants were arbitrarily assigned to either the chatbot therapy group or the wait-list control group. While the chatbot therapy group had 8 weeks of therapy via a chatbot, the control group did not get any treatment during this time. The chatbot therapy group showed considerably fewer depressed indications than the control group (BDI-II) according to data from the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) and the Beck Depression Inventory-II. Participants in the chatbot therapy group also showed high levels of satisfaction with the chatbot interface and therapy. These findings suggest that chatbot-based therapy could be a viable alternative to traditional depression therapy, particularly for those who might not have access to it. We predict that the quality of life and anxiety levels of the chatbot therapy group will be much higher than those of the control group. The model’s accuracy was 75% when it was returned. All of these results suggest that chatbots could be a helpful and practical tool for providing depression support. Gap: Accuracy level was limited to 75% only.

[6] The authors have experimented with the variations in customer sentiments in the domain of retail services. They have proposed a hybrid sentiment analysis approach that is automated. It has been revealed that the overall sentiment toward chatbots is comparatively less negative than that associated with human agents. Moreover, they have found that these sentiments vary according to the sectors (fashion and telecommunications).

Finally, it was found that sentiments toward human agents in these sectors became very negative after the deployment of chatbots. Conversational AI, or chatbots, is becoming more and more popular among retailers for customer support because of its alleged advantages and lower operating costs. However, little is known about how users of chatbots interpret these encounters and how they might affect other customer support initiatives. This study looks into how customers feel about chatbots differently in different retail sectors and how they affect their expectations and feelings about other service contacts with online human agents. By employing a hybrid automated sentiment analysis methodology, they ascertain that:
- the general perception of bots is less negative than that of online human agents.
- These attitudes vary between the fashion and telecommunication industries.
- After a retailer deploys a chatbot, opinions about online human agents in both industries get worse.

Using a hybrid model that included a classical machine learning model called Naïve Bayes with a VADER lexicon-based classifier, they examined how consumers felt about chatbots and online human agents on Twitter. Researchers concentrated on the chatbots employed by three businesses in the online fashion and telecommunication industries, which are two distinct retail and consumer service sectors. These sectors rank among the top six most popular subjects on social media in terms of posts and shares. Furthermore, because of the variations in cost and level of service, they differ in terms of the kind of services offered and customer interaction. As a result, they anticipated that customer responses to chatbots will also vary (H2). Gap: They have dealt only with the general perception of bots in comparison with human agents. Their approach lacks fine-grained sentiment analysis of chatbot-consumer interaction.

Finding a breakdown in a discussion is crucial to raising user satisfaction and enhancing the chatbot’s functionality. Recent methods, both supervised and unsupervised, have concentrated on simulating conversation breakdown utilizing several methods. Applying unsupervised strategy to the breakdown job is difficult due to its huge datasets. Bias in the dataset’s human annotation and the breakdown’s management procedure present another difficulty for conversation breakdown prediction. To address this matter, we have created a supervised ensemble automated method that uses dialogue breakdown to gauge the Chatbot Quality of Service (CQoS).
