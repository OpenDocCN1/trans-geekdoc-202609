The primary area of image segmentation is to distinguish and separate symptoms from the surrounding environment. Numerous researchers are delving deeply into the matter. The Delta E color difference algorithm was applied by Ali et al. in 2017 [35] to identify the disease-infected area. Generally speaking, there are four main approaches used to segment images; these are covered in more detail in the paragraph that follows [36]. In image segmentation, some researchers integrate additional techniques with the region of interest (ROI). For instance, in their study, Kao et al. used a convolutional autoencoder as a background filter for the ROI [37].

The next approach is limited to segmenting regions. According to Pujari et al. (2013), features of images were extracted, and they were claimed to be divided into multiple regions, each with a unique meaning [38]. A real-time synchronous image-processing model was presented by Akram and associates. It can perform feature vector, contrast stretching, and salient region recognition by partitioning the image into three color spaces [39]. Additionally, other researchers have employed DL methods for image segmentation and detection. To maximize the recognition of plant diseases in photos, Marko et al. suggested a depth-based recognition algorithm and employed a two-stage algorithm [32]. Thresholding is a frequently used segmentation technique. Li et al. (2018) used multilevel thresholding methods built on gray histograms for image segmentation [40]. Using multilevel thresholding, Mohamed and Diego presented a novel multiobjective meta-heuristic for grayscale image segmentation based on a multiverse optimization algorithm [41]. But one thing is certain—it cannot be disregarded. In practical applications, color based machine vision algorithms, region of interest, and thresholds often perform inadequately due to the intricate nature of color information in complex environments.

There are numerous challenges in identifying plant diseases when using feature extraction techniques. The essential requirements for extracting disease features include features related to motion, texture, shape, and color [42, 43]. A technique for disease recognition using color and texture features was presented by Raza and colleagues [44]. Hu et al. presented multifеature integration and the Dempster-Shafer (D-S) model of evidence as feature extraction methods. Variance was then introduced into the results processing to enhance the D-S evidence theory's decision rules [45].
