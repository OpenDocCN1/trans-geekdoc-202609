The agriculture sector significantly contributes to a nation's economic growth. However, plant diseases pose a substantial risk leading to crop destruction, reduced agricultural yields, and substantial financial losses. Primary recognition of plant diseases is crucial for avoiding such destruction. While previous research has utilized ML or DL techniques for disease prediction, deep learning demands substantial training data and computational resources, and machine learning methods lack automated feature extraction capabilities. Moreover, the existing model consumes more computational resources, time, and produces lower accuracy. In response, this study introduces a novel E-ENet basis that combines the benefits of both ML and DL to enhance the efficiency of plant disease identification with improved accuracy.

# 10.3.1 Dataset

The PlantVillage dataset, which comprises 54,305 photos and 38 classes representing 14 distinct plant species—12 of which are healthy and 26 of which are diseased—was used in this study [66]. The dataset comprises colored images of various sizes, including an additional class specifically designating 1,143 background images. In total, there are 55,448 images within the dataset. Figure 10.1 illustrates eight randomly selected pairs of distinct plant–disease combinations from the dataset.

Geetharamani and Pandian [9] augmented the PlantVillage dataset using six different techniques. The researchers have made both the unique and augmented datasets accessible for scientific research, and these datasets can be found at https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/tywbtsjrvj/1. Through augmentation techniques, the sample count for classes with fewer than 1,000 samples were increased to 1,000, while the images in other classes remained unaltered.
