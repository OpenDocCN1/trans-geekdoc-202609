Dataset Description: Our study employed the MHEALTH DATASET-A mobile health (MHEALTH) benchmarked dataset. It was gathered from 10 subjects utilizing records of body movements and vital signs made by wearing SHIMMER2 sensors. Each patient had these sensors on their chest, left ankle, and right wrist using elastic bands. Mobile health systems capture body dynamics and assess diversity brought about by motion of body components, such as acceleration and orientation of magnetic fields, by utilizing a variety of sensors. The chest-mounted sensors provide two lead ECG measurements. This multimodal body-sensing dataset can monitor the heart, check for arrhythmias, or examine how exercise affects the ECG. This dataset’s sampling rate, 50 Hz, was adequate for capturing the simulated human actions. All performed sessions were videotaped to categorize data and check for signal anomalies.

The Human Activities and Postural Transitions (HAPT) Dataset, collected using smartphones: The information is updated in the UCI Human Activity Recognition Using Popularity Data collection. There are 12 different kinds of action in the HAPT data collection. First, it has six fundamental motions, including three static and three walking motions. It offers six possible transitions from two static movements: standing to sitting, sitting to standing, lying to sitting, and lying to standing. Thirty volunteers, aged 19 to 48 years, participated in the study, while each wore a smartphone around their waist. The built-in gyroscope and acceleration sensor collect data, and the sampling frequency is 50 Hz. Video recordings of the experimental process are made to ease later data marking.
