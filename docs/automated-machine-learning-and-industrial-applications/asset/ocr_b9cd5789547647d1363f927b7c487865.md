Instead of categorizing all plant diseases within the dataset, it has been observed that a significant portion of trainings in the literature utilize the Plant Village dataset for diagnosing diseases in individual or a limited number of plants.

Sladojevic et al. used Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) in one of these studies to classify 13 distinct plant diseases [16]. In their investigation, they trained their suggested model on a dataset of 30,880 images and used 2,589 images for testing. An accuracy of 96.3% was attained on average with their suggested model. Chen et al. utilized DENSINCEP, a deep TL (Transfer Learning)-based model, for detecting rice plant disease [29]. Chen et al. modified the VGGNet module to conduct disease categorization on maize and rice plants in another investigation [30]. Five studies have been found to categorize every disease found in the PlantVillage dataset. These studies comprise a total of 39 classes, which include a class for background images devoid of leaves and 38 different types of plant diseases. CNN methods, like GoogLeNet and AlexNet, were applied by Mohanty et al. to classify diseases of plants [12]. They achieved 99.35% accuracy in classification in their investigation.

CNN algorithms, like DenseNets, Inception V4, Resnet152, ResNet101, ResNet50, and VGG16, 121, were utilized by Too et al. According to reports, the research's DenseNet design provided 99.75% test accuracy with less parameters and a shorter calculation time than the rest of the models [31]. Using the PlantVillage dataset, Geetharamani and Pandian trained a nine-layer CNN model with varying epochs, batch sizes, and dropout rates. They then compared the results of the final models to well-known transfer learning techniques. On the test dataset, their suggested model produced 96.46% accuracy in classification [9]. However, two additional studies have been conducted using larger datasets from PlantVillage that contain additional images. Ferentinos employed 87,848 images and utilized models, like AlexNetOWTBn, GoogLeNet, Overfeat, AlexNet, and VGG, to categorize 58 distinct diseases affecting 25 different varieties of plants in their study. With 99.53% accuracy, the VGG architecture employed in the study produced the best results [17].

Arsenovic et al. created an extended version of the PlantVillage dataset, named the Plant Disease dataset, comprising 79,265 images for their second research. They utilized both datasets in their experimental investigations. Their proposed Plant Disease Net model, a two-stage classification approach, initially identifies the plant variety using leaves in the initial stage and subsequently categorizes the identified leaves in the next stage.
