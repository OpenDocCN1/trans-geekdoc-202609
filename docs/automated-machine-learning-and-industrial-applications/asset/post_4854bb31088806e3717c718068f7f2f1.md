Unquestionably, crop plant diseases represent a serious risk to agricultural productivity and sustainable development. By taking preventative measures against the disease’s attacks, early disease attack prediction helps to effectively control the disease. Precision Agriculture (PA) applications, which promote sustainable development, heavily rely on modern information and communication technologies (ICTs).

Solutions for the early disease attack prediction are desperately needed to prevent plant disease attacks. Only after the disease has manifested itself can the current computer vision-based approach to disease detection identify its presence. The effort of this research is to progress an ML technique that uses the Internet of Things (IoT) to directly sense environmental data from crop fields to forecast the probability of early disease attacks. Environmental factors have a strong correlation with the life cycles of pest diseases. Plant disease incidence is predicted by the environmental factors in crop fields. Given the relationship between environmental conditions and disease attacks, the study employs Multiple Linear Regression (MLR) as the machine learning model. Leveraging an ML method, the environmental conditions of crop fields derived from the Internet of Things (IoT) contribute to accurate predictions of plant disease occurrences. To calculate the performance of the approach, the model is applied to tea plants (Camellia sinensis) to identify the occurrence of blister blight (Exobasidium vexans). Using the proposed model from 2015 to 2019, the disease’s occurrence could be predicted up to 91% of the time in 2019.

## 10.3 Materials and Methods

### 10.3.1 Dataset

The PlantVillage dataset, which comprises 54,305 photos and 38 classes representing 14 distinct plant species—12 of which are healthy and 26 of which are diseased—was used in this study [66]. The dataset comprises colored images of various sizes, including an additional class specifically designating 1,143 background images. In total, there are 55,448 images within the dataset. Figure 10.1 illustrates eight randomly selected pairs of distinct plant–disease combinations from the dataset.

Geetharamani and Pandian [9] augmented the PlantVillage dataset using six different techniques. The researchers have made both the unique and augmented datasets accessible for scientific research, and these datasets can be found at https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/tywbtsjrvj/1. Through augmentation techniques, the sample count for classes with fewer than 1,000 samples were increased to 1,000, while the images in other classes remained unaltered.
