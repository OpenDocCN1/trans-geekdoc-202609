A method for extracting significant audio features from music was used so that the visualizations could react to the music [48]. In addition, several innovative methods for applying feature extraction have been proposed in recent research. Meziani et al. developed two new spectral estimators that effectively handle non-Gaussian, non-linear signals and are robust to non-stationary EEG data [49]. Liu et al. obtained high-dimensional time-frequency spectrum structures by enhancing the signal-to-clutter ratio (SCR) and using the residual neural network [50]. To assess the wear of milling tools, Xu et al. developed a feature extraction technique that makes use of the Hilbert marginal spectrum [51].

To obtain precise identification, a great transaction of research and development has gone into developing techniques. By focusing on class labels for training images, the recognition model created a fine-grained image arrangement system [52]. A hybrid clustering-based plant disease leaf image recognition technique was presented by Zhang et al. [53]. Patil et al. (2017) outlined a content-based image retrieval (CBIR) system to extract texture features and calculate color features [54]. An SVM classifier was used for classification in the aforementioned studies. These studies’ primary goals were to create classification schemes and perform image analysis to extract and identify features. More precise and accurate methods for diagnosing the illness have just been introduced. Based on the choice of images and concise text descriptions, a novel technique that can be retrieved remotely from a smartphone, desktop, or personal digital assistant helped non-experts identify plant diseases [55].

A scheme for actual, on-site imaging of diseased plants using a mobile phone was introduced by Pertot et al. [56]. The method involved segmenting leaf images and spotting disease patches using improved k-means clustering. A method for microscopy image detection using a decision tree–confusion matrix and a synergistic valuation of texture and shape features was presented by Yang et al. [57]. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have shown promise in image-based identification tasks and are therefore frequently used for disease identification. A system that can automatically identify plant diseases in field-acquired photos of maize plants was developed by Chad et al. [58]. Ni et al. developed an automatic corn detector by training a deep convolutional neural network with 1,632 images of corn kernels [59]. A method for recognizing rice diseases with deep convolutional neural network (CNN) techniques was presented by Lu et al. [60].
