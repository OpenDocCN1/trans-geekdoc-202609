# 13.2 Current Day Usage of AI

The healthcare sector is currently utilizing artificial intelligence (AI) and related technologies, which are spreading throughout society and business. Numerous facets of patient care as well as administrative procedures used by payers, pharmaceutical companies, and healthcare providers may change as a result of these technologies.

AI has already been shown in numerous tests to be either as effective as humans or even more so in medical tasks like illness diagnosis. Radiologists can already identify potentially harmful cancers using algorithms, and they may also advise researchers on how to create cohorts for costly clinical studies. But before AI completely replaces people in medical processes, a number of years will pass. In this section, we take a look at some of the current day practices making use of AI to make it more efficient.

# 13.2.1 Deep Learning and Neural Networks

There is no lack of medical data. Each year, a hospital or clinic generates a vast amount of data from tests conducted on patients as well as forms filled and operations conducted. Now, if you consider a country or even just a city with the amount of data present, a model can be trained to accurately predict, for example, based on past data, which particular treatment would be more effective for a patient. This has potential to outperform human expertise since the model trained would have access to a much larger repository of data than any single hospital/clinic could have.

Neural networks have been used since the past 60 years for mapping inputs to outputs and make predictions for new data given to it. They function analogous to how the neurons work in the brain where we have different layers in our model with separate activation functions, which help process the data bit by bit until we get our desired output.
