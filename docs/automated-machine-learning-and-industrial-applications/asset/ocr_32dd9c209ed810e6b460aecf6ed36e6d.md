Information and communication technology (ICT)-based health surveillance systems connect people and infrastructure to improve overall quality of life. In addition to supporting the fundamental requirements of health monitoring systems, industrial stability, sustainable development, quick accessibility, and access to dependable medical care are targets that are reached by mandating and appropriately transmitting data from dedicated networks [1]. Wireless body area networks (WBANs), which scientists and researchers have developed, are made up of tiny sensors with wireless communication capabilities that can be put inside patients’ bodies. The devices continuously gather biological signals from patients in hospitals and quarantine facilities using various sensors and then transmit the data collected to the local analysis unit (LPU). To improve health warnings so that healthcare practitioners can quickly respond by taking preventative measures when they see patients’ falling health conditions or any emergency, LPUs should be robust and capable of processing health data in real time. LPU data may also be transferred to distant databases (DB) for long-term study and preservation [2]. WBANs have several advantages, including enabling doctors to monitor patient-specific parameters remotely, improving diagnostic accuracy and efficacy, and lowering healthcare costs per patient by giving doctors a convenient way to track patients’ well-being all the time.
