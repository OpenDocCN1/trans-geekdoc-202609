[5] Depression is a predominant and debilitating mental health condition that moves millions of entities globally. Standard therapy techniques, including cognitive–behavioral therapy (CBT) and medication, are helpful for many people, but not everyone can access or afford them. Chatbots, conversational machines designed to replace human discourse, are a potential replacement for depression therapy. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of a chatbot-based depression therapy in a randomized controlled experiment. The participants were arbitrarily assigned to either the chatbot therapy group or the wait-list control group. While the chatbot therapy group had 8 weeks of therapy via a chatbot, the control group did not get any treatment during this time. The chatbot therapy group showed considerably fewer depressed indications than the control group (BDI-II) according to data from the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) and the Beck Depression Inventory-II. Participants in the chatbot therapy group also showed high levels of satisfaction with the chatbot interface and therapy. These findings suggest that chatbot-based therapy could be a viable alternative to traditional depression therapy, particularly for those who might not have access to it.
