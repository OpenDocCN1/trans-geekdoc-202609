\( [\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{G}] \xrightarrow{f} \hat{\mathbf{X}}^{(t+1)(t+H)} \)

where \(\mathcal{X} = [\mathbf{X}^{(t-S+1)}, \mathbf{X}^{(t-S+2)}, ..., \mathbf{X}^{(t)}] \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times S \times C}\) \(\mathbf{X}^{(t)} = [\mathbf{x}_1^t, \mathbf{x}_2^t, ..., \mathbf{x}_N^t] \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times C}\) denotes the traffic condition at time step t, C is several features of every node, and \(\hat{\mathbf{X}}(t + 1):(t + H) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times H \times C}\) is the prediction value. Using only a small amount of data in the target domain, the pre-trained function f is further optimized in step 2 to anticipate the traffic situation for all nodes on graph G 0 in the following H time steps. This method is described as Equation 2.29:

\( [\mathcal{X}', \mathcal{G}'] \xrightarrow{\rho(t+1)(t+H)} \hat{\mathbf{X}}^{\rho(t+1)(t+H)} \)

where \(\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{(t+1)(t+H)|L} \in \mathbb{R}^{N' \times H \times C}\) and \(\mathcal{X} = \mathbf{X}^{(t-S+1)}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times S \times C}\) are the prediction results in the target domain. Graph convolution networks (GCNs) are commonly utilized in traffic prediction to extract spatial correlation. In more detail, we may acquire the output representation \(\mathcal{Z} = \mathbf{Z}^{(t-S+1)(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times S \times F}\) given the input signal \([\mathbf{X}^{(t-S+1)}, ..., \mathbf{X}^{(t)}] \in \mathbb{R}^{N' \times S \times C}\), and the adjacency matrix \(\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}\).
