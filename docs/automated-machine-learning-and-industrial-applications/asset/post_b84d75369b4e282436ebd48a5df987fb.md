## 10.8 Summary

In addition to helping farmers automate tasks, artificial intelligence in agriculture is enabling more precise cultivation, which uses fewer resources to produce higher-quality and higher-yield crops. Businesses that are enhancing AI and machine learning-based goods and services, such as drones, automated machinery, and training data for agriculture, will benefit from future technological advancements that will enable them to develop more beneficial applications for this industry assisting the global community in addressing challenges related to food production for an expanding population. AI tools in agriculture, such as Precision Agriculture and Drones, enable farmers to enhance processes and adapt to environmental changes. These technologies optimize field management, provide crop health insights, and streamline operations for increased efficiency. Additionally, while preserving the operations’ long-term viability, this will raise yields and revenues.

This chapter illustrates the high training efficiency, high precision, good universality, and unsupervised nature of the deep learning-based plant disease recognition model. Despite these strengths, there are several challenges to the practicality and accuracy of identifying plant diseases in complex environments. This chapter presents a recognition model that combines the RPNv2 algorithm, LCV algorithm, and E-ENet system to improve the identification process and handle related issues. The combined method seeks to maximize efficiency and accuracy in object identification and classification within the specified context. The proposed recognition model effectively tackles the challenge of identifying plant diseases in complex environments enhancing identification accuracy and adaptability. In contrast to conventional models, the approach ensures the robustness of the convolutional neural network. Consequently, the model holds the potential to assist agricultural production staff in promptly preventing and treating plant diseases.

In a practical context, an approach that addresses the challenge of environmental complexity can yield accurate identification results. This study not only contributes to the improvement of accuracy but also advances existing theories shedding light on the crucial role of complexity in plant disease identification. Recognizing this importance is essential for studying plant diseases in complex environments. Moreover, the proposed approach supports the sustainable development of smart farming by integrating information technology into agricultural production. However, certain issues remain to be addressed. For instance, the Chan–Vese Level II algorithm, while effective, may suffer from slow execution and repetitive iterative calculations impacting its ability to deliver swift identification results. The E-ENet model achieves high test accuracy (98.87%) and validation accuracy (99.21%) for 30 epochs based on comparison results.
