A typical patient monitoring method deployment in a fog-cloud environment is shown in Figure 2.1. MIoT devices gather patient data and send it to a GW via an access point after being felt. The GW is primarily utilized for IoT devices and processing model communication. Following the first in, first out principle, the incoming requests for health data are accumulated in a buffer and delivered to the GW. The GW should decide how long the requests for health data continue. Two routes for health data traffic are proposed in the architecture: as (i) The GW can send some of sensed data to a private cloud, which is entirely separate as it does not sync with a public cloud. (ii) The public cloud can see the state and health information of the fog nodes, and the GW can transfer some of the sensed data to fog computing (FC) nodes that communicate with it. A public or private FC layer is possible. Local telecom service providers may offer FC nodes, or the public cloud may offer them through a point of presence. To further decrease the reaction time of the cloud datacenter, localized processing and storing are also carried out on the FC layer installed between MIoT devices and the cloud. Many physical machines (PMs) in public cloud datacenters are often virtualized to form a resource pool of virtual machines (VMs). VMs offer discrete running environments and share PM resources. Authorized users, including doctors, staff, emergency personnel, patients, hospitals, carers, and health ministries, have access to, and are given, the data obtained from both the private and public sectors. Any device, including a smartphone, a personal digital assistant, or a web browser, can access the recorded data.
