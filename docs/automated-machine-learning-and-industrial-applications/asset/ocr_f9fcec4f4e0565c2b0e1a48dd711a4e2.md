Zhang et al. created an image-identifying network for agricultural machinery using a deep learning algorithm [61]. Additionally, Zhang et al. enhanced deep convolutional neural networks [62] to recover the precision of maize leaf disease identification. Images were fed into AlexNet and VGG-16 net, two deep learning-based architectures, for detection [63]. Transfer learning was introduced by Coulibaly et al. as a technique for feature extraction in the construction of an identification system [64]. However, the time-consuming nature of the training process poses a challenge and hinders the widespread adoption of the approach, as it demands substantial hardware resources, high-quality traditional neural network models, and extensive datasets.

The agricultural industry is increasingly adopting artificial intelligence technologies to enhance various tasks across the food supply chain, including improving crop yields, pest control, monitoring soil and growing conditions, organizing information for farmers, and assisting with workloads. India, as the second-largest tomato producer globally with 18.7 million tons, faces significant challenges due to various bacterial, viral, and fungal diseases affecting numerous parts of the plant, such as the fruit, stem, and leaf. Many techniques rely on algorithms for pattern recognition and image processing applied to pictures of infected plant leaves.
