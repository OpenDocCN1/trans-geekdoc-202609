Chen et al. utilized DenseNet, a deep TL (Transfer Learning)-based model, for detecting rice plant disease [29]. Five studies have been found to categorize every disease found in the PlantVillage dataset. CNN algorithms, like DenseNets, Inception V4, ResNet152, ResNet101, ResNet50, and VGG16, DenseNet121, were utilized by Too et al. Using the PlantVillage dataset, Geetharamani and Pandian trained a nine-layer CNN model with varying epochs, batch sizes, and dropout rates. However, two additional studies have been conducted using larger datasets from PlantVillage that contain additional images. Ferentinos employed 87,848 images and utilized models, like AlexNetOWTBn, GoogLeNet, Overfeat, AlexNet, and VGG, to categorize 58 distinct diseases affecting 25 different varieties of plants in their study. Arsenovic et al. created an extended version of the PlantVillage dataset, named the Plant Disease dataset, comprising 79,265 images for their second research. They utilized both datasets in their experimental investigations. Their proposed Plant Disease Net model, a two-stage classification approach, initially identifies the plant variety using leaves in the initial stage and subsequently categorizes the identified leaves in the next stage. With respect to accuracy, their proposed model performed well achieving 93.67% in the Plant Disease dataset [32]. ## 10.2 Literature Review

Feature extraction, disease recognition, and diseased leaf image segmentation are the three main areas of current research on plant disease recognition in complicated environments. In a complex environment, the crucial challenge lies in effectively segmenting images to identify and localize the leaves of diseased plants. The primary area of image segmentation is to distinguish and separate symptoms from the surrounding environment. Numerous researchers are delving deeply into the matter. The Delta E color difference algorithm was applied by Ali et al. in 2017 [35] to identify the disease-infected area. Generally speaking, there are four main approaches used to segment images; these are covered in more detail in the paragraph that follows [36]. In image segmentation, some researchers integrate additional techniques with the region of interest (ROI). For instance, in their study, Kao et al. used a convolutional autoencoder as a background filter for the ROI [37].

The next approach is limited to segmenting regions. According to Pujari et al. (2013), features of images were extracted, and they were claimed to be divided into multiple regions, each with a unique meaning [38]. A real-time synchronous image-processing model was presented by Akram and associates. It can perform feature vector, contrast stretching, and salient region recognition by partitioning the image into three color spaces [39]. Additionally, other researchers have employed DL methods for image segmentation and detection. To maximize the recognition of plant diseases in photos, Marko et al. suggested a depth-based recognition algorithm and employed a two-stage algorithm [32]. Thresholding is a frequently used segmentation technique. Li et al. (2018) used multilevel thresholding methods built on gray histograms for image segmentation [40]. Using multilevel thresholding, Mohamed and Diego presented a novel multiobjective meta-heuristic for grayscale image segmentation based on a multiverse optimization algorithm [41]. But one thing is certain—it cannot be disregarded. In practical applications, color-based machine vision algorithms, region of interest, and thresholds often perform inadequately due to the intricate nature of color information in complex environments.

There are numerous challenges in identifying plant diseases when using feature extraction techniques. The essential requirements for extracting disease features include features related to motion, texture, shape, and color [42, 43]. A technique for disease recognition using color and texture features was presented by Raza and colleagues [44]. Hu et al. presented multifeature integration and the Dempster-Shafer (D-S) model of evidence as feature extraction methods. Variance was then introduced into the results processing to enhance the D-S evidence theory's decision rules [45].
