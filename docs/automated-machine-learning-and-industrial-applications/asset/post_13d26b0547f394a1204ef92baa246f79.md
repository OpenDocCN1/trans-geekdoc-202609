## Abstract

As a consequence of the rapid expansion of social networks, such as consumer review networks, discussion forums, blogging and publishing networks, and other such Internet-based networks, the volume of comments and evaluations about day-to-day activities has increased tremendously. Sentiment analysis is a significant research area that serves as a powerful and efficient technique capable of analyzing such reviews and comments to gather valuable insights regarding the various aspects of a product or service. Businesses, governing bodies, and individuals may all benefit from public sentiment by gathering data and making decisions based on it.

One of the interesting fields where sentiment analysis finds application is evaluating customer’s interactions with chatbots. With an effort to enhance customer service and reduce corporate expenses, AI chatbots have been designed for customer support. The practice of understanding a customer’s tone of voice and words while communicating with a chatbot—whether they are happy, sad, fearful, or angry—is known as chatbot sentiment analysis. Just because a customer interacted with a chatbot does not mean they will ignore or forget a bad experience.

Customers seek assistance in resolving issues they may be having with the item or service you provide. If the chatbot or virtual assistant is unable to assist them, they will revert to the default procedure, which is typically interacting with an agent in person (a live agent). They will choose to work with a different brand if they are unable to receive assistance there. So, there is a greater need to carry out sentiment analysis on customer interactions with a chatbot. As chatbots are a cost-effective solution to the issue of improving customer satisfaction, it is critical to conduct chatbot sentiment analysis to better understand them and deliver chatbot conversations that satisfy users.

To guarantee customer satisfaction, trustworthy chatbots that can swiftly handle consumers’ concerns must be developed. Businesses may better understand their consumers’ demands and take the appropriate action with the aid of sentiment analysis-capable bots. The process of automating the activities complicated in using machine learning to solve real-world issues is recognized as automated machine learning (AutoML). From the initial dataset to the final deployable machine learning model, AutoML covers the entire pipeline.

The specifics of performing chatbot sentiment analysis are covered in this chapter. We will investigate the practice of performing chatbot sentiment analysis and how to automate sentiment detection from customer–chatbot conversations. In addition, the chapter explores how text sentiment can be analyzed, classified, and how businesses can perform chatbot sentiment analysis to automatically classify customer remarks as good, negative, or neutral assisting them in understanding client sentiment precisely.

## 7.1 Introduction

A chatbot, at its simplest level, is a computer program that mimics and analyzes human interaction (either written or spoken) allowing humans to communicate with digital devices as if they were speaking with a real person. While not all chatbots have artificial intelligence (AI) built in, contemporary chatbots increasingly make use of conversational AI strategies, such as natural language processing (NLP), to comprehend user inquiries and generate automated responses. By instantly responding to queries and requests via text, audio, or both, chatbots can enable users’ information discovery and remove the need for manual search or human interaction. ## 7.1.1 Background

Currently, chatbots are pervasive. You can find them in everything from home smart speakers to consumer-facing SMS, WhatsApp, and Facebook Messenger apps to business messaging platforms like Slack.

The current iteration of AI chatbots, also identified as “virtual agents” or “intelligent virtual assistants”, is capable of automating pertinent tasks in addition to sympathetic natural language exchanges through the use of advanced language models. In addition to the well-known intelligent virtual assistants targeted at consumers, such as Amazon Alexa and Apple’s Siri, virtual agents are now being utilized more and more in business settings to support clients and staff [3]. Since the beginning of the computing era, several attempts have been made to model human behavior in machine agents. In the history of chatbots, ELIZA, being the first, modeled the behavior of a psychologist. Later, huge investments were made toward the design and implementation of smarter chatbots, though they were not able to succeed due to the lack of technicalities at that period of time. Still, chatbots could not understand conversations as well as humans. They can do simple tasks, and when they lack proper responses, they redirect the user to human agents.

The first chatbots were just interactive FAQ systems that were designed to provide pre-written responses to a small number of frequently asked inquiries. Because they were unable to understand natural language, users were typically forced to choose from a list of basic words and phrases to advance the discussion. Such basic, traditional chatbots cannot handle complicated queries or respond to straightforward inquiries that the developers have not anticipated [7]. Chatbots are software-based agents developed to engage in conversation with people using natural language, which has seen so much attention nowadays in online services. The challenge is regarding the approaches to be followed in measuring the service satisfaction of chatbots experienced from the customer end. Chatbots can be pre-trained to interpret customer inquiries and generate appropriate responses [8].

As chatbot algorithms advanced, they were able to achieve increasingly intricate rule-based programming and even natural language processing, which made it possible for users to ask questions in a conversational way. A new breed of chatbot emerged as a result, one that was equipped with machine learning and contextual awareness to continuously improve its capacity to accurately appreciate and anticipate requests by being exposed to an increasing amount of natural language.

Today's AI chatbots can now accept open-ended user input by using natural language understanding (NLU), which helps them get past errors like typos and translation difficulties. The meaning is then mapped by sophisticated AI techniques to the precise “intent” that the user wants the chatbot to act upon, and conversational AI is then used to provide a suitable answer. With a few subtle modifications, these AI skills combine machine learning and deep learning to create a more and more detailed knowledge base of queries and answers derived from user interactions. This sophistication, which makes use of recent developments in large language models (LLMs), has resulted in more adaptable chatbot applications and higher customer satisfaction.
