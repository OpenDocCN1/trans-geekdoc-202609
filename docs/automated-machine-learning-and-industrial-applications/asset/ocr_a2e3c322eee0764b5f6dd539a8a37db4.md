# 13.1.1 Some of the Recent Publications and their Findings

Taojian Fan et al. [1] discussed the various 2D models and imaging currently in use in the biomedical industry as well as their potential shortfalls. They point out why it is vital to deploy drugs directly at the target site and also reviewed various 2D models currently in use.

Pandiraj Manickam et al. [2] researched on the current usage of the Internet of Medical Things(IoMT) in the industry and how, with the addition of AI to it, we can make it much more efficient. Their study found that AI helped improve the IoMT models as well as point of care (POC) devices.

LM Patrick et al. [3] leveraged AI techniques to improve data quality and better detect endogenous metabolites and diseases linked by applying it to the field of high-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS).

Xiangzhou et al. [4] reviewed various recent practices, such as drug delivery at site development of biomembrane cloaked nano platforms derived from inherent host cells. Their study also highlighted potential paths for better vaccine development inspired by their diverse moieties.

Hua Ai et al. [5] demonstrated that thin films can be deposited on virtually any surface with the use of biomaterials up to a precision of 1 nm. JN Acosta et al. [6] developed techniques for low-cost utilization of multimodal datasets for healthcare. They also reviewed current industry practices and recent developments that make use of low-cost genome sequencing, more data from medical imaging, etc., to make a more robust model.

Gao et al. [7] worked on 2D biomedical imaging data and applied deep learning techniques, and one of their proposed model was a data-driven diagnostic model for hip dysplasia. They also developed a chest CT prediction model, which predicted the presence of the corona virus by comparing it to an NHL via a radiologist and their developed CT analysis software.

F. Qian et al. [8] utilized AI techniques for biogenesis and composition for extracellular vesicle (EV) cargo analysis. Ahmadi et al. [9] observed the reaction on horseradish peroxidase catalyzed by hydrogels. They used AI techniques to simulate and model the bio-fabrication process.

Tao Tu et al. [10] developed a medical large language model (LLM) Med PalM M, which can flexibly encode and interpret biomedical data. Their reports were preferred 40% more than the ones generated by radiologists showing real-world viability.
