
Farmers are well aware by now that there is opportunity to optimize the use of pesticides. Unfortunately, there are significant limits to both programmed and manual application procedures. While manually applying chemical fertilizers can be labor intensive and time consuming, it allows for greater precision when focusing on particular areas. Although automated pesticide spraying is faster and requires less labor, it frequently lacks accuracy, which can contaminate the environment. Drones driven by AI combine the best features of each strategy without any of its disadvantages. Drones that utilize computer vision can calculate how much pesticide should be sprayed on each area. Even though this technology is still in its infancy, it is advancing quickly in precision. Yield mapping analyzes big datasets instantly using machine learning algorithms. Better planning is made possible for farmers as a result of their increased understanding of the patterns and traits of their crops. Farmers can forecast soil harvests for particular crops by combining approaches like 3D mapping with information from sensors and drones. Multiple drone flights provide data collection for increasingly accurate analysis through the application of algorithms.

With the aid of these methods, farmers are able to accurately forecast upcoming yields for particular crops assisting them in seeding locations and timing as well as resource allocation for optimal return on investment. Just as computer vision can classify diseases and pests, it can also be employed to recognize weeds and invasive plant species in agricultural settings. Computer vision examines the shape, color, and size of leaves to identify weeds from crops when paired with machine learning. These kinds of remedies can be employed to program machines for projects like automatic weeding that are part of robotic process automation (RPA). Indeed, a robot just like this has been put to good use already. As these innovations become more widely available, smart bots may eventually perform both cultivation and weeding tasks completely.

## 10.1.1 Obstacles in the Agricultural Sector

### 10.1.1.1 Soil Erosion

The natural method of soil erosion occurs when water or wind carry soil particles from one location to another. Farmers face severe issues when this occurs on a large scale. Many factors contribute to soil erosion, such as excessive soil tillage that degrades the soil's condition and reduces water retention. The top soil layers are swiftly removed during flooding. To use less water in irrigation, protecting soil from wind and water erosion with mulch and crop covers, and limiting livestock grazing excessively, are all ways to avert soil erosion.

### 10.1.1.2 Absence of High-Quality Seeds

To increase the production of crops, farmers must use high-quality seeds. Achieving steady growth in agricultural output is also dependent on it. The distribution of superior seeds holds equal significance to that of seed production. Most farmers, especially marginal and small farmers, usually cannot afford seeds of good quality because of their prohibitively expensive prices.

### 10.1.1.3 Lack of Contemporary Farming Machinery

The absence of proper farm equipment is one of the biggest issues farmers face, and it can make it difficult for them to adjust to the demands of contemporary farming methods. Farmers can have major improvements in their lives when they are trained to use the equipment. It is crucial to use the aforementioned equipment.

## 10.1.2 Challenges of AI in Agriculture

Many believe that AI is unrelated for farming tasks and only applies to the digital world. In general, this assumption stems to an insufficient amount of knowledge about AI tools. The general public, particularly those in non-tech fields, is not well informed about artificial intelligence (AI), which is causing the agricultural industry to adopt AI more slowly. Despite numerous progressions in agriculture during its long history, various farmers are still more familiar with using conventional systems.

Furthermore, suppliers often do a poor job of outlining the benefits of novel technologies as well as their applications. Tech firms face a significant task in educating the public about the application of AI in agriculture. Despite the clear benefits of AI for sustainable farming, there is a need to inform farmers about its practical advantages and encourage its adoption in the agricultural sector. However, there are still certain obstacles to be addressed.

## 10.1.3 Existing Plant Disease Identification Methods

Stunted growth from plant diseases can have a negative impact on yields [3–5]. Worldwide, an estimated $20 billion or more is lost economically each year [6–8]. Because of regional variations that could make accurate identification more challenging, multiple circumstances present the greatest challenge to researchers [9, 10]. Traditional techniques often rely on experts, knowledge, and manuals [11], but they tend to be expensive, labor intensive, and time consuming. Moreover, they face challenges in accurately identifying objects [12]. For this reason, it appears that identifying plant diseases quickly and accurately is crucial for the sake of agriculture’s economy and ecology. Web technologies have advanced quickly, especially the availability of multimodality data from a diversity of sensors, such as sensor networks with the Internet of Things [13]. To tackle the issues mentioned earlier, this study introduces an innovative plant leaf identification model based on DL. The model employs an integrated deep learning algorithm to execute image segmentation, identification, and leaf retrieval seamlessly within its functionality. The first task is retrieving leaves, but in this complicated atmosphere, numerous considerations, like soil and illumination, make accurate identification difficult [14].

Achieving sustainable and efficient agriculture, while preventing unnecessary waste of resources, hinges on the initial stage and accurate diagnosis of plant diseases. Certain plant diseases lack obvious symptoms, so it is necessary to employ sophisticated analytical techniques in cases of such diseases. Nonetheless, most of the plant diseases exhibit outward signs, and the currently recognized method involves having a skilled plant pathologist visually inspect infected plant leaves to identify the disease [15, 16]. Expert and intelligent systems that can reliably and independently identify plant diseases provide agriculturalists with substantial benefits and are a great help in managing crop health. However, applying such a scheme with an easy-to-use smartphone application is also a commendable accomplishment for farmers lacking access to agronomic and phytopathological assistance facilities [17].

Modern agricultural techniques are depending more and more on artificial intelligence-based systems that can identify a variety of disorders [18]. In the past decade, various conventional ML models have been introduced for the identification and categorization of plant diseases. Rumpf et al. employed Support Vector Machine (SVM) to explore the initial identification and categorization of illnesses in sugar beet using spectral plant guides [19]. Al-Hiary et al. employed a segmentation approach by utilizing k-means clustering on preprocessed features to identify and differentiate five distinct diseases in plant leaves. Following texture and color-based extraction of features, Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) were then used to classify those regions [20]. Revathi and Hemalatha suggested a technique to identify six distinct disease kinds that are present on cotton leaves. They propose an approach that uses Particle Swarm Optimization to choose characteristics from a feature vector that includes color, texture, and edge-based features acquired through image processing. The disease is subsequently classified using a Cross Information Gain Deep Forward Neural Network [21].

Mokhtar et al. conducted an investigation employing the Support Vector Machine (SVM) technique to detect and identify two distinct viruses, which cause disease and exhibit symptoms on tomato leaves [22]. In a separate study, Pantazi et al. utilized features extracted from the Local Binary Pattern method to identify three different vine leaf diseases through the Support Vector Machine (SVM) approach [23]. In a different study, Johannes et al. suggested using Naive Bayes classification system and image processing-based candidate hotspot detection for mobile-based initial stage diagnosis of three distinct wheat diseases. They tested their suggested approach in a real-world field setting using cellphones [24]. To automate the identification of plant diseases, Chen et al. have recently introduced a novel model known as the GMDH (Group Method of Data Handling) logistic algorithm [25]. Categorizing using machine learning involves a demanding process known as feature extraction, which directly influences the nature of classification.

DL architectures, which use methods to analyze raw data without the necessity for custom features, represent a performance breakthrough. This is made feasible by the enhanced capabilities and speeds of Central Processing Units (CPUs) with Graphical Processing Units (GPUs) [26]. Deep neural network styles, which have numerous layers and neurons for processing information, achieve exceptionally well at handling difficult tasks, like voice and image recognition, while handling massive amounts of data with ease.
