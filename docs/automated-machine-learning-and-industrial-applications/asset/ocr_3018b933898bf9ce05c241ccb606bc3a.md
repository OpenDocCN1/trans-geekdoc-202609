Since the beginning of the computing era, several attempts have been made to model human behavior in machine agents. In the history of chatbots, ELIZA, being the first, modeled the behavior of a psychologist. Later, huge investments were made toward the design and implementation of smarter chatbots, though they were not able to succeed due to the lag of technicalities at that period of time. Still, chatbots could not understand conversations as well as humans. They can do simple tasks, and when they lack proper responses, they redirect the user to human agents.

The first chatbots were just interactive FAQ systems that were designed to provide pre-written responses to a small number of frequently asked inquiries. Because they were unable to understand natural language, users were typically forced to choose from a list of basic words and phrases to advance the discussion. Such basic, traditional chatbots cannot handle complicated queries or respond to straightforward inquiries that the developers have not anticipated [7].
