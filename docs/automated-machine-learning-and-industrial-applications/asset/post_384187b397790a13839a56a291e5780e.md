
- Hyperspectral sensors: These sensors measure the reflectance of light across a wide range of wavelengths from the visible to the near-infrared spectrum that provides high-resolution spectral data, which can be used to identify detailed changes in leaf pigmentation and structure.
- Multispectral sensors: These sensors measure the reflectance of light at specific wavelengths, typically in the visible and near-infrared spectrum, providing lower-resolution spectral data but at a higher spatial resolution.
- Fluorescence sensors: These sensors measure the fluorescence emitted by plant leaves when exposed to light, providing information about the photosynthetic activity and health of the plant.
- Thermal sensors: These sensors measure the infrared radiation emitted by plant leaves, providing information about the temperature and water stress of the plant.

The sensors work by emitting light or radiation onto the plant leaves and measuring the amount of energy reflected or emitted back. The spectral signature of the reflected or emitted energy can then be analyzed to identify specific wavelengths or patterns associated with different plant diseases or stress conditions.

Spectral analysis using these sensors can provide accurate and non-invasive methods for detecting plant leaf diseases and monitoring plant health, enabling early intervention and management to prevent crop losses.
