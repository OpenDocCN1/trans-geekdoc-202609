## 12.8 Blockchain in Finance

Blockchain operates on a consensus mechanism, a set of policies figuring out how transactions are confirmed and introduced to the ledger. Consensus mechanisms consist of Proof of Work (PoW) and Proof of Stake (PoS), in which every distinct method is considered for transaction validation. Enhancing security within the financial area is of paramount importance within the financial sector, in which sensitive records and substantial sums of money are at stake. Conventional monetary structures, relying heavily on centralized databases, are susceptible to cyber-attacks, fraud, and unauthorized access [32]. Blockchain, with its decentralized and cryptographic attributes, provides a strong method to those protection-demanding situations.

Immutable record-keeping: A key protection characteristic of blockchain is its immutability. As soon as a block is brought to the chain, it becomes impervious to alteration or deletion. This feature guarantees a tamper-resistant record of transactions mitigating the risk of fraud and manipulation. In finance, this immutability feature safeguards against unauthorized adjustments to financial statistics decreasing the likelihood of fraudulent activities.

Cryptography for secure transactions: Blockchain employs cryptographic strategies to secure transactions. Each player in the network owns a unique cryptographic key, consisting of a public key noticeable to others and a private key known only to the proprietor. This guarantees the integrity and confidentiality of transactions, adding an additional layer of safety to economic transactions.

Decentralization: Traditional monetary structures often have a single point of failure containing a government or server that, if compromised, can result in catastrophic outcomes. Blockchain's decentralized structure gets rid of this susceptibility [33]. Even if one node is compromised, the veracity of the entire community remains complete, appreciably lowering the risk of safety breaches. Blockchain introduces a brand-new paradigm of transparency via offering a real-time, shared, and auditable document of transactions.
