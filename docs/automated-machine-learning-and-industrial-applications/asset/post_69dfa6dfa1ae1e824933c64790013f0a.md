## Plant Disease Identification Using Extended-EfficientNet Deep Learning Model in Smart Farming

## Abstract

The cornerstone of successful and precise prevention of plant disease in complex environments lies in the accurate identification of the specific disease affecting the plants. Advances in decision support, intelligent analysis, and planning are made possible by the digitization and data-driven detection of plant diseases brought about by the quick development of smart farming. This research introduces a mathematical model for diagnosing and recognizing plant diseases using deep learning, aiming to improve training efficiency, accuracy, and applicability. The leaves are first identified and located in complex situations using the region proposal network version 2 (RPNv2) algorithm. Next, the Chan–Vese Level-II (LCV) method is applied to retrieve the symptom features from images that were segmented based on the RPNv2 algorithm’s results. Proposing the use of Extended-EfficientNet (E-ENet) deep learning architecture, this study suggests its application in plant leaf disease classification, with an evaluation of its effectiveness compared to other current deep learning models. Models were skilled using data from PlantVillage. Training datasets comprising 55,448 and 61,486 images, respectively, were used to train all the models. The E-ENet architecture outperformed existing models in the original datasets, as evidenced by a 98.87% test accuracy and a 99.21% validation accuracy, according to the test dataset results. As a result, the chapter's deep learning algorithm is crucial for ecological preservation, agricultural productivity, and smart agriculture.

Keywords: Plant disease, smart farming, extended-efficientnet, classification, ecological preservation, agricultural productivity

## 10.1 Introduction

Indeed, it is an undeniable reality that artificial intelligence is progressively and steadfastly taking over tasks that were traditionally performed by humans in the contemporary era. Artificial intelligence (AI) is a rapidly developing area of technology that focuses to generate machines that can think like humans using algorithms that are trained to solve complex problems. AI refers to the emulation of human intellect in machines enabling them to replicate human thought processes and behaviors like problem solving and learning; within this domain, machine learning plays a crucial role allowing systems to recognize, comprehend, and analyze patterns in data. In the advanced realm of computer science, artificial intelligence stands as a major focal point for research and development. Due to its robust application in problems that are primarily beyond the capabilities of traditional computing structures and humans, as well as its rapid technological advancements, this technology is becoming increasingly prevalent rapidly [1].

Despite the availability of much improved technology, farmers still face many difficulties from planting to harvesting. The application of artificial intelligence approaches has empowered us to comprehend the intricate details of various situations allowing for the provision of optimal solutions tailored to specific issues. The growth of various AI methods is enabling innovative solutions to complex challenges. To address the issues facing agriculture today, a number of approaches have been put forth, such as database integration and decision support systems [2]. The proximity of artificial intelligence (AI) and agriculture, while initially unconventional, has gained relevance in recent times. Despite agriculture being a cornerstone of human civilization for millennia, the rapid evolution of AI, even in its simplest forms over the past few periods, has ushered in transformative fluctuations. Amid a surge in novel concepts across various sectors, agriculture has witnessed significant advancements in technology. With threats to the sustainability of our food system, such as population growth, climate change, and supply scarcity, the position of agricultural advancements becomes even more critical. The integration of AI addresses numerous challenges and alleviates drawbacks associated with conservative farming practices.

AI systems make independent crop management conceivable. By examining soil moisture content and climate data, algorithms can determine the ideal crop water requirements in real time when used along with Internet of Things (IoT) sensors. The goal of an autonomous crop irrigation system is to support sustainable farming methods while preserving water. Artificial intelligence plays a crucial role in identifying leaks in irrigation systems by leveraging algorithms that can discern patterns and anomalies in data indicative of potential leaks. Machine learning (ML) models can be trained to recognize specific signatures associated with leaks, such as deviations in water pressure or flow, enhancing the efficacy and accuracy of leak detection in irrigation systems. Initial identification is made conceivable by real-time monitoring and study, which helps to avoid both possible harm to crops and water loss.

For detecting areas with elevated water consumption, artificial intelligence (AI) integrates climate data with crop water requirements, enhancing water efficiency by automating leak detection and issuing timely alerts, thereby assisting farmers in conserving resources. The health and growth of crops can be significantly impacted by the incorrect combination of nutrients in the soil. With AI, farmers can quickly recognize these nutrients and determine how they affect crop yield allowing for suitable modifications to be made. While the accuracy of human assessment is limited, computer vision models can collect precise data by monitoring soil conditions. Then, using these data from plant science, yield predictions and crop health are made, along with any specific problems being highlighted. In actuality, AI has demonstrated a level of speed and accuracy unmatched by humans in tracking the stages of wheat growth and tomato ripeness.

In addition to measuring yield of crops and soil condition, computer vision could classify diseases and pests. This operates by perusing photos meant for insects, rot, mold, and other hazards to crop health utilizing artificial intelligence. When used in connection with alert mechanisms, this enables farmers to take prompt action to eradicate pests or isolate crops to stop the spread of illness. Apple black rot has been identified by AI with a more than 90% accuracy rate. It is equally accurate at looking at other kinds of insects such as flies, bees, moths, etc. Researchers first gathered pictures of the pests to generate the training dataset that was required for the algorithm to be trained effectively.
