Modern agricultural techniques are depending more and more on artificial intelligence-based systems that can identify a variety of disorders [18]. In the past decade, various conventional ML models have been introduced for the identification and categorization of plant diseases. Rumpf et al. employed Support Vector Machine (SVM) to explore the initial identification and categorization of illnesses in sugar beet using spectral plant guides [19]. Al-Hiary et al. employed a segmentation approach by utilizing k-means clustering on preprocessed features to identify and differentiate five distinct diseases in plant leaves. Following texture and color-based extraction of features, Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) were then used to classify those regions [20]. Revathi and Hemalatha suggested a technique to identify six distinct disease kinds that are present on cotton leaves. They propose an approach that uses Particle Swarm Optimization to choose characteristics from a feature vector that includes color, texture, and edge-based features acquired through image processing. The disease is subsequently classified using a Cross Information Gain Deep Forward Neural Network [21].

Mokhtar et al. conducted an investigation employing the Support Vector Machine (SVM) technique to detect and identify two distinct viruses, which cause disease and exhibit symptoms on tomato leaves [22]. In a separate study, Pantazi et al. utilized features extracted from the Local Binary Pattern method to identify three different vine leaf diseases through the Support Vector Machine (SVM) approach [23]. In a different study, Johannes et al. suggested using Naive Bayes classification system and image processing-based candidate hotspot detection for mobile-based initial stage diagnosis of three distinct wheat diseases. They tested their suggested approach in a real-world field setting using cellphones [24]. To automate the identification of plant diseases, Chen et al. have recently introduced a novel model known as the GMDH (Group Method of Data Handling) logistic algorithm [25]. Categorizing using machine learning involves a demanding process known as feature extraction, which directly influences the nature of classification.

DL architectures, which use methods to analyze raw data without the necessity for custom features, represent a performance breakthrough. This is made feasible by the enhanced capabilities and speeds of Central Processing Units (CPUs) with Graphical Processing Units (GPUs) [26]. Deep neural network styles, which have numerous layers and neurons for processing information, achieve exceptionally well at handling difficult tasks, like voice and image recognition, while handling massive amounts of data with ease.
