Furthermore, suppliers often do a poor job of outlining the benefits of novel technologies as well as their applications. Tech firms face a significant task in educating the public about the application of AI in agriculture. Despite the clear benefits of AI for sustainable farming, there is a need to inform farmers about its practical advantages and encourage its adoption in the agricultural sector. However, there are still certain obstacles to be addressed.

# 10.1.3 Existing Plant Disease Identification Methods

Stunted growth from plant diseases can have a negative impact on yields [3–5]. Worldwide, an estimated $20 billion or more is lost economically each year [6–8]. Because of regional variations that could make accurate identification more challenging, multiple circumstances present the greatest challenge to researchers [9, 10]. Traditional techniques often rely on experts, knowledge, and manuals [11], but they tend to be expensive, labor intensive, and time consuming. Moreover, they face challenges in accurately identifying objects [12]. For this reason, it appears that identifying plant diseases quickly and accurately is crucial for the sake of agriculture’s economy and ecology. Web technologies have advanced quickly, especially the availability of multimodality data from a diversity of sensors, such as sensor networks with the Internet of Things [13]. To tackle the issues mentioned earlier, this study introduces an innovative plant leaf identification model based on DL. The model employs an integrated deep learning algorithm to execute image segmentation, identification, and leaf retrieval seamlessly within its functionality. The first task is retrieving leaves, but in this complicated atmosphere, numerous considerations, like soil and illumination, make accurate identification difficult [14].

Achieving sustainable and efficient agriculture, while preventing unnecessary waste of resources, hinges on the initial stage and accurate diagnosis of plant diseases. Certain plant diseases lack obvious symptoms, so it is necessary to employ sophisticated analytical techniques in cases of such diseases. Nonetheless, most of the plant diseases exhibit outward signs, and the currently recognized method involves having a skilled plant pathologist visually inspect infected plant leaves to identify the disease [15, 16]. Expert and intelligent systems that can reliably and independently identify plant diseases provide agriculturalists with substantial benefits and are a great help in managing crop health. However, applying such a scheme with an easy-to-use smartphone application is also a commendable accomplishment for farmers lacking access to agronomic and phytopathological assistance facilities [17].
