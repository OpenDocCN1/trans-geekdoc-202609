
(a) A positive sign before the estimate means that the probability of event (`Y` = 1) increases, that is, the employee leaving the organization as the value of the feature increases.

(b) A negative sign before the estimate means that the probability of event (`Y` = 1) decreases as the value of the feature increases.

Let us read the model summary of `step_logistic_model` and interpret the outcome for features/levels which are significant (`p`-value < 0.05). With other features being held constant:

1. The probability of employees in Operation role (`EMPLOYEE_CATEGORY`), leaving the organization is higher compared to the probability of employees leaving the organization who are in Non Sales role (base category).

2. It is more likely that employees with an Under Graduate degree (`DEGREE_TYPE`) will leave the organization compared to those with a Graduate degree.

3. For every unit increase `PRIOR_WORK_EXP` the probability of an employee leaving the organization goes up. This suggests that experienced employees who join the organization come with good exposure and the demand for them in the market is also higher.

4. For every unit increase in salary the previous year (`CTC_PREVIOUS_YEAR`), the chance of an employee leaving the organization goes up. The model may not suggest that salary should not be increased for employees but merely reflects that higher salary is paid to an experienced employee and the chances of an experienced employee leaving the organization are more.

5. With every unit change in CTC from previous year to current year (`CHANGE_IN_CTC`), the likelihood of an employee leaving the organization goes up. This is related to the previous statement as well.

6. For every unit increase in number of days worked in last quarter (`DAYS_WORKED_IN_LAST_QUARTER`), the chance of an employee leaving the organization goes up. This may be due to extra work pressure, which impacts the employee’s work-life balance and prompts him to look for a better opportunity outside. On a lighter note, this conforms to the statement, ‘All work and no play makes Jack a dull boy.

7. With every unit increase in leave over last year (`LEAVE_OVER_LAST_YEAR`) and unit increase in leave taken in current year (`LEAVES_CURRENT_YEAR`), the chance of an employee leaving the organization goes down. This exactly ties back to the previous statement and confirms that work-life balance has an important role to play in keeping employees engaged.

**Model selection strategy** helps select features in the final model. Strategies like forward selection, backward elimination or both, works in such a way that with every iteration of model building stage the `AIC` or `deviance` reported decreases. The model converges when the `AIC` or `deviance` of the model does not reduce with addition or removal of the features.

8. With every unit increase in the number of female reportees to a manager (`RM_REPORTEE_FEMALE`), the probability of an employee leaving the organization goes down. It seems as if diversity in the team has some role to play in employee retention.

9. With every unit increase in age difference (`AGE_DIFFERENCE`) between manager and employee, the likelihood of an employee leaving the organization goes up. It may have something to do with generation gap and seems to conform to the hypothesis that ‘Employees leave their manager and not the organisation.
