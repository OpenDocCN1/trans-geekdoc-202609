## [6.1 Introduction](#ch14.xhtml#h1-044b)

Forecasting is a highly challenging task. Various statistical models and soft computing techniques have been proposed by researchers and professional analysts to obtain more accurate forecasts. Among the various forecasting models, artificial neural networks (`ANNs`) are the most accurate, fast and reliable techniques. `ANN` finds its application in almost all the fields whether it is social, economic, engineering, atmospheric sciences or field of applied mathematics. The most salient features of `ANN` are that it is able to handle non-linearity, seasonality, noise and robustness of data ([Khashei and Bijari, 2010](#ch14.xhtml#r210)). The motive behind `ANN` based computation is to design such mathematical algorithms that impersonate works in parallel to information processing as done by the human brain ([Basheer and Hajmeer, 2000](#ch14.xhtml#r190)). The most significant feature of `ANN` is that they have the capacity of approximating any continuous function with high precision ([Zhang and Min Qi, 2005](#ch14.xhtml#r234); [Chen et al. 2003](#ch14.xhtml#r195)). `ANN` has the unique ability of generalizing. The unseen part of a sample can be correctly inferred with `ANN`. No prior assumptions about the model are required while using `ANN` unlike linear/regression models. This data driven approach makes `ANN` a magnificent tool in forecasting real-life complex problems, thus making it an appropriate tool for forecasting stock markets, environment pollution, river flow forecasting, weather, agriculture and many more areas of science and engineering where forecasting plays a pivotal role. In forecasting the multi-layer feed forward method is the widely used paradigm of `ANN`. The present chapter is an attempt to provide researchers in the field of forecasting a detailed study about the development of different types of neural networks and the methodology of modelling and forecasting different types of time series. This chapter also deals with the review of supervised learning techniques using `ANNS` to solve forecasting problems related to environment, stock market, hydrology and agriculture.

## [6.2 Historical Developments in the Field of ANN](#ch14.xhtml#h1-045b)

The McCulloch-Pitts neuron model of computing element was designed by Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts in 1943. In this model, each element is assigned the weighted sum of the inputs and then the threshold logic operation is applied ([McCulloch and Pitts 1943](#ch14.xhtml#r213)). Several analytical computations were performed using combinations of these computing elements ([Figure 6.1](#ch14.xhtml#f45)). The major shortcoming of this model was that the weights were fixed, which makes the model incapable of performing learning operations.

![Figure 6.1 McCulloch-Pitts neuron model.](html/images/9780367720919_C006_001.jpg)

**Figure 6.1** McCulloch-Pitts neuron model.

A learning scheme for adjustable connection weights was proposed by Donald Hebbin 1949, which became a vital learning rule in the field of neural networks ([Hebb, 1949](#ch14.xhtml#r204)). An adaptive learning scheme was created by Marvin Minsky in 1954, which could adjust connection strengths automatically ([Minsky and Papert, 1969](#ch14.xhtml#r214)). The famous perceptron model was proposed by Rosenblatt in 1958. In it weights were adjusted by using the perceptron learning law ([Rosenblatt, 1958](#ch14.xhtml#r221)). The law is best suited for pattern classification problems using the multilayer perceptron ([Figure 6.2](#ch14.xhtml#f46)).

![Figure 6.2 Rosenblatt’s perceptron model of a neuron.](html/images/9780367720919_C006_002.jpg)

**Figure 6.2** Rosenblatt’s perceptron model of a neuron.

The major disadvantage of the perceptron model was the inability to adjust the weights in reorganizing the classification task. This was overcome by Minsky and Papert in 1969 ([Minsky and Papert, 1969](#ch14.xhtml#r214)). In 1960, the Adaline model for the Ae: adaptive element was proposed by Widrow and Hoff to adjust the weights ([Widrow and Hoff, 1960](#ch14.xhtml#r228)) ([Figure 6.3](#ch14.xhtml#f47)). Due to convergence of the algorithm, it was successfully applied in adaptive signal processing problems.

![Figure 6.3 Widrow’s Adaline model of a neuron.](html/images/9780367720919_C006_003.jpg)

**Figure 6.3** Widrow’s Adaline model of a neuron.

In 1964, Hu in his thesis used Widrow’s adaptive network to forecast weather. Due to a lack of training algorithms the research was limited until 1986 when the back-propagation algorithm was introduced by [Chauvin and Rumelhart (1995)](#ch14.xhtml#r193).
