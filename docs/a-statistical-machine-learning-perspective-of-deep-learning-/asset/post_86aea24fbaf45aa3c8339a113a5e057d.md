## Recap: Variational Inference

- Consider a generative model \(p_\theta(\boldsymbol{x}|\boldsymbol{z})\), and prior \(p(\boldsymbol{z})\)
  - Joint distribution: \(p_\theta(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{z}) = p_\theta(\boldsymbol{x}|\boldsymbol{z})p(\boldsymbol{z})\)
- Assume variational distribution \(q_\phi(\boldsymbol{z}|\boldsymbol{x})\)
- Objective: Maximize lower bound for log likelihood

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log p(\boldsymbol{x})
&= \mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(\boldsymbol{z}|\boldsymbol{x}) \| p_\theta(\boldsymbol{z}|\boldsymbol{x})\right)
 + \int_{\boldsymbol{z}} q_\phi(\boldsymbol{z}|\boldsymbol{x}) \log\frac{p_\theta(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{z})}{q_\phi(\boldsymbol{z}|\boldsymbol{x})} \\
&\ge \int_{\boldsymbol{z}} q_\phi(\boldsymbol{z}|\boldsymbol{x}) \log\frac{p_\theta(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{z})}{q_\phi(\boldsymbol{z}|\boldsymbol{x})}
 := \mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\phi};\boldsymbol{x})
\end{aligned}
\]

- Equivalently, minimize free energy

\[
F(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\phi};\boldsymbol{x}) = -\log p(\boldsymbol{x}) + \mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(\boldsymbol{z}|\boldsymbol{x}) \| p_\theta(\boldsymbol{z}|\boldsymbol{x})\right)
\]

## Wake Sleep Algorithm

- [Hinton et al., Science 1995]
- Train a separate inference model along with the generative model
  - Generally applicable to a wide range of generative models, e.g., Helmholtz machines
- Consider a generative model \(p_\theta(x|z)\) and prior \(p(z)\)
  - Joint distribution \(p_\theta(x,z) = p_\theta(x|z)p(z)\)
  - E.g., multi-layer belief nets
- Inference model \(q_\phi(z|x)\)
- Maximize data log-likelihood with two steps of loss relaxation:
  - Maximize the variational lower bound of log-likelihood, or equivalently, minimize the free energy

    \[
    F(\theta, \phi; x) = -\log p(x) + \mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(z|x) \| p_\theta(z|x)\right)
    \]

  - Minimize a different objective (reversed KLD) wrt \(\phi\) to ease the optimization
    - Disconnected from the original variational lower bound loss

      \[
      F'(\theta, \phi; x) = -\log p(x) + \mathrm{KL}\left(p_\theta(z|x) \| q_\phi(z|x)\right)
      \]

- Free energy:

  \[
  F(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \boldsymbol{\phi}; \boldsymbol{x}) = -\log p(\boldsymbol{x}) + \mathrm{KL}\left(q_{\phi}(\boldsymbol{z}|\boldsymbol{x}) \| p_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{z}|\boldsymbol{x})\right)
  \]

- Minimize the free energy wrt \(\boldsymbol{\theta}\) of \(p_{\theta}\) \(\rightarrow\) *wake phase*

  \[
  \max_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \mathbb{E}_{q_{\phi}(\boldsymbol{z}|\boldsymbol{x})} \left[\log p_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{x}, \boldsymbol{z})\right]
  \]

  - Get samples from \(q_\phi(z|x)\) through inference on hidden variables
  - Use the samples as targets for updating the generative model \(p_\theta(z|x)\)
  - Correspond to the variational M step

![](img/cb490ed30bca4eda5fbba81c52a2c173_110_0.png)

- WS works around the difficulties with the sleep phase approximation
- Minimize the following objective \(\rightarrow\) sleep phase

  \[
  F'(\theta, \phi; x) = -\log p(x) + \mathrm{KL}\left(p_\theta(z|x) \| q_\phi(z|x)\right)
  \]

  \[
  \max_{\phi} \mathbb{E}_{p_\theta(z,x)} \left[\log q_\phi(z|x)\right]
  \]

  - “Dreaming” up samples from \(p_\theta(x|z)\) through top-down pass
  - Use the samples as targets for updating the inference model
- (Recent approaches other than sleep phase are developed to reduce the variance of gradient estimate: slides later)

### Wake sleep

- Parametrized inference model \(q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})\)
  - Wake phase:
    - Minimize \(\mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}) \| p_\theta(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})\right)\) wrt \(\theta\)
    - \(\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})}\left[\nabla_\theta \log p_\theta(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})\right]\)
  - Sleep phase:
    - Minimize \(\mathrm{KL}\left(p_\theta(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}) \| q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})\right)\) wrt \(\phi\)
    - \(\mathbb{E}_{p_\theta(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})}\left[\nabla_\phi \log q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})\right]\)
    - Low variance
    - Learning with generated samples of \(\mathbf{x}\)
- Two objectives, not guaranteed to converge

### Variational EM

- Variational distribution \(q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})\)
  - Variational M step:
    - Minimize \(\mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}) \| p_\theta(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})\right)\) wrt \(\theta\)
    - \(\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})}\left[\nabla_\theta \log p_\theta(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})\right]\)
  - Variational E step:
    - Minimize \(\mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}) \| p_\theta(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})\right)\) wrt \(\phi\)
    - \(q_\phi^* \propto \exp[\log p_\theta]\) if closed-form is available
    - \(\nabla_\phi \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}\left[\log p_\theta(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})\right]\)
      - Need variance reduction in practice
    - Learning with real data \(\mathbf{x}\)
- Single objective, guaranteed to converge

## Reparameterized gradient

- Optimize \(\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \boldsymbol{\phi}; x)\) wrt \(\boldsymbol{\phi}\) of \(q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(z\mid x)\)
  - Recap: gradient estimate with log-derivative trick:

    \[
    \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\mathbb{E}_{q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}\left[\log p_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(x,z)\right]
    = \mathbb{E}_{q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}\left[\log p_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(x,z)\,\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\log q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(z\mid x)\right]
    \]

- High variance:

  \[
  \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\mathbb{E}_{q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}\left[\log p_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(x,z)\right]
  \approx \mathbb{E}_{z_i\sim q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}\left[\log p_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(x,z_i)\,\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\log q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(z_i\mid x)\right]
  \]

  - The scale factor \(\log p_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(x,z_i)\) can have arbitrarily large magnitude

- Gradient estimate with the *reparameterization trick*

  \[
  z \sim q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(z\mid x) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z = g_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\epsilon,x), \qquad \epsilon \sim p(\epsilon)
  \]

  \[
  \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\mathbb{E}_{q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(z\mid x)}\left[\log p_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(x,z)\right]
  = \mathbb{E}_{\epsilon\sim p(\epsilon)}\left[\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\log p_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\left(x,z_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\epsilon)\right)\right]
  \]

- (Empirically) lower variance of the gradient estimate
- E.g., \(z\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}(x), L(x)L(x)^T) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1),\; z=\boldsymbol{\mu}(x)+L(x)\epsilon\)
