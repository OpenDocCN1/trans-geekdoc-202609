In all applications, the matrix \( A \) is not full rank and not square either, and the projections are not consistent due to noise. If the matrix \( A \) is rank deficient, you could use the singular value decomposition (SVD) to find a pseudo-inverse.

The SVD technique is a powerful and stable method to find a generalized inverse and diagnose the system condition. Now we use SVD to find the least-squares solution for \( AX = P \) as follows.

Assume that matrix \( A \) has \( m \) rows and \( n \) columns and is denoted as \( A_{m \times n} \). Using SVD, the matrix \( A_{m \times n} \) can be decomposed into

\( A_{m \times n} = U_{m \times m} \Sigma V^T_{n \times n}, \quad \text{(6.15)} \)

where

\( V^T V = I_{n \times n}, \quad \text{(6.16)} \)

\( U^T U = I_{m \times m}, \quad \text{(6.17)} \)

and

\( \sum_{m \times n} = \begin{bmatrix} \text{diag}\{ \sigma_i \} & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix} \quad \text{(6.18)} \)

with the singular values arranged in the descending order:

\( \sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \cdots \ge \sigma_i \ge \cdots \ge 0. \quad \text{(6.19)} \)

A generalized inverse (or pseudo-inverse) is defined as

\( A^+ = V \Sigma^+ U^T, \quad \text{(6.20)} \)

where

\( \Sigma^+_{n \times m} = \begin{bmatrix} D_r & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}, \quad \text{(6.21)} \)

and the diagonal matrix \( D_r \) with a cutoff index \( r \) is defined as

\( D_r = \text{diag}\left\{ \frac{1}{\sigma_1}, \frac{1}{\sigma_2}, \dots, \frac{1}{\sigma_r}, 0, \dots, 0 \right\}. \quad \text{(6.22)} \)

The reconstructed image is given as

\( X = A^+ P = V \Sigma^+ U^T P. \quad \text{(6.23)} \)
