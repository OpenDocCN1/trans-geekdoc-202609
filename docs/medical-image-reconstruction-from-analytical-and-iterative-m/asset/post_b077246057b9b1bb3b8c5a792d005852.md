
Therefore,

\(\gamma = 2 \sin^{-1} \left( \frac{\sin \psi}{\sqrt{1 - \left( \frac{\omega_v \sin \theta}{\sqrt{\omega_u^2 + \omega_v^2}} \right)^2}} \right) = 2 \sin^{-1} \left( \frac{\sqrt{\omega_u^2 + \omega_v^2} \sin \psi}{\sqrt{\omega_u^2 + \omega_v^2 - \omega_v^2 \sin^2 \theta}} \right) = 2 \sin^{-1} \left( \frac{\sqrt{\omega_u^2 + \omega_v^2} \sin \psi}{\sqrt{\omega_u^2 + \omega_v^2 \cos^2 \theta}} \right), \quad (5.24)\)

which readily leads to (5.13).

## 5.4.3 Three-dimensional Radon inversion formula (FBP algorithm)

The 3D Radon inversion formula can only be applied to 3D plane-integral data:

\(f(x,y,z) = \frac{-1}{8\pi^2} \int\int_{4\pi} \frac{\partial^2 p(s, \vec{\theta})}{\partial s^2} \Big|_{s=\vec{x}\cdot\vec{\theta}} \sin \theta \, d\theta \, d\phi, \quad (5.25)\)

where \(\vec{\theta} = (\sin \theta \cos \phi, \sin \theta \sin \phi, \cos \theta)\) and \(\vec{x} = (x, y, z)\). The coordinate systems are shown in Figure 5.23. If we backproject over a \(2\pi\) solid angle, then \(-1/(8\pi^2)\) should be replaced by \(-1/(4\pi^2)\).

## 5.4.4 Three-dimensional backprojection-then-filtering algorithm for Radon data

Let the backprojected image be

b(x,y,z) = \iint_{2\pi} p(s, \overline{\theta}) \bigg|_{s=x \cdot \overline{\theta}} \sin\theta d\theta d\phi.

For the 3D Radon case, the Fourier transform of the projection/backprojection PSF is \(1/(\omega_x^2 + \omega_y^2 + \omega_z^2)\). Therefore, the Fourier transform of the image \(f(x, y, z)\) can be obtained as

F(\omega_x, \omega_y, \omega_z) = B(\omega_x, \omega_y, \omega_z) \times (\omega_x^2 + \omega_y^2 + \omega_z^2).

In the spatial domain, the backprojection-then-filtering algorithm for Radon data can be expressed as

f(x,y,z) = \Delta b(x,y,z) = \frac{\partial^2 b(x,y,z)}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 b(x,y,z)}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2 b(x,y,z)}{\partial z^2},

where \(\Delta\) is the Laplacian operator.

## 5.4.5 Feldkamp's algorithm

First, let us write down the fan-beam FBP reconstruction algorithm for the flat detector and express the image in polar coordinates (see Figure 5.24):

f(r, \varphi) = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^{2\pi} \left(\frac{D}{D-s}\right)^2 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{D}{\sqrt{D^2+l^2}} g(l, \beta) h(l'-l) dl d\beta,

where \(h(l)\) is the convolution kernel of the ramp filter, \(D\) is the focal length, \(g(l, \beta)\) is the fan-beam projection, \(l\) is the linear coordinate on the detector, \(s = r \sin(\varphi - \beta)\), and \(l' = \frac{D r \cos(\varphi - \beta)}{D - r \sin(\varphi - \beta)}\). In this formula, \(D/\sqrt{D^2+l^2}\) is the cosine of the incidence angle. When this algorithm is implemented, we first multiply the projections by this cosine function. Then we apply the ramp filter to the pre-scaled data. Finally, we perform the fan-beam backprojection with a distance-dependent weighting [D/(D-s)]\(^2\), where s is the distance from the reconstruction point to the virtual detector, which is placed at the center of rotation for convenience.

Feldkamp's algorithm is almost the same as this fan-beam algorithm, except that the backprojection is a cone-beam backprojection. The ramp-filtering is performed in the row-by-row fashion. There is no filtering performed in the axial direction. Let the axial direction be the z-direction (see Figure 5.25), then

\( f(r, \phi, z) = \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{2\pi} \left( \frac{D}{D-s} \right)^2 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{D}{\sqrt{D^2 + l^2 + \tilde{z}^2}} g(l, \tilde{z}, \beta) h(l' - l) dl d\beta. \) (5.30)

In this formula, g(l, \(\tilde{z}\), \(\beta\)) is the cone-beam projection, D/\(\sqrt{D^2 + l^2 + \tilde{z}^2}\) is the cosine of the incidence angle, and \(\tilde{z}\) and l' are defined in Figure 5.25.

## 5.4.6 Tuy's relationship

Tuy published a paper in 1983. In this paper, he established a relationship between the cone-beam data and the original image, which plays a similar role to the central slice theorem. Let us derive this relationship in the section.

The object to be imaged is f: Let the cone-beam focal-point trajectory be denoted by a vector \(\vec{\Phi}\) and let \(\vec{a}\) be the unit vector, indicating the direction of a projection ray. Therefore, the cone-beam data can be expressed by the following expression:

![](img/dd555effd38151c67af30d84800c554a_128_0.png)

Fig. 5.25: The coordinate system for Feldkamp's cone-beam algorithm.

$g(\bar{\Phi}, \bar{\alpha}) = \int_0^\infty f(\bar{\Phi} + t\bar{\alpha}) dt, ||\bar{\alpha}|| = 1.$

We now replace the unit vector $\bar{a}$ by a general 3D vector $\bar{x}$, and the above 2D projection becomes an extended 3D function:

$g(\bar{\Phi}, \bar{x}) = \int_0^\infty f(\bar{\Phi} + t\bar{x}) dt.$

Taking the 3D Fourier transform of this function with respect to $\bar{x}$ and using notation $\bar{\beta}$ as the frequency domain variable, we have

$G(\bar{\Phi}, \bar{\beta}) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(\bar{\Phi}, \bar{x}) e^{-2\pi i x \cdot \bar{\beta}} d\bar{x} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{0}^{\infty} f(\bar{\Phi}, t\bar{x}) e^{-2\pi i x \cdot \bar{\beta}} dt d\bar{x}.$

Let $\bar{y} = \bar{\Phi} + tx; dy = |t|^3 d\bar{x}$. The above expression becomes

$G(\bar{\Phi}, \bar{\beta}) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{0}^{\infty} f(\bar{y}) e^{\frac{-2\pi i}{t^3} (\bar{y} - \bar{\Phi}) \cdot \bar{\beta}} \frac{1}{|t|^3} dt d\bar{y}.$

Let s = 1/t; ds = (1/t^2) dt. We then have

G\left(\overline{\Phi}, \overline{\beta}\right) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(\overline{y}) e^{-2\pi i s(\overline{y}-\overline{\Phi})\cdot\overline{\beta}} |s|^3 \frac{1}{s^2} d\overline{y} ds, \tag{5.35}

G\left(\overline{\Phi}, \overline{\beta}\right) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \left( \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(\overline{y}) e^{-2\pi i \overline{y}\cdot(\overline{s}\beta)} d\overline{y} \right) |s| e^{2\pi i s(\overline{\Phi}\cdot\overline{\beta})} ds. \tag{5.36}

Recognizing that the inner triple integral is the 3D Fourier transform of f, that is,

F(s) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(\overline{y}) e^{-2\pi i \overline{y}\cdot(\overline{s}\beta)} d\overline{y}, \tag{5.37}

we have

G\left(\overline{\Phi}, \overline{\beta}\right) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} F\left(\overline{s}\beta\right) |s| e^{2\pi i s(\overline{\Phi}\cdot\overline{\beta})} ds. \tag{5.38}

Next, we change the limit from [0, \infty) to (-\infty, \infty) and obtain

G\left(\overline{\Phi}, \overline{\beta}\right) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} F\left(s\overline{\beta}\right) |s| e^{2\pi i s(\overline{\Phi}\cdot\overline{\beta})} ds + \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} F\left(s\overline{\beta}\right) \frac{is}{i} e^{2\pi i s(\overline{\Phi}\cdot\overline{\beta})} ds. \tag{5.39}

Using 3D Radon transform's central slice theorem, F(s\overline{\beta}) is the Fourier transform of the plane integral of the original image f in the direction \overline{\beta}. The factor |s| is the ramp filter in the Fourier domain, and the factor (is) corresponds to the derivative in the spatial domain. Each of the two terms at the right-hand side of the above expression is in the form of an inverse Fourier transform, which is, in fact, a convolution.

Therefore,

G\left(\overline{\Phi}, \overline{\beta}\right) = p_{\overline{\beta}}^{-}(t) * h(t) - ip_{\overline{\beta}}^{-}(t) * \delta'(t), \tag{5.40}

where i = \sqrt{-1}, t = \overline{\Phi} \cdot \overline{\beta}, h(t) is the ramp-filter convolution kernel

h(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |s| e^{2\pi i st} ds \tag{5.41}

and the Radon transform of the original image f is defined as

p_{\overline{\beta}}(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(\overline{x}) \delta\left(\overline{x} \cdot \overline{\beta} - t\right) d\overline{x}. \tag{5.42}

The left-hand side of eq. (5.40) is related to the cone-beam projections, and the right-hand side is related to the plane integral of the original image.
