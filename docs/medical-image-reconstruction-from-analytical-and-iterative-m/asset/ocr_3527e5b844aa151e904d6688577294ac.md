\( F(k_x, k_y) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x,y)e^{-i2\pi[x(yG_x t)+y(yG_y T)]}dxdy, \quad (7.20) \)

with \( k_x = yG_x t \) and \( k_y = yG_y T \). When we sample the time signal over time \( t \), we get the samples of the x-direction frequencies, \( k_x \). When we repeat the scan with a different value of \( G_y \), we get the samples of the y-direction frequencies, \( k_y \). For this reason, people often call the MRI signal space the k-space (see Figure 7.17), which is the Fourier space. During data acquisition, the k-space is filled out one line at a time according to \( k_x = yG_x t \) and \( k_y = yG_y T \).

Finally, we will consider a polar k-space scanning strategy in which the x-gradient and the y-gradient are turned on and off simultaneously (see Figure 7.18). In this case, we do not have the phase-encoding step; we only have the readout gradient, which is determined by both the x- and y-gradients.
