
## Chapter 7

### Identity Theft—Federal

Identity theft laws in the United States have wide variation across all fifty states and at the federal level. We will start with an overview of the federal law currently in place and then proceed to cover state laws and the commonalities and differences between them.

The federal law in place for the United States ([18 U.S.C. § 1028](https://www.westlaw.com/Document/N2F5DFDC0CF3B11DAADF7EE7F81C867F0/View/FullText.html?transitionType=Default&contextData=(sc.Default)&VR=3.0&RS=da3.0)) covers eight different scenarios, using various requisite intents and acts. All of these crimes are classified as “fraud and related activity in connection with identification documents.” The first, [section 1028(a)(1)](https://www.westlaw.com/Document/N2F5DFDC0CF3B11DAADF7EE7F81C867F0/View/FullText.html?transitionType=Default&contextData=(sc.Default)&VR=3.0&RS=da3.0), prohibits knowingly producing an identification document. The second punishes transferring identification documents knowing they are stolen or made unlawfully. Third, it is a crime to possess with the intent to unlawfully use or transfer five or more identification documents (other than one’s own documents or with the permission of the owner). Fourth, under [section 1028(a)(4)](https://www.westlaw.com/Document/N2F5DFDC0CF3B11DAADF7EE7F81C867F0/View/FullText.html?transitionType=Default&contextData=(sc.Default)&VR=3.0&RS=da3.0), it is a crime to knowingly possess an identification document (with the same exceptions as above) to defraud the United States.

The fifth violation, [section 1028(a)(5)](https://www.westlaw.com/Document/N2F5DFDC0CF3B11DAADF7EE7F81C867F0/View/FullText.html?transitionType=Default&contextData=(sc.Default)&VR=3.0&RS=da3.0), does not necessarily fall into the same category as the other seven listed in the federal law. It covers when one has, transfers, or makes a “document-making implement or authentication feature” with the intent it will be used to make a false document or another document-making implement.

[Section 1028(a)(6)](https://www.westlaw.com/Document/N2F5DFDC0CF3B11DAADF7EE7F81C867F0/View/FullText.html?transitionType=Default&contextData=(sc.Default)&VR=3.0&RS=da3.0) prohibits possession of an identification document or authentication feature of the United States knowing it was stolen or unlawfully produced. The seventh is knowingly transferring, possessing, or using a means of identification belonging to another person with the intent to commit any federal offense or a felony of any state. Finally, one cannot knowingly traffic in false or actual authentication features to use on false identification documents under [section 1028(a)(8)](https://www.westlaw.com/Document/N2F5DFDC0CF3B11DAADF7EE7F81C867F0/View/FullText.html?transitionType=Default&contextData=(sc.Default)&VR=3.0&RS=da3.0). The statute defines authentication features to include things like watermarks.

The federal identity theft statute has five verbs associated with the offenses: produce, transfer, possess, use, and traffic. Most of the violations contained in [Section 1028(a)](https://www.westlaw.com/Document/N2F5DFDC0CF3B11DAADF7EE7F81C867F0/View/FullText.html?transitionType=Default&contextData=(sc.Default)&VR=3.0&RS=da3.0) require something more than mere possession. [Section 1028(a)(3)](https://www.westlaw.com/Document/N2F5DFDC0CF3B11DAADF7EE7F81C867F0/View/FullText.html?transitionType=Default&contextData=(sc.Default)&VR=3.0&RS=da3.0) prohibits possession of five or more identification documents with the intent to use or transfer the documents unlawfully. [Section 1028(a)(4)](https://www.westlaw.com/Document/N2F5DFDC0CF3B11DAADF7EE7F81C867F0/View/FullText.html?transitionType=Default&contextData=(sc.Default)&VR=3.0&RS=da3.0) prohibits possession with the intent to defraud the United States. [Section 1028(a)(5)](https://www.westlaw.com/Document/N2F5DFDC0CF3B11DAADF7EE7F81C867F0/View/FullText.html?transitionType=Default&contextData=(sc.Default)&VR=3.0&RS=da3.0) prohibits possessing document-making tools or authentication feature with the intent that these items contribute to the production of false identification documents. [Section 1028(a)(7)](https://www.westlaw.com/Document/N2F5DFDC0CF3B11DAADF7EE7F81C867F0/View/FullText.html?transitionType=Default&contextData=(sc.Default)&VR=3.0&RS=da3.0) prohibits possessing identification documents belonging to another person with the intent to commit a crime. The exception to this is [Section 1028(a)(6)](https://www.westlaw.com/Document/N2F5DFDC0CF3B11DAADF7EE7F81C867F0/View/FullText.html?transitionType=Default&contextData=(sc.Default)&VR=3.0&RS=da3.0), which prohibits the possession of identification documents issued by the federal government when the possessor knows that the document was stolen or produced without lawful authority.
