
## 1.4.1. Lavrov's classification of knowledge

**Procedural (algorithmic) knowledge.** These include various skills, technologies, procedures, algorithms, their software implementations, and typical problem-solving approaches—that is, those forms of knowledge that are almost always used explicitly for the computational solution of any problem.

**Factual knowledge.** These are the most diverse quantitative and qualitative characteristics of certain objects, that is, data. For example, various data in databases, constants in programs, etc.

Of course, there is no impenetrable boundary between the types of knowledge (see also paragraph 0). In addition, it sometimes happens that knowledge is difficult to assign to a specific type. For example, algorithms for calculating mathematical functions (`sin`, `cos`, etc.) are represented as a set of expansion coefficients in special polynomials for the sake of efficiency. These coefficients are of course data, i.e. factual knowledge, but are used as algorithmic knowledge. Nevertheless, this knowledge classification is very useful and effective when it comes to applied artificial intelligence systems, because you can focus on the issues of conceptual knowledge representation important for AI, using the methods of representation of algorithmic and factographic knowledge as a ready-made foundation.

**Lavrov Sviatoslav Sergeyevich** (1923–2004), Russian scientist, corresponding member of the Russian Academy of Sciences. Author of works on mechanics, automatic control, computer mathematics and programming. Winner of the Lenin Prize in 1957. Lavrov's scientific biography is in a sense unique. At a very young age he became the founder of missile and space ballistics in the USSR and an undeniable authority on controlled flight dynamics and automatic piloting. The advent of digital computing technology caused a sharp turn in S.S. Lavrov's activities and within a few years made him a programming classic in the USSR.

Lavrov's classification of knowledge is accepted as fundamental in this book.

### 1.4.2. Classification according to the degree of use of different types of knowledge

Depending on what types of knowledge are used and how it is done, artificial intelligence systems can be classified in different ways.

1.  **Database (DB).** If a software system uses only factual knowledge, then such a software system is called a database (DB) and is not usually considered artificial intelligence under modern conditions.[^22]
2.  **Application software package.** If a software system mainly uses algorithmic knowledge, then such a software system is usually referred to as an application software package. The assignment of such a software package to an AI system is usually stated explicitly. If an application package is used simply as a library of procedures, it is not classified as an artificially intelligent system. For example, if it uses AI methods, if the program to solve a specific problem is created not by the user, but by an automatic planner, then such a package of application programs can be considered an artificial intelligence system.
3.  **Artificially intelligent system.** If conceptual knowledge is used in some form in a software system, then such a system is said to be artificially intelligent.

Note that advanced application packages usually include or actively use an external database, and AI application systems almost always use both software packages and databases (Fig. 11). For example, a developed chess program has a huge database of openings and endgames, a special software package for working with positions and moves during a game, and a set of strategic move-selection rules related to conceptual knowledge.

### 1.4.3. Classification according to the form of knowledge representation

When the need is recognized and felt to share knowledge about all three types, a way must be chosen to represent them in a computer. The powerful mechanisms that a person uses to represent knowledge: natural languages, mathematics, visual images, are suitable for representing all types of knowledge, but are unsuitable for implementation in a computer. More precisely, on the contrary, modern computers are not adapted to use these mechanisms—computers are too primitive and unintelligent.

There is therefore a need to invent such types of knowledge representation that, on the one hand, enable the representation of knowledge of all kinds and, on the other hand, would be feasible in modern computers.

Mechanisms for representing factual and algorithmic knowledge in computers are well understood and established. For factual knowledge, these are database management systems (DBMS), in modern conditions mostly relational. For algorithmic knowledge, these are libraries of procedures, mostly object-oriented under modern conditions, i.e. libraries of types and classes.

The classification according to the form of knowledge representation thus first considers the representation of conceptual knowledge.

Many different mechanisms for representing conceptual knowledge are proposed. Currently, the following three are most commonly used (Fig. 12).

*   **Rules or products (Production systems).** Rule-based artificial intelligence systems are currently the most widespread. From a mathematical point of view, such systems use computability models that approximate normal Markov algorithms. Production systems are covered in detail in Chapters 2 and 3 of this book.
*   **Formulas of Logical Calculus.** Artificial intelligence systems based on logical formulas are currently the second most common. From a mathematical point of view, such systems use calculi close to the first-order predicate calculus and in some cases to Church's `λ`-calculus. The representation of knowledge with formulas of logical calculus is considered in the fourth chapter.
*   **Frames and Semantic Networks.** Now this mechanism is used relatively rarely, although in the recent past it was not. From a mathematical point of view, such systems use the apparatus of graph theory.

All three common mechanisms are comparable in terms of expressiveness and other general mathematical properties.

The preference for one or another knowledge representation mechanism is often given on the basis of considerations of familiarity, ease of programming, familiarity with the theory of developers of applied systems. It is particularly important how compact the representation of knowledge about a specific task is (see paragraph).

### 1.4.4. Classification by type of response when solving problems

When solving a specific problem, the applied AI system receives knowledge in one form or another at the input and gives an answer at the output, which also has a certain form and represents (new) knowledge. According to the introduced classification of knowledge types, systems of artificial intelligence can be classified according to the level of the given answer.

0.  **Logical answer** (yes or no); not very convenient to use, rarely used now.
1.  **Factual answer** (answer-fact); If a question is answered specifically, such systems are often referred to as information systems. Currently the most common class of systems.
