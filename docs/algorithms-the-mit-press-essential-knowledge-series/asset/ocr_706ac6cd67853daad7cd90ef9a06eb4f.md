7. To fold paper n times, the paper must be large enough. If you fold it always
along the same dimension, you will need a long sheet of paper. The length is

by the formula $L = \frac{\pi}{6}(2+4)(2-1)$, where $t$ is the paper's thickness
and $n$ is the number of folds. If you fold a square in alternate
directions, then the width of the square is $W = \pi t^{(3/2^{n-1})}$. The reason
why the formulas are more complicated than simple powers of two is that
every time you fold the paper, you lose some of it as it curves along the
edge of the fold; it's from calculating these curves that $\pi$ enters the picture
in these formulas. The formulas were found in 2002 by Britney Crystal-
livan, a junior in high school. She went on to demonstrate that a 1,200
meters–long sheet of toilet paper could be folded in half 12 times. For a nice-
troduction to the power of powers (including this example), see Strogatz 2012,
chapter 11.
