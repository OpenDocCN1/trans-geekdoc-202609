Once you know about it, the solution is straightforward. You do not calculate the middle as (m + n) / 2 but rather m + (n - m) / 2. The result is the same, but no overflow occurs. In retrospect it seems simple. In hindsight, though, everybody is a prophet.

We are interested in algorithms, not programming, here, but let the author share a bit of advice for those who write or want to write computer programs. Do not despair if you find yourself wretched poring over a line of code that does not not do what you think it should do. Do not be dismayed if the following day you realize that, indeed, the bug was before your eyes all the time. How could you have failed to see it? You are not unique. It happens to all; it happens to the best.
