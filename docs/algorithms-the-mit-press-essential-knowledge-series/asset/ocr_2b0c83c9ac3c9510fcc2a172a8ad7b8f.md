In an artificial neuron, we approximate the plasticity of synapses, their excitatory or inhibitory role, through weights we apply to the inputs. In our model artificial neuron, we have inputs, $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$. To each one of them we apply a weight, $w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n$. Each weight is multiplied by the corresponding input. That final input received by a neuron is the sum of the products: $w_1x_1 + w_2x_2 + \dots + w_nx_n$. To this weighted input we add a bias $b$, which you can think of as the propensity the neuron has to fire; the higher the bias, the more likely it is to be activated, while a negative bias added to the weighted input will actually inhibit the neuron from firing.

The weights and bias are the parameters of the neuron because they influence their behavior. As the output of a biological neuron depends on its inputs, so the output of an artificial neuron depends on the input it gets. This happens by feeding the input into a special activation function, the result of which is the neuron's output.
