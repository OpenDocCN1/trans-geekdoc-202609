
# Let’s look at the prediction of body height

It is a component of the diagnosis. We collected thousands of smartphone scans of children plus the manual measurement of the body height. We used the data to train machine learning algorithms with a focus on the mean absolute error. It is a critical metric expressing the difference between the prediction and the actual height. With traditional manual measurement as a gold standard, the mean absolute error of 1.2 cm (0.47 in). Our challenge is being more accurate while also being fully reliable with no wrong prediction (false negative) for any child.

# For the machine learning model

We examined a variety of approaches for utilizing the data from the point cloud. Neural network architectures showed some promise. With a plain convolutional neural network using depth maps, we achieved a mean absolute error of 2 cm or better. Our research indicates that by turning to a multi-artifact CNN with Late Fusion (Karpathy et al 2014) we may reduce the mean absolute error to 1cm or better.

# For body weight and the middle-upper arm circumference (MUAC)

Our approach is similar. We know that the mean absolute error for traditional body weight measurement is 210 grams, and for MUAC is 3.3 mm. We train a neural network to predict body weight and MUAC.

Adding artificial intelligence to machine vision Machine vision typically provides image-based automatic inspection, sorting, and robot guidance. In manufacturing, such a system usually is based on an operator station, camera units, lighting, and a labeling device. Machine vision is about extracting information from images for automated action.

Machine vision is superior to manual inspection and typically also cheaper. It addresses the perennial issue of scrap and waste in manufacturing. Yet, it does not sufficiently address the opportunity of generating large amounts of data and learning from them. If you add computer vision algorithms for image processing to the mix, your system improves continually with higher accuracy. Of course, this requires investment in real-time data management. Yet, among the immediate benefits are data-driven reports and insights, and the standardization of quality inspection.

This simplified representation of adding artificial intelligence to machine vision makes the difference clear between the rigid approach of machine vision and the more comprehensive and flexible model you achieve with artificial intelligence. Use cases in manufacturing industries Thorough quality inspection in a large manufacturing organization is very data-intensive. For example, General Motors deploys 2000+ smart cameras, for 2+ million inspections per day, resulting in more than 1 million dataset uploads. High data volumes have challenges, e.g. you need improved image compression for storage without compromising data quality and model accuracy.
