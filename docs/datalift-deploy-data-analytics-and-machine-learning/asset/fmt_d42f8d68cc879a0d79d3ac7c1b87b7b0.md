
# Automated credit risk assessment

## How Klarna scales online shopping transactions

Vaibhav Singh, Senior Data Science Manager, Klarna  
Moderated by Corrie Bartelheimer

For a leading online payment solutions provider, automated decision-making needs to be fast and accurate. Klarna uses Machine Learning models to provide a one-click solution at the point of sale that effectively takes the risk away from both the seller and the buyer.

[Klarna](https://www.klarna.com/) is a payments solution provider processing >1m online payments daily for 200k+ merchants. On peak days, up to 2m shopping transactions are processed with three principal payment options: Pay now or later (e.g., after delivery) or slice it. Consumers have a risk-free purchase because of the Klarna buyer protection policy. Merchants have a risk-free sale because Klarna takes on the fraud and credit risk. Klarna is now the most highly valued fintech in Europe at >$10bn.

## Credit risk and fraud risk assessment at Klarna

Klarna facilitates a one-click checkout and payment process and takes on the credit risk. The risk is the probability of default weighted by exposure (size of the transaction plus any interest) by the likelihood of payment or recovery versus default. We analyze each determinant to minimize the aggregate risk.

The supervised modeling of credit risk has challenges. Most important is getting the targets right. Loans mature, so we need at least six months of data. Moreover, the prediction must be specific for each credit product and each customer over the loan’s lifecycle, including a customer switching between products. Next, information from credit scoring partners is not normalized. For fraud risk, Klarna distinguishes between 1st party (misrepresentation or false information by the buyer), 2nd party (money mules knowingly participating), and 3rd party (stolen identity) fraud.

## Architecture for automated decisions in real-time

The key elements are the data pipelines, which may be done on one or several machines with various tools. Data Scientists at Klarna have the freedom to choose a preferred setup. A typical flow might look as follows: You extract the data sets, create features on top of it, split the data for training, look at your models’ performance, validate, and recalibrate a model if needed. Once models are deployed, we track for model drift. Whenever that happens, we adjust.

“For monitoring model drift, we must figure out which metrics to have in place, e.g., mean model scores so that the alarm sounds at the right time.” Vaibhav Singh, Senior Data Science Manager

“It is most interesting how Klarna deploys machine learning in such a highly regulated area and the challenges that come with that. For certain applications, Klarna's models are audited every six months. To me, their success shows that machine learning still adds substantial value, even when used in such highly regulated areas.” Corrie Bartelheimer, Data Scientist

Data is derived from many sources, and hence quality and completeness are an ongoing challenge. Solving this challenge is vital for feature engineering, especially for determining how many features a model will have. Often, modeling is about adding or subtracting features and hyperparameter optimization to improve accuracy. For exploration and modeling, Klarna uses AWS Sagemaker, but for the actual hosting, we have a proprietary platform. Some of the features we are looking for in the credit risk assessment include the following:

* Transactional (e.g., shopping cart, shipping)
* Customer-based (e.g., frequency of purchases or payments)
* Merchant-based (e.g., default rates)
* Graph-based (e.g., links among transactions or customers)

# Image metadata extraction at scale

## How OLX productionizes Deep Learning models for its online classifieds’ platform

Alexey Grigorev, Lead Data Scientist, OLX  
Moderated by Henrieke Max

OLX applies deep learning models to 10 million images per day (i.e., >100 per second) for content moderation and determining the quality of images.

[OLX Group](https://www.olx.com/) is a network of trading platforms, operating in 30+ countries under 20+ brands, including Avito, OLX, Otomoto, Property24, and many more. Three hundred million monthly active users find their home, buy or sell a car, find a job, sell things they no longer need or strike a deal on something they need.

## Images sell products

Online images sell the product, not just daily consumer products but also commercial goods, real estate, and high-value items. Some critical challenges for OLX are to build an image classifier, assess image quality, detect policy violations, and prevent the listing of illegal items. The image classifier has as many categories as there are products and helps determine if the listing and the image are congruent. The classifier also prevents illegal sales. Assessing image quality (good versus bad) is a service to sellers because the image’s quality drives buyer interest. Checking the image content protects buyers because we can determine if illicit information or content is included in the picture.

## Scaling image classification

OLX productionizes several models for a vast number of images by extracting and storing metadata. This metadata service looks as follows. We utilize a model wrapper that gets the images from a database for all the necessary preprocessing, like resizing and normalizing the images. Then we run Deep Learning models on the metadata to make predictions about, for example, categories or quality. Typically, the metadata service is used by internal clients requiring predictions like the moderation or the marketing team.

“Our clients don’t care about the finer details of serving models; they give us the image and get back the predictions.” Alexey Grigorev, Lead Data Scientist

“Imagine having to monitor 10 million images for quality and appropriateness — every day. How many content moderators would it take? As an online marketplace, OLX uses deep learning to automate the quality assurance and compliance of images for goods and services, scaling its solution to accommodate large amounts of visual data and shrinking costs by optimizing the production infrastructure.” Henrieke Max, Data Analyst
