In a study in this issue of Nature Machine Intelligence, et. present ImmunoScope, which circumvents this requirement by using deep learning to predict the peptides that are displayed to T helper cells, enabling analysis of which displayed peptides trigger and activate immune response. By merging diverse sources of data, ImmunoScope offers rapid mapping of antigen-presenting T helper cell targets, with clear implications for personalized immunotherapy.

T cells scan the body for protein fragments known as peptides that appear on cell surfaces. A subset known as CD4 T cells recognizes these peptides and releases signals to other cells. To understand how T cells recognize peptides, it is important to consider the role of antigen-presenting cells. These cells break down proteins from tumour mutations, or during normal cell into short peptides. These peptides must bind to MHC-II molecules. Once bound, the MHC-II complex on the cell surface presents the peptide to T helper cells.
