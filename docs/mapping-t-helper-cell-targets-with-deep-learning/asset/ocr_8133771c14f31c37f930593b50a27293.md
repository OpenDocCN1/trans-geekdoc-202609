ImmunoM uses multiple-instance learning to treat each mutant as a candidate of peptide-allele pairs and de novo predictions through iterations. A poly-anchor triplet lost clusters confirmed binders and non-binders models in the internal representation. This approach enables ImmunoM to generalize to unseen peptides and alleles, achieving an area under the recall curve (AUC-PR) of 0.856 and an area under the operating characteristic curve (AUC-ROC) of 0.825, outperforming tools in nearly 90% of cases.

ImmunoM, which predicts which peptides will trigger T helper cell activation, uses learned embeddings trained on over 75,000 peptide-allele pairs with measured release of interferon-γ in T helper cells. New peptides predicted by them show no activation. Ambiguous records are resolved and roughly 17,900 examples are revised for evaluation. ImmunoM reflects the prediction embeddings using cross-entropy loss, a loss to address class imbalance, and a triplet loss to separate epitopes from data. ImmunoM achieves an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.909, outperforming tools that score over 0.885 and 0.875.

To demonstrate real-world utility, the authors applied ImmunoScope to the discovery of melanoma neantigens and SARS-CoV-2 immune escape. In 42 patients with melanoma, ImmunoScope predictions show strong correlation with mass spectrometry data from tumor samples. In SARS-CoV-2, ImmunoScope forecasts spike protein fragments from the original strain and the variant identified by common mutations, showing a 68% reduction in T helper cell activation for mutant peptides, matching observed reductions in CD4+ T cells.
