## 6.5 Conclusion and Limitation

In this Chapter, we study a novel and promising research direction dubbed Out-of-Modal (OOM) Generalization which aims to leverage knowledge from existing modalities to generalize to an unknown modality without instance-level correspondence. We consider two scenarios where there are a few correspondences and no correspondence, i.e., semi-supervised and unsupervised cases, respectively. To tackle these problems, we propose a Connect&Explore (COX) method which first learns connections across modalities to extract common knowledge and then explores the unique knowledge of OOM data based on modality dissimilarity. Extensive experiments are conducted to justify the proposed method and intuitive insights are provided to inspire future studies. However, our research is limited to several aspects which we hope to address in the future. First, although challenging as it is, the performance is relatively limited compared to fully-aligned models, which requires more investigations to enhance generalization. Second, our OOM generalization is mostly conducted within the modalities from the same dataset. In the future, we hope to discover scenarios where the OOM data is from a different dataset with a large modality gap.

### 7.1.1 Update Rule

Before our theoretical analyses, we need to make the update rule for explicit. We have to pay attention to the fact that our algorithm is stochastic that we not directly get the value of $\nabla L$, rather we estimate it by batches: $g_{\theta} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^M \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}(\theta; (x_i, y_i))$ and $g_{\omega} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^M \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \omega}(\theta; (x_i, y_i))$, which hold. So the iteration is executed as follows:
$\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\theta_t, \omega_t)$;
$\omega_{t+1} = \omega_t + \eta_{\omega}g_{\omega}(\theta_t, \omega_t)$.
We further give a notation for $\theta_{t+1/2} = \theta_t + \rho g_{\omega}(\theta_t, \omega_t)$, so the update for $\theta$ can be simplified as $\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta_{\theta}g_{\theta}(\theta_{t+1/2}, \omega_t)$.

### 7.1.2 Assumptions

We also have to make some necessary assumptions on our problem for this convergence:

ASSUMPTION 8 (Bounded variance). The unbiased estimation about the gradient of the loss function has bounded variance:
$\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\omega}[\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}(\theta; (x, y))] = \nabla_{\theta}L(\theta, \omega), \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\omega}[\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}(\theta; (x, y)) - \nabla_{\theta}L(\theta, \omega)\|^2 \le \sigma^2;$
