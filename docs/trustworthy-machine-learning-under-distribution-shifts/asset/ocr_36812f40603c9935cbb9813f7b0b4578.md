# # 6.1 Introduction

To understand the world, we use various data modalities, as image [3, 307, 308] and text [309, 5]. Each modality describes objects through a certain physical perspective, thus contributing to understanding objects. Therefore, multi-modal learning (MML) [245, 310, 311, 225, 312, 313, 314] which learns from multi-modal data has been a core research topic in AI. Thanks to the utilization of various modalities, the learning performance has shown benefits on various tasks compared to uni-modal learning [315, 316, 225, 317], such as cross-modal retrieval [279, 318, 319], human-computer interaction [320, 321], and robot-ics [322, 323].

However, existing states of the art are not satisfactory, and emerging modalities need to be leveraged effectively like the relatively new data of geomagnetic fields [324], sound waves [325], and electromagnetic waves [326]. Therefore, emerging techno-ologies have constantly leveraged new sensors to enhance their performance. For example, Embodied Als [327] already possess abilities 3D vision and language, but they are still exploring novel skills, such as tactile and bio-sensing. Since it is hard to leverage such un-common and inperienced skills in practice, adapting the knowledge from common mod-alities to understand the novel ones could be beneficial, as shown in Figure 6.1.
