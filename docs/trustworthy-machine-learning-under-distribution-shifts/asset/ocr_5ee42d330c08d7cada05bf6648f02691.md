This Chapter studies a practical yet unexplored problem Out-of-Modal (OOM) generalization. Particularly, given several modalities, i.e., In-Modal (IM) data, the goal is
to generalize to an unknown modality or without them with scarce correspondence. Such seeming implies the real-world utilization of novel modalities: Even though human perception is limited to certain modalities, e.g., touch, sight, sound, and we can still
understand imperceptible ones such as magnetism by utilizing inherently-possessed senses, e.g., feel the force when pulling two magnets; or see the magnetic field by observing
the alignment of iron filings around a magnet.

an OOM learner who learns novel modalities without any prior knowledge. Analyzing the interactions between latent features, we show theoretically and empirically that the
