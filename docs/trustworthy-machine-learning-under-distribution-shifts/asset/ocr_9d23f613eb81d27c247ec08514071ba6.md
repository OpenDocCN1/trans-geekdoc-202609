# 5.2.2 Multimodal Large Language Models

The field of vision-language models has witnessed widespread advancements in recent years, driven by the growing synergy between computer vision and natural language processing. Notably, this synergy has led to the exceptional zero-shot performance [289] of CLIP [225], a model that employs a two-tower contrastive pretraining approach to align image and text information. In the rapidly evolving landscape of LLMs, such as GPTs [254], LLaMA [259], and Vicuna [290], it has become evident that LLMs possess the capacity to process information from diverse domains. BLIP-2 [249], for instance, serves as a foundational model, aligning visual features and text features using a Querying Transformer (Q-former) and utilizing OPT [291] and FLAN [255] as language models. Building upon BLIP-2, Instruct-BLIP [292] has enhanced instruction-proficiency of multi-modal models, LLaVA [251] and Mini-GPT4 [253] have introduced meticulously constructed instruction sets, which have found widespread application in various multi-modal models. mPLUG-Owl [252] introduces a two-stage paradigm, fine-tuning the visual encoder and then refining the language model with LoRA [293]. This approach effectively fuses image and text features.
