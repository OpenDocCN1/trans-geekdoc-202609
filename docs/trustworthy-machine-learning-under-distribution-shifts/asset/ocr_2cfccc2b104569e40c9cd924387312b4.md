# Abstract

Machine Learning (ML) has been a foundational general topic in artificial intelligence (AI), providing both theoretical groundwork and practical tools for its exciting advancements. From ResNet for real-world recognition to Transformer for vision-language alignment, the AI models have achieved superior capability to humans. Furthermore, the scaling law has enabled AI to initially develop general intelligence, as demonstrated by Large Language Models (LLMs). To this stage, AI has had an enormous influence on society and yet keeps shaping the future for humanity.

However, distribution shift remains a persistent “Achilles' heel”, fundamentally limiting the reliability and general usefulness of ML systems. As AI becomes increasingly integrated into real-world decision-making and social infrastructures, the complexity of the problems we ask it to continue to grow. These complex environments naturally introduce diverse and unpredictable distribution shifts, which can severely degrade model performance.

Moreover, generalization under distribution shift would also cause trust issues for AIs. For instance, when employing medical AI across regions, they might perform unsatisfily and cause harm. Thus, we also consider the responsibility of AI, i.e., the Trustworthiness of ML, aiming to enhance reliability rather than focusing on accuracy.

Motivated by these challenges, my research focuses on Trustworthy Machine Learning under Distribution Shifts, with the goal of expanding AI's robustness, versatility, as well as its responsibility and reliability. We carefully studied the three common distribution shifts into: (1) Perturbation Shift, (2) Domain Shift, and (3) Modality Shift. For all scenarios, we rigorously investigate trustworthiness via three aspects: (1) Robustness, (2) Explainability, and (3) Adaptability. Based on these dimensions, we propose effective solutions and fundamental insights, meanwhile while aiming to enhance the critical ML problems, such as efficiency, adaptability, and safety.
