### Exploration of Uniqueness

Uniqueness can be achieved via selecting and exploring OOM data with high uniqueness. To identify these data, we can leverage the agreement and disagreement achieved by optimal classifiers from IM data. Our final goal is to optimize

$$\min_{h^O} KL(h^O(y|x_a^O) \| h^O(y|x_b^O)), \quad x_a^O \in \mathcal{D}, x_b^O \in \mathcal{A}, \quad (6.11)$$

in which $h_a^O$ and $h_b^O$ denote the optimal classifiers in two IM data $x_a^O$ and $x_b^O$, respectively, and $x_a^O$ and $x_b^O$ are selected from OOM data with modality disagreement $\mathcal{D}:=\{x^O : h_a^O(x^O) \neq h_b^O(x^O)\}$. Here we use two modalities for simplicity, but the conclusion can be extended to multiple modalities. Moreover, data with agreement is considered anchor points that guide the exploration over those with disagreement. This objective aims to map the posterior of OOM data uniqueness $h^O(y|x^O)$ to the one of anchor points $h^O(y|x_a^O)$. To justify this, we define modality disagreement: training generative models in input space is computationally inefficient, we propose to connect modalities in the feature space where raw data $x$ is replaced by latent feature $z$.

**DEFINITION 6** (Modality disagreement). Given $X_1, X_2$ and target $Y$, as well as their corresponding optimal classifiers $h_1^*$ and $h_2^*$, their modality disagreement is defined as $\alpha(h_1^*, h_2^*) = \mathbb{E}_{p(x_1,x_2)}[d(h_1^*, h_2^*)]$ where $d: \mathcal{Y} \times \mathcal{Y} \to \mathbb{R}^+$ is a distance function in label space scoring the disagreement between $h_1^*$ and $h_2^*$.

**THEOREM 7**. Given two optimal classifiers $h_1^*$ and $h_2^*$ from two modalities, under relaxed triangle inequality, Lipschitz condition, and classifier optimality assumptions, the modality disagreement is upper-bounded by

```math
$\alpha(h_1^*, h_2^*) \leq I(X^O, X_1^O, Y|X_2^O) + I(X^O, X_2^O, Y|X_1^O) + 2I(X^O, Y|X_1^O, X_2^O). \quad (6.12)$
```

Finally, based on the decomposition of the task-related mutual information $X^O$:

$$I(X^O, Y) = I(X^O, X_1^O, Y|X_2^O) + I(X^O, X_2^O, Y|X_1^O) + I(X^O, Y|X_1^O, X_2^O) + I(X^O, X_1^O, X_2^O), \quad (6.13)$$

as shown in Figure 6.3, we can achieve

$$\alpha(h_1^*, h_2^*) \leq I(X^O, X_1^O, X_2^O, Y) + I(X^O, Y|X_1^O, X_2^O), \quad (6.14)$$

where the first term denotes the overall information, the second term indicates the commonality shared between all modalities, and the third term stands for the uniqueness only preserved in OOM data. Intuitively, when we try to increase the modality disagreement, the commonality is decreased and OOM uniqueness is increased, which fully justifies our objective: In order to explore the uniqueness of OOM data, we can explore the ones with high modality disagreement;
