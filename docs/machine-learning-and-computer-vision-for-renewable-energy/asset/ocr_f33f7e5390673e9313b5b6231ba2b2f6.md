Next, we examine how the Kalman filter affects the performance of the suggested GBDT-KF model. The ability of the estimated GBDT-KF model to generalise is enhanced by using the Kalman filter prior to data entry into the regression tree (Ye, 2024). The counts for regression tree ranges from 500 to 1500, with 500 serving as the initial step with a maximum depth of 10. Figure 4 compares the results of GBDT with various error matrices and shows that a lower MSE, RMSE, MAE, and MAPE can be obtained by applying a Kalman filter before the general GBDT technique.

Lastly, model power and Wilcoxon signed-rank test are employed to compare the significance of the model. The former is seen in the work of Chen et al. (2017) and Bui et al. (2016) while the latter is found in the empirical analysis of Makatjane and Moroke (2016). The null hypothesis for this approach is that the model is statistically insignificant, whereas the alternative is in contradiction. A 5% significance level is used in the calculation of the results. The model is further evaluated considerably using the z and p-values. The null hypothesis is rejected and conclude that the performance of the model is clearly different when the z values are greater than the critical values and if the calculated probability values are less than the significant value of 5%. Table 3 displays the outcomes of the Wilcoxon signed-rank and power test. As may be observed, there have been a notable differences in the GBDT-KF model performances since the calculated probability value is 0.01% which his less than 5% level of significance. Additionally, the model shows a high prediction power of 92% in the training data and 90% on the test data.
