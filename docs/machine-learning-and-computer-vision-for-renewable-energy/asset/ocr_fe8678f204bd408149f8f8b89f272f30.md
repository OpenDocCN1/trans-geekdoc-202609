# # Forecasting Performance of Hourly Wind Power Generation

Firstly, the performance of our proposed GBDT-KF algorithm under different number of decision trees is compared. The processing scale and centre of the algorithm are set to 450 and re-sampling is cross-validated at 10 fold which is repeated 3 times. Tuning parameter ‘shrinkage’ is held constant at a value of 0.01; while, tuning parameter ‘n.minobsinnode’ is held constant at a value of 50. The performance is measured using mean square error (MSE), root mean square error (RMSE), mean absolute error (MAE) and mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) of GBDT-KF with different number of CART regressors. Grid search is used to determine the initial parameters for Kalman filter, and the maximum depth of a single regression tree is set to one, two and three. The final values used for the model are n.trees = 450, interaction.depth = 3, shrinkage = 0.01 and n.minobsinnode = 10. As recommended by Tien et al. (2016) and Subramani et al. (2023), all the condition factors are removed with null predictive values. Experimental results show that the error matrices used decreases as the number of trees are growing. In general, the more the base learners (CART), the better the prediction precision in the test dataset. However, as the number of regression trees exceeds 2000, we find that the performance drops due to overfitting of the model.

An alternative perspective holds that the GBDT-KF ensemble’s performance and that of each base leaners are influenced by the depth of the regression tree. Regression trees with more depth according to Hu et al. (2023) are typically predicted to better suit training sets. In this chapter, we keep hourly wind power generation to 1500 gigawatts and altered the depth of regression tree from 1 to 10. Thus, we assess the performance of GBDT-KF model. Figure 3 outlines this procedure and shows that, for an hourly wind power generation dataset, performance peaks when the depth equals to 6 and thereafter declines beyond depth of 7. Similar results are found in the work of Li et al. (2020) who found that at depth 7, the model begins to produce worse results.
