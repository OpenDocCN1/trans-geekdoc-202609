Xiao et al., 2020) applied ANN-based ML models to predict the Power Conversion Efficiency (PCE) of three types of silicon solar cells: mono-crystalline, multicrystalline, and amorphous crystalline. Training data were obtained by characterizing experimental solar cell samples under various conditions of incident light intensity and ambient temperature. Prediction results were evaluated based on correlation coefficient and mean square error (MSE). The study found that prediction results aligned with traditional physics-based studies of solar cells. The authors also suggested optimal numbers of hidden layers in the ANN architecture for each of the three solar cell technologies and discussed the significance of the number of hidden layers in determining prediction quality. According to their analysis, multi- and amorphous crystalline cells utilizing 3 or 4 hidden layer units demonstrated optimal results. Regarding single crystalline cells, the optimal results were achieved with eight hidden layer units, resulting in a high correlation coefficient and low MSE.

Anti-reflection coatings (ARCs) are employed on solar cell surfaces to minimize light reflection and enhance light absorption, leading to increased efficiency and power output (Shivam et al., 2020). In a recent study, (Shivam et al., 2020) suggested utilizing Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) to predict the optimal thickness of ARC coatings based on solar cell-generated voltage and current. The authors utilized PC1D, a 1-D numerical simulation tool, to establish the training database for their ANN model. Results indicated that augmenting the ARC coating’s thickness can contribute to higher power output from the solar cell.

(Kaya and Hajimirza, 2020) crafted a Neural Network (NN) surrogate model to forecast the optical performance of thin film multi-layered amorphous-silicon-based solar cells using Finite-Difference Time-Domain (FDTD) simulation methods for dataset creation. The NN-based approach exhibited accuracy and increased speed compared to FDTD simulations in predicting optical absorption concerning incident light wavelength and cell geometry. The study explored the fundamental reasons behind the ability of an ML-based surrogate model to approximate device physics-based simulation model results. The optimization solution suggested a substantial improvement in external quantum efficiency compared to bare silicon and a randomly designed structure.

(Wasmer et al., 2018) proposed an ensemble of decision trees with feature sub-sampling as the ML model to comprehend the Power Conversion Efficiency (PCE) of mass-produced Q. ANTUM solar cells based on p-type Czochralski Silicon (Cz-Si) wafer. Their objectives were to understand the global hourly time trends of solar cell efficiency in a 1-week dataset and the impact of different features of solar cell production on the temporal evolution of performance. The training data, collected from 500,000 solar cells through inline measurements over one week, encompassed 329 features grouped using hierarchical clustering. The ML model demonstrated high prediction accuracy for solar cell efficiency over time, indicating its potential application in real production lines.
