## ABSTRACT

Wireless sensor networks (WSN) have emerged as an effective solution for monitoring and recording physical conditions of the environment. Forests, being an important part of the ecosystem, are prone to several challenges including forest fires and tree poaching. Uncontrolled anthropogenic activities, abnormal natural conditions, and tree smuggling are some of the factors that contribute to these problems. To address these issues, the authors propose a WSN-based system for preventing tree poaching and forest fires. The system will consist of spatially distributed sensors that will continuously monitor the physical conditions of the forest, including temperature, humidity, and presence of human activity. The data collected from these sensors will be processed in a central location to detect any abnormal changes in the environment. In case of a potential forest fire or tree poaching activity, the system will send alerts to the relevant authorities, allowing them to take timely action. By leveraging the power of WSN, the proposed system aims to prevent and monitor forest fires and tree poaching.

## Anti-Poaching of Tree Monitoring and Forest Fire Detection

### 1. INTRODUCTION

Forests are a vital resource for human survival and social development, playing an essential role in maintaining the ecological balance of the earth. However, the occurrence of forest fires due to uncontrolled anthropogenic activities and abnormal natural conditions is frequent. Forest fires are considered among the most serious disasters, posing a threat to forest resources and the environment. Preventing and monitoring forest fires has become a global concern for forest fire prevention organizations (Ananthi J, et al., 2022).

Additionally, incidents of tree cutting and smuggling, particularly of valuable tree woods like sandalwood, are increasing due to the significant amount of money involved. To curb such illegal activities and protect forests worldwide, preventive measures need to be adopted (Faroudja, A. B. I. D., et al., 2020).

Current forest fire prevention methods primarily involve patrols, observation from watchtowers, satellite monitoring, and recently, wireless sensor networks (WSN) (V. Parthipan, et al., 2019). However, the observation from watchtowers method has several limitations as it requires human supervision, which is not always possible in dense and remote forests (Eunna Jang, et al., 2019).

WSN refers to a group of dedicated and spatially dispersed sensors that monitor and record physical environmental conditions and send the collected data to a central location (Sakr, G. E., et al., 2010). These sensors measure temperature, sound, pollution levels, humidity, wind, etc., and rely on wireless connectivity for data transfer, similar to wireless ad hoc networks (Daxini Nidhi, et al., 2015).

### 2. EXISTING SYSTEM AND CHALLENGES

The destruction of forests is a significant worldwide problem that endangers the ecological balance of the planet. The primary reason for this damage is the unlawful felling of trees, resulting in the loss of millions of acres of forest every year. Additionally, extreme weather events such as heavy rainfall can also damage trees and contribute to the destruction. It is crucial to halt the illegal trade of trees and implement strategies to safeguard forests from such activities.

The current system employs manual labor to combat forest fires, which can be hazardous and ineffective. In order to minimize the risk to human life and decrease reliance on manual labor, research is underway to explore wireless system-based monitoring (Venkatesan, Prof. Latha, et al., 2013).

Current research on wireless networks for wildlife tracking has resulted in standardized solutions, which may not be entirely effective. This ‘one size fits all’ approach involves the design of a single tracking device for all types of wildlife (Jin, X., et al., 2012). However, this approach may not be suitable for tracking trees and preventing forest fires, since each tree species may have distinct needs and susceptibilities. As a result, it is necessary to create more specialized wireless systems that can be customized for specific forest environments and tree species (Wark, T., et al., 2007). To achieve this, cooperation among researchers, forest management organizations, and other stakeholders is necessary to establish effective and sustainable methods of safeguarding our valuable forest resources.

- Fixing of Sensors to wild animals is also big task.
- Sensors used in WSN should resist weather changes and environmental conditions.
- Detection of tree cutting in thick forest is also a risky task.

### 3. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY

This topology combines embedded technology and Zigbee wireless communication to create a system that monitors and tracks the health of animals by using a ZigBee transceiver and GPS. Forests are essential resources, and preventing and detecting forest fires has been a hot research topic in forest fire prevention departments worldwide. To address the shortcomings of conventional forest fire and conservation detection in terms of real-time monitoring accuracy, a wireless sensor network technique for forest fire and conservation detection was introduced, along with satellite monitoring. This enables related government departments to make timely and accurate decisions regarding fire extinguishing and conservation prevention.

This scheme proposes a wireless sensor network paradigm for real-time detection of forest fires and conservation efforts, which can detect and forecast forest fires, increases in carbon dioxide, decreases in soil moisture, and falling trees more efficiently. The paper mainly focuses on data collection and processing in wireless sensor networks for real-time forest fire and conservation detection, and applies a neural network method for in-network data processing. Additionally, a lightweight system is attached to the neck of animals to sense body temperature, which is sent to the microcontroller. The GPS modem receives a string from satellites, extracts latitude and longitude information from the string, and sends it to the Zigbee modem for transmission to the server room.
