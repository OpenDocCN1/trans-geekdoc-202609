South Africa has taken the lead in investing heavily in green energy efforts, seeing the enormous potential that exists within its borders. Tall windmills collect the unrelenting sea winds along the coast, while vast solar farms absorb the sun’s unending beams across parched landscapes. In addition, waterways across the country are crossed by flowing rivers, which provide the kinetic energy that hydroelectric facilities placed strategically can harness (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change report, 2020). This chapter’s value extends beyond only enhancing the operational efficacy of renewable energy plants. By presenting a novel framework customized to the unique features of South Africa’s rapidly changing energy sector, it plays a crucial role in promoting international attempts to tackle climate change (Uhunamure and Shale, 2021). This chapter develops a rigorous methodology for renewable energy anomaly identification, contributing to the regional context and serving as a model for similar global endeavors. This holistic approach functions as a lighthouse, directing initiatives in the direction toward establishing a more robust and sustainable world energy grid.

This shift is not devoid of its complexities, though. Renewable energy integration into current systems is a complex process. Unlike traditional power sources, which provide a regular and controlled output, the generated supply of energy from renewable sources is inherently unpredictable and affected by factors such as climate, seasonal fluctuations, and cyclical cycles. To ensure a steady and dependable energy supply, this unpredictability adds another level of complexity (Shahzad et al., 2022). Unexpected variations in energy output or consumption are referred to as anomalies, and they pose a significant threat. These abnormalities could upset the electricity grid’s equilibrium and cause instability and possible outages if they are not quickly found and fixed (Jithish et al., 2023). The successful integration of renewable energy sources is reliant on the proficient handling of these irregularities. Anomalies can be caused by a variety of factors, including unexpected changes in wind speed or cloud cover, equipment failures, or irregularities in trends of energy utilisation. They can also take multiple forms. Strong anomaly detection systems that can quickly find and fix these deviations are essential for ensuring the stability and dependability of the electricity grid (Madabhushi and Dewri, 2023).

With an emphasis on the changing energy landscape of South Africa, this chapter attempts to create a customised framework for identifying aberrations in renewable energy infrastructure. The goal is to effectively manage abnormalities in energy generation and usage patterns to ensure the successful integration of renewable energy. These anomalies may arise from factors such as changing meteorological patterns, equipment malfunctions, and challenges in grid integration. Traditional anomaly detection techniques, effective in conventional power systems, may not readily apply to the dynamic nature of renewable energy (Mohammadpourfard et al., 2018). Hence our primary objective is to establish a comprehensive framework for identifying and mitigating anomalies in green energy systems, addressing unexpected variations in energy patterns that hinder the seamless integration of renewable sources into existing systems (Chahla et al., 2020). Leveraging the efficacy of the One-Class Support Vector Machine (One-Class SVM) model and advanced data analytics, the chapter aims to surpass conventional methods in anomaly detection tasks. The study utilises One-Class SVM because it is effective in high-dimensional feature spaces. In real-world scenarios, data is represented in a high-dimensional space, and traditional distance-based methods are plagued by several dimensions. One-Class SVM is less affected by this issue.
Moreover, the One-Class SVM algorithm is less prone to abnormalities present in the training data compared to other anomaly detection methods. Using kernel functions, it can simulate non-linear decision boundaries and concentrate on finding the area in the domain of features that contains most of the data points (normal examples), treating the remaining data points as anomalies.
