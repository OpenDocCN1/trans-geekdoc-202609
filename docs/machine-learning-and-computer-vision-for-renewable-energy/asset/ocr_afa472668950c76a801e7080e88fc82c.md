# A Deep Learning Framework for Modeling Temporal Dependencies

Cordeiro-Costas et al. (2023) evaluate the effectiveness of temporal convolution networks, random forests, support vector machines, extreme gradient boosting, multilayer perception, and LSTM. In comparison to the other artificial intelligence models, the study demonstrates that the LSTM has the best forecasting accuracy. Sequence modeling is best left to LSTMs, while declining frequency variation problems are best handled by CNNs. As a result, having two architectures helps the models work well together. According to Slater et al. (2022), hybrid models that incorporate many forecasting methodologies have demonstrated potential in enhancing the efficacy of distinct algorithms for precise forecasting.

The possibility of the empirical mode decomposition embedded with CNNs and LSTM (EMD-CNN-LSTM) for short-term residential load forecasting is investigated by Lotifpoor et al. (2024). Over four distinct forecasting horizons, the hybrid EMD-CNN-LSTM model's performance was evaluated. CNN and LSTM, which are single forecast models, perform worse than the hybrid model. The most popular hybrid deep learning models are CNN-RNN.

A study of machine learning techniques, particularly the CNN, for prediction was given by Rahman et al. in 2021. They investigate the accuracy with which various data-driven artificial neural network models can forecast renewable energy. They made use of the RNN, CNN, and LSTM among other neural network architectures. Their study's findings demonstrate the suitability of the RNN, CNN, and LSTM models for short-term forecasting of renewable energy

This chapter presents RNN-LSTM-CNN-Copulas (herein referenced recurrent neural network-long short-term memory-convolutional neural network) coupled with copulas. This is believed to be a deep learning framework that operates on the electricity consumption data recorded at predefined intervals to provide accurate electricity consumption forecasts. The RNN-LSTM-CNN -Copulas employs (i) k-means clustering to perform cluster analysis to understand hourly electricity demand load pattern/trend; (ii) Convolutional Neural Networks to extract complex features with non-linear interactions that affect hourly electricity demand load; and (iii) long short term memory neural networks to handle long-term dependencies through modeling temporal hierarchies in the time series data. Finally, the copula handles dependencies on hierarchies in time series data.

The efficiency and applicability of RNN-LSTM-CNN-Copula are demonstrated using real-time hourly electricity demand load data acquired from ESKOM, South Africa. The performance of RNN -LSTM-CNN-Copulas is compared with the k-means variant of the state-of-the-art hourly electricity demand load forecast models in terms of well-known quality metrics. Additionally, it is anticipated that CNN-LSTM-Copula will be a good deep learning model for hourly electricity demand load forecast problems because of its capacity to learn the spatiotemporal dependencies in the energy consumption data, which results in an accurate energy demand forecast.

The ESKOM company will benefit from accurate forecasting by being able to recognize future crises and take appropriate action to avoid significant consequences. As a result, the suggested RNN-LSTM-CNN-Copula model can help with future planning, decision-making, and adopting preventative measures to deal with unfavorable events. A sustainable grid within the ESKOM smart grid will be attained by integrating the suggested state-of-the-art hybrid model with their forecasting toolkit.

The limitation of this study is that the proposed framework in this study was evaluated using data from ESKOM, South Africa. As a result, the findings from other countries may be different.
