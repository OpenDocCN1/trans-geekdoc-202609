# UNSUPERVISED LEARNING

The primary objective is to identify and analyse patterns, structures, or correlations present in the dataset. There are two main categories of unsupervised learning methods: clustering and dimensionality reduction. This particular unsupervised learning methodology entails the categorization of similar data points according to their proximity or resemblance. Unsupervised learning methodologies are highly advantageous in scenarios involving vast amounts of disorganised data necessitating investigation, particularly in cases when the precise target variable is ambiguous. The utilisation of unsupervised learning has been extensively employed in the field of renewable energy forecasting, particularly in the clustering of meteorological data.

Within the field of renewable energy forecasting, unsupervised learning has been employed to choose relevant characteristics from a more extensive collection of input variables. The usage of choosing features in the process of predicting renewable energy acts as a strategy to minimise the computational difficulty of models based on machine learning and to increase the precision of predictions pertaining to the output of renewable energy. Within the area of renewable energy forecasting, unsupervised learning has been shown to be significantly effective in the evaluation of large amounts of disorganised data to a significant degree. This topic covers a wide range of applications of autonomous learning, including grouping, identifying anomalies, and feature selection, among others. An overwhelming majority of the efforts that are now being made and are focused on the development of innovative algorithms that are intended to handle the specific issues that are inherent in the forecast of renewable energy.

# ALGORITHMS FOR REINFORCEMENT LEARNING

Reinforcement learning (RL) is a domain within the science of ML that focuses on the agent’s capacity to make optimal decisions within a given environment. The Q-values serve as indicators of the expected future rewards associated with each activity across all states. The algorithm known as the policy gradient is a reinforcement learning methodology that facilitates the direct acquisition of policies without the requirement of calculating Q-values. A reinforcement learning strategy known as the actor-critic algorithm combines elements from value-based and policy-based approaches. This is achieved by employing an actor-network for action generation and a critic network for assessing the value attributed to these acts.

Reinforcement learning (RL) has been utilised in several applications, such as the prediction of renewable energy.
