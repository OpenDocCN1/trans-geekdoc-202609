## Cost and Benefits of Energy Transition

The study demonstrates the cost-effectiveness to decarbonize the energy system. While the overall energy expenditure needed is substantial, by 2050 the additional expenses associated with the transition to a low-carbon energy sector will account for 0.4% of global GDP. Over the period of 2015–2050, the increased total investments would amount to 27 trillion USD, or an average of 0.77 trillion USD year during the same period (Gielen, 2019). More movements will be needed to decarbonize the energy sector, with a primary focus on end-use sectors (buildings and transportation) to increase energy efficiency. Infrastructure (such as energy storage, hydrogen and CO2 pipelines, transmission and distribution lines, and electric vehicle recharging stations) will also be included in these expenditures.

According to the E3ME econometric model, the REmap Case is expected to result in a 1% rise in global GDP by 2050 when compared to the Reference Case. The potential benefit from increasing GDP might be approximately USD 52 trillion between 2015 and 2050. Pro-growth policies, the use of carbon pricing and recycling of its proceeds to lower income taxes, and increased investment in energy efficiency and renewable energy sources are the main drivers of economic growth. According to the REmap Case, the global energy shift and faster overall economic growth might result in the creation of over 19 million additional direct and indirect jobs in the flexibility and grid augmentation, renewable energy, and energy efficiency sectors by 2050.

## CONCLUSION

Green energy offers a more ecologically friendly substitute for many of the energy sources available now, suggesting that it is a part of the world’s future. These readily replenishable energy sources are good for the environment, help create jobs, and seem to be economically viable in the long run. In actuality, fossil fuels are not a sustainable energy source and should be gradually phased out of our energy mix. We can build diverse green energy sources that don’t harm the environment in which we all live, and thereby produce a truly sustainable energy supply in the future. It is imperative to use renewable energy for preserving, enhancing, and maintaining the state of the environment globally. Complete transition from fossil fuel-based, non-renewable energy to renewable energy is necessary. They come with a host of safety advantages that are good for the environment and the people who use renewable resources. That is only possible if certain problems are resolved. These challenges include the public’s acceptability, government rules, political pressures, old infrastructure, expensive plant building costs, surplus capacity, and corporate dominance. Realizing the role that people play in maintaining environmental health, many countries have devised strategies to allow the corporate sector to profit from now-affordable renewable energy sources. Although the renewable energy market is becoming more favorable over time, more has to be done to promote the wider usage of these sources. Focusing on building the technology and infrastructure needed to capture renewable energy sources, such wind turbines, dams, and levees, is essential. It is also essential to keep the marine and aquatic environments clean and healthy if you want to get the most out of hydropower. The market for these technologies will expand and public acceptance of them will increase with the encouragement and promotion of solar panel installation in residential buildings. The development and utilization of renewable energy must be given top priority, particularly in light of the increasing awareness of the harmful environmental effects linked to the industrial process that uses fossil fuels. The need for sustainable energy development is growing rapidly on a worldwide scale. Widespread usage of renewable energy is necessary to attain sustainability in the energy sectors of both developed and developing countries.
