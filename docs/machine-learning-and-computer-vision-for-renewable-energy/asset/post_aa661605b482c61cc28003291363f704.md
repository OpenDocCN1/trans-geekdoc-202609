## LITERATURE REVIEW

Researchers, practitioners, and operators of power systems play a crucial role in ensuring that modelling and forecasting of electricity demand is as accurate and reliable as possible for decision-making purposes. A vast body of knowledge in the literature has been developed for improving electricity demand forecast models. It is evident that for the last decade, modelling and forecasting architectures have evolved more specifically around two main techniques: statistical methods and machine learning methods. However, hybrid methods that combine statistical and machine learning methods are beginning to take priority in the literature. This section reviews and discusses the literature on the latter techniques for electricity demand forecasting.

In the study for forecasting electricity demands, Kartikasari et al. (2023) adopted the TBATS model, which is a traditional univariate statistical technique developed as an extension of the exponential smoothing technique because of the presence of multiple seasonal patterns, nested and non-nested, non-integer and non-linearity seasonal periods. They found that the TBATS model can capture the dispersed patterns in seasonal periods with an acceptable accuracy measurement between observed and forecasted given by a symmetric mean absolute percentage error (SMAPE) of 8.13%. Several other research studies have demonstrated that TBATS with variations that include adjustment of the error using autoregressive moving average models, Box-Cox transformations, and trigonometric seasonality has high accuracy in forecasting the complex seasonality of the time series (Alduaisji et al., 2021; Gellert et al., 2022; Kim et al., 2023; Kim and Kim, 2021).

The architecture of Convolution Neural Network (CNN) and Long-Short-Term Memory (LSTM) was noted by Khan et al. (2019) as the Artificial Intelligent (AI) methods that mimic the behaviour of biological neurons. They implemented CNN, Deep CNN (DCNN), and Recurrent NN (RNN) to model the non-linear and non-stationary electricity demand time series for Victoria in Australia. The authors found that CNN and RNN are the worst-performing models, while DCNN is the best. According to Rosas et al. (2022), the limiting factors of RNN are based on the parameter risk that drives the learning process of the network, where the gradient becomes smaller and ultimately disappears during the training of the long time series. The CNN will also experience the same phenomenon only when the convolution layers are continuously added to the network, which has a well-established structure for learning internal representations of long historical time series (Durairaj and Mohan, 2022).

LSTM has an attractive network structure for preventing and controlling the gradient and flow of long and short historical dependency within the time series. The LSTM architecture produces the best forecasts for electricity consumption as compared to the other existing architectures (Torres et al., 2022). However, it should be noted that the high accuracy of LSTM is directly correlated with the efficiency of the configurations of architectures, which is well illustrated by Staudemeyer and Morris (2019).

Wang et al. (2023) implemented a hybrid CNN-LSTM model for power demand forecasting in a multi-source heterogeneous data environment and showed that CNN exhibits compelling ability in feature extraction. On the other hand, LSTM has the advantage of handling the long-term memory function of the time series very well. This experimental finding demonstrates the advantage of the advanced hybrid model structure that can handle non-linearity and serial correlation when faced with long-term time series history. The predictive power of the hybrid CNN-LSTM architecture with high accuracy is well demonstrated by many researchers in the different disciplines of scientific research (Alharkan et al., 2023; Kuang et al., 2021; Kuyumani et al., 2023; Muhammad et al., 2022). However, Kuyumani et al. (2023) draw attention to the weakness of the CNN-LSTM characteristics as well as the lack of hybrid deep learning architecture required to capture the complex dynamics of the time series better, especially when the seasonality effect is also considered. Therefore, the focus of this article is on proposing the hybrid TBATS-CNN-LSTM architecture that will be able to account for both linear and non-linear long historical time series with different properties such as single, multiple, extreme-period, continuous seasonality with effects for dual calendar periods.

## Methods and Procedures

This section presents the methods and procedures used in this study. The data used in this study comprised hourly residual demand for electricity in South Africa for the period from 01 April 2019 to 25 October 2022. Let X<sub>t</sub> be the hourly electricity demand and X<sub>t-1</sub> be the hourly electricity demand on hour t-1. Although the generalization ability of the resulting machine learning model would have been enhanced by a wider availability of historical electricity demand forecast data, the available dataset still covered the entire year, so the seasonal influence is completely captured. To further test the generalization performance of the model as presented in Figure 1, we also randomly selected seven complete days from the original dataset. Following del Real et al. (2020), the remaining data sets are randomly divided as follows:

- Training Dataset (80% of the data): The sample of data used to fit the model.
- Validation Dataset (10% of the data): The sample of data used to provide an unbiased evaluation of a model fit on the training dataset while tuning model hyperparameters.
- Test Dataset (10% of the data): The sample of data used to provide an unbiased evaluation of a final model fit on the training dataset.
