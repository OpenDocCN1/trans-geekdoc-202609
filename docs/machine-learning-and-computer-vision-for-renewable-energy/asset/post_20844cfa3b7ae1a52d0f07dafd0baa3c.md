## 2. BLUE WIND ENERGY: OVERVIEW, DEFINITION, AND PRINCIPLES OF BLUE WIND ENERGY

Artificial Intelligence (AI) and IoT stand out as a groundbreaking technology with immense potential across various fields, and energy management is no exception (Tabor et al., 2018) As the demand for effective energy use rises and energy systems become more intricate, AI has the power to transform how it handle and enhance our energy resources. This paper delves into the impact of AI on smart energy management, delving into its advantages, the hurdles it faces, and the promising possibilities that lie ahead (Szeliski, 2022).

The wind turbine is a device designed to transform the energy present in the wind into electricity. This differs from a ‘windmill,’ which converts wind power into mechanical power. Wind turbines, serving as electricity generators, are integrated into various electrical networks (Rao et al., 2022). These networks encompass battery-charging systems, power setups for residential use, networks for isolated or island locations, and expansive utility grids. In terms of quantity, the most common wind turbines are relatively small, typically around 10 KW or less. But, when considering total generating capacity, the majority of capacity comes from larger turbines, generally ranging from 1.5 to 5 MW. These larger turbines are primarily utilized in extensive utility grids, initially prevalent in Europe and the United States and more recently in China and India (Saini et al., 2023). ### 2.1 Offshore Wind Energy

Offshore wind power also known as offshore wind energy, involves generating electricity through wind farms located in bodies of water, typically at sea. The advantage of offshore locations lies in the higher wind speeds experienced compared to onshore areas, resulting in a greater electricity yield per unit of installed capacity (Tomar et al., 2022). Moreover, offshore wind farms tend to be less contentious than their onshore counterparts since they have a smaller impact on both people and the landscape. It's worth noting that, in the context of offshore wind power, “offshore” extends beyond open seas and includes inshore water areas like lakes, fjords, and sheltered coastal regions, as well as deeper-water areas. While most offshore wind farms currently use fixed-foundation wind turbines situated in relatively shallow water, the exploration of floating wind turbines for deeper waters is in the early stages of development and deployment (Del Ser et al., 2022).

### 2.2 Benefits and Challenges of Blue Wind Energy

Wind energy’s fundamental strength is its capacity to generate power without damaging the air or water and without adding to greenhouse gas emissions (Aslam et al., 2021). In addition, wind energy has a number of significant environmental and socio-economic benefits.

- **Environmental Benefits:** Wind energy is not worsening climate change. Despite the energy costs connected with making and shipping turbines, studies on their life cycle shows that their carbon impact is covered by CO2 reductions after only six months of operation. In 2019, wind power avoided producing an amount of CO2 comparable to that produced by 43 million vehicles.

- **Source of Renewable Energy:** Wind energy is a renewable and sustainable energy source since it is based on an infinite and unrestricted source is wind. Wind energy, unlike traditional energy sources, does not require extraction from the ground and transit via rail or truck, which adds extra energy, pollutants, and costs to fossil fuel burning facilities. Furthermore, continual improvements in wind turbine models lead to increased efficiency.

- **Emission-Free:** A wind turbine or wind farm generates no wastewater or pollutants once constructed. Scrubbing of smoke stacks is no longer required, and the removal or processing of harmful pollutants is no longer required because they are not formed in the first place.

- **Economic Benefits- Minimum Operating Expenses:** Wind turbines have cheap running expenses after installation and there are no source costs as because wind is a free and limitless resource, the cost of the energy source is basically nothing. Wind energy is a more cost-effective choice than coal-burning facilities, which are the most common power source.

- **Affordable:** Wind energy may need less subsidies to remain affordable and all major energy distribution systems, including coal plants and wind farms, get government subsidies. However, depending on the exact criteria considered, the fossil fuel industry may receive far more substantial grants and tax advantages than renewable sources.

- **Mitigate Health and Pollution Hazards:** Wind turbines do not add to air pollution or the health hazards linked with it. Coal-fired power plants, on the other hand, have been demonstrated to have negative impacts on human health, resulting in related medical costs. These health-related expenditures are sometimes overlooked when calculating the overall “cost” of generating power from coal. It is critical to recognize these factors as either expenses or cost-savings related with wind power, whether seen as an economic effect, a public health risk or both.

- **Flexibility:** Wind energy is flexible and promotes energy independence. Wind power, unlike fossil fuels, which normally require centralized power facilities for optimal energy generation, is size and location adjustable. Even oil-powered domestic generators, which are frequently utilized during crises, are inefficient and contribute to neighborhood air pollution

Although some challenges are also associated with wind energy as:

- **Wind Dependability is Variable:** While turbines may generate energy 90% of the time, their production may not achieve 100% capacity on a constant basis, averaging approximately 35% capacity. Because of this unpredictability, low or no wind can cause a wind turbine to shut down, as can overly high winds to protect the gear. Maintaining a regular electricity supply at such times will entail either using stored wind energy from batteries or switching to another power source.

- **Disturbances in Sound and Vision:** Wind turbines have the capacity to emit noise in the 40-60 decibel range, which is comparable to the sound generated by a medium-sized window air conditioning unit. This noise can be bothersome, especially for people who live near even smaller turbines. However, reliable studies on the health effects of wind turbine noise are lacking.

- **Wildlife Effects:** Wind turbine noise may also have an impact on wildlife, notably birds and bats, as well as other organisms that communicate through vocalizations.
