The wind turbine is a device designed to transform the energy present in the wind into electricity. This differs from a ‘windmill,’ which converts wind power into mechanical power. Wind turbines, serving as electricity generators, are integrated into various electrical networks (Rao et al., 2022). These networks encompass battery-charging systems, power setups for residential use, networks for isolated or island locations, and expansive utility grids. In terms of quantity, the most common wind turbines are relatively small, typically around 10 KW or less. But, when considering total generating capacity, the majority of capacity comes from larger turbines, generally ranging from 1.5 to 5 MW. These larger turbines are primarily utilized in extensive utility grids, initially prevalent in Europe and the United States and more recently in China and India (Saini et al., 2023).
