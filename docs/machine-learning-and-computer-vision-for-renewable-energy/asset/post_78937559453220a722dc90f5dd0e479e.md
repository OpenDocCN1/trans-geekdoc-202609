### Green Energy: Helping the Environment

Green energy significantly improves the environment because it is generated from renewable resources like sunlight, and wind. These renewable energy sources are in direct opposition to the non-renewable, carbon-emitting fossil fuels that have sustained humans for more than a century.

A significant step in the direction of a better future is producing power with a low carbon impact. If we can use the environment to suit our demands for industry, transportation, and electricity, we can significantly minimize our impact on it.

### Green Energy vs. Renewable Energy vs. Clean Energy

There is a distinction between clean, green, and renewable energy, as was previously mentioned. Although these terms are sometimes used synonymously, a resource might have more than one of these characteristics. For instance, it can be renewable but not clean or green (like certain types of biomass energy).

“Green energy” is energy that comes from the sun or other natural sources. Sustainable energy originates from regenerative sources, like solar electricity, wind power, and hydropower, and does not discharge any pollutants into the atmosphere.

Renewable energy and alternative energy are sometimes confused, while there is ongoing discussion on which is better. If a hydroelectric dam affects the ecosystem and changes streams, can it still be considered “green”? In contrast, wind power comes from an environmentally favorable, non-polluting source, making it clean, green, and renewable.

### Working of Green Energy Products

It’s crucial to remember that, whichever product you select, the energy you use will be a combination of conventional, renewable, and green energy. This is due to the fact that all energy sources in the electric grid are integrated upon joining the infrastructure for power transmission. In Canada and the US, a variety of regional systems provide power to homes and businesses.

Buying green energy only entails paying a little premium to offset the costs of adding more renewable energy to the grid, not buying green energy for your house. This is the greatest approach to lessen your carbon footprint related to your energy use if you want to go green at home but don’t have the room or money for a solar panel installation. It’s also the most economical approach to promote significant investments in renewable energy and increase the accessibility of green energy for households and companies.

  - b. Indirect gain, which is comparable to direct gain but involves the use of heat-storing, releasing, and holding materials between the sun and the living area (usually the wall).
  - c. Isolated gain: This system gathers solar energy at a distance from the primary living area. For instance, warmer air from a sunroom that is attached to a house is collected and naturally circulated throughout the house.
