Researchers have reported that hydrogen production from energy sources like coal can provide high hydrogen yield if compared to natural gas and other notable sources viz., solar, wind and nuclear power (Bartels et al., 2010). But with recent advances in renewable sectors these data may have altered in a substantial manner. Interestingly, one can effectively raise the hydrogen yield by down-regulating targeted metabolic pathways followed by limiting hydrogen consuming reactions. Besides this, competently designed bioreactors can potentially escalate light penetration that upturn ultimate yield. In certain cases, where carbon dioxide is a byproduct along with hydrogen, implementation of gas separation technique can effectively ensure the purity of the product. Moreover, implementation of hybrid hydrogen production methods employs combined culture of green algae and photosynthetic species which utilizes the different light spectrum regions to improve ultimate gain (Melis et al., 2006). Other studies have stated the ability to increase product yield by assimilating both dark fermentation and photofermentation (Ding et al., 2015; Chookaew et al., 2015; Ghimire et al., 2015; Morsy et al., 2015; Rai et al., 2015; Xie et al., 2016, Salem et al., 2018). In addition to this, if dark fermentation can be combined with microbial electrolysis cell, then the yield is getting significantly improved (Nguyen et al., 2020).

Japan is efficiently growing towards the concept of building up a fully driven hydrogen society. Harumi Flag is making that dream into reality right in the middle of Tokyo. This flagship project has 23 residential constructions with 5,632 units and retail assets. The entire energy requirement will be fulfilled by hydrogen and solar power. Japan is also soundly implementing Fuel Cell Vehicles, where Toyota and Honda’s contribution have shown substantial future prospects (Toyota, 2020). Furthermore, Yamanashi Prefecture is a perfect example of public-private partnership model which potentially aspire towards decarbonization efforts (Japan Science and Technology Agency, 2021). The Californian government has already promoted fuel cell electric vehicles since 2015 (Sinha et al., 2021).

South Korea aimed to establish six cities under the roof of the ‘Hydrogen City Creation Project’ whose daily life will be powered by hydrogen (Hydrogeninsight, 2023). The government is insistently endorsing fuel cell electric vehicles by providing subsidy on purchase. Hyundai is leading the market in this aspect.

China has also improved its decarbonization efforts by elevating the figure of fuel cell electric vehicles as well as refueling stations in the past few years (Factsheet-China-FC-EN; now-gmbh.de, 2020).

In Europe, Germany and its associates (e.g. the Netherlands, Austria, as well as Norway) have spent substantial amount of capital to compete with rest of the world by encouraging hydrogen production from bioresources(IRENA, 2019).

With futuristic policies, the current popular method of hydrogen production via electrolysis can be slowly replaced by biohydrogen as per the IRENA report (IRENA, 2019).
