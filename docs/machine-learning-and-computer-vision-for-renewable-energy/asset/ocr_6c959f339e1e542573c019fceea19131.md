# SOLAR FORECASTING

Among the best methods for reducing the unpredictability and variability of solar power brought on by atmospheric changes is solar forecasting. In order to estimate current meteorological conditions and predict future global solar irradiance (GSI) or power, ranging from seconds to years ahead, the majority of solar forecasting techniques use ground or remote sensing data as input. Known also as operational solar forecasting, solar forecasting has found widespread application in the power and energy sector. (Cellura et. al, 2008; Chen et. al, 2008; Dehghani et. al., 2019). Forecasting for the very short term, short term, medium term, and long term are the general categories into which solar forecasting can be divided based on various lead times and horizons.

Notwithstanding the differences in these definitions, forecasts of solar over every timeline are crucial for power system planning and operations. Solar forecasts for the long term are usually used in resource assessment, system planning, and energy trading. Energy markets and daily power system operations rely on short- and very-term solar forecasts, which are typically characterised as projections up to a few days
