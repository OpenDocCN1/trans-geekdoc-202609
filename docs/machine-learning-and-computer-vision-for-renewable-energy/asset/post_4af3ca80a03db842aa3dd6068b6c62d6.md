## Projected Primary Energy Consumption in India

The demand for energy is significantly influenced by a nation’s population and rate of expansion. As of January 2019, India has 1.368 billion people living in it, making it the second most populous nation in the world (TWI-Global, n.d.). It makes up about 17.74 percent of the world’s population and grows at a rate of 1.18 percent every year. By the end of 2020, 2030, 2040, and 2050, the population of the nation is projected to be 1.383 billion, 1.512 billion, 1.605 billion, and 1.658 billion, respectively. The world’s largest immigration source each year is India; in fact, the population of several of its states exceeds that of several other countries.

By 2040, India will have the fastest-growing energy consumption of any large nation. The majority of this demand will be met by coal, with renewable energy coming in second. By 2020, renewable energy has become the second most significant domestic power generation source, surpassing both gas and then oil. ## Environmental Health Strategy

Population growth has raised the need for fossil fuel energy sources, which has exacerbated pollution. Fossil fuels are known to be non-renewable energy sources, and using them excessively would cause them to disappear. Because fossil fuels contain a significant amount of carbon, using them releases greenhouse gases into the atmosphere, including CO2, CH4, N2O, O3, and H2O vapors. These gases have a negative impact on the environment because they cause global warming, which in turn causes climate change. Excessive reliance on fossil fuels and urbanization have also contributed to rising energy costs. As a result, the world economy looks for inexpensive, alternative energy sources that also enhance environmental health. Renewable energy sources include sun, air, rivers, oceans and geothermal energy create thermal and electrical energy and reduce greenhouse gases (Jaiswal, 2022).

Solar energy is abundant and can be converted into electrical energy using sustainable, waste-free technology. Another consideration is the development of advanced solar power facilities, which require meticulous planning, financing, and implementation. For example, biomass energy turns plant, animal, and food waste into biofuels by using organic waste. The leftovers can be fed to animals or used as manure. Additionally, this minimizes and controls appropriate waste disposal and management procedures. Renewable energy should take the place of fossil fuels since it is better for the environment.

## The Main Benefits of Green Energy

Investing in green energy has several benefits, including the ability to reduce carbon emissions, prevent further environmental harm, and provide employment. Everyone can help move that future closer by investing in green energy.

The sun produces more than enough energy each day to power the entire planet, but something is wrong. Since solar energy cannot be converted into electricity using current technologies, many people find that using other traditional sources is more economical. Being green involves increasing funding for renewable energy projects like solar, wind, and others, as well as developing technologies to better utilize these sources and make them more widely available and affordable.
