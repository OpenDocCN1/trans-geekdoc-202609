# The One-Class SVM Paradigm

According to Yang et al. (2021), the One-Class (SVM) algorithm is a fundamental element of our anomaly recognition system because of its remarkable capacity to detect outliers and abnormalities inside datasets. This technique works by identifying the limits of normal behavior, which separates it from abnormalities. As mentioned by Hollingsworth et al. (2018), anomalies in renewable energy plants are noteworthy because they frequently occur infrequently and escape notice, which makes the One-Class SVM model especially appropriate for scenarios with little labeled data. Schölkopf et al. (2001) highlighted that the strength of this model is its ability to develop a hyperplane that best divides typical data points from the remaining parts of the feature space. Amer, et al. 2013) emphasized how the model may be used for the detection of unsupervised anomalies because it can learn a decision boundary from the normal distribution of data. Since then, more research looked at the use of One-Class SVMs in various fields, including renewable energy systems, cybersecurity, and finance. The One-Class SVM method is a promising method for identifying abnormalities in renewable energy systems, including equipment malfunctions, odd patterns of energy use, or sudden variations in energy output. For instance, Tan et al.’s (2019) study used One-Class SVMs to detect anomalies in green power generation systems and showed how well they worked to spot departures from normal functioning.
