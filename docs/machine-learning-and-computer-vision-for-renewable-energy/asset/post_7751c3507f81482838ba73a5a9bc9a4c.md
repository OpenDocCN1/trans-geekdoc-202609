## ABSTRACT

The chapter compiles the existing landscape of research and applications in energy from renewable sources, highlighting emerging trends and critical challenges. It explores the key types of renewable energy sciences, stressing the increasing integration of advanced materials, storage solutions, and smart grid technologies. Despite notable progress, barriers such as intermittency, scalability, and commercial viability persist. The chapter provides a concise survey of the evolving research landscape and the complexities that guide the widespread adoption of energy from renewable sources.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

The worldwide push towards sustainable and clean energy sources has led to a surge in research and applications of energy from renewable sources. As the globe grapples with the challenges of climate change and the depletion of established hydrocarbon deposits, renewable energy technologies have gained meaningful consideration. This chapter explores the trends and challenges in the ongoing research and applications of energy from renewable sources, shedding light on the progress, challenges, and potential solutions in this rapidly advancing field.

## 1.1. Trends in Renewable Energy Research

### Solar Energy

Solar energy remains a key focus of research, with advances in photovoltaic (PV) technologies, solar thermal systems, and emerging materials. The trend toward increasing efficiency, reducing costs, and exploring innovative solar-powered designs is driving the progress of this field.

### Historical Evolution of Solar Energy

Ancient Solar Technologies: Overview of early solar technologies, including the use of magnifying glasses to concentrate sunlight for ignition.
Examples of ancient civilizations, such as the Greeks and Romans, utilizing passive solar designs in architecture.

The Birth of Photovoltaics: Exploration of the discovery of the photovoltaic effect and the creation of the first solar cell by Edmond Becquerel in the 19th century (Ashwani Kumar, et al., 2010). The contributions of scientists like Albert Einstein and Bell Labs in advancing photovoltaic technologies (Kanna, et al., 2016).

Solar Thermal Technologies: Overview of solar thermal technologies, from early uses in the 19th century to the development of concentrated solar power (CSP) plants (Chow, et al., 2018) (Huide Fu, et al., 2017).

Modern Solar Technologies: The contemporary era has witnessed extraordinary advances in solar technologies, making them more efficient, affordable, and accessible. This section investigates the key elements of modern solar power systems.

Photovoltaic Solar Cells: In-depth study of various types of photovoltaic cells, including monocrystalline, polycrystalline, and thin-film technologies. Discussion on efficiency improvements and cost reductions (Fang, et al., 2017) in solar panels over time (Mills, et al., 2004).

Solar Thermal Systems: Overview of modern solar thermal systems, such as parabolic troughs, solar towers, and dish collectors (Baljit, et al., 2016). Examining the applications of solar thermal systems in power generation, heating, and desalination (Mahmud, et al., 2018).

Energy Storage Solutions: Exploration of energy storage technologies, such as batteries and molten salt storage, to address the intermittent nature of solar power (Tyagi, et al., 2012).

Discussion on the significance of storage in enhancing the reliability and scalability of solar power (Platzer, 2014).

### Environmental Benefits of Solar Energy

One of the most compelling aspects of solar power is its beneficial impact on the environment. This section delves into the material benefits of harnessing energy from the sun as a clean and sustainable energy source.

### Reduced Greenhouse Gas Emissions

Examination of how solar power contributes to mitigating climate change by reducing greenhouse gas emissions. Comparative analysis of the carbon footprint of solar energy versus conventional fossil fuel sources.

### Land Use and Biodiversity

Discussion on the minimal land footprint of solar installations and their interaction with land use. Addressing concerns regarding the impact of solar projects on local ecosystems and biodiversity.

### Water Conservation

Exploration of how solar power helps in water conservation, particularly in the context of solar thermal technologies (Anand, et al., 2021). Case studies highlighting the water-saving benefits of solar power in arid regions (Kazem, et al., 2016).

### Challenges and Opportunities

While the development of solar power has been significant, it is not without challenges. This section addresses the barriers that solar power faces and the opportunities for further advances.

### Intermittency and Grid Integration

Discussion on the intermittent nature of solar energy and designs for integrating it into existing power grids. Examination of smart grid technologies and energy management systems to enhance grid reliability.

### Technological Innovations

Overview of ongoing research in solar technologies, including perovskite solar cells, solar paint, and solar windows. Exploration of how these innovations could transform the solar energy landscape.

### Policy and Economic Considerations

Analysis of the role of government policies and incentives in advancing solar energy adoption (Ogunmodimu, et al., 2018). Discussion on the economics of renewable energy and the decreasing costs driving solar installations (Ramos, et al., 2017).

Solar Energy in Developing Countries: Examination of the role of solar energy in providing access to power in remote and underserved areas. Case studies reveal successful solar projects in developing countries.

## 1.3 Hydropower and Ocean Energy

Hydropower remains a major source of renewable energy, with ongoing research into improving efficiency and reducing environmental impacts. Ocean energy, including tidal and wave energy, is an emerging field with the potential to provide reliable and predictable power. Research is focused on developing viable technologies and addressing practices or policies that do not negatively affect environmental concerns (Tong, et al., 2019) (Thorburn, et al., 2006) (Sipahutar, et al., 2013). Hydropower, one of the oldest forms of energy conversion, traces its origins back to old water mills used for grinding grain (Figure 3. shows the Hydropower system) (Ibrahim, et al., 2019).

### Overview of Ocean Energy

Ocean energy encompasses various technologies that harness the power of the sea's natural forces, including waves, tides, currents, and temperature differentials. Unlike hydropower, ocean energy is an emerging field with different technologies at various stages of development (Zhang, et al., 2022).

### Wave Energy: Point Absorbers

These devices use buoyant structures to capture energy from the vertical motion of waves (Figure 4. shows the wave energy). The motion is converted into power through hydraulic systems and energy conversion engines (Falco, et al., 2010).

- Attenuators: Attenuators are long, multi-segment structures that move with the motion of waves. The relative motion between segments generates hydraulic pressure, which is then converted into electrical power.
- Overtopping Devices: These devices capture the energy of waves that overtop a structure, leading to the accumulation of water in a reservoir. The stored water is released through turbines to generate power.

### Tidal Energy: Tidal Stream Systems

Tidal stream systems capture energy from the horizontal flow of tidal currents (Figure 5. Tidal Energy System). Turbines installed on the seabed extract kinetic energy from the moving water (Chowdhury, et al., 2021).

### Tidal Range Systems

Tidal range systems, such as tidal barrages, utilize the difference in water levels between two points during tides. This potential energy is harnessed by allowing water to flow through turbines during tidal changes.
