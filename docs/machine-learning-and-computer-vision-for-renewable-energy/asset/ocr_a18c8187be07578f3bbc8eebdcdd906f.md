# 7. ENVIRONMENTAL AND ECONOMIC CONSIDERATIONS

The predicted increase in offshore wind farm projects to meet global decarbonization objectives has generated concern in the scientific community about the marine environment’s biological well-being. It investigates the environmental consequences and changes related with offshore wind farms, with an emphasis on their compliance with the Marine Strategy Framework Directive’s Good Environmental Status. The Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) is an important tool for decision-makers to examine the environmental consequences of planned operations (Vinichenko, 2018). Regulatory bodies frequently use EIA to ensure that projects meet environmental protection goals. Furthermore, EIA acts as a critical link between scientific research and regulatory procedures, guaranteeing the appropriate use of current information while also supporting the production and dissemination of new scientific discoveries (Arbabzadeh et al., 2019).

The rapid growth of renewable energy efforts, particularly offshore wind farms, brings a plethora of artificial structures in maritime ecosystems that will ultimately require decommissioning (Ilyas et al., 2022). The Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) is carried out prior to decommissioning activities in order to assess potential environmental implications. EIA provides a chance to study appropriate environmental decommissioning options when used early in the planning phase during the design of wind farms. EIA should be used to help make decisions by assessing effects, developing mitigation plans, and tracking the asset’s life cycle.
