The cause of this increase in wind power generation in South Africa according to Karamanski and Erfort (2023) is because, independence power producers (IPPs) have been introduced to assist with sustaining South Africa's electricity demand. The IPPs that have come online are all renewable energy sources, as this technology has become more financially competitive with conventional energy sources around the world (Kåberger, 2018). The renewable energy sector in South Africa, specifically wind energy, has seen a rapid growth in recent years, starting in 2011 with 633.99 MW (Peters, 2011) and now with 3357 MW of installed capacity, which is expected to increase to 17742 MW by 2030. There are a total of 36 wind farms in operation in South Africa. The renewable energy detractors maintain that this type of energy is unreliable and requires significant backup to be viable. In the UK, discussions around wind's ability to provide power when needed highlight the benefits of offshore wind as a more secure supply source Thornton et al. (2017). A highly interconnected grid allows for the rapid adoption of renewable energy due to the grid flexibility Sijm et al. (2019), but this is not always replicable. A study was conducted in 2022, where the placement of renewable energy sources was found to be very important for an efficient implementation Kockel et al. (2022).

Adefarati and Obikoya (2019), also indicated that this increase is the result of South Africa's Department of Energy implementing an integrated resource plan (IRP). The Western Cape, Free State, and Eastern Cape provinces are well-suited to large-scale wind farm development. The abundance of renewable energy resources in the country has made several provinces suitable for large-scale grid-connected and freestanding power systems. The country has a reasonable potential for wind energy applications ranging from modest to large-scale microgrid power plants.

Beaufort West in Western Cape Province is the windiest area in South Africa; the town has tranquil conditions for only 2% of the time during the year. The average wind speed in the town is about 6.9 m/s, with 42.1% of wind speeds greater than 8 m/s. The most appealing locations for wind turbine (WT) installation in South Africa include coastal areas like as Cape Town, as well as mountainous areas in the Eastern Cape, Free State, and Northern Cape provinces. Lesotho's mountainous environment, which is only a few miles from Bloemfontein and Kimberley, has contributed significantly to the area's wind energy output. The inland hilly areas of the Eastern Cape such as Bhisho is another attractive location for installation of the WT in South Africa (Nobela et al., 2019). A total of 2078 MW has been established as the capacity of wind farms in South Africa. Mostafaepour et al. (2020) state that an additional 840.9 MW of wind farms are currently under construction, with the largest wind farm in South Africa, with a capacity of 148 MW, being built at Nxuba (Sagel et al., 2023). South Africa has around 40% more installed wind power capacity than solar electricity. Hence the significance increase seen in Figure 1.
