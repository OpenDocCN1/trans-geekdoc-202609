The diagnostic plot of the estimated TBATS-CNN-LSTM model in Figure 8 shows that the train and validation losses decrease as the number of epochs increases. The loss stabilizes around the same point, showing a good fit. The third threat is related to the choice of alternative machine learning for comparison. The spectrum of techniques used for the energy prediction problem may be misrepresented. For this concern, the most successful techniques, according to the literature exploration, are selected. A stronger strategy to avoid this threat is to carry out a specific study to identify the top techniques for each class of prediction circumstances. This latter is one of the objectives of future works.

The external validity of this chapter is concerned with the generalization of results. In other words, the TBATS-CNN-LSTM may not perform well when it is used in very different circumstances of energy consumption. This threat is mitigated by two choices. The first is the fact that a deep learning approach is used in this chapter that is known to recognize good patterns from a large amount of data and from far-reaching history in the case of time series. Deep learning is a variation of ANN that produces a robust model. The second choice is the use of a large amount of training data (30,274) out of 40,523 observations with a rigorous validation method that evaluates the robustness of the models against being trained on different training sets. Besides, one of future works will be applying this methodology, in particular TBATS-CNN-LSTM modelling, to many data sets from distant energy contexts and training more parameters of the CNN-LSTM model via stacking ensembles in a distributed manner. For instance, this approach could be applied to forecasting several decision variables, such as the energy flow and the load, in the context of urban vehicle networks (Scellato et al., 2010). Many alternatives to validation methods for this approach can be inspired by the extended discussion on validation in Bouktif (2005).
