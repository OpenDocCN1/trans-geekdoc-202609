LSTM has an attractive network structure for preventing and controlling the gradient and flow of
long and short historical dependency within the time series. The LSTM architecture produces the best
forecasts for electricity consumption as compared to the other existing architectures (Torres et al., 2022).
However, it should be noted that the high accuracy of LSTM is directly correlated with the efficiency of
the configurations of architectures, which is well illustrated by Staudemeyer and Morris (2019).

Wang et al. (2023) implemented a hybrid CNN-LSTM model for power demand forecasting in a
multi-source heterogeneous data environment and showed that CNN exhibits compelling ability in feature
extraction. On the other hand, LSTM has the advantage of handling the long-term memory function of
the time series very well. This experimental finding demonstrates the advantage of the advanced hybrid
model structure that can handle non-linearity and serial correlation when faced with long-term time
series history. The predictive power of the hybrid CNN-LSTM architecture with high accuracy is well
demonstrated by many researchers in the different disciplines of scientific research (Alharkan et al.,
2023; Kuang et al., 2021; Kuyumani et al., 2023; Muhammad, et al., 2022). However, Kuyumani et al.
(2023) draw attention to the weakness of the CNN-LSTM characteristics as well as the lack of hybrid
deep learning architecture required to capture the complex dynamics of the time series better, especially
when the seasonality effect is also considered. Therefore, the focus of this article is on proposing the
hybrid TBATS-CNN-LMST architecture that will be able to account for both linear and non-linear long
historical time series with different properties such as single, multiple, extreme-period, continuous
seasonality with effects for dual calendar periods.

# Methods and Procedures

This section presents the methods and procedures used in this study. The data used in this study hourly
comprised a residual demand for electricity in South Africa for the period from 01 April 2019 to 25 Oc-
tober 2022. Let X<sub>t</sub> be the hourly electricity demand and X<sub>t-1</sub> be the hourly electricity demand on hour t-1.
Although the generalization ability of the resulting machine learning model would have been enhanced
by a wider availability of historical electricity demand forecast data, the available dataset still covered
the entire year, so the seasonal influence is completely captured. To further test the generalization per-
formance of the model as presented in Figure 1, we also randomly selected seven complete days from
the original dataset. Following del Real et al. (2020), the remaining data sets are randomly divided as
follows: (1) Training Dataset (80% of the data): The sample of data used to fit the model. (2) Validation
Dataset (10% of the data): The sample of data used to provide an unbiased evaluation of a model fit on
the training dataset while tuning model hyperparameters. (3) Test Dataset (10% of the data): The sample
of data used to provide an unbiased evaluation of a final model fit on the training dataset.
