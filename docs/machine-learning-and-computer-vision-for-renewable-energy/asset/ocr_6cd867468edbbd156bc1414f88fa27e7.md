# LITERATURE REVIEW

Researchers, practitioners, and operators of power systems play a crucial role in ensuring that modelling and forecasting of electricity demand is as accurate and reliable as possible for decision-making purposes. A vast body of knowledge in the literature has been developed for improving electricity demand forecast models. It is evident that for the last decade, modelling and forecasting architectures have evolved more specifically around two main techniques: statistical methods and machine learning methods. However, hybrid methods that combine statistical and machine learning methods are beginning to take priority in the literature. This section reviews and discusses the literature on the latter techniques for electricity demand forecasting.

In the study for foresting electricity demands, Kartikasari et al. (2023) adopted the TBATS model, which is a traditional univariate statistical technique developed as an extension of the exponential smoothing technique because of the presence of multiple seasonal patterns, nested and non-nested, non-integer and non-linearity seasonal periods. They found that the TBATS model can capture the dispersed patterns in seasonal periods with an acceptable accuracy measurement between observed and forecasted given by a symmetric mean absolute percentage error (SMAPE) of 8.13%. Several other research studies have demonstrated that TBATS with variations that include adjustment of the error using autoregressive moving average models, Box-Cox transformations, and trigonometric seasonality has high accuracy in forecasting the complex seasonality of the time series (Alduaisji et al., 2021; Gellert et al., 2022; Kim et al., 2023; Kim and Kim, 2021).
