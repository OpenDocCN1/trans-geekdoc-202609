## Discussion, Recommendation, and Conclusion

Precise long-term predictions for electricity demand are vital for strategic planning and decision-making within power utility companies and governmental bodies that oversees energy security. These forecasts impact key aspects such as determining the appropriate generation capacity, maintenance of scheduling, selection of an optimal energy mix, and planning future expansions of infrastructure. According to Conteh et al. (2023), forecasts ensure that resources are efficiently allocated, preventing either excess capacity or shortages. Moreover, the forecasts guide economic planning, influence policy formulation, and facilitate the implementation efficiency initiatives for energy. Overall, these predictions play a fundamental role in fostering a reliable, sustainable, and economically sound energy supply (Aziz et al., 2023). Decisions in the realm of electricity demand planning are inherently influenced by uncertainties; and, utilizing probabilistic modelling is a strategy to account for these uncertainties, therefore, empowering decision-makers to enhance quality of their choices when faced with such unpredictabilities. The consequences of these decisions are significant, as opting for infrastructure expansion might lead to the construction of unnecessary power generation facilities, while choosing not to expand the existing infrastructure could result in a failure to meet future electricity demand.

In the context of planning, Rangapuram et al. (2023) emphasised that, integrating probabilistic forecasts for long-term electricity demand enables managers to explore a spectrum of potential future values for electricity demand. This approach facilitates more informed decision-making regarding the necessity for additional (or reduced) power stations, mitigating risks associated with such strategic choices. Within this chapter, we employ the enhanced GBDT-KF algorithm designed for time series forecasting. Our application focuses on predicting future wind power generation in South Africa, providing insights into the 24-hour wind power generation cycle. By incorporating a Kalman filter to eliminate the noise from the initial time series, we elevate the performance of the conventional GBDT. Notably, the GBDT-KF algorithm boasts a relatively low level of complexity when compared to neural network approaches like LSTM. This methodological choice underscores the efficiency and effectiveness of the algorithm in handling wind power generation time series forecasting.

In 2019, the South African government announced intentions to split Eskom into three entities: generating, transmission, and distribution. The decision was spurred by an urgent necessity to address the utility’s substantial debt levels. South Africa’s National Treasury is formulating a measure to handle the USD23 billion debt load of the troubled national utility (Mohlakoana et al., 2023). The National Energy Regulator of South Africa (NERSA) has awarded the National Transmission Company South Africa (NTC) authorization to operate a transmission system inside South Africa’s territorial limitations beginning in July 2023. Eskom, a South African electrical utility provider, has welcomed the NERSA action, which constitutes a critical step in the process of legally separating the transmission sector. With rising demand for embedded generation, the South African energy storage industry is predicted to reach ZAR14.5 billion by 2035, becoming a cornerstone of the future energy services market (Jahns, 2023). This is evidenced by the considerable increasing trends observed in the empirical analysis of this chapter. This will provide opportunities for investors, manufacturers, suppliers, and energy end users along the energy storage value chain. Because of the warning of Eskom death spiral by the national energy regulator of South Africa (NERSA), price elasticity of industrial demand is found to be an emerging primary driver of the lack of demand. This has therefore, served to exacerbate a “vicious cycle” in which increasing electricity prices drive declining sales, thereby resulting in the utility having to recover the same cost base from a shrinking customer base. Hence the results of this chapter are more precise to predict precise hourly load demand.

## Future Research Directions

The practical application of our findings enables service providers to anticipate network congestion locations and times, allowing for proactive measures. This capability empowers them to address potential issues before they occur. Subsequent efforts should focus on incorporating additional factors into the time series prediction process for a more comprehensive analysis. Additionally, our future endeavours involve exploring alternative nonlinear filters with more intricate structures, supplementing the Kalman filter in the pre-processing stage. This approach aims to enhance the versatility and effectiveness of our predictive modelling framework.

## CONCLUSION

This chapter used the enhanced gradient-boosted decision tree algorithm, based on the Kalman filter technique, to predict and forecast short-term hourly load wind power time series in South Africa. We study the potential of using all of the series for prediction rather than selecting the series from certain parts of the hierarchy. Furthermore, we see the hierarchical structure as a defining feature of the data generation process, and use it to instantaneously produce forecasts for each level of the hierarchy. This integrated technique employs the Kalman filter to directly handle hourly demand load dependencies such as products of complementarity and energy substitution, which are typically neglected. The results of this chapter showed our enhanced GBDT-KF algorithm is the perfect algorithm to predict short-term hourly wind power load in South Africa with prediction power of 92% in the training data and 90% on the test data.
