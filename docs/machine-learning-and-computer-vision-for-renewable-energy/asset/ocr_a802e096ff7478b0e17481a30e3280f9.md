# OBSTACLES AND POTENTIAL OUTLOOK

Forecasting the generation of energy from renewable sources (RES), such as wind and solar energy, can be accomplished through the use of a wide variety of machine learning, or ML, and DL techniques. Every methodology has a unique set of advantages and disadvantages that are specific to it. It is dependent on the particular application as well as the data that is available to determine which method of ML (machine learning) or deep learning (DL) is the most appropriate for predicting renewable energy sources (RES). When it comes to establishing a baseline model, linear regression is a reasonable choice; however, random forests, xgboost, and support vector machines are more suited for dealing with non-linear correlations and complex datasets. When it comes to the field of time-series forecasting, recurrent neural network (RNN) models, which are classified as deep learning (DL) models, provide a number of significant advantages. However, in comparison to traditional machine learning (ML) models, the training process for these models may need a significant amount of computer resources. It is a big problem to ensure a dependable and constant supply of energy that can fulfil the necessary demand, and the fluctuation of wind and solar energy presents a considerable obstacle in this regard. Intermittency is a term that refers to the fluctuation of energy generation that is caused by environmental factors such as weather patterns and diurnal changes. It may be necessary for traditional forecasting methods, which are based on historic data and assumptions, to take into account the complex and ever-changing nature of sources of clean energy in an adequate manner. Using sophisticated approaches that acquire information from real-time data and adapt to shifting situations can provide a promising response to this challenge. These techniques can be used to address this issue.
