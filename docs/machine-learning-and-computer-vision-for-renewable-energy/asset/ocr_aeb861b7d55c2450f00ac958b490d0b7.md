(2019) exploratory research presents the benefits of ML in sales forecasting for short shelf-life and highly-perishable products, as it surpasses the accuracy level of traditional statistical techniques and, as a result, improves inventory balancing throughout the chain, reducing stockout rates at points of sale, improving availability to consumers and increasing profitability.

In the series of publications (Hofmann & Rutschmann, 2018; Ke et al., 2017; Martins & Galegale, 2023; Zhou et al., 2017) research aims to evaluate the main methods and identify the one with the greatest accuracy in sales prediction. Based on an integrative literature review, three main methods were evaluated: time series, artificial neural networks and ML algorithms. The results show that ML is more suitable in terms of accuracy, particularly when models contain exogenous and endogenous variables, in addition to allowing the identification of hidden patterns in demand that can be used to identify market trends.

In Rohaan et al. (2022); Theoharidis et al. (2023) considered deep learning research direction and proposed a hybrid deep learning model that merges Variational Autoencoders and Convolutional LSTM Networks (VAE-ConvLSTM) to forecast inflation. Such results suggest that macroeconomic forecasting could take advantage of deep learning models when tackling nonlinearities and nonstationarity, potentially delivering superior performance in comparison to traditional econometric approaches based on linear, stationary models.

Many studies use ML to predict atmospheric pollutant levels, prioritizing accuracy over interpretability (Ampomah et al., 2020; Balaji et al., 2021; Houdou et al., 2024; Htun et al., 2023; Kureljusic and Karger. 2023; Rahimi et al., 2023). These methods identify important atmospheric features, enhancing researchers’ understanding and making ML outcomes more accessible to non-experts. This can enhance prediction and prevention of adverse weather events and air pollution, benefiting public health. Some of papers devoted to predicting article quality scores with ML that is outside of technical issue (Akella et al., 2021; Alchokr et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2022; Thelwall et al., 2023). In the area of electrical energy production and electrical networks power grid machine learning is used for considering challenging problems in smart grids (Akhtar et al., 2023; Bomfim, 2020; Duchesne et al., 2020; Eskandarpour and Khodaei, 2016; Kumbhar et al., 2021; Mira et al., 2023; Srinivasan et al., 2023). These challenges lead to make fundamental theoretical contributions in the areas of artificial intelligence and smart grids, building new bridges between them. These models can be classified as AI- or mathematically inspired models and include dynamical systems, Bayesian inference, stochastic differential equations, ML methods, deep learning, reinforcement learning, and reservoir computing.

Different papers examines the current state of applying artificial intelligence and machine learning algorithms in energy conversion and management and optimization tasks (Entezari et al., 2023; Franki et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2022). It highlights the latest developments and the most promising algorithms and assesses their merits and drawbacks, encompassing specific applications and relevant scenarios. Furthermore, the authors propose recommendations to emphasize the prioritization of acquiring real-world experimental and simulated data and adopting standardized, explicit reporting in research publications.
