+   8. **Energy Storage:** In order to stay up with the ever-accelerating development and acceptance of green energy technologies for distributed generation and smart grid supply, energy storage must be paired with renewable energy. All other forms of storage, with the exception of battery systems, depend on the effectiveness of different types of turbo machines to compress gases and pump fluids. Recently, there has been interest in a new generation of flywheels, with multiton carbon-fiber rotors spinning in a vacuum on magnetic bearings. Many megawatts of backup power can be stored in each flywheel, which will be used to stabilize electricity for fluctuating demands and absorb power fluctuations from intermittent energy sources like solar and wind. In one version, a green-energy motor pushes a flywheel to its maximum speed. Then, by reversing the process and converting the motor into a generator, the rotor retains its inertial energy “indefinitely” until it is released.

+   9. **Passive space heating by solar energy:** By using architectural features like wide south-facing windows and materials in the walls or flooring that absorb heat during the day and release it at night when it’s most needed, passive solar room heating makes advantage of the sun’s warmth. There are three types of passive solar design systems:
+   a. Direct gain, the simplest technology, warms materials like tile or concrete by absorbing heat from the sun’s direct beams into the building and slowly releasing it.
+   b. Indirect gain, which is comparable to direct gain but involves the use of heat-storing, releasing, and holding materials between the sun and the living area (usually the wall).
+   c. Isolated gain: This system gathers solar energy at a distance from the primary living area. For instance, warmer air from a sunroom that is attached to a house is collected and naturally circulated throughout the house.
