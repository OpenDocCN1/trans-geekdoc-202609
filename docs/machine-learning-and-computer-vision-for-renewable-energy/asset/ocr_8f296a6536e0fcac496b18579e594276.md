# ML IN THE DEVELOPMENT OF TRADITIONAL SOLAR CELLS

Abadi et al. (Balle et al., 2018) introduced an innovative approach to predict device performance and unveil structure-property correlations in organic photovoltaics (OPVs) by combining Machine Learning (ML) and Taguchi Design Experiments (TODE) (Wasmer et al., 2018). Their database comprised 240 small-molecule organic solar cells with 220 diverse donor materials blended with two fullerene-based acceptors (PC61BM and PC71BM). Using ten quantum chemical features related to energy conversion as descriptors, the model employed TODE and Artificial Neural Network (ANN) algorithms to derive design guidelines. The predictions indicated that reducing the optical band gap enhances organic solar cell performance by increasing photon absorption. Additionally, the model identified optimal values for 1L (energetic difference of LUMO and LUMO+1) and 1H (energetic difference of HOMO and HOMO-1) corresponding to each optical band gap. Lower values of 1L and 1H were associated with higher Power Conversion Efficiency (PCE) in materials with a low optical band gap, and the model suggested that having 1L and 1H nearly equal to 0 leads to good PCE values.

# The Role of Internet of Things in the Renewable Source of Energy

The internet of things (IOT) plays a significant role in the monitoring, protection and overall control mechanism for accessing any energy sources. The concept of automation along with the network has made the roadways much conducive to make the assistance to the energy control much flexible and cost effective (Syed et al., 2018). The IOT system has been appointed for data acquisition, processing, transmission, and storage. The grid architecture has become such an instance which provides lot of security aspects of the energy source. The IOT system comprises different sensors which helps in capturing data and send them to different control centers. The decision strategy has been executed by means of control commands. The communication goes on the control systems and some micro grids. The whole IOT process has been broken into umber of layers. The outcome of the entire system is the sum total of the outcomes of the integrated IOT components.
