
The diagnostic plot of the estimated TBATS-CNN-LSTM model in Figure 8 shows that the train and validation losses decrease as the number of epochs increases. The loss stabilizes around the same point, showing a good fit. The third threat is related to the choice of alternative machine learning for comparison. The spectrum of techniques used for the energy prediction problem may be misrepresented. For this concern, the most successful techniques, according to the literature exploration, are selected. A stronger strategy to avoid this threat is to carry out a specific study to identify the top techniques for each class of prediction circumstances. This latter is one of the objectives of future works.

The external validity of this chapter is concerned with the generalization of results. In other words, the TBATS-CNN-LSTM may not perform well when it is used in very different circumstances of energy consumption. This threat is mitigated by two choices. The first is the fact that a deep learning approach is used in this chapter that is known to recognize good patterns from a large amount of data and from far-reaching history in the case of time series. Deep learning is a variation of ANN that produces a robust model. The second choice is the use of a large amount of training data (30,274) out of 40,523 observations with a rigorous validation method that evaluates the robustness of the models against being trained on different training sets. Besides, one of future works will be applying this methodology, in particular TBATS-CNN-LSTM modelling, to many data sets from distant energy contexts and training more parameters of the CNN-LSTM model via stacking ensembles in a distributed manner. For instance, this approach could be applied to forecasting several decision variables, such as the energy flow and the load, in the context of urban vehicle networks (Scellato et al., 2010). Many alternatives to validation methods for this approach can be inspired by the extended discussion on validation in Bouktif (2005).

## CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

With the growth of smart grids, load forecasting is becoming more and more important. The prediction of electrical loads with high accuracy is a difficult problem. The non-linearity and volatility of real-time energy consumption make it difficult to estimate energy demand and consumption. Accurate load forecasting is essential for power system planning and rapid decision-making. Accurate energy consumption prediction is an important part of energy management for sustainable systems.

In this chapter, TBATS-CNN-LSTM is built and optimized for load demand forecasting, providing accurate short-term (days to two weeks) and medium-term (weeks to months) forecasts. For comparison, seven prediction techniques were implemented, representing the spectrum of machine learning approaches commonly used in the field of energy prediction. The best-performing model from the studied consumption data set is used as the reference model. Encapsulated and nested feature selection techniques, namely recursive feature elimination, and incremental tree regression, are used to test the significance of model inputs. Hyperparameter tuning is used to further improve the performance of ensemble-based machine-learning models. These methods remove the unpredictability of quarterly characteristics and focus more on historical lags for hourly electricity demand load forecasts. This is because temporary load parameters encapsulate the information originally contained in the workday specification. For time-lagged features, a common problem is choosing the appropriate lag length, which is solved using a genetic algorithm (GA).

The validation of results using simple train test splits, multiple train test splits, and various time horizons showed that the TBATS-CNN-LSTM-based forecasting method has lower forecast errors in the challenging short- to medium-term electric load forecasting problem compared to the base learners. Electricity load forecasting has been modelled as a univariate time series problem, and thus the approach can be generalized to other time series data. Future works will include applying this methodology and, in particular, TBATS-CNN-LSTM modelling many data sets from distant energy contexts. Another objective is to carry out an empirical study to identify the top techniques for each class of prediction circumstances. Examining the results using single-train tests, multiple-train tests, and different time horizons, the TBATS-CNN-LSTM-based forecasting method is found to have low forecast errors for short- and medium-term electric load forecasting problems. Since power load forecasting is modelled as a univariate time series problem, the approach can be generalized to other time series data. Future work may include applications of this methodology, particularly TBATS-CNN-LSTM modelling on multiple datasets in distant energy contexts. Another goal is to conduct empirical research to determine which method is most appropriate for each class of forecasting situations.

It is also anticipated that the results from this research can open roads toward future implementation of advanced calculation methods regarding energy demand and consumption. It is expected that such models will help energy planners to accurately plan for the future and utilize sustainable and renewable energy resources to a greater extent. The models will help policymakers and administrators make decisions for a greener tomorrow. In addition, the results of this study are expected to lay the foundation for the introduction of advanced computational techniques related to energy demand and consumption in the future. These models are expected to help energy planners accurately plan for the future and use more sustainable and renewable energy sources. The model helps decision-makers and managers make decisions for a greener future.
