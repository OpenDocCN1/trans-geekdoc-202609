Even when taking into consideration all of their life cycles, green energy sources emit far fewer greenhouse gases and relatively little air pollution than fossil fuels. Both the environment and the health of the animals and humans who breathe the air benefit from this.

Energy prices are more stable due to the fact that green energy is primarily produced locally, making it less vulnerable to supply chain interruptions, price surges, and geopolitical crises. One of the economic benefits is the creation of jobs through the building of structures that help the communities in which the workers are employed. 2018 saw the creation of 11 million new employments globally as a result of renewable energy.

The localized production of energy from sources like solar and wind power makes the energy infrastructure more flexible and less reliant on centralized sources that are subject to disruptions. It is thus less vulnerable to weather-related climate change. A cost-effective answer to many of the world’s energy demands is also provided by green energy. Green energy will get easier to access as costs go down, especially in poorer nations.

Historically, the energy demands of humans have been met by commodities like coal, oil, and even kerosene. However, these fuels are non-renewable and detrimental to the biosphere and ecosystem. These resources will eventually run out as a result, raising the possibility of shortages and scarcity. Even worse, the damage they do to the ecology.

When traditional energy sources are consumed, they release greenhouse gases into the atmosphere. Toxic pollutants released into the atmosphere by coal and oil have a negative impact on general health, cause respiratory problems, and shorten life expectancy (National Center for Biotechnology, n.d.). The extraction of oil and coal alone has the potential to severely damage nearby businesses, people, and landscapes (National Resources Defense Council, n.d.). Green energy may be able to mitigate or even prevent some of these problems, thus the sooner we move to renewable energy sources, the better.

# Potential of Global Primary Energy Consumption

Advancement in society requires access to an energy source. The past few decades have seen a rise in the demand for energy due to the rapid economic growth of developing nations. It is likely that this pattern will persist (Kaygusuz & Bilgen, 2008). To investigate appropriate environmental and economic measures, an anticipated future electricity demand is necessary (Chang et al., 2010). Likewise, forecasting future energy needs aids in planning future renewable energy expenditures. The growth of human society as well as global political and economic trends (Wang et al., 2014) have made energy supply and security critical issues. Therefore, past, present, and future power use can be ascertained with the aid of worldwide comparisons.
