The conventional energy grid designs frequently underestimate the relevance of information and energy storage, despite the fact that a genuinely dynamic power system requires both. This method would optimize load and market operations by allowing storage technologies to provide the full range and value of services. Their capacity to supply energy services targeted to specific applications across diverse grid components uniquely positions them to respond to signals inside smart grid systems in a timely and effective manner. As a result, classifying energy storage as a separate asset class will increase the value of storage investments while also contributing to the overall efficiency of the smart grid.

With the advancement in smart grid technologies and designs, energy system planning must abandon the current view of energy storage in favor of a more effective and sustainable framework. Although energy storage systems have been incorporated into the energy grid design, their use has been limited due to the high prices and inefficiencies associated with many storage systems, reducing their total value and usefulness (Manoharan et al., 2021). The common perception of storage as only a tool for supporting generators in load balancing over time fails to recognize the vast potential of energy storage technology (Ghai et al., 2022). Smart grid design and storage technology improvements provide considerable opportunity to disrupt the current paradigm. To achieve a completely dynamic and efficient grid, especially while moving to a low-carbon energy system, planners must fully embrace the benefits of renewable electricity generation and post-generation energy storage (Pimitha et al., 2023).
