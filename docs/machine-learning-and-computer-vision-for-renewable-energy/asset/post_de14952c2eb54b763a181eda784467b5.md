## OBSTACLES AND POTENTIAL OUTLOOK

Forecasting the generation of energy from renewable sources (RES), such as wind and solar energy, can be accomplished through the use of a wide variety of machine learning, or ML, and DL techniques. Every methodology has a unique set of advantages and disadvantages that are specific to it. It is dependent on the particular application as well as the data that is available to determine which method of ML (machine learning) or deep learning (DL) is the most appropriate for predicting renewable energy sources (RES). When it comes to establishing a baseline model, linear regression is a reasonable choice; however, random forests, xgboost, and support vector machines are more suited for dealing with non-linear correlations and complex datasets. When it comes to the field of time-series forecasting, recurrent neural network (RNN) models, which are classified as deep learning (DL) models, provide a number of significant advantages. However, in comparison to traditional machine learning (ML) models, the training process for these models may need a significant amount of computer resources. It is a big problem to ensure a dependable and constant supply of energy that can fulfil the necessary demand, and the fluctuation of wind and solar energy presents a considerable obstacle in this regard. Intermittency is a term that refers to the fluctuation of energy generation that is caused by environmental factors such as weather patterns and diurnal changes. It may be necessary for traditional forecasting methods, which are based on historic data and assumptions, to take into account the complex and ever-changing nature of sources of clean energy in an adequate manner. Using sophisticated approaches that acquire information from real-time data and adapt to shifting situations can provide a promising response to this challenge. These techniques can be used to address this issue. Especially in circumstances that are marked by uncertainty and unpredictability, the models that were stated above are able to efficiently depict the intricate correlations that exist between environmental variables and the generation of energy. This makes it possible to make correct forecasts. It is possible to use typical machine learning methods for the goal of forecasting; however, the effectiveness of these approaches may differ when compared to the effectiveness of specific time-series prediction methodologies such as autoregressive models, moving averages, and recurrent neural networks (RNNs). This problem is mostly caused by the prevalent hypothesis in traditional machine learning models, which is that data points are distinct and evenly distributed. However, this assumption frequently turns out to be not applicable to time series data, which constitutes the primary contributor to this phenomena. There is a strong indication that the values that are observed at a particular time point are affected by the values that were observed at earlier time points, as indicated by the temporal dependencies that are detected in time series data. As a result of the assumption that input variables are independent of one another, traditional algorithms for machine learning have challenges when attempting to comprehend the trends and patterns that are present within the data. Moreover, it is of the utmost importance that

traditional machine learning approaches be particularly adjusted in order to effectively address the obstacles that are provided by time-varying features or irregularly spaced data, both of which are frequently encountered in the field of forecasting time series.

For the purpose of effectively managing time series data with irregular intervals and accurately detecting patterns and trends in order to generate precise predictions, it is recommended that specialised time-series forecasting approaches be utilised. The use of hybrid models is becoming more and more accepted in the process of forecasting renewable energy sources (RES). The combination of traditional analysis of time-series with ML approaches in these models greatly enhances accuracy and solves the issues associated with overfitting or underfitting.
