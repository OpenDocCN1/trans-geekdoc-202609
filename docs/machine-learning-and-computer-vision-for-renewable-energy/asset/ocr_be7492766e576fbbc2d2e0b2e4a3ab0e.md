# Environmental Health Strategy

Population growth has raised the need for fossil fuel energy sources, which has exacerbated pollution. Fossil fuels are known to be non-renewable energy sources, and using them excessively would cause them to disappear. Because fossil fuels contain a significant amount of carbon, using them releases greenhouse gases into the atmosphere, including CO2, CH4, N2O, O3, and H2O vapors. These gases have a negative impact on the environment because they cause global warming, which in turn causes climate change. Excessive reliance on fossil fuels and urbanization have also contributed to rising energy costs. As a result, the world economy looks for inexpensive, alternative energy sources that also enhance environmental health. Renewable energy sources include sun, air, rivers, oceans and geothermal energy create thermal and electrical energy and reduce greenhouse gasses (Jaiswal, 2022).

Solar energy is abundant and can be converted into electrical energy using sustainable, waste-free technology. Another consideration is the development of advanced solar power facilities, which require meticulous planning, financing, and implementation. For example, biomass energy turns plant, animal, and food waste into biofuels by using organic waste. The leftovers can be fed to animals or used as manure. Additionally, this minimizes and controls appropriate waste disposal and management procedures. Renewable energy should take the place of fossil fuels since it is better for the environment.
