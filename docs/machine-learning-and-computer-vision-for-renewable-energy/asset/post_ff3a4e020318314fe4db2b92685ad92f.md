This front-line technology employs membrane-separated semiconductors as electrodes in an electrolyte, where water splits into hydrogen and oxygen in the presence of sunlight. Photoelectrochemical cell is an extremely environmentally prospective method for biohydrogen manufacture (El-Dalatony et al., 2020). ## Challenges With Biohydrogen Production

Biohydrogen has been identified as an environmentally friendly and sustainable replacement in comparison to hydrocarbon deposit-based energy resources till date. Challenges are still there not only to properly scale up the available processes, but also to enhance the ultimate yield. Additionally, economic feasibility is a crucial factor for its unanimous commercial-level production in a sustainable manner.

Biophotolysis method has dealt with the problem regarding the process sensitivity to oxygen. One has to either bring down the level by employing oxygen-binding proteins or by holding back the oxygen production through inert gas sparging at the headspace of the reactor. This may add additional operation cost to the entirety of the process. One way to tackle this problem is to negate certain nutrients (e.g. sulfur, nitrogen) from the algal cultivation medium that in turn will raise biohydrogen yield. It has already been observed that metabolically engineered cyanobacteria can reduce hydrogenase-based hydrogen consumption. Alternatively, a report has unveiled improved light conversion efficiency by modified green algae and cyanobacteria through genetic engineering. Some other major concerns are safety issues with product-level explosive mishmash, necessity of synthetic illumination for closed systems, light-capturing as well as carbon dioxide fixation efficiency of the involved microalgae species (Nagarajan et al., 2017; Kosourov et al., 2017; Kumar et al., 2019; Khetkorn et al., 2014).

Replacing conventional substrate with alternative choice like environmental wastes can potentially lower the operational cost in case of fermentative biohydrogen production. Selection of suitable micro-organism is of utmost concern as it substantially influences the hydrogen yield. Besides this, sometimes proper pretreatment is essential to reduce the complex sugars into simpler ones for better fermentation. A separation process has to be incorporated as carbon dioxide can drastically hamper the carbon neutrality of the surrounding environment as an obvious byproduct. Reports also suggested the presence of possible metabolite by-products, methane and a minute quantity of hydrogen sulfide gas that in turn affects hydrogen yield (Sarangi et al., 2020; Łukajtis et al., 2018).

Microbial electrolysis cell-based process still has a lacuna in pointing out the actual mechanism behind electron transfer between a microbial species and the electrode. Moreover, methanogenic activity near the anode and cathode contest with the species of interest for organic substrates as well as they utilize generated hydrogen (Kadier et al., 2017). Though chemical inhibitors have been found to be useful in combating the methanogens but operational costs become higher (Chae et al., 2010). Finally, one has to search for low-cost materials with high conductivity for electrodes. Biocathodes or microbial cathodes are a good alternative as they are cheaper in this respect (Croese et al., 2011).

Japan is also soundly implementing fuel cell vehicles, where Toyota and Honda’s contribution have shown substantial future prospects (Toyota, 2020).
