# 4.4 Mathematics Expressions

The dataset which combines the data sets from three CROHME competitions offers more than 10,000 handwritten statements from hundreds of writers from various nations. From a corpus of utterances the writers were instructed to replicate printed expressions. The corpus was created to accommodate the variety that the various tasks offered and it was selected from pre-existing math corpora as well as expressions that were contained in different research articles. Different scales and resolutions have been employed because different devices (such as tablets with reasonable screens, white-board input devices and digital pen technologies) have been used. The only online signal provided by the dataset are INKML and MATHML standards where the CROHME dataset contains the segmentation, labeling and layout of every mathematical expression. The two tiers of ground truth data at both the expression and symbol levels that are manually executed. Additionally the file now includes some generic information about the image data of the characters read.

# 4.5 Chinese Characters

Studies pertaining to language acquisition and processing require the use of lexical databases. Little is known about reading materials for children and how lexical features from these materials affect children’s reading comprehension. The majority of prior Chinese lexical databases have concentrated on materials for adults. The current study included the first extensive database of 2999 Chinese characters and 2182 words which were gathered from the official textbooks which are recently released by the People’s Republic of China’s Ministry of Education (MOE) for the majority of Mainland China’s elementary schools along with norms from both adults and children of school age. From these lexical units, the database includes important orthographic, phonological and semantic components. To find out how these elements affected adults’ and kids’ word processing and character, word-naming tasks were employed.

# 4.6 Arabic Printed Text

Certain features of the Arabic script contribute to how difficult it is to recognize. Its cursive writing style and the way its character forms change depending on where they are in the word give it these qualities. Arabic characters alter in form based on the calligraphic style that is being utilized as well as their placement within the word. Arabic calligraphy comes in a variety of forms including Neskhi, Thoulthi, and Diwani. Using the training and test sets of the character image dataset, as well as the K-nearest neighbor classifier with K=1, the topological feature was selected to assess the statistical and structural aspects. The Arabic printed text recognition system with an open vocabulary can be tested using this database. The large-scale benchmarking of Arabic text recognition algorithms with multiple fonts, sizes, styles and open vocabularies are the aim of this database.
