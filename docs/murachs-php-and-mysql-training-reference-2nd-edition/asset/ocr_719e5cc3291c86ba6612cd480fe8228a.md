# Automated Container Terminals – A Literature Review on the Operational Research Contributions

Nils Kemper, Asir Asad, Jürgen W. Böning

Received: 24 March 2021 / Revised: 10 November 2021 / Accepted: 15 November 2021 © The Author(s) 2021

Abstract Due to constantly increasing container volumes and the continuously increasing size of container vessels, the optimization and automation of container terminals has become more and more important. Especially in the operational area, tetra- minal capacities can be increased through an optimized design and utilization of the existing resources. The focus of this literature review is the evaluation of past and current research contributions towards the automatic or semi-automatic control of a container terminal. Hereby, it is possible to assist the future planning and decisions related to the standardiza-tion of the terminal automation regarding logistics processes, equipment, and control systems. A special focus is put on the (semi-)automated control of logistic processes such as the stowage planning, the allocation of resources, and their scheduling. We also detail the operational research methods used to solve the often complex optimization problems. The searched literature consists of research articles published in the leading scholarly journals as well as in the relevant conference proceedings of the last decade. We can show that most of the state-of-the-art approaches are characterized by sophisticated mathematical models and tested against terminal environments, whereas the entire process chain of a modern automated container terminal is rarely represented in the literature. In most cases, only the optimization of individual processes is considered. Additionally, the applied models are rarely reused or even compared to alternative models of the same context. To this end, we propose a standardization of the definition of the overall as well as of sub-processes in the context of an automated container terminal to facilitate the comparability of different approaches.

Keywords Automated container terminal · Container terminal · Operations research · Logistics · Automation

# 1 Introduction

The constant growth in global container shipping traffic and the increasing size of container vessels exert pressure on seaport container terminals to increase their productivity, throughput, and efficiency. Especially since the era of containerization, ports constitute one of the most important nodes in the global supply chain. In fact, around 90% of the global trade volume is handled by maritime transportation . A constant growth in global container shipping traffic and the increasing size of container vessels exert pressure on seaport container terminals to increase their productivity, throughput, and efficiency. A special focus is put on the (semi-)automated control of logistic processes such as the stowage planning, the allocation of resources, and their scheduling. The operation of a container terminal is a highly complex process, which requires the interplay of several different subsystems. This is especially true for automated container terminals. As the complexity of the processes is high, sophisticated methods are required for planning and controlling the processes in the terminal. However, the available approaches often target specific sub-processes or optimization issues.

# 2 State of the Art of Automated Container Terminals

2.1 Functional Areas, Material Handling Equipment, and Processes

In general, a container terminal can be separated into six different areas. These areas are the berth, the quay, the yard, the gate, the railway, and the depot. The berth is the area where vessels are moored and where loading and unloading of the containers takes place. The quay is the area where the containers are stored temporarily before they are transported to the yard. The yard is the area where containers are stored for a longer period. The gate is the area where containers enter and leave the terminal by truck. The railway is the area where containers are transferred between the terminal and the railway. The depot is the area where empty containers are stored. The material handling equipment (MHE) used in a container terminal can be classified into three categories: quay cranes, yard cranes, and internal transport vehicles. These are used to move containers between the different areas of the terminal. The processes that are executed in a container terminal can be classified into four groups: the vessel process, the quay process, the yard process, and the gate process. The vessel process includes all activities that are related to the vessel, such as the berthing and the departure of the vessel. The quay process includes the loading and unloading of the vessel. The yard process includes the stacking and retrieval of containers in the yard. The gate process includes all activities that are related to the gate, such as the check-in and check-out of the trucks.
