Table 1. Timeline of major database technologies.

| Year | Database Technology | Pros | Cons | Database Systems |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1960 | Hierarchical and Network Databases | High performance and throughput. | Application programmes are difficult to create and adapt, even for straightforward queries, as a result of the lack of or restricted data independence. | IMS, IDS |
| 1970 | Relational Databases | By separating the logical data representation and physical data representation, it offers a significant level of data independence. | Mismatch of impedance is encountered by software developers, with the growing use of object-oriented programming languages for application programmes. | Oracle, DB2, MySQL, PostgreSQL |
| 1980 | Object-Oriented Databases | The database and application programme are seamlessly connected. | Due to the significant expenditures made in relational databases, it failed to gain dominance. | Matisse, ObjectStore, Versant |
| Early 2000s | NoSQL Databases | High availability and scalability. | Understanding the existing NoSQL Stores and their various unique functionalities in-depth is necessary for the 'Migration from SQL to NoSQL'. | Cassandra, MongoDB, Riak |
| Late 2000s | NewSQL Databases | Handle the stringent scalability and reliability needs of contemporary OLTP applications. | Doesn't support full access to the traditional SQL tools. | H-Store, NuoDB |

Almost every area of our contemporary civilization is now impacted by “Big Data,” including trade, governance, health, and research in almost all of the domains, including life sciences, industry, the natural sciences, and the arts and humanities. Big Data is able to revolutionise not only just research but also much more than that. The most thorough enhancement and adoption of Big Data technologies, led by enterprises centered on the Web like LinkedIn, Facebook, Microsoft, Twitter, Quantcast, and Yahoo, can be attributed to the work of Google on Google File System and MapReduce, as well as upcoming open-source work on systems such as Hadoop. Big Data’s benefits for healthcare have been argued for with conviction. But many myths surround the term “Big Data,” as highlighted in a study [5].
