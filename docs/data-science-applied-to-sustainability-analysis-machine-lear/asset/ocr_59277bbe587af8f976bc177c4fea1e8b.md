LCA practitioners also often conduct sensitivity and scenario analyses. Sensitivity analyses are conducted to test assumptions and assess uncertainty in results. This is done by varying model inputs over a range of potential values and observing how this changes the model results. For example, if the forage to concentrate diet ratio in a beef cattle LCA is uncertain, environmental impact results should be produced over the range of realistic forage to concentrate ratios. In a scenario analysis, process inputs are adjusted to represent conditions different from those of the original system of interest in order to assess differences in environmental impacts. In the beef LCA example, scenario analysis could be used to predict effects of climate change on the environmental impacts associated with beef production.

# Uses and limitations of LCA

LCA is applied in product development and improvement, strategic planning, public policy making, marketing, and more (ISO 14040, 2006). LCA is generally only relevant for comparative analyses, such as assessing the environmental impacts of different designs for a particular product or multiple products with the same the function. LCA is useful for determining relative, not absolute, environmental sustainability—it can help us figure out which decision is better but does not enable us to label things as “sustainable” or “unsustainable.”

# Tools and data sources for LCA

# Process LCA

The most popular comprehensive tools for process LCA are OpenLCA (http://www.openlca.org/), SimaPro (https://simapro.com/), and GaBi (www.gabi-software.com). Of these, OpenLCA is the only open source tool. Other comprehensive LCA tools include umberto (https://www.ifu.com/en/umberto/), and Sustainable Minds (http://www.sustainableminds.com/software).

There are also process LCA tools that are limited to specific types of analyses. For construction, these include BEES, Athena LCA, and One Click LCA. BEES, developed by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), and Athena, by the Athena Sustainable Materials Institute, are both free software. For energy system LCAs, the National Energy Technology Laboratory (NETL) hosts a free LCA of Energy Technology and Pathways database, which includes the Grid Mix Explorer, a tool that allows users to browse LCI and LCIA data for regional energy mixes and model customized energy mixes.
