Defining a functional unit is a critical step in LCA. The functional unit is the quantified performance of the product system for use as a reference unit (ISO 2006a). An appropriately defined functional unit allows the impacts of a product to be compared fairly to those of other products with the same function. In a beef LCA, the appropriate functional unit could be kilograms of beef, or a unit that quantifies nutritional content, like calories or protein; in these cases, all environmental impacts in the beef LCA would be reported per kilogram beef, per calorie, or per protein. The reported impacts could then be compared to environmental impacts estimated for other food products, like beans or chicken.

# Inventory analysis

The inventory analysis step, also known as the life cycle inventory (LCI), involves identifying all the relevant inputs (e.g., energy and resources) and outputs (e.g., products created and emissions) of the system of interest. However, we often want to be able to quantify environmental impacts at a finer resolution than for the entire system of interest, so inputs and outputs are identified or estimated for each unit process within the system of interest. For a corn production unit process, inputs might include seeds, water, fertilizers, machinery, and energy. Outputs include the corn product, and might also include nutrient losses from the fertilizers, and emissions from burning fuel to power agricultural equipment. Taking inventory of all the inputs and outputs to every unit process can be a huge endeavor. Luckily for LCA practitioners, much work has already been done to build databases of common unit processes and their associated inputs and outputs. However, LCA practitioners should consider the scope and age of a process dataset, and the location for which it was developed when determining whether it is appropriate to use in any particular LCA. For more on tools and resources for LCA see the **Tools and Data Sources for LCA** section.

# Impact assessment

Also known as life cycle impact assessment (LCIA), the impact assessment step converts the inventory data into meaningful environmental impacts. LCIA is used to improve understanding of the environmental significance of output flows identified in the LCI. Typical environmental impact categories in LCA include global warming, acidification, eutrophication, ozone depletion, smog formation, ecotoxicity, and human health carcinogenics, human health non-carcinogenics, resource depletion, and respiratory effects). The LCIA calculations are performed using impact assessment tools, which utilize databases of characterization factors (CFs). CFs are based on environmental mechanisms and are applied to convert life cycle inventory analysis results to the standard units of each impact category. They can be derived such that they convert to midpoint or endpoint environmental impact categories (Fig. 8.4).
