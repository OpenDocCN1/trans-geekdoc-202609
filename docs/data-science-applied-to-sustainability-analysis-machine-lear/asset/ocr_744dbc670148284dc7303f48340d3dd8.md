GREET (Greenhouse gases, Regulated Emissions, and Energy use in Transportation) estimates energy use and emissions of fuel and vehicle technologies. In addition of the tools above, researchers conducting LCAs often use spreadsheets or other data organization programs to build custom models with data from a variety of sources, including process LCA databases, IO-LCA models, and academic literature.

LCA databases contain complete LCIs for many processes and can be accessed and manipulated using the comprehensive LCA tools listed above. OpenLCA Nexus is an online repository for LCI data, providing download pathways for both free and for-purchase LCA databases, and a search engine that allows users to find relevant datasets in any of the LCA databases on Nexus. There are many free LCA databases on openLCA Nexus; probably the most comprehensive is the USLCI database, which is maintained by the National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL), and the USDA LCA collection. Popular for-purchase databases on Nexus are ecoinvent and GaBi thinkstep, both of which have a wide range of manufacturing process and products.

Most LCA software packages have built-in LCIA methods. The openLCA LCIA methods database, which is also free on openLCA Nexus, includes a variety of impact assessment datasets: TRACI, USEtox, ReCiPe, ILCD, eco-indicator 99, Cumulative Energy Demand, and CML. Acero et al. (2015) describe these impact assessment datasets in detail and Landis et al. (2008) compares the differences in their results.

# IO-LCA

There are several freely available IO-LCA tools. These tools are typically developed using national-level economic data and each is therefore applicable within a specific country or set of countries. The first was developed by Carnegie Mellon University (CMU). CMUs Economic Input-Output LCA (EIO-LCA) model includes national-level IO tables for the United States (US), Peru, Germany, Spain, Canada, and China, and state-level tables for Pennsylvania and West Virginia. Other IO-LCA tools include the US Environmental Protection Agency (USEPA)’s Environmentally-Extended Input-Output (USEEIO) model for the US, and EXIOBASE, which was developed by a European research consortium. The latest version of EXIOBASE includes 43 countries and 5 “rest of world” regions. VitalMetrics’ US Comprehensive Environmental Data Archive (CEDA) is not free, but university faculty and students can obtain a free academic version.
