# General methods for process LCA

A standard process LCA has four major components: 1) goal and scope definition, 2) inventory analysis, 3) impact assessment, and 4) interpretation and analysis (ISO 2006a). These components are often addressed sequentially and refined iteratively throughout the process of building an LCA model (Fig. 8.2).

# Goal and scope definition

The first step in LCA is to develop a clearly defined goal that specifies the system of interest, the intended use of model results, and the intended audience. For example, the goal of an LCA on beef may be “to quantify the environmental impacts of beef” to “identify areas of potential improvement in the process” or “enable consumers to compare the impacts of beef to those of other foods” (Cederberg et al. 2003; Dick et al. 2015). The scope of the LCA is then defined such that the goal can be accomplished. Defining the scope means setting the boundaries of the system to be modeled (e.g., from raw materials extraction to consumption or disposal by the consumer), specifying the level of detail required for the analysis, and choosing a functional unit. The level of detail required is defined by the scale of the unit processes selected. In a beef LCA, high-level unit processes include fertilizer production, crop and pasture farming, cattle raising, and slaughter (Fig. 8.3).
