# SUMMARY

The regulatory sequences of vertebrate genomes remain largely understood. To address this, we developed an ultra-throughput, ultra-sensitive single-nucleus assay for transposase-accessible chromatin sequencing (UUATAC-seq) protocol that enables the construction of chromatin landscapes for one-day experiment. Using UUATAC-seq, we mapped cis-regulatory elements across five representative vertebrate species. Our analysis revealed genome size differences across species influence the number but not the size of CREs. We introduced NVAce, a deep learning framework, to identify regulatory elements and predict dCRE landscapes dynamically from genomes with high precision. NVAce demonstrated that regulatory grammar is more complex than nucleotide sequences and that sequences govern distinct functional modules. Moreover, NVAce predicted the effects of mutations on lineage-specific CRE function, using causal trait loci (QTLs) and genome editing results. Our study provides a valuable resource for decoding the vertebrate regulatory landscape.

# SUMMARY
