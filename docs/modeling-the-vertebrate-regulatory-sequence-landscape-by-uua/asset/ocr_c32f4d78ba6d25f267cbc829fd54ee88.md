Construction of the NWA model at the single-cell level and landscape
To explore the genomic landscape in diverse cREs, we used the deep-learning¹²[10.1038/s41586-xxx] to build a predictive model with given features in different species. By including NWA in the meta-deep learning network and built an NWA pipeline (Figure 5g). We optimized the parameters of the model size, sequence length, and TCR quality of the training datasets for snATAC-seq prediction. Our NWA model outperformed other approaches, including axolotl, chicken and zebrafish, showing better chromatin accessibility landscape prediction (Figure 5E). We found that higher quality data is better for model performance. Using exactly the same model, the predictions from our data landscape showed higher accuracy than the model from previous published-based data line in all cells (Figure 5F). The final NWA uses 500 bp DNA sequences, which go through connection block 128 hits with a set of 7 filters, with ReLU activation function, a bottleneck layer, and a fully connected layer, and predict better accessibility in each nucleus in a multitask way (Star Methods).

Given the conservation of cRE motifs revealed by we then tested whether our model is capable to predict chromatin accessibility landscapes from other vertebrate genomes. By a priori of principle, we used the NWA model trained on mouse to scan other vertebrate genomes with a 500 bp sequence.
