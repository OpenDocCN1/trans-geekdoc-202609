## Tn5 Tagging of Open Chromatin (First round)

A tagmentation buffer was prepared by combining 5 µL of 2x buffer (10 mM MgCl2 and 20% dimethylformamide in 10 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.5), 5 µL of PBS, 0.1 mL of detergent, and 0.1 mL of 10% Tween-20. A total of 11 mL of this was added to each well containing 100,000 cells. The Tn5 tagging was initiated by adding 2 µL of ligated TTE. The wells were sealed and centrifuged at 300 g for 5 minutes at 4 °C to achieve complete homogenization of the reaction mixture. Next, 2 µL of nuclei-nucleus suspension (containing 10,000 nuclei per µL) was added to each well to initiate Tn5 tagging. The reactions were incubated at 55 °C for 30 minutes on a rotary mixer at 10 rpm to facilitate extension. To stop the reaction, 15 mL of 40 mM ATAC and PBS (0.5% Tween-20 in PBS) were added to each well. The reactions were incubated for 15 minutes. The supernatant was removed and the pellet was resuspended in 1 mL of PBS to remove any residual reagent mix. This washing step was repeated twice to ensure complete removal.

The scale of the tagmentation experiment, ensuring genome volumes and reaction numbers (types of TTE), can be proportionally scaled up or down according to the quantity and target yield of single-nuclei data required for subsequent analysis.

## Adapter Isolation (Second round)

For the second round of indexing, 1 µL of 10 mM Buffer (dissolved in 10 mM Tris-HCl, pH 8.0) was dispensed into each well of 96-well plate using a multi-channel pipette. Then, 5 µL of 78 HA antibody (with 78 unique barcodes, dissolved in 10 mM Tris-HCl, pH 8.0) was added to each sequence. The wells were sealed and centrifuged at 300 g for 30 seconds to ensure thorough mixing. The wells were incubated using the antibody for 90 minutes at 37 °C (75 °C for 1 min; 60 °C for 2 min; 50 °C for 20 min; 25 °C for 15 min), and a final hold at 4 °C with a cooling rate of -0.1 ℃/min.

For the ligation step, the pellet was resuspended in a reaction-ready max buffer containing 38 µL of 0.1 U of DNA ligase (NEB), 768 µL of 5% PEG8000 and 498 µL of PBS. 3 µL of this mixture was aliquated into each well containing the annealed oligos, covering a total of 78 reactions. The eight plates were incubated at 16 °C for 2 hours using the cycling incubator. The reactions were then incubated at room temperature for 5 minutes. Next, 10 µL of cold PFBS was added to each well, and each was placed in a 5 mL centrifuge tube. The tube was centrifuged at 600 g for 5 min at 4 °C. Finally, the nuclei were washed twice with PFBS as described to remove any residual ligation reagents.

The scale of the ligation experiment, ensuring genome volumes and reaction numbers, can be proportionally based on the sample size and the desired single-nuclei data yield for downstream analysis.

## Adapter Switching and Gapping (Third round)

Taking barcodes of Y B objects, as outlined, the adapter switching and gapping procedure is described below. 100 µL of lysis buffer (10 mM Tris-HCl;pH8.0, 1.2% SDS, and 0.1 mg/mL Proteinase K (Sango)) was thawed with 6 µL of 10 mM Buffer (dissolved in 10 mM Tris-HCl, pH 8.0). The indexed lysis buffers were allocated into 12-tube strips and distributed 3 µL per well across 96-well plates using a 12-channel pipette, with each unique barcode. Nuclei were resuspended in (10 mM NaCl and MgCl, 10 mM Tris-HCl pH7.5) and adjusted to a concentration of 500 nuclei/µL. The nuclear density is three-fold the product of the number of Tn5 barcodes and ligation barcodes. Typically, we used nuclear concentration of 100 nuclei/µL. 2 µL of suspension was added to each well. Gap filling and adapter switching were performed using the following protocol: 5 min at 55 °C; 15 min at 65 °C; 30 min at 75 °C; 50 min at 80 °C; 60 min at 90 °C. To neutralize, 2 µL of gap filling buffer (1X Reaction buffer, NEB) was added to each well, followed by centrifugation at 300 g, 5 min at 4 °C to remove debris. Then, µL DNTP was added and incubated at 37 °C for 30 min. The reaction to modify concentration, only 24 oligonucleotides were used. If additional, their corresponding barcodes could be obtained from Sequencing (see Table S1). The scale of the experiment, including reagent volumes and numbers, can be reasonably adjusted according to the desired single-nuclei data required for subsequent analyses. The temperature-controlled adapter switching represents a key innovation in our protocol, preventing thermal regulation to enable robust in vitro adapter switching. Bridge oligo (Tm 58 °C) and Adapter I (Tm 56 °C) were used; bridge oligo fully dissociates, ensuring high-affinity binding for barcodes (at 10 minutes). During the gradient phase, barcodes binds to the genomic DNA 3' end, the adaptor region and the sequence within fully anneal to the DNA end. Gap-filling completes two steps: first, the gap between the two bases is filled and ligated to the nick to generate a continuous DNA strand, which serves as the template for subsequent primer ligation. T4 DNA ligase was used for strand ligation, and adapter B was incorporated into the primer because it operates effectively at higher temperature. The temperature gradient allows for gap ligation to occur at elevated temperatures necessary for gap repair, simultaneously optimizing ligation efficiency. This temperature-sensitive approach achieves near adapter replacement in UAC-seq protocol. It ensures highly specific PCR linker addition and resolves the issue of self-tagging in hetero-adapter-ATAC-seq (Figure B).

## PCR Amplification (First round of indexing)

Using an 8-channel pipette, the gapfilling fragments from 96-well plates were pooled. Specifically, 12 µL from each well was collected and transferred to 1.5 µL of EB buffer. This resulted in a total of 9 volumes to remove. 5 µL of DNA Polymerase (V3, no. C44011) was added to each tube, and PCR was performed for full amplification. Each amplified product was diluted with 2 µL of 2X HotStart Taq (Biosystems, no. K2020) and 1 µL of 5'-ATAC primers. The products were pooled on a 96-well plate. PCR was performed with mix of 2 µL of 2X HotStart Taq (per well) with the following conditions: 5 min; 2 min at 90 °C; 20 min at 65 °C; 2 min at 72 °C; 11-14 min at 90 °C; 20 min at 65 °C; 30 min at 72 °C; 3 min at 80 °C; and stop. To remove residual primers and fragments, the amplified products were purified with DNA Clean Buffer. Briefly, 25 µL of Clean Beads were added to each well, and the supernatant was transferred to the mixture, and excess primers were removed by centrifugation. An additional Clean Bead was added to the mixture to capture the assembled strand. The scale of the fragment amplification setup, including required reagent volumes and numbers, can be properly adjusted based on the amount of nuclei required for a single experiment.

## Sequencing on Illumina NovaSeq

For NGS sequencing (PE150.0, no. C940-002809-2), labeled with detection sequencing indexes were used in molar ratios to achieve a total of 40 ng per cell, with an expected output covering approximately 70,000 nuclei. The pooled libraries were sequenced using the NextHT Kit for NGS (Vamine, no. NM2201) to generate single-stranded cDNA, which was subsequently used for the sequencing of DNA Nanoblocks (DNA) in accordance with the manufacturer's guidelines. Following this, all sequences were sequenced on the NovaSeq platform using a custom sequencing + 19 cycles: Read 1: 24 cycles + 10 cycles + 19 cycles + 90 cycles; Read 2: 10 cycles + 30 cycles + 19 cycles + 91 cycles. Each chip runs can be performed simultaneously, with a single chip loading approximately 5000 reads, corresponding to data for around 70,000 nuclei.
