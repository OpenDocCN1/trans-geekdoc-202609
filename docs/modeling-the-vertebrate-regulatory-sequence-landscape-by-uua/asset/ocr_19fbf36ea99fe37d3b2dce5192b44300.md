We spent 1 day for library generation of more than one million barcoded nuclei and 1 day for sequencing half of the library. With moderate sequencing depth, 284,269 nuclei passed the filter in a single experiment. The quality of this experiment was exceptional compared with single-batch data of all other methods (Table S2). Cumulative UF enrichment of nuclei recovered by a single UATAC-seq experiment was much higher (Figure 2H). Overall, we believe that UATAC-seq offers ultra-high throughput and ultra-efficiency for large-scale cell datasets.

Next, we benchmark UATAC-seq against nine other protocols on mouse brain, tissue and tissues (Table S3). UATAC-seq outperformed all other methods because the number of nuclei processed and sequenced were standardized pipeline. We found that UATAC-seq showed the highest UF (Figure 2F), the highest peak number (Figure 2G), the highest FPR (Figure 2H), the highest DA peaks (Figure 2I), and the highest duplicate rate (Table S2). In addition, UATAC-seq showed high TSS enrichment (Table S2), the highest reads in promoter (Figure 2S), and the highest promoter rate (Figure 2F). With comparison to sequencing depth, UATAC-seq showed the best TSS score and accessibility, showing a high level of sequencing efficiency, and we found that high FPR has a negative correlation with TSS enrichment and UATAC-seq achieved high FPR values regardless of sample size number (Figure 2S). UATAC-seq uses nuclei for chromatin profiling, de-novo accessibility (Table S2) and better recovery of CRE in inactive regions.¹17 Foot profiling analysis from UATAC-seq showed similar patterns with the other methods. Finally, of all available mouse snATAC-seq methods, the cell types identified were consistent with the published mouse models (16–48). UATAC-seq detected all major cell types in the mouse brain (Figure 2J, Table S2).

The construction of chromatin accessibility layers for vertebrates

The performance of UATAC-seq prompted us to highlight the relationship between genome sizes and regulatory mechanisms, which we covered with diverse genome species, including zebrafish, gecko, chicken, and mouse. Due to the ultra-throughput UATAC-seq, we were able to construct a chromatin accessibility layer for each organ in a single-day and single-batch experiment. For gecko, male and female samples were from three individuals; and for zebrafish, samples from three individuals; all samples were pooled from each individual.
