### Application of NaWce
Since NaWce is an integrated deep-learning model that performs multitask jointly to decode the genome to cell type, we extract its high-dimensional representation of genome sequences to understand the cause of lineage-specific chromatin. At the final layer, NaWce recognizes high-dimensional features in the genome sequence which relate directly to our unsupervised clustering (Figure S7). These sequence modules correspond to distinguished functions, such as module 1 for neural function, module 2 for RNA processing, module 3 for immune function, and module 4 for lipid metabolism (Figure S8). Our results show that NaWce identified cell-specific grammar in the sequence which might be the cause for lineage-specific chromatin patterns.

To explore the sequences that are recognized by the NaWce model, we extracted deep-learning-based motifs using Transcription Factor Discovery from Information Source (TF-Motifs Disco) for each species. We visualized the predicted motifs for each species (Table S9). We plotted the cell-specific CRE tree to demonstrate the similarity among motifs (Figure S6). Deep-learning-based motifs can be mapped to known motifs (Figure S5), but we discovered a large proportion of short motifs not reported in existing studies^8,10. When we use the NaWce model to show the ~900 and -250 regions, we observed the signals of the 1000 bp window for the ~900 regions were 5 times stronger than for the -250 regions. These provide new interpretation of function of CREs but also supported the idea that promoters and enhancers have a shared-chromatin architecture that is primarily defined by local features^5,66.
