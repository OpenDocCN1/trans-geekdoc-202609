# Identification and validation of nCREs

The mouse ENCREs (mm10, Reg19) were downloaded from ENCODE: https://seqenh.org/ncre/. The nCREs relative to axolotl were identified by using OverlapsPeaks from ChIPpeak R package with arguments "minmapover=100"; To validate the reliability of nCREs using IUATAC-seq data, we compared the nCREs with peaks by H3K4me3 and CTCF. CUT&tag data was calculated using "macs3 calpeak" in narrow mode for H3K4me3 and CTCF, for model for H3K9me3. For samples not covered in ENCODE, the adrenal gland and pituitary gland, the nCREs marking markers of specific cell types in the tissue were extracted. Then we compared the new nCREs with CUT&tag data of adrenal gland and pituitary gland, calculating the fractions of overlapping regions.

# Motif enrichment in cross species

To investigate the functional aspects of various nCREs, motif enrichment analyses were conducted for three categories: nCREs at TSSs, nCREs at promoters (<1kb), and at distal regions (2-3kb). The nCREs annotated by Homer were computed by ChIPpeakseeker to match nCREs at TSSs and nCREs at promoters (<1kb) using the argument "distanceTSS". Then the matchMotif using the motifrichr R package (v1.22.0) was used to match motifs for HOMER The enrichment score of each motif is represented by the weighted average of scores across all nCREs, with a score of 0 assigned to nCREs lacking a proper motif.

# Quantifying motif cross species conservation

To investigate regulatory patterns, we conducted the analyses at the lineage level. Motif discovery was performed using findMotif.Genome from HOMER (v11.1, https://genome.com/avrigiguez/HOMER/) with arguments "scale-300 to 4" for nCREs in each lineage of species. For each lineage, using R (version 5.3.3) in two pairs of the species with similar motif pattern. A motif with a weight meeting the cutoff of 0.01 was considered "matched". For each motif in a particular species, we tabulated the number of nCREs with a matched motif, identifying the conservation degree. We compared all pairs of species.

# Identification and enrichment of substantial elements

We identified species-unique (UA) elements using UCSC Liftoools defined as elements that could not be made from other species' to the genome reference. For species with UCSC Chain assemblies, such as zebrafish and chicken, we coordinated the coordinates of nCREs to mouse genome reference (mm10) using the minimum match rate of 0.10 for cross-species conversion. For gecko, we generated custom chain files and applied the sample procedure. Elements that failed to align mouse genome were defined as UA elements. To investigate their functional relevance, we identified UA elements in specific cell types or tissues by intersecting cell-specific cCREs, using a minimum length of 100 bp.
