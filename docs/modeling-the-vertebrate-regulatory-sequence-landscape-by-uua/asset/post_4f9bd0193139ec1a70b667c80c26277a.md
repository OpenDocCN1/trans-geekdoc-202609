## Resource

DNA sequence-based models have recently demonstrated the ability to infer regulatory logic across biological processes [7](10.1016/cell.abc6789). At present, deep learning appears to be the most effective approach for interpreting transcriptional emergence. In our work, we adopt the ResNet architecture, which is better suited for large-scale, multitask learning on cellular landscape data. NWVAE is capable of predicting tissue-specific regulatory sequences from genomes it has never encountered before, highlighting strong enhancers.

Using NWVAE, we predicted the synthetic effect on cCRE with an accuracy that is higher than the models generated from MFPRAs or ENCODE multi-mode data. While large genomic models, such as Enformer and Evo, are still struggling to capture cell-type specific regulatory elements, NWVAE not only captures tissue-specific regulatory grammar (Table S7) but also covers all key elements in vivo. By interpreting the deep-learning model, we presented a conserved regulatory logic and architecture in learning genome. We observed that the first layer captures TF binding motifs in the genome, the other layers represent motif interactions and genomic interactions, and the output predicts different features for different lineages and finally predicts the target gene at all.

## Limitations of the study

NWVAE is designed to predict chromatin accessibility and effects of mutations in genomic sequences. However, it lacks the capacity to determine the function of individual enhancers or to predict changes in gene expression caused by mutations in enhancers or repressors. While our findings demonstrate that grammar plays a critical role in euch and heterochromatin states, we cannot rule out the possibility that chromatin mutations may affect gene expression in certain contexts. Moreover, although NWVAE achieved high prediction accuracy across cell types, the functional validation we presented focuses on the human lineage as a proof of principle. Nonetheless, we believe NWVAE should demonstrate comparable accuracy in predicting synthetic cCREs across all vertebrate cell types.
