When a node joins the network, it must declare accounts that it can control, while the amount of cryptocurrency in those accounts is used as the stake that weights the node's votes in the hashgraph virtual voting algorithm. As mentioned, each node is paid by Hedera on a daily basis (for serving as a node) – the payments are proportional to its stake, so the stake of a node is earning interest.

The voting in the virtual voting algorithm is relatively easy to implement – it can be done through a simple redefinition of the notions of the majority, supermajority, and the median value. Instead of at least 2/3 of the number of nodes in the community, the term supermajority now represents a group of nodes whose stakes in the sum have at least 2/3 of the total amount of money on the accounts of all nodes in the community. Similarly, the term majority is redefined, and now means more than 50% of the total amount of stakes. The median of the timestamps in a set S events becomes the weighted median, which can be considered as the median value of the enlarged set, $Enlarged$ of the timestamps, where each timestamp $t_i$ from $S$ is, represented by $n_i$ copies of itself, such that $n_i$ is equal to the stake of the node that has created the event with timestamp $t_i$.

The new meaning of supermajority implicitly changes the definition of seeing events in the Hashgraph. Together with the new meaning of the median (i.e., weighted median), it influences the order of the consensus transactions, so that the wealthier (the more trusted) nodes have more power to reach the consensus as some group of new nodes with insincere intentions.

Besides the described mechanisms incorporated in the Hashgraph, such as the fee and payment model aimed at incentivizing node operators to participate, or the PoS model for inhibition of Sybil attacks and other malicious behaviors, Hedera introduces the codasharding architecture to ensure future scalability of the platform, which is also necessary for the success of a public permissionless ledger. This comes from the separation of governance from consensus. As the network is expected to expand over time to millions of nodes (all voting on distributed consensus), the sharding architecture enables continuous platform decentralization.

In its initial phase, the Hedera network will certainly have a relatively small number of nodes belonging to a single shard (partition, group). With the expected increase of their number in the network, nodes will be grouped in different shards. The entity in charge of allocating nodes to shards is called the master shard, which randomly assigns new nodes to different shards, once a day. At the same time, the master shard moves nodes between shards to ensure that the total amount of cryptocurrency staked in a shard is large enough and that no node in a shard owns a large fraction of that amount. All nodes in a shard establish consensus on the transactions they collected from their clients and gossiped among themselves, that is, they share the state of their client's accounts, which is a subset of the state of the entire ledger.
