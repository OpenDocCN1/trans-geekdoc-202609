# 1.3 IOTA project – tangle

The Internet of Things (IoT) is becoming a reality with the expectation of deploying billions of IoT devices (shortly). Many of them are to be involved in different kinds of money transactions and micropayments. Therefore, there appeared a need for providing a universal platform that would support a huge number of transactions efficiently. This support should be better than what the existing blockchain platforms provide. A solution that should obtain key support to the IoT is offered by the IOTA project [14]. It is a new distributed ledger technology significantly different than the blockchain. IOTA is based on a specific DAG structure known as tangle.

The IOTA project began in 2015, intending to provide a more suitable cryptocurrency for payments (transactions) between billions of IoT devices and obtain a reliable platform for many other applications, which might include M2M communication between those devices. As such, the implementation of the IOTA protocol had to be cheap, fast, and scalable, which was the key explanation for developing the tangle, instead of using already mature blockchain technology. The rapid growth of IOTA significantly increased the demand for micropayments between IoT devices. At first, the BTC did not prove to be a good choice for these transactions. The first is the relatively high fees as the amounts of money included in transactions with micropayments, and the other reason is the roles of participants.. some miners verify transactions and "normal users" who issue transactions in the BTC society; here, miners may be issuers. With this ambiguity, the probability of possible conflicts stays high, and conflict resolution wastes ever resources.

The differences between IOTA and other blockchain-based systems such as the BTC or Ethereum are that IOTA's DLT includes no miners, no transaction blocks, no transaction fees or rewards paid in IOTA tokens, and no difficult PoW. Instead, when a participant broadcasts a transaction, the first thing to do is validate that, is, approve two (randomly chosen) previous transactions and perform a small amount of PoW. As a reward, the transaction is allowed to be attached to those previous ones. This pay-for-it verification system makes financial rewards unnecessary.

Besides, the whole amount of cryptocurrency (the IOTA tokens or IOTAs, in short) has already been created at the beginning of the tangle (i.e., at the genesis moment) and distributed to the project founders. After different transactions between the participants (the founders and the new participants), IOTAs change hands.
