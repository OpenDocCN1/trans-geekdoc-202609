Various software-release data can be stored in a distributed ledger – the baseline documentation, development projects, changes, versioning of development, etc. The transaction logic incorporated in the distributed ledger, by design, ensures that any change of a data record initiates a new transaction/event, making the document process irreversible. In that way, besides achieving maximum transparency of the process, the damaging risk resulting from significant blackouts is considerably reduced.

In the second half of 2023, blockchain technology started being used for physical assets, giving the possibility to use the public ledger to store and validate documents instead relying on a central authority. A user can store the signature and the timestamp associated with a document in the blockchain and validate it at any point using blockchain mechanisms. Similarly, as for software versioning, DLTs could help in tracking various documents' requests. Using a distributed ledger, a goal achieved here is that all participants can have access to documents in a secure, reliable, and transparent way, assuming that each document's release is protected against any uncoordinated change. Apart from that, a distributed ledger with high scalability property (e.g., hashgraph or tangle) will be able to support high data volumes as well as the high frequency of documents' release.

# 4.1.1.4 Multi-agent robot systems

Among the numerous niches emerging from the concept of Industry 4.0 [68] where blockchains and DLTs could find their use, are multi-agent robot systems (MARSs). This term formally represents a collection of two or more autonomous robots working together in a specific environment and working on different tasks. A team of mobile autonomous robots/agents working together to achieve some (well-defined) goals can be homogenous or consist of robots with different characteristics. In any case, they are allowed to communicate and coordinate with each other to be efficient as possible (e.g., fast and with minimal consumption of energy or resources). Finding the optimal working strategies and routines for each given objective may represent a huge challenge, even for simple tasks.

Car Industry 4.0 is highly reliant on robots, for example, in a smart factory, several robots with different levels of autonomy and mobility could co-operate amidst dynamic and ever-changing car processes. Therefore, the use of DLTs, as support to MARSs and generally to multi-agent systems (MASSs), becomes even more significant. In line with this, specific importance has to be given to the information support when groups of robots have to operate under external disturbances and environmental changes. Successful solutions for these could be found in the distributed ledger technology.
