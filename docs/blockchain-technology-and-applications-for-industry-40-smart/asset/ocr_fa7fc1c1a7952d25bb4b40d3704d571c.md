# 2.7.1.2 Hyperledger ecosystem

The Hyperledger ecosystem represents a suite of open-source frameworks, tools, and libraries for enterprise-grade blockchain deployments. It involves more than 250 teams and companies and is hosted by the Linux Foundation. The work is organized into projects, which develop ledger technologies, comprehensive tools, and supporting libraries. The maturity of projects is clearly indicated, as well as their coherence.

Hyperledger's (Burrow, Fabric, Indy, Iroha, Sawtooth) primarily target private and consortium networks. Therefore, there are no public Hyperledger networks, and consequently, no public cryptocurrencies. Only the Hyperledger Besu, a Java-based Ethereum client implementation, can be attached to Ethereum networks. Since Hyperledger networks are meant to support the operations of various organizations, they private or consortium-based. While multiple organizations access the same ledger or even multiple ledgers having part of the data exposed to the public, these are still considered private. Different approaches to smart contract development are taken in HL, which depend on the selected ledger technology. Hyperledger supports many and widely used programming languages are Solidity and Go. Extensive documentation is available for all projects.

Each project has its team of collaborating organizations from world-renowned ICT projects to small teams dedicated to the Hyperledger project development. Prominent Hyperledger use cases include logistics projects, financial projects, humanitarian and philanthropic projects.
