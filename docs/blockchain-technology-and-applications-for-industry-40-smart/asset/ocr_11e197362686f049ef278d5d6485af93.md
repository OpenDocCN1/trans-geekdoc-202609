At the same time, the other solved block (the orphan) will stale, and the transactions from this block (the ones are not included in the winning block) will wait for the next round, to be collected and verified within some of the forthcoming candidate blocks. The triggering moment for the decision of which two solutions will win is about 10 minutes, when the next block is added the first time (somewhere in the network) on top of the winning block. Then, the newly added block will be broadcast as the solution (where the previous winning block is included in blockchain). The community will quickly accept it, assuming that the last added block was the offered solution at the moment, which is the most common case.

Having received the news that the candidate block has been solved, a miner should only verify that the solution is correct, as the solution is already known. However, although a solved block can be added to the blockchain only if it is verified by at 50% of miners, they do not have to verify it. A miner first decides whether to wait for the block before starting the next block or immediately with solving the next block, assuming the previous block was correct. The benefit of making the assumption is that there will be no wasted time on verification, which increases the profit by about 10%. As a result, large mining pools will skip this verification process and, just using the newly appended blockchain, immediately starting on the next block.
