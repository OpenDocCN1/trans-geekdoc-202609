
## 32 Distributed ledger technologies

The hashgraph is divided into portions of events called rounds (see Fig. 1). It starts with round 1, and the starting events (from each node) are called witnesses of round 1. As the hashgraph grows, an event will appear at one moment which strongly sees a supermajority of the round 1 witnesses. Round 2 begins from that moment, while this event becomes a witness of round 2. The division of rounds is based on the rule that each round begins with the first event which strongly sees supermajority of the witnesses in the last round (the event does not have to see all the witnesses from the last round). This event becomes the first witness of the next round. Each node may have created at most one witness in a round implying that some nodes may have created no witness in the round. Also, when an event is first gossiped to a node, that node can immediately calculate the created round of the event, that is, the serial number the event belongs to.

The round created of an event is calculated as follows: if the event sees a supermajority of witnesses from round $r$, where $r$ is the max of the round created of the event's parents, then the round created of the event is $r+1$; otherwise, the round created of the event is $r$.

The following step is to determine which of the witnesses in a given round will be (virtually) elected as famous witnesses. Every witness in a round can become famous if some conditions are satisfied. The $i$-th witness, $W_{n,i}$, of round $n$ will be famous or not to the witnesses from upper rounds, $n+1$ and $n+2$ (in most cases). Firstly, each witness, $W_{n+1,i}$, from round $n+1$ gives his virtual vote or the answer is "Yes" if he can see $W_{n,i}$ (strongly seen is not mandatory) or the answer "No" in the opposite case. Next, votes are counted by witnesses from round $n$ to $n+2$.

As a result of this, $W_{n+2,k}$ from round $n+2$ takes into account (i.e., counts) only the votes from round $n+1$, strongly seen, which he counts. The votes from other round $n+1$ witnesses, not seen by $W_{n+2,k}$ (if any exist), are not considered. If the number of collected "Yes" votes is greater than or equal to 2/3 of the total number of nodes (a supermajority), then it is decided that $W_{n,i}$ is famous.

The virtual voting procedure is performed for each round $n$ witnesses, so eventually a group of the famous witnesses of round $n$ will be formed, the fame of which was finally decided by one witness from round $n+2$, by a witness who counted the votes of round $n+1$ witnesses. It is mathematically proven that the same result of the election of the round $n$ famous witnesses would be obtained if any other round $n+2$ counts the witnesses of round $n+1$.

The nodes that have created famous witnesses of round $n$ are given the allowance to make decisions on consensus timestamps of some events (i.e., transactions) from round $n-1$. More precisely, the creator nodes of the round $n$ famous witnesses are qualified to determine the consensus timestamps of the events from $n-1$, which are seen by all famous witnesses of round $n$. If an event is seen by all the round $n$ famous witnesses, in round $n$, that is, it has created round of $n$. For example, to determine the consensus timestamp of a $n-1$ event, $X_{n-1}$ which has created round of $n$, each node $A_i$ from this privileged group (the creators of the round $n$ famous witnesses) first picks the event, $Y_i$, which was created by that node when it first received a gossip given given event, $X_{n-1}$ (it may often happen that $Y_i$ is just the famous witness of round $n$, created by that node).
