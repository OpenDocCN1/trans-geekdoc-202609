Still, a new block is being added to the blockchain every ten minutes. How is it achieved?

The answer is in the enormous processing power by the combined effort of the computers of all miners in the world. The precise number of BTC miners is difficult to be determined, as it is always changing, with a growing trend. When they are not mining BTC, many use their computers for mining other cryptocurrencies as well, and a few are mining from time to time, for example, during the intervals when a miner's computer is not used for playing games. Eventually, the exact number of miners is not important, as the processing powers of their computers are different. On the other hand, however, it depends on the miners' readiness to invest in hardware, together with all expenses for electric power, among others.

These days, a greater number of miners are gathered in mining pools, as they are aware that mining alone is not profitable, because a block's verification process would take an unacceptable long time. Mining pools are joints of miners who acquire advantages such as more frequent verifications of blocks. Miners from a pool share the rewards for such verifications proportional to the miners' mining powers contributed to the pool. Apart from that, most BTC mining is done on the Application Specific Integrated Circuits (ASICs) in large, thermally-regulated data centers and warehouses with low-cost electricity. The owners of these warehouses rent the processing resources to miners.

Suppose we want to assure that a block is verified every 10 min. In case, Difficulty should be tuned relative to the computational power of the entire Bitcoin network, so that it can make a target number of calculations per second needed for achieving of the goal that average time for finding a hash below or target_value is ten minutes. $Hash rate = \frac{N(Difficulty)}{10\text{min}}$

$N(Difficulty) = \frac{2^{256}}{target\value}$

## 1.2 Blockchain 11

### 1.2.1 Proof of work

The answer to the previous questions and to how new BTCs are issued can be found in the applied concept, PoW. The concept of PoW is not new. It has been used in computer networks with client-server mechanisms as a measure to discourage Denial of Service (DoS) attacks and other abuses as spam. The main property of PoW is its asymmetry – a client's relatively hard work compared to the ease with which the result can be checked. In the case of blockchain, the result of the very difficult job of finding a valid hash by a successful miner (i.e., mining pool) is easily verified by other nodes.

It has already been mentioned that the trustworthiness of the blockchain also contributes to the decentralization. Let us remember that the blockchain, as a DL of transactions blocks is realized using nodes, that the computers connected to the BTC network. BTC full nodes are the computers running the BTC Core client with the complete blockchain. A transaction is a process of sending BTCs from the BTC wallet (i.e., address) of a sender to the BTC wallet of a person who receives money. Money senders broadcast transactions to the network. Full nodes validate transactions and relay to other nodes by broadcasting messages across the network. Within this decentralized infrastructure (P2P), a set of miners collect transaction records, verify that each transaction is correct and not conflict with previous transactions. They also check that the sender (whose identity is hidden behind an address) does not move more BTCs than is contained in the address (passing through the entire blockchain) and perform other functions.

Each miner independently collects valid transactions and aggregates them into a candidate block. Then, a miner attempts to solve his candidate block through the PoW, that is, through the process of finding a valid hash (as described in Section 1.2). The first miner who successfully solves a candidate block broadcasts his solution to the network (including other miners) to be added to the blockchain. The key thing is here that the new block will be added to the blockchain only if more than 50% of miners say it has been solved correctly. Before saying that, miners have to make their calculations on the solved block, comparing their results to the solution that was broadcast.
