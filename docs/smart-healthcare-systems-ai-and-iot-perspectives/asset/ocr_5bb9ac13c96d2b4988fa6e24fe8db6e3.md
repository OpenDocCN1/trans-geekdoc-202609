# Review of ML Algorithms for Diabetic Prediction

AI calculations were utilized in the review to anticipate the risk of Type 2 Diabetes. Six calculations, including Strategic Relapse (precision 74%), K-Closest Neighbor (exactness 70%), Backing Vector Machine (precision 74%) Guileless Bayes classifier (exactness 69%) Choice Tree (exactness 70%), and Arbitrary Woods classifier (exactness 75%), are utilized in this review.

Muhammad Extell al., the use of supervised machine learning to predict diabetes is the focus of this paper, which also stresses the significance of early disease detection and treatment to prevent serious complications. The authors described how the diabetes-causing factors are incorporated into the machine learning model. The study findings demonstrate that the machine model accurately predicts diabetes. The authors concluded that healthcare professionals could use machine learning to prevent and manage diabetes.

The study mentions that different learning algorithms were compared to see how accurate they were when classifying similar data. The authors claim that the Pima Indians Database, a Kaggle-accessible public dataset, provided the study’ data. Consequently, it is likely that k-nearest neighbors, decision trees, logistic regression, support vector machines (SVMs), and other machine learning methods were used to classify diabetes data in the study. The suggested model classification used the Pima Indians Database dataset, which predicts diabetes on a variety of health characteristics using two machine learning algorithms—k-nearest neighbor (k-NN) and Naive Bayes. The authors compare the accuracy, precision, and recall of these two algorithms and conclude that Naive Bayes performs better than k-NN in all areas.

Huna Naz al., the author addresses the need for early diabetes detection and lifestyle interventions to mitigate its devastating consequences. In order to build a system for diabetes prediction using the PIMA dataset, this research used advanced machine-learning techniques to leverage healthcare data, including electronic records and omics data. The results show that classifiers like Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), Naive Bayes (NB), Decision Trees (DT), and Deep Learning (DL) have outstanding accuracy rates ranging from 90% to 99%. Notably, DL has the best accuracy (98.07%), which gives healthcare providers access to a predictive tool. These discoveries open the door for the creation of a robotic early detection system.

Traditional diagnostic methods require patients to visit a center, which is both time-consuming and costly (Febrian et al., 2023). A system that accurately predicts a patient's level of diabetic risk is the goal of this research. The model utilizes characterization strategies, including Decision Tree, Artificial Neural Network (ANN), Naive Bayes, and Support Vector Machine (SVM) algorithms.
