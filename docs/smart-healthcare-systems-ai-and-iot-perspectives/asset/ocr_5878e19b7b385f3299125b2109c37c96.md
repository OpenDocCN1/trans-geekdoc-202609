Environmental monitoring systems were installed in both residential and commercial areas to track atmospheric variables, contributing to a comprehensive understanding of health factors. The dataset, combining environmental and health data, is crucial for AI-driven analytics to detect patterns and guide medical interventions.

The study utilized a large dataset of biometric data collected over three months, including measurements of oxygen saturation, body temperature, heart rate, blood pressure, and pulmonary function, using various medical devices (Martinez-Martin et al., 2020). This extensive data collection allowed for continuous monitoring of the patient's cardiovascular and respiratory health.

The dataset, structured as 43-element pandas DataFrame, included twelve columns health indicators (Lu and Mao, 2021). Despite some missing data due to device malfunctions or non-compliance, columns like FEVI and PEF were largely complete, essential for pulmonary function assessment (Sayal et al., 2023). The pandas 'describe()' function provided a statistical summary of health metric, aiding in initial data analysis.

Data processing excluding certain columns with significant values and translating columns from English to Italian (Riva et al., 2020). A SimpleImputer from the sklearn library was used for mean value imputation to handle missing values. Risk assessment was added to the dataset, calculating a score for each patient based on clinically significant thresholds for health metrics.

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) involved visualizing correlations between health metrics using a heatmap (refer to Figure 2.1 and Figure 2.2) and differentiating patients risk for pulmonary conditions through various charts. Histograms and boxplots (refer Figure 2.3) were used for a detailed examination of the dataset, revealing the distribution characteristics of each metric.

For predictive modeling, the dataset was split into training and test datasets. A stratified splitting strategy ensured distribution of the target variable, and feature normalization was performed using z-scores (Rattan et al., 2005).

Model evaluation tested various machine learning models like Logistic Regression, Random Forest classifier, K-Neighbors classifier, Decision Tree classifier, Gradient Boosting classifier, Support Vector Machine, and Gaussian Bayes. Each model's predictive performance was assessed using accuracy scores and confusion matrices. The evaluation highlighted the need for additional validation to ensure the models' relevance to new data (refer Figure 2.4 and Figure 2.5).
