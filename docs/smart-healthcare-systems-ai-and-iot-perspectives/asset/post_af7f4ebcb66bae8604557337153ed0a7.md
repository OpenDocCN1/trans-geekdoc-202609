
## 9.6.3 DATA MANAGEMENT AND POWER ISSUES

The heterogeneity of medical devices running different applications in the AIoT system creates a data management problem, such as storage and incompatible dataset formats created by various legacy third-party systems. Moreover, the large volume of data (big data) generated needs to be stored and analyzed. The need for scalable and intelligent learning algorithms for processing such large amount of patient data becomes vital. Finally, most devices in the AIoT have low power capacities, which affect the operational time (Riya et al., 2023).

## 9.6.4 BIAS

AI-enabled health suffers from the dangers of prejudice and inequality because such systems, by design, learn from the information fed into them, leading to the absorption of biases from supplied datasets (Alaban et al., 2023). Regardless of how the AIoMT systems are trained on accurate, representative datasets, bias will still be reflected, as noted in a study where an AI system trained on healthcare datasets learns to recommend lower painkillers to patients of African American race. Such a decision is purely based on systemic bias instead of biological traits.

## 9.7 LIMITATION OF AIOMTs

Regardless of the benefits of AIoMT, some limitations are identified and discussed subsequently. As a result of the ongoing development of technology, business processes in every industry, including medicine, are undergoing continuous change. One of these industries experiencing this problem is the healthcare industry. In the coming years, the IoTs, IoOMTs, and generally AIoMT will bring countless opportunities for the healthcare industry. Using AIoMT, we are able to derive additional value from the data we acquire. The IoOMT improves the effectiveness and quality of the results delivered by various devices. However, there are few few that are encountered in every technological progress, including data privacy concerns, security issues due to the use of wireless medical devices, the cost of these AIOMTs, and inaccuracy while handling real-time medical data and operation.

## 9.8 FUTURE RESEARCH DIRECTION

The adoption of AI in the healthcare sector through its convergence with the IoMT is transforming healthcare delivery in aspects such as patient experience, how medical workers practice medicine, and how drugs are being manufactured. Soon, AIoMT is expected to improve the precision of wearable devices and their ability to collect, store, and interpret data patients' health conditions. Consequently, this will allow medical workers to engage with their patients with the help of telemedicine to improve healthcare service delivery. Overall, the new means of getting healthcare services will drastically reduce the healthcare costs incurred for all groups of patients.

Because of how AI-Enabled IooT operates is unknown to layman, the policies and legal frameworks that govern its use, especially in the healthcare sector, will have to be instituted to promote confidence in its use and protect citizens from any fallout from its deployment. The European Union has made initiatives in that direction through its quest to come with the AI Act, which is aimed at promoting uniformity in the governance of AI technologies in different sectors, among the health sector. The other initiative is Pan-Canadian AI strategy, which, along with European one, will help medical community monitor and explainability of AI technologies and how their use impacts different contexts. This will need to be addressed. AI technologies are capable of learning patterns and modifying their recommendations in ways not envisioned by their creators. Through a combination of standards and regulations, the accuracy, reliability, and health use of AI technologies will need to be addressed as the technology evolves. Adopting a community-led approach by medical workers, technologists, and other stakeholders is one way that can be adopted to develop standards for data collection and testing of AI-enabled technologies. IoMT trends such as voice assistants (Google Assistant, Apple Siri, and Amazon Alexa) proved useful in responding to COVID-19-related questions and emergencies.

## 9.9 CONCLUSIONS AND SCOPE

Due to its potential to transform the delivery of medical care, the IoMT has rapidly gained the interest of researchers since its inception. IoMT devices allow us to monitor medical records and take the necessary safety precautions. IoMT plays a significant role in boosting the efficiency and precision of electronic devices in the medical industry. In this chapter, a comprehensive examination of electronic equipment in IoMT has been conducted. For convenience, identified and extensively used medical equipment, including electronic signals for IoMT, are highlighted. In addition, IoMT's architecture demonstrates significant development by utilizing intelligent sensors to collect, aggregate, and manage data for intelligent healthcare. To IoMT, the patient experience is improved, and workflows are optimized. Patients benefit from increased participation and fewer in-person appointments. Providers can better assist patients now that they have access to accurate data, diagnostics, and more effective time management. This chapter is limited in that it cannot cover many issues related to electronic devices and IoMT.
