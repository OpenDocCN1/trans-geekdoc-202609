A comparative study was conducted between the cloud-based and edge-based fall detection process using the Whoops system and found that edge-based fall detection serves better with good scalability for large-scale data as presented in Figure 22.1.

The IoTE-fall system contains four major modules (Yacchira et al., 2017); first as wearable devices, secondly, a wireless communication network followed by a third, cloud services, and finally, the IoT gateway. The wearable devices are connected with MEMS's sensors, to calculate the body movements of elderly people to transmit to the IoT gateway using a low bandwidth wireless network (Singh et al., 2021). The received data is analyzed by the IoT gateway using an ensemble RF classifier to detect the falls rapidly and responds to the healthcare professionals and the people concerned in a fraction of the time. The cloud service plays a major role in securing data transmission through quick authentication and data modelling. The IoTE-fall architecture is represented in Figure 22.2.

The review of the fall prediction and prevention (Rajagopalan et al., 2021), is based on recent trends and some future directions (Kaur and Bhambri, 2019). They categorised the fall prediction as context-aware and wearable devices free prediction. Context-aware includes some of the ambient sensors such as cameras, vision, and infrared sensors to organise the daily activities of elderly people. Where wearable devices use sensors as accelerometers and gyroscopes to collect the data to analyze the acceleration magnitude, body posture, and sensor velocity to obtain the highest fall sensitivity and lowest false positive rate. The core conflict in fall detection and prevention includes evaluating performance among elderly people who fall frequently, user-centric design, secured data transmission (Haritha and Anitha, 2023), and energy optimisation. There is a need for data fusion from the ambient sensors, interface designs, environmental fall risk factors, and clinical assessments based on reliable data transfer using an IoT-enabled prevention system for elderly people. ## 22.2 RELATED WORKS

Human fall is one of the leading causes of mortality among the elderly. Accidental incidents in the elderly can result in numerous irreversible disabilities or even death (Manna and Anitha, 2023b). Fall detection has become a crucial research issue in the healthcare domain, which requires more dependable and effective methods for intelligently classifying fall activities. The physical consequences of falls are increasingly fatal, and the risk of falling increases with age. A design (Chowdhary et al., 2023) to acquire some level of the senior citizen monitoring system with the help of IoT sensors and Python framework for the elderly was created. Tracking objects and detecting a person’s fall reduce the probability of emergencies with the help of cameras. A camera-based peer-to-peer (P2P) network for detecting falls (Hu et al., 2019), spreading alert events, and distributing captured media broadcasts in an elderly person’s activity location was demonstrated. Multiple Wi-Fi and IoT-enabled cameras can transmit this information to identify human posture using Long Range (LoRa) and mpu-9250 sensors to collect data and transmit it to a cloud using random forest machine learning algorithms were proposed (Song et al., 2021). Various state-of-the-art challenges (Kumar et al., 2017), quantifying movement-related problems faced by the elderly and preventing difficulties through interpretation to accommodate information communication technology (ICT) based IoT solutions were addressed. A monitoring system of various IoT sensors (Al Husaini et al., 2022), as well as the development of a health surveillance system and a failure detection system that alerts users if the normal condition changes was recommended. A fall detection system was proposed based on deep learning Convolutional Neural Networks (Manna and Anitha, 2023b) and the lime AI model to classify fall activity (Jeshayree et al., 2022).
