## 15.3.4 GRADIENT BOOSTING CLASSIFIER (GBC)

For the prediction of heart disease, GBC, introduced by Friedman [35], is a new kind of ensemble learning method in which error correction between weak learners combines to produce accuracy. The GBC process is a mathematical series of iterative processes designed to gradually enhance the model's performance:

- 1. Initialization: For each observation, $F_0(X)$ is the initial prediction, which is often a straightforward forward learner. The residuals are calculated as $r_0 = Y - F_0(X)$, where $Y$ is the binary outcome that determines the presence or absence of cardiac disease.

- 2. Sequential Model Building: Create a new learner, $h_n(X)$, for every iteration in order to forecast the residuals, $r_{n-1}$. To update the model, add the learning rate, denoted as $v$, is a hyperparameter that governs the influence of every weak learner—$F_n(X) = F_{n-1}(X) + v \times h_n(X)$ is obtained.

- 3. Gradient Descent:

The pseudo-residuals are defined as $R_n = \frac{\delta K(Y, F_{n-1}(X))}{\delta F_{n-1}(X)}$, in this case,

K symbolizes the loss function that quantifies the variation between the anticipated and exact values.

Adjust struggling to the pseudo-residuals:

```
$h_n(X) = \arg \min \sum K(Y, F_{n-1}(X_j)) + h_n(X_j)$
```

—Revise the model: $F_n(X) = F_{n-1}(X) + v \times h_n(X)$.

- 4. Iteration: The Gradient Descent and Sequential Model Building methods must be repeated for a given number of times or until the convergence condition is satisfied.

Using the GBC has many advantages in heart disease prediction. It's really good to find intricate relationships between patient data and is able to provide a pretty complete picture of the causes, with how different factors are related to the changes of heart disease. Because the algorithm has an iterative structure, it not only makes its accuracy excellent but also allows it to handle complex relationships and prevent overfitting. Using GBC's intrinsic feature significance measurement, healthcare practitioners can discern the important factors influencing prognosis for heart disease. In fact, the GBC is a powerful tool in the arsenal of predictive modeling for heart disease. The model can make more effective predictions, and the whole is better than its parts. It uses ensemble learning [32].

## 15.3.5 RANDOM FOREST CLASSIFIER

The Random Forest classifier is a particularly effective and flexible machine learning technique that is especially suited for predicting cardiovascular disease. The Random Forest approach creates a number of decision trees, and takes the average of their results to make predictions more accurate, like an ensemble of trees. For prognosis of cardiac disease, this method has several advantages. It manages the crowded relations between age, blood pressure, cholesterol, and other risk variables of cardiovascular. Random Forest avoids overfitting by averaging the predictions of many decision trees, making a model more robust and wisely used. Furthermore, the algorithm provides a natural means for measuring feature contribution to predicting heart disease. The feature importance displayed by the Random Forest classifier also makes it possible to provide more accurate and understandable predictions for effective clinical decision-making, as well as handling nonlinear relationships and missing data. This makes it a good analytical tool in the field of cardiovascular health [35].
