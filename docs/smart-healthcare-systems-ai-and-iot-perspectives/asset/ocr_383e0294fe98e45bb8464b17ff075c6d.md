A comparative study was conducted between the cloud-based and edge-based fall detection process using the Whoops system and found that edge-based fall detection serves better with good scalability for large-scale data as presented in Figure 22.1.

The IoTE-fall system contains four major modules (Yacchira et al., 2017); first as wearable devices, secondly, a wireless communication network followed by a third, cloud services, and finally, the IoT gateway. The wearable devices are connected with MEM's sensors, to calculate the body movements of elderly people to transmit to the IoT gateway using a low bandwidth wireless network (Singh et al., 2021). The received data is analyzed by the IoT gateway using an ensemble RF classifier to detect the falls rapidly and responds to the healthcare professionals and the people concerned in a fraction of the time. The cloud service plays a major role in securing data transmission through quick authentication and data modelling. The IoTE-fall architecture is represented in Figure 22.2.

The review of the fall prediction and prevention (Rajagopalan et al., 2021), is based on recent trends and some future directions (Kaur and Bhambri, 2019). They categorised the fall prediction as context-aware and wearable devices free prediction. Context-aware includes some of the ambient sensors such as cameras, vision, and infrared sensors to organise the daily activities of elderly people. Where wearable devices use sensors as accelerometers and gyroscopes to collect the data to analyze the acceleration magnitude, body posture, and sensor velocity to obtain the highest fall sensitivity and lowest false positive rate. The core conflict in fall detection and prevention includes evaluating performance among elderly people who fall frequently, user-centric design, secured data transmission (Haritha and Anitha, 2023), and energy optimisation.
