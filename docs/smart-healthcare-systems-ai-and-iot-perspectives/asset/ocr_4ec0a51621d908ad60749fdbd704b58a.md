# 15.1 INTRODUCTION

As the incidence of illnesses rises (caused by complex interactions between genetic and lifestyle factors) [1], the use of highly specialized technologies to analyze clinical data is becoming more important than ever. Heart disease is one of the most significant threats to one's health, despite the fact that there are many other health issues. It is the main cause of death on a global scale. The World Health Organization (WHO) considers coronary artery disease (CAHD) to be a public health problem because it is responsible for the deaths of millions of people each year. In its most basic form, heart disease is characterized by an obstruction in the regular functioning of the heart [2, 3]. One of the potential causes of heart disease is the constriction or obstruction of the coronary arteries [4–6]. One more alarming factor of coronary heart disease is that up to twenty-five percent of victims can die without any outcast [7]. This demonstrates how challenging it is to treat the illness, which is so important to place an emphasis on prevention. If left untreated, CAHD greatly compromises heart health and increases the risk of deadly attacks. Early detection of illness signs and prompt diagnosis can significantly lessen the effects of CAHD [4].

Numerous values that raise the chance of developing heart disease have been found by researchers. The following are primary risk factors including hypertension, diabetes mellitus, raised blood cholesterol, smoking, and tobacco use [8]. Furthermore, acknowledged as risk factors, alcohol and stress are known to contribute, however it is unclear how much of a role they play or how often they are. is crucial to emphasize that some of these variables may be changed, providing a way to control and intervene [9].
