### 12.1.1 COVID-19 PANDEMIC AND VENTILATOR DEVICE

Following the novel outbreak of SARS-CoV-2 in December 2019, the world was faced with a global pandemic. The World Health Organization (WHO) determined that COVID-19 was a pandemic. During the early phases of the pandemic, the demand for ventilator devices was critical around the world. In April 2020, experts estimated that approximately 880,000 ventilator devices would be required globally. The number of current and additional respiratory devices according to countries is shown in Figure 12.1.

Individuals infected with COVID-19 were admitted to the ICU in Wuhan, China. At the time of admission, 56 percent of their patients needed non-invasive ventilation, and 76 percent needed mechanical ventilation (Iyengar et al., 2020). The number of ventilator devices according to countries is shown in Figure 12.1 (STATISTA, 2020).

According to the World Health Organization in April 2020, it is stated that the total number of ventilator devices in 41 countries in Africa is approximately 2000, and these numbers are far from what is required in an effective fight. Even one year after this situation, the ratio of ventilators to total COVID-19 cases has not changed significantly in these regions, and this situation has been creating a significant strain on healthcare systems. Even though the need for ventilator devices has decreased at this stage of the pandemic due to increased production capability and strengthening of supply chains of ventilator devices, only half of the ventilator devices required in India can be obtained.

Researchers are investigating creative methods to meet the requirement for low-cost manufactured ventilator devices. In a comprehensive study of 1042 COVID-19 patients, ventilator devices are one of the most important factors in the fight against the COVID-19 disease and the positive effect of the use of ventilators on the death rate is clearly seen (Nicholson et al., 2021). Because of the stated importance of ventilator devices for the COVID-19 pandemic, researchers have been looking for innovative solutions that will reduce ventilator production costs and speed up production.
