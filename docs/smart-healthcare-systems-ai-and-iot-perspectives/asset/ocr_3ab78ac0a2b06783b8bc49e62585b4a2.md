The ventilator device is powered by a battery consisting of nine 3.7V 3000mAh 18650 Li-ion cells or by connecting directly to the grid plugs (Franco, 2015). The parameters regarding the designed ventilator have been identified according to the MIT-Event project. Rates have been determined to meet the urgent need of an adult person during the COVID-19 pandemic. Arduino Uno microcontroller was used to control the device and it has six analog to digital converter (ADC) with ten bit (Cameron, 2021). These ADC inputs are used to adjust flow rate and detect pressure anomaly in the ventilator device for this. A pressure sensor is used to detect airflow to determine possible faults in the ventilator device and the model of used pressure sensor is MPXV505GP (EI Majid et al., 2020). It has been determining level with a maximum 2.5 percent error between zero and 50kPa range. With this pressure sensor, the device gives a warning by a buzzer as for a possible obstruction or blocking of oxygen and this error is on Liquid Crystal Display (LCD) with the information (Shrivastava et al., 2021). PWM motor driving technique has been established between the duty cycle of the generator signal and the amount of flow. In addition, buttons are used to start the ventilator device and stop it in case of emergency. DTH11 temperature and humidity sensor has been added to the ventilator system to instantly observe the temperature and humidity of the environment on the LCD panel. An optional Covid electrostatic filter has been added between the NIV mechanical ventilator mask and the air connection to reduce the risk of disease transmission through the air released into the environment by the COVID-19 patient (Ari et al., 2016). Thanks to these filters, the heat and humidity needed in the respiratory tract are created.

# 12.2.1 3D Printer Technologies and NIV Mechanical Ventilator Design

The body frame and hose connections of the ventilator device in this study were created with a 3D printer and it is suitable for improvement. Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) 3D printing technique has been used for the NIV ventilator devices against COVID-19 for this (Mwema and Akinli, 2020). The process of producing all components of a ventilator device with a 3D printer took approximately 16 hours (Singh and Bhambri, 2023). The operation of the proposed lowcost ventilator device has been tested for approximately 600 hours and it has been observed that it works successfully in this process. These values were obtained with a conventional 3D printer produced for a home user. The ventilator device produced consists of several components and its case structure and connection points were produced by 3D printing technology (Bhambri et al., 2023). The diameter of the hose connections in the device is designed as 22 mm and they are suitable for the standard ventilator connections. 3D-printed stabilizers were used to keep the components stable inside and prevent their movement during transport. As a result, a light construction with a stiff body and composed of 3D recyclable material has been created.
