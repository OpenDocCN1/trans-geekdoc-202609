The technology proposed by (Mastorakis and Makris, 2012) can accurately and consistently detecting falls without accounting for false actions (such as lying on the floor) in real-time. Calculations on both speed and inactivity are used to determine whether a fall fallen. Their main contribution lies in evaluating nobody based on changes in the 3D bounding box. This eliminates the need for prior scene by directly leveraging the 3D bounding box because the set of identified activities is sufficient to fully implement the fall detection procedure. However, with this, detection is only local, and a carer is not alerted. It lacks extensive monitoring capabilities, prolonged data transmission, and the ability to gather data for large-scale fall pattern mining because it is not a part of bigger telemonitoring centers.

To identify falls, Nghem et al. (Nghem et al., 2012) suggest a head recognition method using depth from Kinect camera, which identifies fall locations and based on these locations, identifies individual falls by an algorithm designed to calculate head speed, the body content, and the distance between them and the floor. Scanning the head on the exterior contour of the human searches for falls positions quickly; thus, head detection is a variation of the histogram of oriented gradients for the head. However, scalable computing resources are needed for modern telemedicine, which aims to meet the increasing demand for monitoring elderly patients. Nghem et al. just justified the data and not consider alerting the caregiver for the recovery of the sufferer.

Salah et al. propose a fall identification system for older people that use an ML architecture with accelerometers (Salah et al., 2022). The results obtained yielded 96.3% accuracy and 96.4% specificity. In addition, execution times of less than 40 ms are recorded. However, the range of different sensors used is restricted in this case due to hardware constraint. For the elderly residents of nursing homes, De Rae et al propose a Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)-centered fall recognition and alerting system (De Rae et al., 2022). The authors point out that current systems depend on wearable devices, pressure sensors, gyroscopes, and accelerometers. These devices, however, may not be appropriate for those with cognitive impairments may be difficult and uncomfortable for adults to work. The subject-wise accuracy, or percentage of wise decisions, ranges from 84.89% to 92.65%.
