## 15.2.4 TRANSPORT LAYER

The transport layer of the WSNs manages end-to-end connections. In the case of WSNs in the healthcare and medical industry, where delay-free delivery of critical data is crucial, it becomes imperative to have a reliable data delivery mechanism. The prime targets for the adversaries are the endpoints such as the transmitting end and receiving end of the data. There exist a variety of reliability mechanisms for different layers such as automatic repeat request (ARQ) at the MAC layer.

The transport layer in WSN-based IoT technology encounters two big threats such as flooding and de-synchronization. Flooding is defined as memory exhaustion at the end nodes by recurring connection requests. This is generally done to exhaust resources or reach a maximum limit by initiating new connection requests. Several solutions to this problem exist, such as setting up constraints before creating a connection in which each node has to solve CAPTCHA or puzzles. Another solution is to set up a maximum request limit from a single user to track and eliminate multiple requests from a single user. The idea behind these solutions is to exhaust the adversary of their resources and time. One major loss from such attacks is that legitimate requests are not addressed quickly in the event of an attack.

De-synchronization leads to the destruction of an existing connection or communication channel, thereby preventing the end users from exchanging data. This is done by spoofing messages repeatedly to retransmit missing frames or reporting error reception that causes resource depletion at the receiving end. For the prevention of such attacks, authentication of each exchanged packet is done, which makes the path secure.

Since a lost packet of information in the healthcare industry would lead to serious implications and could lead to misinterpretation, end-to-end reliability in medical sensing networks is of utmost importance and may require packet-level or event-level solutions. The wide implications of the loss of data can be observed in the case of periodic traffic from vital-sign-sensing devices where packet-level reliability is important. Mechanisms to control congestion and flow in the transport layer are becoming the end of the hour yet are difficult to find. For example, the need to deliver a sufficient number of packets led to the development of event-to-sink reliable transport (ESRT) protocol for event-reporting applications, while on the other hand, other protocols addressing and relaying periodic collection of data such as physiological reporting and vital-sign reporting such as electrocardiogram (ECG) where every packet needs to be delivered are being developed [9].

## 15.2.5 NETWORK LAYER

In the discussion so far, we have realized that optimizing delay and energy efficiency are the two underlying challenges. With the increasing number of multimodal and multimedia-based sensor networks, the challenges including optimizing data traffic congestion and mechanisms to channel emergency data traffic over continuous data traffic have increased. For this purpose, load-balancing protocols are still under study and development phase to check rate control and avoid data congestion along with several data compression techniques also being inculcated into the systems.

The other challenge of resource limitation in the network layer is also being researched. It is observed that when sensors are subjected to heavy utilization, they tend to overheat and may cause serious damage to the device components as well as the user of the device. These problems become specifically pronounced in in vivo sensor networks. Thus, the trade-off among data delay mechanisms, load balancing, data congestion and prioritization, and energy efficiency is continuing to vex researchers and developers.

The network layer is one of the most vulnerable layers in the WSNs, owing to which there occur multiple attacks to breach security and secure high-priority data. The attacks on the network layer can be broadly classified into the following categories:

- Sybil Attacks: It is a type of malicious node attack in which the node acquires multiple identities and creates virtual redundancy in the network. This kind of attack is still to be addressed in the most fault-tolerant protocols. The few countermeasures that could be introduced to counter such attacks include limiting the number of neighbors and applying a strong verification scheme to avoid Sybil attacks although they may prove to be inefficient.

- Sinkhole/Wormhole: The sinkhole is a type of attack in which the adversary accesses data passing through the malicious node, while a wormhole is a type of attack in which the weak link between two nodes is attacked by the attacker. These nodes make the entire network prone to attacks since they make the entire network vulnerable. Authentication of nodes and monitoring are the only two available defense mechanisms against such attacks.

- Selective Forwarding: This kind of attack occurs when a malicious node is accessed by an adversary to drop certain or all data packets being transferred using the malicious nodes and may choose to forward a few other selectively introduced new data packets. This creates unorganized data reception at the receiving terminal. The blackhole attack is a specific kind of selective forwarding attack in which all the data passing through the malicious node are dropped. The countermeasure for selective forwarding includes the identification of malicious nodes by rigorous monitoring and the introduction of redundancy in data transmission by relaying data through multiple paths.

- Hello Flood Attack: In such an attack, the attacker sends hello messages to the network. The attacker may use a high-power antenna to broadcast these messages through nodes. This causes a legitimate node to connect with a malicious node and may eventually die. To prevent such attacks, bidirectional verification is recommended to verify the radio range of the sender since such attacks are done using high-power antennas and signals from beyond the radio range.

- Acknowledgment Spoofing: Acknowledgment messages can be spoofed in a network by a malicious node by overhearing packets to another node that may not be active or in range. This can be done using a dead or weak connection. Encryption of messages and sequence number verification are available methods used to tackle such attacks.

These kinds of attacks become a pivotal point of discussion since the data being transmitted via nodes in healthcare monitoring require a high degree of privacy and authentication. The introduction of spoof messages in such networks may lead to interrupted or unorganized data collection at the receiving end, especially in case of emergencies. Also, a higher degree of encryption and security needs to be maintained, which is done either by end-to-end encryption or by hop encryption and by mechanisms such as TLS/SSL, IPSec, and anti-DDoS.

## 15.2.6 APPLICATION LAYER

The application layer of the WSN-based IoT has three important roles to serve: organizing the sensor data procured, analyzing meaningful insights from the organized data, and coordinating among various layers of the WSNs. Organizing the sensor data is the foremost task and requires that the data are sorted and assessed based on medical terms. The up-and-coming field of machine learning and artificial intelligence has been playing a significant role in doing this. With self-learning techniques and analyzing the various parameters and vital signs for each user, the machine learning algorithms are paving the way for tailor-made autonomous healthcare monitoring systems.
