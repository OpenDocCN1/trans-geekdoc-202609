## 15.1 INTRODUCTION

The wireless sensor networks (WSNs) are highly useful and have a pervasive presence in all walks of life, ranging from the highly sophisticated healthcare industry to extremely hazardous manufacturing industry conditions due to their wireless nature of transmission and small size. This section begins by presenting a general overview of the contemporary WSN- and IoT-enabled technologies. Then, it outlines the various design considerations to be taken into account for WSN-based IoT technology solutions. Lastly, it highlights the relationship between WSNs and IoT and demarcates the difference between them.

### 15.1.1 OVERVIEW OF THE CONTEMPORARY WSN- AND IOT-ENABLED TECHNOLOGY

The discovery of wireless communication in the late 1800s was a historic event and could undoubtedly be marked as the birth of a new era in the field of information and communications technology (ICT). The world has since then witnessed unprecedented advancements that have touched nearly every human being by its ubiquitous presence. Another such major event that indented world history was the development of the Internet in the late 1990s. One of the fields that rose to prominence with it was the Internet of things (IoT).

The IoT can be defined as the collection of interconnected devices that can be used to gather, track, monitor, and exchange data over the Internet. With IoT growing at an unprecedented rate, the development of WSNs soon took place. The WSNs can be defined as an assortment of several homogeneous and heterogeneous sensing devices connected to gather information about physical parameters from remote areas. The information collected is then transmitted in the form of data packets over the wireless mode of communication to the base station (or sink node).

In IoT-enabled technologies, every device is offered a unique IP address for the transmission of data. As the number of smart devices around us increases at a pace faster than human beings, the need for commercially available IoT frameworks has grown. The different commercially available IoT frameworks are being provided by different companies to support multiple devices like SmartThings by Samsung, Azure IoT Suite by Microsoft, HomeKit by Apple, Brillo by Google, and AWS IoT by Amazon. The above-mentioned list of IoT frameworks and their providers is not exhaustive but remains a few of the most commonly used IoT frameworks across the industry.

WSN- and IoT-enabled technologies have been pioneering not only in terms of their reach but also with the multitude of industries in which they have found applications. Before discussing the contemporary WSN- and IoT-enabled technologies, it is imperative to note that these devices are resource-constrained and used for sensing surrounding parameters. For discussion, the similarities shared by IoT and WSNs are that they consist of sensing devices that are capable of procuring and transmitting ambient physical variables and are used in hazardous environments for tracking, monitoring, and surveillance.

Applications of WSN-enabled IoT technologies are as follows:

- Home Automation Systems: The interconnection of home appliances with smartphones has greatly revolutionized home automation. It has allowed the home appliances to be used and automated from a single touch on smartphones from anywhere in the world. This has been especially beneficial for the elderly and young [1].

- Smart Agriculture: The growing world population has led to an even greater rate of food consumption, hence creating a burden on agriculture. The agriculture-based industry in India has been largely unorganized and remained to stay distant from the advancements of technology so far. But with rising demands and innovation around the corner, smart agriculture is no longer a distant dream for an average farmer in India. WSN- and IoT-based technologies are now being used to assess soil quality, humidity, moisture content, and nutrients in the soil to predict the best kind of crop to be sown in a particular season upon consultation from scientists remotely along with automating the irrigation needs of the farmers. This can pave a path for efficient water utilization and fertilizers in a country where depleting groundwater levels and excessive use of fertilizers degrading the soil quality are a matter of grave concern [2].

- Smart Traffic Management System: Effective management of traffic on the road has been one of the remarkable areas of operation for WSNs and IoT. Since with the growing burden of cars in metropolitan cities, effective tracking of cars has been a major hurdle and raising an alarm by central governing authorities for emergency services like an ambulance or fire-police truck to avoid a certain route has been made possible. Also, this technology can be used to track repeated violators of traffic rules using an inbuilt sensor in a car [3].

- Smart Health Monitoring Systems: One of the leading users of WSN- and IoT-enabled technology happens to be the healthcare and medical industry where the patients' data can be remotely tracked by medical staff and their progress of treatment can be tracked effectively. The use of smart health monitoring systems accelerated because of the need to reduce the cost for repetitive daily checkups for patients suffering from chronic illness and to monitor and track the biomedical data, i.e., blood sugar levels, blood pressure, and heartbeat rate, and to notify in case of emergencies, especially for the elderly.

- Flood Detection and Avoidance System: In areas where flood is a commonly occurring natural disaster, these systems help to detect and raise early red flags. This is done using the level of humidity, temperature, water flow, and flow level. One of the sensors used in this technology is the float sensor that comprises a water rotor, a Hall effect sensor, and a plastic valve body. It is used to check the flow of water in a water body [4].

Apart from the above-mentioned fields, this technology has also found applications in industrial processing, smart mobility, and smart grid for electricity transmission, public safety, and environmental monitoring.
