# 15.2.1 HARDWARE LEVEL

Among the various layers of challenges encountered in WSN-based IoT technologies, the foremost problem arises at the hardware layer. The hardware layer of WSNs comprises physical devices such as a sensor, a transmitting unit, a processing unit, and a battery to support the entire hardware setup. All these components can be compacted to a size less than a centimeter cube in volume. For research, two of the biggest challenges are energy efficiency and processing capacity of the hardware unit.

The devices and components at the hardware level become resource-constrained and have to operate at limited energy and storage and processing abilities. Due to this, high-level encryption techniques and similar advanced security solutions, although seem necessary, are impractical in real-world technologies, which lead to security concerns arising at this level. Data encryption is largely limited by processing capacity since it is necessary to ensure the confidentiality of data. This in turn requires that the lightweight encryption techniques be used instead to reduce the burden over the resources. Furthermore, data privacy is another challenge. Privacy, in general, is largely governed by the consent of the user before the data are being recorded and analyzed. Similarly, it is critical to ensure that the data being transmitted from a patient may serve as critical information for attackers, and they must be protected and secured while being recorded, transmitted, and analyzed.

Coupled with these challenges is the vulnerability to attacks such as denial of service (DoS) and physical interferences that might hamper the authentication of data.

+   • Unobtrusiveness: One of the most common applications of WSN-based IoT technology in health care is fall detection systems. The patients carry these sensors attached to their bodies to detect the motion of them and to inform the medical staff upon the fall of them. Unobtrusiveness becomes a major challenge here. The need for less bulky and unobtrusive devices has become more and more prominent, much like bandage-type ECG sensors or watch-shaped activity recorders. The research and study of sensor devices integrated into fabric has garnered attention from a gamut of researchers and papers [5].

+   • Sensitivity and Calibration: Ensuring the sensitivity and calibration in different geographies for long durations of time is one of the major challenges in hardware devices. For devices used in the medical field, robustness in harsh environments and against physical factors needs to be taken into account for designing safe and durable sensing devices. Sweat is known to deteriorate the calibration of transducers in body-worn devices and degrade sensitivity. Suggestions to introduce self-calibrating algorithms similar to triaxial accelerometers have been made [6]. There is an urgent need for low-maintenance, highly sensitive, and self-calibrating sensors for enhancing the scope of the healthcare systems.
