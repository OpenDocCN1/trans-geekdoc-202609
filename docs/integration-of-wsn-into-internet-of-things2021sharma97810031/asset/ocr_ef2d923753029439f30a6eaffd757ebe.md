# FIGURE 10.1 Security requirements.

+   * Authentication makes sure that when connecting to a service you are whom you say you are. This is different from identification as you not only have to tell who you are but prove it also.
+   * Authorization ensures that you have a sufficient amount of access/privilege. This may be implemented by using the principle of least privilege (PoLP) [5]. The principle, first introduced in 1975, says that access should only be granted to an extent that is necessary for the user to complete the work and no more than that. This ensures that in case of a compromised authentication, the damage is minimum and restricted to a specific domain.
+   * Accounting refers to having a record of everything that a user does on any given service while logged in. This means having irrefutable proof of actions performed by a user and nonrepudiation. In case of any calamity, the damage can be traced back to the attacker or malicious user with the help of digital fingerprint tracing or other measures put in place to have a record of actions.
