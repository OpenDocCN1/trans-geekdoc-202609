In Ref [24], the objective was to develop, implement, and characterize a device to monitor energy in the low-voltage single-phase electrical network. The prototype developed was based on a development kit with a DSP and a printed circuit board with additional circuits. The DSP was programmed with the necessary algorithms for detecting and classifying various disturbances that usually occur in the electrical network. The power supply was also implemented to power the entire system.

The power quality analyzer was able to detect disturbances in the electrical network and, at the same time, did not consume high computational resources. For that, the methods of analysis of the power quality are implemented in a DSP to monitor the network in real time.

The application of all the algorithms, whether for signal analysis or disturbance detection, works correctly. All of them produced viable results and, fundamentally, contributed to the proper functioning of the analyzer, that is, simulated or real disturbances of the electrical network were detected, with well-defined amplitude and duration values. Morphological operations are commonly used for image analysis that can play an important role in power quality measurements. Due to the way the algorithms were implemented, the threshold levels can be easily adjusted to levels of greater sensitivity, if the programmer wishes so [24].

The method proposed in Ref [25] detects transients, distortions in the waveform, disturbances of short and long duration, and disturbances such as sags or swells. The method was developed with special attention to its suitability for implementation in a DSP and real-time operation. The proposed method employs two sets of algorithms. Digital filtering and mathematical morphology are used in the case of transient and waveform distortions and in the case of short- and long-term disturbances (such as sags, swells, or interruptions), the analysis of the root mean square (RMS) effective value of the voltage is used.

The performance of the method proposed in Ref [25] was verified during a long time monitoring of a single-phase power system. In comparison with the solutions based on the discrete wavelet transform (DWT), the proposed method is simpler and requires less computational expenditure. While DWT requires decomposition to higher levels of decomposition (for example, sixth level), the presented method uses a simple high-pass filter and calculates the RMS value of the input signal to detect a wide range of disturbances in QEP (Quality of Electric Power). All algorithms used by the proposed method are suitable for implementation and execution in a DSP based on the test system.
