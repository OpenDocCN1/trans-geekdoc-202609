
## 4.1 INTRODUCTION

Today, networking and communication represent the basic need of every human being. As a consequence, it becomes more and more significant to make them as secure as possible (Stallings et al. 2012). The term secure in this context means that it is impossible for an adversary to eavesdrop, modify, or influence the communication between two or more participants in any other way. Nowadays, securing communication lines becomes one of the most important research areas in the fields of information and network security. Disciplines that have the highest contribution to advancement in the field of network security are computer science and mathematics. They initially brought the majority of security algorithms, practices, systems, etc., that are used today. With technology becoming more advanced, as well as security threats, other disciplines got interconnected with this topic. One of them is physics. One example where physicists contribute to security is the development of the quantum computer security algorithms (Bernstein, 2009). Compared to today’s modern computers, quantum computers have significantly more computing power.

Using the property called “quantum superposition”, they can do numerous tasks in a single moment. To explain how fast they are, we can imagine a very complex password like this: “7tn)hNLDMVh+<_yuGs]/UMHD(?”. Taking the assumption that today’s computers can crack at the speed of 17042497.3 kps (key password attempts per second), it will take an infinite number of attempts to crack a password like this. For much simpler passwords than the example above, “als@dagl@”, it would take 5 centuries, 6 decades, 3 years, 11 months, 3 weeks, 1 day, 15 hours, 53 minutes, and 27 seconds. Even for a simpler password, it will still take a lot to crack it by a brute force attack. On the other hand, with a strong quantum computer, the complex password given above could not be cracked at the moment (Kalai, 2016). Global security that could be cracked at the moment is not adequate, so the security researchers need to address this problem before it appears globally.

The examples mentioned above should emphasize the importance of getting more fields to research and improve communication and network security difficulties. For example, even nontechnical fields, like psychology, should cooperate and contribute. Among security experts exist the common phrase: “there is no patch for human stupidity”. By this, psychologists mean that however much they improve the system and process security, they cannot ever significantly address the mistakes humans make by regularly communicating with each other. On the other hand, psychology specialists could give greater enhancements and directly increase security in a specific environment.

One of the newer fields that is increasingly involved in communicational and network security is artificial intelligence (AI). Because of the ability to automate the majority of the tasks (e.g. object recognition and stock market predictions), AI is widely used in every segment of network security. An example where AI is used today is network intrusion detection (Azar et al. 2017). Precisely, it is about detecting when a non-authorized person gains access to the specific private network. Here, the AI algorithm regularly monitors the behavior of regular users, and if a certain uncommon behavior is detected, an alarm occurs, and the system is eliminating the intrusion out of the network. Overall, most of the researches where AI is used implies to exploit hardware leaking or bad algorithm implementation (both in software and/or hardware). Having these assumptions, the overall network is already compromised even without applying the proposed method from every paper. As a consequence, most of the systems are already secured from the aforementioned assumptions, so today and surely in future, the proposed techniques would not work when a real-world problem appears.

The most appropriate and, at the same time, the most realistic approach would be to come up with a method that will use pieces of information that are available to everyone during the communication (even to the intruder). One example is an encrypted message (cypher). Bearing this in mind, we developed a methodology that allows examining the security of the given encryption algorithm using only its cypher messages that are available to everyone during the communication between two or more entities.

The main contribution of this chapter is a specific novel methodology by avoiding long-time modification of the original implementation to match the algorithm and hardware implementation. For cryptanalysis to test the newly created (or some already existing) algorithm, one only needs to use methodology implementation from this chapter and to change the reference algorithm. So, every existing (and nonexisting today) algorithm could be tested fast with this methodology to further ensure the security of the referent algorithm. Using this methodology and at the end exported model, an adversary only needs to gather cypher messages encrypted by the referent algorithm and use the model to gather information about the secret key. The core of the proposed methodology presented in this chapter is to determine if it is possible to find out certain information about the secret key, using only encrypted texts. To be precise, it is possible to identify a certain part of the secret key (or even the whole secret key) using artificial neural networks (ANN). More details about the proposed methodology will be presented in this chapter.

Besides details about the stated methodology, a survey in the field of research will be presented with relevant proposals where AI is used in the field of cryptographic security. Also, to make it easier to reproduce the given results, details of the environment setup will be shown. Furthermore, detailed results will be explained as well as datasets that were used, the process of generating training data and the overall process of training the network.

The rest of the chapter is organized into six sections. Section 4.2 discusses the related work in cryptanalysis and security evaluation using ANN. Section 4.3 discusses the theoretical overview of the research with an emphasis on the basics of cryptanalysis and ANN. In Section 4.4, we present a research methodology that represents a new approach to examine the security of cryptographic algorithms. Section 4.5 discusses experimental results arranged into four categories determined by the dataset type (character, word, sentence, and essay), where the essay category has additional subcategories determined by the type of data augmentation (full cypher length, partial cypher length, and random partial cypher length). Concluding remarks and proposals for further research are given in Section 4.6.

## 4.2 RELATED WORK

AI utilizes ciphertexts to gather meta-information about the encrypted message and uses them to create the reference profile of the victim. One approach is to determine which encryption algorithm has been used to encrypt the message during communication (Vetrivel and Shantharajah, 2015; Khavidi and Momtazpour, 2010; Sharif et al. 2010). With this information, an intruder has higher odds to breach into the secured system.

Vetrivel and Shantharajah proposed a technique for distinguishing the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) algorithm from the other encryption algorithms (Vetrivel and Shantharajah, 2015). They used neural networks combined with Lorenz information measure to define relevant differences between the cryptographic algorithms. Khavidi and Momtazpour used a similar approach. The difference is that they tested multiple machine learning algorithms: linear discriminant analysis (LDA), support vector machines (SVM), and K-nearest neighbors (KNN). As a result, they managed to get a 30% error with a 0.5 pattern-repeating rate (Khavidi and Momtazpour, 2010). They presented that the error rate decreases as the pattern-repeating rate increases in the cyphers. Another approach in classifying cryptographic algorithms is proposed by Sharif et al. To confirm the results, they used eight AI algorithms as well as four cryptographic algorithms with their variations: AES (128, 192, 256), DES, IDEA, and RC2 (42, 84, 128). Results showed that the rotation forest algorithm had greater overall accuracy. On eight class problems, they obtained an accuracy of 30.83%, whereas on four classes, the accuracy was 53.33%. The most common approach that researchers engage while using AI in the field of network security is to exploit hardware flaws of encryption algorithms (Heuser and Zohner, 2012; Lerman et al. 2015; Hospodar et al. 2011; Maghrebi et al. 2016; Gilmore et al. 2015). Strategies in this domain provide excellent results, but it is difficult to use them in the real-world scenario. Heuser and Zohner found a way to exploit hardware leaking using the SVM algorithm (Heuser and Zohner, 2012). They tracked how much electricity does the hardware implementation of the algorithm consumes, and using that information, they managed to train SVM classifiers to gather additional information.

Yousef-Azar et al. had different perspectives on applying AI. They utilize auto-encoders to automatically find anomalies in the vector space of the network traffic. Those anomalies represent a possible intruder in the network, which is automatically detected. With this approach, they managed to get 83.3% accuracy in detecting intruders.

Aggarwal et al. (2019) focused on creating a low-cost smart device that can be used to monitor water quality at different places. Soni and Bhushan (2019) discuss how several algorithms of machine learning can be used to overcome the popular issues faced by cybersecurity. Also, there are various types of machine learning methods used in cybersecurity (Puri and Bhushan, 2019).
