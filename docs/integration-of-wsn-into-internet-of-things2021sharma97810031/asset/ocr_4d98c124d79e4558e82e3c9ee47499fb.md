Hussain et al. [41] proposed an improved algorithm for demand response (DR)
synergized with renewable energy sources and energy storage system optimal dis-
patch (DRSREOD)-based home energy management system (HEMS). This heuris-
tic-based algorithm considers DR, photovoltaic availability, the state of charge and
charge/discharge rates of the storage battery, and the sharing-based parallel opera-
tion of more than one power source to supply the required load. Due to the rapid
increase in demand for electricity, developing countries are facing large-scale load
shedding (LS). An innovative algorithm is also proposed for the optimal sizing of
a dispatchable generator that can supply the DRSREOD-based HEMS during LS
hours to ensure an uninterrupted supply of power.

# 8.3.4 Environmental Data

Data streamed suspiciously at high volume in distinct speed and assemblies are
usually constituent of data from numerical weather predictions [18]. The equa-
tions that are used to solve the data are very complex and are usually nonlinear.
Another application where big data and environmental sciences come together is
the public and environmental health. Monitoring the data through methods such
as environmental sampling is one of the tried and tested methods of today, but
with big data, we can compute this information more accurately and can ensure
that information is computed more rapidly and precisely. On the topic of geo-
graphic data in environmental science, it is important to note that simple methods
such as cartography and geographic surveying to even modern applications like
geographic information surveying (GIS) all flourish on big data [42]. The ability
of GIS at its core solidifies to be able to not only present statistical data but also
utilize and consolidate it.
