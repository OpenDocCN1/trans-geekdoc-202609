The so-called smart cities are among the main aspirations of modern society. Among the premises of the smart cities are the interconnection of communication systems, the monitoring of services, and the rational and optimized use of energy. In addition to the concept of smart cities, the concept of a smart grid or Smart Grid appears. This concept is broad and can be approached from different aspects, but always to ensure, efficiently and economically, a sustainable energy system with low losses and high levels of quality and security of supply [7–8].

The introduction of the Smart Grid concept produces a convergence between the energy generation, transmission and distribution infrastructure, and the digital communications and data processing infrastructure. The latter functions as internet of equipment, interconnecting the so-called intelligent electronic devices (IEDs) and exchanging information and control actions between the various segments of the electrical network. This convergence of technologies will require the development of new methods of control, automation, and optimization for the operation of the electrical system, with a strong tendency to use distributed problem-solving techniques based on the use of multiagents [9–11]. Electric power utilities in Brazil necessarily need to prepare for this convergence.

In addition to the accurate measurement of energy consumption at consumer units, it has also been a factor of concern for energy concessionaires to determine the energy quality indices that they supply, according to Resolution No. 424 of December 17, 2010, from the National Electric Energy Agency (Brazil). Thus, it is essential to develop systems that can maintain continuous monitoring of the electrical quantities of the distribution network at various points of it and also that are capable of efficiently signaling possible failure events and their locations [12–14].

Thinking about the end user, the electric bill does not discriminate how much each electrical equipment in the residence consumed and the effect of that on the electric bill. This could be an efficient way to encourage changes in habits and/or the replacement of equipment with others of lower consumption. Based on this principle, and as described above, this study is motivated to propose a method to analyze and identify the main electrical loads in a home. For this and aligned with that motivation, a new algorithm is proposed for the disaggregation of loads based on the current measurement of electrical equipment in a home. A database was created to train the main loads present in Brazil, and a method that uses wavelet transform coefficients and neural networks allows the load to be distinguished.

Therefore, this chapter aims to create a current signal database for domestic loads and proposes a technique for identifying such loads, a necessary step for the disaggregation of loads in the Smart Grid context. The disaggregation of the proposed technique is based on the use of neural networks and wavelet transform. The identification of electrical loads aims to discover what equipment is connected to utility power. Thus, it is possible to calculate separately for each device the electricity consumed. The results obtained from the information derived from the proposed algorithms are discussed and presented. The algorithm processing and load identification by wavelet and neural networks were developed using the MATLAB® environment. The results prove the efficiency of the proposed technique.

### 9.2.1 POWER QUALITY

The principles of energy quality are established mainly to discover and avoid points of waste and reduce losses that generate less profit to concessionaires and, with this, discourage greater investments in sectors that are dependent on this source, mainly the industrial sector. In recent years, there has been a great emphasis on modernizing the industry, with more automation and more modern equipment. This generally means energy-efficient electronic control of equipment that is often much more sensitive to voltage supply deviations than its electromechanical predecessors. Thus, like light bulbs blinking in homes, industrial customers are now more aware of minor disturbances in the electrical system. There may be a large amount of money associated with these disorders [15,16].

With the increase in nonlinear loads in the electrical system, the problem of harmonic distortion has become increasingly significant. Some conservation measures, such as the application of frequency inverters to control the speed of motors, dimmers, use of compact fluorescent lamps with electronic ballasts, power controllers for showers, among others, can interfere in the quality of the electrical system, in order to increase losses and even cause damage and loss to consumers and concessionaires [17,18].

The aspects considered for the quality of the product in the permanent or transitional regime are as follows:

- a. the continuity of supply, quantified by employing the duration and frequency of interruptions (DEC [equivalent interruption duration per consumer unit] and FEC [equivalent interruption frequency per consumer unit] indices) of energy supply:
- b. the appropriate voltage level, obtained by controlling the minimum and maximum voltage limits of consumers, as well as an index that assesses the frequency of violation of the same limits for connected consumers;
- c. the distortion of the waveform by assessing the presence of harmonic and interharmonic frequencies;
- d. voltage regulation around the nominal values, even with variable loads, quantifying the amplitude and frequency of voltage fluctuations;
- e. the nominal frequency of the network, which is currently established through the energy balance between the producer and consumer system;
- f. the power factor, whose current minimum value (0.92) is regulated by employing specific legislation;
- g. the imbalance between phases, given as a percentage value of the negative and zero sequence components, measured concerning the positive sequence [19,20].

### 9.2.2 RESEARCH ON POWER QUALITY

In Ref [21], a harmonic estimation technique was proposed for implementation in a low-cost digital signal processor (DSP), which addresses a new technique for the harmonic estimation (amplitude and phase) with reduced computational complexity for implementation in a DSP. The technique uses the demodulation of the signal followed by filtering. As a result, harmonic estimations in 1/2, 1, and 2 cycles of the fundamental component were obtained. Results obtained with synthetic electrical signals and real (measured) signals indicate that the proposed technique is quite interesting for implementation in a low-cost DSP. The suitability of the technique for implementation on a finite precision is assessed using the DSP TMS320F2812.

In [22], a system for detecting and classifying disturbances in the quality of electrical energy (QEE) was presented, which is based on the decomposition of the voltage signal into two new signals, referring to the fundamental component and the error signal. Then, it uses eighth-order statistics to extract representative parameters from each class to simplify the detection and classification algorithm. As a detector, an algorithm based on Bayes’ theory was used, and to implement the classification algorithm, an artificial neural network was used. The system was tested in simulations for six classes of disturbances, presenting an overall efficiency close to 100% for such disturbances.

In another study [23], the problem of detecting disturbances in electrical power quality monitoring applications when the voltage signal interval analyzed corresponds to submultiples of a cycle of the fundamental component of the electrical power signal was presented. Besides, a technique for detecting disturbances in single-phase signals, when the discrete version of the same consists of at least 16 samples, was proposed. This detection technique makes use of a second-order notch filter (band rejects) to decompose the voltage signal into two components. From these components, parameters based on higher-order statistics are extracted and, next, they are used as a vector of parameters by the Bayes detector based on the maximum likelihood criterion.
