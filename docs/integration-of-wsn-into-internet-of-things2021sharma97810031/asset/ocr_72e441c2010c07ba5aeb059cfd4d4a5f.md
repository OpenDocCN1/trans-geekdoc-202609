Although safeguarding the volume of quantum computing-derived data seems to be an inherent part of blockchain's evolution, we ought to consider the danger that quantum computing poses in terms of blockchain safety [18].

Quantum computing can expand deciphering the encryption of blockchain. This poses a serious threat to the so-called impregnability of blockchain. The most serious concerns come from the finance sector, the alma mater of the healthcare-related blockchain [31]. Given that quantum computers are faster than conventional computers, they can accelerate the way passwords are broken or encryptions are decrypted.

Hacking with quantum engines casts doubt on the encrypted networks’ safety standards. The expected ramifications on encrypted blockchain networks can have a considerable impact rocking the boat as far as the protection of unauthorized access is concerned [31, 34].

Quantum computers have been perceived as the Achilles' heel of the bitcoin blockchain. Their overwhelming computing acceleration advantage theoretically enables them to disrupt the activity of a wide variety of encryption methods including decentralized blockchain ledgers.

Quantum computers can accelerate the production and activation of cracking algorithms. Bitcoin was based on one of the most resistant “uncrackable” algorithms so far. However, from this perspective, quantum computing as a tool of human ingenuity increases the odds of dissolving blockchain’s privacy [24, 35].

Generally, blockchain can be a target of quantum computing because of financial interests, either independent of healthcare data or related to healthcare data, when it comes to insurance companies or employers.

Regardless of the grounds of penetrating blockchain’s impregnability, in case it is achieved, healthcare data will be at stake [36].

# 16.7.2 Legal Limitations

The blockchain feature of the powerful data uniformity caused inviolability where the data, once warehoused in the blockchain, cannot be changed or removed. Meanwhile, if the register contains health data, these individual data would be safeguarded by regulations for keeping confidentiality; several laws would not permit storing individual data permanently. The EU’s General Data Protection Regulation has reinforced the person’s rights to ask for deleting such data. A rule among the privacy principles of the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development that inspired several regulations for safeguarding data allows personal removal of these data. For the susceptibility of health data, those who intend to implement blockchain for warehousing them should not disregard such a lawful obligation [7].
