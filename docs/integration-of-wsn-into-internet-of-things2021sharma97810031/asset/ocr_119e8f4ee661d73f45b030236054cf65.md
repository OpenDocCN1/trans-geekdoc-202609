# 13.3 MACHINE LEARNING: BASIC CONCEPT

Generation after generation, humanity has progressed further and further. This progression is mainly due to the discovery of new things that reduced human labor. This was at its peak when we developed the best tools and complex control systems (computers). However, there is a limit to everything, as not everything can be hardwired or written as ones and zeros. Similarly, as is the case for computers, there are no general sets of rules that can be programmed to differentiate between a cat and a dog or a cow and a buffalo. This is because a computer doesn't see a cow as a cow; rather, it sees it as an image with many numbers to it—in simple words, a collection of colored dots. Thus, it is nearly impossible to write a set of rules to find the difference. However, this is not the end of advancements. With the rise of modern computers, we are now able to imitate a human brain in the form of numbers as well. Although our capabilities are far off from modeling the whole human brain, we can imitate enough of it to do very menial tasks that traditional computers cannot carry out. For example, we can now classify a cat as a cat and a dog as a dog with computer software. To explain the process behind it, we can take the example of how a child learns to ride a bike. They do not learn it in one day. It is done by trial and error. With each failure, they gain more insight until they can ride it flawlessly. This is how a computer learns, but in a more intense manner. We start by breaking the image down into numbers and applying mathematical operations on it. If the computer guesses correctly, we give it a metaphorical pat on the back. If it fails to do so, we inform the computer of its mistake and let it correct itself. This process happens many times until the applied mathematical operation improves enough through clever methods of trial and error. This method of teaching computers to learn is known as machine learning (ML) [12]. Although this is a great technology, the complexity of a trained model (the final mathematical operation after training) is far too complex for us to understand. In practice, we call it a black box because we know how we have trained it but we do not know how it is using the numbers and mathematical operations to do its job. Thus, it is sometimes hard to predict what this system will do in unusual cases. This introduces many of the problems that we face today with ML, with the biggest being the uncertainty. This technology is also being coupled with IoT. It is a technology that will make human life easier. The main idea is to build a network of Internet-connected devices to control/automate the tasks we do ourselves, from turning on the fan to watering the plants in your home from wherever you are. In this aspect, machine learning plays an important role in IoT, as it is the backbone of the predictive models and smart algorithms used in IoT devices that help them work automatically without any input from the user, analyze patterns and sequences in large data sets, and give us predictions or suggestions based on those patterns and sequences. These devices coupled with ML tools will automate everything there is to automate. For example, some farmers use IoT-based devices to run their farms and monitor their livestock.

Now that we have some idea about AI and ML, let us learn about the technology that has brought about a complete revolution in the technological world—the “Internet of things”.
