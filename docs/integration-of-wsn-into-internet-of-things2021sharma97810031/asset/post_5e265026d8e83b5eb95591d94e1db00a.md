### 10.1 INTRODUCTION

Security, defined as a state of being free from danger or threat, is derived from the Latin word securus—which means “free from care.” It is defined by the standards put in place to secure something from any kind of danger it may face. Today, we use security in our day-to-day life, be it while emailing, instant messaging, cloud computing, interactions with Internet of Things (IoT) devices, or general interactions with protected domains over the internet. This type of security is known as computer security, information security, or cybersecurity [1], better defined as measures of protection taken to prevent damage or disruption intended to cause harm to the whole structure, or in part, of services provided, hardware, software, or electronic data. The need for security is now important more than ever before as we transcend from the traditional way of computing to more interconnected computing with the use of wireless networks, smart devices, and the IoT.

Information security or cybersecurity has proven to be crucial for the diverse variety of use cases in which the same can be implemented. According to Ref [2], this field affects various contemporary societies, from low-level, such as smart identifications, usage to much higher, complex, and sophisticated operations and interventions, such as military operations.

Here, we discuss how the level of intensity of security depends on the value of data or a thing you are securing, that is, the degree of security should be directly dependent on the value of information in question and how much damage can the information do if or when it is exposed. The thing about security is that we have to be prepared to deal with, and minimize, the damage in case of an intrusion. As it is highly unlikely to know the exact type of attack our system is exposed to, it is hard to be well prepared. Therefore, we propose a probabilistic approach to define measures to be taken based on the available data and past experiences to minimize the impact and damage as low as possible. We also discuss general parameters for implementing such an approach.

This chapter is structured as follows: Section 10.2 introduces security primitives for establishing the security along with the basic need of security. Then in Section 10.3, we discuss security paradigms for wireless sensor networks (WSNs), IoT, and cyber–physical systems (CPS) one by one listing security requirements and briefly discuss the architecture of these as well. We discuss in Section 10.4 about security challenges in WSNs, IoT, and CPS with using some of the attacks that might occur on each layer and how to prevent these attacks as well. In Section 10.5, we introduce our framework for the security of CPS. This framework provides a unified, scalable, and resource-efficient solution for each layer described in our architecture. Finally, Section 10.6 concludes the chapter.

### 10.2 SECURITY PRIMITIVES

The reliance on security is a key factor to ensure a balance of the ability to protect data circulation, integrity, and availability. This is known as the CIA triad [3]— confidentiality, integrity, and availability.

- Confidentiality is the ability to enforce a level of access for authorization.
- Integrity is to ensure that only authorized personnel can modify, update, or delete data to protect it from unauthorized modifications.
- Availability is the ability to serve the data when needed and as needed.

Now, to achieve these requirements and maintain balance, we may utilize the AAA framework from many available standards. The AAA or triple-A model refers to authentication, authorization, and accounting [3,4]. These requirements are well illustrated diagrammatically in Figure 10.1:

- Authentication makes sure that when connecting to a service you are whom you say you are. This is different from identification as you not only have to tell who you are but prove it also.
- Authorization ensures that you have a sufficient amount of access/privilege. This may be implemented by using the principle of least privilege (PoLP) [5]. The principle, first introduced in 1975, says that access should only be granted to an extent that is necessary for the user to complete the work and no more than that. This ensures that in case of a compromised authentication, the damage is minimum and restricted to a specific domain.
- Accounting refers to having a record of everything that a user does on any given service while logged in. This means having irrefutable proof of actions performed by a user and nonrepudiation. In case of any calamity, the damage can be traced back to the attacker or malicious user with the help of digital fingerprint tracing or other measures put in place to have a record of actions.

Security mechanisms are based on the prediction or type of an attack which is going to occur at any point in time. However, the nature and severity of the attack are not known to security analysts. Therefore, security mechanisms are based on several aspects to ensure proper prevention from the attack. Figure 10.2 shows a general timeline where a potential threat or vulnerability may become an attack. Hence, primarily all security mechanisms are designed based on the threat or vulnerability.

The aspects on which security mechanisms are imposed depend upon the strength of security required. The security strength depends upon the prediction of any possible attack and the measures taken to counter it. Security strength is measured by assessing the ability to handle attacks. Security is proportional to strength, and strength is based upon various factors which we have classified as the value of a particular asset, the environment, risk associated with assets, the severity of the attack, and prediction.

1. Value of an Asset: this refers to the utility and worth of an asset that needs to be secured. If something is highly classified and for the internal circulation of an organization, it is the value that may be treated as high as possible. For example, draft patents for designs, state secrets, and code word documentations. On the other hand, if something should be publicly accessible and available in the open domain, placing security on accessing it seems a bit over. For example, it would be of little to no use if we placed full authentication and authorization mechanisms for users just trying to use a search engine. Therefore, security should be positively correlated with the value of an asset.
2. Environment: the place where an asset is deployed is a known environment. A secure environment may refer to an environment where chances of attack and intrusions are low. If the environment is vulnerable for the asset, like public domain, the security should be tightened.
3. Risk Associated: certain assets come with their own set of risks. These risks are associated with assets. For example, hardware comes with a risk of physical access and the software comes with a risk of faulty code. Rarely, an asset comes without risks; therefore, we must take a note of these assets and possible associated risks.
4. The Severity of Attack: refers to a measure of how big of an attack can be dealt with. The limit of tolerance can only be extended if it is known. Therefore, the tolerance of attacks and limits should be known.
5. Prediction: refers to predicting how likely an attack is to be made by looking at previous attempts, both failures and successes, which are similar in nature and environment. This is where knowledge and experience weigh a lot as this will allow benchmarking the security standards in place and help create a more secure environment.

The emerging applications of IoT show great potential. Figure 10.2 shows a flow of actions that takes place chronologically. Along with convenience from IoT devices comes the risk of exposure of personal artifacts and other identifiable information that can be used to either manipulate the device itself or use the data for other malicious usages. The complexity of these applications from access cards to intelligent device management [6] yearns for more security. The security cannot be ignored as a break in the security of an IoT device will lead to exposure of information and might affect the entire structure and have an unthinkable overall outcome [7].
