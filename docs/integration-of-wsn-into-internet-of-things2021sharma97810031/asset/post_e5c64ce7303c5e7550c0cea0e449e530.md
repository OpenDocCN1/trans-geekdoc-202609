## 15.2.1 HARDWARE LEVEL

Among the various layers of challenges encountered in WSN-based IoT technologies, the foremost problem arises at the hardware layer. The hardware layer of WSNs comprises physical devices such as a sensor, a transmitting unit, a processing unit, and a battery to support the entire hardware setup. All these components can be compacted to a size less than a centimeter cube in volume. For research, two of the biggest challenges are energy efficiency and processing capacity of the hardware unit.

The devices and components at the hardware level become resource-constrained and have to operate at limited energy and storage and processing abilities. Due to this, high-level encryption techniques and similar advanced security solutions, although seem necessary, are impractical in real-world technologies, which lead to security concerns arising at this level. Data encryption is largely limited by processing capacity since it is necessary to ensure the confidentiality of data. This in turn requires that the lightweight encryption techniques be used instead to reduce the burden over the resources. Furthermore, data privacy is another challenge. Privacy, in general, is largely governed by the consent of the user before the data are being recorded and analyzed. Similarly, it is critical to ensure that the data being transmitted from a patient may serve as critical information for attackers, and they must be protected and secured while being recorded, transmitted, and analyzed.

Coupled with these challenges is the vulnerability to attacks such as denial of service (DoS) and physical interferences that might hamper the authentication of data.

- Unobtrusiveness: One of the most common applications of WSN-based IoT technology in health care is fall detection systems. The patients carry these sensors attached to their bodies to detect the motion of them and to inform the medical staff upon the fall of them. Unobtrusiveness becomes a major challenge here. The need for less bulky and unobtrusive devices has become more and more prominent, much like bandage-type ECG sensors or watch-shaped activity recorders. The research and study of sensor devices integrated into fabric has garnered attention from a gamut of researchers and papers [5].

- Sensitivity and Calibration: Ensuring the sensitivity and calibration in different geographies for long durations of time is one of the major challenges in hardware devices. For devices used in the medical field, robustness in harsh environments and against physical factors needs to be taken into account for designing safe and durable sensing devices. Sweat is known to deteriorate the calibration of transducers in body-worn devices and degrade sensitivity. Suggestions to introduce self-calibrating algorithms similar to triaxial accelerometers have been made [6]. There is an urgent need for low-maintenance, highly sensitive, and self-calibrating sensors for enhancing the scope of the healthcare systems.

- Energy: As discussed earlier, one of the greatest dilemmas in WSN-based IoT is energy consumption and its effective utilization. For medical purposes, carrying rechargeable batteries along with sensors can be cumbersome for the patients and elderly owing to its weight and the need to recharge after use. Designing low-power sensors especially in the case of body-worn sensor devices is one of the biggest hurdles for engineers. The speculations to use solar-powered sensors have proven to be futile for the devices worn under clothing. Therefore, the development of body-heat scavenging techniques for recharging batteries is on the rise.

- Data Acquisition Efficiency: Data acquisition efficiency of sensors is of utmost importance in healthcare devices to ensure a quick and effective response from healthcare systems. This need is manifested in 3-lead ECG where the device is sufficiently equipped to identify cardiac arrest. Furthermore, the analysis of data along with procuring them is essential. Synchronization of the device with time stamps and chronological ordering of events play a crucial role in the research and analysis of patients.

## 15.2.2 PHYSICAL LAYER

Like a radio communication system, the WSNs work on transmitting modulated electromagnetic waves for transmitting data between two nodes. The physical layer of the WSN-based IoT is responsible for frequency selection and generation while modulating and demodulating encrypted digital data. The physical layer of the sensor networks is most prone to two major types of attacks, namely jamming and tampering.

Jamming is a kind of attack where the adversary disrupts or interferes with the frequencies of the channel of communication. It is a highly widespread form of attack and often involves the use of power to disrupt signals as well as networks. Tampering is a kind of attack in which the adversary physically damages or accesses the node either by replacing the device physically or breaking it or by node interrogation to gain access to the key to access sensitive information. Although these kinds of attacks are relatively less widespread in the medical and healthcare industry-based WSNs, these have to be protected against natural causes for ensuring due data encryption and privacy.

Various methods are used to protect the devices against such attacks, especially tampering, by installing tamper-proof packages. A possible preventive measure includes allowing certain authorized individuals to operate and physically handle the devices while in operation and users to be advised strictly against tampering and maintaining security measures. Another form of defense is self-destruction where the device destroys the memory and data procured upon being compromised. Although this is an effective method to prevent data loss, it can be expensive and may lead to disruption in the system.

The following are the challenges faced at the physical layer:

- Signal Characteristics: The WSNs operate using a relatively low bandwidth for communication. The new sensor nodes operate at nearly 250 kbps using duty cycling mechanisms that help lower the power consumption even further by reducing the actual available bandwidth. Although this serves the dual purpose of lower power consumption and transmitting data at a lower bandwidth, this method proves to be inefficient in the case of transmitting diagnostic medical imaging data which instead requires data transmission at a rate nearly up to MBps. This has led to the development of compression algorithms that compress the multimedia-based data while maintaining low energy consumption and low processing capacity and memory.

- Error Resilience and Reliability: With low bandwidths being used for sending and receiving data which require low transmission power for consumption, sensors tend to have lower signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs). This is an obstacle to useful data transmission across nodes in a network, especially in the medical industry. With reducing SNRs, the chances of bit error increase and further reduce the network coverage area from each node. Lowering energy consumption for communication and increasing bandwidth thus become major trade-offs for error-free medical data transmission. The Time Division Multiple Access (TDMA) based protocol has been proposed as one of the potential solutions for error resilience and reliability. In this network coding technique, every sensor transfers data through two relays, and then, the relay nodes XOR the data packets before relaying. This technique has proven to be of value by reducing packet loss rate through simulations, and trials for real-time transmission of EEG and ECG-type physiological signals are under way. These proposed solutions are being tested and researched to be used for reliable data transmission in body area sensor networks (BASNs).

- Interoperability: Often the sensing nodes used for transmission relay information among them and are integrated while being connected to other devices operating at different frequencies. To exacerbate the trouble, the different devices interconnected in a network might use different protocols along with different frequencies. This is a common challenge faced when the devices use unlicensed industrial, scientific, and medical radio bands that lead to interference. The devices are being developed for successful interoperability and for widening the scope of pervasive and interconnected healthcare systems.

## 15.2.3 MAC Layer

The MAC layer of WSNs deals with the standards intended for short-range and wireless communication. In the case of their applications in the medical industry, the standards are designed to operate at low complexity, low cost, low power, and high reliability for durable and robust performance within or around the human body. These often involve technologies such as wireless body area networks (WBANs), wireless personal area networks (WPANs), GPRS/UMTS technology, and radio frequency identification (RFID) for low energy transmission and wearable equipment.
