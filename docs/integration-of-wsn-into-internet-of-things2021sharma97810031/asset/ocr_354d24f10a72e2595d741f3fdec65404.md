# 15.1.2 DESIGN CONSIDERATIONS OF WIRELESS HEALTHCARE MONITORING SYSTEMS

The wireless healthcare monitoring system being used in the modern day has a multitude of applications, each varying greatly from the other in terms of the type of data being procured by each sensing node for different networks. Therefore, a general overview of design considerations and attributes that play a role while designing a wireless healthcare monitoring system includes the following:

+   • Ergonomic Considerations: For a wireless health system, the design considerations include the durability of the device, wearability, degree of comfort, ease of movement, and unobtrusiveness. The above-mentioned attributes are important contributing factors for the devices that are generally worn by individuals whose regular physiological and biomedical data are being tracked continuously. The physiological parameters that are frequently monitored are inclusive of blood sugar levels, heartbeat rate, blood pressure, or electrocardiography (ECG); thus, maintaining close body contact with the sensor nodes is crucial without restricting the patient's mobility.

+   • Hardware Considerations: The WSNs are generally attributed to operating within limits of the available resources such as limited power supply and low computational power. These attributes form the key challenges that need to be accounted for while designing the hardware of the sensor nodes. A careful balance has to be achieved for the sensor nodes to operate energy-efficiently and to expend optimum power in transmitting signals without hampering the signal quality.

+   • Application Traffic Profile: Yet, another consideration that plays a pivotal role in designing the transmission frequency and signal bandwidth is the application traffic profile or the type of data being communicated. For example, a wireless system detecting motion and fall would not require the same bit frequency, delay sensitivity, and sampling frequency as in the wireless system monitoring electrocardiograph. Therefore, the transmission frequency and its related attributes are governed by the application traffic profile.

+   • Network Architecture: The application and location of the patient and his/her mobility also play an important role in designing network architecture. For a patient with low mobility and low data rates, a homogenous WSN architecture would suffice. In such situations, the patient is near an information sink, and therefore, a lower density of sensors and a simpler architecture for lower energy consumption are used. For patients with high mobility or those operating in areas of high density, the complex, heterogeneous architecture may be required along with a higher transmission rate, thus requiring higher power consumption, to serve a larger number of patients at once.

+   • Frequency Selection: To avoid mutual interferences with other WSNs operating in the same frequency range as other networks in the same coverage area, the considerations of frequency range tend to play a key role due to an overlapping frequency range.
