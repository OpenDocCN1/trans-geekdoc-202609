The wide variety of IoT devices and hardware profiles limits the ability to have standard encryption processes and protocols, since all IoT encryption must be accompanied by encryption key life cycle management processes, or even by blockchain technology. Once that administration becomes inadequate, overall security will be impaired [49–52].

IoT network security in environments with ubiquitous computing is a little more challenging than traditional network security, as there is a wider range of communication standards, protocols, and device features, which can present more complex problems. The main resources needed are traditional security tools, such as antivirus and antimalware, in addition to firewalls and intrusion prevention and detection systems [49–52].

However, still pondering the solutions that collect, aggregate, monitor, and normalize data, add sophisticated machine learning techniques, artificial intelligence, and big data, providing predictive modeling for anomaly detection, in addition to reducing the number of false positives. So, analytical security in the IoT will be increasingly necessary to detect specific intrusions in the environment of the “things” connected in environments with ubiquitous computing. Pondering those complex attacks that arise every day, which cannot be identified by traditional network security solutions, such as firewalls [49–52].

The cybersecurity with respect to the security aspect of IoT and ubiquitous computing involved in the communication between different systems is essential to protect the integrity of the data exchanged between the devices and the back-end software. Still considering criteria such as the use of complex passwords to reduce the effectiveness of brute force attacks; the adoption of safer standards to protect wireless networks; the monitoring of the entire network infrastructure looking for digital failures and intruders. Or same, the implementation of a firewall system; the quick update of the firmware of each gadget; the definition of rules for access and control of devices to limit the number of sensitive areas of the infrastructure that can be accessed. And even with relation to the adoption of data encryption to store information collected by devices and exchange data between devices securely [49–52].

In this sense, the success of these applications depends mainly on the ease generated for users and the transparency in the confidence in their security in environments with ubiquitous computing, since many of these communications and actions are generated automatically. IoT devices, regardless of their use with ubiquitous computing, complexity, or degree, are an attractive target for cybercriminals, as they collect private information about user behavior in areas such as finance, health, and education, among others [49–52].

Taking these precautions, IoT devices have a high level of reliability, since all connections need to be monitored dynamically, still considering whether the equipment can be isolated against vulnerabilities of IoT devices, even if cyber-attacks are performed, the data will have more cybersecurity against exposure [49–52].
