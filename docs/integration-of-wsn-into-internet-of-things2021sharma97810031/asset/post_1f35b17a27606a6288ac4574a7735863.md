In Ref [24], the objective was to develop, implement, and characterize a device to monitor energy in the low-voltage single-phase electrical network. The prototype developed was based on a development kit with a DSP and a printed circuit board with additional circuits. The DSP was programmed with the necessary algorithms for detecting and classifying various disturbances that usually occur in the electrical network. The power supply was also implemented to power the entire system.

The power quality analyzer was able to detect disturbances in the electrical network and, at the same time, did not consume high computational resources. For that, the methods of analysis of the power quality are implemented in a DSP to monitor the network in real time.

The application of all the algorithms, whether for signal analysis or disturbance detection, works correctly. All of them produced viable results and, fundamentally, contributed to the proper functioning of the analyzer, that is, simulated or real disturbances of the electrical network were detected, with well-defined amplitude and duration values. Morphological operations are commonly used for image analysis that can play an important role in power quality measurements. Due to the way the algorithms were implemented, the threshold levels can be easily adjusted to levels of greater sensitivity, if the programmer wishes so [24].

The method proposed in Ref [25] detects transients, distortions in the waveform, disturbances of short and long duration, and disturbances such as sags or swells. The method was developed with special attention to its suitability for implementation in a DSP and real-time operation. The proposed method employs two sets of algorithms. Digital filtering and mathematical morphology are used in the case of transient and waveform distortions and in the case of short- and long-term disturbances (such as sags, swells, or interruptions), the analysis of the root mean square (RMS) effective value of the voltage is used.

The performance of the method proposed in Ref [25] was verified during a long time monitoring of a single-phase power system. In comparison with the solutions based on the discrete wavelet transform (DWT), the proposed method is simpler and requires less computational expenditure. While DWT requires decomposition to higher levels of decomposition (for example, sixth level), the presented method uses a simple high-pass filter and calculates the RMS value of the input signal to detect a wide range of disturbances in QEP (Quality of Electric Power). All algorithms used by the proposed method are suitable for implementation and execution in a DSP based on the test system.

## 9.2.3 SMART GRID

Smart Grid is an electricity network that can efficiently integrate the behavior and actions of all users connected to it, in order to economically ensure a sustainable energy system with low losses and high levels of quality and security of supply [11,26].

Consumers will be able to exercise control over the use of the energy they consume and exercise their environmental responsibility, resulting in greater efficiency of electricity [26,27].

This revolution in current network standards, being more modern and efficient, brings countless benefits to all consumers of electricity. The Ministry of Mines and Energy of Brazil issued an ordinance, Ordinance No. 440, of April 15, 2010, creating a working group to analyze and identify actions that are necessary to support the establishment of public policies for the implementation of a Brazilian Smart Grid Program—Smart Grid.

Smart Grid makes this transformation possible, bringing new philosophies, concepts, and technologies that allow, for example, the use of the internet as a utility for the electrical network. Most importantly, it allows existing technologies from other engineering sectors to modernize the network to reach its full potential [27,28].

The introduction of the Smart Grid concept will produce a marked convergence between the energy generation, transmission and distribution infrastructure, and the digital communication and data processing infrastructure. The latter will function as internet of equipment, interconnecting the so-called IEDs and exchanging information and control actions between the various segments of the electric network. This convergence of technologies will require the development of new methods of control, automation, and optimization of the operation of the electrical system, with a strong tendency to use distributed problem-solving techniques based on the use of multiagents [28,29].

The construction of energy generation, transmission, and distribution systems based on the Smart Grid concept necessarily uses telecommunication systems to perform their essential functions [30,31].

As this is a change in the way energy is delivered to the consumers, the concessionaires’ relationship with the customers will be remodeled, and the amount of information available may change consumption habits and increase the quality of services provided. This technological change will affect several sectors of the economy, from industry to the specialization of the workforce, and could make the power grid more reliable [31,32].

Smart Grid is an electrical network that, intelligently, can combine the actions of everyone connected to it—producers, consumers, or both, whose objective is to efficiently distribute the sustainable, economical, and safe supply of electricity [32,33].

Within the context of Smart Grid, some new technologies appear that end up becoming outstanding characteristics of the network [33,34]. The interest in monitoring the QEE that travels through the transmission and distribution system has increased in the last two decades, since disturbances caused mainly by large consumers started to interfere in the energy supplied to other consumers, compromising sensitive loads such as communication, computer, and other systems.

In recent years, especially with the rise in globalization and privatization of the electricity sectors, there has been a race to establish indicators capable of evaluating energy quality in real time and that is valid for any type of installation or supply condition. What is sought is to extract instantaneous indicators that really express the quality of the energy at a given point of analysis, when the local conditions of voltage and current are very far from the ideals of supply and consumption [35,36].

Having obtained such indices, it is possible, employing preestablished limits, to monitor electricity locally or remotely, qualifying and quantifying the disturbances caused by one or more consumers and/or by the companies that have concessions for generation, transmission, and distribution of electricity. National and international standards present a series of definitions and recommendations that can and should be used to assess energy quality [35,36].

## 9.2.4 Smart House

According to Ref [37,38], the concept of a smart house is related to the term “domotics,” which results from the combination of the Latin word “domus” (house) with “robotics” (automated control of something). This last term enriches the system, making people’s daily lives simpler, satisfying their communication, comfort, and security needs. When home automation appeared (with the first buildings in the 1980s), it was intended to control lighting, air conditioning, security, and the interconnection between these three elements.

Nowadays, the basic idea is the same, the difference is the context for which the system is designed: no longer a military or industrial context, but a domestic one. Although still little known and publicized, for the comfort and convenience it can provide, home automation promises to have many fans. In this way, it allows the use of devices to automate the routines and tasks of a home. Normally, room temperature, lighting, and sound controls are performed, distinguishing it from the normal controls by having a center that controls everything, which is sometimes coupled to a computer and/or internet [38,39].

Within the concept of a smart house, the optimization and monitoring of consumption behavior of each equipment is a fundamental element, especially at the time when alternative and localized sources of energy production, such as those obtained by photovoltaic plates, will be used on a large scale [39,40].

## 9.2.5 Monitoring Systems

One of the areas of study within the Smart Grid concept is the possibility of discriminating the electric bill by equipment connected to the installation. From this, it is possible to know which equipment is consuming more and even analyze the efficiency of each piece of equipment installed in the residence. In this way, a more rational use or even the replacement of some equipment is possible [41,42].

The various works in the area have been researching ways to identify the equipment in use to allocate their share of the energy consumed. When techniques such as videos and recordings are used to determine the type of equipment, the technique is called invasive [43–45]. The big problem with this technique is exactly the issue of privacy. On the other hand, noninvasive techniques use sensors to determine the status of equipment, thus maintaining people’s privacy [46].
