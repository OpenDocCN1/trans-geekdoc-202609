# 6.3 ROUTING PROTOCOL IN MANETs

Routing protocols specify how communication takes place between the routers, sharing information that helps them to select the best-possible route between the two nodes [41]. It determines the choice of route. Routers contain information about the network directly attached to it. Routing protocols share this information with neighboring nodes and then in the network [42]. This helps to know about the topology of the network. The main characteristics of routing protocols are avoidance of loops in routing, selection of preferred routes, hop cost information, scalability, reliability, routing convergence time, etc. [43]. In order to overcome ad hoc network limitations such as maximum power consumption and low bandwidth in high error rates, different types of algorithms were introduced. In this chapter, we are considering two different types of protocols such as proactive and reactive. Proactive algorithms are known as table-driven routing protocols. Reactive algorithms are algorithms where sources are initiated on demand [44]. Examples include AODV and DSR.
