### 8.3.3 Load Side and Storage Battery Data

Current, voltage, temperature, fault detection data, state of discharge, state of health, terminal voltage, etc. are data that are included in battery management systems [39]. Another extensive source of big data is load side production. Daigaku Honyaku Center (DHC) communication, life patterns of the customers, consumer questions, status of water, consumption of gas and data such as room temperature, audience in the building, etc. are some of the data that come under load management sources. The obtained load information can also be shared by the consumer [40]. The consumption patterns obtained from the type of energy that is consumed reveal the load type in which big data is used. Electronic vehicle data is also a source of big data. Tire stress, velocity, motor drive, pedal reading, vehicle location, faults, etc. encompass the other sources.

Hussain et al. [41] proposed an improved algorithm for demand response (DR) synergized with renewable energy sources and energy storage system optimal dispatch (DRSREOD)-based home energy management system (HEMS). This heuristic-based algorithm considers DR, photovoltaic availability, the state of charge and charge/discharge rates of the storage battery, and the sharing-based parallel operation of more than one power source to supply the required load. Due to the rapid increase in demand for electricity, developing countries are facing large-scale load shedding (LS). An innovative algorithm is also proposed for the optimal sizing of a dispatchable generator that can supply the DRSREOD-based HEMS during LS hours to ensure an uninterrupted supply of power.

### 8.3.4 Environmental Data

Data streamed suspiciously at high volume in distinct speed and assemblies are usually constituent of data from numerical weather predictions [18]. The equations that are used to solve the data are very complex and are usually nonlinear. Another application where big data and environmental sciences come together is the public and environmental health. Monitoring the data through methods such as environmental sampling is one of the tried and tested methods of today, but with big data, we can compute this information more accurately and can ensure that information is computed more rapidly and precisely. On the topic of geographic data in environmental science, it is important to note that simple methods such as cartography and geographic surveying to even modern applications like geographic information surveying (GIS) all flourish on big data [42]. The ability of GIS at its core solidifies to be able to not only present statistical data but also utilize and consolidate it.

Wu et al. Chih-Lin et al. [44] proposed a method to significantly facilitate the local content provision, dynamical network and functionality deployment, user behavior awareness, fine-tuned network operation, and globally optimized energy-saving solutions.

### 8.3.5 Wireless Sensor Networks

Out of all the sources that are used in data generation, a good majority of attention in the field of environmental monitoring is bestowed upon the WSNs [45]. The physical conditions of the environment are recorded and monitored with the help of a large number of sensor nodes present while the collected data are routed to a sink node. Weather conditions such as temperature, sound, pollution levels, humidity, and winds are easily measured with the aid of WSNs, and the problem here arises while transporting the sensor information to the sink node [46]. Due to the limited capacity, a single node possesses only a narrow wireless link in direct comparison with the other networks. Thus, for overcoming the limitations of WSNs, there is a need to analyze and provide a common perspective for WSNs and big data systems to help improve the system performance. The data of these sensors are usually presented in key-value pairs (e.g., time step).

Ding et al. [47] proposed a real-time big data gathering (RTBDG) algorithm based on an indoor WSN for the risk analysis of industrial operations. In this algorithm, sensor nodes can screen the data collected from the environment and equipment according to the requirements of risk analysis. Harb et al. [48] put forth a two-level data reduction approach for sensor networks. The problem of repairing confident information coverage holes for big data collection in a large-scale heterogeneous WSN, which widely spreads over a geographic area with thousands of stationary sensor nodes and mobile sensor nodes, was formulated and worked upon. Recently, Feng et al. [49] worked upon the concerns of severely degrading quality of service (QoS) and lifetime of WSNs due to coverage holes generated by random deployment or battery exhaustion of sensors. Dao et al. [50] also proposed a new scheme of identifying collected information correctness for aggregating data in cluster heads in a hierarchical WSN based on improving the classification of a support vector machine (SVM). Big sensor-based data environments and the emergence of large-scale WSNs (LS-WSNs), which are spread over wide geographic areas and contain thousands of sensor nodes, require new techniques for energy-efficient data collection. Ang et al. [51] presented analytical approaches to determine the node energy consumption for LS-WSN multiple description coding schemes and gave models for determining the optimal number of clusters for minimizing the energy consumption.

### 8.3.6 Cloud Storage and IoT

Cloud storage represents the data storage of a computer. The digital data here are collected in logical groups. The physical environment that the cloud operates on is usually handled and maintained by one company. There are a lot of different servers, occasionally even in various locations, providing the entirely required physical atmosphere. The data garnered from homes with smart appliances, sensors, meters, etc. can easily be stored in cloud storage [52]. The various features that support security, fault tolerance storage, and availability also play in favor of cloud storage. A system of various devices and living beings interlinked together along with unique identifiers and the ability to transport information over an integrated network without the intervention of any sort can be defined as IoT [27]. The devices here could be using a computer, mechanical, or digital platform and can even have humans along with objects and animals involved. All these could be used harmoniously together as cloud can provide a workplace that operates commonly for both IoT and big data. Cai et al. [53], in their study, provided a functional framework that identifies the acquisition, management, processing, and mining areas of IoT big data and also defined several associated technical modules while describing them in terms of their key characteristics and capabilities. Bao et al. [54], with the aim of minimizing the network resource consumption of repairing data, proposed an adaptive erasure code for JointCloud storage of IoT big data called ACIoT. Sharma et al. [55] proposed a novel framework for coordinated processing between edge and cloud computing/processing by integrating advantages from both platforms. ## 8.4 APPLICATIONS OF BIG DATA IN WSNs

The present state of ever-changing and evolving technology promotes the growth of various different networks, which leads to production of data that are too vast to be accounted for or handled by normal methods [56]. This hinders the growth of big data, which must be handled effectively to help positively influence this presently surmounting information. There are various approaches provided to remedy this problem, but one such solution that gathers quite a considerable amount of attention is the association of data with WSN technologies [57]. Network clustering is the first link in that chain; this determines the location, distance, and arrangement of nodes with respect to other nodes as well as their base station (BS) [58]. This also decides the mode, direction, method, and strategy used while transmitting data through the network. The second step is to generate and store data in very big amounts. This data also need proper analysis, done in record time to produce valuable results. This processed data must also be stored securely, which is the biggest challenge we face because of the lack of research here. The last link that completes the chain is clustering the said nodes in a manner that not only increases the life expectancy of the network but also improves the energy efficiency of the system. The following sections explain the whole process.
