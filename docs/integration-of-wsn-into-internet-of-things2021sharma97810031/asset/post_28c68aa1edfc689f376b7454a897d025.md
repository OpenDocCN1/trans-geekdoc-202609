Hash function trees were first used for constructing one-time signature schemes. The disadvantage of these systems was that a key pair can sign and verify a single document; thus, many key pairs would be required. Hash-based signatures (of practical use) were invented by Merkle in 1989, who proposed to use binary trees for reducing the validity of many one-time verification keys (each one as a leaf) to the validity of one public key at the root of the tree. The main advantage of hash-based signatures is that they are simple and only require well-understood underlying primitives.

## 5.4.4 SYSTEMS OF MULTIVARIATE QUADRATIC EQUATIONS

These systems are based on the NP-hardness of the problem of solving nonlinear equations over a finite field. For these constructions, the public key is a sequence of quadratic nonlinear polynomials. The evaluation of these polynomials at any given value corresponds to either a signature or verification [31]. The most popular crypto-system in this category is known as HFE-. The main advantage of these solutions is the short signature size. Nonetheless, their security needs to be carefully evaluated as multiple proposals have been broken over the years.

## 5.4.5 ISOGENIES BETWEEN SUPERSINGULAR ELLIPTIC CURVES

The use of isogenies between supersingular elliptic curves was introduced in 2011 for cryptographic applications [33]. The best algorithm for constructing isogenies between supersingular elliptic curves has exponential complexity. One of the strong points of these systems is the ability to replicate DH-like exchanges, whereas under almost other post-quantum schemes, key exchange is solved by means of key encapsulation. Another advantage is that the hardness of the underlying problem allows employing reasonably short key pairs, smaller than many other post-quantum schemes. Their computation time is long, however. Lastly, given the novelty of this area, there is still uncertainty regarding the strength of these constructions. Further research is required in order to assess their security.

## 5.4.6 STANDARDIZATION

Due to increased interest in PQC, NIST initiated a standardization process of quantum-safe algorithms with aims to determine the best security algorithms that resist the state-of-the-art classical attacks as well as potential quantum attacks [34]. The standardization process began in February 2016 with a presentation by NIST at PQCrypto 2016. This led to a call for nominations in December 2016. The call for proposals closed on November 30, 2017, and by December 21, 2017, NIST presented 69 algorithms nominated for standardization that were compliant with the requirements defined. In early 2019, NIST selected a reduced number of proposals for a second round of evaluation. By 2020, NIST expects to select the algorithms for standardization or to start a third round if deemed necessary.

From this review of the literature, it is possible to note that substantial work has been done in evaluating the efficiency of these algorithms, mainly for Cortex devices and some hardware instances. Nonetheless, the WSN ecosystem is heterogeneous, and only a handful of devices are equipped with a Cortex processor, as can be observed in Table 5.6.

Runtime is a key metric for evaluating the performance of cryptographic algorithms. In the context of lightweight cryptography, lengthier delays represent greater energy expenditures. Some constrained devices are powered by batteries and must adhere to tight energy budgets. In most of these systems, the processing power is also limited, and if the security application takes too long to run, the device might not be able to perform its intended task in real time. The literature survey provided shows that most realizations of PQC have latencies surpassing a million cycles. If this is coupled with the low operational frequencies of some WSN devices, these security algorithms become borderline unusable.

In the context of general-purpose cryptography, modern computers might not have any trouble to allocate the thousands of bits required to store the keys for most PQC algorithms. In contrast, some constrained devices (for which lightweight cryptography is required) would find it difficult to sustain these overheads. Multiple challenges ought to be addressed before PQC is of practical use for WSN motes and similar
