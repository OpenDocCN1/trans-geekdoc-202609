The union of augmented reality and ubiquitous computing generates a kind of mixed real- reality that merges the real and the virtual, encompassing two possibilities concerning the predominant environment that is the real world (Ubiquitous), and with respect to the environment becoming predominantly virtual (augmented virtuality), inserting virtual objects in the physical environment, shown to the user, in real-time, through the support of some device that makes the interface of the real environment adapted for visualization and manipulation of these real and virtual objects [53].

Augmented reality is aligned with the basic premise of ubiquitous computing with regard to computing that moves out of workstations and personal computers, which becomes a pervasive context in people’s daily lives wherever they are. Considering the characteristic similarity between augmented reality and ubiquitous computing concerning the focus on the disappearance of a user’s conscious and intentional interaction with an information system [53].

Through augmented reality, ubiquitous computing can be applied in all human senses providing the digital user with a safe interaction, without the need for training and specific knowledge, due to the intuitive factor that digital devices have, since they can bring to the real environment virtual objects (employing computer vision and computer graphics techniques), increasing and increasing the user’s vision concerning the real world, in relation to the sense of vision [53,54].

Ubiquitous computing systems such as diffuse computing devices maintain the notion of explicit and intentional interaction, which combined with augmented technology allows virtual objects to be introduced in real environments, providing the user with the handling of these objects with their own hands, with relation to the sense of touch (digital) allowing a natural and attractive interaction with the environment. Through the use of a natural interface making communication more sensitive and easy, through ways of interacting with people, such as gesture, speech, and vision, still evaluating the aspect of context-sensitive computing, making it possible to capture the information through digital devices (IoT, sensors, among others), which can be easily detected [55].

Thus, ubiquitous computing in this context of augmented reality, will allow even more to the digital user, without realizing it, the use of this technology anytime and anywhere, from a computer system, through software and/or digital interface [56–60].

# 3.9 CONCLUSIONS

The motivation of this chapter was to offer an overview of pervasive computing that provides information, media, context, and processing power to users wherever they are, assessing that the technology is characterized by vast networks of connected microprocessors embedded in everyday objects. At the same step that the path to ubiquitous computing has advanced a lot in recent years, not only smartphones, tablets, computers, televisions, or e-books are integrated, but also several objects that are not technological, following a trend of integration between the devices, such as chairs, tables, pens, refrigerators, and others that will be interconnected and adapted according to the needs of each person.
