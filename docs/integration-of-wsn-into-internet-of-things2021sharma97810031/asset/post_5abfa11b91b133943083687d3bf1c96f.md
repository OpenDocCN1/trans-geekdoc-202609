### 7.1 INTRODUCTION

Wireless sensor networks (WSNs) along with artificial intelligence (AI) now allow the farmers to analyze weather conditions, soil conditions, and water usage in the field of smart agriculture and automate so many physical tasks and decision-making activities. A WSN refers to a system that is formed of wired or wireless communications, sensors, and actuators capable of acquiring and transferring data to the Internet or cloud. In smart agriculture, Internet of Things (IoT) sensors, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) or drones, satellites via WSNs, and other resources produce huge data constantly. This big data is analyzed with the help of cloud computing using machine learning (ML) algorithms to support decision-making in smart agriculture. Now, the farmers are capable of recording sensor data and predicting the yield. Hence, they are better equipped technically and can face the natural disaster and climate conditions in an effective way. AI-based smart agriculture allows to detect plant diseases after analyzing the pictures taken continuously with drones. In situations of pandemics like today in COVID-19, robots can be trained to do the physical labor of harvesting and maintaining the farms efficiently. Further, the AI-based solutions enable our farmers to plow in a better and smart way. They can manage their resources in an effective way. Recent surveys advocate the potential role of digital evolution in the smart farming [1–4].

The motivation behind this study is that WSNs and IoT are prominent technologies in smart agriculture. A huge volume of research work has been published in this field, which needs to be reviewed in an oriented way.

The overall contribution of this work is to bring a light on the use of WSNs and IoT in agriculture and to highlight that how the sensor-based wireless networks with IoT technology enhance the agricultural activities and result in a better and smart agriculture. WSNs with data analytics [5,6] using ML techniques for the huge amount of data gathered using sensors are resultantly boosting the agricultural applications.

This chapter is organized as follows: Section 7.2 brings light on the recent advances in WSNs along with AI in smart agriculture. Then, Section 7.3 discusses the ML techniques that are commonly used within the WSN-enabled smart agriculture, and a state of the art is also summarized highlighting the utilization of the ML technique for WSNs. In Section 7.4, a case study on WSN-based smart agriculture solution is discussed. In Section 7.5, the chapter is concluded with remarks on future scope.

### 7.2 WSNs AND SMART AGRICULTURE

WSNs along with AI lead to three mainstreams in the field of agriculture—namely building and utilizing AI robots, monitoring the soil and weather conditions along with crop disease detection, and decision support system by making drawing predictions. The physical load of farming can be transferred to autonomous robots. They can work as farmers in the field irrespective of the weather conditions and can handle essential agricultural activities like plowing the fields, planting seeds, controlling weeds, and harvesting [7,8]. Robotics is being deployed as labors in harvesting. For example, CROO is supporting farmers in picking and packing strawberries [9].

The soil and weather conditions are monitored using sensors to sense various factors like moisture and temperature, along with the satellite images for weather forecasting. All these information updates the farmers with better resource allocation and automation. For example, if the moisture level of soil and surroundings is recorded and analyzed, then irrigation can be controlled in a better way or even can be automated. ML techniques are utilized significantly to detect crop disease and to monitor the soil health. For instance, Plantix is used to detect soil defects and plant diseases [10]. It works on an image processing paradigm, utilizing ML techniques to analyze the soil patterns and detect the problems with soil or the plants. Such applications provide a good graphical user interface to farmers so that they can see the images and predict the problems. It leads to smart agriculture as it also suggests the optimal techniques and solutions to fix the reported problem. The implementation of deep conventional neural network to monitor cassava plants has already been made [11]. It identifies three types of crop diseases and two types of pest damages [12,13].

The sensor data and satellite data are analyzed to make future predictions and to build decision support systems. The most popular use of such predictions is associated with assess the weather and crop sustainability and to identify the pest and disease. The use of UAVs (drones) to gather information on vast acres of land within a short period is appropriate. With processing the data sensed, crop health can be improved, and cost of farming can be reduced [14]. The decision support system is also implemented in the “smart irrigation system.” It is a WSN-based smart system that monitors soil properties and weather conditions to regulate the irrigation and automatically sprinkles water on the crops [15]. Table 7.1 gives a summary of various field activities that can be supported by WSNs to enable smart agriculture.

In this way, our farmers are enabled to deal with natural disasters, climate conditions, and constant soil/crop monitoring with the intelligent information systems supported by WSNs in association with ML, IoT, and AI. Smart agriculture is a solution toward an improved and efficient agricultural dimension. The agricultural activities include a range of tasks from plowing the fields through monitoring the growth of crops to the sale of the products in an efficient way.
