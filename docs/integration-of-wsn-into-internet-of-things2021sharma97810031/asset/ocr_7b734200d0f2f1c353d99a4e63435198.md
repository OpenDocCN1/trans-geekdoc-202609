The technological model of mobile computing does not change while the user moves; that is, mobile devices are not able to identify the environment where they are and cannot adjust themselves to it, but combined with the access capacity, mobile computing has to turn computing into an activity that can be carried anywhere. The limitation concerning the environment in which the mobile devices are can be overcome by manually adjusting the device settings made by the user; however, this alternative overrides the main idea of mobile computing, which is practicality and ease [33].

Mobility is the ability of a device to be easily moved, i.e., portability transported, concerning two main characteristics: the size of the device and accessories and the weight of them, offering a set of functions, being able to connect with other systems and easy to use. This mobility can still be achieved through innovative mobile devices such as wearables, which connect to various operating systems, having sensors to monitor health, integrated with operating systems, and even respond to voice commands, or even integrated to types entertainment and sports monitoring applications. Or yet, wearables like augmented reality glasses that don’t depend on smartphones designed to interact with 3D objects [34].

Usability concerning the fact that it is easy to use by different types of people; this property touches characteristics that influence the use of devices concerning the user are their capacity, dexterity, and knowledge; and even the device and its size which results in flexibility. Concerning functionality, devices serve multiple purposes and different types of functions, evaluating their applications with objectives and activities [30–34].

These properties link ubiquitous computing with the provision of services when and where necessary, in a noticeable way or not. In the same sense as computational pervasiveness concerning the domain of personal computers, the philosophy of technology is that practically anything, from cars to clothes, from stores to houses, outdoor and indoor environments, and even wearables on the human body, can be equipped with chips that connect with other networked devices [30–34].

Analyzing characteristics that support the best intelligent device “quiet and invisible”, should extend a person’s unconsciousness; creating tranquility in usability concerning services accompanying the user, in the same sense as transparency concerning devices, hardware, and software, which must be discreet. Ubiquitous technology derives from the calm technology that runs on the periphery of the network of technological environments in which smart devices are present; i.e., it does not affect the people’s perception center, and it is “one that informs but does not demand focus or attention” [30–34].

In this sense, mobile computing refers to the use by people of portable mobile devices with great operational and functional power, with the mobile device’s ability to connect to other devices, obtain data, and provide them to other users and even devices [35, 36].

In this context, it is possible to highlight the four pillars of mobility concerning portability, defined as the ability to be easily transported, the usability in which it is tangible to be usable by different types of people in different environments.
