Policymakers are expected to demand more sound safeguards before they issue provisions for blockchain. If they do not take action early, the lack of safety may appear as multiple-minimal or one blowing-up issue. In any case, whether it is to keep patients' records confidential or whether it is to avoid turmoil in the international economy, policymaking will have to come to terms with the blockchain realm [38].

For addressing such limitations, the off-chain data warehousing concept was proposed, where data is preserved exterior to the blockchain in traditional or distributed data repositories; however, the data hashes are warehoused in the blockchain. In this proposed model, health data are warehoused off-chain and meanwhile could be safeguarded, updated, or deleted as needed. Simultaneously, changeless health data hashes are warehoused on-chain for verifying the precision and legitimacy of the off-chain health records. Yet, this model is challenging for the stricter global data safeguarding laws and the efforts of confidentiality boards to label metadata of individual data as also personal. Consequently, individual data hashes could be labeled personal as well; hence the entire argument of applying blockchain to warehouse individual data might again come to light [7].

As we embark on the fourth industrial revolution, it is important to note that blockchain may still be a decade away from having a significant presence in the health sector. However, large corporations, governments, and academic institutions need to prepare for the disruption it may cause across most sectors and more importantly our lives. Companies and initiatives based or related to blockchain have already appeared in the healthcare sector [39].

## 16.8 CONCLUSIONS

Considering the nature of the health sector, maintaining privacy, security, and integrity of health data is inevitable; therefore, it necessitates a safeguarded system for managing data. The technology could substantially improve the quality of patient care through optimizing data analytics to make informed clinical decisions, possibly minimizing the expenses via allocating resources effectively. Health data and services are gradually transferred to the cloud for the practicality showed in the real-time accessibility to a full patient medical history and the savings showed in managing health data and healthcare economics. Yet, in a growing cloud-based setting, applying traditional cryptographic primitives and access control patterns to solve privacy and security issues is challenging. Consequently, blockchain has gained attention for safeguarding health data. While data uniformity and shared warehousing/ accessibility of blockchain provide the potential for managing health data, there are challenges related to the blockchain-derived techniques which necessitate thorough investigation. Moreover, it is debatable whether healthcare providers will be willing to switch to blockchain technology, as long as there is no sound proof of blockchain's safety against quantum computing modalities.
