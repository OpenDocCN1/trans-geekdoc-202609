
### 15.1.2 DESIGN CONSIDERATIONS OF WIRELESS HEALTHCARE MONITORING SYSTEMS

The wireless healthcare monitoring system being used in the modern day has a multitude of applications, each varying greatly from the other in terms of the type of data being procured by each sensing node for different networks. Therefore, a general overview of design considerations and attributes that play a role while designing a wireless healthcare monitoring system includes the following:

- Ergonomic Considerations: For a wireless health system, the design considerations include the durability of the device, wearability, degree of comfort, ease of movement, and unobtrusiveness. The above-mentioned attributes are important contributing factors for the devices that are generally worn by individuals whose regular physiological and biomedical data are being tracked continuously. The physiological parameters that are frequently monitored are inclusive of blood sugar levels, heartbeat rate, blood pressure, or electrocardiography (ECG); thus, maintaining close body contact with the sensor nodes is crucial without restricting the patient's mobility.

- Hardware Considerations: The WSNs are generally attributed to operating within limits of the available resources such as limited power supply and low computational power. These attributes form the key challenges that need to be accounted for while designing the hardware of the sensor nodes. A careful balance has to be achieved for the sensor nodes to operate energy-efficiently and to expend optimum power in transmitting signals without hampering the signal quality.

- Application Traffic Profile: Yet another consideration that plays a pivotal role in designing the transmission frequency and signal bandwidth is the application traffic profile or the type of data being communicated. For example, a wireless system detecting motion and fall would not require the same bit frequency, delay sensitivity, and sampling frequency as in the wireless system monitoring electrocardiograph. Therefore, the transmission frequency and its related attributes are governed by the application traffic profile.

- Network Architecture: The application and location of the patient and his/her mobility also play an important role in designing network architecture. For a patient with low mobility and low data rates, a homogeneous WSN architecture would suffice. In such situations, the patient is near an information sink, and therefore, a lower density of sensors and a simpler architecture for lower energy consumption are used. For patients with high mobility or those operating in areas of high density, the complex, heterogeneous architecture may be required along with a higher transmission rate, thus requiring higher power consumption, to serve a larger number of patients at once.

- Frequency Selection: To avoid mutual interferences with other WSNs operating in the same frequency range as other networks in the same coverage area, the considerations of frequency range tend to play a key role due to an overlapping frequency range.

- Selection of Adequate Data Packets: The number of samples transmitted per packet limits the network performance in the case of the CSMA-CA MAC mechanism, since packet loss rates and packet delays are directly related to longer packet lengths. However, longer packet lengths become acceptable since it results in lower packet loss rates and supports a larger number of users. Therefore, striking a balance among the three parameters is important for selecting the number of samples being sent per data packet.

### 15.1.3 OVERVIEW OF THE RELATION AND DIFFERENCES BETWEEN THE IoT AND WSNs

With the ubiquitous presence of WSN- and IoT-based devices, the difference between the two technologies often becomes blurred. Hence, a set of logical questions that often follow is: If the WSN devices are connected to the Internet, what is the difference between WSNs and IoT? Are these words complementary or interchangeable? What are the implications of WSN technology on IoT?

The IoT can be defined as a network that connects uniquely identifiable devices having sensing and potential programmability capabilities. The IoT uses the standard communication protocols to connect various devices and components referred to as “things” to form an adaptive and complex network. These devices possess sensing and actuation capabilities and have unique IP addresses for communication. These “things” are operated remotely at any time with or without human intervention. WSN is a spatially distributed network of nodes that coordinate to sense and control the ambient physical parameters and are connected wirelessly to transfer data to a central location. These wireless networks are made of three components: sensor nodes, gateways, and the user. The gateways and sensor nodes are interconnected wirelessly although they may or may not be connected to the Internet.

Hence, from the above-mentioned technical definitions, the connection to the Internet is one of the most distinguishing factors between WSNs and IoT. Therefore, WSNs can be thought of as a large set of sensor nodes in an IoT system. WSN is thus a mesh-like network that can be used to gather and relay data through a sink node or base station or a router to the Internet in an IoT system. Based on the above definition, a wired device connected to the Internet is a part of the IoT system but may or may not be a part of WSN.

Depending further on the types of application requirements, IoT and WSNs can be easily differentiated based on the relevance of the application and the type of network. Depending on the need of the network and the application, the sensitivity of the WSN and hence the security and privacy can be variable from medium to high. For example, the privacy required in anti-theft management systems is high as compared to flood detection systems. Similarly, the data processing capabilities of the WSNs are nearly negligible while, on the other hand, IoT systems are programmed to be smart networks.
