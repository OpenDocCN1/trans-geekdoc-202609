## 3.2 UBIQUITOUS COMPUTING CONCEPTS

Computing arose from the concepts of mobile computing and pervasive computing (Figure 3.1), with the main idea referring to the use of mobile devices, their services, and resources capable of discovering in the environment around other services and resources, or even mobile devices making it possible to connect to them [9].

With the constant evolution of computer technologies, the use of computers has driven the use of the Internet in recent decades. Still considering the progress of telecommunications and the different forms of connectivity, which is increasingly an integral part of people’s way of life. Resulting in a society more dependent on computational devices, considering the various devices cooperate with each other and provide the user with instant access to new services in a transparent manner, which facilitates day-to-day tasks (whether personal or professional) [10,11].

In this context, ubiquitous computing is introduced, derived from the perception that it is or can be everywhere at the same time, it is considered the technology where services, mobile users, and resources meet other users, services, and resources, using computer knowledge mobile and pervasive computing. Still considering the responsibility of the technology for the execution of the services that are used by these users, which are distributed among several other devices, considering that each one of these devices has its specific characteristics and functions [10,11].

The concept of ubiquitous computing is related to any objects with computational capacities that can be integrated with each other in a hidden way in the environment for ease in carrying out daily tasks, describing the direct and constant presence of information technology and technology in people's lives, in their houses and social environments. When applied, it manages and uses the combination of the characteristics and advantages of the concepts of mobile and pervasive computing. In other words, technology is the integration of mobility with the distributed, intelligent, imperceptible, and highly integrated presence of computers and their applications [12].

The ubiquitous technology benefits from the advancement of mobile technology with increasingly portable devices, consequently of greater practicality, and thus of easy transport (mobile computing), in the same sense. That it benefits from the ability of computing devices to act intelligently, if these devices become increasingly present concerning the advancement of artificial intelligence technologies and the emergence of new communication networks (pervasive computing). In the same sense as to ponder the degree of embedding of these devices, aiming to specify the degree of intelligence, for certain actions performed by users, such as dynamically detecting, exploring, and building models of their environments and computers embedded in a pervasive environment [13].

There are three principles identified in relation to computational ubiquity: diversity, which refers to specific devices to perform specific tasks as quickly and easily as possible; decentralization, which means that all devices communicate with each other and synchronize specific information about each one, not only having a central server, but creating an “intelligent” environment, where all equipment works and cooperates with each other; and connectivity, which means that applications and devices move together with the user (mobility) in a connected and uninterrupted manner, through the connection between wireless long-distance networks and medium- and short-distance networks—several networks are created heterogeneously. Based on these three principles, it is possible to use computational devices in different environments, which have connectivity standards and need to have specific characteristics for each task, communicating with each other [14,15].

The principle of diversity is related to the existence of several devices that perform a certain task, considering that this diversity of devices is currently huge and few of them have unique capabilities, even considering those that are more specific than others, depending on the type of task. One aspect of this principle is related in that the computer systems must be able to manage the different properties of the most diverse equipment, choosing automatically the one that best suits the given situation [14,15].

The principle of decentralization relates to the responsibility of applications for the construction and exchange of information, with the need for servers to be robust and at the same time flexible, in order to manage a large number of different synchronized devices [14,15].

The principle of connectivity has a more direct relationship with the user, derived from the need to always be connected through their devices. In this sense, it is necessary to standardize the communication and protocols between the devices in order to achieve the desired interoperability based on applications in common standards concerning the specification of open connectivity standards [14–16].

Focused on obtaining an excellent interaction between man and machine, related to the objective of ubiquitous computing, using computational devices or any other object with computational capabilities interacting visibly or invisibly with the user, fully integrating the technology/machine relationship with the user’s human beings, in the sense of automatic (use without realizing it), it is necessary to achieve these three principles concerning the accomplishment of day-to-day tasks [14–17].

For this objective to be achieved, it is necessary to use a natural interface making communication more sensitive and easy, through ways of interacting with people, such as speech, gesture, and vision. Still considering context-sensitive computing, making it possible to capture situations through electronic devices, such as acquisition mechanisms and context inference, or even aspects of human-computer interaction, or even through Ubiquitous Computing services and applications in different domains, for example, applicable in the most diverse types of contexts [18].

Nowadays computing is becoming so ubiquitous that it is practically impossible to separate the physical and digital worlds, concerning people being more and more digitally connected from waking up to sleeping, absorbing, and generating a very large volume of content. Whether daily, at home, or at work, it is increasingly difficult to separate “at home” from “at work” when it comes to the digital world. In this context, ubiquitous computing is increasingly present in people’s lives, from the technological setting through not only cell phones, computers, televisions, tablets, or e-books, but also smart devices such as a table, chair, keychain, refrigerator, pen, calendar, and other items that do not belong to informations, sharing data, information, and knowledge simply and easily [18–19].

In the same sense as through ubiquitous computing, along with other technologies, systems, and interfaces, they are capable of storing information and literally learning from previously performed functions. What represents an era in which technology is part of the human evolution process, is through a type of super-connectivity, through 3G and 4G connections with a quality signal, which users no longer need to search for information, since it will be available at any time through any object that is used frequently. It also relates the sharing of data using concepts of cloud computing and automatic synchronization of data via the network between notebooks, tablets, and smartphones, generating a much more productive daily life [19].
