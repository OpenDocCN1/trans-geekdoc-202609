special case of the Daubechies 1 wavelet, which is widely used in many applications, especially in pattern recognized.

As some signals resulted in very close coefficients, thus making it difficult to identify loads, it was decided to calculate the sum of the coefficient norms in an attempt to obtain different values for close coefficients, thus facilitating the identification process. In this way, it is possible to increase the difference between similar coefficients.

This calculation was performed by the third program, which subsequently compares this value with a database assembled from the sum of the norms of the wavelet coefficients of “standard” signals for each load. This signal corresponds to the T signal, used as a target in the neural network. In this way, it is possible to know whether the signal coming from the neural network (signal Y) really corresponds to the signal being measured (signal S). In the identification, an error of 1% is allowed between the compared signals, and if the error is greater, the algorithm will return that it was not possible to identify the load.

# ## 9.5 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

In all experiments carried out with the loads measured individually, the algorithm was able to correctly identify the load within the stipulated tolerance of 1%. In experiments with the sum of the signs, identification is possible, increasing the tolerance to 2%, 3%, and 11%, depending on the sign. But as tolerance is increased, the previously correctly identified signals begin to become confused.

After simulating all loads, the results were promising, since the proposed algorithm was able to identify all measured loads, as well as sets of loads measured at the same time, assuming an error of 1% between the simulated load and the standard charge. However, the proposed algorithm requires more studies and details when it comes to the identification of simulations based on the sum of individual signals.
