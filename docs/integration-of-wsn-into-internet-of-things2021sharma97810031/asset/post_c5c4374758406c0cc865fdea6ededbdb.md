## 8.2.3 TECHNOLOGIES AND TOOLS

The technologies that find their applications in power grids incorporate the use of big data. Some of these technologies are discussed in the subsections below.

- IoT: Smart home appliances, transport, community facilities, etc. constitute the data that are derived from the IoT. Energy productivity, consistency, and grid security are applications that enjoy benefits that are made accessible by IoT, which are possible due to the rising number of progressions happening in this field. IoT has characteristics that are very similar to those of the big data in terms of their data generation and the type of data being generated [28, 29].
- Cloud Computing: The storing and processing of big data should be done with low cost while being analyzed fast, effectively, and consistently. The use of this technology ensures that big data can be developed to change IT substructure, make valuable conclusions regarding businesses, and also solve the problems of storing and processing by applying parallel cloud computing.
- Further Used Technologies: Network functions virtualization is a network technology that can transfer the majority of the data by providing malleable resource division and optimization possibilities. The reaction of the information processing in real time is very critical to microgrid applications. The storm is one such application that embodies the same.
- Hadoop: This includes applications in searching for a network, analysis on clickstream, filtering of spam, etc. Cloud view uses Hadoop to analyze the data generated by a machine with the framework of cloud computing. Hadoop includes a file system that is distributed and stores data and an execution engine that processes the data.
- Data Centers: This is a platform for bulk data storage, which also provides a lot of services such as data attainments, establishments, and supervisions. Constructing a center with desired qualities (proficiency, dependability, expandability, etc.) can help provide infrastructure to previous services, which in turn decreases cost and energy emissions.

## 8.2.4 BENEFITS FROM BIG DATA ANALYSIS IN MICROGRIDS

The stability of a system and its reliability are two major features that are always prioritized when dealing with microgrids. These have been further divided into subfeatures ranging from the stability of voltage to detection of oscillation, etc. The new and ever-evolving budding applications of big data now provide an opportunity to search for new possibilities for the old and futile monitoring and detection methods [30]. The use of these resources and increased adeptness are two features that have been credited to be complementary to big data. They are of particular help when dealing with understanding the physical confines of the resources, authorizing and rectifying the models, and incorporating the renewable energy resources with the aid of provided tools. One such instance can be of the meters, which are smarter than traditional meters and have been mounted in houses and corporate buildings around the globe. This experiment permits detection of fraud, better predictions of power failure, comparatively easier process for billing, etc. This, however, requires a higher rate of sampling than other meters, among other shortcomings.

### 8.3.1 DATA PERTAINING TO THE EQUIPMENT

The data from transformers, circuit breakers, switch gears, cables, and substations are equipment data that result from management and monitoring. All the data about pressure of oil, ground current, data release, nominal data, producer data, size data, assembly, chilling system, transformer model, fault recorder, report, source of fault, dielectric loss of transformer, index of polarization, resistance, insulation information, humidity content, load, short circuit current, and tap changed information are data that belong to the transformer [31]. Discharge, humidity content, and information about discharge conclude data about switchgear. A sag in voltage, signal waveform, and inclination of tower come under transmission line data. Temperature, discharge, and circulation current come under power cable data. All the information such as ID, type, setting, name, and status of pollution are a part of substantial data. Constraints data include information regarding current, minor voltage, capacity, model of the connection, manufacturer, cooling system, data about the release, operational data, recorder, source and action of faults, name of a substation, tripping details, SF6 examination report, insulation resistance, mechanisms of control, production data of the used equipment, and quantity of circuit breakers.

Yan et al. [32] explored a novel idea for power equipment monitoring and found that RMT is suitable for modeling the massive datasets in this situation. Wu et al. [33] focused on the analysis and utilization of assembly big data in the manufacturing process and studied the key technologies of big data analysis and assembly scheduling optimization for complex equipment. Lai et al. [34], to recognize industrial equipment accurately in manufacturing systems, adopted the long short-term memory to analyze big data features and build a nonintrusive load monitoring system.

The major source of big data is represented by the equipment monitoring data. The data that energy management systems include certain toward voltage of the equipment, electricity, power, frequency, etc. Oil status or lubricant, ventilation, and transformer healthy operation can be represented by the equipment real-time data monitoring. Total harmonic distortion and flicker index are power quality calculations that are performed with voltage and current waveforms [35]. For monitoring renewable energy, remote telemetry units are used [36,37]. Digital protection data, fault description, previous disturbances, old faults, locations, trip numbers, and dips in the voltage records can all be sorted as a part of equipment diagnostic data.
