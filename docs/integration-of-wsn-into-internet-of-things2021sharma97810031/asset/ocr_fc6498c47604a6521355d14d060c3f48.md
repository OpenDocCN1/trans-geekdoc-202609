# ### 3.1 INTRODUCTION

Ubiquitous computing is derived from pervasive computing, another nomenclature is environmental intelligence, describing the direct and constant presence of information technology and technology in people’s lives, in their homes, and in social environments. It referring to devices connected everywhere in a way so transparent to the human being that not perceive that it is there. Through this technology, everyday life becomes simpler through sensitive, adaptive, and intelligent digital environments. Assessing that the presence of this technology on a day-to-day basis is so immersed in the normality of everyday life, so much so that users do not need to have technical knowledge in computer science to handle them [1,2].

The objective of ubiquitous computing is the total integration in relation to the technology/machine paradigm with human beings, in such a way that it is invisible, in the automatic sense, for example, the use without the perception of the presence of the technology.
