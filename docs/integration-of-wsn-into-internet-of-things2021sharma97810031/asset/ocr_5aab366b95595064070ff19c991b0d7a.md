Mechanisms to control congestion and flow in the transport layer are becoming the end of the hour yet are difficult to find. For example, the need to deliver a sufficient number of packets led to the development of event-to-sink reliable transport (ESRT) protocol for event-reporting applications, while on the other hand, other protocols addressing and relaying periodic collection of data such as physiological reporting and vital-sign reporting such as electrocardiogram (ECG) where every packet needs to be delivered are being developed [9].

# 15.2.5 NETWORK LAYER

In the discussion so far, we have realized that optimizing delay and energy efficiency are the two underlying challenges. With the increasing number of multimodal and multimedia-based sensor networks, the challenges including optimizing data traffic congestion and mechanisms to channel emergency data traffic over continuous data traffic have increased. For this purpose, load-balancing protocols are still under study and development phase to check rate control and avoid data congestion along with several data compression techniques also being inculcated into the systems.

The other challenge of resource limitation in the network layer is also being researched. It is observed that when sensors are subjected to heavy utilization, they tend to overheat and may cause serious damage to the device components as well as the user of the device. These problems become specifically pronounced in in vivo sensor networks. Thus, the trade-off among data delay mechanisms, load balancing, data congestion and prioritization, and energy efficiency is continuing to vex researchers and developers.

The network layer is one of the most vulnerable layers in the WSNs, owing to which there occur multiple attacks to breach security and secure high-priority data. The attacks on the network layer can be broadly classified into the following categories:

+   • Sybil Attacks: It is a type of malicious node attack in which the node acquires multiple identities and creates virtual redundancy in the network. This kind of attack is still to be addressed in the most fault-tolerant protocols. The few countermeasures that could be introduced to counter such attacks include limiting the number of neighbors and applying a strong verification scheme to avoid Sybil attacks although they may prove to be inefficient.

+   • Sinkhole/Wormhole: The sinkhole is a type of attack in which the adversary accesses data passing through the malicious node, while a wormhole is a type of attack in which the weak link between two nodes is attacked by the attacker.
