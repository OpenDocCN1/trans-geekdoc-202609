The practical use of quantum computing is generally unknown given that it is not available for commercial use. Innovative research facilities have access to such modalities. IBM has lately released a platform where researchers across the globe can simulate quantum computing-based data analysis, shedding light on its potential [26, 32].

The enlargement of blockchain-protected data in finance has covered several gigabytes. The growth is expected to become exponential as binary computing switches to quantum computing. Up to this point, quantum computing raises some challenges regarding the complexity and size of the blockchain. However, safety is yet to be discussed.

Recently, Rajan and Visser, researchers from the School of Mathematics and Statistics of the Victoria University of Wellington in New Zealand, published a paper on a conceptual quantum blockchain. According to them, data can be encoded in the form of photons. These photons can be linked chronologically, moving the entanglement from space to time. Quantum data can be encoded in two photons that coexist in the present and the past. As they are entangled with each other, attackers attempting to violate the quantum blockchain are incapable of accessing the previous photon that no longer exists.

The nature of blockchain is such that all blocks are interconnected in a serial manner, for example, the current block with the previous one [32]. Any interference with the current quantum state of the photon will affect the photon connected with it in the past. Even so, this paradigm stands as a strong identifier of the endless possible—conceptual for now—applications of quantum computing. Moreover, it indicates how far a solution designated to a medical issue can go and unravels novel paths of securing biomedical data (clinical trials, drug molecules, patient records, etc.) [18].

# 16.7 LIMITATIONS

# 16.7.1 TECHNICAL LIMITATIONS

While data uniformity and shared warehousing/accessibility of blockchain provide the potential for managing health data, there are challenges related to the blockchain-derived technique which necessitates thorough investigation.

Blockchain techniques could be disruptive and need a new reconsideration and large venture all over the setting, such as modifying the workflows of business or changing the current systems.

Prior to adoption, government-sponsored healthcare givers should initiate a cost–benefit analysis to realize the return on investment and other possible financial or legal issues. For instance, the same record could lie in several network nodes, placed in various nations with diverse confidentiality and data safeguarding prerequisites [33].

An additional pragmatic problem is how to prepare blockchain to warehouse health data, which is yet unknown.
