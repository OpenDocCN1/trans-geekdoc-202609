# ### 16.5.1 IMPORTANCE OF BLOCKCHAIN IN HEALTH CARE

Consider the possibility that each patient had secure and private access to their whole clinical history. Imagine a scenario where you could see a patient’s finished history of imperative signs, every medicine taken, data about each specialist’s visit, treatment performed, and medical procedure. How helpful would that be for patients as well as for their training and the business when all is said and done?

In 21st-century health care, probably the most common currency is data—individual patient’s data, clinical trials’ data, as well as various experimental data. Individual healthcare practitioners are most commonly dealing with individualized datasets such as patients’ history, laboratory, or imaging test results. Various state or commercial entities from academia and hospitals to the pharmaceutical industry deal with larger patients or nonpatient populations’ datasets [29].

Owing to the success of bitcoin lately, blockchain has gained more attention because of the need to safeguard health data. Generally, blockchain is a technique for creating an open and disseminated on-cloud repository that contains a list of connected data entities called blocks where a block directory to the next one, the so-called blockchain. Such blocks, not centrally housed, are disseminated across several clusters of a framework. Every block consists of a timestamp of its development, the hash of the former one, and the processing data (patient’s health data and the healthcare giver information) [30].
