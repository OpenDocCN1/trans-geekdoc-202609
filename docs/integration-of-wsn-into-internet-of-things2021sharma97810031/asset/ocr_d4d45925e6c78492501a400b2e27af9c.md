When applied, it manages and uses the combination of the characteristics and advantages of the concepts of mobile and pervasive computing. In other words, technology is the integration of mobility with the distributed, intelligent, imperceptible, and highly integrated presence of computers and their applications [12].

The ubiquitous technology benefits from the advancement of mobile technology with increasingly portable devices, consequently of greater practicality, and thus of easy transport (mobile computing), in the same sense. That it benefits from the ability of computing devices to act intelligently, if these devices become increasingly present concerning the advancement of artificial intelligence technologies and the emergence of new communication networks (pervasive computing). In the same sense as to ponder the degree of embedding of these devices, aiming to specify the degree of intelligence, for certain actions performed by users, such as dynamically detecting, exploring, and building models of their environments and computers embedded in a pervasive environment [13].

There are three principles identified in relation to computational ubiquity: diversity, which refers to specific devices to perform specific tasks as quickly and easily as possible; decentralization, which means that all devices communicate with each other and synchronize specific information about each one, not only having a central server, but creating an “intelligent” environment, where all equipment works and cooperates with each other; and connectivity, which means that applications and devices move together with the user (mobility) in a connected and uninterrupted manner, through the connection between wireless long-distance networks and medium- and short-distance networks—several networks are created heterogeneously. Based on these three principles, it is possible to use computational devices in different environments, which have connectivity standards and need to have specific characteristics for each task, communicating with each other [14,15].

The principle of diversity is related to the existence of several devices that perform a certain task, considering that this diversity of devices is currently huge and few of them have unique capabilities, even considering those that are more specific than others, depending on the type of task. One aspect of this principle is related in that the computer systems must be able to manage the different properties of the most diverse equipment, choosing automatically the one that best suits the given situation [14,15].

The principle of decentralization relates to the responsibility of applications for the construction and exchange of information, with the need for servers to be robust and at the same time flexible, in order to manage a large number of different synchronized devices [14,15].

The principle of connectivity has a more direct relationship with the user, derived from the need to always be connected through their devices. In this sense, it is necessary to standardize the communication and protocols between the devices in order to achieve the desired interoperability based on applications in common standards concerning the specification of open connectivity standards [14–16].
