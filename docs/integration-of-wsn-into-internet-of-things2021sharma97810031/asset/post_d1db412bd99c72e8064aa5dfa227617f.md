### 12.3.1.1 DoS Attacks

DoS attacks are accountable for interrupting system’s services by producing many unnecessary demands. Hence, the user cannot communicate and access the IoT devices, which make it a bit hard to take the appropriate conclusion. Also, DoS attacks consistently maintain the IoT devices to be switched on, which in turn affect the battery lifespan. Distributed DoS (DDoS) is a special type of attack that occurs when several attacks take place with different IPs to keep the server busy and create some requests. This makes it difficult to compare it with the attack traffic and normal traffic [24]. Over the past few years, a virus of IoT botnet known as Mirai introduced catastrophic DDoS attacks that caused harm to many IoT devices by intrusions.

### 12.3.1.2 Spoofing Attack

During the spoofing attacks, the system’s problem is the detectability of IoT devices, which is a security issue. The attacks are done by creating a routing loop, producing a false error message, and a few other techniques. Spoofer in the beginning does not convey a signal even to obey to the relevant transmitter. However, when the relevant transmitter ceases sending the signals to the appropriate receiver, then spoofer begins to send the unfaithful signals. For example, an intruder contaminates the network by sharing the phoney routing data. On Instagram, a user is demanded to “like” for him/her to be directed to the fake Instagram page. This phoney page takes user’s credential, then redirect to the real page before showing a login error to the user. This example shows how the information from the user is stolen [25].

### 12.3.1.3 Sybil Attack

Surfacing of IoT bares a system for a Sybil attack, which possesses numerous identities with single node. It means that antagonist can be at many locations at a single time. It degrades the resource utilization and integrity of security of data. In 2012, Online Social Network shows the statistical evidence of Sybil attack—about 20 million of Sybil was on Twitter each week and 72% (76 million) fake users on Facebook. Sybil attack is used to sneak the data from a website by generating its malware. Considerably, Sybil poses as a disguise which looks similar to the regular users, but in reality it is not. Social media such as Instagram, Twitter, and Facebook are quite vulnerable to Sybil attacks. Hence, nowadays, it is important to maintain the IoT system and have a security defense so as to work correctly. Sybil and spoofing attacks basically prey the identification (medium access control (MAC) and radio frequency identification (RFID) addresses) of the users to acquire the systems illegitimately in IoT. Also, it is observed that TCP/IP suite do not have a reliable security, hence making the devices of IoT more prone generally to spoofing. Furthermore, Sybil attacks can even help to initiate other severe attacks that can cause damage to the system, such as DoS and man-in-the-middle attacks.

### 12.3.1.4 Jamming Attack

Jamming uses intentional radio interferences to cause harm to the wireless communications by keeping the communication medium busy, due to which whenever the wireless medium is busy the transmitter backs off. Jamming attacks mostly take place at the physical layer but cross-layer attacks are also possible sometimes. Proceeding transmissions in the wireless network by sharing undesirable signals to IoT devices creates some difficulties for users because of the consistently busy network [26]. Also, this attack somewhat corrupts the execution of IoT devices by utilizing extra memory, bandwidth, energy, etc.

### 12.3.1.5 Man-in-the-Middle Attack

In this man-in-the-middle attack, the invader can eavesdrop on the message and also attempts to trade off the information between the two nodes and makes the nodes believe that they are communicating directly. For example, some malicious commands may be sent by the intruder to the actuators, shamming to share faulty response by sensors to the PLC or to be the PLC. Also, the invader can manipulate or discard messages. Valid syntax is used by these types of attacks; therefore, rule-based IDs cannot identify it from the message format. Such attacks can be prevented by encryption techniques. Hence, the man-in-the-middle intruder or an attacker poses to be in the communication system while the attacker is directly connected with another user device. The attackers or the intruders can effortlessly cease conveyance of a message by misleading and presenting forged data so as to handle the original data and intercept the information by eavesdropping.

### 12.3.1.6 Selective Forwarding Attack

In selective forwarding attack, the communication system functions as a node that permits leaving few packets of data in the course of transmission. Due to this, a hole is created in the network. It is quite difficult to avoid and identify this attack. This attack occurs by carefully choosing the packets to be forwarded. With the help of this attack, it is easier for DoS attack to be launched. This attack’s main goal is to filter any protocol and disrupt routing paths. In routing protocol for low-power and lossy networks (RPL), the intruder can share all the control messages of RPL and release the remaining traffic. Creating a dynamic path or disjoint path between the children and parent can be a solution to this attack. In another solution, the attacker would not be able to pinpoint the traffic flow by using the encryption method. Heartbeat protocol, which is usually required for the revelation of any kind of disturbance in the network topology, is also required for protection against the selective forwarding attack. For the detection of IDs, solution of this attack provides end-to-end adaptation of algorithm packet loss. Such attacks should be removed and detected.

### 12.3.1.7 Node Capture or Tampering Attack

Node capture is a type of compound attack formed by the combination of physical, active, and passive attacks. Single adversarial device helps in eavesdropping by gathering all the information related to the target node. Other than this, an adversary can act as an active participant in the network. The attacker gathers sufficient information, which needs some time to capture a node. This time to gather all the information can vary depending on different adversaries based on their configuration, strength, and other network constraints. This attack can be further categorized into three types on the basis of their processing ability, energy, communication capability, and strength of adversary in terms of memory such as long attack, medium attack, and short attack. Long attack occurs when a node is being compromised because the intruder takes more than 30 minutes. Medium attack takes place when a node is being compromised because the intruder takes less than 30 minutes. Short attack occurs when a node is compromised because the intruder takes less than 5 minutes [27].

### 12.3.1.8 Denial of Sleep Attack

In 1999, Anderson and Stajano introduced denial of sleep attack. This attack targets the MAC layer and also prevents radio from going into the sleep mode. These attacks can reduce the sensor lifetime and deplete the battery life. Since MAC protocol controls the transceiver, it is targeted by different denial of sleep attacks. MAC protocol tells the transceiver when to sleep, when it should listen to the channel, and when it should transmit the packets. Most of the protocols were made in order to conserve energy by keeping the transceiver in the sleep mode.

### 12.3.1.9 Wormhole Attack

The key objective of this attack is to disturb the traffic flow and network topology. Also, this attack occurs because of the selective traffic being transmitted through it and by creating a tunnel between the two tunnels. Merkle tree authentication construction can be used to prevent the wormhole attack. In Merkle tree, the formation is initiated from the leaf to the root node, and in RPL tree, the formation is initiated from the root to leaf nodes. It uses public key and ID of node for the calculation of hash. Each child identifies its parent. Authentication of the node is initiated at the root node and ends at the subjected node. The children nodes ignore the incorrect selection of parent in case of failed authentication. A wormhole node attracts the packets by forming a fake route between the destination and source. The packets that are extracted by the wormhole nodes can modify, be released, or share the information with a mediator for harmful motives. Wormhole attacks are a bit hard to recognize, and they can strongly influence the implementation of a network service, for example, synchronization of time, fusion of data, and localization. This attack is a major threat toward the wireless network, especially in case of routing protocols [28].

### 12.3.1.10 MQTT Exploit

MQ telemetry transport (MQTT) is an application layer, open source, publish–subscribe messaging protocol that is planned for IoT communications and lightweight M2M. Two examples of some well-known projects that exploited MQTT as a protocol of application layer are Flood Net and Smart Lab. Its architecture is mainly based on the publish–subscribe model, whose sole purpose is to exchange information among the clients by a common broker with an ineradicable reliability. In publish–subscribe architecture, all the information is published under a specific address or topic which could be used by all the other nodes that are subscribed to that topic. All the nodes that are subscribed by the broker to a specific topic would be able to receive the relevant messages.
