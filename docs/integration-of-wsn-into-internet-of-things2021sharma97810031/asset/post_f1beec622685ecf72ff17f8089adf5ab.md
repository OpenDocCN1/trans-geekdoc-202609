## 16.4 BLOCKCHAIN TECHNOLOGY

According to IBM, blockchain technology can be defined as “a shared, immutable ledger for recording transactions, tracking assets, and building trust” that is adopted by an increasing number of businesses worldwide. Such a ledger enables the secure transfer of the ownership of units of value using public-key encryption and proof-of-work methods [24]. The beginning of blockchain can be traced back to 1991 when Haber and Stornetta cryptographically secured a number of blocks. The inhibition of the interference of document timestamps was the greatest asset of this technology. In 1992, Merkle hashes, also known as Merkle trees, were incorporated into the blockchain design, enabling multiple document certificates to be gathered in the same block [25]. Merkle trees were patented in 1979 and consist of a cryptographic pattern allowing secure verification of the contents of large data structures.

In 2008, the blockchain design was conceptualized in finance, relieving timestamp blocks from the burden of being signed by a trusted party and making the stabilization rate, with which blocks are introduced to the chain, more complicated [26]. Briefly, this consisted of the rationale of cryptocurrency at the time. Cryptocurrency enables the members of a blockchain cryptocurrency to verify and audit transactions independently and economically. Naturally, the potential for blockchain technology is not limited to bitcoins. It has already gained considerable attention in a wide range of sectors including financial services, charities and non-profits, the arts, and e-commerce [27].

The key features of blockchain technology are synchronization, consensus, provenance of assets, and decentralization [25, 28]. Blockchain is suitable for multiorganizational business networks such as supply chains, financial consortia, and healthcare networks because it has been designed to be distributed and synchronized. The consensus is an incremental part of blockchain safety. Transactions supported by blockchain are agreed between participants in advance under the form of “smart contracts”. Transactions that do not abide by the consensus principle are classified as fraudulent and are excluded. The provenance of assets ensures accuracy despite the protection of privacy and the exclusion of the timestamp. It is possible to track the current location and the history of any asset through the immutability of the recorded data. Decentralization is considered as the most striking point of this technology. A blockchain network is sustained without central control, whether this stands for state services, corporations, or banks. Decentralization is a dynamic concept evolving in parallel to the enlargement of the network.

## 16.5 BLOCKCHAIN IN HEALTH CARE

The difficulties confronting the national wellbeing framework now are notable. From fragile information-sharing methods and the vagueness of clinical preliminaries and clinical examination to complex accusing and problematic consistence, medicinal service companies are feeling the need to control expenses and keep on conveying quality patient consideration.

### 16.5.1 IMPORTANCE OF BLOCKCHAIN IN HEALTH CARE

Consider the possibility that each patient had secure and private access to their whole clinical history. Imagine a scenario where you could see a patient’s finished history of imperative signs, every medicine taken, data about each specialist’s visit, treatment performed, and medical procedure. How helpful would that be for patients as well as for their training and the business when all is said and done?

In 21st-century health care, probably the most common currency is data—individual patient’s data, clinical trials’ data, as well as various experimental data. Individual healthcare practitioners are most commonly dealing with individualized datasets such as patients’ history, laboratory, or imaging test results. Various state or commercial entities from academia and hospitals to the pharmaceutical industry deal with larger patients or nonpatient populations’ datasets [29].

Owing to the success of bitcoin lately, blockchain has gained more attention because of the need to safeguard health data. Generally, blockchain is a technique for creating an open and disseminated on-cloud repository that contains a list of connected data entities called blocks where a block directory to the next one, the so-called blockchain. Such blocks, not centrally housed, are disseminated across several clusters of a framework. Every block consists of a timestamp of its development, the hash of the former one, and the processing data (patient’s health data and the healthcare giver information) [30]. ### 16.5.2 SHARING AND MANAGING MEDICAL DATA

Blockchain can facilitate the transfer of patient records between healthcare systems at the national and cross-border levels, improve management–health management coordination, reduce transaction costs and risks, and even support medical tourism. A single device has no control over the medical data stored on blockchains. Therefore, it can be distributed between groups of individuals and organizations. Each transaction in the chain is time-stamped and becomes a permanent registration of the set, which cannot be changed later.

### 16.5.3 DRUG DEVELOPMENT AND SUPPLY CHAIN INTEGRITY

The healthcare blockchain could improve the traceability and transparency of the supply chain with both the big picture and the smallest details of each pharmaceutical transaction. The blockchain technology is already being used to optimize the integrity of supply chains by companies like IBM, Walmart, and UPS. With supply data stored in a blockchain, patients can access real-time information about each drug, from production to arrival at the retail pharmacy. Bar code tagging allows other features to be integrated into the system, such as:

- Monitoring the availability of medicines in outpatient clinics
- To whom the drug was transferred
- The quantity of drugs transferred

In this way, blockchains can transform the system from disconnected data segments belonging to a single device to the source of truth for any source, be it a patient’s record or a drug circulating in the supply chain.

### 16.5.4 COMPLAINTS, INVOICE MANAGEMENT, AND FRAUDS

Using a blockchain automation-based system can dramatically reduce administrative costs associated with complaints, payments, and processing time, making intermediaries unnecessary. As the Capital One–Gem Health collaboration shows, blockchain can potentially improve the significant challenges of tracking logistics information related to reliability.

### 16.5.5 ORIENTED MAINTENANCE

Blockchains could provide an accurate source of information about vendors. A blockchain ledger could bring together all network stakeholders—payers, providers, and patients—and increase the efficiency of the system by requiring only an update on the network. Smart blockchain contracts can change the manual way in which member complaints and disputes are currently handled.
