# 16.5.6 MEDICAL EXAMINATION

Having a secured centralized storage of clinical trials and patient outcomes for new treatments can dramatically improve patient care and outcomes. Immutable, time-stamped blockchain records of clinical trials and results could help reduce results sharing, data monitoring, and unethical reporting, reducing both fraud and error.

Blockchains could mobilize new innovative research initiatives by sharing patient outcomes more widely (with the patient's consent). In addition, new and innovative research initiatives would be catalyzed by a broader sharing of patient outcomes (with the patient's consent) and thus stimulate remarkable collaboration between participants and researchers.

# 16.5.7 CASE MANAGEMENT

Blockchains can also have a positive impact on patient management. Once a patient sees a physician and a treatment plan has been developed, the treatment schedule can be added to the blockchain book as part of the patient's medical records.

Then a smart contract can be developed with all elements of patient management. The contract specifies the framework for the patient's treatment goals in combination with the rules defined in the smart agreement to monitor and evaluate the patient's progress. When the patient record is integrated into the blockchain, it can provide chronological data on the provision of care and the patient's response.

Another layer of benefits can be found when a patient's health information is removed from portable social media monitoring devices and apps and then uploaded to the patient's personal blockchain registry. Again, smart contracts can measure how well patients are performing against their health goals.

The advantage of the process is that everyone in the network can see patient data, but only when the patient gives consent, which eliminates the need for centralized control and reduces the risk of PHI data theft.
