- Things that receive information and then act on it:
  We have all used objects that work by giving them a signal to do something that they act on. For example, when we send our printers a document, they print it. The automatic locks on our cars work at the press of a button. These commands can be as simple as an on/off one or as complex as Beethoven's melodies playing on your Bluetooth speaker.

- Things that can do both:
  The most powerful of all things connected to the IoT are those that can collect information, send it, receive information, and act on it. This is when the real potential of IoT is seen.

Let us take an example to understand the first two points and then merge them to show how powerful IoT systems can be:

Suppose a farmer has installed moisture sensors on his farm which directly send information to the computers or mobile devices at his home to tell him the timely moisture percentage. This is an example of a very basic wireless sensor network (WSN). Using this information, the farmer can easily irrigate his crops when it is needed, thus preventing over-irrigation or under-irrigation and subsequent crop failure, saving him a fortune. Now, suppose he has installed an automatic irrigation system that irrigates the crops. What if that system is automated and connected to the Internet? In that case, the sensors in the soil, when they get signals that the moisture percentage is low and that the crop needs irrigation, can process them and directly send them to the automated irrigation system. The system will irrigate the crops automatically, reducing the workload of the farmer. As everything is connected to the Internet, we can put in algorithms for the system to check weather updates. Depending on the weather predictions, irrigations can be planned in advance. For example, if the weather shows that it is going to rain in the next hour, then the automatic irrigation can be canceled. This lowers the farmer's costs further.

## 13.4.3 SCOPE OF IOT: APPLICATIONS AND EXAMPLES

The IoT brings to the table a possibility that can benefit every organization, irrespective of its field of work, to add value to their work and expand it in previously unimaginable ways. If we continue to add devices, things, people, and the environment, we will be able to achieve results that would seem like magic. Consider the things we encounter in our daily life that are examples of IoT in action. We have used or know people who use smart watches, smart LED bulbs, or even ceiling fans. Nowadays, many devices that are connected to the Internet and are a part of an IoT system of our own are considered 'smart.' Those watches or other smart wearables collect physical data and give us insights about our health and daily activities. You can also control many smart appliances through your mobile devices from anywhere as long as you are connected to the Internet. For example, you can use them to turn on the air conditioner 5–10 minutes before reaching home and switch on the geyser from your couch before going for a bath. Over time, these appliances learn your habits and patterns and start doing these tasks accordingly by themselves. For ease of understanding, keep in mind that the IoT does one or more of the following jobs: increasing efficiency, improving health/safety, and enhancing user experience.

### 13.4.3.1 Increasing Efficiency

Efficient means giving the best possible output from whatever input we are getting. A bulb is considered efficient if it utilizes all the electric power it gets and converts it completely into light. Based on this definition, an increase in efficiency will mean better results with the same amount of work or the same results with a lesser amount of work. This work/output can be anything from electricity consumed and light produced to crop yields per harvest.

We can afford to be inefficient at times on an individual level. However, when we talk about organizations or industries, it becomes very important to increase efficiency, as it directly affects investments and revenues. Some examples are given below:

- Factories can embed sensors in their manufacturing equipment that will help them identify some of the bottlenecks in their workflow. By addressing those bottlenecks, they can cut down on the time taken or waste of labor or material, hence increasing the efficiency.

- Predictive maintenance is a part of some modern industries in our times. It is the use of advanced sensors or sensing machinery and analytics which helps in predicting the maintenance periods of equipment. Instead of doing maintenance according to a schedule, the companies can service the machines when it is needed. This has two benefits. Firstly, it will prolong the life of the equipment as it will be serviced whenever it needs to be serviced. Secondly, it will help reduce the cost of servicing because there will not be any unnecessary servicing. This reduces the chances of accidents occurring, because the machines are being monitored in real time, preventing any malfunction.

### 13.4.3.2 Improved Health and Safety

This aspect of IoT is related to both individuals and mass-scale organizations and even governments. This is because of the more efficient monitoring, detection and surveillance measures that IoT provides. Some examples would be the following:

- In hospitals, patients can be monitored over time for even minute changes in their physiological conditions. Those machines can then learn to predict the behavioral outcome of a patient over time based on his or her physiological condition. A well-trained algorithm can deduce heart stroke even before the patient starts to show any symptoms or experiences any pain. Thus, doctors can be warned and more lives can be saved because we will know what is going to happen.

- IoT sensors and analytical machinery can play an important role in disaster warning systems. With enough data and real-time monitoring, sensors can detect minute changes in the environment that precede events of mass natural destruction such as tsunamis or cyclones. Thus, evacuation warnings can be issued ahead of time so that people can leave safely or shelter themselves properly. This will reduce the damage to life and property.

### 13.4.3.3 Enhancing Experience

Over time, through IoT and ML, the world of electronic devices or anything that can connect to the Internet will start to shape itself in a way that will make us feel more comfortable as things will be easier to use, devices will respond according to our mood, etc. It will be a big leap compared to how devices work now, passively responding to our inputs. They will automatically adjust to our needs and will act based on patterns that are derived from observing our activities over time. For example, living in a smart home that has a smart thermostat can adjust its settings based on the weather outside.

Now, from our knowledge, we can see that although AI, ML, and IoT are very closely related to each other and that their domains intersect each other, there is still a slight but significant difference between them which we need to acknowledge. Let us try to understand this difference. Imagine you are playing a game in which you have to burst all white balloons from many different colored balloons thrown at you by the rival team in different directions, without bursting the balloons of any other color.

You will do it in three steps:

- 1. You look at the balloons and see how many balloons of different colors there are.
- 2. Based on that, you will decide which balloons are white.
- 3. You finally judge which balloons you have to burst and which ones you have to save from bursting impulsively in the heat of the moment.

In the above example, Step 1 deals with the IoT. We know that IoT includes objects with sensors on them that can capture and transmit data over the Internet.

Step 2 encompasses ML because the player tries to interpret the colors of the balloons through logical reasoning. This can be done by developing a program that helps the machine differentiate between the different colors.

Step 3 refers to AI, the branch that trains the machines to become independent and decide for themselves whether or not to burst a balloon. Thus, it helps in decision-making, a natural ability of humans.

IoT devices capture large amounts of data. Thus, the machines should be properly trained and capable enough to make decisions about the data, such as separating them into different categories so they can be stored systematically on the servers and retrieved easily as and when required. Storage is just one aspect of it. AI is required by the IoT devices to make decisions and act accordingly.

In summary, AI and IoT are both on their own incredibly and amazingly powerful technologies. Their integration has modernized human lives in such a way that our forefathers thought impossible.
