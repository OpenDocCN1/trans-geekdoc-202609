Technology has advantages that make people’s lives easier: providing completely computerized houses; intelligent buildings; and smartphones allowing greater technological mobility. However, there are disadvantages related to information security: complexity, and cost of implementation, in general, for the development of this type of environment; still evaluating the cost of maintaining this digital structure [21].

Pervasive computing does justice by making the computer or digital device have the ability to obtain information from the environment in which it is embedded and use it to dynamically build computational models, i.e., perform the control, configuration, and adjustment of the application to better serve the needs of the device or user. In this sense, it is clear to point out that the most profound and lasting technologies are those that dissipate in everyday things until they become indistinguishable. Considering that the environment can and should also be able to detect other devices that can be part of this environment, reflecting on the features of these devices becoming “invisible” to the user, where the user uses these devices naturally. From this interaction, the ability of digital devices to act “intelligently” in the environment populated by sensors and computer services appear [21,22].

The concept of pervasive computing implies that the computing means are distributed in the environment, with the ability to extract very detailed information from each part of that environment, whether that information is perceivable and/or unnoticeable to the user. From this, pervasive computing is able to use it to control, configure, and adjust applications, dynamically building computational models, to better meet the needs of a device or a user. Considering the number of sensors, computers, and applications in the environment, detecting the existence of each other and interacting in order to build an intelligent context [21–23].

When thinking about pervasiveness, it means to be everywhere, however without being noticed. Examples that illustrate this concept are related to starting the car without worrying about all the engineering behind the plastic, metal, and electrical conductors that make up the whole ensemble enabling citizens to move around the streets and avenues of cities, or even activating a switch at home without worrying about the entire electrical infrastructure that includes equipment from transformers, and electrical networks that run kilometers away to the hydroelectric plants (matrix of all energy), in order to hope that the only result of this action (pressing the switch) is that the light turns on. This is pervasiveness, i.e., technologies that are part of people’s lives without them being aware of their existence or participation in your daily life [22,23].

It is worth noting the difference between ubiquitous computing that seeks to add functionality and availability of computing services, considering that the focus of technology on the user is directed on the task and the tool. That is, making this human-computer interaction invisible, integrating information technology with people’s natural actions and behaviors. It is important to consider that the concept of invisibility is not literally with a sense of vision, but in a way that users do not even realize that they are giving commands to a computer [22–24].

Thus, ubiquitous computing benefits from advances in mobile computing and pervasive computing; therefore, it arises from the integration of mobility with the distributed, imperceptible, intelligent, and integrated presence of devices and their applications. Pervasive computing, on the other hand, refers to the view of devices or computers as an effective part of the users’ daily lives; i.e., through these many devices in the same environment, a pervasive system is generated that treats each one of them with some “intelligence” [24,25].

The challenges related to pervasive computing are as follows: scalability, this is related to “smart” environment demands growth, i.e., for each new device, a new application; heterogeneity, concerning masking unequal conditions within the environment, i.e., differences in communication protocols, in order to provide operability between networks and applications developed for specific devices; integration, concerning the various devices in a single environment, and the priority of use between each of these devices/applications, concerning aspects of reliability, quality of service, and security; invisibility, concerning that the system requires minimal human intervention, reconfiguration techniques are dynamic, and human interference is only in the sense of improving the system’s “intelligence”; perception, which is directly related to the system and the devices scattered around the environment, considering them perceiving/capturing signals, i.e., “feeling” the environment in which they are inserted—that is, evaluating this aspect does not depend on the user’s experience; and the management of the context, which starts from the perception, and the acquisition of data from the environment, which intelligent control can be implemented, in order to add “intelligence” to the system—that is, the temperature and brightness of the environment can be controlled depending on the user’s taste [26–30].

Concerning limitations and difficulties, the cost of equipment and energy consumption are linked, the question of bandwidth, information security, and privacy, reflecting on the evolution of the means of communication and quality in data transmission, more specifically the evolution of wireless networks concerning Bluetooth, WiFi, and WiMax technologies, among others [27–31].

Thus, pervasive computing technology is transparent in the sense that the user does not feel that he is interacting with a computer, or that at some point in his life, he is being controlled by one. That is, it is nothing more than the transparent human–machine interaction, emphasizing “machine” in the sense of “computational devices” around the world, considering that different platforms are divergent paradigms. In the same sense that ubiquitous computing is a pervasive computation, where the difference is in the “ubiquitous” sense, which means “it is at the same time everywhere” [32].

## 3.4 MOBILE COMPUTING CONCEPTS

Mobile computing is defined as the competence to transport computing devices easily and practically, allowing access to the content of the devices regardless of location, in the same sense as providing their presence and use anywhere. That is, compact computers that can be carried practically by their user, which over the recent past have become ever-present devices, increase the user’s ability to use services regardless of their location [30–32].

The technological model of mobile computing does not change while the user moves; that is, mobile devices are not able to identify the environment where they are and cannot adjust themselves to it, but combined with the access capacity, mobile computing has to turn computing into an activity that can be carried anywhere. The limitation concerning the environment in which the mobile devices are can be overcome by manually adjusting the device settings made by the user; however, this alternative overrides the main idea of mobile computing, which is practicality and ease [33].

Mobility is the ability of a device to be easily moved, i.e., portability transported, concerning two main characteristics: the size of the device and accessories and the weight of them, offering a set of functions, being able to connect with other systems and easy to use. This mobility can still be achieved through innovative mobile devices such as wearables, which connect to various operating systems, having sensors to monitor health, integrated with operating systems, and even respond to voice commands, or even integrated to types entertainment and sports monitoring applications. Or yet, wearables like augmented reality glasses that don’t depend on smartphones designed to interact with 3D objects [34].

Usability concerning the fact that it is easy to use by different types of people; this property touches characteristics that influence the use of devices concerning the user are their capacity, dexterity, and knowledge; and even the device and its size which results in flexibility. Concerning functionality, devices serve multiple purposes and different types of functions, evaluating their applications with objectives and activities [30–34].

These properties link ubiquitous computing with the provision of services when and where necessary, in a noticeable way or not. In the same sense as computational pervasiveness concerning the domain of personal computers, the philosophy of technology is that practically anything, from cars to clothes, from stores to houses, outdoor and indoor environments, and even wearables on the human body, can be equipped with chips that connect with other networked devices [30–34].

Analyzing characteristics that support the best intelligent device “quiet and invisible”, should extend a person’s unconsciousness; creating tranquility in usability concerning services accompanying the user, in the same sense as transparency concerning devices, hardware, and software, which must be discreet. Ubiquitous technology derives from the calm technology that runs on the periphery of the network of technological environments in which smart devices are present; i.e., it does not affect the people’s perception center, and it is “one that informs but does not demand focus or attention” [30–34].

In this sense, mobile computing refers to the use by people of portable mobile devices with great operational and functional power, with the mobile device’s ability to connect to other devices, obtain data, and provide them to other users and even devices [35, 36].

In this context, it is possible to highlight the four pillars of mobility concerning portability, defined as the ability to be easily transported, the usability in which it is tangible to be usable by different types of people in different environments.
