# 12.3.1.8 Denial of Sleep Attack

In 1999, Anderson and Stajano introduced denial of sleep attack. This attack targets the MAC layer and also prevents radio from going into the sleep mode. These attacks can reduce the sensor lifetime and deplete the battery life. Since MAC protocol controls the transceiver, it is targeted by different denial of sleep attacks. MAC protocol tells the transceiver when to sleep, when it should listen to the channel, and when it should transmit the packets. Most of the protocols were made in order to conserve energy by keeping the transceiver in the sleep mode.

# 12.3.1.9 Wormhole Attack

The key objective of this attack is to disturb the traffic flow and network topology. Also, this attack occurs because of the selective traffic being transmitted through it and by creating a tunnel between the two tunnels. Markle tree authentication construction can be used to prevent the wormhole attack. In Markle tree, the formation is initiated from the leaf to the root node, and in RPL tree, the formation is initiated from the root to leaf nodes. It uses public key and ID of node for the calculation of hash. Each child identifies its parent. Authentication of the node is initiated at the root node and ends at the subjected node. The children nodes ignore the incorrect selection of parent in case of failed authentication. A wormhole node attracts the packets by forming a fake route between the destination and source. The packets that are extracted by the wormhole nodes can modify, be released, or share the information with a mediator for harmful motives. Wormhole attacks are a bit hard to recognize, and they can strongly influence the implementation of a network service, for example, synchronization of time, fusion of data, and localization. This attack is a major threat toward the wireless network, especially in case of routing protocols [28].

# 12.3.1.10 MQTT Exploit

MQ telemetry transport (MQTT) is an application layer, open source, publish–subscribe messaging protocol that is planned for IoT communications and lightweight M2M. Two examples of some well-known projects that exploited MQTT as a protocol of application layer are Flood Net and Smart Lab. Its architecture is mainly based on the publish–subscribe model, whose sole purpose is to exchange information among the clients by a common broker with an ineradicable reliability. In publish–subscribe architecture, all the information is published under a specific address or topic which could be used by all the other nodes that are subscribed to that topic. All the nodes that are subscribed by the broker to a specific topic would be able to receive the relevant messages.
