## 3.5 IoT ON UBIQUITOUS COMPUTING

The concepts and utilities that pervade ubiquitous computing reach the Internet of things (IoT) with regard to the consequences of ubiquity in people's daily lives, since the IoT deals with the Internet connection for everyday objects, such as televisions, automobiles, cell phones, cars, clothes, doors, notebooks, smartphones, refrigerators, microwaves, webcams, and game consoles, among other intelligent objects that provide ubiquity [36].

The concept of the Internet of things (IoT) is related to devices connected everywhere, allowing the total integration of the technology/human relationship in such a subtle way that its use becomes invisible, being done so naturally that it is not even noticeable. Because information through IoT devices will flow everywhere, at all times and in any situation, since the main objective of technology is to provide that everyday objects can connect to the Internet through intelligent sensors that grant the ability to communicate and processing of data and information. Related to the principle that users do not always realize they are connected (ubiquitous computing), reflecting on the use of physical objects connected to the Internet, forming a network capable of collecting, and thus analyzing and transmitting data, besides making decisions without interference based on that [37].

Ubiquitous computing presents a new communicative paradigm for society in relation to technological advancement, changing the form of interaction and social communication in both virtual and physical reality, by proposing the connectivity between different types of objects and the Internet, since technology is adapted and integrated into people’s daily lives, mainly through smart and wearable devices, such as bracelets, watches, and glasses [35–37].

In this context, IoT’s main feature of ubiquitous technologies is the ability they have to mix and adapt to the activities of the individual. Considering the premise of direct collaboration between smart sensors, in order to create a range of smart applications, making decisions taking place without human intervention. In this context, ubiquitous computing is about a computing revolution, which comes from the rapid advancement of mobile technologies, as well as the use of IoT through sensors embedded in everyday things [37,38].

Still considering that the current systematic evolution of society is a reflection of this ubiquitous computational advance, since information permeates all aspects and acts in all human activities. Performing a daily immersion in ubiquitous technologies closely related to psychological factors. Since people do not think they are using this type of technology, i.e., they simply end up not realizing that they are connected. Thus, it is in this context that IoT emerges, as a new form of information transmission between man and machine (and between machine and machine). Considering that technology is a global network infrastructure, including the existing and evolving Internet, as well as network developments, interconnecting physical and virtual objects through the connective exploration of data capture and communication capabilities [37–39].

Thus, the immense sum of digital supports provided by ubiquitous computing in concomitance with the growth and evolution of the Internet and the Web has to do with the continuous growth of the amount of data present in the digital world coming from these sensors and smart devices. Since information is not only present in books and printed materials, but also in peripherals and devices that can transmit information anywhere, making access completely ubiquitous, it also comes from the ubiquitous transmission, reception, and sharing of information and, consequently, knowledge [37–39].

The IoT is also possible for the optimization of business processes, offering many ways to be applied, by obtaining in real-time information about the work environment, or considering the factory floor and the production gain of a machine, by using sensors or mobile device apps to locate devices or other functions. With regard to the number of things that are connected, it allows long-distance monitoring, giving the possibility of gaining and making better use of time [40].

Thus, this evolution of the Internet shows a very promising quality, i.e., ubiquitous computing, bringing the era of “calm” technology, when technology retreats to the backdrop of our lives, since ubiquitous technology is based especially on the phenomenon of IoT in the development of a contemporary technological society, which allows more interaction between users and equipment, as well as between devices [41].

As well, through IoT, as an intrinsic part of ubiquitous computing, it is based on the change in interactivity between users, becoming something natural, imperceptible, proactive, and mainly omnipresent, which can create countless applications that allow human beings to integrate information and assist in areas by transferring time–space consumptions for a given object [42].

In this sense, a wave of technology starts in ubiquitous computing where through common objects they are integrated employing high technology, converting them into “smart” devices that when connected acquire the ability to generate and exchange information between themselves and their users. IoT brings to the daily lives of people the creation of intelligent and interactive environments, which refers to the current moment of modern society [41–42].

Thus, in summary, IoT with respect to “things that think”, is the ability that ubiquitous devices have to acquire intelligence, perception, and interaction with each other is made up. In the same sense that through ubiquitous computing it revolutionizes the relationship between human beings and machines by producing objects endowed with “intelligent” functions that interact with each other. Still reflecting that these devices produce and exchange information in a way that has a profound impact on social relations, relating from the user’s point of view ubiquity evokes and grants the human desire to overcome spatial and temporal barriers in order to achieve the gift of omnipresence [43].
