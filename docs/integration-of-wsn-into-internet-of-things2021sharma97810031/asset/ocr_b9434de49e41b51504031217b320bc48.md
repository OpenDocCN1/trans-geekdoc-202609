The security issues in a sensor network are broadly classified into two categories, namely system security and information security. System security covers concerns surrounding network management-related issues that arise out of the architecture of the network and are further classified into administrative-level, physical-level, and technical-level security. Information security concerns revolve around issues regarding confidentiality, encryption, integrity, authentication, and freshness protection of valuable data to reduce the risk of sabotaging, theft, or manipulation. The sensor networks are highly exposed to the physical world and lack external security features, making them extremely vulnerable to physical threats such as tampering. Also, the virtual nature of data transmission further exposes the data to virtual attacks from attackers with malicious intent. Few attacks that have been more common recently include eavesdropping, data modification, impersonation, and replaying. The special attacks on biomedical data in transit such as eavesdropping by time token, data modification, and impersonation attacks have helped to highlight the importance of information security wherein the attackers attempt to deteriorate the data integrity, data encryption, and freshness protection of data.

The countermeasures that often have been proposed to mitigate these attacks have been to introduce a well-defined user hierarchy along with strong authentication protocols for access control and restraining unauthorized access. This measure has proven to be beneficial to reduce administrative-level security issues along with reducing overheads in network management. Physical-level security issues can be reduced by careful design of sensors to make them tamper-proof and restricting physical access to sensors. Technical-level issues that include intrusion and routing inconsistencies were earlier reduced by monitoring of servers, disks, and other physical devices, which later proved to be cost-ineffective and shifted the focus toward solutions such as secure routing methods and the creation of secure user groups. In the domain of securing information-level security, a proposal of using ID-based cryptography has been discussed along with the novel architecture for the protection of sensitive data [12].

+   User-Friendliness: The diverse nature of patients in the healthcare monitoring applications and sensors poses a challenge of user-friendliness. Since a major proportion of users of the sensor networks are the elderly and people with special abilities, using a technical and skill-requiring environment may reduce interaction. A study surveyed the degree of user-friendliness and concluded that the user interface of the applications needs to use user-modeling involvement to increase interaction and make the interface more natural [13]. This is true for improving not only the patient side of interaction but also healthcare professionals alike.

+   Ease of Development and Scalability: It is estimated that nearly 20 million devices would be connected to IoT networks worldwide by the end of 2020. A pervasive healthcare system in such a large ecosystem of devices would require the user to be tracked and monitored for multiple vital parameters simultaneously to track any developing or undiscovered ailments.
