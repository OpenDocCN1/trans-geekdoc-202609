Technology has advantages that make people’s lives easier: providing completely computerized houses; intelligent buildings; and smartphones allowing greater technological mobility. However, there are disadvantages related to information security: complexity, and cost of implementation, in general, for the development of this type of environment; still evaluating the cost of maintaining this digital structure [21].

Pervasive computing does justice by making the computer or digital device have the ability to obtain information from the environment in which it is embedded and use it to dynamically build computational models, i.e., perform the control, configuration, and adjustment of the application to better serve the needs of the device or user. In this sense, it is clear to point out that the most profound and lasting technologies are those that dissipate in everyday things until they become indistinguishable. Considering that the environment can and should also be able to detect other devices that can be part of this environment, reflecting on the features of these devices becoming “invisible” to the user, where the user uses these devices naturally. From this interaction, the ability of digital devices to act “intelligently” in the environment populated by sensors and computer services appear [21,22].

The concept of pervasive computing implies that the computing means are distributed in the environment, with the ability to extract very detailed information from each part of that environment, whether that information is perceivable and/or unnoticeable to the user. From this, pervasive computing is able to use it to control, configure, and adjust applications, dynamically building computational models, to better meet the needs of a device or a user. Considering the number of sensors, computers, and applications in the environment, detecting the existence of each other and interacting in order to build an intelligent context [21–23].

When thinking about pervasiveness, it means to be everywhere, however without being noticed. Examples that illustrate this concept are related to starting the car without worrying about all the engineering behind the plastic, metal, and electrical conductors that make up the whole ensemble enabling citizens to move around the streets and avenues of cities, or even activating a switch at home without worrying about the entire electrical infrastructure that includes equipment from transformers, and electrical networks that run kilometers away to the hydroelectric plants (matrix of all energy), in order to hope that the only result of this action (pressing the switch) is that the light turns on. This is pervasiveness, i.e., technologies that are part of people’s lives without them being aware of their existence or participation in your daily life [22,23].

It is worth noting the difference between ubiquitous computing that seeks to add functionality and availability of computing services, considering that the focus of technology on the user is directed on the task and the tool.
