
## 15.4.2 MEDICAL STATUS AND MEDICINE INTAKE APPLICATIONS

Monitoring the medical status of a patient continuously over some time and keeping a track of medicine intake is one of the two largest areas of application of WSN-based IoT technology. Studies suggest that the duration of medicine intake and the dosage remains one of the biggest challenges for the elderly and cognitively challenged patients. Therefore, systems and devices that could help notify medical staff remotely about the regular intake of the medicines could be used as a valuable input to keep track of the progress of treatment.

Using RFID tags and sensor networks to keep track of the medicine intake of the patient is one of the most cost-efficient and effective methods [35]. High-frequency RFID tags are used for medical-bottle identification and weigh scale to keep track of the medicine removal. Besides, the patient wears an ultra-high-frequency RFID tag, which is a part of the patient monitoring system to alert the patient to take required medicines. This medical monitoring system and patient monitoring system together are connected to the base station that maintains the overall system and analyzes the dosage based on treatment for each patient. In contrast, healthcare monitoring devices face a challenge of wearability. For example, MobiHealth integrates the different wearable devices to the mobile phone and uses multiple technologies such as BAN, PAN, and WAN simultaneously in health care [36].

CodeBlue is developed by Harvard University where the hardware part consists of a pulse oximeter, electrocardiograph, and motion analysis sensor board, and the software framework is based on a subscription routine framework [37]. AlarmNet is another wireless healthcare monitoring system that is used to monitor vital signs such as heart rate, the saturation of oxygen in the blood, and electrocardiograph along with location-sharing capabilities. AlarmNet collects data using wireless sensors and accelerometers, stores data for data mining, and uses IP networks to ensure privacy and security [38].

In certain areas, the development of healthcare tracking applications can prove to augment the industrial process a person is involved in. For example, FireLine is a project designed especially for firefighters to track their cardiac activity and take necessary action in case of an emergency. This way, using a wireless sensor, heart rate sensor, and three electrodes, the cardiac activity of firefighters is tracked from the base station and a check is kept on baseline measurement and deviations from baselines in case of abnormalities [29]. Similarly, LifeGuard was originally designed for astronauts who tracked parameters such as ECG, blood pressure, pulse rate, and oximetry [40]. The wearable device used in LifeGuard collected and stored data in the central physiological observation device (CPOD).

Further, the use of WSN-based IoT technology has been extended for COVID-19 tracking and rehabilitation using symptom data collection, quarantine/isolation center monitoring, data analysis center, and an interface to health physicians. In all of the above-mentioned components, biosensors that are placed on the body are used to track the health and symptoms of the individual such as fever, cough, sore throat, fatigue, and shortness of breath. Further, the symptoms are generally tracked using different biosensors including oxygen sensors, temperature sensors, motion and heart rate sensors, and image-based classification. These symptoms, once recorded, are transmitted over wireless channels to the data analysis center, which analyzes these symptoms along with the health conditions of the neighbors of the individual to identify and track the disease transmission. Further, the WSN-based IoT is also being used to track health and non-technical data using machine learning algorithms including travel history of the patient and contact history of the past 3–4 weeks, age, gender, and medical history. These parameters have proven to be extremely important to assess and predict the fatality rate among various age-groups. The use of WSN-based IoT has also helped healthcare professionals to be available to patients while maintaining social distancing [41, 42]. Further, applications have been developed that anonymously track the contact history of patients till they are tested positive for COVID-19, thus ensuring the privacy of the patients [43].

## 15.4.3 Movement Tracking Applications

Studies have proven that accidental falls in people over the age of 65 have been a leading cause of death. Hence, movement tracking applications have been critical for tracking fall detection, posture, and gait of the elderly. The technology developed for this application uses components ranging from accelerometers and gyroscopes to unobtrusive video camera sensors connected to a network.

One of the proposals by Information Technology for Assisted Living at Home (ITALH) uses a video camera and accelerometer for the detection of falls. Once the fall is detected using accelerometer data in real time, the mote turns on the Bluetooth-enabled camera to livestream the fall to the phone and then connects emergency contact if the initial request to the phone fails. This is one of the most efficient fall detection mechanisms used in the healthcare industry [44].

Smart home care network is one of the fall detection systems used. The key feature of this technology is that it is a multimodal fall detection system that coordinates among various wireless and image sensors to form a network of sensors. In this network, accelerometers are placed along with user badges that have the capability of voice transmission and use image sensors for analyzing the situation and raising the alarm. If the fall is confirmed, the received signal strength indicator (RSSI) is activated to send the approximate location of the patient and the user badge uses IEEE 802.15.4 radio link to establish a connection.

Movement tracking applications not only are limited to the fall detection systems but extend to posture and gait analysis. One of the applications of the posture analysis system is found in HipGuard. Hip replacement surgery requires a long recovery time of 12 weeks and is quite common in the elderly. This system operates by placing nodes on the waist, thighs, shins, and feet. These nodes are connected to a central control unit that raises an alarm in the form of haptic vibration when the position of the limbs upon exertion of load exceeds the limits set by professional healthcare staff [45]. For this system to allow ease of movement and be comfortable, sensors can be fitted into the garments.

## 15.5 CONCLUSION

With the advancements in information and communications technology (ICT) day in and day out, there is a growing demand for technology and systems that would make human life easier and more comfortable. This is not a new trend, yet the field of applications to which humans have applied WSNs and IoT has been unprecedented. Health care is still a luxury for many around the globe, and optimum health care despite the best efforts from doctors and the medical fraternity has still a long way to go before it reaches the most obscure and remotest parts of civilization. In this light, WSN-based IoT in the medical and healthcare industry is a boon to patients and doctors alike.
