# ## 9.2.3 SMART GRID

Smart Grid is an electricity network that can efficiently integrate the behavior and actions of all users connected to it, in order to economically ensure a sustainable energy system with low losses and high levels of quality and security of supply [11,26].

Consumers will be able to exercise control over the use of the energy they consume and exercise their environmental responsibility, resulting in greater efficiency of electricity [26,27].

This revolution in current network standards, being more modern and efficient, brings countless benefits to all consumers of electricity. The Ministry of Mines and Energy of Brazil issued an ordinance, Ordinance No. 440, of April 15, 2010, creating a working group to analyze and identify actions that are necessary to support the establishment of public policies for the implementation of a Brazilian Smart Grid Program—Smart Grid.

Smart Grid makes this transformation possible, bringing new philosophies, concepts, and technologies that allow, for example, the use of the internet as a utility for the electrical network. Most importantly, it allows existing technologies from other engineering sectors to modernize the network to reach its full potential [27,28].

The introduction of the Smart Grid concept will produce a marked convergence between the energy generation, transmission and distribution infrastructure, and the digital communication and data processing infrastructure. The latter will function as internet of equipment, interconnecting the so-called IEDs and exchanging information and control actions between the various segments of the electric network. This convergence of technologies will require the development of new methods of control, automation, and optimization of the operation of the electrical system, with a strong tendency to use distributed problem-solving techniques based on the use of multiagents [28,29].

The construction of energy generation, transmission, and distribution systems based on the Smart Grid concept necessarily uses telecommunication systems to perform their essential functions [30,31].

As this is a change in the way energy is delivered to the consumers, the concessionaires’ relationship with the customers will be remodeled, and the amount of information available may change consumption habits and increase the quality of services provided. This technological change will affect several sectors of the economy, from industry to the specialization of the workforce, and could make the power grid more reliable [31,32].

Smart Grid is an electrical network that, intelligently, can combine the actions of everyone connected to it—producers, consumers, or both, whose objective is to efficiently distribute the sustainable, economical, and safe supply of electricity [32,33].

Within the context of Smart Grid, some new technologies appear that end up becoming outstanding characteristics of the network [33,34]. The interest in monitoring the QEE that travels through the transmission and distribution system has increased in the last two decades, since disturbances caused mainly by large consumers started to interfere in the energy supplied to other consumers, compromising sensitive loads such as communication, computer, and other systems.
