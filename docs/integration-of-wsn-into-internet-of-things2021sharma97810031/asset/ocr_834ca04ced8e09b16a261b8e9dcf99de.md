In the educational field, ubiquitous computing expands a wider range of educational opportunities as well as developing knowledge hunters. Since technology allows accessing information in highly personalized ways from a variety of sources and sharing it in a flexible way (through whatever access device), since these devices were previously "isolated and disconnected islands" in the hands of a student or teacher in a classroom or even in a library, making it a continuous search device for varied information through technology integrated into everyday life. Generating a pedagogical approach through access to, conversion of, and flow of information results in very short cycles of remembering, understanding, and applying knowledge [44].

In the health field, pervasive computing can be incorporated into portable and implantable devices (biosensors) that monitor, maintain, and ensure the well-being of patients and detect and record vital signs such as heart rate, temperature, and even breathing of the user, or even bracelets (wearables) that measure the heartbeat, among other data. Still considering that the medical devices will react in case of emergency, issuing a help alert electronically to doctors and specialists. Or even with regard to surgical instruments equipped with ubiquitous technology capable of detecting changes in a patient's vital statistics, increasing accuracy, analyzing side effects of prescribed drugs, allowing faster responses, and expanding the rescue options available in intensive care settings, and thus improving the quality of care [45].

In the automotive field, the ubiquitous technology can be applied by the communication devices integrated into the vehicles and using your energy sources, considering parking sensors together with embedded systems allowing a series of applications to passengers ranging from listening to music and watching movies. Contextualizing a macro application of an intelligent transport system that makes use of contextual information and ubiquitous computing, it could use the location histories of a large number of vehicles, also could be combined and aggregated to provide real-time digital road map updates to better adapt to traffic situations and support users wherever they are [46].

In the environmental field, ubiquitous computing can use satellite information, sensors, and users in order to create personalized maps of land use, deforestation, and conservation, allowing remote monitoring of forests in real-time. Through technology, complex data and analyses of constantly changing environmental conditions are provided, since the sensors provide better detection of earthquakes, floods, and fires, quickly assess scenarios involving natural phenomena, such as hurricanes, and allow for much more accurate predictions with other natural disasters by improving communication, coordination, and rescue response [47].
