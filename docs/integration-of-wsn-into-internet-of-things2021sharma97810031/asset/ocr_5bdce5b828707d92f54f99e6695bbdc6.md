# ## 5.6 CONCLUSIONS AND FUTURE WORK

In this chapter, we have explored the security challenges that must be solved for integrating WSNs into the IoT, given the potential avenue of QEA. We have covered the state of modern lightweight cryptography, the potential of quantum computing, and the progress in the development of quantum-safe security algorithms. The principal risk in selecting new cryptographic algorithms is the possibility of unforeseen vulnerabilities. Valuable research and development resources might be invested in created solutions that are suddenly demonstrated insecure. This is the reason why quantum-safe solutions for WSNs must be reviewed carefully. Auditing and scrutiny of potential proposals can lower the likelihood of overlooking hidden threats. Most areas concerning PQC are just now beginning to be explored. Cryptanalysis, side-channel attacks, protocol designs, and implementation optimization represent areas where much work is needed in the short and medium term. The end goal of the analysis provided in this work is to showcase that some of the post-quantum algorithms seem to satisfy conditions that are deemed acceptable for WSN applications, even in an early stage. It is quite possible that once some algorithms are standardized, their implementations will be improved to reach more efficient solutions. A likely possibility is that new WSN devices will adapt to the new security requirements and the associated overheads imposed. This would potentially result in greater monetary costs for the end user. If the application can absorb this cost, then it is likely that the demand will sustain novel directions on the design of constrained devices. Our findings show that some of the NIST post-quantum candidates selected for the second round of the standardization process show promising characteristics for use in the IoT and WSNs. Remarkably, their computation time is not too far from the acceptable latency of lightweight cryptography solutions, even when accounting for the technology variations. Regarding storage requirements, there are some competitive schemes that would fit well with the memory availability of most commercial motes. Researchers interested in PQC alternatives for their use in the IoT and WSNs might start by looking at these algorithms.

The creation of efficient realizations of post-quantum algorithms will play a significant role in their adoption by WSNs and the integration of this technology into a quantum-enabled IoT. Future research can be done for exploring implementation alternatives of post-quantum algorithms on commercial WSN devices in order to assess their usability and costs with metrics better quantifiable through experimentation such as quality of service, side channel footprints, and energy consumption profiles. Alternatively, the system-on-chip approach can also be further studied as a viable platform for efficient acceleration of PQC algorithms.
