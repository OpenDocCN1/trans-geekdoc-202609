
## 7.3 STATE-OF-THE-ART UTILIZATION OF ML WITH WSNs FOR SMART AGRICULTURE

ML enables to learn from experience and acquire intelligence from raw data gathered from sensors to make smart decision-making [20,21]. The beauty of ML algorithms is that it learns from the massive datasets without prior knowledge of relationships among the features of datasets. And as the learnt model is fed with more datasets, it is learnt in a better way. ML is a powerful tool to learn the nonlinear dataset relationships and solve complex real-life problems [22,23]. The multidimensional dataset may include the sensor data or images captured with a camera. It allows to make smart and quick decisions with the advantage of learning from the past experience. ML techniques are profoundly being used in diverse fields of real-life applications. Supervised ML includes applications to the problems based on regression and classification. Unsupervised ML is applied to the segregation-based problems. The performance of ML-based application depends upon the model selection, parameter selection, quality and amount of dataset, parameter tuning, and understanding attained of the problem. ML allows to automate the extraction of patterns existing within the huge dataset and to learn and then make smart decisions without human intervention.

Smart agriculture involves making many decisions like how to use resources (soil, manure, and water), which crop yields better in a particular weather and soil quality, and how long it will take to prepare land for farming, continuous monitoring, and preventive measures from crop diseases. In such complex real-life situation, WSNs along with ML techniques work well to improve the crop production and enhance the quality of the final product. WSNs using sensors gather data and then ML techniques find the information hidden, thus allowing farmers to do agricultural activities in a smarter way. With a range of sensors, data are collected regarding the texture and structure of soil, the percent of organic content available in the soil, the amount of minerals present in the soil, and the moisture maintenance capacity of soil. With this dataset, some important decisions that can be made are as follows: for which crop is the soil appropriate? Which fertilizer and in what amount will it be required? How and when to irrigate the farm? Which crop rotation pattern will be suitable? What method is appropriate for pest control and weed control and when to apply? In a similar way, environmental factors such as rainfall pattern, solar radiation, wind availability, ambiance temperature, and moisture are monitored. The dataset also allows to make further decisions as weather is another important factor that affects the farming activities. Gathering the data and processing it to obtain the power of smart decision-making enable to perform the agricultural activities in a smarter way and improve the crop yield. Next, some of the applications of WSNs and ML in smart agriculture are reviewed.

Ravi et al. [24] used fuzzy rules to predict which crop is to be grown in the candidate soil and environment conditions. They installed sensors are connected with a WSN to the cloud to monitor the climate temperature, environment humidity, and pH level of the candidate soil. Fuzzy rules were framed and applied to the sensor gathered data and which crop to be grown is predicted. The productive information associated with the weather, climate, and farm was made available to the farmers via their cell phones connected using global systems for mobile communications (GSM) and global positioning system in the WSN.

Kumar et al. made use of Naive Bayes Kernel algorithm [25] to analyze the real-time sensor data and predicted which pest or crop disease would affect the crop and monitored the farm continuously to accurately predict when it will occur. They collected real-time data and historic data to find the correlation and quantify the possible impact of pests and diseases. The environmental parameters collected were climate humidity, environment temperature, and air pressure. The soil features gathered include the pH value, content of nitrogen, moisture level of soil, and temperature of soil. Suitable sensors were installed using the ZigBee WSN to monitor the crop and assess all the features. With the application of Naive Bayes Kernel algorithm, the authors contributed an accurate way to predict which disease (or pest) will affect the crop and when it will occur along with the degree of impact of disease on the yield.

Joshi et al. deployed the Bayesian statistics [26] successfully to manage resources effectively and schedule the irrigation activity in an automated way. To schedule irrigation events, they utilized climate conditions, weather monitoring, soil conditions, and water need of the crop being grown. Sensors were installed in the WSN and weather data were gathered to forecast the requirement of watering the farm and automated the schedule using a cloud-assisted system. Further, they contributed to decision-making for weed extraction. This was achieved using a camera and image processing. Wi-Fi module and MQTT sensor assembled using Raspberry Pi and Arduino controller were the major components for this work.

Vincent et al. [27] made huge contribution in the field of smart agriculture and explored an accurate method to assess the land suitability and to decide which crop will grow best in that land. Their work was based on artificial neural networks (ANNs) and multilayer perceptron. A WSN was installed with electromagnetic sensors to measure the pH level of soil, moisture content of soil, and soil salinity. The gathered data were analyzed using the ANN model. The dataset with selected features was fed as input to the ANN model and output neurons returned one of the four decision categories. By formulating a four-class classification problem, the land suitability was assessed.

Zeng et al. [28] estimated the quality and quantity of the yield from the seeds of sunflowers. They used ANN and least square regression to predict the characteristics of the crop. The remote sensing approach using WSN was implemented. High-resolution dataset gathered features like salinity of soil, the amount of nitrogen in soil, and other climate variables.

These days, drones are being utilized to monitor and assess the crop health, to spray pesticides over crops, to plant seeds, and to analyze the fields and crops [29]. Satellites placed in earth orbits are used to capture the images of the fields and to monitor the crop production. A high-definition camera and sensors play a vital role in sensing the environmental state variables and soil condition. In this way, a WSN with ML techniques are used for irrigation scheduling, crop prediction, and disease detection in the arena of smart agriculture. ## 7.4 CASE STUDY—A SMART IRRIGATION SYSTEM

In the previous section, we have seen the emergence of smart agriculture. It is evident that IoT plays a key role in smart agriculture. Sensors are necessary to gather information about agricultural fields, climate parameters, and soil. Now, we present a case study—a smart irrigation system using automation. We developed a smart irrigation system at the laboratory using the WSN and sensors [31,32].

### 7.4.1 INTRODUCTION

The proposed system makes use of WSNs and collects data using multiple sensors installed at different nodes and sends the gathered data to the cloud via the wireless protocol. This smart agriculture system is developed using Arduino UNO, temperature sensor, moisture sensor, water level sensor, direct current (DC) motor, general packet radio service module, and Wi-Fi module. Irrigation system starts with checking the water level, humidity, and moisture level. It informs the farmers by generating an SMS alert on their phones regarding the levels and automatically starts the water pump. If the temperature goes above the threshold level, it automatically starts the fan. It constantly displays the status using LCD display. Constantly, the recordings are sent to cloud where it can be analyzed to find out the pattern and predictions can be made. The block diagram for the entire setup is shown in Figure 7.1. It shows the input (via sensors) and the output (via actuators or motors). The processor is Arduino UNO. The live data are stored to cloud.

The agenda is to assist the farmers by providing live environmental data like temperature and moisture for better environment and soil monitoring and enable the smart farming. It will increase the crop yield, and the quality of products will be enhanced. The live data feed is stored using cloud “ThingSpeak.com.” It is tested over diverse agricultural lands and shows a high accuracy of 98%.
