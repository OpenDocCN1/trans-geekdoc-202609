## 13.5.6 HOME AUTOMATION

The advancement of technology is incomplete until this modernization reaches the comfort of our homes. Home automation is a term for the modification of our homes in such a way that there is a shift from the manual framework of doing tasks to the automatic framework. The most common example of this is the robot vacuum cleaner Roomba® [22]. Its sensors aid in detecting obstacles such as walls or furniture while it is cleaning the room. Just like normal vacuum cleaners, it has brushes that clean the surface on which they are mounted. Being connected to the Internet, the homeowner can switch it on or off and clean the house even when they are away from home. This is a great advantage in today's world, as people can easily carry out necessary tasks without lifting a finger. Another example can be automated smart lightings, which can be connected with our user interface (UI). Smart locks are yet another extremely powerful application. With this powerful tool, people need not worry about losing their keys, theft, or being late. Smart locks enable a person to lock their homes from anywhere, but when they detect some suspicious activity around them using their visual ability, they inform the house owner by sending them a notification. Natural language-based AI robots, home air quality/water quality monitors, and smart mirrors that can even be converted to television screens are few other examples. Enhanced comfort and care and improved security are the ultimate goals of bringing this technology into our homes.

## 13.5.7 BLOCKCHAIN

Blockchain is a distributed ledger technology that is decentralized and eliminates the need for a third party as their supervisor [23]. The main characteristics of blockchain are as follows:

- Distributed: It is a distributed technology that allows data/transactions to be distributed among all entities in the chain, instead of being copied or transferred.
- Decentralized: It means there is no need for a third party to manage the data and transactions because every entity in the chain has the exact copy of the ledger.
- Immutability: Data once stored on blocks cannot be changed because the data structure used in making this technology allows only to append data, but we can add a new block if a majority of the nodes think it is valid. If the nodes think it is invalid, it will be rejected. This is also attributed to the incorruptible nature of this technology.
- Transparency: The addition of a single block or transaction between any two persons is reflected in all the blocks. Thus, nothing is hidden from anybody.
- Peer-to-Peer Network: Blockchain allows transactions to occur intimately between two parties, excluding the need for any third party. The transaction can be digitally signed by the sender using his/her private key and the receiver’s public key. Only the correct receiver will be able to decipher the correct code using his/her private key and the sender’s public key. Hashing algorithms—which are one-way functions, like a trapdoor—are an important part of the signature generation, which makes breaking the key practically impossible. The sender can rest assured that cybercriminals cannot tamper with their data.

What are blocks? Blocks are nothing but digital records on the blockchain that are linked to each other, forming a chain of records.

The convergence of these technologies is already in progress, and combining the best features of all leads to the development of such applications, which truly justifies the essence of modernization. In the above applications, we have seen how AI, ML, and IoT together can help transform industries, but adding them to blockchain takes the advantages to a completely different level. Security is a major concern, and no industry or individual wants to compromise on it. Though AI and IoT technologies merge seamlessly, security is still a concern that bothers professionals. Cybercriminals are always ready to attack and steal our data to use as it suits them. This issue is most reliably addressed by blockchain technology through the use of blind or digital signatures [24]. Digital signatures are the cyber-counterparts of hand-written signatures backed up by very hard algorithms. They can be decoded only by the receiver with the use of their own private key and the sender’s public key. This helps make the whole transaction personalized, satisfying the users with the level of security provided.
