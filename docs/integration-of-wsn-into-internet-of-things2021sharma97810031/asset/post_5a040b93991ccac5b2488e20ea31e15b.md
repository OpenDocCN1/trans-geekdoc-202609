## 8.2 BIG DATA BACKGROUND

Big data as a term has been used for more than the better half of the past century. However, the implication that it carries is shifting throughout times. The search for different techniques to perform highly complex and analytical functions while employing the least amount of time promotes everyday creativity and innovation in this field.

### 8.2.1 CHARACTERISTICS OF BIG DATA

For providing better services, control, and proper transmission of data, there are certain technologies that microgrid includes [16]. The data collected through microgrids are continuously growing in size and in comprehensibility level. Conclusively, the problem is said to be a case of big data if the obsolete methods of processing it cannot surmount toward handling either the volume and constant increase in data or the format that makes the variety of data. Big data does not simply equate to tremendous amounts of data but also toward the data variety and its velocity [17]. There are certain characterizations present that can help sort big data [18].

The value extracted from the microgrid data is a product of big data analysis. More savings for the clients could be achieved with the help of the bidirectional flow of information between the customer and the grid because it provides opportunities such as utility profit, dynamic pricing between generation and load, and better prices for consumers. The humongous data that one encounters in the microgrid are formed from a wide array of sources, which includes sensors, gear, real-time attainment of data, supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA), energy market, data regarding power inputs, and waveforms of signals [19]. This generally accounts for the massive sheer volume of data encountered [20]. This integrated plethora of data demands various different sorts of protocols to comprehensively work with, which are present in microgrids. These data of microgrid provide a link between the designed, semistructured, and formless data [21]. The organized data are displayed with a clear and structured representation in databases, which could be directly used to portray the consumer consumption along with the various trends present in the market. Semiorganized data are sculpted with the help of JSON and XML tools. Weather data representation is one such example of sensor data used here. Unstructured data consist of human interaction data, data from social media, and other graphic information.

Another factor to consider here is the velocity of data transmission. The rate of data generation and processing is increasing due to the technologies that take very little time in communication and are embedded inside the microgrid. Data inside the microgrid could be present in groups, online or collected from streaming and cyber-security sources. One of the most important aspects of using the data in a microgrid is whether the data being used is correct and valid for their intended purpose or not. This is to ensure that the information fed is correct and not fabricated.

Another important feature is the veracity. Communication of data between microgrid elements can be reliable, but also noisy and in certain cases not truly competent. The observational data must be checked for any other reasonable change that may occur [22]. One of the most common examples for the same would be testing whether the range of temperature can be transformed to a certain value within a limited amount of time or else this observation can be discarded. Unpredictability is also a very important element in ensuring that the microgrid data are not unnecessarily abandoned after processing. Figure 8.1 depicts some of these big data characteristics.

The whole architecture of big data can be boiled down in the following phases. During the working of each of these phases, the data that are acquired from various different sources, usually raw, are refined and manufactured into data that can be used for various different purposes [23]. The second step is the procurement phase in which the data collected are amassed to procure and distinguish correct information for other phases. This contains three further divisional phases: the process of gathering data from various sources that create such raw information is the first phase. The sources used in microgrids here range from sensors to smart homes, etc. The second phase is the transmission of data, which usually represents the process of transporting data for storage and handling to a center of data, and this is realized by the use of communication arrangements. The third phase is to tackle the problems that occur with the presence of repeating and error-filled data, and, hence, preprocessing of data is applied. This compresses and reduces the repeating characters along with identifying and cleaning faults.

Storing of data with the purpose of further working and handling it is done at the third step. The fourth step is known as the analysis of data phase, which accelerates the inquiry of the gathered data with the help of various sculpting and review methods that aid in creating a comprehensive and confidential extract of all the raw information garnered from the phases before. This is a crucial phase in the processing of big data as its objective is to extract all the valuable information for future applications.
