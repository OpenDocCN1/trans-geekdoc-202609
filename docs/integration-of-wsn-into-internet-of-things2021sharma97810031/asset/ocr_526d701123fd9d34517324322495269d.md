# 12.3.1.1 DoS Attacks

DoS attacks are accountable for interrupting system’s services by producing many unnecessary demands. Hence, the user cannot communicate and access the IoT devices, which make it a bit hard to take the appropriate conclusion. Also, DoS attacks consistently maintain the IoT devices to be switched on, which in turn affect the battery lifespan. Distributed DoS (DDoS) is a special type of attack that occurs when several attacks take place with different IPs to keep the server busy and create some requests. This makes it difficult to compare it with the attack traffic and normal traffic [24]. Over the past few years, a virus of IoT botnet known as Mirai introduced catastrophic DDoS attacks that caused harm to many IoT devices by intrusions.

# 12.3.1.2 Spoofing Attack

During the spoofing attacks, the system’s problem is the detectability of IoT devices, which is a security issue. The attacks are done by creating a routing loop, producing a false error message, and a few other techniques. Spoofer in the beginning does not convey a signal even to obey to the relevant transmitter. However, when the relevant transmitter ceases sending the signals to the appropriate receiver, then spoofer begins to send the unfaithful signals. For example, an intruder contaminates the network by sharing the phoney routing data. On Instagram, a user is demanded to “like” for him/her to be directed to the fake Instagram page. This phoney page takes user’s credential, then redirect to the real page before showing a login error to the user. This example shows how the information from the user is stolen [25].

# 12.3.1.3 Sybil Attack

Surfacing of IoT bares a system for a Sybil attack, which possesses numerous identities with single node. It means that antagonist can be at many locations at a single time. It degrades the resource utilization and integrity of security of data. In 2012, Online Social Network shows the statistical evidence of Sybil attack—about 20 million of Sybil was on Twitter each week and 72% (76 million) fake users on Facebook. Sybil attack is used to sneak the data from a website by generating its malware. Considerably, Sybil poses as a disguise which looks similar to the regular users, but in reality it is not. Social media such as Instagram, Twitter, and Facebook are quite vulnerable to Sybil attacks. Hence, nowadays, it is important to maintain the IoT system and have a security defense so as to work correctly. Sybil and spoofing attacks basically prey the identification (medium access control (MAC) and radio frequency identification (RFID) addresses) of the users to acquire the systems illegitimately in IoT. Also, it is observed that TCP/IP suite do not have a reliable security, hence making the devices of IoT more prone generally to spoofing. Furthermore, Sybil attacks can even help to initiate other severe attacks that can cause damage to the system, such as DoS and man-in-the-middle attacks.
