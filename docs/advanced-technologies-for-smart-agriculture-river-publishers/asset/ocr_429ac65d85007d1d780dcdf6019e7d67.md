# 5.3.2.2 Results

With the newly established infrastructure, finding forestry-related study material is now easier and accessible to more people than ever before. Integrated modeling in forestry, including the use case mentioned above, incorporates climate adaptation, and this infrastructure is excellent because of its flexibility to support multiple heterogeneous data sources. The system's primary benefit is that it can gather all of the forest-related information that is currently spread out in many places and organize it in one central location. The platform has made available over 300 datasets from European data archives. Small businesses and individuals are also provided with a publishing portal for their metadata. In addition, new modelers are allowed to submit reference datasets for use in integrated modeling.

Due to a lack of quantity and quality, linking concepts in metadata with ideas in an external ontology is difficult. The system may also automatically choose datasets that academics will find useful, such as those from simulation and modeling-related technical disciplines. Insufficient depth and structure in metadata fail to convey the intricate nature of scientific datasets. Commonly used but crucial domains like lineage lack the necessary structure to handle the intricate data generation operations. The shallowness of the metadata structure prevents the systematic addressing of data. The described system fixed this problem by establishing associations between the separate AGROVOC vocabulary pieces and a comprehensive subdomain with domain-specific semantics. According to appearances, this is not a one-size-fits-all answer but rather shows a complex and individualized system.
