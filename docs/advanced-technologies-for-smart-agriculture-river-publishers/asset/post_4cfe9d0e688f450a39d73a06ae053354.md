### 2.2.8.2 Motion PIR sensing element

PIR sensors detect motion and are frequently used to determine whether someone has moved into or out of the sensor range. These sensors are small, low-cost, low-power, and easy to operate. As a result, they are usually found in applications and devices used in homes or companies. This sensor is usually referred to as PIR, passive infrared, or pyroelectric sensor.

PIRs are generally made up of a pyroelectric sensing element that detects the amount of infrared light radiation. Everything produces low-level radiation; the hotter something is, the more radiation it emits. A motion detector's sensing element is divided into two halves. Since the goal is to identify motion rather than average IR levels, the two parts are connected in such a way that they cancel each other out. When one observes more or less IR radiation than the other, the output might swing high or low.

Along with the pyroelectric sensing element, there may be a number of supporting electrical components such as resistors and capacitors. The BISS0001 appears to be used by most small amateur sensors ("Micro Power PIR Motion Detector IC"). This chip receives the sensing element's output and performs some processing to generate a digital output pulse from the analogue sensing element.

Basic statistics:

- Dimensions: Compact.
- Digital pulse high (3 V) when triggered, digital low while idling. Pulse lengths vary from sensing element to sensing element and are governed by resistors and capacitors on the PCB.
- Sensitivity varies: up to 20 feet (6 m) detection range of 110° × 70°.
- Power supply: 5 V–12 V input voltage for many modules, although 5 V will be fine if the regulator has other specifications.

### How PIRs Work

The PIR device itself has two slots, each made of a unique IR-sensitive measurable element. It is observed that the two slots can see past a distance because the lens used here is not doing much. The device is at rest, and each slot sees an equal amount of IR transmitted from the area, walls, and outside. When a warm body, such as a human or animal, passes, half of the PIR device is interrupted, causing a positive difference change between the two halves. When a warm body leaves the detection area, the device creates a negative differential change, causing the device to produce a negative difference change. The sensed pulses are detected.

The IR device is protected by a window made of IR-transmissive material that is hermetically sealed to improve noise/temperature/humidity.

### 2.2.8.4 L293D motor driver

The L293 and L293D are high-power quadruple half-H drivers. The L293 is designed to provide bidirectional drive currents of up to one amp at voltages ranging from 4.5 V to 36 V. The L293D is intended to generate 600 mA of bidirectional drive power at voltages ranging from 4.5 V to 36 V.

### 2.2.8.5 Servo motor

The rotary or linear actuator servo motor can precisely control angular or linear position, velocity, and acceleration. It consists of a suitable motor and a position feedback device. It also requires the use of a complex controller, which is usually a separate module developed mainly for servomotors.

The term servomotor, while widely used to describe a motor suitable for use in a closed-loop control system, does not refer to an explicit type of motor.
