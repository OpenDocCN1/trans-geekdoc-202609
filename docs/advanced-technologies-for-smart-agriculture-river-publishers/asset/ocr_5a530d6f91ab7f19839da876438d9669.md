The CNN includes three distinguishing characteristics which make a potentially valuable tool in additional crop yield modeling. (i) A CNN model has been developed which represents environmental elements as well as the genetic transformation in seeds through time despite knowing their genotype. (ii) The model has proven the capacity that extends yield prediction across untested conditions while maintaining high accuracy. (iii) When combined with the back propagation approach, the model was able to demonstrate the level towards which weather prediction accuracy, weather conditions, management practices and soil characteristics may influence crop production variation. During 2008 and 2016, Khaki and Wang [24] developed the deep convolutional neural network technique which estimates the corn yield in 2247 locations. A deep learning architecture was developed by Kim et al. [25] during 2006 and 2015 on agricultural yield prediction. The soybean crop production in Argentina and Brazil, which uses deep learning and transfer learning approach, has been estimated by Wang et al. [26]. With remote sensed pictures, Yang et al.
