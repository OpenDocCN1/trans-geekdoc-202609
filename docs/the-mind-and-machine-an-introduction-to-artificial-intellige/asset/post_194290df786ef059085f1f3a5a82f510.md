In the 1970s, the focus of AI research shifted to developing expert systems. These systems were designed to mimic the decision-making abilities of human experts in specific domains, such as medical diagnosis or financial forecasting. They used a combination of rule-based systems and knowledge representation techniques to represent the expertise of a human expert.

In the 1980s and 1990s, machine learning (ML) emerged as a new approach to AI. ML algorithms are designed to learn from data, rather than relying on predefined rules. They can identify patterns and make predictions based on the data they have been trained
on. This approach has been particularly successful in fields such as image and speech recognition, natural language processing, and computer vision.

Today, AI is used in a wide variety of applications, from self-driving cars and virtual assistants to medical diagnosis and financial forecasting. The field is rapidly evolving, and new developments in areas such as deep learning and reinforcement learning are enabling machines to perform tasks that were once thought to be the exclusive domain of human intelligence.

In terms of types of AI, there are several different types of AI that have been developed over the years, each with its own unique capabilities and applications. Some of the most common types include:

- Reactive Machines: These are the simplest type of AI, and they do not possess the ability to form memories or use past experiences to inform current decisions. They can only react to the current situation and perform a predetermined task.
- Limited Memory: These types of AI have the ability to use past experiences to inform current decisions, but they do not possess the ability to form long-term memories.
- Theory of Mind: This type of AI is capable of understanding and simulating human emotions and mental states.
- Self-Aware: This is the most advanced type of AI, and it is capable of understanding and
introspecting its own mental states and emotions.

The field of AI has come a long way since its inception in the 1950s. From rule-based systems to expert systems, and now machine learning, the field has evolved to become one of the most promising and rapidly growing areas of computer science. With the advancement in technology, we may see AI that can understand and simulate human emotions, introspect its own mental states and emotions and may become self-aware in the future. However, as with any rapidly developing field, it is important to consider the ethical and societal implications of AI as it continues to advance.
