The first approach to the automatic detection of arousals was based upon the MP decomposition of only the C3–A2 single EEG channel and the standard deviation of EMG by implementing the rules established by the American Sleep Disorders Association (ASDA) [\[31\]](#c12.xhtml#c12-bib-0031). The MP structure shows the frequency shifts within different frequency bands. Such shifts lasting three seconds or longer are related to arousal. To score a second arousal a minimum of ten seconds of intervening sleep is necessary [\[27\]](#c12.xhtml#c12-bib-0027). In [Figure 12.6](#c12.xhtml#c12-fig-0006) the results of applying the MP algorithm using Gabor functions for the detection of both rhythms and transients can be viewed. Each blob in the TF energy map corresponds to one Gabor function. The 8–12 Hz alpha wave, sleep spindles (and one K‐complex), and SWA of stages II and IV are respectively presented in [Figure 12.6](#c12.xhtml#c12-fig-0006) (a), (b), (c), and (d).

The sleep spindles exhibited inversely relate to the SWA [\[32\]](#c12.xhtml#c12-bib-0032). The detected arousals decrease in relation to the amount of light NREM sleep, with particular concentration before the REM episodes [\[33\]](#c12.xhtml#c12-bib-0033).

The MP algorithm has also been used in the differentiation of single randomly evoked K‐complexes in response to stimuli from spontaneous periodic ones [\[27\]](#c12.xhtml#c12-bib-0027).

The tools and algorithms developed for recognition and detection of sleep stages can be applied to diagnosis of many sleep disorders such as apnoea and the disturbances leading to arousal.

The MP‐based time–frequency domain analysis was later improved by filtering the signals using singular spectrum analysis (SSA) before feeding the signal into the time–frequency analysis block [\[34\]](#c12.xhtml#c12-bib-0034). During the SSA operation on a single EEG channel of C3‐A2: (i) the eigentriples with the eigenvalue λ[*j*] is rejected if *j* > *η* where:

[(12.2)](#c12.xhtml#R_c12-disp-0002)
```
η = min { h : (∑_{i=1}^h λ_i) / (∑_{i=1}^M λ_i) }
```
where *M* is the total number of eigenvalues or embedding dimension; and (ii) among the remaining eigenvalues only those with a pair of similar amplitudes representing sinusoidal components are selected. After applying the MP‐based method a multiclass support vector machine (SVM) is used to classify the sleep stage. A block diagram of the overall system is depicted in [Figure 12.7](#c12.xhtml#c12-fig-0007).

The scoring result compared with manual scoring by a sleep expert can be seen in [Figure 12.8](#c12.xhtml#c12-fig-0008), which is reasonably accurate and acceptable for being used in clinic.

### 12.6.3 Detection of Normal Rhythms and Spindles Using Higher‐Order Statistics

Use of long‐term spectrum analysis for detection and characterization of sleep EEG waveforms has been very popular [[35](#c12.xhtml#c12-bib-0035), [36](#c12.xhtml#c12-bib-0036)]. However, these methods are unable to detect transient and isolated characteristic waves such as humps and K‐complexes accurately.

In one approach, higher‐order statistics (HOS) of the time domain signals together with the spectra of the EEG signals during the sleep have been utilized to characterize the dynamics of sleep spindles [\[37\]](#c12.xhtml#c12-bib-0037). The spindles are considered as periodic oscillations with steady‐state behaviour which can be modelled as a linear system with sinusoidal input, or nonlinear system with a limit cycle.

In this work, second and third‐order correlations of the time domain signals are combined to determine the stationarity of periodic spindle rhythms to detect transitions between multiple activities. The spectra (normalized spectrum and bispectrum) of the signals, conversely, describe frequency interactions associated with nonlinearities occurring in the EEGs.

The power spectrum of the wide sense stationary discrete signal, *x*(*n*), is the power spectrum of its autocorrelation function given by:

[(12.3)](#c12.xhtml#R_c12-disp-0003)
```
P(ω) = ∑_{n=-∞}^∞ R(n) e^{-jnω} ≃ (1/N) ∑_{i=1}^N X_i(ω) X_i^*(ω)
```
