
(10.10)
$$
\hat{\mathbf{R}}_x = \mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^T = \hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}_s \hat{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_s \hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}_s^T + \hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}_e \hat{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_e \hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}_e^T
$$

where the first $m$ left singular vectors of the decomposition are designated as $\hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}_s$ and the remaining eigenvectors as $\hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}_e$. Accordingly, the diagonal matrix $\hat{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_s$ contains the first $m$ eigenvalues and $\hat{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_e$ contains the remainder. To estimate the above parameters the general rule using least‐squares (LS) fitting is:

(10.11)
$$
\{\hat{\mathbf{\rho}}, \hat{\mathbf{\theta}}, \hat{\mathbf{S}}\} = \arg\min_{\mathbf{\rho}, \mathbf{\theta}, \mathbf{S}} \|\mathbf{X} - \mathbf{A}(\mathbf{\rho}, \mathbf{\theta})\mathbf{S}^T\|_F^2
$$

where $\| \cdot \|_F$ denotes Frobenius norm. By optimal substitution [\[40\]](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0040) we achieve:

(10.12)
$$
\{\hat{\mathbf{\rho}}, \hat{\mathbf{\theta}}\} = \arg\min_{\mathbf{\rho}, \mathbf{\theta}} \|\mathbf{X} - \mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^\dagger\mathbf{X}\|_F^2
$$

where $\mathbf{A}^\dagger$ is the Moore‐Penrose pseudo‐inverse of $\mathbf{A}$ [\[41\]](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0041). Given that the rank of $\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{\rho}, \mathbf{\theta})$ is $m$ and the rank of $\hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}_s$ is at least $m$, the smallest subspace correlation value, $C[m] = \text{subcorr}\{\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{\rho}, \mathbf{\theta}), \hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}_s\}[m]$, represents the minimum subspace correlation (maximum principal angle) between principal vectors in the column space of $\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{\rho},\mathbf{\theta})$ and the signal subspace $\hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}_s$. In MUSIC, the subspace correlation of any individual column of $\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{\rho}, \mathbf{\theta})$, i.e. $\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{\rho}_i, \mathbf{\theta}_i)$ with the signal subspace must therefore equal or exceed this minimum subspace correlation:

(10.13)
$$
C_i = \text{subcorr}\{\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{\rho}_i, \mathbf{\theta}_i), \hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}_s\} \ge C_m
$$

$\hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}_s$ approaches $\mathbf{\Phi}_s$ when the number of samples increases or the signal‐to‐noise ratio (SNR) becomes higher. Then the minimum correlation approaches unity when the correct parameter set is identified, such that the $m$ distinct sets of parameters $(\mathbf{\rho}_i, \mathbf{\theta}_i)$ have subspace correlations approaching unity.
