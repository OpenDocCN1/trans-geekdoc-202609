1. Compute the fast Fourier transform (FFT) of the signal at the low resolution.
2. Set `j` to `n[p]` (number of WT resolutions); perform the following iteration steps:
3. Compute the FFT of the wavelet coefficients at the scale `j`.
4. Multiply the wavelet coefficients `W[j]` by ![images](images/c4-i0025.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mover accent="true"><mi>G</mi><mo stretchy="true">~</mo></mover></math>.
5. Multiply the signal coefficients at the lower resolution `C[j]` by ![images](images/c4-i0026.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mover accent="true"><mi>H</mi><mo stretchy="true">~</mo></mover></math>.
6. The inverse Fourier transform of ![images](images/c4-i0027.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><msub><mi>W</mi><mi>j</mi></msub><mover accent="true"><mi>G</mi><mo stretchy="true">~</mo></mover><mo>+</mo><msub><mi>C</mi><mi>j</mi></msub><mover accent="true"><mi>H</mi><mo stretchy="true">~</mo></mover></math> gives the coefficients `C[j-1]`.
7. `j = j - 1` and return to step 3.

The use of a band‐limited scaling function allows a reduction of sampling at each scale and limits the computation complexity.

The WT has been widely used in EEG signal analysis. Its application to seizure detection, especially for neonates, modelling of the neuron potentials, and the detection of EP and ERPs will be discussed in the corresponding chapters of this book.
