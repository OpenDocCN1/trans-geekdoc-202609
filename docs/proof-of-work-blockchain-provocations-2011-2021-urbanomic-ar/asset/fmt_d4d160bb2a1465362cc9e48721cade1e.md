
## RM

I talk about nature being encrypted, and about gematria as a side channel attack on God’s private signing key—this is very flowery, but at the very least at the level of metaphor, this is what gematria is: gematria is the idea that the encrypted book of nature has a weakness in its cipher that we can, in cryptographic terms, attack, or in hacker terms, exploit, to get it to leak information from the dark forest of creation. So I zoom into and out of metaphoric mapping and literal operation, being deadly serious about the history of ideas and deeply ironic about the actual application of them.

In using the hashes, one idea that was important was Deleuze’s idea of the ‘dark precursor’, which is any coincidence in the structure of knowledge or culture that you can hang something onto. And again, this is coincidence intensification, which is magic, and critique as disenchantment with the self-description of something.

## MBK

Some of your work seems to operate by showing that, when brought into contact with the density of cultural and historical associations available to us—which, in fact, it always is in contact with—Bitcoin as a ‘machine for truth’ (to adopt the most boring-sounding description of it) can be used to produce something oracular and impossible to resolve. [Is Art](#57-Is-art.xhtml) (2014, 2016/2017) is the most acute possible case of parlaying the blockchain model of truth into something that we regard as being a truth worth thinking about, a profound but irresolvable truth.

## RM

It is absolutely correct that Bitcoin is a truth machine, but in keeping with the theme of bathos, it is a truth that would make a philosopher weep. There is no profound truth to ‘this coin has been created by the system, this coin has been spent by this person, we have moved this value from address a to address b’. All of that may be entirely true, and it is a truth made real by a substantial fraction of the planet’s computing machinery, which makes it a truth that the writer of a dictionary or a law or a sermon or a book of philosophy can only dream of having at their disposal. A truth underwritten by that much computing power really is a truth that really is generally acknowledged. But it’s not the sort of truth you can guide your life by, ‘the block reward went to address 1La8w72…’.

In Is Art, you have people choosing to spend Ether as, you know, gas—so there’s an opportunity cost for this and there’s a pricing effect to this—just to toggle the state of this artwork. And this truth will then be made real, intensified, or underwritten, with each block of the chain making it harder and harder and harder to materially undo. This is a model of almost precisely how truth doesn’t work. There’s a passage in *The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy* where Arthur Dent is asking the Heart of Gold starship drinks machine for a cup of tea, and it keeps on analysing his brain to find out what will be absolutely perfect for him and producing a small cup of something that is almost but not entirely unlike tea. Blockchain truth tastes something like that.

Disobedience-in-obedience again: I enjoy producing these monstrous and thereby critical misunderstandings or overly literal understandings of people’s takes on what truth is and how it relates to socio-economic reproduction. That’s a lot of weight to put on one bit of text and a few lines of code, but it’s there if you want it. If you don’t, it’s a fun bit of the history of art, it’s a fun little early Ethereum hack. But for me it problematises this model of truth whereby truth is something we arrive at either by agreement or through power and which then just sort of spreads by some mysterious osmotic process through society. In Hitchhiker’s, again, the ultimate truth of the answer to the question of life, the universe, and everything is 42. That is quite profound and understandable compared to the average truth on the average blockchain—and yet those truths are unassailable.

## MBK

To tie this question of truth back to magic, perceptibility, and attention: there’s something to be said here about the unquenchable human need for aesthetic stimulation and how it betrays the supposed technological quest for efficiency and reason, whether that’s conceived of as progressive and virtuous or fascistic and oppressive. Vanity addresses, for example: Why isn’t the type of truth produced by the blockchain enough? Why isn’t it enough for you to just be a whale, why do you also want to have the equivalent of a personalised number plate for your Bitcoin address?

## RM

The structure of those early Base58 bitcoin addresses is different from modern Bech32 ones, which are more effective in many ways but less interesting aesthetically. You do weird things to the hash of a public key and a checksum and a version identifier which gives you a guaranteed first letter. So for early bitcoin that was 1, and for Dogecoin it was D. And that was followed by a series of letters, and from that you can unpack the hash of the public key, which means that you can check that something has been signed by the right person without them having to reveal their public key. You know you’ve got the right one because of the checksum, and you know that you’ve got the right chain because of that letter prefix. The old ones have sort of various computer science problems and problems as to how you process them, which is why there are newer address formats. But as a piece of both information and user experience and aesthetic design they’re wonderful, they’re instantly recognisable.

Ultimately, this question of truth, I think truth is one of those ideas that’s like love. There are many different phenomena that we call love and they are absolutely real but they are also massively confused through the imprecision of language. With truth we have facts, we have deeply held wishes, we have resonant statistical phenomena, we have, you know, the outcome of applying computer code—these things are all ‘truth’. The ability to produce truths in Bitcoin is very clearly and directly a product of the possession of capital. Miners can censor the inclusion of transactions, although they cannot change the meaning of transactions sent by other people. And if they do so they are economically irrational, they are economically disincentivised to do this.

## MBK

They play the role of the judges of truth?

## RM

The miners are the producers and the underwriters and the judges of truth, but they have no capacity to decide what is true or not in the sense of the truth procedure: they can only apply it, they can’t even say I would like to let you off from this criminal charge but the law won’t allow me to, they just say Yes, this computer program will run, that’s fine, I am calculating this, look at this hash with a long string of series at the start, yeah, give me some money. I’m deliberately playing fast and loose with the word ‘truth’ here, I am pretending I’m using a philosopher’s conception of truth, when I mean merely the fact of a logical one rather than a logical zero. Ultimately, the truth of the transaction having taken place in this dark forest of pure intensive value tells us very little about what has happened on the social plane. If a philosopher could produce as much truth as a Bitcoin miner they would be delighted to death; but if a philosopher could only produce the type of truth that miners produce they would die of disappointment. So as far as the production of truth goes, it’s this ironisation: Hurt me! It’s possible to have absolute truth but with no meaning! No, not like that!
