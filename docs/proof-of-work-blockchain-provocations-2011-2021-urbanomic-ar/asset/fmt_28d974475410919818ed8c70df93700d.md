## Commissions (23/04/2014)

Make a blue artwork 8x8 pixels and submit it for `n` Ether.

Artwork must be a `PBM`.

Artwork must be 8x8.

Artwork must be 40-95% blue (`#000010..#0000FF` etc.)

The contract will check these criteria before paying.

The contract will check uniqueness before paying.

The contract will only accept one artwork from each address.

The contract will only accept `n` artworks total.

This is the simplest possible meaningful commission template. Size and colour could be dropped but their presence ensures family resemblance and is a good approximation of e.g. the requirements for a mural.

Format, size, colour are easy to check in a simple contract script. A more complex script could ask for a portrait and check for faces.

A `kollabor8`-style script could ask for a work based on the previous one then check for similarity to ensure it is a derivative. Again this would be much more complex.

A consequences/Mad Libs style commission could ask for `n` drawings of objects or `n` drawing strips continuing from the last few pixels of the one above then combine them into a larger whole/finished image.

A Sheep Market-style series commission could specify a subject (‘draw a sheep facing left’) and collect the results.

Images can be sent as transaction data. Write a tool to facilitate this? Take format, size, depth, even evaluation (pre-)conditions from contract comments or data.

## Reviews/Critiques (23/04/2014)

Ask people to view `n` images and:

- Star rate each.
- Rank them in order.
- Tag each with 3/5 words.
- Select the ones they like (simple binary choice).
- Rate how long it is interesting for (tingle…).

To avoid abuse, include `n` dummy images.

Seek consensus.

Use images from outside a series, chosen as great or current art. These provide baselines and contrast.

Obfuscate the contract, use multiple contracts rather than a single one that can be taken up `n` times.

Post contracts at different times of day to get different demographics/regions.

Post different images and different ‘do not rank this one’ dummies in each contract.

Provide a grammar for tagging, or require a particular ontology to be used? No, this adds too much complexity.

Be suspicious of all 1-star or 5-star or same-tag reviews? Use dummies that should be rated to filter this?

Ask people whether they think a review is serious/valid as a next stage? Or just rely on multiple reviews converging & remove outliers?

## Shows (23/04/2014)

Either use a multi-commission process or:

1. Choose a theme and describe it verbally or by visual example.
2. Ask people to recommend images by image url.
3. Ask another round of people to evaluate those submissions. Use the review/critique system, with ‘does this image fit the show criteria’ as one of the questions.

Either:

- Store the results in the contract’s data as the show.
- Offer the show as a contract to whoever wants to pay to show it.
- Start a new process to commision a web page or gallery slot for the show.

How to commission catalogue essays and/or a press release for the show?

Use a functional equivalent: a tag cloud or some other outcome.

Ask `n` people to write a paragraph, get consensus or ask a second wave to score or even glue together.

Ask people what they think should go in a show then ask people how to achieve that.

At each stage get a second wave to evaluate the seriousness/interest of the contributions.

## Contracts (24/04/2014)

Ethereum reifies Libertarianism’s fetishisation of state-law-free contracts into technology. Lessig’s ‘code is law’ as an instruction manual.

There is a history of critiquing the legal and economic ontology of art in art. This is part of a more general critique of art’s ontology, although that is also mediated by and through art’s institutional/legal/economic representations.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_’s artwork that is only an artwork at particular times. Have a contract that can turn the art status of itself on and off. Combine this with options, shorts, HFT.

A+L’s ‘Invisible Painting’. A contract that reveals its content only to purchasers. Or that can be queried for properties in return for payment. Or that contains the artwork unrecoverably encrypted. Or that has a key available separately.

A+L’s ‘Guaranteed Painting’. Create the original, show it to `n` people, get them to sign a guarantee of its content, then encrypt & attach & destroy the original. Or just encrypt & sign as a guarantee. Is there any way of selling-on the insurance?

A+L’s ‘100% Abstract’. Describe the construction of a work: the contract itself. This goes with Dan Graham’s ‘Poem Schema’. Self-identity works. See Matt Butler’s XML translation of Graham’s Schema.

On Kawara’s date paintings. Use a nonce instead? How to relate?

Kosuth’s ’One and Three Chairs’. Different representations of the same object. Which object, which representations, how presented? Three values, e.g 1,0001, 0,9999, 1,0 `round()`ed. Three contracts with the same subject. Three options/derivatives?

Weiner’s ‘Statements’. Are these usable? Use the fact of an evocative proposal that is not executed . So a closure/contract (subcontract) that has this character.

LeWitt’s drawing instructions & schemas. A contract (or contract engine) that accredits images (`PBMs`) submitted to it with payment as being instantiations if they validate.

Carey Young’s ‘donorcard’: requires two signatures to become art for the duration. How to set the duration?

Carey Young’s ‘Artistic License’. Use public keys.

An artwork registry contract. Submit hash & payment for the smart property or ID to be added to the list of objects recognised as art. Nomination.

Hirst’s ‘For The Love of God’ and other fractional artwork owning schemes. A coloured coin as an artwork that people can buy & sell fractional parts of.

Dave’s [David Bausola’s] account that briefly held a million. A contract that once held `n` Ether, and the contracts required to make that happen.
