
As ‘Rare Art’, blockchain token-based digital editions have become the latest expression of ownership of value that would previously have escaped capture. The technology of blockchain-based digital certificates of ‘ownership’ of digital art images was initially developed ironically (‘Rare Art’ comes from ‘Rare Pepe’ trading cards). It was then captured by startup capital that cannot explain its contradictions. In its Rare Art iteration, Ethereum `ERC-721` Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs) for digital image URLs are presented as solving the problem that digital art is infinitely reproducible and therefore cannot be sold as a rare asset. They establish authentic provenance and ownership where this was not previously possible.

It is difficult to represent offchain truth onchain, however. It is prohibitively expensive to put bitmapped digital images onchain. And it is impossible to make sure that a digital image is represented uniquely even within the same smart contract on a single blockchain. This means that we must trust the claim that the image represented by each `ERC-721` token is what its creator claims. The authenticity of Rare Art images is threatened by ‘piracy’, where an artist’s images are copied from one Rare Art platform to another and sold without their consent. It is also threatened by ‘trash art’, where the art produced is an undeclared auto-tracing or other mechanical transformation of stock images. Artists who feel entitled to the monetary and reputational value of the art they produce regardless of the method used and collectors who feel entitled to react simply to the price of Rare Art tokens are clashing over matters of authenticity in platforms that were supposed to render such questions moot.

Why not turn this risk into value and make a virtue of inauthenticity?

To make the link between offchain truth and the problem of authenticity explicit I have created a Rare Art edition of Certificates of Inauthenticity for the Shareable Readymades series. If you own one of these tokens you can prove that I have not made the instance of Urinal, Balloon Dog, or Pipe that you have 3D printed or placed in virtual reality (which the `CC-BY-SA` license on the work gives you full permission to do). Print out the certificate that you own the NFT for and display it proudly in a physical gallery, or display it as a digital image on a virtual wall.

Then if anyone questions the authenticity of the art that you are displaying and your right to own it, you can answer that the art is provably inauthentic and that you have no more ownership of it than anyone else. And that you know this to be true with all the cryptographic certainty of the blockchain.

# Shared Secret

## 2020 TOTP QR Code

![](image/shared-secret-qr-code-new1.png)

Shared secrets are part of the invisible structure of society. They constitute a form of currency.

Computing security culture defines ‘secrets’ as passwords, private keys, as unguessable slices of entropy. They are to be revealed only briefly to cross the threshold of a computing system. The consequences of sharing them with attackers, which is to say other people, are catastrophic—leading to your presence in the system or the system itself becoming compromised, or 0wned.

Such sharing is therefore strongly taboo.

Two secrets are stronger than one when establishing that the person presenting them is authentically the person they claim to be. To lose one secret may be regarded as a misfortune; to lose both looks like carelessness. The second ‘factor’ of many such contemporary systems is generated by software using the ‘Timed One Time Password’ (TOTP) algorithm, which generates a new secret value every thirty seconds from a seed value.

Sharing the secret value compromises your security for the thirty seconds during which it is valid. Sharing the seed value means you can be 0wned permanently.

But if that seed isn’t being used for security purposes, sharing it is harmless. It becomes a shared secret in the sociological rather than the security culture sense, currency rather than taboo.

Doesn’t it?

# 1 Doge = 1 Doge

## 2021 Rasterised vector image NFT

![](image/one-doge.png)

Such proof that 1 Doge = 1 Doge. Wow.

A cryptographic ‘proof’ of the assertion that 1 Doge = 1 Doge, rendered in the form of a cryptographic wallet key ‘seed’ and the derivation of a Bitcoin address from it, presented using the deliberately naive typography and colour palette of ‘Doge’ memes such as those that promote the Dogecoin cryptocurrency.

This assertion may resemble the law of identity, as seen in Plato’s Theaetetus (and the writing of Ayn Rand…), but here it is ‘proven’ ironically rather than taken as an axiom, reaching inside a statement of economic fungibility that has become an expression of faith in the lasting social value of cryptocurrency separate from its dollar value.

The ‘proof’ was generated using a modified Bitcoin vanity address generator that selected only seed values starting with `1Doge`, and then searched for Bitcoin addresses beginning with the same prefix. Dogecoin addresses could not be used as they start with ‘D’ rather than ‘1’, and structuring the ‘proof’ for use on another chain adds a layer of referentiality and robustness.

Since the private key for this address has been published in the artwork, it should not be used to actually receive Bitcoin as anyone will be able to steal the results.

# Glossary

## 10K Project

A series of mechanically generated profile picture (PFP) images in a shared style sold as NFTs. Usually as an edition of 10,000 after CryptoPunks, which is the original template for such projects.

## 51% Attack

A network attack on a blockchain that uses the greater share of computing power devoted to mining new blocks to censor or rollback transactions rather than properly extend the chain.

## Accelerationism

The strategy of aligning oneself with and increasing the rate of the decoding or deconstructing effects of the system (…capitalism) in order to break down prior restrictive social forms. More generally: intensifying something in order to increase its rate of becoming.

## Anarcho-capitalism

A social order without the state and its coercive institutions but with money, contracts, and the commodity form of property. The former is the ‘anarcho’ part, the latter the ’capitalist’.

## Anarcho-communism

A social order without the state and its coercive institutions and also without any alienating economic activity or social forms. The former is the ‘anarcho’ part, the latter the ‘communist’.

## ASIC

`ASIC`s (Application-Specific Integrated Circuits) are custom-manufactured computer chips that implement the cryptographic hashing algorithms used in mining cryptocurrency. They are faster than `CPU`s or `GPU`s and were regarded as cheating or otherwise unfair by some people historically.

## BIP-0039

A Bitcoin Improvement Proposal standard that specifies how to encode cryptographic private keys using a standard list of words rather than numbers.

## Block

A collection of transactions or `txes` from a particular period of time validated by the network.

## Blockchain

A series of blocks of validated transactions, each linked to its predecessor by its cryptographic hash. Bitcoin’s innovation was not to produce a blockchain, which is essentially just a Merkle list, it was to produce a blockchain in a securely decentralised way.

## Burn

To destroy or render a cryptographic token inaccessible by sending it to an address that has no associated cryptographic key.

## Ccru

The Cybernetic Culture Research Unit, an experimental philosophical group founded by Sadie Plant at Warwick University in 1995 that escaped the gravity well of academia to become something increasingly more (self-)mythologising, until its dissolution around 2000.

## Copyleft

The use of copyright law in a licence to ironise its restrictions into lasting permissions.

## CounterParty

An early cryptographic token platform based on top of the Bitcoin blockchain.
