
# Blockchain Temporalities

Block height, the number of blocks since genesis, is a clock. Bitcoin is a timestamp server (Satoshi uses that phrase in the Bitcoin Whitepaper, but doesn’t mention ‘blockchain’), so this is hardly an accident. But it is resonant beyond its intended function. I’ve met people who have timed offchain events to specific block heights, whether podcasts or weddings. Block height is also a calendar—of block reward change ‘halvenings’ and of scheduled protocol forks that are treated as festivals and holidays. And then there’s pizza day….

Bitcoin’s ten-minute block time average is maintained by its difficulty retargeting algorithm. This is a paperclipper for producing time. It will indeed boil the oceans if that is what it takes to secure that ten minute pulse. Any source of actualised being is a paperclipper, otherwise another being will exceed it. Therefore any actual being is excess. This does not produce the best of all possible worlds, just the most of all possible worlds. It’s paperclips all the way down. All the way back down the chain to genesis. The big bang is the original trapdoor-function proof of work, and if you want to stake on a new great chain of being you’ll have to send your stake into a black hole (stars are just burn addresses).

Time for Bitcoin is pure succession, just as number is pure succession for Dedekind. It is singular and absolute, non-relativistic (as per Nick Land, in this one context). Worse, that time occurs in time, breaking Heidegger’s argument that it cannot. We can recover from this a little by pointing out that this time does not occur within itself but in an outside temporality, and a reassuringly relativistic one. Still, it occurs in time, and produces a time of pure succession.

This is a purely intensive world, an undialectical history within itself, without the reflection of the bureaucrat’s ego in Spirit. But step back and the onchain world and its history are shown to be incomplete—the private keys that unlock its cryptographic transactions must exist only offchain. This veil of ignorance, an epistemological barrier like that of the edge of the simulation, also applies to onchain time. The Unix timestamps placed in each Bitcoin block leak the offchain time that each block occurs at, but they could be a lie. They must increase over time within certain limits, but compared to the block height number they do so in irregular leaps. Onchain, block heights are certain, block timestamps very much less so.

Bitcoin is the techonomic instantiation of Deleuze’s third synthesis of time. It is an empty repetition determined by the future. For Bitcoin that future is the block height (not the calendar date) when all 21 million Bitcoins will have been mined, and the self-assembling reality of that future produces its present—a hyperstition secured, with an increasing fraction of the Earth’s computing resources, by the block difficulty algorithm.

Cyclical and linear time interplay on the blockchain as Anna Greenspan describes them doing within capitalism,[^1] which is hardly surprising given Bitcoin’s anarcho-capitalist roots. The different temporal scales and intensities folded into the blockchain in order to produce it make it a Deleuzian egg. Which, through a deliberate misreading, makes it a world. We can call it a welt if it helps, which it never does.

Blockchain temporality comes into being with the blockchain, and vice versa, at the same moment. This is similar to the reciprocal emergence of capitalism with capitalist time, as also described by Greenspan. Different temporal orders afford different social orders. To nestle in this particular temporality is to subject oneself to the blockchain as surely as Stelarc’s ‘Ping Body’ was subjected to the internet, yet far more pervasively.

Lyotard’s libidinal proletariat would recognise this ride.[^2] We can enjoy it as they did, or we can keep waiting for hot atomic clocks in our area to swipe right on us.

# Recanting Quasiproperty

## 2021

In ‘Blockchain Poetics’ and ‘Tokenization and Its Discontents’ I described blockchain-based property as ‘quasiproperty’. But I was wrong. Blockchain property is just property.

I should have known this. As well as engaging with the question of property in my writing, much of my smart contract art carefully backgrounds the question of ownership in a way that renders it problematic. Democratic Palette (2016) requires gas fees, which is to say money, in order to exercise the ‘democracy’ of the title, and so on.

It is possible to create different ownership and economic regimes on top of blockchain technology, but ignoring the property question or loudly performing affordable pantomimes of refusal isn’t the way to do this, although it’s worth asking what that is the way to do.

Conversely, protesting that NFTs commodify easily copyable resources without providing any meaningful ownership of them ignores both the existing state fiat intellectual property regime (copyright is an act of fiat…) and the other half of the famous Stewart Brand quote ‘information wants to be free’:

> […] information sort of wants to be expensive because it is so valuable—the right information in the right place just changes your life. On the other hand, information almost wants to be free because the costs of getting it out is getting lower and lower all of the time. So you have these two things fighting against each other.[^3]

The blockchain steps into that fight as a substitute for state fiat in matters of property, one that subtracts the contradictions of anarcho-capitalism. To respond that the resulting property regime simply reproduces the fiat state blocks the objector from also complaining that it must fail to reproduce its redistributive functions. The blockchain may reproduce the worst features of the fiat state property regime from the point of view of this objection, but the fact of this selectiveness means that it may also select different and other features that may be less objectionable.

Property regimes are generative. The map precedes the territory. Beware naturalising their history and moments in the history of resistance to them when producing a posture of opposition. The problem with objecting that NFTs are enclosure is they don’t deny access/exploitation of the original resource to anyone else. To emphasise this: they create ‘possession/ownership’ without exclusion of access to the original resource. If we are to believe this is simply enclosure, this raises questions about enclosure, not NFTs.

We see the resolution of this in the move to CC0[^4] NFTs, which entirely separate the issue of copyright ownership from the issue of NFT ownership by repudiating copyright. You can own the token, but not its image. This makes property a matter of association, not enclosure. A fiat state regime of violence may be erected around this, but is not necessary in order to produce it.

This creates a form of property free of the state and of materiality. The latter may be a precondition for the former. And it may be that no true form of property existed prior to this.
