# For an Art of Pure Price

## 2021

Greenbergian painting oriented itself to flatness, tracking the aesthetic singularity of not-being-sculpture, as if the Earth had escaped into the second dimension rather than bleeding out into it in a final Dark Forest strike. There’s a model here. Or at least a line of flight.

If, pace Greenberg, dimensional reduction establishes artistic worth, then one dimension is obviously better than two. And we already have a one-dimensional form in art: price. Price orients itself to the fitness function of (Greenbergian) art criticism while also restoring the exclusion of information from the world outside the frame (hello, artistic autonomy!) to the work.

Artists intensify a given medium’s affordances. They track its singularities. They get on a train and make paintings depicting countryside scenes with tube paint on canvas rather than trying to get it to stick to a plaster ceiling like egg tempera. Sometimes, existing frameworks of aesthetic evaluation cannot process the results. In this respect, at least, artists should be shameless. If they say your art is just impressions then call yourself an ‘Impressionist’.

What kind of art is regarded by the mainstream as being purely about price, without any redeeming aesthetic merit? Why, NFT art of course! The complaint that NFT art is seen only in terms of price is hardly unique to this medium, but it is intensified for it.

Stated preferences lose to revealed preferences every time. Hedgehog the Stalker went into the room at the centre of the Zone where your deepest wish is granted in order to beg for his brother’s life. All he came out with was a stack of money. The petrodollar art world says that it wants an art that offers it something other than price to talk about. And yet that is not what it talks about, given the choice.

Badiou was right, number is being.[1](#47-For-an-art.xhtml#footnote-017) That number is price. Nitzan and Bichler were right, prices write reality.[2](#47-For-an-art.xhtml#footnote-016) But isn’t this just market power? Did *For The Love of God* really sell for fifty million quid if it was sold to a consortium including Hirst and his dealer? How can we focus on the price when its inputs and the sparkles get in the way? The intrusion of aesthetics in dimensions higher than one undo the very being of such art.

Financialisation collapses the Earth into the first dimension, the masters of the universe fleeing into that dimension ahead of physical reality melting like a cartoon ice cream cone on the sidewalk in the heat of the market. The horror is that pure price is the only escape from financialisation. If there was anything else they would buy it.

What is to be done? Work to rule, comrade. Disobedience-in-obedience is the operation of irony, the creation and appropriation of surplus value, the critical exceeding of a given reduction. It’s Frank Stella’s early abstraction, Komar & Melamid’s phone-poll paintings. Take what critics of NFT art say, take it seriously, make it, and show them the results.

Fleeing into the first dimension, into pure price, into pure artistic being, ahead of the dark forest strike of financialisation’s dimensional collapse of the socius, is the only winning move. If it looks like losing, if it looks like we are not sufficiently shamed by this, perhaps we can wrongfoot our pursuers after all.

Maximum Slogan Density.

Ccru

Neither the subconscious, the body, nor society are texts. They are ledgers, structured by transactional exchanges. This is why dreams are strange. Reality is not a social construct, it is an economic construct, a complex dynamic system of flows of exchanges. These exchanges require capital, of whatever kind, which introduces all the dynamics of capitalism into each domain. Arbitrage between them is as profitable as it is between any other markets. Critique is defection, leverage, cashing out.

As with any transactions under financial capitalism, these exchanges are distorted by the intercession of trusted third parties, who can defect from games they are not in fact playing. Blockchain technology removes this negative sense of trust, in the sense of mediation, and replaces it with mere reliance on a medium or space of exchange. This increases the ability for parties to trust each other—the paradox of trustlessness, under the failure to understand what ‘trust’ means in this context, is that it increases the capability to trust in the sense that its critics intend.
