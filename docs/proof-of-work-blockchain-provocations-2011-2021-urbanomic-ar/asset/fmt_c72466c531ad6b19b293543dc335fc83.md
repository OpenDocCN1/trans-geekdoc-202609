
Three years later, that 1200 dollars worth of Bitcoin had become 50,000 dollars worth. I had stored the puzzle solution on my laptop for most of that time, before realising that this was a really bad idea and transferring everything to cold storage. Marguerite and I occasionally shared details about the encoding in order to check that we hadn’t made a mistake with it, in emails that we then deleted—no doubt frustrating for future art historians (and Vice journalists), but some indication of how our security concerns grew along with the value of the prize.

Fortunately, it was then that someone solved the puzzle.

# Blockchain Aesthetics

## 2014–2015, 2019 HTML5 and JavaScript; Unique giclee prints, each with accompanying 1:1 NFT

![](image/btc-first-halvening-block-hash-40in-8bit-squares.png)

First Send, 2018. Bitcoin Transaction Hash, 8 Bit Palette, Circles.

![](image/btc-first-send-satoshi-finney-tx-raw-40in-8bit-squares.png)

Genesis Block, 2019. Bitcoin Block Hash, 8 Bit Palette, Squares.

![](image/btc-first-halvening-block-hash-40in-8bit-circles.png)

Genesis Block, 2019. Bitcoin Block Hash, 8 Bit Palette, Squares.

The sheer complexity of modern cryptography and the sheer volume of transactions and blocks that utilise it on the Bitcoin network are difficult to imagine. `Blockchain Aesthetics` uses grids of simple tokens of artistic form to do so.

Rosalind Krauss’s re-enchantment of the explicit modernist grid misattributed its effect to myth, to addition or supplement.[^1] The grid has the schematic, overarching, guiding character of myth, to be sure, but its interplay of the abstract and the concrete comes from political rationality rather than storytelling. From subtraction or reification. Krauss recognises this in her consideration of the explicit grid’s early utopian associations. The postmodern grid was a critique of this rationality and utopianism, replacing them with contingency and randomness in order to achieve its meaning.

Injecting new content into the postmodern grid cannot restore it to its modernist significance. It can however place modernist rationality and postmodernist play in tension with each other and focus the resulting attention on that content as a subject of the gaze and of contemplation.

Mechanical encoding of messages into aesthetics is the stuff of kitsch where it has no remainder. `Blockchain Aesthetics` feeds on data from a blockchain explorer web API and visualises it as modernist, postmodernist, and generative art commonplaces—coloured spots or squares, abstract poetry, random lines or grids. The numeric information of transactions or blocks is (mis-)rendered as something more substantial, giving it the task of communicating an impression of the speed and volume and density of that information.

It’s a kind of landscape. Maybe.

In 2019 a set of prints was generated and printed for Kate Vass Galerie, using the same algorithms as `Blockchain Aesthetics` but from historical data associated with significant events in blockchain history:

- The mining of the first block—the ‘genesis block’—in the Bitcoin blockchain. This marks the start of Bitcoin as running software rather than an idea described in a whitepaper.
- The sending of the first transaction between two parties on the Bitcoin blockchain. This marks the start of Bitcoin as a usable and useful system for exchanging value without intermediaries.
- The first halving of reward payments by the protocol for mining Bitcoin transactions into blocks. This marks the successful operation of Bitcoin’s software as long-term governance and a generator of economic and historical events in itself.

They’re a kind of history painting. Maybe.

# Critical Coins

## 2015 Counterparty / Dogeparty Tokens

![](image/critical-coins-xdp.jpg)

To make the process of art reviews and criticism more transparent and quantifiable, we can use cryptographic asset tokens to represent critical opinion and valences. Search for the token names provided below in your favourite DogeParty or CounterParty block explorer.

### Critical Approval

Here’s a token of simple, uninflected critical approval:

`CRITIQUE`

### Positive and Negative Critique

Positive-only measures can distort valences. Having tokens for both approval and disapproval addresses this:

`POSCRIT`

`NEGCRIT`

### Star Ratings

We can use a range of tokens to express a traditional star rating:

`ONESTAR`

`TWOSTAR`

`THREESTAR`

`FOURSTAR`

`FIVESTAR`

### Sending Tokens

We can use three different kinds of recipients for critical opinion represented in the form of tokens. If the artist (or work) has a Dogecoin address the tokens can be sent to that address directly. Otherwise we can create an address (preferably a vanity address) for them to hold the tokens in escrow. Or we can create an unspendable address (again preferably a vanity address) to send the tokens to. This prevents the tokens being transferred, which is either a bug or a feature depending on whether we want this to be possible or not.

Presumably art is the product of aesthetic competence, and if I as an art historian or critic approve of your exercise of this competence I cannot fault you if your exercise of that competence in the evaluation of someone else’s art or critical competences leads you to transfer the tokens of my critical approval to a third party. So transferable tokens make sense and in fact the history of their transferals adds value: it provides a historical record of regard, influence, and the reach of my original opinion.

# Democratic Palette
