
I’m not an expert on ideas of gender, I’m not even an expert on me, I can certainly talk about my own experience, but one of the things I had to do was to let go of trying to explain it. This is an overwhelming reality, I will worry about the impossibility of any of it later but this is happening, so we’re gonna ride it out because trying to deny it was threatening to destroy me. Once I became aware of it, it became aware of me—in the best tradition of horror movies. And that’s where [‘Necronomicon’](#52-Necronomicon.xhtml) begins.

**MBK** There’s a great deal to explore here in terms of encryption, signatures, time, identities and their backpropagation…. This process, also, involves not a transcendent given but a generative regime: gender has to be pragmatically enacted in order to find out what kind of virtuality is produced, and the generative effects of that virtuality have to be navigated.

**RM** Rerolling your character sheet halfway through the campaign. Why not? Aly Chace points out that hyperstition is just Butlerian performativity….

**MBK** How do you see the contrast between how you imagined blockchain art in 2014 and its actually existing reality in 2022?

**RM** There’s both the danger of romanticising paths not taken and that of romanticising the early experiments. But everyone needs to look at Plantoid, everyone needs to look at Terra0, everyone needs to look at these early projects that answer so many questions about how we can do these things in a way that doesn’t simply build platforms that will be captured by venture capital and destroy the environment and won’t support artists. They are amazing applications of the real affordances of this technology to the real problems of artistic production.

In my case, I was very anticapitalist, I thought, we need to bring socialism into this, not just transactional economics. That meant that I was completely wrongfooted by NFTs because I was leaning away from commodification and financialisation. The model I had was the one I work through in the representations of art in [‘Artworld Ethereum’](#6-Artworld-ethereum.xhtml)—these different functions and entities in the art world operationalised as code that you can then hack on to modify the performance of those functions. By the time I wrote [‘Ethereum Standardized Contract API’](#16-Ethereum-Standardised.xhtml), which included resale rights, and handled registering and buying and selling and listing works onchain on Ethereum, that brought up the legal, financial, and moral responsibility of actually making that into a business, which is what I would have had to deal with in order to make it into a real thing, and I couldn’t cope with that.

But in that early work there are so many models of things that people simply haven’t done since, and there are so many representations of artworks that people simply haven’t used. By the time you get to [‘Standardized API’](#16-Ethereum-Standardised.xhtml), that’s a complete model for representing artworks not as tokens—I didn’t know artworks were going to be represented as tokens—but as smart contracts that can offer themselves for exhibition, take exhibition fees, evaluate opportunities for exhibition, and interact with curators in order to show the (presumably electronic) artwork that they manage. There’s nothing like that out there. There are wonderful curating and collector DAOs—disclosure, I am collected and curated by various DAOs—but there is absolutely a more social model of the art world to be had; there are still these interesting models, different representations of things that are artworks and different possibilities of organising the commissioning, production, exhibition, sale, and critique of art in ways that simply haven’t been done.

There are the actually existing rare art platforms, NFT platforms, and there’s the potential of DAOs, which are intensifying their way toward very interesting production. And for me there’s the historical example of technologically accelerationist art, modelled as autonomous or quasi-genetic entities on the blockchain.

So there’s interesting work to do here that isn’t simply treating my early work as an 8-bit aesthetic, or using it as a signifier of lost futures or as retrotech camp. I would love people to rip off my early work, I’d love people to combine it with modern NFT standards. But if we want this sort of social organisation against the capitalist reification of value and extraction of value then the best way out may be DAOs, with their alignment with economic incentives. This is precisely the horror story of [‘Maximum Transaction Density’](#48-Maximum-transaction-density.xhtml): the best way of doing this involves everything being bought and sold, timestamped with the mark of the beast….

**MBK** The only way to the best is through the worst?

**RM** It’s the worst from the point of view of an anticapitalist, antifinancialist psychology, but it’s not the worst from the point of view of material existence. You don’t need everything to collapse socioeconomically in order to get to maximum transaction density, quite the opposite. You do need to let go of your ego and your investments and prejudices in exculpatory fantasies. We’re into cosmic horror with economics as the only limit, outside of the human subject. And the social subject is going But I just want to draw furry art all day and for people just to give me food for no reason. How about that, but via a DAO? No, not like that!

**MBK** Given that your work addresses two different constituencies which are generally suspicious of or indifferent to one another, and you take great pleasure in prodding and provoking both of them, who are your audience?

**RM** Art & Language talked early on about producing work without an audience: they had an imagined future audience—probably after the revolution—but there was not an actually existing audience for their work. And with my stuff, if you wanted to get a full understanding of it you would need to know meme culture, computer programming, some French philosophy, arguments from bulletin boards, and so on—but to get the impression of it, which will certainly do a lot of its work, you need to know a lot less, you just need to be open to the idea that there may be something there.

As for the book, some readers will pick this up and go What is this? I wanted doge memes and there’s a technical standard here that no one has ever used, there’s something claiming to be art which is basically a computer program with one bit of information in the variable, and then there’s a story that does have doge memes in it but I can’t read it because the narrator is unreliable… So if I pretend not to be me (which I spent many years doing) and step back from this, it looks sprawling and chaotic. But I think it’s actually very consistent—not that I planned it all out from the start. The previous phase of my work was broadly around the idea of freedom, free culture, free software and the limits on that. And then it became very much about secrets and making them very secure. But the persistent, repeating question here is: Are you sure? Are you sure this is a horrible right-wing technology that has no value for your socialist project? Are you sure the art world is all blue-haired hipsters who want to ban you from owning firearms and silver? Are you sure that perfect security is possible? A constant voice of doubt, but not the kind of doubt that sits there and points and feels very secure in having found a place outside the world from which to act superior. I’m diving into the mud.
