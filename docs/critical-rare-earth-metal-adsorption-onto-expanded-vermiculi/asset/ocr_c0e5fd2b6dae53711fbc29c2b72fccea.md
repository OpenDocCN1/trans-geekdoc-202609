The species in solution in the pH range studied (1.3 until 4.7) are essentially Dy³⁺ and H⁺ (Fig. 2(a)). For pH < 2.9, hydrogen is the dominant species in the medium, while for pH > 2.9, the Dy³⁺ has the highest concentration in the medium. As the pH increases, the H⁺ concentration drops to zero (pH ~ 5.0). This behavior explains the positive effect of pH for the adsorption of Dy, since the augment in pH diminishes the competition between Dy³⁺ and H⁺ for the known negatively charged sorption sites of vermiculite. In the analysis of vermiculite zeta potential (Fig. 2(b)), the isoelectric point was determined at pH = 2.5, i.e., at this pH value, the sum of the positive and negative charges on the vermiculite surface was zero (Duman and Tunç, 2008). When the pH > zero charge potential, the surface of the vermiculite is negatively charged, promoting an attraction of Dy³⁺ cations, which also justifies the positive effect of pH on the adsorption of dysprosium. The effect of pH can be evidenced by comparing the results of Table 1 (runs 13 and 14), where only pH was varied; at pH 1.3, the %R was 27.6%, and at pH 4.8, the %R was 98.9%.

Table S3 presents the variance analysis (ANOVA) for the completed model with main and interaction effects. The complete model (Equation (3)) was significant with 95% confidence, although the model considers the non-significant effects. It could be concluded by Fcal/Ftab = 3.7. The model adjusted the data with an R² of 0.9455 and an adjusted determination coefficient (R²adj) of 0.8755. However, the ANOVA of the reduced model (Table 2) after the backward elimination (Equation (10)), i.e., elimination of the non-significant effects, showed an Fcal/Ftab = 10.8, an R² = 0.9217 and an R²adj = 0.8949. The estimated effects of the reduced model are available in Table S4 (supplementary material).

The increase of the F-test value from 3.7 to 10.8, and the R²adj from 0.8755 to 0.8949 indicated that the reduced model is more significant than the completed model. The reduced model was chosen to represent the %R because it presented Fcal/Ftab > 10; a ratio recommended in the literature to consider a helpful regression for forecasting purposes (de Barros Neto et al., 2010). The observed versus predicted values (Fig. 3) and the response surface (Fig. 4) were based on the %R model of Equation (10) for normalized values of B (adsorbent mass) and C (pH).
