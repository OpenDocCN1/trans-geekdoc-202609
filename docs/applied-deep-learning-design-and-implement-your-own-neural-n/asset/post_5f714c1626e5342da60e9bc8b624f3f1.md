Statistical models consist of a group of probability distribution functions, which can be used to define different variabilities which data or random variables denote. In these types of models, defining the model invariance is done by using group theory. All types of statistical estimators and statistical hypothesis tests can be derived by using statistical models. So, statistical modeling allows us to find out the probability of happening of any event.

Let us take an example of a pair of dice as shown in figure 1.38:

![](img/7e519fee1b702b91799cdf1664598392_77_0.png)

Figure 1.38: Pair of dice

We discuss the two statistical assumptions regarding dice as follows.

- Statistical assumption for unbiased pair of dice

  The probability of the occurrence of every face (say 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, or 6) is 1/6 when both the dice are unbiased. From this assumption, we can calculate the probability of getting four on the faces of a pair of dice as follows.

  1/6 × 1/6 = 1/36

  Similarly, we can calculate the probability of occurrence of any event, such as (1 and 3), (2 and 4), (5 and 6), and so on.

- Statistical assumption for weighted pair of dice

  The probability of the occurrence of every face (say 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6) is 1/8 when both the dice are weighted.

From the preceding assumption, we can calculate the probability of getting four on the faces of a weighted pair of dice as follows:

$$
\frac{1}{8} \times \frac{1}{8} = \frac{1}{64}
$$

However, for the case of weighted dice, we cannot find the probability of other non-trivial elements because the probability of other faces is not known. If you observe the preceding two assumptions, the first assumption belongs to a statistical assumption because, in this case, by using the assumption, we can determine the probability of occurrence of any event.

For the second assumption case, it does not belong to the statistical assumption because here, we cannot determine the probability of the event by using the assumption.

### Hyper planes

In geometry, a hyperplane can be defined as a subspace whose dimension of its ambient space is one more than that of its subspace. So accordingly, the hyperplane of the space is two-dimensional if the dimension of the corresponding space is three. The hyperplane can be used in any type of general space in which the concept of subspace dimensions is present.

Figure 1.39 shows the hyperplane of two dimensions, and its space dimensions are three:

![](img/7e519fee1b702b91799cdf1664598392_78_0.png)

*Figure 1.39: 2-D hyperplane*

When it comes to machine learning, hyperplanes can be defined as the decision boundaries which are used for the classification of data points. As the data point lying on one side of the hyperplane can be termed as one class, and data points lying on the other side of the hyperplane as another class.

The number of dimensions of the hyperplane relies on the number of features. So, let us say if the input features are two, and then the hyperplane is just a line. However, if the number of features is three, then the hyperplane becomes a 2-D plane. The equation of 2 feature hyperplane is given by the following:

$y = mx + b$ (1.33)

where $m$ is the slope of the line and $b$ is the intercept on the $y$-axis. The equation of hyperplane having more than two features is given by the following:

$w^T x + b = 0$ (1.34)

Where $w$ is a set of weights, $b$ is the bias, and $x$ denotes the input vector and $w^T x$ represents the dot product of two vectors. Suppose we have two vectors:

$x = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ x \\ y \end{pmatrix} \text{ and } w = \begin{pmatrix} -b \\ -m \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$

Then,

$w^T x = -b * 1 + (-m) * x + 1 * y$ (1.35)

$w^T x = y - mx - b$ (1.36)

Figure 1.40 shows a straight line where R$^2$ represents the subspace of 2-D:

![](img/7e519fee1b702b91799cdf1664598392_79_0.png)

Figure 1.40: Hyperplane in R$^2$ is a line

Figure 1.41 shows a 2-D plane when R³ is a 3-D subspace:

![](img/7e519fee1b702b91799cdf1664598392_80_0.png)

Figure 1.41: Hyperplane in R³ is a 2-D plane

The preceding two figures represent two different types of hyperplanes.

The hyperplanes are mostly used with support vector machines in **Natural Language Processing (NLP)**, image classification, and biosciences. In NLP, support vector machines are used in hypertext classification as they can reduce the number of labeled training instances. In biosciences, the combination of these two is used to classify the proteins.
