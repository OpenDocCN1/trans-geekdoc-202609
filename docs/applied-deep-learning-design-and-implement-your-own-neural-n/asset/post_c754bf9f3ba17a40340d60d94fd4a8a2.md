### Introduction to GAN

The credit for GAN goes to Ian Goodfellow and his fellow researchers at the University of Montreal in the year 2014. GANs are a deep learning technique for generating fairly realistic synthetic samples that appear to be different and indistinguishable from real samples, although as real as the original. In a sense, GANs can be considered robotic artists, generating impressive outputs or, we can even say, generating art. GANs are the techniques that are used for both semi-supervised and unsupervised learning.

To understand the working of GANs intuitively, consider an example of a forger and an art dealer. The forger is the one who tries to create a fake painting by any famous artist, for example, Picasso. Initially, the forger is not as efficient in making a fake painting, so he tries to merge and mixes some of his own fake work with authentic Picasso’s work and shows it to an art dealer. After an assessment, the art dealer immediately recognizes it as fake and gives it back to the forger. The forger then tries to improve his art and learns to make a more convincing fake painting, which the art dealer may again reject as he is an expert in his work. Now, as time goes by, the forger becomes competent enough at imitating the style of Picasso and generating better paintings. On the other hand, the art dealer becomes an expert in spotting fakes. In this competing situation, the forger reaches such expertise that it can generate excellent fake Picasso paintings that can even confuse the art dealer.

This is the exact idea behind the GANs, where two networks, generator (forger) and discriminator (art dealer), both neural networks, compete with each other to being experts in their domains, and finally reaching a point where the generator, with the indirect help of discriminator, can generate plausible samples in a specific area (like painting in the preceding example).

### Basics of generative models

Deep learning primarily deals with supervised and unsupervised learning, along with two major categories of models: descriptive models and generative models. One should have a clear idea about these terms to have a better understanding of these concepts.

Supervised learning is currently a dominating branch of deep learning, which deals with discriminative models. Here, the algorithm learns the mapping from the input (variable X) to the output (variable Y). The main goal of the algorithm is to learn this mapping efficiently, such that when a new input is given to the algorithm, it can predict the correct output. Generally, most of the deep learning applications today belong to this category. For example, image classification, natural language processing, speech recognition, object detection, and so on.

In terms of the data distribution, discriminative models classify the data points of the input data distribution, or in other words, what input data points belong to which output class. Discriminative models learn the boundaries of the classes present in the input distribution. Unsupervised learning is another branch of deep learning that is gaining popularity these days. It involves summarizing or automatically learning and discovering the patterns present in the data distribution or understanding the correlation present in the input data, such that it can generate new data samples that match the input data distribution and appears to belong to the original dataset.

After training, when a random input is given to the generative model, it tries to transform it into a new sample that appears to belong to the input original dataset. So, in general terms, generative models summarize the data distributions. Nowadays, these generative models are gaining popularity because of the scope they are providing in generating new plausible samples that do not exist in the dataset and giving a chance to create a robust dataset. With a versatile dataset, deep learning models could learn better and perform successfully.

### Generative adversarial networks

For a better understanding of GAN, try to understand the individual term in GAN as follows:

- **Generative**: That is capable of generating something new.
- **Adversarial**: That works competitively.
- **Network**: Any deep learning model.

The name itself suggests the basic idea behind GANs. It is an unsupervised deep learning architecture in which two deep learning models, such as neural networks or convolutional neural networks, are trained in an adversarial manner to generate new samples.

### GAN architecture

The GAN architecture involves two sub-networks working in an adversarial manner, a generator (G) and a discriminator (D). Here both the generator and the discriminator are neural networks.
