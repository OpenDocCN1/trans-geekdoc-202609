## Introduction to neuron

The neuron is the main functional block of the nervous system. Also known as a biological neuron, it is a nerve cell used in the transmission of data or information through the body cells. These nerve cells are responsible for the communication of information or data in both chemical and electrical forms.

![](img/7e519fee1b702b91799cdf1664598392_137_0.png)

Figure 3.1: Biological neuron

A typical biological neuron is shown in figure 3.1. To understand the artificial neural network, one should understand the working of the biological neuron. A biological neuron consists of a cell body with a nucleus inside.

The cell body of the biological neuron transmits signals to other neurons in the form of electrical signals. The path that the neurons use to transmit signals to other neurons is called as axon.

To receive the transmitted signals, each neuron uses dendrites that are attached to the cell body of every neuron. Between the axon of one neuron and the dendrite of the other neuron, there is a small gap known as synapses. So, whenever a signal reaches the end of a neuron, the release of neurotransmitters takes place, which carries the transmitted signal to the next neuron across synapses.

## McCulloch and Pitts network

It is well known that the most important unit of the deep neural network is known as the perceptron or artificial neuron. However, the actual first step in the direction of the perceptron, which we use nowadays, was actually taken by McCulloch and Pitts in 1943 by impersonating the functionality of the biological neuron.

In a biological neuron, the term dendrites are the segments of the neuron that conduct electrical messages for the cell to be active. Soma or cell body is where the signals from the dendrites are joined and passed on to other cells. Figure 3.5 shows McCulloch and Pitts neural network. Principally, a neuron or node takes a signal (input) in the dendrite, then further processes it like the CPU in the soma and passes the produced output using a cable-like structure to other connected neurons (axon) to synapse to the other neuron’s dendrite:

![](img/7e519fee1b702b91799cdf1664598392_140_0.png)

Figure 3.5: McCulloch and Pitts neural network
