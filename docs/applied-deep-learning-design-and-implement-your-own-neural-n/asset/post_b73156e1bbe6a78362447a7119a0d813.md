## Dual GAN

Image-to-image translations come under the computer vision and image processing field, where images from one domain are mapped into another domain. Image-to-image translations have a scope in numerous applications such as black and white to colored transfer, stylization, image segmentation, season transformations, sketches-to-photos, and so on. The previously-discussed models in this domain, that is, conditional GAN, have shown improvements in this field, but it requires labeled data on which the training of the model is conditioned. Now, the main issue with conditional GAN is the non-availability of labeled data and the difficulty in converting huge datasets with millions of images to labeled datasets. To overcome this need for labeled data for GAN training, Zili proposed a novel approach, Dual GAN, which is an unsupervised dual-learning framework inspired by the Dual-learning approach in natural language translations. In this dual-learning approach for natural language translation, two different language translators are trained simultaneously, for example, Chinese-to-Russian and Russian-to-Chinese. In this arrangement, one translation becomes the primal task, and the reverse translation becomes the dual-task. Now, while working in pairs under closed-loop forces both translators to learn through reinforcement learning.

![](img/7e519fee1b702b91799cdf1664598392_586_0.png)

Figure 8.14: The architecture of dual-GAN

Inspired by the preceding concept, dual GAN is trained in an unsupervised manner, where two unlabeled and unpaired datasets belonging to the source (U) and target (V) image domains, respectively, are used for training. As shown in figure 8.14, there are two generator networks and two discriminator networks used to implement their approach.

Now, the primal task of the dual GAN is to train the generator GA that can map from domain U to V, that is, (GA: U → V), and the reverse task, that is, the dual task, is to train generator GB that can map in reverse from domain V to U that is, (GB: V → U). To measure the learning progress of both the generators GA and GB, two discriminators, DA and DB, respectively, are there that can identify the fake and real images from their corresponding domains.

Table 8.5 shows the inputs, outputs, and aims of generator and discriminator networks in dual-GAN:

| DualGAN | Input | Output | Aim |
|---|---|---|---|
| Generator (GA) | Noise Vector (z) and images from domain:U | New generated samples in domain:V | Realistic new samples in domain:V |
| Generator (GB) | Noise Vector (z) and images from domain:V | New generated samples in domain:U | Realistic new samples in domain:U |
| Discriminator (DA) | Real and generated samples by GA in domain:V | Probability score to identifying real or fake samples in domain:V | Correctly identify between real or fake sample in domain:V |
| Discriminator (DB) | Real and generated samples by GB in domain:U | Probability score to identifying real or fake samples in domain:U | Correctly identify between real or fake sample in domain:U |

Table 8.5: Inputs, outputs, and aim of generator and discriminator networks in dual-GAN

## Pix-to-Pix GAN

Pix-to-pix GAN is based on the conditional GAN, which gives control over target image generation in image-to-image translation, conditioned on some input images instead of class or label or tags. This model was suggested by Phillip in the year 2018 paper Image-to-image translations with conditional adversarial networks. This model is designed to make it useful for general image-to-image translations. As the name itself suggests, Pix2pix images are translated on a pixel-by-pixel basis. The generator and discriminator networks are doing the usual tasks discussed in the previous models. The discriminator identifies real and fake images and the generator’s aim is to generate images to make the discriminator confused but on a pixel-by-pixel basis. The Pix-to-pix model is trained in both the source and target image pairs. By image pairs, it means that if a GAN is to be trained to translate day-time images to night-time images, then we need the exact same images taken at the day-time and at night-time, respectively. As this model is for general purposes, it can be trained on a wide range of image translations such as image-to-sketch, day-to-night images, aerial view-to-map view, thermal-to-colored photos, and so on, as shown in figure 8.15:

![](img/7e519fee1b702b91799cdf1664598392_588_0.png)

Figure 8.15: Generator structure in pix-to-pix GAN

Pix-to-pix GAN uses U-Net architecture which is similar to auto-encoders architecture. Auto-encoders are artificial neural networks that can be used in computer vision for unsupervised learning. Auto-encoders consists of two main components: an encoder and a decoder. The encoder function is to extract the main features of the image and reduce it to a compressed latent representation (or bottleneck). The second component is the decoder, which takes this latent representation as input and tries to reconstruct the original image out of it.

Figure 8.16 shows the basic structure of encoder–decoder in auto-encoders. Auto-encoders are generative models in which the encoder takes an input image and compresses or downsamples it to some representation, layer by layer. This is taken as input by the decoder to upsample or un-compress it and regenerate the image again over some layers, as shown in figure 8.16:

![](img/7e519fee1b702b91799cdf1664598392_589_0.png)

Figure 8.16: Auto-encoder architecture

In this down-sampling up-sampling process, some low-level and local information gets lost during the process of compression. It is very crucial in image-to-image translations to share this low-level information between the input and output layers. So, to overcome this issue, U-Net uses skip-connections among corresponding encoder–decoder layers having the same sized feature map to pass on this local information and be able to generate sharp images.

Figure 8.17 shows the U-Net architecture and the skip connection in between the layers. It is called U-Net because of the formation of a U-like shape due to these skip connections in between the corresponding layers of encoder and decoder. The authors have used Patch GAN (a term coined by authors), in which the model takes an n*n patch image and predicts real or fake for every pixel in that patch, thereby giving sharp and detailed images, as needed, for example, image-to-sketch translations:
