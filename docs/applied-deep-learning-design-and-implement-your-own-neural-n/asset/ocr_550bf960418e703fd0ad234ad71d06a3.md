In this down-sampling up-sampling process, some low-level and local information gets lost during the process of compression. It is very crucial in image-to-image translations to share this low-level information between the input and output layers. So, to overcome this issue, U-Net uses skip-connections among corresponding encoder–decoder layers having the same sized feature map to pass on this local information and be able to generate sharp images.

Figure 8.17 shows the U-Net architecture and the skip connection in between the layers. It is called U-Net because of the formation of a U-like shape due to these skip connections in between the corresponding layers of encoder and decoder. The authors have used Patch GAN (a term coined by authors), in which the model takes an n*n patch image and predicts real or fake for every pixel in that patch, thereby giving sharp and detailed images, as needed, for example, image-to-sketch translations:
