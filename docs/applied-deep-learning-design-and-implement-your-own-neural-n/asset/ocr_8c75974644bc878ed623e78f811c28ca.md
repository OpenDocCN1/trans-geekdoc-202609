# Log-likelihood

Consider the following mean square error function.

\( L = \sum_{n=1}^{N} (target_n - prediction_n)^2 \)

\( L = \Sigma_{n=1}^N(t_n - y_n)^2 \quad (3.5) \)

where \( t_n \) represents the target value and \( y_n \) denotes the predicted value.

Minimize the squared error is the same as to maximize the log-likelihood (MLE) when the error is Gaussian distributed. The random variable Gaussian distribution probability density function PDF is given as follows:

\( t_n = \prod_{n=1}^N \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} exp(\frac{-1}{2\sigma^2} (t_n - y_n)^2) \quad (3.6) \)

\( t_n \sim N(y_n, \sigma^2) \)

Where \( y_n \) is mean and \( \sigma^2 \) is variance of Gaussian PDF. For MLE:

\( y_n \sim f(x_n; w) \)

and

\( w = arg max(L) \)
