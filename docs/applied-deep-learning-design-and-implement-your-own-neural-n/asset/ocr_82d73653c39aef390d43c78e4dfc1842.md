Now, in the process of backpropagation, if we follow the path from $h(t)$ to $h(t-1)$, as shown in *figure 7.10*:

Then, the backpropagation process here will already cover the Gradient $\frac{\partial E}{\partial h(t)}$ where $E$ is the cross-entropy loss error here. Therefore, we do not have to find out this part here, and hence, we need to find the gradients for $o(t)$ and $c(t)$, that is, $\nabla o(t)$ and $\nabla c(t)$.

So, let us calculate $\nabla o(t)$ first,

$\Delta o(t) = \frac{\partial E}{\partial o(t)}$

Applying the chain rule, we get the following:

$\Delta o(t) = \frac{\partial E}{\partial o(t)} = \frac{\partial E}{\partial h(t)} \times \frac{\partial h(t)}{\partial o(t)}$
$\Delta o(t) = \frac{\partial E}{\partial o(t)} = \nabla h(t) \times tanh(c(t))$

Thus,

$\Delta o(t) = \nabla h(t) \times tanh(c(t))$

Now, let us calculate $\nabla c(t)$,

$\Delta c(t) = \frac{\partial E}{\partial c(t)}$

Applying the chain rule, we get the following:

$\Delta c(t) = \frac{\partial E}{\partial c(t)} = \frac{\partial E}{\partial H(t)} \times \frac{\partial H(t)}{\partial c(t)}$
$\Delta c(t) = \frac{\partial E}{\partial c(t)} = \nabla h(t) \times o(t) \times (1 - tanh^2(c(t)))$

Therefore,

$\Delta c(t) = \nabla h(t) \times o(t) \times (1 - tanh^2(c(t)))$
