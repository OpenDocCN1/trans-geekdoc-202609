The process is like conv-pool, conv-pool, and so on. It depends on us how deep we want to stack the layers. We have to decide where to stop and then aggregate the responses. Every time it is not possible that we apply filters, and so on. We have to aggregate the responses and do the voting. As shown in figure 4.151, the 4*4 responses are aggregated at last, where top left (1.00) is the response for the top left section of the figure showing X, top right (0.55) is the response for the top right section, bottom left (0.55) is the response for the bottom left section and bottom right (1.00) is the response for the bottom right section of the image X shown to convolutional network with respect to Filter 1. Similarly, for the remaining two filters, aggregated responses are generated (see figure 4.151). In CNN, we cannot ask very specific questions (filters). If we ask very specific questions, then the CNN model becomes complicated, and if it becomes complicated, then it may cause overfitting of data. So generally, we ask very general questions such as left line, right line, and center element (filters) so that we get global information and our model does not get overfitted. Asking a question means applying filters, and these questions are built using the proposed objective function.
