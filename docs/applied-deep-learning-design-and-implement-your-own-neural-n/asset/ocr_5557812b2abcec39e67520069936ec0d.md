Example 4.3: Let us take an example of a 1-D scan line (horizontal scan of intensity values) as shown in figure 4.17:

| 5 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 4 |

![](bbox=[0.18, 0.19, 0.85, 0.375])

Figure 4.17: Result of sharpening (high pass) filters

The values denoted by the black dots are the intensity values as per the scanned 1-D line.

As we can observe from the figure, it includes the discontinuities as an intensity ramp, an intensity step, and sections of constant intensity. The black dot in the square shows changes or onset in the intensity, that is, intensity transition.

Now, we know that the first-order derivative in 1-D is given as follows:

```
f'(x) = f(x + 1) - f(x)
```

In the scanned line, the first value f(0) is considered as a neighborhood. So, we start from the second value, which is also f(1):

```
f'(1) = f(1 + 1) – f(1)
f'(1) = f(2) – f(1)
f'(1) = 5 – 5 = 0
```

Similarly,

```
f'(2) = f(2 + 1) – f(2)
f'(1) = f(3) – f(2)
f'(1) = 4 – 5 = –1
```
