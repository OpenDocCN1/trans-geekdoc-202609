
Substituting equation (3.25) and equation (3.31) in equation (3.22), we get the following equation:

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_i - \left( \frac{y_i}{a_i} - \frac{(1-y_i)}{(1-a_i)} \right) * \frac{e^{-z_i}}{(1+e^{-z_i})^2} * x_i$$

Rewriting and substituting \(\frac{e^{-z_i}}{(1+e^{-z_i})^2} = \frac{1}{1+e^{-z_i}} * \frac{e^{-z_i}}{1+e^{-z_i}}\) in the preceding equation, we get the following equation:

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_i - \left( \frac{y_i(1-a_i) - a_i(1-y_i)}{a_i * (1-a_i)} \right) * \frac{1}{1+e^{-z_i}} * \frac{e^{-z_i}}{1+e^{-z_i}} * x_i$$

Rewriting and substituting \(e^{-z_i} = 1 + e^{-z_i} - 1\), in the preceding equation, we get the following equation:

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_i - \left( \frac{y_i - y_i a_i - a_i + y_i a_i}{a_i * (1-a_i)} \right) * \frac{1}{1+e^{-z_i}} * \frac{1+e^{-z_i} - 1}{1+e^{-z_i}} * x_i$$

Rewriting and substituting \(\frac{1+e^{-z_i}-1}{1+e^{-z_i}} = \frac{1+e^{-z_i}}{1+e^{-z_i}} - \frac{1}{1+e^{-z_i}}\) in the preceding equation, we get the following equation:

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_i - \left( \frac{y_i - a_i}{a_i * (1-a_i)} \right) * \frac{1}{1+e^{-z_i}} * \left[ \frac{1+e^{-z_i}}{1+e^{-z_i}} - \frac{1}{1+e^{-z_i}} \right] * x_i$$

As \(\frac{1+e^{-z_i}}{1+e^{-z_i}} = 1\), by substituting this in the preceding equation, we get the following equation:

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_i - \left( \frac{y_i - a_i}{a_i * (1-a_i)} \right) * \frac{1}{1+e^{-z_i}} * \left[ 1 - \frac{1}{1+e^{-z_i}} \right] * x_i \quad (3.32)$$

As \(a_i = \frac{1}{1+e^{-z_i}}\) (3.33)

Substituting equation (3.33) in equation (3.32), we get the following result:

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_i - \left( \frac{y_i - a_i}{a_i * (1-a_i)} \right) * a_i * (1-a_i) * x_i$$

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_i -(y_i - a_i) * x_i$$

Therefore,

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_i x_i * (a_i - y_i) \quad (3.34)$$

The preceding equation represents the minimized loss function. From equation (3.34), we can visualize that it is good that the equation eliminates the effect of the considered activation function, as \(\frac{\partial L}{\partial w}\) directly depends on the difference between the predicted and actual \((a_i - y_i)\) values and our main objective is to reduce this, to have the best fit.

The following formula shows the gradient descent algorithm for updating the weight factor to achieve local/global minima.

$$w' = w - \eta * \frac{\partial L}{\partial w}$$

By substituting \(\frac{\partial L}{\partial w}\) in the preceding equation, we get the updated weights. \(\frac{\partial L}{\partial w}\) represents the slope of the gradient descent, and \(\eta\) denotes the learning rate. The dimension of \(\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_i x_i * (a_i - y_i)\) (equation (3.34)) equals to \(D \times K\) as follows:

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = [((x_{N \times D})(a_i - y_i)_{N \times K})]$$
$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = [((x_{D \times N}^T)(a_i - y_i)_{N \times K})]$$
$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = \left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_{D \times K}} \right)$$

Cross-entropy function works best for the neurons having the sigmoid activation function. Moreover, the larger the error, the faster the neuron learns as \(\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} \propto (a[predicted] - y[actual])\). It is free from activation functions or their derivative \(\delta'(z)\).

The error is calculated as the difference between the predicted and actual output. Here, we have used the squared error loss function, which can be defined as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}

&\quad + (1 - 1) \log(1 - 0.65)] \\
L &= 0.23 + 0.09 \\
L &= 0.32
\end{aligned}
$$

## Backpropagation of error

Weights and bias are the parameters of the loss function. The goal of parameter update is to minimize the value of the loss function to 0 by changing the values of weights and bias. Accordingly, what must be the value of W5 if L(W,B) tends to 0, which can be written in terms of the partial derivative as follows:

$$\frac{\partial L(W,B)}{\partial w_5} = \frac{\partial L(W,B)}{\partial a_1} * \frac{\partial a_1}{\partial z_3} * \frac{\partial z_2}{\partial w_5} \quad (3.39)$$

As we know that:

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_i x_i * (a_i - y_i)$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial L(W,B)}{\partial W_5} &= \frac{1}{N} \sum_i x_1 * (a_1 - y_1) \\
&= \frac{1}{N} * x_1 * (a_1 - y_1) \\
&= \frac{1}{2} * 0 * (a_1 - y_1) \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial L(W,B)}{\partial W_6} &= \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i} x_2 * (a_1 - y_2) \\
&= \frac{1}{N} * x_2 * (a_1 - y_2) \\
&= \frac{1}{2} * 1 * (0.661 - 1) \\
&= -0.1675
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial L(W,B)}{\partial W_7} &= \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i} x_1 * (a_2 - y_1) \\
&= \frac{1}{N} * x_1 * (a_2 - y_1) \\
&= \frac{1}{2} * 0 * (a_2 - y_1) \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial L(W,B)}{\partial W_8} &= \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i} x_2 * (a_2 - y_2) \\
&= \frac{1}{2} * 1 * (0.65 - 1) \\
&= -0.175
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, the updated weight can be given as follows:

$$new_{weight} = old_{weight} - learning_{rate} * \Delta W$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_5 &= W_5 - 0.1 * 0 \\
W_5 &= 0.3
\end{aligned}
$$
