DALYs are connected with QALYs in that metrics seek to quantify the impact of health
quality and length of life. However, while QALYs focus on the benefits with and without
medical intervention, DALYS measure the overall burden of diseases.

The D metric is calculated by summing the **years of life lost (YLLs)** due to premature
death and the **years lived with disabilities (YLDs)**. This metric provides a comprehensive
measure of a population's health status by highlighting both mortality and disability. For
example, consider a population with a life expectancy of 80 years. Individuals who die before
reaching this age contribute to the YLLs, while those living with disabilities add to the
YLDs. The sum of these values gives the total DALYs for the population, which can then
be compared globally to assess health status and identify areas needing improvement in
facilities, research, and investment.

# 2.1.3 Health-Adjusted Life Years (HALY)

Adjusted Life Years (HALY) is a metric that combines both of DALYs and
QALYs to provide a comprehensive measure of health outcomes in a population. HALYs
quantify the burden of disease and the overall quality of life experienced by individuals,
measuring the number of years of healthy life lived by a population accounting for both
mortality and morbidity. as as the impact of these conditions on individuals' quality of life is incorporated in the
calculation of HALYs.

HALYs adjust expectancy levels based on the prevalence of health conditions and
the associated disability. Weights reflect the severity of different health
conditions and their impact on daily functioning. By applying these weights to the prevalence
of each condition, researchers can estimate the overall burden of disease in terms of years
healthy life lost, similar to the calculation of DALYs. Unlike DALYs, which focus primarily
morbidity and mortality, HALYs incorporate measures of individuals' quality of life,
alallowing for a more comprehensive assessment of health outcomes.
