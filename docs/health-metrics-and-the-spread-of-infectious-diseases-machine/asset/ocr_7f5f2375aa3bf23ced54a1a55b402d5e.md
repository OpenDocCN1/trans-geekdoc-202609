Let's observe in mathematical terms what happens when we start investigating the relationship between the response and the predictor, to explain the behaviour of $eta_0$ and the variables.

The following mathematical formulation represent our observed data, where $eta_0$ and $eta_1$ are the equivalence between $y$ and $x^5$.

> $y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x$

When a model is attempted, the values of the slope $(\hat{eta}_1)$ and the intercept $(\hat{eta}_0)$ are estimated$^6$ in order to replicate the values $(y)$ of the response by applying $x$ to our model function.

> $\hat{y} = \hat{eta}_0 + \hat{eta}_1 x$

where $\hat{eta}_0$ is the estimate value of the intercept, and $\hat{eta}_1$ is the estimated value of the slope. So, the estimated data will approximate the observed data, with some margin of error $(\epsilon)$:

> $y \sim \hat{y} + \epsilon$
