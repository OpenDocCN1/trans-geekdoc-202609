The difference between y and \tilde{y} is the error we make when applying a model.

$$y - \tilde{y} = \epsilon$$

Reducing the error (\epsilon) as much as possible can lead to better fit of the model.

While the case of having just one predictor influencing our response variable might be useful for educational purposes, as illustrated in the data above, scenarios involving more than one predictor are more realistic and lead to increased complexity in the model function. This situation typically results in a multivariate linear model, referred to as **linear regression**.

$$y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_1 + \beta_2 x_2 + \dots + \beta_n x_n$$

In this case more than one predictor is used to investigating the effects of the response as: A compact model of the model function with more than one predictor can be stated

$$y = \beta_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{p} \beta_i x_i = \beta_0 + \beta X$$

In this notation, the capital X represents the head of a matrix containing various $x_i: X = (x_1, x_2, x_3, \dots, x_p)$, the predictors, and the $\beta = $(eta_1, \beta_2, \beta_3, \dots, \beta_p)$ are the coefficient to be estimated.
