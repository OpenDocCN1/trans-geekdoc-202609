# Examples of bias

An initial promise and hope of the use of AI that it would not make the same mistakes humans make, including the types of biases that we as humans may or may not have. Our society has begun to use AI programs to help us in the work that we do, it has become clear that this promise is in reality false. Increasingly, there has been an awareness of biased AI as a problem facing our society, a problem which has received more attention recently. There have been some prominent examples in the last few years of the different types of bias that AI programs have. Whether this was Amazon's resume filter program, due to the algorithms it relied, disqualified women for jobs, voice recognition programs in cars which struggle to identify women' commands, or even an algorithm used in the US court system which falsely predicted that black citizens were twice likely to be at risk of committing crimes than ones. What these examples demonstrate that bias is a problem in AI and one has serious gendered and racial implications contributing to inequalities in society.

# Lack of diversity and different entry points for bias into AI

To mitigate bias in AI, it is important to understand the different ways that bias may become embedded into AI. While there are a number of contributing factors, one key factor is that the field of AI development has a diversity problem. Recent numbers show that 80% of AI professionals are men, with women making up only 15% of researchers at Facebook, and 10% at Google When it comes to black workers, this number drops to 4% at Facebook and Microsoft, and 2.5% at Google. To extent this is a main cause of the problem when it comes to AI and bias is something that is unclear and still in need of investigation, but there are those who believe that this is one of the main contributing factors to biased AI. While there are examples of how this lack of diversity is contributing to biased AI, there are also other factors that play a role. These will now be explored at two different levels, including: 1) bias from the developers, and 2) bias in the data.
