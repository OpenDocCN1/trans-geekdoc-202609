The Chinese defence industry during the Air Show China in Zhuhai in November 2022 unveiled its first dog robot, which is armed with an automatic gun system. The Indian Army has a Daksh automated robot developed by India's Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO), which is a remote controlled robot on wheels to recover, locate and destroy explosives. The USA, Russia, China, France, Germany, UK, China, India are developing both for ground and underwater roles with increasing integration of AI Navies are increasingly seeking for Unmanned Underwater Vessels (UUVs). The USA, for instance, has ordered Extra Large Unmanned Underwater Vehicles (XUVs) to be built by Boeing for the United States Navy (USN). These would operate independently for months underwater and cover up to 6,500 nautical miles on a single fuel cycle. These will be fully autonomous vehicles, which can undertake the role of a submarine without any human in the vessels.

The Washington Times reported on 16 December 2020 that the US Air Force for the first time successfully tested an artificial-assisted control and navigation system on a U-2 Dragon Lady spy plane in a training flight. This is a precursor to many future AI-powered developments for air force. Development of AI-integrated military robots and other programs are kept under secrecy until disclosed by the developers. It is therefore difficult to exactly state in what stage developments have reached and for what purpose.

The SGR AI robots, developed by Samsung, which have been deployed in the demilitarised zone since 2014, can undertake functions like surveillance, tracking, voice recognition and firing. This robot is functioning with human supervision but has the built-capacity to act independent of human supervision. The IDF claims that the Jaguar is one of the first military robots globally to have the ability to replace combat soldiers in border patrolling tasks.
