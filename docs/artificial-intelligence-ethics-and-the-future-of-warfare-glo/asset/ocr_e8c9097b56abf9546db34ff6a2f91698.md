The third law orders robots to protect themselves insofar as doing so is in accordance with the first two laws.These laws were partially modified by Asimov in his later writings, with the basic philosophy behind ‘unharm humans’ has stayed and remains applicable to AI developers and users. Ways and means have been recommended, as checks and balances, to put in place ethical practices that apply to AI-enabled products. While developing, AI engineers must take note of the following guidelines to ensure the ethical design of AI, which are follows.

+   - Transparency – the most important ethical domain. Its adherence protects fundamental human rights and the privacy, dignity, autonomy and well-being of society (UNI Global Union, 2017). Organisations should be clearly decipher and explain how their AI is respecting current legislation.
- Justice and Fairness – issues of fairness, unbiased, equality and equity must be built into the algorithm model.
- Non-maleficence – Avoidance of harm to human beings been an important aspect of concern in AI ethics, particularly in areas like killer robots and drones (UAVs).
- Responsibility and accountability – Liability and accountability for making the war must be traceable and under judicial review. AI should adhere to the privacy and data protection standards outlined in the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) (2018).
- Benefic – AI should bring benefits to the society, as highlighted in IEEE, 2019.
- Freedom and autonomy – AI developers should ensure no impediments to the freedom and liberty of human beings.
- Trust and sustainability – trust is important for the ethical deployment and use of AI as documented in the HLEG (2019). AI organisations need to ensure that their entities are environmentally sustainable (Special Interest Group on Artificial Intelligence, 2018).

Nations, which are technologically advanced and increasingly acquiring sophisticated AI-assisted weapons for their armed forces, are being questioned on ethical and legal properties. AWS has social and political ramifications too. Removing human footprints from the AI-driven decision loop during war would put ethics at stake and make the war immoral. A non-human technology AI, which has no accountability, unlike humans, should not be the one making the decision over a human.
