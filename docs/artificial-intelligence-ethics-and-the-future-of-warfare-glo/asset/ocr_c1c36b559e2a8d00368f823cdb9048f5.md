Different states (like Norway, for example) are now building digital border defences against digital threats from hostile states and non-state groups. A digital border defence means the capacity to defend against digital threats before they enter that particular national territory. Cyberoperations (also known as Computer Network Operations [CNO]) are of two types: Offensive and defensive. Defensive cyberoperations include monitoring and hacking cyberattacks to disclose reconnaissance, infiltration and attacks as early as possible. What is ironic is integration of AI in cyber warfare by a party increases its capacity for conducting both offensive cyberoperations as well as strengthening the potential of that party's CNO against any hostile threat. Mallick in this chapter warns that using AI in cyber warfare, a country defending its assets without AI would face a catastrophe. The defender could only successfully defend itself by using AI algorithms. Actually, it is becoming a war of algorithms against the hostile party's algorithms.

Besides cyberspace, the use of AI in outspace is becoming a reality. Control of the space domain (the area above the altitude where atmospheric effects on orbit become negligible) is necessary for all domain military operations. Most of the space operations occur within the geosynchronous Earth Orbit (GEO), Highly Elliptical Orbit (HEO), Medium Earth Orbit (MEO) and Low Earth Orbit (LEO). GEO is for global communications, surveillance, observing large-scale weather patterns and warning about missile launches by the enemy. A spacecraft at HEO could loiter for a long time over a particular target, especially at high altitudes. M orbits between LEO and GEO. Spacecraft at LEO are relatively close to Earth. These spacecraft use less powerful transmitters for communication and achieve higher resolution imagery with similar-sized apertures compared to satellites in the HEO and GEO. However, spacecraft at LEO could remain over a particular target in the terrestrial plane for a short time. These spacecraft at various orbits (altitudes) constitute the orbital segment of space power. The terrestrial segment includes terrestrial-based radars and all the link segment connects the signals in the electromagnetic spectrum (EMS) and the nodes that connect the orbital segment to the terrestrial segment, as well as the individual terrestrial segments with each other.
