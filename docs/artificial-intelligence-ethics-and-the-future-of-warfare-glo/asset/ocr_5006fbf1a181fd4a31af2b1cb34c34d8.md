As can be expected, the cost of a sixth-generation fighter, whenever it fructifies, would be huge and already there is a debate between the USA, the most affluent of sixth-generation fighter designs, about the affordability of such an aircraft. US fifth-generation fighters F-22 and F-33, despite their match performances, have minimal sales due to unaffordable price tags. The huge cost of fifth-generation fighters has scared away many other nations. Some of the nations with current sixth-generation programmes have leapfrogged the fifth. There is a thought process of shunning fifth and sixth-generation fighters, rejuvenating of fourth-generation programmes and giving them some additional features of later generations – least those that can be refitted without major changes. This thought process points towards the emergence of a 4.5th generation, which achieves a compromise between cost (far less than a fifth/sixth-generation aircraft) and capability (a lot better than fourth-generation). The single major differentiating design change would be AI.

Already, elements of AI are being retrofitted on some older battle-proven aircraft. Aircraft, more than four decades old, have been given updates of onboard memory and its networking speeds are being enhanced considerably so as to enable essential AI functions (data base access, organisation, scanning, viewing, organising targeting, radar warning, images and video). AI-autonomous fusing sensor information has been introduced and is helping to lower pilot workload related to flying and navigating, thus lowering pilots’ cognitive faculties for mission-related tasks. The F-15 is being given AI and ML algorithms to enhance the capabilities of its EW systems¹⁴ under the ‘cognitive EW’ concept.
