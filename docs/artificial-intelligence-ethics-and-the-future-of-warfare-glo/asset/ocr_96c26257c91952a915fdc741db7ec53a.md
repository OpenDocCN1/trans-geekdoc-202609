# First artificial intelligence war

Israeli military action, the Operation 'Guardian of the Walls' in May 2021 in the Palestinian territory of Gaza, has Israel relied heavily on ML and data gathering. A senior Israeli officer involved in digital conflict said, this is the first artificial intelligence war AI used broadly across an operation. Israel used AI to coordinate attacks on Gaza. The AI had fed sufficient raw data related to warfare and intelligence related to the conflict. The Israel Defense Forces (IDF) used resources such as intelligence (SIGINT), visual intelligence (VISINT), human intelligence (HUMINT), geographical intelligence (GEOINT), data collected from satellites, aerial reconnaissance vehicles, field agents, and ground intel. The Israeli military gathered the intelligence reports by adapting readily available AI technologies. Vast amounts of data were analysed for precision attacks. Drawing on satellite imagery, sensors and other sources, Israel was able to build 3D geographical information about Gaza and identify the locations of rocket launchers that Hamas installed. Algorithms and 3D modelling helped them to be precise and accurate.^45

IDF set up a multi-disciplinary centre to help relevant targets, aiding the military in effective planning and targeting.^46 An advanced AI technological platform was created that centralises all data on the militant groups in the Gaza Strip on one system to help with the analysis and intelligence crucial for.

According to a report by Jerusalem Post, it was IDF's elite intelligence officers in Unit 8200, the Intelligence Corps unit of the IDF that specialises in code decryption and signal intelligence, who created advanced programs named Alchemist, Gospel and Depth of Wisdom, which were developed and used during the conflict. The Gospel helped the IDF's military intelligence unit to enhance the intelligence recommendations for the officers and helped identify 'quality targets,' which were then passed to the air force to launch the strikes. Unit 8200 created multiple algorithms using signal, graphical and human intelligence to pinpoint strike targets, which were then passed to command to order a strike.^47
