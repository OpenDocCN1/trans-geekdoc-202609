# AI: Current status of technology

AI is a maturing technology. A general definition of AI is the capability of a computer system to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as visual perception, speech recognition and decision-making. Functionally, AI-enabled machines should have the capability to learn, reason, judge, predict, and initiate action. In layman's terms, AI implies trying to emulate the brain. There are three main ingredients that are necessary for simulating intelligence: The brain, the body and the mind. The brain consists of the software algorithms that work on available data, the body is the hardware, and the mind is the computing power that runs the algorithms. Technological breakthroughs and convergence in these areas are enabling the AI to rapidly mature.

In 2016, in a significant development, Google DeepMind’s AlphaGo defeated South Korean Master Lee Se-dol in the popular board game Go, and the terms machine learning and deep learning were used to describe how DeepMind worked. The easiest way to think of their relationship is to visualise them as concentric circles, with AI the largest, machine learning, and finally deep learning – which is driving today’s AI explosion – fitting inside.¹ AI is any technique that enables us to mimic human intelligence. Machine learning is a subset of AI, which focuses on the development of computer programs that can change when exposed to data, searching through data to look for patterns and adjusting program actions accordingly. Deep learning is a further subset of machine learning that is composed of algorithms which train itself by using multi-layered neural networks (which are designed on concepts borrowed from a study of the neurological structure of the brain) to vast amounts of data.
