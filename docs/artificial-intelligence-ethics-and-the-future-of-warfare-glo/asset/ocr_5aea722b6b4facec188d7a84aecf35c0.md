For ethical, legal and military requirements, it is necessary that an AWS must not be given unlimited autonomy. Most of the researchers dealing with LAWS speak of MHC. For me, MHC means the human commander must have the dominant role in selecting and engaging the target. Maake Verbruggen uses the term 'human control', which is defined as the mechanism for achieving a human commander's intent and refers to the extent of human influence on the outcome of a mission. Verbruggen continues that loss of human control could occur because of automating certain functions of the AWS and also to deterioration of critical human judgements by the human commander in the context of the information provided by the intelligent machines.68

Human control could be exercised broadly through three levers. One is fixing on the weapon system's parameters of use, including measures that restrict the type of targets and the task AWS are assigned. Temporal and spatial limits further constrain its operation. There should be a mechanism for deactivation if necessary conditions arise. The second level of control involves establishing control on the environment. This refers to the control fabric warns that conditions or structures the environments in which the AWS is used, for instance, deploying AWS in areas where no civilians are present. The third level involves the human-machine interface. The human must always supervise the machine and retain the right to intervene in its operations when necessary.69 However, operationalising these measures is immensely difficult.

It is questionable whether AWS possesses the ability to distinguish legitimate targets. Then, AWS poses a challenge to the principle of proportionality. Proportionality requires that incidental civilian casualties must be balanced against the anticipated military advantages that could be derived from the destruction of the target. Further, AWS have no sense of ethics. So, in case of violations of IHL, the warring machines could not be held responsible for committing the criminal acts. In such a scenario, who would take responsibility? Professor Paolo Gaeta of Switzerland says: 'State responsibility could have a considerable deterrent effect on States and give them an incentive to make sure that the autonomous weapon systems comply with IHL.'70

Transparency could be referred to as the end users clearly understand how an AI-implanted machine works and makes decisions. In Chapter 4, Kritik Roy warns that the mechanism through which complex algorithms of AI makes decisions remains opaque. We have to understand how the AI is making a particular decision and not merely what solutions it is providing to a problem. This is essential for clear transparency of the AI's decision-making process. Only when we get a clear idea of it, can we assess the AWS's reliability.
