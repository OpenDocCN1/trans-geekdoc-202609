While the USA has been going alone with the NGAD programmes till recently, indications from reported statements by USAF Secretary Frank Kendall are that the US may be willing to discuss collaboration with Australia, Japan and the UK, especially the Collaborative Combat Aircraft (CCA) unmanned complement of the NGAD. However, such partnerships may not be easy to forge due to the UK and Japan already being involved in other sixth-generation programmes.

Europe has two sixth-generation programmes afoot. The first is the Future Combat Air System (FCAS) or Tempest developed by the UK, Sweden and Italy, none of which have a fifth-generation programme. Japan has its own F-X sixth-generation programme but is partnering on some parts of the Tempest programme since 2020. In December 2021, it was announced that the UK and Japan are jointly joining to produce an engine for Tempest and F-X. The collaboration could extend to other areas of development (of Tempest) too. In 2022, Defense News reported that the Italian Air Force Chief would travel to Japan to talk with its Japanese counterpart about collaboration on sixth-generation technology. Beside the craft and fusion features of fifth-generation, the Tempest is expected to have significant AI to assist the pilot and for management of teamed platforms. It is expected to enter service in the mid-2030s and, according to the UK Ministry of Defence (UKMOD), the demonstr aircraft would fly by 2027.

The second European sixth-generation fighter is developed by a consortium comprising France, Germany and Spain. Its official name is FCAS, but to differentiate it from the UK programme, it is often referred by its French abbreviation SCAF (standing for Système de combat aérien du futur). It aims at producing a Next Generation Fighter (NGF) which will connect through the cloud to a series of UAVs for offensive and surveillance roles. This programme was initially slated to do its first test flight in 2027 and go operational by 2045. However, some problems about work share are remembered, and there may be delays. While the workshare problems appear in the near future, the business issues involving the various governments and OEMs are immense to summit, and not before 2050, according to Dassault's CEO Eric Tripp.

Russian news agency Telegramnoy Agentstvo Sovetskogo Soyuzka (TASS) reported in 2016 that Russia was working on a sixth-generation aircraft. Later, unconfirmed reports claimed the programme Perspektivnyy Aviatsionnyy Kompleks Dal'nogo Perekhвата or PAKDP (which translates to Prospective Air Complex of Long-Range Interception) and gave it the name MIG-41, projecting for it a first flight in the mid-2020s and entry into service after 2030.
