In the new millennium, the Indian armed forces conceptualise doctrine in the following manner: The terms 'doctrine' and 'strategy' are interrelated but not interchangeable.... While Doctrines provide precepts for development and employment of military power, strategy is a plan of action for developing and deploying military forces so as achieve National Security Objectives.¹19 The Indian armed forces' joint doctrine provides a foundation upon which the three Services operate in synergy. Just, joint doctrine under the development of Service-specific strategies which complement the former.¹20

The Indian armed forces use a plethora of acronyms like Low Intensity Conflict (LIC), Sub-conventional Warfare, Asymmetric Warfare, etc. for categorising COIN operations. India's military officers differentiate between insurgency and terrorism. Lieutenant-Colonel Vivek Chadha of the Indian Army insightfully opines:

> Terrorism which enjoys little popular support, will employ sensational and visible violence to terrorise and break the will of the people and in make a government acknowledge its demand. Insurgency, however, will enjoy considerable popular support and therefore need to be handled with far more caution and understanding, let it progresses to the stage of a revolution or civil war.¹²¹

In India, either the insurgency, which had begun in the countryside, gradually spread its tentacles in the cities where the insurgents carried out terrorist acts; or is the other way round. Terrorism, which had initially started in the big cities, gradually spread to the surrounding countryside where insurgent militias started operating.

The Indian forces make no distinction between COIN and counterterrorism (CT). CT is regarded as a component of COIN. The Indian DOCTRINE has evolved within the framework of dharmayuddha and hence displays a high level of ethics. There is absolutely zero tolerance for ethical failings. It is imprinted that the insurgents will deliberately flout the International Humanitarian Law (IHL) of warfare, the COIN forces should strictly follow the laws of warfare. The insurgents do not respect the warrior's code of honour but there should be no transgression on the part of the security forces. This is considered necessary for gaining public support and retaining legitimacy. The temptation for vengeance, revenge, reprisal or the gratuitous use of force against the armed non-state actors is not allowed.²²
