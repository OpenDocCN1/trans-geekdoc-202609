High-performance embedded edge computing is critical to the success of AI missions. For operating in a contested environment, which basically means a scenario involving restricted bandwidth and degraded communications, the UGVs be in trouble. Here, the tactical use of cloud-based computational processing capability may be a liability. It has been realised that the computational processing must reside on-premise to ensure the low latency and real-time speed demanded of AI-based applications. Hence, recent developments in commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) technologies are known to assist with practical embedded edge computing using unmanned vehicles. The high-powered embedded computers provide the processing power needed to run new AI algorithms for keeping UAVs, UUVs and UGVs on the move.²2

The military industry has realised that juxtaposing AI on existing robots could allow these systems to make their own decisions. There are both advantages and limitations in putting such systems in the battlefield. Robotics involves developing a system with the assistance of various branches of engineering including electrical, mechanical, electronics, information and communication technologies. Present-day AI involves intelligent decision systems which allow the systems to think and decide on their own. Such systems take inputs on a range of issues from weather conditions to terrain conditions to capabilities of the attacker, nature of possible incoming location, type and nature of counterattack to be launched, situational awareness for possible target engagement, etc.

Various robotic systems assisted by AI for the purposes of military are getting designed and developed. Such systems could be both defensive systems and offensive systems. Here, defensive systems essentially indicate non lethal systems. Such systems could be reconnaissance systems, which are robotic and autonomous in nature. Important applications could be perimeter fencing systems, where cameras could report to the happenings in their area of operation.

For transporting logistical, the US agencies have developed a mechanical mule,²³ carries the load to the inhospitable region in order to reduce the burden and cater for the risk to human life. Such robotic system (BigDog) was developed to be capable of navigating rough terrain. This mule could follow humans automatically for 24 hours and cover a distance of 32km with a load of 180 kg in one go. However, the military has most likely shelved development of this robotic pack owing to performance issues, being apparently too noisy to make a good soldier (a loud robot that's going to give away their position).²⁴ In this case, there are possible issues related to mechanical engineering aspects of the system development that require improvement.
