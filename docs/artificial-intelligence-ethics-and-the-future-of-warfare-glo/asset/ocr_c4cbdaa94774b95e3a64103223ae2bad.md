Pakistan's civilian and military elite raised the ante by lowering the nuclear threshold. The official Pakistani position was that in case of a limited conventional strike by India across the India-Pakistan border, Islamabad would resort to a countervalue nuclear response. China and Pakistan, unlike India, did not accept the policy of No First Use of nukes. In December 2021, Pakistan tested Babur-IB cruise missile with a range of 900 km, which could carry conventional and nuclear warships. India is in the process of buying the S-400 missile defence system (Ballistic Missile Defence/BMD) from Russia. This BMD will be deployed in Punjab and in the region around Delhi. In retaliation, Pakistan has deployed Baber-3 (Sea Launched Cruise Missile) with Multiple Independent-entry Vehicles capabilities.

Thus, Pakistan's much smaller nuclear arsenal, in comparison to India, has more or less India's nuclear option and conventional superiority. According to one estimate, in 2009, Pakistan had 60 nuclear warheads. Both India and Pakistan are densely populated. In the case of a nuclear exchange between India and Pakistan, one estimate is that 12 million people would die. India lacks the option of launching a decisive conventional strike across the border under the nuclear umbrella to teach Islamabad encouraging insurgencies and terrorist strikes in India. This allows Pakistan to operate with impunity, the terrorist and insurgant camps cross the LOC in Pakistan Occupied Kashmir.

Pakistan's internal scenario also supports the promotion of jihadi terrorism and insurgency. In 2010, Pakistan's population was 185 million and it is still growing. Further, Pakistan's population has a youth bulge. 60% of the population is below the age of 25. Many of these male youths are educated in the madrasahs, and are underqualified for employment in the service and manufacturing sectors, which are the principal growth areas of Pakistan's economy. Most of them strongly support the idea of Pakistan being an Islamic state. A substantial chunk of Pakistan's population is concentrated in the urban centres which are emerging as principal hubs of Islamic extremism and recruitment bases for the radical groups.

This is because a significant chunk of the world's population (and the most important and powerful segment of the populace) resides in the megacities.
