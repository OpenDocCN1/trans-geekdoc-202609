+   (d) Finally, the practice of war, going back to ideals of chivalry, is based on an idea of a certain level of reciprocal risk: Both parties assume risk by going to war, leading to restraint and mutually beneficial rules. Increased use of AWS could reduce or remove such risk if one is able to deploy autonomously functioning weapons that operate without any human support or presence, while the other party would be fighting physically with man- and womanpower. This latter argument is certainly not new and is not restricted to AI-based AWS; it also applies to high-altitude bombing, for instance, where risk is unevenly distributed. However, the lack of reciprocity increases arguably with autonomous weaponry.

At some elements of the “Ban Killer Robots’ movement arguably contribute to muddying the waters by not tending sufficiently to the advantages of AI-based systems in decision-support functions, nor to the generics in favour (at some) AI-based systems used in decision-making capacities, not least to increase protection of civilian lives and force protection. Simultaneously, the development towards battlefields, whether kinetic or cyber, where human beings are minimally involved in, and cannot stop or change, where ML systems develop in a way that creates an increasing distance between users’ intent on the hand and the actual decision points on the other, must be addressed as maybe the key concern militating against deployment of these systems.³⁶ Insisting on the ethical obligations of all developers and users of such systems might be a useful way to address this without agreeing in favour of blanket bans. In the legal area, the establishment of international regulations on the use of autonomous weapon systems would likely be of interest to many states, including those invested in such systems, because of the safety and escalation risks that these systems pose. Limitations on use would impact first and foremost in on military operators but would not directly raise the thorny issues of dual-use that would impact on producers. However, as indicated below in our recommendations, a ban on selected AWS, for instance as applied to nuclear launch decisions or hypersonic missiles, could be desirable.
