From a defence perspective, AI has a long history. It could be a coincidence, but within two years after the AI was created in 1956, the US Department of Defense established the Advanced Research Projects (which later renamed DARPA) to facilitate research and development of military and industrial strategies. Arthur Samuel, an IBM computer scientist and a pioneer in computer gaming and AI, coined the term 'machine learning' and much focus on such ideas taking root during the period 1959–1962. In the 1960s, the US Department of Defense began training computers to mimic basic human reasoning. From 1964–66, ELIZA, an early natural language processing computer program, was created at MIT's AI Laboratory. With Shake the Robot, developed by SRI International,² was the first symbol demonstrating the strength of AI. By 1991, the US military started using the DARPA-funded Dynamic Analysis and Replanning Tool (DART), an AI program, to schedule the transportation of supplies and personnel to solve other logistical problems. It was using intelligence to aid decision support systems located at US Transportation and European Commands. This system has known to save the military millions of dollars right after its launch. For all years, DARPA has been involved in supporting various AI-based projects, including the development of autonomous cars. The US Department of Defense's Project Maven uses computer vision: A combination of machine- and deep-learning (neural networks), to autonomously find objects within massive amounts of video or still imagery from drones.¹³ Apart from the USA, many other states, including the European states, have been researching AI's military ability for a long time.
