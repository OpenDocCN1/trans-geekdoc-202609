# Philosophy of emergent logic

Indian philosophical traditions have a long and different history of engaging with this 'duality' through the principle of tattva or 'thatness' as a tool to understand the nature of knowledge and experience. As a philosophical foundation for a new system of non-status quoist logic for non-carbon sentence, two contemporary Indian philosophers – Jiddu Krishnamurthy and U.G. Krishnamurthy – useful to use and identify some of the key tenets that can be turned into principles (control laws). Both the philosophers, in their own ways, make the point that there is fundamentally no distinction between the observer and the observation, as the act of observation involves the observer transforming what is observed into an act of observation through knowledge. J. Krishnammurthy^17 in Nature of the New Mind puts out the role of knowledge in observation that turns the relationship between the observed and the observed from observation to an act of observation.^18

There is no ideal in observation. When you have an ideal, you cease to observe; you are merely approximating the present to the idea, and therefore there is duality and conflict.

An ideal and/or a thought is knowledge, and knowledge is something that is documented, commonly accepted, collectively shared, and experienced. In many ways, a tree cannot be categorised as a tree unless there is a prior knowledge that categorises it as tree. This conundrum, in a nutshell, is the problematic of empirical falsification. U.G. Krishnamurthi puts it more directly and as a clear contrast to the concept of rationality, and by logical extension the foundations of existentialism derived from Cartesian 'I think therefore I am'^19 logic with an emphatic 'I don't think therefore I am not.'

Man is a memory. You understand things around you by the help of the knowledge that was put in you. I am not anti-rational, just irrational. You may infer a rational meaning in what I say or do, but it is your doing, not mine.^20

Both Jiddu Krishnamurthi and U.G. Krishnamurthi provide the philosophical framework for an emergent logic where the observer, observation and the observed are not different from the thinker, thinking and thought. A philosophy of emergent logic provides for every observation being an observer moment^21 facilitating a 'here and now' emergent logic that exists for the moment and as long as the context requires it.
