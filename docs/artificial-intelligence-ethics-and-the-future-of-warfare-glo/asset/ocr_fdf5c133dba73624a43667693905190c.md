# Ethics of AI-based systems in the military

+   Courage signifies the ability to confront challenges, but at the same time to do so rightly in the face of fear, chaos or temptation; this includes a readiness to signal one's moral concerns in the face of powerful processes or institutions, which may not be taking those moral concerns into account.

+   Prudence, or practical wisdom, the ability to use actively one's experience and competence, and that of others, and to weigh many factors against each other, not least when faced with complex and fateful decisions. Prudence calls on us to acquire skills we may need to carry out tasks effectively; for AI applications, which will require enhanced digital competence on the part of all who make use of these applications. Prudence also signifies a precautionary approach, where dangers and opportunities are taken into account at every stage of a process.

+   And finally, justice signifies the ability to take the moral concerns of all involved – we would say the human dignity and human rights of all involved, as well as environmental concerns – into consideration when decisions are made, and to let the common good of one's community guide politics. Ultimately, the particular good of one or more parts of that community guide grave ramifications of, that community encompasses all human beings, meaning that we must take into consideration international and global concerns, and also the human dignity and human rights of those we would consider our adversaries. Most would add – under the heading of environmental justice – that includes proper care of the natural environment of which human beings are.

We believe these virtues will be crucial in the development of both military ethics and political ethics as the power and potential of AI-based military systems are developed and deployed.

Other virtues will similarly be needed; obedience, for instance, the virtue by which we respond to command authority promptly but never rely or automatically. Also, we must take care not to confuse machine input and recommendations with the order of a superior and adjust our understanding of obedience accordingly.

According classical thinking about virtues, not least inspired by the Greek philosopher Aristotle, the proper operation of virtues in general – and of moral modern psychology in particular – requires training (indeed, education) and habituation. Virtues are not learnt that simple facts or purely automated routines or tasks are learnt. Virtues are learned and acquired through a combination of understanding and repeated action over time. This also explains why the ethical challenges associated with using AI-based systems must be integrated into basic training and into our broader understanding of military ethics.
