+   Human on the loop: Human-supervised systems that remain the oversight of a human operator.

+   Human out of the loop: Fully autonomous systems that carry out operations without any interference or oversight of a human operator.

+   Reactive Systems: Follow condition-action rules, commonly referred to as 'if-then' rules.

+   Deliberative Systems: Employ a model of the world (information on how the functions work and how actions by the system are responded by the world), a value function (which offers information about the intended goal), and a set of alternative rules to search for and plan ways to accomplish the goal.

+   Learning systems: Become more effective over time by gaining experience.

+   Operational tasks include 'mobility, health management (fault detection), etc.'.

+   Mission tasks include 'target identification and selection, explosive detection, etc.'

AI has been segregated into three branches: Narrow (weak) AI, General Intelligence (AGI) and AI Super Intelligence (ASI). ASI is said to be a futuristic capability where the machines would surpass the intelligence of humans who have the brightest and most gifted minds. AGI has been linked to an advanced level of intelligence that would least match, or not surpass, that of a human. It is supposed to be so advanced that it would be able to comprehend the environment around it and also make decisions and predictions based on this understanding, something more akin to what was portrayed in the movie *The Terminator*. To date, we have a narrow phase of AI today. This phase has improved over last time and is continually developing, as machines are able to perform tasks such as object identification or language translation, but as per the user-defined inputs and parameters. Often these parameterized inputs are broadly defined but allow a substantial range of autonomy by establishing some limits on the AI's behaviour. For instance, AlphaZero – a computer program, developed by DeepMind – mastered the game of chess. Playing chess allowed the technology to initiate moves as per its own understanding (providing freedom to navigate the chess board) but within the rules of the game. Hence, AI in the current context is defined as 'purpose-built, problem-specific and context-driven.'¹⁸
