## UAVs/Drones

UAVs/Drones could be defined as remotely operated, autonomous or automated robots, that are capable of sensing information, processing and executing a physical action without a human pilot on board.¹⁹. Progressively, there has been an evolution of drones since their first use by Austria at war with Italy for the bombing of Venice city on 15 July 1849, where it used balloons carrying explosives which had fuses as control mechanisms to trigger the explosion. The Second World War witnessed the use of flying bombs by both the Allied and Axis Forces. The USN retrofitted B-24 bombers with remote-controlled mechanisms that dropped bombs on the German trenches; and German V-1 bombers similarly attacked many cities of the Allied nations using similar remote-controlled mechanisms.⁰.

Modern UAVs, which are now being widely used, were initially invented for military purposes by the Ryan Aeronautical Company. Beginning in 1951, the Ryan Firebee was a series of target drones developed by the Ryan Aeronautical Company. Firebee 124is UAVs were successfully used by Israel against Arabs during the 1973 Yom Kippur War for reconnaissance and decoys. The USA used drones in Vietnam, Iraq and Afghanistan; they were largely used for reconnaissance and intelligence gathering in the initial stage. Advances in AI are the benchmark of power among the superpowers. Modern warfare in essence is more complex, on account of an increasing number of micro variables. A change in any one variable could create an exponential impact on battle outcomes – and even on the war itself.¹¹.

## Ethics, laws on war and AI-driven warfare 195

Visibly, in the last couple of decades, drones, in their avatar as weapons, have changed the face of war. In the year 2020, the world witnessed the pivotal role Turkish drones played in Azerbaijan's impressive victory over Armenia in the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict. also following the Russian invasion in Ukraine the TV footage with Kamikaze drones supplied by the US brought heavy losses on Russian tanks, proving how military drones are playing a pivotal role in Ukraine's defensive war. Undeniably, AI-powered UAVs (drones) have now joined the armed forces of the world in their inventory as they are becoming indispensable in modern war.

A new kind of war was born when the USA and its allies commenced using drones in countries where they were not formally at war or without the proper consent of the sovereign states. In such places, UAVs targeted individual masterinds or terror groups in so called 'war against terror.' Turkey and some other countries have been using drones for transnational assassination in Syria. The use of drones in the war against terror and the misuse of drones for terrorism calls for an in-depth analysis of the ethical and legal issues. But the most contentious issue here is the use of autonomous drones/weapon systems without human imprint or negligible human control because (lethal) autonomous weapon systems support extrajudicial killings²².

In a message to the Group of Government Experts, Antonio Guterres, UN Secretary General on March 25 2019 wrote: 'Autonomous machines with the power and discretion to select targets and take lives without human involvement are potentially unacceptable, morally repugnant and should be prohibited by international law.' He further reminded the expert dealing with AI-assisted weapons that human responsibility for decisions on the use of weapon systems must be retained, such accountability cannot be transferred to machines.³.

So far, the international community has not prohibited the use of such weapons. It is difficult for the UN to garner the support of all members to make them agree to ban AI-powered autonomous weapons/AI-embedded technology that may be used for offensive purposes in the form of a law. And that leads to the lowering of how use such systems (autonomous vehicles, fire-and-forget/loitering missiles, or drones loaded with explosives) and reducing the probability of being held accountable would increase the probability of their use. The cruel asymmetry where one side can kill with impunity, and thus has no reasons to do so, already exists in conventional drone zones with remote-controlled weapons (e.g., USA in Pakistan).²⁴.
