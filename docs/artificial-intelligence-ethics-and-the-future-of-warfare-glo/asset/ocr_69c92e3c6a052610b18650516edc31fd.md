New Delhi plans to equip the surveillance and monitoring drones with software and algorithms that are all capable of reading identity cards and possessing facial recognition capability.³31 This will aid the security forces to track down the insurgents and terrorists before they can strike.

In 2013, India's Ministry of Defence published a firstly roadmap for the Indian armed forces. This document noted that with the passage of time, not only should UAVs (also termed the Indian armed forces as Remotely Piloted Aircraft Systems [RPAS]) be miniaturised but yet also become more intelligent. Swarming drones which are capable of working together, and teaming of UAVs with manned platforms will become the norm in the near future. Swarm intelligence stands for the collective behaviour of decentralised self-organised systems. The principal characteristics of a swarm intelligence system is its capacity to function in a coordinated manner. Swarming occurs when several units conduct a convergent attack on a target several axes. The era of urban warfare is coming. According to one estimate, by 2050, two-thirds of humans will reside in cities.³33 There are distinct advantages to using robots in built-up urban areas. Small robots can see whatever satellites and airborne sensors are unable to see. Further, the use of lethal robots will keep soldiers out of harm in the confined terrain of megacities.³34 For urban warfare, the CAIR is working on a project named Multi Agent Robotics Framework, which the Indian Army already possesses wheeled robots, four-legged and six-legged robots, and wallimbing robots that can operate in a variety of terrains.³35 CAIR is working to make these robots mission-capable.
