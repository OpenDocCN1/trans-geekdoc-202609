# Can AI help in defending India?

India has fought three wars with Pakistan and one with China. Defending India, with two nuclear-armed adversaries – China and Pakistan – is a great challenge. And that challenge becomes formidable if there is a collaborative two-front threat. India also faces additional challenges of militancy and insurgency internally as is beset with terrorists' attacks in Jammu and Kashmir and other places, in addition to prolonged insurgency in the North-Eastern states. Who can forget the dastardly26/11 attack in Mumbai in 2008 by the Pakistani terrorist group Lashkar-e-Taiba? Pakistan's role in spreading terrorism is well established. Bleeding India through thousand cuts is a long-term strategy that terror groups based in Pakistan are implementing. India is not the lone sufferer. More than half of the major terrorist plots against the West between 2004 and 2011, for instance, had connections with Pakistan.58

For management of insurgency in North-East India, there have been occasional difficulties for Delhi to garner support and consistent cooperation from neighbouring countries, like Bangladesh and Myanmar, for intelligence and assistance in law enforcement. For terrorism in Jammu and Kashmir and other places in India, Pakistan hardly ever cooperated, though on many occasions they overtly denounced these terrorists, originating from their soil, as non-state actors, or freedom fighters whom their Inter-Services Intelligence (ISI) was covertly abetting and supporting. The USA was able to get support of the Pakistan government when they were droning terrorist hideouts in the Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) region, but for India it remained a distant dream. Obviously, there will be more difficulty in locating targets if India wants to make use of drones against the terrorists based in Pakistan. After years of terror strikes, supported by Pakistan, India was compelled to carry out a surgical strike to avenge the deaths of the Uri attack attackers in an army camp on 18 September 2016. During the operation, the army commanded, in a daring, precise, and quick operation across the Line of Control (LOC) in Pakistan occupied Kashmir (Po), dismantled six terrorist camps. Days after 40 Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) personnel were killed on 14 February 2019, in a suicide attack on Pulama, Jammu and Kashmir, the Indian Air Force launched an air strike at the Balakot terrorist camp on 26 February 2019. Through this air strike, the Indian Government, displaying strong political will, sent a clear-cut message to the terrorist group Jaish-e-Mohammad and Pakistan that attacks like Uri and Pulama would come at a cost. If India had AI-powered drones, possibly they could have been employed for these kinds of tasks or employed in tandem after assessing the target value. AI-augmented drones would have been a valuable addition, if not a substitute, to combat soldiers and airmen.
