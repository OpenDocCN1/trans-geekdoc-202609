Ethics, in simple terms, are do's and don'ts, i.e., virtues and evil we all learn and are expected to follow because ethics can be defined based on well-founded standards of right and wrong that prescribe what humans ought to do, usually in terms of rights, obligations, benefits to society, fairness or specific virtues.* We need to speak of ethics for AI and especially machine intelligence, independent of merely human ethics. Ethics of AI design encompasses the moral behaviour of the engineers involved in designing, industries and making AI-augmented products, including for defence services, policy and decision-makers who allow the use of such products and finally the end users. As it consider the behaviour of machines; ethics also becomes a subject of scrutiny. The ethics of AI is the branch of ethics of technology to artificially intelligent systems. It is sometimes divided into a concern with the moral behaviour of humans as they design, make, and use artificially intelligent systems, and a concern with the behaviour of machines, in machine ethics. It also includes the of a possible singularity due to superintelligent AI*

AI has been added to conventional weapon systems to achieve better results or to lessen the human exposure to harm. Even though AI-assisted weapons may act on their own volition, if a human in the loop the machine cannot outsmart man and behave unpredictably beyond what it was preprogrammed or designed for. A possible singularity could be to superintelligent AI, which may surpass human intelligence and thereafter become unstoppable, where human beings will no more control. Form of superintelligent AI may not be an immediate possibility, but Ray Kurzweil, Google's Director of Engineering, believes that today could be the reality by 2045 or so; but he asserts that it should not be a cause of worry, as human beings will, by then, adapt to merge with the new form of AL.

These are assumptions at this stage; AI surpassing human intelligence and technology making its own decisions be a problem, raising a burning question in respect of the autonomous weapon systems (AWS) – should we mortgage our freedom to algorithm? The question also leads to issues on ethics and a universal adoption of regulations to check or use of AI. During the 41st Session of UNESCO Global Conference, held in November 2021 in Paris, an agreement, called 'Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence,' was adopted, renewing cooperation on this much-deb issue. Many organisations have also come up with codes of ethics to guide stakeholders facing ethical dilemmas when deciding on the adaptation and use of AI.
