# AI as cockpit crew

On 20 August 2020, in the finale of the AlphaDogfight trials under US Defense Research Projects Agency's (DARPA's) auspices, an algorithm developed by Heron Systems and installed in a simulator as a simulated F-16, comprehensively defeated an US Air Force pilot in five rounds of mock combat. Almost exactly a year ago, DARPA had emphatically stated that, 'No AI currently exists, however, that can outduel human strapped into a fighter jet in a high-speed, high-G dogfight.'18 Deeper analysis would show that the simulation conditions were biased in favour of AI. The human pilot was not in an actual aircraft which was trained to fly by the seat of his pants, and had a Virtual Reality (VR) headset to give him situational awareness. However, the demonstration was insignificant as it ratified the all-inanimate construct of AI replacing a pilot in the cockpit for something as dynamic as a dogfight.

Civil aviation has already models of AI in the cockpit. The Autonomy system from Garmin enables, in case of pilot incapacity, for on-board systems to take either automatically or on activation by the pilot or a passenger, stabilise the aircraft and then autoland, if required. Interestingly, this, now certified by US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), is not projected as an AI system but as automation.

The patent application for Emergency Autoland System19 did not use the term 'artificial' or 'intelligence' anywhere.
