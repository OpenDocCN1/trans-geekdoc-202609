The Human Rights Watch (HRW) definition differs. As per HRW, fully autonomous weapons are those that, initiated, will be able to operate without Meaningful Human Control (MHC). They will be able to select and engage targets on their own, rather than requiring a human to make targeting and killing decisions for each individual attack. However, in the absence of consensus on what MHC is to be specified, it concedes that there is a lack of clarity on the definition of LAWS.$6$

There is a view that rather focus on autonomous systems alone, there is a need to leverage the power of AI for increasing the combat power of the current force. This approach is referred to as Narrow or Weak AI. Narrow AI could lead to many benefits, such as using image recognition from video feeds to identify imminent threats, anticipating bottlenecks, automating administrative functions, etc. Such applications would permit force restructuring, with a smaller staff of scientists replacing large organisations. Narrow AI also has the potential to help the Defence Forces improve their teeth-to-tail ratio.$7$

Another focus on the evolutionary route to the development of autonomous weapons is what can be termed as human-machine teaming, wherein machines and humans work together in a symbiotic relationship. Like the mythical centaur, this approach envisages harnessing human speed and power to human judgement, combining machine precision and reliability with human robustness and flexibility, as well as enabling computers and humans to help each other to think, termed as cognitive teaming. Some functions will necessarily have to be completely automated, like missile defence lasers or cybersecurity, and in all cases where there is no time for human intervention. But, at least in the medium term, most military AI applications are likely to be teamed: Computers will fly the missiles, ai lead the signals, read the sensors, and pull all the data together over a network, putting it into an intuitive interface, which humans, using their experience, can use well-informed decisions.$8$
