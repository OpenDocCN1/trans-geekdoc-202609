At the same time, it is imperative that the Indian Army keeps pace with the changing nature of warfare in the twenty-first century, driven by rapid advances in technology on AI and robotics after decades of false starts, today appear to be at an inflection point and are rapidly being incorporated into a range of products and services in the commercial environment. It is only a matter of time before they manifest in defence systems significant enough to usher in a new RMA. Notwithstanding the worldwide concern over development of LAWS with legal and ethical points it is clearly that, no matter what conventions are adopted by the UN, R&D by major players in this area is likely to proceed unhindered.

Given India's own security landscape, the adoption of AI-based systems with increasing degree of autonomy in operational scenarios is expected to yield tremendous benefits in the given. There is a need to adopt a radically different approach for facilitating the development of AI-based systems, utilising the best available expertise within and outside the country. As with any transformation, this is no easy task. Only a determined effort, with specialists on board and due impetus from the apex level, is likely to yield the desired results.
