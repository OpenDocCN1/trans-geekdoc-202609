AI can be transformative as well as destructive depending on its adoption. In essence, there can be as good as well evils of AI and that calls for the developers, users, policymakers and leaders to differentiate between ethical and unethical uses of AI. The most important aspect in the design and deployment of AI-assisted weapons is that it would be unethical and illegal to delegate the decision to take a human life to a machine. There is a moral component to this position, that we should not allow machines making decisions for others – or nobody – will be culpable.¹⁶¹

For the past couple of years, there has been a considerable increase in debate in public forums questioning the ethics, legality, and importance of the development and use of AI-powered weapons. Many scientists and engineers researching in the AI domain concur with view that the non-inclusion of attributability of taking human lives will lead to extrajudicial killings, makes AI-powered weaponry instruments of extreme violence. Many organisations are coming together under umbrella to express their concerns through an open letter of pledge against such weapons, where it has been highlighted that lethal autonomous weapons have characteristics quite different from nuclear, chemical and biological weapons, and the unilateral actions of a single group could spark an arms race. The international community lacks the technical tools and global governance systems to manage an AI generated arms race. Stigmatising and preventing such an arms race should be a high priority for both national and global security.¹⁶² One also needs to ponder whether possession of AWS can change the balance of power and global order.

Offensive drone strikes at targeting transnational terror groups have become part of the strategy of the USA, Turkey and a few other countries, and one will not be surprised if more countries follow a similar strategy to fight terrorism. When one compares the two world wars of the last century with the contemporary armed conflicts, the latter can be as low-level affairs, yet they pose serious and disruptive problems.¹⁶³

Henry Kissinger feels that weapons and AI applications (as AWS) greatly complicate the current dangerous prospects. Unlike nuclear weapons, weapons embedded with AI are ubiquitous, relatively inexpensive to develop and use. Because the threshold for their use is so low, and their destructive ability of such weapons – or even their threat – can turn a crisis into a war or turn a war into a nuclear war through unintentional or uncontrolled escalation.¹⁶⁴
