US policy of using drones for targeted assassination is indeed questionable, but at the same time one wonders to do in cases where terrorists, harboured by a state which is not open at war, are engaged without collateral damage (like killing of innocent civilians) in pursuance of a nation's aim to eliminate terror. Such military use of drones will be marked by kind of asymmetry characteristic of peacetime reprisal.⁴⁹ Its purpose is coercive and to repress terrorists, hiding/sheltering the country’s border, in these cases in faraway lands to prevent the recurrence of terrorism. Deployment of such advanced systems, besides eliminating a target, revenge can be taken through deployment of AWS. For a historical reference of a classic case of peacetime reprisal, one may recall when on 26 December 1968, two terrorists attacked an Israeli plane, preparing to take off from Athens. Later, after getting caught by Athenian police, it discovered these two terrorists, who had Lebanese documents, were actually members of PLO, who then had their headquarters in Beirut, Lebanon. Providentially, out of 50 passengers on board, only 1 was killed. Two days after the attack, in a daring reprisal operation, Israeli commands landed by helicopter in Beirut airport and destroyed 13 Lebanese civilian planes. This command was considered a spectacular military success, but more importantly, it was considered great success from the moral point of view, despite repeated warnings by the Lebanese government for not allowing its soil to be used for PLO terrorist activities, Israel was not left with much choice but to undertake this dramatic reprisal, resulting in no civilian deaths despite loss of life of an Israeli citizen in the Athens airport attack, and the retaliation is parallel and proportionate.
