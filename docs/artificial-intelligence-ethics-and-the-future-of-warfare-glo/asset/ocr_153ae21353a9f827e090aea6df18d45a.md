In addition to the lack of consensus about terminology, other points of disagreement (some explicit, others only implicit) hamper the international discussions about AWS. For instance, some parties speak as though AWS is already in operation and has been for some time; others, by contrast, assume that deployment of AWS is still off in the future. The disparity would suggest that in their exchanges the parties are not referring to an identical set of technologies. In other words, for some a ban would prohibit all future weapon systems, while others recoil because a ban would also cover existing technologies, of which some are well entrenched and considered valuable to some militaries.

Other ambiguities surround key terms of the discussion, for instance the meaning assigned to ‘autonomous functioning,’ ‘targeting’ and so. If a ban on AWS is to become a matter of prescriptive law, these ambiguities would have to be ironed out, so that the parties could operationalise what exactly is or not excluded from their arsenals.⁷⁷ Finally, among those who embrace the language of ‘meaningful human control,’ disagreement surrounds the relevant point of comparison. For some, the term implies human must be involved in every decision where a specific human being is targeted with lethal harm. This would entail that no automation is allowable whenever a ‘trigger’ is pulled, as in ‘hand-to-hand’ combat situations. For others, ‘meaningful human control’ refers instead to the person commanding an operation; he or she delegates the execution of the command to subordinates whether human or machine – and adequately oversee what they do. In this connection, it is important to recognise that even when a human executes a commander’s ‘meaningful’ control is not equivalent to ‘perfect’ control; human subordinates cannot be counted to invariably act in accordance with the intent of their commanders; by consequence, setting perfect control as a standard would perhaps set an idealistic expectation for the acceptable use of AWS in battlefield scenarios.

What are the effects of AI on the chain of command? Even in conventional warfare, not all decisions can be carried out by way of obedience. Two situations can render it inoperative/inapplicable: (1) time constraints do not allow for receiving instructions from above; (2) communications links are severed, not allowing for receipt of commands. Obedence, moreover, can fail in its basic intent, as commanders can misconstrue a situation and, based on error, order actions that run counter to what they would have intended.
