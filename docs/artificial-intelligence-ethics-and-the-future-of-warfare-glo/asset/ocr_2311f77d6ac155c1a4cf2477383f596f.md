Thus, there is a general indication that AI could have a transformative impact on both defensive and offensive cyber weapons. However, the likely impacts of introducing AI-cyber tools within weapon systems may range from being a positive reinforcement to a destructive scenario. From a defensive perspective, the combination of AI-cyber tool could help enhance the performance of weapon systems and detect irregularities and deviations. The combination could build a robust network against any form of cyber subversion. Several cybersecurity firms are cognizant of the growing advantages of implementing AI solutions to provide rapid insights and cut through the noise, reducing the response time during eternity. From a national perspective, as the United States’ report, “AI can enhance our ability to predict, identify, and respond to cyber and physical threats from a range of sources.”24 The DOD’s Project Maven is believed to ‘employ AI to decipher video footage and is expected to among others, increase the precision of drones.’23 It is also being expanded to other areas such as speeding up the reading of hard drives obtained from non-state actors or terrorist entities.26

From an offensive perspective, the introduction of AI within weapon systems may make the systems vulnerable to different types of cyberattacks and data manipulation. For example, an adversary could deploy malware, install a backdoor to take control of the system or manipulate/fool the behaviour of autonomous systems. Furthermore, the data fed to autonomous systems could be poisoned, wherein intruders feed an algorithm to alter the system’s behaviour according to their own aims. Offensive attacks such as data manipulation or spoofing would be relatively easy to execute, but very difficult to detect, attribute or effectively count. After witnessing the capabilities of Mayhem, Pentagon’s startup-centric Defense Innovation Unit Experimental (DIU)) initiated a project Dubbled Triton is said to offer Mayhem-like capabilities to several defence agencies to find coding flaws in both operating systems and custom programs used by the US military.27

The introduction of AI-cyber tools within weapon systems highlights a prevalent strategic paradox wherein, on the one hand, these technologies are making operations easier and eliminating redundant tasks. While the other hand, the same technology would make systems more prone to cyberattacks, vulnerability exploitation and an increased probability of technical glitches.
