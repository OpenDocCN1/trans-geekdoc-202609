These two factors are, to a great extent, responsible for university-educated urban middle-class youths not considering the army as their preferred career.78 One way the Indian Army could overcome the officer shortage is by using more intelligent warring machines (UGVs with human commanders at the back) in place of infantry units for tackling the insurgents.

Further, the Indian Army is thinking of using loitering munitions fitted with precision-guided munitions in sub-conventional warfare. A loitering munition is a UAV which can engage explosive targets. These UAVs are fitted with high-resolution optical and infrared cameras which allow the target to locate, survive and track the target. This sort of UAV is able to hover over the target area for an extended period before striking, thus enabling the operator to decide what to target and when. The robust sensor-to-shooter communications will also allow a human-in-the-loop command system to continue. Loitering munitions are perfect for targeted assassinations of insurgent commanders and also for crowd control by taking out the leaders. In addition, urban centres, the loitering drones with non-lethal weapons could temporarily deactivate the hostile crowd. This will prevent non-combat fatalities as collateral damage in urban operations always causes a political hue and cry.79

## Conclusion

Technology is neutral. Both India and its non-state opponent are using AI-related technologies, and their use by both sides will increase in scope and frequency in the near future. The issue is which side will use it effectively. Complete autonomy for LAWS remains a chimera in the foreseeable future. At most, in the near future, we will see semi-autonomous weapon systems deployed for operations in combination with human soldiers and manned platforms. The Indian armed forces need to update their joint and service-specific doctrines for blending of AWS with other conventional systems. Along with doctrine, training with and against AWS needs to be elaborated and implemented. Such amalgamation and deployment of AI-related technologies are necessary for effective force planning, force structuring and force deployment. For the time being, the officers of the Indian armed forces are comfortable using AI as a junior partner to reduce the OODA loop and increase tempo, more autonomy should be given to AWS. Such a scenario will run counter to the Indian government’s prescription of using AI as an adjunct of humans in the megacities and countryside. In the near future, we will see robo-soldiers, UGVs, and drones fighting the insurgents and terrorists in the urban areas. More challenging is to establish a balance between human and machine. The ‘face of war’ is indeed changing. Clausewitzian ‘fog’ must not be replaced with ‘AI generated fog.’
