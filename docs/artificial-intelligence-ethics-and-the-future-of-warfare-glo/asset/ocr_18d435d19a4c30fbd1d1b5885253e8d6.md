We should remind ourselves here of the phases of the targeting cycle we are now dealing with:

+   Phase 4, ‘Commander’s decision, force planning, and assignment,’ involves final approval of the prioritised targets and communication of these from the operational to the tactical level. Relevant constraints, articulated as rules of engagement, are passed to the assigned units.

+   Phase 5, ‘Mission planning and execution, involves positive identification of targets to be engaged with the predetermined weapon systems. At this juncture, a tactical targeting cycle is operational, and is described by NATO at F2T2EA: Find, fix, track, target, engage and assess.29 It is that robotic combatants could be deployed, who would autonomously run through the entire F2T2EA cycle.

Lethal weapons can be defined as lethal weapons that are ‘capable of selecting and attacking targets without human intervention’¹3⁰ in other words, they are weapons that, depending on the level of autonomy achieved, can perform several of the actual decision-making functions within these two phases.

# A summary of the debate

The debate around autonomous weapons has produced arguments for and against their development and use.31 Very broadly, we could say that the debate is between, on one hand, optimists, who believe such weapons can be used within the framework of IHL and be under meaningful human control (a phrase we will return to), and that their use will reduce overall bloodshed and collateral damage; and, on the other hand, pessimists, who hold that such weapons will take killing – the crucial and fateful decision to kill – out of the hands of human decision-makers, thus reducing or removing human control and potentially leading to warfare not regulated effectively by legal and moral boundaries.
