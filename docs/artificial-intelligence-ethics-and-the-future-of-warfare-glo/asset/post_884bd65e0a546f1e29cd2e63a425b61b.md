## DEFENCE APPLICATIONS OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

### Introduction

AI commonly identifies as the simulation of human intelligence by machines. The main focus here is on information communications technologies (ICT). Generally, AI systems involve specific hardware, software, and algorithms. Today, AI is impacting practically every sector, from science and industry to education and medicine. It is also becoming an integral part of security. Various military applications of AI are becoming evident. This chapter takes a broad overview of and ends with a discussion on legality, arms controls, and ethics. The idea of Artificial Intelligence (AI) is not new. However, this technology remains evolving and there is a requirement for much innovation.

Actually, some armed forces are already inducting this technology in their overall architecture. From a military perspective, AI has both offensive and defensive dimensions. For some years now, there is an ongoing debate on Lethal Autonomous Systems (LAWS). This debate, which mainly revolves around aspects of ethics and morality, is still evolving. This chapter discusses various AI-related developments.

### Context

Technology shapes warfare. It defines, circumscribes warfare and is known as the instrumentality of warfare. It is viewed that technology shapes warfare; and, conversely, war shapes technology. Also, military technology is not deterministic but opens doors for experimentation. The first two decades of the twenty-first century have seen an explosion in technological advancements affecting cultures, economies and governments around the world. Militaries and the realm of warfare have been no different. Technological innovation is known to modify the way armies engage with each other, and in doing so, it has redefined the boundaries of how conflicts are fought. Seismic changes in waging have always been to the introduction of new technologies. From the horse-drawn armies of the 1910s, through the mechanized forces of the 1930s and jet-propelled aircraft of the 1940s, to the nuclear age, a technology has dictated the processes of warfare. General Richard Barr – a former head of Britain’s Joint Forces Command – argues that: ‘We are seeing changes on the battlefield as profound as anything in the past 150 years as data, artificial intelligence and connectivity become the key components of warfare. But we are in the foothills of what is coming.’

John McCarthy, a professor emeritus of computer science at Stanford, is credited with having the term ‘artificial intelligence.’ McCarthy was a giant in the field of computer science and a seminal figure in the field of AI. While at Dartmouth in 1955, McCarthy authored a proposal for a two-month, ten-person summer research conference on ‘artificial intelligence’ – the first use of the term in publication. He defined AI as the science and engineering of making intelligent machines computer programs.
