The twenty-first century is witnessing the global naval powers preparing a strong road map for underwater warfare. Historically, submarines are known as the authentic source of surveillance and data gathering. Submarines are important instruments forming a strong defense strategy with the deterrent power to stop any adverse situations. During April 2016, the US Navy launched the Sea Hunter, a 40-meter unmanned and completely autonomous warships designed (a DARPA for conducting anti-submarine warfare. The entire navigation of this vehicle is controlled by AI with zero crew onboard. During April 2021, a MSubs for Britain’s Royal Navy was launched. These are UUVs (Unmanned Underwater Vehicles), an exclusive research prototype for XLUUV (Extra Large Underwater Vehicle). Today, global powers are working towards introducing XLUUV for its capability to handle various underwater challenges.¹²¹

UAVs and UUVs have unique design problems. The most difficult system to design is an unmanned ground vehicle (UGV). This is mainly because the nature of obstructions a system encounters when undertaking operations on the ground is far more than in the air or underwater. The unmanned systems in full autonomous roles require a lot of processing power. The analysing large amounts of data to complete complex deep learning algorithms requires significant processing capabilities. A large number of devices like cameras and sensors are in use for collecting and processing data. With more devices collecting data, there are issues associated with the bandwidth. As such, in the case of unmanned systems of any kind, the size of the unit is also important. High-performance embedded computing (HPEC) helps in this regard. HPC computing, coupled with AI capabilities, brings forth a computing solution in size, weight, and power (SWAP) in an optimised form. All this fuels AI and deep learning and offers unmanned systems access to on-demand intelligence during the operational phase.
