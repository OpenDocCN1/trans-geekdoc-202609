The 'Loyal Wingman' project of USAF Research Laboratory (AFRL) involves a manned command aircraft with unmanned aircraft serving as an adjunct flying platform, scouting for threats and taking them on, if (thus inviting the term loyal wing). Initial live demonstrations were with F-16s—both manned and unmanned—and were impressive enough to ensure the concept being grafted onto the sixth-generation programmes.

NATO's conglomerate of analysts, while the F-16 tests were contested in a autonomous mode over predetermined parameters, future plans include 'flocking' and 'swarming'. In flocking, more than one unmanned wing would operate under the manned aircraft, which would give more abstract commands than exercise direct control over them, while 'swarming' would obviate the possibility of the command aircraft having situational awareness over each of a huge swarm of UAVs but permit it to exercise command over the swarm as an aggregate. A demonstration related to this concept was made in October 2016 with F/A-18 Super Hornets swarming a swarm of 103 autonomous micro drones, which then proceeded to exhibit 'collective decision-making, adaptive flying, and self-healing.' The swarming UAVs are especially alluring as they represent myriad possibilities. Some UAVs could have offensive roles, some could be scouts (ISR) tasks, could carry EW payloads, some others could aid in target recognition and tracking, and others could be loitering munitions with possible air-to-air capability, that they can sacrifice themselves in defense of their command aircraft. The possibilities are myriad.

As can be, UAVs present a persuasive alternative to exposing expensive (the almost unaff), scarce, manned aircraft to high threat environments. They can be used again and like manned aircraft and are comparatively economical, so greater risks can be taken with them, with no danger of losing a valuable human pilot. They are referred to Low Cost Attributable Aircraft (LCAA) or Attributable (A/R) UAVs, for short life spans (in comparison to manned fighters), recoverable (if not downed during a mission), low cost, and usable with multiple manned aircraft. The AI technologies that are lodged in UAVs enable them to be part of MUM-T programmes like the Skyborg, one of 'Vanguard programmes' launched by AFRL.
