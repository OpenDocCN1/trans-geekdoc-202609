In general, swarming holds immense potential and capability to revolutionise warfare. It can be applied to different types of military tasks. Swarms can patrol large areas with greater efficiency and shorter reaction times than human personnel. Swarms allow missions in various fields from time to factor saving lives. They can be put to use both defensive and offensive missions. Intelligence gathering, search and destroy missions against enemy aires, submarines or over-horizon target, combat, and anti-access/area denial (A/AD) and counter-insurgency missions could be some of the missions where swarms could be used.³34

The US defence establishment has major interests in armed drones. The Perdix system consists of autonomous drones operating as cooperative swarms of 20 or more flying drones. The drones are launched to achieve a specific goal, are expected to engage in collective decision-making and are known to possess swarm self-healing abilities whereby, in the case one or more drone units are forced to drop out, the entire system reconfigures itself automatically for mission completion. Work on this system began in 2013. During this time, several missions have been launched and the system's software is currently being upgraded. The US Army is finishing Project Convergence, which is a campaign of learning to evaluate how the army will fight with modern equipped and advanced capabilities. The idea is to have a low-cost swarm capability with the ability to identify and engage threats with the use of a single controller.³35
