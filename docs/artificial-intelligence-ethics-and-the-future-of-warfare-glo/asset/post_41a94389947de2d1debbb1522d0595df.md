# COIN tactics and India's AWS arsenal

Officers of the Indian armed forces are working to chart out a comprehensive scheme for using AI in a variety of ways for fighting insurgents and terrorists. The Indian military officers are eager to utilise the image recognition algorithms while conducting COIN along the India-Pakistan border. Deep learning algorithms can, with extreme accuracy, read human lines, synthesise speech, and to a great extent, simulate facial expressions. Deep learning is a branch of machine learning (ML) algorithms which can analyse data through multiple layers of complex processing. Each layer's output becomes the input to the next layer in order to carry out analysis and classification. ML means a computer has the ability to learn without being explicitly programmed, i.e., unsupervised learning. Civilian and military officers are eager to use these technologies to move the movement of insurgents between India-Pakistan and India-China borders. The Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) set up the Centre for Artificial Intelligence and Robotics (CAIR) in 1986. In the new millennium, the CAIR is carrying out research in artificial neural networks and computer vision among other things.28

The length of India's land border is 15,200 km. Incorporation and integration of AI-enabled deep learning algorithms, image recognition mechanism and speech recognition (the capacity of the computers to recognise and interpret spoken languages) software with the border management scheme will increase the speed and efficiency of border patrols to prevent the movement of the insurgents across the India-Pakistan and India-China borders.29

In 2002, IAF officer named A.K. Tiwari wrote about the various ways in which the Unmanned Aerial Vehicle/drone (UAVs) can carry out COIN tasks. UAVs fitted with cameras can supplement satellite images. As the UAVs, compared to the satellites, far closer to the surface of the earth, the scale and resolution of the images taken by the former are better. Tiwari notes that however computer software may be used for processing and interpreting the images, human interpretation is necessary, especially if one is looking for small fleet targets like a few insurgents in a large area. Tiwari is realistic in his evaluation. He writes that UAVs at best provide near real-time intelligence rather than real-time intelligence as the targets after observation would move away or conceal themselves.30 India is operating UAVs equipped with high-resolution cameras and sensors for Intelligence collection, Surveillance and Reconnaissance (ISR) of the
