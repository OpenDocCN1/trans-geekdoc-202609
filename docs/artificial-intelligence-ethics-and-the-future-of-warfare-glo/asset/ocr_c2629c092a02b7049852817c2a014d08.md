This section considers AI as ‘any artificial system that performs tasks under varying and unpredictable circumstances without significant human oversight, or can learn from experience, and improve performance when exposed to data sets.’ This idea poses a few vital points worth note; unpredictability, the limitation of significant human oversight, a capacity to learn from experiences, and the introduction of datasets, which exposes some ethical challenges and opportunities not found in many definitions.

Autonomy denotes independence. It must be noted that there are diverse perceptions of autonomy given its context in different disciplines. R.of suggests that due to the absence of a universal definition, negotiating a new treaty to regulate or ban autonomous weapon systems often ends up inoperferences between autonomous and automated systems. Here, the internal cognitive structures of an automated system can always be back-tracked to a human in the loop, autonomous systems are ‘goal-oriented,’ ‘self-directed’ systems, ‘sophisticated enough that their cognitive processes are supposed to be intelligible to the user, who understands the task is supposed to perform but not necessarily how the system will perform that.’The Proba-1 satellite system, which commanded essentially from the ESA’s ground station in Red, Belgium, has onboard autonomy and ground-segment systems that enable it control satellite passes ‘with no operator attendance.’The US Defense Science Board defines autonomy as ‘a capability (or set of capabilities) that enables a particular action of a system to be automatic or, within programmed boundaries, ‘self-governing.’It can be concluded from these descriptions that while autonomy denotes independence, its use in machines still indicates a minimal amount of human interference; a lower level of interaction than what is available in automated and autonomous systems.
