- Deep belief network. The deep belief network (DBN) is an architecture that essentially incorporates stacked layers of Restricted Boltzmann Machines. It is a feed-forward architecture with many hidden nodes. DBNs can be applied to handle unsupervised learning tasks to reduce the dimensionality of features and can likewise be applied to address supervised learning tasks to build clustering models or regression models.

To assemble a DBN, there are two stages: layer-by-layer learning and fine-tuning. Layer-by-layer learning refers to unsupervised training of each Restricted Boltzmann Machine (RBM), and fine-tuning refers to using back-propagation error algorithms to calibrate the parameters of the DBN after unsupervised learning is complete. Deep neural networks are retrained using algorithms called greedy algorithms. Recently, DBNs have gained increased popularity in AI due to their promising advantages. DBNs offer fast analysis and can effectively model complex network structures.

However, unlike all Boltzmann machines and deep convolutional neural networks, they employ both deductive and generative approaches in top-down and bottom-up manners, enabling DBMs to better reveal representations of input architecture. ## 4.12 Case Studies of Deep-Learning Techniques in Healthcare

Nevertheless, deep learning is continuously finding its path into real-world clinical applications. There are numerous use cases involving patient-centric applications, as well as several techniques aimed at improving the well-being of IT users and fostering future growth.

- Imaging analytics and diagnostics. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are deep-learning strategies particularly suitable for classifying images such as MRI and X-rays, as demonstrated by specialized engineers at Stanford University, enabling networks to work more efficiently and store larger images. Subsequently, some CNNs approach—and in some cases outperform—the accuracy and precision of human diagnosticians while identifying significant features in diagnostic imaging studies (Cheng et al. 2016). Researchers have created a deep neural network capable of diagnosing severe neurological conditions, such as stroke and brain hemorrhage, 150 times faster than human radiologists.

- Purpose of natural speech signal processing. Nowadays, neural networks and deep-learning approaches are significantly supported by natural speech signal processing tools to achieve major improvements in the healthcare industry for analyzing and interpreting documentation. Meanwhile, these neural networks can differentiate distinct etymological or syntactic components by “gathering” similar words together and comparing them, helping the network make accurate sense of complex semantics. It can be difficult to understand the nuances of spoken communication, i.e., words that generally appear close to one another in an informal expression may end up being something altogether different than equivalent words that appear in unique contexts. Although sufficiently accurate language-to-text translation has become a reasonably common skill for communication tools, producing reliable and meaningful translation from free-text clinical information remains a highly challenging exercise (Cheng et al. 2016).

- Electronic health record (EHR) for tracking patients' health. Deep learning has been applied to process EHRs, handling both structured (e.g., dosage, medication, laboratory tests) and unstructured information (e.g., free-text clinical notes). Notable examples include systems processing EHRs from a healthcare framework based on specialized engineering for commonly supervised predictive clinical tasks. For typical approaches, deep learning can achieve outcomes superior to conventional AI models. To learn detailed descriptions of clinical concepts such as diseases, prescriptions, and laboratory tests for diagnosis and prediction, neural language deep learning models have also been applied to EHRs (Cheng et al. 2016; Vyas 2023). Providers, scientists, and insurance companies will soon use EHR data to identify patterns in terms of diseases and medication. AI uncovers critical information that is frequently buried deep within text in health records. Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) neural networks are among the best-known models used for NLP.
