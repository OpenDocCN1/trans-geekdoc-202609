
These questions are designed to test your understanding of the regulatory issues and international standards discussed in [Chapter 9](#ch09.html#ch09), focusing on the intricacies of governing AI, blockchain, and quantum computing technologies.

**1.** What is the primary focus of the European Union's Artificial Intelligence Act?
A. To promote AI innovation across all industries
B. To establish legal standards for AI systems based on risk levels
C. To enforce unified cybersecurity measures across member states
D. To deregulate the digital market to foster competition
**Answer: B. To establish legal standards for AI systems based on risk levels**

**2.** Which country integrates its AI policies with broader strategic goals for technological supremacy?
A. United States
B. European Union
C. China
D. Japan
**Answer: C. China**

**3.** What is the role of the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) in the United States regarding AI?
A. Regulating data privacy for AI applications
B. Developing AI standards and guidelines that influence industry practices
C. Funding AI startups
D. Overseeing ethical AI development
**Answer: B. Developing AI standards and guidelines that influence industry practices**

**4.** As discussed in the chapter, which regulatory challenge is associated explicitly with Quantum Computing?
A. Intellectual property disputes over algorithms
B. It’s potential to compromise data and national security frameworks
C. Lack of skilled professionals to manage quantum systems
D. Quantum technology's impact on environmental sustainability
**Answer: B. It’s potential to compromise data and national security frameworks**

**5.** How do international standards like ISO/TC 307 impact blockchain technology?
A. They restrict the types of blockchain that can be developed
B. They enhance blockchain interoperability and security across different sectors
C. They dictate the price of blockchain transactions globally
D. They limit the geographical areas where blockchain can be implemented
**Answer: B. They enhance blockchain interoperability and security across different sectors**

**6.** What is a significant focus of the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) regarding Quantum Computing?
A. Reducing the cost of quantum computing implementation
B. Facilitating discussions on quantum computing's impact on global communications
C. Developing entertainment applications using quantum computing
D. Ensuring that quantum computing remains a public sector domain
**Answer: B. Facilitating discussions on quantum computing's impact on global communications**

**7.** Which approach is characterised by dynamic and modular regulatory structures to accommodate rapid technological changes?
A. Predictive Regulatory Frameworks
B. Static Regulatory Frameworks
C. Traditional Regulatory Frameworks
D. Historical Regulatory Frameworks
**Answer: A. Predictive Regulatory Frameworks**

**8.** What challenge does the global nature of digital technology pose for individual nations?
A. Harmonizing international regulations with local demands
B. Ultimately adopting foreign technologies without adaptation
C. Ignoring international standards means focusing solely on local development
D. Prioritizing traditional technologies over emerging digital technologies
**Answer: A. Harmonizing international regulations with local demands**

**9.** Which of the following best describes the role of regulatory sandboxes?
A. They offer a competitive environment for technology companies to outperform each other
B. They provide a controlled environment for testing new technologies without full regulatory consequences
C. They restrict the development of technologies to specific areas
D. They serve as educational platforms for the public to learn about new technologies
**Answer: B. They provide a controlled environment for testing new technologies without full regulatory consequences**

**10.** Which principle aims to ensure that similar technologies are subject to the same regulatory standards?
A. Technological supremacy
B. Technological neutrality
C. Technological singularity
D. Technological determinism
**Answer: B. Technological neutrality**

### Exercises

#### Exercise 9.1: Regulatory Frameworks and Standards

**[1](#ch09.html#ch09exe1_1).** Which Act in the European Union categorises AI systems based on risk levels?
A. General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)
B. European Cybersecurity Act
C. Artificial Intelligence Act
D. Digital Services Act

**[2](#ch09.html#ch09exe1_2).** Describe the role of the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) in the context of blockchain technology.

**[3](#ch09.html#ch09exe1_3).** What is the primary concern of Quantum Computing regulation, as discussed in the chapter?
A. Cost management
B. Speed of computation
C. Data security and national security
D. Energy consumption

**[4](#ch09.html#ch09exe1_4).** Identify the significant difference in AI regulation between the United States and the European Union.

**[5](#ch09.html#ch09exe1_5).** Discuss the impact of China's New Generation Artificial Intelligence Development Plan.

##### Answers

**[1](#ch09.html#ch09exe1_1a).** Answer: C. Artificial Intelligence Act

**[2](#ch09.html#ch09exe1_2a).** Answer: ISO, through ISO/TC 307, develops international standards covering various aspects of blockchain technology, such as terminology, governance, security, and privacy, ensuring uniformity and interoperability across different sectors and regions.

**[3](#ch09.html#ch09exe1_3a).** Answer: C. Data security and national security

**[4](#ch09.html#ch09exe1_4a).** Answer: The United States follows a sector-specific approach without a single federal body overseeing AI, focusing on fostering innovation, while the European Union adopts a comprehensive and precautionary approach, emphasising transparency, accountability, and users' rights under the proposed Artificial Intelligence Act.

**[5](#ch09.html#ch09exe1_5a).** Answer: The plan aims to position China as a global leader in AI by 2030, integrating AI policies with strategic goals for technological supremacy, focusing on enhancing state security and technological governance.
