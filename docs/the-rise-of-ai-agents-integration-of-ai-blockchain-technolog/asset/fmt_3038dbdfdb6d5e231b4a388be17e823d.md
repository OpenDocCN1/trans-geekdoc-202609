### Answers:

[1](#ch07.html#ch07exe1_1a). Post-Quantum Cryptography aims to develop cryptographic systems that are secure against an adversary with a quantum computer, using mathematical problems believed hard for quantum algorithms. Quantum Cryptography, like Quantum Key Distribution (QKD), uses principles of quantum mechanics to secure data transmission, ensuring any interception can be detected.

[2](#ch07.html#ch07exe1_2a). Lattice-based cryptography is significant because it is based on problems, such as the Shortest Vector Problem (SVP) and the Learning With Errors (LWE), that no efficient quantum or classical algorithms are known to solve, making it a robust choice for quantum-resistant cryptographic systems.

[3](#ch07.html#ch07exe1_3a). NTRU works by encrypting data using polynomials over a finite field, offering both speed and security against quantum attacks. Its operations include polynomial multiplication modulo, a fixed polynomial and an integer, capitalising on the hardness of finding short vectors in a lattice, which remains secure against quantum attacks.

[4](#ch07.html#ch07exe1_4a). Hash functions in hash-based cryptography convert data into an almost unique fixed-size string. They are crucial for maintaining integrity and security in cryptographic systems, offering pre-image and collision resistance, which are properties not directly threatened by quantum algorithms.

### Exercise 7.2: Practical Application and Implications

[1](#ch07.html#ch07exe2_1). How does the BB84 Quantum Key Distribution protocol ensure the security of communications?

[2](#ch07.html#ch07exe2_2). Discuss the potential vulnerabilities of the BB84 protocol to Photon Number Splitting (PNS) attacks and how decoy-state protocols help mitigate this risk.

[3](#ch07.html#ch07exe2_3). What are the practical challenges mentioned in the chapter that are associated with implementing quantum cryptography in real-world systems?

[4](#ch07.html#ch07exe2_4). Explain the potential of integrating quantum cryptographic methods with blockchain technologies. What benefits does this integration bring?

[5](#ch07.html#ch07exe2_5). Reflect on the broader implications of quantum-resilient technologies for global security and privacy.

### Answers:

[1](#ch07.html#ch07exe2_1a). BB84 uses properties of quantum mechanics, such as the no-cloning theorem and Heisenberg's uncertainty principle, to secure communications. It encodes information on quantum states or qubits sent over insecure channels, where any eavesdropping attempt would inevitably disturb the quantum states, thereby revealing the presence of an intruder.

[2](#ch07.html#ch07exe2_2a). The BB84 protocol is vulnerable to PNS attacks when weak coherent pulses emit multiple photons. Decoy-state protocols are used to insert random states with different photon intensities to detect and deter these types of eavesdropping attempts by causing noticeable discrepancies in photon statistics at the receiver's end.

[3](#ch07.html#ch07exe2_3a). Practical challenges include aligning and maintaining the polarisation states over long distances, reducing photon loss in optical fibers, and integrating quantum systems with existing classical network infrastructures, which require new technological solutions and standards.

[4](#ch07.html#ch07exe2_4a). Integrating quantum cryptography with blockchain can enhance the security of blockchain against not only classical but also quantum attacks. It can provide a robust method for achieving consensus and securing transactions, making blockchain more resilient to future technological advances.

[5](#ch07.html#ch07exe2_5a). The broader implications include ensuring long-term security for digital communications in various sectors, from financial services to governmental operations. It helps in safeguarding sensitive information against emerging quantum threats, thus maintaining confidentiality, integrity, and availability in a post-quantum world.
