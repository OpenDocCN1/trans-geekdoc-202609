
## 14.3.2 Time Series Model

(a) Testing the Stationarity of Time Series

Principles of stationarity are depicted in Figure 14.4 with respect to time-dependent mean, variance, and covariance.

By using the method of second differencing, non-stationary data is changed to stationary so that future predictions can be carried out. The existing techniques are Auto Regressive (AR), Moving Average (MA), and Auto Regressive Moving Average (ARMA) [1, 7–9].

(b) Autocorrelation Function

The degree of similarity between given time series data and a lagged version of itself over successive intervals is depicted in Figure 14.5, which is non-stationary in mean.

$$\eta_{k}=\frac{\sum_{t=k+1}^{n}\left(Y_{t}-\overline{Y}\right)\left(Y_{t-k}-\overline{Y}\right)}{\sum_{t=1}^{n}\left(Y_{t}-\overline{Y}\right)^{2}}$$

(c) Partial Autocorrelation Function (PACF)

The summary of the relationship between an observation in the current predicted values and the previous time steps, by removing the intervening observations, is displayed in Figure 14.6.

$$Y_{t}=b_{0}+b_{1} Y_{t-1}+b_{2} Y_{t-2}+\ldots+b_{k} Y_{t-k}\ k=1,2,\ldots$$

From the figures, it is observed that there are variations over time. It points out that the mean of the daily affected cases and deaths is not set and changes over time. These details reveal that daily affected cases are non-stationary in both mean and variance.

Figures 14.9 and 14.10 represent the ACF and PACF values against 16 time lags. The non-stationarity in mean can also be observed from Figures 14.9 and 14.10. It can be noted from the ACF plots that all the spikes are lying outside the confidence limits. The time lag at 1 is very large and lying outside the confidence limits. Figures 14.11 and 14.12 represent the ACF and PACF values against 16 time lags. The non-stationarity in mean can also be noted from Figures 14.11 and 14.12. It can be observed from the ACF plots that all the spikes are lying outside the confidence limits. From PACF, the time lags 1, 3, 5, 6, 8 are very large and lying outside the confidence limits. So it is necessary to transfer the non-stationary data to stationary data. Because Figures 14.9, 14.10, 14.11, and 14.12 confirm the non-stationarity in mean, the process of differencing is carried out in the data to eliminate the non-stationarity. To remove the non-stationarity in mean, the technique of second differencing is applied. After two times differencing is carried out, it can be observed from the following results that stationarity is maintained with respect to mean and variance.

The value of ACF and PACF of 16 lags are maximum, falling inside the confidence limits and also not so far from the central value.
