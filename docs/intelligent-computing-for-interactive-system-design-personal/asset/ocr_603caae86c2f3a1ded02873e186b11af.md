# 9.3.5 Q5: What Are the Privacy and Ethical Implications of Developing this Classifier?

One of the issues with developing these classifiers was the sheer volume of sensitive data we were asking for from our participants. While we had institutional review board (IRB) approval from our university's IRB, ultimately we were asking for our participants to trust us with their communication records.

There are also some serious implications to consider if the classifier ends up working well. Having this kind of contextual information about a person's relationships can be very valuable for certain types of social engineering attacks. Another issue is that, depending on how this classifier was used, it might be possible to be gamed. For example, if the classifier is used to automatically determine privacy and sharing preferences, an adversary could potentially communicate with a target in such a way that the classifier is fooled into thinking the adversary should get access to data that they should not.

Finally, there are potential ethical implications to knowing about an individual's social support, especially if they do not have much support. For example, if someone is socially isolated they might be at risk for depression or self-harm behaviors. Or they might just have multiple phones, and so the data we have access to is an incomplete picture of the user's social interaction. What responsibility does a developer or researcher have in this situation? Should they solicit help? Suggest that the user seek help? Simply ignore this information? While we were not planning to deploy this classifier, these concerns are real and complex.

# 9.3.6 Q6: A General Classifier or Personal Classifiers?

In the case of these classifiers, a personal classifier doesn't make much sense, because these (life facet and tie strength) are things that are not likely to change, and if they do they will probably change slowly over a long period of time. Thus, having a personalized classifier to tell me who my four closest people are makes no sense—I know who those people are, and once I provide that training data to the classifier the classifier does not need to classify those contacts again. The value of this classifier is in a user never having to tell the phone, but the phone automatically inferring this information, and thus a general classifier makes more sense here.
