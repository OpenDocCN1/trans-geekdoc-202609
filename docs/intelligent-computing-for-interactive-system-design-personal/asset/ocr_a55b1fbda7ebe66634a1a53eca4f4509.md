Two-dimensional capacitive array: While nearly all mobile devices use capacitive technology in their touchscreens, most processing of the capacitive input is done in the hardware. The most apparent capability of this sensor is detecting when a hand grasps a phone. Perhaps less obvious is that this sensor also detects the presence of a person's leg through the lining of a trouser pocket. As a result, we built a prototype capacitance-sensing device similar to that of Saponas et al. [2011]. Our device reports a raw capacitance signal from a 10 x 20 grid at roughly 100Hz. The device was designed in a form factor similar to that of today's mobile phones (see Figure 9.2, left).

Multi-spectral sensor: One way to detect where the phone is placed is to gain information about the material that the device is up against. Following as closely as we could to the approach taken by Harrison and Hudson [2008], we constructed a sensing device comprising red, green, blue, ultraviolet (UV), and infrared (IR) light-emitting diodes (LEDs), a photo resistor, and a TSL235R light-to-frequency converter (Figure 9.2, right). The device was controlled by an Arduino Pro Mini (Arduino LLC, Boston, MA, USA), logging readings from the two sensors to an onboard microSD card. While the current form of the device does not fully satisfy our criterion of not disturbing the user, we believe that a carefully engineered version of this sensor might be nearly unnoticeable. Like the capacitive sensor, we constructed this device to mirror the form factor of a phone.
