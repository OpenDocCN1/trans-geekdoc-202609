# 9.3.8.2 Tie Strength Classification

We trained the tie-strength models using the Weka Toolkit's [Hall et al. 2009] implementation of an SVM. We conducted a leave-one-participant-out cross-validation (each fold contained data from one participant). This prevents any anomalies within a particular participant's data from causing a performance overestimate. We trained nine models, varying two aspects of input data. First we varied what the model was classifying (as described in Q1 between a ternary classifier and two different binary classifiers (first column of Table 9.6). Second, similarly to the life facet models, we also varied the input data for the classifier (second column of Table 9.6).

Classification results vary considerably (Table 9.6), ranging from 46.28% (κ = 0.179), to 91.55% (κ = 0.361). The κ statistic measures the agreement between predicted and observed categorizations, correcting for agreement that occurs by chance. Table 9.6 reveals clear trends. First, within each of the class conditions, classifiers perform best for all, second best for contactlist and worst for somecomm. Most of the contacts who are not in the contact list (thus excluded from contactlist models) or who have no communication history (thus excluded from the somecomm models) are not strong ties, and thus are easier to classify. As a result, the models that include them perform better.
