These proofs allow participants to validate transactions efficiently without needing to access the entire block or the entire blockchain.

## CONSENSUS MECHANISMS IN BLOCKCHAIN

Consensus mechanisms are fundamental to blockchain technology as they enable participants in a decentralized network to agree on the state of the blockchain and validate transactions. Let's explore some key consensus mechanisms used in the blockchain:

- a) **Proof of Work (PoW):** PoW is the consensus mechanism that gained prominence with the introduction of Bitcoin. In a PoW-based blockchain, miners compete to solve complex mathematical puzzles to validate transactions and add new blocks to the blockchain. The first miner to solve the puzzle earns the right to add the block and is rewarded with cryptocurrency. PoW ensures the security and immutability of the blockchain through its requirement of significant computational power and the consensus of the majority of participants.
- b) **Proof of Stake (PoS):** PoS is an alternative consensus mechanism that offers a more energy-efficient and more environmentally friendly approach compared to PoW. In PoS-based blockchains, participants, known as validators, are chosen to create new blocks and validate transactions based on the number of coins they hold or are willing to "stake" as collateral. The probability of being chosen as a validator is proportional to the stake one holds. PoS provides security and consensus through economic incentives and reduces the reliance on computational power.
- c) **Delegated Proof of Stake (DPoS):** DPoS is a variation of PoS that further enhances scalability and speed. In DPoS-based blockchains, participants can delegate their voting power to a smaller group of trusted nodes, called "delegates" or "witnesses." These delegates are responsible for block production and transaction validation. DPoS offers fast block confirmation times and efficient block production by utilizing a selected group of delegates, making it suitable for high-performance blockchains.
- d) **Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT):** PBFT is a consensus algorithm commonly used in permissioned blockchain networks. Unlike PoW and PoS, PBFT requires a predetermined set of trusted nodes, known as "validators," to reach an agreement on the order of transactions. PBFT achieves consensus through multiple rounds of voting and ensures finality, meaning that once a block is confirmed, it cannot be altered. PBFT offers high transaction throughput and is often used in enterprise blockchain solutions.

### TYPES OF BLOCKCHAIN NETWORKS

Blockchain technology encompasses a diverse range of networks, each with its characteristics and applications. In this chapter, we will delve into the intricacies of various types of blockchain networks, including public, private, consortium, permissioned, permissionless, hybrid, and federated blockchains. By examining these types in detail, we can gain a comprehensive understanding of their strengths, weaknesses, and real-world use cases.

### PUBLIC, PRIVATE, AND CONSORTIUM BLOCKCHAINS

- a) **Public Blockchains:** Public blockchains are open networks that allow anyone to participate in and validate transactions. These networks operate in a decentralized manner, with a distributed network of nodes collectively maintaining the blockchain's integrity. Public blockchains, such as Bitcoin and Ethereum, are known for their transparency, immutability, and resistance to censorship. They rely on consensus mechanisms like Proof of Work (PoW) or Proof of Stake (PoS) to secure the network and validate transactions. Public blockchains are widely used for applications like cryptocurrencies, decentralized applications (dApps), and decentralized finance (DeFi).
- b) **Private Blockchains:** Private blockchains, also referred to as permissioned blockchains, are restricted networks where participation is limited to specific entities. These entities, often pre-approved participants, possess the authority to validate transactions and access the blockchain. Unlike public blockchains, private blockchains prioritize privacy and confidentiality, making them suitable for industries where data access control is critical, such as healthcare, supply chain management, and financial institutions. Private blockchains may not rely on native cryptocurrencies and can employ consensus mechanisms like Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT) or variants of Proof of Stake (PoS) to achieve consensus among participants.
- c) **Consortium Blockchains:** Consortium blockchains are a hybrid model that combines aspects of both public and private blockchains. In a consortium blockchain, a predefined group of organizations or entities forms a consortium to jointly operate and govern the network. Consortium blockchains offer shared infrastructure where multiple participants collaborate to maintain the blockchain. This type of blockchain enables industries and sectors with multiple stakeholders to work together while maintaining a certain level of control and privacy. Applications of consortium blockchains include trade finance, interbank transactions, and supply chain networks.

## PERMISSIONED VS. PERMISSIONLESS BLOCKCHAINS

- a) **Permissioned Blockchains:** Permissioned blockchains restrict network participation to authorized entities or nodes. These entities are granted explicit permission to validate transactions, propose new blocks, and participate in the consensus process. Permissioned blockchains prioritize regulatory compliance, controlled governance, and data privacy. They often employ consensus mechanisms that are more efficient than the resource-intensive PoW, such as PBFT or PoS variants. Permissioned blockchains are well-suited for enterprise settings, where compliance requirements and controlled access to data are paramount.
- b) **Permissionless Blockchains:** Permissionless blockchains, also known as public blockchains, allow anyone to participate in the network without requiring explicit permission. These blockchains operate based on open protocols, and their consensus mechanisms, typically PoW or PoS, enable participants to validate transactions and secure the network. Permissionless blockchains offer transparency, openness, and resistance to censorship. However, they may face scalability challenges due to the resource requirements of their consensus mechanisms. Permissionless blockchains find applications in areas where decentralized participation, trustlessness, and openness are valued.
