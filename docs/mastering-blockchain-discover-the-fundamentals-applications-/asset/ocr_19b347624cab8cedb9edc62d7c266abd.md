+   a) **Cryptographic Techniques**: Blockchain relies on cryptographic techniques to secure and authenticate transactions. Cryptography ensures that transactions and data remain confidential, integrity is maintained, and participants can prove ownership of assets. For example, cryptographic hash functions help ensure the integrity of data stored in blocks, while digital signatures provide proof of authenticity and ownership.
+   
+   b) **Consensus Mechanisms**: Consensus mechanisms enable participants in a blockchain network to agree on the validity of transactions and the order in which they are added to the blockchain. Different consensus mechanisms, such as Proof of Work (PoW), Proof of Stake (PoS), and Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT), ensure that all participants reach a consensus without relying on a central authority. These mechanisms incentivize honest behavior, prevent malicious actions, and maintain the integrity of the blockchain.
+   
+   c) **Distributed Ledger Technology**: Blockchain utilizes a distributed ledger, which is a replicated and synchronized database maintained by multiple participants in the network. Each participant holds a copy of the blockchain, ensuring redundancy and making it difficult for any single participant to manipulate the data.
