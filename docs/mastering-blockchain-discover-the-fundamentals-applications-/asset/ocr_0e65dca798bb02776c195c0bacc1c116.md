b) **Decentralized Systems:** In contrast, decentralized systems distribute power, control, and decision-making among multiple participants. Each participant, also known as a node or peer, has an equal role in maintaining the system's integrity and consensus. Decentralization aims to remove the need for a central authority and create a more democratic and resilient system. In a decentralized system, no single entity holds ultimate control. Instead, participants collectively validate and verify transactions through a consensus mechanism. Examples of decentralized systems include peer-to-peer file sharing networks, decentralized cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin, and blockchain-based applications. Decentralized systems offer several advantages. They eliminate the reliance on a single point of failure, making the system more robust and resistant to attacks. They also enhance transparency, as participants can independently verify transactions and maintain a transparent record of all activities. Additionally, decentralized systems empower individuals by giving them more control over their data and transactions.
