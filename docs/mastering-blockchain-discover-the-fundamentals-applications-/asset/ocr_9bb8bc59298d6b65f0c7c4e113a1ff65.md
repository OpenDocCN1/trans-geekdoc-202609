+   - **Distributed Ledger:** The ledger is a digital record that stores all the transactions that have occurred on the blockchain network. It is maintained and updated by the network's participants, ensuring transparency and integrity.

Blockchain operates on a peer-to-peer network, where participants work together to validate and approve transactions through a consensus mechanism. This eliminates the need for a central authority or intermediary, making transactions more efficient, secure, and resistant to censorship.

# BRIEF HISTORY OF BLOCKCHAIN

The concept of blockchain-like systems can be traced back to the 1990s with the development of digital timestamps and cryptographic systems. However, the real breakthrough came with the introduction of Bitcoin in 2008 by Satoshi Nakamoto. Bitcoin was the first successful implementation of a blockchain-based digital currency. It gained attention for its ability to enable secure and decentralized transactions without the need for a central authority.

Following Bitcoin's success, blockchain technology continued to evolve and diversify. In 2015, Ethereum introduced the concept of smart contracts, enabling the creation of decentralized applications (DApps) on the blockchain. This expanded the possibilities beyond digital currencies, showcasing the potential of blockchain for various industries and use cases.
