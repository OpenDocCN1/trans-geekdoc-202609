The digital signature is generated by applying a mathematical function to the transaction data and the private key. This process produces a unique string of characters that serve as cryptographic proof that the transaction or data has been authorized by the owner of the private key.

b) **Verification with Public Key:** The digital signature, along with the transaction or data, is shared with the network. Any participant can use the sender's public key to verify the signature. By decrypting the signature using the public key, they can confirm that the signature matches the original data, ensuring that the transaction or data has not been tampered with.

Digital signatures provide several benefits in the blockchain context. They ensure the integrity of transactions and data, as any alteration to the transaction or data would result in an invalid signature. Additionally, digital signatures enable non repudiation, meaning that the sender cannot deny their involvement in the transaction or the authenticity of the data once it has been signed.
