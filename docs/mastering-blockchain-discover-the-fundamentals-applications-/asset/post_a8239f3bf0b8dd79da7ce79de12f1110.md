## KEYWORDS IN BLOCKCHAIN

- **Blockchain**: A decentralized and transparent ledger that securely records and verifies transactions.
- **Cryptocurrency**: Digital or virtual currencies that utilize cryptography for secure transactions.
- **Smart Contracts**: Self-executing contracts with predefined rules and conditions encoded on the blockchain.
- **Decentralization**: The distribution of authority and decision-making power across a network.
- **Consensus**: The mechanism by which participants in a blockchain network agree on the validity of transactions.
- **Distributed Ledger**: A synchronized and shared record of transactions maintained across multiple nodes.
- **Immutable**: Unable to be changed or tampered with, ensuring the integrity of data on the blockchain.
- **Mining**: The process of validating and adding new transactions to the blockchain through computational effort.
- **Wallet**: Software or hardware used to store and manage digital assets securely.
- **Privacy**: Techniques and mechanisms to protect the confidentiality of transactions and user identities.
- **Scalability**: The ability of a blockchain network to handle increasing transaction volumes without compromising performance.
- **Tokenization**: Representing real-world assets or rights as digital tokens on the blockchain.
- **Interoperability**: The ability of different blockchain networks to communicate and share data seamlessly.
- **Governance**: The framework and processes for making decisions and managing the operation of a blockchain network.
- **Security**: Measures and protocols to protect blockchain networks from unauthorized access and attacks.
- **Transparency**: The visibility of transactions and data on the blockchain, providing trust and accountability.
- **Peer-to-Peer**: Direct interaction and exchange of information or value between network participants.
- **Fork**: A split or divergence in a blockchain network, resulting in two separate chains with different rules.
- **Digital Identity**: The use of blockchain to establish and verify the identity of individuals or entities.
- **Supply Chain Management**: Using blockchain to track and trace the movement of goods and ensure transparency.
- **Internet of Things (IoT)**: Integrating blockchain with IoT devices to enable secure and decentralized data sharing.
- **Data Integrity**: Ensuring that data stored on the blockchain remains unchanged and tamper-proof.
- **Auditability**: The ability to trace and verify transactions on the blockchain for auditing purposes.
- **Peer-to-Peer Lending**: Facilitating direct lending between individuals through blockchain-based platforms.
- **Cross-Border Payments**: Streamlining international payments by leveraging blockchain's speed and efficiency.
- **Asset Tokenization**: Representing ownership of real-world assets, such as real estate or artwork, as digital tokens.
- **Digital Voting**: Implementing secure and transparent voting systems using blockchain technology.
- **Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs)**: Unique digital assets that represent ownership of a specific item or piece of content.
- **Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC)**: Digital forms of national currencies issued by central banks.
- **Decentralized Finance (DeFi)**: Applications and platforms that provide financial services using smart contracts and blockchain technology.
- **Consensus Algorithm**: The algorithm or protocol used to achieve agreement among network participants.
- **Zero-Knowledge Proof**: A cryptographic method that allows for the verification of information without revealing the actual data.
- **Sidechain**: A separate blockchain that is interoperable with the main blockchain, enabling scalability and specific use cases.
- **Hybrid Blockchain**: A combination of public and private blockchains, offering the benefits of both models.
- **Proof of Stake (PoS)**: A consensus mechanism where the probability of validating blocks is proportional to the amount of cryptocurrency held.
