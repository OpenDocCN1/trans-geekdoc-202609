Sensor platforms are required for early and accurate diagnosis of CVD. There are various sensor platforms based on the optical and mechanical properties of the bio- sensing materials. Electrochemical-based detection techniques are preferred due to their high sensitivity, reduced cost, and faster detection; electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) has exhibited excellent results in detecting low concentrations of many clinically important biomarkers used to diagnose CVDs. The application of bio- markers that can be recognized in blood has attracted many researchers’ interest recently in this area [17].

The vascular wall discharges molecules into the blood, gives necessary information about the relevant pathological processes happening in the human system. The concen- trations of the released molecules provide potential information of different pathological processes and can act as biomarkers.

An ideal cardiac biomarker has some characteristic features, such as strong clinical sensitivity, which are very specific in marking the target molecules. It is quickly released in the blood, leading to fast diagnosis, maintains a high elevation level for a longer dura- tion in the blood, and is able to assay quantitatively.

A wide range of potential biomarkers need simultaneous analysis for the correct diag- nosis of CVD [18–20]. One research study reported a set of 177 plasma markers for the detection of CVDs [20].

The quantification of biomarkers is required to detect CVD quickly and accurately to plan a proper strategy for the care of cardiac patients. Quick and reliable detection of these biomarkers helps physicians to distinguish between diseases that show similar symptoms. As the clinically significant sensing ranges of some important biomarkers have very low concentrations, they need highly sensitive assay methods.

# ### 10.3.1 Optical Biosensors

Optical biosensors are very sensitive and efficient biosensors. They detect biomarkers by observing the variation in frequency, phase, amplitude, and state of polarization of the input light responding to the biorecognition processes. Optical biosensors make use of material properties for detection of CVD, such as colorimetry, fluorescence, luminescence, fibre optics, etc. [21]. ELISA (enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay) based biomarkers use optical properties of chromogenic reporters (Figure 10.3). These biomarkers give signals which are colorimetric, fluorogenic, or luminescent in nature. ELISA has been extensively used for cardiac marker detection [22–25]. Research con- tinues into further miniaturization of the sensor platform without compromising on detection ability.
