In the study of Anand et al. (2012), an algorithm was developed to predict impending acute HF decompensation [178]. A total of 543 patients were monitored over three months using a device with multiple sensors measuring heart rate, respiratory rate, body impedance, and physical activity. It is reported that the algorithm predicted HF events with 63% sensitivity, 92% specificity, and a false positive rate of 0.9 per patient-year.

# 5.4 Discussion

HF is a chronic cardiovascular disease which results in frequent hospitalizations, increasing the economic burden in healthcare systems and negatively affecting the hospital workload. Many rehospitalizations are unnecessary or can be avoided by employing remote monitoring of HF patients.

Hemodynamic effects of HF decompensation begin about 30 days before hospitalization [185]. In current practice, in-hospital invasive techniques are applied for assessing the HF state by measuring ventricular pressure levels. However, filling ventricle pressures can be determined using a catheter and cannot be applied frequently because of their invasive nature. An accurate and low-cost non-invasive modality is an important requirement for the recognition of HF decompensation. The signs of HF decompensation can be sensed in the form of intrathoracic impedance alterations measured by non-invasive tools up to 15 days before hospitalization. Systematic use of non-invasive techniques allows remote monitoring of the patient's condition. With the development in sensor technologies and data science, lightweight and wearable sensors can be designed and the measurements taken can be remotely monitored by clinicians.

The exact role and effectiveness of remote monitoring remains unclear [3, 24, 182]. Although several RPM studies have reported reduced mortality rates and HF-related hospitalizations, large-scale trials tend to give neutral results for mortality and hospitalizations [19]. In future, it is necessary to elucidate the effect of remote monitoring modalities for HF prediction on large-scale RCTs. We have seen that using only one monitoring parameter is not sufficient to reduce hospitalization and mortality rates, and multiparameter methods with optimized algorithms are recommended for the increased benefit and for improving the life quality of patients [22].

The use of weight scales is common among RPM trials, but the increase in weight provides an imprecise estimation because weight can change due to many factors other than HF decompensation [186]. A relative increase in thoracic bioimpedance provides better prediction of HF-related congestion [3]. ECG is among the under-investigated techniques for HF remote monitoring [182].
