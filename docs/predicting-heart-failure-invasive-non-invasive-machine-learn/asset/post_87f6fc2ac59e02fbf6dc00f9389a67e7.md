
## 10 Clinical Applications of Artificial Intelligence in Early and Accurate Detection of Low-Concentration CVD Biomarkers

Meena Laad, Sajna M.S, Kishor Kumar Sadasivuni, and Sadiya Waseem

### 10.1 Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are a mix of several types of disorders affecting the heart and blood vessels (Figure 10.1). Mainly, a plaque is formed in the artery walls and narrows the arteries, giving rise to a severe condition called atherosclerosis. This narrowing of arteries obstructs the blood flow and makes the free flow of blood difficult, leading to a heart attack or stroke. CVDs are one of the leading causes of death globally and a major obstacle to sustainable human growth and wellbeing [1]. A study reported that the occurrence of the majority of CVDs was observed in industrialized countries having high incomes [2]. But in the last few decades, the absolute burden of CVDs is seen more in developing countries and is expected to grow more in the twenty-first century [2, 3]. To assess the CVD associated risk factors, several health examination surveys have been conducted in some of the countries with low and middle income [4]. A systematic evaluation of data collected through various resources can reduce the CVD mortality rate to a great extent.

### 10.2 Risk Assessment and Early Detection of CVDs Using Biosensors

Traditionally, CVD prevention begins with identifying potential risk factors. Treatment cost and healthcare expenditures are escalating; therefore, there is a crucial requirement for early detection followed by proper treatment of asymptomatic CVD [5]. Also, many individuals indicating low-risk levels have shown early evidence of CVD. There is an important need to understand the factors responsible for the initiation, associated symptoms, and early and accurate detection of CVD, thereby reducing the high risk of unexpected death. The early and fast diagnosis of CVDs is essential not only to save patient's life but also to save huge hospitalization costs and time in successful prognosis of the disease.

Present CVD detection methods rely greatly on conducting laboratory tests and take several hours or sometimes days to produce the results.

According to the World Health Organization, the criteria for diagnosing CVD are that patients should show characteristic conditions such as severe pain in the chest, changes in diagnostic ECG, and elevated levels of biomarkers in blood sample [6].

A biosensor is considered as a very effective analytical tool in medical diagnostics for rapid detection of CVD disease at an early stage. Biosensors have revolutionized CVD diagnosis and prognosis. A biosensing device is made to detect and measure specific target molecules. The underlying principle used in the design of biosensors is to immobilize a biological receptor material on an appropriate transducer’s surface, which further transforms this biochemical signal into measurable electronic signals such as optical, piezoelectric, electrochemical, magnetic, or acoustic signals [7–11].

A variety of CVD detection sensors is available, which are very accurate, quick in detection, easy to carry, and don’t cause any damage to the environment. Recent research studies in microfluidics and advanced nanomaterials have led to the development of more efficient diagnostics requiring little analysis time. They incorporate several clinical assays into a compact, single portable unit. The nanotechnology industry is rapidly growing and creating highly efficient tools based on multidisciplinary research involving nanofluids, drug delivery, microelectronics, and analytical chemistry [12–16].

Current CVD diagnostic techniques require blood tests to assess relevant biomarker levels. A biomarker is measured on a biosample such as a blood, urine, or tissue test. It can also be the data recording obtained from a person’s blood pressure, ECG, etc., and includes imaging tests like an echocardiogram or CT scan.

Different imaging techniques, such as electrocardiograms, computed tomography (CT), and angiograms, are also used for CVD detection. These clinical methods provide vital information about cardiovascular health, but they consume periods of time. They also require sample preparation and depend on the skill and experience of the physician to interpret the results correctly, which delays the treatment. This long time period during diagnosis sometimes results in the deterioration of the condition of the patient.

Sensor platforms are required for early and accurate diagnosis of CVD. There are various sensor platforms based on the optical and mechanical properties of the biosensing materials. Electrochemical-based detection techniques are preferred due to their high sensitivity, reduced cost, and faster detection; electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) has exhibited excellent results in detecting low concentrations of many clinically important biomarkers used to diagnose CVDs. The application of biomarkers that can be recognized in blood has attracted many researchers’ interest recently in this area [17].

The vascular wall discharges molecules into the blood, gives necessary information about the relevant pathological processes happening in the human system. The concentrations of the released molecules provide potential information of different pathological processes and can act as biomarkers.

An ideal cardiac biomarker has some characteristic features, such as strong clinical sensitivity, which are very specific in marking the target molecules. It is quickly released in the blood, leading to fast diagnosis, maintains a high elevation level for a longer duration in the blood, and is able to assay quantitatively.

A wide range of potential biomarkers need simultaneous analysis for the correct diagnosis of CVD [18–20]. One research study reported a set of 177 plasma markers for the detection of CVDs [20].

The quantification of biomarkers is required to detect CVD quickly and accurately to plan a proper strategy for the care of cardiac patients. Quick and reliable detection of these biomarkers helps physicians to distinguish between diseases that show similar symptoms. As the clinically significant sensing ranges of some important biomarkers have very low concentrations, they need highly sensitive assay methods.

### 10.3 Different Types of Cardiac Biosensors

There are various types of biosensor, as shown in Figure 10.2.

#### 10.3.1 Optical Biosensors

Optical biosensors are very sensitive and efficient biosensors. They detect biomarkers by observing the variation in frequency, phase, amplitude, and state of polarization of the input light responding to the biorecognition processes. Optical biosensors make use of material properties for detection of CVD, such as colorimetry, fluorescence, luminescence, fibre optics, etc. [21]. ELISA (enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay) based biomarkers use optical properties of chromogenic reporters (Figure 10.3). These biomarkers give signals which are colorimetric, fluorogenic, or luminescent in nature. ELISA has been extensively used for cardiac marker detection [22–25]. Research continues into further miniaturization of the sensor platform without compromising on detection ability.

![](img/721c7a2c71f4dc63a8585e2ef46838f7_244_0.png)

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) is an effective and widely used optical transduction sensing technique used in most of the commercial optical biosensors. These biosensors work on special electromagnetic waves that are surface plasmon and identify variation in the refractive index (RI) on the surface of metal [20]. Surface plasmons are surface electromagnetic waves that propagate in a direction parallel to the metal interface.

#### 10.3.2 Electrochemical Biosensors

In electrochemical biosensors, an immobilized detecting probe holds together the target molecules selectively. The detection of target molecules is based on observing the variations in current or voltage at a local surface. The potentiometric (Figure 10.4), amperometric, and impedimetric transducers used in these biosensors convert vital chemical information into a measurable amperometric signal. These biosensors are low cost and easy to manufacture [27].
