# 11.2.2.1.1 Electrocardiography-Based Monitoring Systems

There are many types of external electrocardiography-based systems available, one of them being the Holter monitor. Holter monitors are small, wearable electrocardiography devices that contain multiple electrodes and data recording devices. A general diagram of a Holter monitor is given in Figure 11.1.

The purpose of a Holter monitor is to record the ECGs of an individual over a period of time (24–72 hours). Atrial fibrillation [19] is a type of arrhythmia predominantly found in patients with heart failure. The recordings from Holter monitors enable doctors or physicians to detect arrhythmias, which helps in the early diagnosis of heart failure. In certain cases, resting ECG conducted in clinics fails to detect heart rhythm abnormalities in patients experiencing early symptoms of heart disease. In this scenario, the physician or cardiologist might suggest using a Holter monitor for 24–48 hours. Furthermore, patients with a history of heart disease or recovered from heart-related surgeries are also advised to use Holter monitors for a couple of days. There is very little risk associated with wearing a Holter monitors, the only possible risks being associated with having electrodes attached to the skin, as the attached electrodes can often cause skin irritations. However, the Holter monitor is not water-resistant and patients are advised to not bathe or swim while the device is attached to their body for recording the physiological values. Moreover, the patients are advised to stay outside the proximity of metal detectors, microwaves, or other devices that can break off the signals from the electrodes attached to the body. After fitting a Holter monitor on a patient’s body, the cardiologist provides the patient with a diary to record their activities and symptoms.
