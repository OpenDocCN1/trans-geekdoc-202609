# 11.3.2 PA Pressure Sensors

Unlike the RV pressure monitoring sensor (the Chronicle IHM), PA pressure sensors measure the diastolic PA pressure directly. The CardioMEMS (Cardio-Microelectromechanical) HF System developed by Abbott, St. Sylmar, CA, is a PA pressure sensor that is placed in any branch of the pulmonary artery. This sensor measures the diastolic, systolic, and mean pulmonary artery pressures every day. The collected data is then transferred over a secure network. The increase in the PA pressure is an indication of the onset of the decompensation and happens in advance of other clinical symptoms. The sensor is placed on the femoral vein during right heart catheterization and is powered externally. Unlike Chronicle IHM, CardioMEMS is MRI- and CRT-compatible with increased longevity.

The CHAMPIONS (CardioMEMS Heart Sensor Allows Monitoring of Pressure to Improve Outcomes in NYHA Class III Heart Failure Patients) trial was conducted in the US in 2011 to study the CardioMEMS sensor [57, 58]. The initial study included 550 patients who were NYHA class III, had had previous hospitalization, and had guideline-recommended HF therapy. The study group was divided into control and treatment groups. The pressure information is only available for the treatment group and not for the control group. Two studies – MEMS-HF (CardioMEMS European Monitoring Study for Heart Failure) and the US Post-Approval Study (PAS) – confirmed that CardioMEMS reduced the number of hospitalizations and improved the quality of life of the patients. CardioMEMS is the only FDA-approved invasive sensor for monitoring PA pressure with a European Conformity (CE) mark.

Yet another PA pressure sensor, the Cordella Pulmonary Artery Pressure Sensor System, has been developed by Endotronix, Inc., Chicago. Cordella is like CardioMEMS with additional benefits provided by the Corella HF system. Some vital parameters, such as BP, oxygen saturation, weight, and heart rate are recorded in addition to the diastolic PA pressure. The studies are ongoing, with two clinical studies currently being carried out. The first is the SIRONA trial being conducted on 15 NYHA class III patients [59]. The trial did not show any complications or system-related failures. The values measured by the PAP sensors matched with the right heart catheterization at the end of the 90-day trial period. The second study, the European SIRONA II CE Mark Trial was conducted on 60 patients from the NYHA class II with compromised ventricle ejection. The PROACTIVE-HF IDE Trial is a similar trial being conducted in the US. Cordella is not FDA approved, does not possess the European Conformity mark, and is not available for commercial use.
