# 3.6 Conclusion

Coronary and peripheral artery disease, congenital heart disease, heart failure, hypertension, and stroke are responsible for an extremely high mortality rate. CVDs are known as the most common disease in the world. The use of cardiac biomarkers is one of the best tools for early diagnosis of the disease, monitoring the disease stage, determining the treatment protocol, and so on. Early-stage biomarkers of CVDs can potentially save many lives and help to fight against CVDs in clinical trials. Troponin I, CRP, and lipoproteins (LDH, HDL) are evaluated traditional biomarkers, and these types of biomarker biosensors have developed extensively in the last 10 years.

Biosensors are obtained from a combination of biological affinity material and transducer. The analyte is recognized from the bioreceptor, analyzed with a transducer, and then evaluated after signal processing. In this chapter, cardiovascular biosensors have been evaluated under five different subtitles. Electrochemical biosensors are widely used for the determination of cardiac biomarkers. The advantages and challenges of biosensors are evaluated within the framework of short- and mid-term related healthcare applications.

The many current biomarkers are used clinically for the detection of last-stage disease. The discovery of new potential biomarkers for CVDs along with current ones has increased the importance of fully validated cardiac biomarker biosensors and has led to new approaches for CVDs-based research.
