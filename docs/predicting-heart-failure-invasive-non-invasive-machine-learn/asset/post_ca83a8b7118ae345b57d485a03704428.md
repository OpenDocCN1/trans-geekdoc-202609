## 4.6 Conclusion

The method of charging chips implanted inside the human body were simulated for radio frequency energy and verified experimentally in this work. Ambient RF harvesting is clean and controllable, and the implanted slices operate independently of patient movement, anytime, anywhere. Conversely, inductive radio transmission produces heat at the implant position, interfering with the body's physiological activity.

In this chapter, we examined how an implanted SoC chip can be charged wirelessly. We demonstrated the possibility of using inductive flux and RF harvesting signals to activate any microchips implanted inside the body. We tested this research, and we prove that the S-band (WiFi) is a suitable power for charging the SoC chip. The fractal harvester antenna has a reflection coefficient of S11 of –16.23 dB. Therefore, the maximum gain direction of this antenna is –7.06 dBm. We chose Schottky diode HSMS2822 and fractal combiner antenna dimensions tested and approved for the full-wave bridge rectifier topology.

Each harvester circuit can supply 1.92 V, and this energy is sufficient to operate the sensor implanted under the skin wirelessly. In this study, we agreed that we could wirelessly improve the configuration of implant chips and can unlock a new solution for using radiation in our lives as a source of energy to activate sensors implanted inside a human. This technology is essential for many applications both in civilian life and in military sectors.
