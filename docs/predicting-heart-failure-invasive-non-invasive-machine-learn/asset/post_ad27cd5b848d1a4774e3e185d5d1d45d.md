## 11.2.2 BP and Heart Failure

Hypertension is one of the main causes of cardiovascular disease and its associated risks, including death. Many previous research studies have shown that hypertension will worsen the condition of heart failure patients and it is essential to maintain an optimum BP value [21]. In this context, the daily BP measurement of an individual diagnosed with heart failure is required. Frequent BP readings and reporting them to the doctor help to reduce the impact and risk of hypertension in patients with heart failure. Many home-based and clinical-based BP sensing devices are currently available. Most of the home-based BP sensing devices are small in size, portable, and affordable. An individual with minimal technical knowledge can operate these devices to measure BP. However, the accuracy of home-based BP systems is still a concern. A cross-sectional study was conducted by Ruzicka et al. [22], analyzing the accuracy of home BP devices. The result of the study suggests that home BP monitoring can aid in the early detection of hypertension. But the inaccurate readings of several of the available devices can adversely affect hypertension management and cause risks related to hypertension.

BP can be controlled naturally in many ways. Overweight is one of the main causes of raised BP. Reducing body weight lowers BP, as can increased activity and daily exercise. Increased activity and daily exercise will increase the heart rate and breathing rates which make the heart stronger and thereby less effort is required by the heart to pump. Proper diet can have a big impact on lowering BP. Controlling sodium intake can lower BP in healthy individuals, while increased potassium intake can reduce the effect of sodium in the body and lowers BP. Individuals with high BP are advised to have potassium-rich foods like low-fat dairy products, fish, fruits, and vegetables such as avocado, orange, banana, tomato, greens, etc. Smoking can cause sudden fluctuations in BP and continuous use of tobacco products can cause damage to the lining of artery walls and increase BP. Stopping smoking can reduce BP as well as increasing overall health. Excess stress control and restful sleep for at least 8 hours per day can also lower BP to an extent.

## 11.2.3 Body Weight Monitoring and Heart Failure

Body weight is considered to be one of the factors for predicting whether a heart failure condition is getting worse or not. It is based on the fact that heart failure leads to the accumulation of fluid (edema) in the body followed by rapid weight gain [23]. The rapid weight gain due to edema can lead to urgent hospitalization and, in the worst case, to death. Usually, diuretic therapy is recommended for hospitalized heart failure patients due to fluid retention and rapid weight gain [24]. The Heart Failure Society of America advises heart failure patients to monitor their body weight daily and report to the doctor if any abnormality is found [25]. The body weight measurement tool is an invasive device that can be used to measure fluctuations in body weight. Advanced body weight measurement tools are equipped with innovative features to aid body weight data collection and the ability to share the data across multiple mobile devices.

Even though edema is a symptom of heart failure, monitoring body weight alone will not aid in managing heart failure patients. A research study conducted in 2009 by Zhang et al. [23] examined the effect of body weight alone in monitoring heart failure patients. The study focused on analyzing how well weight gain algorithms can predict hospitalization in patients with worsening heart failure conditions. Three algorithms were compared in the study, with two of the algorithms being very simple and triggering an alert when the weight gain was, respectively, over 1.4 kg or 2.2 kg in a day. The third algorithm analysed the short-term weight gain average and long-term weight gain average to trigger the alert. The 168 patients who participated in the study were advised to measure their body weight twice a day: before breakfast and before the evening meal, after emptying the bladder, while wearing light clothes and no shoes. Among the analyzed algorithms, the third one, based on the average of short-term weight and long-term weight, displayed better performance. In that context, body weight alone is not beneficial for telemonitoring heart failure patients. However, integrating body weight along with other physiological readings, such as ECG or heart rate, can improve the precision of the telemonitoring systems.

Body mass index (BMI) is one of the crucial indirect indicators for heart disease. BMI and BP values are closely related. Higher BMI values are demonstrated as an independent risk factor for hypertension, and hypertension is one of the important risk factors for heart diseases. The relation between BMI value ranges and their associated disease risks for adults older than 20 years is given in Table 11.1.

Table 11.1 BMI value ranges and associated health risks.

| BMI Range | Category | Associated Health Risks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| < 18.5 | Underweight | Osteoporosis, anemia, skin, hair problems |
| 18.5–24.9 | Normal weight | Low risk for heart disease (but there is a higher risk if there is a bad cholesterol level and triglyceride levels are high), diabetes |
| 25–29.9 | Overweight | High risk for heart disease, hypertension, diabetes |
| > 30 | Obese | Very high risk for heart disease, hypertension, diabetes, kidney disease |

It is clear from Table 11.1 that individuals falling into the overweight and obese categories have a high risk for heart diseases. However, individuals with normal weight categories also have a risk for heart disease if their cholesterol levels are not normal. Moreover, the risk of heart disease depends not only on BMI, but also on other factors such as lifestyle, smoking, sleeping hours, etc.

## 11.2.4 Bioimpedance Monitoring and Heart Failure

Bioimpedance analysis is a leading and economically feasible method utilized for estimating body composition as well as health monitoring. Bioimpedance analysis estimates the total body fluid and aids in monitoring the fluid levels in the human body. Heart failure patients can benefit from the application of bioimpedance analysis, which is based on the electrical properties of the human body. It measures how well the body resists the flow of electrical signals when a small electric current is passed through it. The fats in the body have a high resistance against an electric current, while the water has low resistance. There are multiple types of bioimpedance analysis systems available, including systems which are focused on estimating the body composition of the lower body alone (leg-to-leg bioimpedance analysis), upper body alone (hand-held bioimpedance analysis), and the whole body (hand-to-foot bioimpedance analysis).
