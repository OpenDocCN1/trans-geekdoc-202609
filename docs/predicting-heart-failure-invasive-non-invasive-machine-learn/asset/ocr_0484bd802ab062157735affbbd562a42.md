# 11.1 Introduction

According to statistics provided in Roth et al. [1], the total number of cardiovascular cases has doubled, and death-related cardiovascular diseases have increased by 50% globally in the last three decades. Heart failure is a chronic condition where the heart fails to pump sufficient blood and oxygen for the body. A report from the American Heart Association reveals that one in five people is prone to heart failure and 50% of these patients will live a maximum of 5 years after experiencing heart failure [2]. The different risks which can lead to heart failures are high systolic blood pressure (hypertension), high fasting plasma glucose, high low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, high body mass index (BMI), impaired kidney function, ambient and household air pollution, tobacco, dietary risks, low physical activity, sedentary behavior, alcohol, chronic stress, adverse childhood experiences, and excess body/abdominal fat (obesity) [1, 3]. Age, ethnicity, family history/genetics, and gender are some of the non-modifiable factors leading to heart failure [3]. Fatigue, breathlessness, elevated jugular venous pressure, peripheral edema, etc. are the common symptoms of heart failure. Chronic heart failures (CHFs) have become a burden to current healthcare systems, mainly because of the increased hospital admissions. Not only do the admissions themselves increase for acute diseases, but frequent visits to the outpatient clinic are also required to monitor the status of the patient. Furthermore, during the current pandemic, in-person monitoring and frequent hospital visits are not possible, as they are highly risky. Frequent testing and analysis of physiological values, as well as the electronic health records of individuals, aid in assessing the condition of heart failure patients. These health records include various readings such as heartbeats per minute, the fluid level in the lungs, the oxygen level in the blood, ejection fraction levels, etc.

Current technological advancements in electronic health record data acquirement and management allow the recording of laboratory testing reports, medical imaging data, and personal health information into an electronic format with the help of multiple sensors. These recorded health data can be further processed to predict and monitoring of various conditions and stages of heart failure using mathematical models, data mining techniques, and machine learning methods.
