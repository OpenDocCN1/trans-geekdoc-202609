Pulse oximetry constitutes a clinically standardized method for non-invasively assessing the pulse oxygen saturation level, SpO2, in order to evaluate the presence of hypoxemia commonly associated with acute HF. Pulse oximetry has been shown to be useful in diagnosing HF in acute settings such as myocardial infarction [111]. While there are multiple commercially available wearable pulse oximetry devices like the Masimo MightySat, VitalPatch, EQ02 + Lifemonitor, and VinCense WHMS, that offer user-friendly monitoring of the SpO2, as well as the pulse rate, many of these lack comprehensive cardiac monitoring to detect abnormal rhythms and ischemic events, making them insufficient to assist patients at risk, by detecting early warning signs of adverse cardiopulmonary events. Another convenient wristband-based SpO2 monitoring device is the Spryhealth Loop, which has been designed specifically for use by seniors and offers easy assessment of SpO2 levels, as well as HR and respiration. Furthermore, newer devices, like the Everion, which promise a more complete physiological assessment by monitoring 22 vital signs, are being investigated. Similarly, Current Health has designed a mobile monitoring device for continuous SpO2, temperature, heart rate, respiration, and movement measurements, for detection of warning signs of health deterioration.

A 2019 study applied DL on medical data acquired from an internet of things (IoT) wearable medical device and successfully predicted heart disease with 99.03% accuracy [112]. A higher order Boltzmann deep belief NN was utilized to extract features from patients’ physiological data such as ECG, heart rate, blood pressure, peripheral pulse oximetry (SpO2) level, glucose level, blood fat level, and pulse rate, acquired using the wearable IoT device and stored on cloud servers, to predict heart disease by recognizing abnormal heart patterns in just 8.5 seconds [112]. Another multicenter study, LINK-HF [15], evaluated the accuracy of non-invasive remote monitoring in predicting HF rehospitalization using a wearable multisensor chest patch interconnected with a smartphone to continuously upload data to a cloud analytics platform. A similarity based ML model detected HF exacerbation and provided early detection of impending rehospitalization with a sensitivity ranging between 76% and 88%, and specificity of 85% [15]. This met the sensitivity and specificity thresholds aimed at for reliable prediction of HF hospitalization (≥70% sensitivity and 85% specificity), and was comparable to the predictive ability achieved by implantable devices, such as in the MultiSENSE study (70% sensitivity) [75].

In 2020, a pilot study by Shah et al. [113] aimed to predict HF using PPG and accelerometry data acquired from a wearable wristband; using a combination of demographics, medical history, vital signals, and data from the wearable, an SVM based ML model was able to classify HF with a 0.8 AUC, 72% specificity, and 90% sensitivity [113]. In another study, a novel wearable seismocardiogram and ECG sensing patch was utilized by Inan et al. [17] to assess the clinical status of HF patients; using an ML based graph analytic technique, they generated a graph similarity score that was able to differentiate between decompensated and compensated HF and also track differences in the clinical progress of the decompensated patients from the time of admission to discharge [17]. Similarly, Green et al. [101] demonstrated the application of a multi-instance ML classifier in detecting obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy using a combination of PPG signals acquired from a wearable biosensor and echocardiograms, with a C-statistic of 0.99 (95% CI: 0.99–1.0).

These promising early studies highlight the utility of AI based technologies to detect and assess HF progression using wearable monitoring technologies. However, there remains substantial untapped potential for using novel ML/DL strategies to identify and predict HF risk using non-invasive remote monitoring devices, which warrants further large-scale multicenter trials to evaluate the clinical efficacy of AI enabled wearables for improved HF management (Figure 12.1).

## 12.7 Future Outlook on the Use of AI

Computational advances have led to the development of numerous AI approaches for clinical data analyses [114] to assist HF diagnosis and prediction (Table 12.1). Multiple ML/DL strategies with an accuracy of over 90% in classifying HF patients and predicting adverse events have been proposed. However, studies comparing the clinical efficacy of AI based HF prediction models with conventional diagnostic methods have shown mixed results. While some newer predictive models have shown improved performance over conventional methods in classifying and identifying HF patients [22], other studies have shown no improvement in predicting HF readmissions using ML compared to traditional statistical approaches [45, 115]. This discrepancy is due to the fact that the accuracy of AI approaches is greatly influenced by the availability of large amounts of data that can enable optimal feature extraction to efficiently identify patterns in underlying data. Indeed, it has been shown that, while AI algorithms provide good discrimination in identifying HF patients based on demographics and medical history, HF classification efficiency can be affected by the subtleties of the disease progression state (e.g. preserved ejection fraction, mid-range ejection fraction, or reduced ejection fraction) and variability in patient characteristics (e.g. age, gender) [22]. Yet, while novel ML/DL approaches continue to be investigated to address these challenges, improved AI strategies have shown promise in outperforming conventional regression models in predicting HF outcomes and have also demonstrated better accuracy in predicting mortality due to acute HF and HF with preserved ejection fraction [22, 116, 117].

In parallel, remote monitoring devices have seen an uptick in the past decade, with the proliferation of low-cost vital sign monitors available for ambulatory real-time assessment of patients’ physiological state. From smartwatches, electronic finger-rings, and smart garments to dedicated wearable cardiac and muscle activity sensors, miniaturized medical grade ambulatory monitoring devices are readily available to assist patient care. Yet, there is sparse availability of devices that merge these two technologies and fully utilize the powers of AI and wearable monitors for cardiac disease management, in particular HF prediction.

This technological gap in medical treatment strategies is expected to be bridged by the embedding of AI algorithms in wearable ambulatory devices, a transition that will enable superior classification and prediction capabilities of cardiovascular disease progression, thus accelerating the transition to precision medicine for all population groups.
