# 13.1 Introduction

Heart failure (HF) affects 1–2% of the population of developed countries [1] and the number of people affected is increasing day by day. More than 30% of the patients visiting clinics require hospitalization within 12 months [2]. The common clinical practice is to make a decision about a patient based on a single short clinical visit or on many short visits, often repeated over several months. However, advancements in implantable and wearable technology and in the machine learning domain have significantly reduced the number of hospital admissions in recent years. Moreover, medical doctors have more patient data from less symptomatic patients compared to more symptomatic patients and the number of self-reported patients has significantly increased over the years [3]. Self-reported patient data, commonly done using a set of questions in a self-report tool, is a common method that is used to collect medical information and can be used to understand the patient's perspective regarding their own health conditions. Most implantable devices are very expensive and only a limited number are approved for clinical applications; on the other hand, the number of users of wearable devices has significantly grown over the years and it has become evident from the outcome of several research studies of large patient cohorts that wearable devices can play a vital role in the disease screening process; this has been particularly evident during the outbreak of the coronavirus pandemic in 2019 (COVID-19) [4–10].

Implantable devices are a common approach that has been used for a long time to monitor and detect HF, beginning in 1960 with the creation of the first implantable pacemaker. Since then, countless lives have been saved [11]. The first implantable pacemaker, a battery-powered pacemaker with a myocardial lead, was developed in a hospital in Buffalo, New York [12]. According to local news, this work helped the first patient to carry on his life for 21 years. With the advances of wearable sensors and the growth of the smartphone market, a change in the healthcare system is possible by introducing personalized healthcare to billions of people.
