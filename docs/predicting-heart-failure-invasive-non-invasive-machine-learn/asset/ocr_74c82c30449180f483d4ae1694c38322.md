Many shapes can be used for constructing a rectangular-wave oscillator. In this application, we utilize the oscillator NE555 [36, 37]. Our purpose is to establish the frequency oscillation of 50 kHz and duty cycle of 50%. The output signal is insufficient to power-up the biosensor because of the range of 3–5 mm; we will add a power amplifier stage in the oscillator's output.

For a biosensor to be operational, it needs to receive enough energy. The power collected from the square wave oscillator is insufficient to energize the biosensor wirelessly due to the coil losses, the inductive impedance matching losses, skin absorption losses, and RF-DC rectifier losses. Many techniques can be used to experiment with this work to increase the electricity transferred. For example, we can use a power amplifier to boost the output power of electromagnetic radiation. Nevertheless, it should stabilize high power and prevent the device temperature from going over safe levels. We utilize a power MOSFET transistor to drive a transmitter coil (Tx). The power transistor operates from a rectangular oscillator with a frequency of around 50 kHz.

The external reader unit is powered-up with 12 V DC to control the source coil via the switched-mode power amplifier (Figure 4.15). Thus, when the output power signal increases, the wirelessly electricity transmission level also increases. The amount of power required to operate the biosensor is within 2 W.
