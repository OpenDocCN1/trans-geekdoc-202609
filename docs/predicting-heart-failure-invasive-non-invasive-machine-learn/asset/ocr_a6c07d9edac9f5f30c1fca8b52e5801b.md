In the case of a discrete output, we are referring to a classification problem and in the case where the output has a continuous value (range), we have a regression problem. By contrast, in unsupervised learning there is not a single output to predict; the data are unlabeled. The algorithms try to find patterns in the data. Clustering, the most common application, uses algorithms that try to group data in clusters based on similarities they might have.

Early diagnosis of HF, outcome prediction, hospital readmission, and mortality prediction in HF patients are the areas that benefit from the application of supervised ML techniques in the medical field with a discrete outcome.

# 9.1.2.2 ML Applications in HF Diagnosis/Prediction

Models based on ML techniques can be useful in helping physicians in HF diagnosis. Several studies have been conducted to build a model that can diagnose and detect HF at an early stage based on various ML algorithms (Table 9.1).

In 2010, Yang et al. [38] proposed a model based on support vector machines (SVM) to diagnose HF at an early stage using 289 patient cases. Samples were classified into three groups: the control group (70 subjects), the HF-prone group (59 subjects), and the HF group (160 subjects). The accuracy of the model was 87.50% and 65.85% in identifying subjects in HF-prone group and HF group, respectively. In 2012, Son et al. [39] tested an approach that combined rough set (RS)-based model with decision trees on 88 control subjects and 71 subjects with congestive HF. In their study, they utilized data with no missing values and features consisting of demographics information and clinical laboratory findings. The experimental results showed that their method performed with an accuracy of 97.50%, sensitivity 97.20%, and specificity 97.70%. In 2017, Choi et al. [40] applied deep learning (DL) methods and more specifically implemented a recurrent neural network (RNN) for the early detection of HF in 3884 HF subjects and 28,903 controls using electronic health record (EHR) data. The EHR data set included demographics, habits, clinical and laboratory findings, medications, and International Classification of Diseases (ICD)-9 codes. Their model achieved 77.70% area under the ROC curve (AUC), with a 12-month observation window, higher than regularized logistic regression (LR), neural network (NN), SVM and K-nearest neighbor (k-NN) classifier approaches. More recently, in 2020, Le et al. [41] implemented a multilayer perceptron (MLP) NN achieving an accuracy of 88.00%. The data set (299 subjects) they utilized comprised demographics, laboratory findings, lifestyle habits, and ejection fraction. Also in 2020, Potter et al. 2020 [42] applied the RF classifier to build a model able to predict Stage B HF based on energy waveform ECG. They utilized 254 subjects as the training set (135 with HF, 119 controls) and 65 subjects as the test set (27 with HF, 38 controls). Their model achieved AUC 76.00%, sensitivity 93.00%, and specificity 61.00%. Pecchia et al. [43] conducted a study utilizing the short-term heart rate variability (HRV) measures. They analyzed 1914.40 hours of ECG signals from 83 patients, of which 54 were used as controls and 29 were subjects suffering from chronic HF with NYHA classification I, II, and III. They designed a classifier based on the classification and regression trees (CART) method with 94.80% accuracy, 89.70% sensitivity, and 100% specificity on identifying normal subjects vs. subjects with NYHA classification I, II and III.
