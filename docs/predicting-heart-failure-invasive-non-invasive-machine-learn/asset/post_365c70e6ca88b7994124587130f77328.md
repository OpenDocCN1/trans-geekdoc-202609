## 7 Utilizing Data Mining Classification Algorithms for Early Diagnosis of Heart Diseases

Ahmad Musa Altamimi and Mohammad Azzeh

### 7.1 Introduction

Great attention has been paid to medical research to identify heart disease. Heart disease diagnosis is based on the patient's signs and symptoms and is affected by several factors such as cholesterol level, blood pressure, obesity, smoking habit, and other factors [1]. This creates a challenge to provide quality services with low costs. Quality service involves diagnosing patients properly to get effective treatments. According to the World Heart Federation and the World Health Organization, heart diseases are top of the list of various life-threatening diseases, taking around 17.9 million lives each year [2]. However, 90% of those deaths were estimated to have been preventable if patients had been correctly diagnosed early and had improved their habits [3].

In traditional healthcare environments, diagnosing heart diseases is done in the conventional way, whereby physicians review the symptoms and order some tests, such as blood tests, electrocardiogram, and echocardiogram. However, this conventional way leads to uncertainty of diagnosis, which in turn produces complicated therapy and more medical costs. The misdiagnosis results from many physicians not following the known guidelines for evaluating and managing heart failure [4]. Therefore, the high rates of inaccuracy reported in different studies are not surprising [5].

The core element for treatment success is a reliable and precise diagnosis. Expert systems [6] could be applied to emulate the ability of experts to answer not only simple questions like “What is the average age of patients who have heart disease?” or “Identify the patients who have been treated for heart diseases and complaining from other diseases” but also complex ones like “Given patient records, predict the probability of patients who will be diagnosed with a heart disease?” or “Find the most significant risk factor that results in a heart disease?” Using such systems could decrease medical examination errors, reduce practice variation, and enhance diagnosis outcomes.

The main use of machine learning algorithms is to discover huge volumes of data by detecting patterns and summarizing data into a format that can be understood. Three main data mining techniques can classify previously unorganized data into predefined classes or categorize new data into pre-existing categories: examining previously classified data, learning the classification rules, and applying these rules to new data. The same process is also used to identify relationships between them and to develop a pattern of these relationships. These patterns can be then used as a reference for predicting future behavior. Surveys such as those by Abdelrazek et al. [7] and Ranjan et al. [8] investigated the impact and power of machine learning algorithms in predicting systems.

In this chapter, we focus on data mining classification techniques for predicting heart diseases. Five classifiers (Naive Bayes (NB), support vector machine (SVM), random forest (RF), decision tree (DT), and linear discriminant analysis (LDA)) have been used to analyze a medical data set recorded to diagnose cardiovascular diseases. The data set used in this study has 14 clinical attributes with a different full-size training set. Multiple evaluation measures have been used to compare each classifier’s performance: the accuracy, the sensitivity, area under the curve (AUC), and specificity. For this, several experiments have been conducted. Results reported that SVM and NB outperform other classifiers with an accuracy rate above 80% and a large AUC, providing a more effective and comprehensive forecasting mechanism. We believe that these models can be integrated with medical information systems to assist in diagnosing heart diseases in the earlier stages.

The rest of the chapter is structured as follows. Previous studies are discussed in Section 7.2. Section 7.3 presents various classification algorithms. Section 7.4 discusses the research methodology, and the results and discussions are given in Section 7.5. Finally, the conclusions and future works are introduced in Section 7.6.

### 7.2 Previous Studies

Many data mining investigations have been carried out for diagnosing heart diseases [9–11]. In this context, researchers have applied different machine learning algorithms, such as the association rules technique, clustering, and classification algorithms, to extract significant patterns for predicting heart diseases with good levels of accuracy. The association rules mining technique, for example, has been utilized in many works to find frequent items among large patient data sets to diagnose the presence of heart diseases. Panda and Dash [12] and Ordonez [13] used association rules to build a model for determining relations amongst patients’ attributes values in order to discover interesting relations between variables in the data set. Others have followed the same principle with little modification in their models. Ahmed and Hannan [14], for instance, optimized the association rules in the case of a huge generated set to save prediction time and avoid unwanted rules, while Jabbar et al. [15] proposed a new approach based upon sequence number and clustering a small amount of data at a time to produce scalable and efficient prediction of heart diseases. Soni et al. used an integrated approach of association and classification to discover and then use rules in the database, to construct an efficient classifier [16]. However, in Moreno et al.’s study, while association algorithms were successfully applied for prediction in the health area of interest, they also discovered a large number of rules (some of them of no interest); in addition, the performance of association algorithms was generally low [17].

On the other hand, in Karegowda et al.'s study [18], clustering was employed to partition patients data into a set of meaningful clusters, each of which discovers the overall distribution pattern and correlations among the data attributes, thus helping practitioners to understand the natural grouping of patients' data in order to diagnose the presence of heart diseases correctly. However, clustering is very expensive for processing large data sets, so it is better used as a preprocessing step enabling other classification algorithms to get insights into the data distribution. That being said, clustering techniques have been applied to discover hidden patterns with reference to heart patients. Some studies have used K-means to group and extract data relevant to heart disease [19, 20]. The maximal frequent itemset algorithm (MAFIA) has also been applied to discover heart attack predictions rules [20]. It is important to mention that many clustering algorithms in the literature were implemented with a tool called Weka [21], a set of algorithms and tools used for preprocessing data, classification, clustering, and visualization. However, the main drawbacks of such methods are that they do not work well with dense data, it is difficult to predict the number of clusters, and, in the case where different initial partitions are chosen, different final clusters will be produced [22, 23].

The genetic algorithm was also used by Masilamani et al. [24] to get the optimal subset amongst patients' attribute values adequate for heart disease prediction. The genetic algorithm's global optimization benefit was also used by Amin et al. [25] to implement a hybrid system for initializing neural network weights based on a set of risk elements such as hypertension, high cholesterol, obesity, and others. In another system [26], a layered neuro-fuzzy approach produced a very low error rate in predicting heart diseases.

Classification methods have been extensively applied in developing prediction models for heart diseases. Popular classification algorithms such as SVM, DT, NB, and neural networks (NNs) have been used in this context. Mixed results were presented in Xing et al. [27] and Peter and Somasundaram [28] when these algorithms were employed to develop prediction models. For instance, Xing et al. [27] used SVMs, NNs, and DTs with 502 cases. Their experiments showed that SVM was the best prediction model, followed by NN. In contrast, other studies proved that the NB produced more accurate predictions than other popular algorithms such as DT and NN [28]. Furthermore, another study reported that NN was recognized as the best prediction model with 15 prominent features as risk factors in the literature [29]. The hybrid intelligent technique binds to NNs in Chitra and Srinivasagam [30] to produce a prediction model to improve prediction accuracy. The results show that the hybrid intelligent technique offer improvement in terms of accuracy.

Recently, Toughui et al.'s study [31] compared multiple machine learning algorithms implemented in the following tools: RapidMiner, Orange, MATLAB, Knime, Weka, and Scikit-Learn. The results showed that MATLAB was the best tool.

After reviewing the classification related literature, we were motivated to use the classification algorithms to develop a heart disease prediction model based on a clinical data set. Our work is different from the works presented above in two ways: firstly, five classifiers were applied to analyze medical data sets with different sizes; secondly, extensive analysis has been conducted using the MATLAB tool. The simulated implementation produced a very low error rate when calculating the prediction's accuracy.
