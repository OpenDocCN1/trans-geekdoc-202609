Both Melillo et al. in 2013 [66] and Shahbazi et al. in 2015 [67] analyzed standard long-term HRV measures from two public Holter databases. They used the data from 12 patients suffering from mild congestive HF (NYHA I and II), named as lower risk, and 32 suffering from severe congestive HF (NYHA III and IV), named as higher risk. The method proposed by Melillo et al. [66] achieved 85.40% accuracy, 93.30% sensitivity and 63.60% specificity employing the CART method, but was outperformed by Shahbazi et al. [67] that achieved 100.00% sensitivity and specificity. Shahbazi et al. applied generalized discriminant analysis (GDS) as a feature extraction method and the retained features were given as input to the k-NN classifier. Gladence et al. in 2014 [68] used also standard long-term HRV measures from 303 subjects. The Bayesian belief network classifier they used separated lower risk patients from higher risk ones, and reached 74.44% accuracy, 81.40% sensitivity, and 71.70% specificity.
