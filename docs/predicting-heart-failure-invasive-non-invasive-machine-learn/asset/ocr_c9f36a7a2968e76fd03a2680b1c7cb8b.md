Once the monitoring period is over, the cardiologist will go through the continuous ECG recorded in the device over the monitoring period, and carry out a detailed analysis. During the analysis, the cardiologist goes through the patient dairy and, for each recorded activity as well as a symptom, the ECG recorded in the device at that particular time is investigated with the help of computers preloaded with algorithms to discover any abnormalities in the heartbeat. So the precise recording of activities and symptoms in patient dairy is essential in Holter monitoring and any mistake in the recordings can severely affect the result analysis procedure. Medtronic, GE Healthcare, and Abbob are the major producers of commercially available Holter monitoring devices, with recent devices including many functionalities such the ability to communicate directly with patients' Bluetooth-enabled smartphones or PCs.

Another type of portable ECG-based device is the event monitor. Event monitors are similar to the Holter monitors responsible for recording heart signals. However, while Holter monitors are configured to record the heart rhythm continuously for 24 or 48 hours, event monitors are used to record the heartbeats when the patient activates them. Some of the event monitors available on the market can automatically turn on the heartbeat recording when an abnormal heart rhythm is identified. Event monitors are available in many types, with symptom event monitors and memory looping event monitors being the most common. Symptom monitors record the heart's electrical signal after activation, while memory looping monitors record data from a few minutes before the activation. This feature of memory looping monitors provides additional information for doctors to analyze the heart condition. Mobile cardiac telemetry (MCT) devices are similar to Holter monitors and event monitors, but they are equipped with more advanced features to provide real-time assistance for patients.
