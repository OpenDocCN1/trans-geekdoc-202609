
[14, 118, 119]. In addition, AI can account for interpatient variability and provide accurate automated diagnosis based on consideration of multiple clinical factors, which is humanly infeasible.

Despite the clear advantages offered by an AI based ambulatory monitoring framework, the utility of novel invasive and non-invasive health monitoring modalities has been met with skepticism due to the lack of a regulatory framework, as well as reliability, accountability, liability, and security concerns. Recognizing and addressing these critical issues is paramount to the successful implementation, transition, and adoption of AI based ambulatory health monitoring systems:

- 1. **Legislation and liability**: Enabling the universal adaptability of an automated monitoring paradigm, involves participation of developers, researchers, physicians, and legislators alike, to account for the multiple aspects involved in its design, implementation, and end consumer usage. Proper quality checks during development and production, along with thorough assessment ensured by Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval will reduce the physician's liability in cases of undesired outcomes [120], enabling a constructive framework for the implementation of an AI based medical system.

- 2. **Data security**: For ambulatory monitoring devices, cybersecurity is a rising concern, as patient data is recorded on cloud servers and transferred between various digital and IoT platforms. Efficient AI based ambulatory systems need to include reliable signal processing algorithms with data security features protecting patient confidentiality. Similar to the need for informed consent prior to any medical procedure, approval needs be mandated before the implementation of AI systems as well to ensure effective patient involvement and account for data integrity.

- 3. **Reliability and accountability**: To standardize the accuracy and efficacy requirements of ambulatory monitoring systems, AI devices and algorithms should be FDA approved. In addition, with advancements in real-time signal processing capabilities, AI algorithms need to be consistently refined to keep up with the updated clinical practices. The end performance of the AI based ambulatory healthcare system depends on participation by multiple stakeholders, including developers, physicians, and technicians in charge of data handling, along with willing involvement by patients. Hence, all stakeholders are equally accountable for the quality and performance of the AI device.

- 4. **Equity**: Disparities in mortality rates between rural and urban communities has been a growing concern in recent years. Inequitable access to healthcare, socioeconomic differences, and geographic variances are significant attributes for the regional disparity in healthcare efficiency [121–127]. AI based ambulatory monitoring systems hold the potential to enable access to precision medicine by providing clinicians with patient custom methodologies for earlier and improved disease diagnosis. ML/DL algorithms can identify patterns from large physiological observations and provide personalized therapeutic approaches by detecting and potentially eliminating bias from underlying data, hence, enabling a more equitable healthcare paradigm.

## 12.8 Conclusion

Ambulatory, non-invasive, and invasive monitoring devices that employ AI offer novel promising modalities for the prediction and diagnosis of HF. ML/DL strategies can enable real-time quick diagnosis with increased accuracy by providing physicians with tools for improved personalized disease assessment. Thus, AI devices have the potential to bridge the technological vacuum in medical treatment strategies by enabling superior prediction and classification of cardiovascular disease progression, compared with traditional methods. Furthermore, ambulatory remote monitoring devices embedded with AI are likely to enable and facilitate the realization of precision medicine, by offering patients personalized, affordable, low-cost monitors that can improve the overall quality of life. An effective AI enabled medical healthcare system can be fulfilled by the implementation of a comprehensive regulatory framework that successfully addresses, among many, the challenges of reliability, accountability, liability, and data security.
