There is an increasing demand for the design of fast, portable, and low-cost biosensing devices for the treatment and early diagnosis of CVD. Biosensors can play a significant role in the early diagnosis of CVD as an alternative to time-consuming and expensive laboratory analysis. In recent years, many biosensors have been improved to determine cardiac markers. One of the biggest challenges in CVD is estimating an individual's risk of suffering from CVD. There is great attention in research to find early diagnostic biomarkers that can be detected in the blood and predict CVD risk. The best known of these biomarkers is C-reactive protein (CRP), followed by cardiac troponin (cTn) I or T, interleukin-6, interleukin 1, myoglobin, lipoprotein-associated phospholipase A (2), tumor necrosis factor-alpha, myeloperoxidase, and low-density lipoprotein. In this chapter, the available biosensor platforms for detecting various CVD markers are explained in detail, and the publications on this subject are given in tables [12-16]. Biosensors constitute a large proportion of cardiovascular biomarkers detection research over the last 10 years, as presented in Figure 3.1. Troponin I and CRP diagnosis are gaining attraction. Also notable is the stability in the last 5 years in the usage of electrochemical-based biosensors. This chapter will focus on these recent developments in biosensors used to detect cardiovascular biomarkers.
