According to World Health Organization regulations, only one implanted sensor is allowed in a patient. A health professional inserts the sensor in a short outpatient procedure [17]. First, the medical team makes a small incision, after which the bioelectronics sensor is inserted under the skin using a specialized biological chip. The incision is then closed using sterile tapes [18]. The chip implanted inside the body will not affect the body's organs because it does not contain any substances that present any danger for the body. A small chip inserted inside the body can detect many things such as blood pressure, sugar levels, heartbeat, and cancer and can subsequently relay the results at precise times. It can lead to early detection of significant problems inside the body through physiological parameter measurements.

# 4.3 Wireless Power Transmission

Wireless electricity transfer technology transfers power from one place to another without physical support [19] and is therefore beneficial for transmitting electricity to sites and devices that are difficult to energize, such as implant sensors. In this subsection, we will highlight contemporary media for wireless power transmission. There are typically four technologies used for wireless energy transmission, depending on the physical energy grandeur sent [20, 21]:

+   - Microwave wireless power transfer
+   - Electromagnetic induction energy transfer
+   - Laser wireless power transmission
+   - Ultrasonic wireless power transmission.
