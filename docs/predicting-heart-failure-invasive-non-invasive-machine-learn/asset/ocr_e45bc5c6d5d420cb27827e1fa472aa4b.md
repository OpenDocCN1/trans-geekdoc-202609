# 5.1 Introduction

Heart failure (HF) is one of most prevalent clinical syndromes, in which the heart muscles are not capable of providing adequate blood flow to the circulatory system. The symptoms of HF are reported as ankle swelling, dyspnea, and fatigue due to elevated jugular venous pressure, pulmonary crackles, and peripheral edema [1–3]. The estimated number of patients suffering from HF worldwide is reported as 26 million [4]. Frequent hospitalization and poor life quality are serious risks for HF patients. It has been reported that only about 10% of patients with chronic HF survive 10 years after diagnosis [5, 6]. HF is a chronic and progressive disease and, therefore, HF patients have high rehospitalization rates and emergency visits. About 25% of HF patients are readmitted within a month and at least half of these readmissions are due to HF related symptoms [7]. These facts demonstrate the high economic burden and hospital workload for HF treatment.

Hospitalization is required for HF patients in case of congestion, which is the excessive accumulation of body fluid [8]. Congestion is mainly related to water and sodium retention in the body, leading to high left ventricular end-diastolic pressure [9, 10]. The symptoms of congestion can be listed as dyspnea, rales, and edema [11]. The most-used therapy for congestion is to intravenously inject diuretics to minimize the fluid accumulation in the body [8]. The diuretics help kidneys to excrete more sodium into the urine, and the excessive sodium results in more water absorption from the blood, which reduces the amount of fluid in the body.

The current gold standard for HF diagnosis is to measure intracardiac filling pressures using implantable pressure sensors via cardiac catheterization [12]. However, the invasive nature of cardiac catheterization prevents its routine usage for HF patients [10]. In addition, the cost of the implantable pressure sensors is more than US$ 20,000, which is not affordable for millions of HF patients [7]. This demonstrates the need for an inexpensive, non-invasive, easy-to-apply, and accurate method for HF diagnosis.
