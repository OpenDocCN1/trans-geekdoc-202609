# 4.4.3 Microchip Implant

The microchip implant underneath the skin consists of a receiver circuit section and data transmission via an induction link.

The receiver circuit consists of two blocks: a rectifier circuit and a data transmitter circuit through the magnetic field but with a frequency range greater than the frequency of the RF power circuit. The coil antenna is made using wire-wound component on a PCB board. The implanted chip underneath the skin comprises the receiver coil connected to the bridge rectifier and the ultra-capacitor to harvest maximum electricity, as shown in Figure 4.17. To keep a constant output signal, a voltage regulator is used.

The full-wave rectifier is a prominent part of the electronic circuit that transforms the inductive flux signal to stable output voltage, as shown in Figure 4.18. The process is known as rectification. The DC voltage harvested changes according to the RF strength level. We make a simulation with two different input power; the first RF input, 34 dBm, and the output voltage, 9.219 V. The second input RF power is 45 dBm and gives us an output voltage of 20.742 V. This means that if the RF strength is very high, the output is elevated.
