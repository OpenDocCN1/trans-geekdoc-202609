
In 2018, Golas et al. [81] applied deep unified networks (DUNs), a DL network structure designed to avoid overfitting, with an accuracy of 76.40% and AUC 70.50% based on EHR data to predict the 1-month readmission rate for HF patients. Their model was trained on a large data set consisting of 27,334 admissions from 11,510 patients.

Ashfaq et al. [82] in 2019, applied the LSTM neural network on 7500 subjects with congestive HF with AUC 77.00% in predicting the 1-month readmission rate. This study targeted key elements of an EHR, i.e. demographics, diagnoses, procedures, laboratory findings, physical examination, and medications. Also in 2019, Liu et al. [83] utilized clinical notes using DL in order to predict HF readmission rates. Their model, based on a CNN, achieved F1 score 75.60% in general readmission prediction and 73.30% in 1-month readmission prediction. Khayyat et al. in 2019 [84] developed a clinical decision support system (CDSS) that would be able to estimate and reduce the probability of readmission for congestive HF patients. They used as input medical comorbidities and social factors of patients. The model they built achieved an accuracy of 63.00% for the non-readmissions and 63.00% for the readmissions.

In a 2020 study, Liu et al. [85] used the 2014 Nationwide Readmissions Database (NRD), that includes data from 2048 hospitals with nearly 15 million admissions. Their study cohort included 303,233 admissions for HF with 17.70% unplanned 1-month readmission rates. With AUC 64.00%, the artificial NN they implemented outperformed the hierarchical LR that was used as a baseline comparator. Also in 2020, Kawai et al. [86] compared several ML techniques to predict HF readmission rates with different prediction windows from 1-month to 12-months. In their study, 7413 patients were included, with 19% readmission rate at 1 month and 52.00% at 12 months. AdaBoost with bagging algorithm and RF performed better with AUC 61.00%, 64.00%, and 71.00%, for the 1-month, 3-month, and 12-month prediction group, respectively.

## 9.1.2.6 ML Applications in Mortality Prediction

Other studies focus on developing a model to predict mortality among patients with HF (Table 9.4). In 2013, Pocock et al. [87] used the Meta-Analysis Global Group in Chronic Heart Failure (MAGGIC) international database with 39,372 patients from 30 studies, to create an easily used risk score for mortality in patients with HFrEF and HFpEF. They applied Poisson regression with forward stepwise feature selection and concluded to 13 independent features. All were highly significant P < 0.002, and most were overwhelmingly significant P < 0.0001.

In 2017, Miao et al. [88] utilized the public MIMIC II clinical database with 8059 patients and 32 features consisting of demographics, clinical variables, laboratory findings, and medications. They analyzed these features to develop a risk model for mortality prediction in patients with HF. They applied the improved random survival forest achieving C-statistic 0.821.

In 2019, Kwon et al. [89] applied a DL-based artificial intelligence algorithm for predicting mortality of patients with acute HF. The AUC of the model was 88.00% for predicting in-hospital mortality, 78.20% for predicting 12-month mortality, and 81.30% for 36-month mortality. They utilized 2165 patients with acute HF for training and 4759 patients with acute HF for testing. Their data set included demographic features, medications, laboratory results, and ECG and echocardiography findings.

Wang et al. [90] proposed a feature rearrangement-based DL system for predicting in-hospital mortality, and 30-day and 1-year mortality after discharge. The data set used in this study was collected from the EHR system of a hospital in Shanghai. They utilized 10,203 records with 539 death records for in-hospital mortality, 6260 records with 652 death records for 1-month mortality, and 4353 records with 1182 death records for 1-year mortality. Their method scored 81.48% sensitivity and 90.37% AUC for in-hospital mortality, 80.01% sensitivity and 89.13% AUC for 1-month mortality, and 72.15% sensitivity and 89.13% AUC for 1-year mortality. Yang et al. [91] selected 506 HF patients, with more than 25,000 features, consisting of demographic information, electrocardiography measurements, index of HF, echocardiography, and advanced laboratory biomarkers. The RF model performed better on predicting all-cause mortality (AUC: 75.00%). Adler et al. [92] discriminated between low and high risk of death by identifying eight low-cost and commonly available features (diastolic blood pressure, blood urea nitrogen, creatinine, hemoglobin, white blood cell count, platelets, albumin, and red blood cell distribution width) from 5822 patients. A boosted decision tree achieved AUC 88.00%. Jing et al. [93] conducted a large-scale study on 26,971 subjects to predict 1-year all-cause mortality in patients with HF. They collected 26 clinical variables (demographic information, laboratory findings, medications), 90 diagnostic codes, 41 ECG features, 44 echocardiographic features, and eight evidence-based “care gaps” e.g. flu vaccine, blood pressure, and cardiac resynchronization therapy. They tested various ML models that achieved AUC 74.00% to 77.00%, with the nonlinear XGBoost model (AUC: 77.00%) outperforming the linear logistic regression (AUC: 74.00%).

## 9.1.2.7 ML Applications in Mortality or Readmission Prediction

Furthermore, a large number of studies focus on developing models for jointly predicting adverse events, i.e. mortality or rehospitalization in patients with HF (Table 9.5).

In 2013, Zhang et al. [94] utilized decision trees and LR to predict death risk or hospitalization due to worsening HF at one year. The mortality prediction had an accuracy of 89.20% for decision trees and 85.80% for LR and the composite outcome prediction had an accuracy of 79.70% for decision trees and 73.80% for the LR model. Their data set consisted of 284 patients for training and 160 patients for validation from the TEN-HMS (Trans-European Network Homecare Monitoring System) study, utilizing as input features demographics, physical examination, laboratory findings, medical history, quality of life, and EuroQoL. NT-proBNP was the feature with the strongest prediction of mortality.

In 2017, Tripoliti et al. [95] presented an ML based method for the prediction of adverse events (mortality and relapses) in patients with HF by analyzing breath and saliva biomarkers from 27 subjects. The prediction of adverse events achieved 77.00% accuracy using an ROT algorithm.

Awan et al. [96] in 2019, conducted a study on HF patients aged over 65 years and in hospital, where 23.60% of the patients were readmitted or died within 1 month of hospital discharge. They developed an MLP-based approach to predict 1-month HF readmission or death, achieving AUC 62.00%, sensitivity 48.00% and specificity 70.00%.
