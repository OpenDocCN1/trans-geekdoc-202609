In the study of Kotb et al. (2015), it is stated that ECG analysis plays a prominent role in lowering all-cause mortality and hospitalization, and in improving quality of life [182]. The highest HF prediction performance is observed for RPM trials including multiple monitoring parameters. Through these, early warnings of HF may be better recognized using efficient algorithms that analyze various sensor measurements [190].

Another aspect of non-invasive remote monitoring is the adherence of HF patients in order to improve the efficiency of proposed trials. It is stated that most HF patients are older than 65 years [191]. Proposed RPM systems should be portable and easy-to-use for increased adherence. Ware et al. (2019) conducted a study to evaluate patient adherence to a remote monitoring program [192]. It is reported that the highest patient adherence is observed in older patients, while participation of younger patients decreased over time, indicating that older patients adapted better to remote monitoring system [3, 192]. There are several recommended systems for increasing patient adherence, such as the toilet-seat based cardiovascular monitoring system and the clothing-integrated sensor system, which do not require any effort from the patient to develop the habit of monitoring themselves [158, 189].

Non-invasive measurements are cheap and easy-to-apply. As a result, these modalities can be used in large number of patients. Beyond the effectiveness of the methods, these measurements encourage patients to make lifestyle changes and increase their sensitivity to healthcare, thereby increasing the life quality of HF patients. Two important questions for non-invasive RPM are which type of HF patients should benefit from remote monitoring and at what frequency should the measurements be performed. Measurements on a daily basis are recommended for increasing the efficiency of monitoring systems, as relative changes in the measured data provide important clues about upcoming HF decompensation. The viable method for remote data analysis systems is that large numbers of patients with low HF severity should be encouraged to become involved in RPM with non-invasive measurements, and a smaller group of patients with severe HF should be directed to invasive measurement techniques.

## 5.5 Conclusion

In this chapter, we explained non-invasive and minimally invasive measurement techniques and summarized the results of emerging RPM systems to predict impending HF decompensation. The use of remote monitoring techniques employing non-invasive sensors helps to reduce all-cause mortality, HF-related mortality, and rehospitalization. With the advances in sensor technologies, these measurements can be performed by patients at home using smart devices and wearables. The data obtained can be monitored remotely by the clinicians, providing the opportunity to take precautions before serious symptoms are encountered. This also helps reduce the workload of hospitals and the economic burden of HF treatment in healthcare systems. The positive results of clinical trials with large numbers of patients show the high potential of non-invasive sensors for diagnosing HF.
