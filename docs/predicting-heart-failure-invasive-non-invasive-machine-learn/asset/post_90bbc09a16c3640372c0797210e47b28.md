
### 10.5.2 Internet of Things

The internet of things (IoT) comprises a network of physical devices, buildings, vehicles, and other objects embedded with electronic components, software, sensors, and network connections to collect and exchange data. IoT can drastically transform the way healthcare services are provided today and can provide fast medical services in remote areas where there is no immediate medical help available. In the case of CVDs, where timely medical help is highly essential to save patients’ lives, IoT can be a great benefit, effectively sending data received from the patient to remote physicians who can access it and can monitor patient’s physical status in real time.

In cardiovascular medicine, collected data related to blood pressure, ECG, and SpO2 can help the physician to diagnose and prescribe the necessary treatment. It will reduce hospital visits as well as hospitalization costs. IoT techniques along with real-time analytical algorithms can be instrumental in sending pre-warnings to patients about possible cardiac attacks.

Wearable sensors combined with ML analytics can improve clinical outcomes and significantly reduce hospitalizations in cases of heart failure [39–41].

### 10.5.4 Magnetic Resonance Imaging

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is emerging as a strong option for assessing several cardiovascular pathological entities. It is a very effective tool for the non-invasive measurement of left and right ventricular volumes and ejection fraction (EF) [50].

In cardiac MRI, ventricular segmentation is an important application for implementing ML models (Figure 10.8). It not only quantifies the volumetric but also increases the efficiency and reproducibility of clinical assessments [51–53]. It helps in the detailed tissue characterization essential to diagnose and manage the treatment of CVD [54]. It has the ability to determine myocardial and vascular injuries precisely. One research study used DL algorithms for the segmentation of the right ventricular chamber [55]. Another important application of ML was observed in the recognition of subacute or chronic myocardial scar [56].

### 10.5.5 Echocardiography

Echocardiography has a crucial role in the diagnosis and treatment of CVDs and correct quantitative evaluation of cardiovascular functions and structures. AI is successfully applied in automated measurement features including left ventricular EF, chamber dimensions, wall thickness, etc. (Figure 10.9) [57, 58].

AI tools, specifically ML, offer new possibilities of better accuracy in interpreting the image in clinical echocardiography practice. ML models reveal different features in an image, identifying a range of disease patterns by considering each pixel in the image and its connections [59]. These models interpret the unused data generated by multidimensional imaging techniques like 3D echocardiography and speckle tracking, thereby significantly reducing the analysis time and improving reproducibility [43].

### 10.5.6 Cardiac CT

CT is a non-invasive imaging technique that is gaining in popularity for diagnosing and monitoring coronary artery disease (CAD). CT provides excellent visualization of the coronary arteries with high spatial resolution [60–61]. ML models are effectively using image analysis for quick and accurate diagnosis and cardiac risk assessment.

### 10.6 Conclusions and Future Perspectives

Despite significant development in diagnosis and risk assessment techniques, CVDs remain one of the leading causes of morbidity and mortality worldwide. The medical records indicate that CVDs begin with the progression of risk factors that turn into the development of subclinical atherosclerosis and finally end in overt CVD. Biomarkers are used to diagnose the disease, identify high-risk individuals, and enable physicians to make effective CVD treatment strategies. Though clinical assessment results form the basis for patient management, they have their own constraints in terms of time, accuracy, portability, etc.

Along with the fast spread of several diseases affecting heart and blood vessels, cases of CVDs are also on the rise, making diagnosis and prognosis both very complex and challenging. As a result, the need for new biomarkers will continue to grow. The expanding research in the discovery of new biomarkers demands a systematic organization with fast and accurate interpretation of data.

Accelerating the discovery of new biomarkers requires multidisciplinary research. It has become essential to radically shift from traditional methods of material discovery to AI-enabled modern methods. The discovery of new biomarker materials follows a process involving the conception, synthesis, and characterization of the material. These steps are carried out sequentially, and only limited materials can be tested at a time. Computational techniques combined with AI transform the understanding of the material properties and provide a better interpretation of data generated through different material characterization techniques. The faster discovery of materials enables researchers to develop new biomarkers for the accurate and quick detection of diseases.

Technological advancements in AI have opened new areas and tools in the creation of novel modeling and predictive techniques for clinical applications in CVDs and have begun to infuse and reform cardiovascular medicine. AI tools can significantly change the way cardiology is practiced today. Cardiac imaging, ML, and cognitive computing can provide the opportunity to develop efficient and accurate tools for the interpretation of data and making correct clinical decisions.

Rapidly emerging AI techniques are non-invasively revealing the fundamental processes underlying CVDs. Digitalization of healthcare practices is remarkably proving to be an effective diagnostic tool that rectifies complex heart related abnormalities.

Multimodality images, electronic health records, and mHealth devices safely store underutilized data for each patient. AI is capable of improving the status quo, and learning from these voluminous data in order to create new knowledge and understanding and enabling physicians to apply them in specific situations. Each heartbeat contains significant information, and cardiovascular medicine is one area which, when combined with AI, can march toward personalized and precise cardiocare.
