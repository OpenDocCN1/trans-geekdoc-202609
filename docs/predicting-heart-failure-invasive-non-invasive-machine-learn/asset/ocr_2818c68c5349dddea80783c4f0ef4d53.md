Powering represents the fundamental challenge impeding the completion and medical use of implantable bioelectronic microdevices. Biosensors implanted in the body target vital areas to reach essential objectives in bio-medicine and follow the patient's condition from a distance.

The realization of a new technique to collect and store radiofrequency energy in the dynamic and complex area underneath the skin is necessary to implement the biomedical microchip. So far, various techniques of transmitting energy wirelessly have been established, including ultrasonic guided waves, laser beam, electromagnetic induction, and transmission through radio frequency (RF). Including the listed alternatives, wireless energy transmission via RF signal is a highly advanced transmission technique. The uses of inductive charging are wide, but the narrow gap between coils (Rx, Tx) presents a challenge in the medical field. However, it is still a good solution in healthcare applications because skin depth is 3–5 mm. This method produces a heat process that interferes with the biological parameters of the human body. We cannot use all the techniques mentioned earlier in this section to penetrate human skin, for example laser might burn a patient's skin. In this chapter we will discuss the advanced techniques for developing the best technology to power-up the implantation of chips into the human body without intervention. Vital data checking is becoming an essential part of healthcare technologies. The chapter first explains the microchip insertion procedure. It then reviews wireless power transfer methods and gives some examples of applicable biomedical systems. This is followed by a discussion of the study of high-efficiency technology for charging wireless implant sensors, and then a conclusion.
