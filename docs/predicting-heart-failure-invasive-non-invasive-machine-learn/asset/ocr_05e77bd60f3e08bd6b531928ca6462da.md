In 2016, Mortazavi et al. [77] compared the effectiveness of RF, boosting, RF combined hierarchically with SVM or LR, Poisson regression, and traditional LR to predict 1-month and 6-month all-cause readmissions and readmissions because of HF. For the 1-month prediction of readmission, boosting had 92.00% sensitivity and 79.00% specificity. For the 6-month prediction of readmission, RF had 94.00% sensitivity and 15.00% specificity.

In 2017, Frizzell et al. [78] and Jamei et al. [79] predicted the 1-month all-cause readmission rate for HF patients. Frizzell et al. [78] conducted a large-scale study in 56,477 patients, from which 70.00% were used for training and 30.00% for validation. They implemented ML algorithms (tree-augmented NB network, RF, gradient-boosted model, LR) on demographics, socioeconomic status, medical history, characterization of HF, medication, vital signs, selected laboratories treatment, and discharge interventions features. The C statistics for the validation sets were: 61.80%, 60.70%, 61.40%, 62.40% for the implemented models. Jamei et al. [79] implemented artificial NNs on 335,815 hospital visits, from which 32,718 visits resulted in 1-month readmission. Their model achieved 60.00% sensitivity, 24.00% precision, and 78.00% AUC. In the same year, Leong et al. [80] applied a bivariate analysis and LR in a multisource data set from 1475 readmissions from 1098 unique patients from which 60.00% were used for training and 40.00% for validation. The 1-month readmission risk was stratified into low, medium, and high risk with AUC 76.00%, sensitivity 78.30% and specificity 60.70%.
