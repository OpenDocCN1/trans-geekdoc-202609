# 6.1 Introduction

The unique marvel of human intelligence lies in its innate ability to reason, meta-reason, and seamlessly synthesize abstract concepts, distinct from the binary logic of computer science. Artificial intelligence (AI) is a rapidly evolving transdisciplinary field. It integrates computer science, statistics, psychology, neuroscience, material science, mechanical engineering, and computer hardware design to develop algorithms that aim to simulate human intuition, decision-making, and object recognition [1]. The concepts and methodologies underpinning AI have been well-established for some time, but recent technological advancements that support processing large volumes of data have driven a surge in interest in AI [2]. Machine learning (ML) refers to a collection of techniques which enable AI.

The overarching objectives of AI in medicine are threefold: to optimize patient care, to improve efficiency, and to improve clinical outcomes. More specifically, there are growing applications in the fields of basic science drug discovery, genetic-based treatments, radiology, and surgical specialties [1, 2]. In cardiology, there has been a growth in the potential sources of new patient data, as well as advances in investigations and therapies, which position the field well to uniquely benefit from AI: for clinicians, these benefits potentially translate into improving the accuracy of diagnosis, risk assessment, prognostication, and decision-making for treatments; for health systems, these benefits potentially translate into improved workflows and technical protocols, reducing medical errors and costs; for patients, these benefits potentially translate into promoting optimal prevention and treatment of cardiovascular disease.

This chapter aims to introduce the various AI techniques and their respective applications in cardiovascular medicine, to introduce the steps involved in building AI algorithms, as well as to discuss the current limitations and pitfalls.
