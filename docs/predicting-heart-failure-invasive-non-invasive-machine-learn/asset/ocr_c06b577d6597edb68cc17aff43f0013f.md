# 8.1 Introduction

Heart failure (HF) is a chronic disease in which the cardiac system fails to pump the normal blood level in the body, leading to a reduced oxygen level in organs and tissues. It is a global health burden that creates a major challenge for the healthcare systems worldwide. The global prevalence of HF is 64.34 million cases, corresponding to 8.52 cases per 1000 inhabitants. This translates to 9.9 million years lost due to disability. In terms of costs, the HF burden accounts for US$346.17 billion [1]. In the Arab Gulf Region, the prevalence of HF is about 1–2% of the population [2] accounting for more than 4 million patients. The average affected population is 10 years younger than in developed countries [2, 3]. Some of the main risk factors of HF are sex, obesity, hypertension, diabetes, smoking, and previous heart conditions. While, the prevalence of HF as a function of age peaks for older people over 70, a recent study showed that HF risk in the younger population is higher for obesity, hypertension, diabetes, and smoking. These results emphasize the importance of preventive measures across adult life [4, 5].

Due to the progressive and chronic nature of HF, high hospitalization rates and emergency visits are reported for HF patients. About one-fourth of HF patients are readmitted for medical care within a month, and at least half of these readmissions are related to HF symptoms [6]. High hospitalization rates increase the work burden of medical professionals and also increase healthcare expenditure. The healthcare costs of HF management are estimated to be about 1–2% of all healthcare expenditure [7, 8].

The early prediction of HF risk is important in enabling preventive action and limiting the consequences of HF. The traditional method for developing risk factor models for HF is typically using logistic regression models with general health data. Such an approach suffers from a limited predictive power when a large population is used to fit the models. Since logistic regression is a linear model, it cannot capture non-linearity in complex and noisy data. Machine learning (ML) approaches provide good alternatives to traditional HF risk scoring and prediction. High model capacity allows for a better predictive performance limiting the underfitting problem observed with logistic regression models.
