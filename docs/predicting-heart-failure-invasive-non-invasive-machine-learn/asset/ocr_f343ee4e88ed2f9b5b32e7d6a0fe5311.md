# 13.5 Summary of the Cardiac and Wearable Device Data Sets

There is a large number of data sets publicly available for ECG recording. Recently, several data sets have been released for research and PhysioNet has a large collection of ECG data sets. A team from Chapman University and Shaoxing People's Hospital (Shaoxing Hospital Zhejiang University School of Medicine) has collected and disseminated a novel database that contains 12-lead ECGs of 10,646 patients with a 500 Hz sampling rate that features 11 common rhythms and 67 additional cardiovascular conditions, all labeled by professional experts. The data set consists of 10-second, 12-dimension ECGs and labels for rhythms and other conditions for each subject [59]. Another large recent large ECG data set is the PTB-XL ECG dataset [60] containing 21,837 clinical 12-lead ECGs from 18,885 patients of 10-second length. The raw waveform ECG data was annotated by two cardiologists, who assigned potentially multiple ECG statements in each record.

An ECG image data set was released for CVD and COVID-19 patients with 1937 distinct patient records [61]. The data were collected using the ECG device EDAN SERIES-3 from different healthcare institutes across Pakistan. The data set was manually reviewed by experts to categorize them into five distinct categories (COVID-19, abnormal heartbeat, myocardial infarction (MI), previous history of MI, and normal person). ECG data were collected using a 12-lead ECG device which can be used for machine learning-based model development for CVD detection among COVID and other CVD patients.

Bogu et al. [5] reported heart rate and steps data from 106 individuals using wearable devices. There were 25 COVID-19 +, 11 non-COVID-19 illnesses with self-reported symptoms, and 70 healthy individuals with no symptoms or illness during the COVID-19 period (February 2020 to June 2020) were reported in this data set. Resting heart rate was computed by selecting the heart rate intervals where steps were zero for 12 minutes ahead of an interrogated time point.

Similarly, Mishra et al. [10] used the data from consumer smartwatches for the pre-symptomatic detection of COVID-19. Using retrospective smartwatch data, they showed that 63% of the COVID-19 cases could have been detected before symptom onset in real-time via a two-tiered warning system based on the occurrence of extreme elevations in the resting heart rate relative to the individual baseline. The investigation was carried out on nearly 5300 participants; however, 32 patients infected with COVID-19 were identified from the cohort, and the rest weere in the control group.

The MyHeart Counts study [62] used an iOS app among 110,056 users from March 9, 2015, to October 28, 2015. The study cohort described here is composed of contributions from study participants who designated broad sharing of their data (n = 34,189). Among consenting participants, 4900 (10.2%) completed a 6-minute walk test at the end of day 7 in the study. The 6-minute walk test activity not only collects distance traveled but also accelerometry during the walk.
