Without this insight, it becomes increasingly difficult to sense-check an algorithm to see if it is in line with real-world observations. ## 6.4.3 Challenges in Clinical Translation

The novelty of AI algorithms and the lack of prospective, multicenter randomized trials mean that large gaps in evidence exist currently. Most notably, the clinical translational ability of ML algorithms remains relatively unknown, with the practical difficulties of deploying such technologies in real-world clinical practice still not fully investigated. Important considerations include the cost-benefit analysis, the risks of false-positives, and the longer-term clinical outcomes of AI technologies compared with current clinical standards of care. At present, in accordance with the Food and Drug Administration, diagnostic software tools are classified as Class 2 (medium risk devices). If ML algorithms are to be used to guide treatment in addition to diagnosis, they would require a further level of evidence and validation, potentially enabling them to be classified as Class 3 medical devices.

## 6.5 Conclusions

- Clinical decision-making is reliant on synthesizing high-quality data to help solve patient problems. As the amount of available patient data increases, it is likely clinical decision-making will be augmented by AI techniques in the future.
- AI offers the potential to summarize and synthesize this growth of patient data to potentially guide individualized therapy.
- There is a large variety of ML approaches that can be employed as detailed in this chapter. The most appropriate algorithm choice will be guided by the research question and the type of data available.
