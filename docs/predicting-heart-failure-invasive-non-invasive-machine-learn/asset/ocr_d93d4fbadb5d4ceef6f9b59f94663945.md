# 6.2.2.1.3 Examples of Supervised Learning in Cardiology

There are a growing number of studies applying supervised ML algorithms to cardiology, including in personalized medicine, imaging, and disease prognostication. Personalized medicine involves tailoring investigations, treatments, and predictions to individual patients, thereby minimizing reductionism. Reductionism refers to treating all patients with the same disease states identically, irrespective of their genetics, lifestyle, background, and determinants of cardiovascular health [6]. One aspect of precision medicine involves creating a digital twin of the patient which integrates genomics, wearable devices, electronic health records, imaging data, and other data points to create a simulated model of the patient that is updated in real time through statistical and mechanistic modeling [2]. Traditional cardiovascular risk models extrapolate risk from a sample population, inevitably ignoring subtle clinical confounders. In contrast, AI algorithms can personalize risk calculations by including complex and subtle clinical factors.

One example is a study using supervised ML algorithms to predict 5-year all-cause mortality (ACM) in patients with supervised coronary artery disease (CAD) using cardiac computed tomographic angiography (CCTA) images [8]. Included in the development of this algorithm were 25 clinical and 44 CCTA parameters, and the algorithm was found to be superior to the Framingham risk score (FRS), segment stenosis score (SSS), segment involvement score (SIS), and the modified Duke index (MDI) in predicting 5-year ACM (ML: 0.79 vs. FRS: 0.61, SSS: 0.64, SIS: 0.64, DI: 0.62; p < 0.001) [8]. Another example is a study that combined CCTA stenosis and plaque measurements to develop a supervised ML algorithm to predict myocardial flow reserve (MFR) [9]. In the study, 153 arteries in 51 patients undergoing CCTA were analyzed to build this composite risk score and the algorithm was found to be superior to quantitative CCTA stenosis in predicting MFR by 13N-ammonia PET scan (ML AUC = 0.83, CTA stenosis = 0.66; p = 0.005) [9]. A similar multicenter trial evaluated a boosted ensemble ML algorithm built from quantitative CCTA measurements to predict lesion-specific ischemia [10]. In the trial, 254 patients underwent CCTA and invasive coronary angiography with fractional flow reserve (FFR) measurement in 484 vessels [10]. The ML algorithm had a superior area under the curve (AUC) of 0.84 compared with individual CCTA measurements (AUC: 0.76; p < 0.006) [10].

Supervised ML algorithms have also been applied to other cardiac imaging modalities. A recent study used a combination of supervised and deep learning to develop an ML algorithm using 91,232 single-lead ECGs from 53,549 patients to classify a single ambulatory electrocardiogram (ECG) monitoring lead into 12 rhythm classes with an AUC of 0.97, when tested in an independent data set with a consensus committee of board-certified cardiologists [11]. In echocardiography, supervised ML algorithms have been developed to identify and classify different echo views, including one study successfully classifying 15 standard views with an accuracy of 91.7% after training the supervised ML algorithm on 267 transthoracic echocardiogram (TTE) studies [12].
