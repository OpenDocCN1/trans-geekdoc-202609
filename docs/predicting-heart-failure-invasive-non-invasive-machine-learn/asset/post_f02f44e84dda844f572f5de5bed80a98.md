
## 11.3.2 PA Pressure Sensors

Unlike the RV pressure monitoring sensor (the Chronicle IHM), PA pressure sensors measure the diastolic PA pressure directly. The CardioMEMS (Cardio-Microelectromechanical) HF System developed by Abbott, St. Sylmar, CA, is a PA pressure sensor that is placed in any branch of the pulmonary artery. This sensor measures the diastolic, systolic, and mean pulmonary artery pressures every day. The collected data is then transferred over a secure network. The increase in the PA pressure is an indication of the onset of the decompensation and happens in advance of other clinical symptoms. The sensor is placed on the femoral vein during right heart catheterization and is powered externally. Unlike Chronicle IHM, CardioMEMS is MRI- and CRT-compatible with increased longevity.

The CHAMPIONS (CardioMEMS Heart Sensor Allows Monitoring of Pressure to Improve Outcomes in NYHA Class III Heart Failure Patients) trial was conducted in the US in 2011 to study the CardioMEMS sensor [57, 58]. The initial study included 550 patients who were NYHA class III, had had previous hospitalization, and had guideline-recommended HF therapy. The study group was divided into control and treatment groups. The pressure information is only available for the treatment group and not for the control group. Two studies – MEMS-HF (CardioMEMS European Monitoring Study for Heart Failure) and the US Post-Approval Study (PAS) – confirmed that CardioMEMS reduced the number of hospitalizations and improved the quality of life of the patients. CardioMEMS is the only FDA-approved invasive sensor for monitoring PA pressure with a European Conformity (CE) mark.

Yet another PA pressure sensor, the Cordella Pulmonary Artery Pressure Sensor System, has been developed by Endotronix, Inc., Chicago. Cordella is like CardioMEMS with additional benefits provided by the Cordella HF system. Some vital parameters, such as BP, oxygen saturation, weight, and heart rate are recorded in addition to the diastolic PA pressure. The studies are ongoing, with two clinical studies currently being carried out. The first is the SIRONA trial being conducted on 15 NYHA class III patients [59]. The trial did not show any complications or system-related failures. The values measured by the PAP sensors matched with the right heart catheterization at the end of the 90-day trial period. The second study, the European SIRONA II CE Mark Trial was conducted on 60 patients from the NYHA class II with compromised ventricle ejection. The PROACTIVE-HF IDE Trial is a similar trial being conducted in the US. Cordella is not FDA approved, does not possess the European Conformity mark, and is not available for commercial use.

## 11.3.3 LA Pressure Sensors

PA pressures may not be useful in all cases of heart failure. For example, it is difficult to manage patients with advanced CHF with only the knowledge of PA pressure. In this case, the LA pressure or pulmonary capillary wedge pressure has proved to be helpful. Abbott, formerly St. Jude Medical/Savacor, Inc., Abbott Park, IL, developed the HeartPOD LA pressure sensor. This sensor consists of a lead with an antenna coil and is implanted through the femoral vein in the left atrium. The antenna is then connected to the lead through subclavian or axillary veins. This device is compatible with CRT devices and is powered externally by a hand-held computer modified for this purpose. MRI compatibility and longevity are still unknown. A patient advisory module (PAM) is placed on the antenna which reminds patients to measure the pressure and take their medication. The collected data is made available to the physicians through secured networks.

A small clinical study with eight patients concluded that HeartPOD is safe, secure, and practical to use [60]. Another trial, called Hemodynamically Guided Home Self-Therapy in Severe Heart Failure Patients (HOMEOSTASIS), with 40 patients conducted a further study by isolating patients and not monitoring them for 3 months [61]. Later, the LAP data was used to optimize the treatment course. Another trial, the Left Atrial Pressure Monitoring to Optimize Heart Failure Therapy (LAPTOP-HF) trial, was based on the results from the HOMEOSTASIS trial.

Vectorious Medical Technologies, Tel Aviv, Israel developed a more advanced LAP sensor based on the findings of the LAPTOP-HF trial, named V-LAP system. The V-LAP Left Atrium Monitoring System for Patients With Chronic Systolic and Diastolic Congestive Heart Failure (VECTOR-HF) trial is currently being performed to study the V-LAP system.

Biosensors are used to measure a chemical or biological reaction by generating signals proportional to the amount and concentration of the analyte. Portable biosensors take the blood or serum to analyze different factors for analyzing and monitoring patients’ care. The detection of pregnancy by human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) using colorimetric pregnancy tests is the best example of portable biosensors. Wearable biosensors can be used to detect heart rate, BP, respiration rate, skin temperature, and brain activity. The accuracy and stability of the wearable biosensors are questionable. The privacy, security, and storage of the data collected by the biosensors is another factor to be considered. Invasive devices like pressure sensors have better accuracy and longer-term reliability compared with biosensors [62].

## 11.3.4 Intrathoracic Impedance Monitoring

Fluid overload is a common problem faced by heart failure patients and in severe cases can lead to hospitalization. Intrathoracic impedance monitoring can be correlated with fluid overload and can help in preventing hospitalization. The intrathoracic impedance is inverse to the pulmonary capillary wedge pressure and fluid balance. A decreased value of intrathoracic impedance indicates the onset of decompensation [63].

The OptiVol Fluid Status Monitor developed by Medtronic Inc. is used to monitor intrathoracic impedance. The impedance is measured over a period of 14 months to help the physician make informed decisions. A coil to the Can pathway is implanted in the thoracic cavity using the Consulta™ CRT-P. After measuring the impedance values for the entire day, the average impedance value is calculated. From all the available values, a reference impedance value is decided. The following clinical trials have been conducted to study the practical utility of OptiVol: MID-HeFT Trial [63], PARTNERS HF [64], OFISSER [65], FAST [66], and CONNECT [67]. The St. Jude Medical (SJM) CorVue™ congestion monitor is another advanced intrathoracic impedance monitor. ## 11.3.5 Commercially Available Devices for Heart Failure Treatment

Invasive devices which can help in reducing the risks in severe heart failure patients and reduce the chances of open-heart surgery are available on the market. Some of the newly FDA-approved devices that can help extensively in repairing heart failures are given in this section. First, is the LV assist device, HeartMate3, introduced by Abbott and approved by the FDA in October 2018. HeartMate3 has shown its effectiveness in multiple studies – a feasibility study and ELEVATE. Abbott's MitraClip can be used for advanced heart failure and mitral regurgitation also. The Impulse Dynamics Optimizer Smart System is used to strengthen the heart to beat on its own. Shunts are common in treating CHFs. Two shunts – Corvia's InterAtrial Shunt Device (IASD) and V-Wave InterAtrial Shunt Device are currently being considered by the FDA and clinical studies are ongoing.

## 11.4 Discussion

Early detection of the onset of CHF is crucial to reduce the number of hospitalizations, which in turn may become a burden to the healthcare system. Heart failure affects not only the healthcare system but also the life quality of patients. Many research studies conducted in the literature have displayed the effectiveness of non-invasive methods for monitoring heart failure patients. Among these, body weight measurement using a weighing scale seems to be the cheapest solution. However, body weight measurement for detecting fluid retention alone is not sufficient to differentiate the health conditions of heart failure patients. Instead of relying on body weight measurement, recent studies have examined the possibility of bioimpedance sensors for measuring body recomposition and detecting water retention. Furthermore, radio frequency sensors have also shown their effectiveness in estimating pulmonary fluid levels.

Most non-invasive monitoring devices rely on internet services for data transmission and patient monitoring. Even though existing devices are equipped with the capability to store the data locally in the absence of the internet and transmit it to the remote server once the internet connection is restored, steady internet service is required in the patient's premises for the uninterrupted working of the monitoring system. The lack of uninterrupted internet services in rural areas hinders the usage of these devices. Various physiological values, including BP, heart rate variability, ECG, etc., are combined in multiple research works to remotely monitor heart failure patients and obtained results have shown the monitoring effectiveness. Currently, smartwatches and smart wearables have sensors for measuring pressure, heart rate variability, ECG, etc. Some studies have found that the sensors embedded in smartwatches have acceptable accuracy. However, more studies about these embedded sensors are required to show their efficacy for heart failure monitoring tasks.
