Biomarkers are biological materials found in bodily fluids, and they indicate the normal or pathological processes or responses of the body [18]. Biomarkers are critical tools for diagnosing and monitoring many diseases. CVDs involve various disorders, both acute and chronic. The effective, accurate, and rapid diagnosis of acute conditions with biomarkers in the short term can be life-saving [19]. Besides this, for evaluating long-term risks related to CVDs, which can occur in the later stages of patients’ lives, biomarkers are widely used to improve patient’s life quality. Cardiovascular research research with biomarkers includes, in addition to diagnostic studies, post-disease risk and damage monitoring, such as evaluation of damages after stroke, the severity of cardiac damage, and cardiac arrest-related brain injury in Figure 3.2 [20].

There are various biomarkers available for cardiovascular research that have been used for years and are emerging and new. In Table 3.1, the most significant and specific biomarkers for cardiovascular research are classified.

It is possible to categorize biomarkers based on the physiologic processes. For example, inflammation-related biomarkers are CRP, sST2, GDF-15, TNF-α, etc. LDL and MPO are related to oxidative stress [24]. Considered from another angle, lipid-based biomarkers (HDL, LDL, etc.), CRP, and cTnI are considered widely used traditional biomarkers, while sSTN2, TNF-α, Gal-3, and GDF-15 are emerging biomarkers. Copentin is another promising biomarker for cardiovascular research, and there are ongoing studies on copetin that demonstrate its use as a diagnostic biomarker for MI, in addition to the well-known and clinically established biomarkers such as myoglobin and cTnI [22, 34]. Stability and ease of measure of copectin are considered an alternative biomarker. However, the changes on the levels of copetin during the perioperative course should be monitored for establishing copetin as a guiding biomarker [34, 35]. On the other hand, myoglobin, H-FABP, and MPO are add-on biomarkers that are more valuable with a multibiomarker approach [30].

Essentially, an ideal cardiovascular biomarker should be highly specific with definite reference limits in order to provide valuable information for clinical assessment [24].
