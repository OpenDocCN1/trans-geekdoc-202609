Similarly, a study to predict right-ventricular failure (RVF) following left ventricular assist device (LVAD) therapy using a tree-augmented Naive Bayes model, demonstrated an accuracy of up to 97% [77]. Bayesian prognostic models of RVF applied to patient data from the Inter-Agency Registry for Mechanically Assisted Circulatory Support (INTERMACS) who had an implanted LVAD, were shown to be highly accurate in predicting acute, early, as well as late RVF with sensitivity of around 90%, and achieving a maximum specificity of 99% [77]. The Bayesian model was also shown to outperform previous risk scores such as the right ventricular failure risk score [78] (RVFRS) and Drakos [79], obtained by multivariable logistic regression analysis, when applied to the INTERMACS registry (AUC 54.7% and 49.8%, respectively) [77]. Another study by the same group showed that Bayesian prognostic models applied to the INTERMACS registry accurately (76–87%) predicted mortality as well, with an AUC ranging between 0.70–0.71 [80]. Furthermore, they developed a novel Bayesian model for risk stratification and prediction of mortality at different time points, that is 30 days, 90 days, 6 months, 1 year and 2 years, post-LVAD implantation [81]. The AI algorithm exhibited mortality prediction accuracies of 95, 90, 90, 83, and 78%, at each of the five time-points, respectively, with AUCs of 91, 82, 82, 80, and 81%, respectively, performing significantly better than the traditional logistic regression based HeartMate II Risk Score [82] (AUC 57 and 60%, at 90 days and 1 year, respectively) [81].

There are a number of implantable monitoring devices focused on the management of HF, such as CardioMEMs [83, 84], which are implantable impedance monitors [85] to gauge fluid accumulation [86, 87] and dedicated ML approaches to analyze data from CRT devices, LVADs, and implantable cardioverter defibrillator (ICDs) [22, 32, 33, 43, 45, 88]. While devices like CardioMEMs have shown reduction in HF readmissions [12, 72], a major limitation of these devices is that they are invasively implanted, costly upfront, and demand additional personnel and resources to handle the device implantation and data acquisition. In addition, invasive monitoring devices require specialized hardware and data extraction features, often making them cumbersome and impractical for real-time monitoring and assessment of active, ambulatory patients, especially those who live in underserved areas. Furthermore, their cost-effectiveness remains debatable [89–91], highlighting the need for affordable non-invasive approaches for HF monitoring enabled with effective AI based disease prediction strategies.

# 12.6 Non-Invasive Wearable Monitoring Devices for Prediction of HF

The utility of wearable monitoring devices [92–96] in cardiovascular healthcare and, in particular, in HF management has been well established [16, 97–99]. Ambulatory non-invasive monitoring devices that assess various physiological parameters, including blood pressure [100], photoplethysmograms (PPGs) [101], seismocardiograms [17], ECG [95, 102], electromagnetic energy-based dielectric properties [103], oxygen saturation [104], and fluid status, weight, activity, and bioimpedance [105], have enabled access to large amounts of patient data which are ideally suited for applying AI algorithms and predicting HF onset or deterioration (Table 12.2; 98, 106–110).
