There have been studies that proposed the use of selfies and machine learning models to detect heart abnormalities such as coronary artery diseases (CADs). Facial appearance has long been identified as a marker of cardiovascular risk, with features such as male pattern baldness, earlobe crease, xanthelasmata, and skin wrinkling being the most common predictors. Information extracted from the analysis of an individual’s facial photo utilizing the proposed technology can unquestionably benefit the individual, the attending physician, and the healthcare system altogether. Early detection of individuals at risk for CAD can initiate lifestyle and other personal mitigation approaches, guide medication treatment, and inspire a novel approach in diagnostic testing and screening algorithms for the general population. Despite these challenges, the full potential of such novel and out-of-the-box diagnostics lies ahead to the research community. The framework proposed in [77] suggested the use of facial images and deep learning techniques for early detection of patients with risk of CAD (Figure 13.11).

Lin et al. [78] evaluated this framework, where they conducted a multicenter cross-sectional study of patients undergoing coronary angiography or computed tomography angiography at nine Chinese sites to train and validate a deep convolutional neural network for the detection of CAD (at least one > 50% stenosis) from patient facial photos. The performance was compared with radiologist diagnosis. Results concluded that a deep learning algorithm based on facial photos could assist in CAD detection in the Chinese cohort. This technique holds promise for pre-test CAD probability assessment in outpatient clinics or CAD screening in the community. Table 13.2 summarizes the findings of different studies.

## 13.7 Future Directions of Wearables for Early Prediction of HF

It is well known that an ECG of healthy individuals can have morphological variabilities/abnormalities at the baseline with variable prevalence. Similarly, patients following cardiac abnormalities can develop persistent abnormal ECG changes, which can be detected early if they are continuously monitored. This is where machine learning can play a really important role. Machine learning models can be used to detect heart abnormalities at an early stage, but the models should be made aware of how abnormalities might look and that they might be different for different patients. The authors are working on such a machine learning paradigm that can detect cardiac abnormalities for a specific patient early and can instruct the patient, along with their doctor and care-giver, to take precautionary actions (Figure 13.12).

Such a system would adhere to the following framework: (i) continuously record the ECG signals of a patient using a chest strap or patch, (ii) the existing benchmark database will be used to develop a machine learning model to classify the abnormal heart condition, (iii) the existing data set of HF patients will always have healthy and unhealthy ECG signals; healthy ECG signals of a person will be used to generate unhealthy signals of that patient and will be validated with the existing database, (iv) real-time acquired
