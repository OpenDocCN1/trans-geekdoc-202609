# Table 3.1 Specific biomarkers for the evaluation of CVDs.

| Biomarker | Description | Related CVD | Clinical Assessment | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-reactive protein (CRP) | CRP is a hepatic, acute-phase protein that is related to systemic inflammation; it is used for the early-stage detection of CVDs | Coronary heart disease, thrombotic complications, atherosclerosis, and acute coronary syndrome | Elevated CRP levels indicate a higher risk for adverse cardiovascular events (CRP < 1 mg/L low risk, 1 < CRP < 3 mg/L moderate risk, CRP > 3 mg/L high risks) | [12, 17, 21, 22] |
| Cardiac troponin I (cTnI) | cTnI inhibits actomyosin ATPase, and is released by necrosis of cardiac muscle cells | Acute myocardial infarction and ischemic stroke | Increased cTnI levels are related to the acute phase of acute myocardial infarction and ischemic stroke | [20, 23, 24] |
| Procalcitonin (PCT) | PCT is an acute phase reactant and a circulating neurohumoral biomarker | Acute heart failure and acute decompensated heart failure | Higher PCT levels are associated with the characterization and early diagnosis of heart failure | [18, 24, 25] |
| Brain natriuretic peptide (BNP) | BNP is a peptide structured neurohormone, and it has vasodilator, lusitropic and antiproliferative effects | Acute and chronic heart failure, ventricular hypertrophy, cerebral infarction, and cardioembolic stroke | BNP blood level higher than 20 pg/mL is associated with a serious risk of future CVDs. BNP levels are monitored to evaluate cardiac response to treatment | [26–29] |
| Neuron-specific enolase (NSE) | NSE is a glycolytic enzyme that is specific to the brain. Normally, it is present at negligible levels | Stroke, brain injury after cardiac arrest | Evaluation of infarct volume and integrity of the blood-brain barrier | [20] |
| Copeptin | Copeptin is an emerging and novel biomarker, which is also a precursor peptide of arginine vasopressin | Heart failure and acute coronary syndrome | Copeptin level is used to exclude myocardial infarction (MI) and evaluate perioperative myocardial injury risk | [17, 18, 22, 30] |
| Myoglobin | Myoglobin is responsible for binding, transporting, and storing oxygen, and is a non-enzymatic protein | Acute MI | Most reliable and efficient biomarker for early-stage diagnosis of MI; very high levels of myoglobin (up to 900 ng/mL) during MI | [21, 31] |
