Removing the lithium battery as a permanent energizing source can potentially decrease the microchip size. In addition, the power unit (batteries) can cause a high risk of leakage, resulting in severe health risks to older people and necessitating regular replacement. A safe solution to this potential problem involves developing highly efficient wireless energy methods that can charge anytime and anywhere via the radio frequency coming from WiFi signals, television stations, etc. in our environment.

The power-collecting circuit includes one or more multiband antennae to collect any RF signals. The printed circuit board (PCB) is implanted in the body and connected to the biosensor battery. We can charge the microchip battery by collecting radio frequency energy in the environment. Unlike a battery-powered system, wireless battery charging minimizes periodic battery change [12–14]. Much research presents inductive correlation coupling [15] as a potential approach to wireless power charging. The non-surgical energy source is the best and most effective, along with developing a dedicated technology to transmit essential information during the charging method. The standard potentiostat comprises three electrodes: counter electrode, working electrode, and reference electrode. The difference potential between the reference electrodes and the working electrodes catalyzes the chemical reactivity. The counter electrode is related to the output signal provided to the processing unit.
