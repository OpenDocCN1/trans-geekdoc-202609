Moreover the kidney and liver function have to be tested [28]. Apart from a detailed medical history, the electrocardiogram (ECG) is fundamental to reveal arrhythmias, conduction delays, or ischemia, while the echocardiogram is also a standard test to confirm systolic or diastolic dysfunction [29]. Also, the initial evaluation may include a chest X-ray, a coronary angiography (or a computed tomography coronary angiography (CTCA)) when coronary artery disease is suspected. In addition, cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging has become a very useful tool for investigating HF due to its a high spatial resolution and the use of late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) [30, 31]. Static images using T1 and T2 weighted black blood algorithms are valuable for tissue characterization. With CMR, clinicians are informed about anatomic structures, perfusion, and ventricular function while LGE can specify the exact causes of HF, depending on the pattern of scar formation such as amyloidosis, sarcoid, eosinophilic myocarditis, etc. and provide important prognostic information beyond other biomarkers [32, 33].

The measurement of serial natriuretic peptide (B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) or N-terminal pro–B-type natriuretic peptide (NTproBNP)) is also included in the HF-specific laboratory tests (specifically in patients with suspected HF) in order to address the management of HF. According to the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) [5], systolic HF can be effectively ruled out with the levels of BNP or NTproBNP. As BNP levels increase, the specificity increases and the diagnosis of HF becomes easier [34]. Standard management of the HF patient involves a follow-up 2–12 times a year and patients are advised to self-monitor their weight and symptoms.

# ### 9.1.2.1 ML in Medicine

Medicine has always been characterized by (big) data that is suitable for the application of ML. ML is appropriate to clinical research since it is more flexible than traditional statistical methods. ML provides great potential for healthcare experts to exploit massive amounts of data from numerous sources, integrate them in a single system, curate them, and provide assessment and recommendations.

ML models have the potential to improve healthcare efficiency in numerous ways. Prognostic models could empower healthcare experts to select better treatment options for their patients. Additionally, diagnostic models could be used in screening, risk stratification, and recommending appropriate tests. This would decrease the burden on clinicians, save resources, and reduce costs.

According to Obermeyer and Emanuel [35], increasing the ability to transform data into knowledge will affect at least three areas of medicine: (i) it will dramatically improve prognosis, (ii) it will improve diagnostic accuracy and (iii) it will reduce much of the work of radiologists and anatomical pathologists, since these clinicians mainly focus on analyzing digitized images that can easily be processed by algorithms instead.
