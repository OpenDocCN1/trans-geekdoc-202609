The ECG patch or multisensory patch attached to the patient’s body will record multiple sensors’ data or ECG data and send it to the mobile device installed with a software application via Bluetooth or WiFi. The mobile device software is configured to transmit the data to the monitoring center via the internet. The monitor center will receive the data and analyze it to ensure that there is no abnormal event. If any abnormal events are detected, the monitor center will provide emergency instructions to the patient with the help of a qualified doctor. Unlike Holter monitors, MCT devices can be attached to the body of the patient for recording the heart rhythm for up to 30 days. Several research studies have been conducted to demonstrate the benefits of MCT devices over event monitors and Holter monitors. The comparative study conducted by Tsang and Mohan [20] shows that the diagnostic yield of arrhythmia is significantly higher in patients monitored with MCT devices compared to the patients monitored with event monitors and Holter monitors. The study was conducted on a total of more than 200,000 patients over a 5-year period.

Both MCT devices and Holter monitors are designed to assist the cardiologist in monitoring patients with heart diseases. However, there are distinctive differences between them in terms of the monitoring period, recording method, data storage, data analysis, size of devices, etc. In the case of the monitoring period, usually, the Holter monitors are configured to monitor the heart activity for a maximum of 72 hours while MCT devices are assigned for 30 days. There are exceptions in the Holter monitor’s monitoring period. In a few cases, the Holter monitors are prescribed for 7–14 days. Compared with MCT devices, the limited wear time of the Holter monitor reduces the inconvenience to patients caused by monitoring devices. However, the extended wear time of MCT devices is beneficial for asymptomatic patients in certain cases, for example detecting arrhythmia that may not occur during the short period in which a Holter monitor is being worn. Generally, the Holter monitoring is conducted in a manner where the device is assigned to record the heart activities for a given period and the data recorded in the device are analyzed once the monitoring period is over. In Holter monitors assigned for 7–14 days, any abnormalities recorded during the monitoring period will not be addressed and analyzed till the monitoring period is over and the device is returned to the clinic. On the other hand, the MCT devices are configured to continuously record the heart activity and send the recorded data to the server or remote analysis center for instantaneous analysis. The instantaneous recording and monitoring capability of MCT devices enable immediate assistance for the patient if any abnormalities are detected. In this context, MCT devices are more beneficial than Holter monitors for patients with a history of heart failure symptoms as well as for patients suffering from acute heart failure conditions.

In traditional Holter monitors, the data are stored locally using an internal chip. Local storage is enough for the Holter monitor due to the short monitoring period. In old devices, cassettes were used to store the recorded data. Some of the latest Holter monitors have the functionality to backup and store the recorded data in the cloud. The MCT devices allow both local and cloud storage. Usually, the data are sent to the cloud for immediate analysis. However, certain conditions, such as the absence of internet connectivity, affect the cloud storage process.
