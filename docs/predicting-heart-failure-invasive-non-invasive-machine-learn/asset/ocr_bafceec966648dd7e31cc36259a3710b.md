# 8.8.2 Prediction of 30-Day HF Readmission

HF readmission is a major healthcare problem. Patient deterioration is correlated with 30-day readmission and significant costs are attached to it. The early prediction of readmission risk can contribute to the reduction of this problem. Scheduling early follow-up based on this predictive model can play an important role in preventing patient readmission and health complications. J. Frizzel et al. proposed a comparison of MLapproaches for this task [57, 58]. The following models were compared: tree-augmented Naive Bayesian network (TAN), an RF algorithm, a gradient-boosted model (GBM), and logistic regression models (LR and LASSO). The baseline models are statistical models built using EHR data (EHR model) [59] and administrative claims model (CMS) [60]. The cohort included 56,477 patients from the WGTG-HF data set. The study population's overall 30-day readmission rate was 21.2%. The variables included in the analysis were extracted from patient EHRs and cover demographics, socioeconomic status, medical history, characterization of HF, lab results, etc. In total, about 250 variables were used as features in the analysis. The data set was randomly divided into training (70%) and validation (30%). The models' performance was assessed based on the validation set. The C statistic was used as a performance measure to distinguish between a readmission or not. The performance of the ML models and the statistical models are respectively TAN(0.62), LR(0.62), LASSO(0.62), RF(0.61), GBM(0.61), EHR model (0.59), and CMS (0.6). The experimental results revealed that there is no significant difference between ML models and statistical models in this context. One limitation of this study is the use of only C statistics as performance metrics. It is possible that additional metrics might better reveal a difference between the studied models.
