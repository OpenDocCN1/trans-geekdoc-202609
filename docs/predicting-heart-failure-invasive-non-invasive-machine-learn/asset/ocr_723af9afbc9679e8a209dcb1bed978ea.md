In 12-lead ECG measurements, 10 electrodes are placed on limbs and the chest. The electric potential of the heart is measured by taking capitalizing on the different angles between the heart and electrodes

Measurements are generally recorded for 10 seconds so that the electrical depolarization is monitored during the entire cardiac cycle [32, 33]. The main components of ECG signals are P-wave, QRS complex, and T-wave.

Several ECG parameters are utilized for predicting HF and also its worsening in HF patients [3]. The most-used ECG parameters are reduced heart rate variability [34], increased heart rate [35], increase in QRS/T angle [36], atrial and ventricular tachyarrhythmias [37, 38], increased ECG left ventricle mass index [28, 39], QT prolongation [40], increased QRS duration [41, 42], and left ventricle strain [20, 43, 44]. Reduced heart rate variability can be determined using the time differences in heart rate between day and night [34]. The heart rate variability can be determined by examining the standard deviation of sequential 5-minute R-R interval means. If the ECG left ventricle mass exceeds 70 ± 9 g/m² in men and 61 ± 8 g/m² in women [28, 39], or the corrected QR duration is longer than 450 ms in men and 460 ms in women [40], there is a potential risk factor for the presence of HF. QRS durations higher than 100 ms [41, 42, 45], T-wave inversions, and ST segment depression [20] are also evaluated as indications of HF.

# 5.2.3 Bioimpedance Monitoring

Bioimpedance analysis is a non-invasive method for assessing the body composition and fluid overload [46]. Impedance is an electrical parameter defining the resistance to the electrical current flow. In bioimpedance monitoring, a high-frequency and low-amplitude electrical current is used in the body to measure the bioimpedances [47]. Body tissues and fluids have different resistances which change the flow behavior of the electrical current. In an HF decompensation scenario, fluid retention leads to pulmonary congestion, and this fluid overload in the lungs influences the bioimpedance of the thoracic area. The blood and fluids have lower resistance to electrical current flow compared to thoracic tissues and air. This means that bioimpedance decreases with increasing fluid content. Therefore, there is an inverse correlation between the bioimpedance and the amount of fluid [48–50]. Healthy and relatively dry lungs are expected to have higher impedances than lungs with pulmonary edema that develop as a result of HF [51]. The bioimpedance monitoring is important for HF patients, because the thoracic impedance changes start in advance of the observation of serious symptoms and hospitalization.

The traditional non-invasive bioimpedance analysis, also known as transthoracic impedance cardiography, is performed using four or six electrodes placed on the skin for measuring the changes in thorax resistance. Low-amplitude constant electrical current is sent from one of the electrodes and measured by the other electrodes, enabling the voltage difference and impedance through the thorax to be determined [47].
