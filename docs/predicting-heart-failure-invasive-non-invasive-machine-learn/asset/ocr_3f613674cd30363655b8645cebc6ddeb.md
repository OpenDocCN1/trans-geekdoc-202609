Classification supervised ML algorithms have also been studied in echocardiography to differentiate hypertrophic cardiomyopathy from physiologic hypertrophy, and constrictive cardiomyopathy from restrictive cardiomyopathy [13–15]. In CCTA, supervised ML algorithms have also been applied to automatically quantify coronary artery calcification [16]. Algorithms have also been used to identify patients with hemodynamically significant CAD by analyzing the left ventricle (LV) [17]. For example, one study analyzed CCTA scans from 166 patients who underwent invasive FFR and used a series of ML algorithms to segment, classify, and identify ischemic changes in the LV myocardium [17]. After that, a SVM classifier was used to categorize patients according to the presence of functionally significant stenosis with an AUC of 0.74 when compared with invasive hemodynamic testing [17].

Another case of supervised ML algorithms is in disease prognostication. For example, algorithms have been successfully used to predict which patients with CAD will eventually undergo early revascularization [18]. A recent study evaluated 713 myocardial perfusion scans (MPSs) and associated invasive angiography to compare an ML algorithm with visual analysis by two experienced readers [18]. It found the specificity of the algorithm in predicting early both revascularization and 3-year major adverse cardiovascular events was superior (74.7 ± 4.2%) [18].

Other examples of regression-based supervised ML learning include a recent study illustrating that three-dimensional (3D) cardiac motion can predict survival in patients with newly diagnosed pulmonary hypertension [19]. In this study, 256 patients with newly diagnosed pulmonary hypertension underwent cardiac MRI, right heart catheterization and a 6-minute walk test [19]. A supervised ML algorithm was then applied to a 3D model of right ventricular motion and better predicted survival compared with conventional imaging, hemodynamic, functional, and clinical markers (AUC = 0.73 vs 0.60; p < 0.001) [19]. The study found impaired contractile motion in anatomically distinct but functionally synergistic areas of the right ventricle provided early signals as to the severity of pulmonary hypertension and hence mortality [19].

# 6.2.2.2 Unsupervised Learning

Unsupervised learning is a more advanced form of learning using unlabeled data to discover underlying relationships; it is often referred to as knowledge discovery [7]. Unsupervised learning holds great promise in clinical practice where coding large data sets can be prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. Common examples of unsupervised learning techniques include clustering, dimensionality reduction, association, and anomaly detection [2].

Clustering involves dividing objects into groups that share similar characteristics with the aim of finding subgroups within a larger data set. The most commonly used techniques include hierarchical clustering, K-means clustering, density-based spatial clustering of applications with noise, and model-based clustering [7]. Irrelevant features can compromise the efficacy, efficiency, and utility of ML algorithms and dimensionality reduction is a technique used to minimize irrelevant features by identifying the most important features in a data set [20]. Anomaly detection is a technique that identifies outliers in a data set and helps unsupervised ML algorithms remove anomalies and erroneous data entries [6].
