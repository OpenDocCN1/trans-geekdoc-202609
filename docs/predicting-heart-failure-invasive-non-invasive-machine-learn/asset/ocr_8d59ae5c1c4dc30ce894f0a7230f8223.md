# 7.1 Introduction

Great attention has been paid to medical research to identify heart disease. Heart disease diagnosis is based on the patient's signs and symptoms and is affected by several factors such as cholesterol level, blood pressure, obesity, smoking habit, and other factors [1]. This creates a challenge to provide quality services with low costs. Quality service involves diagnosing patients properly to get effective treatments. According to the World Heart Federation and the World Health Organization, heart diseases are top of the list of various life-threatening diseases, taking around 17.9 million lives each year [2]. However, 90% of those deaths were estimated to have been preventable if patients had been correctly diagnosed early and had improved their habits [3].

In traditional healthcare environments, diagnosing heart diseases is done in the conventional way, whereby physicians review the symptoms and order some tests, such as blood tests, electrocardiogram, and echocardiogram. However, this conventional way leads to uncertainty of diagnosis, which in turn produces complicated therapy and more medical costs. The misdiagnosis results from many physicians not following the known guidelines for evaluating and managing heart failure [4]. Therefore, the high rates of inaccuracy reported in different studies are not surprising [5].

The core element for treatment success is a reliable and precise diagnosis. Expert systems [6] could be applied to emulate the ability of experts to answer not only simple questions like “What is the average age of patients who have heart disease?” or “Identify the patients who have been treated for heart diseases and complaining from other diseases” but also complex ones like “Given patient records, predict the probability of patients who will be diagnosed with a heart disease?”or “Find the most significant risk factor that results in a heart disease?” Using such systems could decrease medical examination errors, reduce practice variation, and enhance diagnosis outcomes.
