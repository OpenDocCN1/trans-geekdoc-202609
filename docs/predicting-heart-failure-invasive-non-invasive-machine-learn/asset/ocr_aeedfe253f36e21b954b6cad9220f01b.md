These promising early studies highlight the utility of AI based technologies to detect and assess HF progression using wearable monitoring technologies. However, there remains substantial untapped potential for using novel ML/DL strategies to identify and predict HF risk using non-invasive remote monitoring devices, which warrants further large-scale multicenter trials to evaluate the clinical efficacy of AI enabled wearables for improved HF management (Figure 12.1).

# 12.7 Future Outlook on the Use of AI

Computational advances have led to the development of numerous AI approaches for clinical data analyses [114] to assist HF diagnosis and prediction (Table 12.1). Multiple ML/DL strategies with an accuracy of over 90% in classifying HF patients and predicting adverse events have been proposed. However, studies comparing the clinical efficacy of AI based HF prediction models with conventional diagnostic methods have shown mixed results. While some newer predictive models have shown improved performance over conventional methods in classifying and identifying HF patients [22], other studies have shown no improvement in predicting HF readmissions using ML compared to traditional statistical approaches [45, 115]. This discrepancy is due to the fact that the accuracy of AI approaches is greatly influenced by the availability of large amounts of data that can enable optimal feature extraction to efficiently identify patterns in underlying data. Indeed, it has been shown that, while AI algorithms provide good discrimination in identifying HF patients based on demographics and medical history, HF classification efficiency can be affected by the subtleties of the disease progression state (e.g. preserved ejection fraction, mid-range ejection fraction, or reduced ejection fraction) and variability in patient characteristics (e.g. age, gender) [22]. Yet, while novel ML/DL approaches continue to be investigated to address these challenges, improved AI strategies have shown promise in outperforming conventional regression models in predicting HF outcomes and have also demonstrated better accuracy in predicting mortality due to acute HF and HF with preserved ejection fraction [22, 116, 117].

In parallel, remote monitoring devices have seen an uptick in the past decade, with the proliferation of low-cost vital sign monitors available for ambulatory real-time assessment of patients’ physiological state. From smartwatches, electronic finger-rings, and smart garments to dedicated wearable cardiac and muscle activity sensors, miniaturized medical grade ambulatory monitoring devices are readily available to assist patient care. Yet, there is sparse availability of devices that merge these two technologies and fully utilize the powers of AI and wearable monitors for cardiac disease management, in particular HF prediction.

This technological gap in medical treatment strategies is expected to be bridged by the embedding of AI algorithms in wearable ambulatory devices, a transition that will enable superior classification and prediction capabilities of cardiovascular disease progression, thus accelerating the transition to precision medicine for all population groups.
