## 4.3 Wireless Power Transmission

Wireless electricity transfer technology transfers power from one place to another without physical support [19] and is therefore beneficial for transmitting electricity to sites and devices that are difficult to energize, such as implant sensors. In this subsection, we will highlight contemporary media for wireless power transmission. There are typically four technologies used for wireless energy transmission, depending on the physical energy grandeur sent [20, 21]:

- Microwave wireless power transfer
- Electromagnetic induction energy transfer
- Laser wireless power transmission
- Ultrasonic wireless power transmission

### 4.3.3 Electromagnetic Induction Energy Transfer

Electromagnetic inductive power transmission systems define techniques where inductive power is transferred from a transmission coil to a receiver coil using an alternating magnetic field [25, 26]. Various products utilize electromagnetic induction energy transmission, including mobile phones, trolleys, and cars. The concept of an inductive electricity transmission method is straightforward and integrable. The original design comprises an alternating current (AC) source, a transmission coil, a receiver coil, a rectenna, and the biosensor. The relation between the transmission and receiver coils creates electromagnetic energy. The main advantage of electromagnetic induction wireless power transmission is high efficiency at a low cost. The concept of an induction relation is simple, and highly efficient at small distances. The significant disadvantages of this technology are that the amount of energy transmitted is limited to the geometry of the coil, and the distance between the coils must not be multiples of a few millimeters.
