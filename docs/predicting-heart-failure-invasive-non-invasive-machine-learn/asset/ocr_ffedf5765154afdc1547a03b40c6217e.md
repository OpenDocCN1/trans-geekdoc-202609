Wang et al. [90] proposed a feature rearrangement-based DL system for predicting in-hospital mortality, and 30-day and 1-year mortality after discharge. The data set used in this study was collected from the EHR system of a hospital in Shanghai. They utilized 10,203 records with 539 death records for in-hospital mortality, 6260 records with 652 death records for 1-month mortality, and 4353 records with 1182 death records for 1-year mortality. Their method scored 81.48% sensitivity and 90.37% AUC for in-hospital mortality, 80.01% sensitivity and 89.13% AUC for 1-month mortality, and 72.15% sensitivity and 89.13% AUC for 1-year mortality. Yang et al. [91] selected 506 HF patients, with more than 25,000 features, consisting of demographic information, electrocardiography measurements, index of HF, echocardiography, and advanced laboratory biomarkers. The RF model performed better on predicting all-cause mortality (AUC: 75.00%). Adler et al. [92] discriminated between low and high risk of death by identifying eight low-cost and commonly available features (diastolic blood pressure, blood urea nitrogen, creatinine, hemoglobin, white blood cell count, platelets, albumin, and red blood cell distribution width) from 5822 patients. A boosted decision tree achieved AUC 88.00%. Jing et al. [93] conducted a large-scale study on 26,971 subjects to predict 1-year all-cause mortality in patients with HF. They collected 26 clinical variables (demographic information, laboratory findings, medications), 90 diagnostic codes, 41 ECG features, 44 echocardiographic features, and eight evidence-based “care gaps” e.g. flu vaccine, blood pressure, and cardiac resynchronization therapy. They tested various ML models that achieved AUC 74.00% to 77.00%, with the nonlinear XGBoost model (AUC: 77.00%) outperforming the linear logistic regression (AUC: 74.00%).

# 9.1.2.7 ML Applications in Mortality or Readmission Prediction

Furthermore, a large number of studies focus on developing models for jointly predicting adverse events, i.e. mortality or rehospitalization in patients with HF (Table 9.5).

In 2013, Zhang et al. [94] utilized decision trees and LR to predict death risk or hospitalization due to worsening HF at one year. The mortality prediction had an accuracy of 89.20% for decision trees and 85.80% for LR and the composite outcome prediction had an accuracy of 79.70% for decision trees and 73.80% for the LR model. Their data set consisted of 284 patients for training and 160 patients for validation from the TEN-HMS (Trans-European Network Homecare Monitoring System) study, utilizing as input features demographics, physical examination, laboratory findings, medical history, quality of life, and EuroQoL. NT-proBNP was the feature with the strongest prediction of mortality.

In 2017, Tripoliti et al. [95] presented an ML based method for the prediction of adverse events (mortality and relapses) in patients with HF by analyzing breath and saliva biomarkers from 27 subjects. The prediction of adverse events achieved 77.00% accuracy using an ROT algorithm.

Awan et al. [96] in 2019, conducted a study on HF patients aged over 65 years and in hospital, where 23.60% of the patients were readmitted or died within 1 month of hospital discharge. They developed an MLP-based approach to predict 1-month HF readmission or death, achieving AUC 62.00%, sensitivity 48.00% and specificity 70.00%.
