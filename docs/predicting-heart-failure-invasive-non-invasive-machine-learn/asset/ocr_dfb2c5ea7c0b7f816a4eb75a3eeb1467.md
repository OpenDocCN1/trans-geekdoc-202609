Furthermore, many studies focused on detecting HF based on heart sound characteristics. A healthy heart has two sounds: the first heart sound (S1) and the second heart sound (S2). Extra heart sounds can be found in both normal and abnormal situations. The third heart sound, or protodiastolic gallop, (S3) that correlates with dysfunction or volume overload of the ventricles and the fourth heart sound (S4) that reflects left ventricular stiffness are both associated with HF [44]. In 2015, Zheng et al. [45] suggested a system for chronic HF prediction based on the analysis of cardiac reserve and heart sound characteristics. They applied the LS-SVM (least-squares SVM) classifier in 88 control subjects and 64 with chronic HF with an accuracy of 95.39%, sensitivity 96.59%, and specificity 93.75% of the proposed system for the detection of chronic HF. In 2020, Gjoreski et al. [46] combined classic ML and end-to-end DL to detect chronic HF. The classic ML learned from a large set of features and the DL learned both from the time- domain representation of the signal and the spectral representation of the signal. Their model achieved an accuracy of 92.90%, sensitivity 82.30%, and specificity 96.20%.

A large number of studies focused on predicting HF utilizing HRV measures. Lal et al. [47], Wang et al. [48], and Chen et al. [49] utilized HRV measures to predict congestive HF. Lal et al. implemented an SVM model with 93.10% accuracy on 116 subjects (44 with congestive HF and 72 controls), while Wang et al. applied the long short-term memory (LSTM) network on 156 subjects (44 with congestive HF and 112 controls) achieving 99.22% accuracy. Chen et al. applied the sparse auto-encoder-based deep learning (SAE- based DL) algorithm on 116 subjects (44 with congestive HF and 72 controls), and the results showed that the model achieved 72.41% accuracy. In 2020, Pires et al. [50] analyzed various features of heart rate from 270 subjects by applying NN, a C4.5 decision tree, k-NN, SVM, and stochastic gradient descent (SGD). The highest accuracy (87.69%) was achieved with the implementation of fivefold cross-validation with SGD, the same as was achieved with 20-fold cross-validation and 10-fold cross-validation implementing C4.5 and SVM.

In addition, other researchers have focused on analyzing ECG signals. Masetic et al. in 2016 [51] implemented C4.5, k-NN, SVM, artificial NN, and random forest (RF), classifying ECG signals from 18 patients with congestive HF and 13 controls. RF outperformed the others with 100.00% accuracy. In 2019, Acharya et al. [52] implemented a convolutional neural network (CNN) on ECG signals from 73 patients (15 with congestive HF and 58 controls) with accuracy 98.97%, specificity 99.01%, and sensitivity 98.87%. Also in 2019, Kim et al. [53] analyzed ECG features data from 2209 subjects with chronic HFrEF (1006 subjects) and HFmrEF (1103 subjects) and 18,196 controls. They applied a DL-based analysis with 98.00% specificity and 95.00% positive predictive value. In the same year, Kwon et al. [54] developed an algorithm using a deep neural network (DNN). They analyzed ECG features and demographic information (age, sex, weight, height), heart rate, the presence of atrial fibrillation (AF) or atrial flutter (AFL), QT interval, and QRS duration in 22,765 patients, of whom 1391 had HFrEF. The AUC for identification of HFrEF was 84.30%. In a 2020 study, Ning et al. [55] analyzed ECG signals from 15 chronic HF patients and 18 controls, applying a DL technique which consisted of a CNN and an RNN with an accuracy of 99.93%, sensitivity 99.85%, and specificity 100.00%.
