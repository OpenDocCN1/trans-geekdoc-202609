## 11.2.2.1.1 Electrocardiography-Based Monitoring Systems

There are many types of external electrocardiography-based systems available, one of them being the Holter monitor. Holter monitors are small, wearable electrocardiography devices that contain multiple electrodes and data recording devices. A general diagram of a Holter monitor is given in Figure 11.1.

![](img/721c7a2c71f4dc63a8585e2ef46838f7_259_0.png)

The purpose of a Holter monitor is to record the ECGs of an individual over a period of time (24–72 hours). Atrial fibrillation [19] is a type of arrhythmia predominantly found in patients with heart failure. The recordings from Holter monitors enable doctors or physicians to detect arrhythmias, which helps in the early diagnosis of heart failure. In certain cases, resting ECG conducted in clinics fails to detect heart rhythm abnormalities in patients experiencing early symptoms of heart disease. In this scenario, the physician or cardiologist might suggest using a Holter monitor for 24–48 hours. Furthermore, patients with a history of heart disease or recovered from heart-related surgeries are also advised to use Holter monitors for a couple of days. There is very little risk associated with wearing a Holter monitor, the only possible risks being associated with having electrodes attached to the skin, as the attached electrodes can often cause skin irritations. However, the Holter monitor is not water-resistant and patients are advised to not bathe or swim while the device is attached to their body for recording the physiological values. Moreover, the patients are advised to stay outside the proximity of metal detectors, microwaves, or other devices that can break off the signals from the electrodes attached to the body. After fitting a Holter monitor on a patient’s body, the cardiologist provides the patient with a diary to record their activities and symptoms. The patients have to write down all the activities they do while wearing the Holter monitor, noting also the exact time when they do them. They also have to note in the diary if they experience any symptoms such as fluttering or skipped heartbeats, chest pain, shortness of breath, etc., and the time of occurrence. Once the monitoring period is over, the cardiologist will go through the continuous ECG recorded in the device over the monitoring period, and carry out a detailed analysis. During the analysis, the cardiologist goes through the patient diary and, for each recorded activity as well as a symptom, the ECG recorded in the device at that particular time is investigated with the help of computers preloaded with algorithms to discover any abnormalities in the heartbeat. So the precise recording of activities and symptoms in patient diary is essential in Holter monitoring and any mistake in the recordings can severely affect the result analysis procedure. Medtronic, GE Healthcare, and Abbott are the major producers of commercially available Holter monitoring devices, with recent devices including many functionalities such as the ability to communicate directly with patients' Bluetooth-enabled smartphones or PCs.

Another type of portable ECG-based device is the event monitor. Event monitors are similar to the Holter monitors responsible for recording heart signals. However, while Holter monitors are configured to record the heart rhythm continuously for 24 or 48 hours, event monitors are used to record the heartbeats when the patient activates them. Some of the event monitors available on the market can automatically turn on the heartbeat recording when an abnormal heart rhythm is identified. Event monitors are available in many types, with symptom event monitors and memory looping event monitors being the most common. Symptom monitors record the heart's electrical signal after activation, while memory looping monitors record data from a few minutes before the activation. This feature of memory looping monitors provides additional information for doctors to analyze the heart condition. Mobile cardiac telemetry (MCT) devices are similar to Holter monitors and event monitors, but they are equipped with more advanced features to provide real-time assistance for patients. The ECG patch or multisensory patch attached to the patient’s body will record multiple sensors’ data or ECG data and send it to the mobile device installed with a software application via Bluetooth or WiFi. The mobile device software is configured to transmit the data to the monitoring center via the internet. The monitoring center will receive the data and analyze it to ensure that there is no abnormal event. If any abnormal events are detected, the monitoring center will provide emergency instructions to the patient with the help of a qualified doctor. Unlike Holter monitors, MCT devices can be attached to the body of the patient for recording the heart rhythm for up to 30 days. Several research studies have been conducted to demonstrate the benefits of MCT devices over event monitors and Holter monitors. The comparative study conducted by Tsang and Mohan [20] shows that the diagnostic yield of arrhythmia is significantly higher in patients monitored with MCT devices compared to the patients monitored with event monitors and Holter monitors. The study was conducted on a total of more than 200,000 patients over a 5-year period.

Both MCT devices and Holter monitors are designed to assist the cardiologist in monitoring patients with heart diseases. However, there are distinctive differences between them in terms of the monitoring period, recording method, data storage, data analysis, size of devices, etc. In the case of the monitoring period, usually, the Holter monitors are configured to monitor the heart activity for a maximum of 72 hours while MCT devices are assigned for 30 days. There are exceptions in the Holter monitor's monitoring period. In a few cases, the Holter monitors are prescribed for 7–14 days. Compared with MCT devices, the limited wear time of the Holter monitor reduces the inconvenience to patients caused by monitoring devices. However, the extended wear time of MCT devices is beneficial for asymptomatic patients in certain cases, for example detecting arrhythmia that may not occur during the short period in which a Holter monitor is being worn. Generally, the Holter monitoring is conducted in a manner where the device is assigned to record the heart activities for a given period and the data recorded in the device are analyzed once the monitoring period is over. In Holter monitors assigned for 7–14 days, any abnormalities recorded during the monitoring period will not be addressed and analyzed till the monitoring period is over and the device is returned to the clinic. On the other hand, the MCT devices are configured to continuously record the heart activity and send the recorded data to the server or remote analysis center for instantaneous analysis. The instantaneous recording and monitoring capability of MCT devices enable immediate assistance for the patient if any abnormalities are detected. In this context, MCT devices are more beneficial than Holter monitors for patients with a history of heart failure symptoms as well as for patients suffering from acute heart failure conditions.

In traditional Holter monitors, the data are stored locally using an internal chip. Local storage is enough for the Holter monitor due to the short monitoring period. In old devices, cassettes were used to store the recorded data. Some of the latest Holter monitors have the functionality to backup and store the recorded data in the cloud. The MCT devices allow both local and cloud storage. Usually, the data are sent to the cloud for immediate analysis.
