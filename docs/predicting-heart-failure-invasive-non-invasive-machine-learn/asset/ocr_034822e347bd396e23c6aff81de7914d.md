# 8.8 ML Techniques for Predicting HF

Predicting of HF risks using ML is becoming one of the top priorities for medical doctors and physicians. Specifically, when ML is applied to different kinds of medical records, it helps in predicting survival in patients who have HF symptoms, and detecting the main clinical features leading to HF. However, much work is still needed to improve the accuracy of HF predictors as most of them have failed to reach high performance levels [49, 50]. In addition to the traditional ML models, deep learning algorithms are gaining much popularity because of their supremacy in terms of performance compared with conventional models, especially if they are trained with huge amounts of data, and their ability to improve on human specialists’ performance.

To predict HF risks, data sets including medical records of eHF patients are usually used for training the ML algorithms. The process is similar to the diagnosis and detection of HF already explained in Figure 8.1, but with some differences regarding the types of input data that need to be fed into the ML models and the output to be produced. Therefore, the objectives are also different, as they are more oriented to predicting HF mortality and severity risks, HF destabilizations, HF relapses, and/or HF readmission [37, 51]. In this context, in addition to the EHR data (e.g. demographics, admission characteristics, medical history, visits to emergency departments, history of medication use, and healthcare services out of hospital), telemonitoring data can be utilized for identifying and predicting different HF risks (e.g. readmission risks) [52]. Moreover, by combining EHR data with tele-HF data and wearable sensor data the accuracy of predicting HF risks can be improved as discussed in Mortazavi et al. [53].
