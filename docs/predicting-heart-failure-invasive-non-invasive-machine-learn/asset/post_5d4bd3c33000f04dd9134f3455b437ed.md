Another serious concern while using smart wearables is that of data privacy and regulation policies. Smart wearables record a heavy amount of data including location data as well as physiological data. These recorded data are valuable for commercial firms, they can be utilized for analyzing customer behavior to extend the firms’ market. Various countries around the world have strict rules to ensure that commercial medical devices maintain fair data usage and regulation policies. However, these rules are applicable only for the devices which are developed for medical examination alone. Although non-invasive methods can help to reduce the overall health risk of heart failure patients to an acceptable extent, they are not as effective as invasive devices. Nevertheless, a substantial number of patients worldwide prefer non-invasive devices over invasive devices, because the former are economically feasible and devoid of the safety issues that are associated with internal implantation.

Apart from non-invasive devices, commercially available invasive devices that are currently used for monitoring CHFs have been described in this chapter. Each of the categories discussed is used for different applications and the physicians must choose which device to implant based on the patient’s details and severity of the condition. There are three factors to consider before deciding on the best-suited device. First is safety. The Chronicle IHM had better safety and fewer complications. However, it did not show significant results in reducing the number of hospitalizations in HF patients. The second factor is efficacy, and HeartPOD had better efficacy in preventing the number of hospitalizations, but safety was compromised as the implant led to increased complications. In other words, safety and efficacy are inversely correlated. Cost-effectiveness is the third important factor to be considered. Many devices discussed are not cost-effective and their longevity is still being tested. Overall, the CardioMEMS has proved to have better safety and efficacy, and is cost-effective as well. It must be reiterated that CardioMEMS is the only device that is FDA approved, has a European Conformity (CE) mark, and is available for commercial use.

## 11.5 Future Outlook

This section provides a brief future insight on non-invasive and invasive systems for heart failure monitoring and proposes how the evolving technology can contribute to the future development of heart failure monitoring systems.

- Currently available non-invasive devices, as well as invasive devices equipped with remote monitoring functionality, are providing treatment or advice feedback to patients with the help of a physician or cardiologist. They can focus on implementing treatment algorithms that are based on the latest artificial intelligence methods such as deep learning or machine learning prediction models trained with a large amount of data including various attributes such as laboratory values, pulmonary pressure, symptoms, etc. This will enable the development of fully automated systems for heart failure monitoring.

- This review discussed various smartwatches and smart wearables which are equipped with multiple sensors for recording physiological values. In this context, developing smartphone applications to record and analyze those values from those sensors has the potential to monitor heart failure patients non-invasively.

- The role of mHealth apps for the self-care of heart failure patients was discussed in this review and it was found that the mHealth apps have the potential to educate the heart failure patients about the disease, symptoms, precautions, etc. However, most of the existing apps lack user-centered design. In the future, developers can focus on developing apps with improved user-centric design. Furthermore, they can focus on developing apps for patients who have less knowledge about smartphone operations.

- The existing commercially available devices or proposed devices in the literature have not addressed the security of patients’ data during transfer as well as storage for better privacy and security of the patients’ latest information, where security techniques like encryption and steganography can be employed.

- A mix of invasive and non-invasive devices can be employed in the future to get the benefit from both. A hybrid model which combines both invasive and non-invasive techniques can be developed.

- An effective, efficient, and interdisciplinary collaboration between doctors and engineers is required to develop safe, reliable, and long-term devices to effectively monitor heart failure.

## 11.6 Conclusion

Heart failure is causing an economic burden on the hospital system, due to constant admission and readmission of the patients. It also affects the patients’ quality of life. Currently, heart failure is detected using the symptoms and signs which appear late and are not reliable. Non-invasive and invasive methods are available to detect heart failure at early stages. In recent years, the number of research activities in the field of remote monitoring techniques for heart failure monitoring is increasing rapidly. Non-invasive methods are easy to use and safe, but accuracy and reliability are compromised. Invasive methods are accurate and reliable, but the safety is questionable. Invasive methods are available only in a limited number of cases. In this chapter, the commercially available invasive and non-invasive devices for heart failure monitoring are discussed. Discussion of the advantages and disadvantages of these devices was included. Finally, a future outlook section was added to mention possible future directions in this field.
