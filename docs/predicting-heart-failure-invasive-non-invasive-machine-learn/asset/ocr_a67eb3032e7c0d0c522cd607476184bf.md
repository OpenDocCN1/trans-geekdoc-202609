more than 70 apps were currently available in the market for heart failure self-care. Among these, only about 25 apps have sufficient features to help heart failure patients. Moreover, more than half of the available apps did not even have a basic weight management feature, which is one of the fundamental attributes considered necessary for evaluating the health of heart failure patients. According to the study, existing mHealth apps lack patient-centered design and require advanced features based on the user’s need to guarantee the usability of the app. Among the commercially available apps, a few have been introduced by scientific societies such as the American Heart Association, Swiss Federation of Cardiology, Heart Failure Society of America, etc., but the rest have been developed by private technology companies. The app search conducted by Wali et al. [50] found that the app Heart Failure Storylines, developed by the Heart Failure Society of America, has better functionalities than other commercially available apps. The latest analysis of commercially available heart failure apps conducted by Mortara et al. [51] also rated the Heart Failure Storylines app as the better one for heart failure patients along with the HF Path app introduced by American Heart Association.
