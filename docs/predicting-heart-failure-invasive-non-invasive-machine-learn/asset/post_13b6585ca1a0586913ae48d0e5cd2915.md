### 10.3.2.1 Application of Nanomaterials in Electrochemical Biosensors

Nanotechnology has demonstrated new and unique detection platforms for biomarker detection for cardiac diseases with the applications of different types of nanomaterials like nanoparticles, nanowires, nanotubes, and nanocomposites. Nanotechnology offers a lot of flexibility in the choice of nano-size materials to quantify the signal, which in turn measures the concentration of the analyte.

A group of researchers used micro- and nano-polystyrene polymer structures to design electrochemical biosensors. The study concluded that reducing the surface texture to nano-size improved the detection sensitivity for C-reactive protein biomarkers [28].

### 10.3.3 Magnetic Biosensors

In life sciences, magnetic particles or magnetic beads have been in use in establishing biological assays. Biological cells, pathogens, toxins, antibodies, proteins, etc. can be categorized by connecting them to microbeads which are superparamagnetic in nature. The size of these particles ranges from a few nanometers to micrometers. The structure of magnetic beads comprises tiny magnetite crystallites encapsulated in ceramic or plastic spheres. Biological species like DNA or antibodies are coated on these beads which selectively bind to the target analyte (Figures 10.2, 10.5) [29].

## 10.4 Limitations of Biosensor Technology in the Detection of CVDs

The number of patients suffering from CVDs is growing at an alarming rate in the world. Precious lives can be saved if CVDs are diagnosed at an early stage of their progression, as physicians will be able to give proper treatment and allow faster recovery in patients.

![](img/721c7a2c71f4dc63a8585e2ef46838f7_246_0.png)

Figure 10.5 DNA based magnetic biosensor.

It is critical to fabricate simple and efficient CVD diagnostic devices, which can quickly detect multiple cardiac biomarkers in biological fluids in very low volume. Accurate detection using biomarkers is challenging, as most cardiac biomarkers also mark common inflammation. Hence, it becomes very difficult to differentiate cardiovascular risks. A patient may have an inflammatory process and no CVD at all, and biomarkers can report false positives.

Therefore, there is an urgent need to explore new techniques and tools to enable fast and accurate detection of cardiovascular risks, helping physicians to make correct clinical decisions. It has become essential to identify new and non-inflammatory biomarkers for CVD, developing biorecognition elements, exploring biosensors that can directly detect CVD in biological fluids, and developing miniature, portable electronic transducers.

It’s a big challenge to achieve CVD diagnostic tools with rapid, high sensitivity, specificity, low-cost, and accurate biosensors. Artificial intelligence (AI) provides excellent tools for the discovery of new biosensing materials for the early and precise detection of CVDs. New materials that are more functional can be discovered with a multidisciplinary approach that brings together researchers from diversified fields such as science, engineering, biomedical, and design backgrounds. This demands a radical shift away from traditional methods for the synthesis of new materials. There are several stages involved in materials discovery, such as conception, synthesis, and characterization. The entire process is carried out in the same sequence. As a result, only a limited number of materials can be tested at a time. AI has great potential for faster and more efficient material development for specific applications but AI tools, large computational data sets, and automated robotic systems have not yet been widely used for this purpose.

Computational techniques combined with AI can transform the understanding of vital information from available medical data. These techniques interpret the data in the form of structured, optimized, and meaningful information, which is readily available for the physicians for timely and accurate diagnosis.

Rapidly evolving technologies based on AI are non-invasively illuminating the fundamental processes contributing to CVDs. New insights into CVDs and strokes have important therapeutic implications for an expanding population of patients. Digitized medical data combined with information and telecommunication techniques are revolutionizing the diagnosis and treatment of complex cardiovascular abnormalities.

In making their clinical decisions, medical practitioners need to learn and keep up with rapidly evolving AI tools and techniques, and to apply them in interpreting health-related data received from different sources. AI applications in cardiac care are opening a range of possibilities to provide new personalized healthcare services. Personalized new contacts between patients and medical practitioners are being established, with health data and telemedicine moving healthcare from a passive to a pervasive activity. Physicians, scientists are integrating AI for faster, accurate, and effective diagnosis and treatment.

## 10.5 Application of AI in CVD Detection

In cardiology, AI technologies are being used in cardiac precision medicine, clinical assessment, cardiac imaging analysis, and automated robotic systems (Figure 10.6). They bring faster and accurate results in detecting CVDs, which previously would take hours or days to get detected with traditional methods.

In improving and optimizing CVD outcomes, AI techniques can drastically transform the way cardiology is practiced today. Specifically, the image techniques have offered innovative tools to effectively interpret data and arrive at a correct clinical assessment.

Machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) techniques of AI not only assist medical practitioners with faster and accurate clinical decisions, but also significantly improve medical knowledge by handling huge volumes of complex health data and revealing clinically significant information about CVDs.

The application of information and communication technology (ICT) is becoming central to the creation of a personalized and efficient healthcare service. It not only helps elderly and chronically ill cardiovascular patients to receive good medical care in the comfort of their homes, but also significantly reduces hospitalization costs and improves patients' quality of life [30]. Early and accurate diagnosis and prognosis assessment are crucial to enhance CVD outcomes. The focus, however, rests on imaging when AI is used in cardiovascular medicine.

ML, DL, and cognitive computing are AI techniques that can be effectively applied in early and accurate diagnosis, prediction of CVD outcome, and prognosis assessment of CVD.

A vast number of quantitative, qualitative, and transactional data sets are generated from electronic health records and other resources, and these need AI techniques to be effectively and meaningfully interpreted [31]. ### 10.5.1 Telemedicine and Mobile Health

Telemedicine and mobile health (mHealth) are gaining popularity and playing a significant role in the deterrence of CVDs and overall upgrading of cardiac care services. mHealth forms an integral part of eHealth and ICT such as computers, smartphones, mHealth applications, and patient monitoring services. AI is beneficial for remote follow-up of patients, medication reminders, real-time medical counselling, and early warnings of symptoms. This vital generated information can be used to access the healthcare information and lead to general improvement in healthcare services [32]. mHealth supports individuals in maintaining their health under the monitoring of physicians while allowing them to continue their daily activities. From a clinician’s perspective, AI can prove to be extremely effective in collecting voice medical data and connecting electronic medical records, thereby significantly reducing the work of clinicians [33]. Mobile devices have shown great promise in preventing CVDs, with self-monitoring apps and their use in activities such as diet monitoring, physical activity, blood pressure (BP) and sleep monitoring. These applications help patients to maintain a healthy exercise routine and proper weight, controlling blood glucose and normal BP and lipid profile [34–37]. A study reported by Apple and Stanford Medicine [38] assessed whether the Apple heart study app, used for the analysis of pulse rate data collected from the Apple Watch, could identify irregular heart rhythms.
