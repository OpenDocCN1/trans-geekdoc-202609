
## 8.9 Future Direction

HF is a serious cardiovascular disorder affecting a wide range of the population worldwide. The prevalence of HF increases with age, with the prevalence of HF in people over 75 and over 85 years of age being more than 8% and 10%, respectively. The cost burden for the management of HF is significantly high – about 2% of all healthcare expenditure is estimated to be for HF-related cases. This high expenditure is due to the high prevalence and rehospitalization rates of HF patients because of its chronic nature. It is expected that the number of HF patients in the future will increase as the average life expectancy has increased in developed countries. In order to tackle the excessive cost of HF healthcare and the increased work burden on healthcare professionals, advanced modalities are required, particularly to assist clinicians in the diagnosis and decision-making stages of HF, as is the ability to predict the outcomes of the treatments administered. Early diagnosis of HF and prediction of impending HF decompensation are critically important in preventing high mortality and rehospitalization rates and in increasing the quality of life of HF patients. For example, the use of wearable devices can support the continuous remote monitoring of someone with elevated risk, at reduced cost. It is also an excellent example of implementing an early prediction system that can be easily scaled to a large population of vulnerable subjects. The use of ECG on smart-watches has been shown to be capable of identifying several types of heart attacks with an accuracy of 93–95% [61]. Wearable devices are an important future direction that could leverage ML and artificial intelligence tools to provide time-critical prediction to caregivers.

## 8.10 Conclusion

In modern medicine, the quantity and complexity of huge medical data lead to challenges in solving complex clinical problems. It is here that artificial intelligence and ML applications have important benefits that can help clinicians in the diagnosis, therapeutic decisions, and prediction of the outcomes of HF. Such ML applications guide clinicians to diagnose HF early and prevent the condition from worsening. ML applications have the potential to reduce HF rates and save lives, because these algorithms enable the prediction of HF disease onset months before the clinical diagnosis.

ML applications involve analysis and classification of medical data. Data mining techniques are applied to classify the controls and HF patients, to differentiate HF subtypes with reduced ejection fraction, and to predict the HF severity. In this study, we reviewed the existing ML techniques and their advantages for the diagnosis and prediction of HF, as well as for the prediction of outcomes such as in-hospital mortality and 30-day HF readmission. The results of ML applications demonstrate the high potential of these methods in the diagnosis and prediction of HF. With the development of non-invasive sensor technologies, RPM systems are being introduced to continuously monitor HF patients, and ML algorithms are necessary to discern the markers of HF before observing the symptoms. All these facts point to a revolution in the clinical application of artificial intelligence and ML in cardiovascular medicine that might be seen in the near future. In this chapter we reviewed the following points:

- We gave a brief explanation of ML techniques for medicine, introducing ML principles and the building blocks of an ML system.
- We discussed traditional methods used by clinicians for the diagnosis and prediction of HF.

After we discussed the common clinical practice for both the diagnosis and prediction of HF, we similarly presented ways in which ML and artificial intelligence can contribute to improving the diagnosis and prediction of HF:

- We discussed ML approaches for the detection and diagnosis of HF; we covered ML models trained on biopsy images and how HF can be identified using model trained with large collection of EHRs.
- We considered ML and artificial intelligence methods for the prediction of HF; we focused on approaches for in-hospital mortality prediction, and also examined models for the prediction of 30-day HF readmission.
