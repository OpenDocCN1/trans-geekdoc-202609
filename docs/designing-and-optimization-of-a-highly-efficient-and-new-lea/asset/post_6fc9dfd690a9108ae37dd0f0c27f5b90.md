Lead-free perovskite materials have emerged as an alternative non-toxic semiconductor material for cells with exceptional performance, stability and stability. This work focuses on optimizing Cs2RbGa6 based perovskite solar cell (DPSC) through SCAPS-1D simulation and machine learning. We calculated material properties, like the density of states and carrier mobility, using data reported in the literature. Some other properties, like electron affinity, defect density, and absorber thickness, were optimized using SCAPS-1D software. The suitable Electron Transport Layer and Hole Transport Layer were also considered. We found optimized parameters to achieve a PCE of 22.17%. Machine learning helps verify scientific claims and scientific discovery and research gaps. In the recent increase in the use of machine learning in science, we developed a machine learning model with SHAP summary plot to analyze the impact of thickness and depth of absorber layer, with device operating temperature of the DPSC. To develop this machine learning model, 1008 data points were generated through SCAPS-1D simulations.

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite materials have emerged as contender to replace traditional Si-based solar cells due to their remarkable optoelectronic properties [1–5]. Stability of highly and efficient perovskite solar cells is based on limiting their usage [6–8]. To overcome these limitations, the scientific community has discovered double perovskite materials (DP). These double perovskite solar cells (DPSCs) offer many advantages like high stability and non-toxicity to make them both stable and efficient an excellent choice.

In a recent study, Cs2RbGa6, having a band gap of 1.8 eV has reported its interesting optoelectronic properties for photovoltaics in [9]. The material requires further exploration to investigate its use as a solar cell. In this study we used of reported material properties to calculate density of states and carrier mobility, thereby designing a DPSC based on Cs2RbGa6, and optimized various properties like defect density, electron affinity, thickness of absorber layer, thickness of ETL and HTL, operating temperature.

The implementation of machine learning (ML) techniques in photovoltaics is helping to streamline the process of device design and improve efficiency (PCE) [10]. Compared to traditional simulation methods, ML models provide a faster, more time-efficient approach, even without deep expertise in processing or interpreting the simulation results [11]. Many ML techniques such as artificial neural networks (ANNs), support vector machines (SVMs), random forests (RFs), and XGBoost (XGB), have become easily accessible [12]. These algorithms use large datasets to produce predicted results, which are based on input variables, and target characteristics. Their ability to detect patterns and correlations within the data allows ML models to capture insights that investigators might not reveal.

Moreover, as ML continually learns from available data, they become better at predicting PCE by adapting to factors like evolving material properties, changing environmental conditions, and shifts in device architecture [12–15]. This adaptability allows ML-based models to edge simulators in computational efficiency and speed, sacrificing accuracy. This is especially beneficial for tasks such as designing, optimizing and material screening of PSCs.

# 2. Methodology

## 2.1. Simulation methodology

In this study we used Solar Cell Simulator (SCAPS) for numerical simulation of solar cell devices, while considering de-coupling relations to solar cell characteristics.

We have first made and designed the device structure including its absorber layer, electron transport layer (ETL) and hole transport layer (HTL). Material properties of these layers were taken from previous studies. Some material properties of the absorber layer were calculated. The density of carriers and mobility were calculated and others electron affinity, density and thickness were optimized in this work.

After optimizing the device structure, we developed a machine learning (ML) model. Among several models, Random Forest was selected, as it produced an R² value of 1. This model was trained with a dataset of 1008 points, then optimized through simulation by varying parameters. The absorber layer was affected by varying temperature of the solar cell. These values varied in definition of power efficiency (PCE). A plot comparing the results with the predicted values from the ML model was generated. Similarly, we created a SHAP summary plot which helped identify the effects of various parameters on PCE and compare them. We also added a python script which would predict the PCE of the device given the three input parameters. Finally we compared the ML predicted PCE with that via simulation.
