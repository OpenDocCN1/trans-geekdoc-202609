# ABSTRACT

+   Keywords:
- Machine Learning
- Double Perovskite Solar Cell (DPSC)
- SCAPS-1D

Lead-free perovskite materials have emerged as an alternative non-toxic semiconductor material for cells with exceptional performance, stability and stability. This work focuses on optimizing Cs2RbGa6 based perovskite solar cell (DPSC) through SCAPS-1D simulation and machine learning. We calculated material properties, like the density of states and carrier mobility, using data reported in the literature. Some other properties, like electron affinity, defect density, and absorber thickness, were optimized using SCAPS-1D software. The suitable Electron Transport Layer and Hole Transport Layer were also considered. We found optimized parameters to achieve a PCE of 22.17%. Machine learning helps verify scientific claims and scientific discovery and research gaps. In the recent increase in the use of machine learning in science, we developed a machine learning model with SHAP summary plot to analyze the impact of thickness and depth of absorber layer, with device operating temperature of the DPSC. To develop this machine learning model, 1008 data points were generated through SCAPS-1D simulations.

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite materials have emerged as contender to replace traditional Si-based solar cells due to their remarkable optoelectronic properties [1–5]. Stability of highly and efficient perovskite solar cells is based on limiting their usage [6–8]. To overcome these limitations, the scientific community has discovered double perovskite materials (DP). These double perovskite solar cells (DPSCs) offer many advantages like high stability and non-toxicity to make them both stable and efficient an excellent choice.

In a recent study, Cs2RbGa6, having a band gap of 1.8 eV has reported its interesting optoelectronic properties for photovoltaics in [9]. The material requires further exploration to investigate its use as a solar cell. The implementation of machine learning (ML) techniques in photovoltaics is helping to streamline the process of device design and improve efficiency (PCE) [10]. Compared to traditional simulation methods, ML models provide a faster, more time-efficient approach, even without deep expertise in processing or interpreting the simulation results [11]. Many ML techniques such as artificial neural networks (ANNs), support vector machines (SVMs), random forests (RFs), and XGBoost (XGB), have become easily accessible [12]. These algorithms use large datasets to produce predicted results, which are based on input variables, and target characteristics. Their ability to detect patterns and correlations within the data allows ML models to capture insights that investigators might not reveal.
