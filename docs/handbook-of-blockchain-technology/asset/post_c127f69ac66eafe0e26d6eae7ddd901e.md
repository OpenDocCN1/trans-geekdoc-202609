Our research has several limitations that require future research. Much more work is needed to build reliable predictive NFT pricing models, specifically tracking the evolution of prices for an NFT over time and decomposing the idiosyncratic and persistent components and how they relate to seller characteristics. A challenge is estimating these models in the absence of complete data and the pervasiveness of wash trading. Another limitation is the need for more use cases behind art NFTs that contain utility, especially with clearly defined IP rights that clarify what the buyer receives and what the seller is parting with. Such advances require collaboration among legal and economic scholars alike. Finally, we did not address how NFTs can be enforced by the judicial system, although we are hopeful that NFTs may hold a unique key in a GenAI world (Makridis and Ammons, 2024; Makridis et al., 2024). Part of the solution requires more legal precedent and theory. However, the other part of the solution requires technological and operational progress on the platforms to scan metadata across blockchains to check for similarities before authenticating a newly minted NFT as a legitimate original.

Blockchains and NFTs have the potential to be catalysts for providing greater financial sustainability to artists. Unfortunately, illicit activities like wash trading and the fierce competition between NFT exchanges for lower transaction costs, which led to the discontinuation of royalty fee enforcement for artists, stifle optimism about the bright future of NFTs and blockchains for artists. Nevertheless, it is too early to dismiss these innovations and potential benefits for artists altogether: the technological and economic building blocks are in place to improve financial sustainability for artists, but they need to prove themselves and stand the test of time.
