Additionally, personalized DR relies heavily on anonymous user profiles. These profiles provide essential information for developing individualized charging schedules, which in turn promote energy-efficient consumption habits and enhance the efficacy of DRM. Furthermore, implementing individualized charging schedules can increase the efficacy of DRM, resulting in more strategic and efficient energy utilization. Moreover, active user participation in bi-directional P2P energy trading facilitated by blockchain can considerably improve DRM. This participation in energy trading can influence the energy consumption and generation patterns of users, thus enhancing the efficacy of DRM. In addition, the model places significant emphasis on the relationship between energy consumption, EV energy output, and DRM. Changes in consumption and generation patterns, supported by P2P energy trading, tailored charging schedules, and user profiles, can balance grid load and enhance energy efficiency, thereby boosting the effectiveness of DRM. The connection between primary energy generation and DRM emphasizes the importance of a consistent energy source for a dependable grid. Energy derived from primary sources can balance grid load, improve its dependability, and boost the effectiveness of DRM. Privacy considerations around user data and transactions have a significant impact on digital rights management. Effective privacy measures can increase user confidence, and thus drive active DRM engagement.

The emergence of IoV and blockchain technology presents a novel opportunity for re-imagining DRM. The incorporation of multiple variables, such as tokenized incentives, transparent distribution, user profiles, personalized charging schedules, bi-directional P2P energy trading, energy consumption/generation, privacy, and primary energy generation, presents a multifaceted strategy for the improvement of DRM systems.
