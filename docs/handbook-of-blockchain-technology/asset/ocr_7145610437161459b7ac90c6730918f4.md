# 17.1  INTRODUCTION

A Blockchain is a secure, decentralized ledger that underpins cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin and future distributed computing infrastructures based on consensus truth assertions. It stems from a white paper (Nakamoto 2008) that explains how the Bitcoin cryptocurrency might be built. Due to its supremacy in terms of security, decentralization, and reliability, the Blockchain system has recently gained popularity in a wide range of applications (Homayoun et al. 2019). The Blockchain is a collection of linked blocks that each contain a hash, a link to the preceding block, a timestamp, and transaction information. After completing a successful transaction, the information is forwarded to other participants for verification (via a mining process) before being added to the chain. After that, a group of transactions that have been checked and signed with cryptography are put together into a block. A hashing procedure is done on the block until a good hash value is found. This is done to make sure that the transactions are safe, correct, and hard to change. The block is then added to the chain of blocks that have already been built. Because all transactions are handled without the need for a central authority, the preceding explanation highlights Blockchain’s independence from a trusted third party. Indeed, Blockchain technology has paved the way for transactions to be implemented while maintaining data immutability and establishing trust between unknown parties (Hofmann et al. 2017). As a result of these accomplishments, a plethora of new applications, including healthcare (Junaid et al. 2020), finance (Smith 2020), and cybersecurity (Muheidat and Tawalbeh 2021), have emerged, all of which are reshaping the Internet of Value and bringing about massive transformations (Makridakis et al. 2018). In the coming years, Blockchain is expected to become a critical component of many organizations (Almekhlafi and Al-Shaibany 2021).

The simulation of human intellectual processes by computer systems, which has led to an automated world powered by machine-to-machine interaction (Auzim 2020), is another significant research subject gaining a lot of interest. Daily life activities, financial and banking services (Agarwal 2019), healthcare (Hla’kva 2020), and banking (Vedapradha 2021) are only a few of the applications where Machine Learning (ML) is applied. ML is classified as either supervised or unsupervised (Bhowmik, Sai Siri Chandana, and Rudra 2021). Since 2010, ML has been at the forefront of computer science and technology, and vector space models have strengthened their position at the top of ML. ML can self-learn by analyzing and detecting patterns in vast amounts of data using a variety of deep learning techniques (Makridakis et al. 2018).
