## 4.4.1 Is the NFT Market Yet Another Bubble?

The NFT market experienced its first boom during the summer of 2021, reaching almost 2 billion USD in weekly trading volume. Later on in 2021, NFT trading volumes started to explode, reaching several billion USD in weekly trading volumes until February 2022. After this, volumes declined significantly, reaching significant lows after May 2022 (Figure 4.2).

Since May 2022, declining NFT trading volumes correlate with overall crypto market price declines, for example, ETH, and Bitcoin. Nevertheless, trading volumes are still above 100 million USD per week, and overall, cumulative NFT trading volume has reached almost 80 billion USD since June 2021. Considering that NFTs are a relatively new technology, these are significant volumes, even though the speculative frenzy from early 2022 has seemingly faded.

Next, we investigate the number of trades and traders (Figure 4.3). This view allows a better understanding of the underlying adoption, as potential exchange rate effects are absent.

The first NFT trading boom is also visible, as the number of trades increased sharply in August 2021; however, the increase was less sharp than previously shown in the volumes. The weekly number of trades seems more volatile than previous volume figures. It exhibits a steady decline since its peak in February 2022, as weekly trades remain between 700,000 and 300,000 until April 2023. Only recently have weekly NFT trades decreased to further lows of between 100,000 and 200,000.

Recent studies reiterate these concerns about growing concentration and limited competition in the NFT market. Although it was initially characterized by relatively egalitarian movements, with rarity explaining some of the variations in early blue chip NFT portfolios (Mekacher et al., 2022), top buyer accounts now account for over 80 percent of trading activity with art NFTs based on the most recent data up to 2022 (Colavizza, 2023; Nadini et al., 2021; Vasan et al., 2022). A major mechanism behind these results appears to be the role of networks and homophily: a few collections exert a disproportionate influence on the network because of their connectivity with wealthy and interested buyers, independent of wash trading (Vasan et al., 2022).

The NFT trading market on Ethereum has demonstrated some resilience after the extreme volume drops in early 2022. The market has stabilized, and the number of trades is stable. The declining number of traders might indicate that broader adoption slowly faded. Also, the recent adverse shocks to traders, trades, and volume provide a pessimistic outlook for further market development. Nevertheless, sentiments strongly affect crypto markets, resulting in volatile adoption metrics. Therefore, it is too early to dismiss the NFT trading market.

Unfortunately, wash trading – the trading of NFTs by the same or other fraudulent sellers – has become prolific in the market for NFTs and fungible tokens (Cong et al., 2022). There are various reasons for wash trading, including NFT market price manipulation, tax loss harvesting without losing asset control, and money laundering.

Fierce competition between NFT marketplaces has also led to loyalty and trading incentive schemes to gain a market share. As a byproduct, some traders try to manipulate these incentive schemes by gaming activity, that is, engaging in wash trades. Exaggerated prices and trading volume help farm incentives on NFT marketplaces.

Several academic studies have emerged suggesting different methods to identify wash trades. A common approach is to investigate closed loops in re-purchasing behavior of the same token; for example, von Wachter et al. (2021) identify wash trades by closed cycles across several addresses. They identify 3.9 percent of addresses, 2.0 percent of transactions, 2.2 percent of volume, and 0.5 percent of NFTs associated with wash trades. More recently, Liu et al. (2023) identified wash trades based on repurchasing activity within 30 days. Based on seven popular NFT collections, they found, on average, that 0.14 percent of transactions, 0.11 percent of wallets, and 0.16 percent of tokens in each collection were wash trading related. An analysis by Moulié (2022) suggests several additional heuristics to identify wash trades, namely:

- a) Buyer and seller are identical.
- b) Back-and-forth trades between the same wallets.
- c) Buying the same NFT several times within a specific time period.
- d) The buyer and seller are funded by the same wallet, indicating that they belong to the same user.

Based on these heuristics, Moulié (2022) estimates that over 40 percent of NFT trading volume and 1.7 percent of transactions across major NFT exchanges on the Ethereum blockchain might be wash trading related. This more comprehensive approach suggests that NFT wash trading is much more common than previous academic works suggest. Based on the heuristics of Moulié (2022), we can chart the organic versus potential wash trading NFT trading volume (Figure 4.4).

The data suggests that wash trading was uncommon until January 2022. Regarding wash trades, the volume “bubble” during early 2022 disappears, and volume growth appears more organically over time. Wash trading remains relevant, but the degree, that is, the difference between total volume and wash trading-adjusted volume, is less pronounced than during the early part of 2022.

The extremely high wash trading volumes during early 2022 coincide with the launches of several NFT exchanges on Ethereum that relied on token incentive schemes to gain market share. This observation is also present when comparing organic and wash trading volumes of different NFT exchanges, for example, some marketplaces exhibit exorbitant (> 90 percent) wash trading versus organic volumes (Moulié, 2022). These observations might explain the NFT trading volume bubble in early 2022. In fact, the NFT bubble from early 2022 might not have been a typical price bubble but, to a notable degree, driven by inflated volumes by wash traders. There was no typical price bubble collapse of the market, but less wash trading, accompanied by declining cryptocurrency prices and lower trading volumes in terms of USD.

- 1. **Marketplace fees:** The lower the marketplace transaction fee, the lower the hurdle to gain from a wash trade.
- 2. **Creator royalties:** NFT collections vary in the amount of creator royalties. Wash trades are cheaper for collections without royalties. Therefore, NFTs from these collections might be more prone to being used as wash trading assets.
- 3. **Royalty enforcement:** Royalty enforcement varies across marketplaces. Marketplaces without enforcement impose fewer costs on wash traders, making the marketplace more prone to wash trading activity.
- 4. **Loyalty and incentive schemes:** Some marketplaces offer incentive and loyalty schemes for trading activities. The more attractive these schemes are, the more attractive it is for wash traders to engage in wash trades on these marketplaces to farm incentives.
- 5. **Blockchain transaction costs:** Even though the above analysis focuses on the Ethereum blockchain, we can assume that blockchain transactions also affect wash trading activity. For example, every blockchain transaction costs money, and transaction costs vary significantly across blockchains. The lower the transaction costs, the more profitable a potential wash trade will be. Therefore, blockchains with lower transaction costs than Ethereum might exhibit more wash trading activity.

Wash trading is illegal in traditional asset settings (Liu et al., 2023) because it is often related to price manipulation and other illicit behaviors. Regarding NFT trading, most wash trading activity occurs so as to potentially farm incentives from NFT exchanges, which is not necessarily illegal, especially as NFT exchanges profit from marketplace fees. However, it has yet to be investigated if wash trading for incentive farming on a large scale is a profitable strategy.
