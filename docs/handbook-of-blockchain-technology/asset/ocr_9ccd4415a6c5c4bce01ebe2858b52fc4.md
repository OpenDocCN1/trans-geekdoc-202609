# 10.2.1 Background of Blockchain Technology

The origins of blockchain technology can be traced back to the early 1990s, during which cryptographic techniques and protocols provided the initial framework for its subsequent development. The significant rise of blockchain occurred in 2008 with the release of a seminal white paper titled “Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System”, authored by the enigmatic Satoshi Nakamoto (Nakamoto, 2008).

Blockchain technology was first introduced to the world through the emergence of Bitcoin, leading to significant transformations across multiple industries. Operating as a decentralized ledger, it documents transactions in a sequential and unalterable fashion, guaranteeing the highest level of reliability. The integrity of all transactions is ensured by the consensus mechanism implemented among operating nodes. In addition to its impact on cryptocurrencies, blockchain technology possesses significant potential to revolutionize various industries such as healthcare, supply chain management, and finance, primarily due to the utilization of smart contract applications. Despite the considerable potential of blockchain technology, it encounters various obstacles concerning scalability, privacy, and regulatory concerns. However, there is still significant interest and investment in the technology, as ongoing research and development efforts are motivated by its potential advantages (Dondjio, 2023).

As blockchain technology evolved, it led to the emergence of various iterations, encompassing public and private blockchains, permissioned and permissionless blockchains, as well as platforms such as Ethereum that facilitate the execution of smart contracts. Programmable agreements possess the capability to autonomously execute predetermined conditions, thereby ushering in a novel era of decentralized applications (DApps) and decentralized finance (DeFi) ecosystems (Cunha et al., 2021).

# 10.2.2 Key Blockchain Characteristics

This section looks into the fundamental characteristics that form the basis of the transformative potential of blockchain technology. The fundamental characteristics of blockchain delineate its core nature and distinguish it from conventional centralized systems. Gaining a comprehensive understanding of these fundamental aspects is essential in order to grasp the transformative potential of blockchain in diverse industries.

+   - **Decentralization:** The blockchain is based on a network of computers known as nodes. Because there is no centralized authority governing the whole system, no one entity has total control over the data or transactions.
+   - **Transparency:** Because the blockchain is a transparent and immutable ledger, once a transaction has been recorded, it cannot be changed or tampered with. All network members can see and verify the transactions, increasing trust and accountability.
