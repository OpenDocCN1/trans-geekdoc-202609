- **Research Challenge 2 – Energy Dynamics in the IoV:** The energy generation and consumption patterns within the IoV significantly influence DR in smart grids and the IoV particularly. It is important to explore these dynamics further to understand how varying energy usage impacts grid stability and how DRM can be optimally employed to maintain grid balance. Future research should concentrate on developing and implementing effective incentive models that encourage EVs to sell energy, thereby enhancing grid resilience.

This pertains to each EV user being aware of and declaring their charging preferences and updating this information continuously. Further, a unified model encompassing charging strategies tailored to these individual profiles is not readily available. These strategies should consider various factors such as time-of-use rates, user preferences, grid conditions, and battery health. Future research should aim to address this gap, potentially leading to more efficient energy usage and user satisfaction.

### 16.3.2 Research Gap

Given the analysis and research difficulties presented in this chapter, current studies only partially address these issues; they leave a significant research gap, especially when considering blockchain’s unique characteristics, such as tokenization and user engagement in DRM.

A key aspect of the identified research gap relates to the decentralized architecture of blockchain. While research has demonstrated how this could facilitate P2P energy transactions and increase transparency, there is limited empirical evidence demonstrating the effects of these characteristics on DRM’s efficiency in IoV scenarios. For example, the effect of blockchain’s immutable audit trails on improving trust among participants and influencing their energy consumption or generation behavior remains underexplored.

In addition, tokenization can be used to create incentivization strategies and increase user engagement in DRM. The concept of rewarding participants with tokens that hold value within the ecosystem has the potential to improve the way users interact with DRM. This can lead to a more efficient DR balance. However, there is a dearth of research examining the efficacy of tokenization and its influence on user behavior within the IoV’s DRM framework.

The personalization of charging schedules, made possible by the smart contract functionality of blockchain technology, is another under-researched area. DRM systems could become more efficient and user-friendly if they allowed for personalized charging and discharging schedules based on user preferences and grid needs. However, the optimal design of charging schedules and their effects on energy consumption, user satisfaction, and grid stability require additional research.

To address the identified research gap, we propose a conceptual model in Section 16.4. This model seeks to investigate the relationship between blockchain, DRM, and IoV, and it attempts to address technical blockchain elements and their impact on user engagement, DR balance, and personalized charging schedules.

## 16.4 CONCEPTUALIZATION

This section explores propositions that focus on the role of blockchain technology in addressing challenges in IoV’s DRM. Firstly, blockchain technology can enhance efficiency, security, privacy, and user engagement in DRM by facilitating bi-directional energy trading, promoting trust, and offering tokenized incentives. Secondly, tokenization can drive active participation in the energy market and provide a tangible value proposition for EV owners. Thirdly, customized EV charging schedules through profiling can balance individual energy consumption, contribute to grid efficiency, and address grid stability challenges. Lastly, blockchain technology’s transparency and security enable transparent transactions, protect users’ identities, and enforce personalized charging schedules.

### 16.4.1 Proposed Conceptual Model

The model uses blockchain technology within the IoV to improve efficiency, which depends on a range of factors. These include tokenized incentives, transparent distribution, user profiles, personalized charging schedules, bi-directional P2P energy trading, energy consumption/generation, privacy, and primary energy generation. The interaction between these elements facilitates DRM’s effective implementation.

The model employs tokenized incentives to encourage user participation in DRM activities and profile creation. Given the importance of user engagement to the success of any DRM system, the creation of these profiles is fundamental to the model. These incentives are distributed transparently using the defining characteristics of blockchain technology, namely immutability and robust security. Not only does this increase user confidence in the system’s fairness, but it also addresses privacy and security concerns.

The proposed model takes advantage of user profiles that include specific information about each user’s driving and charging habits. This information is then used to create personalized charging schedules, which facilitate users’ participation in bidirectional P2P energy trading. These schedules can support the development of an efficient DRM system as they account for the unique energy requirements and consumption patterns of individual users.

In addition, the model emphasizes the relationship between energy consumption and generation in bidirectional P2P trading. It focuses specifically on the role of EVs as distributed energy sources that feed excess energy back into the grid. This paradigm shift transforms consumers into prosumers, increases grid flexibility, and improves the overall robustness and resilience of the IoV. Participation in P2P energy trading could result in the adoption of more energy-efficient consumption patterns and increased energy production.

Energy production from renewable sources (e.g., photovoltaics, wind turbines, and other green energy systems) can be incorporated where necessary. When EV-generated energy is insufficient, these primary energy sources provide an additional supply to supplement DRM. The integration of these renewable sources increases the energy system’s sustainability, decreases reliance on EVs alone, and contributes to environmental preservation.
