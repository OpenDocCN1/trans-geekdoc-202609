
### 6.2.1 Tokenization of Assets

The concept of tokenization was first introduced in 2001 by a company named TrustCommerce. They were aiming to reduce the risks linked to the storage of their clients' cardholder information. To deal with these issues, they created a strategy that involved replacing sensitive data elements with a non-sensitive equivalent, termed a token. This token carries no fundamental or exploitable value or meaning. Consequently, the primary account number (PAN) is replaced with symbols generated randomly, making them worthless if they were to be intercepted by hackers (Steiner, 2022). The token acts as a reference to the original data, but it cannot be used to deduce those values. For instance, a credit card number is replaced with a token value within the merchant's storage system. This token value is generated in a way that it cannot be traced back to the original data element. The token value can be used in various applications as a replacement for actual data as shown in Table 6.1. The benefit of tokens is that they have no mathematical correlation to the actual data they represent. The actual data values cannot be retrieved through reversal, making the information worthless in the event of a breach (Morrow and Zarrebini, 2019).

Tokenization of assets refers to the process of issuing a blockchain token that digitally represents a real tradable asset. Supporters suggest that asset tokenization has the potential to disrupt all manner of industries from art to finance to real estate, with no consideration as to how a private ledger system can magically acquire legal effect (Di Angelo et al., 2020). In asset tokenization, an issuer can create digital tokens for securitizing assets, both traded and non-traded, for increased liquidity and faster settlements. Ownership of the tokens becomes more transparent; transactions are less expensive and faster. Tokenization refers to the issuance of smart contract tokens, which allows access to the existing or prospective value generated by a specific asset such as gold, commodities, and even artworks, and more generally, it presents an attractive value proposition for a decentralized ecosystem (Lotti, 2019; Steiner, 2022). While many projects focus on the issuance of tokenized financial assets, it is also assumed that tokenization may serve as an enabler for new services and frictionless collaboration in a new type of economy, called the token economy. Tokenization can also be used in several applications and services, such as for a car-sharing platform or for licensing digital content or other digitized resources (Heines et al., 2021).

Implemented through smart contracts, tokenization has shown characteristics that could change the capital stack of infrastructure investments (roads, railways, ports, airports, power, and communication). Tokenization represents one of the most successful implementations of blockchain technology that transforms its benefits into practice. Given the gradual transition of infrastructures to intelligent systems, blockchain-based tokenization may allow the application of alternative financing models for infrastructure projects. Tokenization has the potential to unlock the liquidity of infrastructure assets, enhance transparency, and reduce transaction time and cost, making the financial industry more accessible, cheaper, faster, and easier, thereby possibly unlocking trillions of euros in currently illiquid assets, and vastly increasing the volumes of trades (Christodoulou et al., 2022; Ebrahimiyan et al., 2021). Digital scarcity acts as a key enabler of a new digital economy relying on assets that are liquid, divisible, and borderless (Tian et al., 2020).

The process of tokenization can be defined as capturing value into tradable units of account, known as tokens or coins. Its disruptive potential stems from the expansion of the concept of tradable value beyond just economic terms to encompass aspects like reputation, labor, copyright, utility, and voting rights. Once these values are tokenized, they can be identified, accounted for, and utilized within a system designed for incentives and equitable distribution of wealth and power. Essentially, tokenization is a digital representation of value. Similar to how the internet facilitated the swift and free circulation of digitized information, blockchain enables the nearly cost-free and borderless exchange of digitized value. Blockchain technology has provided a solution to the double-spending problem algorithmically and introduced the notion of digital scarcity, contrasting the digital abundance associated with the internet. Specifically, digital scarcity will serve as a crucial facilitator of a new digital economy based on assets that are liquid, divisible, borderless (easily transportable and quickly transferable), and, unlike currencies, have the potential to appreciate over time. The deflationary nature of some of these assets could have profound implications in aiding our society's transition from a debt-based economy, leading to significant enhancements in people's lives and democratic processes (Freni et al., 2020).

The process of asset tokenization bears many similarities to the conventional process of securitization. These security tokens are generated through a form of initial coin offering (ICO), often referred to as a security token offering (STO), which can yield various types of research.

### 6.4.1 Benefits of Asset Tokenization

According to hedera.com (2021), tokenization can improve infrastructure assets' liquidity, transaction efficiency, and transparency across intermediaries. The enhanced liquidity emphasizes that tokenization expands the infrastructure asset class from the domestic market to the global market. The fact that tokenization can offer greater liquidity and can be traded on secondary markets is emphasized greatly in Baum (2021). In Smith et al. (2019), the authors present the following benefits of tokenizing an asset: (a) Fractionalization; (b) Customizability; (c) Liquidity; (d) Automation; (e) Cost Efficiency; (f) Settlement Time; and (g) Data Transparency. Tokenization is also expected to affect the way organizations are governed in various ways, and regulatory models of corporate governance will need to be re-examined considering the profound disruption to business caused by tokenization. The token value can, therefore, be used in numerous applications, some of which are considered a substitute for real data (Morrow et al., 2019).

Unlike accredited investors and investment funds, retail clientele have limited access to capital, which narrows their investment opportunity set. On the other hand, certain asset classes experience low liquidity and unbalanced markets due to their prohibitive price levels. Blockchain technology solves the above problems by allowing for the fractionalization of assets, that is, breaking up the asset into smaller pieces. NFT fractionalization (F-NFTs) enables investors to purchase a piece of the NFT, ensuring at the same time diversification potential (Frye, 2021; Mazur, 2021; Mukhopadhyay et al., 2021). Fractional NFTs are tokens that represent fractional ownership of an NFT. Mutual funds work the same way also. Investors pool money into a fund, and that money is used to buy assets. Each investor owns a fraction of the entirety of the fund (Hines, 2020).
