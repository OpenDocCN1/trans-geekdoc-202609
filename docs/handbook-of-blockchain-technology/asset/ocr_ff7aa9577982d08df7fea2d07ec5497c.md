Human Resource Management (HRM) plays a vital role in organizations, with forward-thinking leaders recognizing its strategic importance. Recruiting high-caliber employees and investing in their training and development are crucial for organizational success (Spence, 2018). However, the Human Resources (HR) sector is evolving into a complex environment due to changing work practices and the increasing reliance on technology for enhancing efficiency, flexibility, security, and effectiveness (Michailidis, 2018; Shrivastava and Shaw, 2003; Snell et al., 2002). Organizations are under pressure to integrate technology, including emerging ones like Blockchain, to improve service delivery to customers (Papadopoulos et al., 2020). Despite its potential, however, Blockchain technology is not widely understood, leading to misconceptions about its function (Upadhyay, 2020).

The economic landscape has witnessed the globalization of HR, frequent job changes, the gig economy, new communication channels, and rapid technological advancements (Allen et al., 2010; Frenken et al., 2020; Gajendran and Joshi, 2012; Orlikowski, 2000; Tsui et al., 2007). The COVID-19 pandemic further accelerated changes in organizational behavior (McKinsey & Company, 2020). With organizations operating virtually, expanding to new locations, and offering multitasking services, there is a need for a decentralized structure for data authentication, registration, and transmission (Balon et al., 2022). Towards this end, Blockchain technology emerges to facilitate effective Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) by eliminating intermediary communication channels and simulating the integration of processes and HRM (Balon et al., 2022). Blockchain is a digital technology that provides a secure and traceable system for integrating physical and computational components in smart industries (Nakamoto, 2008). Its advantages include transparency, decentralization, durability, anonymity, audibility, and security. The data stored in Blockchain is immutable, confirmed by the network, and organized into blocks, with each block containing the hash of the previous block (Balon et al., 2022). Blockchain technology combines authentication, cybersecurity, and data archiving, ensuring that data can only be accessed by authorized users (Balon et al., 2022).

This study aims to explore the impact of Blockchain technology on HRM. It will critically review the literature on Blockchain technology and its core concepts, as well as significant perspectives in the field of HRM. The research will also present the methodology used, the findings, and the results of the study. Finally, it will discuss the effects of Blockchain technology on HRM and provide conclusions.
