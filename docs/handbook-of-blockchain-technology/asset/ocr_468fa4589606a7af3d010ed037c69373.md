However, Blockchain technology is not only used in relation to HR activities, but it is also very much related to many applications across numerous industries. Nowadays, it is important to provide secure and reliable transactions and a way of checking their source so that fraud can be avoided, together with payments of ransomware to hackers. To achieve this, there must be stable networks that are reliable. Aishwarya (2018) suggests that the credibility of daily processed information can be checked using Blockchain technology as a system that stores information chronologically and safely holds a digital record of all processed transactions.

Salleh et al. (2019) portrayed a Blockchain network as one that operates on the basis of unique specifications. Decentralization is applied by making each partner on the network responsible for the correct transactions that are verified by themselves without the need for an intermediary. With the use of Blockchain technology and applying this characteristic, the verifications are easier, faster, and cheaper. Only when new cryptographed data are added to the blockchain system can the majority of the network members be sufficiently confident that the information transmitted is valid. Blockchain also provides consistency because it is required to have documentation and authentication over every information block, thus it is unlikely to have an interruption in the information. Furthermore, every information block is tested and confirmed by other means. The whole process is not managed by a single individual or even a particular entity. Instead, it is managed by a global network of computers that participate in the system, which means that there is no need for a third-party agent to check and certify that they are authentic. As a consequence, the Blockchain enables the partners in the system to communicate and share documentation at the same time (Adam-Kalfon and El Moutaouakil, 2017).

According to Makridakis and Christodoulou (2019), because third parties are not needed for the Blockchain system to verify the authenticity of the transactions, there are significant cost savings. Yaga et al. (2018) describe Blockchain systems as not needing any central agency to validate records, as the system allows users to codify transactions in a shared ledger within the system. However, once these transactions are approved, they cannot be reversed. Tamper-proof transactions are made when cryptographical Blockchains are clustered into blocks, which are cryptographically interconnected. When additional information is entered into the system, any disputes arising are resolved according to the rules formed when the system was designed, and tamper resistance is created. Kitsantas et al. (2019) examined the various types of Blockchain systems, including private, public, and consortium Blockchains, revealing their advantages and disadvantages. In the case of a public Blockchain system, anybody can manage Blockchain technology anonymously. Bitcoin mostly uses all the above Blockchain characteristics and is therefore a decentralized system based on users’ cooperation and consensus. This is, however, susceptible to cyber-attacks by capable hackers who can trace the information blocks without being identified.

Anonymous transactions on a private Blockchain system are not accessible to people even if they know each other. A user cannot read any information in a Blockchain system unless they have an “invitation” to enter the system. This is typically used by large organizations with specific permissions from the stakeholders of the Blockchain system.
