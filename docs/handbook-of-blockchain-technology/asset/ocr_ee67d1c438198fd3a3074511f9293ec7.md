However, defending against rises in the cost of transactions on a specific blockchain is also an argument to support multiple providers simultaneously. An approach followed by some is to code for the Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM). This Turing-complete environment is the backbone of the Ethereum network, responsible for executing all operations and transactions. Several other blockchains are EVM-compatible, meaning that developers can write and deploy the same smart contracts across them without making significant changes. Further, the smart contracts can interact with other EVM-compatible blockchains through the EVM, enabling the creation of multi-chain dApps (Leal, 2023).

The Digital Container Shipping Association (DCSA), which creates “vendor-neutral and technology-agnostic standards for the industry” (DCSA, 2023a), addresses other interoperability concerns. On July 12, 2022, DCSA began the final phase of an eBL platform interoperability proof of concept (DCSA, 2022).

As with many application areas of blockchain technology, there are regulatory and legal implications to consider in shipment transportation. According to Vlačić and Čerklć (2021), international legislation and maritime regulations recognize BLS as private documents for sea transport. Consequently, parties can exchange them in any agreed format or style. Additional discussion regarding the use of electronic BLs is available in the International Chamber of Commerce’s “The Legal Status of Electronic Bills of Lading” (Stephen, 2018). A key takeaway is the importance of countries implementing the United Nations Commission on International Trade Law’s (UNCITRAL) Model Law on Electronic Transferable Records (MLETR) (UNCITRAL, 2017). Additionally, it is important to comply with the International Group of Protection & Indemnity (P&I) Clubs’ requirements to ensure liability coverage (IGP&I, 2023).

For road hauling, the Institut du Droit International des Transports (IDIT) is a reference regarding the CMR convention and transportation law (IDIT, 2023). Further reading and FAQs are available from the International Road Transport Union (IRU) (IRU, n.d.).

# 15.6  FUTURE TRENDS AND OUTLOOK

Future-oriented studies can provide valuable insights into the role of blockchain from the companies’ perspective. A publication presented by Bathke et al. (2022) reveals that transportation companies expect that blockchain will be able to provide “a complete, transparent, and reliable data chain amongst all parties in maritime container transportation by 2035” and be one of the most impactful in their sector (among other projections including drones, autonomous vessels, or alternative propulsions). Interestingly, their work showed that blockchain desirability is higher among practitioners compared to academics and politicians, which is aligned with the significant blockchain investments from multiple participating companies. It is stated that “experts with more knowledge in the shipping business assessed higher desirability for a transparent marine supply chain by blockchain” (Bathke et al., 2022), putting technology in the “low-hanging fruit” category in their future. Nevertheless, the findings also highlight that blockchain success will depend on multiple stakeholders’ participation, and the added value is still unclear in the academic literature.
