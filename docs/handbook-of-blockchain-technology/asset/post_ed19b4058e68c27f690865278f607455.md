The European Parliament has spoken out repeatedly in favor of establishing ML laws to ensure a high level of data protection, digital rights, and ethical standards, and to avoid doomsday scenarios involving these integrated technologies. Blockchain and ML technologies could and should be used in full compliance with the concept of Trustless.

A regulatory framework should simultaneously encourage the adoption of innovative technology and combat fraud. As the use of distributed ledger technology (DLT) increases at an exponential rate, it raises new problems in global digital markets. Some DLT applications are incompatible with the current regulatory framework because they disrupt centralized systems and alter established organizations.

Financial regulations or having a clear framework within which to operate lawfully are seen positively by the majority of interviewees. Companies are expected to benefit from these regulations. It is anticipated that establishing a legal framework for tokens will provide both legal certainty and clarity as to what is and is not permitted.

The literature debates the impact of regulatory frameworks on the development of businesses. While some scholars believe that rules inhibit company growth (Jalilian, Kirkpatrick, and Parker 2007; Poel et al. 2014), others claim that they facilitate it (Peck, Jackson, and Mulvey 2018).

### 17.5.1 RQ1: Do You Believe That Machine Learning Can Be Used to Detect Fraud in Blockchain Transactions and Business?

Money laundering and fraud involve hiding an illegal source of money while making it look like it came from a legal source. The establishment of Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Compliance was influenced by two major aspects. First of all, there's the simple truth that illegal enforcement simply didn't have, and still doesn't have, the capability or capacity to detect money laundering. Second, there was a shift in public understanding of private corporations’ position as corporate citizens and the inherent concept of good citizenship, which was linked to prevalent ideas about corporate moral responsibility, sustainability, and good standing and reputation (Chau and Nemcsik 2020). International regulations require banks and other regulated businesses to refuse to do business with criminals and terrorists, identify suspicious activities, and report them to the appropriate authorities. Customers are checked against sources that confirm their identities or lists of people who aren't allowed to make transactions or who are thought to be criminals. This can be done on an individual or national level by businesses that are regulated. When other entities that rely on intelligence, such as the military, emphasize predictive, forward-looking intelligence, their controls tend to look backward. Banks and other institutions are facing increasingly harsh penalties for failures in their controls, but looking backward rather than forward reduces the extent to which the controls achieve their goal of lowering the effect of organized crime and terrorism (Lowe 2017). ML techniques can be used to solve problems and address security and performance issues in Blockchain. Deep learning algorithms can classify Blockchain peers’ behavior patterns automatically. It also aids in the detection of unusual behavior patterns among malevolent peers (Tang et al. 2018). Furthermore, the supervised ML system in the Blockchain network can detect anomalies such as collusion and stop the majority of attacks (Dey 2018) from taking place or anomalous behaviors (Bogner 2017).
