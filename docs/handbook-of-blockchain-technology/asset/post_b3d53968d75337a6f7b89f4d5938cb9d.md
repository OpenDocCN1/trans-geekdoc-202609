### 2.2.2 From the Internet Dawn to Web 3.0 and Beyond

The web was conceived and developed by Tim Berners-Lee, a British scientist working at CERN, the European Organization for Nuclear Research, in the late 1980s and early 1990s. He proposed a way of linking and sharing information among researchers using hypertext. https://home.cern/science/computing/birth-web/short-history-web

The web has gone through several phases of development since its inception. The first phase, known as Web 1.0, was characterized by static web pages that were mainly read-only and provided information to users. The second phase, known as Web 2.0, emerged in the late 1990s and early 2000s, and introduced dynamic web pages that allowed users to interact with each other and create content through social media platforms, blogs, wikis, and other applications. The third phase is what we refer to as Web 3.0, or the decentralized web, and is still evolving.

**Web 3.0.** Web 3.0 is a vision for a web that is not controlled by any central authority or intermediary, but rather by the users themselves. It is a new technological paradigm that builds on the previous generations of the web (Web 1.0 and Web 2.0). Web 3.0 presents new features and functionalities that aim to make the web more intelligent, decentralized, and interoperable. The aim is to create a more open, fair, and resilient web that respects users’ privacy, freedom, and sovereignty (Goel et al., 2022). It uses blockchain technology and incorporates other technologies such as AI, data analytics, IoT, decentralized storage, peer-to-peer networking, cryptography, SCs, and others, to create applications that can process and understand large amounts of data, communicate and collaborate across different platforms and devices, and empower users to control their own data and identity (Limburn, 2023). These technologies enable the creation of new types of applications and services, such as decentralized marketplaces, prediction markets, and social networks (Marek and Zdrálek, 2020).

The decentralized nature of Web 3.0 allows for the creation of a metaverse that is not controlled by a single entity. This means users can own their virtual assets, such as digital real estate or virtual goods, and have more control over their virtual experiences. Moreover, it enables the use of SCs, which can automate transactions and agreements within the metaverse (Nath, 2022).

**The metaverse.** The concept of the metaverse has been around for decades in science fiction, and it’s becoming more of a reality thanks to advancements in technologies like VR, AR, and blockchain. The term ‘metaverse’ was initially introduced by Neal Stephenson in his 1992 novel *Snow Crash*. In this narrative, the metaverse is depicted as a successor to the internet, rooted in VR, where users can partake in immersive, 3D experiences and connect with others within a communal virtual environment. Stephenson created the term by merging ‘meta’ (signifying beyond or transcending) with ‘universe’, suggesting a digital domain that extends beyond the tangible world (Cheng et al., 2022). Since its inception, the idea of the metaverse has gained attention and has been extensively debated in numerous sectors, including technology, gaming, and futurism. It’s often viewed as a potential advancement of the internet and VR. The term ‘metaverse’ has gained popularity as a descriptor for a future integrated digital domain where individuals can participate, innovate, and interact within a virtual setting.

Díaz et al. (2020) define the metaverse as a digital environment that is immersive, three-dimensional, and facilitates interactions among multiple users. It allows individuals to participate socially and economically, irrespective of their geographical location, using computational tools like personified agents and simulations. In this context, the metaverse is perceived as a virtual universe that provides opportunities for exploration, where users can engage through their avatars and experience diverse settings and lifestyles. Similarly, Kye et al. (2021) emphasize the fusion of physical and virtual worlds in the metaverse, where ‘routine activities and economic pursuits are extensions of the real world.’ They point out that avatars in the metaverse are intimately linked to the real identities of the users who operate them. Users’ avatars actively engage in social, economic, and cultural activities within the metaverse’s virtual world. Sarıtaş and Topraklıoğlu (2022) propose a different perspective on metaverse technology, acknowledging its existence in both digital reality platforms and 3D computer software-based platforms. This viewpoint suggests that the metaverse includes numerous virtual worlds where people interact and coexist via avatars, leveraging blockchain and digital reality technologies.

However, defining the metaverse has proven to be a complex task, as Liu (2022) points out. The literature presents a variety of definitions, leading to what Yılmaz et al. (2023) refer to as ‘terminological confusion.’ Jiang and Xu also note that the metaverse’s definition is subject to its continuous evolution. Talan and Kalinkara (2022) have noticed that the metaverse’s definitions are not static and change over time. Yue (2022) emphasizes that many definitions tend to project what the metaverse could potentially evolve into, rather than describing its current state.

## 2.3 METHODOLOGY

The research methodology employed in this study was bifurcated. The first step involved conducting a systematic literature review (SLR) with the objective of identifying and analyzing pertinent peer-reviewed articles on how cryptocurrencies and NFTs are used in the metaverse with AI leveraged as a complement. An SLR is a review type that employs replicable methods to discover, select, and consolidate all existing evidence relevant to a specific research question. It systematically involves the search, selection, critical evaluation, and synthesis of peer-reviewed articles that satisfy preset criteria (Higgins et al., 2011; Themistocleous et al., 2023). This systematic approach ensures a thorough examination of the existing literature and lays the groundwork for comprehending the current state of knowledge.

This procedure and the role of AI in this research are explained below in order to provide transparency and future replicability (Raina et al., 2022). This methodology provides a comprehensive overview of the strategies employed in the collection, selection, and analysis of relevant literature.

When entering the data compilation and review process, there were extensive amounts of literature that related to the basis of this study. Determining which search engines to leverage in the research process directly correlated to which provided the most optimized database of relevant literature. Due to the specificity surrounding blockchain technology in the metaverse, the search engine options were limited to Google Scholar and IEEE Xplore. These engines were chosen as potential options due to their frequent usage in similar meta-analyses and literature reviews (Chan et al., 2022).

To ensure the relevancy of the sources provided, the following search terms were prompted within each engine: (Cryptocurrencies AND/OR NFTs) AND Metaverse; (Cryptocurrencies AND/OR NFTs) AND/OR Metaverse; (Cryptocurrencies AND NFTs) AND/OR Metaverse; (Cryptocurrencies AND NFTs) AND Metaverse (Koohang et al., 2023). Table 2.2 shows the results of the search engines when prompted with the above terms.

Due to the similarities between the outputs and their corresponding search terms, it was decided to utilize the prompt (Cryptocurrencies AND/OR NFTs) AND Metaverse. This prompt generated the most concise and relevant literature, as well as being consistent among both Google Scholar and IEEE Xplore. To narrow the projected results to more precise and related information, a range date was implemented into both engines.

A range date was determined through the evaluation of blockchain technology’s history and the maturation of AR software. As previously mentioned, blockchain technology dates back to 2008 with Satoshi Nakamoto’s creation of BTC (Bao et al., 2022).

The progression of this technology into the more advanced Web 3.0 environment has recently allowed for the peer-to-peer nature of the metaverse to occur; however, modern VR applications only surfaced in 2011 thanks to a young enthusiast, Palmer Luckey. The convergence of these technologies into what we recognize as the metaverse recently dates back to roughly 2019. Due to the lack of time between its creation and the present day, the literature surrounding this topic is limited, thus influencing the decision to exclude any publications.
