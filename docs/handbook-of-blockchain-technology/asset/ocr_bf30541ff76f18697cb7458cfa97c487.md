**Proof of Work (PoW)** is a popular CA used in various blockchains such as Bitcoin. Transactions are recorded in blocks with each block containing the signature (hash) of the previous block. This results in linking the blocks together in a chain. The decentralized nature of the network supports the administration of the transactions in a distributed and totally decentralized way. In PoW all new transactions are sent to a transaction pool. Specialized miners gather transactions from this pool and create a candidate block. Usually the oldest transactions in the pool are included in the candidate block. The exception to this rule are transactions with higher priority. Before creating the new block, specialized miners verify these transactions to ensure that they follow the rules (e.g., not spending more cryptocurrencies than those they have). Apart from the transactions, the candidate block includes a set of metadata, the hash of the previous block, Merkle root, time, target, and nonce. There is also information about the number of transactions included in the block. Once the candidate block is ready it is sent to the miners. To append a new block to the blockchain, participating miners must solve a mathematical challenge and come up with a verifiable solution. For example, miners should find out the correct value for the nonce which is based on the data of the candidate block. Miners compete to achieve this task and the first person who finds the solution broadcasts the answer to the rest of participants for validation. To validate a new block, the majority of the miners (50 percent + one miner) should verify the answer. When this happens, the block is anchored to the blockchain and the miner who solves the problem gets the reward.

The quantity of participants, their computational power, and the volume of network traffic at any given time all affect how complex this puzzle is. Each block’s hash includes the hash of the block before it, a feature that strengthens security and provides a strong defense against any possible breach of individual blocks (Lucas and Paez, 2019).

**Proof of Stake (PoS):** PoS is best illustrated by Ethereum’s switch from PoW to PoS and is considered a substitute for PoW. A key difference between PoW and PoS is that the participants of the first invest heavily in computation power to solve difficult mathematical puzzles, whereas in PoS validators invest in the network’s native money.
