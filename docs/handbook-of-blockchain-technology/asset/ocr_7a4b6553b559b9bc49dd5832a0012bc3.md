# ## 8.5 CONCLUSION

DAOs are a new organizational form that have the potential for a more inclusive and collaborative value creation. They make use of blockchain-based smart contracts to enable decision-making processes for a community. This approach should allow for a fairer and more equal decision-making process: members can create a proposal and after a discussion round, the community votes on the proposal that will be executed automatically if accepted. In practice, many challenges arise: the decision-making process often tends to lean towards centralization, where only a few actors get access to make decisions. Furthermore, the voting power accumulates among wealthier members, as many voting systems have plutarchic tendencies. Lastly, voter participation is low in general, leading to an underrepresentation of community interests.

To counteract these challenges, DAOs have come up with elaborated voting schemes. While the basic voting scheme is based on one-token-one-vote, more recent variants introduce different weights to calculate the voting power. Some systems make use of a mathematical function to break the one-token-one-vote model, while others include time as a factor. There are also voting schemes that rely on market elements such as prediction markets, or they introduce new values like reputation. While all of these voting systems aim to tackle some of the above-mentioned challenges, they also open up other attack points such as sybil attacks. In summary, while all the mechanisms have their strengths, they also have some weaknesses. Thus, the perfect voting system does not exist yet but new and enhanced voting mechanisms might be developed in the future.

Having explored the decision-making process in DAOs, we may notice a few key aspects. First, it is astonishing how much innovation is taking place in the DAOs space. Many different decision-making concepts have been designed, implemented, and experimented with. As DAOs are live systems that produce a lot of data, the analysis of this data provides a huge research opportunity. Second, many DAOs are still in a premature state. Even though the number of DAOs is growing exponentially, many remain at an experimental stage. Therefore, not only is more research needed, but more guidelines and best practices for this novel industry are required. Third, decentralized decision-making is an intricate topic and there are many tradeoffs, such as decentralization vs. efficiency or anonymity vs. fair voting power. Therefore, carefully balancing different goals in a decision-making process seems a sensible approach.

In this chapter we have described and outlined how decision-making in DAOs currently takes place. While we have taken a descriptive perspective, we still hope that we could trigger some thoughts on how to improve current decision-making mechanisms in DAOs. Having fair and efficient decision-making in place is a success factor for future DAOs.
