When entering the data compilation and review process, there were extensive amounts of literature that related to the basis of this study. Determining which search engines to leverage in the research process directly correlated to which provided the most optimized database of relevant literature. Due to the specificity surrounding blockchain technology in the metaverse, the search engine options were limited to Google Scholar and IEE Explore. These engines were chosen as potential options due to their frequent usage in similar meta-analyses and literature reviews (Chan et al., 2022).

To ensure the relevancy of the sources provided, the following search terms were prompted within each engine: (Cryptocurrencies AND/OR NFTs) AND Metaverse; (Cryptocurrencies AND/OR NFTs) AND/OR Metaverse; (Cryptocurrencies AND NFTs) AND/OR Metaverse; (Cryptocurrencies AND NFTs) AND Metaverse (Koohang et al., 2023). Table 2.2 shows the results of the search engines when prompted with the above terms.

Due to the similarities between the outputs and their corresponding search terms, it was decided to utilize the prompt (Cryptocurrencies AND/OR NFTs) AND Metaverse. This prompt generated the most concise and relevant literature, as well as being consistent among both Google Scholar and IEE Explore. To narrow the projected results to more precise and related information, a range date was implemented into both engines.

A range date was determined through the evaluation of blockchain technology’s history and the maturation of AR software. As previously mentioned, blockchain technology dates back to 2008 with Satoshi Nakamoto’s creation of BTC (Bao et al., 2022).

The progression of this technology into the more advanced Web 3.0 environment has recently allowed for the peer-to-peer nature of the metaverse to occur; however, modern VR applications only surfaced in 2011 thanks to a a young enthusiast, Palmer Luckey. The convergence of these technologies into what we recognize as the metaverse recently dates back to roughly 2019.
