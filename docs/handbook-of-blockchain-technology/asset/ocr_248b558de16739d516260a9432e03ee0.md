Especially, the younger workforce doesn’t feel satisfied within enterprises being too centralized in their decision-making processes and too hierarchical in their corporation structures (Vermeulen et al., 2018). Consequently, it can be witnessed that the norm of today’s organizations emphasizes a narrow agenda defined by the few, putting aside broader considerations of the many. Hence, Vermeulen et al. (2018) state that subordinate communities and workers, and other key stakeholders are not included in the success of these corporations. With the help of the rapid developments in cryptocurrency and blockchain technology DAOs have emerged as a phenomenon offering a new way to create more inclusive organizations moving towards ‘community-owned’ platforms, which raise questions about today’s organizational environment. According to the DAO analyst DeepDAO, as of July 2023, a total of 19,553 DAOs have been created and a total of $22.8 billion has been invested into DAO treasuries, while the number of participating members is 7.1 million governance token holders. This number is put into perspective by Wang et al. (2023) discovering a member increase by the multiplication factor of 531 over the past six years in DAOs. Even though the technology is still maturing, and many issues remain unsolved, the new organizational structures of DAOs have become a trending topic (Wang et al., 2019) and the new possibilities are finding wide application within the blockchain-ecosystem. Having said that, DAOs are still considered an experimental and nascent phenomenon. Thus, DAOs challenge the traditional assumptions and norms of how organizations need to be designed, still many issues like centralization of governance processes, concentration of voting power and low participation rates need to be addressed.
