| Voting System | Governance Centralization | Power Concentration | Low Participation |
|---------------|---------------------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Token-based Quorum Voting | Governance is associated with token holdings whereby a members’ preference is simplified into a single vote. | A larger token holder can concentrate voting power and dominate the voting outcome. | A minimum participation required for a vote to pass (quorum) can encourage but also discourage voter participation. |
| Quadratic Voting | Involves a non-linear allocation of resources helping individuals to express the degree of their voting preferences. | Balances power distribution and gives minorities a more prominent say in organizations with large spreads in token holdings. | Discourages people without a vested interest and encourages broad participation by reducing the effect of one single vote. |
| Conviction Voting | Members can vote on various ongoing proposals, and the longer a vote remains unchanged, the more power the vote gets (4.3). | Eliminates the ability to influence votes last minute; reduces attractiveness for sheer token allocation by using decay curves. | Conviction voting gives persistent voters the ability to gather more voting power over time, and helps minor token holders to be heard. |
| Futarchy Voting | Futarchy aims to optimize decision-making by using prediction markets to achieve the best possible outcome for the DAO community (4.4). | Uses wisdom of the crowd and makes it harder for central actors to control both the DAO and society for their own interests. | Reduction of power concentration by opening the decision-making process to the many, instead of having it in the hands of a few. | Encourages to put some skin in the game, but can also lead to risk aversion or merely financially driven speculation. |
