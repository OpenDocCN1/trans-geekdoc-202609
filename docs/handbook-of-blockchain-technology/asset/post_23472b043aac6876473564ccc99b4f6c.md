# 10. Barriers to blockchain adoption in developing countries: a systematic literature review

Blockchain technology has existed since 2009. Interest in this technology has evolved due to its potential to revolutionize areas including banking, supply chain management, and healthcare (Marcinkowski et al., 2022). While developed nations have been eager to study and embrace blockchain technologies, developing countries confront distinct constraints that impede technological adoption (Dondjio, 2023). Blockchain can be defined as a distributed ledger in which completed transactions are recorded in a sequence of blocks. When additional transactions are committed, the chain continues to expand. Decentralization of transactions, immutability, security, trust, and traceability are some of the major properties of blockchain technology. Several diverse technologies, such as cryptographic hash, digital signature, and distributed database, lie at the heart of blockchain’s decentralization. Business transactions are committed in a decentralized way using blockchain, resulting in cost savings (Dondjio and Themistocleous, 2022).

Blockchain has provided the foundation for cryptocurrency, but it may also be used for other purposes. Blockchain may be utilized in online payments and digital assets, for example. Aside from financial institutions, blockchain technology is the future of smart contracts, smart governments, public institutions, and security (Christodoulou et al., 2020).

The growing interest in cryptocurrency and blockchain technology has initiated significant developments in the cryptocurrency market and financial technology sector. While there is a substantial body of literature on the subject, there are still several research gaps that need to be addressed (Dondjio and Themistocleous, 2022; Papangelou et al., 2023; Touloupou et al., 2022). Specifically, there is a need for further investigation into the scalability challenges of blockchain technology, such as the limitations of transaction rates and block size. Additionally, privacy concerns related to user identification and data leakage require more attention. Furthermore, there is a lack of research on the adoption and implementation of blockchain technology in developing countries, which presents unique challenges and opportunities. This chapter aims to bridge these research gaps by conducting a systematic literature review (SLR) and providing insights for future studies and practical applications. The chapter is structured as follows: Section 10.1 introduces the research problem; Section 10.2 encompasses the literature review; Section 10.3 details the methodology (in the form of a SLR); Section 10.4 delves into the pivotal findings; Section 10.5 presents the discussion, and Section 10.6 outlines the conclusion.

## 10.2 LITERATURE REVIEW

This study uses a SLR to achieve two primary objectives: to investigate the acceptability of new systems and to identify significant research trends and gaps in the field. Moreover, the study is based on an objective analysis of several variables, including human factors and technology.

### 10.2.1 Background of Blockchain Technology

The origins of blockchain technology can be traced back to the early 1990s, during which cryptographic techniques and protocols provided the initial framework for its subsequent development. The significant rise of blockchain occurred in 2008 with the release of a seminal white paper titled “Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System”, authored by the enigmatic Satoshi Nakamoto (Nakamoto, 2008).

Blockchain technology was first introduced to the world through the emergence of Bitcoin, leading to significant transformations across multiple industries. Operating as a decentralized ledger, it documents transactions in a sequential and unalterable fashion, guaranteeing the highest level of reliability. The integrity of all transactions is ensured by the consensus mechanism implemented among operating nodes. In addition to its impact on cryptocurrencies, blockchain technology possesses significant potential to revolutionize various industries such as healthcare, supply chain management, and finance, primarily due to the utilization of smart contract applications. Despite the considerable potential of blockchain technology, it encounters various obstacles concerning scalability, privacy, and regulatory concerns. However, there is still significant interest and investment in the technology, as ongoing research and development efforts are motivated by its potential advantages (Dondjio, 2023).

As blockchain technology evolved, it led to the emergence of various iterations, encompassing public and private blockchains, permissioned and permissionless blockchains, as well as platforms such as Ethereum that facilitate the execution of smart contracts. Programmable agreements possess the capability to autonomously execute predetermined conditions, thereby ushering in a novel era of decentralized applications (DApps) and decentralized finance (DeFi) ecosystems (Cunha et al., 2021).

### 10.2.2 Key Blockchain Characteristics

This section looks into the fundamental characteristics that form the basis of the transformative potential of blockchain technology. The fundamental characteristics of blockchain delineate its core nature and distinguish it from conventional centralized systems. Gaining a comprehensive understanding of these fundamental aspects is essential in order to grasp the transformative potential of blockchain in diverse industries.

- **Decentralization:** The blockchain is based on a network of computers known as nodes. Because there is no centralized authority governing the whole system, no one entity has total control over the data or transactions.
- **Transparency:** Because the blockchain is a transparent and immutable ledger, once a transaction has been recorded, it cannot be changed or tampered with. All network members can see and verify the transactions, increasing trust and accountability.
