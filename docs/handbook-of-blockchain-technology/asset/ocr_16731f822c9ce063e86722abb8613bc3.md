# 16.2.2  Blockchain Technology and Its Potential

Blockchain technology is a distributed ledger consisting of a collection of blocks that register various data or transaction records (Christodoulou et al., 2019; Nakamoto, 2008). These may include various types of information such as property ownership, national records, healthcare records, shares, stocks, and several others (Duong-Trung et al., 2020; Farooq, Khan, and Abid, 2020). According to Antonopoulos (2015) blockchain is a decentralized ledger that operates on a P2P network and implies that there is no central authority in control. Each network participant, or node, holds a copy of the entire blockchain (Cunha et al., 2020). Transactions are placed in secure blocks, which are cryptographically linked to one another in a chain. Before a transaction can be added to the blockchain, it must be validated by multiple nodes through a process known as consensus, ensuring the integrity and authenticity of the information. Blockchain has unique characteristics, such as immutability, security, trust, and transparency (Viriyasitavat and Hoonsopon, 2019; Xinyi, Yi, and He, 2018). Immutability is achieved as each block contains a hash, which is a unique identifier that is based on the contents of the block. In case a malfunctioning entity (user) attempts to modify the contents of a block, it will automatically change the hash of that block as well as the hashes of all subsequent blocks. Thus, it is currently impossible to alter a single block without the consent of the network participants. Another characteristic of blockchain technology is its transparency, which makes blockchain ideal for use in situations where transparency and accountability are essential (e.g., energy DRM or energy transactions).

Blockchain technology enables the secure and transparent recording and verification of energy trading transactions (Anoh et al., 2020; Kirpes and Becker, 2018). It also has the potential to remove intermediaries such as banks or energy brokers and enables consumers to buy electricity directly from energy producers (Themistocleous et al., 2018). Blockchain-based energy transactions are recorded on a decentralized ledger that is publicly available for verification. Smart contracts are also used to facilitate the trading of energy (Liu et al., 2019).

**Blockchain Advantages:** The implementation of blockchain technology has extensive and varied benefits, which are summarized below:

+   - Eliminates the need for a central authority or trusted intermediary.
+   - Mitigates the risks associated with centralized system failure, data manipulation, and corruption.
