# 18.4  SANCTIONS ENFORCEMENT AND CRYPTOCURRENCIES

# 18.4.1  The Approach in the EU

Turning to the area of international sanctions enforcement, sanctions are usually imposed against another country, regime, individual or entity, when any of the said is operating against international, national security and order, the public interest and foreign policy of the sanction-imposing country. Insofar as the EU is concerned, a number of measures have been introduced following Russia’s 2022 invasion of Ukraine, to guard against the risk of cryptocurrencies being used to divert funds to support Russia’s military operations in violation of the EU sanction packages. It is worth noting that, while the possibility of sanctions evasion via the use of cryptocurrencies had existed prior to Russia’s invasion in Ukraine, the EU was prompted to adopt targeted measures and extend the scope of sanction measures to include cryptoassets only after the invasion. This was deemed necessary, especially considering the wide uptake of cryptocurrencies across Russia and the increasing number and breadth of EU sanction packages in light of the prolonged conflict. In this regard, the EU has provided guidance on the key regulations which are at the center of the EU’s sanctioning programs, namely, Regulation (EU) No. 269/2014 and Regulation (EU) No. 833/2014 (as those have been amended), to specify that transactions which are prohibited under the regulations shall equally be prohibited in case they are carried out in cryptoassets and that cryptoassets shall not be used to circumvent any EU sanctions (European Commission, 2023, p. 73). Equally, it has been prohibited under EU law to provide cryptoasset wallets, accounts or custody services to Russian nationals or natural persons residing in Russia, or legal persons, entities or bodies established in Russia (Council Regulation No. 833/2014. art. 5b(2)).
