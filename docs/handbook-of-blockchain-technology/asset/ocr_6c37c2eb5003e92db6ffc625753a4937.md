# ## 19.1 INTRODUCTION

In the last decade, we have been experiencing enormous progress in the Information and Communications Technology (ICT) sector with the advent of 5G networks and the ongoing research toward the development of frameworks for Next Generation Networks (NGNs) that enable flexible management over short timescales. NGNs are responsible for providing vital characteristics for the operation of demanding applications, such as ultra-low latency, high data rates, near 100 percent availability, and wide area coverage (3GPP, 2017). To facilitate these characteristics, major advancements have been made in both hardware and software. On the one hand, new hardware components have been developed to guarantee high performance, continuing the efforts of previous decades that tried to accelerate protocol processing in hardware (Vlachos et al., 2007). On the other hand, modern software-based networking technologies aim to transform the legacy network structures into programmable and software-defined ones. Therefore, technologies such as Network Function Virtualization (NFV), Software Defined Networks (SDNs), and Network Slicing (NS) are considered keystone technologies of NGNs.

Beyond the continuous growth of the networking sector, several other technologies have triggered the interest of academia and industry, such as Distributed Ledger Technologies (DLTs), usually referred to as blockchain (despite the fact that blockchain actually represents a subset of DLTs). Blockchain is one of the most hyped technologies since 2009 and has increased its popularity through its well-known application, the Bitcoin network, and its native cryptocurrency. This is also the reason why many people have linked this technology with the finance sector and the cryptocurrency world. What is of high interest and value is that, at the same time, blockchain presents several valuable characteristics that can enhance the security level of an application running in adversarial environments. The ability to create a network of trust among untrusted entities, as well as the implementation of secure transactions among participants and the ability to guarantee the integrity of data stored in the ledger, are only subsets of some of the characteristics of blockchain.

This chapter presents published research that explores the use of blockchain technology in NGNs and discusses how this technology could be used in NGNs in a beneficial manner. Our journey begins with a brief description of the advancements in the networking sector, along with their challenges and limitations, which can be addressed using blockchain technology.
