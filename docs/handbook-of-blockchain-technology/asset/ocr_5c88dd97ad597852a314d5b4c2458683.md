Although the fair use doctrine has not been employed significantly as a legal defense in alleged copyright infringement cases, it could apply in situations where a portion of a sound recording is used or when remixing.⁶ Consider the following two prominent examples of its application:

+   - Art Sector – *Cariou v. Prince* (2013): This is one of the most significant fair use cases in the art world. Richard Prince, a well-known appropriation artist, used Patrick Cariou’s photographs of Rastafarians to create new works of art. When Cariou sued for copyright infringement, Prince argued that his use of the photographs was transformative and thus constituted fair use. The Second Circuit Court of Appeals agreed with Prince, stating that his work “manifest[ed] an entirely different aesthetic” from Cariou’s photographs.

+   - Music Sector – *Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc.* (1994): This case involved a parody of the song “Oh, Pretty Woman” by the rap group 2 Live Crew. The Supreme Court held that a commercial parody could qualify as fair use, noting that “the more transformative the new work, the less significant other factors, like commercialism, may become.” This case is particularly important because it clarified that a work’s commercial nature does not preclude a fair use defense. It affirmed the importance of transformative use in the fair use analysis.

The fair use doctrine is not a blanket permission to use copyrighted works without the owner’s consent, but rather a defense that can be invoked in a lawsuit for copyright infringement. Fair use is determined on a case-by-case basis, considering many factors, including the purpose and character of the use, the nature of the copyrighted work, the amount and substantiality of the portion taken, and the effect of the use upon the potential market for or value of the work.

Lee and Moshirnia (2022) present results from an experiment of 500 individuals surveyed on Mechanical Turk. When the subjects were informed about the fair use doctrine, they were more likely to rule in favor of no liability for the defendant – a result concentrated in cases where there was high similarity. These results suggest that the fair use doctrine can help remediate copyright and NFT clutter in cases where many NFTs of similar content exist. However, more work is required to explore how this would apply to NFTs.

# 4.3 NFTs AS DECENTRALIZED PROPERTY

The subtitle, “NFTs as decentralized property,” comes from a recent contribution by Lee (2023), pointing out that artists have not fared well because of the absence of remuneration for their created content and, in large part, because of a gap between theory and practice in the 1976 Copyright Act.⁷ Instead, Lee (2023) argues that the emergence of NFTs is a game changer for artists because it allows them to take ownership of their IP when they mint their NFT and write the rules into the smart contract.
