The dynamicity of NGNs, the high-quality standards that must be guaranteed, and the high expectations of the end user, make the network management of NGNs a challenging task. New network management tools are being developed, and various network management techniques are being examined to facilitate the flexible management of network services. Such tools and respective frameworks are called Management and Orchestration (MANO), and as their name denotes, they are responsible for the management and orchestration of network slices and VNFs. Trakadas et al. (2020) mention that MANO frameworks are considered a management and orchestration suite for both physical and virtual resources related to the life cycle of the deployed network service. MANOs interact using the Virtual Infrastructure Manager (VIM) component with cloud infrastructures owned by NPs and manage their resources to deploy and manage the requested NSs. However, the increased network complexity, the introduction of new business-oriented services, the need for performance improvement, and the constant research toward the development of future networks beyond 5G forced the research community to examine more sophisticated MANO approaches. The European Telecommunication Standard (ETSI) moved in this direction by creating the ETSI ZSM Group in December 2017 (ETSI Standardization Group). The ultimate goal of this standardization team is the design and development of the ZSM framework. ZSM is designed to enable zero-touch automated network service and management in a multivendor environment to support demanding applications by ensuring the high-performance metrics of NSs.

However, the network management process of NGNs should be characterized not only by dynamicity and automation. In parallel, their security and robustness should be enhanced to defend against malicious actors (attackers). In the last few years, there has been increased research interest in the use of blockchain technology in NGNs, which resulted in the generation of new published works (Maksymyuk et al., 2020; Rebello et al., 2019; Togou et al., 2020) The goal is to harness the benefits of blockchain’s inherent characteristics to improve the security characteristics of the networks. At the same time, researchers examine how they can minimize the impact of blockchain’s drawbacks to fully embrace the advantages of this technology. Blockchain forms a decentralized network of trust in an untrusted environment where secure transactions are initiated between parties that follow the applied consensus mechanism. The content of the transactions is indisputable and immutable as it is registered in the ledger of the blockchain. Moreover, blockchain supports the implementation of logic in the form of immutable computational programs called Smart Contracts (SCs), which allows us to develop many different Distributed Applications (Dapps). As a result, the application area of this technology widens and allows us to examine the use of blockchain in many sectors such as NGNs. To this end, numerous research works have been conducted and published in the community.

Then, in Sections 19.3, 19.4, and 19.5, we conduct a survey of research works that leverage the use of blockchain in NGNs in various aspects, such as the resource management process, NS and ZSM-enabled network management scenarios. After examining these novel works, in Section 19.6 we gather our main research findings and proceed to a discussion aimed at shedding light on how blockchain can be used in an efficient and beneficial manner to address the security issues of NGNs.
