Walmart Canada and its carriers used to have multiple information systems that were unable to communicate with each other. This led to payment disputes and a manual and time-consuming reconciliation process. To overcome this issue, they developed a blockchain-based freight payment system called DL Freight in 2019. The system was a tremendous success, allowing Walmart Canada to reduce the number of invoices with discrepancies (i.e., disputes) from over 70 percent, prior to DL Freight, to less than 1 percent today. Moreover, these disputes are easily flagged and quickly resolved (Vitasek et al., 2022).

Nevertheless, there are also some drawbacks in the process, like the failure of the ShipChain project which aimed to create a token to simplify transactions in shipping, the end of the Hong Kong-based 300Cubits, or the recent shutdown of TradeLens created by Maersk and IBM (Green et al., 2020). These examples reveal that technology is only one side of the coin of blockchain success in traceable shipping data. How the market is fairing, collaborative networks, and standardization carry a significant share of responsibility in blockchain success.

(2021), that also points to the importance of automation in port terminal operations or other less common applications in scheduling and container stacking, asset certification, customs clearance, or prioritizing container shipment using IoT-based blockchain (Ahmad et al., 2021).

Most projects found are related to seaport contexts, opening up new research opportunities to adapt the suggested architectures to dry ports. Significant advances have been made in multimodal information flows and blockchain-based traceability that expand sea transport, like the work of Elmay et al. (2022). Nevertheless, future projects should adhere to essential architectural design requirements, namely: (1) exploring permissioned public blockchains to lower entry barriers and create trust among several actors; (2) enabling interoperability via APIs; (3) preparing for thousands of concurrent transactions per hour in some use cases; (4) pre-identifying the roles of the nodes; and (5) enabling off-chain data storage and encryption (Irannezhad, 2020b).

One of the most recent advances in integrated blockchain-based shipment transportation started in 2023 and is under development at the Port of Sines in Portugal,¹ a strategic location for green and digital port transformation. NEXUS will integrate maritime and road digital documentation using blockchain, supporting critical information flows with dry ports and governmental agencies (e.g., finance).
