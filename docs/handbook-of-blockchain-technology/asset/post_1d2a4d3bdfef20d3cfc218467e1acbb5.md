
## 5.3 ORDINALS RARITY

NFTs are durable. Consequently, Ordinals can be employed for generating Bitcoin NFTs by associating content with a specific Ordinal, thereby designating the Ordinal owner as the NFT owner. To achieve this, a set of relevant transactions need to be created and added to the blockchain to effectively transform these Satoshis into Bitcoin NFTs.

For example, Olivia designs a picture as a new NFT and then establishes a connection between this picture and an Ordinal. To achieve this, she includes the hash of the picture in a blockchain transaction. Alternatively, Olivia may use an off-chain signature, where she declares herself as the picture's issuer, generates a digital signature, and associates the specific Ordinal with the said picture. Then Nicolas expresses his interest to buy Olivia's NFT and negotiates with her. In case of an agreement, Olivia transfers her Ordinal NFT to Nicolas's wallet, and this transaction is then recorded on the Bitcoin blockchain.

An analysis of the existing NFT market demonstrates that the unique characteristics of an NFT, like its rarity, often influence its price or demand, or shape its dynamics. Rarity or scarcity of an NFT refers to its uniqueness within a collection. It is influenced by various parameters like (a) limited quantity, (b) special attributes, (c) historical rarity, or (d) NFT association with significant events or achievements. In various cases, rarity influences the decisions of NFT collectors as they seek unique and exclusive digital assets. Specifically, rarity is considered a key criterion used by many collectors when they evaluate the potential for appreciation and long-term value of an NFT. For that reason, many NFTs are rarer than others by design, as this makes them more desirable in the market.

Rodarmor (2023a) paid attention to the Ordinals' rarity and introduced Rodarmor's Ordinal Rarity Index (RORI). RORI includes six rarity categories that are based on the chronological order in which a Bitcoin was mined. Moreover, he introduced an extra category called "exotic" which is subjective and allows individuals to decide which Ordinals they consider as exotic. Rodarmor's (2023) Ordinal rarity categories/levels are: (a) common, (b) uncommon, (c) rare, (d) epic, (e) legendary, (f) mythic, and (g) exotic.

- **Common Ordinals**: This category encompasses any Satoshi other than the first Satoshi of its respective block. It is estimated that there will be around 2.1 quadrillion common Satoshis as this is the most typical category of Ordinals.
- **Uncommon Ordinals** are considered more exclusive than the common ones. This category includes the first Satoshi of each block, and there will be roughly 6,929,999 uncommon Satoshis in total.
- **Rare Ordinals**: An even rarer level, the rare category consists of the first Satoshi after each difficulty adjustment period (Nakamoto, 2008). Bitcoin difficulty maintains the stability and reliability of the network by adjusting the rate at which new blocks are mined. Every 2,016 blocks, or around every two weeks, Bitcoin adjusts the mining difficulty to keep an average block production time of approximately ten minutes. For example, the difficulty is increased if the average time for mining the previous 2,016 blocks was shorter than ten minutes. In cases where the mining took longer, the difficulty is decreased. Based on this, it is estimated that in the entire Bitcoin network there will be 3,437 rare Satoshis.
- **Epic Ordinals** reach an extraordinary level of scarcity. This category includes the first Satoshi following each halving event, which takes place every 210,000 blocks, or about every four years. Based on the total Bitcoin supply, there will be just 32 epic Satoshis. Obviously, epic Ordinals hold significant value and desirability among collectors.
- **Legendary Ordinals** comprise the first Satoshi of each cycle, and there will be only five in total. According to Rodarmor (2023a), once every six halvings, a special phenomenon unfolds as the halving event synchronizes with the difficulty adjustment, resulting in what is termed a 'conjunction'. A cycle refers to the time between these conjunctions, and it occurs every 24 years. It is estimated that the first Bitcoin cycle will happen around the year 2032, making them highly sought after within the Bitcoin community.
- **Mythic Ordinals**: There is only one mythic Ordinal in the history of Bitcoin as this refers to the first Satoshi of the genesis block. For that reason, the mythic Ordinal has by far the most valuable and supreme rarity level among all Bitcoin Ordinals.
- **Exotic Ordinals** are subjective and depend on individual perspectives and interpretations. They can involve a wide range of meaningful and distinctive elements for different individuals in the Bitcoin community. Some individuals define exotic Ordinals based on historical data, such as those Satoshis involved in:
  - The first-ever Bitcoin sale; the famous exchange of 10,000 Bitcoins for two Papa John's pizzas that occurred on May 22, 2010.
  - The initial transaction between Nakamoto and Hal Finney in January 2009.
  - Significant moments in Bitcoin history, like SegWit, Lightning Network, Taproot upgrades, or the birth of Ordinals.
  - The uniqueness of the Satoshi serial number itself, such as having an integer square or cube root, or based on other criteria defined by users.

Each of the above-mentioned rarity levels contributes to the diversity of the Bitcoin Ordinals landscape, and offers collectors and investors unique opportunities to acquire these scarce NFTs. As the scarcity increases from common to mythic, each ordinal gains rarity and significance. This creates an attractive ecosystem within the world of Bitcoin.

## 5.4 ORDINALS BENEFITS AND CHALLENGES

The emergence of Ordinals has increased interest within the NFT community. Although it offers numerous benefits and opportunities, there is a debate in the ecosystem with community members supporting or opposing it. Clearly, this demonstrates the varying viewpoints and disagreements within the ecosystem but also indicates that there is room for further improvement.

The inclusion of inscriptions is a positive step in Bitcoin's development, and it attracts those who seek more than just a basic peer-to-peer cash system. For that reason, it is expected to drive higher usage of Bitcoin and increase its adoption. The integration of inscriptions is also expected to lead to increased transaction fees for miners, ensuring the network's sustainability as block rewards decrease.

Opponents of Bitcoin Ordinals argue that inscriptions will not only lead to blockchain expansion and network congestion but also hinder the download process. They also suggest that the discount introduced by SegWit (Kedziora, 2022) was specifically intended for Lightning Network operations (Antonopoulos et al., 2022), not inscriptions. Others argue that Ordinals may transform Bitcoin into a decentralized file system, which contradicts its original purpose as a peer-to-peer electronic cash system. Moreover, there are opposers who believe that Ordinals could potentially introduce unforeseen security risks to the Bitcoin network, while others consider Ordinals as a spam network (Thorn et al., 2023). Furthermore, it has been reported that Ordinals are causing an increase in fees and pose concerns about the higher costs associated with maintaining Bitcoin nodes in the long term (D-Core, 2023).

Bitcoin Ordinals provide a unique set of benefits to users, including high security, decentralized trading, customizability, and adaptability. To begin with, they offer enhanced security and ensure users that the NFTs are genuine. Moreover, Ordinals remove the need for centralized exchanges, allowing users to trade Bitcoin NFTs in a decentralized fashion. In terms of customizability, Ordinals permit users to customize their NFTs according to their preferences and needs. Regarding adaptability, they can serve multiple purposes and be used as a store of value, to make payments, or to act as security for loans.

Despite their benefits, Bitcoin Ordinals face challenges such as security and vulnerability, environmental sustainability issues, scalability, and a limited supply of Ordinals.

- **Security and vulnerability:** Analogous to other NFTs, Bitcoin Ordinals are susceptible to security breaches arising from hackings and scams. To address these, users are encouraged to implement increased security measures. These may include digital wallets, private keys, and sensitive information protection. Also, users need to protect themselves from different types of scams such as phishing, rug pull, wash trading, impersonation, and more.
- **Environmental impact:** The Proof of Work (PoW) mechanism used in Bitcoin and blockchain has come under scrutiny due to its carbon footprint (Cunha et al., 2021). The PoW mining process requires a vast quantity of energy consumption, and there are concerns regarding its negative impact on the environment.
- **Scalability:** The increased utilization of Ordinals is expected to result in a demand for expanded block sizes. In turn, this will raise concerns about Bitcoin scalability, and it will require a reassessment and additional measures to address this issue.
- **Limited number of Ordinals:** The generation of NFTs using the Ordinals protocol will face limitations in terms of production capacity, mainly arising from the limited supply of Bitcoin itself. As the Ordinals Protocol utilizes the unique Ordinal numbers of Satoshis to create NFTs, the supply of these units corresponds to the existing Bitcoin reserves.
