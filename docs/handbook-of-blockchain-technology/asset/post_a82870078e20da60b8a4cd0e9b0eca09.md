Platforms such as Decentraland, CryptoKitties, Axie Infinity, The Sandbox, and Roblox provide users with unique metaverse gaming experiences in which the individual users have the freedom to create their own gaming experiences and share them with others through decentralized networks. For example, Axie Infinity is a Pokémon-inspired digital pet universe where players battle, collect, breed, and build kingdoms for their pets. Each Axie is an NFT with distinct traits and characteristics. The game has its own economy with two tokens: Axie Infinity Shards (AXS) and Smooth Love Potion (SLP). Players can earn SLP by playing the game and use it to breed new Axies, while AXS is a governance token that gives holders a say in the game’s future development (Vaughan-Jones, 2022).

The use of cryptocurrency and NFTs within the metaverse goes beyond just traditional gaming. The sports industry has implemented itself within this technology as well. Virtual stadiums have become a recent phenomenon, requiring lower initial financial outlay compared to physical stadiums. For instance, Digital Truist Park, a virtual version of the home stadium for Major League Baseball’s Atlanta Braves, officially opened on May 12, 2022 (Hilbert, 2022). This virtual stadium is a photo-realistic replica of the physical venue and surrounding complex, powered by the Unreal Engine, a three-dimensional computer graphics gaming engine from Epic Games. These stadiums allow for massive social gatherings in which avatars can spectate sports broadcasts within the metaverse. Avatars in the form of NFTs can spectate and even place bets through decentralized sports gambling platforms such as BundlesBets (Hilbert, 2022).

The entertainment industry within the metaverse has very promising applications and potential. The development of various recreational platforms and the emergence of large gaming companies like Roblox and Epic Games indicate that this sector of the metaverse is still in its infancy.

## 2.4.4 Regulation and Legal Framework

The plethora of opportunities and growth within the metaverse has prompted the importance of developing metaverse regulatory frameworks. The variety of different NFT and cryptocurrency use-cases in this landscape poses many issues in legality and enforcement that publications strive to hypothesize (Xiao et al., 2023).

One of the largest topics for debate regarding the use of NFTs in the metaverse is the legal implications for intellectual property rights. NFTs can be protected as intellectual property rights because they are works of art that are encrypted into the blockchain network. Because NFTs are works of art, music, videos, game items, and so on, that are encrypted into the blockchain network, NFTs are often associated with copyrights (Hilbert, 2022). Issues of whether replica NFTs are subjected to copyright are a debate being addressed within the metaverse community. Although separate NFTs have distinct digital IDs, the visual replica could still be imposed on by copyright laws (Napitupulu et al., 2023). The US Patent and Trademark Office has already provided sample descriptions to cover what is considered a good or service within the metaverse, and various companies such as Nike have begun trademarking their NFTs, which are in the form of virtual footwear (Macedo et al., 2022).

Another instance of trademarking and intellectual property disagreements is displayed in Gucci’s virtual handbag, which sold for roughly 350,000 robux, an in-game currency for the Roblox metaverse platform. This handbag NFT was replicated by a third party, Mason Rothschild, who offered a replica NFT branded as METABIRKINS. This third party is now facing an infringement claim from the organization Hermes (Nwaeze, 2022). The development of virtual worlds introduces several issues relating to copyright ownership and infringement. These issues need to be carefully navigated and negotiated. They will challenge IP lawyers on the scope of the metaverse and the attendant legal rights (Wakhariya, 2023).

The shift towards digital assets such as NFTs and cryptocurrencies in the metaverse has also created new challenges in accounting. The vast amounts of monetized assets and currencies in the metaverse lack the enforcement needed to regulate financial gains from this digital property. This also presents opportunities for fraudulent activities such as digital asset over-statements. This proposes the need for auditors to analyze cryptocurrency and NFT holdings within the metaverse, in which public accounting firms are now equipping themselves with high-speed hardware and internet to support this new auditing system (Al-Gnbri et al., 2022).

This emerging concept of revenue recognition and deferment is challenged in the metaverse due to the lack of financial reporting on digital assets currently within the space. In the absence of these standardized regulations, a case-by-case approach is being employed onto digital asset accounting discrepancies (Pandey et al., 2023).

The use of NFT tokenization has promoted the emergence of digital real estate throughout the metaverse. This has progressed into various metaverse applications allowing users to buy virtual land for their own creation or miscellaneous uses (Radhakrishna et al., 2022). For instance, The Sandbox, a virtual gaming world built on the Ethereum blockchain, allows players to own and trade digital assets, including ownership of in-game land parcels, represented as NFTs, referred to as LANDS. This platform has the capability to encompass 166,464 of these land parcels which are gradually released in increments. A study conducted by Kanis Saengchote concludes that the announcement of new land inflates the value of nearby parcels by 8.4 percent. This appreciation has stirred complications in the legality and regulation of these lands (Saengchote et al., 2023).

This study continues to explore how supply and demand movements are applied to this virtual real estate market. It found that when larger quantities of land are released, the appreciation of the real estate is less than when fewer parcels are released. This implies that users purchase this land as a digital asset in the hope of making capital gains, thus imposing more speculation for the need for accounting and regulatory procedures over the NFTs (Saengchote et al., 2023).

This is exemplified in Indonesia, where land sale and purchase transactions in the form of NFTs in the metaverse are recognized as valid according to the laws and regulations in Indonesia. This presents various legal implications, as digital land is considered a crypto asset under Indonesian law. The Regulation of the Commodity Futures Supervisory Agency thus considers digital land in the form of NFTs as an asset subjected to capital gains laws (Amalia, 2023b).

Legal issues regarding one’s identity have been exacerbated by the metaverse, causing a need for additional regulations. As discussed previously, avatars take the form of an NFT within the metaverse, which represents the user’s identity in the AR. Resulting from the highly customizable nature of NFTs, it is becoming prevalent that false identities within the metaverse are being used to manipulate and take advantage of audiences within the landscape (Mitruschenkova, 2023). Individuals are utilizing their avatars to mislead other users through scams, illusions, and so on. This begs the question of how fraudulent behavior regarding one’s identity could be monitored in the decentralized virtual world, if at all. The more a person’s identity is shaped by a digital hyper-reality, the greater the chances of blurring the ethical and legal foundations applied (Flick, 2022).

Aside from the broad legal implications in trademarking, property, and identity, property within the metaverse poses more unique regulatory issues as well. For example, NFTs and cryptocurrency in the metaverse are considered assets, as they hold value in themselves (Smaili, 2022). In disputes such as divorce proceedings, the separation of these digital assets can be difficult. Due to the metaverse now acting as a hub for this value, cases of attempted asset concealment have been identified within the digital reality. This prompts further pursuit of analysis in metaverse holdings. Courts have now been tasked with four responsibilities regarding metaverse assets:

- identifying them;
- characterizing them;
- valuing them; and
- dividing them.

This is yet another implication of how NFTs as a metaverse asset are influencing the development of metaverse regulations (Tang, 2022).

As discussed above, the metaverse offers an expansive array of different interactions, most of which are enabled through the use of cryptocurrency and NFTs. Resulting from the direct correlation between cryptocurrencies and NFTs with most interactions within the metaverse, a plethora of legal questions are presented (Agrawal, 2022). Nearly any in-game item can be considered a virtual asset due to its ability to hold value within the decentralized ledger. This makes regulating the activity in the metaverse much more difficult to police (Qin et al., 2022). This difficulty is what entices criminals to act maliciously in the space, committing crimes such as money laundering, digital theft, tax avoidance, fraudulent behavior, petty scams, and so on. This imposes a need for more cybersecurity models to be applied in the metaverse; however, organizations are already constructing these (Kabanda et al., 2022).

## 2.4.5 Technological Innovations

The technological innovations provided by the use of cryptocurrency and NFTs within the metaverse are ever-evolving with the growth of the technology. The literature surrounding these innovations describes how Web 3.0 technologies are being implemented and utilized in the progression of the metaverse. This section outlines the growing complexities of metaverse technology and the role that cryptocurrency and NFTs fulfill in these innovations.

Possibly the most prominent innovation of the metaverse is its convergence with the decentralized web. The metaverse is built on three key components: hardware resources (GPU and I/O), base communication networks, such as Sixth-Generation (6G) networks, and AI-enabled models. It operates on the underlying Web 3.0 technology, which is intelligent, fully decentralized, supports transactional payments via blockchains, and is supported via NFT-enabled cryptocurrencies. This is the foundation for nearly all additional innovations, which stem from this technology (Bhattacharya et al., 2023).

As the metaverse has advanced, more and more diverse Web 3.0 applications have been applied to it. Traditionally, the decentralized ARs operated through the Ethereum blockchain due to its SC, development, and tokenization capabilities. Platforms such as Decentraland, CryptoVoxels, and Somnium Space operate through this blockchain; however, other metaverse platforms, such as Roblox utilize completely separate systems to run their VRs. This poses the question of interoperability and the communication of various servers (Schöbel and Leimeister, 2023).
