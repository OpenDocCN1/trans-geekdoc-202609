**The metaverse.** The concept of the metaverse has been around for decades in science fiction, and it’s becoming more of a reality thanks to advancements in technologies like VR, AR, and blockchain. The term ‘metaverse’ was initially introduced by Neal Stephenson in his 1992 novel *Snow Crash*. In this narrative, the metaverse is depicted as a successor to the internet, rooted in VR, where users can partake in immersive, 3D experiences and connect with others within a communal virtual environment. Stephenson created the term by merging ‘meta’ (signifying beyond or transcending) with ‘universe’, suggesting a digital domain that extends beyond the tangible world (Cheng et al., 2022). Since its inception, the idea of the metaverse has gained attention and has been extensively debated in numerous sectors, including technology, gaming, and futurism. It’s often viewed as a potential advancement of the internet and VR. The term ‘metaverse’ has gained popularity as a descriptor for a future integrated digital domain where individuals can participate, innovate, and interact within a virtual setting.

Díaz et al. (2020) define the metaverse as a digital environment that is immersive, three-dimensional, and facilitates interactions among multiple users. It allows individuals to participate socially and economically, irrespective of their geographical location, using computational tools like personified agents and simulations. In this context, the metaverse is perceived as a virtual universe that provides opportunities for exploration, where users can engage through their avatars and experience diverse settings and lifestyles. Similarly, Kye et al. (2021) emphasize the fusion of physical and virtual worlds in the metaverse, where ‘routine activities and economic pursuits are extensions of the real world.’ They point out that avatars in the metaverse are intimately linked to the real identities of the users who operate them. Users’ avatars actively engage in social, economic, and cultural activities within the metaverse’s virtual world. Sarıtaş and Topraklıoğlu (2022) propose a different perspective on metaverse technology, acknowledging its existence in both digital reality platforms and 3D computer software-based platforms. This viewpoint suggests that the metaverse includes numerous virtual worlds where people interact and coexist via avatars, leveraging blockchain and digital reality technologies.

However, defining the metaverse has proven to be a complex task, as Liu (2022) points out. The literature presents a variety of definitions, leading to what Yılmaz et al. (2023) refer to as ‘terminological confusion.’ Jiang and Xu also note that the metaverse’s definition is subject to its continuous evolution. Talan and Kalinkara (2022) have noticed that the metaverse’s definitions are not static and change over time. Yue (2022) emphasizes that many definitions tend to project what the metaverse could potentially evolve into, rather than describing its current state.

# 2.3 **METHODOLOGY**

The research methodology employed in this study was bifurcated. The first step involved conducting a systematic literature review (SLR) with the objective of identifying and analyzing pertinent peer-reviewed articles on how cryptocurrencies and NFTs are used in the metaverse with AI leveraged as a complement. An SLR is a review type that employs replicable methods to discover, select, and consolidate all existing evidence relevant to a specific research question. It systematically involves the search, selection, critical evaluation, and synthesis of peer-reviewed articles that satisfy preset criteria (Higgins et al., 2011; Themistocleous et al., 2023).
