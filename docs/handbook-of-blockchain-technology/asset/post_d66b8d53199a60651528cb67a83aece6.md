## 10.3.1 State of Blockchain Technology Adoption

According to a study conducted by Toufaily et al. (2021), the majority of public and private entities, excluding entrepreneurs, are currently utilizing or have plans to employ permissioned blockchains. These permissioned blockchains include popular platforms like Ripple, Hyperledger Fabric, Corda, and private forks of Ethereum. The use of permissioned blockchains allows companies to have control over the rules of the ledger, provides better anonymity, and reduces the risk of consensus-based attacks. However, permissioned blockchains lack the characteristics of being publicly accessible and fully trustworthy. Despite this, industry analysts predict a future trend of shifting from permissioned to permissionless blockchains. Furthermore, the results of Deloitte’s third annual Global Blockchain Survey, titled “From Promise to Reality,” highlight the undeniable importance of blockchain technology in driving organizational innovation in the modern era (Pawczuk, 2021). The survey, which gathered insights from nearly 1,500 senior executives, reveals a diminishing skepticism surrounding the usefulness of blockchain technology. Business leaders are now becoming more inclined to invest money and allocate resources towards blockchain technology in substantial and tangible ways (Pawczuk, 2021). Overall, these findings indicate a growing recognition and adoption of blockchain technology in various sectors, with permissionless blockchains gaining momentum as the future direction. The shift towards permissionless blockchains signifies a desire for more transparency, openness, and decentralization in the world of digital ledgers. As businesses and organizations continue to explore the potential of blockchain technology, its transformative impact on innovation and efficiency becomes increasingly evident.
