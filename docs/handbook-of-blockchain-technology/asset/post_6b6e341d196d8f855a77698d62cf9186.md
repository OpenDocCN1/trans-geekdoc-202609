## 13.3 METHODOLOGY

In this section, the methodology employed in the research study is presented. The chosen approach was quantitative, as it provides a structured measurement method for obtaining fast and objective answers to the research hypotheses.

The study concentrated on both employees working in HR departments and non-HR employees who work in various sectors of the industry in Cyprus that are controlled by the Republic of Cyprus. The sampling framework focused on university-educated employees with a specific background in innovative technologies. This selection criterion was applied because individuals with this educational background are likely to have a better understanding of the impact of technology on different sectors of the economy. The participants’ exact position within their organization or occupational category was not examined, as it fell outside the scope of this research.

The research study aimed to investigate two hypotheses regarding the implementation and impact of Blockchain technology. The hypotheses were as follows:

- **H1:** The adoption of Blockchain technology in HRM leads to operations at reduced costs with upgraded effectiveness and efficiency.
- **H2:** Blockchain technology will greatly expand in all business activities, with HRM functions being the cutting edge of this upcoming transformation.

To collect the necessary data for the study, a survey questionnaire was developed as it proved to be a practical, fast, and cost-effective method. The survey method involved gathering information from mature postgraduate students enrolled in a technology program at the university, or who were also employed in various industry sectors in Cyprus. Participants were asked to respond to 14 statements in the questionnaire, which addressed the following topics:

- 1. The importance of using a fraud-proof network to securely hold and verify HR recruitment data, such as employment applications and CVs.
- 2. Matters related to recruiting information are stored and verified in a decentralized (peer-to-peer) environment with validators across the world.
- 3. The significance of reducing HR data processing and storage costs.
- 4. Matters related to the tamper resistance of HR information.
- 5. The value of transparency in organizational culture.
- 6. Data confidentiality as a high priority in HR activities.
- 7. The importance of confidentiality in HR activities, payment information, contracts, and transfer of ownership.
- 8. Employees’ perception of trustworthy business services in a transparent yet private manner.
- 9. Issues related to smart contracts in the form of employment agreements.
- 10. The importance of acquiring real-time verifiable information on job applicants.
- 11. The level of agreement regarding an employee’s ability to add new virtual HR information to the network with their consent alone.
- 12. The level of support for a network that validates and maintains an employee’s work history for future employers’ access.
- 13. The necessity of a database providing useful information about an organization’s needs and specific training requirements in an automated, decentralized manner.
- 14. The option for employees to choose alternative means of payment, such as cryptocurrencies.

A total of 116 individuals responded to the survey, resulting in a response rate of 97 percent. The participants’ gender, age, exact positions within their organizations, or occupational categories were not examined, as they were not relevant to the present research.

The survey was circulated among the participants primarily through direct and indirect personal contacts, ensuring that most employees were informed about the study and had the opportunity to complete the questionnaire within the specified timeframe. The 116 completed questionnaires were successfully received by the end of the survey.

To ensure ethical considerations, the participants were provided with a clear statement of the study’s purpose on the questionnaire and gave their informed consent to participate. Participation was voluntary, and anonymity and confidentiality were maintained, as explicitly stated on the questionnaire. The design of the questions and possible answers prevented the identification of the respondents or their employing organizations. No special measures were taken to track the completed questionnaires, as this information was not relevant to the study. The study also took into consideration the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) on personal data privacy introduced by the European Union (EU). Ethical considerations were thoroughly assessed, considering the Cypriot mentality. In this case, there were no vulnerable subjects to breach ethical codes, as the participants were not economically or educationally disadvantaged or part of any special category requiring additional attention. The collected questionnaires were analyzed using Google Forms.

A pilot study was conducted initially with ten randomly selected employee participants from the same cohort who were excluded from the sample used in the present study. The pilot study spanned two days and resulted in only minor changes, mainly aimed at clarifying certain questions. Employee surveys should be used cautiously, as they may provide valuable information about routine issues but might not lead to deep reflections on work and behavior. Therefore, it was crucial to ensure that the questions were clear to all participants, ensuring there were no confusing or ambiguous questions that could be misunderstood by individuals with varying levels of literacy.

The duration required to complete the questionnaire, preventing respondents from rushing through and providing misleading conclusions, was estimated to be around 10–15 minutes. After confirming the questionnaire’s reliability and validity in the Cypriot environment, the survey was conducted on a full scale. The findings of the study are reported in the subsequent section in narrative form, with summary results presented using descriptive statistics.
