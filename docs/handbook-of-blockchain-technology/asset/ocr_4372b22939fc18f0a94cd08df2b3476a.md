A prime example of hybrid adoption in public and private sectors is the healthcare industry. The need for patients to maintain personalized healthcare data across medical providers and treatment sites paved the way for the rise of decentralized record management systems to handle Electronic Health Records (Ekblaw et al., 2016). The need for confidentiality and accountability of transacting parties is highlighted through the first use cases of blockchain technology, enhancing electronic public transactions within financial services firms (Mainelli, Smith, and Mainelli, 2015).

Governmental processes are often associated with time-consuming and costly procedures. Blockchain networks can be structured and customized in permissioned and permissionless environments to fit the requirements of administrative processes like real estate registrations and the issuance of licenses and certifications (Ølnes et al., 2017; Themistocleous et al., 2023). The digitalization of governmental procedures through blockchain appears to be promising, taking into account recent developments in public services, in order to reduce bureaucracy and enhance trust in recordkeeping (Allessie et al., 2019; Themistocleous, 2018).

The Fourth Industrial Revolution is rapidly evolving, and blockchain technology is considered one of its most crucial components. Technologies like Artificial Intelligence (AI), Internet of Things (IoT), Virtual Reality (VR) and Cloud Computing (CC), in combination with blockchain technology, are widely adopted in both the public and private sectors. Industries involving supply chain procedures like agriculture and shipping have been widely exploring blockchain networks to simplify their procedures (Mushtaq and Haq, 2019).

Considering the evolution of processes led by blockchain technology, it is challenging to weigh how each country is adopting this technology. Under this new set of circumstances, public and private sectors are faced with the challenge of adapting to technological innovation and mitigating potential risks that may result from the application of an emerging technology. This situation is creating a gray landscape when stakeholders are considering the basis of their blockchain-related operations and activities. The BRI attempts to shed light on this crucial business consideration by identifying the most promising regions worldwide for blockchain adoption.

Readiness indexes tend to display a numerical representation of how advanced an examined item is – such as a country or a region – towards a specific subject or sector. Technological readiness indexes are usually developed to outline new trends and contribute towards efficient decision-making by stakeholders of the public and private sectors (Bui, Sankaran and Sebastian, 2003).
