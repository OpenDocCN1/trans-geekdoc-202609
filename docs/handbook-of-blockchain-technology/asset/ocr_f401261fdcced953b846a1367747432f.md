Integration of ML and Blockchain is thought to have numerous advantages and benefits (Zanardo 2023). It provides a secure, reliable, and distributed platform for sharing massive amounts of data between parties for the purposes of analysis, learning, and decision-making without the use of a central authority or intermediaries (Dabbagh et al. 2020). It can also lead to new opportunities and the development of a number of large-scale applications (Chang 2020; Parizi, Singh, and Dehghantanha 2018). The capability of Blockchain to ensure data accuracy makes it suitable for feeding data into ML systems and storing outcomes (Peng 2021).

This research focuses on a qualitative analysis based on interviews with firms in the financial, legal, and IT sectors to verify their adoption and existing understanding of the integration of Blockchain and ML. The key benefits of this integration are then discussed. We also look at the open research problems that come with developing and using Blockchain’s abilities for ML applications. There is growing concern about regulation and compliance, which are increasingly seen as having a negative impact on the development of financial services by requiring an ever-increasing amount of data reporting and thus deterring innovation. To break out of this stalemate, considerable automation is required, particularly in the regulation of new FinTech entrants (Imerman and Fabozzi 2020).

# 17.1.1  Research Goals

The goal of this quantitative research is to find out how ML and Blockchain are being used in corporations right now and to find and combine studies that look at how the two technologies interact. For this reason, we formulated the following three research questions (RQ):

+   - **RQ1:** *Do you believe that ML can be used to detect fraud in Blockchain transactions and business?*
+   - **RQ2:** *How can ML be used to detect fraud in Blockchain transactions?*
+   - **RQ3:** *Do you believe that any applicable laws or regulations should take into account the algorithmic analysis provided by ML?*

The following are the basis to the findings in this article:

+   1. We conducted a study of 7,155 companies that are involved in Blockchain and ML application cases. These firms are primarily from the *business, finance,* and *information technology* sectors.
+   2. We chose nine specific questions to address and conduct a full study, and provided our research ideas and contributions in the fields of Blockchain and ML use cases.
