Rogers (1976) outlined a definition for this process based on five stages, which include the pioneering/introduction stage, the growth stage, the maturity stage, and the decline stage, as depicted in Figure 10.3.

+   - **Early Adopters: Visionaries** – Those entrepreneurs who can already identify the consequences of a new technology on their company’s competitive edge and are more likely to adopt that new technology at an early stage.
+   - **Early Majority: Pragmatics** – Although they are not very interested in emerging innovation, they are greatly impacted by early adopters. For whatever reason, they are unable to evaluate ideas in their early phases and blindly follow the fashion.
+   - **Late Majority: Conservatives** – The social class rank of this group is below average; people in this class tend to “look before they leap.” They lack regular knowledge of current market trends, since they are either extremely conventional or remote. +   - **Laggards: Very conventional or reclusive**, and occasionally unaware of market changes. They frequently wait until something has become standard before implementing it.

By applying Rogers’s curve to blockchain technology, it is evident that the technology has reached the early adopter phase based on World Economic Forum statistics. However, Toufaily et al. (2021) express skepticism, arguing that blockchain is still in its infancy and in the early stages of its lifecycle. Concerns about blockchain’s technical maturity, regulatory risks, and the lack of well-defined business models are hindering widespread adoption (Cunha et al., 2020). Despite the progress made, blockchain technology still faces challenges that need to be overcome for broader acceptance and utilization. It is crucial for stakeholders to address these concerns and actively work towards resolving them to unlock the full potential of blockchain in various sectors.

# 10.3.2  Technological Barriers

The role of technological obstacles and facilitators in the development and acceptance of technology in developing nations is critical. These elements may either impede or promote success in a variety of industries. Some of the most prevalent technological hurdles and facilitators in underdeveloped countries include infrastructure constraints: inadequate and unstable infrastructure, such as insufficient internet access, restricted electrical supply, and poor transit networks, may stymie technological installation and use.
