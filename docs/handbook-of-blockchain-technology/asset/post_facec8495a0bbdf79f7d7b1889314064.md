
## 5. Uncovering the world of Bitcoin ordinals and BRC-20 tokens

Marinos Themistocleous, Klitos Christodoulou and Leonidas Katelaris

Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs) are not a new development, but they have their roots in the early years of cryptocurrency (Christodoulou et al., 2022). The first prominent use of the concept of NFTs can be traced back to the creation of the "Colored Coins" (CC) on March 27, 2012 (Assia, 2012; Pytel et al., 2020). CC was an early attempt to represent and trade unique digital assets on the Bitcoin blockchain. In practice, CCs are Bitcoins that are assigned distinct colors. This practice allows CCs to differentiate from standard Bitcoins and empowers them with individual properties or attributes. CC can represent various types of assets (e.g., digital collectibles, stocks, commodities, ownership) and can be utilized in various ways (Assia et al., 2012). Based on the CC concept, metadata is linked to specific Bitcoin transactions and denotes the ownership of certain assets. Although CC paved the way for the development of NFTs, they did not gain widespread adoption due to the scripting limitations and capabilities of the Bitcoin protocol. Despite that, CC contributed to further exploring the concept of NFTs.

These efforts came true with the launch of Ethereum in 2015. Ethereum addresses many limitations of the Bitcoin protocol and offers an alternative platform that supports programmability (Cunha et al., 2021; Karamitsos et al., 2022). The development of the Ethereum smart contracts concept can be considered a key success factor for the deployment of NFTs.

In 2014, Kevin McCoy and Anil Dash created "Quantum" in an attempt to overcome the limitations of the CC (Exmundo, 2023). Quantum is the first known case of an NFT, predating the widespread adoption and popularity of NFTs on Ethereum. McCoy and Dash minted Quantum on the Namecoin blockchain, which is an early alternative to Bitcoin. Namecoin allowed for additional data storage beyond simple transactions. Quantum played a key role in paving the way for the NFT revolution that followed in subsequent years, where NFTs became popular across various blockchain networks, especially on Ethereum.

Before Ethereum became a mainstream technology for NFTs, several early adopters pioneered the concept, including card games like "Spells of Genesis" in March 2015 and the "Rare Pepe" (RP) collection in September 2016 (Rare Pepes, 2016). "Spells of Genesis" introduced blockchain-based trading cards (Figure 5.1), allowing players to collect and trade unique assets on the Bitcoin blockchain (EverdreamSoft, 2015).

On the other hand, RP became a notable example of NFTs. RP features a trading card collection based on the popular Internet meme, Pepe the Frog (Klein, 2018). RP collectibles were the first NFTs that gained significant popularity. RP was created on the Bitcoin blockchain, utilizing the Counterparty protocol, and managed to establish ownership and rarity.

It revolutionized the way digital ownership and provenance are established in the blockchain space and became the basis for most NFT projects, including CP and many others.

Other important early NFT projects include CryptoKitties and NBA Top Shot, both launched by Dapper Labs in December 2017 and 2020 respectively (Conti, 2023; Cornish, 2018). Another noteworthy Ethereum project is Axie Infinity (Chandra, 2022) which was released in 2018 and was the first to use the concept of the Play-to-Earn (P2E) model in gaming (Karapapas et al., 2022). Decentraland is an equally important project built on Ethereum that demonstrated the use of NFTs in gaming (Guidi and Michienzi, 2022). Both Axie Infinity and Decentraland illustrate the potential of blockchain to disrupt traditional gaming. This is achieved by providing financial incentives and digital ownership and by creating new economic models. These projects have attracted a significant number of users and have become influential for blockchain gaming and the Metaverse.

Another important addition to the NFT space is the Bored Ape Yacht Club (BAYC), which was launched in April 2021 (Kominers et al., 2022). In a similar fashion to CP, BAYC is a collection of 10,000 NFTs. Each BAYC NFT features a bored ape and has distinct characteristics, accessories, backgrounds, and scarcity. BAYC collection is built on Ethereum, has had major sales, and has gained increased popularity within the NFT space. The project shows the growing demand for unique digital art collectibles and the vibrant community-driven nature of the NFT market. As with many NFT projects, the value of BAYC NFTs can fluctuate based on factors such as market trends, scarcity, and community engagement.

A recent discovery in the NFT space is connected to a new category of NFTs known as "Ordinals," which were introduced on the Bitcoin blockchain by Casey Rodarmor in January 2023 (Rodarmor, 2023). A month later, the "TwelveFold" NFT collection was launched on the Bitcoin network by Yuga Labs on Monday, February 27, 2023 (Yuga Labs, 2023). After the "TwelveFold" launch, "Domo" (a Twitter user) presented a concept called BRC-20. The latter information, such as the diverse digital formats embedded onto Satoshis, is stored directly on the Bitcoin blockchain. This ensures transparency, immutability, and the integration of NFT functionality.

- **Decentralized:** The Ordinals Protocol aligns with the principles of decentralization. By enabling the creation and management of Bitcoin NFTs on the Bitcoin blockchain itself, the protocol removes the need for centralized intermediaries and provides users ownership and greater control over their digital assets.
- **New use case for Bitcoin:** The Ordinals Protocol extends Bitcoin utility. It can now serve as a blockchain for (a) crafting and trading unique digital assets; (b) offering a wide range of possibilities for artists, game developers, creators, and collectors; and (c) creating new business opportunities within the Bitcoin ecosystem.
- **No use of smart contracts:** The Ordinals Protocol achieves the development of NFTs by associating the content with the ordinal numbers. This results in a simplified process that does not require the use of smart contracts for the creation of NFTs. In turn, it reduces potential security risks and enhances the efficiency of NFT operations.
- **No need for sidechains:** There is no need for sidechains or other networks to handle NFT functionalities as the Ordinals Protocol utilizes the capabilities of Bitcoin to streamline the NFT creation process and reduce the complexity related to cross-chain interactions.
- **No separate token needed:** In contrast to many NFT ecosystems that require the use of specific tokens for minting, trading, and governance, the Ordinals Protocol operates solely within the Bitcoin network. It utilizes the ordinal numbers of Satoshis to uniquely identify and represent NFTs, removing the requirement for any additional tokens.

### 5.2.2 Utilizing Ordinals for Bitcoin NFT Creation and Ownership

According to Bitcoin theory, each Bitcoin contains 100,000,000 Satoshis (Antonopoulos, 2015). Each Satoshi has a unique serial number that marks the position-order of a Satoshi in the list, based on the order in which they were mined and transferred (order of transaction inputs and outputs). For that reason, they are called ordinals. The initial Satoshi within the genesis block is assigned the ordinal number 0, followed by the second Satoshi with ordinal number 1, and this sequence continues. In line with Ordinal theory, these ordinal numbers serve as consistent markers for the data linked to Satoshis. For example, on January 3, 2009, 50 bitcoins were mined in the Bitcoin genesis block. This means that there are 4,999,999,999 Satoshis (5 billion Ordinals minus 1) mined in that block. It is estimated that the maximum production of NFT Ordinals is limited to 2.1 quadrillion minus 1 due to the finite supply of Bitcoins, as well as the fact that each Bitcoin consists of 100,000,000 Satoshis.
