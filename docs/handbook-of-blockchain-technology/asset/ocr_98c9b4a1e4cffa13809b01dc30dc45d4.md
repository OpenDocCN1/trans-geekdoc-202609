We have decided to assess all historic data, because if we only assess data for one calendar year, that is, the 365 days of 2021, the results for some countries may seem misleading due to the small amount of data gathered. For example, Montenegro only returned seven positive and negative results for the year 2021, and consequently the algorithm placed Montenegro as the top-scoring country. This is not accurate as the blockchain regulatory stance of Montenegro appears to be questionable compared to other countries (Global Legal Insights, 2021).

The experimental setup followed for this study is as follows:

+   Web search.

Google’s Programmable Search Engine was utilized.3 Specifically, this engine was configured in order to take into account the entire World Wide Web (as indexed by Google and offered via the used service), while the supported language was set to English.

+   Query formulation.

Regarding C, only a single lexicalization was used – the country names included in Appendix A. For the case of pragmatic constraints (members of set S), the following were used: “cryptocurrencies”, “blockchain regulation”, “mining”, “cryptocurrencies as property”, “crypto assets”, “stablecoins”, “CBDCs”,4 “tokens”, “ICOs”, “cryptocurrencies as security”, “cryptocurrency trading”, “Bitcoin”, “blockchain”, “Bitcoin as legal tender” and “NFTs”5 (Antonopoulos, 2015; Christodoulou et al., 2022; Themistocleous et al., 2023). Also, the hq parameter6 of the search engine was set to “regulation of cryptocurrencies”. In order to achieve constant query complexity, for the case of positive and negative lexical cues (members of P and N), the following cues were used:

+   Positive Cues: recognizes, allows, issues, classifies

+   Negative Cues: does not allow, has forbidden, restricts, bans

The addition of the “ ” search operator (i.e., “recognizes” and “restricts”), enables the consideration of synonyms.

The results are presented in Appendix A. It is observed that within the top 50 ranks, the algorithm places nations that have occasionally issued favorable blockchain regulatory guidelines and/or frameworks, such as Bermuda (6th), Malta (12th), Gibraltar (25th), Hong Kong (33rd) and Singapore (43rd). Among the low-scoring countries we find countries that are known for banning cryptocurrency-related activities, like Nepal (129th), Morocco (136th), Tunisia (160th) and Algeria (161st). We also observe an interesting finding; the top five scoring countries are Micronesia, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, Nauru, Kiribati, and Antigua and Barbuda. Some of our top-ranked countries, like Saint Vincent and the Grenadines (2nd), Antigua and Barbuda (5th), St Kitts and Nevis (11th), Dominica (14th) and Grenada (34th) have been pioneers in blockchain regulation as they belong to the economic area of the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank that has issued the “DCash” Central Bank Digital Currency (Auer et al., 2020).
