Sharif and Ghodoosi (2022) categorized the operational and organizational capabilities of Blockchain technology into three main categories so that its benefits can be better understood. First are the functional capabilities related to the entrance of new staff into the organization, second is related to the exit of staff from the organization, and third interorganizational affairs.

The role of Blockchain in the above three main categories is important and is clarified next. With regard to the entry stage, Blockchain can influence the recruitment and selection process of the candidates requesting entry to the organization, whereas at the exit stage, Blockchain can apply more transparent and efficient processes to learn the reasons for their exit from the organization. With regard to the interorganizational processes, Blockchain can create motivational initiatives for employee retention and facilitate disputes by the use of shared leadership.

Through Blockchain, HRM can change its face in the industry, which will be disrupted by innovative services (Mishra and Venkatesan, 2021). This disruption of the industry can happen gradually in three consecutive waves. The first wave will lead to improved verification of employees’ credentials, the second wave will lead to the acceleration of the gig economy and improve trust, and finally the third wave will lead to ideal and autonomous organizations.

During this rise of technology (big data, internet, and cloud computing), the world is facing a real shift in the way organizations operate, communicate with internal staff and external business associates, and compete in the market. More challenges are faced every day by HRM, who deal with HR issues together with other business issues. HRM now invests more time in the screening of candidate applications, authenticating their records by applying validation checks, and verifying the applications so that the risk of recruiting inadequate personnel is minimized.

HR departments have a basic task to recruit applicants through application procedures held internally within the organization or through the use of special websites and traditional recruiting agencies. As per Yi, Yung, Fong, and Tripathi (2020), the checks which are necessary and need to be performed by HRM along the above procedures are very time consuming. Aishwarya (2018) declared that there are many positive effects of the Blockchain system on HR activities when this technology is adopted. Therefore, the challenge is how these advantages can be grasped by HRM by exploiting the advantages of the Blockchain system and improving HR activities by reducing the time it takes and also the cost.
