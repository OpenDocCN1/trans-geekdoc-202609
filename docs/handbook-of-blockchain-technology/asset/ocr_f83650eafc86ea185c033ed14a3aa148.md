+   - **Research Challenge 2 – Energy Dynamics in the IoV:** The energy generation and consumption patterns within the IoV significantly influence DR in smart grids and the IoV particularly. It is important to explore these dynamics further to understand how varying energy usage impacts grid stability and how DRM can be optimally employed to maintain grid balance. Investigating these interrelationships will help in the design of more resilient and efficient energy systems.
+   - **Research Challenge 3 – Incentive Mechanisms:** Current systems do not offer sufficient incentives for EVs with excess energy to act as energy sellers. The absence of robust and transparent incentive mechanisms could lead to the underutilization of EVs as DERs. Future research should concentrate on developing and implementing effective incentive models that encourage EVs to sell energy, thereby enhancing grid resilience.
+   - **Research Challenge 4 – EV Charging Profiling and Strategies:** There is a lack of research from an EV user perspective on charging profiling. This pertains to each EV user being aware of and declaring their charging preferences and updating this information continuously. Further, a unified model encompassing charging strategies tailored to these individual profiles is not readily available. These strategies should consider various factors such as time-of-use rates, user preferences, grid conditions, and battery health. Future research should aim to address this gap, potentially leading to more efficient energy usage and user satisfaction.

# 16.3.2   Research Gap

Given the analysis and research difficulties presented in this chapter, current studies only partially address these issues; they leave a significant research gap, especially when considering blockchain’s unique characteristics, such as tokenization and user engagement in DRM.

A key aspect of the identified research gap relates to the decentralized architecture of blockchain. While research has demonstrated how this could facilitate P2P energy transactions and increase transparency, there is limited empirical evidence demonstrating the effects of these characteristics on DRM’s efficiency in IoV scenarios. For example, the effect of blockchain’s immutable audit trails on improving trust among participants and influencing their energy consumption or generation behavior remains underexplored. In addition, tokenization can be used to create incentivization strategies and increase user engagement in DRM. The concept of rewarding participants with tokens that hold value within the ecosystem has the potential to improve the way users interact with DRM. This can lead to a more efficient DR balance. However, there is a dearth of research examining the efficacy of tokenization and its influence on user behavior within the IoV’s DRM framework. The personalization of charging schedules, made possible by the smart contract functionality of blockchain technology, is another under-researched area.
