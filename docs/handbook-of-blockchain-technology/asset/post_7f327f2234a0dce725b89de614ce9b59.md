## 10.3.2 Technological Barriers

The role of technological obstacles and facilitators in the development and acceptance of technology in developing nations is critical. These elements may either impede or promote success in a variety of industries. Some of the most prevalent technological hurdles and facilitators in underdeveloped countries include infrastructure constraints: inadequate and unstable infrastructure, such as insufficient internet access, restricted electrical supply, and poor transit networks, may stymie technological installation and use. Toufaily et al. (2021) further expounded upon these barriers in the following manner:

- **The digital divide** is caused by unequal access to technology and information among various socioeconomic classes and areas. A lack of access to devices, internet connection, and digital skills may stymie technology adoption and advantages.

- High upfront costs involved in procuring and deploying technological solutions may be a substantial impediment, particularly in resource-constrained regions. Budgetary restrictions and limited financial resources may limit investment in technological infrastructure and innovation.

- The availability of qualified individuals capable of comprehending and efficiently using technology is critical. Due to a lack of access to high-quality education and training programs, there may be a scarcity of trained workers required to propel technological breakthroughs.

- Ensuring effective cybersecurity measures and preserving data privacy are critical for successful technology deployment. Developing nations may encounter difficulties in developing and implementing appropriate cybersecurity policies and legislation.

- Favorable government policies and regulatory frameworks that support innovation, entrepreneurship, and technology adoption may operate as facilitators. However, outdated legislation, bureaucratic roadblocks, and a lack of policy clarity may hinder technological advancement.

- It is critical for advancing technical developments to collaborate among diverse stakeholders, including governments, private sector organizations, NGOs, and academics. Building strong alliances may help with information transfer, resource sharing, and cooperative innovation projects.

- The availability of funding sources, venture capital, and investment possibilities may have a substantial influence on developing-country technology adoption and entrepreneurship. A lack of access to finance and investment may impede technological advancement.

- By establishing a thriving local innovation ecosystem comprised of incubators, accelerators, research institutes, and industrial alliances, it is possible to stimulate technical developments and entrepreneurship.

- Technology solutions often need adaptation and modification to meet the unique demands and settings of developing nations. Localization initiatives may help bridge the gap between technology and local needs.

**Energy consumption** presents another significant challenge. Some blockchain networks employ energy-intensive PoW consensus algorithms, necessitating substantial computational power. It is vital to explore energy-efficient consensus mechanisms or alternative energy sources to mitigate the environmental impact of blockchain technology.

**Interoperability** stands as a notable challenge, with blockchain networks often operating in isolation and lacking compatibility with other platforms. Establishing interoperability standards and protocols would enable seamless data transfer across different blockchain networks, enhancing integration and functionality.

**Regulation** is another critical aspect demanding attention. Blockchain technology currently operates within a regulatory gray area in many jurisdictions. Clear and comprehensive legal frameworks are indispensable to providing guidance and promoting ethical and compliant blockchain technology usage.

**Privacy and data control** pose concerns with public blockchains. While public blockchains offer transparency and immutability, they may inadvertently expose sensitive information. Exploring technologies such as zero-knowledge proofs and secure multiparty computation can bolster privacy in blockchain networks.

**User experience** plays a pivotal role in blockchain adoption. Simplifying procedures, enhancing usability, and providing user-friendly interfaces can significantly contribute to driving adoption by making blockchain applications more accessible and user-friendly.

Overcoming these challenges necessitates collaborative efforts among various stakeholders, including governments, businesses, researchers, and technology providers. Investing in research and development, promoting knowledge sharing, and creating supportive innovation ecosystems are critical components in driving blockchain technology adoption in developing countries.

## 10.5 CONCLUSIONS

Blockchain technology holds immense promise, but surmounting technical challenges, navigating regulatory hurdles, and addressing user concerns are essential for its widespread adoption. The findings in this report enrich the authors’ understanding of blockchain adoption in developing countries and lay the groundwork for future research and practical applications in this field. While the SLR has provided valuable insights into the current status of blockchain technology adoption in developing nations, there remains ample room for further exploration and scholarly inquiry in this domain. Given the rapid evolution of blockchain technology, comprehending its complexities, potential use cases, and obstacles within the context of developing economies is vital.

The establishment of a comprehensive and strong theoretical framework is critical for propelling this discipline ahead and facilitating the execution of appropriate plans. A conceptual framework helps scholars and policymakers understand the complexities of blockchain technology adoption by identifying essential components and evaluating their linkages. Furthermore, it provides a unified strategy for dealing with scalability, energy consumption, regulatory uncertainties, and data privacy concerns.
