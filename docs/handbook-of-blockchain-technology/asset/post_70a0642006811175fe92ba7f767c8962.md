
## Part III: Algorithmic Governance

The third part of the book examines how blockchain enables decentralized decision-making processes through the use of smart contracts, governance tokens, and Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (DAOs). This part presents various aspects of algorithmic governance in DAOs and explains its main challenges. It also discusses issues related to decision-making in DAOs and closes with an assessment of consensus algorithms that are used in blockchain systems.

In Chapter 7, Stamatis Papangelou, Klitos Christodoulou, Marinos Themistocleous, and Leonidas Katelaris investigate algorithmic governance and DAOs. This chapter examines the potential of DAOs to reshape governance structures and explores numerous DAO governance mechanisms like Token-based Quorum Voting, Quadratic Voting, Weighted Voting, Holographic Consensus and more. The authors observe that each mechanism comes with its own advantages and disadvantages. This fact highlights the need for a framework that can be used to assess DAO governance mechanisms.

Continuing the exploration of DAOs and decision-making processes, Florian Spychiger, Michael Lustenberger, and Lukas Küng present their perspective on this topic in Chapter 8. Their work concentrates on governance considerations and outlines how decision-making is conducted within DAOs, from proposing ideas to voting and executing decisions. Similar to Chapter 7, this chapter reviews various DAO governance mechanisms, including Futarchy Voting and Reputation-based Voting. Both Chapters 7 and 8 focus on the same area, discuss DAO governance mechanisms, and provide interesting findings. The combination of the two chapters enriches the discussion and enhances understanding in this area.

The last chapter of this third part of the book sheds light on another interesting topic related to consensus algorithms. Today, a wide array of Consensus Algorithms (CAs) are used to support the operation of numerous blockchains. Each one of these CAs concentrates on improving a specific parameter of the blockchain trilemma. The work of Marios Touloupos, Marinos Themistocleous, and Klitos Christodoulou, reported in Chapter 9, provides an evaluation framework that can be employed to compare and assess CAs. The proposed framework can be used as a tool to clarify the confusion that exists in this area and extends the body of knowledge.

## Part IV: Blockchain Adoption and Maturity

The fourth part of the book navigates through the complexities of blockchain adoption and maturity. To achieve widespread adoption, we need to focus on education, training, infrastructure, capital, and talent. Education contributes to the development of new knowledge, awareness, and best practices, resulting in the development of innovative ideas. The infrastructure can be divided into physical, digital, and regulatory, and it refers to the necessary infrastructure that needs to be in place to speed up adoption. The existence of infrastructure has a positive impact on blockchain investments. It attracts capital as organizations tend to invest in countries where infrastructure is in place. From a different perspective, capital is required to invest in new ideas and startups. Thus, capital attracts talent who generate ideas and develop applications. Last but by no means least, talent participates in all four pillars, from building the infrastructure to converting ideas into products and revenues. Thus, talent generates income and capital.

Part IV explores issues related to these four pillars. In Chapter 10, Irenee Dondjio and Andreas Kazamias explore barriers to blockchain adoption with a particular focus on less developed countries. In these countries, there are many limitations in terms of infrastructure, education, capital, and talents. This chapter reports a systematic literature review on this area, identifies challenges, and contributes to a better understanding of the phenomenon under study.

Chapters 11 and 12 present the research of Andreas Vlachos, Klitos Christodoulou, Elias Iosif, and Marinos Themistocleous. Each of these chapters introduces an algorithmic model related to blockchain adoption and maturity. The one reported in Chapter 11 proposes a model to examine blockchain maturity levels and presents a cross-country perspective. In Chapter 12, an algorithmic model to assess cryptocurrency regulations across different countries is introduced.

The last chapter of this part concentrates on the challenges and the impact of blockchain on human resources and talents. The article by Maria Michailidis, Stavros Demetriou, and Gregoris Demetriou employs a survey to investigate the two research hypotheses defined in this chapter. Empirical data collected from 116 participants support both hypotheses and reveal that blockchain technology is expected to generate new technical and non-technical positions in organizations.

## Part V: Blockchain Applications

The final part of the book explores diverse blockchain applications across industries including smart cities, shipping, Internet of Vehicles (IoVs), Machine Learning (ML) and fraud detection, and blockchain in next-generation networks. The goal of this last part is to demonstrate exemplary blockchain use cases. Through practical examples and future trends analysis, readers gain a comprehensive understanding of how blockchain is reshaping traditional paradigms and unlocking new possibilities.

In Chapter 14 Marius Rohde Johannessen and Lasse Berntzen explore the application of blockchain in smart cities. Specifically, they discuss the role of blockchain in citizen participation within smart cities and highlight its potential to support various areas ranging from decision-making to voting and pooling. Despite the benefits of blockchain, there are challenges and limitations that need to be considered, like transaction costs, scalability, and sustainability. The paper concludes that the implementation of blockchain solutions in smart cities depends on the context and suggests that less developed countries can benefit more compared to developed ones. The former can better utilize blockchain technology to address multiple problems such as corruption.

Chapter 15 is authored by Jacinto Estima, Paulo Rupino da Cunha, and João Barata and focuses on blockchain in shipment transportation. Although a vital sector for economies, markets, and supply chains, shipment transportation has to address various challenges. These include information asymmetry, inefficient and costly processes, and the need for enhanced transparency. In an attempt to address these challenges, the chapter proposes the utilization of blockchain technology. This work contributes to the discussion of the application of blockchain technology in the shipment transportation industry. It assesses various implementation challenges and analyzes current and future trends in this space.

Chapter 16 examines an interesting and timely topic associated with the shift towards the adoption of electric cars, their energy needs, and the requirement for efficient demand.
