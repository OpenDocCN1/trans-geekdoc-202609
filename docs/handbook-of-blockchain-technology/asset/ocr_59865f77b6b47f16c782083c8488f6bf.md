# 10. Barriers to blockchain adoption in developing countries: a systematic literature review





Blockchain technology has existed since 2009. Interest in this technology has evolved due to its potential to revolutionize areas including banking, supply chain management, and healthcare (Marcinkowski et al., 2022). While developed nations have been eager to study and embrace blockchain technologies, developing countries confront distinct constraints that impede technological adoption (Dondjio, 2023). Blockchain can be defined as a distributed ledger in which completed transactions are recorded in a sequence of blocks. When additional transactions are committed, the chain continues to expand. Decentralization of transactions, immutability, security, trust, and traceability are some of the major properties of blockchain technology. Several diverse technologies, such as cryptographic hash, digital signature, and distributed database, lie at the heart of blockchain’s decentralization. Business transactions are committed in a decentralized way using blockchain, resulting in cost savings (Dondjio and Themistocleous, 2022).

Blockchain has provided the foundation for cryptocurrency, but it may also be used for other purposes. Blockchain may be utilized in online payments and digital assets, for example. Aside from financial institutions, blockchain technology is the future of smart contracts, smart governments, public institutions, and security (Christodoulou et al., 2020).

The growing interest in cryptocurrency and blockchain technology has initiated significant developments in the cryptocurrency market and financial technology sector. While there is a substantial body of literature on the subject, there are still several research gaps that need to be addressed (Dondjio and Themistocleous, 2022; Papangelou et al., 2023; Touloupou et al., 2022). Specifically, there is a need for further investigation into the scalability challenges of blockchain technology, such as the limitations of transaction rates and block size. Additionally, privacy concerns related to user identification and data leakage require more attention. Furthermore, there is a lack of research on the adoption and implementation of blockchain technology in developing countries, which presents unique challenges and opportunities. This chapter aims to bridge these research gaps by conducting a systematic literature review (SLR) and providing insights for future studies and practical applications. The chapter is structured as follows: Section 10.1 introduces the research problem; Section 10.2 encompasses the literature review; Section 10.3 details the methodology (in the form of a SLR); Section 10.4 delves into the pivotal findings; Section 10.5 presents the discussion, and Section 10.6 outlines the conclusion.
