# 8.4.7 Rage-quitting Voting

The rage-quitting voting mechanism is not a standalone voting mechanism but is rather used in practice as a complementary mechanism in token-based voting. After a proposal is approved by a majority in a token-based vote, rage quitting introduces a grace period before the proposal is implemented. During this grace period, DAO members who voted against a proposal or did not vote can withdraw their deposits from a DAO if they disagree with the outcome of the proposal and see a risk to the DAO and their investments. Rage quitting provides DAO members with risk protection and some control over the funds invested in the DAO (Wright, 2021). Moreover, as members who voted for a proposal cannot rage quite during the grace period, this mechanism prevents members from exercising self-interest in voting as they risk being left alone in the DAO if everyone else rage quits before the proposal is implemented. To some extent, rage quitting in a DAO protects the token minority from potential ‘dictatorship’ by the (wealthy) token majority, as they can leave the DAO at any time if the majority wants to take advantage of the minority (Ding et al., 2023b). Furthermore, DAOs with a rage-quitting mechanism are particularly attractive to those members for whom active participation in the DAO is more important than financial investment. This helps to keep DAO members more focused on the common goals than the financial aspects of a DAO. However, rage-quitting voting can also have a negative impact on the performance of a DAO, as it takes longer for a proposal to be adopted. This can lead to a decrease in the value of the DAO token or a lower participation in future votes.

Examples: MolochDAO, DAOHaus
