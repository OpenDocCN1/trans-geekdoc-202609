+   - **Provenance Tracking:** The blockchain record can also help track the provenance (history) of a digital asset. Cheap, accessible, and immutable provenance tracking can be particularly valuable in the art world, where the value of a piece often depends not only on its inherent qualities but also on its history and previous ownership.

+   - **Smart Contracts:** NFTs can include smart contracts, which are self-executing contracts with the terms of the agreement directly written into code. These could potentially be used to manage IP rights in innovative ways. For example, a smart contract could automatically transfer a certain amount of royalties to the original creator every time the NFT is sold. Although code can contain vulnerabilities and gaps, generally speaking, these smart contracts will help manage legal risk since the mundane and often error-intensive portions will be automated away.

+   - **Access and Control:** NFTs can provide a way for creators to control access to their work. For example, an artist could mint an NFT that gives the holder access to a high-resolution version of a digital artwork while lower-resolution versions are freely available to the public. Here, the artist has full discretion over what benefits to confer.

While NFTs can provide these benefits, they do not inherently provide the same protections as copyright law. For example, copyright is a legal right that gives the creator of an original work exclusive rights to it, usually for a limited time, whereas NFTs — unless explicitly specified in the contract — do not grant these exclusive rights. Furthermore, since NFTs live on the blockchain, and there is not yet judicial precedent for the enforcement of smart contracts, there may be some delay and/or ambiguity over the enforcement of IP rights on the blockchain. The possibilities are vast, but more work is needed at the intersection of technology and law.
