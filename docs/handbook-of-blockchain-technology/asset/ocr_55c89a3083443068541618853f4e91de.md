# 15. Blockchain in shipment transportation



The transportation of goods plays a crucial role in world trade by connecting various stakeholders operating in different competitive environments, but all are aiming for efficient logistics, low transportation costs, and complete information throughout the process. Therefore, multimodal transportation emerges as an appealing solution. It comprises different carriers performing different modes of transportation, such as trucks, trains, ships, airplanes, and more recently, emerging transportation means such as drones, depending on the distance, destination, and other factors. For example, a shipment may start with a trucking company that transports the goods to a rail terminal from the shipper’s location. From there, the cargo may be loaded onto a train for long-distance transportation to another terminal or port. Finally, the cargo may be transferred to a ship or airplane for final delivery to the destination.

In this scenario, each carrier may be responsible for a different segment of the journey, and they need to work together to ensure the cargo reaches its destination safely and on time. This usually happens with the help of a Multimodal Transport Operator (MTO) who coordinates and manages the transportation process, including handling the logistics and administrative tasks, ensuring that all necessary paperwork and documentation is completed accurately and on time. Such a complex process is usually done manually and is fraught with challenges, such as inaccurate or incomplete documentation, communication gaps, time and security constraints, and complex regulations. To address these challenges, it is important to have effective systems and processes in place for managing documentation and communication across all parties involved in the transportation process, and that is where technology can play a key role in the different stages of the operation. With the advent of digital transformation, defined by Vial (2019) as the “process that aims to improve an entity by triggering significant changes to its properties through combinations of information, computing, communication, and connectivity technology technologies”, researchers and practitioners have been performing several experiments to move from paper-based to a more digital environment in the maritime sector (Tijan et al., 2021). Initially, digitization was done mostly within companies, as it is easier when no external dependencies exist. Nevertheless, the increasing amount of data must be exchanged between the different stakeholders participating in the supply chain, leading to very high administrative costs (estimated to be 15 percent of the total shipping costs). Blockchain technology can be used to address many of the stated issues by providing a trusted, shared, single source of truth for the documentation needed by the diverse stakeholders, increasing efficiency and security and decreasing costs (Groenefeldt, 2017).
