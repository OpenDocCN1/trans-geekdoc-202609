# 16.4.1 Proposed Conceptual Model

The model uses blockchain technology within the IoV to improve efficiency, which depends on a range of factors. These include tokenized incentives, transparent distribution, user profiles, personalized charging schedules, bi-directional P2P energy trading, energy consumption/generation, privacy, and primary energy generation. The interaction between these elements facilitates DRM’s effective implementation.

The model employs tokenized incentives to encourage user participation in DRM activities and profile creation. Given the importance of user engagement to the success of any DRM system, the creation of these profiles is fundamental to the model. These incentives are distributed transparently using the defining characteristics of blockchain technology, namely immutability and robust security. Not only does this increase user confidence in the system’s fairness, but it also addresses privacy and security concerns.

The proposed model takes advantage of user profiles that include specific information about each user’s driving and charging habits. This information is then used to create personalized charging schedules, which facilitate users’ participation in bidirectional P2P energy trading. These schedules can support the development of an efficient DRM system as they account for the unique energy requirements and consumption patterns of individual users.

In addition, the model emphasizes the relationship between energy consumption and generation in bidirectional P2P trading. It focuses specifically on the role of EVs as distributed energy sources that feed excess energy back into the grid. This paradigm shift transforms consumers into prosumers, increases grid flexibility, and improves the overall robustness and resilience of the IoV. Participation in P2Pr energy trading could result in the adoption of more energy-efficient consumption patterns and increased energy production.

Energy production from renewable sources (e.g., photovoltaics, wind turbines, and other green energy systems) can be incorporated where necessary. When EV-generated energy is insufficient, these primary energy sources provide an additional supply to supplement DRM. The integration of these renewable sources increases the energy system’s sustainability, decreases reliance on EVs alone, and contributes to environmental preservation.
