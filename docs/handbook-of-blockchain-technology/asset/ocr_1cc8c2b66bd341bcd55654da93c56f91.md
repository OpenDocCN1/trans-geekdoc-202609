The resource management mechanism is based on the use of an SC that implements three main functions: the *addNetworkProvider*, the *GetBestMatch*, and the *ResourceReservationTransaction*. The first function is used for the registration of an NP in the system, while the other two implement the resource management process. Essentially, when an NP requests resources from the solution, the GetBestMatch function is triggered, which finds the NP that can fulfill the request at the lowest price. Then the ResourceReservationTransaction is triggered to finalize the resource trading process between the two parties. The authors examined the feasibility of their proposal by conducting experiments in real blockchain testbeds using the Ethereum-based Quorum software. Their work extends in Xevgenis et al. (2022), as they perform changes to the structure of the SC and examine the use of different consensus algorithms, such as Raft and IBFT, to identify the most suitable one. The selection of the most suitable consensus is based on the experimental results, taking into account the performance requirements of NGNs, which are used as a reference for the evaluation of the proposed solution.

# 19.4 BLOCKCHAIN FOR NETWORK SLICING

A major challenge associated with NS and resource brokerage is the need to keep a transparent, fair, and open system within the available number of resources and several suspicious players. Blockchain and DLT functionalities can be utilized to address trust and security issues associated with the implementation of NS, either for the coexistence of various applications and services or for both the service and operational use-cases of NPs. The trading of a network slice can be blockchain-based, where the smart contract orders the slice orchestration based on the agreed SLA from the 5G network slice broker (NSB). In this section of the chapter, we present a survey of several research works that examine the use of blockchain in NS.

Nour et al. (2019) propose the use of DLT in NS by presenting a blockchain-enabled NSB. The purpose of the NSB is to guarantee the construction of secure end-to-end network slices in order to support applications of 5G vertical industries, using resources from different stakeholders of the 5G network. When a slice provider receives a request to build an end-to-end slice, it publishes a request in the blockchain for resources regarding each sub-slice composing the end-to-end slice. After receiving the different offers for each sub-slice, the slice provider selects the best offer in terms of cost and the capabilities to meet the requested performance. The proposed solutions introduce the use of two blockchains, one permissionless and one permissioned. The negotiation regarding the resources takes place on the permissionless blockchain, where the prices and capabilities of all offers are visible to everyone. Once the selection of the provider has been made, the permissioned blockchain is used for the creation of the end-to-end service chain. This work examines the use of the Hashcash blockchain, which utilizes a Proof of Work (PoW) consensus, and the results present its poor performance in terms of time needed to instantiate a slice. Additionally, the authors do not mention which platform they recommend for the public blockchain and which for the private. Moreover, the use of wallets is not examined, and the experiments take place on machines located in the same area, which automatically excludes network-related parameters in performance evaluation.
