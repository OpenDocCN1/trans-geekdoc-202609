| Voting System | Governance Centralization | Power Concentration | Low Participation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Holographic Consensus Voting**<br>Voting can be made through a limited number of members locally, as long as those decisions are consistent with the global opinion (4.5). | Centralization is counteracted by decisions made by the few in balance with the opinions of a community majority. | Prediction market structures and predictors deciding on the importance of given proposals make use of collective wisdom. | In introducing a relative-majority based voting system, decisions can be reached even with low participation rates. |
| **Reputation-based Voting**<br>Voting is defined by the reputation a member holds within a DAO community and the ability to assign this voting weight to one's vote (4.6). | Governance centralization is not connected to token holdings; gaining more rights is not possible with more wealth but through reputation. | More reputation equals more voting rights; this concentration in the form of reputation can lead to exclusivity in the voting process. | A reputation system fosters participation and member activity by wanting to gain more reputation and the right to speak. |
| **Rage-Quitting Voting**<br>Members have the option to exit and withdraw their funds from the DAO if they are unsatisfied with the direction the organization is taking (4.7). | The process addresses the danger of majority voters overruling minority voters and hinders governance centralization. | Power is given back to the community to protect their deposits from selfish behavior by other members. | Attracts members who value community exchange and value-alignment by achieving long-term goals. |
