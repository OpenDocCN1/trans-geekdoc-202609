## 1.6 INTRODUCING THE METAVERSE FIVE LAYERS APPROACH (M5LA)

The previous section pointed out that current approaches for organizing Metaverse layers may not cover all aspects. To fill this gap, we suggest the Metaverse Five Layers Approach (M5LA) as a framework for this topic. To make the Metaverse architectural layers easier to grasp, the M5LA is based on five layers, each consisting of sublayers. The top three layers positioned horizontally include the (a) infrastructure, (b) platform, and (c) application layers. In addition, there are two overarching layers: (a) interoperability, and (b) security, which support the functioning of all layers. A simplified overview of the M5LA approach is shown in Figure 1.2, with Figure 1.3 providing a more detailed view of the M5LA approach.

As illustrated in Figure 1.2, the five layers are subdivided into ten sublayers, as detailed in the below paragraphs.

### Layer 1 – Infrastructure

The Metaverse is built upon its infrastructure, which includes the hardware and networks for creating and supporting platforms and applications. This includes cloud computing, storage systems, processing power (Central Processing Unit (CPU) or Graphics Processing Unit (GPU)), virtualization technologies like virtual machines and containers, as well as high-speed networks such as 5G and fiber optic connections. These components are important for facilitating reliable interactions among users in the Metaverse.

### Layer 2 – Platform

The Metaverse platform layer consists of four sublayers, with each one of them supporting operations related to the development, management, and operation of the Metaverse applications and content. The platform sublayers are presented below and include: (a) spatial computing, (b) Artificial Intelligence, (c) decentralization, and (d) content creation.

- **Sublayer 2.1 – Spatial computing** is a key building block of the Metaverse platform as it blends the digital and physical worlds to create virtual spaces. It handles a lot of information and turns real-world actions into lively virtual adventures. This sublayer combines various technologies like AR, VR, and MR, sensors, and other technologies (Bourlakis et al., 2009; Xie et al., 2018).

![](img/ab11be88faa3a9baf1bf1607f21e9805_46_0.png)

- **Sublayer 3.4 – The Economy** sublayer functions as the foundational framework for the virtual world’s financial ecosystem. It acts as the backbone for businesses, services and interactions to flourish. By utilizing technology and decentralized platforms this sublayer ensures transparency, security, and interoperability. It facilitates the integration of assets across different Metaverse platforms. Not only does this sublayer drive activities within the Metaverse but it also fosters innovation, collaboration, and new avenues for creativity. Users within this sublayer participate in transactions such as buying, selling and trading a range of virtual commodities like real estate, goods, collectibles and digital assets such as game items or NFTs.

### Layer 4 – Interoperability

The seamless connection between Metaverse platforms, technologies and digital assets is both a challenge and a key factor for its success (World Economic Forum and Accenture, 2023a). As virtual worlds continue to grow and evolve, ensuring interaction among them becomes more and more crucial. Interoperability guarantees that users can effortlessly move between environments regardless of the technologies or platforms being used. It also fosters collaboration, creativity, and the development of experiences within the Metaverse. In Metaverses users have the freedom to switch between platforms while keeping their avatars, digital possessions, and identities. Addressing this challenge necessitates the establishment of standards, protocols, and cooperative efforts among industry players, as well as effective governance to unlock the full potential of these digital spaces.

- **Sublayer 4.1 – Standards and protocols:** Standards are set rules or criteria that outline practices, formats, and connections in the Metaverse. These guidelines play a role in maintaining uniformity and harmony across environments, enabling users and content to engage seamlessly irrespective of the software or device they employ. While we are still in the early stages of Metaverse interoperability, we anticipate that this sublayer can contribute to the advancement of the following three primary objectives.

  - **Common language:** Standards and protocols could serve as a universal language that allows different Metaverses to understand each other. They define how data is formatted, how avatars interact, and how digital assets are transferred. In doing so, standards and protocols enable seamless movement between these virtual worlds.

  - **Single identity:** Standards could ensure users maintain a single, verifiable identity across different Metaverses, drawing inspiration from concepts like Self Sovereign Identity (SSI). This eliminates the need to create new accounts for each platform, fostering a more unified experience, as discussed by Bard (2023).

  - **Portable digital assets:** In Section 1.3, we highlight the importance of portability as an important characteristic of the Metaverse. One method to achieve portability is through the utilization of protocols. These protocols govern the representation and transfer of digital objects such as clothing or in-game items. This allows users to bring their virtual possessions with them, and it eliminates the disappointment of starting from scratch on each platform.

Governance mechanisms play a role in fostering openness, innovation, and inclusivity within the Metaverse.
