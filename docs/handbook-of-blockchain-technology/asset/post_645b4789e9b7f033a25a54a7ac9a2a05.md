
Perceptions of top management may differ from those of frontline employees. The reliance on employees’ perceptions through questionnaires, rather than concrete data, may introduce biases and distort the true organizational reality. The research was conducted within a single time period, making it susceptible to potential impacts from concurrent unusual events that could bias respondents’ answers. Longitudinal studies are typically employed to mitigate this issue and reveal patterns influenced by business conditions and economic disturbances. However, conducting a longitudinal study involving multiple organizations was beyond the scope of this research.

## 13.4 FINDINGS

This section presents the findings and results of the survey conducted, based on the responses provided by the participants. The survey aimed to explore the implementation and impact of Blockchain technology in HR activities. The results provide substantial evidence to support the two hypotheses proposed in this study. The hypotheses are analyzed in detail below.

- **H1:** The adoption of Blockchain technology in HRM leads to operations at reduced costs with upgraded effectiveness and efficiency.

Blockchain technology has the potential to revolutionize how we exchange value, transfer ownership, verify transactions, and establish trust among users. Similar to the initial skepticism surrounding the value and usefulness of the internet, doubts exist about Blockchain technology. However, with ongoing technological innovations and the emergence of Blockchain startups, practical applications are expected to increase in the coming years. This will lead to improved efficiency and effectiveness in HR activities, reducing costs and eliminating the need for intermediaries. Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (DAOs) and smart contracts will bring automation and transparency to the management and operations of firms, similar to the impact of mechanical and computer automation facilitated by the internet.

- **H2:** Blockchain technology will greatly expand in all business activities, with HRM functions being the cutting edge of this upcoming transformation.

The impact of Blockchain technology will extend beyond HR activities and encompass all business functions. As executives gain a better understanding of its value and potential benefits through publications like this one, Blockchain technology will be embraced in various areas. One of the most intriguing developments will be the emergence of new technological giants comparable to Google, Facebook, LinkedIn, and Twitter. Blockchain technology will enable the exchange of value and ownership while ensuring trust among network members, even among individuals who do not know each other. It will introduce new forms of ownership, value, and creative expressions, capitalizing on decentralization and evolving human needs. To fully leverage the potential of Blockchain technology, it is essential to adapt our visions and avoid assumptions about the possibilities it holds.

### 13.4.1 Results of the Survey

The analysis of the survey data reveals the following key findings:

- The use of a fraud-proof, hack-proof network: A significant majority of respondents (87.1 percent) considered it highly important to use a secure network for holding and verifying HR recruiting data, such as employment applications and CVs.
- Recruiting information in a decentralized environment: While 43.1 percent of respondents agreed that recruiting information should be stored and verified in a decentralized environment without intermediaries, 23.3 percent strongly disagreed with this perspective.
- Acquisition of verifiable information on job applicants: The acquisition of real-time verifiable information on job applicants was deemed highly important by 81.9 percent of the respondents.
- HR data processing and storage costs: Lowering HR data processing and storage costs was considered critically important by 41.4 percent of respondents, and 49.1 percent strongly agreed that HR information must be extremely difficult to tamper with.
- Adding new HR virtual information while validating work history: A majority of respondents (63.8 percent) agreed that employees should have the ability to add new HR virtual information to the network with their consent alone. Additionally, 57.8 percent of respondents supported a network that validates and maintains an employee’s work history for future employers’ access.
- Database with useful information about an organization’s needs: The need for a database providing useful information about an organization’s needs was considered important by 63.8 percent of respondents.
- Choice of payment method and smart contracts: A significant majority (72.4 percent) agreed that employees should have the option to be paid by alternative means, such as cryptocurrencies. Regarding smart contracts, 65.6 percent of respondents believed that transparent and decentralized smart contracts would be beneficial for processing HR applications.
- Data confidentiality and transparency in an organizational culture: Data confidentiality was highly prioritized by 94 percent of respondents, while 89.6 percent believed that confidentiality should extend to payment information, contracts, and ownership transfer. Additionally, 90.5 percent of respondents valued trustworthy business services with transparency and privacy. Transparency in an organizational culture was deemed of high importance by 89.7 percent of the respondents.

### 13.4.2 Acceptance of Hypotheses

Based on the survey results and respondents’ beliefs regarding the importance of fraud-proof networks, decentralized environments, verifiable information, reduced costs, and data confidentiality, it can be concluded that implementing Blockchain technology in HR will lead to improved efficiency, effectiveness, and cost reduction, supporting H1.

Moreover, considering the respondents’ support for decentralized environments, the validation of work history, and the belief in the widespread adoption of Blockchain technology in various business functions, including HR activities, H2 can also be accepted.

The findings of this research can serve as a starting point for further exploration by researchers and practitioners in the fields of HRM and Blockchain. Additional research methods, such as interviews and observations, could be employed to enhance the validity and reliability of the survey method, provided the necessary budget is available. The relationship between strong organizational cultures and strong client relationships can be a focus of future research. Authentic leadership and the value placed on transparency align well with the transparency-promoting nature of Blockchain technology. Further investigation can deepen our understanding of how Blockchain facilitates authentic relationships with customers.

The findings of this study support the conclusion that by applying Blockchain technology, business activity is performed with lower costs, more effectively, and more efficiently. Furthermore, the findings also support the conclusion that Blockchain technology is expected to be embraced as a tool for HRM functions and that it will create new job opportunities in the industry, both technological and non-technological, such as Blockchain designers, developers, quality engineers, algorithm engineers, and legal consultants. Blockchain will contribute to cost and time savings in the selection process, standardization of career profiles, and verification of employees’ qualifications, simplifying the recording and tracing of their career movements.
