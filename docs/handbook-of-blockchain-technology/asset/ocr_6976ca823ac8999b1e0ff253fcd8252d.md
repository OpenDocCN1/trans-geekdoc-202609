The Metaverse is not a new concept, but it traces its origins back to the twentieth century with key stages in its development outlined below: 1969 – Cyberspace: The term “Cyberspace” was coined in 1969. That year Susanne Ussing, a Danish artist, and her husband, the architect Carsten Hoff, established the “Atelier Cyberspace” (Tearle, 2023). Their goal was to investigate the relationship between art and technology to produce designs grounded in open systems. These designs could adapt to various parameters, including human movement or the characteristics of evolving materials. According to Lillemose and Kryger (2015) one of their artworks was entitled “Cyberspace” and featured a collage depicting human figures within a space composed of geometric and organic forms. In one of his interviews Hoff reported that their goal was not to study virtual spaces. For them, “Cyberspace” served as a practical tool, to highlight tangible and concrete spaces (Lillemose and Kryger, 2015).



Twelve years after the inception of Atelier Cyberspace, Vernon Vinge, a Computer Science professor at San Diego University, USA, published his science fiction book titled “True Names.” In this work, Vinge defines and elaborates on the concept of Cyberspace. In “True Names” a computer enthusiast confronts the difficulties of computer hacking by exploring immersive Virtual Reality (VR) and negotiates the complexities of AI within the developing concept of cyberspace (Vinge, 1981).
