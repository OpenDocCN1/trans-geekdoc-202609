# 13.3 METHODOLOGY

In this section, the methodology employed in the research study is presented. The chosen approach was quantitative, as it provides a structured measurement method for obtaining fast and objective answers to the research hypotheses.

The study concentrated on both employees working in HR departments and non-HR employees who work in various sectors of the industry in Cyprus that are controlled by the Republic of Cyprus. The sampling framework focused on university-educated employees with a specific background in innovative technologies. This selection criterion was applied because individuals with this educational background are likely to have a better understanding of the impact of technology on different sectors of the economy. The participants’ exact position within their organization or occupational category was not examined, as it fell outside the scope of this research.

The research study aimed to investigate two hypotheses regarding the implementation and impact of Blockchain technology. The hypotheses were as follows:

+   - **H1:** The adoption of Blockchain technology in HRM leads to operations at reduced costs with upgraded effectiveness and efficiency.
+   - **H2:** Blockchain technology will greatly expand in all business activities, with HRM functions being the cutting edge of this upcoming transformation.

To collect the necessary data for the study, a survey questionnaire was developed as it proved to be a practical, fast, and cost-effective method. The survey method involved gathering information from mature postgraduate students enrolled in a technology program at the university, or who were also employed in various industry sectors in Cyprus. Participants were asked to respond to 14 statements in the questionnaire, which addressed the following topics:

+   1. The importance of using a fraud-proof network to securely hold and verify HR recruitment data, such as employment applications and CVs.
+   2. Matters related to recruiting information are stored and verified in a decentralized (peer-to-peer) environment with validators across the world.
+   3. The significance of reducing HR data processing and storage costs.
+   4. Matters related to the tamper resistance of HR information.
+   5. The value of transparency in organizational culture.
+   6. Data confidentiality as a high priority in HR activities.
+   7. The importance of confidentiality in HR activities, payment information, contracts, and transfer of ownership.
+   8. Employees’ perception of trustworthy business services in a transparent yet private manner.
+   9. Issues related to smart contracts in the form of employment agreements.
+   10. The importance of acquiring real-time verifiable information on job applicants.
+   11. The level of agreement regarding an employee’s ability to add new virtual HR information to the network with their consent alone.
+   12. The level of support for a network that validates and maintains an employee’s work history for future employers’ access.
