A total of 116 individuals responded to the survey, resulting in a response rate of 97 percent. The participants’ gender, age, exact positions within their organizations, or occupational categories were not examined, as they were not relevant to the present research.

The survey was circulated among the participants primarily through direct and indirect personal contacts, ensuring that most employees were informed about the study and had the opportunity to complete the questionnaire within the specified timeframe. The 116 completed questionnaires were successfully received by the end of the survey.

To ensure ethical considerations, the participants were provided with a clear statement of the study’s purpose on the questionnaire and gave their informed consent to participate. Participation was voluntary, and anonymity and confidentiality were maintained, as explicitly stated on the questionnaire. The design of the questions and possible answers prevented the identification of the respondents or their employing organizations. No special measures were taken to track the completed questionnaires, as this information was not relevant to the study. The study also took into consideration the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) on personal data privacy introduced by the European Union (EU). Ethical considerations were thoroughly assessed, considering the Cypriot mentality. In this case, there were no vulnerable subjects to breach ethical codes, as the participants were not economically or educationally disadvantaged or part of any special category requiring additional attention. The collected questionnaires were analyzed using Google Forms.

A pilot study was conducted initially with ten randomly selected employee participants from the same cohort who were excluded from the sample used in the present study. The pilot study spanned two days and resulted in only minor changes, mainly aimed at clarifying certain questions. Employee surveys should be used cautiously, as they may provide valuable information about routine issues but might not lead to deep reflections on work and behavior. Therefore, it was crucial to ensure that the questions were clear to all participants, ensuring there were no confusing or ambiguous questions that could be misunderstood by individuals with varying levels of literacy.

The duration required to complete the questionnaire, preventing respondents from rushing through and providing misleading conclusions, was estimated to be around 10–15 minutes. After confirming the questionnaire’s reliability and validity in the Cypriot environment, the survey was conducted on a full scale. The findings of the study are reported in the subsequent section in narrative form, with summary results presented using descriptive statistics.
