# 2.2 SETTING THE SCENE

# 2.2.1 Blockchain Technology

Blockchain technology, while based on a straightforward concept, has profound implications. It’s a distributed ledger, also known as a decentralized database, that enables users to add, verify, or permanently log transactions. These networks operate on consensus and require community approval for any ledger modifications. Once a transaction is documented in a data block and added to the chain, it’s impossible to alter the processed transaction. This eliminates the need for a central ‘gatekeeper’ to provide trusted transaction verification and support (Rasheed et al., 2021).

Originally, blockchain was introduced as the underlying technology for Bitcoin, the first digital currency that addressed the double-spending issue without requiring a trusted authority. However, with the advent of Ethereum’s SCs in 2015, developers were able to deploy their own logic-based concepts, known as dapps (Cunha et al., 2021). These dapps could be launched on the Ethereum network, allowing network users to interact with them as they would with any traditional application. This opened up a plethora of opportunities for developers and tech enthusiasts to utilize blockchain for non-financial purposes as well (Ruoti et al., 2019).

Shortly after its inception, various industries began to see blockchain’s potential for addressing issues such as logistics and data tampering. The decentralized nature of blockchain technology, combined with its immutability and cryptographic features, makes data tampering difficult.
