Another published work that indicates the growing interest in using blockchain technology for NS and spectrum sharing is presented by Muntaha et al. (2023). This research work focuses on the Dynamic Spectrum Access (DSA) techniques and highlights the main disadvantages of using centralized approaches for spectrum access such as the introduction of SPOF, possible security breaches, and the lack of incentive-based sharing. To overcome these issues, the authors claim that decentralized and distributed technologies, such as blockchain, can be examined and used beneficially. Blockchain’s main characteristics, such as the absence of SPOF, the immutability of data stored in the ledger, increased traceability, and the decentralization of the network, label this technology as ideal for the design and development of a decentralized DSA mechanism. To this end, this study provides a comprehensive overview and state-of-the-art research in DSA and NS followed by an in-depth examination of how blockchain can be used to overcome security issues and challenges in NS scenarios. Moreover, this research examines the characteristics of several open-source blockchain platforms that support the development of SCs in order to identify which of these platforms are the most promising for the creation of a blockchain-based DSA mechanism for NS.

In this section, we survey several research works that examine the use of blockchain technology for secure resource management in ZSM-enabled networks. Since networks are becoming self-managed and self-orchestrated with the introduction of AI technology in the MANO processes, the existence of a highly secure framework that safeguards the proper functionality of the network is extremely important. To this end, several papers have been published which highlight the importance of using blockchain to address security issues, while EU-funded projects have begun aiming to design and develop novel blockchain-based solutions in this direction.

In their survey paper, Liyanage et al. (2022) present the progress of the ZSM standardization and highlight the main goals and challenges. While ZSM’s goal is to provide high-quality E2E services to the end user by automating the functionalities of the core network, there are several security threats to be addressed, especially in cross-domain scenarios. Since ZSM relies on artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) to achieve full automation by implementing closed-loop procedures and operating core management services, components that implement these two technologies need to be secured. The security threats highlighted in this work by the authors are:
- ML/AI-based attacks
- open API security threats
- intent-based security threats
- automated closed-loop network security threats
- threats due to programmable network technologies

Moreover, the multidomain and heterogeneous nature of NGNs lacks trust among different entities as a major issue. According to the authors, these open issues have not been sufficiently explored, although there are some published ideas where the use of blockchain is discussed as a solution.

Benzaid et al. (2021) discuss the considerations regarding trust in modern multi-stakeholder networks and propose the use of blockchain technology to deal with trust issues. SCs deployed in blockchain networks are ideal for creating SLAs among stakeholders and controlling SLA violations in a transparent and secure manner. Based on the table presented by the authors, blockchain can be combined with many other technologies to solve trust and security issues in NGNs. Some of these technologies are VNFs, AI, and ML. Moreover, sensitive data in NGNs can be protected using blockchain to guarantee their integrity and provenance. The authors discuss a use case where data is used as fuel for AI and ML, focusing on the importance of data security and highlighting that data security is extremely important in AI/ML-based solutions. Data must be untampered and protected to avoid dataset poisoning, which may lead to wrong decisions being made by the AI and ML mechanisms. In this use case, the data can be relevant to the service deployment parameters and the measured quality, and blockchain technology could solve the security and trust issues, as stated in Benzaid et al. (2021).

Benzaid and Taleb (2020) describe the concept of Zero-Touch Networks (ZTNs) and how AI can be used to automate the service management of NGNs. The presented research highlights the benefits derived from using AI technology to form ZTNs, where the main characteristics are self-management, self-healing, and minimum human intervention. However, beyond the advantages of AI-driven ZTNs, there are certain limitations highlighted by the authors. Security and trust are considered open issues when AI is used. According to the authors, it has been proven that ML techniques are vulnerable to several attacks targeting both the training phase and the test phase. Since data is used by the AI mechanism, its integrity and provenance are important for the operation of the mechanism. The authors claim that blockchain technology can be the antidote to these security limitations, due to its immutability and distributed nature.

Carrozzo et al. (2020) present a combination of AI technology and DLTs to increase the security and trust in multi-operator mobile/cellular networks. This study highlights the ability of AI to offer characteristics such as self-adaptation and self-reaction to NGNs, which are susceptible to changes regarding network conditions. This research is part of the 5G Zorro project, and its goal is to present a conceptual architecture of a solution that uses AI and DLTs. The advantages of this solution are highlighted, while the authors present several use cases where the use of these technologies could offer significant advantages. Another work on the same project has been published by Theodorou et al. (2021) and proposes the use of SCs coupled with Cloud-Native operational Data Lakes to provide a zero-touch solution for the automated service assurance of multi-domain network slices. The SLAs, which define the performance of the services, are applied in the form of SCs deployed in a blockchain network to increase the transparency of the process and to facilitate the integrity of the agreement. Additionally, AI technology is used to predict SLA violations, which may lead to service degradation. Theodorou et al. (2021) present the architecture for a SC-based service assurance mechanism for network slices in a multi-domain environment that is SLA-driven. Also, this work aims to present a definition of AI-driven SLA breach detection and mitigation mechanisms implemented as modular Cloud-native services. The validation of this solution takes place through the deployment of a CDN scenario on a large-scale 5G testbed. However, this work does not elaborate on the definition of the resources that should be allocated to each service to prevent the SLA breach detected by the AI mechanism.
