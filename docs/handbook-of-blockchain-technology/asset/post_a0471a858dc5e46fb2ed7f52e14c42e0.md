## 2. Exploring the Metaverse: An AI-assisted systematic literature review of Cryptocurrency and NFT

Clint P. Rowe and Soulla Louca

The decentralized web, often referred to as Web 3.0, is a vision for the internet that aims to make it more open, secure, and user-centric. Unlike the current web (Web 2.0), where data and control are centralized in the hands of a few key players, the decentralized web aims to distribute data and control across many nodes, giving users more control over their own data and online experiences. Blockchain is a key technology for Web 3.0, as it provides a distributed ledger that can store and verify transactions and records without the need for a central authority or intermediary. It enables trustless and transparent interactions among users and entities, as well as the creation of digital assets such as cryptocurrencies, Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs), and smart contracts (SCs) (Madine et al., 2022). Blockchain also supports the development of decentralized applications (dapps) and decentralized autonomous organizations (DAOs), which are web applications and entities that run on a network of nodes without relying on centralized servers or platforms (Papangelou et al., 2023; Santana et al., 2022). To define and understand how these technologies are used in what we know as the metaverse, we must first explore the string of development in which they emerged.

The convergence of decentralization and peer-to-peer transactions that blockchain embodies, along with a fusion of other transformative technologies, has been implemented into augmented reality (AR) applications to form the creation of the metaverse (Themistocleous et al., 2022). The metaverse can be described as a multi-technology convergence, where various technologies and disciplines come together to create a unified virtual space. It combines elements of virtual reality (VR), AR, artificial intelligence (AI), blockchain, internet of things (IoT), and other emerging technologies. Each of these components plays a crucial role in shaping the metaverse, and their details are described in further detail below (Ning et al., 2023). This convergence enables immersive and interactive experiences, seamless communication and collaboration, and the integration of physical and digital realms. The metaverse leverages these technologies to create a dynamic and interconnected environment where users can interact, explore, and create new experiences. It represents a fusion of technologies that blur the boundaries between the physical and digital worlds, offering vast possibilities for social, economic, and educational interactions (Far et al., 2023).

The metaverse acts as a blockchain-based digital twin of the physical world in which users can interact with each other through the mirror reality. This AR simply acts as a virtual world in which individuals can interact with each other and their surroundings; however, the current and future implications of this technology can be much more complex (Far et al., 2023). For example, cryptocurrencies act as a financial medium of exchange, while NFTs can resemble nearly anything digitalized from our physical reality. Additionally, SCs carry out any internal functions that facilitate the user experience in the metaverse (Far et al., 2023). Due to the unique indivisibility of NFTs and their substantial role in the metaverse, the plethora of opportunities and uses of this technology is immense. This review aims to systematically analyze the existing literature on the use of cryptocurrencies and NFTs in the metaverse with the additional support of AI.

Due to the ever-growing role of Web 3.0 in the metaverse, the sheer amount of literature on different use cases is continually growing. Cryptocurrencies and NFTs are implemented in the essence of AR, accentuating the variety of roles this technology fulfills (Bao et al., 2022). Resulting from the rapid growth of the metaverse, this technology is expected to be worth over 800 billion USD by 2024 (Yoo et al., 2023). This projected rate of maturation calls for extensive analysis of present data in the space. As the metaverse is magnified in the coming years, the variety of roles cryptocurrencies and NFTs fulfill will likewise synchronously increase. Stakeholders, developers, users, policymakers, and researchers involved in metaverse technology can benefit from the compilation of Web 3.0’s position in AR, as this information ensures their agendas consider all recent activities. This systematic review will implement all the published literature surrounding this topic into a comprehensive composition of relevant cryptocurrency and NFT uses in the metaverse. Due to the extensiveness of this research, AI is employed to partially automate the screening and development processes involved (Sebekin & Kalegin, 2023). ### 2.2 SETTING THE SCENE

#### 2.2.1 Blockchain Technology

Blockchain technology, while based on a straightforward concept, has profound implications. It’s a distributed ledger, also known as a decentralized database, that enables users to add, verify, or permanently log transactions. These networks operate on consensus and require community approval for any ledger modifications. Once a transaction is documented in a data block and added to the chain, it’s impossible to alter the processed transaction. This eliminates the need for a central ‘gatekeeper’ to provide trusted transaction verification and support (Rasheed et al., 2021).

Originally, blockchain was introduced as the underlying technology for Bitcoin, the first digital currency that addressed the double-spending issue without requiring a trusted authority. However, with the advent of Ethereum’s SCs in 2015, developers were able to deploy their own logic-based concepts, known as dapps (Cunha et al., 2021). These dapps could be launched on the Ethereum network, allowing network users to interact with them as they would with any traditional application. This opened up a plethora of opportunities for developers and tech enthusiasts to utilize blockchain for non-financial purposes as well (Ruoti et al., 2019).

Shortly after its inception, various industries began to see blockchain’s potential for addressing issues such as logistics and data tampering.
