Platforms such as Decentraland, CryptoKitties, Axie Infinity, The Sandbox, and Roblox provide users with unique metaverse gaming experiences in which the individual users have the freedom to create their own gaming experiences and share them with others through decentralized networks. For example, Axie Infinity is a Pokémon-inspired digital pet universe where players battle, collect, breed, and build kingdoms for their pets. Each Axie is an NFT with distinct traits and characteristics. The game has its own economy with two tokens: Axie Infinity Shards (AXS) and Smooth Love Potion (SLP). Players can earn SLP by playing the game and use it to breed new Axies, while AXS is a governance token that gives holders a say in the game’s future development (Vaughan-Jones, 2022).

The use of cryptocurrency and NFTs within the metaverse goes beyond just traditional gaming. The sports industry has implemented itself within this technology as well. Virtual stadiums have become a recent phenomenon, requiring lower initial financial outlay compared to physical stadiums. For instance, Digital Truist Park, a virtual version of the home stadium for Major League Baseball’s Atlanta Braves, officially opened on May 12, 2022 (Hilbert, 2022). This virtual stadium is a photo-realistic replica of the physical venue and surrounding complex, powered by the Unreal Engine, a three-dimensional computer graphics gaming engine from Epic Games. These stadiums allow for massive social gatherings in which avatars can spectate sports broadcasts within the metaverse. Avatars in the form of NFTs can spectate and even place bets through decentralized sports gambling platforms such as BundlesBets (Hilbert, 2022).

The entertainment industry within the metaverse has very promising applications and potential. The development of various recreational platforms and the emergence of large gaming companies like Roblox and Epic Games indicate that this sector of the metaverse is still in its infancy.

# 2.4.4   Regulation and Legal Framework

The plethora of opportunities and growth within the metaverse has prompted the importance of developing metaverse regulatory frameworks. The variety of different NFT and cryptocurrency use-cases in this landscape poses many issues in legality and enforcement that publications strive to hypothesize (Xiao et al., 2023).

One of the largest topics for debate regarding the use of NFTs in the metaverse is the legal implications for intellectual property rights. NFTs can be protected as intellectual property rights because they are works of art that are encrypted into the blockchain network. Because NFTs are works of art, music, videos, game items, and so on, that are encrypted into the blockchain network, NFTs are often associated with copyrights (Hilbert, 2022). Issues of whether replica NFTs are subjected to copyright are a debate being addressed within the metaverse community. Although separate NFTs have distinct digital IDs, the visual replica could still be imposed on by copyright laws (Napitupulu et al., 2023). The US Patent and Trademark Office has already provided sample descriptions to cover what is considered a good or service within the metaverse, and various companies such as Nike have begun trademarking their NFTs, which are in the form of virtual footwear (Macedo et al., 2022).

Another instance of trademarking and intellectual property disagreements is displayed in Gucci’s virtual handbag, which sold for roughly 350,000 robux, an in-game currency for the Roblox metaverse platform.
