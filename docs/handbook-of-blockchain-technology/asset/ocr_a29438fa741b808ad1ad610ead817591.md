Co-presence in the Metaverse is about sharing that space with others (friends, strangers, fellow users) and engaging in interactions with them. In both presence and co-presence, avatars or other digital representations are utilized within the virtual environment to facilitate interactions with others. The Metaverse enhances co-presence by offering an endless list of applications. For instance, family members or friends separated by geographical distances, can gather in the Metaverse to participate in shared activities like playing a game, chatting, watching a movie, and more. This enables them to overcome the constraints imposed by physical distance.



User engagement and other kinds of social interaction like communication, collaboration, relationship formation, and experience sharing are used in the Metaverse. Individuals, for example, might engage in social interactions by attending virtual events or collaborating on various tasks (e.g. projects). Using their avatars, the participants can express their emotions through digital gestures. Moreover they can build relationships through shared activities like online gaming or collaborative workspaces. The Metaverse supports a wide range of social experiences, mirroring and extending real-world interactions into the digital space.

Persistence is a very important functionality of the Metaverse designed to maintain modifications, interactions, and user engagements over time. For example, you are playing a Metaverse game that focuses on Mars exploration. Imagine that you are the first explorer on this planet. Upon your arrival you might consider setting up a base or construct your own home. After logging out, you return later to find that more players have joined the virtual Mars community. You can now observe that apart from your house there are streets with additional houses, malls, and even businesses established by other players. Obviously, these developments made by the players persist over time and create a collaborative and continuously evolving experience.

Creativity in the Metaverse involves users’ ability to express innovation, artistic talents, and generate digital content for customization and sharing. Based on this characteristic many open Metaverse platforms allow users to create their own experiences, or amend existing ones, or create artifacts (e.g. clothes, accessories, tools, weapons) that can be used by other users in the same or different Metaverse application.



In order to create interactive experiences, the Metaverse responds in real time to user actions. For instance, imagine that you are participating in a virtual art exhibition. Due to this characteristic, the exhibition could dynamically adjust the display of artworks based on your preferences or other criteria, like the popularity of specific pieces.

Digital economy is a key feature of a virtual economic system within the Metaverse. Here, users participate in various transactions (e.g. buy, sell) related to digital items. Platforms like Decentraland, The Sandbox, and others exemplify this by offering dedicated marketplaces. Within these spaces, users engage in transactions such as buying and selling digital artwork, trading virtual real estate, and exchanging or swapping digital assets, such as in-game items.



Technologies utilized in the development of Metaverse experiences result in the production of realistic and captivating virtual worlds that draw users in and cause users to lose track of reality. Envision entering a virtual concert where you can engage with other attendees via your customized avatar and feel the energy of the crowd in addition to seeing the performance. The ability to provide users with higher levels of engagement and emotional attachment that improve the general quality of digital interactions is a critical success criterion. Immersion experiences change how people interact with digital content
