# 14.4.1 Issue Emergence

Issue emergence is the point where a potential political issue begins to be discussed, and the stage before it becomes part of the political agenda (Birkland, 2001). Traditionally, this would be carried by word of mouth or through newspaper op-eds or letters to the editor before politicians in city hall would raise the issue in the council. Today, social media and digital technologies are also “spaces” where issues emerge (Grzywinska and Borden, 2012). However, politicians can also be more proactive and use technologies to identify problems earlier. Polls, such as regular citizen satisfaction surveys, can uncover underlying issues, and consultations can help better understand these issues. Sensing technologies and data analytics can likewise help uncover problematic areas. Just consider how Google Maps shows traffic jams and suggests alternative routes – the same technology could help discover hidden danger areas for cyclists and pedestrians, for example. For citizens, petitions can be a way of getting an issue towards the agenda-setting stage.

A typical complaint in the process between issue emergence and agenda setting is that special interest groups or certain parties have an easier path to politicians’ ears (Johannessen, Saebo, and Flak, 2016), and this is where the transparent nature of blockchains can help alleviate concerns – as using the blockchain to register these activities would lead to more openness.

# 14.4.2 Agenda Setting

Agenda setting is the next stage of the political process. This is where an issue goes from something being discussed in the local media or on local Facebook groups to something being discussed by politicians in formal sessions (Birkland, 2001). Citizen involvement at this stage varies widely depending on democratic traditions and the case in question. Still, at least for controversial or more significant issues, there is often some formalized hearing process. This can involve technologies for polling citizens on their views, more comprehensive consultation processes with different stakeholders invited to voice their opinions, or citizens making petitions for a case to be put on the political agenda. In direct democracies, citizens can vote on which issues should go to the next stage. This process is similar to the emergence phase, and the primary benefit of blockchain technologies here is transparency and openness. Having an easy way of showing whose concerns have been heard makes it easier to identify groups that have not been consulted.
