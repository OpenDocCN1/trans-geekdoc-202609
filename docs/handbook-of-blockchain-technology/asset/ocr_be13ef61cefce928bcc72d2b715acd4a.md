Another published work that indicates the growing interest in using blockchain technology for NS and spectrum sharing is presented by Muntaha et al. (2023). This research work focuses on the Dynamic Spectrum Access (DSA) techniques and highlights the main disadvantages of using centralized approaches for spectrum access such as the introduction of SPoF, possible security breaches, and the lack of incentive-based sharing. To overcome these issues, the authors claim that decentralized and distributed technologies, such as blockchain, can be examined and used beneficially. Blockchain’s main characteristics, such as the absence of SPoF, the immutability of data stored in the ledger, increased traceability, and the decentralization of the network, label this technology as ideal for the design and development of a decentralized DSA mechanism. To this end, this study provides a comprehensive overview and state-of-the-art research in DSA and NS followed by an in-depth examination of how blockchain can be used to overcome security issues and challenges in NS scenarios. Moreover, this research examines the characteristics of several open-source blockchain platforms that support the development of SCs in order to identify which of these platforms are the most promising for the creation of a blockchain-based DSA mechanism for NS.

# 19.5 BLOCKCHAIN FOR SECURE ZSM-ENABLED NETWORK MANAGEMENT

In this section, we survey several research works that examine the use of blockchain technology for secure resource management in ZSM-enabled networks. Since networks are becoming self-managed and self-orchestrated with the introduction of AI technology in the MANO processes, the existence of a highly secure framework that safeguards the proper functionality of the network is extremely important. To this end, several papers have been published which highlight the importance of using blockchain to address security issues, while European Union (EU) funded projects have begun aiming to design and develop novel blockchain-based solutions in this direction.

In their survey paper, Liyanage et al. (2022) present the progress of the ZSM standardization and highlight the main goals and challenges. While ZSM’s goal is to provide high-quality E2E services to the end user by automating the functionalities of the core network, there are several security threats to be addressed, especially in cross-domain scenarios. Since ZSM relies on artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) to achieve full automation by implementing closed-loop procedures and operating core management services, components that implement these two technologies need to be secured. The security threats highlighted in this work by the authors are: ML/AI-based attacks, open Application Programming Interface (API) security threats, intent-based security threats, automated closed-loop network-based security threats, and threats due to programmable network technologies. Moreover, the multidomain and heterogeneous nature of NGNs labels trust among different entities as a major issue. According to the authors, these open issues have not been sufficiently explored, although there are some published ideas where the use of blockchain is discussed as a solution.
