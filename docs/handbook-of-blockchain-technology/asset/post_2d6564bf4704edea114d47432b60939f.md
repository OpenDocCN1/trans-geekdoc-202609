### 6.1 INTRODUCTION

Blockchain and cryptocurrencies offer a wide range of opportunities and innovation while challenging the traditional ways of doing business. Bitcoin is becoming more and more popular as a new means of payment. Bitcoin and consequently blockchain were born in response to a clear need: a fast, cheap, and secure system to manage financial transactions (Dennis and Disso, 2019; Morra, 2020). Various organizations soon realized the potential of the technology for re-engineering several of their applications (Themistocleous et al., 2018). Examples of decentralized applications include, but are not limited to, Gnosis¹ and Augur,² both prediction marketplace leaders that leverage the capability of blockchain oracles in a decentralized and secure manner (Lo et al., 2020). Other projects like Chainlink³ offer third-party oracle services to smart contracts, allowing blockchains to securely interact with external data feeds and payment systems to verify data and enable interaction with smart contracts (Kaleem and Shi, 2021). Decentralized finance platforms like AAVE allow the borrower to choose between a variable and a stable interest rate (Gudgeon et al., 2020).

Blockchains are paving the way for groundbreaking research, technology development, and disruptive innovation, leading to new revolutionary business models (Sabry et al., 2019). It builds on existing technologies and research from distributed computing (e.g., peer-to-peer networks), data structures, cryptographic techniques, consensus algorithms, and game theory. Blockchain enables the maintenance of a growing digital ledger of data records, referred to as transactions, which are validated and confirmed by participating members (referred to as nodes) in a peer-to-peer decentralized network. Verified transactions are recorded in the ledger, which is kept by all nodes. Blockchain technology provides decentralized solutions in nature while removing all intermediaries. The integrity of the data is confirmed by the consensus of the majority of the nodes (Rasheed and Louca, 2022). Depending upon the use case, consensus among nodes can be achieved in different ways, ensuring better security and safety of the data (Agrawal and Gupta, 2021; Gupta et al., 2020). Blockchain technology can be implemented to resolve many real-life problems across almost every industry in the world. It is expected to revolutionize not only the technical structure of our communication and information technology but also the way our societies function (Chitchyan et al., 2018; Monrat et al., 2019). However, achieving security, scalability, and decentralization simultaneously may not always be possible due to what is referred to by the blockchain community as “the blockchain trilemma.” This refers to the problem of blockchain providing two out of the three benefits, forcing creators to sacrifice one of the three elements (Chen et al., 2021; Hafid et al., 2020; Junis et al., 2019).

Crypto tokens of any kind are a digital medium of exchanging information, built upon blockchain technology. This information can be of a monetary nature, proprietary nature, representing voting rights, or almost anything else. Their main use has primarily been an investment vehicle (Gomez, 2017). Countries worldwide are exploring the crypto industry, each treating cryptocurrencies differently. The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) in the United States has defined crypto as a security (Chohan, 2017; Goforth, 2018), whereas El Salvador has embraced Bitcoin as a legal tender, beginning a new era for the sector as countries start to adopt cryptocurrencies (Gorjón, 2021).

Moreover, the development of the Metaverse provides a suitable social setting for asset tokenization to democratize illiquid assets and investments. These can be tangible real-world assets like real estate, vehicles, commodities, or digital assets such as images, films, recordings, and so on, within the virtual realm. All assets are fragmented into their smallest units of ownership, which can be securely stored and quickly circulated within a Metaverse using blockchain technology (Chang et al., 2023). Numerous Metaverse projects have been constructed on the foundation of blockchain, distinguished by the creation of characters or virtual assets of the virtual worlds as non-fungible tokens (NFTs) (Huang et al., 2023; Themistocleous et al., 2022). The majority of blockchain-based applications in the Metaverse refer to specific types of digital assets. While cryptocurrency, NFT, virtual real estate, user avatars, and user-generated content (UGC) are clearly digital assets, it could be argued that any data generated and stored in the Metaverse can be considered a digital asset due to its commercial value (Truong et al., 2023).

By leveraging blockchain technology, tokenized platforms can establish virtual markets, globalizing liquidity for all assets by creating a platform where these assets can be traded without a central authority. Virtually every tangible asset, from a house to a boat or a painting, could be tokenized. Ownership or other property rights over the asset can be transferred between parties without intermediaries or central authorities when a digital token is exchanged via a smart contract (Juan et al., 2023).

The objective of this chapter is to examine the challenges and advantages associated with tokenization. This analysis is conducted through a systematic review of existing literature and examines its potential to revolutionize alternative investments. Additionally, the study employs VOSviewer to analyze bibliometric networks, providing further insights into the overall structure of scientific knowledge on this topic as well as future trends. Gaining insights into the benefits and limitations of tokenization allows organizations and individuals to strengthen their data security, make informed choices, cut expenses, and refine security protocols. It also allows them to anticipate future trends. Furthermore, these insights will aid researchers in addressing the various challenges associated with tokenization and in developing innovative business models.
