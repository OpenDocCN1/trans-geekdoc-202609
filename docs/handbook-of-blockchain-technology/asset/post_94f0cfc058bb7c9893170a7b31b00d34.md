Blockchain can enable employees to securely share sensitive information with their employers, including skill references, accomplishments, and qualifications, which can be digitally verified for validity. Consequently, major organizations like Google, Facebook, Amazon, IBM, and Walmart are investing heavily in Blockchain technology and testing its applications across various functions (CB Insights, 2019). Thus, Blockchain adds value to systems through a verification mechanism based on an incentive structure, enabling decision-making processes that involve a network where users become decision-makers (Sharif and Ghodoosi, 2022). This creates trust among users who are now not having to rely on a central authority or government, but rather on verification by other users (Tapscott and Tapscott, 2017). This unique decision-making process is facilitated by engaging with a network through Blockchain platforms, where users can use pseudonymous or be anonymous (Aitzhan and Svetinovic, 2016).

The breakthrough of Blockchain began in 2017 when IBM attempted to integrate the technology into the global supply chain, combining it with IoT (Ho et al., 2021). This initiative led to the development of a managerial platform based on the Blockchain concept, which enabled the recording and tracing of spare parts, resulting in effective decision-making, reduced maintenance, and improved inventory control. Blockchain has also been employed in various other domains, such as storing personal health records (Kim et al., 2022), addressing critical broadcasting issues (Shrestha et al., 2020), resolving privacy concerns (Tian et al., 2019), and supporting fault tolerance and decentralization (Feng and Yan, 2019).

However, concerns are growing regarding the digital transformation in organizations and its ability to assist HRM in strategic decision-making (Marler and Parry, 2016). While software tools can assist in reducing administrative work, enabling leaders to focus on strategic tasks and be seen as trusted advisors by HR (Florkowski, 2019; Marler and Parry, 2016), the increasing reliance on technology in HRM increases the possibility of gradually diminishing HR’s functional importance (Parry and Strohmeier, 2014). As we enter the digital age, adopting cloud platforms becomes necessary. Without control over organizational data, the perception of the organization’s importance may decline (Shrivastava and Shaw, 2003). Similarly, HR’s functional significance may gradually diminish as technology is increasingly employed for HR self-service applications.

Some specialized software, such as Customer Relationship Management (CRM) and Enterprise Resource Planning, deals with HRM issues; however, their application is limited because it is expensive to apply massive customization or even adjust to add new functions.

The support of employees in developing capabilities and commitment in a societal context, and not only in an organizational context, is a constituent part of the HRM strategy (Bratton and Gold, 2017).

Within this context, Bratton and Gold (2017) describe three subdomains of HRM: the first is Micro HRM, which reflects the small working groups of employees; the second is the International HRM dimension, which reflects the situation with employees working in global organizations; and finally the Strategic HRM, which reflects the above-mentioned concerns of the organization’s business strategy on the HR strategy.

In line with the above strategies, change management aims to facilitate employees in initiating changes in parallel with their normal routine administrative duties while ensuring the legal and risk compliance of all activities of the organization.

Especially with risk management and the data protection law requirements, writers such as Wang et al. (2017) and Torrington et al. (2020) have tried to classify HRM functions in a way that describes the above activities.

First, Wang et al. (2017) suggested that information management related to HRM is concerned with
- basic information on HRM issues such as employee inclusion
- information regarding post-appointment and title
- information regarding deployment and training
- information regarding salary and performance appraisal

Later, Torrington et al. (2020) classified HRM functions into four big categories concerning
- change management
- administrative activities
- performance
- staffing, which is the traditional HRM activity that now considers performance and contains labor relations

Employee training and development remains in all the above concept classifications and has repercussions in information requirements and remains a core activity of HRM.

Sharif and Ghodoosi (2022) categorized the operational and organizational capabilities of Blockchain technology into three main categories so that its benefits can be better understood. First are the functional capabilities related to the entrance of new staff into the organization, second is related to the exit of staff from the organization, and third interorganizational affairs.
- functional capabilities related to the entrance of new staff into the organization
- related to the exit of staff from the organization
- interorganizational affairs

The role of Blockchain in the above three main categories is important and is clarified next. With regard to the entry stage, Blockchain can influence the recruitment and selection process of the candidates requesting entry to the organization, whereas at the exit stage, Blockchain can apply more transparent and efficient processes to learn the reasons for their exit from the organization. With regard to the interorganizational processes, Blockchain can create motivational initiatives for employee retention and facilitate disputes by the use of shared leadership.

Through Blockchain, HRM can change its face in the industry, which will be disrupted by innovative services (Mishra and Venkatesan, 2021).

This disruption of the industry can happen gradually in three consecutive waves. The first wave will lead to improved verification of employees’ credentials, the second wave will lead to the acceleration of the gig economy and improve trust, and finally the third wave will lead to ideal and autonomous organizations.
- improved verification of employees’ credentials
- acceleration of the gig economy and improve trust
- ideal and autonomous organizations

During this rise of technology (big data, internet, and cloud computing), the world is facing a real shift in the way organizations operate, communicate with internal staff and external business associates, and compete in the market.

More challenges are faced every day by HRM, who deal with HR issues together with other business issues. HRM now invests more time in the screening of candidate applications, authenticating their records by applying validation checks, and verifying the applications so that the risk of recruiting inadequate personnel is minimized.

HR departments have a basic task to recruit applicants through application procedures held internally within the organization or through the use of special websites and traditional recruiting agencies.

As per Yi, Yung, Fong, and Tripathi (2020), the checks which are necessary and need to be performed by HRM along the above procedures are very time consuming.

Aishwarya (2018) declared that there are many positive effects of the Blockchain system on HR activities when this technology is adopted.

Therefore, the challenge is how these advantages can be grasped by HRM by exploiting the advantages of the Blockchain system and improving HR activities by reducing the time it takes and also the cost.

The Blockchain system provides a protected, irreversible function that encourages organizations to assess prospective applicants by checking their prior accomplishments and qualifications in the area they apply for.
