# 2. Types of artificial intelligence applied in lung cancer

The diagnosis of lung cancer has a long history. Pathological findings and analysis of tumor markers have become important diagnostic tools. However, these approaches have limitations. Firstly, for pathological findings, the current gold for lung cancer diagnosis is mainly performed through invasive procedures and lacks extensive clinical accuracy. Secondly, tumor markers used in clinical applications, some of which are cancer nonubigen, which may lead to many errors in clinical diagnosis. Diagnostic approaches (e.g., X-ray, computed tomography) have revolutionized the diagnosis of lung cancer, but small lymph nodes or nodal markers are still not readily detectable and may be missed. As discussed here the various types of artificial intelligence applied in lung cancer. They include NLP, deep learning and machine learning. # 2.1. Machine learning and deep learning

In recent years, there are big data technologies to contribute to a spurt in the scale of medical data. The wide variety of data has placed greater demands on data processing, storage, analysis, and utilization. Advances in these fields have been widely incorporated with clinical applications in lung cancer [4]. All technologies include machine learning, deep learning, and natural language processing, and the relationship between them is shown in Fig. 3.

Despite of the wide range of applications in lung cancer AI diagnosis, AI faces many difficulties in achieving the full process from automatic slide viewing to medical report compilation and using digital health records. Electronic health records are helping the lung cancer diagnosis process and have important implications for pathologists, both in the first and in communicating with patients. However, information systems are only able to extract and translate this knowledge to the manual processing. Manual processing of information is costly, error-prone and has inherent limitations. Information extraction can be improved by deep learning, which is represented by deep layers. As input datasets include high arithmetic requirements and difficulties in data circulation. The process of intelligent health diagnosis can be improved by a deep fusion of NLP.
