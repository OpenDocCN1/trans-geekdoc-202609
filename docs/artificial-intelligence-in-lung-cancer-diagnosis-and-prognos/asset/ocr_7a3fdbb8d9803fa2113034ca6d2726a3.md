# 3.3. Prediction of lung cancer by mutant types

Lung tumors of different subtypes may differ at the molecular level and therefore respond to their therapies. Genetic alterations with prognostic/predictive significance in NSCLC [52] include EGFR and anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) gene mutations. EGFR status is the key to select appropriate treatment. Cox et al. developed a model for gene mutations in adenocarcinoma using deep learning technique [36]. They trained and input into the model to predict mutation-related features from the cancer genome atlas.

80%–90% of lung cancer have different histologies and include their main subtypes, namely squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, and large cell carcinoma. Due to the differences in the subtypes of lung cancer, it is important to accurately classify the types of lung cancer in clinical practice. Deep learning could be used to identify the pathological patterns of lung cancer. Tracheobronchial cytology has been used to identify the different types of lung cancer. Teramato et al. analyzed the classification of 76 cases of lung cancer, including squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, and large cell carcinoma. They used feature extraction to determine the type with an accuracy of 71.1%, which was compared to the classification of trained cytopathologists [54]. Khorani et al developed a new fine-tuned transformer model for the classification of adenocarcinoma from 100% high-resolution localization images with an accuracy of 75%–90% [55]. Cox and al normally used 1634 slide images (1176 tumor cases and 458 normal samples) to train the model with a 70% training set, a 15% validation set, and a 15% test set [56]. The method of image segmentation data were used to train input v-3, and further was used to verify and test in an independent data set. The results showed that the morphological characteristics of the images, such, nuclear shape, were normal, squamous cell carcinoma, and adenocarcinoma. The results showed comparable performance with pathologists' results with an accuracy of 97%.

These major subtypes of lung adenocarcinoma present different prognosis, outcomes [58] and responses to treatment [59]. The classification of subtypes remains a challenge.
