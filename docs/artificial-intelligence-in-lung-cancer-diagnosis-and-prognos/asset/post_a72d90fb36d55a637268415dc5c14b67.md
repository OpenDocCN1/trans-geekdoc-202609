Lung tumors of different subtypes may differ at the molecular level and therefore respond to their therapies. Genetic alterations with prognostic/predictive significance in NSCLC [52] include EGFR and anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) gene mutations. EGFR status is the key to select appropriate treatment. Cox et al. developed a model for gene mutations in adenocarcinoma using deep learning technique [36]. They trained and input into the model to predict mutation-related features from the cancer genome atlas.

80%–90% of lung cancer have different histologies and include their main subtypes, namely squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, and large cell carcinoma. Due to the differences in the subtypes of lung cancer, it is important to accurately classify the types of lung cancer in clinical practice. Deep learning could be used to identify the pathological patterns of lung cancer. Tracheobronchial cytology has been used to identify the different types of lung cancer. ## 5.2. Future

With the development of medical technology, non-invasive AI detection of tumors and analysis of PET/CT has been used in clinical setting. The detection of tumor DNA (ctDNA) in peripheral blood is reported by CAP-Seq. The detection of RNAs and other chemical markers in peripheral blood is expected to be achieved within a few years. To optimize the evaluation and efficacy of different methods still need to be addressed. To date, therapy of lung cancer is not based on the use of AI. Because effective therapy is primarily dependent on PD-1 expression levels in tumors, and because there are many challenges in the development of AI technology for tumor segmentation, morphology, and heterogeneity.

The prognosis of lung patients can be accurately based on the information in pathological sections. A study analyzed 2186 patients with lung adenocarcinoma and squamous carcinoma from TCGA database [69]. The quantitative features of images were extracted and the features were the most significant for predicting survival. The results showed that the Cox model could well predict the survival of patients with lung adenocarcinoma (P = 0.03) and lung squamous carcinoma (P < 0.02). In addition, data from the TCGA was used to verify the efficacy of the prediction. There was no significant difference in the accuracy of the prediction of these two types of tumors (P < 0.036), which indicated that the application of automated image features predicted the prognosis of lung patients (60). However, AI application in lung cancer prognosis is likely to advance and will provide novel decision-making for clinicians.

## 6. Conclusion

In summary, AI technology has made progress in early screening, pathological diagnosis, and classification, prognosis assessment, surgical navigation, and immunotherapy of lung cancer. Here, we evaluated the value of AI in lung cancer diagnosis and prognosis, which provide novel decision-making for the precision treatment of lung cancer patients.
