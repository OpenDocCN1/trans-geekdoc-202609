Lung cancer is one of the malignant tumors with the highest incidence and mortality in the world. The overall five-year survival rate of lung cancer is relatively lower than leading cancers. Early diagnosis and prognosis of lung cancer are essential to improve the patient's survival. With artificial intelligence widely applied in lung cancer, diagnosis and prediction have achieved excellent performance in recent years. This review summarizes various types of AI algorithms in lung cancer, including natural language processing (NLP), machine learning and reinforcement learning. In addition, we provide reference regarding the application of AI in lung cancer diagnostic and clinical prognosis. This review aims to evaluate the utility of AI in lung cancer diagnosis and prognosis as the novel screening decision-making for the treatment of lung cancer.

# 1. Introduction

Lung cancer is one of the malignant tumors with the highest incidence and mortality in the world, where the survival rate is less than 20% [1,2]. In early-stage lung cancer patients, postoperative 5-year survival rate with micro-invasive adenocarcinoma and carcinoma was also over 90% [3,4], while the 5-year survival rate of advanced-stage lung cancer patients was only 2% [5,6]. Clinical diagnosis is still a major challenge to suggest treatment to patients who have advanced-stage lung cancer [3,7]. Early diagnosis of lung cancer and suitable treatment can effectively improve the survival rate of patients by 20% [6,8]. However, the outcome of lung cancer is still not optimal for many advanced-stage lung cancer patients in China.

Biomarkers are expected to solve the current challenges in lung cancer diagnosis [11,12]. It remains challenging to deeply interpret information from data with clinical statistics. Recently, with the development of computer technology, AI has become an effective tool in lung cancer diagnosis. Fig 1 shows the historical milestones of AI in lung cancer diagnosis. In recent years, machine learning and deep learning have been used increasingly to improve the diagnosis and treatment of lung cancer [13]. This review summarizes various types of AI applications in lung cancer, including natural language processing, machine learning and reinforcement learning. In addition, we provide reference regarding the application of AI in lung cancer diagnostic and clinical prognosis. The purpose of this study is to illustrate and provide novel screening decision-making for the treatment of lung cancer. Diverse strategies and treatment of lung cancer have been applied in clinical practice. The survival of lung cancer patients is still not optimal [9]. Several studies show that population screening for lung cancer is cost-effective and has the value of early detection [10].

# 2. Types of artificial intelligence applied in lung cancer

The diagnosis of lung cancer has a long history. Pathological findings and analysis of tumor markers have become important tools. However, these approaches have limitations. Firstly, for pathological findings, the current gold for lung cancer diagnosis is mainly performed through invasive procedures and lacks accuracy. Secondly, tumor markers used in clinical applications (e.g., X-ray, computed tomography) have revolutionized the diagnosis of lung cancer, but small lymph nodes or nodal markers are still not readily detectable and may be missed. As discussed here the various types of artificial intelligence applied in lung cancer include NLP, deep learning and machine learning. As shown in Fig 2, we have the schematic of classical AI in lung cancer screening and detection.

## 2.1. Machine learning and deep learning

In recent years, there are big data technologies to contribute to a spurt in the scale of medical data. The wide variety of data has placed greater demands on data processing, storage, and utilization. Advances in these fields have been widely incorporated with clinical applications in lung cancer [4]. All technologies include machine learning, deep learning, and natural language processing, and the relationship between them is shown in Fig 3.

Despite the wide range of applications in lung cancer, AI diagnosis faces many difficulties in achieving the full process from automatic slide viewing to medical report compilation using digital health records. Electronic health records are helping the lung cancer diagnosis process and have important implications for pathologists, both in the first and in communicating with patients. However, information systems are only able to extract and translate this knowledge to manual processing. Manual processing of information is costly, error-prone and has inherent limitations. Information extraction can be improved by deep learning, which is represented by deep layers. The process of intelligent health diagnosis can be improved by deep fusion of NLP. It enables us to understand unstructured text or verbal data for potential applications in complex mining including information extraction and relation extraction, etc., and to assist decision-making in the treatment of cases. Machine learning techniques are currently achieving significant clinical value in the screening, diagnosis, and prognosis of lung cancer. Deep learning, such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), Recursive Neural Network (RNN), Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN), and others, empower clinicians by automating complex realworld models to achieve powerful learning capabilities and functional genomics.
