When training a predictive model, it’s important to evaluate how it will perform in the real-World. When evaluating the performance of our models, we should never evaluate them using the data we used to train them. This is because models will almost always perform better when making predictions on the data used to train them, than when making predictions on unseen data.

In chapter 3, we learned that an important concept to understand when evaluating model performance is the bias-variance trade-off. As the complexity of a model increases, and the more closely it fits the training set, the more variable its predictions will be on unseen data. Models that are too simple and don’t capture the relationships in the data well are biased towards making consistently poor predictions. As we increase the complexity of our model, its variance will increase and its bias will decrease, while the inverse is also true.
