
where **n** is the unit normal vector pointing outwards at the boundary of $S_{i}$. Rearranging the above equation yields

$$
\rho_{i}C_{i}\frac{\partial T_{i}(t)}{\partial t} = \frac{1}{S_{i}} \int_{\partial S_{i}} k_{i}\left(\frac{\partial T(t)}{\partial \mathbf{n}}\right)_{i} dl - w_{b,i}C_{b,i}(T_{i}(t) - T_{a,i}) + Q_{i} + H_{i} \tag{4.15}
$$

which can be approximated by

$$
\begin{aligned} 
\rho_{i}C_{i}\frac{\partial T_{i}(t)}{\partial t} = \frac{1}{S_{i}} \sum_{j \in N_{r}(i)} \frac{(k_{j}T_{j}(t) - k_{i}T_{i}(t))}{l_{ij}}(l_{uv} + l_{vw}) \\
- w_{b,i}C_{b,i}(T_{i}(t) - T_{a,i}) + Q_{i} + H_{i} 
\end{aligned} \tag{4.16}
$$

where $l_{ij} = \lVert \overline{\mathbf{P}_i \mathbf{P}_j} \rVert$, $l_{uv} = \lVert \overline{\mathbf{uv}} \rVert$ and $l_{vw} = \lVert \overline{\mathbf{vw}} \rVert$. Rearranging the above equation leads to

$$
\begin{aligned} 
\rho_{i}C_{i}\frac{\partial T_{i}(t)}{\partial t} = -\left( \frac{1}{S_{i}} \sum_{j \in N_{r}(i)} \frac{k_{i}(l_{uv} + l_{vw})}{l_{ij}} + w_{b,i}C_{b,i} \right) T_{i}(t) \\
+ \frac{1}{S_{i}} \sum_{j \in N_{r}(i)} \frac{k_{j}(l_{uv} + l_{vw})}{l_{ij}} T_{j}(t) + w_{b,i}C_{b,i}T_{a,i} + Q_{i} + H_{i} 
\end{aligned} \tag{4.17}
$$

It can be seen that Eqs. (4.12) and (4.17) are remarkably similar; therefore, the discrete form of the Pennes bio-heat transfer equation (Eq. 4.17) is embedded into the autonomous CNN (Eq. 4.12) by associating the following parameters:

$$
C_{x} = \rho_{i}C_{i}; \ v_{xi}(t) = T_{i}(t); \ v_{yj}(t) = T_{j}(t); \ I_{i} = w_{b,i}C_{b,i}T_{a,i} + Q_{i} + H_{i}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned} 
A(i;i) = -\left( \frac{1}{S_{i}} \sum_{j \in N_{r}(i)} \frac{k_{i}(l_{uv} + l_{vw})}{l_{ij}} + w_{b,i}C_{b,i} \right) + \frac{1}{R_{x}} \\
A(i;j) = \frac{1}{S_{i}} \frac{k_{j}(l_{uv} + l_{vw})}{l_{ij}} 
\end{aligned} \tag{4.18}
$$

## 4.3.3 CNN Formulation of Bio-Heat Transfer on 3D Grids

### 4.3.3.1 3D Regular Grids

The CNN formulation on 3-D regular grids can be expressed using a generalisation of 2D CNN to higher dimension cases, by a 3D rectangular array of cells. Define a grid point $i,j,k$ associated with the cell $C(i,j,k)$, and $\Delta w_{k-1}=||\mathbf{P}_{i,j,k-1}\mathbf{P}_{i,j,k}||$ and $\Delta w_{k}=||\mathbf{P}_{i,j,k}\mathbf{P}_{i,j,k+1}||$ where $\mathbf{P}_{i,j,k}$ is the position of the grid point $i,j,k$. In analogy to the 2D regular grid case using FDM, the discrete form of the Pennes bio-heat transfer equation on a 3D regular grid can be expressed as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{i,j,k}C_{i,j,k}\frac{\partial T_{i,j,k}(t)}{\partial t} &= -\left(\frac{2k_{i,j,k}}{\Delta u_{i-1}\Delta u_{i}}+\frac{2k_{i,j,k}}{\Delta v_{j-1}\Delta v_{j}}+\frac{2k_{i,j,k}}{\Delta w_{k-1}\Delta w_{k}}+w_{b,i,j,k}C_{b,i,j,k}\right)T_{i,j,k}(t) \\
&+\frac{2k_{i+1,j,k}T_{i+1,j,k}(t)}{\Delta u_{i}(\Delta u_{i-1}+\Delta u_{i})}+\frac{2k_{i-1,j,k}T_{i-1,j,k}(t)}{\Delta u_{i-1}(\Delta u_{i-1}+\Delta u_{i})} \\
&+\frac{2k_{i,j+1,k}T_{i,j+1,k}(t)}{\Delta v_{j}(\Delta v_{j-1}+\Delta v_{j})}+\frac{2k_{i,j-1,k}T_{i,j-1,k}(t)}{\Delta v_{j-1}(\Delta v_{j-1}+\Delta v_{j})} \\
&+\frac{2k_{i,j,k+1}T_{i,j,k+1}(t)}{\Delta w_{k}(\Delta w_{k-1}+\Delta w_{k})}+\frac{2k_{i,j,k-1}T_{i,j,k-1}(t)}{\Delta w_{k-1}(\Delta w_{k-1}+\Delta w_{k})} \\
&+w_{b,i,j,k}C_{b,i,j,k}T_{a,i,j,k}+Q_{i,j,k}+H_{i,j,k}
\end{aligned} \tag{4.19}
$$

The parameters of the autonomous CNN are associated with the parameters of the discrete Pennes bio-heat transfer equation, leading to:

$$
C_{x}=\rho_{i,j,k}C_{i,j,k}; \nu_{x}i_{jk}=T_{i,j,k}(t); \quad I_{jk}=w_{b,i,j,k}C_{b,i,j,k}T_{a,i,j,k}+Q_{i,j,k}+H_{i,j,k}
$$

$$
A(i,j,k;i,j,k)=-\left(\frac{2k_{i,j,k}}{\Delta u_{i-1}\Delta u_{i}}+\frac{2k_{i,j,k}}{\Delta v_{j-1}\Delta v_{j}}+\frac{2k_{i,j,k}}{\Delta w_{k-1}\Delta w_{k}}+w_{b,i,j,k}C_{b,i,j,k}\right)+\frac{1}{R_{x}}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(i,j,k;i+1,j,k)&=\frac{2k_{i+1,j,k}}{\Delta u_{i}(\Delta u_{i-1}+\Delta u_{i})} &; \quad A(i,j,k;i-1,j,k)&=\frac{2k_{i-1,j,k}}{\Delta u_{i-1}(\Delta u_{i-1}+\Delta u_{i})} \\
A(i,j,k;i,j+1,k)&=\frac{2k_{i,j+1,k}}{\Delta v_{j}(\Delta v_{j-1}+\Delta v_{j})} &; \quad A(i,j,k;i,j-1,k)&=\frac{2k_{i,j-1,k}}{\Delta v_{j-1}(\Delta v_{j-1}+\Delta v_{j})} \\
A(i,j,k;i,j,k+1)&=\frac{2k_{i,j,k+1}}{\Delta w_{k}(\Delta w_{k-1}+\Delta w_{k})} &; \quad A(i,j,k;i,j,k-1)&=\frac{2k_{i,j,k-1}}{\Delta w_{k-1}(\Delta w_{k-1}+\Delta w_{k})}
\end{aligned} \tag{4.20}
$$

### 4.3.3.2 2D Irregular Grids

On 3D irregular grids, the CNN formulation of bio-heat transfer is achieved with an extension of the control areas in 2D to control volumes in 3-D [56]. In analogy to a control area in 2D triangular grids, a control volume in 3D tetrahedral grids is established by a dual mesh joining at the edge-midpoints, face-centroids and centroids of tetrahedrons adjacent to a computation point. Similar to the 2D irregular case, for the point $i$ enclosed by the control volume $\Omega_i$, the discrete form of the Pennes bio-heat transfer equation can be expressed as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_iC_i\frac{\partial T_i(t)}{\partial t} &= -\left(\frac{1}{\Omega_i}\sum_{j \in N_i(i)}\frac{k_i\sum^n S_{j,n}}{l_{ij}} + w_{b,i}C_{b,i}\right)T_i(t) \\
&+\frac{1}{\Omega_i}\sum_{j \in N_i(i)}\frac{k_j\sum^n S_{j,n}}{l_{ij}}T_j(t) + w_{b,i}C_{b,i}T_{a,i} + Q_i + H_i
\end{aligned} \tag{4.21}
$$

where $\sum^n S_{j,n}$ denotes the sum of boundary surfaces [57] that are attached to the edge connecting $\mathbf{p}_i$ and $\mathbf{p}_j$.

The parameters of the autonomous CNN are associated with the parameters of the discrete Pennes bio-heat transfer equation, resulting in:

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_x &= \rho_iC_i; \nu_{xi} = T_i(t); \nu_{yj}(t) = T_j(t); I_i = w_{b,i}C_{b,i}T_{a,i} + Q_i + H_i \\
A(i;i) &= -\left(\frac{1}{\Omega_i}\sum_{j \in N_i(i)}\frac{k_i\sum^n S_{j,n}}{l_{ij}} + w_{b,i}C_{b,i}\right) + \frac{1}{R_x} \\
A(i;j) &= \frac{1}{\Omega_i}\frac{k_j\sum^n S_{j,n}}{l_{ij}}
\end{aligned} \tag{4.22}
$$

## 4.3.4 Initial and Boundary Conditions

The initial and boundary conditions must be specified in order to fully describe the CNN models. The initial condition is achieved by assigning the initial state of the cell with the initial temperature of the tissue, i.e.,

$$
v_{x}(0)=T(0)
$$

The boundary condition of the Dirichlet type is enforced by assigning the fixed-state cells with a prescribed constant temperature, i.e.,

$$
v_{x, b}(t)=T
$$

where $v_{x, b}(t)$ represents the state of the cell at the specified domain boundary at time t.

The time evolution of CNN directly provides the soft tissue temperature field governed by the Pennes bio-heat transfer equation, which is subsequently used for thermal dose computation.

## 4.4 NUMERICAL EXAMPLES

### 4.4.1 Patient-Specific Thermal Dose Computation

A set of patient-specific virtual digital models of heterogenous tissues is employed to analyse thermal doses in a personalised thermal ablative treatment of hepatic tumours. As illustrated in Figure 4.4, the patient-specific anatomical models were constructed from a computed tomography (CT) medical image dataset of an anonymised patient (the dataset is available at https://www.irCAD.fr/research/3dicadb/), which was segmented and reconstructed into the liver and the bone. The thermal dose $CEM_{d_3}$ was computed from the tissue heat exposure history (Eq. 4.1) where the tissue temperature was calculated by the bio-heat transfer-embedded CNN whose performance was previously evaluated in Ref. [41]. The heterogeneity of tissues was modelled by setting parameters of cells to different values based on the tissue/tumour material properties at different regions.

As illustrated in Figure 4.5, a volumetric heat source of a Gaussian distribution type was used as the externally applied heat source. The simulated heat source was employed to demonstrate the applicability of the CNN methodology in estimating tissue temperature and thermal doses, and they can be substituted by different heat sources of various sizes and shapes such as those induced by the different thermal ablative modalities.
