Pennnes bio-heat transfer equation [46], which accounts for the effects of heating and cooling of heat conduction, blood perfusion, metabolic heat generation, and external heat sources. It was validated against experiments [47,48] that the Pennes bio-heat model can offer reliable tissue temperature predictions, and it was widely used to model tumour management by hyperthermia and thermal ablative treatments [49–52]. For soft tissues of mass density \(\rho\), specific heat capacity \(C\), thermal conductivity \(k\), blood perfusion rate \(w_b\), blood specific heat capacity \(C_b\), arterial blood temperature \(T_a\), metabolic heat generation rate \(Q\), and subject to an external heat source \(H\), the Pennes bio-heat transfer equation is given by [46]

```
$$\rho C \frac{\partial T(t)}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot (k \nabla T(t)) - w_b C_b (T(t) - T_a) + Q + H \tag{4.3}$$
```

where $\nabla \cdot$ represents the divergence operator, and $\nabla$ represents the gradient operator.
