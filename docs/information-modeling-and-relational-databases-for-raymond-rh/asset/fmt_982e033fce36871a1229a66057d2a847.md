
# Advanced Modeling Topics

This chapter considers several advanced modeling topics and assumes familiarity with the basics of object-role modeling (`ORM`), Barker entity-relationship (`ER`) modeling, and unified modeling language (`UML`) class diagrams. While the main focus is on `ORM`, some attention is also given to the other approaches. Even where `ER` and `UML` fail to provide built-in support for the features discussed, many of the ideas could be applied, at least as extensions, to these approaches.

## Join Constraints

This section examines *join constraints*, first considering join constraints over a single join path, then set-comparison constraints involving joins. Although consideration of these constraints is really part of *Conceptual Schema Design Procedure (CSDP)* step 7, we postponed a detailed discussion of them until now because of their advanced nature.

The notion of conceptual joins was introduced in [Section 4.4](#Ch004_111-158_B9780443237904000232.xhtml#s0035) and further illustrated in [Section 9.7](#Ch009_347-399_B9780443237904000013.xhtml#s0040). With a *conceptual inner join* over two or more roles, the same object plays all the roles. In [Fig. 10.1](#Ch010_401-479_B9780443237904000244.xhtml#f0010)(a), there is a single join path Building contains Room **that** has RoomNr. Linguistically, the pronoun "**that**" in this path expression declares the join, indicating it is the same room playing both the join roles (shaded here for discussion purposes).

![Fig. 10.1 A simple conceptual join in (a) ORM, (b) Barker ER, and (c) UML.](images/B9780443237904000244/f10-01-9780443237904.jpg)

[Fig. 10.1](#Ch010_401-479_B9780443237904000244.xhtml#B-f0010) A simple conceptual join in (a) ORM, (b) Barker ER, and (c) UML.

The external uniqueness constraint declares that each building, roomNr combination applies to at most one room. This is equivalent to an internal uniqueness constraint on building, roomNr in the set of (building, room, roomNr) tuples instantiating the conceptual join path.

[Fig. 10.1](#Ch010_401-479_B9780443237904000244.xhtml#f0010)(b) shows the same schema in Barker ER notation. The external uniqueness constraint is captured by combining the "`#`" and "`|`". [Fig. 10.1](#Ch010_401-479_B9780443237904000244.xhtml#f0010)(c) shows the UML version, with the external uniqueness constraint captured by combining the two `{id}` markers.

In the room example, the two roles participating in the conceptual join are both mandatory for the join object type (Room). This ensures that the conceptual join is an inner join. However, if one or more roles involved in the conceptual join is optional, the use of an external uniqueness constraint needs special care. For example, consider the three ORM schemas shown in [Fig. 10.2](#Ch010_401-479_B9780443237904000244.xhtml#f0015).

![Fig. 10.2 Optional roles may involve an inner join (a) or an outer join (b), (c).](images/B9780443237904000244/f10-02-9780443237904.jpg)

[Fig. 10.2](#Ch010_401-479_B9780443237904000244.xhtml#B-f0015) Optional roles may involve an inner join (a) or an outer join (b), (c).

The ORM schema in [Fig. 10.2](#Ch010_401-479_B9780443237904000244.xhtml#f0015)(a) is a fragment of an ORM metaschema. `ORM` requires each predicate to have at least one reading, but it is optional whether roles are given names. In the figure, the role played by `RoleName` is named "name", but the other roles are not named. The external uniqueness constraint indicates that role names are unique within a given predicate. A predicate may have many unnamed roles, but if a role is named it can be identified by the combination of its name and its predicate.

This situation requires an *inner join interpretation* for the join path underlying the external uniqueness constraint. Let us identify the predicate of the association `Role is in Predicate` as `P1` and its left and right roles as `r1` and `r2`, respectively. Let us also identify the predicate of the association `Role has RoleName` as `P2` and its left and right roles as `r3` and `r4`, respectively. The inner join of the two fact types results in a compound ternary fact type equivalent to the following relational table scheme.

```
Role( roleId, predicateId, [roleName] )       r4     P2      name
```

This relational notation underlines uniqueness constraints; if more than one exists, the primary key is doubly underlined. Optional columns are marked with square brackets. The external uniqueness constraint appears as a compound internal uniqueness constraint. Because we only require roles to have unique names in a predicate if they are named, the conceptual join between the roles of `Role` in [Fig. 10.2](#Ch010_401-479_B9780443237904000244.xhtml#f0015)(a) is an inner join, resulting in the single row population shown.

Using "`?`" to depict a null value, the outer join result would include four tuples: (`r1`, `P1`, `?`), (`r2`, `P1`, `?`), (`r3`, `P2`, `?`), and (`r4`, `P2`, `name`). The first two tuples would then violate the compound uniqueness constraint, so outer join semantics should not be used with this example.

The external uniqueness constraints in [Fig. 10.2](#Ch010_401-479_B9780443237904000244.xhtml#f0015)(b) and (c) are depicted with an *inner "o" through the uniqueness bar*. This indicates that the external uniqueness constraint has *outer join semantics,* with the added proviso that *nulls produced in the outer join are treated as actual values*.

Hence, each course may be referenced by exactly one of the following patterns: the combination of the course title and its department (where the course is offered by a department); or its course title (where the course is offered by no department, e.g., a course offered by a visiting lecturer).

External uniqueness constraints of this nature are verbalized in two sentences, one for the inner join semantics and one for the additional semantics of the outer join. The external uniqueness constraint in [Fig. 10.2](#Ch010_401-479_B9780443237904000244.xhtml#f0015)(b) verbalizes thus:

```
For each CourseTitle and Department at most one Course has that CourseTitle and is offered by that Department.
For each CourseTitle at most one Course has that CourseTitle and is offered by no Department.
```

Consider a sample population with three courses, `C1`, `C2`, and `C3`, with the same title, one offered by the physics department, one by the mathematics department, and one offered by no department. The join of the title and department fact types results in a compound ternary fact type equivalent to the following relational table scheme. The external uniqueness constraint appears as the internal uniqueness constraint spanning courseTitle and departmentCode.

*   ```
    Course( courseCode, courseTitle, [departmentCode] )
    ```

*   ```
                              C1                            Mechanics            PY
    ```

*   ```
                              C2                            Mechanics            MA
    ```

*   ```
                              C3                            Mechanics            ?
    ```

*   ```
    C4 Mechanics            ?                        - - violates constraint
    ```

The fourth row (`C4`, `Mechanics`, `?`) is rejected because in the presence of row 3, it violates the constraint that courses with no department must have different titles. To determine constraint satisfaction, the nulls on rows 3 and 4 are treated like ordinary values, and thus are equated. This effectively applies *outer join semantics*.

Outer join semantics may apply even if the disjunctive reference scheme is the only means of identifying courses, as in [Fig. 10.2](#Ch010_401-479_B9780443237904000244.xhtml#f0015)(c).

External constraints with inner join semantics may be used for the preferred reference scheme of an entity type only if the coroles hosted by that entity type in the constrained relationships are mandatory, as in [Fig. 10.2](#Ch010_401-479_B9780443237904000244.xhtml#f0015)(a).
