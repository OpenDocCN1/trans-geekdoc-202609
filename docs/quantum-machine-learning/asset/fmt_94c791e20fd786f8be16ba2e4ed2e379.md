## 7.1 Quantum Complexity

Quantum complexity is a fundamental concept to identify the complexity of any problem in correspondence to quantum computing [[1](#ch7.xhtml#ref7_1)]. Several parametric resources, such as time, space, and operations, are considered to evaluate the complexity of the problem. The quantum mechanism allows machines to run in multiple states simultaneously. This simultaneous process requires quantum superimposition on the target machine for effective execution [[2](#ch7.xhtml#ref7_2)]. The significant difference in evaluating the complexity of classical and quantum computing is identifying the parallelism and relationship between the execution threads.

The quantum superposition property allows complex calculations to be more efficient, and several quantum algorithms have been implemented to perform such calculations [[3](#ch7.xhtml#ref7_3)]. Quantum algorithms help to solve complex problems in a fast-paced manner compared to classical algorithms. Entanglement is another exciting concept used in quantum computing that unveils correlations in integral parts [[4](#ch7.xhtml#ref7_4)]. Quantum complexity for any complex problems such as `P` and `NP` will be effectively calculated by bounded error quantum polynomial time (`BQP`). The `BQP` calculates bounded error probability in correspondence to quantum operations [[5](#ch7.xhtml#ref7_5)]. Quantum complexity allows developers to understand the pros and cons of quantum computation.
