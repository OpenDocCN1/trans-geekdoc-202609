### 4.4.3 Scalability and network consensus

As the variety of IoT tools and devices rises, scalability concerns and the choice of reliable consensus systems become the main issues. Ineffective consensus algorithms and scalability bottlenecks can endanger smart city infrastructure's safety and real-time responsiveness. Dealing with these obstacles calls for cutting-edge solutions that boost the scalability and performance of Blockchain networks [40]. By continuously developing the technology underpinning these networks, we can better meet the increasing needs of a growing smart city environment.

### 4.4.4 Network security and consensus vulnerabilities

The dependence on consensus algorithms to verify transactions within Blockchain networks presents vulnerabilities, specifically in large IoT implementations. Malicious actors making use of these vulnerabilities can compromise the integrity of transactions and stored data. Advanced consensus mechanisms and routine security audits are crucial to strengthen network security to neutralize this threat [41]. Proactive measures are important to ensure the robustness of the consensus mechanisms, guarding the smart city infrastructure against potential attacks on the Blockchain network.

### 4.4.5 Supply chain security for IoT devices

Ensuring the security of the whole supply chain for IoT devices in smart cities is a complex challenge. Tampering with or compromising IoT devices at any phase of the supply chain can introduce vulnerabilities into the Blockchain network, weakening the overall security of smart city systems. Implementing rigorous supply chain security measures and device attestation procedures is vital to mitigate this risk comprehensively [41]. A proactive and vigilant approach throughout all supply chain phases is important to preserving the integrity of the connected devices within the smart city landscape.

### 4.4.6 Identity management

Incorporating IoT with Blockchain requires robust identity management systems, raising concerns about potentially exposing personally identifiable information. Poor identity protection measures might lead to the compromise of an individual's privacy. As a result, implementing privacy-centric identification services, such as zero-knowledge proofs, is essential to mitigate these risks effectively [42]. By incorporating advanced privacy technologies, smart cities can strike a delicate balance between the benefits of identity management and the preservation of individual privacy.

### 4.4.7 Data linkability

The transparent nature of Blockchain presents the threat of data linkability, allowing entities to link and analyze transactional data. Persistent data linkability threatens resident privacy and confidentiality within the smart city landscape [43]. Continuous advancements in cryptographic methods and privacy-enhancing technologies are vital to counter this threat. These developments are crucial in combating the risks associated with data linkability, ensuring that smart cities can harness the benefits of Blockchain while guarding residents' privacy. Blockchain and IoT are technologies in the evolving landscape of smart cities.

## 4.5 CHALLENGES, LIMITATIONS, AND IMPLICATIONS

Within this section, the challenges and limitations of using Blockchain in smart cities are presented, as well as some implications.

### 4.5.1 Challenges

### 4.5.1.1 Scalability

On the Blockchain front, the problem hinges on effectively managing the large number of transactions happening within a smart city's dynamic and interconnected environment. As the city's infrastructure becomes increasingly digitized, the strain on the Blockchain network can impede transaction processing speed and overall system performance [44]. At the same time, the proliferation of IoT devices exacerbates scalability concerns. The growing number of these interconnected devices, ranging from sensors to smart appliances, increases the complexity of managing their interactions seamlessly. They facilitate data exchange and communication among a substantial array of real-time devices, requiring robust scalability solutions to avoid bottlenecks and ensure the smooth operation of smart city systems.

### 4.5.1.2 Interoperability

This is a critical challenge in integrating Blockchain and IoT within smart cities that focus on the seamless interaction between diverse IoT devices and Blockchain systems. The complex web of interconnected devices with distinct protocols and standards poses a considerable obstacle. IoT devices generate substantial amounts of data that must be efficiently and securely connected to the Blockchain for processing. Achieving a standard communication protocol that can accommodate the diversity of IoT devices is important [45]. Without a common language, the potential benefits of the combined technologies may be compromised, preventing the smart city's ability to harness the full potential of Blockchain and IoT synergies.

### 4.5.1.3 Security concerns

Security is a vital concern when integrating Blockchain and IoT in smart cities. In the realm of Blockchain, the technology inherently provides robust security with its decentralized and cryptographic principles. Nevertheless, vulnerabilities can arise in smart contracts, the self-executing code on Blockchain, or within the network's consensus algorithms [46]. However, IoT devices present another layer of security challenges. Device vulnerabilities, if not properly addressed, can be exploited by malicious actors, compromising the entire system's integrity. In addition, data security issues in the communication between IoT devices and the Blockchain network may expose sensitive information. Secure communication protocols and robust authentication mechanisms will be vital to mitigating these risks.

### 4.5.1.4 Energy consumption

The energy consumption associated with Blockchain's Proof-of-Work (PoW) consensus algorithms is a significant concern, especially when considering their potential application in smart cities. PoW involves complex mathematical problems miners must solve to validate transactions and create new blocks. This process requires significant computational power, resulting in high energy consumption. In the context of smart cities, where sustainability is a key focus, the environmental impact of PoW becomes a notable limitation [47]. The energy-intensive nature of PoW can contribute to increased carbon footprint and threaten the goal of creating efficient and green urban environments. Addressing this challenge requires exploring alternative consensus mechanisms known for their lower energy requirements.

### 4.5.2 Limitations

### 4.5.2.1 Cost

The cost implications of implementing and maintaining a Blockchain infrastructure present a significant challenge for smart cities. The expenditures incurred in establishing the required hardware, software, and network infrastructure, combined with ongoing operational and maintenance costs, can strain the budgets of municipal authorities. Smart city projects commonly require significant financial investments, and adopting Blockchain technology presents an additional financial commitment [48]. Costs relate to the initial deployment and include expenses for ensuring security, scalability, and compliance over time. Addressing these economic factors is vital for effectively integrating Blockchain in smart city initiatives, requiring careful budgeting and strategic planning to balance technological advancement with financial responsibility.

### 4.5.2.2 Regulatory uncertainty

This poses a substantial challenge to the widespread implementation of Blockchain and IoT in smart cities. The lack of clear and standardized regulations creates an atmosphere of uncertainty, inhibiting public and private sectors from fully adopting these transformative technologies [49]. The complex nature of Blockchain and IoT applications demands comprehensive legal frameworks to address data privacy, security, and interoperability issues. Without clear regulations, stakeholders may hesitate to invest in or adopt these advancements, fearing legal issues. As a result, it is important for policymakers to collaboratively develop and establish clear regulatory guidelines that foster innovation while addressing the unique challenges posed by the integration of Blockchain and IoT in the context of smart cities [50].

### 4.5.2.3 Adoption barriers

The adoption of Blockchain and IoT technologies in smart cities faces significant barriers, mainly rooted in resistance to change and the need for a comprehensive overhaul of existing systems. Cities and urban infrastructures often operate on established structures, and introducing disruptive technologies like Blockchain and IoT requires a fundamental shift in mindset and infrastructure [51]. Stakeholders may be reluctant to embrace these changes due to concerns about the complexity of the integration, potential disruptions during the transition, and the costs involved. Overcoming these adoption barriers requires strategic planning, clear communication of the benefits, and gradual implementation strategies that mitigate the perceived risks associated with such transformative technologies [52].
