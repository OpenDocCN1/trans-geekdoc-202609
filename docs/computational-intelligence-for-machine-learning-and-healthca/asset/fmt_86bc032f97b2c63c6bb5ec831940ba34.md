
## 6.1 Introduction

Psychological disorder refers to all of the diagnosable mental disorder patterns in the social & occupational lives; these are psychological systems that have an impact on multiple areas of life. Incidence of mental disorders is rising at a high rate worldwide. According to the World Health Organization, one out of four people will be affected at some point in their lives by psychological or neurological disorders, thus becoming a second major source of global disease till 2020 ([Bruce and Edward, 1984](#bodymatter-006.xhtml#b_9783110648195-006_ref_006)). Psychological disorders are becoming most prominent in teenagers and all teens experience some degree of psychological disorders. Half of all the psychological conditions start by the age of 14, yet most cases go undetected and untreated. According to the study reported in WHO, approximately 6.5% of the Indian population is suffering from a serious form of mental disorder with numbers increasing at a rapid pace. Stress is the common mental illness in Europe, affecting about 16% of the total population ([Kessler et al., 2016](#bodymatter-006.xhtml#b_9783110648195-006_ref_049)). Psychological disorders are the most ignored and undertreated form of disorders, which lead to immense healthcare and medical costs. Psychological disorders cost around more than $42 billion in the U.S. per annum, while in Europe the cost amounts to 74.4 billion Euros per annum. Psychological disorder is a term often used interchangeably with mental disorders, mental illness, and psychiatric disorders. Mental disorder is a condition that in the unconscious and emotional state of a person is usually characterized by severe disruption. Throughout our social & professional lives, they are generally associated with anxiety and other essential tasks. Mental disorders are the most ignored forms of disorders due to the fear of social stigma that prevents people from getting a proper diagnosis and treatment plan. Mental illness encompasses a wide variety of conditions and is influenced by several factors such as genetics, biology, daily habits, and environment. Every year, one in 25 people experiences severe mental illness, according to the National Institute for Mental Health. ([Linthicum et al., 2019](#bodymatter-006.xhtml#b_9783110648195-006_ref_062)).

There may be many forms of mental illness, many types may be classified as psychotic disorders, anxiety disorders, heart control & alcohol disorders, mood disorders, post-traumatic disorders, etc.

A paradigm shift in clinical methods and analytical techniques has been generated by the rapid advancement in technology and functional approaches used in medicine, combined with significant advances in computing power, storage, and data collection. Through the use of technology, clinicians can diagnose and treat mental health problems at a remediable stage, thereby improving the overall wellbeing of the patient. The quick and shrewd assessment of restorative learning put away in immense databases worldwide would now be accessible to experts to empower them to precisely analyze and treat sickness. This helps eliminate confusion, saves lives, and decreases treatment costs. The growth in the use of ML & Artificial Intelligence (AI) is being welcomed by medical physicians and is providing significant advantages in diagnostics. Computer-based intelligence is discovering more uses in prescription, from the conclusion of psychological wellness to settling on significant clinical choices. Complex psychiatric conditions like schizophrenia and depression are being detected by the professionals using AI, just by talking to the patients. Common mental health issues such as depression, OCD, and anxiety can be identified by voice recognition software and complex data computer analysis. AI is becoming a great tool for psychologists and psychiatrists for accurate diagnosis of specific psychiatric disorders ([Luo et al., 2016](#bodymatter-006.xhtml#b_9783110648195-006_ref_065)).

## 6.2 Techniques for machine learning

ML is a subfield of AI that was proposed by Allan Turing and authored by Arthur Samuel in the 1950s ([Turing, 2009](#bodymatter-006.xhtml#b_9783110648195-006_ref_097), pp. 23–65) and has a solid connection with the fields of computer science and measurements ([Linthicum et al., 2019](#bodymatter-006.xhtml#b_9783110648195-006_ref_062)). Broadly defined, ML is a computational strategy that learns parameters automatically and methods to find the best solution to the problem with no human intervention for programming. Additionally, ML calculations are ready to develop and change with a new information implementation. Such recognition patterns hold different potential preferences over physically defined models, including effectiveness and unpredictability ([El and Murphy, 2015](#bodymatter-006.xhtml#b_9783110648195-006_ref_028)). The year 1990 can be marked as the year when ([Provost and Kohavi, 1998](#bodymatter-006.xhtml#b_9783110648195-006_ref_078)) machine learning techniques came into medical use.

ML has certain advantages over the traditional approach that helps in accurate prediction for diseases. Machine learning techniques help to maximize clinical importance and generalizability. Algorithms used in ML accommodate countless variables and at the same time think about exceptionally complex blends among those components. High-dimensional datasets incorporating countless algorithms as potential indicators can be processed through machine learning techniques. ML denotes algorithms that learn from data sets. The inputs of ML algorithms are training sets that comprise both labeled and unlabeled data.
