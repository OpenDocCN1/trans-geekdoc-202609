### 5.5.2 Deep learning in robotic surgery

Due to faster rate of recovery and less pain, laparoscopy is used for minimally invasive surgery in various clinical surgeries. There is very narrow operating space and field of view that is insufficient to tactile feedback that can damage surgical instruments, organs, and tissues. So, during laparoscopic surgery for detection of surgical instruments, a real-time model on the basis of convolutional neural network (CNN) was used [Choi et al. (2017)](#bodymatter-005.xhtml#b_9783110648195-005_ref_013). For testing purpose, they have used a dataset of seven surgical tools for learning CNN, then a YOLO unified architecture is used to track surgical instruments in real time. To evaluate a proposed model, a precision and a degree of recall are used for performance that shows an average of 72.26% of accuracy over dataset. It has been predicted that semantic segmentation was a very big problem for robotic instruments surgery in which correct detection of position of instrument was the main challenge for pose tracking and estimation in the vicinity of surgical scenes. In order to address these challenges, there is a need of accurate pixel-wise instrument segmentation; so, for robotic instrument segmentation, [Shvets et al. (2018)](#bodymatter-005.xhtml#b_9783110648195-005_ref_041) have proposed a deep learning-based approach that addresses the problem of binary segmentation. Through a feed from surgery video every pixel of images was labeled as instrument or background and also addresses multiclass segmentation problem, in which they had differentiated between different parts and different instruments from the background. The proposed method is tested and compared with other methods for automatic instrument segmentation that gives improvement.

### 5.5.3 Deep learning in drug discovery

By an increase in drug candidates, there is an increase in social burden of drug discovery. In drug response vitro models, a traditional preclinical drug scene was not able to mimic the complex properties of human biology. Due to this there is need to be contributed. The Gregor Urban et al., has proposed a new micro-physiological system for growing perfused micro-tissues that seems to be suitable for large drug screens and also able to models human physiology accurately. Then in 2018, Gregor U., et al., (2018) have developed a ML model that is able to accurately and quickly predict flag commands that were able to disrupt vascular networks effectively from drug application before and after images in vitro. The proposed system is based on CNN that is able to achieve high or 98.1% of accuracy without putting any false negative effects on system.

In traditional drug discovery, there is problem of cost and time so there is a need to propose a new method that will increase its efficiency. To solve this problem, use of virtual screening (VS) and its database has been a famous method for pharmaceutical research. VS plays a very important role in early stages of drug development and discovery as it helps in reducing the enormous search space of chemical compounds. For effective distinguishing, active and inactive legends are used due to an increase in the count of ligands by time. For big database molecules a screening was done using deep learning algorithms that further classify druglike and nondruglike ligands against a particular protein target. This will result in an increase of VS process speed. [Bahi et al. (2018)](#bodymatter-005.xhtml#b_9783110648195-005_ref_008) have proposed a deep NN-based fast compound classification method for VS that is known as DNNVS. Then from a huge database, a Spark-H2O platform was used to label small molecules. The proposed approach that is evaluated shows that the accuracy achieved using DNNVS is 99% and performed well when compared with ML techniques.

### 5.5.4 Deep learning in disease diagnosis

Breast cancer has become a leading cause of death among women; and for diagnosis and screening of breast cancer, a mammogram is used as a standard. To standardize mammographic reporting, a breast imaging reporting and data system (BI-RADS) lexicon has been developed by the American College of Radiology that enables the decision-making facility of biopsy and assesses cancer risk. Due to the use of inappropriate terms and missing data, unstructured and semistructured free text were made for current biopsy decision making due to variation in their accuracy. Due to BI-RADS lexicon application and occurrence of substantial interobserver variability, a variation takes place that makes critical to incorporate an accurate and novel technique for breast cancer decision-making data. Then to develop an analytic model, [He et al. (2017)](#bodymatter-005.xhtml#b_9783110648195-005_ref_021) have combined deep learning methods and NLP that target well-defined and characterized specific breast-suspicious patient subgroups. This model is able to give better results when compared with SVM, Gaussian mixture model, and PCA algorithm. [Tourassi (2017)](#bodymatter-005.xhtml#b_9783110648195-005_ref_043) has discussed both theoretical and practical perspectives of the latest deep learning technology that is relevant to clinical pathology NLP reports.
