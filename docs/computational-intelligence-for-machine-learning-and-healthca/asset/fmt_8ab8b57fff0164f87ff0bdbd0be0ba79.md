
Till 2012, an experienced physician judgment was the only way to diagnose coronary heart disease. At that time, current topic was to use computer-intelligent algorithms for coronary heart disease diagnosis in ML. Then coronary heart disease detection was done by [Zhang et al. (2012)](#bodymatter-005.xhtml#b_9783110648195-005_ref_046), using statistical learning theory-based `SVM`. In their work, the test data is classified using classifiers with different kernels along with feature extraction and data preprocessing of original data. They have concluded that radial basis function (`RBF`) accuracy is highest than all other classifiers. For `RBF`, an optimization parameter grid search method was used for selecting a best parameter of kernel function and penalty factor. Various researchers have achieved good accuracy in diagnosis of heart disease using artificial neural network (`ANN`) technique. But still there are some drawbacks of `ANN` technique such as tuning of number of parameters, activation function must be differentiable, long training process, and obtained solution gets stuck in local minima. Due to all these disadvantages, [Fathurachman et al. (2014)](#bodymatter-005.xhtml#b_9783110648195-005_ref_015) have used an extreme learning machine (`ELM`). The `ELM` is considered as better generalization model learning algorithm that is faster than `DT`, `SVM`, and `ANN` and also has simple tuning. The results obtained using `ELM` model shows that it helps in diagnosing heart disease with an accuracy of 83%. Due to involvement of large amount of data, large noise interference, and high data dimension, diagnosis of heart disease is a difficult task that creates a need of using data-driven approaches. So, for diagnosis of heart diseases, [Yang et al. (2018)](#bodymatter-005.xhtml#b_9783110648195-005_ref_045) have presented an `SVM`-based two-dimension–reduction methodologies. For the purpose of diagnosis, more relevant features are achieved using `SVM`-recursive feature elimination method than for the principle component analysis `SVM`. The proposed approaches are evaluated, which shows that `PCA-SVM` is able to achieve 88.24% of classification accuracy via `RBF` kernel using only size principal components.

### 5.4.2 Using ML to predict diabetes

As mentioned in the last section, there is rapid advancement in technology and an increase in data that makes a healthcare domain as very important field for researchers. Big data analytics approach has started using in dealing with this large amount of healthcare data. [Mir et al. (2018)](#bodymatter-005.xhtml#b_9783110648195-005_ref_032) motive is to propose a `WEKA` tool using a classifier model that is able to predict the diabetes disease. For analysis purpose, they have used random forest, `SVM`, simple `CART`, and `NB` algorithm. On the basis of efficient results achieved by evaluating each algorithm on the dataset in prediction of diabetes diseases, a best algorithm is find out by them. From performance, it has been observed that in prediction of diabetic disease `SVM` gives maximum accuracy. As like other diseases, early prediction of diabetes can save human life. Due to this various diabetes-related risk factors are explored by [Md. Faruque et al. (2019)](#bodymatter-005.xhtml#b_9783110648195-005_ref_031), using ML techniques as it was able to give efficient results in terms of knowledge extraction. Prediction models are constructed for extracting knowledge from collected diagnostic medical datasets of diabetic patients. This helps in predicting diabetic patients. In their work to predict diabetic mellitus, they have mainly used adult population data such as `k`-nearest neighbor (`k-NN`), `NB`, `C4.5 DT`, and `SVM` ML algorithms. On all these four algorithms, an experiment was conducted that shows `C4.5 DT` with highest accuracy.

Then again in 2019, [Alic et al. (2019)](#bodymatter-005.xhtml#b_9783110648195-005_ref_002) have used ML for predicting the future development of type-2 diabetes. For analysis purpose, a San Antonio Heart study data was used and ten features along with `SVM` were used to build the prediction model. A 10-fold cross-validation was used for training model because in terms of class labels a dataset was unbalanced. In average of 100 iterations, an 81.1% recall rate and an 84.1% of accuracy are achieved that help in identification of the population who was at high risk of developing type-2 diabetes.

### 5.4.3 ML in prediction of liver abnormalities

The automation is used for prediction of liver abnormalities using ultrasound images. To do it, [Hamid et al. (2018)](#bodymatter-005.xhtml#b_9783110648195-005_ref_018) have used ML model for generating normal and abnormal labels and also detect when its prediction is likely to be incorrect. If the proposed model is not able to predict the abnormalities, then it will not generate the label of a test example. The currently used ML models still generate a label for a given example even in the cases of low prediction confidence. Because of it for learning a solver-based novel stochastic gradient decent was proposed by Kanza and then practical, state-of-the-art method was proposed using it. This method was proposed for liver disease classification and then its testing was done using dataset of 100 patients from Pakistan, Multan, and MINAR. To avoid the drawbacks of biopsy, noninvasive ML approaches are used. [Hashem et al. (2018)](#bodymatter-005.xhtml#b_9783110648195-005_ref_020) have used a number of ML techniques (genetic algorithm, multilinear regression, particle swarm optimization, and `DT`) for predicting advanced fibrosis. In this they have combined the clinical information and serum biomarkers for developing a classification model. There were around 40,000 patients who were divided into two sets of prospective cohorts with chronic hepatitis C. To advanced fibrosis age, aspartate transaminase, albumin, and platelet count were found to be significant that has been found by evaluating the proposed model with receiver operating characteristics. The `AUROC` and `HCC` achieved while predicting advanced fibrosis in patient is between 0.73 and 0.76, and accuracies achieved using ML algorithms were 66.3% and 84.4%. [Vats et al. (2018)](#bodymatter-005.xhtml#b_9783110648195-005_ref_044) have worked on `k-means`, affinity propagation, and density-based spatial clustering of applications with noise (`DBSCAN`) ML techniques due to its popularity and good results achieved in the field of data science. It is further compared in terms of computational complexity and prediction accuracy of liver disease prediction in patients. The liver disease-related healthcare dataset was used, and the performance measurement of the above-mentioned three ML algorithms was compared using the Silhouette coefficient. According to this measurement, a `k-means` is an optimal method in predicting liver diseases.
