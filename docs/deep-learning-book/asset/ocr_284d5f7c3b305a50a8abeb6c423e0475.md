a mean of zero, $\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{x}] = \mathbf{0}$. If this is not the case, the data can easily be centered by subtracting the mean from all examples in a preprocessing step.

The unbiased sample covariance matrix associated with $\mathbf{X}$ is given by

$$\operatorname{Var}[\boldsymbol{x}] = \frac{1}{m-1}\mathbf{X}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{X}. \tag{5.85}$$

PCA finds a representation (through linear transformation) $\boldsymbol{z} = \mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{x}$, where $\operatorname{Var}[\boldsymbol{z}]$ is diagonal.

In section [2.12]{.red}, we saw that the principal components of a design matrix $\mathbf{X}$ are given by the eigenvectors of $\mathbf{X}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{X}$. From this view,

$$\mathbf{X}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{T}}. \tag{5.86}$$

In this section, we exploit an alternative derivation of the principal components. The principal components may also be obtained via singular value decomposition (SVD). Specifically, they are the right singular vectors of $\mathbf{X}$. To see this, let $\mathbf{W}$ be the right singular vectors in the decomposition $\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{T}}$. We then recover the original eigenvector equation with $\mathbf{W}$ as the eigenvector basis:

$$\mathbf{X}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{X} = (\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{T}})^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{T}} = \mathbf{W}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{2}\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{T}}. \tag{5.87}$$

The SVD is helpful to show that PCA results in a diagonal $\operatorname{Var}[\boldsymbol{z}]$. Using the SVD of $\mathbf{X}$, we can express the variance of $\mathbf{X}$ as:

$$\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}[\boldsymbol{x}] &= \frac{1}{m-1}\mathbf{X}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{X} \tag{5.88}\\
&= \frac{1}{m-1}(\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{T}})^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{T}} \tag{5.89}\\
&= \frac{1}{m-1}\mathbf{W}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{U}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{T}} \tag{5.90}\\
&= \frac{1}{m-1}\mathbf{W}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{2}\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{T}}, \tag{5.91}
\end{aligned}$$

where we use the fact that $\mathbf{U}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{U} = \mathbf{I}$ because the $\mathbf{U}$ matrix of the singular value decomposition is defined to be orthogonal. This shows that the covariance of $\boldsymbol{z}$ is diagonal as required:

$$\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}[\boldsymbol{z}] &= \frac{1}{m-1}\mathbf{Z}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{Z} \tag{5.92}\\
&= \frac{1}{m-1}\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{X}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W} \tag{5.93}
\end{aligned}$$
