Under a local Taylor series approximation, the gradient is given by

$$\nabla_w \hat{J}(w)=H(w-w^*). \tag{7.34}$$

We are going to study the trajectory followed by the parameter vector during training. For simplicity, let us set the initial parameter vector to the origin, $w^{(0)}=0$. Let us study the approximate behavior of gradient descent on $J$ by analyzing gradient descent on $\hat{J}$:

$$w^{(\tau)}=w^{(\tau-1)}-\epsilon\nabla_w\hat{J}(w^{(\tau-1)}) \tag{7.35}$$
$$=w^{(\tau-1)}-\epsilon H(w^{(\tau-1)}-w^*), \tag{7.36}$$
$$w^{(\tau)}-w^*=(I-\epsilon H)(w^{(\tau-1)}-w^*). \tag{7.37}$$

Let us now rewrite this expression in the space of the eigenvectors of $H$, exploiting the eigendecomposition of $H$: $H=Q\Lambda Q^\top$, where $\Lambda$ is a diagonal matrix and $Q$ is an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors.

$$w^{(\tau)}-w^*=(I-\epsilon Q\Lambda Q^\top)(w^{(\tau-1)}-w^*) \tag{7.38}$$
$$Q^\top(w^{(\tau)}-w^*)=(I-\epsilon\Lambda)Q^\top(w^{(\tau-1)}-w^*) \tag{7.39}$$

Assuming that $w^{(0)}=0$ and that $\epsilon$ is chosen to be small enough to guarantee $|1-\epsilon\lambda_i|<1$, the parameter trajectory during training after $\tau$ parameter updates is as follows:

$$Q^\top w^{(\tau)}=\left[I-(I-\epsilon\Lambda)^\tau\right]Q^\top w^*. \tag{7.40}$$

Now, the expression for $Q^\top\tilde{w}$ in equation 7.13 for $L^2$ regularization can be rearranged as

$$Q^\top\tilde{w}=(\Lambda+\alpha I)^{-1}\Lambda Q^\top w^*, \tag{7.41}$$
$$Q^\top\tilde{w}=\left[I-(\Lambda+\alpha I)^{-1}\alpha\right]Q^\top w^*. \tag{7.42}$$
