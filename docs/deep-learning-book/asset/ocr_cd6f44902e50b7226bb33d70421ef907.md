Figure 14.4: A denoising autoencoder is trained to map a corrupted data point $\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}$ back to the original data point $\boldsymbol{x}$. We illustrate training examples $\boldsymbol{x}$ as red crosses lying near a low-dimensional manifold, illustrated with the bold black line. We illustrate the corruption process $C(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}} \mid \boldsymbol{x})$ with a gray circle of equiprobable corruptions. A gray arrow demonstrates how one training example is transformed into one sample from this corruption process. When the denoising autoencoder is trained to minimize the average of squared errors $|| g(f(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})) - \boldsymbol{x} ||^2$, the reconstruction $g(f(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}))$ estimates $\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}, \tilde{\boldsymbol{x}} \sim p_{\text{data}}(\boldsymbol{x}) C(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}} \mid \boldsymbol{x})} [\boldsymbol{x} \mid \tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}]$. The vector $g(f(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})) - \tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}$ points approximately toward the nearest point on the manifold, since $g(f(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}))$ estimates the center of mass of the clean points $\boldsymbol{x}$ that could have given rise to $\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}$. The autoencoder thus learns a vector field $g(f(\boldsymbol{x})) - \boldsymbol{x}$ indicated by the green arrows. This vector field estimates the score $\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}} \log p_{\text{data}}(\boldsymbol{x})$ up to a multiplicative factor that is the average root mean square reconstruction error.
