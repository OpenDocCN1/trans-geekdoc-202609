### 12.1 Introduction

Today, the Internet of Things (IoT) is an important part of the current system, and it is the fastest-growing technology. Using the IoT, the number of cities can be expanded to include smart cities, automation in industrial settings, agriculture, and healthcare. IoT is a network that uses different network gadgets, such as sensors, software, and other technologies. It also allows data collection, sharing, and analysis of data. From wearables and smart thermostats to more complicated systems, including agricultural sensors and medical equipment—these types of gadgets are used every day in households [1]. IoT is a new paradigm in computing that seems to have the capacity to enhance impact in smart city implementation [2]. All over the world, IoT is accepted, and it not only unlocks great opportunities but also introduces important sensitivity. IoT systems are more attractive targets for threats because of the large number of gadgets using internet connectivity. Security in an IoT environment is important so that it can ensure the privacy, integrity, and availability of data. Powerful security measures are needed to protect against breaches such as data tampering, unauthorized access, and denial-of-service attacks, which can have serious effects, specifically in sensitive domains like agriculture and healthcare [3].

For remote monitoring and early diagnosis, wearable health monitors and medical transplants can offer real-time data on patients’ health states. However, for hackers, the main target is stored personal health data as well as transmitted sensitive data. Precision farming is made possible by IoT technology, which monitors crop health, weather, and soil conditions to minimize and cut resource usage in agriculture. Cyberattacks can disrupt operations, resulting in financial losses, lost data, and even jeopardizing food security, which is a weakness of these systems [4].

In light of these challenges, intrusion detection systems (IDSs) are essential for the security of IoT ecosystems. Because IoT networks are dynamic and varied, conventional IDS techniques, including signature-based detection, sometimes fall short [5]. IoT devices constantly produce large volumes of statistics, making manual analysis and anomaly detection unfeasible. A component of machine learning, deep learning offers sophisticated ways to identify intrusions in IoT settings. From huge datasets, deep learning (DL) algorithms quickly identify difficult patterns. As a result, they will be able to discover diffused, unidentified risks in IoT structures [6].

Numerous devices with varying capabilities and safety techniques define IoT systems. It is very difficult to make sure of security because of the diversity of IoT devices and the fact that a number of them are located in unprotected regions. Furthermore, it is difficult to use conventional, resource-extensive safety solutions due to resource limitations, including processing strength, storage, and battery life obstacles [7]. Furthermore, the size of IoT networks makes it even more difficult to reveal and discover security issues, as malicious activity can go undetected within massive record sets [8]. DL has proven transformative capabilities in numerous domains due to its capacity to process and analyze large volumes of complicated information. Through the years DL models have been utilized in numerous domains viz., healthcare [9, 10], autonomous systems, agriculture [11], finance, manufacturing and Industry 4.0, IoT, and smart cities to name a few.

The challenges of intrusion detection in IoT systems have shown notable promise for DL and other areas of machine learning. The capacity of DL models to deal with huge volumes of complex records and automatically extract features pertinent to anomaly or intrusion detection sets it apart from older techniques. In dynamic conditions like the IoT, those models succeed because they may be adaptable and might learn from new data patterns and emerging risks. Moreover, DL techniques like convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) can process a variety of record types, inclusive of images, network traffic, and sensor readings, making intrusion detection extra thorough and accurate [12].

The promise of DL in an IDS, the specialized applications of these technologies in IoT environments, especially in healthcare and agriculture, and the special security threats presented by IoT will all be covered in further detail in the upcoming chapters.

### 12.2 Overview of IoT Security Challenges

The IoT has transformed healthcare and agriculture by enabling real-time data collection and decision-making via networked devices as shown in Figure 12.1. To enable remote monitoring and individualized care, IoT devices like pacemakers and medical wearables—such as glucose sensors and heart rate monitors—collect and transmit critical health data to medical specialists. These gadgets frequently operate within a network of linked systems to provide a comprehensive picture of patient health.

Some IoT technology tools are soil moisture sensors, smart irrigation systems, and automated farming. These devices frequently need to communicate data back to centralized control systems over large, remote locations. However, because any device may be a potential target for malevolent attacks, this interconnectedness also expands the attack surface [13]. It poses serious problems because of the variety of devices and the limitations of many IoT devices, like their low computing power, short battery life, and constrained network bandwidth [14].
