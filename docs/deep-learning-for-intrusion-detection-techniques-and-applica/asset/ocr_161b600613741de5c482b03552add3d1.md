Intrusion detection systems (IDSs) are among the most prevalent methods for detecting zero-day attacks. While IDSs have demonstrated effectiveness in tackling the exponential rise of zero-day threats, they still exhibit limitations when compared to detecting previously known attacks [12]. Zero-day exploits capitalize on novel vulnerabilities or manipulate known vulnerabilities in unforeseen ways, rendering signature-based IDSs ineffective since these exploits do not match existing known patterns. The rapid expansion of internet-connected devices further increases exposure to new types of attacks, raising the probability of encountering zero-day vulnerabilities [13]. To enhance IDS performance, researchers have increasingly turned to DL techniques. These approaches enable the IDS to identify previously unseen patterns and behaviors indicative of sophisticated attacks. This paper focuses specifically on artificial intelligence (AI)-based algorithms for zero-day attack detection. It reviews prior methodologies and introduces contemporary techniques that have emerged in recent years. By providing insights into various approaches and evaluating their performance, the work addresses a gap in comparative research on zero-day detection strategies, offering an up-to-date perspective on advancements in this critical area of cybersecurity. Zero-day attacks pose one of the most significant challenges in cybersecurity. These attacks exploit vulnerabilities in software or systems that are unknown to the vendor or security community, rendering traditional IDSs ineffective. DL, a subset of AI, offers a promising approach to tackling this complex problem by leveraging advanced algorithms capable of learning patterns and detecting anomalies without relying on predefined signatures [14]. Zero-day attacks are characterized by their novel nature and stealthy behavior. Since the vulnerabilities they exploit are undiscovered, there are no existing signatures or rules that can be used for detection. Attackers often obfuscate their payloads to bypass conventional security measures, increasing the difficulty of detection. The impact of zero-day attacks can be devastating, affecting critical infrastructure and compromising sensitive data before a patch is available. DL brings a paradigm shift to intrusion detection by focusing on data-driven pattern recognition. Unlike traditional machine learning, where features are manually selected, DL automates feature extraction, making it more adaptable to complex data. Its ability to detect anomalies and model intricate relationships in data positions it well for identifying previously unseen attack patterns. Moreover, DL models excel in handling non-linear and high-dimensional data, making them suitable for modern network environments [15].
