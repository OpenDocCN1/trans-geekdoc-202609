# 6.1 Introduction

The increasing prevalence of security threats across the internet and computer networks necessitates highly dependable security measures. Intrusion detection systems (IDSs) and intrusion prevention systems (IPSs) are essential components in safeguarding modern networks, as they help in identifying and preventing a wide range of cyber attacks. To ensure the effectiveness of these systems, reliable benchmark datasets are essential for testing and evaluating their performance. Several well-known datasets, such as DARPA98, NSLKDD, and ISCX2012, have been widely used by researchers to assess IDS and IPS solutions. However, many of these datasets have become outdated, lack diversity in attack types, and fail to reflect current network threats. Additionally, some datasets suffer from limited traffic volume, anonymized packet payloads, or missing metadata, making them less effective for modern cybersecurity research. The expanding network infrastructures and the advancing complexity of cyber threats emphasize the necessity for high-quality, realistic, and publicly accessible datasets. This chapter explores the existing intrusion detection datasets, their strengths, and limitations. Furthermore, it discusses the evaluation of network traffic features and various attack categories. Datasets for intrusion detection include labeled data, such as network activity, system behaviors, and traffic logs, categorized as either malicious or benign.
