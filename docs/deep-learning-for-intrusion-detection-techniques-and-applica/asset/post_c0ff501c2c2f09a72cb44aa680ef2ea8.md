## 12.6 Challenges and Future Directions

### 12.6.1 Current Limitations

With only a few issues that need to be addressed in terms of acceptance and efficacy, the DL-based IDS offers a number of benefits for improving security in IoT contexts.

- a) Computational Overhead of DL Models: One of the primary problems with IoT systems is the DL or the necessary processing cost. In general, resource-intensive jobs are handled by the different DL algorithms, like CNNs and RNNs, which require greater memory, storage, and processing power. Many IoT devices, such as wearable devices or low-power-constrained devices that use limited computational resources make it difficult for the DL models to get directly deployed on the devices [47]. Hence, significant efforts are required to optimize these models to reduce the computational overload on the edge layers instead of the devices themselves.

- b) Lack of Standardized Frameworks for IoT Security: IoT systems are basically heterogeneous, which consists of various devices having different processing capacities, security requirements, and communication protocols. The absence of standardized frameworks for intrusion detection in IoT security highlights a need for the development of a unified approach for securing IoT environments for different IoT devices. Hence, it is hard to implement and maintain security measures over the diverse IoT system, allowing systems to be exploited.

### 12.6.2 Research Opportunities

- a) Developing Energy-Efficient DL Algorithms: Many IoT devices have limited energy resources and computational processing power; therefore there is a crucial demand to integrate energy-efficient DL algorithms. Presently, lightweight models offer the same level of detection accuracy as the traditional DL models but significantly reduce computational burden. Knowledge distillation, quantization, and model pruning are the various different techniques that reduce the complexity of the models and allow them to run efficiently in resource-constrained IoT devices. Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) or Field-Programmable Gate Arrays (FPGAs) are specialized processors used for hardware acceleration and could further optimize the processing power required for DL in IoT environments. To cite [48].

- b) Expanding Datasets for Better Model Generalization Across Diverse IoT Applications: The datasets needed for DL models need to be large in number with diversity. However, many IoT security datasets are limited in size and scope, making it difficult for DL models to provide generalized solutions across diverse applications. To enhance model performance and guarantee that DL-based IDSs may be used successfully in a variety of industries, it is essential to grow and diversify datasets that represent the real-world diversity of IoT environments. To collect extensive data for model training, this may entail the development of publicly available datasets or cooperative initiatives between government agencies, businesses, and academia.

Furthermore, the resilience of IoT networks may be substantially increased via integrating self-recovery structures, which are capable of both detecting and responding to security problems on their own. Without human intervention, these systems may isolate compromised devices, patch vulnerabilities, and automatically reconfigure themselves in reaction to an intrusion, making sure of ongoing security throughout expansive, difficult IoT ecosystems. The future of IoT security may be shaped through structures that might be flexible, scalable, and capable of maintaining high levels of security as IoT environments alternate through emphasizing collaborative protection and self-learning models.
