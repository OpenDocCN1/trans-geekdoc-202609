Smart environments—which include smart homes, cities, and industrial Internet of Things (IoT) systems—create a new way of life by integrating a comprehensive network of interconnected and intercommunicating devices and sensors that exchange data in real time. These systems are specially designed to improve efficiency, convenience, and sustainability. In smart homes, the heating, lighting, and security systems can be automated, while in smart cities, sensors monitor traffic, air quality, and public services (Figure 9.1). On an industrial level, industrial IoT optimizes manufacturing processes by collecting data about machines and production lines to increase productivity and safety [1]. These smart environments rely on a continuous data exchange system whereby devices interact with each other and centralized cloud platforms. Interconnectivity is a primary enabler of performance optimization, energy efficiency, and innovative services that drive their use. The vast amount of connected devices creates significant security issues. A breached IoT device in one aspect of the system can eventually lead to cascading failures across the entire ecosystem, further exposing sensitive personal, financial, and operational data to malicious parties. It is important to secure this environment against potential cyberattacks [2].
