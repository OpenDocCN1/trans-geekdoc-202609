DL is built on ANNs, which is a mathematical modeling of biological assemblies of neurons. ## 5.7 Limitations

Application of DL for IDS has its own limitations. It can result in injustices as courts and the police do not know how to interpret the results of the software.

### 5.7.1 Mr. Williams’ Case

Image recognition by AI assists law enforcement agencies in the investigation process and complements it; police accept the hints of the computer as formal allegations and are not considered as evidence in a court of law. Relying on a piece of information from the AI image recognition software that suggested Mr. Williams was a shoplifter in a shop, the police were convinced and arrested him. Police ignored all mandatory legal and procedural safeguards and relied upon the facial recognition software while formally charging Mr. Williams. After this, IBM, Microsoft, and Amazon withdrew their facial recognition software from police use.

Based on the facial recognition algorithm, Mr. Williams was arrested and made an accused of a crime by the police that he had not committed. According to technology and law experts, Mr. Williams’ case may be the first known case of an American being arrested wrongfully based on a defective facial match from a facial recognition technology. This led to debate across America leading to the announcement of the withdrawal of this technology by Amazon, IBM, and Microsoft from use by law enforcement agencies.

According to studies by M.I.T. and the National Institute of Standards and Technology, or NIST, the technology works differentially for different demographics such that its results are relatively good for white men, whereas it is less accurate for other demographics; this may be partly due to lack of diversity in the images used to develop the underlying databases [19].

### 5.7.2 Challenges

Intrusion detection frameworks suffer from significant challenges due to high-dimensionality of data in the face of an increasing number of attacks and incursions; reduction of dimension helps achieve better results in terms of accuracy, for example, by “removing dimensions from correlation and information gain, ANN is applied to the KDD99 dataset produced better results” [29]. The scope of intrusion detection mechanism becomes very limited as datasets (existing or proposed) are either contrived or biased and there is no standard validation mechanism for it, and complete accuracy is difficult to accomplish [22].

The process of DL has some inherent challenges such as (i) for training and efficient performance it needs a lot of data which has inherent risks; (ii) the agent involved in deep reinforcement learning self-learns and explores to achieve rewards, and maintaining a balance between is difficult; (iii) maintaining safety and ethics is a cause of concern, as the agent, by learning unintended behaviors may result in wrong actions; and (iv) real-life situations are often unpredictable and uncertain, which can impact the efficiency of a model [30].

DL is a very significant tool for intrusion detection, but it has its own share of challenges to address that can be as follows:

- Maintaining accuracy.
- Self-learning may become insufficient in the light of new forms of attack as it may work on outdated data.
- Generalization becomes a challenge as labeling all problems into algorithms is difficult.
- How the neural network arrives at a conclusion/solution.
- It consumes a lot of power and is costly.
- Legal and regulatory as it has cross-border ramifications.

## 5.8 Conclusion

Like any other tool, DL, to serve greatest benefit and avoid harm must be used with diligence and responsibility. DL has considerably improved the IDSs. Given the diversity and complexity of threats, it is very difficult to design a comprehensive IDS. The increased threat to the national and international network system is a cause of concern. As the entire intrusion detection mechanism relies upon datasets, based on scientific principles, there is a need to have a validation/authentication mechanism for it.
