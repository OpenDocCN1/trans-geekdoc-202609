One of the most effective methods is anomaly detection, which focuses on identifying deviations from normal behavior rather than relying on predefined rules or signatures. DL models, with their exceptional pattern recognition capabilities, are particularly well-suited for this task.

The ability of DL to automatically learn complex features from data marks a significant advantage over traditional machine learning models. Conventional methods often require manual feature extraction, which can be time-consuming and limited in scope. DL architectures, such as autoencoders and RNNs, have demonstrated superior performance in anomaly detection. Autoencoders learn to reconstruct normal data patterns, flagging deviations that may indicate potential zero-day attacks. Similarly, LSTM networks, a type of RNN, excel at detecting temporal anomalies in sequential data like network traffic. Beyond anomaly detection, behavior-based threat detection has gained prominence as a strategy for identifying zero-day exploits. Unlike signature-based methods that rely on known attack signatures, behavior-based models analyze application and system behavior to detect suspicious activities. DL techniques, including graph neural networks (GNNs), have proven effective in modeling complex relationships between system components, enabling more precise detection of behavioral anomalies. Furthermore, deep reinforcement learning (DRL) offers a dynamic approach to mitigating zero-day threats by learning optimal response strategies in real-time attack scenarios. Natural language processing (NLP) is another domain where DL enhances zero-day attack detection [40]. The cybersecurity landscape is inundated with unstructured data, including threat reports, vulnerability descriptions, and security advisories. DL models, such as transformer architectures, can analyze and extract valuable insights from this vast corpus of text-based information. By leveraging word embeddings and contextual representations, these models contribute to proactive threat intelligence and vulnerability prediction, offering a preemptive edge against zero-day attacks. Real-world applications of DL in zero-day attack detection further highlight its efficacy. For example, autoencoder-based systems have been deployed to monitor network traffic, successfully identifying anomalous patterns indicative of previously unknown exploits targeting IoT devices. Similarly, CNNs have been used for malware classification, achieving impressive accuracy in detecting novel malware samples by analyzing their binary representations. Adaptive defense strategies, powered by DRL, simulate attack scenarios to optimize real-time defense mechanisms, enhancing overall system resilience. These applications can prevent cyberattacks and ensure the security and stability of both software and hardware systems by identifying threats before they cause widespread damage [11]. Additionally, DL models can be trained to automatically generate patches or updates to fix vulnerabilities, significantly reducing the risk of future zero-day attacks.
