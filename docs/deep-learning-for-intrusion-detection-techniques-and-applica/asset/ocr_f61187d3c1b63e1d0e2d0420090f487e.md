ML models such as K-nearest neighbors (KNN), Random Forest (RF), Decision Trees (ID3), and others were tested for their effectiveness in detecting intrusions, with RF emerging as the best-performing algorithm due to its high accuracy and fast execution time [24].

Unlike many previous intrusion detection datasets, CIC-IDS-2017 was designed to meet eleven essential evaluation criteria, ensuring its completeness and reliability. It includes a comprehensive network configuration with routers, switches, firewalls, and multiple operating systems (Linux, Windows, and MacOS). The dataset captures full network traffic, provides labeled data for training ML models, supports multiple protocols (HTTP, FTP, SSH, etc.), and encompasses a diverse range of attacks. Additionally, it includes metadata and a well-documented structure, making it a valuable resource for research in intrusion detection and cybersecurity.

CIC-IDS-2017 can be accessed by researchers publicly. The dataset includes both raw network captures (PCAP files) and preprocessed CSV files containing extracted features, making it suitable for training and testing IDS models as shown in Table 6.8. Given its comprehensive nature, CIC-IDS-2017 remains one of the most reliable datasets for evaluating modern intrusion detection systems.
