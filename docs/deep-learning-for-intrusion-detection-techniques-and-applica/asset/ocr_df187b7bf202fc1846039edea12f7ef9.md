# 3.4.2.3 Deep Learning Models Commonly Used for IDSs

+   - **Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)**: CNNs are particularly effective for analyzing traffic patterns and identifying anomalies in network data. These models are inspired by the visual cortex and are well-suited for processing structured data, such as network packets. CNNs can automatically extract spatial features from the data, making them highly effective at detecting patterns associated with attacks such as DDoS or malware [57].
+   - **Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) Networks**: RNNs and LSTM networks are designed to handle sequential data, such as time-series data or network logs. These models are particularly effective at detecting attacks that involve repeated patterns over time, such as brute force attacks or insider threats. LSTM networks are capable of learning long-term dependencies in the data, making them well-suited for detecting subtle anomalies that may indicate an attack [58].
+   - **Autoencoders**: Autoencoders are a type of neural network used for unsupervised learning and anomaly detection. These models work by learning a compressed representation of normal data and then reconstructing it. When presented with anomalous data, the autoencoder struggles to reconstruct it accurately, allowing the system to flag it as a potential threat. Autoencoders are particularly effective for detecting insider threats, as they can model normal user behavior and identify deviations that may indicate malicious activity [59].

# 3.4.3.1 Financial Institutions

Financial institutions are prime targets for cyberattacks due to the sensitive nature of the data they handle. Deep learning-based IDSs have been successfully deployed in this sector to detect fraudulent transactions and prevent unauthorized access to financial systems. For example, some banks have implemented CNNs to analyze transaction patterns and identify anomalies that may indicate fraud. These systems can process vast amounts of transaction data in real time, enabling the bank to detect and respond to fraudulent activity before significant damage occurs [60].
