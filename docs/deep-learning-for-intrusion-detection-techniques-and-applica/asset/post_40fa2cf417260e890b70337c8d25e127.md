Intrusion Detection Systems (IDSs) are security solutions designed to monitor and analyze network or system activities to identify suspicious patterns that may indicate security breaches or malicious activity. By continuously examining incoming and outgoing data, an IDS can detect and respond to threats in real-time or near real-time. There are various types of IDSs, such as host-based IDS (HIDS), which focus on individual devices, and network-based IDS (NIDS), which monitor network traffic. These systems can operate in a signature-based mode, detecting known attack patterns, or in an anomaly-based mode, identifying deviations from normal behavior [1].

### Importance of Intrusion Detection Systems:

- 1) Early Threat Detection: The IDS can detect and flag potentially malicious activities before they escalate into full-scale attacks. This early warning capability enables system administrators to intervene and mitigate threats promptly, often preventing data breaches, service outages, or other severe consequences.
- 2) Real-Time Monitoring and Response: By providing continuous monitoring, IDS enables organizations to respond to incidents as they happen, significantly reducing the time it takes to detect and manage threats. This real-time capability is essential for high-security environments, such as financial institutions, healthcare organizations, and critical infrastructure.
- 3) Protection Against Insider Threats: The IDS can identify unusual activity originating from within the network, helping detect insider threats from employees or contractors with malicious intent. This capability is crucial for preventing data theft, sabotage, and unauthorized access.
- 4) **Enhanced Security Posture:** With intrusion detection as a part of a layered security approach, organizations can strengthen their overall security posture. IDS complements other security measures, such as firewalls and antivirus software, by providing an additional layer of detection that targets subtle or sophisticated attacks.
- 5) **Forensic Analysis and Incident Investigation:** IDS logs and analyzes data on suspicious activities, creating an audit trail for post-incident investigation. These records are invaluable for understanding the nature and source of an attack, determining what assets were affected, and identifying vulnerabilities that need addressing to prevent future attacks.
- 6) **Adaptability to New Threats with Machine Learning:** Modern IDS equipped with ML can adapt to evolving cyber threats by learning from new attack patterns. This adaptability allows them to detect previously unseen threats, making them more resilient against advanced persistent threats (APTs) and zero-day exploits.
- 7) **Regulatory Compliance and Risk Management:** Many industries are subject to regulatory standards, such as HIPAA– *Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act*, GDPR-General Data Protection Regulation, and PCI DSS-Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard, which require robust security measures to protect sensitive information. IDS supports compliance by ensuring real-time monitoring and detection of potential violations, minimizing the risk of penalties and enhancing risk management practices [2].

IDSs are a critical component of any organization’s cybersecurity infrastructure. They provide crucial insights into potential security threats, helping organizations to detect, respond to, and prevent attacks, thus protecting valuable data and maintaining the integrity of their systems.

## 2.1.1.1 Types of IDSs: Host-Based, Network-Based, Hybrid

According to the elements they are designed to monitor and their location within the network or system, IDSs can be classified into three types. HIDSs, NIDSs, and hybrid IDS (HIDs) are the three main varieties. Each type possesses specific features that make them suitable for specific tasks in intrusion detection and management.

### Host-Based Intrusion Detection Systems (HIDSs)

**Definition:** An HIDS is a security system that is resident on single-host machines like computers, servers, or workstations. Various internal activities of the host are observed, including system logs, the integrity of the files, applications and their activities, and configurations to detect abnormal or malicious behavior.

- Inspects data packets for signs of suspicious activity through deep packet inspection (DPI).

### Advantages:

- Provides a broad view of the network, making it effective for detecting attacks that spread across the network.
- Monitors real-time traffic, enabling quick detection of large-scale network-based attacks.
- Less resource-intensive on individual hosts as it is deployed on network devices or segments.

### Limitations:

- Limited visibility into encrypted network traffic without decryption capabilities, which may allow certain attacks to go undetected.
- Cannot detect attacks that occur entirely within a host’s system or through legitimate-looking traffic.
- Prone to generating false positives in environments with high volumes of traffic or legitimate but unusual activity.

### Hybrid Intrusion Detection Systems

**Definition:** An HID combines elements of both the HIDS and NIDS, leveraging both host-level and network-level data to provide a more comprehensive view of potential intrusions [4]. A hybrid IDS integrates host-based and network-based monitoring for a layered detection approach, often using central analysis tools to correlate data from multiple sources.

### Key Functions of Hybrid IDS:

- Collects data from both network traffic and individual hosts, allowing for cross-correlation of events.
- Provides a unified view of security across the network and its endpoints, improving the detection of complex, multistage attacks.
- Can implement advanced analytics to detect blended attacks that affect both hosts and the network.

### Advantages:

- Offers a comprehensive security view by combining the granular monitoring of an HIDS with the broad network visibility of a NIDS.
- Can reduce false positives by correlating events from different sources, improving accuracy.
- Effective at detecting multistage attacks, such as APTs, which may involve both network traffic and host exploitation.

### Limitations:

- More complex to deploy and manage due to the integration of multiple detection sources.
- Higher cost and resource requirements, as it requires both host-based and network-based components.
- Requires sophisticated correlation algorithms and central management to handle and interpret large volumes of data.

## 2.2 Role of Machine Learning in IDSs

ML plays an increasingly significant role in enhancing IDSs. Traditional IDS methods, often reliant on rule-based and signature-based detection, face limitations in adapting to evolving threats, such as zero-day attacks and APTs. ML addresses these limitations by leveraging data-driven models that can recognize complex patterns, adapt to new threat types, and provide a more dynamic approach to intrusion detection [5].

### 2.2.1 Benefits and Challenges of Using Machine Learning in IDSs

### 2.2.1.1 Benefits of ML in IDSs

- **Adaptive Threat Detection:** ML models can learn from new data, making them capable of recognizing novel and emerging threats without requiring explicit programming or updates for each new threat.
- **Reduced False Positives:** By using advanced algorithms that analyze patterns in data, ML-based IDS can reduce the number of false positives, leading to more accurate detection and less time spent on investigating false alarms.
- **Behavioral Analysis:** ML can move beyond static signature detection to detect unusual or anomalous behavior that may indicate an intrusion. This behavior-based approach can identify threats that signature-based systems may miss.
- **Scalability and Efficiency:** ML algorithms, particularly in large-scale environments, can process and analyze massive volumes of data more effectively than rule-based systems, making them suitable for high-traffic networks.
- **Predictive Capabilities:** ML-based IDS can use historical data to predict potential future threats, aiding in proactive cybersecurity measures. These systems can also recognize patterns that indicate early stages of complex, multistage attacks.
