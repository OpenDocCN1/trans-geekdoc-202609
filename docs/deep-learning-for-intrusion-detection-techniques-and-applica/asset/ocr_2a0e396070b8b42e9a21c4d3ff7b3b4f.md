Loss functions estimate the difference between predicted and actual outputs. The goal is to minimize the difference in training by decreasing the distance between its prediction and the real target. This recurrent process reaches its peak in the final output layer, generating the desired result. The simplicity, convenience, and capability of mapping intricate relationships within data are the power of ANNs [8]. These models can learn from massive databases and generalize effectively to new data, making them widely utilized in real-world applications.

# 10.5.3 Conventional Neural Networks (CNNs)

Convolutional neural network (CNN) is a significant image processing, feature learning, and classification model framework. It consists of three basic layers and is optimized to efficiently deal with input image data, making it a key component of a variety of applications.

+   - **Traditional Layer (CONV)**: Convolutional layers are the building block of CNNs, with the primary task of performing convolution operations. To accomplish convolution processes, the layers use kernels or filters that move horizontally and vertically according to the stride rate. The convolutional layer utilizes nonlinear activation functions, the most common of which is the ReLU). ReLU improves the network's ability to understand complex trends by introducing nonlinearity.
+   - **Pooling Layer (POOL)**: The pooling layer, which handles dimensionality reduction, minimizes computational expenses while maintaining significant information. Two common types are maximum pooling, which picks the maximum value in a region of a kernel, and average pooling, which calculates average values in the kernel. Pooling enhances network resilience and aids in improved management of computing resources.
+   - **Fully Connected Layers (FC)**: The FC layer, in conjunction with a flattened input, connects each input to all neurons, initiating the classification process. FC layers are usually found at the end of the network and execute mathematical operations to finalize the process of classification as shown in Figure 10.6. Apart from these steps, other elements contribute to CNN's performance. The activation function influences the classification output, especially in the final fully linked layer. The softmax function, commonly used, normalizes outputs to represent target probability classes. Dropout layers prevent overfitting during training, eliminate individual neurons' contributions, and promote more resilient learning from various batches of training data.
