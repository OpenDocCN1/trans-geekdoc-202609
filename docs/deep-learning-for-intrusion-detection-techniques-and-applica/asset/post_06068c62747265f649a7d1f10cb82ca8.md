## 11.5 Applications of Deep Learning-Based IDS

The fast-paced development of wireless networks, especially IoT, 5G, and MANETs has thrown up new security challenges that require sophisticated intrusion detection solutions [20]. The problem is that we can no longer rely on the old security mechanisms that were built into our computers. Why? Because we no longer have computers the way we used to. Instead, we now have decentrally distributed networks that we access through a wide variety of devices, many of which are under our direct control—mainly smartphones and tablets, but also smart TVs, home routers, and a growing number of other devices that are “smart” in one way or another. Moreover, all of these devices have their own operating systems, software, and built-in security imperatives. So, in a very real way, we are all inside the IoT [12].

This section covers DL techniques and their applications in IDSs. It focuses mainly on IoT security, 5G network security, and man-portable MANET security. It delves into effective DL methods and their corresponding challenges. It ranges from security vulnerabilities and the newly minted attack surfaces for the connected world of things to the potential for DL methods to offer not only improved performance for IDSs but also for the connected world of things.

### 11.5.1 IDSs in IoT Security

The IoT ecosystem consists of billions of interconnected smart devices, from wearable technology to industrial sensors. Often, these devices are in resource-constrained environments and have no way to secure themselves, making them perfect targets for hackers. The IoT’s interconnected nature means that threats to any one of its devices can affect the much bigger network, which is the IoT.

#### 11.5.1.1 Security Challenges in Resource-Constrained IoT Devices

IoT networks face several security challenges that necessitate the use of DL-based IDS [21]:

- **Limited Computational Resources:** Many IoT devices have low processing power, memory, and energy availability, restricting the deployment of complex security algorithms.
- **High Attack Surface:** IoT devices operate in diverse environments with weak authentication mechanisms, making them vulnerable to botnets, distributed denial of service (DDoS) attacks, and malware infections.
- **Scalability Issues:** The vast number of connected IoT devices generates enormous amounts of data, making traditional IDS solutions ineffective for real-time monitoring.
- **Lack of Standardized Security Protocols:** IoT security is fragmented, with different manufacturers implementing varying security mechanisms, leading to vulnerabilities.

#### 11.5.1.2 Deploying Lightweight Deep Learning Models on IoT Gateways

To address these challenges, lightweight DL models can be deployed on IoT gateways, which act as intermediaries between IoT devices and the cloud [22]. Some effective DL techniques are as follows:

- Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for extracting key features from network traffic with minimal computational overhead.
- Recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and long short-term memory (LSTM) models for analyzing time-series traffic and detecting anomalous behavior.
- Autoencoders for unsupervised anomaly detection in IoT traffic, identifying zero-day attacks without labeled data.
- Federated learning (FL) for decentralized IDS deployment, enabling IoT devices to train models collaboratively without sharing raw data and enhancing privacy and efficiency.

#### 11.5.1.3 Real-World Implementations

- CNN-based IDSs deployed on IoT gateways achieved 94% accuracy in detecting botnet traffic [23].

#### 11.5.2.3 Real-World Implementations

- Transformer-based IDSs achieved 99% accuracy in detecting DDoS attacks in 5G networks [27].
- GAN-enhanced anomaly detection models effectively identified stealth attacks on network slices.
- Federated deep learning IDSs secured 5G edge nodes, improving latency and scalability [28].

By integrating DL with SDN, edge computing, and AI-driven orchestrations, IDSs can effectively mitigate threats in 5G and future wireless networks.

### 11.5.3 IDS for Mobile Ad Hoc Networks (MANETs)

A MANET is a wireless network formed by a group of nodes that can move around freely and communicate with one another. This kind of network is often seen in the military and emergency response sectors and can support dynamic vehicular networks [29]. Like any form of mobile infrastructure, however, the ad hoc network is highly vulnerable to a range of eavesdropping, spoofing, and DoS attacks.

#### 11.5.3.1 Challenges of Decentralized and Dynamic MANET Security

MANETs face unique security challenges that complicate intrusion detection as detailed below:

- Lack of Centralized Control: No dedicated security infrastructure, making IDS deployment difficult.
- High Mobility and Topology Changes: Frequent node movement leads to unpredictable traffic patterns, requiring adaptive IDS models.
- Limited Computational Resources: Battery-powered nodes require energy-efficient security mechanisms.
- Routing Vulnerabilities: Attacks such as wormhole, Sybil, and blackhole exploit weaknesses in MANET routing protocols.

#### 11.5.3.2 Deep Learning Techniques for Detecting Routing Attacks

A DL-based IDS enhances MANET security by detecting malicious routing behavior [30] using the following:

- Graph neural networks (GNNs) to analyze MANET topology and detect abnormal route modifications.
- LSTM networks and RNNs to model temporal traffic patterns and identify deviations.
- Autoencoders for unsupervised anomaly detection, flagging previously unseen attack behaviors.
- Reinforcement learning (RL)-based IDSs that dynamically adjust detection parameters as the network topology evolves.

#### 11.5.3.3 Real-World Implementations

- GNN-based IDS achieved 96% accuracy in detecting blackhole attacks and Sybil attacks in MANETs [31].
- LSTM models successfully identified malicious route alterations in dynamic vehicular ad hoc networks (VANETs) [32].
- RL-driven IDSs adapt to changing MANET topologies, improving threat detection over time.

Adaptive, real-time IDS solutions in resource-constrained and decentralized MANET environments are provided by DL. These solutions secure communication in military networks, IoT networks, and vehicular networks. Modern wireless networks have been revolutionized by DL-based IDSs, which counteract security threats in the IoT, 5G, and MANET environments—scenes rife with not just traditional but also entirely novel forms of cyberattack. Using CNNs, RNNs, LSTMs, GANs, transformers, and RL, anomaly detection in IDS models can recognize routing attacks, DDoS threats, and network slice vulnerabilities with high precision [33].

### 11.6 Challenges and Future Research Directions

DL-based IDSs have substantially improved network security in recent times. They fall under the category of artificial intelligence (AI), along with other sophisticated machine learning methods. DL relies on artificial neural networks for its framework and is not directly interpretable. Because packet features handily separate benign and malicious traffic, the realization has occurred that, with a few exceptions, well-designed IDSs are not necessary to achieve a high level of security. We explore these key challenges with AI-based intrusion detection and outline potential future solutions.

DL models are vulnerable to adversarial attacks, where attackers manipulate input data to mislead IDS models into misclassifying malicious traffic as benign. These attacks exploit weaknesses in DL-based IDSs, reducing their reliability in real-world scenarios [34].
