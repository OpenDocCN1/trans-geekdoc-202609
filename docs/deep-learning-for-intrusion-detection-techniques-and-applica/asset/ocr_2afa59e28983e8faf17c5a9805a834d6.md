Despite its widespread use, the DARPA IDS evaluation dataset has faced some criticism. One significant limitation is the lack of publicly available traffic generation software, which makes it difficult to assess the accuracy of the background traffic used in the evaluation. Additionally, the evaluation criteria did not consider factors such as system resource usage, ease of use, or the type of system being used, which are important for real-world applications. Critics like McHugh and Mahoney and Chan have pointed out other flaws, such as the use of synthetic background traffic that lacks the complexity of real-world network conditions, including packet storms and anomalous traffic. McHugh also criticized the reliance on scripts and programs for generating attacks, and the simple models used for background traffic, which do not adequately simulate real-world noise, potentially leading to an inflated detection rate. Mahoney and Chan further highlighted issues such as inconsistencies in packet TTL values between normal and attack traffic, which could make detection easier even for basic IDSs. The dataset’s isolation from the internet also means it misses anomalous but non-malicious traffic commonly seen in real-world scenarios, which could affect the realism of the dataset’s evaluations. While these limitations have led to ongoing discussions about the adequacy of the dataset for real-world testing, the DARPA dataset remains a foundational resource in IDS research. The inclusion of detailed system logs and file system snapshots in addition to network traffic allows for a more in-depth analysis of attack scenarios. As a result, the DARPA 1998 dataset remains a crucial resource for the continued advancement of network security, offering valuable insights for researchers and practitioners working to improve the accuracy and effectiveness of IDSs.
