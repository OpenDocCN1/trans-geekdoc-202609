# 6.5 Conclusion

Intrusion detection datasets play a crucial role in evaluating and improving the effectiveness of IDSs and IPSs. A well-structured dataset must contain diverse attack types, realistic traffic patterns, and comprehensive labeling to ensure accurate system performance evaluation. While traditional datasets such as KDD99 and NSL-KDD have been widely used, they suffer from outdated attack types, redundancy, and a lack of modern threats. More recent datasets like CIC-IDS-2017 and UNSW-NB15 have been developed to address these issues by incorporating realistic network traffic, modern cyber threats, and enriched feature sets. However, challenges such as data anonymization, lack of encrypted traffic, and limited real-world variability still exist.

Selecting the right dataset depends on the specific research objectives and evaluation criteria. Packet-based datasets provide detailed payload information, whereas flow-based datasets offer efficient, metadata-driven insights. Some datasets integrate both approaches, combining network and host-based attributes for a more comprehensive view of cyber threats. Additionally, factors like labeling accuracy, traffic diversity, and dataset structure significantly impact the reliability of IDS evaluations.

In conclusion, while significant progress has been made in developing high-quality IDS datasets, there is still a need for continuous updates and improvements to reflect the evolving nature of cyber threats. Future datasets should aim to capture real-world encrypted traffic, include advanced attack techniques such as AI-driven threats and APTs, and ensure balanced data distributions for more effective IDS model training and testing.
