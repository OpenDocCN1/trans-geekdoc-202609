
The application of ML techniques has become prevalent in the field of IDS, where various methods and algorithms, such as neural networks, clustering, nearest neighbors, association rules, genetic algorithms, and decision trees, are utilized to extract insights from intrusion datasets. ML methods have been instrumental in developing multiple IDS to enhance accuracy and reduce the reliance on human expertise. Research in this field primarily focuses on identifying patterns and creating IDSs using datasets. Generally, ML methods fall into three categories: unsupervised, supervised, and semi-supervised.

### a) Supervised Methods in IDS:
IDS employing supervised learning techniques detect intrusions by using labeled training data. This approach involves two main phases: the training phase and the testing phase. During the training session, essential features and categories are identified, and the algorithm learns from these data points. In supervised learning-based IDS, each record includes a host or a network data source along with a corresponding output value, indicating either an intrusion or a normal condition. Feature selection may be applied to eliminate redundant characteristics. Once features are selected using training data, a classifier is trained through a supervised learning approach, enabling it to learn the relationship between its labeled output value and input data. In the testing session, the previously trained model is utilized to classify unknown data into either normal or intrusion categories. The classifier, developed through this process, transforms into a predictive model capable of determining the potential category of input data by analyzing a set of feature values. Various classification methods are available, such as nearest neighbors, decision trees, neural networks, support vector machines, naive Bayes, and rule-based systems techniques. Each method employs a learning methodology to construct a classification model. An effective classification method should not only handle the training data well but also accurately determine the class of records it hasn’t encountered before. The learning algorithm plays a crucial role in creating classification models with reliable generalization capabilities.

### b) Unsupervised Methods in IDS:
Unsupervised learning aims to derive meaningful insights from input datasets that lack class labels. In this approach, input data points are treated as random variables, and a joint density model is created for the entire dataset. Unlike supervised learning, which relies on output labels for training and predicting new data, unsupervised learning operates without any labeled data. Instead, it autonomously groups the data into different classes. In the context of developing IDS, unsupervised learning is employed to detect intrusions by training the model with unlabeled data [17]. After clustering the records, instances found in small clusters are identified as intrusions, as larger clusters are presumed to represent normal occurrences rather than anomalies. Additionally, the inherent dissimilarity between malicious intrusions and normal instances ensures that they cannot belong to the same cluster.

### c) Semi-Supervised Method in IDS:
Semi-supervised learning occupies a middle ground between fully labeled training data in supervised learning and the absence of labeled data in unsupervised learning. Research has demonstrated that semi-supervised learning can improve the effectiveness of IDS by leveraging a limited amount of labeled data, thereby mitigating the associated time and cost. This becomes particularly advantageous in situations where obtaining labeled data is challenging or infrequent within various IDS scenarios.

The use of ML in IDS brings forth various benefits, such as enhanced performance, adaptability, flexibility, and the capability to identify emerging attack types. However, there are associated challenges with deploying an ML-based IDS [18]. Firstly, ML algorithms rely on assumptions about the data, making them susceptible to biases originating from historical data or prior knowledge. Dealing with outliers can impact the effectiveness of ML algorithms, and the detection and prevention of unknown attacks pose challenges due to the evolving complexity of cyber threats over time. The continuous growth in the complexity of attacks presents an ongoing challenge for ML-based IDS, and handling large datasets with millions of network connections can be cumbersome. High dimensionality, characterized by hundreds of dimensions, poses difficulties for ML algorithms, and data preprocessing is intricate, requiring transformation from monitoring system data to a format suitable for analysis. Effective detection may demand contextual characteristics beyond reliance solely on IP addresses. ML algorithms may not always incorporate domain-specific knowledge crucial for accurate intrusion detection. Lastly, the process of feature engineering and parameter selection may prove more critical than the actual choice of algorithm.

## 1.1.5 Need for Deep Learning Based IDS
The utilization of deep learning for intrusion detection is motivated by the limitations of traditional methods, such as signature-based IDS and anomaly-based IDS [19]. While signature-based IDS excels in detecting known attacks, it struggles with unknown or novel threats due to pre-installed rule limitations. On the other hand, anomaly-based IDS is adept at identifying new attacks but often generates high false-positive rates [20]. Challenges in feature selection for anomaly detection arise from the evolving nature of attack scenarios, where features effective for one class may not generalize well. Additionally, the absence of labeled datasets from real networks hampers NIDS development. Deep learning, a subset of ML, addresses these challenges by employing hierarchical information-processing layers for feature learning. Although commonly associated with image classification, deep learning’s versatility extends to various domains, including intrusion detection. It offers benefits such as automatic feature extraction, efficient handling of large datasets, and effective reduction of the feature space. Despite these advantages, challenges like the lack of contextual knowledge, feature engineering complexities, susceptibility to evasion attacks, and the need for labeled data and expertise pose considerations in its implementation for intrusion detection. Overall, deep learning holds promise in overcoming the hurdles faced by traditional methods, envisioning improved NIDS capabilities.
