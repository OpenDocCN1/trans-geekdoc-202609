# 3.4.3.2 Technology Companies

Technology companies, particularly those with large-scale networks, have also adopted deep learning-based IDSs to monitor network traffic and detect unusual patterns. For instance, some of these companies have employed RNNs and LSTM networks to analyze network logs and identify potential threats, such as DDoS attacks or unauthorized access attempts. These systems can process sequential data, such as network traffic over time, and detect patterns that may indicate an attack. By leveraging deep learning, these companies can enhance their ability to detect and respond to threats in real time, reducing the risk of data breaches or service disruptions [61].

# 3.4.3.3 Healthcare Organizations

The healthcare sector has also benefited from the implementation of deep learning-based IDSs. Healthcare organizations handle sensitive patient data, making them attractive targets for cyberattacks. Deep learning models, such as autoencoders, have been used to monitor user activity and detect anomalies that may indicate an insider threat. For example, an autoencoder-based IDS can model normal user behavior and flag unusual access patterns, such as unauthorized access to patient records. This capability is particularly valuable in healthcare, where the consequences of a data breach can be severe [62].

# 3.4.3.4 Government Agencies

Government agencies have also adopted deep learning-based IDSs to protect critical infrastructure and sensitive data. For example, some agencies have implemented CNNs to analyze network traffic and detect potential threats, such as malware or unauthorized access attempts. These systems can process large volumes of data in real time, enabling the agency to detect and respond to threats quickly. Additionally, deep learning-based IDSs have been used to monitor user activity and detect insider threats, such as unauthorized access to classified information [63].

# 3.4.3.5 Retail and E-Commerce

Retail and e-commerce companies have also leveraged deep learning-based IDSs to protect their systems and customer data. For example, some companies have implemented RNNs to analyze transaction data and detect patterns indicative of fraudulent activity. These systems can process sequential data, such as purchase histories, and identify anomalies that may indicate fraud. By leveraging deep learning, these companies can enhance their ability to detect and respond to threats, reducing the risk of financial loss or damage to their reputation [64].
