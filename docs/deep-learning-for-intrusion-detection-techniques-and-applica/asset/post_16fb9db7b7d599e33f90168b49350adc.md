
- **Availability:** Availability ensures immediate access of the system resources to the legitimate users when needed, or the system resources must be readily available to authenticated users and restricted the services and resources from unauthorized users.

## 1.1.4 Intrusion Detection System

In the context of cybersecurity and computer systems, an intrusion refers to any unauthorized or malicious attempt to access, manipulate, or compromise a computer system, network, or data. Intrusions can take various forms and can be perpetrated by individuals or automated programs (malware) with malicious intent. The primary goal of an intrusion is to gain unauthorized access, disrupt system operations, steal sensitive information, or compromise the security and integrity of the targeted network or system [6, 7]. Common examples of intrusions include the following:

- **Unauthorized Access:** Intruders may attempt to gain access to a network or system by exploiting vulnerabilities, such as weak passwords, unpatched software, or misconfigured security settings.
- **Malware Infections:** A malware infection is a situation where a computer system, network, or digital device becomes compromised by malicious software, often referred to as “malware.” This compromise occurs when the malware gains unauthorized access to the system or runs on it, potentially causing harm or performing actions that the user or owner of the system did not intend or authorize. Malware infections can lead to a variety of negative consequences, including data loss, system disruption, theft of sensitive information, unauthorized access, and other harmful activities. To reduce the potential dangers linked to malware contaminations, individuals and organizations employ various cybersecurity measures and practices to prevent, detect, and remove malware from their systems.
- **Denial of Service (DoS) Attacks:** A DoS attack represents a malevolent effort to disrupt the regular operations of a computer system, network, or online service by inundating it with an excessive volume of traffic or requests. The main objective of a DoS attack is to render a resource, such as a website or server, inaccessible, either temporarily or permanently, to its intended users. DoS attacks typically target vulnerabilities in a system’s infrastructure or exploit weaknesses in its configuration to exhaust its resources.
- **Data Breaches:** A data breach is an occurrence, where unauthorized individuals or entities, whether deliberately or accidentally, obtain access to information that is sensitive, confidential, or protected. During a data breach, data that is typically kept confidential and secure becomes exposed, potentially leading to negative consequences for the individuals or organizations affected.

### 1.1.4.1 Intrusion Detection Technologies

ID technologies are generally classified into four types:

- An intrusion detection system based on network.
- An intrusion detection system based on host.
- An intrusion detection system based on wireless technology.
- An intrusion detection system based on network behavioral analysis.

#### An Intrusion Detection System Based on Network

![](img/a13b588839130d750aecd8a32b6143d9_42_0.png)

**Figure 1.4** Network intrusion detection system.

Network-based IDS (NIDS), as shown in Figure 1.4, is tasked with overseeing network traffic to ensure the security of network devices [11]. These systems scrutinize various protocols, including application, transport, and network protocols. The analysis in NIDS primarily occurs at the application layer, although hardware layer analysis is also possible. Typical components of NIDS include management servers, sensors, multiple consoles, and database servers. With the exception of sensors, these components are commonly found in other IDS technologies. Sensors play a crucial role in monitoring and analyzing network activities. These network IDSs are usually distinct physical devices separate from the systems under surveillance, strategically located upstream on the network. Their design is intended to monitor multiple systems on a shared network. NIDSs often operate covertly, escaping detection by the systems under surveillance. This characteristic provides robust isolation and diminishes susceptibility to interference from potential attackers.

However, their effectiveness is constrained by a restricted view of the internal state of the monitored systems, presenting a potential challenge in the detection process [12]. NIDSs frequently experience a significant number of false negatives and false positives. Initially, many of these IDSs rely on signature-based detection techniques to identify known and straightforward attacks.

While network-based IDSs possess advanced capabilities for detecting attacks, they also come with limitations. For instance, they are unable to identify attacks occurring on encrypted network traffic and may struggle to perform comprehensive analysis during periods of high network traffic. Additionally, the use of stateful protocol analysis by IDS sensors introduces the possibility of certain events going undetected.
