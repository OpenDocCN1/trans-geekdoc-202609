# 11.1 Introduction

The modern communication world is inseparably connected to wireless networks. They are essential not only for mobile telephony but also for applications in the Internet of Things (IoT), industrial automation, and cloud computing. However, as in all other domains, the widespread use of wireless technologies has created new cybersecurity problems. Today, criminal hackers and hostile nation-states use more sophisticated methods to achieve better results in stealing and harming [1]. In the case of wireless networks, the basest of these methods involve sending large amounts of packets to deny service to legitimate users. Other methods, like eavesdropping (listening in), databases, and legwork, have also become more manageable for today’s cyber gangsters. Moreover, for today’s wireless networks, a more significant, faster, and more insatiable storm of packets means that the potential for severe harm increases. Deep learning (DL) is a subdomain of artificial intelligence (AI) that has emerged as a transformative technology for improving intrusion detection systems (IDSs) in wireless networks [2]. Rather than relying on the hand-crafted features that traditional machine learning models require, DL techniques automatically extract meaningful patterns from high-dimensional network traffic data. Intrusion detection tasks have been successfully solved using various deep learning architectures. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are great at detecting structural patterns in network traffic, while recurrent neural networks (RNNs) are good at capturing sequential attack patterns [3]. A third type of DL architecture, the long short-term memory (LSTM) network, is a specialized RNN that has shown even better performance than regular RNNs on some intrusion detection tasks.
