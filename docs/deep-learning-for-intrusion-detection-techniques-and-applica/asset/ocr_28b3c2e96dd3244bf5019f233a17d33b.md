For example, GANs can be used to generate synthetic attack scenarios, helping refine patching strategies. Automated patch generation not only reduces the time required for response but also alleviates the burden on human security teams, enabling them to focus on more complex tasks. This proactive approach to defense increases the resilience of systems, making them better equipped to withstand evolving threats. Despite its transformative potential, deploying DL for zero-day attack detection presents several challenges. One significant obstacle is the limited availability of labeled datasets specific to zero-day exploits. Supervised DL models rely heavily on annotated data, and the scarcity of labeled zero-day attack samples hinders model training. Additionally, the computational demands of DL models require substantial resources, making their deployment costly and complex. Another critical challenge is the lack of explainability in DL systems, often referred to as the “black-box” problem. This opacity limits trust and adoption in security-critical environments where transparency is paramount.

Looking to the future, advancements in federated learning and hybrid approaches offer promising solutions. Federated learning allows collaborative model training across organizations without compromising data privacy, enabling broader access to diverse training datasets. Hybrid models that combine DL with traditional detection methods can enhance robustness, leveraging the strengths of both approaches. Additionally, the development of explainable AI (XAI) frameworks will be crucial in making DL predictions more interpretable, fostering greater trust in automated security systems. In conclusion, DL represents a formidable tool in the fight against zero-day attacks. Its capacity for adaptive learning, sophisticated pattern recognition, and behavior-based detection provides a strong foundation for developing advanced cybersecurity systems. While challenges related to data scarcity, computational complexity, and explainability remain, ongoing research and innovation continue to push the boundaries of what DL can achieve. By embracing these technologies, the cybersecurity community can stay one step ahead in the ever-evolving battle against zero-day threats.
