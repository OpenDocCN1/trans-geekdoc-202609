Where:

+   - H(X) and H(Y) are the individual entropies of variables X and Y.
+   - H(X,Y) is the joint entropy of both variables.

If two features are independent, their MI is zero. However, if they are highly dependent, the MI score will be large, indicating that one feature provides substantial information about the other.

**MI in IDSs:** In IDSs, MI can be used to evaluate which features of the network traffic are most informative in distinguishing between normal and attack traffic. By selecting features with high MI with the class labels (normal or attack), IDSs can focus on the most relevant data, improving accuracy and efficiency. MI can also help identify redundant features, which do not contribute additional information and can be removed, thus reducing the complexity of the IDS model.

**Advantages of MI:**

+   - **Nonlinearity:** MI can capture both linear and nonlinear relationships between features, making it more versatile than linear methods like PCA and LDA.
+   - **Interpretability:** MI provides a clear understanding of how much information a feature shares with the target variable, which can help in interpreting the results of IDS models.

**Limitations of MI:**

+   - **Computational Complexity:** MI can be computationally expensive, especially for large datasets, as it requires the calculation of joint and marginal distributions.
+   - **Handling Continuous Data:** When dealing with continuous data, estimating the entropy and joint entropy accurately can be challenging, which may affect the performance of MI in high-dimensional IDS datasets.

# 7.2.4 Chi-Squared Feature Selection

The Chi-Squared (χ²) test is a statistical method used to evaluate the independence of two categorical variables. In feature selection, the Chi-Squared test is applied to determine whether there is a significant relationship between a feature and the target variable. Features that are highly dependent on the target are selected, while those with little or no relationship are discarded.
