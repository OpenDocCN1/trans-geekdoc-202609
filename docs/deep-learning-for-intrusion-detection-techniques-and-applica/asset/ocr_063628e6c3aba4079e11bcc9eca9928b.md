An ADC is responsible for the conversion of the sensor’s analog signal to electrical impulses or digital format for further processing and transmission.

2) Processing Unit: The processing unit is an essential component of the sensor node. The processor collects, manages tasks, and controls other components. The processing unit’s services are pre-programmed and loaded into the sensor nodes’ processors. The processor’s energy utilization rate changes based on node functionality.

3) Communication Unit: A common transceiver serves as a communication device and is primarily utilized for information transmission and reception between nodes and the base station, and vice versa. The communication unit is in four states: transmit, receive, idle, and sleep.

4) Power Unit: A power unit provides power to the node. As the sensor nodes are often battery-driven, energy conservation is a primary design concern.

Sink nodes, also known as base stations, are key parts of WSNs. They gather data from sensor nodes and send it to users or other systems for more study. Sink nodes have more power and computing skills than regular sensor nodes, letting them handle data well and link to external networks like the Internet. Depending on the network’s use and size, wireless tech such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or Zigbee is used to send data between the nodes in WSNs. To carry out smooth and effective working, middleware systems in WSNs facilitate handling the data complexity aggregation, processing, and routing. Further middleware systems support scalability and interoperability by abstracting the complexities of individual software and hardware.
