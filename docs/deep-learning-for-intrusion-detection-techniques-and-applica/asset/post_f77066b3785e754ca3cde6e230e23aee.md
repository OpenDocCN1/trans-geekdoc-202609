## 10.6.2 Use Cases of CNNs for DDoS Detection

CNNs identify DDoS attacks by analyzing network traffic data in several dimensions, such as time series or packet header data. Packet sizes, source/destination IP addresses, and traffic volume are encoded as matrices and sent to CNN layers. CNNs use convolution and pooling methods to extract hierarchical features that distinguish between legitimate and malicious data. In real-time systems, these models can accurately classify incoming traffic as benign or malicious in wireless networks [18]. Furthermore, the rapid inference speed of CNNs allows them to operate at scale, making them perfect for real-time applications where latency is critical. The main advantage of using CNNs for DDoS detection is their ability to generalize and adapt to various attack patterns, including zero-day attacks [5]. Their autonomous learning characteristic enables them to evolve with new attack techniques, providing a significant edge over traditional rule-based systems. Furthermore, implementing CNNs into real-world systems necessitates rigorous optimization to achieve low computational cost while retaining high throughput.

CNNs detect DDoS assaults by taking network traffic data as multidimensional inputs, such as packet header information or time series. Packet lengths, source/destination IP addresses, and traffic volumes are converted to matrices and supplied into the CNN layers. CNNs learn hierarchical features by applying convolution and pooling layers, with a focus on separating benign from malicious traffic. In real-time systems, these models can scan incoming traffic streams and accurately assess whether they are benign or malicious [6]. Furthermore, CNNs with fuzzy logic enhance inference speed, enable them to scale up, and detect malicious nodes; they can be employed in real-time applications where latency is an issue [19].
