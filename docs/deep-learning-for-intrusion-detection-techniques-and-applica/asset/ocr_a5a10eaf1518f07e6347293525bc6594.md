# 3.4 Applications of Deep Learning in Intrusion Detection

# 3.4.1 Types of Cyber Threats and Attacks

Deep learning-based IDSs have emerged as a powerful tool for identifying and mitigating a wide range of cyber threats and attacks. These systems leverage the ability of deep learning models to analyze vast amounts of data, recognize complex patterns, and detect anomalies that may indicate malicious activity. Below, we explore some of the most common types of cyber threats and how deep learning-based IDSs can address them.

# 3.4.1.1 DDoS Attacks

DDoS attacks are among the most prevalent and disruptive forms of cyberattacks. In a DDoS attack, an attacker overwhelms a target system, such as a website or network, with a flood of traffic, rendering it unavailable to legitimate users. Traditional IDSs often struggle to distinguish between legitimate traffic spikes and malicious DDoS traffic. Deep learning-based IDSs, however, can analyze traffic patterns in real time and identify abnormal spikes that deviate from normal behavior. By leveraging models such as CNNs and RNNs, these systems can detect subtle patterns indicative of DDoS attacks, even when the attack traffic is designed to mimic legitimate traffic [51].

# 3.4.1.2 Malware and Ransomware

Malware and ransomware are malicious software programs designed to infiltrate, damage, or gain unauthorized access to systems. Ransomware, in particular, encrypts a victim’s data and demands payment for its release. Traditional signature-based detection methods are often ineffective against new or evolving malware variants. Deep learning-based IDSs, on the other hand, can analyze the structure and behavior of executable files to identify hidden patterns associated with malware. For instance, deep learning models such as CNNs can process binary files and detect anomalies that suggest malicious intent. This capability is particularly valuable for identifying zero-day malware, which has no known signature [52].
