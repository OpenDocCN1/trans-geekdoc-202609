2) Processing Unit: The processing unit is an essential component of the sensor node. The processor collects, manages tasks, and controls other components. The processing unit’s services are pre-programmed and loaded into the sensor nodes’ processors. The processor’s energy utilization rate changes based on node functionality.

3) Communication Unit: A common transceiver serves as a communication device and is primarily utilized for information transmission and reception between nodes and the base station, and vice versa. The communication unit is in four states: transmit, receive, idle, and sleep.

4) Power Unit: A power unit provides power to the node. As the sensor nodes are often battery-driven, energy conservation is a primary design concern.

Sink nodes, also known as base stations, are key parts of WSNs. They gather data from sensor nodes and send it to users or other systems for more study. Sink nodes have more power and computing skills than regular sensor nodes, letting them handle data well and link to external networks like the Internet. Depending on the network’s use and size, wireless tech such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or Zigbee is used to send data between the nodes in WSNs. To carry out smooth and effective working, middleware systems in WSNs facilitate handling the data complexity aggregation, processing, and routing. Further middleware systems support scalability and interoperability by abstracting the complexities of individual software and hardware.

Deployment of sensor nodes in the target area represents the first of several coordinated steps forming the operation of a WSN. Sensor nodes can be dispersed randomly or intentionally, depending on an application. Having placed the nodes, they start monitoring the ambient factors identified motion, vibrations, and changes in temperature. Sensors on each node convert physical phenomena into electrical signals, which are then converted into digital numbers by the ADCs. For this raw data, preliminary processing is carried out in the node to cut communication overhead and redundant information. This processed data can be sent over single-hop or multi-hop toward the sink node. In the case of single-hop communication, data directly moves from the sensor node to the sink node. But in the case of multi-hop communication, where nodes are vast in number for large-scale networks and may be located far away from the sink, it implies intermediate nodes forward the data. WSN routing protocols, like Geographical Adaptive Fidelity (GAF) and Low-Energy Adaptive Clustering Hierarchy (LEACH), are developed to provide energy efficiency and low latency while ensuring delivery. This is because sensor nodes often work with less battery and are difficult to replace or recharge for a time period in many applications. The first priority is energy efficiency. Aggregated data from multiple sensor nodes is processed and transmitted outside to other systems to be further scrutinized at the sink node. Advanced WSNs can use cloud-based platforms and machine learning (ML) algorithms to analyze and make decisions in real time. WSNs operate in domains that may pose challenges like interference, physical obstructions, and extreme weather. High network architecture and flexible communication schemes are required for these challenges to be met. In addition, WSNs are susceptible to attacks such as DoS, data manipulation, and eavesdropping, making security a significant issue.

## 10.2 The Importance of Security in Wireless Networks

Wireless networks are essential parts of modern communication systems and form the base for applications in healthcare, industry, defense, smart cities, and the IoT. These networks minimize the need for physical infrastructure, making them more flexible, scalable, and cost-effective. However, the same characteristics that make wireless networks attractive such as their reliance on open communication channels, decentralized architecture, and mobility also make them susceptible to a variety of security risks. The security of the wireless networks is of paramount importance for the following reasons:

- 1) **Protection of Sensitive Data:** Personal, financial, or government information is often conducted over wireless networks. In the absence of adequate protection, such data may be intercepted, stolen, or changed in ways that violate privacy and confidentiality.
- 2) **Insecure Medium:** The distributed nature of wireless communication makes it vulnerable to eavesdropping, interference, and signal jamming. Unlike wired networks, which may be controlled physically, the wireless network is based on radio waves and, therefore, the first preference of attackers.
- 3) **No Centralized Control:** A wireless network lacks centralized control, and hence requires strong algorithms for routing, security, and power optimization using wireless communication technologies like Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, etc. The management of power is essential as the devices are often battery-driven and smart routing assures low energy use.
- 4) **Decentralized Nature:** Decentralized architectures exist in networks like MANETs and WSNs, where the existence of a permanent architecture is not required. Due to this decentralization, nodes need to collaborate to pass data. This results in the disruption of transmission or damage to network operation by mischievous nodes.
- 5) **Dynamic Topology:** Nodes in MANETs join, exit, and change locations within the network on a regular basis. Because of this dynamic architecture, it is more difficult to build static security measures, hence resilient and adaptable safety measures are required.
- 6) **Modern Technology and IoT Integration:** Because the majority of IoT devices do not have robust security features, the number of wireless network devices has grown, expanding the attack surface. Smart cities, smart homes, and industrial security are based on securing these intricate networks.
- 7) **Accessibility and Reliability:** Security is necessary to make sure network services are always accessible and reliable. For example, denial-of-service (DoS) attacks, eavesdropping, and packet tampering attacks, can render a network useless, resulting in severe disruptions and damage
