
# Applicability of Blockchain in Social Media Platforms

Applicability of blockchain is massive in social media platforms; thereby, businesses can generate real value. The proposed value proposition model will help the social media industry to take a leap in digital transformation. In this chapter, readers will get to know how to utilize the blockchain value proposition for transforming social media business models.

## Introduction

The social media business model helps organizations discover ways by which they can derive and generate value through social media tools and platforms, which was not possible otherwise. Typically, two types of organizations utilize social media models. Firstly, those organizations that own and manage the social media platforms and tools. Secondly, those who use them to share, communicate, and collaborate within and outside the organizations. The business model helps the business navigate their value to prospects, customers, partners, and stakeholders (Wirtz, 2011; Zott, Raphael, & Massa, 2011). The business model covers the constructs of the business rather than the strategy that directs the implementation and action plans. The constructs help streamline the whole business vision and align the business goals. Although business models have been around for ages, people have used varied terms and constructs to define them. For example, Morris, Schindehutte, and Allen (2005) suggested 24 different elements contributing to business models after studying 19 definitions of business models. However, the more common elements widely used are value proposition, target market, customer relationship, infrastructure, and economic model. The business model should at the bare minimum cover value proposition, value creation and provision, as well as financial endurance (Wielki, 2010).

## Identity

Identity is associated with the individual to distinguish one from the other. Several pieces of information contribute to the identification of an individual in the social media setting. These can include name, age, sex, location, and additional critical information by which one's identity is revealed. A research study (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2010) has disclosed that users knowingly or unknowingly, through conscious and unconscious efforts, reveal subjective information such as behavior traits, thoughts, feelings, and emotions. However, to tackle such issues, users have a different set of preferences and features provided by social media sites and applications to manage their identities.

The identity threat is the primary threat associated with social media platforms. Manipulating information and influencing the social network by the use of fake IDs are becoming common. There exist many platforms to work around people setting up their IDs (personal profiles). For example, `Facebook` allows users above a certain age to create a self-profile before they become part of the social network. They also get various controls on the way they share their posts or messages. Keeping several IDs to work with multiple social media platforms is daunting, and thus secondary ID services like `DandyID` are becoming famous, providing one solution to prevent and manage all IDs securely.

Moreover, social media users are now promoting social media user profiles through services like `Retaggr`. It facilitates them to showcase their different profiles and encourage others to follow them. As identity is core to social media platforms, organizations have concerns about the privacy and security of its presence and getting engaged with other sites and applications. Several lawsuits have been filed and initiated in violation of privacy related to social media sites (Kravets, 2010). The social community has identified a few strategies, for example, real identity vs. virtual identity, to work with, which help them establish self-promotion (e.g., `Facebook`) or self-branding (e.g., `LinkedIn`). Specific sites provide anonymity to users considering the critical context of involvement in the community. For example, `Divorce360` includes help and support for the social group going through the phase of divorce, and thus, people participate in discussions when anonymity is addressed. Several technological advances have also been in progress, such as the development of the `OAuth` protocol (Hammer-Lahav, 2007). By the `OAuth` protocol, an individual can give access to information without revealing identity information. For example, `Twitter` provides use of the `OAuth` protocol by sharing several tokens and keys so that an individual can handshake to utilize the `Twitter` application and work with `Twitter` data.

> **Note:** Social media identity management considering the balance between sharing identities and protecting privacy is crucial, and thus a solution promising such a balance mix is required.
