The existing deep learning frameworks demonstrate the defects of convolutional algorithms mainly in terms of calculation speed. The majority of them mainly remain in 1D or 2D geometries, and they are always circumscribed to settle a specific inversion task (i.e., temperature-dependent or space-dependent case). In practical scenarios, the IHCP problems are intricate in geometry shapes and thermal properties, thus it is inconvenient to build a network for each situation. Moreover, these frameworks are highly dependent on commercial data collection at the forward pass. To this end, it is imperative to establish a DL network to work independently of commercial software.

In this chapter, a deep learning framework is erected to retrieve the thermal conductivity of an industrial product through temperature measurement at the external surface. It is noted that the entire process of data collection, training, and testing are merely based on neural networks while the validation phase of the proposed framework is capable of recovering the space, temperature or time-dependent thermal conductivity. First of all, abundant training data is constructed by the physics-informed neural network (PINN). To simulate the measurement error, Gaussian noise is added to the calculated temperature. To augment the noise, a U-net is introduced to improve the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). The output, which is the denoised signal, is ultimately sent to the nonlinear mapping module to retrieve the thermal conductivity. The developed framework only requires one round training and then can be utilized for online prediction.

## 4.3 Physical Model and Data Generation

### 4.3.1 2D Heat Conductivity

The heat conductivity problem in 2D cases can be written as

$$\rho C_p \frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial k}{\partial x}\frac{\partial T}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial k}{\partial y}\frac{\partial T}{\partial y} + k\nabla^2 T + P \quad (4.18)$$

where $\rho$, $C_p$, and $k$ are density, heat capacity, and thermal conductivity, respectively. As presented in Figure 4.5, the domain is a square whose side length is 1 m.

### 4.3.2 3D Heat Conduction

The physical model of 3D in this section is an alloy, which is made up of cuboid and quadrangle. As for the top, a cavity is truncated by an ellipsoid. To predict the problem, here, the heat conduction equation in the model is depicted as

$$\rho C_p \frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = \nabla\cdot(k\nabla T) + P \quad (4.20)$$

All the surfaces of the model comply with convective boundary conditions:

$$-k\frac{\partial T}{\partial n} = h(T - T_0) \quad (4.21)$$

### 4.3.3 Data Generation

Since the inversion framework is data-driven, it is requisite to acquire abundant specimens for training, whose target is to acquire the temperature distribution through thermal conductivity. For the 2D problem, the forward solver is on COMSOL and a stochastic thermal conductivity producer on MATLAB. The output temperature acts as the input of the inversion framework and the generated conductivity is treated as the ground truth of the network. It is observed that the total data sets include 4000 samples, where 2000 have spatial-dependent thermal conductivity while the remaining have temperature-dependent.

As for the 3D scenarios, this chapter has modest innovations. The existing generation approaches largely depend on commercial software (i.e., Ansys, COMSOL) or conventional numerical algorithms (FEM, FVM), which need mesh designs and cumbersome computations. However, it is not realistic to spend excess calculation time and resources in the forward procedure, and therefore it is essential to present an efficient method for data generation.
