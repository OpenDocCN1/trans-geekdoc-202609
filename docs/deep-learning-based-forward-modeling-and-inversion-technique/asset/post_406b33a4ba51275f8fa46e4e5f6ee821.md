
# Deep Learning-Based Forward Modeling and Inversion Techniques for Computational Physics Problems

Yinpeng Wang
Qiang Ren

![](img/020125e0e698f9771fbbd32f2ace1e80_0_0.png)

This book investigates in detail the emerging deep learning (DL) technique in computational physics, assessing its promising potential to substitute conventional numerical solvers for calculating the fields in real-time. After good training, the proposed architecture can resolve both the forward computing and the inverse retrieve problems.

Pursuing a holistic perspective, the book includes the following areas. The first chapter discusses the basic DL frameworks, the heat condition problem is solved by the classical U-net in Chapter 2, involving both the passive and active solvers. Afterwards, the sophisticated heat flux on a curved surface is resolved by the presented physics-informed DL structure using Conv-LSTM, exhibiting high accuracy and efficiency. Additionally, a physics-informed DL structure with a nonlinear mapping module is employed to obtain the space/temperature/time-related conductivity via the transient temperature in Chapter 4. Finally, in Chapter 5, a series of the latest frameworks and the corresponding applications are introduced to computational physics.

As deep learning are experiencing vigorous development in computational physics, more people desire related reading materials. This book is intended for graduate students, professional practitioners, and researchers need DL for computational physics.

Yinpeng Wang received the B.S. degree in Electronic and Information Engineering from Beihang University, Beijing, China in 2020, where he is currently pursuing his M.S. degree in Electronic Science and Technology. Mr. Wang focuses on the research of electromagnetic scattering, inverse scattering, heat transfer, computational multi-physics, and deep learning.

Qiang Ren received the B.S. and M.S. degrees in both electrical engineering from Beihang University, Beijing, China and Institute of Acoustics, Beijing, China in 2008, respectively, and the PhD degree in Electrical Engineering from Duke University, Durham, NC, in 2015. From 2016 to 2017, he was a postdoctoral researcher with the Computational Electromagnetics and Antennes Research Laboratory (CEARL) of the Pennsylvania State University, University Park, PA. In September 2017, he joined the School of Electronics and Information Engineering, Beihang University as an “Excellent Hundred” Associate Professor.
