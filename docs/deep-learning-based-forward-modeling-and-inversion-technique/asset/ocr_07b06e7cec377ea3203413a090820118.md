Edalitar et al. [30] employ a deep learning network based on a convolutional neural network to solve the two-dimensional steady-state evolution problems. The model is presented in Figure 2.3 (a), which consists of three convolution layers, one bottleneck layer, and three deconvolution layers. The input of the framework contains two parts, which characterize the geometry and boundary conditions, respectively. Figure 2.3 (b) displays the input boundary condition, representing the temperature value on the boundary. Figure 2.3 (c) shows the input geometry, where 1, 0.5, and 0 indicate the solution domain, the boundary, and the empty area. Figures 2.3 (d) and (e) exhibit the temperature distribution calculated by the finite volume method, the distribution predicted by the network, and the error between the two. It can be concluded that the network can predict the temperature field distribution with high precision. Besides, the author also compares two error functions (mean square error and maximum mean square error) in the training processes and explains their respective advantages.
