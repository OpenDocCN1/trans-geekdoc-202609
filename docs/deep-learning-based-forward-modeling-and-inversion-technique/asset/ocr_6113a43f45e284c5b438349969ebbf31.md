# 5.1.2 Convolutional-Based PINN

Recently, the convolutional PINN [24, 25] is progressively becoming prevalent for the merits of parameter sharing kernels, spatial feature extractions, and light network weights. Diverse from the FCNN based PINN, the CNN based framework does not fit differential operators, but approximate differential to difference. In a sense, it is similar to the finite difference method. Compared with PINN, the CNN architecture has smaller volume and better generalization performance.

The CNN based PINN consists of two parts, and the parameters of the corresponding kernel are trainable and fixed, respectively. The trainable convolution kernel is the same as the traditional supervised learning, while the fixed convolution kernel is used to calculate the PDE solution.
