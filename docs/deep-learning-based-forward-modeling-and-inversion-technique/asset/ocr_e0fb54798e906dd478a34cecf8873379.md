# 1.1.2 Monte Carlo Method

The second method introduced here is the Monte Carlo method [12, 17, 18], which is also called the random sampling method. The algorithm makes use the random number generated by the computer for statistical experiments and takes the statistical characteristics of the equation as the mean value and probability as the numerical solution of the equation. In recent years, the development of computer technology, the Monte Carlo algorithm has been widely used in the field of computational physics. Although with classical numerical methods such as the finite element method and finite difference method other methods cannot replace. For example, this method can solve the value of any point in the region independently without solving on the basis of other solved points, so it can realize fast parallel computing.

When applying the Monte Carlo method to solve partial differential equations, it is usually necessary to establish a probability model to obtain the probability of an event through numerical simulation, so as to obtain the numerical solution of the point. This chapter will take the steady-heat conduction equation as an example to introduce the process of using Monte Carlo simulation to solve the PDE approximate solution.
