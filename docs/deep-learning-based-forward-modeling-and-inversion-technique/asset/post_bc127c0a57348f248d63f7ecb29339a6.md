# 4 Reconstruction of Thermophysical Parameters Based on Deep Learning

## 4.1 Introduction

The inversion of thermophysical parameters is a typical representative of inverse heat conduction problems (IHCP), which has a broad application prospect in scientific and engineering problems such as nondestructive testing, materials processing, geometry optimization, biomedical detection, architectural design, food technology and so on. During the inversion procedure, the thermal parameters in the entire domain are determined by the measured boundary physical quantities. However, due to high nonlinear and ill condition, the reconstruction of the thermal parameters is a herculean task. In this chapter, the workflow of utilizing deep learning techniques to retrieve thermophysical parameters is exhibited. In the beginning, several conventional algorithms together with their characteristics will be introduced. Next, the recent research status with regard to applying DL techniques to reconstruct the thermal conductivity are discussed in the following part. Finally, the analyses of the DL architecture is presented in the last part.

## 4.1 Physical Foundation

The mechanisms behind heat conduction are quite different among various materials, hence it is arduous to derive from first principles. thermal conduction generally occurs when the molecules collide. For dilute monatomic gas, a basic expression can be formulated

```
$k = \beta\rho C_v \frac{2k_B T}{\pi m}$$
```

Where $\beta$, $k_B$, $\lambda$, $C_v$ and $m$ are the coefficient, Boltzmann constant, mean free path, specific heat, and molecular mass, respectively. It is worth noting that this formula is merely applicable to low-pressure gases since the interparticle attractions in real gases are ignored. The heat conduction mechanisms in liquids are intricate and lacks accurate theoretical formula. For metals at low temperature, the heat conduction process is carried out by free electrons where the thermal conductivity can be formulated as

With the temperature increases, the mean path is restricted by phonons, thus the thermal conductivity tends to reduce. In fact, the thermal conductivity $k$ and the electrical conductivity $\sigma$ has the following:

$L = \frac{k^2}{\sigma} = 2.44 \times 10^{-8} W\Omega K^{-1}$ (5)

Where $e$ is the charge of an electron. For non-metals, the heat transfer is accomplished through the vibrations of the lattice (or other words). The thermal conductivity of these materials lacks appropriate theory, hence has to be measured. Broadly speaking, the experimental approaches to determine the thermal conductivity can be divided into two categories, namely the steady-state and transient ones. For steady-state methods, the thermal conductivity can be inferred from measurements of the steady state, while transient techniques rely on temperature history. It is observed that the steady-state methods do not demand sophisticated signal analysis, while the-the process of obtaining thermal conductivity by experiment generally requires the inverse problem of heat transfer. However, in most practical scenarios, the thermal properties (including thermal conductivity and heat capacity) relate on space, temperature or time. Consequently, the differential equation of heat tends to be nonlinear. Besides, the parameters in the whole domain are determined by the boundary values, the task possesses the characteristic of highly underdetermined. In fact, the nonlinearity and ill condition are the two major difficulties encountered in the reconstruction of the thermal properties. Therefore, it is laborious to gain an accurate solution in the HCICP for long years. ## 4.2 Progress in Inversion Research

### 4.2.1 Gradient-Based Methods

The Gradient-Based Methods have the advantages of high accuracy and fast convergence. However, they are computationally cumbersome and the optimization results are unstable, which is possible to fall into the local minimum.

- Yang [1] employed the Newton-Raphson method to solve the 1D thermal property HCs and realized fast convergence.
- Safaw et al. [2] adopted the Levenberg Marquardt method to evaluate the linear temperature-related thermal properties and the conductivity and capacity.
- Telejko [3] employed theroy-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shanno (BFGS) method with the finite element method (FEM) to invert the thermal conductivity whereas the above researches are all about temperature-related conductivities.
- Huang et al. [4] exploited the conjugate gradient method (CGM) in reconstructing the thermal conductivity reality was validated by experiments and simulations.
- Tovaren et al. [5] utilized a Gaussian-Newton method with the Bayesian algorithm to achieve the reconstruction of space-related thermal properties via experimental data.
- Yang et al. [6] applied an amended CGM to extract the thermal conductivity in time-domain heterogeneous scenarios through boundary element method (BEM).
