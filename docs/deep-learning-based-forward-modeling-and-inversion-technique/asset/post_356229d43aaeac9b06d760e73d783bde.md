## 4.5 Inversion Process

After removing the noise, the time profile is utilized to recover the thermal conductivity. In fact, the inversion problem is treated as an optimization task whose target $F(k)$

$F(k) = \sum_{i=1}^{N_d} \sum_{j=1}^{N_t} |T_j(k) - T_{mea}\|^2. (4.34)$

where $N_d$ and $N_t$ are number of space and time domain points. $T_j(k)$ and $T_{mea}$ indicate the computed temperature and the measured temperature, respectively. It is feasible to acquire the best estimation of $k$ by iterations:

$k^{(n+1)} = k^{(n)}+\beta(n). (4.35)$

$p(n) = \nabla F(n) + \gamma(n-1). (4.36)$

where $\beta, \gamma, P$ are the step size, updating coefficient and searching direction, respectively. The superscript $(n)$ indicates the iteration times. However, implementing the iteration algorithm to obtain a stable convergence needs a lot of computing resources and time. Therefore, it is urgent to bring out a substitution approach.
