Here, the method of combining the supervisory loss and PDE loss is used to speed up the convergence of the network. The input of the network consists of two parts, where one is the dielectric constant of the scatterer, the other is the information of the incident wave. The output of the network also contains two parts, namely, the real part and the imaginary part. In the process of electromagnetic wave propagation, the electric field in the passive area satisfies

> $\nabla^2\mathbf{E} + k^2\mathbf{E} = 0$ (5.23)

The two-dimensional Laplace operator can be replaced by the convolution kernel in Table 5.1, and the wave number $k$ is defined

> $k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda_0}\sqrt{\epsilon_r}$ (5.24)

where $\lambda_0$ is the wavelength in vacuum and $\epsilon_r$ is the permittivity of the scatterer. Figure 5.5 shows an example of random selection in the test set. The scatterer is a nano triangle whose real and imaginary part of the permittivity is 4.19 and 4.74. The incident wave is a TE wave propagating along the $x$-axis, with a wavelength of 37.42 nm. Besides, the traditional algorithm finite difference frequency domain (FDFD) is employed to calculate the scattering problem, whose results are regarded as the ground truth. It is lucid that the predicted results by the framework is consistent with the ground truth.
