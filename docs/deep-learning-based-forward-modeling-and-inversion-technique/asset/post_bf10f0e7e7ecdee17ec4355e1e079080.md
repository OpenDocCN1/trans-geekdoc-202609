### 4.3.3.1 The Architecture of the PINN and Its Loss Functions

Figure 4.7 displayed the detailed structure of the proposed framework, which is based on a fully connected neural network (FCNN). The input is a tensor in $\mathbb{R}^4$, representing the points $x, y, z$ and $t$. The output denotes the temperature $T(x, y, z, t)$ at the corresponding points. Between the input and output layers, there are hidden layers. For sampling points from the inner solving domain, the boundary conditions and initial conditions are necessary. The numbers of them are $N_f, N_s$ and $N_t$, respectively. In addition, for interior or boundary points, the thermal conductivity $k$, its gradient $\nabla T$ and the unit normal vector $n$ at the surface are also required in computing the loss function.

In this section, operators are introduced to simplify the expressions. Suppose that $\mathcal{L}_t$ is a first-order differential operator

$$\mathcal{L}_tT = n \cdot (k\nabla T) + h(T - T_0).$$

As $n \cdot (k\nabla T) = k\partial T/\partial n$, Equation 4.22 and Equation 21 are invariant.

### 4.3.4 Deep Learning-Based Forward Modeling and Inversion Techniques

Let $L_a$ be a second-order differential operator

$$L_a T = \nabla \cdot (k\nabla T) - \rho C_p \frac{\partial T}{\partial t} + P. \quad (23)$$

The heat conduction equation can be expressed as

$$L_a T(\mathbf{r}; t) = 0, \quad r \in \Omega, \text{ and } t \geq 0, \quad (24)$$

The Robin boundary condition and the initial condition can be rewritten

$$L_b T(\mathbf{r}; t) = 0, \quad r \in \partial\Omega, \text{ and } t \geq 0, \quad (25)$$

$$T(\mathbf{r}; 0) = T_0(\mathbf{r}), \quad r \in \Omega, \text{ and } t = 0. \quad (26)$$

The loss function is crucial in utilizing the PINN to solve the PDE, an exquisitely loss function not only promotes training speed but also yields more precise convergence. However, due to the poor robustness of $L_2$ loss, outliers caused by second-order differential terms have a negative impact on network training. Therefore it is imperative to introduce a balance term to curb the influence of outliers. Here, the $L_{\infty}$ norm is used to guarantee a more stable training process. Consequently, the final function $F(T)$ can be described as

$$F(T) = \lambda_1 ||L_a T||_2^2 + \lambda_2 ||L_a T||_{\infty} + \mu ||L_b T||_2^2 + \eta ||T - T_0||_2^2 \quad (27)$$

where $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \mu$ and $\eta$ are variables. It is noted that the first two terms enforce the physics with the PDE while the third term $L_b T$ and the fourth term $||T - T_0||_2$ implement the boundary and initial condition. In order to control the loss, heat conduction models have conductivity $k$ and its derivatives $k_x, k_y$ and $k_z$ directly fed to the framework participating in computation. The established PINN solver is enabled to generate abundant data for training and testing. In this experiment, heat conduction models have the same geometries and heat distributions. The data generation process is carried out with PyTorch [25] and the training is conducted on an NVIDIA RTX 2080 Ti graphic card with a Dell 7920 workstation. The learning rate, suitable optimization and activation function are critical. It is remarked that L-BFGS [26] can seek a good solution with fewer iterations than Adam [27] for smooth PDEs. However, for stiff solutions L-BFGS tends to be stuck in local minima. Besides, it is also observed that the acceleration of training can be realized by employing a self-adaptive activation function.

### 4.3.3.2 Comparison with Commercial Software

During the data generation process, it is crucial to ensure the quality of the developed PINN solver. Therefore, the computed results are supposed to compare with the commercial software COMSOL. To this end, the PINN and COMSOL are applied considering the same forward heat conduction problem. One of the comparisons is presented here. Here, (a) exhibits the thermal conductivity distribution, while (b) and (c) demonstrate the surface temperature distribution obtained by COMSOL Multiphysics and PINN and (d) emerges the deviation of the two methods. It is listed that the numerical results calculated by the PINN agree well with COMSOL. To quantitatively depict the precision of the PINN, the average relative error is defined as

$$Err = \frac{1}{MN} \sum_{i=1}^{M} \sum_{j=1}^{N} \frac{|TPINN(i,j)-COMSOL(i,j)|}{COMSOL(i,j)} \quad (28)$$

where $M$ and $N$ are the sampling points from the spatial and temporal domain. In fact, the average relative error of the three listed examples is 3.79%, 2.83%, and 3.05%. From both the qualitative and quantitative perspective, it can be summarized that the constructed PINN can be confidently employed to produce sufficient training data for future investigations. The whole dataset generated by PINN contains 18000 specimens, including 6000 for space-related, 6000 for temperature-related, and 6000 for time-related thermal conductivity.

## 4.4 Denoising Process

In the inversion process, the temperature measurement obtained for reconstruction are obtained by probers on the external surface of the object. As the temperature rises, thermal noises of electronic components will result in detrimental impacts on the results. Therefore, the accuracy for the inversion cannot be ensured. In this experiment, the receiver error is simplified to Gaussian noise, which will be attenuated by the denoising model to improve the input of the inversion network and the reconstruction accuracy. Here, the denoising process can be measured by a mathematical mapping

$$\mathcal{S} : y \rightarrow x$$

### 4.4.1 Conventional Denoising Approach

Nowadays, a large number of algorithms have been developed to remove noise, including singular value decomposition, wavelet transform, and so on. Taking the singular value decomposition as an example, it first decomposes the $m \times n$ noisy image into the product form of three matrices:

$$Y = U \begin{pmatrix} \Sigma & O \\ O & O \end{pmatrix} V^H$$

where $U$ and $V$ are orthogonal matrices, and $\Sigma$ is a diagonal matrix whose diagonal elements are $r$.

### 4.4.2 Deep Denoising Framework

In recent years, with the vigorous development of deep learning technology, denoising algorithms based on convolutional networks are becoming in the ascendant. As researchers use convolution-based networks to achieve the function of denoising, because it can fully extract the information of each point in space.

$$y = x + n$$

where $n$ is additive Gaussian noise.
