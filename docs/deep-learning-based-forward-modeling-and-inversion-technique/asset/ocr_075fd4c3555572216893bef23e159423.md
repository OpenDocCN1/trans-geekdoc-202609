Let $L_a$ be a second-order differential operator

```
$L_a T = 
abla \cdot (k 
abla T) - ho C_p \frac{\partial T}{\partial t} + P. \quad (4.23)
```

The heat conduction equation can be expressed as

```
$L_a T(\mathbf{r}; t) = 0, \quad r \in \Omega \text{ and } t \geq 0, \quad (4.24)
```

The Robin boundary condition and the initial condition can be rewritten

```
$L_b T(\mathbf{r}; t) = 0, \quad r \in \partial\Omega \text{ and } t \geq 0, \quad (4.25)$
```

```
$T(\mathbf{r}; 0) = T_0(\mathbf{r}), \quad r \in \Omega \text{ and } t = 0. \quad (4.26)
```

The loss function is crucial in utilizing the PINN to solve the PDE, an exquisitely devised loss function not only promote the training speed, but also yield a more precise convergence value. However, due to the poor robustness of the extensively $L_2$ loss, outliers caused by second-order differential terms have a negative impact on network training. Therefore, it is imperative to introduce a balance term to curb the influence of outliers. Here, the $L_{\infty}$ norm is used to guarantee a more stable training process. Consequently, the final function $F(T)$ can be described as

```
$F(T) = \lambda_1 ||L_a T||_2^2 + \lambda_2 ||L_a T||_{\infty} + \mu ||L_b T||_2^2 + \eta ||T - T_0||_2^2 \quad (4.27)
```

where $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \mu$ and $\eta$ are variables. It is noted that the first two terms enforce the physics to accord with the PDE while the third term $L_b T$ and the fourth term $||T - T_0||_2$ implement the boundary and initial condition. In order to control the loss, the heat conduction models have the conductivity $k$ and its differential $k_x, k_y$ and $k_z$ are directly fed to the framework which does not participate in the deactivation computation.

The established PINN solver is enabled to generate abundant data for training and testing. In this experiment, the heat conduction models have the same geometries and heat density distributions. The data generation process is carried out with PyTorch [25] and the training is conducted on an NVIDIA RTX 2080 Ti graphic card with a workstation of Dell 7920. The learning rate, suitable optimization and activation function are critical. It is remarked that L-BFGS [26] can seek out a good solution with fewer iterations than Adam [27] for smooth PDEs [28], for L-BFGS adopts second-order derivatives while Adam relies on first-order derivatives. However, for stiff solutions L-BFGS tends to be stuck in local minima. Besides, it is also observed that the acceleration of training can be realized by employing a self-adaptive activation function.
