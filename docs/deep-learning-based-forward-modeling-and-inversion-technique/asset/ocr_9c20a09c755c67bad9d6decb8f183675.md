# 4.2.3 Deep Learning Approach

With the development of learning techniques, computational physics has also experienced gigantic strides. After being well trained, the surrogate framework can extract the intrinsic characteristics of practical scenarios. In the field of computational electromagnetics, Wei et al. [13] developed a deep network to retrieve the dielectric constant of unknown scatterers. The proposed model can reduce the solving time to 1 s with considerable accuracy. In the realm of geoscience, Li et al. [14] exploited a fresh algorithm on the basis of an end-to-end network to recover the velocity immediately from seismic data. The presented DNNs achieve achievable precision on the velocity distributions. For the inverse heat conduction problems (HCP), [15, 16] first introduce the Convolutional-LSTM to invert the time-domain heat flux through the temperature. The developed heat conduction models are to be applied with 3D sophisticated structures with radiative conditions and nonlinear parameters.

As for the reconstruction of thermal properties, in 1999, Glorieux et al. [17] implemented neural networks to retrieve the 1D thermal conductivity profile via the time-domain data. As shown in Figure 4.2 (b), the proposed framework is a dedicated fully connected network consisting of three input neurons with hyperbolic tangent activation functions and an output neuron with linear activation. During the training process, the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm is employed to optimize the loss function, which is defined as the root square error.
