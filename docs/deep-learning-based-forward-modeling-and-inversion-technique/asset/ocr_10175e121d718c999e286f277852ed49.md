# 4.3.3.1 The Architecture of the PINN and Its Loss Functions

Figure 4.7 displayed the detailed structure of the proposed framework, which is based on a fully connected neural network (FCNN). The input is a tensor in $\mathbb{R}^4$, representing the points $x, y, z$ and $t$. The output denotes the temperature $T(x, y, z, t)$ at the corresponding points. Between the input and output layers, there are hidden layers. For sampling points from the inner solving domain, the boundary conditions and initial conditions are necessary. The numbers of them are $N_f, N_s$ and $N_t$, respectively. In addition, for interior or boundary points, the thermal conductivity $k$, its gradient $
abla T$ and the unit normal vector $n$ at the surface are also required in computing the loss function.

```
$\mathcal{L}_tT = n \cdot (k\nabla T) + h(T - T_0).$
```
