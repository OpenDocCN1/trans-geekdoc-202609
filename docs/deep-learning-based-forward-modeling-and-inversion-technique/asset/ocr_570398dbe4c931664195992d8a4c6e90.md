In this section, the denoising ability of the framework is evaluated via numerical analysis. Here a total of $N_d=400$ detectors are evenly placed on the eight profile surfaces, each of which receives the transient temperature of 10s with $N_t=1000$ points. It can be observed that the input and output of the framework are matrices with the dimension of $N_d \times N_t$. To intuitively demonstrate the denoising performance, three examples randomly selected from the testing data are exhibited in Figure 4.12. Here, 14400 temperature sequences with Gaussian noise are employed to train the model, while the remaining 3600 are used for testing.

To quantify the denoising capability of the network, the peak signal to noise ratio (PSNR) [37] with the structural similarity (SSIM) [38] are extensively adopted in the digital image processing as introduced. The calculated values of the two indicators in the input and output image are displayed in Table 4.1. In order to make the calculation reasonable, the matrix elements are mapped to 0-255.
