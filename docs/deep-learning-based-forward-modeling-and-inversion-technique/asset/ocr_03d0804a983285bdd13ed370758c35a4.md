Accordingly, the operator learning architecture is developed, which surmounts the imperfection of the PINN and emerges a pervasive application prospect.

The deep learning technique appears as a reformative approach with remarkable performance in scientific computing, but it is never an obscure and prohibitive method to be employed in various areas. This book is here to furnish comprehensive insights and practical guidance for pertinent research to establish a complicated numeric solver using deep learning networks. The intended audience contains researchers interested in implementing machine learning techniques in the field of computational physics, particularly forward and inverse problems. To start with, the first chapter provides a detailed introduction to DL frameworks, as the prevails connected network, the convolutional network, the recurrent network, the generative adversarial network, and the graph network. After that, the paradigm for employing the deep learning mechanism to settle computational physics mission is discussed, involving data physical constraints, operator learning, and DL-traditional fusion methods. The original and concrete experimental results are demonstrated in how the complete procedure can be done to set up the DL-based solver. Chapter 2, the steady-state forward heat conductivity problem is handled by the classical U-net, including the passive and active scenarios. Chapter 3, the emerging conLSTM architecture is used to reconstruct the surface heat flux of curved surface. Chapter 4, a physics-informed neural network (PINN) and a feedback mechanism are adopted to reconstruct the intricate thermal conductivity. Ultimately in Chapter 5, several of the latest advanced network structures are investigated, consisting of the application of PINN in generalized coordinates, the living example of graph neural networks in solving electrostatic fields, and the instance of coupled Fourier networks in tackling multiphysics field problems.

Employing the deep learning technique in the modeling of computational physics is stepwise from a spark of practice into a mainstream trend, where physicists, mathematicians, and algorithm gather together to tackle multiscale and multiphysics problems. Considering this background, the authors anticipate that all the readers will benefit a lot from this

book.
