```
$\mathcal{L} = \alpha\mathcal{L}_{pde} + eta\mathcal{L}_b + \gamma\mathcal{L}_i$
```

where $L_{pde}$, $L_b$ and $L_i$ are defined as the PDE loss, the boundary loss and the initial loss. The coefficients $\alpha$, $eta$ and $\gamma$ are adjustable parameters in different models. The three losses are defined as

```
$L_{pde} = \sum_{i=1}^{N_f} |\mathcal{L} [\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}, t)] - f|^2$
```

```
$L_b = \sum_{i=1}^{N_b} |\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}_b, t) - \mathbf{u}_b|^2$
```

```
$L_i = \sum_{i=1}^{N_i} |\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}_i, t_0) - \mathbf{u}_0|^2$
```

where $N_f, N_b$, and $N_i$ are the number of corresponding sampling points in the equations, boundaries and initials. Among the physics-informed DL frameworks, the FCNN is the most mature one, which has already been adopted in extensive realms.

In the field of thermology, He et al. [16] utilize the PINN to realize both the forward and inverse heat conduction problems. To adapt to the sophisticated case, the skip connection in the ResNet is employed to elevate the accuracy and stability. The results have testified that the architecture is able to yield a precise prediction fast. In electromagnetics, Zhang et al. [17] exploit the PINN to solve the time domain electromagnetic simulations. The proposed scheme is able to calculate both electric and magnetic fields simultaneously, which can be applied to inhomogeneous and complicated problems. In science, Rasht-Behesht et al. [18] applied the PINN in the wave propagation and full wavelet inversions with limited memory and computational cost. The results corroborate that PINNs can give wonderful results for inversions in various cases. In fluid dynamics, Jin et al. [19] developed the NSFnet to overcome some defects in incompressible laminar or turbulent flows. It is affirmed that the NSFnets can sustain turbulence at a Reynolds number of over 1000. It is noticed that the NSFnet has the potential to be applied in ill-posed problems with fragmentary or noisy boundary conditions as well as inverse problems.
