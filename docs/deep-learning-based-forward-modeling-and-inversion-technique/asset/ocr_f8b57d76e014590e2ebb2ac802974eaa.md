Applying Equation 3.2, the heat flux on the outer surface can be obtained as

```
$q_0(t) =-\rho\ C_{\rho} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(2n-1)!} \left(\frac{\delta^2}{\alpha}\right)^{n-1} \frac{dT_0}{dt^n}++
q_0(t) + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(2n)!} \left(\frac{\delta^2}{\alpha}\right)^{n} \frac{dg_0}{dt^n}$ (3.5)
```

In practical application, only the first few terms of the infinite series expansion are usually taken as approximations. For example, when the thickness is small

```
$q_0(t) \approx-\rho\ C_{\rho} \frac{dT_0}{dt} + q_0(t)$ (3.6)
```

O.R. Burggraaf [8] extended Equation 3.6 to cylinders and spheres, and gave examples to verify it. It is worth noting that although the analytical method is accurate in calculation and low resource consumption, its application scope is not wide. It is difficult to find that the method is only applicable to very simple geometric conditions, and requires that the known temperature and heat flows are separated in time and space infinitely differentiable. When faced with two-dimensional or dimensional situations, it will be difficult to obtain explicit solutions, so the introduction of numerical methods becomes necessary.

The traditional numerical methods usually include offline methods and online methods. For example, the offline method needs to integrate the data all time to obtain the unknown heat flux, while the online method only needs the temperature measurement data for a short period of time. The common off-line methods are all functional-based optimization processes, which can be further divided into deterministic and stochastic optimization. Deterministic optimization depends on the gradient value of the function. In each iteration, the forward heat problem is calculated with the current parameters, while the error between the calculated temperature value and the measured temperature value is obtained. The iterative process is constantly updated to finally reach the minimum value of the cost function. Researchers have applied a variety of gradient algorithms to the heat flux reconstruction.
