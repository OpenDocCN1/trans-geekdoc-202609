The analytical aspect of Big Data occurs by combining the ability to work with data – from different sources, structured or unstructured, in huge quantities, in real time – with the possibility of developing predictive statistical models, conceived through information from Big Data. Birek et al. (2018) conceptualized that Big Data Analysis refers to the techniques used to examine and process the data so that what is hidden can be revealed from patterns of behavior and bring insights related to the applications’ context developed.

Therefore, computing systems or services can (1) filter large amounts of data and (2) identify the information that is considered most relevant. While the human being explores the data interactively to discover trends and patterns, this professional will be able to make decisions that make sense based on careful analysis.

These two components operate in coordination, allowing a continuous and cooperative analytical loop between different firm functional levels (Cybulski et al., 2015; Valdez et al., 2016; Chang et al., 2020). The adoption of all these models lacks managerial support and is also cited as a critical factor affecting Big Data initiatives’ success (LaValle et al., 2011).

In this sense, a firm needs an appropriate combination of its financial, physical, human and organizational resources to create a Big Data Analytics Capability solution, which is difficult to be surpassed by its competitors. Firms need to systematically adapt their resources according to the changes that the market imposes (Teece, 2014). However, this must be possible that firms are aware of the resources needed to build this analytical capacity (Gupta and George, 2016).

Big Data analysis is performed to support decision-making based on information about the user’s context and the planning, management and learning for the systematic use of data. There are models of Descriptive, Diagnostic, Predictive and Prescriptive analysis (Figure 1), in which each analysis capacity has separate services and purposes (Gim et al., 2018).

2.1.1 Descriptive analysis. Descriptive analysis is this type of analysis is used to assist in the display of data, usually at the beginning of a study, to summarize the information and explore the behavior of the data, helping to understand the frequency of certain information being analyzed. This is a set of technologies and processes to understand and analyze utility results using data (Song et al., 2014). It is the most basic process when working with a database.

2.1.2 Diagnostic analysis. Diagnostic analysis is done to understand the purpose of the work itself. The reason, who should consume certain products, when and where are the types of questions that this type of analysis proposes to clarify. It also helps to assess the impact and scope of any action taken. It is a way to assist in the development of strategies to drive the expected result, or to avoid surprises (Gim et al., 2018).

2.1.3 Predictive analysis. Predictive analysis is this type of analysis is used based on historical data, or known trends, thus making it possible to see future trends. It is also used when analyzed in conjunction with the context in which that data was entered, consequently being able to predict their behavior in a similar scenario. This method predicts results based on data entry using a variety of statistical and mathematical techniques. This method analyzes a variety of relationships based on predictions and suggests a model for maximizing business results (Song et al., 2014).
