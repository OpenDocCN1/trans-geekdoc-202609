# 3. Methodology

# 3.1 Grounded theory (GT)

This study uses an exploratory qualitative approach to investigate the interactions between luxury brands and the providers of blockchain solutions in supply chains or traceability projects. Additionally, unlike previous studies, it adopts the grounded theory (GT), which is often used to analyze complex (Flint et al., 2012), new issues (Strauss and Corbin, 1998). Moreover, this method is useful for developing a global explanation, model or theory shaped by participants’ viewpoints who are familiar with the particular issue (Corbin and Strauss, 2008; Strauss and Corbin, 1998), especially for understanding how people conceptualize and experience a phenomenon. This study considers the respondents’ conception of the traceability of luxury goods, which involves an understanding of the content, determining factors and outcomes of such a phenomenon. After collecting the respondents’ perspectives, it then formulates a theoretical proposition on the traceability of luxury goods. Therefore, using this method, we propose new insights into to the implementation of a new technological solution (blockchain) in the luxury goods domain.

Furthermore, to analyze all the transcribed interviews, this study uses the manual coding method based on the axial coding process (Strauss and Corbin, 1990, 1998). Data are reassembled in new ways following an open coding stage, allowing associations between a category (“second-order category”) and its sub-categories (“first-order category”) and reaching toward “aggregate dimensions,” as presented in Table 1.

# 3.2 Sampling and data collection

# 3.2.1 Research protocol.

Following the GT approach, the authors used four protocols in their sampling and data collection phase (as summarized in Figure 1): (1) the use of memos and theoretical sampling (detailed under “data collection” below), (2) the constant comparison of the data and concepts, (3) the assessment of the data and concept saturation and (4) abstraction (Strauss and Corbin, 1990, 1998). Together, these protocols helped explain the phenomenon of luxury goods’ traceability.

# 3.2.2 Data collection.

The interviews covered three major themes: (1) the general context of supply chains in luxury companies, (2) the specific challenges of supply chain management and (3) the potential use of blockchain technology in the respondents’ in-house practices. First, following each interview, the authors mapped out the emergent memos and ideas as the first step of the data collection process.
