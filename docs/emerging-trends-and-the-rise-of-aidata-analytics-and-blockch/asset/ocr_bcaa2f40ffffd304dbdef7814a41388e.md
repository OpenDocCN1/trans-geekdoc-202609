This is because the blockchain system possesses attractive attributes for banking and financial services markets, including resilience, integrity, transparency and immutability (Treleaseen et al., 2017). Blockchain technology also provides increased cost savings, risk control and financial gains by changing business models and the way they transact (Casino et al., 2019; Chang et al., 2020). Many experts predict that it will eventually revolutionize the present central structure system in the financial sector as well as non-financial sectors such as health care, supply chain and public services. For example, it has been proposed that a decentralized blockchain platform can serve as a new way to share, store and maintain sensitive and private medical data. Substantial attention has been paid to the use of blockchain in healthcare services to secure medical information, as it has the benefit of easy access and reduced maintenance costs. Even more significant positive benefits can be expected when blockchain technologies are connected with IoT and the cloud environment (Kaur et al., 2018). Blockchain technology has also been applied to the supply chain, logistics and transport operations across a wide variety of industries (Kshetri and Loukianova, 2019; Iansiti and Lakhani, 2017). A blockchain-based supply chain allows all relevant supply chain partners to access the same data, and it improves the transparency and traceability of the inventory in that supply chain (Park and Sung, 2019; Pournader et al., 2020). Altogether, the market scale is expanding as enterprise-type blockchains are actively being introduced and extended to various industrial fields. The market for blockchain is also increasing as it can address the problems faced by existing industries in various fields, such as by using Smart Contracts (Staples et al., 2017). Specifically, a platform is being established to share, distribute and trade various types of intangible assets through the advancement of smart contracts, which represent one of the key elements of blockchain technology. Thus, data and technologies from other industries are being merged to expand the scope of use of blockchain throughout various industries.

Integrations of blockchain with other components and techniques can generally have a broader impact than blockchain alone. For example, integration with other systems such as AI, data analytics, big data and a structure combined with the cloud can lead to a new stage of development and further improve the efficiency gain from blockchain technology (Zheng et al., 2018). Blockchain ensures the reliability and integrity of transaction records by using security technologies such as cryptographic hash functions, digital signatures and encryption through a peer-to-peer (P2P) network that jointly verifies and records the transaction information of all traders on the network without requiring other third parties (Allessie et al., 2019). In addition, since it involves a decentralized form of information distribution, it has good security and reduces data protection costs (Gover et al., 2019). Therefore, blockchain can be considered as the optimal technology for implementing various application services based on a distributed network.

In an initiative to help businesses effectively address their specific needs by building custom blockchain applications, the Linux Foundation launched the Hyperledger Project. Hyperledger is an open-source project that provides businesses and developers with a diverse suite of frameworks, encourages the reuse of building blocks and enables innovation in distributed ledger technology components (Takyar, 2020). The modular architecture in the Hyperledger framework enables companies to customize blockchain applications to meet their unique needs. Hyperledger Fabric is one of the blockchain projects within the Hyperledger, and it allows participants to manage their own transactions (Mantelli, 2018). The leading Hyperledger projects, such as Indy and Iroha, are used to build blockchain applications for a variety of uses, including blockchain-based supply chain management, digital payment and sovereign identity.
