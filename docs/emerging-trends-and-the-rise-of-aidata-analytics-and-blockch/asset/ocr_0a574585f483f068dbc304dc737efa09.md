Notwithstanding their usefulness, IoT devices are connected to the Internet via a standard communication protocol, which exposes them to security threats (Burgess, 2018; Mendez et al., 2017). The main security challenges in the IoT environment are privacy, authorization, access control, information storage and system configuration (Kouicem et al., 2018). Since those devices cannot afford sophisticated security technologies (Alotaiibi, 2019), traditional security mechanisms such as asymmetric encryption that conform to the low-cost premise of IoT infrastructure are often adopted (Noor and Hassan, 2019). Nevertheless, those mechanisms are insufficient to preserve the five goals of security; i.e., data integrity, availability, confidentiality, authentication and nonrepudiation which threatens further adoption of IoT in the future (Alaba et al., 2017; Ali et al., 2018).

A Deloitte report by Jain (2020) confirms that security remains the main issue for IoT ecosystems to grow. The authors propose blockchain as a solution to address those vulnerabilities and accelerate IoT adoption. However, the author argues that for a blockchain-IoT marriage to survive, security challenges in blockchain like network privacy, transaction confidentiality, device integrity and scalability must be addressed. Those claims were also voiced in the recent relevant literature. Indeed, extant research suggests blockchain as a viable solution to facilitate the growth of the IoT ecosystem, but persistent security issues need to be resolved using minimum technological resources (see Chanson et al., 2019; Firdaus et al., 2019; Reyna et al., 2018; Alaba et al., 2017). While blockchain has been able to solve various challenges facing IoT applications (Jesus et al., 2018), limitations to its full-blown across the various IoT sectors still persist (Wei et al., 2019). Besides, there are emergent new challenges such as stalker attacks (Jesus et al., 2018) and data redundancy (Si, 2019), which are not yet resolved by present-day blockchain technology.

Accordingly, the main objective of this systematic literature review is to identify and structure security threats to IoT ecosystems according to the established IoT architecture as well as the security goals they negatively affect. Moreover, in our review we map the conventional security mechanisms implemented in IoT environments to the security threats they address. Lastly, we explore the features of blockchain technologies and evaluate their suitability in tackling IoT security threats that current mechanisms are less adequate to address. To justify the suitability of Blockchain for IoT security, we conducted a literature review that presents a systematic analysis of the most recently adopted blockchain security applications. We started by investigating the important topics that are associated with blockchain. To do this, we utilized a natural language processing technique suggested by Siddiqi and Sharan (2015) to systematically analyze the abstracts as well as article titles. Accordingly, we statistically analyzed articles' abstracts, titles, and keywords to better understand the current applications of blockchain in addressing IoT security as well as identify the relevant articles to include in our review.

In this section we also provide justifications why our systematic literature review provides a more comprehensive and accurate picture of the current state of this topic. Afterwards, we specify the main goals of the review and describe our methodology. Section 6 thoroughly explains the results of our review and tie them to goals of our work. Lastly, we discuss those results in Section 7 and provide a conclusion and future research direction.
