The focus of the *network layer* is to transmit data received from the perception layer; usually using the IPv6 protocol to this effect (Moin et al., 2019). This layer is composed of a wired and wireless system for transferring and processing sensor data obtained by the perception layer (Wang et al., 2019). The third layer of the IoT architecture is the *processing layer* (also known as support layer) which is tasked with processing the data, verifying the authenticity of requesters, and protecting from threats before transporting the data to the network layer (Burhan et al., 2018). The *application layer* is responsible for managing the services deployed on the IoT device (Burhan et al., 2018) and delivering application specific requirements (Sengupta et al., 2020).

The security goals sought in IoT ecosystems are multifaceted. Originally, IT security management research specify security goals according to the CIA (confidentiality, integrity and availability) triad. Those goals were then extended to include authentication and non-repudiation. The *Confidentiality* goal dictates that information ought not made accessible to unauthorized persons, organizations or processes (Kouicem et al., 2018). *Integrity* refers to the principle of ensuring that data is not tampered with in an unauthorized or undetected way (Kouicem et al., 2018). In order for any information system to fulfill its purpose when needed, the IoT device and its information and applications must always be *available* (Kouicem et al., 2018). As for the *authentication* security goal, it refers to the authenticity of user credentials that are paired with the type of services requested. Lastly, the *non-repudiation* principle seeks the assurance that all parties transacting data over IoT devices meet their obligations; that is confirming the transmission of data across the network (Kouicem et al., 2018).

The reviewed articles yielded 48 different IoT security threats. The threats identified underwent five rounds of card sorting by two graduates with a masters in computer science. The objective of this exercise is to organize the threats according to the two dimensional layer/goal framework specified above. Specifically, the sorters were asked to classify the threats under their corresponding (1) IoT layer and (2) security goals. The sorters were given the choice of attributing a threat to more than one layer or security goal. Descriptions of the threats, layers, and goals were provided to the sorters. After each phase, the threats that sorters disagreed in classifying where returned to them for another round. The sorters were asked to investigate the threats further and reclassify them. After the fifth round, an inter-rater agreements (Cohen's Kappa) of 0.92 and 0.83 were achieved for IoT layer and security goals classification respectively.
