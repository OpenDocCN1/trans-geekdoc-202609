### 5.4 Measurement model

The normality of the data was verified by the univariate skewness and kurtosis and the Mardia's multivariate skewness (b = 401.541; z = 8298.503; p < 0.001) and kurtosis (b = 1158.110; z = 16.631; p < 0.001). These tests for the indicators were “very significant” with p < 0.001, which indicates non-normality, which was already predicted. This procedure was necessary to limit the possibility of using some statistical analysis techniques that have the normal distribution of data as a characteristic.

Afterwards, the structural model was estimated. In traditional PLS methods, latent variables are estimated as weighted aggregations of indicators without the inclusion of measurement errors (Henseler et al., 2014). After the first interaction, the results of the factorial loads obtained by the variables were presented; it was found that it was only necessary to exclude four variables that had factor loads below 0.7 for model adjustment (SUBD2, BDO1, SAT3, DF3 and MP2). In the sequence, convergent validity was verified, which demonstrates the extent to which the latent variable correlates with the items chosen to measure that variable and the discriminant validity involves the correlation between the constructs of the theoretical model.

The analysis of the measurement model must precede the analysis of the relationships between the constructs or latent variables. The next step was to examine the measurement model, which involved: Cronbach's alpha (CA), composite reliability (CR), average variance extracted (AVE), determination coefficients (R²), predictive relevance (Q²), effect size (f²) and the Goodness of Fit (GoF) (Hair et al., 2016) according to Table 1.

CAs coefficients ranged between 0.858 and 0.938. These results indicated a high internal consistency of the scales used. The CRs obtained in this study varied between 0.908 and 0.951 indicating that the results were satisfactory in this regard (Hair et al., 2009). For this model, the AVEs ranged between 0.522 and 0.783. All latent variables showed an AVE greater than 50%, which reaches the criteria of Chin (1998) for the indication of the existence of convergent validity.

The R² value measures the predictive accuracy of the model, representing the combined effects of endogenous variables on exogenous variables. In the present study, the R² values demonstrated that the model has accuracy and predictive relevance in all constructs. Cohen's f² is used to estimate the effect size in correlated samples (repeated measures, longitudinal data, grouped data) for two continuous variables. It is evaluated how much each construct is “useful” to adjust the model. Values of 0.02, 0.15 and 0.35 are considered small, medium and large, respectively (Hair et al., 2016). The $\hat{f}^2$ values are obtained by reading the communalities. What is perceived in this research is that the constructs were considered medium and large. The Stone–Geisser’s $Q^2$ test assesses how close the model is to what was expected of it (or the quality of the model prediction or the accuracy of the adjusted model). According to the criterion used to assess the accuracy of the adjusted model, $\hat{Q}^2$, all constructs were considered to be adequately accurate, as they presented values greater than zero (Akter et al., 2017).

The GoF is the global adjustment measure, that is, the square root of the multiplication of both the averages of the AVE and the $R^2$ of the endogenous variables. The value found for the GoF is 0.6936 (69.36%) and was considered large enough for the validity of the model in the PLS (Wetzels et al., 2009). The discriminant validity assessed items that reflect the factor or that are correlated with other factors. In this research, the AVEs were greater than or equal to the square of the correlation between the factors, as shown in Table 2, with the definitive Fornell–Larcker criterion with all the factor loads of each indicator with values above 0.5. It was not necessary to exclude variables to adjust the model.

### 5.5 Common method bias

We checked common method variance (CMV) by applying Harman’s one-factor test (Podsakoff and Organ, 1986) on the 31 items, and we extracted five components with an eigenvalue greater than 1.0. The variance extracted by the first component was 47.9%. (The result was below 0.5, validating the test). We realized in this research that there are many first-order latent variables and that they are highly correlated with each other because they measure second-order latent variables. It may cause a high percentage (%) of extracted variance, but this does not mean that it is a Common Method Bias problem.

## 6. Discussion

Big Data Analytics Capability was found to have a positive association with all the direct and indirect relationships (mediations) of the proposed theoretical model. The H1a hypothesis (β = 0.353; p < 0.001) described the causal relationship of the path “Big Data Analytics Capability” and “Product Innovation Performance” was accepted because the firms understood that product innovation is necessary. This realization happened since they are in a constantly changing environment, and only by adding new technologies and tools – as is the case of Big Data and its analysis capacity – it is possible to improve Product Innovation Performance (Hooi and Abu, 2018). Big Data Analytics Capability can directly impact a firm’s Product Innovation Performance, strengthening the underlying process of dynamic innovation capabilities, specifically in firms that work with a high amount and diversity of data, which would be impossible to analyze and interpret another way differently (Wamba et al., 2017).

Using Big Data within the product innovation process, firms allow for greater accuracy in their research and development investments because there will be greater prior intelligence in knowing what will return to the organization. This intelligence will come from the larger amount and variety of information that the organization will have, allowing for more refinement in its investment strategy. The innovation capacities in the firms observed in this research indicate a dependence on the effort of the teams involved collectively and not on the individual effort in the use of Big Data. It is also essential that there is a cross-flow of knowledge between different people working in parallel on different aspects of a project (Koberg et al., 2003).

The H1b hypothesis (β = 0.600; p < 0.001), described as the causal relationship in “Big Data Analytics Capability” and “Process-oriented dynamic capabilities”, was accepted because organizations note that it will be a crucial competitive differentiator allowing the firms to stand out from the competition (Wixom et al., 2013). It was noted in the survey that the responding managers associated the Big Data Analytics Capability with the dynamic capabilities, as the organization is facing its competition. That is, the better the Big Data analytics applied, the better its differential is to the rest of the market (Wamba et al., 2017). In fast-paced environments, constantly changing conditions (unpredictable and volatile), existing modes of operation wear out quickly. Therefore, process-oriented dynamic capabilities are necessary to maintain competitiveness (Wilden and Gudergan, 2015). The role of Big Data Analytics Capability that operates as drivers of process-oriented dynamic capabilities has been noted in the literature (McAfee et al., 2012; Mikalef et al., 2019a, b; Singh and El-Kassar, 2018).

Regarding the H1c hypothesis (β = 0.740; p < 0.001), described as “Big Data Analytics Capability” and “Business Value”, it was accepted because the survey respondents note that there are significant gains to the firm when the Big Data tools are applied correctly. That is, the perception of managers and analysts is that the organization is ahead of its competition, enabling a better understanding of its customers and their relationship with them (Wamba et al., 2017). Big Data analysis is evident from firms’ growing investments, particularly those that operate in complex and fast-paced environments (Wang et al., 2016; Mikalef et al., 2019a).

The H2 hypothesis (β = 0.428; p < 0.001) described in the causal relationship between “Process-oriented dynamic capabilities” and “Product Innovation Performance”, was accepted.
