To this end, a hybrid technology could be implemented, i.e., blockchain security features can be integrated with traditional security mechanisms which makes it a promising future research topic. IoT devices can signal invasive interference to their IoT ecosystems that central authorities can physically verify. Device specifications that hackers require to conduct invasive attacks can be concealed from nearby attackers which can be supplemented by blockchain ecosystems modified to detect out-of-service or possibly tampered with IoT devices within their networks.

Our endeavor to systematically review the relevant literature on blockchain-enabled IoT security yielded an exhaustive framework of IoT security threats along with the traditional and blockchain mechanisms to address those threats. The extant research does not take a holistic approach to investigate how blockchain technology can address IoT security altogether but instead focus on specific threats where blockchain has a superior impact. Accordingly, our framework can be used further investigate IoT security as a system. Specifically, future research can empirically examine whether IoT ecosystems that intelligently and economically incorporates traditional mechanisms with blockchain features are indeed more protected than using solely traditional IoT security methods or blockchain technology. That said, the implementation of blockchain technology in IoT ecosystems should focus more on gauging security with respect to the needs of the industry that it intends to support. In other words, there is no blockchain versatile enough for all IoTs. A configurational approach is required to align the IoT security requirements with the consumption of resources of different blockchain features is necessary in order to realize the proper balance between security, efficiency and feasibility.

# 9. Conclusion

Blockchain technology has the potential to overcome the aforementioned challenges and become the technology of choice when implementing wide scale IoT ecosystems. Our literature review reveals that the technology can address with supremacy the massive inventory of security threats that faces IoT devices. Besides, blockchain as a novel technological architecture does not yet stem new security threats that are noteworthy of mentioning. That said, this technology, in its current form and principles, requires substantial energy, storage, bandwidth and time resources. IoT ecosystems, especially in areas such as supply chain, healthcare and smart living cannot yet afford those resources given the low-cost and low-resource premise of IoT infrastructure. To this end, considerable transfigurations are necessary to adapt blockchain to the various industries and contexts. For instance, in private (trusted) IoT ecosystems, decentralized consensus mechanisms might not be necessary; thereby reducing the time and energy wastage. Pertaining to the incessant size growth of blockchain, forking (and self-forking) can help reduce the blockchain storage requirements to manageable volumes (Magnani et al., 2018; Basu, 2019).
