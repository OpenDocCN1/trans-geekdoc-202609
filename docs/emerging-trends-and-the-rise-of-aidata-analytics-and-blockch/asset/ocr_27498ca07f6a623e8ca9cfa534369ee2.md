Several fields such as the food business (Hang et al., 2020), finance (White et al., 2020); Tapcott and Tapscott, 2017; Chang et al., 2020a, b), stock market (Ji et al., 2020), e-voting systems (Baudier et al., 2021) and health systems (Mukherjee and Singh, 2020) are showing great interest in blockchain technology. However, it is more commonly used in smart contracts, supply chain management and logistics (Hughes et al., 2019).

Within supply chain management, blockchains are primarily introduced to manage the flow of a company’s distribution channel (Helo and Hao, 2019; De Giovanni, 2020). While traditional supply chains often seek to establish a multi-stakeholder collaborative environment through mutual trust to remove communication barriers and improve efficiency (Korpela et al., 2017), they face several critical issues due to centralized planning and coordination, which limits control (Korpela et al., 2017), lowers transparency and reduces the trust in existing mechanisms. Operating blockchains efficiently, especially in e-commerce platforms, requires all involved parties to participate in creating a cooperative supply chain network, including the global manufacturers and e-tailers that resell luxury products (Niu et al., 2021). As the rise in counterfeit products and scandals about product quality can negatively impact the entire supply chain (Abeyrathne and Monfared, 2016), introducing blockchains can help re-establish trust and transparency and improve the quality and speed of supply chain management while reducing risk (Kshetrti, 2018).

Owing to its decentralized and shareable data system that can be used by multiple suppliers, blockchain technology is especially suitable for luxury companies, as it enables them to trace their products. Therefore, this study aims to answer the following research questions:

+   (1) How can improving the traceability of luxury goods help optimize supply chain management?
+   (2) Within the luxury business, what conditions and contexts could influence the implementation of blockchain solutions?
+   (3) Can blockchains solve the ongoing issues faced by luxury firms, such as counterfeiting and the gray market?

The remainder of this article is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the theoretical background on blockchain, supply chain management and luxury brands, focusing on the luxury goods ecosystem and need for the traceability of luxury products. Section 3 presents the qualitative methodology, in which 12 stakeholders (managers of international luxury brands and blockchain experts from the luxury sector) were interviewed to gather diverse points of view on supply chains, blockchains and traceability. Sections 4 and 5 present and discuss the results. Finally, Section 6 provides the theoretical and managerial contributions of the study, followed by the limitations and future research directions.
