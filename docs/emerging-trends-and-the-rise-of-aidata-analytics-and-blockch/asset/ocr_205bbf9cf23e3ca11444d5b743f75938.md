Symitsi and Chalvatzis (2018) investigated spillover effects from technology stock indices and energy to Bitcoin by employing an asymmetric multivariate VAR-GARCH model. The results of the study indicate that technology companies have short-run volatility spillovers to Bitcoin.

In contrast, it has long-run volatility effects on energy companies. They also indicate that Bitcoin-stock index portfolios indicate that Bitcoin experienced higher returns and lower risks because of the low dependence of Bitcoin on the stock indices. So, these studies conclude that there is an interconnection between technology factors and technology stocks between cryptocurrencies. Umar et al. (2021) examined the interconnection between the technology sector in different countries and cryptocurrencies by applying Diebold and Yilmaz’s (2012, 2014) method for measuring connectedness to technology sector equity indexes and cryptocurrency index returns ranging from August 2014 to October 2018. The results indicate that there is a trivial interconnection between cryptocurrencies and the technology sector around the world.

By now, it is shown that many studies examine different aspects of cryptocurrencies and their relationship with other asset classes, and only two studies examined the relationship and connectedness between cryptocurrencies and technology companies and sectors; however, in our study, we aim to investigate the dynamic correlation between several leading cryptocurrencies and technology companies that mostly operate on blockchain platform by employing BADCC-MGARCH model that has not been used in related literature. Before introducing methodology in the next section, we want to present some literature on investors’ sentiment on cryptocurrencies price and stock prices of technology companies.

Many studies examined the effect of investors’ sentiment on cryptocurrencies and stock price return. For instance, López-Cabarcos et al. (2021) investigated the impact of investor sentiments on Bitcoin price by employing the GARCH models and showing that Bitcoin volatility is significantly affected by investor sentiments. Kristoufek (2015) investigates the drivers of the Bitcoin prices wavelet approach and concludes that the interest of investors in cryptocurrencies is one of the drivers of Bitcoin prices. Garcia et al. (2014) and Kristoufek (2013) arrived at the same conclusion. Kraaijeveld and De Smedt (2020) examined the predictive role of public sentiment presented by the Twitter signal in predicting several cryptocurrencies using cryptocurrency-specific lexicon-based sentiment analysis and the Granger causality method. The results indicate that Twitter sentiment helps predict the price of several cryptocurrencies, including Bitcoin cash coins and Litecoin. This result is supported by Li et al. (2019b).

Several studies also investigate the impact of investor sentiments on stock prices (Ho et al., 2017; Li et al., 2020; Verma et al., 2008). Ji et al. (2020) investigated the effect of investor sentiments on the stock return of blockchain-based technology companies by employing the Fama-French three-factor model. They add sentiment factors to the model to improve the explanation of the model, and their result indicates a positive relationship between the stock return of these companies and investors’ sentiments.
