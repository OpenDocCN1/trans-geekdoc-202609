Therefore, this study presents a case of applying blockchain to the public sector, specifically in e-government, by introducing the actual application of a blockchain-based identification system. This study promotes the use of blockchain in the public sector and provides a foundational understanding for further research examining the potential benefits of blockchain technology. The following section presents the possible research directions for blockchain adoption in government.

Existing identity management systems convey the contents of the identification card to a central system to be checked and authenticated by a corporate or national central authority. This system is at risk of being compromised by various hacking and counterfeiting incidents, which makes it difficult for individuals and institutions to gain trust in the system. By introducing blockchain technology instead of a central server that manages identity information in a distributed network, hacking or counterfeiting issues can be addressed transparently. Users can also manage their own personal information and make their own decisions about how their information is shared. This type of personal identification access with self-determination provides easy log in access to multiple home pages, thus simplifying the steps involved in identification and authentication methods. However, despite the advantages of blockchain-based identity systems, there are still various challenges associated with using blockchain in the government sector: First, there is a lack of contextual understanding of and user experience with blockchain-based systems. The design of a blockchain-based identity management system enables the reuse of information, thereby improving the granularity with which users can disclose their identity information. However, it is still daunting to build trust in these attributes, because users have a noticeable lack of experience with the emerging approach to identity management systems (Dunphy and Petitcolas, 2018). This means there is a need for research into user experience to determine whether users can intuitively understand the meaning of the DID attribute. Future studies should focus on understanding the context of DID and user acceptance in blockchain-based identity management systems and on how to build trust and reputation between users and businesses. As suggested by Gil-García and Flores-Zúñiga (2020) in the Implementation Adoption Model of Digital Government Success, it is advisable to study the implementation and adoption of blockchain technology in the public sector.

Second, one of the factors impeding the development of new approaches to ensuring safe and reliable identities on the internet is the weak regulatory environment regarding the storage and processing of personal data (Yeoh, 2017). The introduction of new technologies such as blockchain into the government sector requires a clear division of duties and support from relevant policy and regulatory agencies. Relevant laws, norms, regulations and principles should also be supplemented or amended to support the introduction of blockchain. Introducing a new system such as a blockchain digital identification system requires an understanding of and compliance with applicable laws. Therefore, to increase the applicability and usefulness of a blockchain-based digital identity management system, it must be accompanied by extensive knowledge of national laws and regulations, and of data protection laws in particular, so that these can be improved to better support such a system. Research proposing a successful blockchain adoption model for governments can also contribute to improved policies and enforcement.

Third, there may be a need for research into the creation of a blockchain ecosystem. Many countries and companies are developing technologies in a mutually exclusive manner in an attempt to take the lead in the market, and there is little sharing of research results and technologies. However, to activate the full potential of identity management systems and strengthen sovereignty through the use of blockchain technology, it is necessary to establish an ecosystem consisting of financial institutions, mobile carriers, governments and platforms (Wadhwa, 2019). To this end, a consortium including regulators, blockchain technology companies and infrastructure operators should be established, and there is also a need for research into appropriate policymaking and the design of a suitable business model. In the same vein, research should be conducted from a long-term perspective to examine the benefits and added value that can be gained from a blockchain-based identity system. Core technologies such as AI, cloud, big data and IoT will be applied everywhere in the near future, and money, products, space and everything else will be shared, thus causing the entire ecosystem to change. It is, therefore, suggested that future research examine long-term measures that can be taken, as well as the preparations that can be made. It is additionally suggested that infrastructure research be conducted.

Fourth, there are still challenges remaining with digital transformation in the public sector. Most of the literature argues that advancements can be made in technology implementation, but that the real difficulties lie in the changes in the structure and the culture that must follow, as well as in the integration and participation of citizens into and in digitalized public services (Sivarajah et al., 2015). As indicated by Mergel et al. (2019), a digital transformation needs to be seen as a holistic approach rather than as the simple digitization of a process. Research needs to analyze how the organizational structure and process in the public sector can change when adopting advanced technologies and how digitization could change the relationships with citizens and other stakeholders.

Blockchain-related academic research is still in its infancy. Although a number of systematic literature review papers have examined blockchain-related topics (Alketbi et al., 2020; Casino et al., 2019; Zheng et al., 2018), there are still substantial research gaps in blockchain research, including usability, scalability and social issues such as ethical dilemmas involving security and privacy (Yli-Huumo et al., 2016); these issues should be addressed in future studies. While digitization in public sectors has many potential benefits, little attention has been paid to the ethical, social, privacy and security issues that could potentially arise. For example, governments and companies could use data analytic tools and big data algorithms to identify users and access private data without the consent of the individual(s) in question. Users (in this case, citizens) often do not fully understand how their information and opinion can be used and manipulated. Researchers should pay attention to ethical challenges in digital use in the public sector (Domanski et al., 2018). As many scholars have pointed out, ethics are not limited to the existing laws. Therefore, new ethical frameworks are needed to address the ethical challenges resulting from digitization in the public and private sectors (Chang et al., 2020).

## 4.2 Practical implications

Blockchain-based identity management systems aim to fundamentally block the hacking of corporate and personal information as well as the unauthorized use of such information through decentralization, which allows people to directly manage their personal data. The proposed blockchain-based system simplifies the identification process and increases the ease of use through an online (mobile) service platform. It can reduce operating costs by improving the identification methods and reducing the service processing time. Eliminating the need for physical cards or tickets – which can become lost and require reissuing – can further reduce operating costs. In addition, the proper reuse of personal information can increase the efficiency of the process between users and validators, and the conversion of the online service operation system can increase the productivity of the entire community, including both the public and private sectors.

By storing and managing personal information on the blockchain and providing mobile apps to customers, this system allows users to log in or retrieve previously authenticated personal information without having to go through an authentication process every time. As it does not require a verification process every time, it is expected that only the necessary personal information can be used more quickly and conveniently, without needing to provide unnecessary details. Moreover, blockchain-based public service operation effectively increases the transparency and reliability of services and reduces social costs caused by personal information leakage. By introducing standardized platforms such as AWS, unnecessary system deployment costs were reduced, and flexibility and scalability in data were secured. We also considered the scalability of the authentication service by providing various additional services through the provision of app services. Indeed, South Korea is currently accelerating the adoption of blockchain-based digital IDs through a series of new rollouts and consolidation measures. For example, airline passengers will soon be able to board a plane with their digital identity, which will be provided through government apps (Identity Review, 2020). Secure blockchain-based mobile authentication reduces the risk of identity theft, unlike physical identity cards which reveal personal information. In terms of industrial expectations, the introduction of DID-based ID cards and the dissemination of them to public services can speed up the development of blockchain technology.

Lastly, the development and introduction of blockchain-based identification systems affect human resource development and employment. This study has reviewed a government project that developed a server and mobile (user/verifier) apps around blockchain technology. As a result, we have found that blockchain developers and experts are scarce and must be nurtured. It is critical to properly educate and train skilled workers for blockchain work, as developing a blockchain-based system requires the handling of sensitive personal information. It is desirable to train talented people to have a good understanding of the overall process and knowledge of the blockchain; in this way, we can increase the employment rate of the skilled workforce centered around the development of blockchain-based systems.

Regarding the practical aspects of blockchain implementation, there are several challenges that remain to be solved. The first issues to consider are latency, size and bandwidth (Swan, 2015). Blockchain involves a relatively slow response, because the number of bitcoin transactions that occur per second is small. The pilot of this design shows that the processing speed of using blockchain technology in the system is relatively slow. In general, using a database will incur around 10,000 transactions per second (TPS); for blockchain, it will not exceed 1,500 TPS, no matter how fast the system is. This shows the need for research aiming to improve the shortcomings of these blockchains.
