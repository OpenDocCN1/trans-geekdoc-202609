In addition, existing identity management systems have shown problems with simplifying user identification and authentication, controlling the flow of personal information and ensuring user trust and security (Liu et al., 2020). The size and complexity of many identity management systems, combined with the necessary privacy and security requirements, have also resulted in serious usability issues (Dhamija and Dusseault, 2008).

Blockchain is a form of decentralized computing wherein transactions are democratized through the introduction of a consensus mechanism, thereby providing a new means of organizing across many domains to record transactions, certificates and ownership (Di Pierro, 2017; Grover, 2019; Lim et al., 2018). Given these characteristics, blockchain technology is often considered an important technology for solving the problems of traditional identity management systems by leveraging the benefits of convenience, reliability and information security (Chaudhary et al., 2019; Lesavre et al., 2019). A blockchain-based identity management system can provide a secure self-sovereign digital identity to each user, increase users’ trust and control, and ease the maintenance burden (Jindal, 2019; Zelenovic, 2020). The main factors undermining data sovereignty are centralized privacy systems and unclear information management structures. As a result, blockchain, which is characterized by decentralization, transparency and availability, has recently attracted attention as a technology that can return data sovereignty to users (Allessie et al., 2019). In other words, a blockchain-based identity management system enables user-centered ID management, which directly transfers the management and control of ID information to individuals while simultaneously increasing security by blocking the use of personal data without user consent (Dumphy and Petitcolas, 2018). According to an IDC report, “20% of government[s] will issue blockchain-enabled identities by 2023” (Valliant et al., 2020). The accuracy and efficiency of identity management system blockchain-based transactions makes blockchain attractive to many decision makers in the public sector.

The primary function of a blockchain-based identity management system is decentralization. A decentralized identifier (DID) is an identification/certification service that establishes a distributed ledger or decentralized system using blockchain technology (Kim et al., 2020). The W3C characterizes a DID as “a globally unique identifier that does not require registration with a centralized registrar because it is registered in distributed ledger technology or other forms of a decentralized network” (W3C, 2021). Unlike existing identification/authentication, DIDs are issued by an agency but managed by the data subject themselves. Individuals can store certificates issued by a single financial or affiliated organization, as well as create separate identities for their intended use (Won, 2020).

As can be seen from the recent frequent occurrence of personal information leakage and hacking accidents involving financial institutions, large portal sites and social networking sites (SNSs), there is a high risk of personal information being exposed if the central server of a major institution is verified. However, a DID system only requires a mobile ID to verify one’s identity, which can minimize the anxiety associated with signing up or logging in to a vulnerable or unstable site. In addition, when users log in to various homepages, the password management policies can differ and are often confusing; by contrast, using a mobile ID can minimize the inconvenience of having to log in every time a user uses the service. It also has the effect of expanding the right to self-determination of personal information. DID is a trend in the blockchain ecosystem, and many global companies such as Microsoft, Apple and Facebook are leading development in DID services (Tung, 2019). DID is considered the best alternative for managing sensitive personal information, but it is currently implemented using various methods and techniques.
