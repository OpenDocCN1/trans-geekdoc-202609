# 5. Discussion

The COVID-19 pandemic has accelerated the digitalization of luxury brands’ supply chains and need to ensure safety in the digitalization process (Nandi et al., 2020). Therefore, this study aimed to answer three research questions about the implementation of blockchains that focused on its main benefits such as traceability, the contexts and conditions that promoted such a disruptive technology and the solutions provided to the luxury industry. To answer these research questions, we highlight three propositions.

*Proposition 1.* Blockchain removes intermediaries in the supply chain, improves traceability and facilitates management.

The interviewed managers suggested that the digitalization of the luxury industry has led to a complex and difficult-to-control business structure due to the specialized nature of its products and growing demand. Despite using digital platforms, the luxury industry is organized in an overcomplicated manner and involves numerous intermediaries (Song et al., 2019). Academics and managers agree on the need to improve supply chain processes and role of blockchain solutions to bring about the required change (Al-Talib et al., 2020). Many studies confirm that blockchain can make the supply chain efficient and remove unnecessary intermediaries (Casado-Vara et al., 2018; Al-Talib et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2019). Thus, disintermediation using blockchains (Tönnissen and Teuteberg, 2020) enhances the transparency, traceability and visibility of the supply chain and provides security against counterfeiting (Wang et al., 2019), thereby minimizing uncertainty in the supply chain’s operations (Xu et al., 2019).

The transparency and traceability of business processes are the key benefits expected by the interviewed experts from the implementation of blockchain solutions. While the luxury industry needs to trace the path of its products throughout the supply chain to maintain high quality and guarantee their products’ origins (Shen et al., 2020), brands need to prove their environmental engagement with customers (Ho et al., 2016). Both these actions can significantly improve the brand’s image. However, regardless of its benefits, the luxury industry is still reluctant to incorporate blockchains.

*Proposition 2.* The implementation of blockchain solutions within the luxury sector is influenced by certain conditions and contexts.

The interviewed experts expressed two contexts that hinder the implementation of blockchains. First, the luxury industry may resist rapid change. Second, luxury brands may not concede to the degree of openness and knowledge required to incorporate blockchain technology into their operations. The experts confirmed that while the adoption of disruptive technologies corresponds to the values of the luxury industry (Yeoman and McMahon-Beattie, 2018), their approval requires demonstrable financial benefits (Frizzo-Barker et al., 2020). That is, blockchains must provide decision-makers with short-term ROIs. This is in line with previous studies that have focused on business efficiency (Azzi et al., 2019), including transaction costs (Kshetri and Loukoianova, 2019). Luxury brands accept blockchain technologies when they are considered to improve performance (Maersh, 2018), by significantly contributing to revenue streams (Deloitte, 2018) [4]. Nevertheless, some problems can decelerate the adoption process, such as the potential cultural resistance at the industry or country level, the lack of digitalization by suppliers and trust issues (Kano and Nakajima, 2018; Kshetri, 2018; Reyna et al., 2018; Zou et al., 2018).

The openness of blockchain, which promotes traceability and transparency, might also present obstacles because of the industry’s competitive nature and lack of collaboration (Kshetri, 2018; Helo and Hao, 2019). Additionally, increased complexity affects supply chain performance (Qrunfleh and Tarafdar, 2014), especially for blockchain solutions, which are still rarely used in the luxury sector.
