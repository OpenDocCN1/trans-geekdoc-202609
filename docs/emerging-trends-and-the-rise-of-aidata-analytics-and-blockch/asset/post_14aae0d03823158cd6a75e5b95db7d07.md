
It uses scrypt as a proof of work scheme, making Litecoin mining more accessible for users than Bitcoin mining that requires advanced hardware systems. Litecoin also has a relatively large share in the crypto market, but it is still below Bitcoin. It is the seventh cryptocurrency by market cap.

Monero is another important cryptocurrency that is considered in this study. It was launched in 2014. The most essential and distinguishing feature of Monero is its privacy-oriented characteristics. Information like the identity of the trader and the amounts of transactions is anonymous. This feature is enabled by employing the concepts of ring signatures and stealth addresses. Along with anonymity, Monero has other useful features, like untraceable transaction history, equal and easy mining and fungibility, which means that two units of a currency can be substituted like gold. Monero's market cap reaches 1.1 billion dollars by May 2020.

The next cryptocurrency is Dash that is launched in January 2014 to become a medium for daily transactions. It is a fork of Bitcoin with some additional properties. Similar to Monero, Dash provides faster and more anonymous service users by offering two new services, including PrivateSend and InstantSend. As a result of these features, the transactions become untraceable. Dash coins are equal and fungible. Dash runs on a masternode network. Masternode is a specialized server that is responsible for PrivateSend and InstantSend services, as well as the governance of the system. Dash also has a different form of governance. It employs a self-funding model of governance as 10% of all the mining rewards go back to Dash. Dash is number 21 on the list of top cryptocurrencies by market capitalization.

The other cryptocurrency in this study is Peercoin that was launched in August 2012. Peercoin is designed based on the Bitcoin framework. The distinguishing feature of Peercoins is that it is the first cryptocurrency that employs a hybrid system of proof-of-stake and proof-of-work. This makes Peercoin a secure and energy-efficient cryptocurrency as using the proof-of-stake as a hashing method makes block generation more energy-efficient and more secure.

The Stellar is an open-source blockchain-based network that allows for fast, cross-border transactions between any pair of currencies. It is very similar to Ripple. The cryptocurrency of Stellar is called lumens. Stellar works under the Stellar Consensus Protocol (SCP), under which transaction verification is carried out by a set of honest nodes instead of the whole network of nodes. It reduces the costs and time for a transaction significantly. The Stellar network automatically carries out the currency conversion at the best possible rates. By May 2020, Stellar is the 11th cryptocurrency by market capitalization.

## Literature review

There is ample research that examined the effect of blockchain technology on the financial and economic systems around the world. Joo et al. (2019) tried to identify the contribution of blockchain technology to finance by reviewing this technology and its applications, like cryptocurrencies. They find that cryptocurrencies, as the first application of blockchain, can boost money transfers worldwide. They also mention that the legislative changes and gaining users' trust are essential factors in the future development of blockchain technology in finance. Chang et al. (2020) describe the impact of blockchain on the financial industry and mention the challenges and ethical issues that this technology faces, such as scalability, security and privacy leakage. They distinguish between blockchain technology and cryptocurrencies and argue that this technology has wide application in the financial sector, especially in the banking system. They also claim that blockchain is an immaturity technology, and governments should take measures to restrict the illegal actions that can be carried out via this platform. They also argue that blockchain technology has great potential, and financial services should adopt this technology unless they are not competitive in the future. Chen and Bellavitis (2020) investigate the benefits, challenges and limitations of blockchain technology. They argue that as this technology is a decentralized peer-to-peer network, it can lower the transaction cost. In addition, this technology can increase the interpretability of financial services and encourage more innovation in the financial system. They also claim that blockchain technology is borderless and transparent. They also mention some challenges blockchain technology faces, including illegal activities, high volatility in cryptocurrency prices, usefulness and regulatory uncertainty. Zhang et al. (2020b) investigate blockchain technology and its applications and challenges in finance and economics. They claim that blockchain solves two main problems that concern the digital economy by delivering value via the chain and building trust without any cost. However, they refer to some challenges that blockchain may face, like some limitations in processing transactions, difficulties to impose regulation to prevent illegal activities and lack of consensus among countries in accepting cryptocurrencies. Lee (2019) investigates how blockchain technology and cryptocurrencies have developed and evolved over time and claims that this technology and cryptocurrencies constantly evolve by adopting more efficient mechanisms and methods. In addition, companies have tried to adapt to this technology increasingly. He also argues that blockchain technology is expected to be a major technology in developing the token economy as a new economic paradigm in the future. Thus, almost all of these studies emphasize that blockchain technology has great potentials and many applications in economy and finance; however, this technology is in its infancy, and it faces some challenges that need to be addressed.

There is also plenty of literature that examines the different characteristics of cryptocurrencies, especially Bitcoin. For example, the price discovery procedure has been examined by Brandvold et al. (2015), Kapar and Olmo (2019), Baur and Dimpfl (2019) and Brauneis and Mestel (2018). Besides, the existence of bubbles in cryptocurrencies has also been assessed at several studies such as Cheah and Fry (2015), Cheung et al. (2015), Corbet et al. (2018a), Fry and Cheah (2016). Also, the presence of structural breaks in Bitcoin returns has been analyzed by Thies and Molnár (2018), Mensi et al. (2019) and Bouri et al. (2019). The volatility in the price of the cryptocurrency has also been examined by Ardia et al. (2019), Chaim and Laurini (2018), Katsiampa (2017), Phillip et al. (2018) and Troster et al. (2019). However, these topics are not our primary concern in this study.

At the end of this section, we will present some studies examining the interaction between stock returns of technology companies and other factors and focus on two studies investigating the interaction between cryptocurrencies and blockchain-based technology companies and technology sectors.
