The key benefits of Big Data technology for management are reduced costs and increased revenues, increased operational efficiency, improvement in decision-making, improvement of products and services, and improvement in the processes of innovation and development of new products and markets (Leeflang et al., 2014). Therefore, organizations must prepare to generate business formed on the analysis of Big Data based on the information that becomes assets of strategic value for organizations (Chen et al., 2012).

Innovation in products and services results in changes to existing ones or the creation of new ones. This can often be achieved by offering a new central benefit or developing a new way of providing a central benefit. Product and service innovation takes multiple forms in various sectors (Miles, 2008) and is evident in traditional service industries and manufacturing industries (Baines et al., 2009). This sometimes results from the use of specific methods and sometimes requires organizational and cultural changes (Rothenberg, 2007). Innovation in sustainable products and services requires continuous interactions of development, operations and improvement. Stimulating innovation in products and services is a timely research topic that presents a big gap between importance and current knowledge, especially when we observe central phenomena in emerging technologies such as Big Data (Singh and El-Kassar, 2018).

## 3. Research hypothesis and research model

### 3.1 Big Data Analytics Capability: antecedents factors

Big Data Analytics Capability is recognized as the competence to provide business insights using the capacity of data management, infrastructure (technology) and talent (personnel) to transform a business into a competitive force. Big Data Analytics Capability was selected to understand strategic integration by applying the capabilities of this technology’s features to shape and support business strategy. Big Data Analytics Capability can be understood by Gunday et al. (2011), Hannachi (2015) and Hooi and Abu (2018) as:

- (1) Big Data Operations: the firm’s ability to identify sources where large volumes of various types of data flow at high speed, collecting, storing and analyzing Big Data to meet the firm’s strategic and operational goals.
- (2) Updating IT infrastructure: applications, hardware, data and networks to enable Big Data Analytics Capability staff to rapidly develop, deploy and support the necessary system components for a firm.
- (3) Advanced Analytics: professional capacity of the Big Data Analytics Capability team, including skills or knowledge to perform assigned tasks.
- (4) Strategic Uses of Big Data: the ability to manipulate routines in a structured (rather than ad hoc) way to manage IT resources according to the needs and priorities of the business.

### 3.2 Big Data Analytics Capability: consequent factors

### 3.2.1 Product innovation performance

Mikalef et al. (2019a, b) and Sena et al. (2019) examined the relationship between Big Data Analytics Capability and a key determinant of the firm’s intangible resources, namely, innovation capabilities. In addition, Aydiner et al. (2019) and Dubey et al. (2019) concluded that the desired level of performance (in the case of this survey, Product Innovation Performance) could not be achieved in organizations that fail to respond effectively to external pressures or environmental demand. That said, Product innovation may be the result of Big Data Analytics Capability.

In Product Innovation Performance, the subjective Product Innovation Performance measures were largely adapted from Wang et al. (2012) and Wamba et al. (2017). This makes it possible to understand the management’s perception of the market performance of new products and services through the firm’s performance analysis, facing the market, in two dimensions analyzed in this research: Financial performance and market performance. Following is the conceptualization of these first-order constructs.

- (1) Financial performance: it is often related to profitability or return on investment (ROI) and can be improved by an organization’s learning ability. Firms that can learn about their customers or potential customers, competitors and regulators have a better chance of detecting and acting on market events and trends. The growth in sales or provision of services drives financial performance (Wang et al., 2012; Wamba et al., 2017). One of the main objectives of any organization is to capture a large market share, which is only possible when organizations obtain respectable margins and profits through their sales and economically because their optimized operations strengthen the firm’s financial aspirations (Gupta et al., 2020).
- (2) Market performance: defined as the firm’s success in entering and bringing new products or services to the market. The existing literature suggests that when there is greater support from the IT area in its strategies, operational and market performance tend to improve competitively (Wang et al., 2012). For Gupta et al. (2014) and Gupta et al. (2020), market performance can be achieved through innovation, which has a negotiating effect between market orientations and organizational learning.

The following hypothesis was made based on the presentation of these two second-order variables: Big Data Analytics Capability and Product Innovation Performance.

- H1a. Big Data Analytics Capability positively influences Product Innovation Performance.

### 3.2.2 Process-oriented dynamic capabilities

Process-oriented dynamic capabilities help to understand how the firm is facing methodologies and business processes compared to the competition. Product development is an example of process-oriented dynamic capabilities and includes the basic elements of strategizing (practice, process and practitioners). Together with learning and isomorphic mechanisms present in the organizational field, they explain how the product development process occurs (Goes, 2014; McAfee and Brynjolfsson, 2012). We see Big Data Analytics Capability as an important organizational capability, leading to a sustainable competitive advantage in the Big Data environment (Singh and El-Kassar, 2018). Organizations must have specialists with the knowledge and experience of working in a competitive and highly dynamic market, which requires the adoption of Big Data as a way to remain competitive (Eisenhardt and Martin, 2000; Helfat et al., 2007; Wamba et al., 2017), so the following hypotheses were elaborated:

- H1b. Big Data Analytics Capability positively influences process-oriented dynamic capabilities.
- H2. Process-oriented dynamic capabilities influence Product Innovation Performance.
- H3. Big Data Analytics Capability positively influences Product Innovation Performance when it is mediated by process-oriented dynamic capabilities.

### 3.2.3 Business Value

Big Data Analytics Capability evaluation results in an affective or emotional response, such as Business Value. Given this, the existing literature identifies that the analytical excellence of Big Data has a significant positive impact on Business Value. The literature suggests that the greater the perception of the novelty of products and services on the market, the greater the potential value for users involved in the firm (Nambisan et al., 2017). Therefore, it can bring many benefits to the firm, among them, a closer relationship with the client and the development of its analytics capability (Chen et al., 2014). Therefore, the significance of the association between Big Data Analytics Capability and Business Value was highlighted by the existing literature (LaValle et al., 2011; Wixom et al., 2013).

Competitive advantage is achieved by deploying and using distinct, valuable and inimitable resources and capabilities (Bhatt and Grover, 2005). The concept of Information Technology capacity assumes that, although resources can be easily replicated, a distinct set of resources mobilized by a firm is not easy to replicate and will lead to competitive advantages (Santanam and Hartono, 2003).

As part of incorporating higher-order Big Data Analytics Capability into a causal network, the study models it with criteria variables, such as Business Value (Akter et al., 2017). Business Value is seen as the degree of perceived benefits for the organization at a strategic level, such as competitive advantage.

The empirical analysis assumes that a firm's ability to obtain value from Big Data Analytics Capability is influenced by complementary factors, such as technical and managerial capacity, as well as the presence of a data-based organizational culture (Sena et al., 2019). The final products or services offered to allow you to gain value from a good customer experience. This means that organizational goals have been achieved. The success of technical work or investments associated with computational technologies in Big Data Analytics Capability can generate costs and produce revenue bringing Business Value (Bowman, 2003; Chang, 2017). This analytics capability on the Business Value is a dominant concern in the Big Data environment. Business Value is one of the central items of Big Data, as it is directly influenced by its Big Data Analytical Capability (Wixom et al., 2013). In this sense, the following hypotheses were elaborated:

- H1c. Big Data Analytics Capability positively influences Business Value.
