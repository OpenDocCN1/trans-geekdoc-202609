As is a significant innovation, blockchain has applications not only in finance and economy but also in health, science, art and culture (Chang et al., 2020; Swan, 2015). Many businesses and firms have started to use this innovative and modem platform widely; for example, some companies or startups use this platform to match customers and producers directly or build a scientific network for sharing scientific research and even monitoring food distribution (Lee, 2019).

Cryptocurrencies are the product of blockchain, so they inherit the benefits and drawbacks of this technology. However, blockchain is evolving, and since the emergence of the first generation of blockchain was embodied in Bitcoin, two generations of blockchain have developed. The second generation of blockchain emerged based on the idea of smart contracts and can be represented by Ethereum. Also, the third generation of blockchain has wide applications and can be used in different fields of science and technology (Chang et al., 2020; Swan, 2015). Thus, blockchains are advancing and maturing quickly, especially regarding scalability and system performance via improving the consensus algorithms (Lee, 2019) as in the decentralized network in the first generation of the blockchain (Zheng et al., 2017). For instance, in the PoS method, nodes with more coins (or share) prioritize creating blocks for accelerating transaction processing (Lee, 2019). The cryptocurrencies created after Bitcoin use these new consensus mechanisms that make them more applicable with enhanced technology (Lee, 2019) (the examples of different cryptocurrencies will be shown in the following section under the title of cryptocurrencies). Besides, using these new methods and technology by new cryptocurrencies can help them overcome the initial cryptocurrencies’ challenges.

There are thousands of cryptocurrencies nowadays with different functionalities and applications; however, the debate on cryptocurrencies is still open. Conceptually, cryptocurrencies have three functionalities of money (Ali et al., 2014): the storage of value, a medium of exchange and a unit of account. Some researchers hold that they are best regarded as technology rather than a currency (Maurer et al., 2013). However, others believe that they are like assets rather than currencies (Baek and Elbeck, 2015; Blau, 2017; Dyrhrberg, 2016b; Katsiampa et al., 2019). Yermack (2013) claims that Bitcoin seems to be a speculative asset than a currency as the transactions carried out by Bitcoin are far fewer than its market capitalization. Also, it is claimed that although there is high volatility in Bitcoin prices, it is profitable enough to include it in a diversified portfolio (Eisl et al., 2015; Gandal and Halaburda, 2014).

Overall, regardless of the nature of cryptocurrencies, the cryptocurrency market has developed significantly over the past years in terms of both market valuation and product liquidity. The cryptocurrency market cap was 289 billion dollars in August 2019. However, by August 2020, the cryptocurrency market cap reaches 338 billion dollars, which shows a 16% increase. Bitcoin, Ethereum and Ripple have the highest share in the cryptocurrency market capitalization gain throughout the years.
