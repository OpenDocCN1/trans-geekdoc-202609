# 4.2 Practical implications

Blockchain-based identity management systems aim to fundamentally block the hacking of corporate and personal information as well as the unauthorized use of such information through decentralization, which allows people to directly manage their personal data. The proposed blockchain-based system simplifies the identification process and increases the ease of use through an online (mobile) service platform. It can reduce operating costs by improving the identification methods and reducing the service processing time. Eliminating the need for physical cards or tickets – which can become lost and require reissuing – can further reduce operating costs. In addition, the proper reuse of personal information can increase the efficiency of the process between users and validators, and the conversion of the online service operation system can increase the productivity of the entire community, including both the public and private sectors.

By storing and managing personal information on the blockchain and providing mobile apps to customers, this system allows users to log in or retrieve previously authenticated personal information without having to go through an authentication process every time. As it does not require a verification process every time, it is expected that only the necessary personal information can be used more quickly and conveniently, without needing to provide unnecessary details. Moreover, blockchain-based public service operation effectively increases the transparency and reliability of services and reduces social costs caused by personal information leakage. By introducing standardized platforms such as AWS, unnecessary system deployment costs were reduced, and flexibility and scalability in data were secured. We also considered the scalability of the authentication service by providing various additional services through the provision of app services. Indeed, South Korea is currently accelerating the adoption of blockchain-based digital IDs through a series of new rollouts and consolidation measures. For example, airline passengers will soon be able to board a plane with their digital identity, which will be provided through government apps (Identity Review, 2020). Secure blockchain-based mobile authentication reduces the risk of identity theft, unlike physical identity cards which reveal personal information. In terms of industrial expectations, the introduction of DID-based ID cards and the dissemination of them to public services can speed up the development of blockchain technology.

Lastly, the development and introduction of blockchain-based identification systems affect human resource development and employment. This study has reviewed a government project that developed a server and mobile (user/verifier) apps around blockchain technology. As a result, we have found that blockchain developers and experts are scarce and must be nurtured. It is critical to properly educate and train skilled workers for blockchain work, as developing a blockchain-based system requires the handling of sensitive personal information. It is desirable to train talented people to have a good understanding of the overall process and knowledge of the blockchain; in this way, we can increase the employment rate of the skilled workforce centered around the development of blockchain-based systems.

Regarding the practical aspects of blockchain implementation, there are several challenges that remain to be solved. The first issues to consider are latency, size and bandwidth (Swan, 2015). Blockchain involves a relatively slow response, because the number of bitcoin transactions that occur per second is small. The pilot of this design shows that the processing speed of using blockchain technology in the system is relatively slow. In general, using a database will incur around 10,000 transactions per second (TPS); for blockchain, it will not exceed 1,500 TPS, no matter how fast the system is. This shows the need for research aiming to improve the shortcomings of these blockchains.
