Therefore, this study presents a case of applying blockchain to the public sector, specifically in e-government, by introducing the actual application of a blockchain-based identification system. This study promotes the use of blockchain in the public sector and provides a foundational understanding for further research examining the potential benefits of blockchain technology. The following section presents the possible research directions for blockchain adoption in government.

Existing identity management systems convey the contents of the identification card to a central system to be checked and authenticated by a corporate or national central authority. This system is at risk of being compromised by various hacking and counterfeiting incidents, which makes it difficult for individuals and institutions to gain trust in the system. By introducing blockchain technology instead of a central server that manages identity information in a distributed network, hacking or counterfeiting issues can be addressed transparently. Users can also manage their own personal information and make their own decisions about how their information is shared. This type of personal identification access with self-determination provides easy log in access to multiple home pages, thus simplifying the steps involved in identification and authentication methods. However, despite the advantages of blockchain-based identity systems, there are still various challenges associated with using blockchain in the government sector: First, there is a lack of contextual understanding of and user experience with blockchain-based systems. The design of a blockchain-based identity management system enables the reuse of information, thereby improving the granularity with which users can disclose their identity information. However, it is still daunting to build trust in these attributes, because users have a noticeable lack of experience with the emerging approach to identity management systems (Dunphy and Petitcolas, 2018). This means there is a need for research into user experience to determine whether users can intuitively understand the meaning of the DID attribute. Future studies should focus on understanding the context of DID and user acceptance in blockchain-based identity management systems and on how to build trust and reputation between users and businesses. As suggested by Gil-García and Flores-Zúñiga (2020) in the Implementation Adoption Model of Digital Government Success, it is advisable to study the implementation and adoption of blockchain technology in the public sector.
