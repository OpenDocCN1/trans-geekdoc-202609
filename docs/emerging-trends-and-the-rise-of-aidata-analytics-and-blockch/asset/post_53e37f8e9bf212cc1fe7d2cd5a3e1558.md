## 4. Contributions

To our knowledge, this systematic literature review is the first attempt to structure IoT security threats according to a two-dimensional framework; IoT architectural layers and security goals. More importantly, the framework facilitated the classification of the security mechanisms that help address IoT threats. Additionally, the methodical process that we followed (see section 5) to identify the most pertinent topics vis-à-vis blockchain and analyze – first systematically and second subjectively – the large body of research on the topic provides validity to our findings. Hence, another contribution of this work is in the adopted methodology that combined natural language processing (NLP) suggested by Siddiqi and Sharan (2015) to analyze a large corpus of articles with the review approach specified by Okoli and Schabram (2010) to systematic review the relevant literature. By adopting an NLP method, we sifted through 1,851 blockchain related articles that were published in the past 5 years and identified the most prominent congruent topic alongside blockchain. This resulted in identifying blockchain-driven IoT security as the most trending topic with 217 relevant articles. We further selected 43 primary studies that meet the criteria we set for quality assessment. These studies can provide suitable benchmarks for relative studies contrary to similar research. Finally, we conducted a comprehensive analysis of the data contained within the subset of 43 studies and organized the data in a way to answer the aforementioned research questions.

## 5. Research methodology

This work is guided by the systematic literature review methodology described in Okoli and Schabram (2010) which specifies the techniques for literature identification, selection, and quality evaluation. To ensure that our review is comprehensive, we kept our article search terms as broad as possible and incorporated the natural language processing (NLP) method specified by Siddiqi and Sharan (2015) that helps automate the preliminary analysis of a large corpus of articles. Figure 1 explains the methodology process.

### 5.1 Selection of primary studies

This literature review is concerned with the applicability of blockchain technology as a security mechanism for IoT ecosystems. To identify the relevant articles, we broadened our search to include all articles that refer to the terms “blockchain” or “Distributed ledger technology” in the title or abstract published between 2015 and 2020. The databases searched were Web of Science, IEEE Xplore Digital Library, ACM Digital Library and Scopus. A total of 2,303 articles were identified out of which 1851 were retained after removing all duplicates.

### 5.3 Data analysis results

The first phase of analysis revealed that blockchain technology emerged as an infrastructure for value exchange via cryptocurrencies. The NLP analysis of the 1,851 abstracts shows that the main topics investigated in the early stages of blockchain inception relates to fund security and confidence in cryptocurrencies. Nevertheless, blockchain matured to become a generic secure medium for data communication. Topics such as healthcare, supply chain, smart-grids were researched alongside blockchain with blockchain-based IoT ecosystems being the top investigated topic in 2019 and 2020. Figure A1 and Table A1 in Appendix depicts the most investigated topics related to blockchain overtime. The title analysis revealed similar patterns (see Appendix). IoT security remained the dominant area of blockchain research for 2019–2020 while security in general was the dominant area of blockchain research between 2016 and 2018. The keyword analysis also led to similar results, in which “security” has been the dominant topic for the years 2017, 2018 and 2019, while “IoT” is the dominant topic for the year 2020.

Based on our trend analysis (see results in Appendix), it is clear that over the past five years, “security” and “IoT” became the most researched topics alongside blockchain while “fawning” and “bitcoins” are less relevant. This substantiates the claim that blockchain is developing toward becoming a suitable security platform in different industries including energy, healthcare, and supply chain but most importantly for IoT ecosystems.

### 5.4 Inclusion and exclusion criteria

To be included in the review, only articles that investigated IoT security and blockchain were selected. The NLP method we adopted facilitated the identification of 1,033 articles that address blockchain-based IoT research. Only empirical studies published between 2015 and 2020 that are written in English were included. Table 2 shows the key inclusion and exclusion criteria. After checking against the inclusion and exclusion criteria, we remained with 217 articles.

### 5.5 Quality assessment

In order to find the relevance of the papers to the research questions, we evaluated the quality of the papers based on article quality criteria. The assessment process followed the instructions of Orabi et al. (2020). Accordingly, a quality assessment checklist was formed based on seven quality rules. If an article fulfils the rule, one mark is added to its score. Articles that partially fulfils the rule or fails to meet it, get 0.5 or 0 mark respectively. Only articles that score five or more out of seven were included in our review. The quality assessment rules are listed here:

- (1) Are the research goals clearly identified?
- (2) Does the article contain adequate background information on IoT security threats?
- (3) Are the security threats within every IoT layer clearly defined?
- (4) Does the article propose blockchain as a solution for IoT security issues?
- (5) Does the article include the security mechanisms provided by blockchain?
- (6) Are the security threats of blockchain when implemented for IoT identified?
- (7) Is the article well structured?

Two graduates who hold a master’s degree in computer science and specialized in security were tasked to evaluate the selected articles. Each judge coded the 217 articles based on the above 7 quality criteria. We used Cohen’s kappa to evaluate the level of agreement between the two judges. After two rounds of evaluation and re-examination of inconsistencies, an inter-rater agreement (Cohen’s Kappa) of 0.88 was achieved. As a result, 174 articles scored less than 5 out of 7 and therefore were removed from our SLR. In order to ensure the reliability of the raters’ scores when evaluating the quality of the included articles, we conducted an Intraclass Correlation (ICC) test based on the analysis of variance (ANOVA) and compared the variability in their ratings. Accordingly, ICC value achieved was 0.86 indicating a high similarity between judges’ ratings.

### 5.6 Data extraction

All papers that have passed the quality assessment phase, had their data extracted. The data were extracted based on the relevance to the research goals and questions. The data below was extracted from the 43 papers:

- (1) Author(s), article title, publication date, journal name, volume and issue.
- (2) IoT layers (when available).
- (3) Security threats within IoT layers and corresponding countermeasures.
- (4) Unsolved IoT security issues, i.e., IoT security challenges.
- (5) Blockchain mechanisms used for addressing IoT security threats.
- (6) New security threats posed by blockchain when addressing IoT security issues.

## 6. Findings

### 6.1 IoT security threats

#### 6.1.1 RQ1: What are the existing security threats to IoT devices?

The IoT security threats identified from the select literature were organized based on a two-dimensional framework comprised of (1) the four IoT architectural layers (see Burhan et al., 2018; Sengupta et al., 2020) and (2) the five IoT security goals (see Alaba et al., 2017; Kouicem et al., 2018). This framework facilitated the classification of threats and accentuated the IoT layers that are most vulnerable to attacks and the security goals that are most affected.

The IoT Architecture Framework adopted consists of four layers: perception, network, processing and application (Burhan et al., 2018; Sengupta et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2019).
