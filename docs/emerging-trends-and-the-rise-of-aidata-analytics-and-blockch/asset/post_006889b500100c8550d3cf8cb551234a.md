
The estimation of the Bayesian DCC-MGARCH parameter for the bivariate combinations of the cryptocurrencies and the return of stock indices of blockchain-based technology companies are reported in Tables 8–11. The results indicate that the hypothesis of the CCC model ($a = b = 0$) is rejected based on the marginal posterior distributions of the DCC conditional correlation parameters $a$ and $b$, like the general model. Hence, the assumption of constant correlations between cryptocurrency and the return of stock indices of blockchain-based technology company pairs might be inaccurate or misleading (Katzke, 2013; Qian and Diaz, 2017; Shiferaw, 2019). Besides, the necessary condition of $a + b < 1$ is satisfied, which implies that the model is compatible in estimating the dynamic correlations (Katzke, 2013). As skewed mvGED is selected as the optimal distribution for error terms based on EAIC, EBIC, DIC and wDIC statistics values, the values for $\delta$ must be greater than zero.

The DCC-MGARCH with mvGED estimates of the correlation between selected indices of blockchain-based technology companies and cryptocurrencies is shown in Figures 1–4. The high volatility of the conditional correlation in some periods indicates a dynamic correlation between selected the return of stock indices of blockchain-based technology companies and cryptocurrencies.

Table 12 shows the percentage of the number of days when there is a dynamic, positive conditional correlation between cryptocurrencies and indices of blockchain-based technology companies to the total number of sampling days. Returns of XMR, XRP, DSH and XLM have the highest percentage of days with a positive correlation with the whole sampling days with the return of indices of blockchain-based technology companies, respectively. Moreover, among the stock indices of blockchain-based technology companies, AF and DB have a positive conditional dynamic correlation with cryptocurrencies for 85 and 78% of the sampling days, respectively, which is the highest compared to other indices. Bitcoin, Litecoin and Ethereum positively correlate with the return of stock indices of blockchain-based technology companies for 62, 67 and 17% of sampling days on average, respectively. However, except for Bitcoin, Litecoin and Ethereum, other cryptocurrencies' returns have a positive dynamic conditional correlation with the return of stock indices of blockchain-based technology companies for above 90% of whole sampling days. Besides, the returns of stock indices of blockchain-based technology companies also have a positive dynamic conditional correlation with cryptocurrencies' price returns for more than 70% of the sampling days. These results indicate a positive linkage between the price return of cryptocurrency and the returns of stock indices of blockchain-based technology companies. As mentioned in the introduction section, the positive linkage between cryptocurrencies and the return of stock indices of blockchain-based technology companies can be due to two reasons.

The first reason is concerned about investors' sentiments toward cryptocurrencies and blockchain technology and the companies that operate on that platform as many studies show that the sentiment of investors can affect the cryptocurrencies prices as well as stock prices (Garcia et al., 2014; Ho et al., 2017; Ji et al., 2020; Kraaijeveld and De Smedt, 2020; Kristoufek, 2013; Li et al., 2019b, 2020; López-Cabarcos et al., 2021; Teti et al., 2019; Verma et al., 2008). On the other hand, both cryptocurrencies and blockchain-based technology companies are backed by blockchain technology, and any advancement or decline in this technology affects investor sentiments. So, it can affect the cryptocurrency price return and return of blockchain-based technology companies in the same direction.

The other reason for the linkage between these companies and cryptocurrency is the fact that the main driver of cryptocurrencies and blockchain-based technology companies is technological innovation (Umar et al., 2021), and any innovation in blockchain technology or cryptocurrencies can affect both the popularity and application of cryptocurrencies and efficiency and capabilities of these blockchain-based technology companies.
