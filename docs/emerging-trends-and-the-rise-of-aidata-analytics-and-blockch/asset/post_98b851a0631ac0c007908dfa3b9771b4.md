## Understanding of blockchain-based identity management system adoption in the public sector

Chang Soo Sung
Department of Technology Entrepreneurship, Dongguk University,
Seoul, Republic of Korea, and
Joo Yeon Park
Discipline of Information Technology, Media and Communication,
Murdoch University, Perth, Australia

### Abstract

Purpose – This study aims to understand the benefits and challenges associated with the adoption of a blockchain-based identity management system in public services by conducting an academic literature review, and to explore the design of such a system that can be applied to the Korean government.

Design/methodology/approach – This study explores the adoption of a blockchain-based identity management system using a literature review and an actual design case intended for use by the government sector.

Findings – Blockchain-based identity management systems can significantly improve transparency, accountability, and reliability in the user control of one’s own data while reducing the time and cost needed to deliver public services, as well as increasing administrative efficiency. However, it is not always easy to implement such systems, and introducing new technologies in the government field requires a complicated, time-consuming process. There is currently an appetite for research extending beyond the typical technology-driven approach to elucidate the government adoption of new technologies and explore its implications.

Practical implications – The idea behind this system is that by storing and managing personal information on the blockchain and providing mobile apps to customers, users can log in or retrieve previously authenticated personal information without having to go through an authentication process. Since users do not need to go through the verification process every time, it is expected that they will be able to access only the necessary personal information more quickly and conveniently without having to deal with unnecessary details. In addition, the blockchain-based operation of a public service effectively increases the transparency and reliability of that service and reduces the social costs caused by personal information leakage.

Originality/value – This study introduces the design of a blockchain-based identity management system that can be used in public services, specifically in the Korean government sector for the first time. Along with a literature review, the implications that this study gleans from these real-world use cases can contribute to this field of research.

Keywords Blockchain technology, Identity management system, Decentralized systems, Public service, E-government
Paper type Literature review

### 1. Introduction

The rapid growth of new technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI), data analytics and blockchain, along with the increasing interest in the various benefits offered by such technologies, have led to the current era of hyper-connected networks. This hyperconnectivity, along with the emergence of the internet of things (IoT) environment, has the potential to provide substantial benefits to various industries and public sectors, specifically in terms of enhancing effectiveness and efficiency. Companies have placed a high priority on digital transformation, as they expect that new technologies will make increasingly large strategic contributions to their overall business (Bonnet et al., 2012).

They assess their ambitions in digital transformations in the form of adopting new technologies and changing their business models, products, processes and organizational structures with the aim of value creation (Downes and Nunes, 2013; Hess et al., 2016; Matt et al., 2015). For example, leading technology companies have invested heavily in blockchain-based IoT, and many manufacturing firms have incorporated blockchain in their supply chains to enhance customer value and product quality (Angelus and Silva, 2019; Kshetri and Loukoiovanova, 2019).

Many national and local governments have also adopted new emerging technologies in their digital government strategies to carry out innovative initiatives. It has been reported that more than 84% of nations offer digital government services that take place through online transactions (UN, 2020). The Organisation for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD) has recommended that nations pursue digital government strategies that include openness, transparency and inclusion, citizen participation in policymaking and changes to relevant governance and legal frameworks (OECD, 2020). An ideal digital strategy also includes the coherent use of digital technology in various policy areas, e.g. intelligent data analytics could assist police departments in predicting security threats, specifically by contributing to resource allocation and decision-making in police departments (Savage, 2018). The current demand for user-driven, transparent and cost-effective public services is leading to increased expectations for the digital services offered by governments. In particular, amid pandemics such as the current COVID-19 pandemic, emerging technologies are rapidly being implemented in public service applications; e.g. AI and sensors are used in practice to measure and control social distances. In addition, AI and analytics tools are used in virtual medical treatment, and communication engagement apps are being built to enable direct interaction with citizens and constituents (GSDM, 2020). Digital government services are expected to have many benefits in building trust between a government and its citizens, as well as in increasing operational efficiency. However, despite all the benefits associated with adopting emerging technologies in the government sector, most digital government projects are unsuccessful, as they achieve inefficient results while wasting large amounts of development funds (Baheer et al., 2020). In addition, when adopting new technology, there are several issues that must be considered, such as scalability, as well as regulatory and ethical dilemmas, including data security and privacy (Chang et al., 2020). These issues raise questions about how new technologies are being implemented in the public sector and how they can support public services.

The digitization movement has led to the creation and trade of huge amounts of data, which can be an essential resource for innovation growth and stimulating the national economy. It is expected that a nation’s competitiveness can be revitalized through the efficient use of data combined with advanced data processing and analysis technologies. Meanwhile, as the value of data continues growing, the balance between active service and privacy is becoming an increasingly important topic. To this end, the authority to protect and control personal information is expanding to the subjects of the information itself (Couldry and Mejias, 2019). As a result, there has been an increasing research interest in information sovereignty, which is the right for individuals to directly identify and control information about themselves. There has also been a growing discussion about legal amendments, technology and services for the enforcement of information sovereignty. Among the various emerging technologies in this space, blockchain technology is attracting attention as a new technology that can lead to a new horizon in data sovereignty, with its characteristics of decentralization, accessibility and transparency.
