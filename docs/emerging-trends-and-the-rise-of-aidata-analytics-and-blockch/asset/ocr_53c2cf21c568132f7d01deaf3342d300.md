# Results

Following the research of Fioruci et al. (2014) and Shiferaw (2019), from the posterior distribution in the Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling, 20,000 samples were generated. The first 5,000 were dismissed as the burn-in, and the rest of the generated samples were used for estimation. In this study, an MCMC within the Metropolis–Hastings algorithm applies to daily cryptocurrencies and the return of stock indices of blockchain-based technology companies.

In MCMC updates based on the Metropolis–Hastings algorithm, all the parameters are updated as a block. A random walk version of the Metropolis algorithm is employed where new values at each iteration are generated from a multivariate normal distribution centered around the current value with a variance-covariance proposal matrix, as calculated from a pilot tuning procedure (Fioruci et al., 2014). This pilot tuning was conducted by running one-dimensional random walk Metropolis updates with univariate normal candidate distributions whose variances were calibrated to attain reasonable acceptance rates (Fioruci et al., 2014). The goodness of fit statistics, including EAIC, EBIC and DIC values for obtaining the different Bayesian ADC-MGARCH models, are presented in Table 6.

Based on EAIC, EBIC and DIC, the Bayesian DCC-MGARCH model with skewed mvGED yields a better fit than other models because it can express the fat tails and skewed features in the cryptocurrencies returns and the return of stock indices of blockchain-based technology companies.
