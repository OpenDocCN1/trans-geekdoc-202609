## Introduction

The introduction of cryptocurrencies as a new and disruptive technology by Nakamoto is regarded as a revolution in financial markets and systems of payments. This innovation resulting from blockchain technology makes electronic transactions cheaper, more comfortable and quicker by removing intermediaries. These currencies are Internet-based payment protocols, and they do not need government or other third parties to verify transactions. Cryptocurrencies have drawn massive attention from academics, policymakers, investors and media (Böhme et al., 2015). The blockchain technology that supports cryptocurrencies offers a decentralized system for creating new crypto and confirming transactions.

As mentioned, the blockchain is the cornerstone of cryptocurrencies, introduced by Bitcoin to the financial area. A blockchain is a block of data chained together. It is a major offshoot of financial technology or fintech (Chang et al., 2020). As it becomes widespread, it dramatically changes businesses (DiNizo, 2018). The most significant characteristic of blockchain is a decentralized system that results in simpler, cheaper and faster transactions (Staples et al., 2017). Together, these characteristics can lead to financial, commercial and even industrial revolutions and change the whole economic system around the world. Chang et al. (2020) mention four major characteristics of blockchain, including decentralization, meaning that the database and history of a transaction are accessible for either side of transactions without any permission from a third party (Tapscott and Tapscott, 2017). This feature also restricts individuals or institutions from committing criminal activities or fraud (Rennick et al., 2018). The other feature of blockchain is the user’s anonymity. Any participant in the network has a unique alphanumeric address, and no third party stores the information of users (Tapscott and Tapscott, 2017; Zheng et al., 2018). The next feature is the consensus mechanism meaning that 51% of the nodes in the network should approve creating a new record in the network (Huang et al., 2019). Finally, the last feature is the execution, referred to as the ability of users to carry out transactions between nodes based on some rules and algorithms.

The blockchain has many potentials to change the whole financial infrastructure (Pilkington, 2016), although it is immature and contains some issues and challenges, like security, privacy, latency and scalability (Underwood, 2016). Drescher (2017) emphasizes the openness and the nonexistence of the coordinator party that is the fundamental feature of the blockchain system as the major barrier to the widespread usage of blockchain. Some researchers consider four challenges related to the blockchain, including scalability, security, privacy and leakage energy consumption (Chang et al., 2020; Drescher, 2017). These challenges hinder the widespread usage of blockchain. Scalability points out the relatively slow procedure of conducting transactions by some cryptocurrencies, like Bitcoin and Ethereum compared to Visa or PayPal (Jackson, 2018) because of its encrypted and distributed nature and complexity (Marr, 2018). Security refers to the idea that blockchain is prone to attacks from miners (Werbach, 2018; Zheng et al., 2018). There is also some clue that blockchain is prone to privacy leakages as the information of transactions is open to the public (Kosba et al., 2016; Meiklejohn et al., 2013), and it is possible to find users’ profiles by tracing their transactions (Barcelo, 2014). The last challenge is the high energy consumption of blockchain-based money and extensive data. They consume more energy than ordinary electronic money transfers (Staples et al., 2017). In addition, Chang et al. (2020) mention three ethical issues concerning the blockchain, specifically privacy risks, regulatory uncertainties and illegal and criminal activities within the network.

As a significant innovation, blockchain has applications not only in finance and economy but also in health, science, art and culture (Chang et al., 2020; Swan, 2015). Many businesses and firms have started to use this innovative and modern platform widely; for example, some companies or startups use this platform to match customers and producers directly or build a scientific network for sharing scientific research and even monitoring food distribution (Lee, 2019).

Cryptocurrencies are the product of blockchain, so they inherit the benefits and drawbacks of this technology. However, blockchain is evolving, and since the emergence of the first generation of blockchain was embodied in Bitcoin, two generations of blockchain have developed. The second generation of blockchain emerged based on the idea of smart contracts and can be represented by Ethereum. Also, the third generation of blockchain has wide applications and can be used in different fields of science and technology (Chang et al., 2020; Swan, 2015). Thus, blockchains are advancing and maturing quickly, especially regarding scalability and system performance via improving the consensus algorithms (Lee, 2019) as in the decentralized network in the first generation of the blockchain (Zheng et al., 2017). It is a procedure of verifying transactions and storing them in blocks by nodes in the network. However, it is a relatively slow process (Zheng et al., 2017). Accordingly, other consensus mechanisms, including PoS [1], DPoS [2] and PBFT [3], have been introduced to improve the downside of PoW [4]. For instance, in the PoS method, nodes with more coins (or share) prioritize creating blocks for accelerating transaction processing (Lee, 2019). The cryptocurrencies created after Bitcoin use these new consensus mechanisms that make them more applicable with enhanced technology (Lee, 2019) (the examples of different cryptocurrencies will be shown in the following section under the title of cryptocurrencies). Besides, using these new methods and technology by new cryptocurrencies can help them overcome the initial cryptocurrencies’ challenges.

There are thousands of cryptocurrencies nowadays with different functionalities and applications; however, the debate on cryptocurrencies is still open. Conceptually, cryptocurrencies have three functionalities of money (Ali et al., 2014): the storage of value, a medium of exchange and a unit of account. Some researchers hold that they are best regarded as technology rather than a currency (Maurer et al., 2013). However, others believe that they are like assets rather than currencies (Baek and Elbeck, 2015; Blau, 2017; Dyrhrberg, 2016b; Katsiampa et al., 2019). Yermack (2013) claims that Bitcoin seems to be a speculative asset than a currency as the transactions carried out by Bitcoin are far fewer than its market capitalization. Also, it is claimed that although there is high volatility in Bitcoin prices, it is profitable enough to include it in a diversified portfolio (Eisl et al., 2015; Gandal and Halaburda, 2014).

Overall, regardless of the nature of cryptocurrencies, the cryptocurrency market has developed significantly over the past years in terms of both market valuation and product liquidity. The cryptocurrency market cap was 289 billion dollars in August 2019. However, by August 2020, the cryptocurrency market cap reaches 338 billion dollars, which shows a 16% increase. Bitcoin, Ethereum and Ripple have the highest share in the cryptocurrency market capitalization gain throughout the years.
