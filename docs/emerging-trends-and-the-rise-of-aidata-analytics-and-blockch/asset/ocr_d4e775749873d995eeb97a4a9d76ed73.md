Chen and Bellavitis (2020) investigate the benefits, challenges and limitations of blockchain technology. They argue that as this technology is a decentralized peer-to-peer network, it can lower the transaction cost. In addition, this technology can increase the interpretability of financial services and encourage more innovation in the financial system. They also claim that blockchain technology is borderless and transparent. They also mention some challenges blockchain technology faces, including illegal activities, high volatility in cryptocurrency prices, usefulness and regulatory uncertainty. Zhang et al. (2020b) investigate blockchain technology and its applications and challenges in finance and economics. They claim that blockchain solves two main problems that concern the digital economy by delivering value via the chain and building trust without any cost. However, they refer to some challenges that blockchain may face, like some limitations in processing transactions, difficulties to impose regulation to prevent illegal activities and lack of consensus among countries in accepting cryptocurrencies. Lee (2019) investigates how blockchain technology and cryptocurrencies have developed and evolved over time and claims that this technology and cryptocurrencies constantly evolve by adopting more efficient mechanisms and methods. In addition, companies have tried to adapt to this technology increasingly. He also argues that blockchain technology is expected to be a major technology in developing the token economy as a new economic paradigm in the future. Thus, almost all of these studies emphasize that blockchain technology has great potentials and many applications in economy and finance; however, this technology is in its infancy, and it faces some challenges that need to be addressed.

There is also plenty of literature that examines the different characteristics of cryptocurrencies, especially Bitcoin. For example, the price discovery procedure has been examined by Brandvold et al. (2015), Kapar and Olmo (2019), Baur and Dimpfl (2019) and Brauneis and Mestel (2018). Besides, the existence of bubbles in cryptocurrencies has also been assessed at several studies such as Cheah and Fry (2015), Cheung et al. (2015), Corbet et al. (2018a), Fry and Cheah (2016). Also, the presence of structural breaks in Bitcoin returns has been analyzed by Thies and Molnár (2018), Mensi et al. (2019) and Bouri et al. (2019). The volatility in the price of the cryptocurrency has also been examined by Ardia et al. (2019), Chaim and Laurini (2018), Katsiampa (2017), Phillip et al. (2018) and Troster et al. (2019). However, these topics are not our primary concern in this study.

In the following, we focus on studies that address the entity of cryptocurrencies, the interconnection among the price return of different cryptocurrencies and the interaction between the price return of cryptocurrencies and other classes of assets, like gold, currencies, commodities and other securities. At the end of this section, we will present some studies examining the interaction between stock returns of technology companies and other factors and focus on two studies investigating the interaction between cryptocurrencies and blockchain-based technology companies and technology sectors. Finally, we present some studies that analyzed the impact of investors' sentiments on the price return of cryptocurrencies and the stock prices, specifically the stock prices of technology companies.

Some literature addressed the entity and the fundamental role of cryptocurrencies. A few studies claim that cryptocurrencies can be slightly regarded as currency. For instance, Selgin (2015) argues that Bitcoin belongs to a new class of money called the synthetic commodity money class. They do not have intrinsic value like fiat money, and they are scarce like commodity money like Bitcoin with limited supply and no intrinsic value. Also, Kaplanov (2012) believes that Bitcoin is more like commodity money. Dyhrberg (2016a) investigates the nature of Bitcoin using the GARCH model. She mentions that Bitcoin resembles both gold and currency. Then, she concludes that Bitcoin is something between currency and commodities and can have the advantages of both of them so it can be used for risk analysis and portfolio management.
