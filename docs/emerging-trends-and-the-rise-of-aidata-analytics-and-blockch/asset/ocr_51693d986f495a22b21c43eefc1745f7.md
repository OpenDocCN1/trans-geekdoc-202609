# Abstract

Purpose – Decision-making in human beings is affected by emotions and sentiments. The affective computing takes this into account, intending to tailor decision support to the emotional states of people. However, the representation and classification of emotions is a very challenging task. The study used customized methods of deep learning models to aid in the accurate classification of emotions and sentiments. Design/methodology/approach – The present study presents affective computing model using both text and image data. The text-based affective computing was conducted on four standard datasets using three deep learning customized models, namely LSTM, GRU and CNN. The study used four variants of deep learning including the LSTM model, LSTM model with GloVe embeddings, Bi-directional LSTM model and LSTM model with attention layer. Findings – The result suggests that the proposed method outperforms the earlier methods. For image-based affective computing, the data was extracted from Instagram, and Facial emotion recognition was carried out using three deep learning models, namely CNN, transfer learning with VGG-19 model and transfer learning with ResNet-18 model. The results suggests that the proposed methods for both text and image can be used for affective computing and aid in decision-making. Originality/value – The study used deep learning for affective computing. Earlier studies have used machine learning algorithms for affective computing. However, the present study uses deep learning for affective computing.

Keywords Affective computing, Deep learning, Visualization, LSTM, CNN, GRU

Paper type Research paper

# 1. Introduction

We live in the age of social media, where people publicly convey their emotions and feelings on social media platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, Twitter and Snapchat. The emotions expressed on social media platforms can be positive, negative and neutral (Tomkins, 1962). Some of the studies have found that people on social media mirror the positive or negative emotions that their friends express in their posts (Kramer, 2015). People who see more “positive” posts in their feeds responds more positively, while the people who see more “negative” posts in their feeds responds with negative feelings of their own (Kramer et al., 2014). Thus, the expression of emotions on social media plays a significant role for the individuals and their immediate connections on social media. Human emotions can be expressed in the form of anger, joy, sadness, surprise, trust, anticipation and so on. Affective computing, in general, refers to the techniques for recognizing, interpreting, detecting and predicting such human emotions (Picard, 2000).

Affective computing has lately become a matter of great interest to various researchers from various domains like those of artificial intelligence and social science research. The analysis of affective computing can be grouped into two streams, namely, unimodal analysis and multimodal analysis. The unimodal analysis refers to the analysis of only one mode of affective computing, i.e. either text OR images OR audios OR videos. On the other hand, the multimodal analysis refers to multiple modes of affective computing, i.e. either the combination of text and images OR a combination of images and videos OR a combination of text, audio, images and so on. The current work uses two separate unimodal analyses for understanding affective computing. The unimodal analysis was conducted on both text and image data.
