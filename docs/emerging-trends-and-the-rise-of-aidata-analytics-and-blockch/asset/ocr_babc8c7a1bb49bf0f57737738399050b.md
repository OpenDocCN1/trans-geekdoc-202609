An immutable blockchain property is that data cannot be removed, which can lead to privacy concerns. However, general data protection regulations require organizations to store personal data only for as long as required for their original purpose. These regulations should be reviewed with respect to the new data transaction methods offered by blockchain and other emerging technologies. It should be noted that changes in the legal framework often lag behind the pace of technology development due to the involvement of multiple actors (Batubara et al., 2018).

For blockchain application to the government sector, it is also important to establish a new governance model for usability and acceptance. Most of the problems associated with using blockchain for recording and verification have been attributed to the strict regulations on data protection and privacy, as well as a lack of infrastructure and public understanding (Stanford Business, 2019). In addition, most countries do not have clear legislation regulating digital identities based on blockchain.

# 2.3 E-government and blockchain

With the continued innovation in emerging technologies, e-government or digital government initiatives have increased substantially worldwide (Baheer et al., 2020; Gil-García and Flores-Zúñiga, 2020; Tambouris et al., 2014). For example, major countries such as Denmark, Australia, South Korea and the UK are now using advanced technologies in their government sector. The governments of most countries are even specifically participating in blockchain-based projects for public services to varying extents. South Korea has already launched significant data initiatives in its government sector (UN, 2020; Kim et al., 2014), while Estonia has introduced electronic residency (Savage, 2018). The United Nations (UN) investigated the level of e-government development in each country in terms of new technology adoption, legal and institutional frameworks, strategy and implementation, and user satisfaction (UN, 2020); Table 1 lists the leading countries in e-government development according to the results of this survey. Europe is a leader in e-government development as well as the provision of online services, with Denmark having the highest E-Government Development Index (EGDI) value among the top seven countries in Northern Europe. South Korea is a global leader in online service provision and has the best EGDI performance in Asia.

As presented in the table, many countries are implementing policies that enable the sharing of information, including government data and policy-related data, through digitization to induce active citizen participation in policymaking and to ensure process transparency. As a result, blockchain technology is attracting attention from many governments and public services. Blockchain-based digital identity systems enhance government innovation and lead to economic advantages by providing secure and reliable authentication online. Specifically, they reduce the costs associated with customer service and increase satisfaction with fast and reliable service transactions. Blockchain-based electronic voting systems, as have been proposed in many countries, have also been reported to prevent security attacks and manipulations while increasing transparency (Dararomla and Thebus, 2020).

While implementing new technologies in government services can have a wide variety of benefits, practical implementations of such technologies in the government sector are rare, and the existing ones are still in their infancy; there are also certain problems and challenges that remain to be solved. These concerns include several factors beyond technical issues, such as a lack of policy enforcement, a lack of user awareness, a lack of information control and a lack of knowledge on digital government architecture (Baheer et al., 2020).
