By inviting their suppliers to participate in their blockchain process, luxury brands can establish a long-term collaboration to promote product quality rather than merely optimizing the product cost. “Nowadays, employing a sourcing strategy is essential to ensure product quality; we want to implement real long-term partnerships with our suppliers” (18). Finally, the interviewees discussed the difficulties of releasing company information in a blockchain consortium framework. Tough competition in the luxury sector prevents them from increasing the number of participants in their blockchain network, which reduces its appeal. Moreover, owing to their divergent criteria, finding a common framework for blockchain rules presents a real issue. Thus, the luxury sector’s use of public blockchain is limited because firms want to control the spread of information within the network and are reluctant to deal with data that are unremovable and uncontrolled. “Brand X decided not to implement a public blockchain because it is not good for their brand image (...) they want to control their blockchain content” (11); “Traceability yes, but for our eyes only” (15). Thus, luxury brands prefer to implement permissioned blockchains instead. According to interviewee 10, “permissioned blockchains, rather than a public ones, are often used by multi-actor luxury platforms, limiting information access to identifiable and authorized participants only” (10). However, Interviewee 6 considered that improving the accessibility of blockchain data could improve the trust levels of end-users, especially in the second-hand market, which is struggling against the gray market and counterfeit products. “The second-hand market is 10 times more important for luxury watches than new watch brands. Having a certificate attached to the product on a blockchain and confirming the origin of the product would increase customers’ trust, enhancing the brand image in the second-hand market and the overall market” (111).
