On the other hand, data integrity can be achieved with blockchain as a result of its immutability feature, which prevents a registered transaction from being changed (Moin et al., 2019). Furthermore, it helps in tracking and carrying out transactions from various devices, thus eliminating centralized servers in IoT applications (Koshy et al., 2020).

Blockchain systems allocate a unique key pair when a smart device connects to the system the first time (Yu et al., 2018). These keys (public keys with their matching private keys) are stored in the blockchain (Yu et al., 2018), thereby providing a higher level of security. The devices can then freely interact with each other, and the signatures together with the public keys can be used to verify them (Zhu and Badr, 2018; Yu et al., 2018).
