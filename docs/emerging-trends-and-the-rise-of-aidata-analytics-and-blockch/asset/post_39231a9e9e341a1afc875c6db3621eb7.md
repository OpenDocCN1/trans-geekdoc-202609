## Blockchain as a mean to secure Internet of Things ecosystems – a systematic literature review

## Blockchain as a mean to secure IoT ecosystems

Mazen El-Masri and Eiman Mutwali Abdelmageed Hussain

Accounting and Information Systems, Qatar University, Doha, Qatar

**Abstract**

**Purpose** – Blockchain is evolving to become a platform for securing Internet of things (IoT) ecosystems. Still, challenges remain. The purpose of this literature review is to highlight the applicability of blockchain as a medium to secure IoT ecosystems. A two-dimensional framework anchored on (1) IoT layers and (2) security goals is used to organize the existent IoT security threats and their corresponding countermeasures identified in the reviewed literature. The framework helped in mapping the IoT security threats with the inherent features of blockchain and accentuate their prominence to IoT security.

**Design/methodology/approach** – An approach integrating computerized natural language processing (NLP) with a systematic literature review methodology was adopted. A large corpus of 2,303 titles and abstracts of blockchain articles was programmatically analyzed in order to identify the relevant literature. The identified literature was subjected to a systematic review guided by a well-established method in IS research.

**Findings** – The literature evidently highlights the prominence of blockchain as a mean to IoT security due to the distinctive features it encompasses. The authors’ investigation revealed that numerous existent threats are better addressed with blockchain than conventional mechanisms. Nevertheless, blockchain consumes resources such as electricity, time, bandwidth and disk space at a rate that is not yet easily accessible to common IoT ecosystems.

**Research limitations/implications** – Results suggest that a configurational approach that aligns IoT security requirements with the resource requirements of different blockchain features is necessary in order to realize the proper balance between security, efficiency and feasibility.

**Practical implications** – Practitioners can make use of the classified lists of conventional security mechanisms and the IoT threats they address. The framework can help underline the countermeasures that best achieve their security goals. Practitioners can also use the framework to identify the most important features to seek for in a blockchain technology that can help them achieve their security goals.

**Originality/value** – This study proposes a novel framework that can help classify IoT threats based on the IoT layer impacted and the security goal at risk. Moreover, it applies a combined man-machine approach to systematically analyze the literature.

**Keywords** Blockchain, Internet of things, Security, Systematic literature review

**Paper type** Literature review

## 1. Introduction

The Internet of things (IoT) constitute low-power lossy networks (LLNs) of objects which utilize few technological resources and electrical power (Sultan et al., 2018). Those objects contain embedded sensors to capture and exchange data with other connected devices and systems (Sultan et al., 2018). IoTs are encroaching on the way we drive, how events are triggered and decisions made (Kouicem et al., 2018). IoT devices are often cheaply accessible, thereby speeding their adoption in urban development (e.g. smart buildings, smart cities), healthcare, finance and more (Kouicem et al., 2018). Indeed, in 2019, the number of operational IoT devices jumped over 400% to reach around 27 billion devices (security today, 2020) which exceeded the predictions by Mendez et al. (2017) of reaching 20 billion by the end of 2020.

Notwithstanding their usefulness, IoT devices are connected to the Internet via a standard communication protocol, which exposes them to security threats (Burgess, 2018; Mendez et al., 2017). The main security challenges in the IoT environment are privacy, authorization, access control, information storage and system configuration (Kouicem et al., 2018). Since those devices cannot afford sophisticated security technologies (Alotaiibi, 2019), traditional security mechanisms such as asymmetric encryption that conform to the low-cost premise of IoT infrastructure are often adopted (Noor and Hassan, 2019). Nevertheless, those mechanisms are insufficient to preserve the five goals of security; i.e., data integrity, availability, confidentiality, authentication and nonrepudiation which threatens further adoption of IoT in the future (Alaba et al., 2017; Ali et al., 2018).

A Deloitte report by Jain (2020) confirms that security remains the main issue for IoT ecosystems to grow. The authors propose blockchain as a solution to address those vulnerabilities and accelerate IoT adoption. However, the author argues that for a blockchain-IoT marriage to survive, security challenges in blockchain like network privacy, transaction confidentiality, device integrity and scalability must be addressed. Those claims were also voiced in the recent relevant literature. Indeed, extant research suggests blockchain as a viable solution to facilitate the growth of the IoT ecosystem, but persistent security issues need to be resolved using minimum technological resources (see Chanson et al., 2019; Firdaus et al., 2019; Reyna et al., 2018; Alaba et al., 2017). While blockchain has been able to solve various challenges facing IoT applications (Jesus et al., 2018), limitations to its full-blown across the various IoT sectors still persist (Wei et al., 2019). Besides, there are emergent new challenges such as stalker attacks (Jesus et al., 2018) and data redundancy (Si, 2019), which are not yet resolved by present-day blockchain technology.

Accordingly, the main objective of this systematic literature review is to identify and structure security threats to IoT ecosystems according to the established IoT architecture as well as the security goals they negatively affect. Moreover, in our review we map the conventional security mechanisms implemented in IoT environments to the security threats they address. Lastly, we explore the features of blockchain technologies and evaluate their suitability in tackling IoT security threats that current mechanisms are less adequate to address. To justify the suitability of Blockchain for IoT security, we conducted a literature review that presents a systematic analysis of the most recently adopted blockchain security applications. We started by investigating the important topics that are associated with blockchain. To do this, we utilized a natural language processing technique suggested by Siddiqi and Sharan (2015) to systematically analyze the abstracts as well as article titles. Accordingly, we statistically analyzed articles' abstracts, titles, and keywords to better understand the current applications of blockchain in addressing IoT security as well as identify the relevant articles to include in our review.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. The next section provides an overview of the previous attempts to review the literature on blockchain and IoT security. In this section we also provide justifications why our systematic literature review provides a more comprehensive and accurate picture of the current state of this topic. Afterwards, we specify the main goals of the review and describe our methodology. Section 6 thoroughly explains the results of our review and tie them to goals of our work. Lastly, we discuss those results in Section 7 and provide a conclusion and future research direction.
