As a representative case, Krupa and Akhil (2019) argued that blockchain technology has the potential to revolutionize the real estate sector as it can prevent fraudulent activities and increase transparency. Spielman (2016) claimed that blockchain-based title record keeping is more advantageous than the traditional title recordkeeping platforms.

The role of blockchain technology in the healthcare sector is also examined by many studies, including Agbo et al. (2019), De Aguiar et al. (2020), Höβlb et al. (2018), Kaur et al. (2018), McGhin et al. (2019), Mettler (2016), Siyal et al. (2019), Tanwar et al. (2020), Yue et al. (2016) and Zhang et al. (2018). For example, McGhin et al. (2019) named three blockchain applications in the healthcare industry, including smart contracts, fraud detection and identity verification. Zhang et al. (2018) mentioned that blockchain technology could offer a suitable solution for some problems in the healthcare system, including incomplete records at the point of care and difficult access to patients’ health information. It can also grantee the interoperability of healthcare system.

Albrecht et al. (2018), Andoni et al. (2019), Brilliantova and Thurner (2019), Imbault et al. (2017), Li et al. (2017), Mengelkamp et al. (2018), Sedlmeir et al. (2020), Teufel et al. (2019), Wang and Su (2020) and Wu and Tran (2018) investigated the impact of blockchain technology in the energy sector. Wang and Su (2020) declared that blockchain technology could promote renewable energy and improve energy sustainability. Teufel et al. (2019) argued that blockchain technology could have a key role in transforming the electricity sector to renewable sources. In addition, the implication of this technology in the energy sector can lead to cost reduction via the elimination of middle person and process automation. Besides, the energy source can be determined as all the transactions are traceable on the blockchain platform. Moreover, this technology enables prosumers to access the energy market easily. This technology also allows consumers to analyze and evaluate their power consumption more qualitatively (Teufel et al., 2019).

Azzi et al. (2019), Blossey et al. (2019), Casado-Vara et al. (2018), Cole et al. (2019), Dujak and Sajter (2019), Dutta et al. (2020), Francisco and Swanson (2018), Korpela et al. (2017), Min (2019), Queiroz and Wamba (2019), Saberi et al. (2019) and Treiblmaier (2018) evaluated the implementation of blockchain technology in the supply chain. For a typical case, Dutta et al. (2020) claimed that blockchain technology has incredible features, like transparency, security trust and reduction of cost and waste that can transform the supply chain. In terms of data management, this technology can enhance the security of stored data and increase data collaboration across supply chains. This technology also improves transparency by tracking the status of goods and automates data analysis. This technology also can improve the speed of process, make the process robust, ensure the security of all transactions, protect intellectual property protection, reduce risks associated with the procedure and help in process design in the supply chain (Araz et al., 2020; Dutta et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2020a).

(2021), Schär (2021), Shah and Jani (2018), Tapscott and Tapscott (2017), Treleaven et al. (2017) and Zhu and Wang (2019) investigated the applications of blockchain technology for the financial sector. Kiayias and Panagiotakos (2017), Koba et al. (2016), Mendling et al. (2018) and Osmani et al. (2021) argued that blockchain technology could enable banking systems with operational benefits, including faster transactions, improved security, cost reduction, immutability, privacy and transparency. Besides, by eliminating the intermediaries, blockchain technology can realize peer-to-peer finance that includes peer-to-peer lending, peer-to-peer equity investing, peer-to-peer foreign exchange, peer-based investing and peer-to-peer foreign exchange hedging (Moenninghoff and Wieandt, 2013).
