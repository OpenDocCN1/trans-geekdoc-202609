
The firms were identified through a database obtained by the Higher Education Institution through a partnership. The representation of the sample was considered adequate due to the analyzed position being predominantly analytical than executive.

All selected firms are located in the state of São Paulo, Brazil. In addition, it is important to inform that the industry in São Paulo is the most modern and diversified in Latin America and is supported by a robust technological base, generating products with high added value in various economic sectors. In sum, the State of São Paulo concentrates 36% of Brazilian industrial production.

In Brazil it is not easy to bring firms closer to Universities due to the prevalence of the closed innovation model instead of the open innovation model. However, there has been a movement of change, but not yet at the necessary speed. The firms that participated in this research are leaders in product and service innovation development in their respective sectors. It agrees with Ransbotham and Kiron (2017), who found that leaders in adopting Big Data analysis are more likely to produce new products and services than others.

Two management academics were consulted to refine the questionnaire and validate the adaptation of the research instrument to the Brazilian context. Based on their feedback, some items were reformulated. Through operationalization, the research variables were reduced to constructs that can be observed and measured. To measure each item of the constructs, the Likert scale was used with endpoints anchored in “totally disagree” (1) and “totally agree” (5) for all 35 statements (scrambled assertions in the questionnaire). The constructs and corresponding survey items used in this questionnaire are based on previously published latent variables with psychometric properties that support their validity. For aspects of the characterization of the demographic profile and organizations, specific objective questions were elaborated.

The data collection method was convenience sampling, a non-probabilistic technique, a fact that limits the generalization of research results. The questionnaire was made available by email and LinkedIn to facilitate access to managers and professionals from the firms that participated in this research. For this study, a pre-test was performed with 25 individuals to verify the research instrument’s understanding (Hair et al., 2009). Around 450 firms were contacted to participate in the survey. However, 141 valid questionnaires were obtained, which were analyzed in IBM SPSS 25 and SmartPLS 3.3.3. In this research, we used the G*Power 3.1.9 software to calculate the sample’s test power (1-b err prob) is equal to 98.78%.

## 5. Analysis of results

### 5.1 Non-response bias

A bias that occurs due to systematic differences between responders and non-responders. To determine whether there was any non-response bias in our sample, the respondents’ profile was carefully selected and compared with the others who did not participate in the survey. These were observed by the mailing list and by LinkedIn – with the profile of each manager (“middle management” and “top management”) initially contacted, also observing the characteristics of each firm (Mikalef et al., 2019a). In addition to non-response, we also examine late-response bias by comparing early (first month) and late responses (last second month). We found no significant difference between respondents and non-respondents. Considering that all data were collected from a single source (survey) at one point in time, in a cross-sectional study, and that all data were perceptions of key respondents.

### 5.2 Profile of respondents

The sample had most male individuals (n = 104/73.8%). About age, the largest number of respondents in the survey is concentrated up to 35 years (n = 83/58.9%), and respondents were characterized by a sample of managers (45.4%) and professionals (54.6%).

In this study, the sample indicated the female public's low participation (n = 37/26.2%), reflecting the demographic disproportion in Brazil about leadership positions. Women represent only 20% of all technology professionals, even though they reveal that women have a higher education level than men in the sector. Also, Brazilian firms still promote women in technology positions more than three times less often than men. Finally, Brazilian firms pay women an average of 30% less than the wages paid to men in technology (IBGE, 2019; BRASSCOM, 2020).

Regarding education, one-third of the respondents have completed graduate studies (n = 47/33.3%) and are working in middle and senior management positions (n = 116/82.2%). The average length of time working in the organization is x̄ = 66.61 months, or approximately 5 years and 6 months. Further observing the length of employment of these professionals in the firm, it appears that, by separating into groups that are “less” and “more” time than the average, those who stay less are identified as middle management professionals, such as: Analysts, Experts, Developers, Designers, Statistics, Data Analysts and BI Developers (n = 50/35.7%). Conversely, when analyzing the longest stay in the firm, senior management is identified, such as: Managers, Customers Success Managers, Coordinators, Supervisors, Consultants and Technology Leaders (n = 23/16.4%).

When performing an intergroup analysis of length of stay in organizations, respondents who spend “less” time had an average age x̄ = 30 years, and those who spend “more” time in the organization had an average age x̄ = 40 years (t(132) = 6.278; p < 0.001). This indicates that professionals aged 30 years or less are seeking employment within the field in other firms, and those aged 40 years and over remain in organizations longer. In the intergroup analysis of the variable “sex” in relation to “age”, the data indicated that the respondents do not have differences in means. Male professionals represented in the sample were an average age of approximately x̄ = 33 years and the female group x̄ = 36 years. Despite the small difference, the T-test indicated equal variances (t(133) = 1.393; p = 0.166).

### 5.3 Characterization of organizations

Most of the organizations participating in this research were national (n = 82/58.2%) compared to international ones (n = 57/40.4%). The service sector was the one with the highest participation in this research with 70.9% (n = 100). Organizations with a “large” size also indicated most of the sample with 65.2% (n = 92). When analyzing the person or persons responsible for the investment of technological systems that involve Big Data, the responsibility in general rests with the “Board”, with 66% (n = 93) and the item “other” was indicated by 4.3% (n = 6) representing management outside the country or executive councils. It was also possible to verify that no respondents identified themselves with the item “low management” – thus, for this research, the terms “middle management” and “top management” were used. The average age of organizations is x̄ = 572.67 months or approximately 47 years and 7 months, which indicates that most organizations have been established for almost half a century in the country, having gone through different economic scenarios, as well as technological transformations. It was also possible to verify that 24.8% (n = 35) of the organizations are national, in the service sector and large.
