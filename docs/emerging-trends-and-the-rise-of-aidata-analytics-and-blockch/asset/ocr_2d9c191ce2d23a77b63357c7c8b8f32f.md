# 2. Related work

The study derives its applications from the affect theory, which organizes human emotions into nine categories, namely, joy and excitement for positive emotions; a surprise for neutral emotions; and anger, disgust, dis-smell, distress, fear and shame for negative emotions (Tomkins, 1962). Affect theory is adapted from the psychology domain and focuses on organizing affects, sometimes called emotions. Affect theory is further explored in philosophy, gender studies and art theory (Catherall, 2006). Lately, affect theory has been gaining attraction in the field of Information Sciences (Kratzwald et al, 2018). We have used affect theory for analysis, where we have taken both text and images data.

The study is further based on the behavioral decision theory (Simon, 1959), which states that humans are prone to have cognitive biases while making decisions, and the decisions are entirely dependent on the situation. Thus, an individual will decide based on his/her state of mind and the environmental conditions. This presents a unique opportunity to marketers where they can analyze the customers' state of mind and present the alternatives to them. For instance, a customer who is identified in a state of sadness can be offered a discount on a particular product. Research also suggests that behavioral strategy redefines strategic directions in an organization (Lovallo and Sibony, 2018).

# 2.1 Text-based affective computing

Researchers have focused on various approaches such as lexicon-based approach, machine learning models and deep learning models for understanding affective computing and sentiment analysis. The difference between affective computing and sentiment analysis is that sentiment analysis detects only the polarity of the text, and affective computing detects the different emotions in the text. We have described the literature for both affective computing and sentiment analysis.

# 2.1.1 Lexicon based approaches.

Various researchers studied lexicon-based methods for sentiment analysis (Ding et al, 2008; Melville et al, 2009; Taboada et al, 2011). Lexicon-based approaches assume that the nature of contextual sentiment is an aggregate sum of the sentiment orientation of each word or phrase. The lexicons were compiled manually in the beginning, but later, scholars started using machine learning for lexicon-based approaches. The shift from manual to machine learning was primarily because manual labelling was prone to errors, inflexible and expensive. One of the significant difficulties for both manual and machine learning approaches in lexicon-based is understanding the linguistic structures among social media users, including the usage of slang, acronyms, emoticons and sarcasm.
