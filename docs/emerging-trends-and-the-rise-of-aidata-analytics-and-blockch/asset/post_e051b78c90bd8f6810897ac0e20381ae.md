
This research highlights the opportunities and threats of the implementation of blockchain solutions at the managerial level. The opportunities include (1) protection from counterfeiting and the gray market; (2) product relationship management that allows brands to track their products during their lifecycle; (3) the transparency of the supply chain; (4) the traceability of products, allowing brands to provide warranties for products’ quality and origins; (5) long-term collaboration with third parties; and (6) brand rejuvenation. The potential threats preventing the implementation of blockchain solutions include (1) knowledge gaps regarding blockchains, (2) long-term ROIs rather than short-term ones, (3) difficulty in convincing the numerous suppliers involved and (4) the lack of human resource training on the application of blockchains.

This study focused on luxury companies; nevertheless, the above-highlighted managerial recommendations are also useful for other stakeholders of the supply chain, notably consumers and policymakers. Indeed, the implementation of blockchain protects the interests of consumers by ensuring product quality, thanks to the traceability of goods and potential decrease in counterfeiting. Moreover, this output concerns the whole luxury ecosystem from final consumers to distributors and business partners such as luxury resale platforms. However, blockchain use involves major changes in regulations. Therefore, policymakers (governmental and international institutions) should compare the possible damage (misuse of technology) of blockchain in the supply chain with its potential benefits (control over counterfeiting) and the supply chain, reduction of costs.

### 6.2 Limitations and future research directions

The study’s limitations mainly pertain to the sample selection. First, as only 12 samples were included in this study, its findings should be considered as exploratory. Second, as the respondents were experts on blockchain within the luxury business, where this technology has been already implemented, their responses may not reflect the reality of the entire luxury industry. Third, while global firms were interviewed, all the respondents were located in France. Fourth, only brand representatives were interviewed, which excluded other stakeholders’ points of view about the luxury ecosystem. Finally, potential ethical issues concerning blockchains require further investigation (Chang, 2021).

Based on the aforementioned limitations, we highlight the following opportunities for further research: (1) quantitatively testing the three propositions on luxury firms, (2) carrying out a longitudinal study analyzing the evolution of the perceptions of the adoption of blockchains within the brands studied in this research, (3) extending the study to other brands in other countries to shed light on potential cultural differences, (4) measuring customers’ acceptance of blockchain solutions in the luxury business and (5) including luxury goods suppliers in the analysis to investigate the feasibility of blockchain solutions in their supply chain processes.
