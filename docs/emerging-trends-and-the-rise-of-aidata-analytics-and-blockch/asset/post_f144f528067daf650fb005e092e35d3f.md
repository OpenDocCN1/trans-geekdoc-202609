By inviting their suppliers to participate in their blockchain process, luxury brands can establish a long-term collaboration to promote product quality rather than merely optimizing the product cost. “Nowadays, employing a sourcing strategy is essential to ensure product quality; we want to implement real long-term partnerships with our suppliers” (18). Finally, the interviewees discussed the difficulties of releasing company information in a blockchain consortium framework. Tough competition in the luxury sector prevents them from increasing the number of participants in their blockchain network, which reduces its appeal. Moreover, owing to their divergent criteria, finding a common framework for blockchain rules presents a real issue. Thus, the luxury sector’s use of public blockchain is limited because firms want to control the spread of information within the network and are reluctant to deal with data that are unremovable and uncontrolled. “Brand X decided not to implement a public blockchain because it is not good for their brand image (...) they want to control their blockchain content” (11); “Traceability yes, but for our eyes only” (15). Thus, luxury brands prefer to implement permissioned blockchains instead. According to interviewee 10, “permissioned blockchains, rather than a public ones, are often used by multi-actor luxury platforms, limiting information access to identifiable and authorized participants only” (10). However, Interviewee 6 considered that improving the accessibility of blockchain data could improve the trust levels of end-users, especially in the second-hand market, which is struggling against the gray market and counterfeit products. “The second-hand market is 10 times more important for luxury watches than new watch brands. Having a certificate attached to the product on a blockchain and confirming the origin of the product would increase customers’ trust, enhancing the brand image in the second-hand market and the overall market” (11). ## 5. Discussion

The COVID-19 pandemic has accelerated the digitalization of luxury brands’ supply chains and need to ensure safety in the digitalization process (Nandi et al., 2020). Therefore, this study aimed to answer three research questions about the implementation of blockchains that focused on its main benefits such as traceability, the contexts and conditions that promoted such a disruptive technology and the solutions provided to the luxury industry. To answer these research questions, we highlight three propositions.

*Proposition 1.* Blockchain removes intermediaries in the supply chain, improves traceability and facilitates management.

The interviewed managers suggested that the digitalization of the luxury industry has led to a complex and difficult-to-control business structure due to the specialized nature of its products and growing demand. Despite using digital platforms, the luxury industry is organized in an overcomplicated manner and involves numerous intermediaries (Song et al., 2019). Academics and managers agree on the need to improve supply chain processes and role of blockchain solutions to bring about the required change (Al-Talib et al., 2020). Many studies confirm that blockchain can make the supply chain efficient and remove unnecessary intermediaries (Casado-Vara et al., 2018; Al-Talib et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2019). Thus, disintermediation using blockchains (Tönnissen and Teuteberg, 2020) enhances the transparency, traceability and visibility of the supply chain and provides security against counterfeiting (Wang et al., 2019), thereby minimizing uncertainty in the supply chain’s operations (Xu et al., 2019).

The transparency and traceability of business processes are the key benefits expected by the interviewed experts from the implementation of blockchain solutions. While the luxury industry needs to trace the path of its products throughout the supply chain to maintain high quality and guarantee their products’ origins (Shen et al., 2020), brands need to prove their environmental engagement with customers (Ho et al., 2016). Both these actions can significantly improve the brand’s image. However, regardless of its benefits, the luxury industry is still reluctant to incorporate blockchains.

*Proposition 2.* The implementation of blockchain solutions within the luxury sector is influenced by certain conditions and contexts.

The interviewed experts expressed two contexts that hinder the implementation of blockchains. First, the luxury industry may resist rapid change. Second, luxury brands may not concede to the degree of openness and knowledge required to incorporate blockchain technology into their operations. The experts confirmed that while the adoption of disruptive technologies corresponds to the values of the luxury industry (Yeoman and McMahon-Beattie, 2018), their approval requires demonstrable financial benefits (Frizzo-Barker et al., 2020). That is, blockchains must provide decision-makers with short-term ROIs. This is in line with previous studies that have focused on business efficiency (Azzi et al., 2019), including transaction costs (Kshetri and Loukoianova, 2019). Luxury brands accept blockchain technologies when they are considered to improve performance (Maersh, 2018), by significantly contributing to revenue streams (Deloitte, 2018) [4]. Nevertheless, some problems can decelerate the adoption process, such as the potential cultural resistance at the industry or country level, the lack of digitalization by suppliers and trust issues (Kano and Nakajima, 2018; Kshetri, 2018; Reyna et al., 2018; Zou et al., 2018).

The openness of blockchain, which promotes traceability and transparency, might also present obstacles because of the industry’s competitive nature and lack of collaboration (Kshetri, 2018; Helo and Hao, 2019). Additionally, increased complexity affects supply chain performance (Qrunfleh and Tarafdar, 2014), especially for blockchain solutions, which are still rarely used in the luxury sector. The knowledge gap regarding blockchain technology can limit its acceptance by non-technically oriented decision-makers and employees, complicating its implementation (Baudier et al., 2021). However, to address concerns about the spread of information within blockchain, greater knowledge of the private blockchain framework and an understanding of the technology's benefits can remove barriers to its implementation (Wamba et al., 2020; Lu, 2019).

*Proposition 3.* Blockchain's technological capabilities can solve the luxury sector's fundamental problem of tracing counterfeit goods and the trade of luxury goods in the gray market.

Blockchain technology's most promising application in the luxury industry, specifically in its supply chain, is combatting counterfeiting and the gray market. All the interviewees expressed their concerns and aspirations regarding solving the counterfeiting problem, as this reduces profit and damages reputation in an era in which globalization and digitalization make it almost impossible to trace counterfeit products. All the interviewees believed in blockchains' potential to solve the luxury industry's problems. Today, blockchains' anti-counterfeiting platforms (Liu et al., 2020) offer data traceability and property rights protection. However, luxury brands must follow the government's regulations on the implementation of blockchains and counterfeiting (Liu et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2019).

Nevertheless, the introduction of blockchains requires collaboration and integrity among the actors involved, necessitating the industry's transformation (Soon and Manning, 2019). Our interviews revealed that trust in the industry and technology influences the acceptance of blockchain. Its capabilities for tracking, authenticating a product's origin (the “made in” label), and providing proof of transaction (Casado-Vara et al., 2018) justify blockchain's use in the supply chain. Owing to its swift and provable fraud detection (Casado-Vara et al., 2018), the companies involved can work more openly with lower financial losses (Guercini and Runfola, 2009). The luxury industry is one of the most affected from counterfeiting and the gray and copycat markets (Hou et al., 2020; Choi, 2019), which could be addressed through the application of blockchain solutions in the supply chain (Kshetri, 2018). While the respondents emphasized the persistent constraints (Helo and Hao, 2019) and underlined the reasons for the slow transformation of the traditional supply chain to incorporate blockchain solutions, the combination of digitalization, COVID-19 and increasing consumer demands make blockchains' application to the luxury sector's supply chain more lucrative.

## 6. Conclusion, limitations and future research directions

### 6.1 Conclusion

Using blockchain to improve supply chain efficiency is critical to several industries, including the luxury industry, which must digitalize its traditional supply chain management by switching to e-commerce platforms and adopting innovative technologies. This need has been heightened during the COVID-19 crisis when luxury firms (especially in the fashion sector) had to solely rely on online channels to meet consumer demand (Kumar et al., 2020; Nandi et al., 2020).

This study makes both methodological and theoretical contributions. Based on axial coding methods (Strauss and Corbin, 1998; Flick, 2009), it proposes a framework to understand the dimensions of supply chain and traceability, which is required to provide blockchain solutions to the luxury industry. Moreover, this study enriches the literature on supply chain management and blockchain solutions against counterfeiting in this industry. Beyond COVID-19, few studies have focused on the issues emerging because of the rapid digitalization of the luxury industry. Therefore, this study also discusses the specific conditions hindering blockchains' implementation in this sector.
