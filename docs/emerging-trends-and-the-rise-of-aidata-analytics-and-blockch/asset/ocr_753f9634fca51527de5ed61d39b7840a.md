The current work further explores the research by using both text and image data for understanding affective computing.

There is an old adage which states that images or picture is worth a thousand words. Thus, images speak louder than words and can play a much crucial role in expressing emotions. Therefore, the analysis of the sentiments of images becomes very much pertinent. Some of the researchers like Ghani and Krema (2014) have studied the sentiment of the images by feature extraction method, while other researchers like Machajdik and Hanbury (2010) have used background and color of the images for classifying the emotions of the image. The present study uses the facial emotion recognition method to classify the sentiment depicted by the image.

The affective computing for text data is carried out on four standard datasets, namely PsychExp dataset (Wallbott and Scherer, 1986), ISEAR dataset (Wallbott and Scherer, 1986), Literary Tales dataset (Alm, 2008) and Headlines dataset (Strapparava and Mihalcea, 2007). PsychExp dataset consists of 7,840 records belonging to six classes, i.e. joy, fear, anger, sadness, disgust, shame and guilt. The ISEAR dataset is similar and consists of records of self-report of experiences. Headlines dataset is also known as SemEval-2007 dataset and consists of annotated headlines. Finally, Literary Tales is a larger dataset consisting of affect labelled literary tales. On the other hand, affective computing for images was carried out on images extracted from Instagram. Images were extracted using the Application Program Interface (API) of Instagram. The images obtained from Instagram are then classified into two classes of sentiments, namely, happy class and sad class.

We have used various deep learning models for effectively capturing affective computing for both text and images. For text-based affective computing, we have used three types of deep learning models- Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) and Convolutional Neural Network (CNN). We have also deployed various variants of LSTM, GRU and CNN models to improve the accuracy of text classification. For LSTM Models, we have used four different variants of LSTM Models: simple LSTM model with randomly initialized embeddings, LSTM model with Global Vector (GloVe) embeddings, Bi-directional LSTM model and LSTM model with an attention layer. Similarly, four variants of GRU models were also used to improve the accuracy of text classifications. The variants of GRU includes those of the GRU model with randomly initialized embeddings, GRU model with GloVe embeddings, Bi-directional GRU model and GRU model with an attention layer. The third type of deep learning model used in the study was the CNN model with randomly initialized embeddings and the CNN model with GloVe embeddings. The Bi-directional CNN model and the CNN model with the attention layer are not possible in deep learning. Bi-directional CNN gives the same output as that of CNN because the entire thing is taken as an input in one instance. For image-based affective computing, we have used three deep learning models: CNN model with three convolutional layers and three fully connected layers, transfer learning model with pre-trained VGG-19 network and transfer learning model with pre-trained ResNet-18 network. Further, we have presented the visualization of images analysis using t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE) and Principal Component Analysis (PCA) of Instagram images.

The present study draws its inspiration from some of the existing deep learning techniques used in earlier researches (Kratzwald et al., 2018; Kraus and Feuerriegel, 2017) and then extend it to the context of affective computing. Furthermore, we have used an additional deep learning model, namely CNN in addition to the standard LSTM and GRU used in earlier researches of Kratzwald et al. (2018). Besides, we have also used an attention layer in both LSTM and GRU. Finally, we have further compared our results with the earlier proposed techniques to validate the importance of the proposed methodology.
