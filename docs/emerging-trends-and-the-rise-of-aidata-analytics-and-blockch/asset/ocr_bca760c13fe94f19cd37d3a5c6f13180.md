# # Introduction

The introduction of cryptocurrencies as a new and disruptive technology by Nakamoto is regarded as a revolution in financial markets and systems of payments. This innovation resulting from blockchain technology makes electronic transactions cheaper, more comfortable and quicker by removing intermediaries. These currencies are Internet-based payment protocols, and they do not need government or other third parties to verify transactions. Cryptocurrencies have drawn massive attention from academics, policymakers, investors and media (Böhme et al., 2015). The blockchain technology that supports cryptocurrencies offers a decentralized system for creating new crypto and confirming transactions.

As mentioned, the blockchain is the cornerstone of cryptocurrencies, introduced by Bitcoin to the financial area. A blockchain is a block of data chained together. It is a major offshoot of financial technology or fintech (Chang et al., 2020). As it becomes widespread, it dramatically changes businesses (DiNizo, 2018). The most significant characteristic of blockchain is a decentralized system that results in simpler, cheaper and faster transactions (Staples et al., 2017). Together, these characteristics can lead to financial, commercial and even industrial revolutions and change the whole economic system around the world. Chang et al. (2020) mention four major characteristics of blockchain, including decentralization, meaning that the database and history of a transaction are accessible for either side of transitions without any permission from a third party (Tapscott and Tapscott, 2017). This feature also restricts individuals or institutions from committing criminal activities or fraud (Rennick et al., 2018). The other feature of blockchain is the user’s anonymity. Any participant in the network has a unique alphanumeric address, and no third party stores the information of users (Tapscott and Tapscott, 2017; Zheng et al., 2018). The next feature is the consensus mechanism meaning that 51% of the nodes in the network should approve creating a new record in the network (Huang et al., 2019). Finally, the last feature is the execution, referred to as the ability of users to carry out transactions between nodes based on some rules and algorithms.

The blockchain has many potentials to change the whole financial infrastructure (Pilkington, 2016), although it is immature and contains some issues and challenges, like security privacy latency and scalability (Underwood, 2016). Drescher (2017) emphasizes the openness and the nonexistence of the coordinator party that is the fundamental feature of the blockchain system as the major barrier to the widespread usage of blockchain. Some researchers consider four challenges related to the blockchain, including scalability, security privacy privacy and leakage energy consumption (Chang et al., 2020; Drescher, 2017). These challenges hinder the widespread usage of blockchain. Scalability points out the relatively slow procedure of conducting transactions by some cryptocurrencies, like Bitcoin and Ethereum compared to Visa or PayPal, (Jackson, 2018) because of its encrypted and distributed nature and complexity (Marr, 2018). Security refers to the idea that blockchain is prone to attacks from miners (Werbach, 2018; Zheng et al., 2018). There is also some clue that blockchain is prone to privacy leakages as the information of transactions is open to the public (Kosba et al., 2016; Meiklejohn et al., 2013), and it is possible to find users’ profiles by tracing their transactions (Barcelo, 2014).
