# 2.1 Blockchain overview

Blockchain is a distributed database that is shared among all members of the distributed network, and it is a technology that can be easily recorded and audited without changing or manipulating the transaction data (Allessie et al., 2019; Dai and Vasarhelyi, 2017; Di Pietro, 2017). Blockchain is characterized by anonymity, transparency and immutability. By using a public key, users can ensure anonymity when executing transactions. Each block in the blockchain contains information from the previous block, thus allowing users to monitor and review previous transactions and prevent third parties from removing or changing data (Iansiti and Lakhani, 2017). Moreover, to ensure transparency, public blockchains such as Ethereum and Bitcoin use fully transparent digital ledgers (Underwood, 2016).

Many experts predict that blockchain will be a key technology in the fourth industrial revolution because it can provide economic value to both businesses and users in a wide variety of industries by offering privacy, security and transparency (Grover et al., 2019; Yeoh, 2017). According, governments and companies are making substantial efforts to vitalize the blockchain market. Blockchain technology is currently applied in various fields that require reliability and stability, such as finance, distribution, logistics, manufacturing and public services. The use of blockchain is particularly expanding in areas that require data security and reliability.
