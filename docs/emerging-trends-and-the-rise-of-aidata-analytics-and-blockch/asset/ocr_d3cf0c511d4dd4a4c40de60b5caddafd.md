Values of 0.02, 0.15 and 0.35 are considered small, medium and large, respectively (Hair et al., 2016). The \(\hat{f}^2\) values are obtained by reading the communalities. What is perceived in this research is that the constructs were considered medium and large. The Stone–Geisser’s \(Q^2\) test assesses how close the model is to what was expected of it (or the quality of the model prediction or the accuracy of the adjusted model). According to the criterion used to assess the accuracy of the adjusted model, \(\hat{Q}^2\), all constructs were considered to be adequately accurate, as they presented values greater than zero (Akter et al., 2017).

The GoF is the global adjustment measure, that is, the square root of the multiplication of both the averages of the AVE and the \(R^2\) of the endogenous variables. The value found for the GoF is 0.6936 (69.36%) and was considered large enough for the validity of the model in the PLS (Wetzels et al., 2009). The discriminant validity assessed items that reflect the factor or that are correlated with other factors. In this research, the AVEs were greater than or equal to the square of the correlation between the factors, as shown in Table 2, with the definitive Fornell–Larcker criterion with all the factor loads of each indicator with values above 0.5. It was not necessary to exclude variables to adjust the model.
