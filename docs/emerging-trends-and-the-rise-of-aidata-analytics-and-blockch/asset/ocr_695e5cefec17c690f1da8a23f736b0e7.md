# 2. Literature review

In this section, we review two areas of the literature relevant to this study: BIoT and literature review methods.

# 2.1 Overview of BIoT technologies

In the era of digitalisation, IoT and blockchain are regarded as two emerging technologies with complementary characteristics that can generate a synergy to support technological advances. IoT is defined as the Internet-mediated interconnection of physical objects such as machines and devices to obtain analytical insights and operational improvements (Nord et al., 2019). To organise IoT solutions, service-oriented architecture (SoA) is constructed to establish interconnections between physical objects and digital platforms to formulate various industrial applications, such as supply chain management and manufacturing analytics (Lade et al., 2017; Papert and Pflaum, 2017). However, every node in an IoT system can be a point of failure for cyber-attacks, and the centralised IoT deployment makes the system relatively vulnerable to data confidentiality and authentication issues (Panarello et al., 2018). Moreover, it is difficult to ensure the data integrity and reliability of IoT systems, which may negatively affect data analytics and information management. Blockchain represents a promising solution for these limitations. Blockchain is a type of database for storing data in blocks that are chained together after the data are validated in a decentralised manner. The technology was introduced in 2008 to create a peer-to-peer (P2P) electronic cash system (Nakamoto, 2008). Blockchain’s major goals are to achieve trust, consensus and information security in P2P networks through decentralised, transparent and immutable data recording. Currently, blockchain applications can be classified into public, consortium and private applications (Lin and Liao, 2017). However, blockchain has its own weaknesses related to managing large data storage and system scalability. Moreover, decentralised data validation consumes excessive energy. The growing blockchain size is expected to jeopardise the health of the P2P network if all the data are stored in blocks and chained together. For instance, the Bitcoin blockchain size was about 320 GB by the end of 2020, given that the one-input two-output transaction size for Pay-to-Public-Key-Hash (P2PKH) transactions is around 250 bytes. Therefore, simple blockchain deployment is difficult for data-intensive industrial applications such as supply chain management and healthcare (Reyna et al., 2018). Also, complete transparency in a blockchain may cause data privacy challenges, threatening the users’ anonymity. Therefore, the integration of blockchain and IoT was proposed to improve system capabilities, including scalability, autonomy, identification, reliability, security and service variety. Since BIoT research is still preliminary, it is likely that BIoT R&D will grow rapidly in the near future. To facilitate such R&D, the knowledge structure of BIoT must be investigated to identify the trends and implications emerging from preliminary research studies.
