## 5.3 Visualization of images

Visualization is one of the essential skills for deep learning. Visualization helps the end-users in understanding and further interpreting the results. The visualization of Instagram images for human facial emotion has been done using a tensorboard embedding projector visualization tool. This tool provides various features such as PCA analysis, t-SNE (Bunte et al., 2012) visualization with a graphical interface. The tensorboard embedding projector visualization tool also provides flexibility to change different hyperparameters such as perplexity, learning rate and enable the control to view in 3D or 2D. We have obtained the visualization of Instagram images using both t-SNE and PCA. The visualization of Instagram images using tensorboard can be seen in Figure 11.

From Figure 11, it can be observed that there are two distinct classes, i.e. happy class and sad class. The oval marked in blue color shows the images belonging to the happy class, and the oval marked in red color indicates the images belonging to the sad class. The t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE) is a nonlinear dimensionality reduction algorithm used for exploring high-dimensional data (Bunte et al., 2012). t-SNE, in general, gives the feel of how the data is arranged in a high-dimensional space. Principal Component Analysis, or PCA, on the other hand, is a linear feature extraction technique. PCA performs a linear mapping of the data to a lower-dimensional space in such a way that the variance of the data in the low-dimensional representation is maximized. We have used both t-SNE and PCA for the visualization of images.

## 6.1 Implications

The findings of the present study have implications for policy decision making and academic research. The study would help in various application areas of decision support systems, including those of marketing, recommender systems and customer support. These processes can be improved significantly with the aid of affective computing. Similarly, other systems where human-computer interactions are required would also get benefits from sentiment analysis and emotion recognition. Analyzing and then subsequently responding to the emotional states of customers, users, employees, and patients have the potential to change the way businesses operate. Management can take a cue from the state of customers to determine the marketing and sales strategy. For instance, the customer who is identified in a state of sadness can be offered a discount on the product. As per the IBM Model for ethical analytics of the study focused not only on technical capabilities but also human and legal aspects as well (Chang, 2021).

The study has implications for researchers. The study also helped researchers across the world to understand the usage of customized deep learning models for carrying out different tasks. The study used different variants of deep learning models, including GloVe embeddings, attention layer and transfer learning. Researchers can make use of them in deep learning methods to improve the efficiency of the models. For instance, researchers of the finance domain can make use of the LSTM/GRU model with an attention layer to determine the stock prices effectively. Similarly, researchers can make use of transfer learning in the video data. Recently, researchers have started using deep learning in a way that these approaches/technologies should assist people in finding true meaning to their life and improving their standard of living while giving equal importance to happiness, truth, sensation, imagination, and diversity (Gee and Esteban-Guitart, 2019). The core idea is artificial intelligence should not overtake human emotions and decision making in future.

## 7. Conclusion

The study presents an affective computing analysis both for text and image data. The text-based affective computing was conducted on four standard datasets using three deep learning models, namely LSTM, GRU and CNN. The result of the proposed methods is then compared with that of benchmark models on standard datasets, and it was found that the proposed model outperforms the earlier models on most of the parameters. For image-based affective computing, the data was extracted from Instagram, and facial emotion recognition was carried out using three deep learning models, namely CNN, transfer learning with VGG-19 model and transfer learning with ResNet-18 model. The visualization of images based on affective computing is also shown in the study. The study would have insights both for academics and policymaking.

This work, like any other work, has some limitations. The images extracted from Instagram are classified into only two classes: happy and sad. For the best comparison, the images should have been classified into seven classes like the standard text dataset since the manual classification of images is prone to errors. Therefore, we have classified the images only into 2 classes. In future, the standard dataset of images of 7 classes is required for researchers for comparison purposes.

There are multiple avenues for the future scope of the current work. The work can be extended to incorporate the data from video and audio for multimodal affective computing.
