## 2.4 Blockchain used in public services

Government agencies worldwide are looking for opportunities to adopt emerging technologies in the public sector to reduce costs and bureaucracy while also increasing efficiency by leveraging secure, open, decentralized and affordable database technologies (Atzori, 2015; Allessie et al., 2019). The use of blockchain has attracted attention in the public sector, wherein new technology trends often emerge, with particular focus on its merits such as security, transparency, scalability and integrity. Blockchain technology can be used for any transaction or exchange of information that is involved in a public service. It can be implemented in various ways to exchange information; tangible assets such as physical property and inventory; and intangible assets such as patents, ideas and reputations (Swan, 2015). Researchers have argued that blockchain can be used to address the inefficiency of centralized systems and increase the efficiency of public service activities by providing more secure and transparent real-time transactions (Ojo and Adebayo, 2017; Ølnes et al., 2017). Ølnes et al. (2017) also noted that blockchain technology would be expected to promote innovation and change in government processes by facilitating direct interaction between citizens and providing government-supplied administrative services without central government control. Blockchain is being implemented in many service areas in the public sector, such as identity authentication through digital identity, personal information management services, the automation of tax payments and welfare payments, online voting, the sharing of medical information and the transmission of electronic records. Traditional centralized systems used by government bureaucrats involve ownership and the operation of information with centralized responsibility and authority; by contrast, a blockchain platform can enable information distribution, sharing and authentication through consensus, thereby realizing horizontal cooperative governance (Ju and Kang, 2018). Estonia has been using blockchain in its digital government designs since 2012, and it has been successfully applied in several states in Estonia for health services, property and business registration, digital court systems and surveillance/tracking information systems (Martinson, 2019). The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) has used blockchain to track specific diseases such as opioid addiction and hepatitis A (Clavin et al., 2020; Orcutt, 2017). Another major blockchain application case can be found in the World Food Program (WFP), which uses blockchain to help facilitate cash transactions to acquire essential needs for refugees (WFP, 2021).

Blockchain technology can be used to simplify most bureaucratic processes in government, which could have many benefits, such as improving the quality of government services and providing citizens and businesses with transparent and open government information (Allessie et al., 2019); doing so can increase government trust as well. Blockchain technology can also provide profitable solutions for public services by enabling the secure storage of information, improving service efficiency and promoting private investment expansion (Sin, 2019). Therefore, blockchain technology provides a new approach for governments to offer faster and more transparent access to public sector services, prevent fraud and establish trust (Borrows et al., 2017). Blockchain offers many benefits in terms of avoiding management errors, strengthening privacy and reducing the time and cost requirements that can arise from frequent data integration and sharing.

The use of blockchain in public services is still in its early stages (Staples et al., 2017). It is being piloted in functional areas, with a focus on examining the stability and efficiency of the service. For example, blockchain has been applied in e-residency, e-voting, identity verification (Sullivan and Burger, 2017), grain trading, agri-food value chain management (Gunasekera and Valenzuela, 2020; Zhao et al., 2019) and a reliable taxation system (Casino et al., 2019). There have also been various applications of blockchain in the education and healthcare sector to secure user records and simplify transactions (Alammary et al., 2019; Clavin et al., 2020; Orcutt, 2017). ## 3. Blockchain-based identity management systems

Identity management refers to “the management of individual identities, their authentication, authorization, roles and privileges within organization or across boundaries” (Mármol, 2010; Kumar and Bhardwaj, 2018). Identity management systems are not a new concept. However, considering the potential exposure of personal information, including name, age, address, employer, credit score and many other types of data, that can occur when information is passed through a network, identity management has recently become an extremely important issue, with the goal being to allow users to have complete control over their shared data.
