The retinal fundus of the normal patient reveals the arteries (which can be large or small) and the optic disc very clearly. Less volume of blood vessels is seen in a patient whose eye is compromised by mild cataract, whereas, in the retinal fundus in patients with mild cataract, only large blood vessels are visible. Finally, absolutely nothing is found in the case of extreme cataract. These cataract classes differ between 0 and 3 with 0, 1, 2 and 3, respectively, meaning mild, medium, moderate and severe cataracts. Detection and classification of cataracts relies highly on the details of the blood stream and the optic disc. Segmentation of a blood vessel manually followed by its identification is a very time-consuming procedure. Consistent scientific attempts have been made in the field of retinal image processing [49]. A few statistical methods were designed to obtain the most powerful characteristics. Low levels such as morphology, colour, diameter of the vessel, shape, sharpness and thickness have indeed been incorporated inside the blood vessel, but some are equipped with advanced equipment and do not fit in remote clinics without specialist experts. Intermediate characteristics such as the retinal fundus image saliency diagram, are used in retinal blood vessel extraction classifiers [50]. Vessel characteristics such as tortuosity, thickness, angle and width are extremely complicated to analyse for the early onset of retinal disease [51]. Manual segmentation is often very costly and time-consuming as an ophthalmologist needs to be employed [50]. Automatic extraction and differentiation of vessels through the use of the AI is also important for the early identification of the disease.

In the Kaggle diabetic retinopathy challenge held in 2015, Van Grinsven et al [52] released the first paper on retinal fundus images that focused on deep learning. According to our understanding, since then, only 17 deep learning scientific articles have been published. Out of this, 12 are connected to the segmentation of the retinal vessel, which is the current priority. Here, we provide a thorough review of these 12 approaches by examining and evaluating their advantages and weaknesses. Study results will encourage the precocious and reliable identification of retinal disease based on the latest machine learning study. The article gives recommendations for potential technology for significantly reducing the loss of vision, increasing the accuracy of early detection and significantly improving the quality of life of the patient. Flexible and accurate solutions are explored, which would automatically be able to detect blood vessel changes and anticipate future illness from the outset.

### B. Retinal vessel segmentation methods

It can be further classified as unsupervised and supervised [6, 34, 35]. Classification techniques belonging to the supervised category train by using ground truth labels. Some of the most popular supervised ML techniques are —support vector machine (SVM), random forest, k-nearest neighbour (kNN), Neural Network (NN), bayes network, AdaBoost, and discriminant analysis [6, 34, 35]. On the other hand, Fuzzy C-means (FCM), Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM), and k-means clustering algorithms are some popular unsupervised ML techniques. Another type of ML is Deep Learning that uses two-dimensional Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) to learn attributes from images. Some of the noteworthy research works that apply these ML and DL techniques are discussed in the following subsections.

### C. Deep learning

A desirable characteristic of DL models such as the CNN is the ability to measure description and categorisation representations. Vessel segmentation through CNN was implemented in [53, 54], and also jointly with random forest, and Conditional Random Field (CRF) [55–57]. Improved models that used more sophisticated deep learning layers has been implemented. An example that can be cited is the combined detection of blood vessels and optic disc highlighted in [53, 58, 59]. They used a pre-trained CNN with a large number of images which occur naturally. The use of Dense CRF as well reinforcement datasets aimed at reducing training time, which are a challenge to traditional techniques have been used effectively for thin vessel segmentation [60, 61]. In contrast to networks utilizing only fully-connected layers, a fully convolutional network (FCN) was also identified as improving results of segregation of vessels. [62–67] have also demonstrated techniques for integrating a multiple number of networks with an aim to reducing training volume. Such techniques also enable analysis in multiple scales for detecting blood vessels of specific lengths [43], or for implementing in a Hanning window-based wavelet transform [60]. The efficiency of segmentation in spite the presence of several types of lesions can be improved by using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), which can generate synthetic images from labelled images [68, 69].

### D. Supervised methods

Neural Networks (NNs) which are based on backpropagation are used in combination with spatial textures to segment retinal vessels [70]. On similar lines, researchers have also made use of colours, pixel intensity, and moment independent features [71–73]. Vega et al [72] observed that lattice-based NN which has only one feed forward layer can provide improved convergence. In a different approach, researchers of [74] have demonstrated a wider and very deep NN that has cross-modality learning for segmenting vessels which is corrupted by noisy pathological artefacts. Local Binary Patterns (LBPs), and different shape-based feature descriptors were implemented with NN for segmenting thin blood vessels [75]. In another blood vessel enhancement technique, features derived from GMM, wavelet, and Gabor features are mixed with intensity, and SIFT features to train NNs and LDA classifier [76].

A parameterized and curvilinear supported Gaussian filter known as SCIRD-TS was used for segmenting thin retinal vessels [77]. Researchers of [78] used pixel intensities, Gabor features and textures together with ensemble classifier to differentiate between vessel and background [78, 79]. The attributes observed through AdaBoost algorithm have been demonstrated to extract vessel data from normal and pathological retinal images by using various combinations of linear classifiers [73]. Directional, Gaussian, and multiple scale LBP feature descriptors have been used with fuzzy inference systems as well [80]. The use of divergence vector based ensemble learning, dictionary-based sparse classifier, and fusion of multiple classifiers have been able to impart resistance against clutter in lesion images [81–83]. In order to deal with poor segmentation issues related to weak priors, SVM has been combined with fully connected CRF. Each such pixel was perceived as a function of matched filter (MF), and gradient magnitude [84]. In [85] it is shown that features derived from the retinal vessel’s shape and pixel intensities have a capacity to enhance retinal vessel segmentation that has lesions. Also, it is reported in [86] that a combination of hard k-means clustering and the SVM classifier can remove parallel retinal vessels that lie in proximity. Through the work of [86, 87], good image segmentation in lesion-affected hypertension and DR images have been achieved by using features such as Gabor, Binary Hausdorff symmetry measure, and multi-fractal features with the SVM classifier. In the random forest classifiers, visual attention modeling was used to deal with issues of near parallel vessels, CVR, slender vessels having low contrast, lesions and non-uniform illumination [88].

### E. Unsupervised techniques

The main advantage of unsupervised segmentation techniques of blood vessels is that manual annotation is not necessary. The pixel is grouped into a vessel and non-vessel category by means of image features. Vessel segmentation has also utilized the GMM-expectation maximization (EM) technique. The EM algorithm was entrusted by the task to impart either a maximum or most likely probability to pixels as belonging to the vessel or non-vessel category [89]. In this technique, the enhancement of blood vessels have been carried out with either top-hat transformation or high-pass filters. Here, optic disc and blood vessels have been processed by using a combination of GMM and gray voting. In another research work [90], the FCM soft clustering approach was implemented with multiple filters such as Frangi’s, Gabor wavelets, and matched filter for eliminating blobs in retinal fundus image, and enhancing thin blood vessels in due course [90]. Nature-inspired algorithms like bee colony optimization have also been used for identifying blood vessel specific cluster, and identifying background by evaluating texture and spatial dependence probabilities [91].

## 12.3 Brain tumour segmentation

Cell differentiation and irregular development in the brain tissue are brain tumours. Brain tumours can be classified according to their origin, as either primary or metastatic brain tumours. The primary cell’s origin is cells with brain tissues, whereas metastatic cancer cells spread to the brain or some other part of the body. Gliomas are a form of glial cell brain tumour. It is the dominant brain tumour type and many of the research areas concentrate on the segmentation of brain tumours.
