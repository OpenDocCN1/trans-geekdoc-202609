# 11.6.2 Early detection of systemic disease from fundus photography

Apart from the prior diagnosis of diseases of retina, early diagnosis of diseases of systemic circulation such as cardiovascular diseases or disorders can also be determined using fundus photography. Such determination is based on the calculation of retinal vessel characteristics, for heart stroke, hypertension or myocardial infarction [22, 23].

# 11.6.3 3-Dimensional OCT and retinal diseases—image guided therapy

3D OCT imaging, explains about the retinal morphology which helps in the close monitoring of a retinal disease like retinopathy caused due to high blood glucose level, glaucoma, tear, optic neuritis etc. The application of image guided treatment technology is proved to be beneficial in the treatment of retinopathy, macular edema in patient suffering from diabetes where the central part of the retina, the macula, is filled with leakage fluid causing visual loss or blindness. According to the research, the Early Treatment in Diabetic Retinopathy Study [24-26] shows that therapy taken for a diabetic macular edema (DME) with a focal laser, can prevent deterioration in the situation. The use of novel anti-vascular endothelial growth factor drugs, for example—ranibizumab combined with focal laser depicts better results in the treatment of DME. Recently, OCT imaging has shown the extent and amount of thickening in the retina, which helps with further treatment.

Neo-vascularization of the choroid is another form of vision loss category disease that has shown good results from image guided therapy. With the introduction of the anti-vascular endothelial growth factor agents like ranibizumab and bevacizumab, case improvements related to vision have been noticed [28, 29]. 3D representation of the optic nerve can be applied for early diagnosis and precise treatment to reduce the risk of blindness due to glaucoma [30].

# 11.7 Fundus image analysis

The most reliable means of fundus imaging is retinal imaging. Until recently, fundus image analysis was the only source of quantitative indices reflecting retinal morphological features. Subjects that lend themselves for fundus image analysis include: quantification of image quality (verification of image quality; detection, imaging of artifact; iatrogenic lesion detection (laser scars, etc); retinal structure’s location and segmentation (retinal vessels, vessel diameter; artery and vein classification; vessel occlusion detection, fovea, optic disc-cup and rim; cupping); abnormalities segmentation (blood vessel related abnormalities/hemorrhages; microaneurysms; neovascularizations; nerve fiber infarcts—cottonwool spots); pigment epithelium related abnormalities (drusen hyper and hypopigmentation); choroid related abnormalities (nevus and melanoma detection; uveitis related choroidal lesions).
