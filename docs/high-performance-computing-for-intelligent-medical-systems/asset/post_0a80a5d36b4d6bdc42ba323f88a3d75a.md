## 11.5 Current status of retinal analysis

The last 160 years saw the progression of imaging of the retina, which is now the backbone of clinical care of the eye patient having various retinal and systemic diseases. These techniques are used successfully for the diagnosis of diseases and disorders of high blood sugar, high blood pressure, dementia, atherosclerosis and other related cardiovascular diseases, which are detected by fundus photography. The popularly chosen methods are optical coherence tomography (OCT) and fluorescein angiography. In addition to this, OCT is also used in vitreo-retinal surgery and its follow up.

### 11.5.1 Fundus imaging

In fundus imaging, there is a two-dimensional image projection of 3D retinal tissue. The intensity of the photo is directly proportional to the quantity of reflected light. The fundus imaging is cheap providing fine observation to the analyser.

The various categories of fundus imaging are fundus photography method, colour fundus photography method, stereo fundus photography method, hyperspectral imaging technique, scanning laser ophthalmoscopy technique, adaptive optics SLO, fluorescein angiography and indocyanine angiography methods.

- (I) Fundus image analysis for the detection of diabetic retinopathy

The principle behind the detection of diabetic retinopathy from fundus images is based on the micro diagnosis of micro aneurysms, blood leakage and micro blood clots observed on the fundus, which reflects the inner parts of the retina.

- (II) Image acquisition

This is the first step in the direction of detection of diabetic retinopathy. The acquisition of fundus colour image and its analysis helps us to observe abnormalities of the retina. Actually, in the process of fundus imaging there is projection of 3D half transparent tissues of the retina on the imaging plane. A special fundus camera is used to take the high-resolution image. The various manufacturers of cameras are Topcon, Canon, Kowa etc. The camera can be categorised into two types: mydriatic camera involving dilation of the pupil, and the other category of non-mydriatic camera. There are many databases available having numerous fundus images such as DRIVE, STARE, MESSIDOR, DIARETDB1, ARIA and ROC.

- (III) Image pre-processing

Due to many limitations, the image quality of the image get deteriorated because of many factors such as low focus, unwanted motion of the patient, non-uniformity of light and improper calibration. The unusual features can only be detected if the quality of fundus images is up to standard. It is therefore suggested to apply image pre-processing techniques. Various algorithms are designed to remove the background abnormalities of the image. Three of the best known methods are histogram specification, histogram equalization and grey level normalization.

There are multiple features that indicate the damage caused due to retinopathy:

- 1. There is bright appearance of the exudates;
- 2. Dark appearance of vessels of blood as well as fovea (grey border);
- 3. Appearance of the optic disk as orange or pink in colour with diameter of 1.5 millimetre;
- 4. Micro-aneurysms are characteristic anatomical features that are dark circle shaped regions;
- 5. Haemorrhages are considered a major hallmark for determining the background of retinopathy caused by high glucose level;
- 6. Appearance of ‘dot and blot’ pattern and the colour is the same as that of vessels.

### 11.5.2 Optical coherence tomography

The theory behind the working of OCT is to estimate the ‘depth at which a specific backscatter arises by calculating its time of flight’. Backscatters arises due to the differences in refractive index in transitions from one tissue to another tissue. The deeper tissues backscatter can be differentiated from backscatter arising in more superficial tissue due to longer time for the light to arrive at the sensor. Interferometry is used to measure the differences in ‘time of flight’ [17]. There are three major OCT theories:

- I. Time-domain OCT, also called time-of-flight;
- II. Swept-source or time encoded frequency domain OCT;
- III. Spectral-domain OCT [18].

## 11.6 Disease specific analysis of retinal images

In order to increase the precision of eye patient management, to increase the eye clinician productivity and to reduce the cost, the requirement of reliable computerized methods or approaches for the retinal image analysis is the need of the time.

### 11.6.1 Early detection of retinal disease from fundus photography

Retinal disease detection is the most obvious example of a retinal screening application, in which the person suffering from eye disease is photographed in a distanced teleconsultation. This approach involves the utilization of cheap cameras for fundus photography which test the retinal images, and then focus on the preparation of the final output. It is considered the best example of telemedicine [19]. The preliminary diagnosis of retinopathy in a patient suffering from high blood glucose level through this technology is the hot spot nowadays but a screening program is available for the diagnosis of hypertension, dementia, disorder of optic nerve and various cardiovascular disorders. It has been observed that problems in the retinal vision can be prevented by the early diagnosis or detection of diabetic retinopathy [20, 21]. The work can be done successfully by applying the aforementioned sophisticated technology.

### 11.6.2 Early detection of systemic disease from fundus photography

Apart from the prior diagnosis of diseases of retina, early diagnosis of diseases of systemic circulation such as cardiovascular diseases or disorders can also be determined using fundus photography. Such determination is based on the calculation of retinal vessel characteristics, for heart stroke, hypertension or myocardial infarction [22, 23].

### 11.6.3 3-Dimensional OCT and retinal diseases—image guided therapy

3D OCT imaging, explains about the retinal morphology which helps in the close monitoring of a retinal disease like retinopathy caused due to high blood glucose level, glaucoma, tear, optic neuritis etc. The application of image guided treatment technology is proved to be beneficial in the treatment of retinopathy, macular edema in patient suffering from diabetes where the central part of the retina, the macula, is filled with leakage fluid causing visual loss or blindness. According to the research, the Early Treatment in Diabetic Retinopathy Study [24-26] shows that therapy taken for a diabetic macular edema (DME) with a focal laser, can prevent deterioration in the situation. The use of novel anti-vascular endothelial growth factor drugs, for example—ranibizumab combined with focal laser depicts better results in the treatment of DME. Recently, OCT imaging has shown the extent and amount of thickening in the retina, which helps with further treatment.

Neo-vascularization of the choroid is another form of vision loss category disease that has shown good results from image guided therapy. With the introduction of the anti-vascular endothelial growth factor agents like ranibizumab and bevacizumab, case improvements related to vision have been noticed [28, 29]. 3D representation of the optic nerve can be applied for early diagnosis and precise treatment to reduce the risk of blindness due to glaucoma [30].

## 11.7 Fundus image analysis

The most reliable means of fundus imaging is retinal imaging. Until recently, fundus image analysis was the only source of quantitative indices reflecting retinal morphological features. Subjects that lend themselves for fundus image analysis include: quantification of image quality (verification of image quality; detection, imaging of artifact; iatrogenic lesion detection (laser scars, etc); retinal structure’s location and segmentation (retinal vessels, vessel diameter; artery and vein classification; vessel occlusion detection, fovea, optic disc-cup and rim; cupping); abnormalities segmentation (blood vessel related abnormalities/hemorrhages; microaneurysms; neovascularizations; nerve fiber infarcts—cottonwool spots); pigment epithelium related abnormalities (drusen hyper and hypopigmentation); choroid related abnormalities (nevus and melanoma detection; uveitis related choroidal lesions).
