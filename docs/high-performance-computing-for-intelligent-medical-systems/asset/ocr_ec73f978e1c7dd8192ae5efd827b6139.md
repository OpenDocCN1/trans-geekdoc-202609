It is the characteristics and structures of the vocal tract and the vocal folds that produce sounds that are unique to all. The ‘pitch’ that is derived from the vocal fold lingers in the nasal cavities, and the throat that has a distinct acoustic structure is another source of individuality for any voice. There are three distinct layers in the laryngeal vocal fold, that is, the vocal ligament, the mucosa, and the vocal fold. Since these tissues are tender, they can be continuously affected by air pressure and release the mucosal waveform. The ability of the mucosa to produce pitch rates determines whether the voice is regular or abnormal.

For speech specialists such as phoniatricians, a voice has several characteristics of an individual, such as mental well-being, physical trauma, vocal pathologies in the vocal fold, the person’s gender, emotional state, etc. A deviation from the normal vocal attributes of a person as opposed to those who share the age group and gender can be defined as a problem of laryngitis or other vocal fold disease. Vocal fold disease is caused by gradual alteration in the voice synthesizing organs and tissues in the body. These changes may be due to over-manipulation of the vocal cords, frequent intake of medications, or neurological disorders. Abuse of vocal cord can lead to conditions such as unilateral nerve paralysis, laryngeal cancer, polyp, nodule, and folding for a prolonged period of time. These situations can be defined by the hoarseness of one’s voice. In addition to self-abuse, an unhealthy lifestyle may contribute to an increase in vocal disorders [1, 2]. Singers, mothers of children and educators are people who unknowingly abuse their vocal cords. The reasoning is that they have to say words constantly during the work plan. Such excessive use of the voice fold leads to more rigidity over time. The vagus nerve, which is responsible for the proper functioning of the larynx, is also highly likely to be dysfunctional. This condition is known as unilateral nerve paralysis, which is usually noticed from the speaker’s breathing phonation sound. Other issues are also reported, such as air deprivation, trouble swallowing and mild coughing [3]. Irregularity occurs in the vocal box or larynx due to repetitive abuse due to the nature of the work that can contribute to breathing and harsh vocals. Physical features, including elasticity, shape and volume, associated with vocal folds, are modified when disorders, including gastric reflection or anterior-to-posterior (A–P) squeezing, begin. This leads to an unusual variant of vibration. Swelling of the vocal folds are caused by gastric reflux and laryngitis. While muscle tension damage is caused by constant A–P squeezing.
