# 12.4 Discussion and conclusion

This chapter presents the work, i.e., pre-existing and current techniques, accomplished by other researchers related to biomedical images segmentation, especially retina vessel segmentation and brain tumour segmentation. Here every referenced paper is summarized. It presents numerous types of segmentation by using different methods. Each segmentation approach reviewed here has its leads and constraints, and setting a particular benchmark for comparison is inappropriate. The segmentation of biomedical images has been broadly categorized in this paper. The validation of segmentation outcomes is a quintessential subject in biomedical image analysis since it directly influences surgical judgments. Orthodox machine learning algorithms can be further improved. For example, the method of extraction is mostly performed using a series of pre-specified filters in most conventional machine learning algorithms. Thus it would greatly increase efficiency if data-driven extraction methods were devised for conventional learning algorithms. Recent research on the segmentation of biological images relies mostly on the paradigm of deep learning. Finding high-quality biomedical image data is always a challenge. Deep architectures like ResNet, VGGNet and GoogLeNet typically overfit the learning framework significantly in cases where there are not enough training images, even after including regularisations, dropouts, and model averaging. The accuracy of CNN in the classification task of biomedical image datasets with GoogLeNet architecture has been steadily increased due to an increase in training images. The current level of classification is too simple for practical biomedical imaging processing, such as segmentation, but a fundamental relationship between efficiency and consistency of the training data set has been identified. Researchers have trained 3D segmentation models with only 2D segmentation in brain tumours segmentation study.
