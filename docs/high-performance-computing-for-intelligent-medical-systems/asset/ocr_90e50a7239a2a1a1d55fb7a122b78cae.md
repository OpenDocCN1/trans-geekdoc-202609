Model (GMM), and k-means clustering algorithms are some popular unsupervised ML techniques. Another type of ML is Deep Learning that uses two-dimensional Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) to learn attributes from images. Some of the noteworthy research works that apply these ML and DL techniques are discussed in the following subsections.

# C. Deep learning

A desirable characteristic of DL models such as the CNN is the ability to measure description and categorisation representations. Vessel segmentation through CNN was implemented in [53, 54], and also jointly with random forest, and Conditional Random Field (CRF) [55–57]. Improved models that used more sophisticated deep learning layers has been implemented. An example that can be cited is the combined detection of blood vessels and optic disc highlighted in [53, 58, 59]. They used a pre-trained CNN with a large number of images which occur naturally. The use of Dense CRF as well reinforcement datasets aimed at reducing training time, which are a challenge to traditional techniques have been used effectively for thin vessel segmentation [60, 61]. In contrast to networks utilizing only fully-connected layers, a fully convolutional network (FCN) was also identified as improving results of segregation of vessels. [62–67] have also demonstrated techniques for integrating a multiple number of networks with an aim to reducing training volume. Such techniques also enable analysis in multiple scales for detecting blood vessels of specific lengths [43], or for implementing in a Hanning window-based wavelet transform [60]. The efficiency of segmentation in spite the presence of several types of lesions can be improved by using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), which can generate synthetic images from labelled images [68, 69].

# D. Supervised methods

Neural Networks (NNs) which are based on backpropagation are used in combination with spatial textures to segment retinal vessels [70]. On similar lines, researchers have also made use of colours, pixel intensity, and moment independent features [71–73]. Vega et al [72] observed that lattice-based NN which has only one feed forward layer can provide improved convergence. In a different approach, researchers of [74] have demonstrated a wider and very deep NN that has cross-modality learning for segmenting vessels which is corrupted by noisy pathological aretefacts. Local Binary Patterns (LBPs), and different shape-based feature descriptors were implemented with NN for segmenting thin blood vessels [75]. In another blood vessel enhancement technique, features derived from GMM, wavelet, and Gabor features are mixed with intensity, and SIFT features to train NNs and LDA classifier [76].

A parameterized and curvilinear supported Gaussian filter known as SCIRD-TS was used for segmenting thin retinal vessels [77]. Researchers of [78] used pixel intensities, Gabor features and textures together with ensemble classifier to differentiate between vessel and background [78, 79].
