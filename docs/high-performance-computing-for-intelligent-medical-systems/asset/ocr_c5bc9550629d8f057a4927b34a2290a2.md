The expression glioma is being used to identify multiple kinds of gliomas, from low-grade gliomas like astrocytoma and oligodendrogliomas up to high-grade multiform glioblastoma and even the most damaging primary malignant brain tumour [92]. Radiotherapy, surgery and chemotherapy are procedures used, typically in tandem, for the treatment of gliomas [93].

Early glioma diagnosis plays a crucial role in enhancing treatment options. Imaging technologies including CT, PET, MRI have been employed in order to ensure valuable information about the structure, magnitude, location and metabolism of the brain tumour. While these methods could be used in conjunction with brain tumours to provide the best knowledge, the definitive soft-tissue contrast and wide adoption make MRI the base standard. MRI is a near-invasive in vivo imaging framework for implementing radio frequencies to influence target tissues to produce their internal images, under the effects of a very strong magnetic field. Images from various MRI sequences are created by altering the time when the image is recovered. Different MRI modalities provide different images of tissue contrast that provide useful structural information and allow the identification and segmenting of the tumour in conjunction with its sub-regions [94]. During the acquisition of MRI, although it can differ from device to device, almost a hundred slices of 2D pictures are generated to reflect the 3D brain volume. Moreover, when the fragments from the required normal modalities are fused for study, the data becomes densely populated and complex.

During a surgical procedure aimed at removing/depleting tumour cells, care must also be taken to preserve as much healthy tissue as possible. Brain tumour segmentation involves identification, delineation and differentiation of the tumour tissues of healthy brain tissues from necrotic cells or activated cells and edema. The strategy requires the manual annotation and segmentation of a large number of multimodal MRI images in a routine manner. However, since handcrafted segmentation is a pro-lengthy procedure, the development of robust automatic segmentation methods to provide effective and accurate segmentation has become an interesting and prominent scientific discipline in recent years [95]. Recently, high segmentation results achieved by deep learning strategies make them good nominees to accomplish this task.

# 12.3.1 Brain tumour segmentation databases

+   A. BrainWeb

The McConnell Brain Imaging Centre developed the BrainWeb dataset; an anatomical representation of normal and dysfunctional brains is provided by an MRI simulator. The BrainWeb's strength scale consists of 0, 20 and 40 non-uniformity ranges, with each of the slices having thicknesses of 1 mm, 3 mm, 5 mm, 7 mm and 9 mm. The noise level remains 0 percent, 1 percent, 3 percent, 7 percent and 9 percent. The Centre for Morphometric Research has developed the Internet Brain Segmentation Repository (IBSR) (CMA). The brain tumours MRI dataset provides files of 16-bit integers formatted as 256 × 256 images.
