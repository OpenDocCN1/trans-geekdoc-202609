The research work therefore emphasizes the use of time–frequency (TF) features to capture the dynamics of the pathological voice signal. Their binary classification using a k-means unsupervised clustering technique with these characteristics resulted in a substantial accuracy of 98.6%.

Vowel /a/ recording is very widespread in voice pathology techniques and is widely found in all well-known databases such as MEEI, SVD, and APVD. Undoubtedly, its implementation has achieved reasonable accuracy. However, investigators of [129, 130] used both the running speech and the sustained vowel /a/ recordings found in the APVD data collection. They achieved more than 99% accuracy. The authors of [131] used a signal pre-processing technique known as Voice Activity Detection (VAD) before extracting MFCC features. The VAD module is a form of filter that distinguishes the silent or voiced sections or the noise spectrum present in the signal. The training of the VAD module initially involves the development of a signal mixed with the noise spectrum, after which the useful and noisy regions of the signal are manually annotated. These annotated noisy and useful regions are collectively categorized as the voiced component of the signal. In this way, the classifier identifies the spectra of noise, the clean signal, and the silent part of the signal spectrum. This is a sophisticated and nuanced method. The implementation of the MFCC features with the VAD module on the 117 and 23 abnormal and safe recordings present in the MEEI database resulted in an accuracy of 96%.

Another binary classification technique by [132] qualified Hidden Markov Model (HMM), GMM, and SVM with six separate feature descriptors, namely—Relative Average Perturbation (RAP), shimmer, jitter, soft phonation index (SPI), HNR and Amplitude Perturbation Quotient (APQ). The pathological voice category consisted of numerous laryngeal diseases such as laryngeal cancer, cyst, palsy, Edema, nodule, polyp, and laryngitis. The implementation result showed that the GMM with these feature vectors had the highest accuracy, even higher than the artificial neural network (ANN)-based implementation with which it is compared.

A smartphone-based hardware prototype for diagnosing individual dysphonia has been seen in a landmark and recently published research work [133]. The disorder has a high association with the unhealthy manipulation of vocal cords on a regular basis due to the nature of the work. The key features of this type of condition are non-uniformity in loudness, degradation of pitch and voice quality. It is composed of around 10% of the other voice pathologies that affect individuals worldwide. The investigation demonstrated the efficiency of several features with different ML techniques, such as logistic model tree, support vector descriptors, berry model, decision tree, and instance-based learning. Features that have been used in this work are—HNR, SPI, shimmer, spectral features, jitter, MFCC, RAP, disruption, energy, temporal features, noise, and wavelet features. The comparison of efficiencies was made using popular metrics such as sensitivity, specificity, apart from the use of precision as widely used for other techniques. The investigation concluded that SVM and a decision tree with features could provide very good results.
