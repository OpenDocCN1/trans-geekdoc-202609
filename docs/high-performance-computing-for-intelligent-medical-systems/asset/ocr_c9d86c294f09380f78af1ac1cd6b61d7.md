# 6.3 Related work

The number of confirmed COVID-19 cases by machine learning algorithms was discussed in one study. They used a K-Means machine learning algorithm based on disease prevalence, air population metrics, and healthy system courage to analyse country data. The authors created a model for 155 countries using three PCA parameters and six clusters to show the relevance of group countries [15]. In another research the authors analyzed COVID-19 incidence of Google trends by data mining and deep learning. They used linear regression and long–short-term model to detect the incidence of COVID-19 actual cases. The authors calculated the values of RMSE 7.562 (SD 6.492) and the RMSE of the LSTM 27.187 (SD 28.705) [16]. Other researchers computed the growth and trend of COVID-19 by machine learning and cloud computing. They predicted COVID-19 disease by mathematical model, machine learning and cloud computing on the basis of baseline. The authors proposed the Robust Weibull model and predicted better than baseline [17]. Machine learning algorithms were used to analyse the COVID-19 epidemic in India. The author calculated root mean square error using six regression analysis-based machine learning algorithms models. For forecasting over long time intervals, the author discovered that a 6° polynomial model calculated a significantly lower root mean square error (RMSE) (the standard deviation of the residuals (prediction errors)) [18]. Another research team predicted a comparative analysis of machine learning with soft computing (multilayered perception, adaptive neural network, fuzzy system and ANFIS) for COVID-19. The authors used SIR (susceptible, infectious, recovered) and SEIR (susceptible, exposed, infectious, recovered) models that help for COVID-19 discovery for outlook and found SEIR performed better [19]. The research team analyzed the COVID-19 outbreak in India and used SEIR with a regression model. The authors calculated the values of RMSE for the SEIR model and they found the SEIR model widely predicted the COVID-19 data compared to the regression model. Pandey et al measured the values of RMSE 1.52 for SEIR and 1.75 for the regression model[20]. The researchers measured healthy policy in each country by time and intensity in COVID-19. They used deep reinforcement learning and finally they found higher intensity lockdown and travel restrictions public health interventions during COVID-19 [21]. Rustam et al discussed forecasting by machine learning to decision making on future courses of action. They used linear regression, Lasso, support vector machine and exponential smoothing in COVID-19 forecasting. Finally, they found exponential smoothing performs best results compare to other algorithms [22]. Wang et al discussed COVID-19 infection with symptoms and the effect of infection on the human body. They found the effects of infection are greater in men than women, and no deaths were reported between the ages of 0 and 9 [23]. Other authors considered several cases of pneumonia with unfamiliar etiology in china. The National Health Commission of China reported details on viral pneumonia [24]. The researchers analyzed the details of the novel corona virus and diagnosis of corona virus infection in China. The National Health Commission visited a sea food market and released details of the infected animals, birds and other food sources. After analyzing the infected person details, the commission provided details of human capacity for the virus and exposed infection symptoms with the spread of infection penetrating human lungs through the nose or mouth [25–28].
