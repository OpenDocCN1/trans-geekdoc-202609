
An investigation to establish a detector for vocal larynx defects by extracting a combination of different feature descriptors from a pathological voice sample was proposed in [103]. The key problem with pathological voice identification and classification tasks is that ML algorithms are only equipped to distinguish between regular and abnormal voice samples. It is therefore a matter of binary classification. While the key problem that delays actual hardware deployment is the inability to pinpoint the exact disease. On similar lines, [103] investigators have conducted a binary classification using 30 and 20 pathological and normal voice samples, respectively. A mixture of different types of feature descriptors, such as the well-known MFCC, Zero-Crossing Rate (ZCR), ZCR mean, and LPC, was used for segmented voice recordings with lengths of 200, 300, 400 and 500 milliseconds. In their work, a threshold level was manually set for each function variety to establish a division between usual and abnormal voice signals.

Speech problems may not simply be due to vocal fold defects, but may also be due to Parkinson’s disease, hypernasal lip and palate dysfunction in children/individuals. In this line, another research study [104] proposed a technique to diagnose these disorders as well as vocal fold disorders. They experimented with numerous feature descriptors to include automatic detection, such as simple frequency accuracy measurement, noisy spectrum measurement, non-linear amplitude and signal interference, cepstral features. It has been found that adding consistency measures to fundamental frequency is acceptable for speech disorders of laryngeal and Parkinson’s, whereas cepstral features function well with hypernasal speech. However, implementation with a noisy spectrum can properly diagnose dysphonic speech. It can therefore be established that the use of a single feature type or a combination of different feature vectors to diagnose multiple disorders with a single classifier will not yield a good result. It is therefore very important to investigate the best suited combination of a feature-classifier pair for all major voice pathologies.

The [105] research used time and frequency domain features with multiple type of ML algorithms to identify voice pathologies. Their experimentation led to the conclusion that spectral characteristics were among the most important feature descriptors. While it was discovered that pitch or fundamental frequency was not very significant. It was also realized that residual signal features extracted from a signal filtered using an inverse LPC filter have the highest effect on accuracy.

### B. SVM classifier.

This is a supervised learning method that uses the framework of support vector statistics to classify a feature vector as an acceptable class [106]. In other words, it uses a function to map the output category to the input vector. An example can be used to better understand the term ‘supervised learning’. Suppose a programmer creates and labels four folders in the directory as Class-A, Class-B, Class-C, and Class-D. Then they place the unique voice samples for these categories. Using basic programming, they show the machine that the labels for all the samples within it are the folder names. The computer will now be taught that voice samples or vector features extracted from a particular folder belong to a specific collection. ML algorithms use these data and have shown excellent results for high-dimensional data. It has also been reported in many practical applications [107]. Its underlying principle is to create a hyperplane that uses a maximum margin to separate feature vectors from different sets or classes. A set of support vectors is used to demarcate the boundary of the decision. A non-linear or linear hyperplane can be used. To have a very strong class differentiation, the basic principle is to construct an ideal hyperplane. In the diagnosis of laryngeal paralysis, the authors of [108] discussed the effectiveness of the use of entropy and energy-based features. To extract tenable features from voice signals affected by unilateral voice fold paralysis, wavelet decomposition was initially used for this purpose. A total of 32 and 11 active sub-bands were used to obtain entropy and energy characteristics, respectively. Before training with a linear SVM classifier, the authors used genetic algorithms to optimize the vector features. By using SVM with entropy and energy characteristics, they achieved an accuracy of 100% and 93.62%, respectively. The properties of entropy have been shown to be useful for the diagnosis of laryngeal paralysis. In another pathological voice detection technique described in [109], the researchers used sustained vocal samples from the MEEI dataset using a modulation spectrum technique. Specifically, it is a mixture of modulation frequency and harmonics of voice. In addition to the modulation spectrum method, the reduction of dimensionality was also used before the training of vectors with the SVM classifier. A special feature of this extraction process is the observation of a distortion in the amplitude pattern of a voice sample with pathology, which can provide rich cues for detection and accurate classification. A cumulative accuracy of 94.1% has been observed. However, it is important to study their application with other current data sets in order to assess their potential efficacy. They also looked at the performance of other traditional extraction methods and highlighted critical challenges related to pitch estimation, global signal estimation. Wavelet filter banks have also been used to differentiate natural and abnormal voice samples. More specifically, the wavelet filter banks were used to measure eight energy features using the lattice factorization technique. The feature vector extraction technique was applied to a privately collected dataset and to the MEEI dataset. Investigators are said to have reached a classification rate of 100% on their issue of binary classification. However, it remains to be seen if this remarkable precision can be accomplished for multiple pathology databases. A universal classifier that can diagnose voice pathology for patients living in any corner of the world is still a major challenge for researchers prior to any realistic implementation in hospitals [110].

Another landmark research using entropy for the binary classification of healthy and pathological voices was proposed in [111]. They evaluated the efficiency of PCA, LDA and SVM classifiers with different vector extraction techniques, such as wavelet packet transformation (WPT), STFT, CWT. Statistical evaluation showed that using an active subband of 6 with a WPT technique with LDA and SVM learners was able to achieve an overall accuracy of 100%. It can also be concluded that disturbance, inconsistency, and non-linearity of irregular voice and entropy extraction can provide efficient binary classification. Entropy is indeed a good way to measure the statistical randomness of a pathological voice. A multi-class voice pathology classifier that uses a rich feature vector derived from diverse and common speech features has been presented in [112]. The features used together to create a single vector function are MFCC, HNR, pitch and amplitude disturbance, Mel-frequency, cepstral energy, LP Cosine Transform (LPCT) coefficients, and autocorrelation. Feature vectors belonging to healthy, vocal nodules and diffuse voice lesion samples have been trained with the SVM classifier to provide over 90% overall accuracy. The results of their implementation have also been related to the recommendations of the three phoniatricians. It has been noted that the use of sustained vocals alone for automatic prediction is much more successful than human phoniatrician predictions. Researchers of [37] used low-level MPEG-7 audio with false discovery rate (FDR) features to select and categorize four-class vectors—vocal fold nodules, keratosis, laryngeal paralysis, and polyp. They used the feature vectors to train the SVM classifier and achieved 99.99% accuracy. Authors of [113] proposed a binary classification of stable and pathological voice recordings using the M-band and SVM classifiers. Their technique yielded a combined accuracy of 100% using four and five bands. Voiceprint was also extracted by compressing and averaging MFCC voice recording frames with three sustained voice tones, that is, /a/, /u/ and /o/ [114]. Using these features, they conducted a binary classifying discriminatory analysis using the SVM classifier and achieved an overall accuracy of 100%. The authors also claimed that voice printing is more acceptable for the diagnosis of voice pathology with a sustained vowel /u/. A multi-band wavelet analysis for the multi-class classification of voice pathologies in the MEEI dataset was also suggested in [115]. The proposed technique used the fractal dimension (FD) power dimension with a three-level discrete wavelet transformation (DWT). Out of a total of 173 abnormal recordings seen in the MEEI dataset, only 168 recordings were used because the MDVP parameters for 5 recordings were not available. They used FD features with a total of 22 MDVP parameters to achieve a 2.26% increase in accuracy over their previous research work. Research by [116] has shown that features derived from the pathological vocal tract area exhibit inconsistent disturbances over successive frames for continuous voice recordings. The metrics used to determine inconsistency are kurtosis, variance, mean and skewness. These signal disturbances have been exploited to distinguish a safe voice from an abnormal voice. Their approach was based on a natural mechanism for voice production. During their investigation, two important points were identified: (a) the supraglottic tract plays a more prominent role in the voice production process than the remaining regions of the vocal tract; (b) the variance is a more critical parameter than the mean area of the vocal tract. The thorough investigation of the MEEI and SVD voice pathology databases showed an accuracy of 99.22% and 94.7%, respectively. A further MDVP-based detection of speech pathologies of voice recordings from MEEI, SVD and AVPD databases has been proposed in [2]. Three common abnormalities have been considered, that is, vocal fold polyp, cyst, and laryngeal paralysis. Study also used the FDR method for sorting the extracted features. Their research into the use of MDVP features with the SVM classifier resulted in an accuracy of 88.21%, 99.68% and 72.53% for the above three databases, respectively. In another uncommon research, a non-linear Gaussian kernel SVM unsupervised learner was employed in three separate phases to distinguish voice pathologies via a comparative approach.
