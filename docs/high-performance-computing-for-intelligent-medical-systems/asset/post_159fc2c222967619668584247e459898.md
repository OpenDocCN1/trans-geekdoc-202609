

## 13.3 Analysis of the biomedical system

Biomedical engineering (BME) is the well-known branch of engineering which is the combination of engineering ethics and methods used in the medicinal area. To improve the patient’s health and life quality, it is necessary to use the engineer’s skill with medicinal and natural discipline. Only conventional instruments were used previously, for disease identification and monitoring. There are numerous biomedical systems developed with new technology that modernize the health care system. Nowadays, hospice and health care centers use a variety of advanced and stylish instruments due to technological improvement [61].

An apparatus that is used for a particular job is called an instrument. Most commonly used instruments are [62]:

- 1. Diagnostic instruments (DI): ECG (Electrocardiogram), EEG (Electroencephalogram);
- 2. Imaging instruments (II): e.g., MRI, CT Scan;
- 3. Therapeutic instruments (TI): e.g., Pace-makers (PMs).

ECG: It is used to measure the electrical signal generated by the heart muscles in cardiac functioning. It was invented by Wilhelm Einthoven. Electrocardiography is a technique to sense the electrical waveform generated by the heart. It is a specific arrangement of sensor (electrodes) in which two sensors are placed to receive the electric signals and another is for reference purposes. The electrical signals are of few millivolts, so they need to be amplified. After the proper conditioning of received signal they are ready to be saved or to display on screen. The ECG is an instrument which is used to measure the cardiac waveform to be stored, to display on screen or to print on paper. It indicates the functioning and condition of the heart, like heart beat cardiac output and myocardium etc. It gives information about the rate and rhythm of the heart and the condition of the heart muscles. Some diseases of the heart can be identified by it such as: heart attack, arrhythmia, cardiovascular etc.

EEG: A device that is used to measure the minute impulsive electrical movements of the brain by means of metal disc sensors positioned around the head. The technique of graphical representation of electrical waveforms generated by the brain is known as electroencephalography. ‘Brain death’ can be diagnosed with the help of EEG.

Imaging instruments (II): These are used to diagnose disease or any disorder of an organ in the very early phase, while the general or common diagnostic methods are not able to detect the same.

Magnetic resonance imaging: MRI gives images for organs or tissues of any layer of the body. This instrument was designed by Raymond Damadian and Bloch and Purcell. A computed tomography (CT) scan is a form of x-ray that is more advanced than a traditional x-ray. CT scan is an illustration technique which combines a computer to visualize the internals of the body with higher definition. A rotary arrangement of x-ray source and detector are used to imaging the body in a 360 angle.

Ultrasonography (USG): This works on the principle of Doppler effect in which a sound source with sound detectors is used to get an image of the internal body. The sound waves reflect from the internal organs are received by the detector to generate the two-dimensional image on the computer screen.

Therapeutic instruments (TI): An instrument that is used for therapy purposes. The PM is a small instrument which is used to keep the heart rhythm under control. It provides necessary electrical signals externally to the heart to maintain the proper heart beats.

### 13.3.1 Classical controller in a biomedical system

Feedback controllers are generally used in industry as well as in the biomedical field, known as PID. Elmer Sperry invented the first PID controller in 1911. PID has fast transient response and the ability to conquer steady state error. It has a quick momentary reaction and a lot of energy to beat steady state error. The PID has three controlling parameters: first is ‘P’ that depends on present change in the error; the second is ‘I’ that depends on past error; third is ‘D’ that depends on future error. By adjusting these three parameters, researchers get the desired system outcome, as shown in table 13.3, according to need [63].

Error is the variation among the output (process variable) and the input (set point) of the controller. A PID controller uses a feedback phenomenon which can overcome the problem of error by adjusting the three parameters, i.e. P, I and D, as shown in figure 13.2 and in equation (13.1), according to system requirement.

### 13.4.1 MAP control

Mean arterial pressure (MAP) observation and controlling is necessary at the time of surgery. For same, researchers presented many controlling techniques, for example: PID control [64–66], model adaptive predictive control [67], fuzzy logic control [68], PI-PID control tuned with traditional technique [69] and PID tuning with PSO technique [70]. Figure 13.3 shows controlling of MAP using PID optimization techniques. Patient blood pressure can be represented by [71]:

G_p(s) = \frac{Y(s)}{U(s)} = \frac{K(1 + \tau_3 s)e^{-Ls}}{[(1 + \tau_3 s)(1 + \tau_2 s) - \alpha](1 + \tau_7 s)} \quad (13.2)

P = p_0 - Y \quad (13.3)

- where: G_{p1} = MAP Laplace based model
- Y = decrease in the main burden because of the medicine effect;
- p_0 = preliminary trunk pressure, P the subsequent pressure;
- P = resulting pressure;
- U = percentage of medicine infusion;
- K = system gain;
- L = time delay of system.

### 13.4.2 Intracranial tumor's temperature control

The primary aim is to automatically control the minimum temperature of brain tissues of a tumor patient who received interstitial hyperthermia treatment (IHT) [72]. In IHT, a catheter array is embedded in the tumor tissue during neurosurgery. The catheters work as temperature sensors as well as actuators. Tumor's temperature control using PID optimization techniques is shown in figure 13.4. The model for tumor tissues temperature control was established in [72] and can be represented by:
