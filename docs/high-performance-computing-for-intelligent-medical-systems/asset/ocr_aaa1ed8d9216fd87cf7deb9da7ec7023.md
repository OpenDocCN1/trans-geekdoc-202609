# 7.4.2 The role of timely feedback

The purpose of patient feedback systems is to offer high quality health information. Such information can work as an enabler for enhanced organizational and patient outcomes [59]. Patients’ opinions about their visits to the doctor offer beneficial information for health professionals and comprehensive awareness of the causes of diagnostic errors with critical, unique insights [6, 60]. Healthcare providers should ensure that systematic feedback on diagnostic performance extends to doctors and care teams involved in care and organizational management [61, 62]. Periodic feedback is necessary for improved diagnostic performance and quality improvement in healthcare but is often absent [17, 53, 63, 64]. Such feedback, however, could be valuable as a regular part of the assessment policy [2].

Diagnostics centered feedback systems should provide evidence-based information on critical areas of clinical practice [2]. Such feedback is welcome from both professionals and healthcare consumers [2]. Healthcare clients’ more prominent role in the diagnostics phase means that patients are ‘at the center.’ Such policy reflects ‘rehumanized medicine’ [62]. High-quality feedback from diagnostics can mean enhanced organizational and patient outcomes that benefit both patients and doctors by eliminating preventable adverse events and improving the diagnostic process [2, 53, 59, 60, 65].

Patient involvement should thus have a stable position in professional development within the clinical practice. However, this presupposes the existence of such cultural attitudes and values that foster patient-centered care and continuous learning [57, 58]. Even complete re-engineering of services and novel technologies without correct staff engagement do not change policies accurately [30]. Feedback should be part of learning systems that focus on improving diagnostic performance [56]. This helps target individual counseling assistance and aids in identifying doctors who may need educational support or retraining [66].

Patient engagement is a matter of supporting people in benefiting more from the healthcare system [2]. However, this engagement may be challenging for patients and their families [2, 67]. Disease-related stress with lower physical health can prevent people from engaging genuinely in the diagnostic process [2]. Some patients prefer active involvement in all aspects of the decision-making process, while others would instead rely on a doctor’s assessment [2]. Feedbacks systems should also allow space for those health consumers who have already experienced a diagnostic error. Such individuals are often ready to share their experiences to enlighten future patients’ diagnostic processes [2, 68, 69].
