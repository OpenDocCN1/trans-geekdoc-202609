The discrete cosine is then checked for transformation coefficients or energy-related cepstrum. MFCC is unevenly distributed around various frame indices for pathological voices. Thus, MFCC is commonly used in many works to differentiate between a pathological voice and a regular voice.

- **C. Linear Predictive Coding (LPC).** Digital signals have been compressed for efficient storage and transmission using LPC. It is one of the most effective methods of language analysis and has become popular as a formant evaluation tool [88]. The LPC analysis is based on the approximation of the vocal tract as a linear all-pole IIR filter. For a signal segment with a sampling rate of 8 kHz, a voice encoder is used to calculate the optimum excitation, voice pitch time, gain and coefficients. The LPC is based on the mean-square error approach. Using this approach, a linear combination of the earlier samples is used to measure the voice signal. The LPC coefficients vary considerably in some portions of the voice signal. However, the magnitude is not significant for other parts of the voice signal. Pathological speech can be differentiated by a continuum of magnitude from normal voice.

- **D. Glottal flow signal.** It can be extracted by inversely filtering a speech signal that eliminates redundant voice artifacts from the mouth and vocal tract and preserves the original message signal released during the glottal flow [89]. The Pitch Synchronous Iterative Adaptive Inverse Filtering algorithm was developed to extract glottal signal attributes as one of the MATLAB toolboxes known as Apart8. The time domain characteristics that can be obtained from the glottal flux signal are: the closing and opening instants, the opening threshold, the amplitude threshold, the quasi-open threshold, the normalized amplitude threshold, and the speed quotient. Frequency domain parameters are: the difference between the harmonics and its richness factor.

- **E. Spectrograms.** The waveform of speech consists of a sequence of events that differ from time to time. This time differential refers to the spectral characteristics that vary considerably over time. This type of fast-time signal cannot be acquired by a single Fourier transform, and instead the [90] STFT is used. The STFT consists of a Fourier transform with a sliding waveform part window. The distribution of the spectral power of a voice varies greatly with time and frequency, so that regular to pathological tones can be distinguished. For a normal voice, the spectral density of power is consistent with time and frequency, but is not consistent with a pathological voice. The spectrogram is also considered to be a strong segregator of pathological voices from ordinary voices.

- **F. Formants.** The formant analysis is a powerful voice characteristic studied by researchers. The frequencies that form the vocal tract are the resonance frequencies that change the form factors of the different vocal tracts. Usually, the formant refers to the full spectral resonance contribution. The peaks of the continuum are approximately the same as those produced by the vocal tract response. In a formant map, there are different peaks on certain frequencies. Peaks are also separated by a certain frequency band and are located in decreasing order of magnitude. Unlike a typical voice, the irregular or pathological voice has many different styles of voice. For example, if a pathological voice is used, any three peaks may be closely placed and of the same magnitude. However, the peaks are located at a constant distance from each other and their amplitude gradually decreases in the ordinary voice [91].

- G. Wavelet transform and analysis. By integrating continuous wave transforms (CWTs), the problem of resolution faced by the STFT has been mitigated. However, the CWT analysis is carried out in the same way as the STFT with the signal multiplied by a function as the window function used in the STFT. The transformation is calculated in a piecewise manner with different segments of the time-domain signal. However, there are two significant variations between CWT and STFT. The first thing that is not taken into account is the Fourier transformation of the window signals, so the single peak that corresponds to the sinusoid is noticed. Simply put, the negative frequencies are not calculated. Second, when the CWT of all spectral components is measured, the length of the window appears to vary. This is the most critical aspect of the transformation of the wavelet. The transformation of wavelets is a very important and widely used concept in the field of voice pathology. The following is therefore a mathematical illustration of its context principles.

The standard form of a CWT is

```
CWT_x^ψ(τ, s) = ψ_x^ψ(τ, s) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{|s|}} \int x(t)ψ^*\left(\frac{t - τ}{s}\right)dt
```

The function is a mother wavelet, which is often referred to as a transformation function. Finally, the signal obtained is due to the induction of two essential parameters, namely translation (τ) and scaling (s) parameters. Wavelet means small waves originating from the main feature known as the mother wavelet. The wavelets are deduced using a finite length window function, whereas the word wave refers to the oscillatory aspect of the function. More simply, the windowed functions or segments of the signal are formed from the mother wavelet [92]. High-frequency and time–frequency attributes correlated with pathological speech are also measured using CWT. Integer time and scale values are used to index the actual valued CWT coefficients [93].

## 3.4.2 Voice pathology analysis and detection techniques

Machine learning is an essential factor that classifies a speech signal as regular or as part of a particular voice pathology. It uses a range of acoustic and speech features, such as those listed in section 3.4.1, to teach computers how different pathologies look. Over the last decade, various ML and deep learning classifiers have been used for these one-dimensional (1D) features. However, there are also newer algorithms that use time–frequency-based images with advanced dilated fully convolutional architecture to improve the reliability of accurate pathology identification. A number of these aspects have been discussed by researchers in the following section.

- **A. Short and long duration feature descriptors.** A wide variety of long and short feature descriptors have been used for diagnosing voice pathology. Some notable research works that have used long-term features are [86, 94–98]. Some of the features extracted from these long-lasting signals include: amplitude and pitch disturbance quotient, glottal-to-noise excitation ratio, amplitude and frequency disturbance analysis, harmonics-to-noise ratio (HNR), normalized noise energy, etc. Although these feature descriptors have been widely used, they are outperformed by emerging short feature descriptors that do not require a fundamental frequency assessment. It is also extremely difficult to deduce the fundamental frequency of pathological signals due to their non-uniformity. There are several new and notable short feature descriptors that have begun to replace these long ones. Initially, the speech signal is segmented into several frames using a fixed length hamming window(s). Feature vectors such as the MFCC and the linear predictive cepstral coefficient (LPCC) are then extracted for each frame. These feature vectors have been developed specifically to replicate the voice production process and the auditory potential of the human body. These features are used in ML algorithms such as SVM, kNN, Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM), k-means clustering, etc to signal as either regular or abnormal. However, it is important to state that the LPCC as a linear model has ample capacity to deal with an irregular, very non-linear expression. The MFCC descriptors are very dexterous when dealing with irregular or pathological voice signals. However, considering these positive factors and limitations, these short feature descriptors are not very dexterous when testing the classifiers with samples belonging to other databases [98, 99]. The problem is therefore a lack of generality when dealing with datasets belonging to various regions of the world. For example, the MEEI dataset was collected from patients living in the United States of America and thus the voice of a normal individual of Asian or Middle East descent might be categorized as abnormal. This is due to a lack of global fundamental pitch that can usually be considered to belong to an odd group worldwide. This aspect prevents the hardware implementation of voice pathology identifiers on a global scale and, as such, is limited to particular regions only. Cepstral descriptor is a notable group of feature descriptors used for pathological voice analysis. Its two versions are the cepstral peak prominence (CPP) and its derivative the cepstral peak prominence smoothed (CPPS). These descriptors have shown great accuracy to diagnose even the most serious dysphonic voice because they are not susceptible to time variance [100, 101]. In a research report, the authors extracted MFCC features from voice recordings belonging to physiological and laryngeal pathological categories to practice using two approaches. The first method used feature vectors to train the SVM, the Discriminant Analysis (DA) and the Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) individually. However, training them on a case-by-case basis may not have provided a successful result and instead, as a second method, they introduced a standard classification [102].
