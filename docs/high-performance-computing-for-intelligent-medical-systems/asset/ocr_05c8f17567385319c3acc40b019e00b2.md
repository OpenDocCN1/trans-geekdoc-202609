Economical, non-invasive and automated diagnosis of voice signal pathologies for thorough study of glottal signals is a trend for research enthusiasts. It is the future of speech pathology detection. The glottal signal is produced between the vocal fold and the vocal tract. Considering the success of deep learning [4] in various areas of artificial intelligence (AI), the number of studies in the classification of voice pathology will increase further [5–9]. Deep learning is becoming a better option against common classifiers such as decision trees, support vector machine (SVM), k-nearest neighbors (KNN), decision trees, naive bays, etc. Handcrafted speech or acoustic features have been of primary importance for voice pathology detection for the last few decades and cannot therefore be ignored.

Deep learning models such as the Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) and the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) are also used. Prediction scores generated using handcrafted machine learning features and deep learning classifiers can also be combined to further improve accuracy [9]. It can therefore be concluded that computer-based voice analytics of these types should be further researched before they are widely used as screening methods to classify laryngeal pathology.

# 3.1.2 Internet of things in voice pathology

Healthcare is one of the most common sectors for internet of things (IoT) applications. Some potential uses include chronic disease management, smart fitness-tracking wearables, remote patient monitoring, etc. Nowadays, a boom in smart watches that use sensors to monitor different types of everyday fitness activities and uploading to IoT-based smartphones is widespread. Intelligent medical devices and imaging devices are also main components of IoT. Over the coming years, the integration of more miniaturized low-power sensors with better machine learning (ML) algorithms will significantly enhance the cost-effectiveness of these smart IoT products. This will further revolutionize the experience of the consumer. Authors of [10] argued that suppliers view IoT as a tool for remotely reducing the downtime of smart devices. IoT also provides strategic resource planning to ensure that more patients will make the most of their services. Premature testing, health-related problems, chronic diseases and real-time monitoring are expected to be promoted by emerging wireless technologies [11, 12]. A thorough understanding of current IoT research in health applications is expected to help many stakeholders involved in additional research at this point in time. ML has been used to test clinical parameters and their combinations for prediction purposes, such as extracting health knowledge for outcome studies, comprehensive patient treatment, therapeutic planning and support, and predicting disease progression [13, 14].

ML incorporation in the medical sector provides medical professionals with the opportunity to function effectively, create, and enhance the quality and reliability of medical care [15]. Biomedical signals can also be done with real hardware in order to recognize physiological changes in the human body in a variety of different ways.
