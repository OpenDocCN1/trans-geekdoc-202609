+   - We apply two cascaded filters to remove various artifacts like baseline wandering in low frequencies and power-line harmonics and electromyogram noises in high frequencies. The first filter is a low-pass 7-point second-order Savitzky–Golay filter. It smoothes the waveform without disturbing the signal tendency [12]. The second filter is a high-pass Kaiser window FIR filter of order 40 [13]. The overall frequency band of interest is 0.13–40 Hz [5, 14].
+   - We consider R-peak waves as time-references for the segmentation procedure. We implemented the algorithm presented in [15] to detect the R-peak waves. It is based on the remarkable similarities between the ECG waveform structure and the integrated linear prediction residuals in audio signals.
+   - If we define the time occurrence of R-peaks as {t1, t2, ..., tn}, the kth segment includes two heart cycles from t_{k-1} to t_{k+1} time-interval. Hence, two successive segments share a PPG cycle.
+   - Then, the length of each segment (each element of the input vector, X) is adjusted to 250 samples by zero-padding (when its length is smaller than 250), or by discarding some samples (when the length exceeds the length limit). Moreover, the length of each element of the target vector T is two (including invasive systolic and diastolic BP values).
