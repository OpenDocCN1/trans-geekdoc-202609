An investigation to establish a detector for vocal larynx defects by extracting a combination of different feature descriptors from a pathological voice sample was proposed in [103]. The key problem with pathological voice identification and classification tasks is that ML algorithms are only equipped to distinguish between regular and abnormal voice samples. It is therefore a matter of binary classification. While the key problem that delays actual hardware deployment is the inability to pinpoint the exact disease. On similar lines, [103] investigators have conducted a binary classification using 30 and 20 pathological and normal voice samples, respectively. A mixture of different types of feature descriptors, such as the well-known MFCC, Zero-Crossing Rate (ZCR), ZCR mean, and LPC, was used for segmented voice recordings with lengths of 200, 300, 400 and 500 milliseconds. In their work, a threshold level was manually set for each function variety to establish a division between usual and abnormal voice signals.

Speech problems may not simply be due to vocal fold defects, but may also be due to Parkinson’s disease, hypernasal lip and palate dysfunction in children/individuals. In this line, another research study [104] proposed a technique to diagnose these disorders as well as vocal fold disorders. They experimented with numerous feature descriptors to include automatic detection, such as simple frequency accuracy measurement, noisy spectrum measurement, non-linear amplitude and signal interference, cepstral features. It has been found that adding consistency measures to fundamental frequency is acceptable for speech disorders of laryngeal and Parkinson’s, whereas cepstral features function well with hyper nasal speech. However, implementation with a noisy spectrum can properly diagnose dysphonic speech. It can therefore be established that the use of a single feature type or a combination of different feature vectors to diagnose multiple disorders with a single classifier will not yield a good result. It is therefore very important to investigate the best suited combination of a feature-classifier pair for all major voice pathologies.

The [105] research used time and frequency domain features with multiple type of ML algorithms to identify voice pathologies. Their experimentation led to the conclusion that spectral characteristics were among the most important feature descriptors. While it was discovered that pitch or fundamental frequency was not very significant. It was also realized that residual signal features extracted from a signal filtered using an inverse LPC filter have the highest effect on accuracy.
