Covid infection is a dangerous disease for human life. The COVID-19 infection is caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus2. The first Covid infected person in India was detected on 30 January 2020. On 22 March, voluntary public curfew for 24 h was followed by the country’s residents, and on 24 March the lockdown order was established by the government to last for 21 days. The lockdown order was continued from 14 April to 3 May, then from 3 to 17 May the government allowed some relaxation for people, thus from 1 June the government started the process of lock and unlock in three phases [2, 3]. The WHO and the United Nations have described this as a commendable step to remove infection spread in the country. On 21, 22 and 23 June, India was ranked 56th by the Deep Knowledge Group, out of 200 countries. On 12 January 2020, the corona virus had been caused by respiratory disease in people living in China, which had been reported to the WHO on 31 December 2019 [4, 5]. On 30 January, the first person suffering from Covid infection was confirmed in Kerala, which increased to three new cases by 3 February. On 4 March, 22 new cases were found to be Covid positive, in which 14 new cases were reported by one tourist group in the month of March returning from abroad. A large number of Covid infections spread due to Sikh celebrations in areas like Punjab [6–8]. Similarly, on 31 March, the incident of Tabligi Jamaat religious congregation in Delhi became a big incident. After this incident, 4291 cases of Covid infection were reported [9, 10]. On May, 1225 new cases were reported in Punjab in returning pilgrims from Maharashtra, as a result of which the provision of the Epidemic Diseases Act 1897 came into force in more than a dozen states and union territories.
