Patients typically have some insights into where there were problems in their case. Patients are not necessarily always able to evaluate diagnostic steps with such accuracy as professionals. Health consumers’ concerns also typically focus on poorly organized and administered care like long waiting times, problems in access, and loss of other interaction possibilities with doctors beside formal consultations [84]. Thus, care management issues related to rapid and flexible diagnostics are also worthy of patient feedback.

# 7.8 A model for instant patient feedback

# 7.8.1 General principles

When the patient evaluates the level of professionalism, the doctor’s competence in diagnostics, it is most manageable for the client to assess this area with a ready-made format. When dealing with attributes of empathy (e.g., listening to patients properly) both open and structured feedback formats may function well. It is essential that the patient’s experience and vision are presented in a specified way; the doctor responsible for the diagnostic process can understand the areas for which clients have open questions or remarks. If the patient is encouraged to provide feedback on the diagnostic process’s correctness as part of standard practice, giving feedback on the diagnostic process will be more comfortable. Accordingly, the patient feels that he or she is not only providing feedback on the specific aspect of the care process but is also a full participant in assessing the quality of his or her care. With a detailed but reasonably simple questionnaire, patients can give summary feedback on the diagnosis they have received. It is essential that the patient accurately identifies those aspects they believed were deficient in the diagnostic process. The proposed model gives the doctor a better idea of the patient’s experience than is typically possible during a limited consultancy session. This information helps the doctor improve their working methods and self-evaluation.
