In the diagnosis of laryngeal paralysis, the authors of [108] discussed the effectiveness of the use of entropy and energy-based features. To extract tenable features from voice signals affected by unilateral voice fold paralysis, wavelet decomposition was initially used for this purpose. A total of 32 and 11 active sub-bands were used to obtain entropy and energy characteristics, respectively. Before training with a linear SVM classifier, the authors used genetic algorithms to optimize the vector features. By using SVM with entropy and energy characteristics, they achieved an accuracy of 100% and 93.62%, respectively. The properties of entropy have been shown to be useful for the diagnosis of laryngeal paralysis. In another pathological voice detection technique described in [109], the researchers used sustained vocal samples from the MEEI dataset using a modulation spectrum technique. Specifically, it is a mixture of modulation frequency and harmonics of voice. In addition to the modulation spectrum method, the reduction of dimensionality was also used before the training of vectors with the SVM classifier. A special feature of this extraction process is the observation of a distortion in the amplitude pattern of a voice sample with pathology, which can provide rich cues for detection and accurate classification. A cumulative accuracy of 94.1% has been observed. However, it is important to study their application with other current data sets in order to assess their potential efficacy. They also looked at the performance of other traditional extraction methods and highlighted critical challenges related to pitch estimation, global signal estimation. Wavelet filter banks have also been used to differentiate natural and abnormal voice samples. More specifically, the wavelet filter banks were used to measure eight energy features using the lattice factorization technique. The feature vector extraction technique was applied to a privately collected dataset and to the MEEI dataset. Investigators are said to have reached a classification rate of 100% on their issue of binary classification. However, it remains to be seen if this remarkable precision can be accomplished for multiple pathology databases. A universal classifier that can diagnose voice pathology for patients living in any corner of the world is still a major challenge for researchers prior to any realistic implementation in hospitals [110].

Another landmark research using entropy for the binary classification of healthy and pathological voices was proposed in [111]. They evaluated the efficiency of PCA, LDA and SVM classifiers with different vector extraction techniques, such as wavelet packet transformation (WPT), STFT, CWT. Statistical evaluation showed that using an active subband of 6 with a WPT technique with LDA and SVM learners was able to achieve an overall accuracy of 100%. It can also be concluded that disturbance, inconsistency, and non-linearity of irregular voice and entropy extraction can provide efficient binary classification. Entropy is indeed a good way to measure the statistical randomness of a pathological voice. A multi-class voice pathology classifier that uses a rich feature vector derived from diverse and common speech features has been presented in [112].
