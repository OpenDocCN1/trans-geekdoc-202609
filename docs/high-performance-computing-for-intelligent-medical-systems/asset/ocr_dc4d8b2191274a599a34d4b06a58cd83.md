# 3.2 Electroglottography

This technology is low-cost, non-invasively measuring changes in the relative vocal fold area during the production of laryngeal voices. EGG was introduced 30 years ago [42, 43] in the late 1980s and early 1990s [44–49] and has passed through a golden age of growing scientific interest. A high frequency, low ampere current is transmitted between two electrodes located on either side of the thyroid cartilage. Vocal fold vibration transmits changes to the vocal fold contact analysis (VFCA), allowing the EGG signal to vary by VFCA. The lack of spatial information during the calculation of EGG is a crucial weakness. Vocal fold vibration is a complex three-dimensional phenomenon that is mapped to a time-variable value, that is, VFCA. Differences in vibrational features, such as inferior-superior or front-posterior [50], are difficult to resolve. The concept of a multi-channel EGG estimate was demonstrated by [51]. Three years later, two channels using different modulation frequencies were used to capture both horizontal and vertical signals [52]. The concept of a multi-channel EGG was also explored in [53] by setting up a system to decode 36 multiplexing current paths with two 2×3 electrode grids. The ability of the device to monitor vertical and horizontal larynx positions during singing and swallowing was demonstrated by limited pilot data obtained using the prototype of this design.
