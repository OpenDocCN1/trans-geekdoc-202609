#### 11.2.1 The cornea

In the front of the iris and pupil lies the most anterior part of the body called the cornea. It is considered to be a highly dense innervated tissue of the body. The average dimension of the cornea of the adult human eye covers 11.5 mm horizontal diameter and 10.5 mm of the vertical diameter with constant curvature [1]. The trigeminal nerve has the ophthalmic entity which is the source of the corneal nerve [2]. Four millimetre is the size of pre-pupillary cornea, also termed the optic zone and situated in the middle of the cornea in a photopic condition. There is a difference in the physiology and pathological condition of both the central and the other type of cornea, that is, peripheral [3].

- The human eye consists of five layers:
  - I. The epithelium;
  - II. The Bowman's membrane;
  - III. The lamellar stroma;
  - IV. The Descemet's membrane;
  - V. The endothelium.

#### I. The corneal epithelium

Its surface is covered by the tear film which has the vital role of protecting the corneal surface from microbes, toxic chemicals or any xeno substances. The tear film has an outer lipid layer and inner water mucus layer. The tear film gets spread with each eyelid blink due to interaction between the epithelial cells and the outer lipid layer. The corneal epithelium consists of:

- (a) 2–3 layers of superficial cells;
- (b) 2–3 layers of the wings cells;
- (c) one layer of basal cells.

The folding of microplicae contribute irregularity to the surface of the superficial cells of the epithelium and shows interaction with the overlying tear film. Around seven to ten days is the renewal time of the cells of the corneal epithelium. The presence of the cell junction provides impermeability, stability and anchoring to the corneal epithelium [4].

#### II. The Bowman's layer

It separates the epithelium from the corneal lamellar stroma. It is a cellular zone of 10-15 micrometers.

#### III. The corneal lamellar stroma

It provides structural anchoring to the cornea of the eye. The transparency and the hydration state of the cornea is maintained by the stroma keratocytes (stellar shaped cells) which release collagen and proteoglycans, collagen fibres in parallel arrangement (n = 200–250) running from limbus to limbus [5] constitute the bulk of the stromal extracellular matrix. Collagen fibres’ regular organisation contributes to the corneal transparency and also provides mechanical strength to the cornea. Due to the compact packaging of the fibril more in the pre-pupillary cornea than the peripheral cornea, the cornea has mechanical strength and dioptric stability [6]. Proteoglycans, which surrounds the stromal collagen fibrils consists of keratin sulfate or chondroitin sulphate/dermatan sulphate side chain, thus regulating hydration of the eye.

#### IV. The corneal endothelium

It is 5–7 sided cuboidal cells in a single layer. They secrete Descemet’s elastic membrane which causes separation between stroma and the endothelium. The endothelium has an osmotic transport system where the osmotic gradient generates a constant percentage of the water in the stroma maintaining clarity and transparency of the cornea (hypoosmotic stroma to hypertonic aqueous); this whole process is called deturgescence, and its disturbance results in corneal edema.

Note: in a normal transparent cornea, visible light neither gets absorbed nor scattered. The condition of corneal scattering occurs due to:

- corneal edema;
- restoration of the collagen fibrils;
- hazes;
- surgical irregularities.

#### 11.2.2 The human retina

The inner surface of the eye is lined by the inner tissues surrounding the vitreous cavity and held by the surrounding sclera and cornea. The neural retina is inside of the optical wall encircling the vitreous cavity, whereas the outside of the wall is surrounded by pigment epithelium (sclera and choroid) [7, 8]. Mullerian glia is the backbone of the neural retina and the light is captured by neural retina, having six main class of neuron—ganglion cells, bipolar cells, horizontal cells and amacrine cells. The outer nuclear consists of a nucleus of the photoreceptor cells, and in the inner nuclear of the retinal lies the cells of bipolar, horizontal, amacrine and Maglia. The inner nuclear further has the layer of the outer plexiform composed of photoreceptors joined with horizontal and bipolar cells. On the other hand, a layer of the inner plexiform has amacrine and bipolar cells which have synapses with the cells of the ganglion. Mullerian glia processes extend to the retina. In a human there are two types of photoreceptor—rods and cones. Cones consist of the pigment with the absorption signal in various spectral ranges of colour of yellow, blue, and green. Rods having pigment show absorption in the colour region of green and blue. Rods are not involved in colour vision, they are active in low light levels [9].
