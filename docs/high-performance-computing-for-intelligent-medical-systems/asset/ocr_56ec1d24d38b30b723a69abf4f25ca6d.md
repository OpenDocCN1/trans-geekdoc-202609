# G. Wavelet transform and analysis.

By integrating continuous wave transforms (CWTs), the problem of resolution faced by the STFT has been mitigated. However, the CWT analysis is carried out in the same way as the STFT with the signal multiplied by a function as the window function used in the STFT. The transformation is calculated in a piecewise manner with different segments of the time-domain signal. However, there are two significant variations between CWT and STFT. The first thing that is not taken into account is the Fourier transformation of the window signals, so the single peak that corresponds to the sinusoid is noticed. Simply put, the negative frequencies are not calculated. Second, when the CWT of all spectral components is measured, the length of the window appears to vary. This is the most critical aspect of the transformation of the wavelet. The transformation of wavelets is a very important and widely used concept in the field of voice pathology. The following is therefore a mathematical illustration of its context principles.

The standard form of a CWT is

```
CWT_x^ψ(τ, s) = ψ_x^ψ(τ, s) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{|s|}} \int x(t)ψ^*\left(\frac{t - τ}{s}\right)dt
```

The function is a mother wavelet, which is often referred to as a transformation function. Finally, the signal obtained is due to the induction of two essential parameters, namely translation (τ) and scaling (s) parameters. Wavelet means small waves originating from the main feature known as the mother wavelet. The wavelets are deduced using a finite length window function, whereas the word wave refers to the oscillatory aspect of the function. More simply, the windowed functions or segments of the signal are formed from the mother wavelet [92]. High-frequency and time–frequency attributes correlated with pathological speech are also measured using CWT. Integer time and scale values are used to index the actual valued CWT coefficients [93].
