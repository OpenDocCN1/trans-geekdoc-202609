QRS interval: is defined as the time difference between endings of P-wave and starting of T-wave. Normal QRS interval is less than 100 ms, if it is greater than 100 ms it indicates a bundle branch block. R–R interval: is the time difference in between two consecutive R-waves. A normal R–R interval is 60–120 ms. Long-lasting HPT affects all these intervals, which is reflected in the ECG test. However, there may be no clinically visible ECG disturbances in the early stage of HPT, and it is difficult to diagnose manually with ECG [11]. Automatic detection of HPT using ECG signal is an emerging research topic in the field of biomedical engineering. Since the recorded ECG signal is contaminated with various types of noise it may cause variation in the width and height of these intervals and error occurs in the prediction of disease [19]. So, preprocessing of ECG is required before feeding the classifier. Preprocessing can be divided into two phases: the noise removal phase suppresses noise present in the signal and the feature extraction phase extracts information from the signal [20]. Jain et al developed the ECG denoising method based on empirical mode decomposition using Riemann–Liouville fractional integral [21]. The severity of HPT can be detected automatically using noise-free and segmented ECG signals [22].

# 1.1.2 Related work

To date, researchers have proposed various methods for detection and classification of HPT using ECG signals. Tripathi et al classified the features extracted by using filtering to detect fast and slow heart rhythms [23]. Rajput et al determined the HPT diagnosis index which has been used to differentiate in between HPT and normal subject using wavelet filter bank [11]. Chuang et al applied empirical mode decomposition to extract different linear and non-linear features [24]. Kiat Soh et al used discrete wavelet transform and extract different subbands to diagnose the HPT [10]. Ni et al developed multi-scale HRV analysis for calculating the severity of HPT by using wavelet transform to detect R peaks [11]. Melillo et al designed a tool for prognosis of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events using HRV analysis through the series of beat intervals [25]. Zhang et al have done analysis of the autonomic nervous pattern in HPT based on short-term HRV by using the RR interval. Different features are extracted to distinguish between hypertensive heart-beat rhythms and normal ones [26]. Kale et al extracted the R–R interval for prediction of sensitivity and specificity of a cardiovascular event, random forest classifier has been used for classification of the event [27]. Ortiz-Martín et al analyzed the entropy of the LSB values and the Pan–Tomkins algorithm has been used to extract the R-peaks and extracted inter pulse interval [28]. Alkhodari et al implemented a machine learning model that handles imbalance data and predicts vascular events in hypertensive patients [29].
