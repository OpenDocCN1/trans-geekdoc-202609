
## Chapter 2

### Computational intelligence in surface electromyogram signal classification

Turker Tuncer, Sengul Dogan, Fatih Ertam and Abdulhamit Subasi

The objective of this book chapter is to introduce a solution for disabled people based on a man–machine interface, which can work on data processed by computational intelligence techniques. The model utilizes computational intelligence techniques for hand movement classification. On this basis, a novel computational intelligence approach, which is called a spider network for the recognition of basic hand movements employing surface electromyogram signals is presented. The main components of the spider network are pre-processing, feature extraction, feature reduction, and classification. In the preprocessing phase, the surface electromyogram signals from two different channels are concatenated. In the feature extraction phase, Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) and Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) are applied to concatenated signals to obtain frequency coefficients of the surface electromyogram signal. Besides, Singular Value Decomposition and Ternary Pattern are utilized. The extracted features are divided into two groups according to feature extractors. To select distinctive features, Neighborhood Component Analysis is employed as a weight generator. By using these weights and tangent sigmoid activation function, the features are reduced. In the classification phase, Linear Discriminant Analysis, Artificial Neural Network, k-nearest neighbor, and Support Vector Machine are employed for the classification task. The proposed model is optimized and validated based on the accomplishment of the built framework, which can produce accurate and consistent solutions for the surface electromyogram based prosthetic device control, intending to help amputees or congenitally deficient limbs. The proposed model achieved 100% classification accuracy in hand movement classification.

### 2.1 Introduction

As demonstrated by the various applications in wellness, biomedicine, and biomedical engineering, there has been increased attention in computational intelligence (CI) over recent years. The usage of CI in surface electromyogram (sEMG) signal classification is presented in this book chapter. The usage of engineering principles and methods to problems in medicine and healthcare is biomedical engineering. Typical applications include prosthesis and medical instrument design/development, health monitoring with imaging equipment, and diagnostic software. Computational algorithms involving artificial intelligence and machine learning are CI approaches. Many CI methods require the establishment of nonlinear mapping between a model's inputs and outputs reflecting the operation of a biomedical system in the real world. Computational intelligence (CI) for diagnostic systems is a new concept in the medical field. CI is a mixture of machine learning algorithms and artificial learning. For the analysis of massive amounts of data, these techniques are extremely useful and have been effectively implemented to problems of pattern recognition and signal classification. In short, CI methods are a potential solution to the rising demand for automatic diagnostic and control systems [1]. In this chapter, we will discuss the basics of automatic hand movement recognition schemes and focus on the fields where CI methods were effectively used for sEMG signal classification.

Surface electromyography (sEMG) signals have been widely employed for myoelectric control as it includes important information on muscular action. The hands play a crucial role in manipulating and grasping several items. The defeat of even a single hand can expressively disturb the human activity. Hence, a prosthetic hand is an attractive solution to furnish the armless person [2–4]. The usage of sEMG differs from rehabilitation apparatus to a man–machine interaction scheme. Several intelligent methods were developed to classify the neuromuscular actions in prosthetic hand control. In this chapter, the sEMG signals based prosthetic hand movement control framework is presented. The hand prosthesis based on the myoelectric control employs the sEMG signals that are taken from the skin surface during the contraction of muscles. Most muscles that control the finger motion are positioned on the left side of a stump. Thus, the action of these muscles can be employed for the prosthesis control after a hand amputation. In order to control prosthesis motion with muscle activity features extracted from the myoelectric signals can be utilized. The feature extraction from sEMG can be achieved by employing numerous techniques involving signal characteristics in time and frequency domain, autoregressive model coefficients, and time-frequency characteristics [5]. On the other hand, a robust hand movements recognition by employing the sEMG signals present diverse challenges. To enhance the classification performance and get rid of those problems, a proper arrangement of the feature extraction and selection methods should be employed. Additionally, an appropriate model to improve the classification performance should be utilized.

Noce et al [6] proposed an approach for nerve control in hand prostheses. In the proposed method, a comparative analysis was performed with a pattern recognition technique used for muscle signals and adapted Spike Sorting Algorithms (SSA) for nerve signals. Through the SSA, the peak points of the nerve signals were determined, and the movement was transferred. EMG and ENG signals are used as input parameters. Accuracy is chosen as the performance parameter and the results are presented. The results showed that ENG signals were more effective decision makers.

Nishad et al [7] proposed tunable Q wavelet transform based sEMG recognition technique. The main goal of the developed approach is to classify electromyogram signals in accordance with the basic six hand movements. The developed approach includes preprocessing, feature extraction, feature selection and classification. The accuracy of this approach was chosen as the success criterion and the results were compared with the existing and widely used techniques in the literature. Yabuki et al [8] designed a glove made of silicone rubber with a detection feature. The designed glove has a structure including appearance (shape, texture, color), material (strength, flexibility), mechanical (fingertip shape, joint movements) and detection. In their study, a structure was created for real hand movements through sensors and control units. Shi et al [9] proposed a study on the evaluation of electromyogram signals for a bionic glove design. The main objective of this work is to present a methodology, which can mimic the movements of the human hand. Servo motors controlled by Arduino have enabled the movement of bionic fingers. In their study, k-nearest neighbor algorithm is used in the classification phase. Accuracy is selected as the performance parameter. Enoka [10] provides a different perspective for the measurement and analysis of electromyographic signals. According to the results obtained, it is emphasized that EMG signals need to elaborate signal waveforms with various algorithms and detection systems and defined waveforms should be evaluated effectively. Ryu and Kim [11] proposed an approach EMG Signal Graph Matching (ESGM) algorithm based method. They evaluated the physiological muscle activity using their methods. They used conventional feature extraction and classification methods. Junior and de Seixas [12] investigated noise reduction performances on the EMG signals. In their study, the aim is to clear ECG interference from EMG signals as a noise signal. In the proposed method, unlike high-pass filtering, it was observed that most of the EMG spectral content was maintained at low frequencies. Jochumsen et al [13] introduced a Bayes based sEMG classification technique to recognize main movements of the arms. Chowdhury et al [14] suggested an approach that relies on the correlation of EMG and EEG signals. The key objective of the research was to design a neuro-rehabilitation system. The proposed approach consists of the phases of preprocessing, feature extraction and classification. Accuracy was employed as the evaluation parameter in the study. Tavakoli et al [15] developed a prosthetic glove using EMG signals. In the study, basic hand movements are defined, and hand movements are aimed to be transformed into a flexible structure. The accuracy and error matrix parameters have been utilized to test the performance of the study. Tavakoli et al [16] designed a wearable glove from EMG signals. This designed glove basically recognizes four movements. The major goal of their research was to provide high mobility with four basic movements defined. To accomplish this objective, an original glove hardware was designed and they constructed their dataset using the glove constructed. In the second stage, the statistical features were extracted from the data obtained. In the third stage, LDA and SVM classifiers were used to obtain performance.
