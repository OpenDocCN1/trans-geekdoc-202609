
The expression glioma is being used to identify multiple kinds of gliomas, from low-grade gliomas like astrocytoma and oligodendrogliomas up to high-grade multiform glioblastoma and even the most damaging primary malignant brain tumour [92]. Radiotherapy, surgery and chemotherapy are procedures used, typically in tandem, for the treatment of gliomas [93].

Early glioma diagnosis plays a crucial role in enhancing treatment options. Imaging technologies including CT, PET, MRI have been employed in order to ensure valuable information about the structure, magnitude, location and metabolism of the brain tumour. While these methods could be used in conjunction with brain tumours to provide the best knowledge, the definitive soft-tissue contrast and wide adoption make MRI the base standard. MRI is a non-invasive in vivo imaging framework for implementing radio frequencies to influence target tissues to produce their internal images, under the effects of a very strong magnetic field. Images from various MRI sequences are created by altering the time when the image is recovered. Different MRI modalities provide different images of tissue contrast that provide useful structural information and allow the identification and segmenting of the tumour in conjunction with its sub-regions [94]. During the acquisition of MRI, although it can differ from device to device, almost a hundred slices of 2D pictures are generated to reflect the 3D brain volume. Moreover, when the fragments from the required normal modalities are fused for study, the data becomes densely populated and complex.

During a surgical procedure aimed at removing/depleting tumour cells, care must also be taken to preserve as much healthy tissue as possible. Brain tumour segmentation involves identification, delineation and differentiation of the tumour tissues of healthy brain tissues from necrotic cells or activated cells and edema. The strategy requires the manual annotation and segmentation of a large number of multimodal MRI images in a routine manner. However, since handcrafted segmentation is a lengthy procedure, the development of robust automatic segmentation methods to provide effective and accurate segmentation has become an interesting and prominent scientific discipline in recent years [95]. Recently, high segmentation results achieved by deep learning strategies make them good candidates to accomplish this task.

## 12.3.1 Brain tumour segmentation databases

- A. BrainWeb

The McConnell Brain Imaging Centre developed the BrainWeb dataset; an anatomical representation of normal and dysfunctional brains is provided by an MRI simulator. The BrainWeb's strength scale consists of 0, 20 and 40 non-uniformity ranges, with each of the slices having thicknesses of 1 mm, 3 mm, 5 mm, 7 mm and 9 mm. The noise level remains 0 percent, 1 percent, 3 percent, 7 percent and 9 percent. The Centre for Morphometric Research has developed the Internet Brain Segmentation Repository (IBSR) (CMA). The brain tumours MRI dataset provides files of 16-bit integers formatted as 256 × 256 images.

- B. AANLIB database

In the AANLIB repository released by the Harvard Medical School there are six major types of images, which are inflammatory, normal, degenerative, cerebrovascular, neuro-imaging and neoplastic anatomy. A computer-aided imaging analysis methods for diagnosis of brain disorders such as autism, Alzheimer’s disease and dementia have been developed by the Section for Biomedical Image Analytics (SBIA) of the University of Pennsylvania. The Cancer Imaging Archive (TCIA) created the Reference Image Collection to Assess Therapy Response (RIDER) which includes imaging data collected from 19 patients. The neuroimaging initiative for Alzheimer’s disease (ADNI2) covers two types of Alzheimer’s disorder which includes imaging reports from 400 patients. The Allen brain atlas includes a typical dataset of photographs containing imaging data from 20 patients.

## 12.3.2 Classification of brain tumour

A brain tumour is classified on the basis of the origin or cell involvement of the cancer cell. In the former, the cell genesis is found in the brain, and is not extended to other areas of the body, whereas, the latter develops in various parts of the body and is then subsequently transferred to the brain. Lung, colon, kidney and skin are among the most important organs that can spread cancer cells to the brain. They are also referred to as metastatic brain tumours.

Cell-based tumours are the least invasive category and are non-cancer cells of the brain tumour. Benign tumours normally grow slowly, and other neighbouring tissues do not penetrate. Malignant brain tumours range between non-invasive and highly invasive. Depending on growth rates, adjacent healthy brain tissues can invade various cancer cells. Probably the most widely-reported type of primary brain tumour in adults is glioblastomas, also called glioblastoma multiforme (GBM). Glioblastomas are a type of glial cell that develops rapidly. High-grade gliomas (HGGs) have a higher rate of proliferation than low-grade gliomas (LGGs), so they require intensive plans for therapeutic therapy. Most GBM patients die within less than one year, with almost no long-term chances of survival. The research community has drawn huge attention to these tumours; for the sufferer’s early detection and therapeutic preparation. It will strengthen the patient’s survival. In reality, because of the wide variety of appearances, configuration and orientation of the brain, the segmentation of multimodal MRI scans is one of the most critical tasks in bioimaging. The MRI offers detailed brain anatomy information across all three planes: axial, coronal and sagittal. It is beneficial for the diagnosis of a tumour, treatment plans, aid in operation and recovery.

## 12.3.3 Brain photography

Two kinds of MRIs exist: (1) fMRI (Functional MRI): it monitors brain activity through blood flow transition, (2) sMRI (Structural MRI): it captures brain structure and pathology. The emphasis of this chapter is focused on sMRI to deal with the pathology in the brain. Different modalities of sMRI capture responses of the brain tissues which lead to distinct biological information in the images. Different sMRI modalities are:

- A. Diffusion-weighted image (DWI) MRI: It measures water molecules diffusion within the voxels of tissues. DWI is also used for hyperintensity visualisation.

- B. Flair MRI: A sequence of MRI pulses that suppress the fluid and promotes edema in the image. The water molecule signal is essentially suppressed in FLAIR images, which can distinguish between cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and edema.

- C. T1w MRI: Standard MRI pulse sequence that captures tissue variations in longitudinal relaxation time. Time constant needed for exciting protons to revert to equilibrium. T1 images are typically used to differentiate between healthy tissues.

- D. T1Gd MRI: Gadolinium is injected into the body as a contrast enhancement agent and then the T1 sequence is obtained. This contrast enhancement agent shortens the duration of T1, leading to the bright emergence of blood vessels and tumour-like pathologies. The tumour contours can be comfortably separated from the active cell of the tumour tissue by the bright signal of the accumulated contrast agent such as gadolinium ions. Necrotic cells do not intervene, which enable them to be accurately segmented from the active cell zone, by the hypo-intense portion of the tumour central core.

- E. T2w MRI: a simple MRI pulse sequence that catches tissue variations in transverse relaxation time (T2). T2 imaging is being practised to identify the area of the edema which creates a bright image signal.

## 12.3.4 Challenges

Fully automated brain tumour segmentation is a highly challenging task. The tumour-bearing brain MRI data is 3D data that can vary greatly from patient to patient in its structure, size and location. In addition, with discontinuities, tumour borders are usually ambiguous and pose an important challenge for traditional borders. Brain tumour MRI knowledge from laboratory scanners or synthetic databases [96], by contrast, is highly complex. For each individual picture frame in the datasets, the MRI devices and protocols used for the acquisition vary greatly from scan to scan, which leads to intensity distortions and other variations.

There are some additional difficulties in implementing deep learning methods and algorithms to process brain-tumour images. The shortage of extensive data sets is a severe obstacle for deep learning methods. MRI and CT mechanisms that accumulates loads of medical images were implemented in different installations over the past decade. In several other disciplines, image data are used for a specific purpose. CT instruments are not commonly used in other fields of medicine, such as pathology and ophthalmology.

In addition to prolonging the labelling of the tumour sample, a high level of awareness that is problematic in research on brain tumours is also essential.
