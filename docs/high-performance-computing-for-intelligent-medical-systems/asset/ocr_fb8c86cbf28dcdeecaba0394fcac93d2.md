# A. Short and long duration feature descriptors.

A wide variety of long and short feature descriptors have been used for diagnosing voice pathology. Some notable research works that have used long-term features are [86, 94–98]. Some of the features extracted from these long-lasting signals include: amplitude and pitch disturbance quotient, glottal-to-noise excitation ratio, amplitude and frequency disturbance analysis, harmonics-to-noise ratio (HNR), normalized noise energy, etc. Although these feature descriptors have been widely used, they are outperformed by emerging short feature descriptors that do not require a fundamental frequency assessment. It is also extremely difficult to deduce the fundamental frequency of pathological signals due to their non-uniformity. There are several new and notable short feature descriptors that have begun to replace these long ones. Initially, the speech signal is segmented into several frames using a fixed length hamming window(s). Functional vectors such as the MFCC and the linear predictive cepstral coefficient (LPCC) are then extracted for each frame. These feature vectors have been developed specifically to replicate the voice production process and the auditory potential of the human body. These features are used in ML algorithms such as SVM, kNN, Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM), k-means clustering, etc to signal as either regular or abnormal. However, it is important to state that the LPCC as a linear model has ample capacity to deal with an irregular, very non-linear expression. The MFCC descriptors are very dexterous when dealing with irregular or pathological voice signals. However, considering these positive factors and limitations, these short explanations are not very dexterous when testing the classifiers with samples belonging to other databases [98, 99]. The problem is therefore a lack of generality when dealing with datasets belonging to various regions of the world. For example, the MEEI dataset was collected from patients living in the United States of America and thus the voice of a normal individual of Asian or Middle East descent might be categorized as abnormal. This is due to a lack of global fundamental pitch that can usually be considered to belong to an odd group worldwide. This aspect prevents the hardware implementation of voice pathology identifiers on a global scale and, as such, is limited to particular regions only. Cepstral descriptor is a notable group of feature descriptors used for pathological voice analysis. Its two versions are the cepstral peak prominence (CPP) and its derivative the cepstral peak prominence smoothed (CPPS). These descriptors have shown great natacity to diagnose even the most serious dysphonic voice because they are not susceptible to time variance [100, 101]. In a research report, the authors extracted MFCC features from voice recordings belonging to physiological and laryngeal pathological categories to practice using two approaches. The first method used feature vectors to train the SVM, the Discriminant Analysis (DA) and the Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) individually. However, training them on a case-by-case basis may not have provided a successful result and instead, as a second method, they introduced a standard classification [102].
