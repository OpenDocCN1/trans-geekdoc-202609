The main goal of the developed approach is to classify electromyogram signals in accordance with the basic six hand movements. The developed approach includes preprocessing, feature extraction, feature selection and classification. The accuracy of this approach was chosen as the success criterion and the results were compared with the existing and widely used techniques in the literature. Yabuki et al [8] designed a glove made of silicone rubber with a detection feature. The designed glove has a structure including appearance (shape, texture, color), material (strength, flexibility), mechanical (fingertip shape, joint movements) and detection. In their study, a structure was created for real hand movements through sensors and control units. Shi et al [9] proposed a study on the evaluation of electromyogram signals for a bionic glove design. The main objective of this work is to present a methodology, which can mimic the movements of the human hand. Servo motors controlled by Arduino have enabled the movement of bionic fingers. In their study, k-nearest neighbor algorithm is used in the classification phase. Accuracy is selected as the performance parameter. Enoka [10] provides a different perspective for the measurement and analysis of electromyographic signals. According to the results obtained, it is emphasized that EMG signals need to elaborate signal waveforms with various algorithms and detection systems and defined waveforms should be evaluated effectively. Ryu and Kim [11] proposed an approach EMG Signal Graph Matching (ESGM) algorithm based method. They evaluated the physiological muscle activity using their methods. They used conventional feature extraction and classification methods. Junior and de Seixas [12] investigated noise reduction performances on the EMG signals. In their study, the aim is to clear ECG interference from EMG signals as a noise signal. In the proposed method, unlike high-pass filtering, it was observed that most of the EMG spectral content was maintained at low frequencies. Jochumsen et al [13] introduced a Bayes based sEMG classification technique to recognize main movements of the arms. Chowdhury et al [14] suggested an approach that relies on the correlation of EMG and EEG signals. The key objective of the research was to design a neuro-rehabilitation system. The proposed approach consists of the phases of preprocessing, feature extraction and classification. Accuracy was employed as the evaluation parameter in the study. Tavakoli et al [15] developed a prosthetic glove using EMG signals. In the study, basic hand movements are defined, and hand movements are aimed to be transformed into a flexible structure. The accuracy and error matrix parameters have been utilized to test the performance of the study. Tavakoli et al [16] designed a wearable glove from EMG signals. This designed glove basically recognizes four movements. The major goal of their research was to provide high mobility with four basic movements defined. To accomplish this objective, an original glove hardware was designed and they constructed their dataset using the glove constructed. In the second stage, the statistical features were extracted from the data obtained. In the third stage, LDA and SVM classifiers were used to obtain performance.
