## Analysis of IoT interventions to solve voice pathologies challenges

Wahengbam Kanan Kumar, Mithlesh Prasad Singh, Aheibam Dinamani Singh, Rajesh Kumar and Kishorjit Nongmeikapam

Speech is the simplest mode of expression, and any alteration of the vocal cord disrupts its smooth flow. Speech disorders increase vocal exhaustion, pressure, dysphonia, roughness, glottal attack, sore throat, etc. Disruption of a smooth voice in most situations leads to a wide range of psychiatric disorders resulting from stigmatization from peers and the community. It can have detrimental effects in the search for jobs, as well as on social and academic terms. Speech disability also results from brain damage, hearing loss, stroke, and developmental delay. A variety of aspects of voice pathology research are the subject of the present chapter. Applications for the Internet of Things (IoT) and related research work are analyzed in depth. Detailed background information on electroglottography is highlighted, which includes its development, production of vocal folded tone, glottal closure and instant opening, quantitative analysis in terms of frequency and amplitude. Information on some of the most common datasets among researchers are also highlighted. Finally, two separate parts dealing with several important acoustic feature extraction techniques, along with significant pathological voice analysis and identification techniques, are also discussed in great detail.

### 3.1 Introduction

The most important way to exchange ideas and feelings between two people is by speaking. It is also the simplest and most concise way to convey one’s viewpoint, considering that it contains a variety of non-verbal opinions. Creating a voice from one’s body is nevertheless a complicated process. Some major organs and tissues play an important role in the three-stage voice production process. They are—(a) generating air volume pressure, (b) supplying vibration, and (c) resonance. Collectively, the rib cage, diaphragms, lungs, and muscles in the abdominal region create an articulate air volume pressure that generates a ‘pitch’ by continuous oscillation of the vocal folds. It is the characteristics and structures of the vocal tract and the vocal folds that produce sounds that are unique to all. The ‘pitch’ that is derived from the vocal fold lingers in the nasal cavities, and the throat that has a distinct acoustic structure is another source of individuality for any voice. There are three distinct layers in the laryngeal vocal fold, that is, the vocal ligament, the mucosa, and the vocal fold. Since these tissues are tender, they can be continuously affected by air pressure and release the mucosal waveform. The ability of the mucosa to produce pitch rates determines whether the voice is regular or abnormal.

For speech specialists such as phoniatricians, a voice has several characteristics of an individual, such as mental well-being, physical trauma, vocal pathologies in the vocal fold, the person’s gender, emotional state, etc. A deviation from the normal vocal attributes of a person as opposed to those who share the age group and gender can be defined as a problem of laryngitis or other vocal fold disease. Vocal fold disease is caused by gradual alteration in the voice synthesizing organs and tissues in the body. These changes may be due to over-manipulation of the vocal cords, frequent intake of medications, or neurological disorders. Abuse of vocal cord can lead to conditions such as unilateral nerve paralysis, laryngeal cancer, polyp, nodule, and folding for a prolonged period of time. These situations can be defined by the hoarseness of one’s voice. In addition to self-abuse, an unhealthy lifestyle may contribute to an increase in vocal disorders [1, 2]. Singers, mothers of children and educators are people who unknowingly abuse their vocal cords. The reasoning is that they have to say words constantly during the work plan. Such excessive use of the voice fold leads to more rigidity over time. The vagus nerve, which is responsible for the proper functioning of the larynx, is also highly likely to be dysfunctional. This condition is known as unilateral nerve paralysis, which is usually noticed from the speaker’s breathing phonation sound. Other issues are also reported, such as air deprivation, trouble swallowing and mild coughing [3]. Irregularity occurs in the vocal box or larynx due to repetitive abuse due to the nature of the work that can contribute to breathing and harsh vocals. Physical features, including elasticity, shape and volume, associated with vocal folds, are modified when disorders, including gastric reflux or anterior-to-posterior (A–P) squeezing, begin. This leads to an unusual variant of vibration. Swelling of the vocal folds are caused by gastric reflux and laryngitis. While muscle tension damage is caused by constant A–P squeezing.

### 3.1.1 Pathology assessment

The invasive technique used to diagnose pathologies in human laryngeal arrangement is an age-old and modern procedure that is still popular throughout the world. Examples of the two endoscopic procedures required to insert large instruments into the human body in order to map the internal structure include laryngostroboscopy and operational microlaryngoscopy. Intrinsic anxiety and pain are caused to the patient under surveillance. In the past, scientists have also developed non-invasive approaches to minimize pain to a certain degree. In one such scenario, electroglottography (EGG) is done where a person’s voice is analyzed. Patients have been able to ease the pain to some degree. There is, however, a problem with teaching individuals to use these machines, which, if applied on a mass scale, would result in pointless costs. Economical, non-invasive and automated diagnosis of voice signal pathologies for thorough study of glottal signals is a trend for research enthusiasts. It is the future of speech pathology detection. The glottal signal is produced between the vocal fold and the vocal tract. Considering the success of deep learning [4] in various areas of artificial intelligence (AI), the number of studies in the classification of voice pathology will increase further [5–9]. Deep learning is becoming a better option against common classifiers such as decision trees, support vector machine (SVM), k-nearest neighbors (KNN), decision trees, naive Bayes, etc. Handcrafted speech or acoustic features have been of primary importance for voice pathology detection for the last few decades and cannot therefore be ignored.

Deep learning models such as the Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) and the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) are also used. Prediction scores generated using handcrafted machine learning features and deep learning classifiers can also be combined to further improve accuracy [9]. It can therefore be concluded that computer-based voice analytics of these types should be further researched before they are widely used as screening methods to classify laryngeal pathology.

### 3.1.2 Internet of things in voice pathology

Healthcare is one of the most common sectors for internet of things (IoT) applications. Some potential uses include chronic disease management, smart fitness-tracking wearables, remote patient monitoring, etc. Nowadays, a boom in smart watches that use sensors to monitor different types of everyday fitness activities and uploading to IoT-based smartphones is widespread. Intelligent medical devices and imaging devices are also main components of IoT. Over the coming years, the integration of more miniaturized low-power sensors with better machine learning (ML) algorithms will significantly enhance the cost-effectiveness of these smart IoT products. This will further revolutionize the experience of the consumer. Authors of [10] argued that suppliers view IoT as a tool for remotely reducing the downtime of smart devices. IoT also provides strategic resource planning to ensure that more patients will make the most of their services. Premature testing, health-related problems, chronic diseases and real-time monitoring are expected to be promoted by emerging wireless technologies [11, 12]. A thorough understanding of current IoT research in health applications is expected to help many stakeholders involved in additional research at this point in time. ML has been used to test clinical parameters and their combinations for prediction purposes, such as extracting health knowledge for outcome studies, comprehensive patient treatment, therapeutic planning and support, and predicting disease progression [13, 14].

ML incorporation in the medical sector provides medical professionals with the opportunity to function effectively, create, and enhance the quality and reliability of medical care [15]. Biomedical signals can also be done with real hardware in order to recognize physiological changes in the human body in a variety of different ways.
