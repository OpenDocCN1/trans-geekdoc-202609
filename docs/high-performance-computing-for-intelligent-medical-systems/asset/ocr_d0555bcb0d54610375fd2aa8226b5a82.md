# 7.7 Phases of diagnostics and the requirements for doctors

# 7.7.1 Requirements for competence and compassion

Patients expect that the doctor has sufficient expertise to provide both right diagnosis and adequate care. Medical ‘competence’ for a doctor is assumed to include technical capability and willingness to provide needed information. Consumers also expect ‘compassion’, empathy, and moral support when making healthcare decisions [79]. Effective communication, trust, and an encouraging environment are aspects of mature consultation skills [60]. In surgery and intensive care, physician competence, like technical skills, is often preferred while in the end-of-life vignette, compassion is emphasized. In chronic care, preferences for physician competence and compassion vary. In pediatric cases, a doctor’s competence is preferred, but the importance of empathy is also valued [79].

# 7.7.2 Diagnostic process from the view of doctors

In their self-evaluative actions, many doctors use a variety of often individual-tuned process models. These checklists play a role in improving the diagnostic process [2, 80]. The best process models describe different phases of the diagnostic process. With aid of taxonomic models it is easier to detect what went wrong in diagnostic process. Taxonomic failures can be assessed from several standpoints in the diagnostic process; from access and patient history to the final stages of referrals and follow-up. In each step, embedded sub-categories clarify items for evaluation. In ordering tests, e.g., mistakes may include delays in test ordering or sequencing [61].

Schiff and colleagues [81] divide the diagnostic process into seven stages: (1) access and presentation, (2) history taking/collection, (3) the physical exam, (4) testing, (5) assessment, (6) referral, and (7) follow-up. A patient’s clinical history includes documentation of the current health problem, history (medical, family, social), and other needed information like medications [2, 82]. Physical examination skills have a substantial role in diagnostics, but these skills have often been underemphasized in professional education [2, 83]. Diagnostic testing contains areas of laboratory, medicine, anatomic pathology, and medical imaging [2].
