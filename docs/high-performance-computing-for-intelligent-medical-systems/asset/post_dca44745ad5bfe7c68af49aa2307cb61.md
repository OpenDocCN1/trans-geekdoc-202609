
The research work therefore emphasizes the use of time–frequency (TF) features to capture the dynamics of the pathological voice signal. Their binary classification using a k-means unsupervised clustering technique with these characteristics resulted in a substantial accuracy of 98.6%.

Vowel /a/ recording is very widespread in voice pathology techniques and is widely found in all well-known databases such as MEEI, SVD, and APVD. Undoubtedly, its implementation has achieved reasonable accuracy. However, investigators of [129, 130] used both the running speech and the sustained vowel /a/ recordings found in the APVD data collection. They achieved more than 99% accuracy. The authors of [131] used a signal pre-processing technique known as Voice Activity Detection (VAD) before extracting MFCC features. The VAD module is a form of filter that distinguishes the silent or voiced sections or the noise spectrum present in the signal. The training of the VAD module initially involves the development of a signal mixed with the noise spectrum, after which the useful and noisy regions of the signal are manually annotated. These annotated noisy and useful regions are collectively categorized as the voiced component of the signal. In this way, the classifier identifies the spectra of noise, the clean signal, and the silent part of the signal spectrum. This is a sophisticated and nuanced method. The implementation of the MFCC features with the VAD module on the 117 and 23 abnormal and safe recordings present in the MEEI database resulted in an accuracy of 96%.

Another binary classification technique by [132] qualified Hidden Markov Model (HMM), GMM, and SVM with six separate feature descriptors, namely—Relative Average Perturbation (RAP), shimmer, jitter, soft phonation index (SPI), HNR and Amplitude Perturbation Quotient (APQ). The pathological voice category consisted of numerous laryngeal diseases such as laryngeal cancer, cyst, palsy, Edema, nodule, polyp, and laryngitis. The implementation result showed that the GMM with these feature vectors had the highest accuracy, even higher than the artificial neural network (ANN)-based implementation with which it is compared.

A smartphone-based hardware prototype for diagnosing individual dysphonia has been seen in a landmark and recently published research work [133]. The disorder has a high association with the unhealthy manipulation of vocal cords on a regular basis due to the nature of the work. The key features of this type of condition are non-uniformity in loudness, degradation of pitch and voice quality. It is composed of around 10% of the other voice pathologies that affect individuals worldwide. The investigation demonstrated the efficiency of several features with different ML techniques, such as logistic model tree, support vector descriptors, berry model, decision tree, and instance-based learning. Features that have been used in this work are—HNR, SPI, shimmer, spectral features, jitter, MFCC, RAP, disruption, energy, temporal features, noise, and wavelet features. The comparison of efficiencies was made using popular metrics such as sensitivity, specificity, apart from the use of precision as widely used for other techniques. The investigation concluded that SVM and a decision tree with features could provide very good results.

Real-time implementation of pathological and regular voice differentiation using MFCC, ZCR and LPCC function descriptors with ANN has been proposed in the recently published work [101]. The investigation was performed using patient-derived speech samples. Their methods have used less computing energy. Possible effects on the binary classification of speech samples due to the use of acoustic features such as shaping frequency, capacity, shimmer, reflective coefficients, jitter and residual signal have been investigated in [134]. The feature descriptors were implemented with the Radial Basis Function Neural Network (RBFNN) and the SVM classifier and generated mean accuracy of 91% and 83%, respectively.

Identifying the level of severity of a pathological voice is another problem. Much of the research focuses on binary classification, and a limited number of research literature exists for automatic diagnosis of severity. In a unique research study [135], a technique was proposed to automatically diagnose the stage of voice disorders. A neural network-based tool with acoustic features derived from the sustained vowel /a/ records found in the SVD database was used for the work. The level of severity was measured by observing the performance of four speech characteristics against those of normal values. Their approach resulted in an overall sensitivity, precision, and accuracy of 1.6%, 95% and 97.9%, respectively. Accuracy was registered at 90% and 95% for normal and pathological groups, respectively.

Binary classification has also been carried out using the Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and the Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) of the feature vectors [136]. More precisely, the study carried out the experiment by taking 50 normal and pathological samples from the MEEI database. The vector extraction techniques used in their research work include branch-and-bound, entity, backward and forward. In total, 33 different better varieties of features were extracted, but only 22 were chosen for training. To better assess the efficiency of these feature vectors, the authors trained with six different types of classifiers, they are—kNN, SVM, quadratic discriminant classifier, Parzen classifier, ANN, and the nearest mean classifier. The implementation of the proposed extraction vector function, the LDA reduction and the SVM classifier given an accuracy of 94.26%.

## E. Deep Learning based voice pathology classification.

With the advent of deep learning in the field of computer vision [4], the use of deep learning methods for evaluating medical imaging and signal processing has increased considerably. These techniques have been used in several voice recognition applications, one of which is pathological voice research. Various deep learning algorithms have been introduced in speech pathology, often binary classification, that is, healthy voice and pathology.

Researchers at [137] suggested a deep learning system for the diagnosis of voice signal pathologies. Their work is very special in the sense that they used a dataset that they obtained for training, while testing was performed on samples extracted from the MEEI database. More specifically, their training samples are made up of regular and six types of pathological voice disorders. These samples were obtained from a tertiary teaching hospital. The six diseases included in their dataset are: vocal fold nodules, spasmodic dysphonia, neoplasm, polyp, laryngeal paralysis, and cysts. Sustained vowel sound recordings with a duration of 3 s have been used as a source for the extraction of MFCC feature vectors. Deep Neural Network, Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) and SVM Classifiers have been trained with MFCC features and a five-fold cross-validation technique to perform the classification. Testing of the three trained classifiers was carried out using the MEEI dataset. The DNN classifier provided a very strong classification rate for both the male and female groups, that is, 94.26% and 90.52%, respectively. The same classifier also provided a very high overall classification rate of 99.32% for the MEEI database. It is realized from their research work that the use of several neuronal layers and the optimization of weights will allow the DNN to outperform other ML classifiers.

Another recently published research literature [9] dealt with the problem of defining the exact pathology associated with voice dysfunction rather than a simple binary classification. The innovation of the research work is the use of the computer vision (CV) based deep spatial exploitation capability to carry out the proposed multiclass classification. All previous approaches used a range of acoustic/speech-based features for the training of ML algorithms. The use of these handcrafted features to diagnose pathologies has become rather monotonous and overstretched. The work of [137, 138] has also shown that there is a huge amount of untapped potential for deep learning in the field of pathological voice recognition. To this end, the research work of [9] explores the use of multiple techniques such as signal denoiser, multiple time–frequency scalograms, image-based analysis using a dilated fully convolutional neural network, swarm intelligence [139] for iteratively boosting prediction score. Samples were initially extracted from the VOICED dataset, which was followed by a deep learning signal filtering process. The filtered signal corpus was then used to construct morse, bump, and analytical-morlet wavelet time–frequency scalogram image corpuses. Three separate 3D-CNN networks were trained three times each using image corpuses, after which an iterative optimization technique called ‘group decision analogy’ was applied to improve accuracy. The classification task, which includes four groups, that is, normal, hyperkinetic dysphonia, hypokinetic dysphonia, and reflux laryngitis, showed an overall accuracy of 97.7%, while reflux laryngitis and hypokinetic dysphonia were 100% accurate. The approach has been very effective, but some of the factors that have not been applied are five-fold cross-validation and a methodology that uses a single classifier to minimize training and testing times. It can also be further expanded to handle other voice disorders.

[138] uses multi-layer neural networks to automatically extract useful features from raw input data, which can also be related to as an end-to-end approach. In the end-to-end mode, a raw voice signal or spectrogram is directly used to train a deep learning model to target output labels [140, 141]. Multi-layer neural network disadvantages, however, include over-fitting and a conceivably extended training time due to many model parameters. To fix the downside, CNN-based models were proposed using transfer learning and input voice signal representations such as Mel-spectrogram and octave-spectrogram, which achieved a state-of-the-art output of 97.5% accuracy [142]. Researchers also studied the combination of CNN with long short-term memory (LSTM) networks [143] and, similarly, the combination of LSTM and MLP [144] for pathological voice detection.
