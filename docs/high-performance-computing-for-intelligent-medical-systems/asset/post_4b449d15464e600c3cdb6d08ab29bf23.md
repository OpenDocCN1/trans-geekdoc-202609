
### C. Child health and heart studies in England (CHASE_DB1)

It contains different varieties of disorders prevailing in a retinal fundus image and their ground truth vessels [28]. The fundus images have been acquired with a TIF camera of 35 degree FOV and 1280 × 960. The picture has been captured with a complete exposure in the center of the optical disk. The database consists of 14 pairs of left and right eyes obtained from children. Two specialists were used for creating a ground truth mask of the vessel regions. However, diseases are not clearly registered. All images are of high quality and well distinguishable.

### D. HRF

Jan Odstrcilik and his group of researchers developed the high-resolution fundus (HRF) dataset to undertake an automated segmentation architectures critical review. 45 retinal fundus images are provided by the HRF database. It includes 15 samples for healthy eyes, 15 sample images for glaucoma and 15 sample images for DR. All images have a high resolution of 3504 × 2336 [29].

### E. Messidor

For carrying out empirical studies on multiple segmentation algorithms for retinal fundus images the French Ministry of Science and Defense has established this dataset. It is a compilation of 1200 retinal images obtained by the non-mydriatic Topcon TRC NW6. Three of its ophthalmology divisions took part in the image acquisition process, which used 45-degree screen settings in TIFF format with a total colour-channel resolution of 8 bit, 2304 × 1536, 2240 × 1488, and 1440 × 936. 400 sample images do not have pupil dilation while others were taken with dilation. For each picture in the data set, the experts suggested two diagnoses of the potential macular oedema and retinopathy [30].

### F. Retinal vessel images for estimation of width (REVIEW)

It consists of 16 mydriatic and 193 segmented retinal blood vessel images. It is an initiative of the University of Lincoln (United Kingdom), in the year 2008. In order to mark the divisions of blood vessels, three specialists used a special drawing technique. This set of data has been subdivided into four series consisting of 8 photographs with high definition, 2 with central light reflective pictures, 4 of images of vascular abnormalities and 2 pictures of kicks point [31].

### G. ImageRet

The ImageRet project is FinnWell’s technology project for diabetic retinopathy (DR), which began in 2008. This collection has 219 retinal images named as DIARETDB0 and DIARETDB1. DIARETDB0 includes 130 fundus images where 110 healthier and 20 DR. And in DIARETDB1, 84 images have DR and the remaining are healthy. The images were acquired in PNG format with a 50° fundus camera bearing 1500 × 1152 resolution [32].

### H. ROC micro-aneurysm

The 2009 Microaneurysm Diagnosis Retinopathy Online Competition (ROC) took place at the University of Iowa. There are 100 retinal fundus images, which comprise 50 training and test images each. The goal was to delineate micro-aneurysm sites in the given training images. The images have resolutions of 1058 × 1061, 1389 × 1383 and 768 × 576 [33].

## 12.2.2 Fundus photography

A special fundus camera is used to record the principal anatomical structures of the eye, including the optical disc, fovea, blood and macula. A laser or a flash camera with a low power microscope is usually used. The fundus camera, which has specific dyes and filters to represent various intrinsic characteristics, captures various joint structures of the eye. Fundus photography has different shapes, including a colour fundus photography, red-free fluorescent angiography and concurrent stereo fundus photography. The retina has white light and full colour photographs. Special filters having spectral wavelength equal to 540–570 nm that can retain green chromaticity while removing red colour capture superficial lesions. A fluorescent colored dye is imparted in the blood circulation of the patient to record the images during the course of fluorescein angiography. The angiography of sodium fluorescein uses 490 nm blue light, and 530 nm yellow light fluoresces. For studying DR, it is a highly standardized photography technique. The choroidal vessels are observed using indocyanine green angiography using an 805 nm infrared diode

## 12.2.3 Challenges in retinal vessel segmentation

The performance of the retinal vessel segmentation methods recorded in the reports in the test sets and evaluation criteria [6, 34, 35] is usually nearer to that of human observers. Both documents and the latter in particular, a restriction which usually controls medical imaging techniques, are not consistent. The debate on the validation of algorithms and the challenge of international design is increasing internationally [36–39]. Several image processing problems affect a coherent segmentation of the retinal fundus. The most important of them can be summed up here.

- Central light reflection produces a divide in the segmented vessel and two smaller versions are developed on the segmented image.
- Tiny vessels that lack segmentation have poor contrast.
- Split vessels at crossover/bifurcation points.
- Near, parallel blood vessels are segmented as a big one.
- Existence of unwanted imaging artefacts like blur, noise, and non-uniform lighting.
- Injuries like microaneurysms, bleeding, hard and soft exudates and neovascularization interrupts the blood vessel images, block and show false positive effects.

The challenges mentioned above are critical to ensure that the data obtained with a variety of different instruments, procedures, operators and patient specimens are secure, reproducible and extremely accurate and eventually allow for the efficient translation of research technologies to the healthcare sector. Recent methods of deep learning have been largely efficient in the case of vessels that have been evaluated in natural, public data sets, typically DRIVE or STARE, which consists of a few images that contain the ground truth [40-43]. We observe that such a restriction is changing progressively. For example, at MICCAI 2018, the REFUGE challenge created a dataset of 1200 images featuring annotations for optical disc/cup segmentation and detection of glaucoma [44].

### A. Retina image segmentation

Global blindness is primarily caused by eye disorders, such as DR and diabetic maculopathy (DM). DM has a prolonged preclinical period in which eyesight is not impaired and vision loss will usually not be regained until the individual is in the eye clinic. Early diagnosis will avoid and protect the vision of the disease. Thus, retinal vessel research and analysis have become the basis for early diagnosis medical applications and successful monitoring of the retinal pathology, including blood vessels’ diameter, angles and lengths of its branches. Retinal ophthalmology image assessment is a key step towards retinal pathology recognition. Retinal vessel segmentation is a critical element for the diagnosis of eye disease. For the identification of symptoms, the thick and thin vessels are essential.
