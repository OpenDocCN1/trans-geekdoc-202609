# ## 12.1.2 Biomedical image segmentation

Biomedical image segmentation (BIS) has been a comprehensive application since the 1990s. Segmentation is a process of partitioning an image picture into sub-images having similar grey scale intensities. The other elements can be colour, texture and brightness. The segmentation is used to subdivide the objects into detailed pictures used for depth analysis. Some of its crucial tasks are as follows: firstly, investigating the internal structure of human anatomy in detail; secondly, recognizing the precise location of abnormalities such as tumour or cancerous cell growth; thirdly, it helps in identification of multiple numbers of lesions, exudates, and other anomalies; and finally, it measures tissue volume to measure tumour expansion.

BIS approaches can be divided into three distinct classes depending on the extent of human involvement: manual segmentation, semi-automated segmentation, and fully automatic BIS [11, 12]. The following categories are described:

+   A. Manual segmentation of the biomedical image. Here, the region of interest (ROI) is specified by a well-qualified expert after which a contour is drawn around it [11, 13]. Prolonged and challenging work is manual segmentation. This process of manual segmentation is again composed of two different types, which are known as ‘inter-rater’, and intra-rater. In the case of the former, multiple experts segment the image in various ways, whereas in the latter, a single expert segments an image in a variety of ways [14]. Manual segmented images are utilized as ground truths in automated segmentation algorithms for teaching artificial intelligence (AI) on how a disease should look. High-resolution images can lead to images without sharp edges, which leads to small differences in the choice of ROI boundary pixels. The fact that the approach relies on knowledge and experience is another issue of manual segmentation and thus, therefore, experiences considerable heterogeneity between and within the experts [15]. Although manual segmentation is dependent upon the evaluator, it is used in clinical studies with non-significant time limitations [16, 17].
