Healthcare is one of the most important problems in contemporary societies, as the life quality of individuals depends directly on it [18]. Nevertheless, the healthcare area is extremely varied, extensively distributed and decentralized. Access to accurate patient records is needed to provide appropriate medical care from a clinical point of view, which is rarely accessible where and when required [19]. There are inherently different statistical properties of medical results. Researchers and practitioners face two difficulties when analyzing those results: the curse of dimensionality and the heterogeneity of feature origins and statistical properties [20]. These triggers lead to biomedical signal analysis problems and factual errors. The fields of statistics, computing and medicine are now facing the challenge of exploring new approaches for biomedical signal modeling. This need is highly related to advancements in other fields, such as machine learning (ML), AI and CI. As the volume of digitally captured and processed medical data is vast and exponentially increasing, data storage and analysis are also emerging to turn this huge data into information and expertise, which helps them achieve their goals [21].
