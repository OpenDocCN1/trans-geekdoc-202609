### 3.5 Discussion and conclusion

Based on this survey, we can summarize our review as follows.

- The unnecessary delay between the data collection and the processing and analysis capacity is often the reason IoT is not fully and efficiently utilized in the healthcare industry.

- The big challenge that any patient faces is the unavailability of doctors and care in severe situations, and in particular when living in rural areas. Therefore, a new architecture needs to be established through the use of IoT devices.

- There are also cases in which, for example, people with a chronic illness need long-term monitoring. Continual supervision is highly necessary in this respect.

- In order to be able to communicate everywhere the IoT healthcare network has to have the potential to facilitate the flow of patients. This will involve an advanced IoT environment that takes into account networking customization without undermining data protection.

- Most studies concentrated on voice disease and its results only distinguish between a voice that is normal and a voice that has a pathological disorder where a full IoT system for the diagnosis of a particular disease, such as laryngeal cancer, is required. Since laryngeal cancer is a life-threatening condition, early diagnosis of laryngeal cancer requires a new and successful system / model.

- Although ML is being used in medical diagnosis, its design must be sufficiently precise to prevent any mistakes in the classification process. In addition, cybersecurity requirements, as well as legal and privacy challenges must be considered for the development of proven algorithms in order to benefit from the faith of patients and healthcare service providers.

- The delayed implementation of the use of ML technologies in medical diagnostics requires more ready acceptance and assistance by organizations and simplification of existing instruments for patients’ and doctors’ usage.

- There is an absence of a particular data set for a certain illness, like laryngeal cancer, where various voice disorders are found in the currently existing dataset. Moreover, the allocation of samples is highly uneven, making it difficult to diagnose voicing pathology. The explanation is that the entire data collection, like SVD includes only a single form of voice pathology.

- The SVM classification is found to be the most commonly used, especially in comparison with any other ML algorithms. This can be because it is easier to reach a broad database of pathological voices for analysis, and it may be because the SVM classification is best for high dimensional data and for small datasets. In comparison, deep learning and neural networks require a broad dataset to train the model to increase classification precision.

- Most of the advances suggested in the literature rely on methods of voice characterisation, which they refer to as a technique of extraction of traits. A deep learning methodology may be used to extract deep features, but it requires broad training examples. Although DNNs have become a powerful ML tool, relatively few works have been performed for deep learning since there are no large datasets available.

- Many articles also observed that pathology diagnosis was exceptionally reliable, and has in some cases risen to 100%. That being said, the findings are unique to the used database and the method established for one database cannot be expected to extend to all databases with the same amount of productivity. The cross-database and cross-geographic recognition of vocal fold pathologies would be an issue which needs to be solved.

- The existence of massive standard databases is important for the implementation of a clinical infrastructure in real time. As far as we know, the number of common benchmark bases is small because the construction of a database involves the assistance of physicians who are specialists in voice disorder diagnosis. An integrated framework for a specific hospital may be built using the local database. Clinicians usually use sound files for diagnosis, but they cannot detect or preserve the sound files on the basis of diseases over the long term. It would be of immense advantage to provide an IoT based archive storage medium that archives audio recordings instantly depending on the illness.

- Popular methodologies like HMM, GMM, etc have their own boundaries and merits. They provide good outcomes if the framework relies on text. The GMM is very useful in the preparation of a mixture. GMM is a probabilistic approach that gets fuzzy observation. In comparison, there are drawbacks. For text-independent voice comparison framework, methods such as HMM are not appropriate. It is only good for capturing small voices. If there are a massive number of distributions and large datasets are required, GMM is computationally expensive. In comparison, deep learning approaches supply the speaker with straightforward modeling. They have a comprehensive de-noise learning module. They need an optimal framework, though, and overfitting is a challenge.

- The review of literature has shown that it is often difficult to pick voice recordings. Researchers rely on both voiced and unvoiced sample elements, since the unvoiced portion of the speech samples also has evidence of pathology identification. It is also noticed that a letter, phrase or delay may be used as a voice sample. For a complete term, intermediate words and a delay should be taken into account.

- The next challenge for scientists is the extraction of features from the samples. The survey indicates that most of the researchers are more optimistic in using the frequency domain functions, although few researchers focus on time-domain measures for a particular pathology. There are also no unusual acoustic characteristics. It is a long calculation, though, and may be vulnerable to the patient’s anatomy. Also, standard practice as can be seen in the survey, is the multiple feature analysis. The survey also revealed that the CNN-based time–frequency scalogram of voice pathology detection is a very new technique. However, 2D CNN image-based techniques such as GAN can further develop the model to expand the dataset. The scope for further reducing deep learning time can also be provided by using modeling to fuse multiple time–frequency scalograms, like Morse, Bump and Amor into a single scalogram.
