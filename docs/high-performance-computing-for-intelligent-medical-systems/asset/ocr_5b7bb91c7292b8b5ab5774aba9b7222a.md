Real-time implementation of pathological and regular voice differentiation using MFCC, ZCR and LPCC function descriptors with ANN has been proposed in the recently published work [101]. The investigation was performed using patient-derived speech samples. Their methods have used less computing energy. Possible effects on the binary classification of speech samples due to the use of acoustic features such as shaping frequency, capacity, shimmer, reflective coefficients, jitter and residual signal have been investigated in [134]. The feature descriptors were implemented with the Radial Base Functional Neural Network (RBFNN) and the SVM classifier and generated mean accuracy of 91% and 83%, respectively.

Identifying the level of severity of a pathological voice is another problem. Much of the research focuses on binary classification, and a limited number of research literature exists for automatic diagnosis of severity. In a unique research study [135], a technique was proposed to automatically diagnose the stage of voice disorders. A neural network-based tool with acoustic features derived from the sustained vowel /a/ records found in the SVD database was used for the work. The level of severity was measured by observing the performance of four speech characteristics against those of normal values. Their approach resulted in an overall sensitivity, precision, and accuracy of 1.6%, 95% and 97.9%, respectively. Accuracy was registered at 90% and 95% for normal and pathological groups, respectively.

Binary classification has also been carried out using the Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and the Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) of the feature vectors [136]. More precisely, the study carried out the experiment by taking 50 normal and pathological samples from the MEEI database. The vector extraction techniques used in their research work include branch-and-bound, entity, backward and forward. In total, 33 different better varieties of features were extracted, but only 22 were chosen for training. To better assess the efficiency of these feature vectors, the authors trained with six different types of classifiers, they are—kNN, SVM, quadratic discriminant classifier, Parzen classifier, ANN, and the nearest mean classifier. The implementation of the proposed extraction vector function, the LDA reduction and the SVM classifier given an accuracy of 94.26%.

Various deep learning algorithms have been introduced in speech pathology, often binary classification, that is, healthy voice and pathology.
