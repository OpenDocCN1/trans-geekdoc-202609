
## 8.3.3 JPEG-LS

The standard denoted by JPEG-LS summarizes lossless and near-lossless compression procedures for compressing grayscale, bi-level, and color images [51]. Several significant improvements including ease of implementation, lower memory requirement, and lesser complexity are possessed by JPEG-LS. The compression attained by JPEG-LS is very comparative with the methods that are based on more computationally challenging coding practices, like arithmetic coding, despite the simplicity of the coding procedures. Since JPEG-LS provides state-of-the-art lossless compression efficiency, for some medical applications requiring lossless compression, it is especially suitable.

## 8.3.4 JPEG-XR

To compress continuous-tone images like photographic images, the JPEG-XR standard was introduced [62]. Two features of JPEG-XR are the key features that are very important for medical image compression, namely the JPEG-XR standard that supports depths up to 32 bits for the input image. Second, both lossy and lossless compression is provided by JPEG-XR standard with the identical signal flow path.

## 8.3.5 H.265

The Joint Collaborative Team on Video Coding (JCT-VC) suggested another name High-Efficiency Video Coding (HEVC) to H.265 video coding standard which is jointly recognized by the ITU-T Video Coding Experts Group (VCEG) and the ISO/IEC Moving Picture Expert Group (MPEG) [67]. In the case of efficiency in the coding of materials/images captured by the camera, HEVC/H.265 shows better results than the existing coding standard up to 50% reduced bit-rate while keeping the same perceptual quality [67]. In 1993, a new and improved video coding standard, namely Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine (DICOM), was released. This standard is a very well-accepted standard for medical image data processing. It has transformed radiology exercises to digitally equipped data from the film-based workflow.

Three-dimensional information is available in a video signal which is modeled in spatial and temporal domains. The compression of videos is done by eliminating the redundant information available in both domains. Experimental evidence revealed that transform domain coding techniques are suitable to exploit spatial redundancies much better than predictive coding techniques, whereas the temporal redundancies can be exploited using predictive coding like Differential Pulse Code Modulation (DPCM) and Adaptive Differential Pulse Code Modulation (ADPCM). Since videos have both types of redundancies, we need to exploit both by using a Hybrid Video Codec where firstly the temporal redundant information is exploited using predictive coding techniques. Since the inception of coding standards such as MPEG, Hybrid Video Codec architecture is being used. The Hybrid Video Codec structure is displayed in figure 8.1.

The Codec consists of two halves—encoder and decoder part. In the encoder, the prediction error of the residual signal is calculated by subtracting the input video from the predicted video, and then the residual signal is encoded using DCT which is followed by quantizer and entropy coding. The encoder encodes the bit-stream of the residual signal but not the original signal itself. The decoder part of the codec regenerates the error frame which has some difference from the original one due to quantization that was done at the encoder side. Taking the earlier or upcoming frame as a reference, the motion estimator block of the encoder predicts the next frame or past frame and calculates the coordinate displacement vector also called a motion vector (MV) using a practice called motion estimation (ME). The received signal is entropy decoded, de-quantized and inverse transform domain coding, that is, inverse DCT (IDCT) is used at the decoder terminal. Then the predicted video is added to the error to get the input video which is not the replica of the input video/ frames because, at the input end, the lossy compression is used.

## 8.3.6 Types of coding and frames

When the videos are taken and stored for processing, the frames must be encoded using some reference frames, either past or future frames. But as we know when we capture the first frame, there is no past frame nor future frame to refer to. In such cases, such images/frames are treated as still images and only spatial redundancy will exist which will be directly encoded by a process called Intra-frame coding and the frame is called I-frame. Temporal redundancies will not exist in such cases. Here the information is high and the bit requirement will be high. There is another way to encode a frame that uses the previous or coming frame as a reference called the predictive frame (P-frame), and the process is called inter-frame coding or predictive coding. The frames for which both the past frames and the future frames are used as a reference for coding them are known as B-frame and the process is called Bi-Directional prediction, which is also a part of inter-frame prediction. ## 8.4 Motion estimation and the necessity of it in video coding?

ME is the vital component of video compression. ME with motion compensation is used for the elision of temporal redundancy. Since a video is a collection of frames and there is little difference between two closely located frames, there is no need to encode every frame of a video. Rather we can intra-code the first frame and encode the change in information amongst the frames which are represented by MV and pass the same to the decoder side. The ME block of codec determines the displacement of objects in a video in terms of coordinate difference known as MV which is used by a decoder to regenerate the original video. The process of finding the MV is known as ME and this is the most complex part of video compression. The encoding of only the difference of frames, that is, MV will greatly reduce the bit budget to represent the video as the bits required to encode only MV are higher in comparison to the bits required to encode only whole frame. The efficiency of the compression process heavily depends on the complexity of ME process. So, the target of the research work is always to design such a methodology that the whole ME becomes fast and accurate. There are many approaches available for ME but due to the simplicity and speed, the block matching approach is widely accepted and research is ongoing to make the ME process more easy, accurate, and fast by developing new Block Matching Algorithms (BMAs). ### 8.4.1 Forward and backward motion estimation

In the video coding techniques, while removing temporal redundancies, frames are encoded concerning some other past or future frame. This type of coding is known as inter-frame coding or predictive coding. The frames that correspond to this type of coding are known as P-frame. In the encoding process, if the frame k (current frame) is encoded using the past frame say (k−1)th frame (previous frame) to predict what frame number k is going to be, we term it as forwarding prediction (FP) as future prediction is done for the kth frame by calculating MV. ### 8.4.2 Block matching concept

The concept behind the BM is to partition the images into a non-overlapping block called macro-blocks (MBs) of pixels assuming that all pixels within the MB undergo the same amount of displacement and to perform ME efficiently by finding out MV, that is, coordinate difference between the anchor MB and the most similar MB in the target frame based on certain matching metrics.
