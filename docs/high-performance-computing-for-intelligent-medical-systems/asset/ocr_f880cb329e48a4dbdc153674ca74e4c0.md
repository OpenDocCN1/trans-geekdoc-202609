# A. Retina image segmentation

Global blindness is primarily caused by eye disorders, such as DR and diabetic maculopathy (DM). DM has a prolonged preclinical period in which eyesight is not impaired and vision loss will usually not be regained until the individual is in the eye clinic. Early diagnosis will avoid and protect the vision of the disease. Thus, retinal vessel research and analysis have become the basis for early diagnosis medical applications and successful monitoring of the retinal pathology, including blood vessels’ diameter, angles and lengths of its branches. Retinal ophthalmology image assessment is a key step towards retinal pathology recognition. Retinal vessel segmentation is a critical element for the diagnosis of eye disease. For the identification of symptoms, the thick and thin vessels are essential.

One of the principal anatomical structures found in a retinal fundus image is retinal blood vessels. The structure and feature changes in it reflect the impact of cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) such as cataract, DR, and high blood pressure, etc. In the developed world, cataracts are by far the most common cause of blindness, contributing to over half of visual impairments. Effects like blindness can be avoided by early diagnosis and treatment. The region of the eye lens becomes thick and cloudy. It happens progressively as the eye’s protein deposition expands and prevents the lens from transmitting clear images to the retina. Around 0.075 billion blind people are likely to be registered in 2020 [45, 46]. It is of three types depending on its location. Firstly, at the middle of the lens, the nuclear cataract imparts yellow colour and image-blur to the lens. Usually in elderly people, the cortical cataract features are found at the corner of the lens. The capsular cataract behind its lens is the seriously harmful form of cataract [47]. This index rises rapidly in the elderly over 40 years, particularly among older people. The increased incidence is a major problem in early detection and diagnosis for the WHO. Early intervention and treatment may reduce cataract patients’ resilience and avoid vision problems.
