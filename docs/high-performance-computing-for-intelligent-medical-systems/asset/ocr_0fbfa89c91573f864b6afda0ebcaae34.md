# 8.2 Understanding the concept: video compression

Video compression represents the technique for reducing the bit requirement to encode digital video content, which leads to faster processing, less storage requirement, and lower transmission bandwidth requirement. The key idea behind video compression involves the removal of any kind of noise, redundant and irrelevant information which are not critical to watching the video contents. A video compression codec (format) is regarded as an efficient one if it can achieve the above benefits without causing much hardware overhead and without compromising the visual experience of the contents of the video. Higher and efficient data compression is possible for digital video data than that of analog video data. Lossless and lossy are the two techniques for data compression, where with the first type there is no loss of video information and the same information can be decoded as the in the original, while in the second type the loss of video information happens due to the removal of less important or irrelevant information. Balancing the quality, speed, resource utilization and power consumption are the challenges in video compression techniques. To efficiently process the video data, videos must be encoded through a compression process.

As the videos are a collection of images or frames, the encoding of videos will include the encoding of each of the frames. But there is a difference in encoding still images and video sequences. The images or frames only have spatial redundancy, whereas the signals in video vary in space as well as in time and they have both spatial and temporal redundant information.
