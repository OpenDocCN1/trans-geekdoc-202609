Most of the time, the challenge posed by the curse of dimensionality is statistical. When the dimension increases, fewer training samples fall into the exploding number of cells (or defined subspaces) in the input space. Therefore, the number of training samples for various areas in the input space decreases rapidly with increasing dimension. Less training data results in poorer performance inevitably. In such situations, the machine learning algorithm might simply assume that the output corresponding to a new input equals the value for the nearby points, while the further distance between training samples in a high dimensional space reduces the accuracy of such a simplification [17, 21].

As our results in the following sections indicate more clearly, the same problem might have occurred here. Since the input dimension (250) is very high, the LSTM model shows unsatisfactory performance. In the following models, we try to implement strategies to mitigate this problem.

# 4.4.2 PCA-LSTM model

To find out a solution for the difficulties that the LSTM network had with high-dimension input vectors, we apply Principal Component Analysis (PCA) method. It is an approach to reduce the input dimension.

There are two common ways to illustrate PCA. It can be obtained by the orthogonal projection of the data onto a new space with a lower dimension. The new space is named the principal subspace. The projection is carried out in such a way as to maximize the variance of the projected data.

Another illustration considers PCA as a technique to map our data to a new space with a lower dimension while minimizing the projection cost. Projection cost, here, refers to the mean squared distance between primary data and their projected counterparts [22].
