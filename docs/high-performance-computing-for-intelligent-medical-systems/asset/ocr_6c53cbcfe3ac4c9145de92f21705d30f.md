where \(w_n^{(i)}\), \(\beta\) and \(\xi\) are the iteration parameters, learning rate and loss function, respectively. Now, if \(g_{mn}\) is the pixel at \((m, n)\) position and \(y_{mn}\) is the expected output then the loss function is defined as

\(\xi(w, m, n) = h(N(f(w_g, g_{mn})), y_{mn})\) (10.27)

where \(f(.)\) is output of CNN network.

# 10.5 Evaluation criteria

We have defined some criteria of image performance to validate the efficiency of our method. Commonly used performance indications are MSE, PSNR, SNR, Mean, and Variance.

# 10.5.1 Mean square error

The mean square error (MSE) is measured by cumulatively squaring the error between the restored image and the degraded image [47, 48]. Mathematically, MSE is calculated as follows.

\(MSE = \frac{1}{mn} \sum_{x=0}^{m-1} \sum_{y=0}^{n-1} [g(x, y) - \bar{g}(x, y)]^2\) (10.28)

Where \(g(x, y)\) and \(\bar{g}(x, y)\) are degraded and filtered (restored) image, respectively. ‘m’ and ‘n’ are the dimensions of the image.

# 10.5.2 Peak signal-to-noise ratio

The peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) of an image is calculated as the ratio of square of the maximum possible pixel values to the MSE of the image [49, 50]. The high-quality image has a high PSNR. Mathematically, PSNR is represented as

\(PSNR = 10 \log_{10} \left( \frac{MAX^2}{MSE} \right)\) (10.29)

# 10.5.3 SNR

SNR is defined as the ratio of signal power to noise power. It is represented as

\(SNR = 10 \log_{10} \left[ \frac{\sum_{x=0}^{m-1} \sum_{y=0}^{n-1} [g(x, y)]^2}{\sum_{x=0}^{m-1} \sum_{y=0}^{n-1} [g(x, y) - \bar{g}(x, y)]^2} \right]\) (10.30)
