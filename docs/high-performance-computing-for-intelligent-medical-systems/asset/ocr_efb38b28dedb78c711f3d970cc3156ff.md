In mammography, it is believed that connective tissue, fat, and epithelial components can be dysplasia, and the ductal pattern represents periductal fibrosis [9]. A few specialists have discovered that the mammogram’s general measure of radiographic thickness is related to risk [10–12].

Imaging techniques such as MRI, x-ray, ultrasound, mammogram, etc, cannot differentiate cancerous cells from non-cancerous cells [13, 14]. Experienced radiologists identify useful information and interpret that information, but many times false diagnosis might be possible. To improve evaluation skills and diagnosis consistency to the doctor and reduce the subjective error, image processing techniques can be used to identify breast calcification types. Calcifications in the breast are small calcium deposits that appear as high-intensity regions in the mammogram. Calcifications are of two types, micro-calcifications, and macro-calcifications. Macro-calcifications are benign lesions that appear as coarse, scattered calcium deposits, whereas micro-calcifications are isolated calcium deposits that usually appear in clusters.

A micro-calcification cluster in the mammographic image may have breast cancer. The radiologist uses different shapes, sizes, and distribution of micro-calcification to identify malignant or benign. Malignant is a small dot-like structure that varies in size, shape, and density, whereas benign is a large, rounded shape structure homogeneous in shape [15]. These medical images help doctors in the detection of the abnormality in breast tissue. Analysis of the image in the early stage is complicated due to uncertainty in the tumor’s location. In the early-stage healthy and unhealthy cells are overlaid so it becomes very difficult to detect different intensity [16, 17]. Noise can also affect the image’s quality and a noisy image requires more computational time [18, 19].

Computer-aided tools that can compare the results with radiologist data are advantageous [20, 21]. Image processing techniques can be used to identify the area of interest including: filter selection for noise removal, image resizing, contrast enhancement, and removal of noise from the image to distinguish between cancerous and non-cancerous cells. The Otsu thresholding method minimizes the interclass variance of thresholded black and white pixels. Laplacian subband [22] is also a solution for removing unstructured, spurious artifacts from the image. Shukla et al [23] proposed an algorithm based on the K operator with Riesz kernel for image denoising. Adam et al [24] combine averaging and nonlinear Gaussian filtering to reduce speckle reduction and additive random noise reduction. Chen et al [25] suggested median filtering to eliminate impulsive noise. Adaptive median filters [26] can also be used for noise reduction, which replaces the pixel value with the local neighborhood’s weighted median. Still, it has some demerits; image smoothing also removes some sufficient detail in the image [27]. There is another possibility of wrong diagnosis due to blood vessels, and tissues.

The contrast enhancement method increases the contrast of the image over a threshold [28] but it has some drawbacks, like it may cause some regions to be under enhanced or over enhanced, which may result in information loss [29].
