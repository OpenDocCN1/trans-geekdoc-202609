Feature vectors belonging to healthy, vocal nodules and diffuse voice lesion samples have been trained with the SVM classifier to provide over 90% overall accuracy. The results of their implementation have also been related to the recommendations of the three phoniatricians. It has been noted that the use of sustained vocals alone for automatic prediction is much more successful than human phoniatrician predictions. Researchers of [37] used low-level MPEG-7 audio with false discovery rate (FDR) features to select and categorize four-class vectors—vocal fold nodules, keratosis, laryngeal paralysis, and polyp. They used the feature vectors to train the SVM classifier and achieved 99.99% accuracy. Authors of [113] proposed a binary classification of stable and pathological voice recordings using the M-band and SVM classifiers. Their technique yielded a combined accuracy of 100% using four and five bands. Voiceprint was also extracted by compressing and averaging MFCC voice recording frames with three sustained voice tones, that is, /a/./u/and/o/ [114]. Using these features, they conducted a binary classifying discriminatory analysis using the SVM classifier and achieved an overall accuracy of 100%. The authors also claimed that voice printing is more acceptable for the diagnosis of voice pathology with a sustained vowel /u/. A multi-band wavelet analysis for the multi-class classification of voice pathologies in the MEII dataset was also suggested in [115]. The proposed technique used the fractal dimension (FD) power dimension with a three-level discrete wavelet transformation (DWT). Out of a total of 173 abnormal recordings seen in the MEII dataset, only 168 recordings were used because the MDVP parameters for 5 recordings were not available. They used FD features with a total of 22 MDVP parameters to achieve a 2.26% increase in accuracy over their previous research work. Research by [116] has shown that features derived from the pathological vocal tract area exhibit inconsistent disturbances over successive frames for continuous voice recordings. The metrics used to determine inconsistency are kurtosis, variance, mean and skewness. These signal disturbances have been exploited to distinguish a safe voice from an abnormal voice. Their approach was based on a natural mechanism for voice production. During their investigation, two important points were identified: (a) the supraglottic tract plays a more prominent role in the voice production process than the remaining regions of the vocal tract; (b) the variance is a more critical parameter than the mean area of the vocal tract. The thorough investigation of the MEII and SVD voice pathology databases showed an accuracy of 99.22% and 94.7%, respectively. A further MDVP-based detection of speech pathologies of voice recordings from MEII, SVD and AVPD databases has been proposed in [2]. Three common abnormalities have been considered, that is, vocal fold polyp, cyst, and laryngeal paralysis. Study also used the FDR method for sorting the extracted features. Their research into the use of MDVP features with the SVM classifier resulted in an accuracy of 88.21%, 99.68% and 72.53% for the above three databases, respectively. In another uncommon research, a non-linear Gaussian kernel SVM unsupervised learner was employed in three separate phases to distinguish voice pathologies via a comparative approach.
