## 10.7 Conclusion

In this chapter, we have discussed image enhancement techniques both in the spatial domain and frequency domain. In spatial domain techniques, we have used the point processing techniques like histogram, histogram equalization, and histogram matching and some masking techniques like median filtering, mean filtering, Gaussian filtering, etc and in the frequency domain, ideal and Butterworth low pass filter, Gaussian filters, homomorphic filters, etc and observed that low pass filtering causes ringing effect in the output of the image. In the case of the Butterworth filter, it reduces the ringing effect but for higher-order, it also causes a ringing effect. However, a Gaussian filter does not cause any ringing effect. The homomorphic filter can enhance the image with very low illumination.

This chapter also discussed the recent developments in image denoising methods and showed their merits and demerits. Many times, noise in the image makes its analysis very difficult for researchers. A breast mammogram enhancement by removal of Gaussian, salt pepper, and speckle noise based on spatial domain filtering, frequency domain filtering, wavelet transform, and a CNN-based approach is developed. The proposed method is effective in image noise reduction and gives better enhancement for diagnosis. It is also shown that the performance of different filters depends upon the types of noises. The experimental result shows that the CNN-based filtering approach has been proven to be the best technique when the image is corrupted with Speckle, Gaussian, and impulse noise. The wavelet-based approach has been analyzed and results show that it is a good alternative for de-noising images corrupted with Gaussian and impulsive noise, but it also causes over enhancement and an image that appears brighter than the original mammographic image, so it becomes very difficult to identify the microcalcification. It is observed that low pass filtering can remove the noise, but it causes blurring and image smoothing. It can be concluded that the selection of image enhancement techniques either in the spatial domain or in the frequency domain is application-specific. It is also shown that the performance of different filters is varied and depends upon the types of noises.
