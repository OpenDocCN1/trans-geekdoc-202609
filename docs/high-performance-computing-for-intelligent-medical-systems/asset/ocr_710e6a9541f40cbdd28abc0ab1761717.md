# 11.2.1 The cornea

In the front of the iris and pupil lies the most anterior part of the body called the cornea. It is considered to be a highly dense innervated tissue of the body. The average dimension of the cornea of the adult human eye covers 11.5 mm horizontal diameter and 10.5 mm of the vertical diameter with constant curvature [1]. The trigeminal nerve has the ophthalmic entity which is the source of the corneal nerve [2]. Four millimetre is the size of pre-pupillary cornea, also termed the optic zone and situated in the middle of the cornea in a photopic condition. There is a difference in the physiology and pathological condition of both the central and the other type of cornea, that is, peripheral [3].

+   The human eye consists of five layers:
+   I. The epithelium;
+   II. The Bowman's membrane;
+   III. The lamellar stroma;
+   IV. The Descemet's membrane;
+   V. The endothelium.

# I. The corneal epithelium

Its surface is covered by the tear film which has the vital role of protecting the corneal surface from microbes, toxic chemicals or any xeno substances. The tear film has an outer lipid layer and inner water mucus layer. The tear film gets spread with each eyelid blink due to interaction between the epithelial cells and the outer lipid layer. The corneal epithelium consists of:

+   (a) 2–3 layers of superficial cells;
+   (b) 2–3 layers of the wings cells;
+   (c) one layer of basal cells.

The folding of microplicae contribute irregularity to the surface of the superficial cells of the epithelium and shows interaction with the overlying tear film. Around seven to ten days is the renewal time of the cells of the corneal epithelium. The presence of the cell junction provides impermeability, stability and anchoring to the corneal epithelium [4].

# II. The Bowman's layer

It separates the epithelium from the corneal lamellar stroma. It is a cellular zone of 10-15 micrometers.
