The spectral of R–R interval samples have been determined by using Welch’s method [33]. Bossone et al studied the association of features in primary pulmonary hypertension patients [34]. Melillo et al designed a cloud-based intelligent health monitoring system to automatically collect and analyze biomedical signals for the identification of HPT patients [35]. Dami et al predicted cardiovascular events using a deep belief neural network to reduce the dimension of the features, and used long short-term memory for classification of features [36]. Thomas et al applied automated ECG-derived sleep spectrogram technique to prevent stroke and HPT in the sleeping stage [38]. Schillaci et al reviewed the role of ECG signal in the diagnosis of left ventricular hypertrophy to forecast many risk factor adjusted cardiovascular morbidity and mortality in the asymptomatic patients of HPT [40]. Afkami et al developed a method for detection of left ventricular hypertrophy by using discrete wavelet transform to find the statistical features and fed it to a support vector machine (SVM) classifier with RBF kernel function [41]. Jothiramalingam et al used continuous wavelet transform for detection of R-wave and S-wave to obtain the features of left ventricular hypertrophy. SVM, K-nearest neighbour (KNN), ensemble tree (ET) classifier have been used for classification [42]. Kublanov et al compared various machine learning methods for the diagnosis of arterial HPT by using short-term HRV signals [43].

Various methods have been proposed for detection and classification of HPT in the literature. The filtering method requires selection of filter coefficients which are difficult to select, whereas in wavelet based methods selection of mother wavelet and decomposition level is difficult. The main contribution of this chapter is to design an automatic ECG detection system to discriminate between normal and HPT patients using flexible analytic wavelet transform (FAWT) and different machine learning algorithms to identify the most discriminant features and most suitable classification technique.

The rest of the chapter is organized as follows. Methodology is presented in section 1.2. The results are discussed in section 1.3, and the conclusion of this chapter is presented in section 1.4.

# 1.2 Methodology

This section consists of information about the dataset, FAWT, features, and classification techniques. The ECG signals are decomposed into subbands using FAWT. Various time domain features such as mean, mean absolute value (MAV), root mean square (RMS), standard deviation (STD), variance (VAR) and simple square integral (SSI) are extracted from these subbands.
