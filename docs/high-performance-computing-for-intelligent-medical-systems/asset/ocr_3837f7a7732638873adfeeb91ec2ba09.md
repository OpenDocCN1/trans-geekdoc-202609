Diabetes, high blood pressure, cancer etc are some of the most common diseases today. These are also called non-communicable diseases (NCDs). NCDs are chronic in nature and are dependent on genetic, physiological and environmental factors. Today about 41 million people die every year due to NCDs and this figure is 71% of the total number of deaths every year. The World Health Organization (WHO) says this figure will rise to 55 million by 2030, if timely prompt actions are not taken. According to a recent report of WHO, one in four Indians has a risk of dying from an NCD before they reach the age of 70. The Indian Government, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare says that the NCDs' burden has increased from 30% in 1990 to 55% in 2016 and also the proportion of deaths has also increased from 37% to 61% [7].

An illness or state which generally survives for three months or longer and can grow worse by time is called a chronic disease (CD). CD tends to happen in older adults and may generally be handled but not cured. The foremost common categories of CD are cancer, heart disease, stroke, diabetes, and arthritis. The US spends about $5.3 trillion every year on its health care. Some of the common causes of CD are: tobacco use, nutrition deficiency, physical inactivity and alcohol addiction [8].
