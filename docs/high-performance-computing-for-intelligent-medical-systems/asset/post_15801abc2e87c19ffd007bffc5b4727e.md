### 1.1.2 Related work

To date, researchers have proposed various methods for detection and classification of HPT using ECG signals. Tripathi et al classified the features extracted by using filtering to detect fast and slow heart rhythms [23]. Rajput et al determined the HPT diagnosis index which has been used to differentiate in between HPT and normal subject using wavelet filter bank [11]. Chuang et al applied empirical mode decomposition to extract different linear and non-linear features [24]. Kiat Soh et al used discrete wavelet transform and extract different subbands to diagnose the HPT [10]. Ni et al developed multi-scale HRV analysis for calculating the severity of HPT by using wavelet transform to detect R peaks [11]. Melillo et al designed a tool for prognosis of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events using HRV analysis through the series of beat intervals [25]. Zhang et al have done analysis of the autonomic nervous pattern in HPT based on short-term HRV by using the RR interval. Different features are extracted to distinguish between hypertensive heart-beat rhythms and normal ones [26]. Kale et al extracted the R–R interval for prediction of sensitivity and specificity of a cardiovascular event, random forest classifier has been used for classification of the event [27]. Ortiz-Martín et al analyzed the entropy of the LSB values and the Pan–Tompkins algorithm has been used to extract the R-peaks and extracted inter pulse interval [28]. Alkhodari et al implemented a machine learning model that handles imbalance data and predicts vascular events in hypertensive patients [29]. Agliari et al applied statistical mechanics tools and maximum entropy principle to analyzed temporal correlation in heart rate variability (HRV) against various complexes and chaotic cardiac dynamics [30]. Taye et al predicted the occurrence of ventricular tachyarrhythmia which arises from improper electrical heart activity and causes fast heart rate [31]. Agnihotri et al predicted present and future cardiac health of an individual based on fast and slow heart rhythms [32]. Koichubekov et al analyzed nonlinear indices of HRV signal in HPT using the Pan–Tompkins algorithm to detect the QRS complex and calculate R–R intervals. The spectral of R–R interval samples have been determined by using Welch’s method [33]. Bossone et al studied the association of features in primary pulmonary hypertension patients [34]. Melillo et al designed a cloud-based intelligent health monitoring system to automatically collect and analyze biomedical signals for the identification of HPT patients [35]. Dami et al predicted cardiovascular events using a deep belief neural network to reduce the dimension of the features, and used long short-term memory for classification of features [36]. Zhao et al identified the association of nervous system functions with minor stroke, using an ECG signal [37]. Thomas et al applied automated ECG-derived sleep spectrogram technique to prevent stroke and HPT in the sleeping stage [38]. Paragliola et al used a time series-based approach for identification of increases in HPT to discriminate between cardiovascular high-risk and low-risk HPT patients [39]. Schillaci et al reviewed the role of ECG signal in the diagnosis of left ventricular hypertrophy to forecast many risk factor adjusted cardiovascular morbidity and mortality in the asymptomatic patients of HPT [40]. Afkami et al developed a method for detection of left ventricular hypertrophy by using discrete wavelet transform to find the statistical features and fed it to a support vector machine (SVM) classifier with RBF kernel function [41]. Jothiramalingam et al used continuous wavelet transform for detection of R-wave and S-wave to obtain the features of left ventricular hypertrophy. SVM, K-nearest neighbour (KNN), ensemble tree (ET) classifier have been used for classification [42]. Kublanov et al compared various machine learning methods for the diagnosis of arterial HPT by using short-term HRV signals [43].

Various methods have been proposed for detection and classification of HPT in the literature. The filtering method requires selection of filter coefficients which are difficult to select, whereas in wavelet based methods selection of mother wavelet and decomposition level is difficult. The main contribution of this chapter is to design an automatic ECG detection system to discriminate between normal and HPT patients using flexible analytic wavelet transform (FAWT) and different machine learning algorithms to identify the most discriminant features and most suitable classification technique.

The rest of the chapter is organized as follows. Methodology is presented in section 1.2. The results are discussed in section 1.3, and the conclusion of this chapter is presented in section 1.4.

## 1.2 Methodology

This section consists of information about the dataset, FAWT, features, and classification techniques. The ECG signals are decomposed into subbands using FAWT. Various time domain features such as mean, mean absolute value (MAV), root mean square (RMS), standard deviation (STD), variance (VAR) and simple square integral (SSI) are extracted from these subbands. Highly distinguishable features are selected using the Kruskal–Wallis test and classified using different classifiers. Figure 1.1 represents a pictorial view of the proposed methodology.

### 1.2.1 Dataset

The ECG dataset downloaded from smart health for assessing the risk of events from the physionet website (https://physionet.org/content/shareedb/1.0.0/). It contains nominal 24-h ECG holter recording of 139 subjects, and available length of ECG recording of each subject is approximately 2 h 10 min: 12 s. ECG signal of each subject is recorded from three channels III, V3, and V5, and sampled at a sampling frequency of 128 Hz, with 8-bit resolution. It contains 49 females of average age 55 ± 5 years and 90 males having average age 72 ± 7 years, respectively. Out of 139 subjects, 17 were identified as high-risk HPT and 122 were identified as normal [9]. In this chapter, ECG recordings are segmented into 2-min epochs. ### 1.2.2 Flexible analytic wavelet transform

The nature of ECG signal is nonlinear and nonstationary due to which many difficulties are faced in the time-frequency analysis of a signal. Hence this study considered FAWT to decompose the ECG signal into sub-bands. FAWT is the well-structured technique for decomposition of non-stationary and non-linear signal. FAWT contains Hilbert transform pairs of atoms for analyzing positive and negative structure, therefore, it is most suitable for nonstationary signal processing. It uses a bank of filters to decompose a signal into sub bands. An iterative filter bank is used for signal decomposition. It is a combination of two high pass filters and one low pass filter. FAWT low pass and high pass channels are allowed arbitrary sampling rate. In FAWT dilation factor, quality factor and the redundancy can be easily specified by the user, due to this flexibility it allows fast implementation with perfect reconstruction of a finite length signal [44].

The following variables are used in FAWT: low pass up-sampling ($s_1$), low pass down-sampling ($s_2$), high pass up-sampling ($s_3$), high pass down-sampling ($s_4$), controlling parameter $\beta$, quality factor and redundancy rate ($R$).
