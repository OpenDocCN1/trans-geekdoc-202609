# IV. The corneal endothelium

It is 5–7 sided cuboidal cells in a single layer. They secrete Descemet’s elastic membrane which causes separation between stroma and the endothelium. The endothelium has an osmotic transport system where the osmotic gradient generates a constant percentage of the water in the stroma maintaining clarity and transparency of the cornea (hypoosmotic stroma to hypertonic aqueous); this whole process is called deturgescence, and its disturbance results in corneal edema.

Note: in a normal transparent cornea, visible light neither gets absorbed nor scattered. The condition of corneal scattering occurs due to:

+   * corneal edema;
+   * restoration of the collagen fibrils;
+   * hazes;
+   * surgical irregularities.

# 11.2.2 The human retina

The inner surface of the eye is lined by the inner tissues surrounding the vitrous cavity and held by the surrounding sclera and cornea. The neural retina is inside of the optical wall encircling the vitrous cavity, whereas the outside of the wall is surrounded by pigment epithelium (sclera and choroid) [7, 8]. Mullerian glia is the backbne of the neural retina and the light is captured by neural retina, having six main class of neuron—ganglion cells, bipolar cells, horizontal cells and amacrine cells. The outer nuclear consists of a nucleus of the photoreceptor cells, and in the inner nuclear of the retinal lies the cells of bipolar, horizontal, amacrine and Maglia. The inner nuclear further has the layer of the outer plexiform composed of photoreceptors joined with horizontal and bipolar cells. On the other hand, a layer of the inner plexform has amacrine and bipolar cells which have synapses with the cells of the ganglion. Mullerian glia processes extend to the retina. In a human there are two types of photoreceptor—rods and cones. Cones consist of the pigment with the absorption signal in various spectral ranges of colour of yellow, blue, and green. Rods having pigment show absorption in the colour region of green and blue. Rods are not involved in colour vision, they are active in low light levels [9].
