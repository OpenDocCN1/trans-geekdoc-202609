It is possible to assess the investigation intensity in the field of diagnostics by patient basis. The number of tests, examinations, and other research and consultation carried out in connection with the diagnostic process reflects the degree of intensity in the diagnostics phase. If the client believes that the degree of investigative intensity of their own case is too low for proper diagnostics, they must have a pathway to address their concerns. The current problem is that feedback on the validity of the diagnosis does not reach the doctor in charge quickly enough. On the other hand, the patient’s feedback on the medical performance should be specific enough to allow corrective action to be taken [70].

The feedback of diagnostics should be adequately specified, so that feedback delivery would be easy for the patient and beneficial to clinicians. Therefore, carefully constructed but economical (quick to fill) formats may enhance the process. Free-text comments are useful for service improvement if patient feedback is provided in near real-time [63, 71]. Due to AI algorithms, the utilization of free-text format is also more efficient. Thus, narrative feedback from the diagnostics phase also has its place as a basis for broader evaluation.

# 7.6 Enhancing technology acceptance in system development

Healthcare organizations should promote open and transparent environments in which patients can engage in the diagnostic process and share their feedback and concerns with ease [2]. At the same time, feedback systems should be sufficiently detailed, user-friendly, clear, and understandable. Also, there are problems in such areas as survey length, infrequent sampling frequency, and slow feedback. To be useful, patient feedback results should be timely, intelligible, and easy to interpret by all stakeholders. Clarity, ease of use, suitability for frequent use, and multiple features (e.g., feedback with paper, touchscreens, smartphones, tablets, and telephones with automated interactive voice response) are essential characters of a beneficial feedback system [63, 72].

In the technology acceptance model (TAM), the primary determinants for technology acceptance are perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use [73]. The model by Yan et al [74], describes aspects connected to the human–computer trust interaction. ‘Communication trust’ is one incentive for use. It covers areas of ‘perceived privacy’, ‘perceived identity’, and ‘communication context’. The customer must be able to trust that security issues concerning customer privacy are in order, e.g., in feedback system options [74]. Clients do not even use beneficial healthcare services if they are unsure about their information security [75]. Computer system trust relates to aspects of trustworthiness like ‘system and information quality’ and aspects of flexible use [74].
