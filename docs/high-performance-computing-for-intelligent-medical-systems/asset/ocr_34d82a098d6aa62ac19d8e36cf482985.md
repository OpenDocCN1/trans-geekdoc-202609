# Chapter 8

# Soft computing techniques for efficient processing of large medical data

Hussain Ahmed Choudhury and Nidul Sinha

During the last era, the usage of digital photography methodologies in radiology has grown substantially due to the recent developments in digital image processing and storage technology. About 90% of hospitals only take digital radiographs, including computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), ultrasound (US), nuclear medicine (NM), and digital angiography subtraction (DSA). Also, traditional chest or skeletal x-rays, which make up another 70% of the radiographs, can be converted into digital signals for subsequent computed radiography processing (CR). The reason for this interest in digital images is clear: the representation of images in digital form facilitates the transmission of images and allows the transformation of visual diagnostic information in useful and novel ways. There is one possible problem with digital images, despite these benefits, namely, the large number of bits needed for their representation. The amount of data generated by these methods is immense, and when transmitting the data over a network, this may be a problem. In the field of medicine, image compression has been implemented to resolve this problem. Some examples of different biomedical image forms are x-ray images, CT scans, MRI images, Ultrasound images, Mammography images, Optical imaging. So the huge amount of data generated by different tests, videography of medical processes, and medical imaging are compressed to store and process the data efficiently with limited hardware resources. Various software and hardware standards are available for making video and image compression cost-effective and time-efficient. This chapter discusses the various soft computing techniques which are applied in the field of video compression to make the process faster by accelerating the motion estimation.
