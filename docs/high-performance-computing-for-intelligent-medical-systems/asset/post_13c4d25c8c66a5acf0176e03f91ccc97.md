## 7.8 A model for instant patient feedback

### 7.8.1 General principles

When the patient evaluates the level of professionalism, the doctor’s competence in diagnostics, it is most manageable for the client to assess this area with a ready-made format. When dealing with attributes of empathy (e.g., listening to patients properly) both open and structured feedback formats may function well. It is essential that the patient’s experience and vision are presented in a specified way; the doctor responsible for the diagnostic process can understand the areas for which clients have open questions or remarks. If the patient is encouraged to provide feedback on the diagnostic process’s correctness as part of standard practice, giving feedback on the diagnostic process will be more comfortable. Accordingly, the patient feels that he or she is not only providing feedback on the specific aspect of the care process but is also a full participant in assessing the quality of his or her care. With a detailed but reasonably simple questionnaire, patients can give summary feedback on the diagnosis they have received. It is essential that the patient accurately identifies those aspects they believed were deficient in the diagnostic process. The proposed model gives the doctor a better idea of the patient’s experience than is typically possible during a limited consultancy session. This information helps the doctor improve their working methods and self-evaluation.

### 7.8.3 Patient management with the model

The main menu includes essential diagnostic-related sections (see figure 7.1). The patient can check the options that he/she considers to be deficient in ensuring correct diagnostics (marking the item as ‘unsatisfactory’). The areas that are not meant to be labeled are assumed to have proceeded as expected from the patient’s perspective. After completing the eight areas of assessment (with marking only those sections for which the patient believes there are problems), the patient can submit the feedback. It is assumed that, in generally, there are fewer areas for remarks (unsatisfactory areas) than there are acceptable areas. Fractures are examples of cases where there are many misdiagnoses. For example, the patient may believe the session went smoothly but would have liked an x-ray to confirm the diagnosis. In this case, the person marks this subdivision as unsatisfactory. If the patient also feels that the clinical examination was superficial, he or she marks this area as an ‘unsatisfactory clinical examination.’ Thus, the answer requires two entries.

As in all questionnaires, some patients are willing to spend more time giving feedback. In that case, it is helpful to provide two levels of detail for answers. The input could be presented in a more detailed way (i.e., ‘advanced level’) (see figure 7.2). Each feedback point could be opened in the advanced state, where it is possible to provide a more specific description of the response. For example, in the ‘Laboratory and imagiology’ section, the patient may specifically indicate which areas received less attention (laboratory examinations or certain imaging types).

### 7.8.4 Meaning of the fixed format phase of the model—phase 1

Diagnostics is a demanding area in a care process. For a patient, feedback delivery from this part is not always straightforward. Therefore, structured formats are useful in this area. Patients are nowadays intensively searching for information about their own health condition and especially about issues connected to their own possible diagnosis. Thanks to patient self-diagnostic applications or other gained knowledge, the patient may already have an idea of a possible diagnosis when they arrive at the doctor’s consultation. For this reason, it is important that, within the consultancy period, it is already possible to also discuss the healthcare client’s own views on the diagnostic decision. Especially challenging are situations in which the patient experiences symptoms and does not feel healthy, but the test results performed do not show a deviation from normal values. In addition, multi-problem groups, such as the elderly, may be a challenging group in a diagnostic sense.
