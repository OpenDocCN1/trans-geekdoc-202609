## 4.4.4 CNN–LSTM model

In this subsection, we combine the abilities of CNN networks in finding appropriate representations of their inputs with the ability of LSTM networks to memorize temporal information to propose a more optimal architecture for BP estimation.

To achieve these goals, we have designed an end-to-end DNN that includes two stages. The lower stage consists of convolutional layers to extract appropriate morphological indicators from PPG segments. The hidden layers in the upper stage, however, use LSTM units to grasp the time-domain variations of the features extracted by the CNN layers.

- **Proposed end-to-end CNN–LSTM model:** figure 4.10 depicts the proposed end-to-end DNN architecture. It includes two hierarchy levels. They are distinguished by larger blue rectangles in the figure. The lower hierarchy level is a CNN network that deploys four convolutional layers, while the upper hierarchy level encompasses a two-layer stacked LSTM network. Both sectors are designed based on the architectures discussed in the previous subsections. The CNN layers are responsible for learning (or extracting) proper machine-learned features from each segment. The LSTM layers, however, will learn the relationship between the segment features of the input series.

Since CNN layers extract features from each PPG segment independently, we need to use a folding layer before applying the input series containing PPG segments. This layer converts a batch of PPG series to a batch of PPG segments. Besides, we should use an unfolding layer to restore the PPG sequence structures after sequence folding to apply them to the LSTM layers.

## 4.5 Discussion

In general, proposed approaches for BP monitoring using ECG and PPG signals can be categorized into two groups. The first group includes methods based on physiological models. These methods extract hand-crafted features defined based on those models for BP monitoring. The other group includes methods that use machine-learning techniques for both feature extraction and estimation³.

In this chapter, we proposed several models based on state-of-the-art DNN. Step-by-step, we developed our models and made amendments to improve the accuracy. The first model was built upon LSTM layers. However, the high dimension of input vectors caused the performance to deteriorate and made the task exceedingly difficult for the network.

Then, we investigated the effectiveness of the PCA technique to reduce the input dimension and mitigate the curse of dimensionality, although, the results indicated that the solution was not effective enough. Since the PCA approach had led to losing crucial information in the PPG waveform, the accuracy was not satisfactory again. It was due to the reality that variance amplitude can't be the right criterion for all situations. Sometimes a feature with low fluctuations can be very decisive.

Therefore, we decided to use a more intelligent approach for feature extraction. Since, convolutional neural networks have made remarkable accomplishments in many applications, such as natural language processing, image and video recognition, and medical image processing, we decided to utilize them to design the third model. This time the results were promising, and they met the AAMI standard requirements.

The architecture based on convolutional neural networks performed the estimation process merely based on the PPG segment corresponding to the time step of interest for BP calculations. Hence, we suggested extending the architecture by adding some LSTM layers in an upper hierarchy to capture time-domain variations too. The approach was effective, and the accuracy improved even further. It was due to the reality that the LSTM layers could consider temporal information in the calculations.
