# 7.8.5 Meaning and management of the free format phase—phase 2

AI provides more efficient possibilities for processing free text. Free text feedback is especially useful in complex cases where, for example, the patient feels that his or her own diagnosis is prolonged. This may be due to poorly coordinated co-operation between different sites. Poor coordination causes situations that may threaten patient safety and increase healthcare costs [85, 86]. As speech recognition and processing systems have also evolved, it makes sense that the patient can also give free-form feedback on their phone interactively with the help of a robot as flexibly as possible.

Unstructured data can be challenging to organize and analyze. This kind of data is typically in text, such as open-ended text responses. In the case of hospitals, the volume of free-text data can be massive. Analyzing handwritten responses or large volumes of text data is time-consuming, expensive, and subject to human error [87, 88]. Big data is defined as ‘a collection of a very large amount of data with a wide range of types, making it hard to process using conventional database management systems’ [88]. This is why AI-based text analysis and automated knowledge representation and reasoning capabilities can be extremely helpful [87].

Customer feedback may be in the form of PDF documents, photos, or other images, scanned documents, or printed or handwritten documents. Optical Character Recognition (OCR) is the best tool for converting text to digital format in all of these cases (figure 7.4). Most OCR tools provide 98% to 99% accuracy [89]. This level of accuracy would be sufficient in most cases; however, accuracy may suffer in handwritten documents, blurry pictures, and languages other than English.

Handwriting Recognition (HWR), also known as Handwritten Text Recognition (HTR), is an active research area that can deliver a 4.0% Word Error Rate (WER) and 1.6% Character Error Rate (CER) when using deep learning techniques, like bidirectional, long short-term memory networks (BLSTMs) [90].

Another option is feedback in the form of audio. Tools are available to transform spoken audio into digital text. In many European hospitals, doctors' assessments are recorded and converted to text. Digital scribes (speech-processing and natural language processing (NLP) modules) can also convert the audio to text, and to extract salient information from the text [49]. There are many speech-to-text platforms available. They are standard tools, similar to OCR or translating tools. Most of the applications have sufficient accuracy to be usable.

Once open-ended responses are gathered (free-text phase) in digitized text, the material is ready for text analysis. Text analyzing techniques use machine learning algorithms to find quantitative or qualitative data in the text. In free-text form, data is ordinarily unstructured.
