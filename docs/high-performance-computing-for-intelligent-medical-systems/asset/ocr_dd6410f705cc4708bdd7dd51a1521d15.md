# F. Hausdorff distance (h)

This value is a measurement of distance among two test samples set. It is a set's maximal distance from the closest point in another set [27]. Hausdorff distance is shown in equation (12.6).

```
h(X, Y) = max_{x \in X} min_{y \in Y} ||x - y||   (12.6)
```

where X = {x1, x2, x3, ..., xn} and Y = {y1, y2, y3, ..., yn} are sample sets.

# 12.2 Retinal images segmentation

# 12.2.1 Datasets

Several publicly accessible professional data sets containing blood vessel descriptions are available. The first step in the retinal database is to train and assess classifiers for segmenting blood vessels. Researchers have access to these databases—STARE and DRIVE etc, along with their ground truth files, which can be obtained freely. The performance and exactness of the classifier can be evaluated with the following data sets.

# A. Structured analysis of the retina (STARE)

It contains 400 retinal images and possesses a 605 x 700 pixel 8 bit Color channel via TOPCON's TRV-50 fundus camera with 35-degree field of view (FOV). The average diameter of the FOV is 650 x 700. STARE consists of twenty samples of vessel ground truths files which can be used for training classifiers to segment blood vessel to demonstrate the presence of healthy and distinct types of ocular diseases. The very first specialist separated 10.4% of the blood vessel's pixels', whereas the second expert manually segmented the thinner retina vessel 14.9%. The ground truth obtained from these manual segmentations of the first professional is commonly used for evaluating a trained classifier's precision and efficiency

# B. Digital retinal images for vessel extraction (DRIVE)

This dataset is a very widely used for studying segmentation of retinal blood vessels. It is made from 20 photographs of the retinas, each of which reveals mild retinopathy, with 33 healthy retinal fundus images and 7 images suffering from moderate diabetic retinopathy (DR). The Canon CR5 camera with 45 degrees FOV and then a color channel of 8 bit in JPEG format was used for taking photographs at 768 x 584 pixels. The circular field of view of each images has radius of 270 pixels. The DRIVE dataset was divided into 20 pictures each in a training and test package. In the training sets, 14 of 20 sample images were segmented by first specialists, while the second specialists segmented the six remaining sample images. The vessel was segmented twice in the test set for evaluation. During the first circumstance 13 and 7 sample pictures were segmented by first and second specialists, while the second scenario consisted of a segmentation procedure by a third specialist. 12.7% and 12.3% pixels were identified in both the circumstances.
