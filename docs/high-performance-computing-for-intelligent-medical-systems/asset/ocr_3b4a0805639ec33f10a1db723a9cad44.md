B. Semi-automatic segmentation. Semi-automatic segmentation strategies demand a restricted number of user encounters with automated algorithms in order to achieve precise segmentation outcomes [18]. The user interaction may include a set of estimated initial ROI, which is ultimately used to segment the whole picture. To reduce segmentation errors, manual testing and area boundary editing may be needed. The following are examples of semiautomatic segmentation strategies:
(1) The Seeded Region Growth algorithm, which is based on a user's original seed point iteratively merges identical neighbourhood pixels [19].
(2) A level-set active contour model that starts from an original boundary shapes with a specified ROI and updates them iteratively by shrinking or expansion process is not needed [20].
(3) Localised region active contour methods that use region boundary conditions to use tiny local sections to define the image's foreground and background and have the benefit of managing heterogeneous textures [21].

C. Fully automatic segmentation. There is no interference from the customer. However, many of these systems concentrate on supervised methods of learning and training data, such as atlas segmentation methods, support vector machines (SVMs) and deep neural networks (DNNs). In unsupervised learning both training samples and the sample validation require ground truth images. With a completely intelligent segmentation in biomedical images, significant variations in form, size, texture and in some cases ROI colour between patients and low contrast between section are obvious challenges [22].

Noise may also lead to substantial deviations during data capture, as is often the case with modern devices. As a result, most emerging methods based on clustering and machine learning are confronted with the lack of global deployment that restricts their use to a limited range of applications. In addition, the feature extraction techniques used by SVM or neural networks takes a while and does not process natural data in its raw state and may not necessarily adapt to changing data. However, deep learning approaches can examine data without extracting features manually [23]. For semantic segmentation they are most favoured for BIS [24].
