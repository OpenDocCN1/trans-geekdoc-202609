It consists of several skip connections between analysis layers and synthesis path layers. It is based on an approach to a serial ensemble. The very first system segments the entire tumor, then, the core of the tumour is segmented by the second system and, eventually, the output of the second stage is used in the third stage, which is the enhancing stage. The voxel-based image segmentation is initially performed by shallow CNN [123]. In [120] authors suggested the use of CNN multi-path architecture for voxel classification. One route uses a 32 × 32 2D patch and the other uses fully connected 5 × 5 patch-size input with a middle pixel similar to a 32 × 32 patch. There is a patch collection so that the labels are equiprobable. L1 and L2 regularizations overcome overfitting.

The depth of such a DL model increased. For instance, researchers of [124] made use of a DL model where FC layer has separate HGG and LGG layers. A cascaded CNN has been proposed in [125]. The model utilized a two-path deep CNN model initially, after which, the outputs are used with other 2D CNNs. Wang et al. [123] also used residual connections with both pathways of a two-path deep CNN model, and trained using varying input image sizes. A dilated CNN type residual network has also been used for training with image patches in [126].

The authors in [127] implemented brain tumour segmentation using U-net architecture. The authors in [128] changed the 3D input U-net and reduced the network depth to three. In [129], the authors suggested new architectures of encoder-decoders that worked well on several problems with the biomedical image segmentation. For deep segmentation tasks, various CNN approaches using single-path have also been implemented in [130-134]. Ensemble CNNs are more efficient than single CNNs, as was the case in [135], where authors introduced a seven-network ensemble that uses DeepMedic, FCN, and U-net and variations in the three networks.

The authors in [136] had implemented the set of 10 architectures based on the encoder–decoder and the auto-encoder-stream for additional details and regularisation purposes. The authors’ method suggested in [137] was expanded in [138]. They use U-net and DenseNet combinations with an architecture like U-net that includes dense dilated convolution blocks. Three networks, which are WNet, TNet and ENet, were used by the authors in [123] to build a cascaded framework. These networks use axial, coronal, sagittal viewing angles to produce the final segmentation output.

In [139] there is a cascaded network that originally segmented the entire tumour and the tumour centre and reinforced the tumour segmentation.
A multi-scale 3D U-net mask was proposed by [140] authors with spatial pyramid pooling in which the first-network WT segmentation for the TC development was transferred to the second, in sequence to the end-net to establish ET output.
Additional CNN approaches based on an ensemble have been defined in [141-145].

## 12.4 Discussion and conclusion

This chapter presents the work, i.e., pre-existing and current techniques, accomplished by other researchers related to biomedical images segmentation, especially retina vessel segmentation and brain tumour segmentation. Here every referenced paper is summarized. It presents numerous types of segmentation by using different methods. Each segmentation approach reviewed here has its leads and constraints, and setting a particular benchmark for comparison is inappropriate. The segmentation of biomedical images has been broadly categorized in this paper. The validation of segmentation outcomes is a quintessential subject in biomedical image analysis since it directly influences surgical judgments. Orthodox machine learning algorithms can be further improved. For example, the method of extraction is mostly performed using a series of pre-specified filters in most conventional machine learning algorithms. Thus it would greatly increase efficiency if data-driven extraction methods were devised for conventional learning algorithms. Recent research on the segmentation of biological images relies mostly on the paradigm of deep learning. Finding high-quality biomedical image data is always a challenge. Deep architectures like ResNet, VGGNet and GoogLeNet typically overfit the learning framework significantly in cases where there are not enough training images, even after including regularisations, dropouts, and model averaging. The accuracy of CNN in the classification task of biomedical image datasets with GoogLeNet architecture has been steadily increased due to an increase in training images. The current level of classification is too simple for practical biomedical imaging processing, such as segmentation, but a fundamental relationship between efficiency and consistency of the training data set has been identified. Researchers have trained 3D segmentation models with only 2D segmentation in brain tumours segmentation study. A further scientific difficulty in the segmentation of brain tumours is that researchers apply the same fixed size to a kernel, which may mask any helpful information from another area that the kernel does not know. The way to enlarge the data set is more manageable, by random translation, tossing and rotation, to transform the primary data set. In regular machine learning algorithms, this term known as data augmentation is used previously. A lot of hyperparameters are essential for modern and deep learning arrangements. Minute variations in hyperparameters can lead to excessively significant network performance changes. While background and optimization by stochastic gradient descent methods also automatically learn how to weigh the network, many hyperparameters are also involved empirically, such as layer numbers, regularity coefficients and dropout rates. Deep learning methods, however, have still not been adequately improved but related work has been examined to avoid problems arising with these heuristic choices. There are also conditions of health that must be tackled. As we move forward, it is necessary to consider the possible disadvantages of deep learning methodologies.
