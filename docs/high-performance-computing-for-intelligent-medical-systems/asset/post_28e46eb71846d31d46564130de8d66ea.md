
Teaching systems based on deep learning algorithms, which are almost always carried out in 3D networks, require a slice-by-slice specification which is not only complicated but time-consuming. Another major disadvantage of deep learning algorithms lies in their inefficiency to learn from a limited amount of image data. 3D segmentation models proposed by several researchers used 2D segmentation [97]. BraTS datasets are extensively used for the tumour analysis algorithms and to estimate a tumour condition in brain MRIs.

Another issue with the data set is the class imbalance. For example, data increase strategies are employed by applying rotation and scaling technologies to produce additional brain tumour samples. This may cause class disequilibrium problems. Pereira [98] explores data augmentation techniques to overcome class imbalance for brain tumour segmentation.

Another problem is the implementation of the same or fixed kernel size, which can mask useful information that may occur in areas outside the kernel.

## 12.3.5 Image pre-processing

Medical image pre-processing plays a very important role in the appropriate inputs to computer-assisted research techniques. Preprocessed images help to even get accurate outputs since these images demonstrate proper voxel relationships. The repository has images of various diagnostic practice and scanners, the variability is simplified and put all the scans on the same scale. Apart from the image registration and skull stripping, additionally, there are other improved pre-processing methods like:

- **A. Noise reduction (NR)**

  The distortion in the MRI at the time of acquisition is caused by a radio wave or a magnetic field. Various noise amplification methods are suitable for noise elimination.

- **B. Histogram matching (HM)**

  Based on a different configuration of the MRI devices the intensity level of the images acquired may differ. Intensity ratios are to be adjusted to the same scale using the corresponding histogram process.

- **C. Intensity normalization (IN)**

  Different modality images possess a different intensity range. They must map to the equivalent range. Zero mean and unit variance are considered by standardisation of all scans.

## 12.3.6 Image post-processing

Due to inappropriate or incorrect feature selection, the segmentation output formed by computer-assisted procedures could include false segmentation in the image. The use of post-processing approaches, for example, can improve the segmentation.

- **A. Connected component analysis (CCA):**

  CCA clubs the voxels depending on the connectivity depending on the similar voxel intensities values. The consecutive components which are extremely small are excluded from the decision as such components are considered to be false positives due to spurious segmentation outcomes.

- **B. Conditional random field (CRF):**

  The voxel class is predicted by the classifier based on the characteristics associated with that voxel, and does not depend on the voxel's adjacent interaction with other nearby voxels. In order to enforce the dependencies between the predictions, CRF takes this correlation into account and constructs a graphical model.

## 12.3.7 Traditional machine learning in tumor segmentation

In a technique proposed in [99], the researchers applied morphological erosion and dilation operators with global thresholding on a binary tumour masking for segmenting tumour afflicted regions. An overall accuracy of 97% was achieved on a test image dataset which had about 100 MRI images derived from different individuals. In another research work [100], the SRC technique has used K-SVD dictionary-based learning for segmenting MRI brain images. The segmentation result was further refined by applying Active Contours Without Edges (ACWE). The data collection consisted of 80 T2 brain MRI images, 50 and 30 of which, respectively, were used for training and validation. Researchers of [101] proposed an MRI image brain tumour segmentation technique by using multiple fractal dimension, intensity, and texton features with AdaBoost classifier. Some improved versions of Fuzzy C-Means (FCM) algorithms named as Particle Swarm Optimization with FCM (PSOFCM) [102], Genetically Improved Fuzzy Markovian Relational Clustering Matrix (GIFMRCM) [103], Fast and Automatically Adjustable GRBF Kernel FCM (FAAGKFCM) [104], and Enhanced Possibilistic FCM (EPFCM) [105], have been used for segmenting MRI brain images. A Computer Aided Design (CAD) based segmentation technique that used pre-processing, segmentation or Region of Interest (ROI) development, feature extraction, and machine learning phases has been described in [106]. In this work, histogram equalization was the pre-processing method, which was followed by morphological dilation and erosion segmentation. They investigated using a wide variety of machine learning techniques like Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Support Vector Machines (SVMs), Artificial Neural Network (ANN), and K-Nearest Neighbour (KNN). An accuracy of 100% was achieved while using kNN and PSO techniques. Local independent projection-based classification (LIPC) has been used together with connected component analysis and morphological operator for segmenting MRI images [107]. In another different approach for segmenting brain tumour, Jiang et al [108] proposed and demonstrated the usage of a learning process that used a graph constructed from population and patient-centric Gabor and image modality feature descriptors. A 3D PCNN was used with morphological erosion and dilation operators for segmenting brain images of rodents. A spherical shaped mask was used for providing a smooth function [109]. A technique that is based on Markov Random Field (MRF) for segmenting MRI images that has multiple sclerosis (MS) lesions has been proposed in [110]. The MRF parameters were evaluated from the manually annotated images, and contextual information was included in the final stage. Their investigation was carried out on 24 axial T1, T2 and PD weighted images, where each has a resolution of 256 × 256. Another MS lesion identification technique for FLAIR brain MRI image that uses a combination of adaptive mixture model (AMM) and MRF has been proposed in [111]. In a unique approach, the content-based active contour model (CBAC) was used to initially demarcate tumour afflicted regions, after which a group of different feature descriptors were used for extracting features. The feature descriptors employed in their work are—directional Gabor, Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG), circular Gabor, rotation-invariant local binary patterns (RILBPs), GLCM and pixel intensity features. The final stage consisted of the PCA algorithm to reduce the feature vectors, after which the ANN was employed for training [112]. Brain tumour in MRI images has also been segmented by using kNN and CRF in [113]. The investigation was carried out using 40 MRI images, out of which 30 and 10 samples were derived from individuals having HG and LG tumours, respectively. Researchers of [114] used hard k-means with FCM clustering techniques initially, after which the level was set and adaptive thresholding techniques were applied. Before this final segmentation, the brain MRI images were filtered by median filter and brain surface extractor algorithm. Their experimentation on three data sets yielded accuracies of 90.5%, 100%, and 100% having 22, 152 and 81 images, respectively. In another research work, the GMM algorithm was used for extracting rich features from different tumour tissues, which were then combined with random forest and MRF for segmenting brain tumour images. They used a total of 520 brain images derived using multiple modalities—T1, T1C, FLAIR, and T2 [115]. The authors of [116] extracted features from T1 and FLAIR MRI brain images by using 80 different Histogram of Oriented Gradient (HOG) features, 20 GLRLM, 256 IBP, and 112 GLCM features. It resulted in the creation of a feature vector having 475 diverse descriptors. It was reduced to 20 dimensioned feature vectors by applying PCA. A hybrid technique that uses FCM clustering and Hopfield Neural Network has been investigated on the PDC and IBSR databases for image segmentation and visualisation capability [117]. Before training, they performed skull stripping by using morphological operation. The technique by [118] used global thresholding, morphological operation and watershed segmentation on brain MRI images. Another research work [119] also implemented Otsu’s thresholding, hard k-means, and soft clustering for segmenting brain tumour images. An overall accuracy of 90.57% was attained.
