Each recording has a duration of 1.5 s with a sampling frequency of 44.1 kHz. The model that uses the MFCC features extracted from each of the GMM-UBM training sub-bands gave 96.96% accuracy. It is significantly higher than the 85.18% achieved while training GMM-UBM with MFCC features derived from a normal voice, that is, without any DWT disintegration. The main feature highlighted by their technique is the need to overcome the reliance on phonemes such as /a/, /u/ and /i/. MFCC features have been commonly used to classify speakers, speech pathologies, amnesia, Alzheimer’s, and emotional and physical trauma in individuals. It is not robust enough as it does account for the non-linear acoustic structure released from the body during voice development [122]. Due to this aspect, the authors of [122] used an 11 set of non-linear methods with MFCC to extract feature vectors from healthy and abnormal voice samples in the MEEI database. A hybrid training model that utilizes the generative ability of GMM and the discerning capabilities of the SVM classifiers has been introduced. More precisely, the GMM and SVM classifiers are split into two different classification levels. Their methodology showed a significant accuracy of 98.23%. However, it remains to be seen if their strategy is doing well in the light of multiple classification problems.

The SVM classifier has also been trained with a feature descriptor developed by the joint implementation of the Daubechies DWT and the linear cepstral prediction coefficient (LPCC) [93]. Time-frequency analysis was able to effectively identify pathologies in a voice. The use of MFCC and other function vectors with SVM classifiers and GMM for the design of the Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) model [123] was another recent and landmark research work. More precisely, the authors have developed a Hidden Markov Model Tool (HTK) to detect and identify voice abnormalities. A total of 297 speakers were used in their investigation, of which 176 samples were taken from individuals suffering from five forms of vocal fold disorders, of which 121 are typical individuals. Apart from the MFCC features, PLP, RASTA-PLP and LPC features have also been extracted, forming a special vector function for each voice sample class. The normal and abnormal categories investigation uncovered two distinct things. The implementation of the MFCC and LPC features supported 94.44% and 77.5% accuracy, respectively, whereas the use of PLP and RASTA-PLP feature descriptors provided an accuracy of 94.44% and 89.62% respectively. On the other hand, the pathological classification task performed mean accuracy equal to 76.16%, 87.14% and 87.65%, respectively, when the LPC, RASTA-PLP and MFCC function descriptors are used. The use of PLP feature descriptors provided an accuracy of 88.63%, which is the highest of its feature descriptors. The investigation showed reasonable accuracy for standard samples, although the use of the different features of the descriptors individually resulted in poor accuracy. Manual segmentation of voice samples is a significant downside to the proposed work.
