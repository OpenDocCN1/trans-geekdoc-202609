
## Chapter 10

## A comparative analysis of image enhancement techniques for detection of microcalcification in screening mammogram

Gaurav Makwana, Ram Narayan Yadav and Lalita Gupta

Breast cancer is the main source of cancer-related death in women of all ages. Among various imaging techniques, mammography is the most reliable and cost-effective technique for the diagnosis of breast malignancy. Radiologists investigate the presence of microcalcification and the pattern of its spread and interpret the information. However, diagnosis of the abnormality is completely subjective and depends upon the experience and expertise of the radiologists. There is always a possibility of human error due to poor contrast and noise that might cause the misleading diagnosis of abnormalities like calcification, masses, etc. Therefore, it is beneficial to use image processing techniques for mammogram image enhancement to improve the image quality for better interpretation of the image. In this chapter image enhancement techniques are applied to reduce the different types of noises like impulse noise, Gaussian noise, and speckle noise to increase the contrast of the mammogram images. The performance comparison of various image enhancement techniques like spatial domain filtering, frequency domain filtering and machine learning technique is shown. A quantitative measure of performance comparison is provided in terms of the parameters like Mean Square Error (MSE), Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR), mean, variance, etc. The test results indicate that the median filter can reduce impulse noise with the lowest MSE of 0.0206, whereas CNN is a better option to remove Gaussian and speckle noise with MSE of 0.0026 and 0.0017, respectively. We have also seen that these methods increase the contrast that helps in improving the diagnostic efficiency of mammograms.

### 10.1 Introduction

Cancer refers to the uncontrolled growth of epithelial cells in a particular location of the body. Malignant growths can be characterized by the kind of influenced cells or organs where this starts. There are roughly 100 sorts of cancer. It is an intricate and diverse illness since malignant development can happen in any part of the body. Breast malignancy is the uncontrolled development of epithelial cells in the breast. It is the second most common malignancy in women, however, it can affect men also. Additionally, it is the second leading reason for cancer death in women after lung malignancy because many times breast disease does not cause any agony or uneasiness until it has spread to close-by tissue [1]. Invasive ductal carcinoma (IDC) is the most widely recognized sort of breast disease in women of all ages and having a low survival rate [2].

Breast cancer is recognized by various names, contingent upon where it begins in the woman's breast. Bleeding from the nipple or pain in the armpit and a lump can indicate malignancy, and change in the size or shape of the breast is also an indication of abnormality. Breast tumors may be of two types, benign and malignant. Non-cancerous (benign) tumors comprise connective tissue, adenoma, fibro-adenoma. Fibro-adenoma is the most common benign mass due to the combination of connective tissue and epithelial cells in the breast. Two main types of breast cancer are non-invasive breast cancer and invasive breast cancer. In non-invasive cancer, normal epithelial cells are replaced by malignant cells lining the ducts or lobules in the breast tissue. Non-invasive breast cancers stay within the milk ducts or lobules, called carcinoma in situ (CIS). Invasive breast cancer is the most common type of breast cancer, it starts from the milk duct but spreads into the surrounding normal tissue inside the breast. The most common screening method for the identification of breast cancer is mammography. It is used for the early detection of suspicious areas in breast cancer. Doctors detect, diagnose, and evaluate breast cancer by screening mammography. The advantage of using mammography is that it can scan a very small lump. However, mammography still has some disadvantages such as being challenging to diagnose irregularity in the dense breast tissue, diagnosis in young patients [3, 4].

Mammographic screening can recognize a few malignant growths that are not detectable, while a few malignancies are detectable, but not distinguishable on the mammogram [5]. Screening mammography is more unlikely to identify early breast malignant growth at a treatable stage. To begin with, because in younger women breast thickness is commonly high [6, 7] and due to higher breast density, screening mammography brings about more false-positive tests, with the related anxiety and superfluous biopsies. Since women younger than 50 are more unlikely to have malignant breast growth, fewer women benefit from screening and the lower frequency of malignant breast growth increases the cost of mammography screening for a lifetime [8].

Early recognition is essential to the possibility of fruitful treatment since the malignant growth might be intrusive and spread to any part of the body. This recognition can be too troublesome since the principal indications of breast cancer are regularly symptomatic. This is the reason x-ray mammography is broadly used to screen for breast malignant growth. The mammogram can show small breast tissue changes, which may demonstrate too small tumors to be distinguished either by the radiologist or by a specialist doctor. There is an expanded breast malignant growth chance related to the fact that other realized hazard variables might affect breast tissue, which causes long and momentary changes to appear on the mammogram. In mammography, it is believed that connective tissue, fat, and epithelial components can be dysplasia, and the ductal pattern represents periductal fibrosis [9]. A few specialists have discovered that the mammogram’s general measure of radiographic thickness is related to risk [10–12].

Imaging techniques such as MRI, x-ray, ultrasound, mammogram, etc. cannot differentiate cancerous cells from non-cancerous cells [13, 14]. Experienced radiologists identify useful information and interpret that information, but many times false diagnosis might be possible. To improve evaluation skills and diagnosis consistency to the doctor and reduce the subjective error, image processing techniques can be used to identify breast calcification types. Calcifications in the breast are small calcium deposits that appear as high-intensity regions in the mammogram. Calcifications are of two types, micro-calcifications, and macro-calcifications. Macro-calcifications are benign lesions that appear as coarse, scattered calcium deposits, whereas micro-calcifications are isolated calcium deposits that usually appear in clusters.

A micro-calcification cluster in the mammographic image may have breast cancer. The radiologist uses different shapes, sizes, and distribution of micro-calcification to identify malignant or benign. Malignant is a small dot-like structure that varies in size, shape, and density, whereas benign is a large, rounded shape structure homogeneous in shape [15]. These medical images help doctors in the detection of the abnormality in breast tissue. Analysis of the image in the early stage is complicated due to uncertainty in the tumor’s location. In the early-stage, healthy and unhealthy cells are overlaid so it becomes very difficult to detect different intensity [16, 17]. Noise can also affect the image’s quality and a noisy image requires more computational time [18, 19].

Computer-aided tools that can compare the results with radiologist data are advantageous [20, 21]. Image processing techniques can be used to identify the area of interest including: filter selection for noise removal, image resizing, contrast enhancement, and removal of noise from the image to distinguish between cancerous and non-cancerous cells. The Otsu thresholding method minimizes the interclass variance of thresholded black and white pixels. Laplacian subband [22] is also a solution for removing unstructured, spurious artifacts from the image. Shukla et al [23] proposed an algorithm based on the K operator with Riesz kernel for image denoising. Adam et al [24] combine averaging and nonlinear Gaussian filtering to reduce speckle reduction and additive random noise reduction. Chen et al [25] suggested median filtering to eliminate impulsive noise. Adaptive median filters [26] can also be used for noise reduction, which replaces the pixel value with the local neighborhood’s weighted median. Still, it has some demerits; image smoothing also removes some sufficient detail in the image [27]. There is another possibility of wrong diagnosis due to blood vessels and tissues.

The contrast enhancement method increases the contrast of the image over a threshold [28] but it has some drawbacks, like it may cause some regions to be under-enhanced or over-enhanced, which may result in information loss [29].
