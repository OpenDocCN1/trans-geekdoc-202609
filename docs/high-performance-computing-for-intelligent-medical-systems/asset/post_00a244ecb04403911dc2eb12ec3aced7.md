## 8.9.3 Basic block-matching using particle swarm optimization

Kennedy and Eberhart [43] proposed PSO which is inspired by nature and optimizing in nature. This algorithm became popular due to its optimizing ability and speed of convergence. This PSO algorithm is also used in the field of video compression to optimize the ME process. This algorithm is easier to use in practice than GA as it doesn’t use mutation and cross-over to regenerate the offspring. Moreover, PSO is based on the nature of bird flocking and doesn’t have a gradient, and is self-adaptive. Du et al [44] had used PSO for the first time to optimize the ME which overcomes the problem of local optima.

A solution in a search window or solution space in PSO is denoted by a particle that searches through the solution space for the best match location by adjusting the velocity dynamically. The particle position in a solution space which represents the position of a particle with the highest fitness value becomes the best matching location and it gives the required MV. Using the experience of a flying swarm and its knowledge, a particle updates the velocity. In a D-dimensional space, particles are represented as volume less point. Xp(best) represents the personal best coordinates location having the highest fitness value, whereas Xg(best) denotes the best location within the population.

- $S_i = (S_{i_1}, S_{i_2}, ..., S_{i_d})$ denotes ‘i’th particle in PSO and
- $P_i = (P_{i_1}, P_{i_2}, ..., P_{i_d})$ represents the Xp(best) position of that particle.

Each particle updates its velocity vector ‘V’ and position vector ‘X’ according to equations (8.1) and (8.2).

```
$VId(t + 1) = w * Vid(t) + C1 \times rand1( . ) \times (Pid - Xid)
+ C2 \times rand2( . ) \times (Pgd - Xid)$ (8.1)
```

```
$Xid(t + 1) = Xid(t) + Vid(t + 1), 1 \leqslant i \leqslant N, 1 \leqslant d \leqslant D$ (8.2)
```

$N = \text{No. of Particles}, D = \text{Dimensionality}
V_i = (V_{i_1}, V_{i_2}, ..., V_{i_d}), V_{id} \in [-Vmax, Vmax]: 'i$th' particle’s velocity vector
$X_i = (X_{i1}, X_{i2}, ..., X_{i_d}), X_{id} \in [-Xmax, Xmax]: 'i$th' particle’s position vector,
w = inertia weight, C1 and C2 are constants known as acceleration constants and taken as 2.0; $r_1$ and $r_2$ are random numbers between [0,1], Pg and Pi are global best and local best. The steps of PSO based BMA [45-54] are briefed below.

### PSO-BM Algorithm

- 1. Eight particle locations are selected in a different direction and initialized with random velocities.
- 2. Calculate the fitness values, that is, SAD for each particle.
- 3. Personal best ($P_{best}$) and Global best ($G_{best}$) are updated using equation (8.3) by the PSO algorithm based on a particle’s fitness value.

  $$Y_i(t+1) = \begin{cases} Y_i(t) & \text{if } f(X_i(t+1)) \geqslant f(Y_i(t)) \\ X_i(t+1) & \text{if } f(X_i(t+1)) < f(Y_i(t)) \end{cases}$$ (8.3)

  $X_i$ is the position vector of the particle ’$i$’ $f(X_i(t+1))$ is the ’$i$’th particle’s fitness value at $X_i(t+1)$ position $Y_i(t)$ gives the current $P_{best}$ of the particle ’$i$’
- 4. Position vector and velocity vectors of the particle are updated using equations (8.1) and (8.2).
- 5. Until the stopping conditions are met, the above steps are followed.
- 6. The matching MB location and its MV will be the $G_{best}$ position.

## 8.9.4 Harmony search block matching algorithm

Geem *et al* [56] had suggested a new population-based optimization approach called Harmony Search (HS) which mimics the process of continuously polishing the pitches for getting better harmony by a musician. Fitness approximation model attached with HS to get the best MV to get better harmony in music. Figure 8.8 is the block level diagram of HS-BM. The time of execution is greatly saved in doing ME accurately because of the integration of the fitness calculation strategy for finding out the MV.

The solution is also known as ‘Harmony’ in HS and is represented by n-dimensional real vectors. Harmony Memory (HM) is the initial memory in HS where harmony vectors are stored which are generated randomly. A new Harmony candidate is produced or created by applying pitch modification strategy or randomized re-initialization. The newly generated Harmony candidate is matched with the poorest harmony and HM is updated. Until the limiting condition is not met, a better candidate harmony vector replaces the poorest harmony vector.

## 8.9.5 Cat swarm optimization (CSO-BMA)

Hadi *et al* [56] suggested a novel Nature-inspired SC technique for ME called Cat Swarm Optimization (CSO-BM). The CSO-BM is an evolutionary technique. Since the algorithm doesn’t follow any definite search pattern, the chances of getting the global minimum accurately will be more and with a better quality solution. The optimization technique mimics the cat’s behavior.

A cat’s approach can be seen in two phases. The cat is accustomed to observing its target very closely while pretending to be at rest, which can be referred to and framed as seeking model. The next phase of the cat’s approach is framed as a tracing model where the cat goes behind the target. A matching MB is denoted by a cat in the search area in reference or current frame. In each iteration the position of the cat is modified using CSO strategy till a position with minimum cost function, that is, best match is found.

**CSO Movement = Seeking model + tracing Model.**

The working procedure of CSO-BM is briefed below and shown in figure 8.9.

### Algorithm: Steps of CSO-BM algorithm

- **Step 1:** Nine virtual cat’s locations are taken as initial population, that is, 9 MB.
- **Step 2:** Cat starts searching for the best MB for each MB in M dimensional space in different directions with some initial velocities within the maximum limit. A few of the cats are in tracing mode and others are taken as seeking mode as per mixture ratio (MR) which shows the joining of seeking mode with tracing mode. MR = 20% (tracing mode) and MR = 80% (seeking mode).
- **Step 3:** Cats’ positions are used in fitness function, that is, BDM is computed for every cat. The position of the cat with the best solution (xbest) is stored in memory, which is the best solution calculated till now.
- **Step 4:** Based on flag value, cats make a move. Let’s say cat is observing, then the seeking model or the tracing mode process is used for cat.
- **Step 5:** Based on MR values of cats, they are selected, and tracing mode is applied to them and the remaining cats will be in seeking mode.
- **Step 6:** If the limiting condition is met, end the algorithm and the best cat position with the lowest BDM will be selected. Otherwise, steps 3 to 5 are repeated.
