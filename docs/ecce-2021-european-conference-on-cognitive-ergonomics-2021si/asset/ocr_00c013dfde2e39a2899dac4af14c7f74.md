# ABSTRACT

The concept of creativity is an important part of human society and the continuous evolution of artificial society has raised questions about creativity among machines. The aim of this study is to explore machine learning algorithms' ability to be creative. This paper presents a survey of music generated by IBM Watson that have been evaluated using expert consensus and control samples. The results show that one of the machine generated samples showed the same level of creativity as the human generated samples. Here, there are indications that machine learning algorithms can create music that is hard to distinguish from human created music and can be considered creative.

# INTRODUCTION

Creativity has been in existence for hundreds of years [15] and throughout history, the view on the concept of creativity has both changed and gained a lot. Mostly the modern view of creativity, the creation of novel works, were seen as impossible by both artists and machines [6]. Over time, this view idea gradually evolved into today where there is a consensus that creativity can exist on an individual level or, in some words, can be creative. It would seem reasonable to hypothesize that it is possible to construct an artificial mind with the same level of creativity as it would be desirable.

Artificial creativity is still highly controversial and some argue that such a thing does not exist. For example Boden [4] claims that artificial creativity cannot exist at works by machines because lack of 'relevance' and a capacity to refer to mental or cultural aspects that humans have occurred to observe. As such critics say creativity is 'human-centric' and further that creativity is a combining of multiple facets of which some are better for critics and vice versa. Despite this, the research of the topic is limited. The question of creativity will be subject to evaluation in this study.
