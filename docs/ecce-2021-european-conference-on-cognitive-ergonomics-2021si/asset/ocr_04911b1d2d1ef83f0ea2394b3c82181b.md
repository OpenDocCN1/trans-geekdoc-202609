# 3 METHOD

This study will utilize a method that is established within creativity research [3], and briefly explained in section 1.1. The method is built upon the idea that what is 'creative' is to extract the creativity observed in the design. Amable [3] uses this method which employs multiple experts assesses the creativity of the work of art. Expert judges, a crowd of studio artists with 5 years of experience were used. This study also aims to mimic the study by Amable [3]. The judges used in this study were currently employed as music teachers and those who should be considered experts within music and the creation of music.

# 3.1 Participants

For this study, 51 domain experts were used. As previously mentioned the experts were all, or had previously been, employed as music teachers of some form. A majority of the participants (71,7%) had been at their current position for at least 10 years. When asked about total experience working with music, 58.5% stated that they had more than 40 years of experience rating music. Every of the 51 participants had at least 10 years of experience working with music in some style.

# 3.2 Implementation

In order to increase quality, the study was performed to perform quantitative analysis using an online questionnaire. The questionnaire contained four different types of instrumental samples. Two samples were created by humans and two were created by Watson Beat. The judges were asked to rate the creativity of each of the four clips individually on a scale of 1-6 (not creative at all, 6=a very high level of creativity). No even scaled scale was used in order to avoid some bias [7]. In addition to grading the pieces of music, the samples were also provided with the ability to explain their rating in free text format. To prevent bias in the judges, no background on the creation of the samples were provided. Statistical analysis on the gathered data was performed using MATLAB. The entire study were carried during spring 2018.
