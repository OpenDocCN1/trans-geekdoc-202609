
## Priors

Prior $p(\theta)$ should capture our belief and domain knowledge as well as possible

## What is our domain knowledge for a general ML algorithm?

- For most ML algorithms, we know that the weights $\theta$ should be small
- This knowledge can be expressed with a Gaussian prior, e.g.

$p(\theta) = \mathcal{N}(\theta|0, \lambda^{-1}\mathbf{I})$

- Most common for weight vectors (linear regression, neural nets...)
- $\lambda$ is the precision of the prior

- However, many other priors are possible

## Example: Gaussian Distribution

Likelihood (sample): $p(x|\boldsymbol{\theta} = \{\mu, \sigma\}) = \mathcal{N}(x|\mu, \sigma) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} \exp \left\{ -\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} \right\}$

Likelihood (dataset): $p(\boldsymbol{X}|\mu, \sigma) = \prod_{i} p(x_i|\mu, \sigma) = \frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^2)^{N/2}} \exp \left\{ -\frac{\sum_{i}(x_i-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} \right\}$

Prior: $p(\mu) = \mathcal{N}(\mu|\mu_0, \sigma_0) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_0^2}} \exp \left\{ -\frac{(\mu-\mu_0)^2}{2\sigma_0^2} \right\}$

![](img/f01928df50903c99ce023dd3c527a33d_831_0.png)

**Compute posterior $p(\mu|\boldsymbol{X})$ for $\mu$ assuming $\sigma$ is known:**

$p(\mu|\boldsymbol{X}) = \frac{p(\boldsymbol{X}|\mu)p(\mu)}{p(\boldsymbol{X})} \propto p(\boldsymbol{X}|\mu)p(\mu)$

- Can be performed by „completing the square", see appendix

## Basics: Completing the square

**Posterior:** $p(\mu|\boldsymbol{X}) \propto p(\boldsymbol{X}|\mu)p(\mu) \propto \exp \left\{ -\frac{\sum_i(x_i-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} - \frac{(\mu-\mu_0)^2}{2\sigma_0^2} \right\}$

Bring exponent in canonical squared form, i.e.

## Completing the square

- $-\frac{1}{2}a\mu^2_{\text{squared term}} + \frac{b\mu}{\text{linear term}} + c \Rightarrow -\frac{1}{2\sigma_N^2}(\mu-\mu_N)^2 + d$

Then we know that $p(\mu|\boldsymbol{X}) = \mathcal{N}(\mu|\mu_N, \sigma_N^2)$ with:

- Mean: $\mu_N = a^{-1}b$
- Variance: $\sigma_N^2 = a^{-1}$

## Basics: Completing the square for Gaussian posterior

**Posterior:** $p(\mu|\mathbf{X}) \propto p(\mathbf{X}|\mu)p(\mu) \propto \exp \left\{ -\frac{\sum_i(x_i-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} - \frac{(\mu-\mu_0)^2}{2\sigma_0^2} \right\}$
$= \exp \left\{ -\frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{N}{\sigma^2} + \frac{1}{\sigma_0^2} \right)_a \mu^2 + \left( \frac{\sum_i x_i}{\sigma^2} + \frac{\mu_0}{\sigma_0^2} \right)_b \mu + \text{const} \right\}$

**Completing the square:** $p(\mu|\mathbf{X}) = \mathcal{N}(\mu|\mu_N, \sigma_N^2)$

- Mean: $\mu_N = a^{-1}b = \frac{\sigma_0^2}{N\sigma_0^2+\sigma^2} \sum_i x_i + \frac{\sigma^2}{N\sigma_0^2+\sigma^2}\mu_0$
$= \frac{N\sigma_0^2}{N\sigma_0^2+\sigma^2}\mu_{ML} + \frac{\sigma^2}{N\sigma_0^2+\sigma^2}\mu_0$

- Variance: $\sigma_N^2 = a^{-1} = \frac{\sigma^2\sigma_0^2}{N\sigma_0^2+\sigma^2}$

## Example: Posterior Distribution

The posterior is Gaussian with:

- Mean: $\mu_N = \frac{N\sigma_0^2}{N\sigma_0^2 + \sigma^2}\mu_{ML} + \frac{\sigma^2}{N\sigma_0^2 + \sigma^2}\mu_0$
- Variance: $\sigma_N^2 = \frac{\sigma^2\sigma_0^2}{N\sigma_0^2 + \sigma^2}$

![](img/f01928df50903c99ce023dd3c527a33d_834_0.png)

**Observations:**

- Variance decreases with more samples
- Will eventually reach 0
- Posterior mean interpolates between prior mean and sample average

## Example: Computing the predictive distribution

The predictive distribution is given by:

$p(x^*|\mathbf{X}) = \int p(x^*|\mu) p(\mu|\mathbf{X}) d\mu$
$\text{marginal likelihood} \quad \text{likelihood} \quad \text{posterior}$

$= \mathcal{N}(x^*|\mu, \sigma)\mathcal{N}(\mu|\mu_N, \sigma_N) \quad \text{Gaussian propagation (not shown)}$

$= \mathcal{N}(x^*|\mu_{x^*}, \sigma_{x^*}2)$

The predictive distribution is Gaussian with:

- Mean: $\mu_{x^*} = \mu_N$
- Variance: $\sigma_{x^*}2 = \sigma_N^2 + \sigma^2$

**Observations:**

- The predictive mean is the same as posterior mean
- However, the predictive variance also considers the mean

## Maximum-posteriori solution

“man’s” Bayesian Learning:

1. Find the parameter vector $\theta_{MAP}$ that maximizes the posterior
$\theta_{MAP} = \arg\max_{\theta} p(\theta|\mathcal{D}) = \arg\max_{\theta} p(\mathcal{D}|\theta)p(\theta)$
- Uncertainty in $\theta$ is ignored
- Optimization is done in log-domain
$\theta_{MAP} = \arg\max_{\theta} \log p(\mathcal{D}|\theta) + \log p(\theta)$
2. Use $\theta_{MAP}$ for prediction $p(x^*|\mathcal{D}) \approx p(x^*|\theta_{MAP})$
- We still use point estimate - no uncertainty

**Recap (Lecture 1):** The MAP solution is the same as the standard ML solution with L2 regularization (e.g. Ridge regression)

## Intermediate Wrap-up Bayesian Learning

- Treat parameter vector as random variable and estimate posterior
- Estimate probability of “being right” for $\theta$
- Posterior distribution is integrated out for prediction
- All possible parameter vectors are used for the prediction
- Weighted by probability of “being right"
- Posterior quantifies our uncertainty in the model
- Can also be used to quantify uncertainty in the prediction

## Bayesian Linear Regression

For Bayesian Linear Regression, the posterior and the prediction can be computed in closed form:

- For all other cases, we need approximations
- While linear models are limited, it is still insightful to look at the properties of this case

## Model:

- Likelihood (single sample): $p(y|x, w) = \mathcal{N}(y|w^T \phi(x), \sigma^2)$
- Likelihood (full dataset): $p(y|X, w) = \prod_{i} \mathcal{N}(y_i|w^T \phi(x_i), \sigma^2)$
- Write product of independent Gaussians as multivariate Gaussian
- Gaussian prior: $p(w) = \mathcal{N}(w|0, \lambda^{-1}I)$

![](img/f01928df50903c99ce023dd3c527a33d_840_0.png)

## Bayesian Linear Regression

**2 Steps:**

1. Compute posterior
$p(w|X,y) = \frac{p(y|X,w)p(w)}{p(y|X)} = \frac{p(y|X,w)p(w)}{\int p(y|X,w)p(w)dw}$

2. Compute predictive distribution: Integrate posterior out
$p(y^*|x^*,X,y) = \int p(y^*|w,x^*)p(w|X,y)dw$

## Basics: Gaussian Identities

- Eq (1): Joint from Marginal Conditional: $p(x),p(y|x) \to p(x,y)$
$\mathcal{N}(x|\mu_{x}, \Sigma_{x})\mathcal{N}(y|F\mu_{x}, \Sigma_{y}) = \mathcal{N}\left(\left[\begin{array}{c} x \\ y \end{array} \right] \middle| \left[\begin{array}{c} \mu_{x} \\ F\mu_{x} \end{array} \right], \left[\begin{array}{cc} \Sigma_{x} & \Sigma_{x}F^T \\ F\Sigma_{x} & \Sigma_{y} + F\Sigma_{x}F^T \end{array} \right]\right)$

- Eq (2): Marginal and Conditional Gaussian from Joint: $p(x,y) \to p(y|x), p(x|y)$
$\mathcal{N}\left(\left[\begin{array}{c} x \\ y \end{array} \right] \middle| \left[\begin{array}{c} \mu_{x} \\ \mu_{y} \end{array} \right], \left[\begin{array}{cc} \Sigma_{x} & C^T \\ C & C^T\Sigma_{x}C \end{array} \right]\right) = \mathcal{N}(x|\mu_{x}, \Sigma_{x})\mathcal{N}(y|\mu_{y} + C^T\Sigma_{x}^{-1}(x-\mu_{x}), \Sigma_{y}-C^T\Sigma_{x}^{-1}C)$

Can also be derived by “completing the square”...
