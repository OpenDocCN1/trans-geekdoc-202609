
## KL for Gaussian Distributions

We can also compute the KL for Gaussian densities in closed form:

```
$KL(\mathcal{N}_0|\mathcal{N}_1) = \frac{1}{2} \left\{ \text{tr}(\mathbf{\Sigma}_1^{-1} \mathbf{\Sigma}_0) + \log \frac{|\mathbf{\Sigma}_1|}{|\mathbf{\Sigma}_0|} + (\mathbf{\mu}_1 - \mathbf{\mu}_0)^T \mathbf{\Sigma}_1^{-1} (\mathbf{\mu}_1 - \mathbf{\mu}_0) - k \right\}$
```

![](img/f01928df50903c99ce023dd3c527a33d_588_0.png)

- **1st term**: compares "shape/rotation" of distributions
- **2nd term**: compares entropies of distribution
- **3rd term**: compares means of distributions
  - Measured in metric space defined by $\mathbf{\Sigma}_1^{-1}$
  - Higher dissimilarity if there is less variance of $\mathcal{N}_1$ in the update direction of the means

## Minimization of Reverse KL

This minimization is often called (I)nformation-projection of distribution $p(x^*)$

```
KL(q(x)||p^*(x)) = \int_{x} q(x) \log \frac{q(x)}{p^*(x)}dx = \int_{x} q(x) \log \frac{q(x)}{p^*(x)} + \log Z
```

![](img/f01928df50903c99ce023dd3c527a33d_589_0.png)

- No need to evaluate normalizer Z
- **Zero forcing**: If $p^*(x)$ is small, $q(x)$ needs to be small
  - This results in a **mode-seeking behaviour**

- **Non-convex optimization** even for simple distributions $q(x)$
  - Hard to optimize
  - Often has multiple local optima

- **Intuitively:**
  - Good generative model (for sampling)
  - But some areas of high probability might be ignored

## Minimization of the Forward KL

This minimization is often called (M)oment-projection of distribution $p^*(x)$

```
$KL(p^*(x)||q(x)) = \int_x p^*(x) \log \frac{p^*(x)}{q(x)} dx = -\int_x p^*(x) \log q(x) dx + const \quad p^*(x)\napprox -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{x_i\sim p^*(x)} \log q(x_i) + const$
```

![](img/f01928df50903c99ce023dd3c527a33d_590_0.png)

- **Probability forcing**: If $p^*(x)$ is high, $q(x)$ needs to be high
- As a result, it averages over the modes that cannot be represented!
- Requires samples from $p^*(x)$ (unsuitable for VI)
- It is the same as the maximum likelihood solution!

```
$argmax_q \LogLike(\mathcal{D}, q) = argmax_q \sum_{x_i\sim p^*(x)} \log q(x_i)\napprox argmin_q KL(p^*(x)||q(x))$
```

KL(p||q)

![](img/f01928df50903c99ce023dd3c527a33d_590_1.png)

## Interesting fact: So why is it called Moment Projection?

When using exponential family distributions as model,

$q_{\theta}(x) = \frac{\exp(\phi(x)^T\theta)}{Z}, \quad \text{with } Z = \int \exp(\phi(x)^T\theta)dx$

it is easy to show that a maximum likelihood fit matching the average feature vector $\bar{\phi}$ of the data

$$\frac{d}{d\theta}LogLik(\mathcal{D}, \theta) = \sum_{i} \frac{d}{d\theta}\log q_{\theta}(x_i) = \sum_{i} \frac{d}{d\theta}(\phi(x_i)^T\theta) - N\frac{d}{d\theta}\log\left(\int \exp(\phi(x)^T\theta)dx\right)$$

$= \sum_{i} \phi(x_i) - N \frac{\exp(\phi(x)^T\theta)}{Z} \phi(x)dx = N\bar{\phi} - N E_{q_{\theta}(x)}[\phi(x)]$$

**From the optimality condition on $\theta$ (derivative = 0) it follows:** $E_{q_{\theta}(x)}[\phi(x)] = \bar{\phi}$

- I.e the model q's expected features have to match the average observed features
- E.g., for Gaussians $\phi(x) = [x, x^2]^T$ and we match first and second moments

## Comparison of projections for VI

### Information Projection | Moment Projection

![](img/f01928df50903c99ce023dd3c527a33d_592_0.png) | ![](img/f01928df50903c99ce023dd3c527a33d_592_1.png)

$KL(q(x)||p^*(x)) = \int_{x} q(x) \log \frac{q(x)}{p^*(x)} dx$ | $KL(p^*(x)||q(x)) = \int_{x} p^*(x) \log \frac{p^*(x)}{q(x)} dx$

- No need to know normalizer of $p^*(x)$ | Easy to optimize
- Mode-seeking | Covers all areas with high probability
- Good generative model (all samples representative) | Needs samples from $p^*(x)$
- Hard to optimize even for simple $q$ | Generative model might be poor
- Might miss areas with high probability | $q$ might have high density where $p^*(x)$ is low

## The f-divergence

**Interesting fact:** Most divergences are special cases of f-divergence

```
$D_f(p(x)||q(x)) = \int f\left(\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\right) q(x)dx$
```

- with different choices for the function $f$
- This is also true for the forward and reverse KL!

## The f-divergence

| Divergence | Corresponding $f(t)$ | Discrete Form |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\chi^\alpha$-divergence, $\alpha \ge 1$ | $\frac{1}{2}|t-1|^\alpha$ | $\frac{1}{2}\sum_i |\frac{p_i-q_i}{q_i}|^\alpha$ |
| Total variation $(\alpha = 1)$ | $\frac{1}{2}|t-1|$ | $\frac{1}{2}\sum_i |p_i-q_i|$ |
| $\alpha$-divergence | $\begin{cases} \frac{t^\alpha-(1-\alpha)}{\alpha(\alpha-1)} & \text{if } \alpha \neq 0, \alpha \neq 1 \\ t \ln t + t-1, & \text{if } \alpha = 1 \\ -\ln t + t-1, & \text{if } \alpha = 0 \end{cases}$ | $\sum_i p_i \ln \frac{p_i}{q_i}$ |
| KL-divergence $(\alpha = 1)$ | $t \ln t$ | |
| reverse KL-divergence $(\alpha = 0)$ | $-\ln t$ | $\sum_i q_i \ln \frac{q_i}{p_i}$ |
| Jensen-Shannon divergence | $\frac{1}{2}(t \ln t + (t+1)\ln(\frac{t+1}{2}))$ | $\frac{1}{2}\sum_i (p_i \ln \frac{p_i}{(p_i+q_i)/2} + q_i \ln \frac{q_i}{(p_i+q_i)/2})$ |
| Jeffrey's divergence (KL + reverse KL) | $(t-1)\ln(t)$ | $\sum_i (p_i-q_i) \ln \frac{p_i}{q_i}$ |

- **Note:** The exact equations here do not really matter for us, but its good to know that the f-divergence exists
- General approaches exists to minimize the f-divergence, they are typically less effective than approaches tailored for a specific divergence [1]

> [1]f-GAN: Training Generative Neural Samplers using Variational Divergence Minimization, Sebastian Nowozinin, Bob Cesque, Ryota Tomioka, NeurIPS 2016

## Revisiting the Maximum Likelihood Loss

Total training loss for maximum likelihood

$KL(p^*(\underline{x})||q(\underline{x})) \approx -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{\underline{x}_i \sim p^*(\underline{x})} \log q(\underline{x})$

The convergence of the gradient descent depends on the divergence

- ... and on the distributions we use as model $q(\underline{x})$
- Ideally, must have a shape that results in a significant gradient in the right direction outside the optimum
  - To "guide" the algorithm to the right solution

## Desiderata for a good loss function

![](img/f01928df50903c99ce023dd3c527a33d_596_1.png)

- Must be smooth and not have poor local optima
- Low slopes far from the optimum == bad
  - Initial estimates far from the optimum will take forever to converge
- High slopes near the optimum == bad
  - Steep gradients

## Desiderata for a good loss function

- Functions that are shallow far from the optimum will result in very small steps during optimization
  - Slow convergence of gradient descent
- Functions that are steep near the optimum will result in large steps and overshoot during optimization
  - Gradient descent will not converge easily
- The best type of divergence is steep far from the optimum, but shallow at the optimum
  - But not too shallow: ideally quadratic in nature

## Maximum likelihood for different distributions

Desired output: $d$

Desired output: $[0,0, ..., 1, ..., 0]$

Gaussian (constant variance)
L2 $Div = \frac{1}{2}(y-d)^2$

Gaussian (constant variance)
KL $Div = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i} (y_i-d_i)^2$

Bernoulli Distribution (classification)
KL $Div = -d\log(y) - (1-d)\log(1-y)$

Multinomial Distribution (classification)
KL $Div = \sum_{i} d_i\log(d_i) - \sum_{i} d_i\log(y_i)$

- From our discussion we know that L2 and KL are both instantiations of the forward KL (maximum likelihood) with different underlying distributions to model q
