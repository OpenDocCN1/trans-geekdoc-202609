
## Denoising Loss Function

$L_t = D_{KL}(q(x_{t-1} | x_t, x_0) || p_{\theta}(x_{t-1} | x_t)) + C$

Since both inputs of the divergence are gaussians, this further simplifies to:

$L_t = \mathbb{E}_q \left[ \frac{1}{2\Sigma_t} ||\hat{\mu}_t - \mu_{\theta}(x_t, t)||^2 \right] + C$

## Denoising cost function

This lets us rewrite the true mean of the denoising process as:

$$\mu_t = \frac{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t(1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1})}x_t + \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}(1-\alpha_t)x_0}{1-\bar{\alpha}_t} \Rightarrow \mu_t = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}} \left( x_t - \frac{(1-\alpha_t)}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}} \epsilon \right)$$

> Rewrite in terms of noise
$$x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\epsilon \n\rightarrow x_0 = \frac{x_t - \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\epsilon}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}}$$

We can also write the predicted mean as:

$$\mu_{\theta}(x_t, t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}} \left( x_t - \frac{(1-\alpha_t)}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}} \epsilon_{\theta}(x_t, t) \right)$$

This lets us reformulate the loss to present a noise prediction problem:

$L_{t-1} = \mathbb{E}_{x_0, \epsilon} \left[ \frac{(1-\alpha_t)^2}{2\Sigma\alpha_t(1-\bar{\alpha}_t)} \right] ||\epsilon - \epsilon_{\theta}(x_t, t)||^2 + \mathcal{C}$

## Diffusion Models – Training and Inference

Algorithm 1 Training
1: repeat
2: $x_0 \sim q(x_0)$
3: $t \sim \text{Uniform}(\{1, \dots, T\})$
4: $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$
5: Take gradient descent step on
6: until converged

Algorithm 2 Sampling
1: $x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$
2: for $t = T, \dots, 1$ do
3: $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ if $t > 1$, else $z = 0$
4: $x_{t-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}(x_t - \frac{1-\alpha_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}}\epsilon_{\theta}(x_t, t)) + \sigma_t z$
5: end for
6: return $x_0$

## Diffusion Summary

Diffusion models are Hierarchical VAEs with extra restrictions

- The loss is the vanilla VAE ELBO loss with an added denoising term
- The encoder has 0 parameters
- The true denoising posterior can be exactly calculated
- The ELBO is decomposed into each time step: fast to train
- Can be made extremely deep (up to 1k diffusion steps)
- The problem can be reformulated as a noise prediction problem
- There's a lot of math underlying a simple intuition

A lot more research happened the last 2-3 years:

- Score-based diffusion models – models learn the gradient of the log-density
- Use of continuous differential equation (SDE)
