The effective cost of the comparators used in the bit-stream generators is also insignificant considering the fact that each generated bit-stream is being used by a large number of convolution units. For the fixed-point binary design, we report the synthesis results for two cases of non-pipelined and fully pipelined implementations. By replacing costly 8-bit fixed-point multipliers with simple AND gates, more than 23× (37×) hardware area saving is achieved from the non-pipined (fully pipelined) fixed-point design to the proposed hybrid design for implementation of 5×5 convolution engine. The critical path latency and power consumption are also significantly decreased with the proposed design. As reported in Table 4.4, for the entire convolutional layer (including the overhead cost of bit-stream generators), the hardware area cost is around 19× (30×).

The important metric to evaluate the proposed design is still energy consumption. Energy is evaluated as the product of time and power consumption. The output of the convolution unit in the non-pipelined fixed-point design is ready in one clock cycle. This results in a total energy consumption of 24.2 pJ for the convolution unit (Table 4.3) and 14.5 nJ for the entire convolutional layer (Table 4.4). The fully pipelined design carries but significantly higher area and power and consumes 16 nJ per cycle for the entire convolutional layer.

The energy consumption of the proposed hybrid design depends on the number of cycles generating and producing bit-streams. Multiplying the number of cycles by the energy per cycle from Table 4.4 gives an energy consumption of 4.1 nJ when processing eight cycles (8 × 0.52 nJ). The proposed design achieves more than 70% energy saving compared to the non-pipelined fixed-point design while preserving the quality of the results. Although pipelined design increases the maximum working frequency (reduces the critical path latency) and the throughput, it costs higher hardware area and power consumption, longer total end-to-end latency, and higher energy consumption for processing each input data.

With the conventional random stream-based design, a significantly longer processing time (e.g., 64–256 cycles) is required to achieve the same classification rate as the fixed-point design. This longer processing time makes the prior bit-stream designs energy inefficient compared to the fixed-point and to the proposed hybrid design.
