VGG has $20×$ more computation than AlexNet, whereas both have similar amount of FCL computations. Effect of this effect is also visible in FICaffe's efficiency for VGG, but unlike [3], we achieve high efficiency without synthesizing RTL IPs.

Authors in [4] propose to reduce bandwidth requirement for both CL and FCLs. These improvements can be attributed to use of Winograd Transformations for convolution, batching in FCLs and the assumption that all IFMs/OFMs for a layer can be transferred to on-chip machines before processing. Their approach is not automated nor scalable like FICaffe, as they manually fix intra-filter-parallelism and perform Winograd for only $3 \times 3$ filters. Also Gflop/s reported in [4] is not IEEE 754 compliant.

Like FICaffe, authors in [6] also consider integration with Caffe but only report on AlexNet and VGG. Their main contribution is systematic analysis of input-major and weight-major choices for FCLs and is based on empirical modification to utilize model. Unfortunately, their PE efficiency is poor with batching and has very high latency/image. They leave a large gap between achievable performance as they use too intra-filter parallelism by unrolling filter size to $5 \times 5$. Finally, while the work presented in [5] does smaller filters of $1 \times 1$, do not report actual number of PEs and to the best of our knowledge do not achieve PE efficiency either. Notice that choice of FPGA impacts the final latency. A larger FPGA with a plethora of resource always yields a better performance. To compare different architectures, one may normalize the achieved latency by a available resource.
