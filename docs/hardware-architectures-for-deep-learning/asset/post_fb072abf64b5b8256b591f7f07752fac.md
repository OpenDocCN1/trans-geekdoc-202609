### 11.4.4 Impact of unideal condition

#### 11.4.1 Process variation

For most of the applications, the output accuracy (e.g., MSE or CA) strongly depends on how accurately the resistive state can be set during the actual fabrication since there is considerable variability in this process [43]. Thus, to show the effect of this uncertainty on the accuracy of the neuromorphic function, we added random noises to the conductance of all the memristors in the network and measured the network error in the presence of these changes. The> $Conductance_{Noisy} = (1 + \sigma X) Conductance_{Normal}$ (11.38)

where $X$ is a random number that has Gaussian distribution with standard deviation of $\sigma$ and mean of 0 ($X \sim N[0,1]$). For this study, the considered memristor variations (i.e., $\sigma$) were 5%, 10%, and 20% of the nominal values of the conductance and 500 Monte Carlo samples were simulated. The values of the CA and MSE of the network outputs in presence of memristor variation are illustrated in Figure 11.14. As the results show, with 20% memristor variation, the average MSE is still below 0.009 for all of the approximate computing applications. Also, the maximum CA degradation due to the considered variations for MNIST and MHealth applications are about 2%.

#### 11.4.4.2 Input noise

Tolerance to electrical noise is one of the important characteristics of analog circuits. To assess the effect of the electrical noise on the analog parts of the circuit, we injected a random noise with $\sigma = 30$ mV at the analog inputs of the circuit (outputs of the DAC stage) and measured the accuracy of the circuit under this condition. The results, which are shown in Figure 11.14, reveal that even in the presence of the injected noise the accuracy of the circuit remains higher than 93%. Also, the MSE remains below 0.008 for all of the applications.

#### 11.4.4.3 Limited write precision

After the weights of the ANN (conductance of memristors in ANN) are determined by the training algorithm, they should be uploaded to the memristive crossbar circuit. The write has a limited precision which may reduce the accuracy of the network. In order to examine the effect of limited write precision of memristors, we quantized the conductance of memristors with 4-bit precision. As Figure 11.14 demonstrates, the limited write precision of memristors has a very small effect on the accuracy of the circuit.

#### 11.4.4. Combining ideal conditions

Finally, we combined all of the previously mentioned effects (process variation, limited write precision, and input noise), and measured the accuracy of the circuit. Figure 11.14 shows that with considering all of these effects, the accuracy of the circuit is still above 92%. Note, as mentioned earlier, the error of writing on memristors by employing the proposed interface [43] is below 1%. Hence, the impact of memristor variability on the output accuracy is negligible. Also, there are some algorithms (e.g., [54, 55]) that could be used to decrease the effect of process variation not only in memristors but also in CMOS inverters. For example, in [55], the neurons (CMOS inverters) characteristics were extracted from the manufactured chip, and then the network weights of chip were adjusted based on the extracted information. Finally, to compensate the resistance shifting issue of memristors, which is caused by their constant bias [56], the low-cost inline calibration scheme [57] may be implemented in direction.

### 11.5 Conclusion

In this chapter, we presented an ultra-low-power memristive neuromorphic circuit that may be deployed in IoT. We also proposed mixed-signal interfaces which could be efficiently integrated into the proposed neuromorphic circuit. The interfaces consisting of memristive DAC and ADC reduced the area and power consumption. The effectiveness of the proposed neuromorphic circuit was assessed under datasets from wearable sensors, air quality sensors, and several recognition and regression applications. The circuit level HSPICE simulations for the considered datasets showed that the proposed neuromorphic circuit with memristive crossbar and inverter-based neurons was on average 3 × 10³ power efficient than its ASIC implementation in 90-nm technology. We also investigated the effect of memristor process variation and limited write precision on the accuracy. The results revealed that the limited write had a small effect on the CA. Also, the classification accuracy of the circuit with 20% memristor variation remained above 93%. In addition, the results of HSPICE simulations of proposed structures for ADC and DAC were 267x and 278x smaller than those of the previously design method.
