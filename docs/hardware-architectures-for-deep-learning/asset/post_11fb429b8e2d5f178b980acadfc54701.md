### 3.9.2 Performance under realistic workloads

To further evaluate the proposed design, we compared the performance offered by ClosNN with that of H-NoC and Cmesh under some realistic workloads. Table 3.1 shows the structure of NNs selected as benchmarks. All benchmarks have been implemented and evaluated on 128-node example of the three considered networks; multiple neurons are mapped onto a similar PE. In the latter case, the broadcast approach is replaced with a multicast scheme depicted in Figure 3.3. To get a better insight into the performance of the NoCs, the results are normalized to Cmesh. Throughput is defined as the maximum input rate (per cycle) that the network can accept (broadcast the data) before reaching the saturation point. The network saturation point is usually considered the injection rate at which the latency is three times longer than the zero-load.

**Figure 3.11** Throughput under practical applications on 128-node networks. The outcomes are normalized with Cmesh

In these experiments, we set the saturation to 50 cycles. As demonstrated in Figure 3.11, ClosNN delivers a higher throughput than H-NoC and Cmesh. The main reason behind this performance is that ClosNN offers a shorter average hop count and better support for collective communication, so it yields higher throughput compared to other two architectures. ClosNN can deliver up to four packets to all PEs per cycle, while H-NoC can deliver only a single packet to all PEs per cycle. Moreover, ClosNN's diameter is three hops, while H-NoC's diameter is five hops. Due to these topological advantages, ClosNN offers up to 2.4× higher throughput (average 1.93×) than H-NoC.

### 3.9.3 Power comparison

Figure 3.12 shows the power consumption of the three considered NoCs, when they work at the maximum throughput. Different benchmarks inject the same traffic rate to the networks at maximum throughput. As expected, because of shorter hop count, ClosNN consumes less power than the other two networks.

### 3.9.4 Sensitivity to neural network size

As described in Section 3.8, ClosNN can be scaled up to execute larger NNs. To show this capability, we compare 512-node and 2,048-node ClosNNs with the other NoCs. The 2,048-node ClosNN uses a four-layer topology in which four 512-node networks are connected by a four spine layer. Table 3.2 reports the throughput (as described in Section 3.9.2) offered by the networks under the three benchmarks of the previous section that have more than 512 neurons. The performance numbers are again normalized to Cmesh.

As demonstrated in Table 3.2, the shorter diameter and higher bisection of ClosNN still enables it to outperform the competitor design when the size grows. Cmesh still offers the lower throughput, when the size grows, the average hop increases proportionally. More detailed deat can be found in [13].

### 3.10 Conclusion

In this work, we demonstrated the predominant hierarchical network-onchip for NNs. We first showed ClosNN, a novel topology for NNs. In our design, ClosNN can be scaled up to 128-cores in a modular fashion by four spine layer. The experimental results showed that ClosNN can offer higher throughput and scalability.
