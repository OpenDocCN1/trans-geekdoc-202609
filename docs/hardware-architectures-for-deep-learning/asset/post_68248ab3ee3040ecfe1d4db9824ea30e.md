## 9.11.3 Run-time and overall accuracy

### 9.11.3.1 Pruning and/or thresholding

Adopting the pruning or TP creates a trade-off between average delay of classification, the accuracy of the classification. The pruning policy reduces the parameter, and the TP reduces the average FLOP count. However, based on the results (Figures 9.21 and 9.22), increased resources does not always translate into higher accuracy. The hybrid policies, which combine both pruning and thresholding, exhibit the same behavior, which higher number of parameters and FLOPs does not always contribute to higher classification accuracy. Consequently, finding the optimal strategy in the target accuracy is reached with minimal resources (in this case execution time), requires thorough exploration and tuning of the thresholding and pruning parameters.

Figure 9.26 captures the design space of the ICNN when trading the accuracy to reduce run-time through 5k combination of thresholding and pruning policies.

### 9.11.3.2 Deadline-driven

Figure 9.27 shows the overall accuracy of DD policy for iterative AlexNet with a batch size of 1 for 50k validation images on K80 GPU for various time-budgets the same iterations for any given deadline. As opposed to the pruning and/or TPs, all the images go through the function of time-budget. It should be noted that higher time-budgets only increase the accuracy when it is sufficient for execution of the next iteration, which results in the step-like behavior observed in the figure.

Unlike the thresholding and pruning policies, the DD policy only executes the FC layer of one iteration, more specifically the last iteration given the time-budget. However, the pruning and/or TPs require the FC layers of all the iterations to be completed to draw clear conclusion about the early termination and pruning of class labels.

## 9.2 Conclusions

In this chapter, an iterative architecture for processing CNN (ICNN) was introduced. As a case study, ICNN was used to break the large AlexNet into a sequence of smaller networks (uCNN), each processing a subsample of the input, providing the ability to terminate the classification early (when reaching a deadline in real-time system or reaching a required accuracy in low-power system), or carry the classification to the next iteration (if deadline in real-time system is not reached, or classification accuracy is not satisfied in low-power system). ICNN enables us to explore a wide range of complexity versus trade-offs. To explore these trade-offs, a dynamic deadline-driven policy and two hybrid pruning and TPs were introduced. Our simulation results show that with intelligent selection of pruning and TPs, ICNN reduces the average FLOP and parameter counts over 50K validation images by more than 25%, 80%, and 38%, with negligible accuracy loss. The reductions in counts are directly translated to energy saving. Moreover, real-time systems could exploit the dynamic structure of ICNN by reducing the execution time up to 2× by trading off accuracy.
