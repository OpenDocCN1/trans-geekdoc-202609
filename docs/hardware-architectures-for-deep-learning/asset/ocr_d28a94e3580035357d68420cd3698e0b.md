We use an inverter as the activation function for the neurons of the ADC network. This is due to its lowest power consumption and smallest delay among conventional implementations of the activation functions (including comparing or op-amp). It switches the voltage on its input equals to $(V_{dd} + V_{ss})/2$ where $V_{dd}$ and $V_{ss}$ are the positive and negative voltages, respectively. Note that $V_{ss}$ equals $-V_{dd}$ in the differential mode. Therefore, the biases and their corresponding weights can be removed since inverter imposed a bias to its switch point $(V_{dd} + V_{ss})/2$. Also, since the input voltage range of the LTNN in [46] is $[0, 2^i - 1]$ while in the case of our neuromorphic circuit, the range of the input voltage (denoted by x) of the ADC is $[0, V_{dd}]$ (single-ended) or $[-V_{dd}/2, V_{dd}/2]$ (differential), the input weights must be multiplied by $2^n/V_{dd}$ where $n$ is the resolution of the ADC.
