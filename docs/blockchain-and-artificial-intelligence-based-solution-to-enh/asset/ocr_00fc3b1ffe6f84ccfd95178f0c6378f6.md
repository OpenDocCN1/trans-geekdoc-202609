# 7.4.2 HOW IS DISINFORMATION SPREADING SO FAST AND WILDLY ON SOCIAL MEDIA?

Disinformation is hard to perceive for humans. In general, people tend to like, share, and react to this content because they think it is true. They share with their friends and all cluster that it's easier to influence. Furthermore, research indicates that repeating the same fake news increases the probability that humans will believe it (Grimes, 1978).

Cyber actors can create a false impression that millions of people share using automated bots and techniques, like bots and trolls. The disinformation will spread if the users have a lot of followers, such as celebrities or politicians. This author mentioned "one of the most prolific fake accounts during the 2016 election was the official Twitter account of the Tennessee Republican Party @TEN_GOP of" (Cunningham, 2020).

Like humans and bots, algorithms help spread disinformation. Indeed, algorithms the users' engagement (like, tweet, react users). For example, as reported in Sinan’s book, Elon Musk has more than 40 million followers and by adding #bitcoin to tweet and posting about dogecoin, he has increased cryptocurrency market events (Sinan, 2021).

That’s how algorithms can move and spread influence rapidly. And without knowing what’s happening. It’s out of control! Fake news start, different ways to spread and we don’t know when or where it will end or not. It’s a huge spaghetti that takes several servers and thus consumed a lot of energy unnecessarily.

# 7.5 TECHNIQUES OF CYBER INFLUENCE

In this section, we will discuss the set of techniques used to influence people on social media. The most techniques used by hostile actors are, by order of importance:

+   1. Trolls and bots;
2. Fake accounts;
3. Hijacking social accounts/hashtags;
4. Deepfakes.

According to this study made during 2013–2018 (Martin & Shapiro, 2019), trolling techniques are the most used 90% of the time. 50% of the influence is made with an automated message.
