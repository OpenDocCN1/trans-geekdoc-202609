# 5.5.2 ReinFORCE TRANSPARENCY

AIDA lays down rules of transparency regarding the use of AI systems. As shown with online profiling, such a requirement is quite necessary, and allow for the quantity of personal information collected and the nature of what automated processes are applied. That information is fundamental to understand the eventual risks of automation regarding errors or bias. This information also allows for the full exercise of subsequent rights. For instance, the right to give a personal explanation of a decision made, and the right to challenge, cannot be made possible without information regarding the AI system and its function. Likewise, a human-controlled environment implies that a person has knowledge of the AI system to understand it and automation to be capable of evaluating it.

To guarantee transparency, Section 10 of AIDA requires keeping of general terms, the measures taken to abide by legal requirements, as well the measures taken to attenuate the risks and to ensure its respect. It is also expected that a person who carries out activity pertaining to a “high-impact” system must publish its description on a publicly accessible website, using general terms, regarding its intended use, the content it will generate, the predictions, recommendations, or decisions it takes, along with the risk attention measures. Future regulation should also impose an obligation to provide further information. Grantcribing the intended use and the desired results is necessary but not sufficient to achieve a level of transparency that would guarantee an explanation, as well as a right to challenge and recover to individuals.

In contrast, the European Commission’s proposal for regulation on AI foresees measures that should be considered when drafting the upcoming regulations for AIDA. Section 13 of the Proposal for regulation on AI pertains to transparency and the obligation to provide information to users in regard to the “characteristics, capabilities and limitations of the high-risk AI system.¹³ More specifically, the level of accuracy must be specified along with the level of robustness and cybersecurity “against which the high-risk AI system has been tested and validated.¹⁴ Furthermore, Section 13 b (iv) also requires for the “performance as regards persons or groups of persons on which the system is intended to be used.¹⁵ Elsewhere, Section 14 of the Proposal for regulation on AI requires human oversight the design and development of a high-risk AI system to allow effective control during the time the system is deployed.
