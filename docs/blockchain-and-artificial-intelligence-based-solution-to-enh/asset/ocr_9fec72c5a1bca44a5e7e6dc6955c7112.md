Personal information should be personal, and that's why IoT is dangerous. It's because you and I are surrendering privacy, bit by bit, without realizing it. Companies know about more than we know ourselves. Buying or using Internet-connected devices is becoming more and more of an obligation. If we don't buy, the necessary services require their usage, which could compromise our privacy. Most organizations are trying to cope with these issues by creating their policies. But most people do not read privacy policies for each device they buy or every app they download. Most of it would be written in a legal language incomprehensible to the average consumer, even if they do. They also come with incomprehensible terms of use and clauses that oblige the user to withdraw any right to pursue the company in court if they use their data. For example, in 2013, an Electronic Frontier Foundation named Parker Higgins tweeted a phrase he found in the Samsung TV privacy policy. It reads as follows: “Please be aware that if spoken words include personal or sensitive information, that information will be captured and transmitted to a third party through your Voice Recognition "Despite this, you can never be held Samsung accountable Please note that when you watch a video or access applications content provided by a third-party, that provider may collect or receive information about your Smart TV (its IP address and device identifiers), the requested transaction (you to buy or rent the video), and your use of the application or service. Samsung is not responsible for these providers' privacy or security practices. You should exercise caution and review the privacy statements applicable to the third-party websites and services you use." We can only imagine that all of this results in consumer privacy being violated.

Again, IoT is a game-changer in society, and we arrived at a point of no return, meaning that those devices are not going anywhere, and we need to admit that we cannot separate ourselves from them. But also, privacy is equally important, and we should not give up on it quickly. Increased corporate transparency is more than ever, and it's the key to increasing privacy in the IoT. To reach this transparency, people should care more about their privacy and demand more and details of the usage of data and meaningful consent before collecting them that industries start by self-regulation and first government take further actions and severe punishments. Because this is such a complex problem and a fundamental human right is a stack, everyone be involved. Starting with the consumer, who should demand the right to control his data as he sees fit. Because industries will have no choice but to respond because confidence and trust enable their business to keep growing, and it will affect the consumers' desire to purchase connected products, reducing trade and the fulfillment of IoT potential. And most cases, companies need to respond to those demands, for example, after receiving a survey showing the complaints of newly connected cars about their data privacy. The Alliance of Automobile Manufacturers applied and developed new privacy policies to use the collected data.
