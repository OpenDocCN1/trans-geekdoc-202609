# 1.6.1.2 Blockchain Layer

The blockchain layer is the module responsible for the citizen's information security and for the verification of his identity, that is, the user who interacted with the application layer to request public services. This layer is the second module in our architecture placed behind the application layer which is hidden from the citizen. Once citizen interacts with the application layer to ask for a service, his request will be converted into a block that will be incarnated in the blockchain module and then shared between all nodes. This ensures the verification and then the validation of the citizen's digital identity once it is authenticated to public government service.

Several researchers have proposed approaches that blockchain technology to address security and privacy issues in digital identities. In fact, blockchain is an immutable ledger, to record transactions that are occurring within a network. All participants maintain a copy of the ledger and reach a consensus on the states of transactions submitted to the blockchain network [39]. The benefits of blockchain-based techniques in digital identities management systems are various, and we can cite the benefit of removing the single point of failures and preventing the third parties from controlling data. Thus, participants can verify the data integrity and identity sender.

We propose to use public blockchain as one of the characteristics of a public blockchain being an open-access platform [40]. In other words, anyone can read and record transactions. Such aspects prevent blockchain from being hacked because expensive to add a new block and tamper the blockchain content [41].
