## 5.3.5 NATURE OF PERSONAL DATA COLLECTED

Students are often asked to give information when engaging with education software. In addition to identification information such as surname, name, and student number, directly given by students themselves, other information is taken or can be deducted from the tech companies. For instance, Respondus Monitor⁷⁶ notes that they treat data from the exam session such as the date, the time when a student started and finished their exam; the time spent on each question; and when an answer has been modified; the quality of the student's internet connection during the exam, which includes the students connection drop; the activity of the student's mouse, their keyboard, and screen; the quality of the video recording which consists of the lighting, the contrast, and the movements; and the audio recording's quality. This behaviour data constitutes personal information. Furthermore, there are times when online photocopying software utilizing AI collects biometric data, which is considered sensitive data, to identify a student's identity. This type of use would indicate the presence of a database of students' pictures in order to match with a live person recorded by a webcam. Lastly, the list of personal information that is subject to collection is clearly enumerated, which is usually to one's right to information. Additionally, when reading the contractual terms, the minimization principle, the necessary principle, and the proportionality principle do not seem to be respected. Therefore, there is a risk of hyper-surveillance for students.
