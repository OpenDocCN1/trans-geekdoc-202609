# 3.1 INTRODUCTION

One of the objectives of smart spaces and systems is improving society and human life quality, in terms of comfort, efficiency, or productivity. The Internet of Things (IoT) has developed into a backbone innovation for smart environments providing the mentioned features. In a market research, the evaluation that IoT systems could reach a potential economic impact of $11 trillion by the year 2025 compared to the actual $4 trillion, which represents approximately 11% of the world economy Institute [33]. IoT systems are becoming one of the most developed fields in computer science [74], and it's a significant contributor to the economy. Real-time data processing and analysis greatly benefit multiple fields and infrastructures, especially for healthcare systems, antivirus and autonomous vehicle technology, smart cities, etc. Privacy and security are critical elements in any real-world environment established by an IoT system. All of these made a target of cyberattacks due to their security leak compared conventional computing. The report has shown an increase in attacks targeting IoT devices by 600% in the year 2017 Semantic [65]. IoT devices, despite their importance, major security updates, have insecure features, and insufficient data protection. For example, the Mirai Botnet, first introduced in 2016, was able to reach almost 400,000 devices simultaneously, leading to a total of $100 million in damage and shutting down significant platforms such as Netflix, GitHub, and Reddit for multiple months [48]. IoT devices don't have the enough computational or hardware resources to use complex security features such as computers or smartphones [2]. Those systems can use a security layer in their implementation that provide the required security against cyberattacks, such as firewalls, antivirus software, etc. Hence, it is crucial to focus on securing these systems.
