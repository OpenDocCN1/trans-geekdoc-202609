Olson Italis is a PhD candidate in Computer Engineering, in a bi-diploma program at the University of Science, Technology and Advanced Studies of Haiti (ISTEH) and at Polytechnique Montréal. Retached to the Networking and Mobile Computing Research Laboratory (LARIM), his field of research includes computer science, electronic commerce, and Blockchain. He is a lecturer at ISTEAH since 2020 and a lecturer at Polytechnique since 2022. He is also actively involved in the student associations of ISTEAH (AEE-ISTEH) and Polytechnique (AECSP). Wanting to get back what he has obtained, he participated in peer “mentoring” to help new students in his career. Mr. Italis graduated from the Faculty of Sciences of the State University of Haiti (UEH) with a degree in Electronic Engineering and then went on earn a Master’s degree in Computer Engineering and Information Technology from ISTEAH. Meanwhile, he worked at the Bank of Republic of Haiti as a programmer analyst from 2008 to 2019. He was a teaching assistant in mathematics at the preputedic studies of the FDS (2006–2009), in charge of the Introduction to Computer Science at the Centre Technique de Planning et d’Économie Appliquée (CTPEA), from 2009 to 2017.

Meng Kang is a research engineer at Lifegu Digital Health. He received a Master of Applied Science degree from the University of British Columbia. Currently, he is working on a blockchain-enabled secure and privacy-preserving solution and machine learning for IoT wearables in digital health. Previously, he conducted research on decentralized identity-based blockchain for the prevention of unauthorized data usage under the supervision of Dr. Victoria Lemieux.

Rachel Ladouceur, CPA, CIA, is a PhD student in Information Science and Technology at the Université du Québec at Chicoutimi (UQAC), Canada. She also holds a Master’s degree in management from HEC Montréal and a Master’s degree in information technology from ETS. She worked for 13 years at the Government of Québec in information security and 7 years at the Auditor General of Québec as an expert in computer auditing. She is also interested in the detection of disinformation on social networks and artificial intelligence, specifically NLP.

Victoria Lemieux is a professor of Archival Science at the University of British Columbia (UBC) School of Information. She is also founder of Blockchain@UBC, the University of British Columbia’s Blockchain research and education cluster, execution director of UBC’s blockchain graduate pathway, Sa School of Business Distinguished Scholar, and a faculty associate of the Institute for Computing, Information and Cognitive Systems at UBC. Her latest publications include Building Decentralized Trust: Multidisciplinary Perspectives on the Design of Blockchains and Distributed Ledgers (Springer, 2021) and Searching for Trust: Blockchain in an Age of Disinformation (Cambridge University Press, 2022).
