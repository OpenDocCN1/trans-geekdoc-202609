# 1.5 CURRENT EXPERIENCES THROUGH THE WORLD

Digital identity gives the identity to citizens so that they can get their daily tasks done with more ease, security, and effectiveness from anywhere. These tasks could be buying a bus pass, checking the status of a building under construction, receiving funds, and getting registered to any consumer service, getting a passport or a driving license. All these activities could be performed from one place or using a single identity which we call the digital identity. According to report by Lynch [33], more than one billion people in developing countries around the world have no proof of their identity. This unique citizen identity would bring interesting changes in every process of daily life and provides identity [34]. A major example is the Estonian advanced model of digital identity that enables government sector to identify its citizens with their unique digital identity to facilitate with them basic needs. They have the facility of online voting, access to their healthcare records online, and can access all the government processes online like marriage, divorce, real-estate, and any registration or record checking. Other than the developed countries such as Canada, the United States, the United Kingdom, and other European countries, a number of developing countries including Peru, Chile, Pakistan, India, Thailand, Indonesia, and the Philippines, have also trying to adopt digital identity [35].

## 1.6.1 ARCHITECTURE OF THE PROPOSED SOLUTION

We propose in Figure 1.1 the architecture of a secure digital identity application. We define three main necessary layers. Our goal is (1) to ensure citizen data privacy and get it protected with a secure blockchain network, (2) to give to the citizen control of his identity and data, and (3) to allow a quick recovery of his identity.

This verification requests the appropriation of the citizen himself who has to authorize the verification of his identity through another service.
