Traditional computing processes rely on a centralized data center and the amount of data every second counts. Latency issues, bandwidth limitations, and unpredictable network disruptions can be considered since they could provide ambiguous scenarios. An efficient way to respond to those challenges is edge computing architecture [28]. Edge computing moves some portion of the storage and computes resources out of the central data center and closer to the data source itself, as demonstrated in Figure 3.4. Rather than transmitting raw data to a central data center for processing and analysis, that is performed where the data is generated. This well suited for IoT systems. It enables the data collected by sensors to be gathered and processed at the device location rather than sending it to a data center or cloud. For IoT devices to consume compute power is increasingly valuable for real-time analysis. Moreover, having access to close computational power can reduce a large number of issues, such as the latency of communication between IoT devices and the central IT networks those are connected to, the slower response time when sending data back-and-forth the device and the cloud, network bandwidth issues when required to send large amounts of data over slow cellular or satellite connections [10].

It enhances decision-making by deploying analytics algorithms and machine learning models locally.
