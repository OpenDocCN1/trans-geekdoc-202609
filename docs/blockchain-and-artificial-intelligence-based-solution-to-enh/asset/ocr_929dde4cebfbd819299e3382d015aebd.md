# 5.3.2 PROVINCIAL LAWS REGARDING PERSONAL INFORMATION IN THE PUBLIC SECTOR

The use of proctoring software implies that some personal information is being collected in an academic setting. Those data are collected and used by academic institutions the collection is justified by their provincial educational mission, and they are located in different provinces; therefore, provincial laws are applicable. Academic institutions are public entities and the provincial legal framework pertaining to personal information is applicable. The control factor of the Privacy Act²²⁵ clarifies this view of jurisdiction and the consequence of the provincial framework. It applies to personal information and documents collected by public institutions such as universities. Therefore, the provincial public sector framework applies to student and university relationships.

However, when speaking of the control factor, control does not need to be absolute, direct, or permanent. In this regard, in Canada Post Corp. v. Canada (Minister of Public Works),²⁵ the federal control of appeal deemed that the term “control” must be broad and literal interpretation. For instance, if personal information is held by a representative or a service provider of a public institution, then the legal framework regarding the public sector would apply. Given that universities often rely on third party service providers, this precision is paramount when considering the use of online proctoring software. However, the above-mentioned case is not applicable per se. Universities are not only deferring the collection and management of personal information to third parties, but they also put students and third party companies in close operation. Furthermore, third parties are also engaged in data collection practices pertaining to personal data that the universities do not have access to and cannot control. Granted, in this particular situation, on one hand the third party parties are acting for public institutions, but on the other hand, they are seeking to collect students’ personal information for means that they are entirely commercial, often using technological tools and, thus, the universities cannot prevent them from doing so. If universities wish to impose terms pertaining to the protection of personal information inspired by the provincial sector framework and their privacy policies, they are then confronted with the reality of technological tools that they do not fully understand master. Not is only this observation pertinent to the collection of data, but it is also pertinent when using the storing of students’ personal information.
