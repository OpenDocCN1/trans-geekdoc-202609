## 4.1 INTRODUCTION

COVID-19 and the multiple variants associated with it have contributed to an accelerated digital transformation. One of the most viable areas of this mobile payments, which are related to financial technologies or fintech. As of Amit Samsukha [1], in 2020, mobile payments have accounted for 44.5% of all e-commerce transactions, double the amount of debit card payments and triple the amount of credit cards. For 3.5 trillion dollars (USD), in 2018, with its development, should reach, according to Fortune Business Insights [2], 19.9 billion dollars in 2026. In Quebec, the use of in-store mobile payments by adults jumped by 8 percentage points [3], from 17% in 2019, to reach 25%, in 2021.

During the pandemic, contactless digital payments [4], such as cards or e-wallets, were recommended. The need for remote payments is driving the creation of either services for some companies or new players in the financial technology sector. This requires choice of secure infrastructures to collect, store, and use data. Indeed data collection represents a significant risk to privacy, especially when it is used to profile citizens. As shown by the European Data Protection Supervisor (EDPS), it can increase the risk of cyber-attacks [5]. During this pandemic context, the cybersecurity market, including online crime, threats, and the fear breaches, to exploit the vulnerability of individuals and the fear that hold takes in cities [6]. Unprecedented strategies have been used to steal funds and collect data [7]. In the United States, between February and April 2020, cyber-attacking the financial sector increased by 238% [6].
