# 1.5 CURRENT EXPERIENCES THROUGH THE WORLD

Digital identity gives the identity to citizens so that they can get their daily tasks done with more ease, security, and effectiveness from anywhere. These tasks could be buying a bus pass, checking the status of a building under construction, receiving funds, and getting registered to any consumer service, getting a passport or a driving license. All these activities could be performed from one place or using a single identity which we call the digital identity. According to report by Lynch [33], more than one billion people in developing countries around the world have no proof of their identity. This unique citizen identity would bring interesting changes in every process of daily life and provides identity [34]. A major example is the Estonian advanced model of digital identity that enables government sector to identify its citizens with their unique digital identity to facilitate with them basic needs. They have the facility of online voting, access to their healthcare records online, and can access all the government processes online like marriage, divorce, real-estate, and any registration or record checking. Other than the developed countries such as Canada, the United States, the United Kingdom, and other European countries, a number of developing countries including Peru, Chile, Pakistan, India, Thailand, Indonesia, and the Philippines, have also been trying to take the leverage of digital citizen identity [34]. Bitnation a governance platform based on blockchain technology, tries to establish the concept-citizenship digital identity registration on blockchain [35]. Any individual in the world can become the citizen of Bitnation by agreeing to the constitution. The Bitnation Refugee Emergency Response (BRER) program provides an identity system called Blockchain Emergency ID (BE-ID). There are a number of programs, organizations, and software that provide the facility of having a digital identity including ConsSys, ID2020, Hyperledger, Australia Post Office, PIMN (Platform Identity Management Netherlands), the US Homeland Security, ShoCard, and Uport [36].

Other than these applications of digital identity, Canada has emerged as a country that is rapidly adapting to the digital age. They have been leveraging these technologies in every area of life such as shopping, banking, and accessing government services. A report by the Digital Identification and Authentication Council of Canada (DIACC) [37] highlighted the need for having a new digital system that allows citizens to perform all their tasks in person.
