# Security and Corporate Violation of Privacy

Since the late 90s, many attacks have occurred on IoT devices, infiltrating monitors and talking to children to refrigerators and TVs being used to send spam. But the problem is larger than that because the hack of many IoT devices is not to target the devices themselves but use them as an entry point to the network and the data. From then attack only kept. A well-known example is the Stuxnet virus [22] which was discovered in 2010. Malware to infect systems sent by programmable logic controllers, this virus managed to physically damage Iranian centrifuges. It was reported that this attack started in 2006. The primary occurrence happening in 2009 was one of the first examples of an IoT attack, targeting data acquisition systems and supervisory control in industrial control systems in industrial control concerns were raised by implementation were not. Just three years later, in December 2013, the first IoT bot was discovered at enterprise security Proof Inc. 25% of it was made of devices rather than a computer such as household appliances, monitors and smart TVs [38].

The most significant attack against IoT devices was in 2016. Where hackers attacked countless websites using Dyn's network, a domain name system caused the unavailability of a website such as Netflix, Amazon, The New York Times, and Twitter for hours. This attack was made using the Mirai Bot by infiltrating consumer IoT devices, IP cameras, and routers [42].

In 2017, the Food and Drug Administration declared that many health-based IoT devices, including pacemakers, defibrillators, and others, were vulnerable to security intrusions and attacks.

And Lately, in 2021, a group of Swiss hackers were able to hack more than 150,000 live cameras used in monitoring activities inside prisons, schools, hospitals, and companies such as Tesla.

All of these examples confirm that security is a big issue in IoT systems, hence the user’s privacy.
