## 5.2.2 ROLE OF AI

The presence of AI in proctoring software is somewhat of a novelty. During the 2010s, proctoring software was highly dependent on human intervention, notably human proctors who would supervise students in real time. In hopes to improve precision and efficiency of online proctoring, today's software implements artificial intelligence software.⁵⁶ Today's proctoring software now require less human proctor in order to identify exam takers and detect cheating instances due to their algorithmic dependence.⁷⁷

## 5.2.3 FUNCTIONS OF AI SYSTEMS

The features of online proctoring software vary; some of them only lock the exam's screen, while others record students via video and analyse the recordings using AI to detect instances of cheating. For instance, Respond Monitor is an online proctoring solution that is entirely automated which requires students to be equipped with a webcam to record the length of their online exam. Following the exam, the potential cheating instances are flagged and are provided to the instructor for verification.⁵⁸ The video recording functions are associated to AI systems such as facial recognition or automated surveillance to track behaviours that the system seems to be abnormal. These behaviours can vary, but in the case of using facial recognition technology, the disappearance of a subject's face or the appearance of a second subject can trigger the report of a potential cheating instance. Furthermore, the software solutions using facial recognition technology utilize biometric markers to identify students when they're logging into their exam and flag potential cheating incidents during the exam.⁵⁹ Additionally, biometric keystroke analysis which serves to track keystroke data, eye tracking which monitors and analyses eye movements, audio monitoring which records and monitors sonically, and facial detection are all methods that are used by some proctoring software.⁶⁶ proctoring software will then notify the exam supervisor of potential cheating incidents or behaviour it deems to be abnormal for a subsequent human verification and confirmation of the reported incident.⁶¹

Generally, proctoring software is tasked with identifying and authenticating the student, limiting the functionality of the student's computer, analysing student behaviour, and generating a report.⁶² In the case of Proctorio, software is equipped with features such as automated authentication, automated surveillance, and video recording, room reading, and the analysis of the students' behaviours.⁶³

## 5.2.4 RISKS OF ERRORS AND DISCRINATION

Proctorio states that their algorithmic techniques are superior and more effective than human proctoring and could fail in the gaps for human error, resulting in better results than human proctoring for detecting cheating incidents.⁶⁴ many times, Proctorio has been accused of utilizing algorithms that are discriminatory towards visible minorities.⁶⁵ Based on findings, it has been reported that 57% of authentication attempts by student with darker skin tones were unsuccessful.⁶⁶ Furthermore, it's also been said that the best authentication rate for all ethnicity groups is inferior to 75%, which highlights that the error rate is quite significant.⁷⁷

## 5.2.5 HYBRID SYSTEMS AND HUMAN CONTROL

It seems like universities are growing suspicious regarding fully automated systems and are therefore prioritizing hybrid systems. Hybrid systems are ones that combine human proctoring and automated proctoring which integrates artificial intelligence.⁶⁸ The University of British Columbia (UBC) has communicated that it will prohibit the use of automated online proctoring software as Proctorio, in all of their programs with the exception of accredited programmes which require such.⁶⁹ In regard to Proctorio, the software has been used to a Privacy Impact Assessment by UBC and the unsatisfactory results alone could justify abandoning the incident.⁷⁰ In the case of ProctorU, it offers many hybrid solutions as Review+, which utilizes human humans for the authentication and the post-verification of online exams, but the surveillance of exam is automated.⁷¹ ProctorU's Record+ utilizes an automated system for the authentication of students and the surveillance of the exam, and results are then certified by a human proctor prior to emitting an incident report.⁷²

### 5.3.1 MAIN LEGAL ISSUES

Online proctoring solutions have raised ethical and legal issues. Some of these issues are regarding privacy violations, while others are pertaining to the protection of personal information.⁷ and there are also concerns regarding fundamental rights in the implementation of AI.

### 5.3.2 PROVINCIAL LAWS REGARDING PERSONAL INFORMATION IN THE PUBLIC SECTOR

The use of proctoring software implies that some personal information is collected in an academic setting. Those data are collected and used by academic institutions they are located in different provinces; therefore, provincial laws are applicable. Academic institutions are public entities and the provincial framework applies to personal information such as universities. 

However, when speaking of the control factor, control does not need to be absolute, direct, or permanent. In this regard, in Canada Post Corp. v. Canada (Minister of Public Works),²⁵ the court of appeal deemed that the term “control” must be broad and literal. For instance, if personal information is held by a representative or service provider of a public institution, then the provincial framework would apply. Given that universities often rely on third party service providers, this precision is paramount when considering the use of online proctoring software. However, the above-mentioned case is not applicable per se. Universities are not only deferring the collection and management of personal information to third parties, but they also put students and third party companies in close operation. Furthermore, third parties are also engaged in data collection practices pertaining to personal data that the universities do not have access to or control. Granted, in this particular situation, on one hand the third party parties are acting for public institutions, but on the other hand, they are seeking to collect students’ personal information for means that are entirely commercial, often using technological tools and, thus, the universities cannot prevent them from doing so.
