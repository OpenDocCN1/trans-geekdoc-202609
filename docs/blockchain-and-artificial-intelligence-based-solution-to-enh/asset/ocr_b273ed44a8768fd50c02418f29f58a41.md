# 3.4.3 CONFIDENTIALITY

If personal data becomes public, confidentiality and privacy are as right [23]. We expect it to be read only by the intended recipient when we send a virtual message. This expectation is no longer safe. Our communications, medical records, and confidential documents are on the Internet, creating a high risk and enormous consequences for our privacy. If an unauthorized party gets access to that information, you become an easy target, either for criminal actions or blackmailing, or someone could delete it.

# 3.4.4 CONFORMITY TO LAWS AND POLICIES

In the digital world, privacy must be seen as a crucially important right for us as a society. At the conceptual level, it must go through the process of evolution as its older sibling, the right to freedom of expression. Clearly, the existing legal framework needs to adapt to those new concerns; otherwise, privacy will be a nonexistent thing with the enormous collection of data and the growth of the latest technology. That way, new legalizations, and laws are being readjusting the terms of privacy and what is legal and not during this digital age. Below are some of the new rules that are conducting this change.

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) [70] is a law dealing with data protection and privacy that went into effect in the European Union (EU) and the European Economic Area (EEA) on May 25, 2018.
