# OUR PRIVATE LIFE

As Philippe Aries and Georges Duby have shown in Histoire de la vie privée: l'Empire roi15 privacy is a concept as old as the world and manifests itself in different forms in different cultures. However, the meaning we refer to originates from an article that was published in 1890 by Samuel Warren and Louis Brandeis in the Harvard Law Review. It "the right to be alone."16 In other words, the right not to be disturbed by another, without permission. It is also the right to be able to exercise control over the use, and even storage of the data one produces. The web giants have wanted to fight this right to privacy. Eric Schmidt, CEO-of Google, said this: "If you have something that you don't want anyone to know, maybe you shouldn't be it in the first place."17 According to Schmidt, you have nothing to hide. No interest in having right to privacy. You can share, even health information. Social media has picked up on this motto. Citizens and especially Politicians who share Eric Schmidt's vision are now publishing a lot of information about themselves. There is nothing to hide.

Many experts contradict the view of the former Google CEO. For example, 45 minutes after posting a photo of his plane ticket on Instagram, the passport, and phone number of Tony Abbott, former prime minister of Australia, was found by a hacker.18 The information we share online is valuable. It can generate knowledge about us that can help companies offer personalized recommendations. A copy of your data (that you have nothing to hide) on Facebook contains 48 categories of information we share: posts, polls, events, payments, comments and reactions, stories, reels, groups, etc.
