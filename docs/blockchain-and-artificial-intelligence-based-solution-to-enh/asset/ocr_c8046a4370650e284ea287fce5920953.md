Digital identity refers to the association of these attributes in a digital world that be possible with Information Communication Technologies (ICT) [2].

These citizen identity information parameters help in managing access and the usage of a vast range of public and consumer services that include public government services, e-commerce, e-health facilities, e-voting, and toll tax. The citizens can have different identities as proof of identity such as passport, birth certificate, student card, or even health card. In order to take leverage of these identities, the citizens may need to visit those public departments of services and wait for them in the long queues. Now, the governments have extended their customer or public services and improved their public relations this has been made possible with the help of digitalization and ICT [3, 4]. The goal is that all tasks can be performed remotely using the usage of internet-based technologies, a vast number of services, electronic payments, toll roads, or speed cameras, e-commerce, and other forms of electronic services, updated in cases of emergencies or natural disasters. All these services and information exchange between the public and the government are made possible by storing, managing, processing, and updating the information of the citizens, which is known as the digital identity [3, 4]. Thus, the protection of the digital identity of the citizens is an extremely important and challenging task.

Given the significance of digital identity, many developed countries like China, Estonia, India, Australia, the United States, and the United Kingdom are not only making the digital identity scheme as part of e-government tasks and initiatives but also using the same identity for both public and private transactions [5]. These countries have using citizen identity in a wide range of programs such as the Estonian e-residency program which is the first government-authenticated and international digital identity program for individuals who are not Estonian citizens nor residents, or physically present in Estonia, and the EU digital market. China is also trying to implement and use blockchain technology to digital identity. China established the “Guangdong Province Big Data Comprehensive Experimental Area” in the Chenchang District, Foshan City of Guangzhou Province [6]. Moreover, a number of public and private organizations have introduced different citizen digital identity management solution and the use of blockchain technology among the most successful and widely used proposals [7].

Systems based on the management of digital identity have provided new ways of doing business, however, there are some implications associated with it in the form of identity fraud such as the creation of new false digital identities, fraudulent transactions, and money laundering.
