Intracranial pressure (ICP) is commonly monitored to treat patients with brain disorders such as traumatic brain injury (TBI). Established methods to assess ICP are resource intensive and highly invasive. We obtained ICP waveforms continuously from three signals acquired in the Intensive Care Unit (ICU): arterial blood pressure (ABP), plethysmography (PPG), and electrocardiogram (EEG) over 600 h of high-frequency (125 Hz) simultaneously acquired ICP, ABP, and PPG wavelet data from 10 patients. We trained deep learning (DL) models to reconstruct ICP waveforms. The predictive performance of different DL models was evaluated in single-patient and multi-patient tests. The mean absolute error (MAE) of the best-performing models was 1.34 mmHg in the single-patient test and 2.11 mmHg in the multi-patient test. Analysis was conducted to compare statistically independent performances across the models (SD = 0.63 ± 0.75, 0.66 ± 0.96, for EEG, PPG, and ABP; p = 0.42). The results demonstrate the potential of non-invasive continuous monitoring using electrophysiological waveforms. With refinement and validation, this method could provide a more accessible and alternative to invasive ICP, enabling stable assessment and treatment in resource-constrained settings.
