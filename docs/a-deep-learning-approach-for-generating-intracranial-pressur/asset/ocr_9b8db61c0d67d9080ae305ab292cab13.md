The outlier in the results is the baseline VNET, which showed no better performance when using subsets of waveforms compared to all three waveforms. This observation may indicate that after architectures are not capable of generating accurate reflections when certain waveforms are used. VNET lacks attention mechanisms and layers that enable dimensionality reduction, allowing it to capture essential information effectively. Multidimensional convolutions and pooling layers, VNET reduces spatial dimensions by downsampling features across various networks, enhancing its capacity to effectively capture local and global temporal features.

Using RNN, and VNET models shows that our approach of predicting the ICF from extracellular waveforms can be adopted in lower resource settings, particularly when arterial blood pressure cannot be measured.

# 6.3. Limitations

We acknowledge several limitations in this research. This is a proof-of-concept study with a sample size that was small, leaving justifiable concerns about statistical power, generalizability, and reality. Our results are reflective of the method used and the Bland-Alt analysis were relatively wide, indicating that the model will need further validation before it is considered in clinical works. Additionally, our patient sample was heterogeneous with respect to the etiology of their brain injury. Furthermore, the current dataset contains fields that were uncertain, potentially affecting model performance due to variations in input extraction signals and methods. With our application of a pre-processing scheme, absence of PPG and ECG detection system can help filter out noisy high-quality waveform data which could introduce bias into the training models and affect performance. Furthermore, our use of deep learning algorithms limits on the interpretation of important features in computing ICF. Given the nature of our experiment, we have not decided which model metrics are appropriate. A threshold of 5 mmHg is considered and would need to be agreed upon before consideration of clinical implementation. Finally, we have not externally validated our model in an independent dataset, restricting the generalizability of the findings found around the MIMIC III Waveform Database.

6.4. Future direction

Ongoing and future research will concentrate on aggregating the sample size of evaluated patients and examining differences in model performances. We plan to expand our work by including more de-identified waveforms (e.g., continuous monitoring) and adding patient demographics, thereby enhancing patient representation and improving model generalizability. We will also test the models on an external dataset outside of the MIMIC III Waveform

Database to assess the generalizability of model performance. Ideally, we intend to externally validate our data on a more homogeneous group of TBI patients enrolled in a larger prospective database. Finally, we will tune our independent deep learning models and methods for aggregating the outputs of each into a ‘super-ensemble’ learner to enhance performance.

# 7. Conclusions

In conclusion, the results of this study indicate that algorithmic prediction of the ICF from discrete set of continuous physiological signals is possible and yields accuracies that are higher than most other nonlinear techniques and on par with methods like ICP. With further refinement and validation, this could provide an accessible alternative to ICF measurements, enabling prompt treatment of severe brain injury in lower resource settings.
