However, the government’s interest in introducing Islamic banking abated one year after the elections.41

Much to the dismay of all Muslim stakeholders, not much was heard from the Bank of Ghana either. In response to queries on the application process for operating an Islamic bank, in January 2017, the Governor of the Bank of Ghana noted that the new bank law provided an opportunity to trade in Islamic banking products. “You can have a universal banking license and just deal in Islamic financial products, and that makes you an Islamic bank,” he declared. However, in practice, the Governor was not referring to pure Islamic banks but Islamic financial windows operated by conventional banks. He highlighted the possibility of a bank nominating a Shari‘a Advisory Council to provide the bank’s informed operational opinion and advice. Referring to the Bank of Ghana’s application some two years ago, the institution at first received a provisional license.
