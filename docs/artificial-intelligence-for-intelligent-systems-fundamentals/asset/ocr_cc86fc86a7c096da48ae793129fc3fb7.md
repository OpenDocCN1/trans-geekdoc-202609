# 1.7 ETHICAL AND SOCIAL IMPLICATIONS

# 1.7.1 Considerations of bias and fairness in AI applications

Dwork et al. [79] introduce the concept of “fairness through awareness” and emphasize the need to address potential biases in training data and algorithms. The study emphasizes the importance of fairness across different groups and provides guidelines for achieving fairness in machine learning. Racial and gender biases in facial recognition systems are discussed by Buolamini and Gebru [80]. The study shows that darker-skinned and female faces have higher mistake rates, emphasizing the need to correct algorithmic biases and enhance fairness in AI systems. Bolukbasi et al. [81] study how bias affects word embeddings, which are an essential part of models in NLP. The work indicates how social biases can be amplified and encoded by AI systems and also suggests ways to counteract these biases. The fairness of AI algorithms in the criminal justice system is examined by Larson et al. [82]. In this context, the research delves into the differences in risk assessment tools based on race and highlights the significance of fairness metrics and transparency in the development and application of AI systems. The “Ethically Aligned Design” document, released in 2019 by the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE), offers a thorough framework for tackling ethical issues in AI research [83]. It includes a specific focus on bias and fairness, the need for transparency, accountability, and inclusive design practices.
