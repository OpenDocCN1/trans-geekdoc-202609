Singh et al. [30] put forward a method of ensemble learning to predict hepatitis C disease, reaching a high accuracy of 95.69%. The scientists utilized the UCI dataset, with clinical data obtained from a group of over 1,000 people suffering from hepatitis C. The main work consisted of the creation of an ensemble learning structure that was able to increase the accuracy of machine learning models in the field of hepatitis estimation.

Priya et al. [31] evaluated machine learning methods for identifying hepatitis C. The regression (LR) methodology had 97.9% accuracy. Clinical information of more than 1,000 hepatitis C patients was utilized in the UCI dataset. Evaluating machine learning techniques for hepatitis C prediction was the study's major contribution. The research found that the LR approach was the most accurate.

To hepatitis C, Sahoo et al. [32] evaluated multiclass and binary labeling of a dataset. The AdaBoost algorithm developed by the researchers with 54.23% accuracy. Clinical information of over 1,000 hepatitis C patients was employed in the PROMI dataset. This study examined the efficacy of multiclass and binary classifications to predict hepatitis C. The research conducted that binary classes labeled appear accurate.

In their research, Syafah et al. [33] compared four machine learning algorithms, using KNN, naive Bayes, neural network, and RF, to predict hepatitis C. A dataset of 1,200 patients identified as having hepatitis C was utilized in the research. The findings showed that the neural network displayed the greatest accuracy, specifically 95.12%.

Alotabi et al. [34] utilized machine learning methods to identify the presence of liver disease for people suffering from hepatitis C. The researchers employed a dataset comprising 2,038 Egyptian patients from the UCI Machine Learning Repository. Following, they trained four different machine learning algorithms on this dataset, including RF, gradient boosting machine, extreme gradient boosting, and extra trees. The extra model showed better outcomes compared to the other models, getting an accuracy rate of 96.92%.

In their research, Abd El-Salam et al. [35] used machine learning approaches to predict the likelihood of hepatic varices for humans diagnosed with chronic hepatitis C. A dataset of 4,962 patients from Egypt was used, and six different machine learning algorithms were used for learning purposes. The algorithms were neural network, Bayes, decision tree, SVM, RF, and Bayesian network. The Bayesian network algorithm displayed excellent results, with an accuracy rate of 74.8%.
