# How the Brain Learns

The brain learns so that it can make decisions that lead to actions that will allow the individual to survive. Our brain has a hard time attending to things that do not lead to decisions that lead to taking an action that we perceive as important. There are millions of people around the world who still learn every minute of the day in order to get food, clothing, and shelter, and their survival depends on it. Those of us who live in developed countries and have our basic needs met, still need to understand how our brain continues to follow this physiological procedure. Understanding that process is necessary, we can best figure out how to have students learn what is useful for them in order to be prosperous and happy within the current society.

Learning means taking in sensory information, integrating that information and changing it into symbols and language, and then creating ideas, generalizations, concepts, decisions, and plans.

There is one main area of the brain—the cerebral cortex—that is involved in learning. Some parts of this process are done in the front-facing area of the brain and other parts of this process are done in the back-facing area. In order for these processes to work and for us to learn, we need to build rich communication networks between these two areas.
