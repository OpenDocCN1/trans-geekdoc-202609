Within reason, it is almost never too late for a student to make a comeback in my class so long as he or she shows genuine effort to make the attempt. Students are never guaranteed “A” grades. By that same token, a student has to try rather hard to earn a “D” or an “F” by consistently refusing to grab the multiple lifelines I throw their way. More than one student, and parent, has told me this is the single best aspect of my classroom, and they bemoan the rarity of it. I can see why it is rare—it nearly doubles my grading load! At the same time, I do not feel as if I am actually engaged in *teaching* if I do not do this.

# Get the Community Involved as Much as Possible in the Classroom and Vice Versa

When students see a disconnect between their life outside and inside school, their engagement plummets. The more students can use their out of school lives as grist for their in school assignments, the more it all starts to come together for them. When we practice persuasive writing and argumentation, I ask my students to identify real people they want to try and persuade (their parents, their boyfriend or girlfriend, the school principal, the president), and then address their argument to those individuals. We write letters to the editor of the local newspaper, or policy advice to local elected representatives—and then we send them off! One of my students’ perennial favorite activities involves sending compliment and complaint letters to local or national businesses. Quite frequently, the companies write back with gift cards and free samples. This process makes the question of what makes for an effective, persuasive argument for a given audience come alive.

Involving the community as an audience, or even as *assessors*, of student work, has also proved highly successful as a student motivator. I always invite parents, other class’s teachers and students, administrators and community members to our project fairs. It allows my students to know that their work will have an authentic audience beyond just me, and it strengthens ties and lines of communications with other stakeholders in the community. That community contains experts in a variety of fields.
