# Science Process Skills

The following are thinking skills needed to do and understand science.

The basic processes (pre-operational thinkers and beyond):

+   - Observing
+   - Using space/time relationships
+   - Classifying
+   - Using numbers
+   - Measuring
+   - Communicating
+   - Predicting
+   - Inferring
+   - Estimating

The integrated processes (concrete operational and formal thinkers):

+   - Formulating hypotheses
+   - Defining operationally
+   - Identifying and controlling variables
+   - Interpreting data
+   - Graphing
+   - Modeling

# Habits of Mind in Practice

When I was first introduced to the concept of *habits of mind* I had a hard time differentiating them from thinking skills. They seemed to overlap so much. One day I was working with a wonderful colleague of mine, Arthur Camins, who at the time was a science coordinator in the Bedford-Stuyvesant area of Brooklyn, NY. We were struggling with how we were going to have our elementary teachers grasp this important idea if we could not clearly differentiate the two ourselves. That night he telephoned me and said, “You can have a thinking and reasoning skill and can apply it appropriately when asked to do so in school. However, if you do not regularly use it in your daily decision making, then you have not acquired the habit of mind.” That seems to have not only made it clear to me but to all teachers we were working with. If you use the most important decision-making and thinking skills, even when you are not required to, then they become part of the fabric of your thinking.

Some principals and teachers have decided that developing habits of mind is so important for students’ academic success, that they have made them a central part of the mission for their schools. Debbie Meier’s Central Park East Secondary School in New York City is probably the one most cited, but in the past decade I have encountered many other successful schools in New York City, Minneapolis, Atlanta, Buffalo, Oakland and in smaller towns in Mississippi, New Jersey, Maine, and Massachusetts. All these schools specify habits of mind at their core.
