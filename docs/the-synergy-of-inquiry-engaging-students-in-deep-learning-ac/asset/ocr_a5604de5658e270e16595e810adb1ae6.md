# Inquiry Learning at the International High School at LaGuardia Community College

# Principal Eric Nadelstern’s Reflections

I opened the International High School at LaGuardia Community College in September 1985 with a staff of 14, half of whom were teachers, and 59 recently arrived immigrant students. Within four years, 435 students were enrolled and taught by a faculty of 30 teachers. In 2014, there are 16 International High Schools in New York City and two in California, all public schools, serving thousands of English Language Learners (ELLs). The inquiry learning methodologies that my teachers and I developed together are still in practice in these schools, and considered the leading edge of ELL instruction nearly 30 years after they were first implemented.

The following mission and statement of educational philosophy is from the school’s website (http://ihsnyc.org/about/mission/):

> > The mission of The International High School is to enable each of our students to develop the linguistic, cognitive, and cultural skills necessary for success in high school, college, and beyond. We are committed to the following educational principles.

+   1. Limited English proficient students require the ability to understand, speak, read and write English with near-native fluency to realize their full potential within an English-speaking society.
+   
+   2. In an increasingly interdependent world, fluency in a language other than English must be viewed as a resource for the student, the school, and the society.
+   
+   3. Language skills are most effectively learned in context and emerge most naturally in purposeful, language-rich interdisciplinary study.
