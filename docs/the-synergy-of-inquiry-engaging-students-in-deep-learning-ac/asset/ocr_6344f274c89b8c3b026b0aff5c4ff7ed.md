This synergy is explained by a similarity in thinking skills across the subject areas. If students use similar skills as they are engaged in figuring out ideas in each of the subject areas, this reinforcement throughout the school day will cause the neural networks associated with them to become hardwired.

The processes for creating knowledge differ across subjects; for example, what makes something acceptable knowledge in science is different than what makes something acceptable knowledge in literature as demonstrated earlier in this chapter. Nonetheless, there are similarities in the knowledge creating processes. All subjects require evidence, though where the evidence comes from and how it is validated may be somewhat different. In each area, the difference between observation and inference is vital, but what makes for fruitful inference and when it is advisable to use it is different in each individual academic area. However, the thinking skills are analogous and need to be reinforced in each area, so they become a habit of mind. The differences also need to become part of the neuronal connections, as students need to know when to use the distinctions in their daily lives. This rationale also presents yet another reason for carefully planned and facilitated interdisciplinary studies as part of each student’s school week.
