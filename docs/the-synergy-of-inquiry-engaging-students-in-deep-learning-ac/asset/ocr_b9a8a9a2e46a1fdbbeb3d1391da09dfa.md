Let us take a minute here and go back to Maria’s planning stage. Once she created and planned this sequence of exploration and concept clarification, she realized that three thinking skills were most prominent during the exploration:

+   - **Observation**—close and thoughtful scrutiny of materials
+   - **Inference**—based on evidence from careful observations
+   - **Model making**—using careful data collection and inference making

Likewise, she realized she could push students to realize that they were using two particular habits of mind. She could then encourage them to continue to use these habits of mind in their daily lives and give them examples of where they could be used as they were watching television during the past week. These were:

+   - Be curious. Be careful and accurate in observations.
+   - Know that often different explanations can be given for the same evidence, and it is not always possible to tell which one is correct.

Given the focus for subject area teachers to work on literacy skills, she also included an essay-writing assignment later in the lesson. She did not originally have an art subject area objective listed, but when she reflected on the lesson after she taught it, she modified her plan so that the next year she taught the lesson she could focus on building the skills of her artistically talented students, and could encourage all of her students to be as detailed and accurate as possible in their renderings. The objectives for the other subject areas were:

+   - Recognize not only careful and accurate drawings but also the true artistic abilities of gifted students.
+   - Have students create a well-reasoned and clearly-written three paragraph persuasive essay to defend their conclusions about how cell division occurs based on their research teams evidence.
