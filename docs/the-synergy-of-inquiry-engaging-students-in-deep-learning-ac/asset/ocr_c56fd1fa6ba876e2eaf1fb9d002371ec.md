# A Starting Point and Planning Template for ITSLU

If you want to plan an interdisciplinary unit, find someone to collaborate with you. As an elementary teacher in a self-contained classroom, designing an interdisciplinary unit will be different than what you regularly do. So, find someone to work with—another teacher at your grade level, another teacher with an interest in the inquiry approach, a paraprofessional, or a community member with particular expertise. This colleague will be someone with whom you can bounce ideas around and who can assist you in implementing the unit.

If you are a middle or high school teacher, find a teacher from another discipline who is interested in collaborating with you. Together you can search online for examples of ITSLU units where you will get a feel for how teachers are integrating subjects and creating engaging projects for students. In these examples, you will see how teachers match their state standards and use an inquiry-teaching approach. Notice how some teachers use community members as resources. Each time teachers sit down to plan a new unit, they are inspired by a different element that then becomes the starting point. Although it needs to meet subject matter standards, which aspect of the subject matter you will choose is dependent upon many other factors. Sometimes a teacher team begins with an idea for a theme, but other times it is an idea for a service-learning project.
