Developing an ITSLU takes some thought and planning. Figure 5.6 presents a 12-step template for developing an interdisciplinary thematic service learning unit. This template can be found on the Digital Resource CD.

# **Figure 5.6** Template for Planning an Interdisciplinary Thematic Service Learning Unit (ITSLU)

+   1. **Introduction, Overview, and Rationale**—Make clear how inquiry teaching and learning is occurring in each of the disciplines but especially in the small products and project as well as the service learning component.

+   2. **Main Concepts, Skills, and Habits of Mind**—Specify how each of these will be addressed for each discipline in the unit. Include some rubrics that assess at least two small products on various aspects and one rubric that assesses the final project. It is more likely that you will do most of your assessment of academic skills on the small projects leading up to the bigger service learning project. Additional assessment may be necessary based on your particular situation.

+   3. **State Standards**—Identify the standards applicable to the disciplines selected for the unit. Use the standards as benchmarks for assessing development of concepts and acquisition of skills.

+   4. **Engaging Activity**—Plan what you will have students do physically and mentally to hook them on addressing the driving question and engage in the service-learning component.

+   5. **Theme and Essential/Driving Question**—Explain how your theme is effective based on the components of an effective theme (see Figure 5.1). Show the connection of the theme to your essential question and how it is transformed into a driving question that intrigues students sufficiently that they care enough to search for the answer.

+   6. **Timelines: Day-by-Day and Week-by-Week**—Prepare timelines for each discipline and across disciplines. Chart the timelines in a way that one can see when disciplinary classes are meeting alone and when they are meeting combined with each other. If you are doing more than 15 days of activities, then describe these 15 days in detail and the rest in a week-by-week summary.

+   7. **Small Projects**—These projects serve a scaffolding function to teach skills and concepts. Define the progression of small projects that lead up to or are parallel with the service learning project that they support.

+   8. **Service Learning Component**—Describe this component in detail, including information about any community participants, including parents with insights and skills, who will interact with students and support their learning and accomplishment of their project. Be sure that students are dependent upon one another in order to complete the project.

+   9. **Study Skills, Research Skills, and Habits of Mind**—Define those that will be most emphasized (learned and practiced) in the unit. Explain why you selected these and where they will be practiced.
