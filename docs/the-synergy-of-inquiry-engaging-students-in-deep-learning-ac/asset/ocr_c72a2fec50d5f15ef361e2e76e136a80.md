Now I want you to all think a little on your own for a bit. You should write something down, maybe even draw a picture. If you could see inside the tip of the root, look with a microscope, and watch it for two days, what do you think you would see happening?

Following our conversation, Maria and I wrote down what we thought the student answers would be. Maria’s answers were similar to some of mine about needing more cells as the plant grew. We thought some students might say each cell “cloned” to be another exact same cell. We wondered about their thoughts on the roots moving in a direction, and if they thought the cells moved. Then I realized that we needed to stop using our previous knowledge. We realized that another explanation for roots getting bigger was that cells might just get bigger. This makes total sense and is a good model for growth at the cellular level. Therefore, we needed students to see if there was evidence for cells just getting bigger or cells dividing into two cells. We then added a new concept to our list of objectives:

+   - Cells need to replicate, rather than simply getting bigger, when growth occurs in organisms.

# The Idea for Student Exploration

Maria suddenly realized what needed to be done: the process crystalized for her. She became excited as she saw in her mind how students could figure it all out for themselves. When she was in school, students looked at onion roots, but the teacher had told them everything before with all the scientific names. Maria and her classmates just memorized everything. Now, she saw that students could do a real exploration first and figure out the ideas themselves, so she just wrote her plan down step by step.

It was so wonderful to watch her face as she wrote down her plan, just as I would see it unfold in her classroom two and three days later. She really did not tell students any of the concepts. Students figured them all out by themselves. She just cruised the classroom and facilitated with questions when they were in small groups, and helped clarify concepts when they were working as the whole class. It was masterful. Here is how she did it.

If you carefully follow Maria’s thinking in planning the lesson, you can gain the insights to create an inquiry teaching plan for any subject area. Travel with her on this journey and see how she taught a science topic that is usually uninteresting and not well understood by most students. By the end of the lesson, virtually every student in her class had engendered fairly deep understanding because they created the idea themselves.
