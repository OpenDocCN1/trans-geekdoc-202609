“Oh snap. This is still wet,” Ronald yelled to no one in particular as he attempted to wipe the tacky varnish off his left hand onto his pants.

“Let’s crank it up people,” he commanded, loud enough for everyone in the auditorium to hear. “There are already people waiting and we need to let them into the house.”

He had to laugh as he thought to himself what his friends from the Queensbridge housing projects where he lived would say if they saw him now. His mother would not believe that he was nailing this on, no less that he had supervised the building of this whole stage set. For years she barely got him to take out the garbage. But lately everyone, including him, was astonished by his behavior.

Just then Madeleine, a diminutive dark-skinned Dominican girl, walked by donning a black robe.

“Hey, Judge Maddy, be careful what you touch or you are going to be permanently sitting behind this bench,” Ronald commented.

Madeleine responded, “Judge Maddy’, that’s cute Ronald, but what are you talking about?”

As Ronald walked away he responded, “We needed to re-varnish parts of this bench and it is not quite dry, if your robe rests too long against it, it will be glued to the wood.”

Madeleine raised her eyebrows and shook her head. “It’s not bad enough that someone wrote more legal mumbo-jumbo lines into my part than Perry Mason ever said in his life, but now I have to worry about what I touch. Freaking great.”

“Oh my God!” Madeleine exclaimed from her perch on the stool behind the bench. “Is this Steve?” “I can’t believe it, Steve, I’ve never seen you in anything that wasn’t black and that didn’t have cut off sleeves. Where’s your tattoo now?”

All heads turned to look at the stage where a white teenager was straightening a gray suit coat.
