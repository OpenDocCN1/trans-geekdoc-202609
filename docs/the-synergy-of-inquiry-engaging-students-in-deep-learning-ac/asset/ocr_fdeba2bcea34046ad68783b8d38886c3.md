# Interdisciplinary Thematic Service Learning Units (ITSLU)

Terry and I did not start at the points illustrated in the foregoing scenarios. Like with most interdisciplinary project-based classes, we began just trying to connect two subjects and having students create a final product. Likewise, most ITSLUs start with teachers connecting two subjects whether as an individual in an elementary classroom or with a colleague in another discipline working with adolescents. Their initial efforts are simple and manageable; as their skills and confidence build, they add more components to make the units more effective for their students. In the beginning, without many of these components as part of the unit, much of the energy to keep the unit progressing needs to come from the teacher(s). However, once a critical mass of the components is added, students become so engaged that an equal or greater amount of the energy emanates from students. This is why teachers who create *interdisciplinary thematic service learning units* (ITSLU) simply cannot revert back to less self-perpetuating ways of working together with their students. Previously disengaged students are less likely to leave school and teachers are less likely to burn out. The well planned and executed ITSLU results in both students and teachers becoming reinvigorated.

# Components of an Effective ITSLU

The components that contribute to the effectiveness of a ITSLU appear in Figure 5.4. The components are organized in three categories: (1) Curriculum, (2) Structure, and (3) Teacher Support. This checklist will be useful as you begin to create your interdisciplinary thematic service learning units. No ITSLU has all the components, but the more there are, the more effective and self-sustaining the unit becomes.

Figure 5.4 Checklist of Components for Effective ITSLU

| Component Category | Components |
|---|---|
| Curriculum | Specifies themes that:
• Matter to students
• Contain multiple subjects
• Include interesting topics
• Have real-world relevance
Defines a driving question
Defines relevant standards for:
• Concepts
• Skills
