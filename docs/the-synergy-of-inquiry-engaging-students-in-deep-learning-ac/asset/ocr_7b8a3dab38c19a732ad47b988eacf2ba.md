# Success for Teachers and Students

Over the years, the spirit of inquiry became the way we operated at the school. Adults learned that approaching learning and problem solving with elementary students through collaboration and inquiry makes for deeper and more lasting learning. It also encourages students to become independent learners who continue their explorations outside of class and school—on the playground and in their families and neighborhoods. Just as important, it makes for content students and a happy school.

Finally, it is widely agreed upon that the quality of a child’s teacher is a significant factor in the depth of learning for that student. Certainly it must be the goal of every principal to both attract and retain the very best staff members for the school. In order to do that, one must work toward a school climate that is intensely professionally satisfying to the adults who work there. In the words of a superintendent for whom I once worked, “You want your school district to work the best for the best teachers.”

I thought about those wise words every time I put together a hiring committee for a new staff member. I knew I wanted the candidates to be introduced to our teachers and think to themselves, “I would love to work with these people.” I was thrilled when exactly that happened. We were able to attract highly skilled and experienced teachers, including ones who were willing to leave another secure position. “I want colleagues with whom I can discuss the ideas that matter about teaching,” remarked one who chose to make such a move.
