# Think and Reflect

+   1. What appears to be the greatest commonalities among the various teachers’ classrooms and approaches despite their various grade levels and disciplines? How do some of these match where you are currently in your approach to teaching and learning, and what might be your next possible steps on your inquiry teaching journey?

+   2. David Nurenberg speaks about letting students know the value of good questions. How can you get your students to understand why one question might be richer than another on the same topic? Can this be a valuable assessment mechanism for you as well?

+   3. Andrea Egitto explains how as an elementary school teacher she has evolved from using inquiry teaching in just science to over the years making it her method for teaching all the subjects in her classroom. If you are an elementary school teacher, will you begin inquiry teaching in one subject area first, and if so, which one? Will you begin in more than one discipline and might this be better for the students? Do you think you can find another teacher at your grade level and share the planning?

+   4. As with Mike Farmer’s classroom, will technology be employed by your students to actively produce media pieces to communicate their newly constructed understandings to others in the school or neighborhood communities?
