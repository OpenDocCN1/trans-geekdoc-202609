As the term goes on, these big ideas emerge from multiple explorations that students have engaged in, and during the concept clarification section of the lessons, the teacher explicitly points these out as central to the discipline. Eventually, all of these ideas are addressed and the teacher keeps referring back to them so that all the neuronal connections of new ideas created by students are strengthened. Because these are the key organizing structures of the concepts in the course, students will also begin to say, “Oh look, this is just another example of… (one of the big ideas).”

After much discussion among a number of science teacher colleagues, Maria had identified five big ideas that she thought were rich enough, important enough, and that would allow everything studied during the year to fall under one or more of them. After some discussion, we expanded some, condensed others, and clearly stated each of them in language that students in her class could, and would, use. I have done this with many scientists and teachers over the years, and each time, a consensus is reached that closely approximates what Maria and her colleagues had created with just a little nudging from me. There is no right answer to what the big ideas are for a course, they just need to match the criteria in italics in the first paragraph in this section.
