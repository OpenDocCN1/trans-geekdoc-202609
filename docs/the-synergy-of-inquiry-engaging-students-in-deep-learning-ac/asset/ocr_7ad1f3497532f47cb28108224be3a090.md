# Language Acquisition within the Inquiry-Teaching Classroom

The inquiry-teaching classroom provides a perfect opportunity for students to practice *relational meaning* in language since they are simultaneously doing, and translating this doing, into both oral and written language. The syntax, grammar, paragraph writing, and reading comprehension skills and strategies will naturally grow out of students’ small group explorations as they are negotiating their ideas with peers and eventually writing the reports about what they learned in these explorations. This process needs to be mediated by having the teacher consistently respond to extensive student writing and the idea creation that underlies the writing, thereby supplying scaffolding throughout the whole process. Seven steps in the process, described in the following section, reflect key features of the Inquiry Teaching Learning Cycle.

# Step 1: Exploration—Students, working in a small team of four or less, are engaged in manipulating various materials.

+   - Students write the question that they are exploring.
+   - After *messing about* with the materials and discussing ideas with team members, students revise the question to more clearly and concisely state the point of the investigation.
+   - Students work with others on their research team (cooperative group) to create initial procedures that the team will follow.
+   - Each member of the team writes and revises his or her own procedures and findings.
+   - Each team member also writes about the affective part of the team’s exploration that day. A student writes about what he or she was feeling during this investigation, as researchers in academia have traditionally done in their journals in addition to recording their ideas. The teacher needs to be checking that students can differentiate between evidence, inferences, emotions, and opinions. The student subsequently identifies these elements and writes about them in appropriate places.
