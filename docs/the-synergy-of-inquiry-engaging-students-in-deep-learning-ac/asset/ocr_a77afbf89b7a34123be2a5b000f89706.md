+   - Does this lesson answer some aspect of the essential, or better yet, driving question for the unit? Is it clear to students in the transition from the last lesson what question needs to be answered next?

# 3. Identify the main concepts to be learned in the lesson.

+   - Is this concept one expected either from national or state standards for the grade level of my students? What is it?
+   - What are the specific aspects of this big concept that students will learn during this particular lesson? What are the factors involved in this particular aspect of the concept?

# 4. Identify the initial idea for an exploration where students can uncover the whole concept, or at least part of the concept, without the teacher or media doing any telling about the concept.

+   - Can students witness a physical manifestation of the phenomenon, or perform an initial exploration from which they can design an investigation or identify factors involved in the phenomenon?

This is the key step in transforming didactic, transmission lessons to inquiry teaching lessons where students uncover the concept through an exploration, just as academics throughout history did.

+   - How much do students get to manipulate materials during this initial exploration phase?
+   - How much needs to be done as drawing visual models?
+   - Can students use primary sources?
+   - How much can be done with role plays or simulations?
+   - Does this exploration have the appropriate factors involved in a way that students can intuit the relationship between factors in the concept and possibly even create a generalization from their experience?

Once you as the teacher identify the factors involved in the phenomenon, look at the exploration that you have created and see if students can:

+   - design all or part of the investigation needed to find out the answer to the question.
+   - identify the factors involved in the phenomenon, concept, or skill so they can partially design the approach to finding an answer.
+   - hypothesize possible relationships between factors.
+   - begin to understand how particular approaches to problem solving work in particular situations, and can begin to see how they might be used in analogous situations.
