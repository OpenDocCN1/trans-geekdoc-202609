In the past 10 years, I have had the opportunity to work with many effective math teachers in grades 6 through 12 as a mathematics coach. My understanding of teaching and learning has grown immensely through this work. I have sat in hundreds of classrooms where inquiry-based mathematics was the central approach used by teachers. I observed the ways mathematics was presented and how students attempted to make sense of, and understand, mathematical ideas and problems. In the following sections, you will get to hear from some of these teachers and students on how inquiry instruction can change the way mathematics is thought about.

# Inquiry-Based Mathematics Instruction

Many students regard mathematics as a collection of procedures that allows one to answer questions in a short space of time (Schoenfeld 1988). Meanwhile, inquiry is a mode of teaching and learning that “rests on conceptual innovation” (Schwab 1962, 5). Students participating in a community of inquiry, discuss and solve new and unfamiliar problems (Kwon 2005). Problems are used to help develop mathematical understanding of concepts and procedures rather than viewed as applications of already learned procedures (Koehler and Grouws 1992; Hiebert 1999). A teacher’s job is to “encourage conjecturing and arguing and to make the environment safe for students to express their thinking” (Koehler and Grouws 1992, 122). Inquiry proceeds through uncertainty and struggle leading to knowledge that is hard to come by (Schwab 1962).

Two ideas central to inquiry-based mathematics instruction are *patterns* and *problem solving*. Mathematics has been defined as the “science of patterns” and the central role of a mathematics student is that of a pattern hunter. How can I make sense of the information presented to me? What observations can I make from the patterns? What conjectures can I make based on the observed patterns? Can I prove if this conjecture holds true for all cases? When a student understands the centrality of patterns as an underlying structure of mathematics, he or she has a greater chance of developing mathematical understanding.
