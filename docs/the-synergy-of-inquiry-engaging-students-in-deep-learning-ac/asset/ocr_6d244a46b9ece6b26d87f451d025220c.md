You might be saying to yourself that many of these fit other academic disciplines as well, or that they are just traits of logical common sense people. That is the point. These attributes need to become part of the fabric of thinking of all of our students—just part of who they are as they travel through life.

If you are thinking that this is too multifaceted and you are getting worried that you cannot fit all of this into your lessons, remember that all of the concepts, thinking skills, and habits of mind exist naturally, and organically in a well-planned inquiry lesson. Once you approach planning and facilitating lessons in this fashion, you will soon become a master at having your students succeed in each of these arenas. Not only will they be successful, but they will also enjoy themselves immensely while doing it.

# An Example of Three Objectives Accomplished Simultaneously

In planning the second day of the John Brown lesson, Mike planned to have a concept, thinking skill, and habit of mind objective incorporated when students were engaged in the inquiry lesson. These objectives were accomplished simultaneously while students explored and clarified their understanding of John Brown's raid. They are listed here as they were all listed in the objectives part of Mike's plan. In addition, he wrote facilitating and assessment questions throughout the plan for each objective.

**Concept**: Although John Brown's raid of 1859 was an illegal action according to both South Carolina and U.S. law, some saw it as an act of civil disobedience while others viewed it as almost treasonous.

**Thinking Skill**: Students interpret data to recognize how evidence of historical events can be manipulated to present a point of view.

**Habit of Mind**: Students develop perspective by considering how else the events might look if we stepped into other shoes. They learn about and understand viewpoint.

# Apply Knowledge to New Situations

Before we can say that students have acquired deep understanding or mastery of skills, it is necessary for them to demonstrate their *ability to apply* any learning related to objectives for explorations and clarifications to unique new situations. Once again, in inquiry teaching and learning, this assessment is built right into what the teacher and students are already doing. Everything that you have ever learned about effective formative assessment that occurs on a daily basis and informs lessons for the next day is organic to inquiry teaching. Likewise, if you have been working on authentic assessment where students are required to apply their newly acquired understandings or skills to real life situations, you are going to be very comfortable with inquiry teaching. Authentic assessment is one of the key components of inquiry teaching.
