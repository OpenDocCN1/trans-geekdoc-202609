When students have created this *mental landscape* of the concept, moderated by various syntactical structures that communicate fairly accurate (but not necessarily totally expansive) relationships, they are prepared to have a meaningful reading experience.

# **Step 6: Translating**—Students attempt to take oral descriptions and translate them into symbolic (written) language (words and numbers and their relationships).

+   - While engaging in the investigation, homework requires students to organize and synthesize ideas in a written form from the day’s classroom investigation, rather than read a text and answer questions. This approach will provide daily scaffolding for thinking and writing in the final report about the concepts and skills acquired from the exploration.
+   - If the exploration is mainly finished, students will take the cumulative and scaffolded work of the last few days and shape it into a rich and well-written document with the particular format the teacher has requested.
+   - This time is appropriate for students to read trade books or various texts, to wisely use the Internet in a supervised and scaffolded fashion, or to speak to outside experts who might suggest sources to increase their understanding of the phenomenon being studied. Students can use these understandings from the academic community to help them uncover the inconsistencies in their own explorations and include these in their conclusions. Teaching students the strategy of comparing their way of expressing an idea with how professionals are expressing similar ideas, gives them the tools for seeing the structures for clear communication and entry into academic language and thought. Again, the process is that students first have a chance to have an informed naïve conception so that written work can now have deeper meaning as they attempt to read and translate its meaning based on the landscape they have already created in their brains.
+   - Each student then publishes his or her understandings, comparing his or her ideas and the process of getting there with what the *academic* experts have written about the concepts. Students submit their final work to the teacher.

# **Step 7: Mentoring until Competency**—The teacher responds in writing to students’ published writings and has them make revisions until the student clearly can explain conceptual relationships in a written symbolic form.

+   - If the teacher has modeled writing partners (Calkins 1994), students first critique (as critical friends) each other’s written work for academic accuracy and literary clarity.
+   - Students then rewrite their reports or daily work based on the feedback from their rotating writing partners.
