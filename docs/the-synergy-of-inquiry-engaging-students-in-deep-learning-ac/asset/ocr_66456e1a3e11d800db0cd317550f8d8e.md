# A Previously Disengaged Student Engages in a Final Public Project: A Slice of School Life

For Steve, even inquiry-based subject classes were not enough. He tried different science, English, history, physical education, and math classes a number of times and each time he walked away—sometimes literally, sometimes just mentally. Now, here he was rehearsing dialogue for a play he had helped write that focused on gene pools and ecosystems. He had to laugh to himself. Last night he tried on the gray suit that his mom had bought him to go to his uncle’s funeral to see if it still fit him. It had been a little tight, but it would work when he walked on stage as the defense attorney defending the young couple that was challenging the theoretical new law requiring amniocentesis for all pregnancies. The class had argued for weeks about the ethics of the law they devised, and ironically, he was in the center of this debate. This was the same Steve, who only seven months before, the police had picked up on suspicion of assaulting another teen and putting him in the hospital. The friends who were with him that night had not considered the ethics of anything. They knew they had each other and thought surely they were right.

All that seemed so far away now. He still saw them regularly when he was in his neighborhood, but only Manny, Raphael, and Nadine were also part of this life at this school. This was not the first public high school that they had attended. Actually, he had not attended the other one all that much, easily getting lost amongst the other 4,000 students. It was not that his teachers were not dedicated. He could not articulate what he needed, but he knew it was not enough, so he cut out, just walked away. The city was big enough to swallow him up and keep him somewhat occupied. Until this past September, he had not really attended school for over six months.

# Thinking Back on the BONGO Program

Steve and his peers are actual students that Terry and I taught at Middle College High School, a New York City public school, where we taught for over 19 years. This *slice of school life* described from a student perspective is a narrative derived from actual interviews with these students done as part of the 10-year research study. Steve’s journey to success is similar to the pedagogical evolution that I underwent as my colleagues and I created structures that were effective in reengaging at-risk, urban students in learning. The BONGO Program in which Steve was enrolled was the result of a long journey where each of the components was uncovered through a mixture of learning from other teachers, studying at university, and mostly by listening to students.
