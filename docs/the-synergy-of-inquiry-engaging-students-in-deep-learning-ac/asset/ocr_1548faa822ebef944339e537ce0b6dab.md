Students will soon realize that they are being asked to figure things out, to provide evidence, and to work hard with their peers at their thinking. Often, they lack the skills to accomplish all of this. They may also lack the skills to apply the new concepts and skills that they are obtaining to unique real-world situations. It is a process of acclimation. They may moan and groan after a few weeks and say things like, “just tell us the answer.” However, those pioneer teachers who persevere will soon find the whole class slowly climbing out of the gulch.

Persevering through this period of adjustment may be one of the most difficult things the teacher does in his or her career. It is natural to want to go back to the old way of teaching that one has already mastered, but keep in mind that inquiry teaching helps students develop deep understanding.

If you were lucky, maybe you used a microscope for a few minutes to look at a cell that you could not see too clearly anyway.
