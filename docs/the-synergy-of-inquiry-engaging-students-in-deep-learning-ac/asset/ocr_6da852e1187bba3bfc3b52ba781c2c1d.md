When Andrea first looked at the Library of Congress website, she was a bit skeptical about using primary sources with first graders. How could she use materials with them that they could not read or even understand? After planning her lesson and community project, she felt much more confident about teaching with primary sources, even to the youngest students. Primary sources could give them a window into history in a way that literature alone could not; photos, maps, letters, articles and, most of all, walking to the actual historic site in their own neighborhood was an awesome gift. She was so excited! Of course all of this led to deep understanding at a first-grade level of some complex social studies concepts and provided great motivation for Common Core sentence-writing skills.

# Using Nationally Validated Curricula Materials in Science and Mathematics

Nationally validated curricula materials that have been created through field-tested and researched National Science Foundation (NSF) funded projects provide valuable resources for science and mathematics teachers who wish to move toward inquiry teaching. These curricula have taken decades to develop and should be considered by any district or school looking to move to inquiry teaching. They have been field tested with thousands of students including gifted, special education, and language learners in urban, rural, and suburban areas. These materials provide a carefully devised package of the whole-inquiry learning experience. Any school or district looking to move toward inquiry teaching should visit these websites for an up to date list of the materials available

+   - http://www.nsf.gov/pubs/2002/nsf02084/chap1_4.htm
+   - http://www.nsresources.org/topic.cfm?topic=IM&allh=true

In mathematics, some of these curricula have been utilized in urban schools and districts and, when teachers were provided appropriate professional development, students have experienced enviable success. You will hear from a number of teachers who have utilized some of these curricula.

Although some of the middle- and high-school curricula do have some form of student book, the elementary FOSS and STC science curricula do not have textbooks. That is intentional. Each has suggested lists of science nonfiction trade books that are more appropriate for elementary grades and fit seamlessly with language arts lessons being taught in the same classroom. If you are looking for a way to increase engagement, differentiate instruction, and incorporate more informational text and student writing into your curriculum then Teaching with Text Sets (Cappiello and Thulin-Dawes 2012) is your answer. This must-have resource walks you through the steps to create and use multi-genre, multimodal text sets for content area (including science and math) and language arts study.
