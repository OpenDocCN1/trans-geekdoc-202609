Meier and the K–8 teachers at Mission Hill School in Boston focus on five habits of mind they think are seminal to their students’ success:

+   1. **Evidence:** How do we know what is true or false? What evidence counts? How sure can we be? What makes it credible to us?
+   2. **Viewpoint:** How else might this look if we stepped into other shoes? What would it look like if we were looking at it from a different direction? What would it look like if we had a different history or expectations?
+   3. **Connections/Cause and Effect:** Is there a pattern? Have we seen something like this before? What are the possible consequences?
+   4. **Conjecture:** Could it have been otherwise? Supposing that...? What if...?
+   5. **Relevance:** Does it matter? Who cares?

There are many other versions of habits of mind, some which are subject-area specific. For example, some in science follow:

# Experimental evidence might have more than one interpretation.

+   1. Be open-minded, willing to change your mind in the face of reliable evidence, and respect another’s point of view.
+   2. Look at a matter from many sides before drawing a conclusion. Do not jump to conclusions or decide on the basis of one observation; deliberate and examine until you are as sure as you can be.
+   3. Know that hypotheses are valuable, even if they turn out not to be true, if they lead to fruitful investigations.
+   4. Offer reasons for findings and consider reasons suggested by others.
+   5. Know that different explanations can often be given for the same evidence, and it is not always possible to tell which one is correct.
+   6. Know why curiosity, openness, and skepticism are so highly regarded in science and how these are incorporated into the way science is carried out; exhibit these traits in our lives and value them in others.

# Gather accurate and testable evidence.

+   1. Go to reliable sources for evidence. Challenge sources to make sure they are reliable.
+   2. Avoid being superstitious; realize that nothing happens without cause.
+   3. Be curious. Be careful and accurate in observations.
+   4. Keep records of investigations and observations; do not change the records later.
+   5. Know why it is important in science to keep honest, clear, and accurate records.
