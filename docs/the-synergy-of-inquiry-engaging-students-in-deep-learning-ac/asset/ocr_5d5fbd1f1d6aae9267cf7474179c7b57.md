Most of the time, one of the students will bring up how when they did surface area of the outside of similar rectangular prisms the outside surfaces had one centimeter grids drawn on them. By asking how this might help them measure what is inside, students will say what they need are little boxes to put inside, that way there will not be spaces between them if they put them in neatly one atop another.

Earlier Carolina used one clear plastic open-topped box so that students could see that when filled with beans, there are spaces between them, but when using the one centimeter cubes that she has, they can fill the space without gaps.

She gives each group a set of cubes to fill their boxes to see which box holds the most. However, she does not give them enough to fill every box, so they need to figure out a way to extrapolate beyond the level that they are at in the unfilled boxes. They soon realize that once they have a filled the first layer, they can see how many layers there are in the box by stacking just one cube atop another and multiply the number of layers by how many cubes are in each layer.
