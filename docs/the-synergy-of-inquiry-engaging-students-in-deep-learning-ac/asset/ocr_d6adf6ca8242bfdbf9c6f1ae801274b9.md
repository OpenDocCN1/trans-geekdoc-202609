# Observations About Using Physical Materials in Practice

The use of manipulatives is the norm in effective science and mathematics classrooms. However, many teachers think of physical historical materials such as small industrial tools or machines, uniforms or weapons, and such that can be borrowed from or viewed at museums as the only manipulatives that can be used in history classrooms. These are valuable materials. However, much of the time spent on a role play where students get to experience what previous generations in various circumstances had to endure is also a manipulation of materials—their own bodies and minds. We see an example of this with the bags of cotton students experienced in Mike’s class. This is followed by another manipulation of materials—interpretation of primary source documents. A textual analysis of writings related to John Brown’s raid written by contemporaries has students deeply analyze issues of point of view and writing for an audience. The following role play allows students to create their own point of view and defend it while solidifying the concept and enriching students’ common core skills.

The inquiry-process steps used in Mike’s history lesson closely follow the Common Core Reading Standards for Literacy in History/Social Studies for grades six to eight. Figure 1.3 presents these standards.

# Figure 1.3 Common Core Reading Standards for History/Social Studies, Grades 6–8

+   1. CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.6–8.1: Cite specific textual evidence to support analysis of primary and secondary sources.
+   2. CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.6–8.2: Determine the central ideas or information of a primary or secondary source; provide an accurate summary of the source distinct from prior knowledge or opinions.
+   3. CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.6–8.6: Identify aspects of a text that reveal an author’s point of view or purpose (for example, loaded language, inclusion or avoidance of particular facts).
+   4. CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.6–8.8: Distinguish among fact, opinion, and reasoned judgment in a text.
+   5. CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.6–8.9: Analyze the relationship between a primary and secondary source on the same topic.

(CCSSO 2010)

These strategies prepare students for any Common Core-based testing and develop the skills necessary for effective citizenry. The role-playing aspects that lead to preadolescents’ emotional reactions to past historical events deeply engage their personal sense of fairness and justice and transform “dead” history into a vibrant living experience.
