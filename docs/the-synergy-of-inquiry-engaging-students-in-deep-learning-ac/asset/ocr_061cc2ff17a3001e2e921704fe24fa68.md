However, some practitioners will find that even these nationally validated curricular activities can be opened up for more inquiry to allow for richer student experiences. Others, because of internal school politics, need to transform laboratory experiences already utilized in their schools. In either case, the suggestions in the section later in this chapter attempt to create a protocol that teachers could use to transform each of these materials into a richer inquiry experience.

# The Planning Process for Sixth-Grade Mathematics

Carolina Vila, a mathematics teacher from Andrews Middle School in Medford, Massachusetts, has modified a NSF nationally validated lesson from the Connected Math Program (CMP). She has embedded her lesson in a year of units based on social issues that matter to her students. Creating units around social issues allows students to engage in deeply creating academic understandings. (This topic is explored in detail in Chapter 5.)

The following are Carolina’s units for the year for sixth- and seventh-grade mathematics based on the Common Core State Standards (NGA and CCSSO 2010b). For a full description of Carolina’s plans, “Sixth Grade Math Year Overview and Unit Introduction,” refer to the Digital Resource CD.

# Unit 1: The Number System: Is Global Warming in Medford Real?

For this unit, students can delve into the controversial topic of global warming. This topic allows for opportunities in which students can use negative and positive numbers, decimals, fractions, percent, and so on.

# Unit 2: Expressions and Equations: How Did Carlos Slim Helu Become the Richest Man on Earth?

Here, students can use expressions and equations to create an excel sheet (or some other program) that quickly keeps track of a business’s orders, expenses, profits, and so on. They can create their own “businesses” in their small groups.
