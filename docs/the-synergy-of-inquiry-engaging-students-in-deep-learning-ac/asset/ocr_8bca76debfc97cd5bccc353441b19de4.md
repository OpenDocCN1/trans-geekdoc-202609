# Student Writing and Teacher’s Responses

Students in Maria’s class wrote down all the original data they collected and ideas they or their small research teams negotiated in their notebooks so that they would have an immutable record of the evolution of their ideas. However, each evening students began to create parts of their final published report of their explorations and findings. Maria assisted them by scaffolding the process, while simultaneously getting feedback about theirs, prompting students to consider another perspective or to reorganize their ideas in a more logical progression. It is vital that the teacher assist students in translating their oral language ideas into symbolic language. She also gave written responses to the final reports that students published. Unlike their notebooks, these pieces were published on sheets of paper and handed in. Maria also supplied feedback question prompts so that students could revise their, while the second was a hypothetical problem-solving application also involving a written response. The two application tasks are as follows:

+   1. If a plant cell in anaphase and telophase at the end of a mitotic sequence has genetic material made up of eight chromosomes, how many chromosomes will a non-dividing cell from the same plant have? Explain why you think this is the case using what you know about mitosis.
+   2. Look at each of the prepared microscope slides labeled *A*, *B*, and *C*. They are all pieces from a certain part of the same plant. One is from the middle of the stem, another is from the topmost part of the stem, and the other is from an older big leaf. Which one is most likely from the top most point of the stem? Explain your thinking that led you to your answer. Include some drawings.
