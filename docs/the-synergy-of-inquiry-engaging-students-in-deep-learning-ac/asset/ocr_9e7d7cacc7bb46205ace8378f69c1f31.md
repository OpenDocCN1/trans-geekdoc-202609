# Figure 2.7 Rubric for Assessing Student Use of Thinking Skills, Habits of Mind, and Concept Understanding

|  | Novice | Competent | Expert |
|---|---|---|---|
| Process Skill | Makes quick observations and draws without great detail. Places drawings in a sequence without careful thought to what is occurring in each sequence. | Uses careful observations, makes detailed drawings, and infers a sequence (model) based on explanations of what they think is occurring with the nuclear material. | In addition to skills in the competent category, is able to provide a different explanation of why the same or a different sequence can be explained using the same evidence. |
| Habit of Mind | Fails to see that the inferential explanation that they hold to support their sequence of stages is only one possible explanation of the same evidence. | Realizes that more than one inferential explanation can be given for the same sequence and that another sequence might be explained by another interpretation of the same evidence. | In addition to understanding various interpretations for the same evidence, also understands that this is a major component of the nature of science and the scientific enterprise. |
| Concept #1 | Recognizes that the nucleus looks different in various cells near one another, but does not have an explanation for the various layouts. | Recognizes when cells replicate, nuclear material appears in strands that differ from non-replicating cells. This predictable, sequential process is called mitosis. | Not only can sequence the stages, but can clearly explain what is occurring at each stage including an understanding of spindle fibers, the concept of two pairs of chromosomes relative to genes, and so on. |
| Concept #2 | Cannot explain if the genetic material in the two cells after mitosis is identical. | Can explain that genetic material in the nucleus is replicated and is included in both daughter cells. | Can infer that if the new cell needs to create (by mitotic division) other cells, then the material must be identical. |
| Concept #3 | When asked for another theory besides cells dividing that could explain growth, cannot come up with another explanation. | Can explain that cells need to replicate, rather than simply getting bigger, when growth occurs in organisms. | Can think of structural physics-based reasons why the bigger cell theory would literally not hold water. |
