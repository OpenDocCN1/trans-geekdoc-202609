# What Carolina Was Thinking

Unit four easily leant itself to being a hands-on inquiry unit where students uncovered mathematical solutions and a given concept during their small group explorations. Whether it was in 2-D or 3-D, manipulatives could be used each step of the way. Students filled in shapes with squares to prove their areas, as well as filled in figures with blocks to prove their volumes. Working under the essential question of “Can we trust our own eyes?” students also began to develop better estimation skills as they moved through the unit. However, the importance of exact measurement was tied in through explorations into the worlds of medicine, sports, machines, and so on. Since this unit was heavy with formulas, it was essential that students worked to uncover the formulas instead of the formulas being given to them.

This particular lesson aimed to have students uncover the best way to figure out how much a rectangular prism could hold (the volume). Traditionally, teachers told students that *V=lwh* (volume = length x width x height) was the formula and students would input the right numbers to arrive at the answer. This way of teaching volume, however, leads to only a few students fully understanding why the formula looks the way it does; no less being able to apply it to unique situations at the appropriate time. By having students arrive at the formula on their own, they reach a deeper understanding of why the formula is what it is. It also empowers them to be able to create other formulas later in the unit. Lastly, the lesson called for students to apply their own formulas, instead of one found in a math book.

In previous lessons, students have looked at measuring the surface area of boxes and have made boxes with one-centimeter grids on the outside. Carolina now wants them to move their understandings from two-dimensional measurement to three-dimensional measurement: volume.
