Additionally, I removed myself from the jury and acted solely as judge. I did this for two reasons. One, their grade was not based on who “won” the trial but on how they presented their case. By acting as the judge, it allowed me to focus more on their questions, use of the text, and so on. I had a rubric that I completed as each student participated in his or her portion of the trial. The jury (about nine people) was made up of the principal, the head of the English department, two aides (for moral support for some specific students within the class), and students who had taken my class the previous year and thus were familiar with the proceedings. The jury composition created an authentic audience for students. Having an audience other than me increases engagement for students and helps them to take it more seriously since it seems more real. The mock trial was a hit and students of all abilities were motivated, competitive, and showed what they knew about their text and the law system, in an interesting and engaging manner.

# Final Thoughts

Choice is not always easy to work into a lesson or unit. In my classroom, choice sometimes takes the form of options, both for students and me. I never have a single final assessment because that rarely gives a clear picture of what students know and what they can do with such knowledge. A heterogeneous class is comprised of students with different skills and interests. I have found that students engage more when they have some control over what they are doing. In the short story unit, they chose their movie, utilized technology, and interacted in small groups. I heard students talk who never participate during large group discussion. As with the mock trial, they too had an authentic audience comprised of their peers. They were not discussing the video to impress me; they were showing each other what they had learned and why the others should see that film. During the mock trial, students who really struggled with the text demonstrated their understanding of the novel when they worked with their team and then took the stand.
