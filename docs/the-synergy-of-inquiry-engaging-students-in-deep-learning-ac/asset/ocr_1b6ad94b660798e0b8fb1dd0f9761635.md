# Determining Who Contributed to a Group Written Report

With this single method of evaluation, I was not at all sure that each student had worked on the report and had acquired the concepts and skills expected. Many times, some hard-working student would do most of the report to be sure that she or he would receive a good grade. That student had almost always tried to divide up the work but found one or more students in the group did not come through with a level of understanding anywhere near theirs. When they groused about this enough at home, parents would come visit me at school about this unfairness.

In an attempt to get the more aware students on a given topic to begin enlisting the less interested or knowledgeable students during the exploration, I implemented the following. I informed students that there would be a group written report, and they would not know whether I would collect the report or give a quiz on the report to one student in the group the day it was handed in. Occasionally, I would give a two-question quiz based on the report that I held in my hand. For this quiz, I could call on any one person from the group whose answers would represent the grade for the group. This approach encouraged the team members who had more understanding and motivation to be sure they tutored those less enthusiastic. Since they soon discovered that tutoring after the fact was more difficult than getting the student to participate in the actual doing of the exploration, they began to devise ways of motivating these less enthusiastic members. Many other times, I would collect reports or other final projects written by each student in the group. However, as they were working on the exploration, they would not know whether the final “published” document would be a group or individual document. The reports or final projects would have some parts in common, but since each student needed to do the final synthesis, analysis, or creative expression, there would always be great differentiation.

Students and parents generally saw this as fair. Much of the time they were being evaluated on their understanding. Since most parents needed to work on a team at their jobs, they understood when I explained to them that I was giving their sons and daughters group leadership skills that would serve them well throughout the rest of their lives. Even though there were singular team reports, the other parts of the repertoire, used in slightly greater proportion, orchestrated a balance between fairness to individuals and team building.
