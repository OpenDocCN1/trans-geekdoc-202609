# What does this iterative process in the brain mean for instruction?

+   - A didactic information-delivery approach to teaching tends to focus on some of the back cortex functions.
+   - A solely open discovery approach, proposing and testing ideas without teacher intervention, focuses unsuccessfully on the front cortex functions.
+   - Inquiry teaching, where the teacher sets up the learning environment and explorations and facilitates students in their own figuring out, allows for a balanced integration and interaction between these physical entities in the brain.

If we teach to the test for memorization and other lower level thinking, much of what students know will exist in the back integrative cortex and will not be usable for real problem solving, and our students will not develop critical thinking skills. They will not own deep conceptual understanding, cannot apply any of their knowledge well, and it will not likely stay in long-term memory (Bransford, Brown, and Cocking 1999).

Our experiences are from the past, but the concepts that we create are for actions we intend to take in the future. They are plans. We need to transform experiences from *outside* the brain to knowing *inside* the brain. However, we must do more than just organize the data in our back temporal cortex. We need to actively make decisions and create ideas and concepts in the prefrontal cortex. We move the power from external others to ourselves. That action transforms the learner from just a receiver to an active producer of knowledge (Howard 2002).

Many people think of the physical brain and learning about it as a cold, analytical look at learning devoid of many of the more human aspects of our species. It is rather the opposite. It is the interaction of the cognition structures with the emotion structures, the amygdala and the basal structures, of the brain that helps us understand reasoning, motivation, and memory without which learning is not possible. It is why as teachers we need to understand the interplay between these fear and pleasure emotional structures, and the cortical cognitive structures, in order to improve our classroom practice.
