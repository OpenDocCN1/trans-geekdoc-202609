Once these needs are met, students will not only engage in the study of the analogous societal relationships, but will delve into the deepest esoteric questions that are posed. In addition, they will acquire conflict resolution skills and coping mechanisms that they can utilize in their everyday interpersonal relationships. The personalization of the subject matter by having students investigate these analogous interrelationships between their own personal interrelationships and those that exist at a societal level is a central element of the most successful of these programs (Jablon 1989). My partner in team teaching for 13 years, Terry Born, taught me this technique of role plays and together we enriched it to become Analogous Interrelationship Role Plays as described here.

# Example of an Analogous Interrelationship Role Play

Three students assume the roles of the following characters:

+   - Fifteen-year-old high school student who must work long hours to add to her family's income because a parent has recently died
+   - Student’s teacher who is upset by the drop in quality of the student’s work in her class
+   - Student’s parent who is caught between wanting the best for the student and the rest of the family’s welfare

This role play has allowed classes of students studying about the cutting of the rainforests to understand the complex relationship between the poor native inhabitants cutting the rainforests (the student), the government of the third world country who is encumbered with a large international debt (the parent), and the environmentalists trying to help both the native and the future world ecological situation (the teacher). Students not only had a unique insight into world economics but into their own personal relationships and therefore identified positively with their academic classes.

If the psychological needs of students are met as they are engaged in activities with the other students in the societal class, then a positive alternative peer group is created that students can go to as an alternative to their negative neighborhood peer groups where they normally turn in their search for fulfillment.
