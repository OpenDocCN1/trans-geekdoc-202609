Two of the slides had very few or no cells in mitotic division; the third slide of the tip of the stem had a number of cells in various stages of mitosis expected at a place of continual growth and cell replication.

# Additional Assessment Evidence

+   - General notes for monitoring student progress on mitosis from her observations of students while they were working in their small research groups
+   - Written responses that she received from each student for homework where they were asked to sequence their drawings and provide an explanation for their choice of sequence
+   - Published exploration reports that she collected after the concept clarification stage; this was ongoing feedback because written feedback had been provided and students kept revising their thinking and communication of that thinking

# What We Were Thinking

Students will still need to take quizzes and multiple-choice tests throughout their educational careers, so it benefits us to have them practice taking these forms of assessment. This is true even though the tests and quizzes do not challenge their level of understanding as much, nor demonstrate as clearly to us what they have and have not as yet mastered. These quizzes and multiple-choice tests should be a much smaller part of a teacher’s assessment repertoire than is currently the norm. They should be used mainly to allow students to understand how to master this format of testing.
