## The Dark Web and Bitcoin

As soon as bitcoin came to the market, it became a highly demanded currency on the dark web. But before moving forward, for those who are new, let me explain the term **dark web**. The dark web comprises the remaining 96% of the web which we cannot access directly through our normal web browser (Why?). Because it is dark!, meaning that there are websites that involve illegal activities such as selling drugs, cocaine, fake passports, and even human trafficking. Yes! People even do human trafficking through the web. The dark web always liked bitcoin because bitcoin is near untraceable. That’s the reason why bitcoin's popularity was initially associated with illicit use.

One famous example of this is the **Silk Road**, which is famous for illegal activities like selling drugs and fake passports. There is even a recent link where paying some bitcoin would get GRE, TOEFL, and SAT scores at the top! But these cannot be accessed directly; you need to have a specific browser (`TOR browser`) to gain access. We won’t be going much into this topic, but we can conclude that one of the biggest markets for cryptocurrencies is the dark web. Remember there are many things hidden from you; if you start exploring the dark web you’ll find them out, but I won’t recommend that—not until you have enough experience. ## WannaCry and Bitcoin

Let's start by talking about what **WannaCry** is. It was a massive, large-scale ransomware attack that caused losses of up to 4 billion dollars to companies, hospitals, and other individuals. Let's try to understand how this attack got successful at such a large scale.

The ransomware attack exploited the vulnerability of Windows Server Message Block (`SMB`), whose exploit was created by the `NSA` (National Security Agency). The `NSA` named the exploit **EternalBlue**. Rather than reporting the vulnerability to Microsoft, the `NSA` wanted to use it for their profit. Later on, a group of hackers named **The Shadow Brokers** released the exploit publicly. This exploit came under the radar of WannaCry developers. WannaCry was written in `Microsoft Visual C++` so that there wouldn’t be any compatibility issues while attacking.

The WannaCry virus first tries to find a **kill switch** (a simple way of forced shutdown); if it is not found, the ransomware encrypts your data and then tries to exploit the `SMB` (Server Message Block) vulnerability to spread itself to other networks. The WannaCry virus comes with a self-transporting mechanism to spread itself. When it found its host, it uses the `EternalBlue` exploit and `DoublePulsar` tool to gain access to install and execute itself. WannaCry virus came with a display window like the one below where it asks its users to pay through bitcoin, and that’s where our main part comes. Before Microsoft could eventually stop WannaCry, it was able to spread rapidly, and we all know how much it cost. Now the major problem was that WannaCry attacked not only private organizations but hospitals. If a hospital loses its patients' dose records, it would create a life-and-death situation for patients; that was the reason most hospitals paid the ransom.

WannaCry ransomware asks the user to pay through bitcoin. Now the problem was that bitcoin ledgers were public, but from whom to whom the transaction goes was untraceable. That means suppose if the victim paid the attacker the ransom, we can see how many bitcoins are getting exchanged, but we can neither track the attacker nor the victim.

The attackers exploited the advantage of bitcoin, and that’s why the ransomware was completely successful. It was bitcoin at the end which made sure the attacker's identity remained secured. It is just like the money is floating in front of you but you cannot see from where it is originating and where it is ending.

Although the ransomware was stopped by Microsoft emergency patches, it still cost a lot of damage. After discussing this, you may want to ask whether it’s a good idea to continue with cryptocurrency. Well, the answer to that can still be given as a big **YES!** Because at the initial stage, it was the `NSA` and the companies who didn’t respond to the situation properly. The `NSA` could have stopped it if they reported it to Microsoft; the hospitals and companies could have prevented it by continuously updating their software. At least I still believe cryptocurrency is still good. You have the right to disagree :).

## Conclusion

We will finally conclude this chapter by discussing the future of bitcoin. Bitcoin is no doubt one of the safest cryptocurrencies, but can it be implemented for mass usage? Well, for now, it may. Why? The answer is simple: because the cost of systems required to download every single transaction of bitcoin is usually high, and as bitcoin mining becomes expensive, it may lead to freezing of the mining system.

The second important reason is that there is no proper authority to prevent the misuse of bitcoin. That is the reason why bitcoin is usually preferred by the dark web. Also, the dark web requires anonymity, which bitcoin provides in a way; you can say that bitcoin is helping criminals to perform crimes. One of the biggest examples of this can be what was discussed earlier regarding the Silk Road. This is the reason why bitcoin has a low image in the common public.

The third important reason can be given is the anonymous creators themselves. No one knows much about the founders of bitcoin; no one can estimate how much bitcoin they would own. Any of the founders may suddenly come out, reveal their bitcoins, and may cause huge inflation in price.
