# 1.4 Other regression techniques

Although for many chemical problems the use of a linear regression model is often sufficient to provide reliable predictions and, in these cases, the large majority of the applications involve the use of one of the three techniques discussed above, sometimes the functional relation to be approximated by more complex and non-linear methods can be adopted. Since artificial neural networks (ANN) (Marini, Bucci, Magri, & Magri, 2008) and support vector machines (SVM) (Brereton & Lloyd, 2010) are described in other chapters of this book, here we will briefly mention how to implement non-linearities in the context of the models described so far.

In the case of PCR, a non-linear relation between the Y- and X-block can be accomplished by augmenting the scores matrix with quadratic and interaction terms, for putting it estimating for the MLR model. For instance, if three PCs are extracted from the X-block, the scores matrix can be augmented as follows:

```
$T_{aug} = [t_1 \ t_2 \ t_3 \ t_1^2 \ t_2^2 \ t_3^2 \ t_1\circ t_2 \ t_1\circ t_3 \ t_2\circ t_3] \quad (2.33)$
```

where the symbol $\circ$ indicates Hadamard (element-wise) product of the two vectors. Then, the predicted response is estimated as a linear combination of the columns of $T_{aug}$.

On the other hand, in the case of PLS (Berglund & Wold, 1997), non- can be imposed at the level of the inner relation, instead of Eq. (2.28), any non-linear function $f$ can be used to approximate the Y-scores from the X-scores (Wold, Kettah-Wold, & Skagerberg, 1989):

```
$$\hat{u}_a = f(t_a). \quad (2.34)$
```

Moreover, the same kernel trick used in the context of support vector machines (SVM) can also be coupled to PLS regression, leading to kernel PPS (Rosipal, 2003).

# 2. Classification

In chemometrics, “classifying” refers to the possibility of creating models that allow assigning samples to classes (or categories) on the basis of the chemical data for this purpose. The aims of classification are manifold; just to mention some, in the context of quality control, classifiers can be applied to establish whether samples deviate from compliant batches or not; otherwise, in forensic science, classification approaches are widely used in order to prevent frauds (e.g., food or drug adulteration).
