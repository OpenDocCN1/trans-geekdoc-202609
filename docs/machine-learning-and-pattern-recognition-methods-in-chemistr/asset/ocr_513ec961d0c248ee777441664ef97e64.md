# 3. Applications

Gemperline and Cash used the P-ALS algorithm for comparing the results of applying hard and soft constraints in the SMCR analysis of four different experiments (one simulated HPLC-DA data set and three experimental data sets) (Gemperline & Cash, 2003). They demonstrated that the results of the SMCR methods under hard constraints are distorted in many cases, and this can significantly affect the quantitative accuracy of the results. The average peak area errors for simulated HPLC-DA with soft constraints using a penalty weight of 1.0 were less (~2%), whereas the errors with hard constraints, using a penalty weight of 20, were significantly larger (>12%). In the analysis of experimental data sets, a larger fraction of the total variance of data was modified with soft constraints, and the resolved profiles were less noisy. In addition, applying soft constraints allowed a reasonable model with improved profiles in comparison with the hard constraints. Gemperline and coworkers (Lake et al., 2022) also demonstrated the impact of using soft constraints in the analysis of three-way data. The results revealed that the application of the NWAY P-ALS algorithm with soft constraints allows a reduction in the number of influential active constraints and enables monotonic and smooth convergence.

Neymeer, Abdollahi, and their coworkers demonstrated the impact of soft constraints on the range of feasible solutions for three-component systems using FAC-PACK software (Sawall et al., 2013; Sawall & Neymeer, 2014). They investigated the effect of soft constraints on the AFs, namely, constraints on non-negativity, equality, closure, unimodality, and windowing. Several simulated and experimental data sets were used for showing the advantages of applying soft hard constraints. In the case of non-negativity constraints, different levels of the control parameter, e, were checked. Since larger e allow larger negative entries in the resolved profiles, the AFs segments were inflated by increasing the value of e.
