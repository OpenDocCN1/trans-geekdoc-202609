There are two different methods, to overcome the narrow dynamic range and use pH indicators in a broader range, efficiently. An option is to use a mixture of indicators in a solution (Bai et al., 2013; Barati, Shampsipur & Abdollahi, 2006; Lin & Liu, 2000; Martinez-Olmos et al., 2011; Qi et al., 2015; Safavi & Bagheri, 2003; Santos et al., 2014). Another alternative is to use an array of pH indicators (Capel-Cuevas, Cuellar, de Orbe-Payá, Pegalar, & Capatán-Villi, 2011; Lin & Liu, 2000; Martinez-Olmos et al., 2011; Mosley et al., 2004) that each indicator is a sensitive element and all responses from all sensor elements are utilized for pH determination.

Here, two aspects were considered in pH sensors optimization: (1) tuning equilibrium constants of indicators to achieve continuous pH range and (2) designing optimum combination (array or mixture) of pH indicators based on enriching analytical sensitivity.

In order to show the applicability of the idea for experimental data, three different indicators are selected (methyl orange, neutral red, and phenol red). pH is not covered by these three indicators efficiently, in the range between 0.5 and 10. In this study, the chemical behavior of pH indicators in aqueous solution is well known, the chemical sensor was designed in aqueous solution. Also, the chemical models of pH indicator behavior in the micellar solution have been investigated.
