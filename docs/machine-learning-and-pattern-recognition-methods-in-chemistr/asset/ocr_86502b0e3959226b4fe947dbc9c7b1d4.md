The P-ALS approach can demonstrate the impact of soft constraints on set of feasible solutions of the curve resolution problem. However, a fundamentally different approach is to solve the effect of soft constraints on the whole range of feasible solutions. The polygon algorithm (Sawall, Kubis, Selent, Boerner, & Neymeyer, 2013; Sawall & Neymeyer, 2014) can be applied for this purpose in three-component systems by the use of a cost function that has the ability to apply soft constraints. The idea behind the polygon algorithm is to approximate numerically each isolated area of feasible solutions (AFS) by a successive increase in the size of predefined polygons. The feasibility of a certain concentration or spectral profile with the corresponding coordinates in the abstract space (1, $\alpha$, $eta$) is checked by solving a computationally expensive minimization problem. In this problem, a submatrix P ($2 \times 2$) in T would be found to solve the factorization problem with the considered constraints.

$$\mathbf{T} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \alpha & \beta \ 1 & p_{11} & p_{12} \ 1 & p_{21} & p_{22} \end{bmatrix}$ (1.6)
