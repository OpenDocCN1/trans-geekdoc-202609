In case of continuous latent variables, we can use a reparameterization trick for computing unbiased estimator for $
abla_\phi \mathcal{L}_\phi(\mathbf{x})$. This stochastic estimate allows us to optimize the ELBO using SGD (see Fig. 6.8).

For continuous latent variables and a differentiable encoder and generative model, the ELBO can be straightforwardly differentiated w.r.t. both $\phi$ and $	heta$ through a change of variables, also the reparameterization trick (Kingma & Welche, 2013; Rezende, Mohamed, & Wierstra, 2014).

First, we express the random variable $\mathbf{z} \sim q_\phi(\mathbf{x})$ as some differentiable (and invertible) transformation of another random variable $\epsilon$, given $\mathbf{z}$ and $\phi$:

$$$\mathbf{z} = \mathbf{g}(oldsymbol{\epsilon}, \phi, \mathbf{x}) \quad (6.26)$$

where the distribution of random variable $\epsilon$ is independent of $\mathbf{x}$ or $\phi$.
Such a change of variable, expectations can be rewritten in terms of $\epsilon$,

$$$\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})} [f(\mathbf{z})] = \mathbb{E}_{p(oldsymbol{\epsilon})} [f(\mathbf{z})] \quad (6.27)$$

where $\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{g}(oldsymbol{\epsilon}, \phi, \mathbf{x})$ and the expectation and gradient operators commute, and we can perform a simple Monte Carlo estimator:

$$$
abla_\phi \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})} [f(\mathbf{z})] = 
abla_\phi \mathbb{E}_{p(oldsymbol{\epsilon})} [f(\mathbf{z})] = \mathbb{E}_{p(oldsymbol{\epsilon})} [
abla_\phi f(\mathbf{z})] \cong 
abla_\phi f(\mathbf{z}) \quad (6.28)$$

where $\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{g}(\phi, \mathbf{x}, oldsymbol{\epsilon})$ with noise sample $\epsilon \sim p(\epsilon)$. See Fig. 6.9 for an illustration.
