# 4. Conclusions

By applying soft constraints in SMCR methods, small deviations from the constraint settings are allowed. Apart from the algorithm used, this approach may yield more accurate results in many circumstances than obtained by applying hard constraints. The estimated profiles of the SMCR methods with soft constraints are more likely to have fewer active constraints and distortions. These advantages can be attributed to the increased control and flexibility in the implementation of the constraints. Several software implementations are available to users for incorporating a variety of soft constraints, most of which are described in the text. Among them, those who calculate the whole range of feasible solutions under soft constraints are especially helpful. Soft constrained AFSs often have fewer active constraints and are more likely to fit the original research hypothesis in comparison with hard constrained AFS segments. The boundaries can be tuned to allow acceptable levels of noise in a data set to propagate as small deviations into estimated profiles and estimate spectra that would otherwise not be allowed with hard constraints.
