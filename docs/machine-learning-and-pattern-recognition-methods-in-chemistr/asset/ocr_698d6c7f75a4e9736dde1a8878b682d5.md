Therefore,

```
$$K_C' = K_C \frac{1 + K_{C,I}[I]}{1 + K_{A,I}[I]} \quad (4.8)$
```

In this system of complete and apparent reaction schemes, an analytical relationship between the real and the apparent constants can be established. According to Eq. (4.8), the value of the apparent constant is defined by the constants of the complete model $K_C$, $K_{A,I}$ and $K_{C,I}$ as well as the free [I] of the interferent I. Eq. (4.8) also shows that the influence of [I] on $K_C'$ is more prominent if the equilibrium constants $K_{A,I}$ and $K_C$ are dissimilar. If the interferent is added in large excess, its free concentration is approximately constant and thus also the value for the apparent constant $K_C'$. If just the reaction A gets involved in the side reaction, $K_{C,I}$ would be zero, and in the situation where only the product (C) is involved in the side reaction, $K_{A,I}$ is zero.

Now, this question can be asked under what situation, the apparent model and the related constant $K_C'$ are applicable in an equilibrium system. By involving the side reaction(s), total concentrations of all components can be applied as controllable variables for changing [I]. Thus, Eq. (4.5) is applicable, where [I] is independent of the total concentrations of all involved components (A, B, and I).

The question is, “under what circumstances the apparent model and the related constant $K_C'$ be applied to an equilibrium system?” By involving the side reaction(s), the total concentrations of all components will influence [I]. Thus, Eqs. (4.5) and (4.8) are applicable where [I] is independent of the total concentrations of all involved components (A, B, and I). In this section, we investigate situations where the apparent model is valid.

Here, the effect of concentration of interferent I (which is introduced to the system and can be used to tune the apparent constant on [I] and as a result, the stability of the apparent constant during a typical titration is investigated. As an example, in Fig. 4.3, the value of [I] as a function of $C_I$ during the titration for $K_C = 10^6$, $K_{A,I} = 10^{-4.5}$, and $K_{C,I} = 10^{-1.2}$ has been shown. It is clearly manifested that in the low total concentrations of the interferent I, the free concentration of [I], and thus the apparent constant are not independent of the equilibrium situation (Part A in Fig. 4.3), while Part B shows the region where $C_I$ and [I] are independent. Therefore, since [I] in each titration is approximately constant in a certain range of total concentrations of I, (the percent change of [I] during the system under study is $\le1\%$), this range of $C_I$ can be applicable for applying the apparent model.
