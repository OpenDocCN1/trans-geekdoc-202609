Normally, the spectroscopic techniques that are employed do not give a selective response for each chemical species present in a mixture, and the chemical separation techniques (for example, chromatography) cannot isolate one or more species from the mixture in a pure state; thus, the applied constraints do not add sufficient information to completely resolve the curve resolution problem. In such cases, in most MCR fits, nonunique solutions are obtained. In such cases, it is recommended to calculate the whole range of feasible solutions for both identification and quantification purposes. There are different SMCR techniques for calculating the range of feasible solutions either by determining a useful approximation or by defining exact ranges. The interested reader is referred to a good review on this subject (Golshan, Maeder, and Abdollahi, 2013). However, in some circumstances, unique solutions can be obtained in SMCR under the implied constraints. The conditions that are required for getting unique solutions have been the subject of several papers (Karimvand et al., 2020; Lakeh, Abdollahi, and Rajkö, 2022; Rajko, Abdollahi, Beyramsoltan, and Omidikia, 2015).

**Hard soft constraints.** One of the important factors that can affect the reliability of the MCR solutions is how the constraints are applied. Generally, there are two ways for introducing physicochemical information to the MCR model: hard and soft constraints. “Hard” constraints are strictly imposed on the model, while “soft” constraints accept small deviations from the previously known information. For example, small negative deviations are permitted when applying soft non-negative constraints. In practice, soft constraints are useful to allow for the effects of noise and ideal behaviors of chemical mixtures and responses. Several studies illustrate that applying soft constraints in SMCR models usually favors fewer active constraints and lower lock-of-fit in comparison with hard constraints. Thus, soft constraints are more likely to fit the original research hypothesis discussed before. Several algorithms have been proposed for implementing soft constraints in SMCR methods and also for studying the effect of these constraints on the range of feasible solutions. The main algorithms for imposing soft constraints are penalty alternating least squares (P-ALS) (Gemperline & Cash, 2003; Richards, Miller, & Gemperline, 2008), polygon inflation (Rahimdoust Mojdehi, Sawall, Neymeyr, & Abdollahi, 2016; Sawall et al., 2015), and grid search algorithm (Lakeh, Abdollahi, & Gemperline, 2020; Tavakkoli, Abdollahi, & Gemperline, 2020).
