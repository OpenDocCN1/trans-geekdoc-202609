Fig. 1.5 depicts the effect of soft non-negativity constraints on the accuracy of the AFSs in the column space of a simulated data with normally distributed random noise (with a standard deviation of 0.001) described in Rahimdoust Mojdehi et al. (2016). The known concentration profiles of this data, which have small deviations from the non-negativity constraints, were projected into the abstract column space (indicated as black stars in Fig. 1.5). As be seen in Fig. 1.5A, the AFSs obtained with hard constraints do not include the black stars (the coordinates of the known concentration profiles). This means that the boundaries of the hard constrained AFSs were well defined and their corresponding profiles contained active constraints due to the presence of noise. However, as be seen in Fig. 1.5B, applying soft non-negativity constraints with $\varepsilon=10^{-7}$ caused a small expansion of the AFSs. In this way, profiles with small non-negative entries, as well as the known profiles, were accepted to be within the feasible regions.
