# 5. Future work

NIR-HSI is gaining traction as a feasible technique for the detection and classification of microplastics directly in sand, soil, and marine sediments, which is an exciting prospect given the labor-intensive nature of separating microplastics from heterogeneous solid matrices. Given that this is a relatively new application of NIR-HSI, it is important to understand the limits and uncertainty associated with it. The results obtained clearly indicate that:

+   (1) The NIR radiation penetrates the microparticle, suggesting a “translectance” effect between the sand and the plastic. This not only amplifies the signal of the microplastic, but also amplifies the signal of the sand, which appears to have high reflectivity power. The small size of the microparticle, together with its low thickness, suggests that even the spectra de located in the middle of the microparticle suffer from the high influence of the spectra of the sand.

+   (2) The spectra of the edges of the microplastic are certainly highly affected by the scattering of the sand. This suggests uncertainty in the characterization of those pixels as microplastics, creating an uncertainty boundary that must be further studied.

As before, this work is an introduction to a more complex topic where different theories and algorithms will be tested to calculate the spatial and spectral limits of detection.

From a more general perspective, these results call into question the strategy of constructing libraries containing only the spectra of pure plastic and sand (or different varieties of sand). This chapter demonstrates that classification models using standardized signals might create too narrow of boundaries in the classification models, impeding their ability to find microplastics in environmental media.

This last theory presented here about the limits of detection in the spatial and spectral domains requires further study. The proposal is not finished, since there are many aspects that should be considered. Concepts like analyte signal and linearity of NIR radiation will be evaluated to fully understand the capabilities of NIR-HSI and to assess the extent to which NIR-HSI, together with chemometrics, is able to give an accurate and reliable answer.
