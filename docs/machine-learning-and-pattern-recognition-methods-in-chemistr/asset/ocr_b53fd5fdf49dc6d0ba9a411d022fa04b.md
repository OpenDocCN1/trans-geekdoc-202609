In the case of applying hard constraints, as can be seen in Fig. 1.6, the feasible bands of the starting material and the added reagent were estimated inaccurately. However, when soft constraints were used, the AFSs were calculated smoothly and their boundaries defined concentration bands with fewer active constraints. Furthermore, the feasible bands of the spectral profiles obtained with soft non-negativity, unimodality, and closure constraints contain the pure spectral profiles of acetic anhydride and butane.

Additionally, it is demonstrated in Rahimdoudst Mojdehi et al. (2016) that implementing soft constraints instead of hard constraints in data with no rotational ambiguity results in an increase in the accuracy of the model and a decrease in the model lack-of-fit.

Gemperlini and his coworkers used the geometric search algorithm for implementing soft non-negativity, known-value, and trilinearity constraints (Lake et al., 2020; Tavakkoli et al., 2020). In one study (Lake et al., 2020), a second-order consecutive reaction was simulated, and few concentration values of the desired product were used to demonstrate the effect of implementing soft known-value constraints. It was assumed that the known concentration values were obtained from sampling of a batch reaction at four different times. Different levels of noise and deviations from the true values were constrained to approach simulations of the experiments. The results of the gnd search method by obtaining soft nonnegativity constraints showed three distinct regions for both concentration and spectral modes. As can be seen in Fig. 1.7, adding soft known-value constraints to the previous analysis caused a significant decrease in the amount of rotational ambiguity and, as a consequence, an increase in the accuracy of the results. When hard equality constraints were used in the curve resolution of this data, a distorted profile was obtained with its coordinates outside the AFSs in the column space.
