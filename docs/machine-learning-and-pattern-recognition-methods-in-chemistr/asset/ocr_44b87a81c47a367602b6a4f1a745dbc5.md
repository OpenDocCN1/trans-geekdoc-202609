# Machine learning and pattern recognition methods in chemistry

The efficiency of the biosensors and chemo-sensors is governed by the amount of equilibrium constants, which define the sensitivity and selectivity of sensing processes. The difference in the affinity constant of different analytes by receptor makes their quantitative and qualitative analysis possible in competitive reactions. Usually, for improving the sensitivity or selectivity of sensors, the equilibrium constant has been tuned by optimizing the temperature of the reactions. Indeed, by tuning the equilibrium constant, the affinity of analytes to the receptors can be controlled and placed under optimum conditions.

The pH indicators are versatile for the detection of the pH of a solution, a great number of sensors utilize indicators. The color change of pH indicators is dependent on their protonation constant (logK). Thus, by tuning the apparent constants of indicators in chemical environmental conditions, the apparent constant of pH indicators can be shifted to the desired value and also the transition pH. It means that the range of transition interval of an indicator can be tuned.

There are several reports in which pH indicators have been used as the probe in sensor arrays to the identification of different targets (Mac Craith et al., 1997; Musto, Lin, Slick, 2009; Sanchez-Barragan, Costa-Fernandez, & Sanz-Medel, 2005). In such sensors, the identification of an analyte is dependent on the pH of the chemical environment. Interaction of different targets by the appropriate receptors changes the solution of pH, in the transition interval of the probe indicator, which produces the signal for the identification of the target analytes. Since the detection method in such sensors is dependent on the changing pH, by tuning the apparent constant of pH indicators, efficient identification of a range of target analytes will be.
