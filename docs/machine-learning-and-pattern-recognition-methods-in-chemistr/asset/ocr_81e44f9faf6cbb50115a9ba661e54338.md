# 1. Introduction

For many equilibrium systems, it is customary to use a simplified
arent, instead of a complete but rather complex, equilibrium system.
resulting apparent equilibrium constants are altered or controlled by changes
the chemical system, usually modifying the concentration of auxiliary
agents, but the variation in other parameters such the intensity of light in
photo-controlled reactions, types and concentration of solvents or composition
of mixed solvents, the ionic strength of the chemical media, etc. is also possible.

It is common to use the conditional formation constant, or the effective
formation constant in interactions between metal ions and protons of
synthetic chelating agents such as EDTA, EGTA, and with pH indicators.
For example the following equilibrium exhaustively describe all interactions
between metal ion Ca²+ with pH indicator Murexid (M) in aqueous
solution (Ringbom, 1963) (Fig. 4.1).

The collection of 8 equilibrium constants (K₁, K₂, K₃, K₄, K₅, K₁,
₂, and K₈) together with the total concentrations for the three compo-
ents quantitatively defines the species in any solution of the three compo-
rents. For the most relevant applications, i.e., complexometric titrations,
this complex equilibrium system is reduced to an apparent simple equilib-
rium under the condition of constant pH. Most titrations are conducted
at a certain pH in buffered solutions.
