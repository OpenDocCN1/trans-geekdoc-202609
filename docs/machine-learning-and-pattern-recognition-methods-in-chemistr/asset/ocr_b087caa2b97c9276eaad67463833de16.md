The analytical methods are fast enough to calculate and draw the abstract and feasible bands in less than 1s using an average commercial computer. The analytical SMCR methods are limited to use on those systems that have noise only, and they are very useful to investigate the microscopic structures of chemical data and visualize the effects of several constraints and limitations on the accuracy of bilinear curve methods.

The effect of equality constraints on the reduction of the rotational ambiguity can be very different. The relative position of the known component in the area of feasible solution (AFS) explains the behavior. A measure is suggested that the impact of the equality constraints (Sawall et al., 2020). The representation of this measure in the area of feasible solutions shows strong variations in the restrictiveness of equality constraints. This characteristic number can also be considered as restrictiveness measure. A relatively large reduced AFS shows that the equality constraint cannot reduce the rotational ambiguity. The profile that is locked by the equality constraint is compatible to many other solutions. In contrast to this, a small reduced AFS means that the choice of the equality constraint is very restrictive for the factorization problem. An equality constraint strongly reduces the AFS if it has a high restrictiveness.

Similarly, a weak AFS reduction corresponds to a low restrictiveness. A restrictiveness estimator is based on the volume of the reduced AFS under the equality constraint. The constraint is a measure of restrictiveness named κ1. The ambiguity estimators not only calculate for single points, but compute the estimators for every point in the AFS.
