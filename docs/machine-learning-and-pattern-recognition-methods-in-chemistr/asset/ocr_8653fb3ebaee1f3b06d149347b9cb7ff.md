# 1. Introduction

# 1.1 Adding “microplastics” to the dictionary

Though a world without plastics is scarcely imaginable today, wide-scale
plastic production only began in 1950. In the intervening 70 years, the plas-
tics industry manufactured 8300 million metric tons (Mt) of virgin
plastics—a full 60% of which is sitting in landfills or accumulating in the nat-
ural environment (Geyer, Janbeck, & Law, 2017). According to the 2020
Plastics Europe Report, global production of plastics reached 368 million
tons—an 9 million tons from the previous year. Packaging,
which is generally single use and quickly discarded, represented a full
39.4% of the 50 tons of plastics used in Europe in 2019 (Plastics Europe,
2020). During the inaugural International Research Workshop on the
Occurrence, Effects and Fate of Microplastic Marine Debris hosted by
NOAA in 2008, microplastics were defined as all particles smaller
than 5mm in size, based on their potential for ingestion by biota
(Arthur & Baker, 2008). Later guidance refined the definitions as
listed in Table 5.1 (Guidance on Monitoring of Marine Litter in in European
Seas, 2013).
