## 3.1 Case study 1

According to the IUPAC definition, the transition interval of an acid-base (pH) indicator is the concentration range of a chemical species that can be easily detected using the change of color of two forms of an indicator. The capability of indicators in changing their color near the equivalent point in acid-base titrations is dependent on their acidity dissociation constant (pKa). The conditional dependence of the apparent constant of some pH indicators has been reported previously (Safavi et al., 2009). It has been shown that the transition point and transition interval as the apparent constant of pH indicators have been altered under the influence of the different types of ionic liquids and Also, the effect of differing-concentrations of micellar pseudo-phase on the apparent acidity of a pH indicator has been investigated (Babamoradi & Abdollahi, 2015; Sajeddi-Amin, Abdollahi, & Naseri, 2021). Inspiring the tuning of the apparent constants, the goal of this case study is to tune the pKa of acid-base neutral red (NR) in micellar pseudo-phase for improving the range of its transition points and transition interval for more applications.

Aqueous micellar solutions have several applications in analytical chemistry; they can enhance the solubility of solutes, and can also shift the observed equilibrium constants, in particular the protonation constants of weak acids, e.g., pH indicators. Further, shifts in absorption spectra and changing equilibrium and kinetic constants have been observed due to change in the chemical/physical properties of the solutions. These effects are due to interactions of solutes with micelles, ie., the solutes can either remain in the aqueous phase or be dissolved in the micellar phase. Naturally, these solvents will affect many properties of dissolved chemicals (Beltran, Codony, Granados, Izquierdo, & Prat, 1993; Beltran, Prat, & Codony, 1995; Dutta & Bhat, 1993; Funasaki, 1979; Underwood, 1982).

A complete model that describes the behavior of aqueous micellar systems is the partition model in which the solutes are distributed between the aqueous phase and the micellar pseudo-phase (Blatt, Chatelier, & Sawyer, 1984). The acid-base equilibrium of neutral red (NR) in aqueous micellar solutions of Brij-35 (polyethylene (23) laur ether) is considered in the present case (Babamoradi & Abdollahi, 2015). Based on the above model, both forms of NR are distributed between the aqueous phase and micellar pseudo-phase. The corresponding distribution coefficients, $K_{d1}$ and $K_{d2}$, are defined as follows:

$$K_{d1} = \frac{[NR]_m}{[NR]_w} (4.9)$$

$$K_{d2} = \frac{[NRH^+]_m}{[NRH^+]_w} (4.10)$$

where $[NRH^+]_w$ and $[NRH^+]_m$ are the local concentrations of $NR^+$ in aqueous and micellar phases, respectively, and $[NR]_w$ and $[NR]_m$ represent the local concentrations of deprotonated form of NR in both phases, respectively.

By ignoring the distribution of protonation and deprotonation forms of acid between aqueous and micellar phases in the complete model, the simple apparent model of system is the same as Eq. (4.11):

$$H^+ + NR \equiv NRH^+ \quad K'_{NRH,H} = \frac{[NRH^+]}{[H^+][NR]} (4.11)$$

We define $NR^+$ as the sum of the species $NR^+$ in both aqueous and micellar phases ($NR^+ = [NRH^+]_w + [NRH^+]_m$) and $NR'$ as the sum of the species NR in both phases ($NR' = [NR]_w + [NR]_m$). Thus, as the total concentrations of species $NR^+$ and $NR$ in different concentration of surfactant, we can consider:

$$NRH^{+/+} = [NRH^+]_w (1 - R) + [NRH^+]_m (R) (4.12)$$

$$NR' = [NR]_w (1 - R) + [NR]_m (R) (4.13)$$

In these equations, $R$ is the ratio of micellar volume ($V_m$) to the total volume of solution ($V$), which is defined as:

$$R = \frac{V_m}{V} = (C_m - cmc) V\Phi (4.14)$$

pH indicators are commonly used for their availability and stability (Lin & Liu, 2000; Martinez-Ohmos et al., 2011). The disadvantage of pH indicators is limited their dynamic range, and there are two methods to overcome the narrow dynamic range and use pH indicators:

- One option is to use a mixture of indicators (Bai et al., 2013; Barati, Shamps & Abdollahi, 2006; Lin & Liu, 2000; Martinez-Olmos et al., 2011; Qi et al., 2015; Safavi & Bagheri, 2003; Santos et al., 2014).
- Another alternative is to use an array of indicators (Capel-Cuevas, Cuellar, de Orbe-Payá, Pegalar, & Capatán-Villi, 2011; Lin & Liu, 2000; Martinez-Olmos et al., 2011; Mosley et al., 2004) that each indicator is a sensitive element and all responses from all sensor elements are utilized for pH determination.

Here, two aspects were considered in pH sensors optimization:
1. Tuning equilibrium constants of indicators to achieve continuous pH range
2. Designing optimum combination (array or mixture) of indicators based on enriching analytical sensitivity.

In order to show the applicability of the idea for experimental data, three different indicators are selected (methyl orange, neutral red, and phenol red). pH is not covered by these three indicators efficiently, in the range between 0.5 and 10. In this study, the chemical behavior of pH indicators in aqueous solution is well known, the chemical sensor was designed in aqueous solution. Also, the chemical models of pH indicator behavior in the micellar solution have been investigated.
