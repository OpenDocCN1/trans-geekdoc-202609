While many practical applications, it is not crucially important to have a constant value for $K_{C_r}$ or to maintain constant free [II], it is instructive to investigate the change in $K_{C_r}$ during a typical titration.

The change in [II] also depends on the initial concentrations of all involved components A, B, and I. A complete picture can show the effect of all controllable parameters on the tunable range of apparent constant. Fig. 4.5 shows the effect of different total concentrations of reactants $C_A$ and $C_B$ for the mentioned equilibrium system with the determined equilibrium constants. Twenty-eight samples with $C_A$ ($10^{-6}$ to 1M) and $C_B$ ($10^{-6}$ to 1M) were simulated. Each surface in Fig. 4.5 displays the effect of changing $C_I$ for each sample with determining $C_A$ and $C_B$ on the tuned range of apparent constant. Indeed, for the equilibrium system of Eq.
