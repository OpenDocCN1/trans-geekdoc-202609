# 3. Trilinearity constraint

Trilinearity constraint, which is based on the chemical information, guarantees the accuracy of unique profiles in the most favorite cases. Due to the trilinearity constraint, data arrays should contain some similar factors in their slices/modes, while the factors should be independent from experimental conditions. Thus, factors change proportionally (Cattell, 1944). Trilinearity is a very strong constraint that enforces trilinear decompositions to have unique solutions under mild conditions (Alier, Felipe, Hernández, & Tauler, 2011).

In the most second-order data sets with trilinear structure, it is possible to extract the trilinear profile of the generic analyte. In this case, the trilinear behavior of the specific profile is the available real information, and knowing this information depends on the primary knowledge of the studied system. Applying this information in the qualified-way data resulted in a unique solution for the profile of the component of interest.

Matrix-multivariate curve-alternating least square, MACR-ALS, as an extension of MCR-ALS, can be easily adapted to the trilinear analysis of the three-way data sets generated from data matrices with the same row or column and taken the advantages of their structure. Trilinearity constraint is incorporated into the MACR-ALS as a constraint, profiles of a component in data matrices are forced to have common shape during each ALS optimization and they may differ by a scaling factor. When this constraint is inserted into the ALS iterative optimization procedure, it forces the shape of the loading vectors to be the same in all matrices. A wonderful aspect of the trilinearity constraint in the ALS is that the application of this constraint for each component can be optional.

Accuracy of the measurement is the main goal of analytical chemists. Programmers and mathematicians have been developing algorithms, which may produce “unique solution” based on the available information. Most of the algorithms provide only a solution at the end of the procedure but might not be necessarily the true solution (Rajk, 2010). Trilinearity constraint, which is based on the chemical information, guarantees the accuracy of the resolved profiles due to the uniqueness property. Trilinearity may be incorporated in the algorithm as MA-MCR-ALS or as an intrinsic property of a model like PARParallel FactOr analysis (PARAFAC), rank annihilation factor analysis (RAFA), generalized rank annihilation method (GRAM), direct trilinear decomposition (DTLD), etc. (Abdollahi & Nazari, 2003; Sanchez & Kowalski, 1986).
