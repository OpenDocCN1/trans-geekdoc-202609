It can be generalized for any equilibrium system, the tunable range of apparent constant is achieved in permissible ranges of all externally controllable parameters. This algorithm is generally applicable and solves chemical equilibrium problems of any degree of complexity. It calculates the concentrations for a solution for which the total component concentrations, the chemical model, and its formation constants are known.

# 3. The application of proposed strategy in practice

There are several examples in the chemistry literature where the apparent equilibrium constants have been adjusted by variable such as solvent, micelle concentration, or some interfering chemical species (Adam, Bravo, & Mancini, 2014). One application in analytical chemistry is to study the pH indicators in order to shift the chemical equilibrium and therefore the pKa (Caselli, Mangone, Paolillo, & Traini, 2002; Love, Habatta, & Dorsey, 1984; Rostami, Karimvand, & Abdollah, 2019). Garcia and Medal studied the mechanism of surfactant-surface interactions in micelles in solution and deat in Garcia and Sanz-Model (1986). Safavi and Abdollah (2001) observed a significant shift in equilibrium and the apparent acidity of acid-base equilibria of indicators was reported by several works (Adam et al., 2014; Kidani & Imura, 2010; Safavi, Abdullahi, Maleki, & Zeunai, 2009). Also, it has been shown that the investigation of equilibrium constants has been possible by adjusting the equilibrium constants under certain chemical-physical conditions (Okada, 1995; Woolley, Tomkins, & Hepler, 1972). Herein, the proposed strategy is applied for tuning the apparent equilibrium constants using controllable variables, which can have many applications in designing efficient sensors for the determination and quantification of analytes. It is clear that the affinity for tuning the equilibrium constants can be effective in the application of many equilibrium-based chemical processes.

Fundamentally, a chemical sensor is defined as device that has selective response to a analyte through a physical or chemical interaction and can be used for the qualitative or quantitative determination of the analyte. For pH determination, the device can be a pH meter or chemical compounds such as pH indicators in a solution (Hulanicki, Glab, & Ingman, 1991; Lim, Musto, Park, Zhong, & Suslick, 2008; Wencel, Abel, & McDonagh, 2014).
