# 1. Introduction

Self-modeling curve resolution (SMCR) techniques are widely applied for
resolving chemical data to the pure-component spectra and composition
profiles. In most cases, the resolution of bilinear chemical data sets using
self-modeling curve resolution (SMCR) methods produces a range of fea-
sions in multicomponent systems. Hence, the most significant cha-\lenge of the chemometricians who use the SMCR methods is to reduce the
range of feasible solutions by applying proper information in the form of
constraints. The mathematical solutions generated by SMCR obey the
applied constraints coming from a priori physicochemical information about
the system under investigation.

The appropriate constraints can narrow down the band of feasible solu-	ions, even in most favorable cases, can produce unique solutions, and
improve the accuracy of the results of SMCR methods. This highlights the
importance of studying the constraints used in SMCR methods, and the
need for new constraints to achieve more accurate results. Several
have demonstrated that applying some constraints in SMCR methods,
including trilinearity, zero concentration region, correspondence,
local rank, and non-negativity, result in unique profiles.
