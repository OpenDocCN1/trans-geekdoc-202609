As stated before, the coordinates of spectra of the local-rank-window would be in row space because all have the same shape. Thus, the six spectra of the local-rank-one window are indicated as a single circle in Fig. 7.5A–C; other data points are not shown for the sake of clarity. Regarding the Bergen plot in Fig. 7.5, the local-rank window is located inside the feasible region of the third component. Thus, the spectral profiles with rank one can be considered as the pure spectrum for the third component. Fig. 7.5A represents a set of feasible solutions that indicate the localrankwindow as a selective window in data. However, one can find several sets of feasible solutions with this property by fixing the position of the third spectrum and changing the other two positions. As can be seen in elution profiles in Fig. 7.5A, there is a selective window for the third component (elution times 29–34 in arbitrary unit), and it shows that the contribution of other components for building the local-rank-one window is zero. However, it is illustrated in Fig. 7.5B that a linear combination of the spectral profiles of the second and third component can construct the rows with rank-one, i.e., the linear combination of points 1 and 2 in Fig. 7.5B. The corresponding elution profiles confirm the presence of two components in the rank-one sub-matrix!

The local-rank-one window, in the way, may be constructed from the linear combination of three different components, e.g., the points 3, 4, and 5 in Fig. 7.5C. Consequently, due to the rotational ambiguity, one, two, or three components may be present in this local-rank-one sub-matrix. Possibly previous chemical knowledge confirms the presence of more than one component in this window.
