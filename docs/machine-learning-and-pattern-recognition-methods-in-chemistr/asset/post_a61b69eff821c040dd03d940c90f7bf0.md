## 2.4 Cancer subtype classification

Hematopoietic is a malignant malignant in immune system cells. Park, Batbaatar, Piao, Theera-Umpon, and Ryu (2021) used an autoencoder model to find salient features and build predictive models for hematopoietic cancer classification. They also used the oversampling method SMOTE (Chawla, Boyer, Hall, & Kegelter, 2002) to resolve the difficulty of unbalanced datasets. Another advantage of this study was that the SHAP (Lundberg & Lee, 2017) method was used to interpret the predictive model and explain primary genes and proteins for each cancer subtype. The dataset was gene expression data of 2457 patients with hematopoietic cancer, classified into five subtypes. They summed the reconstruction on variational autoencoder and classification loss in the cost function and used various machine learning classifiers (e.g., ANN, RF, KNN, SVM). Observations show autoencoder has a better performance in dimension reduction compared to PCA and NMF.

## 2.5 Dimensionality reduction in spectrometry imaging

The spectrometry method has become a dominant and efficient method in chemistry, pharmacy, and biology. New experiments in this field can generate hundreds of gigabytes of data, and as a result, their analysis requires powerful algorithms. Most methods of analyzing this data begin with a dimension reduction step. Linear methods have been used to reduce the size of this data, but the use of nonlinear methods helps reduce information in this process. Thomas, Race, Steven, Gilmore, and Bunch (2016) used an autoencoder to decrease the size of over 165,000 pixels and more than 7000 spectral channels to a much smaller number of essential features. In order to avoid overfitting the training data, they employ a standard L2 weight regularization. They applied this to mouse brain data, and encoder has shown higher performance than PCA and NMF.

## 2.6 Parkinson's disease progression recognition

Parkinson’s disease is a progressive nervous system disorder that affects movement. Symptoms start gradually, sometimes starting with a noticeable tremor in just one hand. Tremors are common, and the disorder also causes stiffness or slowing of movement.

The Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS) has four parts. Each part has multiple points that are individually scored, using zero for normal or no problems, 1 for minimal problems, 2 for mild problems, 3 for moderate problems, and 4 for severe problems. These scores are tallied to indicate the severity of the disease, with 199 points being the worst and total disability and 0 meaning no disability.

Kazak (2019) used a dataset containing 5875 records from 42 patients diagnosed with early-stage Parkinson's disease: 28 men and 14 women (about 200 records per patient). Each observation is described with the following variables: patient number, age, gender, time interval from baseline recruitment date, 16 vocal attributes based on biomedical measures, and Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS) scores, used for tracking symptom progression. The results show that this method is more effective than traditional linear methods.
