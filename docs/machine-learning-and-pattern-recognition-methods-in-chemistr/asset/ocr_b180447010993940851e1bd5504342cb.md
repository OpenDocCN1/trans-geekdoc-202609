Each point defined by t in the considered space conforms to the duality relation. Thus, it is easy to derive the coefficients to create the hyperplane corresponding to this point in another space (dual space).

```
$n = tS^{-1}$
```

where t represents the coordinates of a fixed point, S is the matrix of singular values, and n is the hyperplane coefficients vector. Point t in one space of matrix has a certain hyperplane defined by Eq. (7.30) in another space (dual space).

```
$n^T z = 0$
```

In the equation of hyperplane, z is a general variable representing the coordinates of an arbitrary point in V- or U-spaces and 0 is a scalar.

Hence, a certain point in the abstract space corresponds to a unique hyperplane in its dual space. A unique profile in the result of an MCR method is equivalent to a certain point in the corresponding abstract vector space of the profile. In fact, the duality concept states that a proper hyperplane should be defined in the dual space to achieve a unique solution for any analyte (the correct and sufficient meaningful physical/chemical information can define the correct and proper hyperplane corresponding to true solution). Therefore, accurate information must be used to determine the hyperplane by the subspace related to all complementary compounds (this specific hyperplane is a subspace of all complementary (interfering) compounds without the contribution of the analyte). Thus, the information of the profile constraints resulting in a unique solution in the vector space of the data would lead to fix a hyperplane in its dual space. Therefore, a general rule for the uniqueness of MCR solutions is based on the duality concept:

> The necessary condition to get a unique solution for the analyte is the possibility of the definition of the hyperplane related to all interfering components in the dual space by implementing adequate physical/chemical constraints (or information).

Indeed, for a (N) component system, a unique solution always fixes a point in one space and an (N–1)-dimensional hyperplane of component components in the dual space. This is a general rule of uniqueness in SMCR methods.
