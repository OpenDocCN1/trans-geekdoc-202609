# 4.1 Advanced topics

In deep learning, CNNs are investigated more, and some architectures for many tasks are given by (He, Zhang, Ren, & Sun, 2015; Krizhevsky et al., 2012; Szegedy et al., 2015). Also, new concepts are introduced, which act successfully in many research areas, including generative adversarial-network (GAN) (Goodfellow et al., 2014), capsule network (Hinton, Sabour, & Frost, 2018; Sabour, Frostst, & Hinton, 2017), graph representational learning (Fey & LeSenn, 2020; Wang et al., 2018), and deep reinforcement learning (Henderson et al., 2018). GAN is an unsupervised method that learns the distribution of the input space. GAN contains two subnetworks: generator and discriminator; these two subnetworks learn throughout the adversarial learning. Hence, it could generate a new sample from the learned generator. The capsule network is designed that it addresses the most drawback of the CNN. CNN could not take into account spatial hierarchies between features. In computational biology, there is more situation in which the input space is represented by graphs. Hence, graph representational learning methods to encode the knowledge play a major role. Deep reinforcement learning is a field of machine learning whose main goal is to predict the policy function (decision-making function) by interacting with the environment. In this case, the agent is encouraged or punished by a reward function based on the selected action.

These concepts get more attention in bioinformatics and computational biology. Yang, Chen, Li, and Wang (2021) introduced the subtype-GAN model with multiple-input and multiple–output to identify tumor types. To this end, they utilized a deep adversarial learning approach. Lan et al. (2020) investigated the applications of GAN in biomedical informatics. Popova, Isayev, and Tropsha (2018) proposed a new approach for Denovio drug design, which is called ReLeASE (Reinforcement Learning for Structural Evolution). They want to generate new molecules libraries. Also, some research works utilize the capsule network for different problems in computational biology (de Jesus, Cuevas, Rivera, & Crivelli, 2018; Liu et al., 2020); Mobiny, Lu, Nguyen, Roy-sam, & Varadarajan, 2019).
