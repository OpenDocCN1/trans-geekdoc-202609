The loading of PC1 did not correspond to the spectrum of pure ABS; instead, PC1 has a positive loading in a region where there are no characteristics in the spectrum of ABS (Fig. 5.5). As such, PC1 does not help locate the ABS microparticle. Unlike PC1, PC2 has a positive loading near the characteristic peak of ABS at 1412nm. However, the majority of the score score of PC2 shows positive scores, indicating that the surface of PC2 is sizable and unable to locate the ABS microparticle. PC3 shows a positive loading near the 1412nm characteristic peak of ABS and a stronger negative in the 1100–1200nm region, where the 1145-nm and 1198-nm characteristic peaks of ABS are located. The majority of the surface of the PC3 score is negative, with several small groups of pixels with positive scores. Meanwhile, PC4 has a negative loading from 1400–1500nm near the 1412-nm characteristic peak of ABS. The PC4 scores is mostly positive with several small groups of pixels with negative scores. As with the previous components, PC3 and PC4 are not exclusive and unable to locate the ABS microparticle.

Similar to the previously discussed PCA of the SNV preprocessed spectra, principal component analysis of the first derivative preprocessed spectra was not able to locate the ABS microparticle (results not shown).
