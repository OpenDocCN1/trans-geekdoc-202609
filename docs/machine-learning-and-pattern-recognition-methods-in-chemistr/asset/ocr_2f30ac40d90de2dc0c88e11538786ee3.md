# 3.1 Case study 1

According to the IUPAC definition, the transition interval of an acid-base (pH) indicator is the concentration range of a chemical species that can be easily detected using the change of color of two forms of an indicator. The capability of indicators in changing their color near the equivalent point in acid-base titrations is dependent on their acidity dissociation constant (pKa). The conditional dependence of the apparent constant of some pH indicators has been reported previously (Safavi et al., 2009). It has been shown that the transition point and transition interval as the apparent constant of pH indicators have been altered under the influence of the different types of ionic liquids and Also, the effect of differing-concentrations of micellar pseudo-phase on the apparent acidity of a pH indicator has been investigated (Babamoradi & Abdollahi, 2015; Sajeddi-Amin, Abdollahi, & Naseri, 2021). Inspiring the tuning of the apparent constants, the goal of this case study is to tune the pKa of acid-base neutral red (NR) in micellar pseudo-phase for improving the range of its transition points and transition interval for more applications.

Aqueous micellar solutions have several applications in analytical chemistry; they can enhance the solubility of solutes, and can also shift the observed equilibrium constants, in particular the protonation constants of weak acids, e.g., pH indicators. Further, shifts in absorption spectra and changing equilibrium and kinetic constants have been observed due to change in the chemical/physical properties of the solutions. These effects are due to interactions of solutes with micelles, ie., the solutes can either remain in the aqueous phase or be dissolved in the micellar phase. Naturally, these solvents will affect many properties of dissolved chemicals (Beltran, Codony, Granados, Izquierdo, & Prat, 1993; Beltran, Prat, & Codony, 1995; Dutta & Bhat, 1993; Funasaki, 1979; Underwood, 1982).

A complete model that describes the behavior of aqueous micellar systems is the partition model in which the solutes are distributed between the aqueous phase and the micellar pseudo-phase (Blatt, Chatelier, & Sawyer, 1984). The acid-base equilibrium of neutral red (NR) in aqueous micellar solutions of Brij-35 (polyethylene (23) laur ether) is considered in the present case (Babamoradi & Abdollahi, 2015).
