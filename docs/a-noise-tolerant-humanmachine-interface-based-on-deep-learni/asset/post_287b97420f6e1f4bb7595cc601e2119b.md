## A noise-tolerant human–machine interface based on deep learning-enhanced wearable sensors

Wearable machine interfaces with inertial measurement units (IMUs) are widely applied across healthcare, robotics and interactive technologies. However, extracting signals remains challenging owing to motion artifacts in real-world environments. Here we present a human–machine interface capable of tolerating diverse motion artifacts through deep learning-enhanced wearable sensors. The system integrates a six-channel IMU, an electromyography module, a Bluetooth microcontroller unit and a stretchable battery, enabling wireless capture and transmission of gestures. A convolutional neural network trained on a composite dataset of gestures and motion artifacts extracts robust signals, parameter-based transfer learning improves the generalizability of the network across users. A sliding-window approach connects the extracted gestures in real-time, continuous control of a robotic arm during dynamic activities such as running, high-frequency vibration, posture changes, ocean wave motion and combinations of these. This work demonstrates the potential of wearable human–machine interfaces for complex real-world applications.

Wearable devices have great promise in various applications, such as continuous health monitoring$^{1}$, energy harvesting$^{1}$, and human–machine interfaces$^{2}$. Wearable inertial measurement units (IMUs) that can capture gestures have proven to be particularly valuable for virtual and augmented reality$^{2}$, non-communication$^{2}$, physiological monitoring$^{2}$, sports rehabilitation$^{2}$ and robotic control$^{2}$.
