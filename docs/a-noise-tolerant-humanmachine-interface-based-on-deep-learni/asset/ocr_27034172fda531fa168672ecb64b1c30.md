To achieve gesture recognition, the acquired signals should be continuously fed into the sliding window mechanism, which allows the window to capture signals from either a single gesture or two consecutive gestures.

In underwater environments, water currents are different from the intricate dynamics of seas, which vary significantly with wave parameters (e.g., wavelength and height). However, unwanted wave-interference can affect the user's gesture signals, complicating the classification of gestures.

When the sliding window slides, the window may contain signals from two consecutive gestures. Therefore, we generated signals using various consecutive gesture combinations. Rather than performing individually, we predicted by concatenating individual perceived gestures, by connecting two signals to end-consecutive gestures.

We trained the model with a dataset that includes sea-wave motions. The training data was originally collected in San Diego Bay (Fig. 5a). However, due to the bay's enclosed environment (compared to an open environment), the amplitude of the waves is different from the open sea.

The sliding window was applied to these concatenated signals, and we achieved a recognition rate of 95% of the duration (Extended Fig. 6).
