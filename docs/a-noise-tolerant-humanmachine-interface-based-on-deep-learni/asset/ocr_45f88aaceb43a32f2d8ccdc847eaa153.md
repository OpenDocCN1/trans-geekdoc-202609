# Methods

# Preparation

Several glass slides were cleaned using acetone, ace-
tyl alcohol (IPA) and deionized water. Two glass slides were
coated with polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA, 940 AA MicroChem)
at at 2,000 rpm for 30 s, for curing on a substrate with a temperature
of80 °C for 3 min to remove a layer. A layer of Ecoflex (00-30-Month 1) was
coated with PDMS at 2,000 rpm for 30 s, for curing in
of oven. The substrate was then coated with
polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS, Sylgard 1844 silicone)
10:1) at 2,000 rpm for 30 s, for curing at a hot plate
at 150 °C for 20 min.

Separately, polymeric (PI, HD Micros) was coated onto
Ecoflex (Oks-Mist) at 2,000 rpm for 30 s, followed by baking on
a hot plate at 150 °C for 1 h and baking in a nitrogen-prote-
cted at 300 °C for 1 h.

# Fabrication of the transfer circuit

The PI-coated and the CNT-coated glass slides were attached
by ultrasonic vibration (250 kHz, series PSX ultrasonic,
QUASOL II, Novacosam). For 1 min, the PI-coated film was la-
dered onto the coated glass slide and then patterned using
pulsed laser (600 W, Laser Mark's) according to
a Custom Auto Editor. After removing the material
ton the patterned film, it was lifted using a water-table
ubstrate and then cured at 80 °C for 10 min. The hot plate
was used to reach 80 °C for 10 min.

The substrate-table was removed using in
deionized water. After drying, the glass film was cleaned by
IPA. The circuit was then bonded with silver solder
(MS33, LTIP). The solder was used for bonding the chips to the
to the patterned film, by curing on the hot plate
at 40 °C for 24 min.

The second layer of Ecoflex was coated at 2,000 rpm and
cured on the first layer slide as the substrate for fabricating
the second layer. A solvent was then applied to remove the Ecoflex
at at designated locations, using a solvent without access to in
the next and second layer slides. Subsequently, the solvent was
removed to reveal the first layer. The micro-circuit was pre-programmed using a
customized evaluation approach,

The third layer was fabricated on an Ecoflex/PMMA-coated
glass slide, aligned and pressed with the first and second
layers through alignment markers observed under a microscope. The
second and third layers were cured by connecting 0.0 Ω
resistors on a hot plate at 240 °C for 5 min.

Finally, the transfer circuit was encapsulated by filling degassed
Ecoflex over the 2 glass slides that sandwiched the circuit, for
low curing at 50 °C for 20 min. The PMMA-coated glass slides
were removed by IPA.

# Fabrication of stretchable substrate

Four inks were formulated into the stretchable substrate: Ag,
ZnO, and TiO (600 mesh). The Ag ink was used for the circuit,
which was formed using a mixture of Ag, polystyrene-ethylene-
(SEBS) resin toner with a weight ratio of 9.0:1.
The Zn ink was used for the substrate and was synthesized using Zn, 
ZnO (25 wt% in water) with a weight ratio similar to Ag. The TiO ink was used as the battery
substrate, prepared by mixing TiO2 with calcium carbonate
(33 wt% in water) with a weight ratio of 9.0:12.6.
The TiO ink, was prepared as the separator using, Triton X-104,
SEBS (40 ml in water) and then mixed in a solvent at a ratio
of3:1:1.1.5. Each ink was used as the separator, poly vinyl
acetate (PVA) was dissolved in 9.1 ml deionized water at 90 °C. The
is then mixed with 14 M NaOH solution (16.7 wt%) and cured-
to get about 67% of the original yield, using a hydrated PVA. The PVA
was then cast onto the substrate to form the electrolyte
cured at 80 °C for 12 h (6 h).
