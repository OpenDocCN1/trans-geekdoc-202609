Fig. 2 Gesture classification via deep learning and transfer learning. a Architecture of the process used to construct the dataset. The composite dataset was assembled by inserting diverse motion artefacts onto gesture signals. b Comparison of analysis recall values across different deep learning models. The CNNs show accuracy with high recall. The graph presents mean values, with error bars indicating the standard deviation (n=13 independent test results). c Fully connected neural network, RNN, recurrent neural network, and architecture of the CNN(s). It includes convolutional layers, max pooling layers and fully connected (FC) layers, with cross entropy as the loss function. d Schematics of the transfer process, which features two stages: First, the model is trained on a dataset from five individuals. Second, the pretrained convolutional layers are tuned at a lower learning rate with a smaller dataset from a new individual. e Confusion matrices of the results before and after transfer. The minimum recognition accuracy of the 19 gestures improved from 53% to 97%. f Schematics of the sliding-window mechanism. The window size is 1 s, with a sliding step of 0.25 s. Each segment serves as an individual input, generating a separate prediction.
