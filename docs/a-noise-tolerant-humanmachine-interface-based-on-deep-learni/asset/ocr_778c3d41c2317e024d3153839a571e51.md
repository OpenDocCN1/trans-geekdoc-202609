Extended Data Fig. 3: Biaxial cyclic stretchability test of the sensors.

a. Photos of the sensors under simultaneous 100-cycle tensile forces along both vertical and horizontal directions up to 200%, showing plastic deformation after 300 cycles of biaxial stretching tests, which demonstrates the robustness of the design. b. Bluetooth signal strength under bi-axial stretching. The RSSI consistently remained above -60 dB across all tested levels, indicating stable and continuous wireless communication. Two competing effects influence signal strength during stretching. First, mechanical strain increases the resistance of the interconnects, leading

to transmission loss (there is, reduced signal strength). Second, as the device stretches, the antenna beam progresses away from nearby electronic components, reducing parasitic capacitance and thereby decreasing signal loss (that is, improved signal strength). Due to these opposing effects, the signal strength initially decreases from increased resistance and subsequently increases as parasitic loss diminishes. The graph presents the average values, with error bars indicating the standard deviation (n = 15 independent measurements).
