**Extended Fig. 4:** Illustration of the process for generating the composite dataset (for example, gesture 2) with 20 signals of gesture 2 recorded while sitting. **b.** Motion artifacts induced by running. These motion artifacts are divided into six stages with each stage lasting 1 s. **c.** Motion artifacts induced by high-frequency vibrations. These motion artifacts are divided into six stages with each stage lasting 1 s. **d.** Motion artifacts induced by the combination of high-frequency vibrations and running. These motion artifacts are divided into six stages with each stage lasting 1 s. **e.** Superimposed signals for training. The superimposed signals are generated by adding motion artifacts to the gesture signal (for example, a signal of gesture 2) superimposed with 18 stages of motion artifacts in **b** and **d**. The stages are fixed rather than by randomly selecting segments from continuous motion artifact recordings. Since random selection can unintentionally introduce overlapping segments, where identical portions of motion artifacts appear in multiple superimposed signals, using fixed stages is key to extracting generalizable features. Otherwise, the model may learn to recognize repeated motion artifact patterns instead. To mitigate this, we deliberately divided the motion artifacts into distinct, non-overlapping stages.
