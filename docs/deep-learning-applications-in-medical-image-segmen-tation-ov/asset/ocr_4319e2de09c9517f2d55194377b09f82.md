# ## 4.1 Introduction

The pediatric brain represents the growing and maturational stage of overall brain development. The phase from birth to 2 years is very important in a child’s brain growth and is characterized by an increase in brain size, volume, nerve maturation, and impulse transmission. Particularly, the stage from birth to the first 28 days is called neonatal period, while the duration from 29 days to 1 year is early infancy, and the second year of life is called late infancy. These three phases are crucial in brain development and are marked by extensive maturation of brain tissues.

The human brain comprises three major tissues: grey matter (GM), white matter (WM) and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) (Devi et al. 2016b). Each component has a distinct functional functionality. The GM is composed of several elevations and grooves that play a major role in human cognition, sensory perception, and learning. WM is located in the interior of the brain and aids nerve impulse transmission. This is enabled by the myelin sheaths in the WM that speed up the conduction of nerve signals. The CSF surrounds the brain in the cranial cavity and protects the brain from injuries and shocks. Although each brain tissue has a unique physiological role in brain development, the maturation of WM (especially, its myelination) is considered clinically significant.
