The remarkable success of GANs in a range of applications such as image processing (Ledig et al. 2017; Kazeminia et al. 2020), artistic style transfer (Knyaz et al. 2019; Zhang et al. 2019), classification (Israel et al. 2017), medical imaging, and image generation (Li et al. 2018; Lin et al. 2017) underscores the substantial progress GANs have made in various computer vision tasks. GAN models have been proposed in numerous variations across different domains over time. In recent years, GANs have emerged as one of the most actively researched algorithms in the realm of DL.

Within GANs, there are two crucial components: the generator and the discriminator. These two components can be implemented using any differentiable system that transforms data from one space to another, although neural networks are commonly employed for this purpose. MLPs were utilized in the generator G and discriminator D of the initial GANs. The role of the generator is to create new data samples while attempting to mimic the distribution of real-world examples. On the other hand, the discriminator typically functions as a binary classifier, aiming to distinguish between generated instances and real instances.

GANs are optimized through a Minimax optimization problem. This optimization process converges at a saddle point, which represents the maximum for the discriminator and the minimum for the generator. In essence, the goal of GAN optimization is to reach Nash equilibrium, at which point the generator accurately captures the distribution of real-world examples.

# 8.3.1.1 GAN Framework

The GAN framework (as illustrated in Figure 8.1) proves to be quite straightforward when both models are neural networks. To grasp the generator distribution, Pg, regarding the data, x, a prior is established for input noise variables known as Pz(z) (Goodfellow et al. 2020), with z representing the noise variable. The generator then functions as a mapping from the noise space to the data space, denoted as G(z, θg). This mapping is executed using a neural network with parameters θg. On the other hand, another neural network, D(x, θd), is defined, also equipped with parameters θd. The output of D(x) provides a singular scalar, representing the likelihood that x originates from the real training data rather than being a product of the GAN model G.
