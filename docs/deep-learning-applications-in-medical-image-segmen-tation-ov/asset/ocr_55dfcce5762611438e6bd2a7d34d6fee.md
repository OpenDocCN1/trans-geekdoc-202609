Deep learning models exhibit impressive performance in anatomical structure segmentation, but a high number of training parameters is a potential issue. This leads to increased memory usage and reduced model generalization, connecting with our earlier U-Net architecture discussion. To address this, Yahyatabar et al. introduced “Dense-Unet,” (Yahyatabar and Jouvet 2020) an extension of U-Net with dense connectivity. Enhancing information flow between layers. The model offers “compact and normal” versions, incorporating squeeze-and-excitation (SE) blocks. The study used JSRT and Montgomery datasets, achieving Dice and Jaccard scores of 97.6/95.3 and 97.9/95.59, respectively, illustrating improved segmentation. Similarly, as in previous studies for automatically segmenting lung regions in chest radiographs, Liu et al. proposed an “Improved U-Net” (Liu et al. 2022) network that incorporates a pretraining EfficientNet-b4 backbone in the encoder, LeakyReLU activation function, and residual block in the decoder to efficiently extract lung field features and prevent gradient instability. The suggested approach was tested on public CXR datasets, JSRT and MC, as well as a private Haut lung segmentation dataset, and it outperformed the traditional U-Net model in terms of “Jaccard Index and Dice sensitivity coefficient (DSC).” Both studies use data augmentation techniques to overcome the training set’s limited size, enhancing generalization to diverse datasets. The U-Net architecture has been extensively preferred in recent medical studies; however, it exhibited shortcomings, the most prominent being the presence of a single dropout function at the final convolution layer, which leads to overfitting and poor model generalization. To address these concerns, Arvind et al. proposed a refined and relatively lightweight variant of the U-Net model, (Arvind et al. 2023) incorporating multiple dropouts in both convolution and deconvolution layers for improved semantic segmentation of lung images in chest X-rays. Their improvements in the architectural components of U-Net, such as batch normalization and fewer convolutions, resulted in fewer model parameters and lower model complexity while maintaining performance metrics. The study offered improved performance on multiple datasets with a generalization accuracy of 93.87%.

Continuing the discussion on encoder–decoder approaches, the researchers introduced the “DED-CNN (dual encoder–decoder CNN)” (Ullah et al. 2023) for precise segmentation of multiorgan anatomical structures in chest X-ray images. The model utilizes a pretrained VGG19 as an encoder, incorporates a SE block for better representation, and employs “Attention Gating Modules (AGMs)” to improve the segmentation of smaller regions in the second encoder–decoder.
