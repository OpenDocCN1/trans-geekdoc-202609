It progresses in the early pediatric stages to reach full maturity. Coupled with this, there is vast variation in myelin development between subjects. This is particularly true in anomalous cases and premature births. However, despite all these concerns, it is clinically important to study myelin progression in the pediatric brain because:

+   1) Myelin formation mirrors brain maturation in the child. Age-appropriate myelination indicates that the growth and development of the brain are normal.
+   
+   2) Abnormal myelination in the form of less-than-normal (hypomyelination), perceived delay (retarded myelination), or abnormality (dysmyelination) points to serious underlying brain disorders that must be detected and treated early.
+   
+   3) Sometimes, even normal myelination (such as preterm births) needs close monitoring to diagnose potential developmental anomalies in the future.

In this light, we have proposed a qualitative and quantitative study of myelination. The major contributions of our work are:

+   • The research entails only structural brain MR images (T1 and T2 scans). We do not involve functional or quantitative MRI like other recent studies (Deoni et al. 2015; Chonka et al. 2016; Dean et al. 2016; Travis et al. 2019; Kuhne et al. 2021, Filimonova et al. 2023).
+   
+   • Our approach acquires both 2D image segmentation and 3D visualization of MWM across a wide age range of babies (0–2 years).
+   
+   • We introduce a novel MI to quantify the amount of WM that is myelinated at any given age. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first method to quantify the extent of WM myelination based on structural MRI scans.
+   
+   • Further, an optimal growth model to accurately represent myelin progress in the pediatric brain is identified. Using this model, potential differences in hemispheric myelination are analyzed.
+   
+   • We also study myelin maturation in premature babies to compare their developmental milestones with normal term-born children.
