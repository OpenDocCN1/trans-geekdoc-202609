The sophisticated framework of FCN serves as the foundation for this concept. It makes several adjustments to avoid having to choose between localization and context sensitivity. ### 2.2.4 Multi-scale-Based Models

Multi-scale segmentation designs were made to deal with the fact that there are different scales in the areas of interest. By dividing an image into numerous homogenous pieces at different scales, multi-scale segmentation schemes can present information at multiple scales. These architectures improved high-level segmentation by using a mix of local and global variables to label each pixel. In 2018, researchers at Arizona State University in the United States suggested U-Net++ to address the shortcomings of U-Net, such as its reliance on same-scale feature maps. Dense-Block was employed in the architecture to boost the traditional U-Net's performance, as depicted in Figure 2.7. To bridge the gap among features across modules and boost gradient flow, this model used convolutions and dense connections on the skip-pathway, in contrast to the traditional model (U-Net) (Zhou et al. 2020). Additionally, it included the idea of deep supervision, which carries out architecture reduction. The proposed design is evaluated on multiple modalities by using four distinct medical image datasets: cell nuclei, colon polyps, liver, and lung nodules. The results show that U-Net++ outperforms the basic U-Net and Wide U-Net models on the basis of the Intersection-over-Union (IoU) measure, even when deep supervision was not utilized.

RNNs are rapidly being used for image recognition, despite having originally been designed for a 1D input. They have been put to use for segmentation issues, and their performance in the MRBrainS competition (Stollenga et al. 2015) was quite encouraging. Since the region of interest (ROI) in medical images is typically spread across numerous neighboring slices (e.g. in CT or MRI), correlations in successive slices are often observed. As a result, RNNs are able to make use of the sequential information supplied by the input slices to determine the contexts that exist between each pair. There are two main components of the RNN structure: the RNN, which handles inter-slice information extraction, and any CNN model, which handles intra-slice information extraction. Among RNNs, LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) (Hochreiter and Schmidhuber 1997) is the most well known. The loss of spatial information that occurs in a normal LSTM network’s inputs is problematic for medical image segmentation. The replacement of vector multiplication with the convolutional operation makes convolutional LSTM (Srivastava et al. 2015) (CLSTM) a promising option. By taking into account contextual information across neighboring slices, CLSTM was added to the deep CNN output layer in Armato et al. (2011) to improve segmentation. With their strategy, the Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) increased to 0.8247 from 0.7976 for the well-known U-Net topology (Ronneberger et al. 2015).
