In abdominal imaging from CT and MRI scans, segmentation identifies structures such as the liver, pancreas, and kidneys. Authors in Christ et al. (2017) developed cascaded fully convolutional neural networks (CFCNs) for automatic segmentation of the liver and lesions in abdominal images. This network uses two cascaded fully convolutional networks (FCNs), where the first segments the liver’s region of interest (ROI), and the second identifies lesions within this ROI. Authors in Zhao et al. (2020) introduced a multi-scale supervised 3D U-Net (MSS) for segmenting kidneys and kidney tumors in CT images. Authors in Man et al. (2019) proposed a method inspired by a deep Q-network (DQN) with a deformable U-Net for pancreas segmentation, using 3D volume split into 2D slices along three axes.

For cardiac imaging, achieving automated heart region segmentation is crucial due to the significant threat posed by cardiac diseases. Authors in Xu, Wu, and Feng (2018) combined Faster R-CNN for rapid detection with 3D U-Net for powerful segmentation, introducing Conditional Function (CFUN) for comprehensive heart segmentation. Authors in Yan, Yang, and Cheng (2019) developed a deep-supervised 3D U-Net to enhance contextual information extraction at various network depths. Authors in Xia et al. (2019) introduced a two-stage framework for automated segmentation of atrial catheters, using a 3D U-Net for initial atrial center identification and another 3D U-Net for precise segmentation.

In breast ultrasound imaging, authors in Xia et al. (2019) divided images into four primary tissues – skin, fibro glandular tissue, mass, and fatty tissue – using three orthogonal image planes. A CNN was used to identify the tissue class of the central pixel within image blocks.
