## 10.2.5 Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)

Utilizing magnetic resonance, it captures electromagnetic signals emitted by human organs and subsequently reconstructs information about the structure of these organs. MRIs with high resolution provide improved structural details, significantly contributing to the precise localization of lesions and accurate diagnosis of diseases. Segmenting MRI images presents various challenges in medical image processing. The intricacies of brain tissue, coupled with artifacts like motion artifacts, ring artifacts, and noise, contribute to the difficulty of achieving accurate segmentation (Prakasha et al. 2023). Furthermore, challenges such as partial volume effects, asymmetrical grayscale, and additional noise further complicate the segmentation process (Veeramalla et al. 2023). In the context of rectum MRI segmentation, the irregular shape of the organ and the proximity of nearby nodes and organs pose challenges in isolating the rectum area accurately (Srivaramangai 2023). Additionally, the intrinsic heterogeneity stemming from different MRI scanners, vendors, acquisition sequences, and diverse stages of neurodevelopment increase the complexity of segmenting infant brain MRI across various ages, modalities, and sites (Zhang et al. 2023).

Overall, MRI segmentation faces challenges related to artifacts, tissue complexity, irregular shapes, and heterogeneity in data acquisition.

## 10.2.6 Other Images

Endoscopy entails inserting a long, nonsurgical tube into the body to visually examine internal organs or tissues in detail. This technique is valuable for studying various systems within the human body, including the respiratory, gastrointestinal, urinary, and female reproductive tract (Jani and Srivastava 2019; Münzer et al. 2018).

Wireless capsule endoscopy is an innovative device designed for painless and noninvasive examination to detect and diagnose diseases such as ulcers and bleeding in the gastrointestinal tract. Soffer et al. systematically analyzed the literature on DL in WCE (Soffer et al. 2020). He et al. (2018) introduced the first deep learning based framework for hookworm detection in WCE images, incorporating two CNN networks for edge extraction and hookworm classification. Some researchers created a CNN model for early gastric cancer identification and prediction of invasion depth, which is crucial for determining the appropriate treatment method (Yoon et al. 2019). Nakagawa et al. (2019) applied DL techniques based on CNN to enhance the diagnosis of an esophageal wall using endoscopy, while Choi et al. (2020) discussed the features and aspects of DL in endoscopy.

Positron emission tomography (PET) is a tool for nuclear imaging that visualizes molecular-level activities within tissues by injecting specific radioactive tracers. Wang et al. (2020) examined the use of machine learning in PET attenuation correction and low-count PET reconstruction, emphasizing the benefits of DL compared to conventional machine learning in PET image tasks. Another research provides an overview of PET image reconstruction, highlighting its direct use in DL or incorporated within conventional reconstruction techniques (Reader et al. 2020).

## 10.3 Challenges Related to the Dataset

The segmentation of medical images contributes to understanding an image, feature extraction, recognition, and the assessment of abnormalities or lesions. It offers crucial information for analyzing pathologies and, in turn, aids in diagnosing and planning a treatment. The main objective of segmentation is to divide an image into distinct regions that exhibit sturdy correlations. This process entails separating an image $I$ into a finite set of regions $R_1, R_2, ..., R_s$, as expressed in Eq. (10.1).

```
$I = \cup_{i=1}^s R_i, R_i \cap R_j = \emptyset \quad \text{and} \quad i \neq j$ (10.1)
```

The efficient operation of machine learning techniques hinges on extracting descriptive features. Identifying these features is often challenging, particularly in applications related to image understanding, an area still under active research. A strategic approach to address this challenge is the development of intelligent machines capable of autonomously learning and extracting the requisite features for image understanding. The CNN model stands out as an intelligent and successful model in this regard, and advancements in various architectures such as U-Net, SegNet, and W-Net have enabled the automatic learning and extraction of essential features for medical image understanding (Badrinarayanan et al. 2017; Ronneberger et al. 2015; Xia and Kulis 2017). Researchers have effectively utilized CNNs in various medical image understanding applications, including tumor detection and classification into benign and malignant categories (Ovalle et al. 2016). Skin lesion detection and identification of anomalies in optical coherence tomography images can also be detected using CNN (Kermany et al. 2018; Nasr-Esfahani et al. 2016). Many researchers have also shown colon cancer detection, blood cancer detection, and heart abnormalities (Kiranyaz et al. 2016; Sirinukunwattana et al. 2016). Kallenberg et al. (2016) have shown how breast abnormalities are detected using CNN. Notably, CNN-based models like CheXNet, employed for classifying 14 different chest-related ailments, have demonstrated superior performance compared to the average expertise of human practitioners (Philips et al. 2020; Rajpurkar et al. 2017).

CNNs have emerged as a predominant force in the realm of COVID-19 detection through the analysis of chest X-rays and CT scans. COVID-19, a global pandemic, is rapidly spreading across the world. The commonly utilized test for detecting COVID-19 infection is reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR). While RT-PCR is considered the gold standard for COVID-19 testing, it is a complex, time-consuming, and labor-intensive process with limited availability and accuracy. In places facing a shortage of RT-PCR kits, chest X-rays could serve as an initial screening tool for COVID-19, offering a more accurate diagnosis. DL techniques have been employed by many researchers to classify chest infections and distinguish between COVID-19 and other ailments. An early model suggested for COVID-19 detection involved utilizing a basic pre-trained AlexNet model, which was fine-tuned using chest X-ray images. To improve the performance of the models, more training samples and better segmenting techniques are required (Hasan et al. 2022). Various factors like insufficient details about the study participants, the randomization process, and the absence of external validation have led individuals to reconsider the use of these diagnostic techniques for this disease (Montazeri et al. 2021).
