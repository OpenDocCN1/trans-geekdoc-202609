# 5.4 Deep Learning Algorithms Employed in the Segmentation of Brain Tumor Images

Brain tumor segmentation refers to a process in medical image processing where algorithms are applied to delineate and identify regions of interest (tumor tissues) within brain images, typically acquired through techniques such as MRI or CT. Segmentation involves partitioning the brain image into multiple regions or classes, such as tumor tissue, healthy tissue, edema, and necrosis. This is important for various medical applications, including diagnosis, treatment planning, and monitoring of brain tumors. The rising popularity of machine learning and deep learning methods, notably CNNs, for brain tumor segmentation stems from their capacity to grasp intricate patterns from extensive datasets and their adeptness in processing high-dimensional medical images. These algorithms are trained on annotated datasets containing manually outlined tumor regions by experts. Subsequently, they can autonomously segment tumors in previously unseen images.

Deepak and Ameer (2019) presented a deep transfer learning approach for classifying glioma, meningioma, and pituitary tumors from brain MRI images, achieving a mean classification accuracy of 98% and outperforming existing methods. Transfer learning proves effective even with limited medical image data, with insights into misclassifications provided for further analysis. Cheng et al. (2015) proposed a method for improving brain tumor classification in T1-weighted contrast-enhanced MRI images by augmenting the tumor region and splitting it into ring-form subregions. This approach significantly enhanced classification accuracy compared to traditional methods, demonstrating its feasibility and effectiveness for computer-aided diagnosis. Cheng et al. (2016) introduced a novel content-based image retrieval (CBIR) for classifying brain tumors in T1-weighted contrast-enhanced MRI images, achieving a mean average precision of 94.68% by augmenting tumor regions, enabling adaptive spatial division for feature extraction, and applying Fisher kernel framework for similarity measurement.
