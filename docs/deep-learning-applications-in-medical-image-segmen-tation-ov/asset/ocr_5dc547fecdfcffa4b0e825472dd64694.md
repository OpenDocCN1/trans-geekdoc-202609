+   - neonatal phase (birth – 28 days): inverted tissue contrast compared to adult brain, that is, hypointensity of WM on T1 scans and hyperintensity on T2 images);
+   - isointense stage (around 6 months): marked by poor WM – GM contrast;
+   - adult-like appearance (more than 1 year): hyperintensity of WM on T1 images and hypointensity on T2 scans (similar to the adult brain).

Apart from this, diffusion MRI and diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) are also utilized to assess WM connectivity and maturation. Recently, high angular resolution diffusion imaging has been employed to estimate the orientation and distribution of WM tracts and fibers (Dubois et al. 2021). Besides, many other quantitative techniques, such as magnetization transfer, susceptibility-weighted MRI, and perfusion imaging, are entailed to study WM myelination in the pediatric brain.

Particularly, Travis et al. combined diffusion MRI and quantitative relaxometry to analyze WM differences, especially in the corpus callosum (Travis et al. 2019). The corpus callosum was divided into four zones based on its location in the brain and the growth trajectory of each zone was compared between premature and full-term babies. Similarly, Grotheer et al. also integrated diffusion and quantitative MRI to validate the various developmental hypotheses of myelination in the child’s brain (Grotheer et al. 2022). The results of the work revealed that myelination pattern is not uniform and increases in the inferior-superior and anterior–posterior directions.

In contrast, Kuhne et al. utilized MPRAGE and MP2RAGE sequences to evaluate myelination in brain anomalies and pathologies (Kuhne et al. 2021). Rapid myelination progress was observed in the corpus callosum, while reduced myelin deposition was found in cases of hypomyelination and dysmyelination. Thus, a reference model to gauge atypical myelin and brain abnormalities was developed. Recently, many researchers have involved T1/T2 mapping to obtain specific information on WM myelination. The major merit of this technique is the widespread availability of T1 and T2 images, coupled with their faster acquisition. Dipnall et al. carried out a detailed examination of myelination tracts from late childhood to the early adolescent stage (Dipnall et al. 2023). They also studied the myelination differences between male and female children. The research work concluded that while there was a significant change in brain microstructure and WM myelination between childhood and adolescent phases, no notable variation was found based on the subject’s sex.
