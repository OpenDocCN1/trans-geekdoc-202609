The idea behind segmentation is to categorize each pixel without giving them a thorough look. As a result, there is no spatial coherence among the pixels. DeepLab v1 was introduced by Chen et al. (2018b) to replace the two pooling strides that were originally used with just one. The padding size was also decreased from 100 to 1. Based on the VGG16 network, DeepLab v1 adds a full convolution layer in place of the final fully linked layer. The last two pooling layers were removed, and instead, they incorporated atrous convolution, a technique also employed for conditional random fields (Krähenbühl and Koltun 2011). DeepLab v2 (Chen et al. 2017b) introduced atrous spatial pyramid pooling (ASPP) and integrated ResNet101 for its enhanced atrous convolution capability. ASPP is able to capture the context of images at varying scales. DeepLabv3 (Chen et al. 2018b) modified the ASPP module to incorporate a second channel of global picture pooling and batch normalization after convolution and before ReLU. To further enhance the segmentation results, especially at the target border, DeepLab v3+ (Chen et al. 2018a) added an efficient decoder module to DeepLab v3. SegNet (Badrinarayanan et al. 2017) is a symmetric encoder–decoder structure that accomplishes pixel-level image segmentation by using FCN's semantic segmentation task, as depicted in Figure 2.3.
