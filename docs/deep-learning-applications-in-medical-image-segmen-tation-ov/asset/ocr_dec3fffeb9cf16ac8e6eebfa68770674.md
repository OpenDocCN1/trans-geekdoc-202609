Classical image segmentation techniques also present numerous challenges, such as over-segmentation and under-segmentation, which can occur in images with complex backgrounds or uneven lighting conditions. In addition, classical segmentation algorithms often involve manual tuning of parameters, which is often time-taking and may be prone to human error, leading to suboptimal results. Moreover, classical techniques may find it difficult to segment images with a lot of noise or artifacts, resulting in inaccurate segmentation results. Finally, some classical segmentation algorithms may not scale well to large images or datasets, limiting their applicability in real-world scenarios.

# 5.3 Deep Learning-Based Image Segmentation Methods for Medical Images

In recent years, deep learning techniques such as CNNs have revolutionized the area of image segmentation, particularly in the context of medical imaging. The architecture of CNNs is well suited for learning hierarchical representations of visual data, making them particularly effective for tasks such as medical image segmentation. In medical image segmentation, CNNs are used to automatically extract and learn key features from raw image data without requiring a manual or handcrafted feature extraction. They can effectively capture complex patterns and spatial relationships in medical images, allowing for more accurate segmentation of anatomical structures, lesions, and abnormalities. Several CNN architectures have been developed or adapted specifically for medical image segmentation tasks (Kayalibay et al. 2017; Malhotra et al. 2022). One popular architecture is U-Net, which has a symmetric expanding path for accurate localization and a contracting path for recording context (Ronneberger et al. 2015; Siddique et al. 2021). U-Net has been widely used in biomedical image segmentation because of its capability to generate high-quality segmentation masks.

Another common approach is the use of FCNs, which uses convolutional layers in place of fully connected layers (Dai et al. 2016). This allows FCNs to process images of arbitrary sizes and produce dense pixel-wise predictions. FCNs have been effectively used in medical image segmentation tasks, allowing for precise delineation of anomalies and structures from a variety of imaging modalities.
