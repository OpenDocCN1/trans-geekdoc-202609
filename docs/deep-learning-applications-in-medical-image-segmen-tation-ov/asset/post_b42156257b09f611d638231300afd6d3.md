## 8.4.1.7 Abdomen Segmentation Using GANs

Deep neural networks have found extensive application in automating the segmentation of organs in abdominal CT scans. In abdominal scan images, distinguishing the spatial boundaries between adjacent organs poses a challenge, compounded by the substantial variations in size among different target organs. Traditional DL approaches struggle to accurately segment each organ due to these complexities.

Yang et al. (2017) and Kim and Ye (2018) applied GAN techniques specifically for liver segmentation, significantly enhancing both segmentation accuracy and processing speed. Using GAN, Liu et al. (2019a) (Liu et al. (2019b)) performed label assignments and generated probabilistic maps, enabling accurate segmentation of colorectal tumors. Ruan et al. ## 8.4.2 Classification on the Basis of Image Modality

In this section, GAN-based medical segmentation methods are organized and categorized according to the specific image modalities they utilize. The section thoroughly examines several image modalities, including MRI, CT, and OCT.

### 8.4.2.1 Segmentation of Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) Using GAN

MRI is a medical imaging method employing powerful magnetic fields and radio waves to produce detailed visuals of the body's internal structures. This technology produces high-resolution three-dimensional images, facilitating the visualization of soft tissues, organs, and various structures such as the brain, muscles, joints, and blood vessels. Notably, MRI stands out as a safer alternative for certain diagnostic needs compared to X-rays or CT scans, as it does not involve the use of ionizing radiation.

Authors in Conte et al. (2021) utilized GAN to generate missing MRI sequences, demonstrating the effectiveness of GAN-generated images in assisting other DL models in segmentation tasks. Authors in Chen et al. (2020) achieved precise segmentation of craniomaxillofacial (CMF) bony structures in MRI images. Authors in Yang et al. (2021) introduced a dilated block adversarial network for segmenting the left ventricle, right ventricle, and myocardium in short-axis cardiac MRI images. Authors in Conze et al. (2021) harnessed GAN for automatic multi-organ segmentation in abdominal MR images, effectively achieving simultaneous segmentation of multiple organs. Authors in Gadermaier et al. (2019) employed GAN for segmenting muscle tissue in MRI images (Table 8.9).

### 8.4.2.2 Segmentation of Computed Tomography (CT) Images Using GAN

CT is a medical imaging method that employs X-rays to produce detailed cross-sectional images of the body, offering comprehensive views of internal structures. This enables the detailed visualization of bones, organs, and tissues. CT scans play a crucial role in diagnosing a range of medical conditions, including injuries, infections, and tumors. The process involves the use of a rotating X-ray machine and a computer to generate detailed two-dimensional images or three-dimensional reconstructions. Due to its rapidity and capacity to capture precise anatomical details, CT imaging is widely utilized in medical diagnostics across various specialties.
