We employed an Adam optimizer for model compilation, while the loss of the model is governed by binary cross-entropy due to the presence of only two classes: leucocytic cells and background. Learning rate decay is implemented for three consecutive epochs, and an early stopping condition is triggered if there is no improvement for five consecutive epochs. The U-net architecture is illustrated in Figure 9.5 and does not include dense layers, allowing it to accept data samples of any dimension. In our classical U-net design, various parameters are denoted by specific symbols, each carrying a distinct meaning. For instance, “2@Conv” denotes a pair of consecutive convolutional layers, while the output tensors of these convolutions are represented by “c1, c2,... cn.” The output tensors of the upsampling layers are prefixed with “u” (e.g. “u6, u7, u8, and u9”), and those of the downsampling layers are indicated by “p1, p2, p3, and p4.” The output of the encoder corresponds to an image size of 8*8*256, while that of the decoder segment is 128*128*1. This signifies that the contraction path reduces the image dimensions and increases the filter depth in deeper layers of the network, whereas the decoder segment reconstructs the original image size by incorporating skip connections with the upconvolutional layers.
