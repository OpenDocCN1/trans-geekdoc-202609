Huo et al. (Huo et al. 2022) introduce a progressive VAE-based framework (PAVAE) intended for creating artificial brain lesions alongside their associated segmentation masks. The authors propose a two-phase strategy, where the first phase involves generating synthetic segmentation masks using a conditional adversarial VAE. In the following stage, photorealistic lesion images are generated by incorporating them with the lesion mask through “mask embedding blocks.” These blocks embed the lesion mask into the feature space during generation, similar to the approach used in Spatially-Adaptive Denormalization (SPADE). A segmentation network is trained using a combination of genuine and synthetic lesions, resulting in an enhanced Dice score compared to a model trained solely on authentic images from 66.69% to 74.18%. Fernandez and colleagues (Fernandez et al. 2022) present brainSPADE, a generative model designed to create labeled brain MRI images suitable for training segmentation models. This model merges a DM with a VAE-GAN, leveraging SPADE normalization in the GAN component to integrate the segmentation mask effectively. The model comprises two main parts: a segmentation map generator and an image generator. The segmentation map generator, a VAE, initially processes a segmentation map, extracting and structuring its latent space. This space is refined via diffusion and denoising with Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) to emphasize meaningful information while filtering out noise. A VAE decoder subsequently generates an artificial segmentation map from this refined latent space. The image generator, a SPADE model, establishes a style latent space from any provided style and incorporates it with the artificial segmentation map to produce the ultimate output image. The effectiveness of brainSPADE is evaluated in a segmentation task using nnU-Net, showcasing similar performance when trained on synthetic data as compared to real data, with a notable performance improvement observed when both types of data are merged.
