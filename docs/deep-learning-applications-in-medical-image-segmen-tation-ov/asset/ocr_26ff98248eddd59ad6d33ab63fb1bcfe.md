# 9.3 Result and Discussion

Medical images often suffer from various types of outliers, leading to inappropriate discrimination of cell imaging subcomponents. Additionally, many relevant studies have recognized the challenges associated with processing biomedical imaging datasets (Jia et al. 2021; Makem and Tiedeu 2020). Some automated approaches, such as pattern-based segmentation and boundary cell segmentation, result in under-segmentation and over-segmentation of data points due to noisy data clusters. To address this issue, we have devised an improvement in our research that focuses on segmenting enhanced biological images. The objective of this approach is to mitigate misregistration of data points to enable smooth extraction of leucocytic cells.

The proposed approach is evaluated using three different biological databases: the ALL-IDB dataset, CellaVision dataset, and the JTSC database. The ALL-IDB contains lymphocytes from acute leukemia patients, with segmented and non-segmented subsets available. For evaluation purposes, we utilized the non-segmented samples from this subset. The medical samples in this subset vary in dimension, necessitating resampling to a common size. The CellaVision and JTSC datasets comprise leukocytes of five different types: basophils, eosinophils, monocytes, lymphocytes, and neutrophils. These datasets are publicly accessible, and manual annotation files are provided for all of them. We adopted a specific ratio (80 : 20) for training and testing the model.

Evaluation metrics: The performance of the model is assessed using various metrics (Dice 1945; Muthu and Murali 2021), including the Jaccard Index (J), F1 measure, accuracy, precision, recall, and specificity. The Jaccard Index (J) gauges the degree of overlap between the model's results and the annotation file masks, ranging from 0 (indicating imperfect segmentation) to 1 (denoting perfect segmentation). The F1 measure strikes a balance between precision and recall, with values ranging from 0 (signifying an imbalanced harmonic mean) to 1 (representing a balanced harmonic mean).

Accuracy is a metric that computes the overall correctness of the model, where perfect accuracy is indicated by a value of 1 and imperfect accuracy by 0.
