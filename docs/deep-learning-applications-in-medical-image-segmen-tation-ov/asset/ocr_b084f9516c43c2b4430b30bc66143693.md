# ## 8.4.3.1 Segmentation Using U-Net Based GAN

Authors in Xue et al. (2018) utilized U-Net as the generator for the GAN and introduced a multi-scale L1 loss method, enabling direct and efficient brain tumor segmentation. Authors in Shi et al. (2019) introduced the UG-net (a modified U-Net within a GAN) specifically designed for segmenting hippocampal subfields. Authors in Guo et al. (2020) utilized U-Net to recognize boundaries and GAN for shape prediction, specifically targeting the segmentation of pectoral muscle regions. Authors in Guo et al. (2020) utilized U-Net and a pyramid structure as the generator and discriminator, respectively, to extract intricate features from coronary arteries. Authors in Sultana et al. (2020) utilized a 3D U-Net with multi-class capabilities for the preliminary segmentation of pelvic structures, followed by the implementation of an enhanced GAN for more precise organ segmentation. Authors in Wang et al. (2020) incorporated the structural elements of U-Net into a GAN to improve the segmentation of the esophagus in OCT images (Table 8.12).

# ## 8.4.3.2 Segmentation Using Conditional GANs (CGAN, pix2pix GAN, and ACGAN)

Authors in Oh et al. (2020) utilized a CGAN implemented within a pix2pix framework to accurately segment white matter in 18F-FDG PET/CT images. This approach led to the precise delineation of brain tissues. Authors in Jiang et al. (2018) introduced CGAN along with the Dice loss function, pioneering lacquer crack segmentation.
