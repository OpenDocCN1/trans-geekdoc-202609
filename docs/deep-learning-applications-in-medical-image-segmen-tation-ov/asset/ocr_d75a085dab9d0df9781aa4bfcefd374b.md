**Lung CT-Diagnosis Dataset:** This dataset was updated in 2014 and is accessible to the public through TCIA. Consisting of 61 patient's data, the dataset is kept up to date by Moffitt Cancer Center. The dataset comprises 4682 images, with a cumulative size of 2.5 GB. These images represent the CT medical imaging modality saved in DICOM format, with the “slice width ranging from 3–6 mm.” Each file contains metadata containing patient details and the instance number of tumor slices. (Cancer imaging archive n.d.)

**NLST dataset:** The NLST dataset, updated in 2013, is a restricted-access repository, a crucial clinical study that evaluates the effectiveness of low radiation CT screening in decreasing mortality rates associated with lung cancer. In addition to pathological images, the dataset encompasses radiological images paired with pertinent clinical data. The collaborative efforts of the “National Cancer Institute’s Division of Cancer Prevention and Division of Cancer Treatment and Diagnosis” underpin this project. This dataset comprises nearly 21,082,502 CT scans amounting to 11.3 TB, involving 26,254 individuals (NLST 2013). (National Cancer Institute - Cancer Data Access System n.d.).

# 7.6 Evaluation Metrics

Evaluation metrics are the quantitative measures used to measure the performance of a model or an algorithm. These metrics help in determining how well a model performs on a specific task. Their significance lies in facilitating comparisons between various algorithms and plays a vital role in ensuring the precision of a model in a practical context. The metrics assess the performance of the model by comparing the predicted results of the model with those of the ground truth. Ground truth means the actual lung segmentation results obtained manually by medical experts. Here's a summary of the commonly used metrics for lung segmentation in medical images.

**True Positive (TP):** When the model predicts something as positive which is positive. In the case of lung segmentation, the model correctly identifies the image pixels as lung pixels when they are lung pixels.

**True Negative (TN):** When the model predicts something as negative which is negative. In the case of lung segmentation when the system correctly classifies image pixels as non-lung pixels that do not belong to the lung region.
