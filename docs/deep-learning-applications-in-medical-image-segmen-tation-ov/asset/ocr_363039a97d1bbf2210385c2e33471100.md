While GAN's advancements offer new prospects for medical image segmentation, practical implementation faces several limitations. First, research in GAN for medical image segmentation leans heavily toward technological aspects, employing evaluation metrics typical in computer science. Most studies assessed the model's feasibility rather than its effectiveness in practical clinical settings, lacking evaluation against medical standards. Second, GAN, as a generative model, encounters issues like non-convergence, training complexity, mode collapse, and mode jump. When applying GAN's trained outcomes to new applications, the model often requires transfer learning to sustain performance, diminishing the repeatability in clinical contexts. Furthermore, GAN's interpretability is notably poor – it operates as a black-box mapping function, taking random variables as input and generating the desired data distribution as output. This lack of interpretability poses a significant hurdle to its practical adoption in the medical field. Hence, researchers must focus on overcoming GAN's challenges related to instability, low repeatability, and lack of interpretability for its successful integration into medical applications.

Furthermore, gaining acceptance from clinicians and patients regarding the data quality produced by GAN poses a challenge. As highlighted by Mazurowski et al. in Zhao et al. (2020), the clinical adoption of artificial intelligence in radiology remains a topic of debate. When implementing GAN in clinical settings, the primary concern revolves around the potential generation of erroneous information. During data generation, GAN might inadvertently create nonexistent disease information or overlook existing disease features as irrelevant. Consequently, cautious consideration is imperative for employing GAN in clinical practice. Similar to other applications, GAN-based implementations necessitate rigorous validation Zhou et al. (2016). In the short term, segmentation outcomes derived from GAN-based methods should undergo review by seasoned clinicians. Moreover, outcomes relying solely on GAN auto-segmentation ought to be limited to controlled experimental ranges. Furthermore, akin to other DL techniques, ethical and legal considerations come into play when employing GAN in clinical scenarios Zhu and Xie (2016). Despite GAN's limitations, judicious and meticulous use holds the potential to introduce numerous novel possibilities for medical image segmentation.

In summary, GAN holds vast potential in the realm of medical image processing. The diverse array of GAN-based methods and their iterations have garnered significant interest, notably advancing the application and evolution of DL algorithms in this field. Nevertheless, gaining acceptance for these methods among clinicians and patients remains a considerable hurdle. Additionally, GAN exhibits limitations, including instability, low repeatability, and interpretability challenges.
