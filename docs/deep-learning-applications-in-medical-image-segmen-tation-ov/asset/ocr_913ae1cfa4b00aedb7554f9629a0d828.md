Chest radiography is crucial in diagnosing lung diseases and heart pathologies, including tuberculosis, pleural effusion atelectasis, consolidation, pneumothorax, and hypercardiac inflation. These images are widely available, cost-effective, and involve lower doses than alternative imaging methods, making them an effective method for widespread screening (Candemir et al. 2018).

The initial tuberculosis screening system was introduced, leveraging transfer learning techniques and deep convolutional neural network (CNN) (Hwang et al. 2016). Rajaraman and Antani (2020a) introduced a specialized ensemble learning approach to identify chest X-ray irregularities. The predictions of these models are consolidated through diverse ensemble techniques to minimize prediction variance. Class-selective mapping is employed to visually highlight affected regions in these images. Loey et al. (2020) introduced a generative adversarial network (GAN) with transfer training for the prediction of COVID-19 in chest X-rays. Due to the limited data on COVID-19 disease, the GAN network generates additional chest X-ray images. The introduction to the CovidGAN technique is dependent on the Auxiliary Classifier Generative Adversarial Network, which produces artificial chest X-ray images for the COVID-19 classification (Waheed et al. 2020). Rajaraman and Antani (2020b) implemented a labeled data augmentation technique to elaborate the dataset for training purposes, thereby improving the prediction of COVID-19 in chest X-ray images.

# 10.2.2 Computerized Tomography

Computed tomography (CT) images are diagnostic medical images generated through a technology called CT scanning, commonly known as CT scanning. CT scans constitute a form of medical imaging that employs X-rays to create comprehensive cross-sectional body images. This technique facilitates the creation of a three-dimensional perspective of internal structures, enabling a more comprehensive assessment compared to traditional X-ray imaging. The various features of CT images are high spatial resolution, contrast enhancement, multiplanar imaging, and dense and soft tissue differentiation. These images are highly used for diagnosis and staging, treatment planning, vascular imaging, etc.
