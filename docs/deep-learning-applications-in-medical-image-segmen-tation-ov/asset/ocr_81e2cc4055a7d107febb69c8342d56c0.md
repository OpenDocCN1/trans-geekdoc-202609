The methodology involves lung parenchyma segmentation with contour correction using the α-hull algorithm. CT slices containing lung nodules undergo preprocessing steps, and an “improved U-Net with Batch Normalization” is employed for accurate segmentation. The training utilizes patches with corresponding ground truth from the “Lung Image Database Consortium and Image Database Resource Initiative (LIDC/IDRI).” The model surpasses state-of-the-art algorithms and achieves a Dice score of 0.8623. Meanwhile, the authors developed the “Hierarchical Segmentation Network (CHS-Net)” (Punn and Agarwal 2022) to identify lung regions affected by COVID-19-infected CT scans. The model was evaluated on combined datasets that are publicly available. CHS-Net addresses the scarcity of COVID-19 data. The model, inspired by advanced architectures such as U-Net, Google’s Inception, and residual networks, employs a multistage approach for the segmentation and extraction of features. CHS-Net achieves a 95% accuracy in segmenting the lung mask and the infected region. Recently, the authors introduced a novel approach named “Wavelet U-Net++,” (Akila Agnes et al. 2024) merging the “U-Net++ architecture with wavelet pooling.” This approach uses Haar wavelet transform to downsample the feature maps in the encoder, capturing both high- and low-frequency details to improve segmentation accuracy, especially for small and irregular nodules. Evaluation conducted on the LIDC-IDRI dataset, consisting of 1018 CT images, yielded promising findings, with a mean Dice coefficient of 0.936 and a mean IoU of 0.878. Furthermore, these advancements underscore the ongoing challenges in lung segmentation, necessitating innovative solutions for improved diagnostic accuracy (Table 7.1).

However, assembling a sizable medical dataset presents challenges due to legal and privacy concerns.
