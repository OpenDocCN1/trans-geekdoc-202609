# 3.8.5 Real-Time Segmentation and Clinical Applications

Contemporary techniques aim to achieve real-time segmentation capabilities, a crucial requirement for applications such as intraoperative guidance and dynamic imaging scenarios. The integration of advanced computational techniques enables faster processing times, making real-time segmentation feasible in clinical settings. This development has profound implications for surgical interventions, enabling precision and efficiency in treatment procedures (Litjens et al. 2017).

The real-time segmentation capabilities are exemplified in studies such as the work by Lorenz et al. (2000) , where automatic segmentation of the left ventricle in 3D echocardiography data was achieved. The application of real-time segmentation in cardiac imaging demonstrates the potential for immediate clinical impact, especially in scenarios where timely decision-making is crucial.

# 3.8.6 Challenges and Ongoing Research

Despite the remarkable progress in contemporary techniques, challenges persist in the field of medical image segmentation. The interpretability of deep learning models remains an ongoing concern, particularly in medical contexts where decisions have direct implications for patient care. The need for robustness to diverse imaging conditions, including variations in resolution, noise, and artifacts, continues to be a focus of research.

Ongoing research endeavors are exploring innovative solutions to address these challenges. Architectural modifications to neural networks, ensemble learning approaches, and the integration of attention mechanisms are among the strategies being investigated. The exploration of unsupervised and self-supervised learning techniques aims to reduce dependence on annotated datasets, providing avenues for more scalable and generalizable segmentation models.
