By incorporating graph-cut techniques, Candemir et al. aimed to address challenges related to variable lung shapes and high computation time. This method initially calculated the average lung shape and determined the boundary using graph-cut techniques. Despite encountering challenges in static shape modeling and facing high computation time, they proposed a method that employed a multi-class classifier approach, (Candemir et al. 2013) achieving segmentation in 8 seconds at a resolution of 1024x1024. The approach was further enhanced by incorporating anatomical atlases and non-registration, (Candemir et al. 2014) resulting in a faster execution time of 20–25 seconds at a resolution of 256x256.

Overall, deformable methods, including AAMs, active shape models, and geometric deformable models, exhibit the potential for precise segmentation of lung structures in chest radiography.

# 7.3.5 Pixel Classifier-Based Segmentation

From the early 1990s onward, various “Machine learning techniques such as k-nearest neighbors (kNNs) and Support vector machines (SVMs)” have been extensively employed for LFS. This pixel classifier (PC)-based approaches use grayscale values to assign pixels to anatomical structures, ensuring stable performance even in situations involving absent or significantly deformed anatomical structures, assuming the classifier is adequately trained. Training these classifiers is, however, challenging due to difficulties in obtaining diverse and high-quality annotated medical training data. PC-based methods are categorized into two groups: one based on shallow learning and the other based on deep learning, depending on the process of extracting features, which can be either manual or automatic (Figure 7.4).
