The quality of biological data notably impedes the generalization of appropriate data representations (Xu et al. 2022; Long 2020).

Strategies have been developed to improve the quality of biological data in response to existing challenges. These strategies are broadly categorized into representative and nonrepresentative learning approaches. In the nonrepresentative approach, a common method involves employing a multiscale transformation (MST) strategy, where data observations transform into high and low frequency data patches (Candès et al. 2006; Bhutada et al. 2011; Do and Vetterli 2005). These decomposed patches are then integrated using inverse transformation techniques, employing spatial fusion strategies. Despite its effectiveness in enhancing data quality, this transformation process often encounters misclassification issues due to inherited conditions within the MST approach. In contrast, the representative learning technique aims to minimize the misregistration of data points by linearly decomposing input observations into local and global geometric features. Fusion of these decomposed observations follows spatial fusion rules, resulting in the generation of improved medical images capable of discerning substructural data patterns (Li et al. 2020; Jia et al. 2021; Makem and Tiede u 2020).

The refined medical observations are fed into the deep learning model for semantic segmentation of cell images. We opted for the U-net model for the subsequent fusion process due to its effectiveness in preserving both local and global geometric characteristics.
