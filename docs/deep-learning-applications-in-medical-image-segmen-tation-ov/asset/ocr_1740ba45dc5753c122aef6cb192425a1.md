The authors built two models for anatomical segmentation using this framework: the anatomically gated fully convolutional network and the anatomically gated U-Net. Experimental results on the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) (Jack et al. 2008) and LONI-LPBA40 datasets show that the suggested algorithms provide higher performance in the ROI segmentation of brain MR images. Nie et al. (2019) took pictures of the brains of 11 healthy babies using T1, T2, and diffusion-weighted modes. The authors optimized the network by combining contextual semantic information and features with different scales. They also used 3D FCN to divide up multimodal brain MRI pictures. The brain tumor segmentation problem was broken down into two manageable tasks by Casamitjana et al. (Mangin and Rivi 2019): whole tumor segmentation and the partition of various tumor sections. When applied to completely convolutional and network topology with DCs, adversarial training improved the segmentation performance of brain MRI, as demonstrated by the work of Moeskops et al. (2017). Xue et al. (2018) inspired by traditional GAN, proposed SegAN, a novel adversarial neural network for segmenting medical images. In contrast to the extensive pixel-level labeling needed for image segmentation, the single scalar output (real or fake) from a typical GAN discriminator might not provide adequate gradient feedback for ensuring network stability. Rather than employing a conventional fully connected network for segmentation, the authors devised a novel adversarial critic network equipped with a multi-scale loss function. This approach compels both the critic and the segmentor to grasp both global and local features. This yields segmentation label maps. The authors show that a SegAN architecture beats the current U-net segmentation method, making it more effective and stable. Datasets from the Medical Image Computing and Computer-Assisted Intervention (MICCAI) BRATS brain tumor segmentation competition were used to evaluate the proposed SegAN approach. Numerous experimental findings showed that the proposed SegAN with multi-scale loss is effective. SegAN performed better than the existing models on BRATS 2015, surpassing in both dice score and precision while still providing performance comparable to the cutting-edge models on BRATS 2013 for whole tumor and tumor core segmentation.
