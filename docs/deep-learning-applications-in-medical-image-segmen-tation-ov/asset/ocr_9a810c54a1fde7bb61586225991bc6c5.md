# 7.3.6 Shallow Learning

In shallow learning, machine learning methods are employed to extract important information. The challenge lies in identifying a fitting class for the extracted features. With growing trends in research, (McNitt-Gray et al. 1993) aimed to build a segmentation technique using the “pattern classification approach.” Numerous classifiers include “linear discriminant, k-nearest neighbor, and feed-forward back-propagation neural network (FFBPNN)” to categorize pixels based on local texture and gray-level features. Each classifier was trained on a dataset comprising 17 CXR images and trained classifiers were evaluated on a distinct set of 16 images. Although the neural network outperformed KNN and LDA, accurately predicting over 76% of pixels, limitations existed. Vittitoe et al. addressed these limitations by introducing Markov random field modeling (MRFM) to identify lung regions and non-lung regions, achieving 94.8%-pixel classification accuracy on a test set of 115 CXRs, (Vittitoe et al. 1998) outperforming some previous methods. However, the computational expense of MRFM was acknowledged as a drawback. Finally, Shi et al. and Jangam et al. in their respective works individually modified the fuzzy c-means clustering algorithm for the segmentation of lungs. Shi et al. implemented an unsupervised approach using a “Gaussian kernel-based fuzzy C-means,” (Shi et al. 2009) achieving 97.8% accuracy on 52 CXRs from the JSRT dataset. On the other hand, Jangam et al. proposed an “optimized fuzzy c-means clustering” method, (Jangam and Rao 2019) incorporating a level set algorithm and spatial fuzzy clustering for segmentation. However, Jangam et al.’s method had a longer computation time of 25–30 seconds. Both studies differ in computational efficiency.

# 7.4.1 Lung Field Segmentation in Chest X-rays

Accurate lung segmentation is crucial in medical imaging because losing even a small part of the lung during the process can have serious consequences. Shallow learning methods for segmentation rely on manually defined features, which can be tedious and error-prone. In contrast, deep learning automatically extracts features from the data, rendering it a robust and efficient approach for lung segmentation.

Deep learning approaches for lung segmentation use advanced neural network architectures to automatically discern lung structures in CXR images.
