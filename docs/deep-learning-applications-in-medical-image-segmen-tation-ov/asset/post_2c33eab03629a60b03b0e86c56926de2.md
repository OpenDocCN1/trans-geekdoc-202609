For segmenting the brain in a 3D MRI image, Wang et al. (2019c) developed a 3D convolutional model with residual units and a pyramid pooling block. While numerous deep learning techniques have been devised for segmenting brain MR images, the majority of them neglect to integrate shape priors. This omission prevents them from leveraging the consistency of brain structures, thus hindering their ability to attain optimal performance. To solve this problem, Sun et al. (2020) presented a deep learning architecture with two subnetworks to perform ROI segmentation of the brain using anatomical attention. The first is a segmentation subnetwork, which is employed to separate ROIs from each input MR image while also extracting a discriminative picture representation. The second is a specialized subnetwork for paying attention to the brain's anatomy, which is fed by a collection of labeled atlases. The authors built two models for anatomical segmentation using this framework: the anatomically gated fully convolutional network and the anatomically gated U-Net. Experimental results on the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) (Jack et al. 2008) and LONI-LPBA40 datasets show that the suggested algorithms provide higher performance in the ROI segmentation of brain MR images. Nie et al. (2019) took pictures of the brains of 11 healthy babies using T1, T2, and diffusion-weighted modes. The authors optimized the network by combining contextual semantic information and features with different scales. They also used 3D FCN to divide up multimodal brain MRI pictures. The brain tumor segmentation problem was broken down into two manageable tasks by Casamitjana et al. (Mangin and Rivi 2019): whole tumor segmentation and the partition of various tumor sections. When applied to completely convolutional and network topology with DCs, adversarial training improved the segmentation performance of brain MRI, as demonstrated by the work of Moeskops et al. (2017). Xue et al. (2018) inspired by traditional GAN, proposed SegAN, a novel adversarial neural network for segmenting medical images. In contrast to the extensive pixel-level labeling needed for image segmentation, the single scalar output (real or fake) from a typical GAN discriminator might not provide adequate gradient feedback for ensuring network stability. Rather than employing a conventional fully connected network for segmentation, the authors devised a novel adversarial critic network equipped with a multi-scale loss function. This approach compels both the critic and the segmentor to grasp both global and local features. This yields segmentation label maps. The authors show that a SegAN architecture beats the current U-net segmentation method, making it more effective and stable. Datasets from the Medical Image Computing and Computer-Assisted Intervention (MICCAI) BRATS brain tumor segmentation competition were used to evaluate the proposed SegAN approach. Numerous experimental findings showed that the proposed SegAN with multi-scale loss is effective. SegAN performed better than the existing models on BRATS 2015, surpassing in both dice score and precision while still providing performance comparable to the cutting-edge models on BRATS 2013 for whole tumor and tumor core segmentation. ## 2.3.1.4 Lung

Segmenting lung images stands as a pivotal initial stage in lung imaging analysis, playing a fundamental role in providing precise interpretations of lung CT images for the detection of lung cancer. Lung segmentation in CT and X-ray images has been thoroughly studied (Gaäl et al. 2020; Pawar and Talbar 2021). For instance, Skourt et al. (Ait Skourt et al. 2018) designed a U-Net architecture for segmenting lungs from a CT image. Conventional lung segmentation methods, reliant on user-generated features, struggle to accurately segment lung parenchyma with nodules. Deep learning, proven to outperform traditional methods in image classification and object recognition, offers a more effective alternative. To alleviate the juxta pleural nodule problem, Wu et al. (Wu and Lin 2020) developed a new dilated convolutional-based weighted fully convolutional neural network for segmenting lung parenchyma. While deep learning models have improved significantly in their ability to segment anatomical structures, there is still cause for concern due to the enormous number of training parameters, which increases memory utilization and decreases generalization. To solve this problem, Yahyatabar et al. (2020) developed a Dense-UNet model. This model improved information flow by increasing the number of connections between each layer in the network. This enabled the maintenance of robust segmentation while designing a network with substantially fewer parameters.
