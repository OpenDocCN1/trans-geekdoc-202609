# ## 9.1 Introduction

Computational advancements in cell biology have transformed the medical field by providing unprecedented insights into morphological cell patterns. Cell image segmentation is pivotal for extracting meaningful patterns from biomedical images, facilitating a deeper comprehension of cellular processes. Precise segmentation of biological cells is crucial for personalized medication, enabling treatments tailored to individual cell characteristics and aiding in the early diagnosis of diseases by identifying abnormal cell patterns. Recently, numerous computational solutions have emerged for automatically segmenting cell images, categorized into three classes: image thresholding techniques, clustering approaches, and deep learning methods. The thresholding technique segments medical images by generating a filter to distinguish between foreground and background pixel categories, calculated based on the pixel intensities of various components within the image. Finding the optimal value for this filter is highly challenging due to inherent preset conditions in the method, which may potentially alter the morphological properties (such as perimeter, intensity, eccentricity, and major arc) of cells (Sheikh and Chachoo 2022a). Consequently, this approach risks damaging the morphological features of the cells. Clustering methods assign identities to data points based on their proximity to the nearest cluster, determined through Euclidean and Manhattan distance measurements. These methods typically employ k-means and fuzzy c-means for medical image segmentation. However, it has been observed that these approaches can suffer from both over-sampling and under-sampling of data points (Sheikh and Chachoo 2022b).
