
False Positive (FP): When the model predicts something as positive which is negative. In the case of lung segmentation when the model classifies image pixels as the lung pixels that belong to the non-lung region.

False Negative (FN): When the model classifies something as negative which is positive. In the case of lung segmentation when the model classifies image pixels as background pixels that belong to the lung region.

Here's a summary of the commonly used metrics for lung segmentation in medical images.

### Accuracy
Accuracy serves as a pivotal metric for evaluating deep learning models, providing insight into their prediction capabilities. It measures the ratio of accurate predictions to the total predictions made by the model, determining the percentage of image pixels correctly classified as lung or non-lung. This computation is achieved through the following formula:

Accuracy = \frac{TP + TN}{TP + TN + FP + FN}

### Jaccard Index
Also known as an overlap measure, it is the measure of similarity between ground truth mask and segmented image. It is also called IoU. It is calculated using the below formula:

Jaccard Index = \frac{TP}{TP + FP + FN}

Its value ranges between 0 and 1, where 0 indicates no overlap or similarity between both the predicted image and ground truth image, and 1 signifies perfect overlap, that is, both segmented image and ground truth are identical. The higher value of the Jaccard Index indicates better similarity and accuracy in the segmentation results. The Jaccard Index is considered balanced because it takes both false positives and false negatives into account. It penalizes the model for either missing or including regions that are not present in the ground truth.

### Dice coefficient
The DSC measures the similarity between two sets, typically used in tasks such as image segmentation and natural language processing. This metric quantifies the overlap between the predicted segmentation and the ground truth. Again, higher DSC indicates better segmentation accuracy. It is also known as “Sørensen Dice coefficient” calculated as follows:

DC = \frac{2 \times TP}{2 \times TP + FP + FN}

### Precision
Precision is a metric that measures the accuracy of positive predictions made by a model. It is calculated as the ratio of TP predictions to the total number of instances classified as positive by the model, regardless of whether those predictions are correct or incorrect. In the case of lung segmentation, it is the number of correctly classified pixels divided by the total number of pixels classified as lung pixels. Precision is an important evaluation metric when false positive predictions are costly. For example, if a model incorrectly classifies healthy tissues as cancerous. High precision means that the errors of incorrectly identifying positive values are minimized. Precision gives us the measure of the accuracy of positive predictions made by the model. It is calculated as follows:

Precision = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}

### Recall
Also known as sensitivity, it is a measure of the ability of the model to correctly capture and identify the TPs. In case of lung segmentation, it is the probability of correctly classifying the lung pixels from an image. It is a useful metric when false negatives are more critical than false positives. It is calculated using the below formula:

Recall = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}

### Specificity
In contrast to sensitivity or recall, specificity is the measure of the ability of the model to correctly identify the TNs. In the case of lung segmentation, it is the probability of correctly classifying a background (or a non-lung) pixel. It becomes a useful metric when the cost of false positives is high or when there is a focus on the accuracy of negative predictions. This metric is derived using the following formula:

Specificity = \frac{TN}{TN + FN}

Specificity tells us how accurately a model can make correct negative predictions. A higher value of specificity means the model is effective at correctly identifying TNs, minimizing the occurrence of false positives. A lower value means that the model is incorrectly predicting negative instances as positive, leading to false positives.

### F1-score
The F1-score is a crucial evaluation metric, offering a balanced assessment of a model's performance by considering both precision and recall. By computing the harmonic mean of precision and recall, the F1-score provides a symmetric representation of both metrics in a single value. With a maximum value of 1 representing perfect precision and recall, and a minimum value of 0 indicating either precision or recall being zero, the F1-score serves as a reliable indicator of a model's accuracy and is calculated as follows:

F1 Score = \frac{2 \times Precision \times Recall}{Precision + Recall}

### Hausdorff distance
Measures the maximum distance between any two points on the predicted and ground truth segmentation boundaries. Lower Hausdorff distance indicates better alignment between the segmentations.

The choice of evaluation metrics for lung segmentation or any medical image segmentation task depends on several factors, including specific goals of the segmentation, dataset characteristics, and the specific qualities desired in the evaluation metric.
