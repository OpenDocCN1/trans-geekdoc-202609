## 2.2.8 Ensemble-Based Models

The ensemble mechanism is effective in decreasing overfitting through training (Maclin 2016). The ensemble module integrates the results of multiple networks running in parallel to perform the necessary segmentation (Maclin 2016). The approach is based on a concept that incorporates both pre- and post-processing modules for images. To enhance the network’s ability to segment the ROI from the input image, a pre-processing unit is used, and then the result is refined in a post-processing stage. The deep learning community has discovered that ensemble mechanisms often outperform more traditional single subnetwork approaches. The training procedure benefits from the unique contributions of each subnetwork in the ensemble module (Li et al. 2018a). The authors of Kamnitsas et al. (2018), who suggested a network-of-networks ensemble to aid in tumor localization, were declared the winners of the BraTs 2016 competition. Multiple U-shaped models were defined by the authors of Li et al. (2018a) to address multiple modalities with MRI data (Han and Ye 2018). Similar to this, Feng et al. (2020) suggested two distinct U-Net-inspired algorithms for the semantic segmentation of medical images. One method involves orthogonal-wavelet frames, whereas the other captures residual signals from a low-resolution path. This strategy delivers superior outcomes compared to the traditional U-Net model.

## 2.2.9 Other Models

R-CNN variants like Mask R-CNN (He et al. 2017) handle the instance segmentation issue, which necessitates simultaneous object identification and segmentation. Object identification and segmentation at two distinct levels are the building blocks of mask R-CNN models. To train deep generative models, researchers have developed the generative adversarial network (GAN) (Goodfellow et al. 2020). As shown in Figure 2.10, it is made up of two networks: a generator network that finds the distribution of data, and a discriminator network that attempts to tell the difference between the genuine distribution of data and the fake data provided by the generator. Together, these models learn the data distribution. A conditional generative adversarial network (M-GAN) for segmenting retinal vessels was proposed by the authors of Park et al. (2020). M-GAN employs layered deep FCN to compensate for losses.

Zhou et al. (2019) presented Model Genesis, a method for segmenting 3D medical picture data using a transfer learning algorithm, in 2019. Transfer learning is essential when working with medical data, which frequently comes in 3-D formats like MRI and CT. To avoid having to train medical images from scratch, it is necessary to convert 3D images into 2D images and make the necessary adjustments. As a result of this alteration, crucial spatial information is lost, affecting the overall performance of the network. To address this issue, the authors of this work introduced Models Genesis, a set of models that can learn on their own and require no human labeling. The authors of the research used a transfer learning approach to create application-specific target architecture. They tested the paradigm on five 3D applications that faced classification and segmentation issues. Experiments showed that transfer learning from the 2D-ImageNet dataset outperformed natively trained models on 3D medical data. The relevance of the suggested framework was demonstrated by its consistent superiority in performance to any 2D technique. Furthermore, the authors have demonstrated their intent to broaden the concept beyond applications to encompass domains and modes of operation.

## 2.3 Applications of Medical Image Segmentation Models

Medical image analysis is the key component of the medical system dedicated to disease diagnosis. Medical imaging provides both visual and functional representations of internal human organs for the purpose of clinical study. The major goal is to categorize medical images into various classifications or to emphasize specific data within an image. Analyzing medical images is significant because it can help radiologists and clinicians accurately diagnose diseases. Medical imaging is also utilized to segment the lesions and organ anatomy.

### 2.3.1 Segmentation of Anatomical Organs

Medical images allow for an accurate examination of organ structure. Medical images can be more quantitatively analyzed for clinical characteristics relating to form and volume, such as brain and heart studies, by segmenting organs and anatomical features. Furthermore, it is frequently required as an initial step in Computer-Aided Detection/Diagnosis (CAD). Bones, vertebrae, blood arteries, and main organs are examples of anatomical structures of the human body. However, because 3D scans contain multiple slices, it is challenging for medical experts to extract anatomy of interest or lesions from these images, and doing so is a time-consuming operation. There are numerous DL approaches available for organ segmentation in medical image analysis. A number of bespoke models have been created to directly target segmentation tasks. ### 2.3.1.1 Eye

The segmentation of retinal blood vessels is a difficult topic in retinal pathology research. Oversegmentation or the absence of small, fragile blood arteries is still an issue. Deep learning-based techniques for segmenting retinal vessels outperform human specialists. The first work to use a deep convolutional neural network for the segmentation of retinal blood vessels was by Liskowski and Krawiec (2016). Since then, several applications for retinal vascular segmentation have primarily used CNNs. A fully residual auto-encoder batch normalization network (PixelBNN) was presented as a quick architecture for retinal vascular segmentation by Leopold et al. (2019). U-Net and PixelCNN serve as its foundation. Within FCN, it takes advantage of both skip connections and batch normalization. The next step was to train, test, and cross-test the model on three different retinal blood vessel segmentation datasets: DRIVE (Staal et al. 2004), STARE (Hoover et al. 2000), and CHASEDB1 (Owen et al. 2009). The execution time and performance in the test were satisfactory. Wang et al. (2019a) proposed a new U-Net with two encoders, one for spatial information and one for context information, to better capture the meaning of the data. The authors created a feature fusion module at the intersection of the two roads to merge the various forms of feature representation. In addition, the authors leverage channel attention in a skip connection to pick a relevant feature map. In addition, the multi-scale prediction module combines low-level and high-level prediction for enhanced precision. The suggested DEU-Net model was able to attain higher accuracy in retinal vascular segmentation on both datasets. Yue et al. (2019) recommended an enhanced U-net structure for vascular segmentation in the retina. Adding a multi-scale input layer and dense block to the classic U-net allows it to make use of more sophisticated spatial contextual information. The segmentation outcomes have been enhanced in particular for thin blood vessels, which are challenging to detect due to their low contrast with the surrounding pixels. To increase accuracy, Zhang et al. (Zhang and Chung 2018) employed U-Net with residual connection to spot blood vessels and implemented an edge-sensing technique to mark the boundary region with more information. Retinal blood vessel segmentation was proposed by Jaemin et al. (Son et al. 2017) using generative adversarial training. There should be less room for error in the segmentation of blood arteries with this procedure. Khan et al. (2021) efficiently segment large, medium, and thin vessels across a wide range of contrast, position, and size of the optic disc, providing a multi-scale kernel based on completely convolutional layers that are extremely lightweight. In addition, the multi-scale kernels, reduced application of pooling operations, and skip connections are all used by the presented architecture to speed up training. In terms of specificity, accuracy, Area Under the Curve (AUC), and sensitivity, the testing results demonstrated that the method consistently outperformed the control group by a considerable margin. The determination of degenerative retinal disorders relies heavily on high-fidelity segmentation of the retina’s macro- and microvascular structure, yet this is a challenging task. Segmentation methods based on autoencoding encounter difficulties in extracting retinal microvascular structure due to consecutive resolution reduction during the encoding phase and the incapacity to recover this lost information in the decoding phase. In response to this issue, Kamran et al. (Kamran et al. 2021) introduced RV-GAN, an innovative multi-scale generative framework tailored for accurate retinal vascular segmentation. This proposed architecture enhanced microvessel detection and segmentation through the utilization of two generators and two multi-scale autoencoding discriminators. When compared to conventional GAN-based segmentation algorithms, the author’s unique weighted feature matching loss significantly improves accuracy. The developed architecture combines reconstruction with weighted feature matching loss to obtain AUC values of 98.87, 99.14, and 98.87 in segmenting retinal vessels from three datasets, DRIVE (Staal et al. 2004), STARE (Hoover et al. 2000), and CHASE (Owen et al. 2009), respectively.
