### 8.4.1.2 Eye Segmentation Using GAN

The challenges stemming from fundus image acquisition limitations, such as prevalent image noise and the intricate, variable nature of the retina, pose significant hurdles for segmentation. Surprisingly, numerous GAN-based approaches outperform human experts in segmenting fundus images. Automatically segmenting fundus images presents significant challenges due to the intricate branching patterns of vessels and the prevalent background noise. Lahiri et al. achieved precise retinal blood vessel segmentation in their works (Lahiri et al. 2017; Mahapatra et al. 2017; Iqbal and Ali 2018; Javanmardi and Tasdizen 2018; Son et al. 2019; Yang et al. 2020). In their 2017 study (Lahiri et al. 2017), Lahiri et al. pioneered the use of GAN semi-supervised learning, marking an effective approach to segmenting retinal blood vessels. Iqbal and Ali (2018) redefined a set of loss functions to generate more realistic retinal images, aiding in the segmentation process. Yang et al. (2020) enhanced both the generator and discriminator network structures to accurately capture vessel edge details during segmentation. Schlegl et al. conducted the segmentation of abnormal lesions within fundus images (Schlegl et al. 2017, 2019; Jiang et al. 2019; Wang et al. 2021a), contributing to the diagnosis of ocular diseases. Glaucoma stands as one of the prevalent eye diseases, where accurate segmentation of the optic cup and optic disc, determining the cup-to-disc ratio (CDR), holds crucial significance. Shankaranarayana et al. employed various adversarial learning methods to address this challenge (Shankaranarayana et al. 2017; Wang et al. 2019; Kadambi et al. 2020; Luo et al. 2021). In contrast to the previously mentioned studies, Liu et al. (2019c) and Tennakoon et al. (2018) employed GAN techniques to segment layer and fluid regions within OCT images. Furthermore, Ouyang et al. (2019) and Yildiz et al. (2021) utilized distinct GAN-based methods to segment the cornea. MedRegNet (Santarossa et al. 2022), an unsupervised GAN-based descriptor module, enhances multimodal retinal image registration without requiring expert annotation, demonstrating improved performance over classical algorithms, even in cases of substantial visual disparity between modalities, offering adaptability and potential application beyond retinal imaging (Table 8.2).

### 8.4.1.3 Cardiology Segmentation Using GAN

Cardiology is the medical specialty primarily focused on the study, diagnosis, and treatment of the heart and its associated circulatory system. Another term that can be used as a synonym for cardiology is “cardiovascular medicine” or “vascular medicine.” These terms encompass a broader scope, including the heart and blood vessels, highlighting the comprehensive nature of the field beyond just the heart itself.

Segmenting the heart in medical images poses greater complexity compared to other anatomical regions due to its dynamic nature as a continuously functioning organ, whose shape varies during physical activity. Consequently, precise computation, modeling, and analysis of the complete heart structure hold immense significance for medical research and practical applications. Because each region within the heart exhibits distinct shapes and operational characteristics, the segmentation methods and challenges also vary across these regions.

Building on this understanding, Dou et al. conducted segmentation of various regions of interest across references (Chartsias et al. 2018; Zhang et al. 2018b; Dong et al. 2018; Joyce et al. 2018; Rezaei et al. 2018b; Upendra et al. 2019; Liu et al. 2019a; Xu et al. 2020; Decourt and Duong 2020; Shi et al. 2020; Wu et al. 2021; Yang et al. 2021; Cui et al. 2021; Gilbert et al. 2021; Chen et al. 2022). At the same time, Xu et al. (2020) introduced a deep spatiotemporal adversarial network, enabling simultaneous segmentation and measurement of the myocardial infarction area. Decourt and Duong (2020) proposed a novel loss function to enhance the accuracy of segmenting boundary pixels, notably improving segmentation precision for the myocardium, endocardium, and epicardium, particularly when dealing with limited annotation data. The implementation of GAN-based methodologies for automatic cardiac region segmentation addresses practical challenges in the field of cardiac medicine to a considerable extent (Table 8.3).
