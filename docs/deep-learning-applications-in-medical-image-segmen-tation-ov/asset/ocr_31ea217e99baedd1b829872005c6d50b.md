# 2.3.1.4 Lung

Segmenting lung images stands as a pivotal initial stage in lung imaging analysis,
playing a fundamental role in providing precise interpretations of lung CT images
for the detection of lung cancer. Lung segmentation in CT and X-ray images has
been thoroughly studied (Gaäl et al. 2020; Pawar and Talbar 2021). For instance,
Skourt et al. (Ait Skourt et al. 2018) designed a U-Net architecture for segmenting
lungs from a CT image. Conventional lung segmentation methods, reliant on
user-generated features, struggle to accurately segment lung parenchyma with
nodules. Deep learning, proven to outperform traditional methods in image
classification and object recognition, offers a more effective alternative. To
alleviate the juxta pleural nodule problem, Wu et al. (Wu and Lin 2020) developed
a new dilated convolutional-based weighted fully convolutional neural network
for segmenting lung parenchyma. While deep learning models have improved
significantly in their ability to segment anatomical structures, there is still
cause for concern due to the enormous number of training parameters, which
increases memory utilization and decreases generalization. To solve this problem,
Yahyatabar et al. (2020) developed a Dense-UNet model. This model improved
information flow by increasing the number of connections between each layer
in the network. This enabled the maintenance of robust segmentation while
designing a network with substantially fewer parameters. The authors claim that
