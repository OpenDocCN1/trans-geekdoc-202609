False Positive (FP): When the model predicts something as positive which is negative. In the case of lung segmentation when the model classifies image pixels as the lung pixels that belong to the non-lung region.

False Negative (FN): When the model classifies something as negative which is positive. In the case of lung segmentation when the model classifies image pixels as background pixels that belong to the lung region.

Here's a summary of the commonly used metrics for lung segmentation in medical images.

**Accuracy:** Accuracy serves as a pivotal metric for evaluating deep learning models, providing insight into their prediction capabilities. It measures the ratio of accurate predictions to the total predictions made by the model, determining the percentage of image pixels correctly classified as lung or non-lung. This computation is achieved through the following formula:

Accuracy = \frac{TP + TN}{TP + TN + FP + FN}

**Jaccard Index:** Also known as an overlap measure, it is the measure of similarity between ground truth mask and segmented image. It is also called IoU. It is calculated using the below formula:

Jaccard Index = \frac{TP}{TP + FP + FN}

Its value ranges between 0 and 1, where 0 indicates no overlap or similarity between both the predicted image and ground truth image, and 1 signifies perfect overlap, that is, both segmented image and ground truth are identical. The higher value of the Jaccard Index indicates better similarity and accuracy in the segmentation results. The Jaccard Index is considered balanced because it takes both false positives and false negatives into account. It penalizes the model for either missing or including regions that are not present in the ground truth.

**Dice coefficient:** The DSC measures the similarity between two sets, typically used in tasks such as image segmentation and natural language processing. This metric quantifies the overlap between the predicted segmentation and the ground truth. Again, higher DSC indicates better segmentation accuracy. It is also known as “Sørensen Dice coefficient” calculated as follows:

DC = \frac{2 \times TP}{2 \times TP + FP + FN}

**Precision:** Precision is a metric that measures the accuracy of positive predictions made by a model. It is calculated as the ratio of TP predictions to the total number of instances classified as positive by the model, regardless of whether those predictions are correct or incorrect. In the case of lung segmentation, it is the number of correctly classified pixels divided by the total number of pixels classified as lung pixels.
