## A Collaborative Cell Image Segmentation Model Based on the Multilevel Improvement of Data

Ishfaq Sheikh, Manzoor Chachoo, and Aijaz Mir

Department of computer science, University of Kashmir, Srinagar, Jammu and Kashmir, India

### 9.1 Introduction

Computational advancements in cell biology have transformed the medical field by providing unprecedented insights into morphological cell patterns. Cell image segmentation is pivotal for extracting meaningful patterns from biomedical images, facilitating a deeper comprehension of cellular processes. Precise segmentation of biological cells is crucial for personalized medication, enabling treatments tailored to individual cell characteristics and aiding in the early diagnosis of diseases by identifying abnormal cell patterns. Recently, numerous computational solutions have emerged for automatically segmenting cell images, categorized into three classes: image thresholding techniques, clustering approaches, and deep learning methods. The thresholding technique segments medical images by generating a filter to distinguish between foreground and background pixel categories, calculated based on the pixel intensities of various components within the image. Finding the optimal value for this filter is highly challenging due to inherent preset conditions in the method, which may potentially alter the morphological properties (such as perimeter, intensity, eccentricity, and major arc) of cells (Sheikh and Chachoo 2022a). Consequently, this approach risks damaging the morphological features of the cells. Clustering methods assign identities to data points based on their proximity to the nearest cluster, determined through Euclidean and Manhattan distance measurements. These methods typically employ k-means and fuzzy c-means for medical image segmentation. However, it has been observed that these approaches can suffer from both over-sampling and under-sampling of data points (Sheikh and Chachoo 2022b). Nevertheless, these approaches may suffer from over- or under-sampling of data points. Deep learning models are extensively utilized in cell tracking and segmentation tasks due to their efficient data representation. Despite addressing major deficiencies in existing techniques, deep learning encounters challenges related to data quantity and quality. The quality of biological data notably impedes the generalization of appropriate data representations (Xu et al. 2022; Long 2020).

Strategies have been developed to improve the quality of biological data in response to existing challenges. These strategies are broadly categorized into representative and nonrepresentative learning approaches. In the nonrepresentative approach, a common method involves employing a multiscale transformation (MST) strategy, where data observations transform into high and low frequency data patches (Candès et al. 2006; Bhutada et al. 2011; Do and Vetterli 2005). These decomposed patches are then integrated using inverse transformation techniques, employing spatial fusion strategies. Despite its effectiveness in enhancing data quality, this transformation process often encounters misclassification issues due to inherited conditions within the MST approach. In contrast, the representative learning technique aims to minimize the misregistration of data points by linearly decomposing input observations into local and global geometric features. Fusion of these decomposed observations follows spatial fusion rules, resulting in the generation of improved medical images capable of discerning substructural data patterns (Li et al. 2020; Jia et al. 2021; Makem and Tiede 2020).

The refined medical observations are fed into the deep learning model for semantic segmentation of cell images.
