Nevertheless, determining the most effective augmentation technique for specific image types remains ambiguous due to variations in diseases, network architectures, and dataset sizes used in different studies. In this section, we will discuss some of the augmentation techniques used in medical image segmentation.

# Rotation

Rotation-based image augmentations entail rotating an image while preserving its initial orientation. This rotation take place within a fresh coordinate system, ensuring the retention of the pixels’ relative positions. The rotation may proceed either clockwise or counterclockwise around an axis within a range spanning from 1° to 359°. Nevertheless, with increasing rotation angles, there is a potential risk of losing image labels. Consequently, the reliability of this augmentation technique hinges on the extent of rotation. While rotation transformations might not be ideal for recognizing digits 6 and 9 in digit recognition tasks, they are typically deemed safe for medical images.

# Flipping

Flipping is a method that produces a mirrored version of an image by reversing the positions of its pixels relative to one of the two axes (in the case of 2D images). Although it can be performed along either the vertical or the horizontal axis, vertical flipping is seldom preferred due to potential inconsistencies in the interchange of the top and bottom regions of an image (Nalepa et al. 2019).

# Translation

Translation entails moving images along an axis by implementing a translation vector. This approach preserves the relative pixel positions, thereby reducing positional bias (Shorten and Khoshgoftaar 2019) and preventing models from excessively focusing on features in a specific spatial location (Nalepa et al. 2019).

# Scaling

Images are subjected to scaling along different axes through the application of scaling factors, which might differ or remain uniform across each axis. Specifically, scaling modifications can be described as either reducing in size (when the scaling factor <1) or enlarging (when the scaling factor >1).

# Shearing

This translation moves one edge of an image along either the vertical or the horizontal axis, leading to a parallelogram shape. Vertical shear displaces an edge along the vertical axis, while horizontal shear shifts an edge along the horizontal axis. The extent of shear is dictated by a shear angle.

# Augmentation Using Intensities

This augmentation includes modifying contrast or brightness, blurring, standardizing intensity, histogram equalization, sharpening, and introducing noise.
