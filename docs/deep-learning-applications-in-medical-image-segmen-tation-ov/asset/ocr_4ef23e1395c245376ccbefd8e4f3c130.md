# 7.4.1.1 CNN-Based Approaches

In the past few years, with the rise of deep learning techniques, especially “CNNs,” the field of artificial intelligence has undergone a remarkable evolution. This study emphasizes the effectiveness of CNNs in automating segmentation processes, thereby reducing the manual effort and time required. Certainly, CNNs can be employed for various applications, including but not limited to image recognition, computer vision, natural language processing, and speech recognition. In medical image analysis, CNNs prove effective for different organ segmentation, automatically extracting features and identifying intricate patterns challenging for human detection. Crucially, this capability enhances accurate and efficient lung segmentation, vital for diagnosing, treating, and monitoring deadly lung diseases. To address challenges such as model overfitting, reducing parameters, and handling imbalanced data, Novikov et al. (2018) developed a robust “fully convolutional network (FCN)” architecture. The three variants – All-Dropout, incorporating Gaussian dropout layers to prevent overfitting; All-Convolutional, which replaced pooling layers with convolutional layers for denser feature extraction; and “InvertedNet”, (Novikov et al. 2018) utilizing inverted residual blocks and Exponential Linear Units (ELUs) for accelerated training – were thoroughly evaluated. The study used the JSRT dataset, which comprises 247 chest X-rays. The training time for InvertedNet was 33 hours, involving 3,140,771 parameters. The study’s findings revealed that InvertedNet performed better than others, showing good results in terms of the Jaccard score, as proven by the study’s results.

Furthermore, Adel Sulaiman and colleagues introduced a lung segmentation model using deep CNNs. The model incorporates convolutional and concatenate blocks, (Sulaiman et al. 2023) with convolutional layers capturing essential features and a transpose layer enhancing spatial resolution of feature maps. Performance metrics such as accuracy, Intersection over Union (IoU), and Dice coefficient were used to evaluate the model’s effectiveness, yielding impressive results. Trained with k-fold validation (k = 5), the model achieved 97% accuracy with minimal loss.
