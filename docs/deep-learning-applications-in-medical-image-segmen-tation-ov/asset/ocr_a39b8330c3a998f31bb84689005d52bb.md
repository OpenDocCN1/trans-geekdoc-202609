**Recall:** Also known as sensitivity, it is a measure of the ability of the model to correctly capture and identify the TPs. In case of lung segmentation, it is the probability of correctly classifying the lung pixels from an image. It is a useful metric when false negatives are more critical than false positives. It is calculated using the below formula:

Recall = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}

**Specificity:** In contrast to sensitivity or recall, specificity is the measure of the ability of the model to correctly identify the TNs. In the case of lung segmentation, it is the probability of correctly classifying a background (or a non-lung) pixel. It becomes a useful metric when the cost of false positives is high or when there is a focus on the accuracy of negative predictions. This metric is derived using the following formula:

Specificity = \frac{TN}{TN + FN}

Specificity tells us how accurately a model can make correct negative predictions. A higher value of specificity means the model is effective at correctly identifying TNs, minimizing the occurrence of false positives. A lower value means that the model is incorrectly predicting negative instances as positive, leading to false positives.

**F1-score:** The F1-score is a crucial evaluation metric, offering a balanced assessment of a model's performance by considering both precision and recall. By computing the harmonic mean of precision and recall, the F1-score provides a symmetric representation of both metrics in a single value. With a maximum value of 1 representing perfect precision and recall, and a minimum value of 0 indicating either precision or recall being zero, the F1-score serves as a reliable indicator of a model's accuracy and is calculated as follows:

F1 Score = \frac{2 \times Precsion \times Recall}{Precsion + Recall}

**Hausdorff distance:** Measures the maximum distance between any two points on the predicted and ground truth segmentation boundaries. Lower Hausdorff distance indicates better alignment between the segmentations.
