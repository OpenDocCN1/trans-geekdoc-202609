# 3.4.5 Computational Advancements and Modern Era

The historical trajectory from heuristic methods to algorithmic precision aligned with the computational advancements of the late 20th and early 21st centuries. As computational capabilities expanded, traditional segmentation methodologies underwent refinement, enabling more precise delineation of anatomical structures (Litjens et al. 2017). This period marks the bridge to the modern era of medical image segmentation, where the integration of computational techniques and the advent of deep learning applications have reshaped the landscape.

# 3.5 Segmentation

Segmentation of organs and substructures in medical images is crucial for quantitative analysis of clinical parameters related to volume and shape. This process is foundational for various medical analyses, including cardiac or brain analysis, and serves as a vital initial step in computer-aided detection pipelines. The segmentation task involves identifying voxels constituting the contour or interior of the object of interest. Within the realm of deep learning applications to medical imaging, segmentation emerges as a predominant theme, showcasing diverse methodologies, including unique CNN architectures and the broader application of RNNs.

One notable CNN architecture in medical image analysis is U-net, introduced by Ronneberger et al. in 2015. U-net incorporates equal amounts of upsampling and downsampling layers, along with skip connections between opposing convolution and deconvolution layers. These skip connections concatenate features from the contracting and expanding paths, allowing U-net to process entire images or scans in a single forward pass. This unique architecture considers the full context of the image, offering advantages over patch-based CNNs (Ronneberger et al. 2015).

Several derivative architectures based on U-net have been proposed. Milletari et al. (2016) introduced V-net, a 3D variant of U-net, employing 3D convolutional layers and an objective function based on the Dice coefficient for 3D image segmentation. Drozdzal et al. (2016) explored short ResNet-like skip connections in addition to the long skip connections in a regular U-net, enhancing the network’s capabilities.
