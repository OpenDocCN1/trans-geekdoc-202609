Internal shape fitting and auto-correction have improved these models by guiding delineation with shape information (Kim et al. 2017). Additionally, certain hybrid techniques that use statistical shape models restricted by probabilistic atlases have been studied. Bayesian methods utilizing expectation–maximization (Van Leemput et al. 1999), possibility clustering (Barra and Boire 2002), histogram-based thresholding, followed by region growth (Purushwalkam et al. 2013), active contours (Zhou et al. 2016), and, more recently, machine learning (ML) (Conze et al. 2017) techniques have all been used to segment images. High levels of intersubject shape variability, weak borders, and noticeable variations in tissue appearance make the aforementioned techniques less than ideal. Typically, their resilience falls short when confronted with the intrinsic challenges of medical imaging, including noise, nonuniform contrasts, and motion aberrations. In addition, there are significant computational costs involved with these methods because many of them are semi-automatic and, as a result, require prior information. Because of this, DL methods have been developed to take advantage of image features (such as variations in contrast, orientation, form, and texture patterns) more effectively using data-driven means.

Recently, Artificial Intelligence (AI) and DL models have excelled in medical image segmentation, becoming the current state-of-the-art. There is now a shift occurring between systems that rely on manually created features (ML) and systems that derive features directly from data (DL). Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are the foundation of the most popular models in use today. The U-Net convolutional architecture (Ronneberger et al. 2015) in particular has been widely accepted due to its capacity to output comprehensive delineations with a relatively small quantity of training data. However, new attention-based paradigms have emerged alongside the growing interest in models based on pure and hybrid Transformers (Shamshad et al. 2023).

Since its inception in 2014, generative adversarial networks (GANs), a DL model, has garnered substantial attention in both academic and industrial circles, experiencing rapid growth in popularity. Currently, numerous high-caliber papers have been published and discussed extensively, while various AI-focused companies dedicate considerable efforts to advancing and advocating GAN models. Our analysis reveals that GAN's utilization in medical image segmentation emerged around 2016. In recent years, owing to GAN's distinct advantages like reduced training data requirements, impressive generation capabilities, and seamless integration with other neural networks, GAN-based segmentation methods have seen a notable rise in their application within medical image research. The utilization of GANs has demonstrated significant enhancements in the outcomes of medical image segmentation, showcasing their efficacy and impact in this specific domain.
