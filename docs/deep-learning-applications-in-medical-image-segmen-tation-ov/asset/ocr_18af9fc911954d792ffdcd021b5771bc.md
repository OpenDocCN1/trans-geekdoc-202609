The neural network architecture known as encoder–decoder, widely used for image segmentation, consists of two essential parts: the encoder and the decoder. The encoder, often referred to as the contracting path, utilizes convolutional layers to downsample the input image, extracting crucial features and reducing spatial dimensions. Conversely, the decoder, known as the expansion path, employs upsampling layers to enhance spatial resolution and generate a segmentation mask. U-Net, a specialized encoder–decoder network initially created for biomedical image segmentation by “Olaf Ronneberger,” (Ronneberger et al. 2015) incorporates skip connections and additional convolutional layers to refine segmentation accuracy.
