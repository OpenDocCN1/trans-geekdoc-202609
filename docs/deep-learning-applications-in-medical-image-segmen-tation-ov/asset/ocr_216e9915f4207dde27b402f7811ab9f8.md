# 8.5 Conclusion

A review of the GAN-based image segmentation techniques is presented in this paper. We briefly present the principles of GAN and several extensions. Regarding the generative model G, its parameter updates differ from optimizing data likelihood; instead, they involve back propagating gradients from the discriminative model D. Additionally, GAN stands apart from traditional probabilistic generation models by circumventing the Markov chain learning mechanism. Moreover, GANs directly perform sampling and inference, enhancing their efficiency in application and broadening their practical use cases.

We also provide a summary of GAN's application, specifically in medical image segmentation. As a versatile design framework, GAN-based DL models have significantly contributed to enhancing the precision of medical image segmentation. The summary in this section highlights the primary improvements made in these models along two main avenues. First, there is a focus on augmenting or refining the loss function. Given GAN's adaptability, a variety of loss functions can be integrated into the GAN model. Consequently, by tailoring diverse loss functions for specific tasks, the model's performance can be notably enhanced.
