# ## 9.2 Methodology

The framework of the proposed approach comprises three distinct stages: pre-processing, data enhancement, and post-processing. During the pre-processing phase, resampling and data generation procedures are carried out. As a result, the image enhancement operation boosts the identification rate of subcomponents, while post-processing conducts segmentation of the enhanced cell images. Pre-processing operations were conducted on three biomedical databases: Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia International Database (ALL-IDB) (ALL-IDB 2024), JTSC (Zheng et al. 2018), and CellaVision (Zheng et al. 2018). The image samples from these datasets encompass leucocytes of five different types (lymphocytes, monocytes, neutrophils, basophils, and eosinophils), featuring varying dimensions that were standardized through resampling to a common size. Subsequently, once the resampling process is complete, two distinct appearances (Magenta and Saturation) of biological images are generated. These color channels contain high luminance information crucial for discriminating cells from background textures (Makem and Tiede u 2020).

The outcomes of pre-processing are directed toward the multilevel decomposition and fusion phase, where a significant enhancement in the quality of biological data occurs. We have constrained the data improvement process to four distinct levels due to the risk of image quality deterioration with further enhancements. This operation involves data decomposition and integration strategies. The medical observation decomposition is executed using latent low-rank due to its resilience to noise and its ability to minimize insufficiently sampled features (Liu and Yan 2011).
