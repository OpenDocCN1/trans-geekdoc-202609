### 7.4.1.6 Multistage and Ensemble Approaches

The use of multistage and ensemble learning frameworks has significantly improved segmentation outcomes in various studies. Ciano et al. proposed a multiorgan segmentation approach (Ciano et al. 2021) employing a GAN with a multistage algorithm for X-ray images. Using PGGANs, Pix2PixHD, and SMANet, the study highlights the importance of accurate segmentation in CAD, particularly in scenarios with limited training data, showcasing advanced segmentation techniques. Similarly, researchers introduced a sophisticated lung segmentation approach (Souza et al. 2019) presenting a cascaded two-stage model. The method employs a reconstruction technique for segmenting lungs, initially using AlexNet on lung patches. In cases with opacities or consolidations, a secondary CNN, ResNet-18, is trained for lung reconstruction. The results from the initial segmentation and subsequent reconstruction stages are combined, resulting in enhanced accuracy in the final segmentation results. Expanding the study of ensemble frameworks for lung segmentation, Rahman et al. conducted studies (Rahman et al. 2021; Rahman et al. 2022) that introduced ensemble deep learning frameworks for lung segmentation using a two-stage approach. While later method employed a conventional CNN for patch classification in the first stage. Both studies incorporated post-processing techniques and a modified U-Net architecture to enhance final segmentation accuracy, providing an alternative for the cases where we have small datasets available for training CNNs.

## 7.4.2 Overview of Deep Learning Approaches for Lung Segmentation in CT Scans

Deep learning techniques have recently emerged as powerful tools for LFS in medical images, particularly in the context of CT scans. This review focuses on the advancements and applications of deep learning-based methods for LFS, emphasizing their significance in medical diagnosis.

Early attempts at lung segmentation relied on conventional techniques such as numerical methods (Abdani et al. 2022), gray-level thresholding (Zulkifley et al. 2022), and shape-based techniques. However, these methods faced limitations in handling the complexity of medical images. The emergence of deep learning, with CNNs leading the charge, has brought about a transformative impact on the domain of medical image segmentation.

Recent studies have suggested several CNN-based approaches for segmenting lung fields (Stofa et al. 2021), leveraging the capabilities of 2D CNNs, 2.5D CNNs, and 3D CNNs. These techniques have shown significant improvements over traditional methods, enabling more accurate and efficient segmentation.

The study presented by Xu et al. proposed a straightforward CNN model for lung segmentation (Xu et al. 2019) using a training dataset generated through a "clustering algorithm-based" approach. The k-means clustering algorithm utilized the mean and minimum intensities of image patches, effectively dividing CT slices into two groups. To ensure accuracy and completeness, the final dataset was generated through a multi-step process involving cross-shaped verification for shape consistency, volume intersection for overlap removal, connected component analysis for region identification, and patch expansion for data augmentation. The network architecture consisted of a single convolutional layer with six kernels for extracting features, a maximum pooling layer for downsampling, and two fully connected layers used for final classification. The model's performance was rigorously evaluated using an eightfold cross-validation approach on the generated datasets.

A complementary approach introduced by Lyu et al. developed algorithms for the automatic segmentation of lung fields aimed at filtering noise from CT images of lungs while preserving lung boundaries. This was achieved by employing a filtering approach based on image decomposition, followed by the application of wavelet transformation and diverse morphological operations to delineate the lung boundaries accurately. The resulting lung outlines underwent correction and smoothing as part of a segmentation refinement process (Liu and Pang 2020), incorporating a method for adjusting contours. This comprehensive set of methodologies illustrates the diverse strategies employed in the domain of lung segmentation, showcasing the efficacy of clustering algorithms, CNN architectures, and image-processing techniques (Liu and Pang 2020; Xu et al. 2019).

An effective approach involves employing a "U-Net architecture with a false-positive" (Khanna et al. 2020) strategy illustrating the efficacy of a more advanced network for lung segmentation. In another study (Comelli et al. 2020), the performance of "U-Net and E-Net models" was compared, highlighting their effectiveness in segmenting parenchyma among patients with pulmonary fibrosis.

A U-Net architecture featuring separate paths for recovering global context and extracting fine details (Ait Skourt et al. 2018) has shown high performance with a Dice coefficient of 0.9502. Additionally (Hu et al. 2020), an integrated approach combining supervised and unsupervised machine learning techniques within a Mask R-CNN methodology demonstrated quick and accurate segmentation with 97.68% accuracy and an average processing time of 11.2 seconds.

Multi-view convolutional networks have been employed to identify lung nodules (Setio et al. 2016), targeting different nodule types. Combining multiple streams of 2D ConvNets with a robust classification approach yielded promising results, with an average of 4 False-Positives/Scan and a "detection sensitivity" of 85.4% achieved.

Moving beyond 2D approaches, some researchers focused on training "3D CNNs" for the autonomous detection of lung nodules. These networks were trained on volumes of interest (VOIs) extracted from datasets such as LIDC, which is a valuable resource for detecting "lung nodules." Researchers also analyzed the performance of 3D FCNN to generate score maps for the whole volume in a single iteration, thereby showcasing the effectiveness of 3D CNNs (Negahdar et al. 2018), showcasing the efficiency of 3D CNNs. With ongoing advancements, Roy et al. proposed an innovative model for the segmentation of lung nodules (Roy et al. 2019), incorporating a complementary fusion of "deep learning and shape-driven level sets." The method utilized "coarse segmentation maps" generated by deep FCNs, facilitating the segmentation process with an initial, broad delineation to refine accurate boundary detection. Initially, shape-driven evolution of level sets was employed for fine segmentation. Moreover, these level sets played a vital role in automatically initializing the model (Thanoon et al. 2023), utilizing seed points derived from the coarse segmentation of the deep network. Segmenting lung nodules is a complex task due to variations in their shapes, contours, textures, and locations. Some nodules may be connected to surrounding tissues, further complicating the determination of their contours. The main objective of lung nodule segmentation is to precisely identify the nodule's contour and separate it from the lung region. Despite various approaches employed for lung nodule segmentation, challenges persist, particularly in accurately segmenting different types of lung nodules. Zhang et al. (Zhang et al. 2021) proposed a robust lung nodule segmentation method using an enhanced U-Net convolutional network. This approach demonstrates precise segmentation for various types of lung nodules. The methodology involves lung parenchyma segmentation with contour correction using the α-hull algorithm. CT slices containing lung nodules undergo preprocessing steps, and an "improved U-Net with Batch Normalization" is employed for accurate segmentation. The training utilizes patches with corresponding ground truth from the "Lung Image Database Consortium and Image Database Resource Initiative (LIDC/IDRI)." The model surpasses state-of-the-art algorithms and achieves a Dice score of 0.8623. Meanwhile, the authors developed the "Hierarchical Segmentation Network (CHS-Net)" (Punn and Agarwal 2022) to identify lung regions affected by COVID-19-infected CT scans. The model was evaluated on combined datasets that are publicly available. CHS-Net addresses the scarcity of COVID-19 data. The model, inspired by advanced architectures such as U-Net, Google's Inception, and residual networks, employs a multistage approach for the segmentation and extraction of features. CHS-Net achieves a 95% accuracy in segmenting the lung mask and the infected region. Recently, the authors introduced a novel approach named "Wavelet U-Net++" (Akila Agnes et al. 2024), merging the "U-Net++ architecture with wavelet pooling." This approach uses Haar wavelet transform to downsample the feature maps in the encoder, capturing both high- and low-frequency details to improve segmentation accuracy, especially for small and irregular nodules. Evaluation conducted on the LIDC-IDRI dataset, consisting of 1018 CT images, yielded promising findings, with a mean Dice coefficient of 0.936 and a mean IoU of 0.878. Furthermore, these advancements underscore the ongoing challenges in lung segmentation, necessitating innovative solutions for improved diagnostic accuracy (Table 7.1).
