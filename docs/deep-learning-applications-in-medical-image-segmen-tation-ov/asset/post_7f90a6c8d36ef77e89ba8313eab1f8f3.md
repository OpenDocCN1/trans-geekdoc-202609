### 10.1 Introduction

Healthcare professionals, including clinicians and radiologists, predominantly handle the interpretation of medical images. However, the constrained availability of human specialists, combined with the exhaustion and inexact methods they use for estimation, limits the efficiency of image interpretation among proficient healthcare practitioners. Challenges arise from variations among experts and the complexities inherent in medical images, making precise disease diagnosis a significant obstacle for these professionals.

Automated computer techniques have simplified the challenging process of image analysis, enabling both semi- and fully automatic procedures. These methods aid experts in achieving objective, rapid, and precise diagnoses. As a result, the ongoing exploration and advancement of deep learning-based approaches for analyzing medical images continue to be a compelling and dynamic area of research.

Integrating deep learning (DL) techniques into medical image analysis is captivating, yet progress faces various challenges. A key limitation hindering DL in medical images is the inherent variation within the data, encompassing factors, such as contrast, resolution, and signal-to-noise ratio, often arising from clinical practice procedures. Nonstandardized acquisition of medical data further complicates the analysis, and the demand for detailed medical image labeling constrains the utilization of DL in this domain.

A significant challenge arises from the scarcity of data, particularly in contrast to other datasets, and the intricate nature of sharing medical datasets. Handling the privacy of medical data is both a technological and sociological concern that requires examination from both perspectives. Constructing Deep Learning Algorithms (DLAs) necessitate a substantial amount of annotated data, and the annotation process is time consuming due to the requisite domain knowledge of radiologists.

To tackle the challenge of limited labeled data, the implementation of semi-supervised learning can leverage existing labeled data alongside extensive unlabeled data. Additionally, developing few-shot learning methods with a significantly reduced dataset presents a potential remedy for the challenge of limited data availability.

In spite of the accomplishments of the DLA, numerous restrictions and hurdles persist in the medical field. DL offers the possibility to decrease medical expenses, enhance efficiency in healthcare, and boost patient satisfaction in healthcare remains uncertain. Nevertheless, conducting clinical trials that show the effectiveness of DL algorithms and establishing guidelines for their application in the analysis of medical images is imperative.

The expanse of medical datasets is extensive, encapsulating a multitude of imaging modalities and analyzing anatomical structures and pathological conditions. ### 10.2.2 Computerized Tomography

Computed tomography (CT) images are diagnostic medical images generated through a technology called CT scanning, commonly known as CT scanning. CT scans constitute a form of medical imaging that employs X-rays to create comprehensive cross-sectional body images. This technique facilitates the creation of a three-dimensional perspective of internal structures, enabling a more comprehensive assessment compared to traditional X-ray imaging. The various features of CT images are high spatial resolution, contrast enhancement, multiplanar imaging, and dense and soft tissue differentiation. These images are highly used for diagnosis and staging, treatment planning, vascular imaging, etc.

In 2016, researchers pioneered the introduction of a deep CNN designed to detect three nodule types: solid, semisolid, and ground-glass opacity (Li et al. 2016). Balagourouchetty et al. (2019) proposed a liver lesion classification ensemble FCNet classifier based on GoogLeNet. The fundamental GoogLeNet architecture underwent three modifications to enhance feature extraction. Masood et al. (2020) introduced the multidimensional region-based fully convolutional network for detecting and classifying lung nodules, achieving an impressive accuracy of 97.91%. Their emphasis was particularly on the detection of micronodules (less than 3 mm) in lung nodule identification without compromising performance metrics. In 2019, Zhao and Zeng introduced a Deep Learning Architecture (DLA) based on supervised MSS U-Net and 3DU-Net for the automatic segmentation of kidneys and associated tumors (Zhao and Zeng 2019). Fan et al. (2020) and Li et al. (2019) applied DL methods to identify COVID-19 from CT images during COVID-19.

### 10.2.3 Mammography (MG)

MG, a specialized medical imaging technique, plays an important role in screening and diagnosing breast conditions, with a primary focus on breast cancer detection. This procedure utilizes X-rays to generate detailed images of the breast tissue. Mammograms are indispensable in the early prognosis of breast cancer and are recommended as part of routine screening protocols for women, usually commencing at a specific age or based on individual risk factors. The automated classification and early-stage detection of masses in mammography continue to be the areas of active research. Over the last decade, DLAs have made significant strides in overcoming challenges associated with breast cancer detection and classification.

Fonseca et al. (2015) introduced a method for detecting breast composition using a CNN to extract features following the ACR standard. Some researchers suggested a CNN model with 12 layers for detecting breast arterial calcifications and assessing coronary artery disease (Wang et al. 2017). In another research, a computer-aided design (CAD) system employing faster R-CNN, which autonomously classifies lesions into benign and malignant in mammogram images, illustrated in Figure 10.3 (Ribli et al. 2018).

A deep CNN trained on more than 1,000,000 mammogram data for the classification of breast cancer (Wu et al. 2019). Another deep CNN-based system has been developed for detecting AQ lesions, including calcified and soft tissue abnormalities, in digital breast tomosynthesis images (Conat et al. 2019). The proposed architecture of fuzzy completely connected layer architecture integrates fused fuzzy rules along with conventional CNN for semantic BI-RADS scoring (Kang et al. 2020). This framework demonstrated superior performance in BI-RADS scoring for both triple and multi-class classifications.
