### 7.5.1.6 COVID-19 Radiography Database

The “COVID-19 Chest X-ray Database” is a vast repository of medical images assembled by scholars from the “The University of Dhaka, Qatar University, Doha, and Bangladesh,” in collaboration with partners from “Pakistan and Malaysia,” along with medical professionals. This dataset has been progressively released in stages, with subsequent updates increasing the volume of images across different categories. All the CXR images are in PNG format and have a resolution of 299×299 pixels. The dataset is continuously evolving, with updates provided in stages. As of the latest information, it includes “3616 COVID-19 positive cases,” “10,192 normal images,” “6012 lung opacity images,” and “1345 viral pneumonia images.” The commitment is to regularly update the database with new radiographs for COVID-19 pneumonia patients. This database serves as a great resource for scientists to generate significant scholarly contributions toward addressing the COVID-19 pandemic. (Kaggle (2022)).

PadChest: The PadChest dataset comprises 160,868 chest X-rays derived from 109,931 studies and involves 67,000 at “San Juan Hospital” (Bustos et al. 2020) in Spain over a period from 2009 to 2017. The images are stored as 16-bit grayscale images, preserving detailed information. Among the reports associated with the X-rays, 27,593 were manually annotated by physicians. An innovative approach involved training a RNN on these labeled reports. The trained RNN was then employed to automatically label the remaining dataset based on the information extracted from the reports. The labels adhere to a hierarchical taxonomy that aligns with the standards of the Unified Medical Language System (UMLS). (Bodenreider 2004).

RSNA-Pneumonia: The RSNA-Pneumonia (RP) dataset, derived from ChestX-ray14, includes 30,000 chest X-rays (CXR) images with meticulous annotations for pneumonia. The dataset consists of 16,248 posteroanterior views, 13,752 AP views, and 4,527 test images. These digital radiographs were converted from PNG to DICOM format, containing patient details. The resolution of each radiograph is 1024×1024, and they are presented in 8-bit grayscale format and labeled with bounding boxes for lung opacities. The categories are normal, lung opacity, and not normal. Widely used in the “RSNA Pneumonia Detection Challenge (2018)” on Kaggle, this dataset is crucial for training and assessing machine learning models for automated pneumonia detection in Chest X-rays. (Kaggle n.d.).

### 7.5.2 CT Scan Datasets

With the advancement of medical imaging technology, CT scan datasets have emerged as vital tools for examining lung diseases, offering researchers access to a rich repository of medical images and associated clinical data. Some of the commonly used CT datasets for lung diseases are the following.

### LIDC-IDRI

Armato et al. introduced the LIDC-IDRI dataset in “The Lung Image Database Consortium (LIDC) and Image Database Resource Initiative (IDRI): a completed reference database of lung nodules on CT scans.” This dataset is a collaborative effort by the Lung Tissue Research Consortium, consisting of 1,018 low-dose lung CT scans from 1,200 patients. Enhanced with lesion annotations meticulously provided by experienced thoracic radiologists, the dataset includes associated characteristics such as calcification and malignancy. This resource plays a crucial role in advancing research in lung tissue analysis and serves as a valuable reference for studying various lung conditions. The CT images in this dataset are in the DICOM format, with a total size of 125 GB and a resolution of [65,764]×512×512 voxels for each scan, along with its associated metadata file. An average scan has 240 slices, with an average slice thickness of 1.73 mm. (Armato et al. 2010).

### LUNA16

The LUNA16 dataset, specialized in lung nodule detection, contains 888 sets of 3D CT images. Focused on pulmonary nodule segmentation, LUNA16 provides a rich resource for the creation and assessment of algorithms targeting the detection and characterization of pulmonary nodules.

### LOLA11

LOLA11, an acronym for Lobe and Lung Analysis Challenge, (Lola11 n.d.) is a dataset specifically designed for challenges related to the analysis of lung lobes. Comprising 55 volumetric chest CT scans from various sources, this dataset offers a diverse range of scans that include significant pathologies and abnormalities.

### SPIE-AAPM

Initiated by the “SPIE Medical Imaging Conference in collaboration with the National Cancer Institute (NCI) and the American Association of Physicists in Medicine (AAPM),” enables researchers to evaluate and contrast their methods in employing quantitative image analysis to categorize nodules as either benign or malignant. This dataset was last updated in 2014 and includes 22,489 CT scans totaling 12.1 GB in size, together with 70 patient records and supporting data that provide ground-truth information in addition to the image. Scientists and healthcare professionals use these datasets to develop and assess deep learning algorithms, machine learning models, and various computational methods aimed at comprehending, diagnosing, and prognosticating various lung diseases. (National Lung Screening Trial (NLST) n.d.)

### Lung CT-Diagnosis Dataset

This dataset was updated in 2014 and is accessible to the public through TCIA. Consisting of 61 patients' data, the dataset is kept up to date by Moffitt Cancer Center. The dataset comprises 4682 images, with a cumulative size of 2.5 GB. These images represent the CT medical imaging modality saved in DICOM format, with the “slice width ranging from 3–6 mm.” Each file contains metadata containing patient details and the instance number of tumor slices. (Cancer imaging archive n.d.)

### NLST dataset

The NLST dataset, updated in 2013, is a restricted-access repository, a crucial clinical study that evaluates the effectiveness of low radiation CT screening in decreasing mortality rates associated with lung cancer. In addition to pathological images, the dataset encompasses radiological images paired with pertinent clinical data. The collaborative efforts of the “National Cancer Institute’s Division of Cancer Prevention and Division of Cancer Treatment and Diagnosis” underpin this project. This dataset comprises nearly 21,082,502 CT scans amounting to 11.3 TB, involving 26,254 individuals (NLST 2013). (National Cancer Institute - Cancer Data Access System n.d.).

## 7.6 Evaluation Metrics

Evaluation metrics are the quantitative measures used to measure the performance of a model or an algorithm. These metrics help in determining how well a model performs on a specific task. Their significance lies in facilitating comparisons between various algorithms and plays a vital role in ensuring the precision of a model in a practical context. The metrics assess the performance of the model by comparing the predicted results of the model with those of the ground truth. Ground truth means the actual lung segmentation results obtained manually by medical experts. Here's a summary of the commonly used metrics for lung segmentation in medical images.

### True Positive (TP)

When the model predicts something as positive which is positive. In the case of lung segmentation, the model correctly identifies the image pixels as lung pixels when they are lung pixels.

### True Negative (TN)

When the model predicts something as negative which is negative. In the case of lung segmentation when the system correctly classifies image pixels as non-lung pixels that do not belong to the lung region.
