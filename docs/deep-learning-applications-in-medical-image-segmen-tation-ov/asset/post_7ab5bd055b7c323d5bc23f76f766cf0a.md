## 5.5 Deep Learning Models for Retinal Vessel Segmentation

Retinal vessel segmentation, integral in medical image analysis within ophthalmology, involves identifying blood vessels in retinal images for diagnosing conditions such as diabetic retinopathy. Accurate segmentation aids in disease detection and progression tracking, and facilitates the development of computer-aided diagnosis systems, playing a pivotal role in managing ocular pathologies.

Jiang et al. (2018) presented a supervised method utilizing pretrained fully convolutional networks and transfer learning for accurate retinal blood vessel segmentation, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy across multiple databases. Additional unsupervised post-processing techniques are applied to refine segmentation results, showcasing the effectiveness of transfer learning in medical imaging applications. Zhao et al. (2020) introduced a retinal vessel segmentation technique employing generative adversarial learning with an extensive receptive field. This approach attained remarkable segmentation accuracy rates, surpassing current leading methods. By effectively capturing both intricate small blood vessels and broad, high-level semantic vessel features, the proposed algorithm showcases promise in aiding the diagnosis of ophthalmological conditions. Guo et al. (2020) introduced a Dense U-Net architecture using inception modules for retinal vessel segmentation, incorporating generative adversarial networks (GANs) in training for improved performance. Evaluation of the DRIVE dataset demonstrates high segmentation accuracy with a Dice rate of 82.15% and AU-ROC and AU-PR values of 0.9772 and 0.9058, respectively, indicating effectiveness in automated retinal vessel segmentation. Atlı and Gedik (2021) proposed Sine-Net, a novel deep learning architecture for retinal blood vessel segmentation, achieving high accuracy and robustness. Experimental validation on three publicly available databases (STARE, CHASE_DB1, and DRIVE) shows Sine-Net outperforms existing methods in certain metrics, demonstrating its potential for clinical applications. Chen et al. (2021a) proposed a comprehensive review of deep learning-based retinal blood vessel segmentation, emphasizing its importance in diagnosing diseases such as diabetes and hypertension to prevent blindness. It surveyed recent publications, identified trends in network architectures, outlined challenges, and suggested future research directions to advance and enhance models in this domain. Begundez-Arias et al. (2021) introduced a deep learning-based method for retinal blood vessel segmentation using a simplified U-Net architecture with novel loss function and batch normalization, achieving robust and accurate results across diverse fundus image datasets. Evaluation against manual segmentations in DRIVE, STARE, and CHASE_Db1 databases demonstrates superior performance compared to existing methods, suggesting its potential for integration into clinical tools for vascular structure segmentation. Liu et al. (2022) introduced an improved U-Net algorithm for retinal vessel segmentation, employing global convolutional networks, attention modules, and multi-scale feature extraction to accurately delineate vessel boundaries. Evaluation of DRIVE and CHASE_DB1 datasets demonstrates superior accuracy, outperforming existing algorithms with an average performance enhancement. Abbi et al. (2023) have created LMBiS-Net, a lightweight pixel-level CNN with a very small number of learnable parameters (only 0.172 M), for the purpose of segmenting retinal capillaries. In order to facilitate information flow between the encoder and the decoder, the network includes bidirectional skip links in addition to multipath feature extraction blocks. Ilesanmi et al. (2023) presented a systematic review of CNN algorithms for retinal fundus image segmentation and classification, highlighting their potential to aid early detection and treatment of eye problems. Analysis of 62 studies reveals CNNs’ effectiveness in enhancing segmentation precision and reducing reliance on human expertise, with consistently high accuracies observed despite some limitations.

## 5.6 Deep Learning Models for Inner Ear Segmentation

Inner ear segmentation involves recognizing and outlining the anatomical elements within the inner ear from medical imaging data, such as MRI or CT scans. The inner ear, a multifaceted structure responsible for both hearing and balance, comprises interconnected components such as the cochlea, vestibule, and semi-circular canals.

Segmentation is essential for understanding the morphology and spatial relationships of these structures, aiding in the diagnosis and treatment of inner ear disorders and pathologies. Accurate segmentation allows clinicians to quantify and analyze the volume, shape, and abnormalities of inner ear structures, which can inform treatment decisions, surgical planning, and disease monitoring.

Expert manual segmentation is both time-intensive and subjective, resulting in inconsistencies between different observers. Therefore, automated segmentation methods, particularly those based on deep learning algorithms, have gained traction for their ability to efficiently and accurately delineate inner ear structures from medical imaging data. These automated methods streamline the segmentation process, reduce human error, and enable consistent and reproducible results across different patients and imaging modalities.

Wang et al. (2019) proposed the challenge of metal artifact reduction in post-implantation CT images for accurate segmentation of intra-cochlear anatomy (ICA) crucial for cochlear implant programming. They introduced a 3D conditional GAN-based approach, demonstrating significant improvement over prior 2D methods by synthesizing preoperative images, enhancing segmentation accuracy without requiring pre-implantation CT scans. Jeevakala et al. (2020) proposed a Mask R-CNN approach driven with U-Net for the detection and segmentation of inner auditory canal and its nerves. On experimenting with the clinical dataset of over 50 different patients of different age groups, they found the model outperforms the state-of-the-art models. Zeng et al. (2021) proposed an ensemble deep learning method for the effective and precise identification of a range of ear diseases utilizing otoscope images, attaining an impressive average accuracy of 95.59% across eight distinct disease categories. The real-time classifier showcased its potential in clinical settings, offering promise for early detection and treatment of ear diseases, particularly in the absence of expert diagnosis. A 3-D U-Net was trained by Vaidyanathan et al. (2021) utilizing 944 MRI scans that had the inner ears manually segmented as reference standards. The model was found to perform equivalent to human experts. The result of the research work also suggests the model was efficient, consistent, and reliable for inner ear segmentation.
