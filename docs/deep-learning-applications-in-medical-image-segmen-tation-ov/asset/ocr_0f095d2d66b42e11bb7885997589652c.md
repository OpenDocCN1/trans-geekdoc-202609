# 5.6 Deep Learning Models for Inner Ear Segmentation

Inner ear segmentation involves recognizing and outlining the anatomical elements within the inner ear from medical imaging data, such as MRI or CT scans. The inner ear, a multifaceted structure responsible for both hearing and balance, comprises interconnected components such as the cochlea, vestibule, and semi-circular canals.

Segmentation is essential for understanding the morphology and spatial relationships of these structures, aiding in the diagnosis and treatment of inner ear disorders and pathologies. Accurate segmentation allows clinicians to quantify and analyze the volume, shape, and abnormalities of inner ear structures, which can inform treatment decisions, surgical planning, and disease monitoring.

Expert manual segmentation is both time-intensive and subjective, resulting in inconsistencies between different observers. Therefore, automated segmentation methods, particularly those based on deep learning algorithms, have gained traction for their ability to efficiently and accurately delineate inner ear structures from medical imaging data. These automated methods streamline the segmentation process, reduce human error, and enable consistent and reproducible results across different patients and imaging modalities.

Wang et al. (2019) proposed the challenge of metal artifact reduction in post-implantation CT images for accurate segmentation of intra-cochlear anatomy (ICA) crucial for cochlear implant programming. They introduced a 3D conditional GAN-based approach, demonstrating significant improvement over prior 2D methods by synthesizing preoperative images, enhancing segmentation accuracy without requiring pre-implantation CT scans. Jeevakala et al.(2020) proposed a Mask R-CNN approach driven with U-Net for the detection and segmentation of inner auditory canal and its nerves. On experimenting with the clinical dataset of over 50 different patients of different age groups, they found the model outperforms the state-of-the-art models. Zeng et al. (2021) proposed an ensemble deep learning method for the effective and precise identification of a range of ear diseases utilizing otoscope images, attaining an impressive average accuracy of 95.59% across eight distinct disease categories. The real-time classifier showcased its potential in clinical settings, offering promise for early detection and treatment of ear diseases, particularly in the absence of expert diagnosis. A 3-D U-Net was trained by Vaidyanathan et al. (2021) utilizing 944 MRI scans that had the inner ears manually segmented as reference standards. The model was found to perform equivalent to human experts. The result of the research work also suggests the model was efficient, consistent, and reliable for inner ear segmentation. Atebani proposed a model where a single, dual-headed volumetric 3D U-Net was implemented in a completely automated workflow, trained using manually labeled internal datasets from clinical practice (N = 43)
