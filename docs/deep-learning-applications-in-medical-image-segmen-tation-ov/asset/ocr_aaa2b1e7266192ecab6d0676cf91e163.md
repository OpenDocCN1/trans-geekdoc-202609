# 6.4.1 Dataset Quality and Availability

The scarcity of extensively annotated datasets for training poses a significant obstacle to the successful use of DL-based image segmentation methods. DL models are highly dependent on large quantities of high-quality labeled data, which are difficult to come by in the medical field, particularly in the case of uncommon retinal conditions (Gunduz et al. 2023). The lack of annotated datasets makes it more difficult to train reliable and accurate models as they could not fully reflect the complexity and diversity of real-world situations. Standardized datasets that may be used as benchmarks for comparing and assessing various segmentation techniques are required to overcome this difficulty. These datasets need to cover a broad spectrum of retinal conditions and include thorough annotations for reliable ground truth. The creation of these datasets will promote the development of DL methods for picture segmentation and allow for equitable comparisons between various strategies.

# 6.4.2 Explainability and Interpretability of the Model

CNNs in particular are DL models that are sometimes referred to as “black boxes” because of their sophisticated learning processes and complex topologies. In medical situations, where physicians must comprehend the logic behind the model’s predictions in order to make confident decisions, this lack of interpretability presents difficulties. DL models must be transparent and easily interpreted for clinical practice to embrace and use them. The development of methods that may shed light on these models’ decision-making process is underway. To improve interpretability and foster clinician trust, techniques such as saliency maps and attention mechanisms emphasize the areas of the picture that provide the most contributions to the model’s predictions.
