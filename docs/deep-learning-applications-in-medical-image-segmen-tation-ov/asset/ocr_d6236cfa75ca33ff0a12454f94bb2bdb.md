Gaussian noise alters intensity values by randomly sampling from a Gaussian distribution. Salt-and-pepper noise randomly assigns pixel values to either white or black. Uniform noise is incorporated by adjusting pixel values through random sampling from a uniform distribution.

# Random Cropping and Erasing

Random cropping augmentation entails choosing a small section from the original image and resizing it to match the original image's dimensions. As a result, this augmentation technique can also be termed scaling or zooming. Random erasing augmentation involves randomly eliminating sections of the image.

# Color Modification

Color modification for image augmentation encompasses various techniques. For instance, an RGB-colored image is represented as an array, with three color channels denoting red, green, and blue intensity levels. This setup allows for altering the image’s color while retaining its spatial characteristics. This process, known as color space shifting, can be executed across multiple color spaces, as the original color channels are combined differently in each space. One approach to augmenting an RGB-colored image through color modification involves transforming the pixel values within the image’s color channels using filters applied to a histogram, thereby creating new images with altered color space attributes. Another method involves applying three shifts (integer values) from RGB filters and incorporating each shift into a channel within the original image. Additionally, image augmentation can involve adjusting hue, saturation, and value components, isolating specific color channels (e.g. blue, red, or green), and converting between different color spaces. However, converting a color image to grayscale should be done cautiously, as this conversion may reduce performance by up to 3% (Chatfield et al. 2014).

# Generative Models

Basic data augmentation methods outlined earlier are inadequate when handling intricate data such as medical images. Several studies have been conducted to propose data augmentation techniques more appropriate for medical applications. The overarching objective is to replicate a data distribution that closely resembles real-world data, making it challenging, if not impossible, to differentiate between newly generated samples and authentic data. Recent advancements in deep generative models have rendered them especially appealing for facilitating data augmentation tasks. For instance, generative adversarial networks (GANs) (Goodfellow et al. 2020) have showcased their capacity to produce life-like images, leading to their widespread adoption in medical applications (Sandfort et al. 2019; Mahapatra et al. 2019).
