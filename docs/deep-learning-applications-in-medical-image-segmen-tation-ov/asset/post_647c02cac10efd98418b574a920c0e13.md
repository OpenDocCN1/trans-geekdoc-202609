Deep learning models exhibit impressive performance in anatomical structure segmentation, but a high number of training parameters is a potential issue. To address this, Yahyatabar et al. introduced “Dense-Unet,” (Yahyatabar and Jouvet 2020) an extension of U-Net with dense connectivity, enhancing information flow between layers. The model offers “compact and normal” versions, incorporating squeeze-and-excitation (SE) blocks. The U-Net architecture has been extensively preferred in recent medical studies; however, it exhibited shortcomings, the most prominent being the presence of a single dropout function at the final convolution layer, which leads to overfitting and poor model generalization. To address these concerns, Arvind et al. proposed a refined and relatively lightweight variant of the U-Net model, (Arvind et al. 2023) incorporating multiple dropouts in both convolution and deconvolution layers for improved semantic segmentation of lung images in chest X-rays. Their improvements in the architectural components of U-Net, such as batch normalization and fewer convolutions, resulted in fewer model parameters and lower model complexity while maintaining performance metrics. The study offered improved performance on multiple datasets with a generalization accuracy of 93.87%.

Continuing the discussion on encoder–decoder approaches, the researchers introduced the “DED-CNN (dual encoder–decoder CNN)” (Ullah et al. 2023) for precise segmentation of multiorgan anatomical structures in chest X-ray images. The model utilizes a pretrained VGG19 as an encoder, incorporates a SE block for better representation, and employs “Attention Gating Modules (AGMs)” to improve the segmentation of smaller regions in the second encoder–decoder. Evaluation of benchmark datasets (JSRT, MCCXR, and SCXR) demonstrated DED-CNN's superior performance in anatomical structure segmentation compared to existing CNN architectures.

Chest X-rays are vital for diagnosing lung conditions, including cancer and other chest infections. In response, the authors proposed “CSR-Net” (Kumar et al. 2023), a fully CNN, integrating “Transfer Learning (TL) with ResNet-101 as a feature extractor,” along with “U-Net serving as a Backbone.” TL proves valuable for overcoming data limitations, enhancing generalization, and reducing training time. CSR-Net's performance is evaluated on the JSRT database, with metrics such as Dice score, precision, recall, accuracy, and Jaccard similarity coefficient (JSC) quantitatively measured.

### 7.4.1.3 Dilated Convolution-Based Approaches

Deeplabv3+ stands as a pivotal architecture, (Chen et al. 2018) offering a robust encoder–decoder framework for accurate image analysis. While its predecessor, deeplabv3, excelled at capturing multi-scale contextual information but faced challenges in accurately delineating object boundaries. Deeplabv3+ addressed this limitation by integrating a decoder module to improve segmentation results, especially along object borders and object edges. Building upon this foundation, researchers have explored various advancements to further enhance its performance and handle specific challenges.

Singh et al. developed Deep LF-Net, (Singh et al. 2021) an encoder–decoder network building upon deeplabv3+, designed to robustly segment lungs in Indian patients with diverse abnormalities. The encoder incorporates atrous convolutions and spatial pyramid pooling for capturing multi-scale contextual information, while the decoder refines segmentation, addressing challenges such as opacities and pleural effusions. Post-processing, including area filtering, effectively eliminates false positives. Evaluated on an in-house Indian dataset and public datasets (Shenzhen, JSRT, and MC), Deep LF-Net demonstrated outstanding performance, achieving 99.37% on JSRT, 99.09% on MC, and 98.31% accuracy on Shenzhen, outperforming existing methods. In a recent study by Boudoukhani et al. (Boudoukhani et al. 2023), a unique methodology was adopted to address common challenges in lung segmentation such as shape/scale variations, device artifacts, false positives, and poor generalizability across devices. Attention gates in the encoder filter out noise, focus on relevant lung features, and minimize false positives, while residual convolutions in the decoder preserve crucial details, leading to sharp, accurate lung boundaries across diverse X-ray datasets, boosting the generalizability of this model. Based on EfficientNet-b4 and ResNet-50 architectures, it outperforms prior models, achieving “Dice coefficient of 98.03% and Jaccard index 96.14%” without pre-/post-processing (Figure 7.6).

### 7.4.1.4 Attention-Based Approaches

Numerous studies adopted the attention mechanism in their segmentation framework. Attention-based models have emerged as powerful tools. Unlike the conventional U-Net architecture, these models strategically integrate attention modules within both contracting and expanding paths, resulting in capturing essential features relevant to the lung region and overall segmentation accuracy. Kim et al. presented a self-attention-based deep learning model consisting of “X-attention and Y-attention modules” (Kim and Lee 2021). The X-attention module concentrates on local features, while the Y-attention module incorporates global context from deeper layers to effectively utilize global features. Both attention modules employ channel and spatial attention, enhancing the feature extraction process. The attention maps derived from these modules are multiplied and utilized in subsequent layers through residual learning. The proposed attention modules contribute to accurate pixel-level attention information, resulting in smooth segmentation masks with reduced noise. To overcome issues faced by previous models in properly segmenting lung areas, particularly in the presence of rib cage edges, irregularities, and changes in size and shape, researchers have introduced a novel segmentation model named “Attention RegSeg (ARSeg)” (Ghali and Akhloufi 2022). This method utilizes an attention mechanism to eliminate irrelevant features. The ARSeg architecture employs RegNetY-32GF as a backbone for detecting and extracting lung features. The decoder consists of three Deconv blocks and a 1×1 convolutional layer that generates a lung mask. The primary aim is to overcome these limitations and improve the lung segmentation tasks. In the meantime, authors proposed a novel attention UW-Net that addresses U-Net limitations and directly segments both large and small regions of interest in chest X-rays, (Pal et al. 2022) without pre-/post-processing steps. The model incorporates an intermediate layer with fully connected convolutions and attention gates, specifically designed to improve segmentation accuracy for small lesions. This method shows promising results even when trained on a subset (200 images) of the NIH X-ray dataset, suggesting potential for efficient and accurate lung segmentation. The generalizability of the model across diverse chest X-ray datasets remains uncertain, as the study did not investigate its performance on different datasets.

### 7.4.1.5 GAN-Based Approaches

The “GAN,” introduced by “Ian Goodfellow et al.” in 2014, (Goodfellow et al. 2020) is an artificial intelligence framework that employs a competitive training approach involving dual neural networks: “a generator and a discriminator.” The generator’s goal is to produce synthetic data, such as images, that are virtually identical to real data, while the discriminator distinguishes between the training data and the generated data, to determine the originality of the input. Using both neural networks, researchers extended the applications of GANs to propose an innovative model. Their model, (Munawar et al. 2020) equipped with a generator structured like a U-net, was trained to produce segmented masks, while four discriminators (“D1, D2, D3, and D4”), acting as binary classifiers, were used to differentiate between ground truth masks and generated one. The “Montgomery County, JSRT, and Shenzhen” datasets were used for evaluation, where the model achieved an “IOU score of 0.943 and a Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) of 0.9740.” In a related study, (El Mansouri et al. 2022) authors explored more about GAN-based lung segmentation, utilizing a similar GAN architecture with a U-Net design. Their focus was on chest X-ray segmentation, specifically employing the JSRT dataset consisting of 240 images (40 for validation, 40 for testing, and 160 for training). The proposed model achieved a notable Dice score of 0.9778. Despite this success, the study acknowledged the computational intensity associated with GANs, highlighting the need for future research to explore diverse datasets and assess performance across varying image sizes.
