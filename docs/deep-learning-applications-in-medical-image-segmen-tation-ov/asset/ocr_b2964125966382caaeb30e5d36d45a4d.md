# ## 2.2.4 Multi-scale-Based Models

Multi-scale segmentation designs were made to deal with the fact that there are different scales in the areas of interest. By dividing an image into numerous homogenous pieces at different scales, multi-scale segmentation schemes can present information at multiple scales. These architectures improved high-level segmentation by using a mix of local and global variables to label each pixel. In 2018, researchers at Arizona State University in the United States suggested U-Net++ to address the shortcomings of U-Net, such as its reliance on same-scale feature maps. Dense-Block was employed in the architecture to boost the traditional U-Net's performance, as depicted in Figure 2.7. To bridge the gap among features across modules and boost gradient flow, this model used convolutions and dense connections on the skip-pathway, in contrast to the traditional model (U-Net) (Zhou et al. 2020). Additionally, it included the idea of deep supervision, which carries out architecture reduction. The proposed design is evaluated on multiple modalities by using four distinct medical image datasets: cell nuclei, colon polyps, liver, and lung nodules. The results show that U-Net++ outperforms the basic U-Net and Wide U-Net models on the basis of the Intersection-over-Union (IoU) measure, even when deep supervision was not utilized.
