An effective approach involves employing a “U-Net architecture with a false-positive” (Khanna et al. 2020) strategy illustrating the efficacy of a more advanced network for lung segmentation. In another study, (Comelli et al. 2020) the performance of “U-Net and E-Net models” was compared, highlighting their effectiveness in segmenting parenchyma among patients with pulmonary fibrosis.

A U-Net architecture featuring separate paths for recovering global context and extracting fine details (Ait Skourt et al. 2018) has shown high performance with a Dice coefficient of 0.9502. Additionally, (Hu et al. 2020) an integrated approach combining supervised and unsupervised machine learning techniques within a Mask R-CNN methodology demonstrated quick and accurate segmentation with 97.68% accuracy and an average processing time of 11.2 seconds.

Multi-view convolutional networks have been employed to identify lung nodules, (Setio et al. 2016) targeting different nodule types. Combining multiple streams of 2D ConvNets with a robust classification approach yielded promising results, with an average of 4 False-Positives/Scan and a “detection sensitivity” of 85.4% achieved.

Moving beyond 2D approaches, some researchers focused on training “3D CNNs” for the autonomous detection of lung nodules. These networks were trained on volumes of interest (VOIs) extracted from datasets such as LIDC, which is a valuable resource for detecting “lung nodules.” Researchers also analyzed the performance of 3D FCNN to generate score maps for the whole volume in a single iteration, thereby showcasing the effectiveness of 3D CNNs, (Negahdar et al. 2018) showcasing the efficiency of 3D CNNs. With ongoing advancements, Roy et al. proposed an innovative model for the segmentation of lung nodules, (Roy et al. 2019) incorporating a complementary fusion of “deep learning and shape-driven level sets.” The method utilized “coarse segmentation maps” generated by deep FCNs, facilitating the segmentation process with an initial, broad delineation to refine accurate boundary detection. Initially, shape-driven evolution of level sets was employed for fine segmentation. Moreover, these level sets played a vital role in automatically initializing the model, (Thanoon et al. 2023) utilizing seed points derived from the coarse segmentation of the deep network. Segmenting lung nodules is a complex task due to variations in their shapes, contours, textures, and locations. Some nodules may be connected to surrounding tissues, further complicating the determination of their contours. The main objective of lung nodule segmentation is to precisely identify the nodule’s contour and separate it from the lung region.
