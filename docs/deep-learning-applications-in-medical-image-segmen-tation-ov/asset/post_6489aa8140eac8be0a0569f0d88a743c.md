### 5.1 Introduction

Image segmentation, also known as segmentation, is one of the fundamental tasks in computer vision. It is the process of separating an image into different parts or segments based on factors such as color, brightness, texture, and motion. Image segmentation is used for a variety of purposes such as medical imaging, autonomous driving vehicles, robots, processing images captured through satellites, and more. To ensure the accuracy of the segmented image, it is necessary to maintain homogeneity, which means that pixels in the same segment should share similar properties, such as intensity, color, texture, or motion. Additionally, the segments should have logical boundaries and be uniform, with smooth transitions between them. The primary objective while segmenting an image is to split it into meaningful areas and, during this process, each pixel or group of pixels is assigned a label based on their similarity to the surrounding pixels.

The fundamental concepts that underpin image segmentation are as follows:

- 1) Smoothness: To prevent over- or under-segmentation, the segments should exhibit smooth transitions at their boundaries.
- 2) Completeness: Every pixel in the image must be segmented, leaving no unassigned pixels.
- 3) Efficiency: In real-time applications, segmentation algorithms must be computationally efficient to handle large-scale images.

Several methods exist for image segmentation, each with its own advantages, disadvantages, and applicability in specific situations. The most commonly used methods include:

- 1) Thresholding: Thresholding is a process of dividing an image based on pixel intensity values. Pixels with intensities above or below a selected threshold are allocated to distinct segments based on the threshold value.
- 2) Region growing: This bottom-up method divides pixels into segments based on similarity standards. If nearby pixels satisfy certain similarity requirements, they are iteratively added to the segment starting from the seed points.
- 3) Clustering: Algorithms for image segmentation, such as K-means or Gaussian mixture models (GMMs), group pixels based on the similarity of features, including color or texture. Each cluster represents a portion of the image.
- 4) Edge detection: Techniques for edge-based segmentation, such as the Canny edge detector and Sobel operator, aim to identify abrupt changes in gradient magnitude or intensity to identify object boundaries.
- 5) Contour-based segmentation: This method uses the edges or contours of an image to determine object boundaries, dividing the images into regions based on these outlines.
- 6) Deep learning methods: Deep learning has revolutionized the image segmentation industry by directly learning hierarchical representations from data. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are commonly used for semantic and instance segmentation tasks, with architectures such as U-Net, fully convolutional networks (FCNs), and DeepLab being frequently employed (Khan et al. 2020).

Image segmentation has numerous applications across various domains, including medical imaging for organ delineation, tumor identification, and illness diagnosis; autonomous driving for video feed recognition of items such as cars, people, and road markings; and object detection and recognition systems for identifying and locating objects within pictures or videos (Ma et al. 2024). Additionally, satellite imagery segmentation is utilized in remote sensing applications for the classification of land cover and land use, precision-agriculture, disaster response and management, monitoring of infrastructure and water resources, urban planning, and climate change. Facial recognition systems also use segmentation to identify and locate facial characteristics such as the mouth, nose, and eyes. Despite advances in image segmentation, complex scenes with overlapping or occluded objects, or with ambiguous object borders, remain a challenge. Future research directions include developing segmentation methods that can effectively handle such complex scenes.

The commonly used medical imaging techniques are computed tomography (CT), positron emission tomography (PET), and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).

In the field of medical imaging, many techniques assist in visualizing the internal organs and aid in diagnosing various health conditions. The common techniques include:

- CT utilizes X-ray technology to produce detailed cross-sectional images of the body, which are checked by medical professionals for identification of fractures, tumors, and internal injuries.
- MRI uses magnets and radio waves to generate high-resolution images of soft tissues, organs, and joints. MRI is very useful for diagnosing medical conditions involving the brain and spinal cord, injuries of joints, and bone tumors.
- PET imaging is based on radioactive materials that help in identifying the metabolic activity in tissues. This is essential to diagnose various types of cancers as well as neurological disorders.

However, manual evaluation of a large number of images generated by these imaging techniques is time-taking and error-prone for medical practitioners such as doctors and radiologists. Therefore, there is a serious need to design a computer-based diagnosing system. The development of any computer-based mechanism for diagnosis, planning, and monitoring of diseases through image processing starts with the fundamental task of segmentation.

In medical image segmentation, the goal is to identify features or abnormalities of interest by separating an image into distinct regions or segments. This process helps with accurate diagnosis and treatment planning and allows professionals to extract quantitative data (Lei and Nandi 2022). Examples of segmentation tasks include organ delineation, tumor detection, lesion localization, and anatomical structure extraction.

Despite being extensively utilized, conventional segmentation approaches can be imprecise, labor-intensive, and frequently necessitate human involvement. The development of deep learning techniques, particularly CNNs, has revolutionized medical image segmentation, automating the process and achieving impressive results (Barbhuiya et al. 2022; Mall et al. 2023).

Deep learning algorithms, including CNNs, are useful for segmenting images because they can automatically learn the hierarchical characteristics of the input images. Initially, conventional machine learning methods such as support vector machines (SVMs) and random forests were used for feature extraction and classification in medical image segmentation tasks. However, the introduction of CNNs, particularly the U-Net architecture by Ronneberger et al. (2015), led to a significant improvement in segmentation accuracy. The U-Net architecture is based on the encoder-decoder concept along with skip links, which allows for accurate structure segmentation and localization even with limited annotated data.

Subsequent architectures, including DeepLab, fully convolutional networks (FCNs), and SegNet, have introduced modifications to address specific challenges in medical imaging, such as class imbalance, small dataset sizes, and fine-grained structure segmentation.

### 5.2 Classical Image Segmentation Techniques

Traditional image segmentation methods, often referred to as classical techniques, are employed to partition images into distinct sections or objects depending on specific features such as color, brightness, texture, or motion. These methods have been extensively studied and implemented in various industries, including medical imaging and industrial quality control.
