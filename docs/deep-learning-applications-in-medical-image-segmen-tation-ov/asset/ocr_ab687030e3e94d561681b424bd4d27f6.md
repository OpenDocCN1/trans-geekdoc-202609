Performance metrics for the segmentation of medical images are used to evaluate the accuracy and quality of segmentation algorithms applied to medical imaging data. In this section, we go through a few of the most popular performance indicators that may be used to assess how accurately the deep learning models segment images. # 1.4.1 Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC)

The DSC evaluates how much of the ground truth region overlaps with the segmented region. It is calculated as the ratio of twice the intersection of the segmented and ground truth regions to the sum of their sizes. A higher DSC value indicates better segmentation accuracy. The Dice coefficient can be mathematically stated as illustrated in Eq (1.1):

```
DSC = \left| \frac{2 \times S_{GT} \cap S_{Segmented}}{|S_{GT}| + |S_{Segmented}|} \right| = \frac{2 \times TP}{2TP + FP + FN} \quad (1.1)
```

When compared to overall pixel accuracy, the DSC is preferable since it accounts for false alarms and missed values within each class. It is preferable because it not only checks whether or not pixels have been correctly labeled but also checks whether or not segmentation boundaries have been correctly drawn. In addition, DSC is often used to evaluate the reliability of a network's performance through the implementation of cross-validation strategies.
