RNNs are rapidly being used for image recognition, despite having originally been designed for a 1D input. They have been put to use for segmentation issues, and their performance in the MRBrainS competition (Stollenga et al. 2015) was quite encouraging. Since the region of interest (ROI) in medical images is typically spread across numerous neighboring slices (e.g. in CT or MRI), correlations in successive slices are often observed. As a result, RNNs are able to make use of the sequential information supplied by the input slices to determine the contexts that exist between each pair. There are two main components of the RNN structure: the RNN, which handles inter-slice information extraction, and any CNN model, which handles intra-slice information extraction. Among RNNs, LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) (Hochreiter and Schmidhuber 1997) is the most well known. The loss of spatial information that occurs in a normal LSTM network’s inputs is problematic for medical image segmentation. The replacement of vector multiplication with the convolutional operation makes convolutional LSTM (Srivastava et al. 2015) (CLSTM) a promising option. By taking into account contextual information across neighboring slices, CLSTM was added to the deep CNN output layer in Armato et al. (2011) to improve segmentation. With their strategy, the Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) increased to 0.8247 from 0.7976 for the well-known U-Net topology (Ronneberger et al. 2015).

# 2.2.7 Attention-Based Models

The human brain served as the model for the attention processes. The human brain does not fully comprehend the vast majority of the data given to the visual and auditory cortex, but it does remember the most crucial information. Both processing speed and efficiency are enhanced by the procedure. Although the RNN was where the attention mechanism first found application, it quickly spread to other areas of artificial intelligence, including image localization, speech recognition, and machine translation (Bahdanau et al. 2015; Chorowski et al. 2015; Vaswani et al. 2017). Researchers had high praise for the recurrent visual attention model proposed by Google DeepMind (Mnih et al. 2014). Similarly, the authors of Bahdanau et al. (2015) employed attention methods to address natural language processing issues. Researchers discovered that attention modules can be used in segmenting medical images, which became the primary research issue. The authors of Jin et al. (2019) applied an attention method for segmenting medical images. The method of attention was used by the authors of Jin et al. (2019) to perform semantic segmentation of medical images. It allowed the model to recover lost information. Additionally, He et al. (2016) use attention gates inside a deep network to separate the digestive tract from Computed Tomography (CT) scans.
