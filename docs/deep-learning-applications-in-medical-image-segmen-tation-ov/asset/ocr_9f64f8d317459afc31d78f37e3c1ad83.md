# ### 7.3.3 Parametric Methods

"Active Shape Model (ASM)" and "Active Appearance Model (AAM)", both belonging to parametric, use a limited set of parameters for lung segmentation in CXRs. These incorporate shape and appearance information to adapt to the variations in lung structures and textures.

In 1995, Cootes et al. introduced the “ASM” (Cootes et al. 1995), which was subsequently refined by other researchers. Wu et al. enhanced the performance of the ASM (Wu et al. 2015) by introducing weighted components and incorporating additional constraints. This modification yielded better accuracy compared to the standard ASM technique. The deformable model proposed by Shi et al. integrates shape statistics at both “patient-specific and population-based” (Shi et al. 2008) levels through hierarchical Principle component Analysis (PCA). The aim was to employ statistical shape modeling for reliable lung segmentation across various anatomies, with the inclusion of scale-invariant feature transform (SIFT) to enhance accuracy. Iakovidis and Savelonas automated landmark extraction for lung segmentation, (Iakovidis and Savelonas 2009) offering an advantage in initial boundary detection but facing challenges in low-contrast images. Dawoud integrated shape information with intensity-based thresholding, (Dawoud 2011) demonstrating improved results compared to ASM. However, it necessitated the use of images with identical resolution for optimal performance. Zhang et al. (Zhang et al. 2014) suggested a comparable approach that combines intensity information and shape priors for lung segmentation. Both studies employed the JSRT dataset for the training and evaluation of their models. The limitation of ASM lies in its exclusive focus on the object’s shape, neglecting factors such as texture and color variations. To resolve this, AAM was introduced, extending ASM to consider both shape and appearance. While AAM is more powerful, it also requires more computational effort. Li et al. (Li et al. 2016) introduced an innovative approach that integrates both shape and appearance models to enhance the segmentation of lung fields. Their approach incorporated a multi-scale and multi-step-size strategy, contributing to improved accuracy. Additionally, Shao et al. proposed the AAM method using learning-based landmark detection and a hierarchical framework, (Shao et al. 2014) ensuring a thorough and strong segmentation process.
