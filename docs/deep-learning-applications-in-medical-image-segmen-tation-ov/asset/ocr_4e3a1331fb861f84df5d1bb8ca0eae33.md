Guo et al. (2020) introduced a Dense U-Net architecture using inception modules for retinal vessel segmentation, incorporating generative adversarial networks (GANs) in training for improved performance. Evaluation of the DRIVE dataset demonstrates high segmentation accuracy with a Dice rate of 82.15% and AU-ROC and AU-PR values of 0.9772 and 0.9058, respectively, indicating effectiveness in automated retinal vessel segmentation. Atlı and Gedik (2021) proposed Sine-Net, a novel deep learning architecture for retinal blood vessel segmentation, achieving high accuracy and robustness. Experimental validation on three publicly available databases (STARE, CHASE_DB1, and DRIVE) shows Sine-Net outperforms existing methods in certain metrics, demonstrating its potential for clinical applications. Chen et al. (2021a) proposed a comprehensive review of deep learning-based retinal blood vessel segmentation, emphasizing its importance in diagnosing diseases such as diabetes and hypertension to prevent blindness. It surveyed recent publications, identified trends in network architectures, outlined challenges, and suggested future research directions to advance and enhance models in this domain. Begundez-Arias et al. (2021) introduced a deep learning-based method for retinal blood vessel segmentation using a simplified U-Net architecture with novel loss function and batch normalization, achieving robust and accurate results across diverse fundus image datasets. Evaluation against manual segmentations in DRIVE, STARE, and CHASE_Db1 databases demonstrates superior performance compared to existing methods, suggesting its potential for integration into clinical tools for vascular structure segmentation. Liu et al. (2022) introduced an improved U-Net algorithm for retinal vessel segmentation, employing global convolutional networks, attention modules, and multi-scale feature extraction to accurately delineate vessel boundaries. Evaluation of DRIVE and CHASE_DB1 datasets demonstrates superior accuracy, outperforming existing algorithms with an average performance enhancement. Abbi et al. (2023) have created LMBiS-Net, a lightweight pixel-level CNN with a very small number of learnable parameters (only 0.172 M), for the purpose of segmenting retinal capillaries. In order to facilitate information flow between the encoder and the decoder, the network includes bidirectional skip links in addition to multipath feature extraction blocks. Ilesanmi et al. (2023) presented a systematic review of CNN algorithms for retinal fundus image segmentation and classification, highlighting their potential to aid early detection and treatment of eye problems. Analysis of 62 studies reveals CNNs’ effectiveness in enhancing segmentation precision and reducing reliance on human expertise, with consistently high accuracies observed despite some limitations.
