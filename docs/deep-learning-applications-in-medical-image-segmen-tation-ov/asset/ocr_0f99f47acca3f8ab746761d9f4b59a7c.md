# 10.3.4 Intensity Inhomogeneities

Intensity inhomogeneities, also known as shading or nonuniformity, refer to variations in pixel intensity across an image that are not related to the underlying anatomical structures. These variations can be caused by factors such as variations in tissue thickness, patient positioning, or imperfections in imaging equipment. The impact of intensity inhomogeneities on medical image segmentation can be significant, leading to various challenges such as inaccurate segmentation boundaries, misleading texture information, difficulty in differentiating tissues, increased false positives and negatives, and compromised qualitative analysis. In images related to pathology, variations in color and intensity are commonly observed (Roy et al. 2011). Intensity inhomogeneities contribute to shading effects on the image, particularly in the segmentation of MR images. Additionally, TEM images may display brightness variations due to the presence of nonuniform support films, posing challenges in the segmentation process.

# 10.3.4.1 Solution

To mitigate intensity inhomogeneities, various algorithms are employed, and numerous nonparametric techniques are proposed in the literature (Roy et al. 2011). The use of prefiltering operations before segmentation is one approach to eliminate inhomogeneities. Moreover, advancements in scanning devices contribute to addressing and enhancing intensity inhomogeneities.

# 10.3.5 Complexities in Image Texture

The complexities in image texture refer to the intricate and detailed patterns and variations present in the visual appearance of an image. Understanding and handling these complexities is essential for image segmentation. In the realm of medical images, diverse artifacts may arise during image manipulation, primarily attributed to the use of different sensors and electronic components for image capture (Guo and Ashour 2019; Hesamian et al. 2019). The captured images often exhibit closely situated grey levels, resulting in indistinct image boundaries. Overlapping tissues and irregularities such as skin lines and hair in dermoscopic images add complexity to the identification of the region of interest in medical images.
