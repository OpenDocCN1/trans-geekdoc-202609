# # 7.4.2 Overview of Deep Learning Approaches for Lung Segmentation in CT Scans

Deep learning techniques have recently emerged as powerful tools for LFS in medical images, particularly in the context of CT scans. This review focuses on the advancements and applications of deep learning-based methods for LFS, emphasizing their significance in medical diagnosis.

Early attempts at lung segmentation relied on conventional techniques such as numerical methods, (Abdani et al. 2022) gray-level thresholding, (Zulkifley et al. 2022), and shape-based techniques. However, these methods faced limitations in handling the complexity of medical images. The emergence of deep learning, with CNNs leading the charge, has brought about a transformative impact on the domain of medical image segmentation.

Recent studies have suggested several CNN-based approaches for segmenting lung fields, (Stofa et al. 2021) leveraging the capabilities of 2D CNNs, 2.5D CNNs, and 3D CNNs. These techniques have shown significant improvements over traditional methods, enabling more accurate and efficient segmentation.

The study presented by Xu et al. proposed a straightforward CNN model for lung segmentation, (Xu et al. 2019) using a training dataset generated through a “clustering algorithm-based” approach. The k-means clustering algorithm utilized the mean and minimum intensities of image patches, effectively dividing CT slices into two groups. To ensure accuracy and completeness, the final dataset was generated through a multi-step process involving cross-shaped verification for shape consistency, volume intersection for overlap removal, connected component analysis for region identification, and patch expansion for data augmentation. The network architecture consisted of a single convolutional layer with six kernels for extracting features, a maximum pooling layer for downsampling, and two fully connected layers used for final classification. The model’s performance was rigorously evaluated using an eighthfold cross-validation approach on the generated datasets.

A complementary approach introduced by Lyu et al. developed algorithms for the automatic segmentation of lung fields aimed at filtering noise from CT images of lungs while preserving lung boundaries. This was achieved by employing a filtering approach based on image decomposition, followed by the application of wavelet transformation and diverse morphological operations to delineate the lung boundaries accurately. The resulting lung outlines underwent correction and smoothing as part of a segmentation refinement process, (Liu and Pang 2020) incorporating a method for adjusting contours.
