(Wang et al. 2015). Based on this cross-validation, weights w1 are derived as follows. For every training dataset d_t(t = 1, 2...29), the corresponding entropy result at each block size s is obtained as e_ts(t = 1, 2...29), (s = 5, 10...50). Given that the manual reference segmentation for myelin in each dataset d_t is denoted by ref_t, the similarity value is represented by equation 4.3:

simval_{ts} = \frac{2 |e_{ts} \cap ref_t|}{|e_{ts}| + |ref_t|} (4.3)

Weights w1(s) are calculated based on these similarity values as given in equation 4.4:

w_1(s) = \frac{1}{29} \left( \sum_t simval_{ts} \right) (4.4)

Thus, the final result E is computed based on the individual entropy outcomes e_s(s = 5, 10...50) as given in equation 4.5:

E = \sum_s w_1(s) * e_s (4.5)

On the other hand, weights w2 are age specific. They are designed to take into account the age-based changes in myelination of WM. The input datasets are split into two groups, that is, neonates (less than 28 days) and infants (older than one month). In each group, the leave-one-out cross-validation approach is used in weight calculation as in equations 4.6 and 4.7. Therefore:

w2_{neonate}(s) = \frac{1}{29} \left( \sum_t simval_{ts} \right), t \in neonates (4.6)

E = \sum_s w2_{neonate}(s) * e_s (4.7)

Likewise, in older infants, the weights and final MWM segmentation are given by equations 4.8 and 4.9:

w2_{infant}(s) = \frac{1}{9} \left( \sum_t simval_{ts} \right), t \in infants (4.8)

E = \sum_s w2_{infant}(s) * e_s (4.9)

As the weights w2(s) closely match the specific age range of the input dataset, they achieve more accurate delineation of myelin in the pediatric brain.
