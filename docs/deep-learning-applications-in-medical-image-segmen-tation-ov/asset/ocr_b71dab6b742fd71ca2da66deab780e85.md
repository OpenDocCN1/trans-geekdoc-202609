Evaluation of benchmark datasets (JSRT, MCCXR, and SCXR) demonstrated DED-CNN's superior performance in anatomical structure segmentation compared to existing CNN architectures.

Chest X-rays are vital for diagnosing lung conditions, including cancer and other chest infections. In response, the authors proposed “CSR-Net” (Kumar et al. 2023), a fully CNN, integrating “Transfer Learning (TL) with ResNet-101 as a feature extractor,” along with “U-Net serving as a Backbone.” TL proves valuable for overcoming data limitations, enhancing generalization, and reducing training time. CSR-Net's performance is evaluated on the JSRT database, with metrics such as Dice score, precision, recall, accuracy, and Jaccard similarity coefficient (JSC) quantitatively measured.

# 7.4.1.3 Dilated Convolution-Based Approaches

Deeplabv3+ stands as a pivotal architecture, (Chen et al. 2018) offering a robust encoder–decoder framework for accurate image analysis. While its predecessor, deeplabv3, excelled at capturing multi-scale contextual information but faced challenges in accurately delineating object boundaries deeplabv3+ addressed this limitation by integrating a decoder module to improve segmentation results, especially along object borders and object edges. Building upon this foundation, researchers have explored various advancements to further enhance its performance and handle specific challenges.

Singh et al. developed Deep LF-Net, (Singh et al. 2021) an encoder–decoder network building upon deeplabv3+, designed to robustly segment lungs in Indian patients with diverse abnormalities. The encoder incorporates atrous convolutions and spatial pyramid pooling for capturing multi-scale contextual information, while the decoder refines segmentation, addressing challenges such as opacities and pleural effusions. Post-processing, including area filtering, effectively eliminates false positives. Evaluated on an in-house Indian dataset and public datasets (Shenzen, JSRT, and MC), Deep LF-Net demonstrated outstanding performance, achieving 99.37% on JSRT, 99.09% on MC, and 98.31% accuracy on Shenzhen, outperforming existing methods. In a recent study by Boudoukhani et al., (Boudoukhani et al. 2023) a unique methodology was adopted to address common challenges in lung segmentation such as shape/scale variations, device artifacts, false positives, and poor generalizability across devices. Attention gates in the encoder filter out noise, focus on relevant lung features, and minimize false positives, while residual convolutions in the decoder preserve crucial details, leading to sharp, accurate lung boundaries across diverse X-ray datasets, boosting the generalizability of this model. Based on EfficientNet-b4 and ResNet-50 architectures, it outperforms prior models, achieving “Dice coefficient of 98.03% and Jaccard index 96.14%” without pre-/post-processing (Figure 7.6).
