Zhou et al. (2019) presented Model Genesis, a method for segmenting 3D medical picture data using a transfer learning algorithm, in 2019. Transfer learning is essential when working with medical data, which frequently comes in 3-D formats like MRI and CT. To avoid having to train medical images from scratch, it is necessary to convert 3D images into 2D images and make the necessary adjustments. As a result of this alteration, crucial spatial information is lost, affecting the overall performance of the network. To address this issue, the authors of this work introduced Models Genesis, a set of models that can learn on their own and require no human labeling. The authors of the research used a transfer learning approach to create application-specific target architecture. They tested the paradigm on five 3D applications that faced classification and segmentation issues. Experiments showed that transfer learning from the 2D-ImageNet dataset outperformed natively trained models on 3D medical data. The relevance of the suggested framework was demonstrated by its consistent superiority in performance to any 2D technique. Furthermore, the authors have demonstrated their intent to broaden the concept beyond applications to encompass domains and modes of operation.
