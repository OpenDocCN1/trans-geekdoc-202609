# 2.3.1 Segmentation of Anatomical Organs

Medical images allow for an accurate examination of organ structure. Medical images can be more quantitatively analyzed for clinical characteristics relating to form and volume, such as brain and heart studies, by segmenting organs and anatomical features. Furthermore, it is frequently required as an initial step in Computer-Aided Detection/Diagnosis (CAD). Bones, vertebrae, blood arteries, and main organs are examples of anatomical structures of the human body. However, because 3D scans contain multiple slices, it is challenging for medical experts to extract anatomy of interest or lesions from these images, and doing so is a time-consuming operation. There are numerous DL approaches available for organ segmentation in medical image analysis. A number of bespoke models have been created to directly target segmentation tasks. # 2.3.1.1 Eye

The segmentation of retinal blood vessels is a difficult topic in retinal pathology research. Oversegmentation or the absence of small, fragile blood arteries is still an issue. Deep learning-based techniques for segmenting retinal vessels outperform human specialists. The first work to use a deep convolutional neural network for the segmentation of retinal blood vessels was by Liskowski and Krawiec (2016). Since then, several applications for retinal vascular segmentation have primarily used CNNs. A fully residual auto-encoder batch normalization network (Pix elBNN) was presented as a quick architecture for retinal vascular segmentation by Leopold et al. (2019). U-Net and PixelCNN serve as its foundation. Within FCN, it takes advantage of both skip connections and batch normalization. The next step was to train, test, and cross-test the model on three different retinal blood vessel segmentation datasets: DRIVE (Staal et al. 2004), STARE (Hoover et al. 2000), and CHASEDB1 (Owen et al. 2009). The execution time and performance in the test were satisfactory. Wang et al. (2019a) proposed a new U-Net with two encoders, one for spatial information and one for context information, to better capture the meaning of the data. The authors created a feature fusion module at the intersection of the two roads to merge the various forms of feature representation. In addition, the authors leverage channel attention in a skip connection to pick a relevant feature map. In addition, the multi-scale prediction module combines low-level and high-level prediction for enhanced precision.
