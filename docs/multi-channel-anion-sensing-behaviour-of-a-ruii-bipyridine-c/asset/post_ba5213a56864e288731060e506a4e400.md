## 4. Conclusions

In course of our ongoing research activities in the promotion of molecular chemistry, by implementing the learning techniques in chemistry, we investigated the anionic sensing characteristics of a benzothiazole-pyrazine raspberry(b)-pyridine hybrid complex via multiple analytical and optical methods throughout a wide range of environmental outcomes. The complexes possess one NH2 group in their outer chemical system which can interact with anions via hydrogen bonding or anion-protonation. Anionic sensing of the probe is thoroughly investigated via absorption and emission spectroscopy. The electrochemical and optoelectronic properties of the complex occurs under the selection of anions. The anionic deion of the complex is also demonstrated through single X-ray crystallography, which shows the “on-state”, while presence of anions causes emission quenching and to the “off-state”. The nature of the initial state of the complex can be feasible in presence of acid. We can see that the fluorescence intensity shortest is the lowest quantum yield of the complex is detected by naked eye. This approach allows visual detection of anions by changing the colour in presence of two ligands in the ligand environment.

Another aspect of the presented work is that the absorption and emission properties of the complex upon the action of specific anions are used to determine Boolean and logic functions. Moreover, several machine learning tools such as FL, ANN, and ANFIS have been used for analysis and prediction of experimentally observed sensing characteristics of the complex. The tools are compared with that of the experimental results. Interestingly, the ANFIS model is found to be superior decision making and learning tool over FL and ANFIS.
