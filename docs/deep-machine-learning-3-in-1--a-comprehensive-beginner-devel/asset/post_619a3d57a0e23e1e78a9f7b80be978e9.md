```
def load_dataset():
    # Here the dataset is about to load on the system
    (trainX, trainY), (testX, testY) = mnist.load_data()
    # I will not attempt to reshape the dataset so that I will have a solitary channel
    trainX = trainX.reshape((trainX.shape[0], 28, 28, 1))
    testX = testX.reshape((testX.shape[0], 28, 28, 1))
    # Here you will see a single hot encode and target values
    trainY = to_categorical(trainY)
    testY = to_categorical(testY)
    return trainX, trainY, testX, testY
```

### It is time to start scaling the pixels of the images in the dataset

```
def prep_pixels(train, test):
    # I will kick off the conversion of from integers into floats
    train_norm = train.astype('float32')
    test_norm = test.astype('float32')
    # This step will show how you can normalize the model into range 0-1
    train_norm = train_norm / 255.0
    test_norm = test_norm / 255.0
    # Here we will have normalized images in return
    return train_norm, test_norm
```

### I will define the convolutional neural network model in the following step

```
def define_model():
    model = Sequential()
    model.add(Conv2D(32, (3, 3), activation='relu',
    kernel_initializer='he_uniform', input_shape=(28, 28, 1)))
    model.add(MaxPooling2D((2, 2)))
    model.add(Flatten())
    model.add(Dense(100, activation='relu',
    kernel_initializer='he_uniform'))
    model.add(Dense(10, activation='softmax'))
    # Now that the convolutional neural network model has been
    # defined. We can start compilation of the model
    opt = SGD(lr=0.01, momentum=0.9)
    model.compile(optimizer=opt, loss='categorical_crossentropy',
    metrics=['accuracy'])
    return model
```

### I will evaluate the convolutional neural network model with the help of k-fold cross-validation technique

```
def evaluate_model(dataX, dataY, n_folds=5):
    scores, histories = list(), list()
    # I am now preparing for the cross validation of the convolutional
    neural network model

    kfold = KFold(n_folds, shuffle=True, random_state=1)

    # I will start enumeration of the splits in the convolutional neural
    network model

    for train_ix, test_ix in kfold.split(dataX):

        # Once again I am defining the convolutional neural
        network model

        model = define_model()

        # I will now select different rows to train and to test

        trainX, trainY, testX, testY = dataX[train_ix],
        dataY[train_ix], dataX[test_ix], dataY[test_ix]

        history = model.fit(trainX, trainY, epochs=10,
        batch_size=32, validation_data=(testX, testY), verbose=0)

        # I will now start evaluation of the convolutional neural
        network model

        _, acc = model.evaluate(testX, testY, verbose=0)

        print('> %.3f' % (acc * 100.0))

        # The next code snippet will store the scores

        scores.append(acc)

        histories.append(history)

    return scores, histories
```

### Business Intelligence

Business Intelligence (BI) use software and certain services for the transformation of data into actionable insights that would inform the tactical and strategic business decisions of a particular organization. BI tools attempt to access data sets and then present analytical findings in the form of graphs, reports, dashboards, maps, and charts to give users detailed intelligence about their business state.

The term business intelligence refers to a wide range of tools that would provide easy access to insights into the present state of a particular organization based on available data.

Business intelligence combines data mining, business analytics, data visualization, and infrastructure and data tools. Together they form the best practices to aid organizations in making data-driven decisions. You should keep in mind that you get control of modern business intelligence when you possess a comprehensive view of your organization’s data. You also get the skills to use the data to power change, drive out inefficiencies and to make possible a quick adaptation to the changes in market or supply of a certain product.

Business intelligence has been defined by many through different periods. Traditional business intelligence came to the limelight in the 1960s in the form of a system of information sharing. It went through several developments in the 1980s. Modern BI solutions would like to prioritize self-service analysis and fast insight into business operations.

Business intelligence is considered an umbrella term that usually covers up different processes and the methods of collecting, storing, and analyzing data from different business operations for optimization of performance. All these things would get together for the creation of a broad view of a particular business, which ultimately helps people in bettering their lives and also make some actionable decisions. Through the past years, business intelligence tends to evolve to include different activities and processes to aid in improving performance.

### The Processes

The first process that came into the loop of business intelligence is data mining. This includes the use of databases and machine learning to unearth different trends in big datasets. The next is the process of reporting. This refers to the sharing of data analytics among the stakeholders of a business so that they can draw multiple different conclusions and make informed decisions. The next process is performance metrics. This includes the comparison of current performance data to a streak of historical data for tracking performance against a set of goals. The performance metrics are generally displayed on a customized dashboard. The next process is known as descriptive analytics and it uses multiple primary data analysis to sort out what has happened. Other processes that come into play are data visualization, visual analysis, and data preparation.

### Business Intelligence Functions

One of the key functions of business intelligence is reporting. It can be regular or ad-hoc based. It depends on the provision of data to key stakeholders of the industry. These reports are destined to take many forms and they can be produced by using multiple methods. However, business intelligence allows you to eliminate the human factor in the preparation of reports. It leverages automation of the entire process and eases off the pain points in the generation of regular reports. This saves lots of time that would otherwise be spent on the collection of documents and data to make ready weekly reports. BI can help enterprises save manpower for other complex tasks. Even if a small business uses BI tools to generate reports, it will give them enterprise-level scalability.

Another function in an enterprise is analytics, the examination of data and the forming of patterns inside the data. Analytics try to be descriptive, predictive, and prescriptive as well. Descriptive analytics would prepare data in the form of modes, means, and medians.
