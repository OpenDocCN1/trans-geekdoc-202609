# What Is Deep Learning?

Deep learning educates computers to do the chores that humans do naturally. Deep learning systems stand out to be efficient because, just like humans, they learn by following an example. If you take a glance around you, you will see driverless cars or at least hear about them in magazines or on TV. The technology behind these cars is known as deep learning systems. These cars amaze people by detecting a stop sign and discriminating between a lamppost and a pedestrian. The same technology can be spotted in your tablets, wireless speakers, smartphones, and tablets. Deep learning has gained and is gaining tremendous attention for quite some time. It has achieved results that were never possible before.

A computer model attempts to classify sounds, images, and pieces of text. Deep learning modes have the potential to achieve top-level accuracy that could match humans. Labeled data trains deep learning models. Another thing that is crucial to the training of the models is neural network architectures that possess multiple layers.

In simple words, you can say that deep learning is a certain type or branch of artificial intelligence (AI) or machine learning (ML) that imitates the whole process of learning abilities of humans. It is considered an important aspect of data science, which includes predictive modeling.

Artificial intelligence (AI) has remained a subject of extreme hype across the media houses. There have been countless articles on the pros and cons of machine learning, AI, and deep learning. Even celebrities got themselves embroiled in verbal duels and Twitter fights over the future of AI and its dangers to the human race. Elon Musk, the Chief Executive Officer (CEO) of Tesla, is one of the few celebrity billionaires who advocated against an unbridled race to achieve brilliance in artificial intelligence. Whatever the facts are, presently we have been promised a fresh and evolved race of intelligent chatbots and Virtual Assistants (VAs).
