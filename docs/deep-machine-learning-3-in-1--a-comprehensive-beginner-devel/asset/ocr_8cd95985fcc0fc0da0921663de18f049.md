# How Do Deep Learning Models Work?

Deep learning is known for its accuracy. They have achieved recognition accuracy more than ever. These models make the foundation of safety-critical applications such as driverless cars. In some instances, deep learning models even surpass in efficiency and speed. Image classification tasks are the example of the fast working of deep learning models.

The concept of deep learning came into existence in the 1980s. However, at that point, it needed a big supply of labeled data. For instance, if you need to build driverless cars, you need thousands of video hours and millions of images. They also required countless computing power. High-performance GPUs and cloud computing were needed to reduce the training time for deep learning networks from months to weeks or even days.

# How Deep Learning Differs From Traditional Models

Deep learning models are considered superior because they are stacked hierarchically. On the other hand, traditional machine learning algorithms are linear. A hierarchical order boosts abstraction and complexity. If you want to understand deep learning models, you ought to imagine a toddler who learns the first-word ‘cat.’ She learns what a cat is by pointing toward an object and uttering the word ‘cat.’ If she is right, her mother would say, ‘Yes, it is a cat.’ Otherwise, she would tell her that it is not a cat. As she points to more objects, she educates her brain about the different features that a cat possesses such as her color, shape, and paws. She is, unintentionally, clarifying a somewhat complex abstraction. The toddler would build a hierarchy of knowledge in her mind. In the hierarchy, each abstraction level is formed on the foundation of knowledge gained from the previous layer.
