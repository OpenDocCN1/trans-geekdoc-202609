
Machine learning systems developed in the 1990s and since then it has fast become a highly popular and successful subfield of AI. As the datasets and hardware got faster and more efficient, machine learning gained popularity and success. Mathematical statistics drives machine learning. Unlike mathematical statistics, machine learning deals with complex and large datasets like the dataset of millions of pictures.

Machine learning demands certain input data points. For example, if the task is recognition of speech, the data points can be speeches by the people. If the task is the recognition of pictures, the data points can be images of people. For a speech recognition task, the expected output can be human-generated transcripts of audio files while in the image task, the outputs can be tags like humans or animals. You also can measure the performance of an algorithm.

A machine learning model transforms the input data into something meaningful. The main problem in the processes of deep learning and machine learning is the meaningful transformation of data. A color image can be easily encoded into RGB format which includes red-green-blue colors. It can also be encoded into HSV format which is the short form of hue-saturation-value. These are considered as the two different representations of the same type of data. If a task appears to be difficult with one representation, you can easily do that with another. The objective of machine learning models are to locate the appropriate representation for the input data.

## Deep Learning Models

Deep learning is a branch of machine learning. You can see it as a new take on educating a system with the help of data that tries to emphasize learning successive layers of different meaningful representations. Deep learning is not a reference to any type of deep understanding that you can achieve by this approach. It denotes the idea of different types of layers of representations. The higher the number of layers is the more the depth of the model is. Other names for the field can be hierarchical representations learning and layered representations learning. Presently, deep learning systems have tens or even hundreds of successive layers of representations. They tend to learn from exposure to training data. The machine learning systems that focus on just a couple of layers of representations are called shallow learning systems.

The models in deep learning systems are known as neural networks that have layers that are stacked on one over another. The term neural network refers to neurobiology as a few central concepts in deep learning are developed by drawing inspiration from the understanding of the human brain. However, deep learning models are not exact models of the human brain because the evidence doesn't exist that the human brain has anything akin to the learning mechanisms of deep learning models.

## How Do Deep Learning Models Work?

Deep learning is known for its accuracy. They have achieved recognition accuracy more than ever. These models make the foundation of safety-critical applications such as driverless cars. In some instances, deep learning models even surpass in efficiency and speed. Image classification tasks are the example of the fast working of deep learning models.

The concept of deep learning came into existence in the 1980s. However, at that point, it needed a big supply of labeled data. For instance, if you need to build driverless cars, you need thousands of video hours and millions of images. They also required countless computing power. High-performance GPUs and cloud computing were needed to reduce the training time for deep learning networks from months to weeks or even days.

## How Deep Learning Differs From Traditional Models

Deep learning models are considered superior because they are stacked hierarchically. On the other hand, traditional machine learning algorithms are linear. A hierarchical order boosts abstraction and complexity. If you want to understand deep learning models, you ought to imagine a toddler who learns the first-word ‘cat.’ She learns what a cat is by pointing toward an object and uttering the word ‘cat.’ If she is right, her mother would say, ‘Yes, it is a cat.’ Otherwise, she would tell her that it is not a cat. As she points to more objects, she educates her brain about the different features that a cat possesses such as her color, shape, and paws. She is, unintentionally, clarifying a somewhat complex abstraction. The toddler would build a hierarchy of knowledge in her mind. In the hierarchy, each abstraction level is formed on the foundation of knowledge gained from the previous layer.

The computer programs that are backed by deep learning models go through a similar process like the one the toddler’s brain has gone through. Each algorithm in this hierarchy uses the knowledge it has gained to build a statistical model. Iterations through the layers continue until the output reaches an acceptable level of accuracy.

Traditional machine learning is different in the sense that the learning process is largely supervised. The programmer needs to be very specific when educating the computer on what types of things it must be looking for to decide whether a certain image is of a cat or a dog or any other animal. The process is dubbed as feature extraction. The success rate of the computer is dependent upon the ability of the programmer to define a particular feature set for a cat. Simply put, you have to define the feature set yourself while in deep learning, the program itself builds this feature without any kind of supervision. This makes the process faster and highly accurate.

The process is largely smooth. The computer program is fed with training data which is a bunch of images that are labeled with a cat or not cat. The labels are known as meta tags. The computer program uses this initial dose of information for training the model. Then it receives a constant feed of data while the model trains itself. The very first form of the model is known as the predictive model. The first model may predict that anything that has four legs is known as a cat. It will label it a cat even if it is a dog because it will match the images by looking for different pixels in the data. Each iteration makes the model complex and highly accurate.

A toddler may take months to decipher and digest the concept of a cat but a deep learning model will take a fraction of that time to learn how a cat looks as it will be able to iterate through millions of images in a short time.

Some people question the accuracy of the model. If a model is not accurate, it is a matter of much concern. Deep learning programs need access to a bulk of processing power and training data. Deep learning models are accurate because they can easily create highly complex statistical models from an iterative output. Constant iterations help the model build accurate models out of unlabeled and unstructured data.

## Deep Learning Model Examples

Deep learning models are deployed in automated driving and medical devices. Automotive researchers use these models for the detection of different objects such as traffic lights and lampposts that are installed along the side of roads. These models are being used in driverless cars to detect pedestrians and discriminate them from moving cars and still objects. That’s how developers expect to lower the number of accidents. These models are also used in defense and aerospace to detect objects and discriminate them from satellites. That’s how experts can declare different zones as safe or unsafe.

These models also are being used in the medical field. Cancer researchers are using these systems to detect cancer cells in the human body. Deep learning models are also used to improve the safety of workers as they automatically detect when objects and people come close to machines and are likely to fall into danger. This technology is also being used for translation of speech and automated hearing.

Deep learning models work on the back of neural network architectures, which is why these models are often labeled as deep neural networks. The ‘deep’ in deep learning means the total number of layers in each neural network. Traditionally, a neural network only had two to three layers while deep neural networks may contain as many as 150 layers. Once you have created these models, you will have to train them with the help of neural network architectures and labeled data.

Deep learning has brought remarkable achievements for human beings and these achievements are visible in the form of automated cars. However, some highly awaited applications such as human-level translation, human-level dialogue systems, and human-level language understanding are largely elusive. However, AI has been through cycles of expectations and disappointments in the past.
