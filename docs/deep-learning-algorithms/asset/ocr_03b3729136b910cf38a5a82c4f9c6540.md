Loss=\lambda_{coord}\sum_{i=0}^{S}\sum_{j=0}^{B}\left[1_{ij}^{obj}(x_i-\hat{x}_i)^2+(y_i-\hat{y}_i)^2\right]

+ \lambda_{coord}\sum_{i=0}^{S}\sum_{j=0}^{B}1_{ij}^{obj}\left[(\sqrt{w_i}-\sqrt{\hat{w}_i})^2+\left(\sqrt{h_i}-\sqrt{\hat{h}_i}\right)^2\right]

+ \sum_{i=0}^{S}\sum_{j=0}^{B}1_{ij}^{obj}(C_i-\hat{C}_i)^2 + \lambda_{noobj}\sum_{i=0}^{S}\sum_{j=0}^{B}1_{ij}^{noobj}(C_i-\hat{C}_i)^2

+ \sum_{i=0}^{S}1_{ij}^{obj}\sum_{C\epsilon class}(P_i(c)-P_i(\hat{c}))^2 + \frac{\lambda}{2n}\sum_{w}w^2.

The last term is the L2 regularization term, which is the sum of the squares of all the parameters w, divided by the sample size n of the training set. \(\lambda\) is the coefficient of the regular term, which weighs the proportion of the regular term and the other terms. There is also a coefficient 1/2, 1/2 is oen seen, mainly for the convenience of the results of the latter, the latter will produce a 2, multiplied by 1/2 just rounded up. The principle and procedure of how L2 reduce overfitting is in the reference [31].
