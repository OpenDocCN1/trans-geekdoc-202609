## 3.2. Throughput

The relationship of THP with different communication parameters is shown in Fig. 5. Effect of PS and MT on THP is highlighted in Fig. 5(a). THP raises with the increase in both PS and MT values. This is intuitive because bigger packets reduce the control data proportion and increase the number of transmission attempts, and therefore can improve reliability. Fig. 5(b) reveals the effect of NN and TP on THP. As witnessed, THP decreases with an increasing number of nodes, whereas it increases with an increase in TP.

Understandably, more nodes make it difficult for communication to take place simultaneously. The effect of PS and NN on THP is shown in Fig. 5(c). The trend of decrease in THP is very consistent with increasing NN and smaller packet sizes. The effect of NN and MT on THP can be witnessed in Fig. 5(d). In addition to the THP trend observed for NN, it can be observed that higher values for MT result in higher THP. Therefore, the behavior of THP against multiple parameters is consistent across these figures.

The effect of MT and TP on THP is shown in Fig. 5(e). THP increases with an increase in both MT and TP values. However, TP appears to be more influential compared to MT. Similarly, the impact of PS and TP on THP is revealed in Fig. 5(f). THP increases as both PS and TP increase. Fig. 5(g) unveils the behavior of THP as IAT and PS are varied. It is apparent that THP increases with increasing PS and decreasing IAT values meaning that packets with more data bits sent at higher rates improve the throughput. The effect of PS on both PDR and THP is shown in Fig. 5(h). It has already been observed (Fig. 4(e)) that PDR tends to drop a bit with increasing PS. However, it casts a different impact on THP as it rises with bigger packet sizes. Therefore, PS proves to be a conflicting metric for optimizing PDR and THP simultaneously.

Fig. 5(i) highlights the impact of DT on both PDR and THP. Distance casts a relatively consistent impact on both PDR and THP as increasing DT cases decline in both PDR and THP. Therefore, the role of DT appears to be more defining for these reliability-related metrics. Similarly, the impact of IAT on both PDR and THP is revealed in Fig. 5(j). It appears that sending packets at shorter intervals causes improvement in THP. However, the same relationship between IAT and PDR diverges. Sending packets at a faster rate causes some decline in PDR. Therefore, IAT also impacts PDR and THP in a conflicting manner and requires careful fine-tuning.

## 3.3. Energy consumption: Radio usage

### 3.3.1. Listen mode

The effect of PS and TP on the Listen period is shown in Fig. 6(a). The increase in the Listen period is clearly in direct proportion to PS. Although the rise in TP also causes Listen period to increase, however, the relationship does not appear to be as strong as of PS with the Listen period. The relationship between NN and PS with the Listen period is depicted in Fig. 6(b). Affirming the relationship of PS (Fig. 6(a)), NN also appears to cause a rise in the Listen period. Particularly, as NN increases to 24 (maximum), the Listen period increases too.

### 3.3.2. Off mode

The effect of PS and TP on the radio Off period is shown in Fig. 6(c). The radio Off period tends to decrease as PS increase, indicating that bigger PS drain more energy. However, the impact of TP does not appear to be very significant. However, there is a slight trend of decrease in the Off period with increasing TP levels. The relationship of the Off period with PS and NN is revealed in Fig. 6(d). Affirming the impact of PS (Fig. 6(c)), the impact of NN on the Off period is in inverse proportion. More nodes tend to increase the radio Off periods of the nodes.

The effect of NN and TP on the CPU LPM period is shown in Fig. 7(a). The LPM period tends to increase with an increase in both NN and MT. However, this increasing trend in the LPM period becomes clearer when PS is used in combination with NN (Fig. 7(b)).

### 3.4.2. Deep Low Power Mode

The effect of NN and TP on the CPU DLPM period is shown in Fig. 7(c). DLPM period increases as NN increases. The behavior of DLPM varies with varying TP levels. ## 4. Results and discussion

### 4.1. Correlations among parameters and metrics

Statistical correlations are described in Fig. 8. It shows the R² scores of the relationship among the parameters/features (y-axis) and QoS metrics (x-axis). The magnitude of correlation scores of MP, TP, IAT, NN, DT, and PS with THP are 0.77, 0.77, 0.59, 0.53, 0.35, and 0.35, respectively. In the case of PDR, DT (0.55) proves to be most correlated, followed by TP (0.27), and NN (0.26). Other metrics that tend to have strong correlations include time in transmission (TiTx), time in DLPM (TiDLPM), and time in CPU (TiCPU). TiTx has correlation scores of 0.54, 0.54, 0.46, 0.30, and 0.27 with MP, TP, IAT, PS, and DT, respectively. DLPM is affected by MP, TP, and IAT with scores of 0.38, 0.37, and 0.31, respectively. CPU time period is determined by MP, TP, IAT, and NN with scores of 0.92, 0.92, 0.72, and 0.33, respectively.

These correlations scores affirm the pictorial relationships shown in Figs. 4, 5, 6, and 7 and provide a basis for predicting QoS metrics based on the variation in the values of the parameters/features under consideration.
