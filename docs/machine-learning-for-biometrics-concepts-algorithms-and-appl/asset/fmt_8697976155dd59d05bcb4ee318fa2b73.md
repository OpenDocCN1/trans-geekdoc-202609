### 3.1 Subjective image quality assessment

Subjective image quality assessment is the product of human expert offering their view regarding the nature of the image. But the outcome may differ from person to person. In arbitrary calculations using histogram, the accuracy of the image is determined. Histograms are easy to measure and often lend themselves as economic hardware-based implementations so it can be effectively used for image enhancement purposes. This makes histogram a common method for processing images in real time. The histogram is a schematic representation of the image in the form of graph as gray-level values in the range of 0–255 versus the frequency. Histogram is divided into four essential characteristics of the gray level: light, dark, high contrast [[^(84)](#fn84)]

For dark images, the components of histogram are centered on the lower side (i.e., nearer to 0 value) of the existing gray scale. In similar manner, the elements of the bright image histogram are skewed against the gray scale’s high edge. A wide spectrum of the gray scale is protected by the histogram in the high-contrast image. The pixel distribution is not very far from. Good quality is observed mostly for high-contrast images.
