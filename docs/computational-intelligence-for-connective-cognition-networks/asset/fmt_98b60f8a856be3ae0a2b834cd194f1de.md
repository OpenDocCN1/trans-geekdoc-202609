
### 3. Basic Iterative Method (BIM) and Projected Gradient Descent (PGD)

BIM and PGD are white-box iterative attack strategies. They create adversarial samples by doing numerous iterations of perturbation on input data. BIM begins with a modest perturbation and adds noise repeatedly to maximise the model’s loss. PGD is a more powerful form of BIM that projects perturbations onto a ball to keep them within a restricted range.

### 4. Momentum Iterative Attack

The motion iterative method is a white-box iterative method that improves BIM by adding motion. It uses gradient information accumulated over iterations to make greater modifications to perturbations. This method aids in avoiding local optima and frequently yields stronger adversarial samples.

### 5. GPS Spoofing

GPS spoofing is the sending of fake or altered GPS signals in order to fool GPS receivers (see Figure 4.1). The purpose of GPS spoofing is to trick the target device or system into thinking it is somewhere else than it is. GPS spoofing is especially troublesome in the case of drones or UAVs. Drones frequently rely on GPS for precise navigation, waypoint tracking and flying stability. When a drone is subjected to GPS spoofing, it can be misled into taking erroneous flight paths, possibly leading to dangerous scenarios. It can also jeopardise the security of operations that rely on precise location data.

### 1. Distributionally Adversarial Attack

Individual samples are not targeted by distributionally adversarial assaults, which focus on perturbing data distributions. They strive to reduce the statistical divergence between the original and altered data distributions. These attacks evaluate model resilience by taking into account the complete data distribution and frequently demonstrate model transferability.

### 2. Blockchain-based attack

A blockchain-based attack on a drone could take a number of different forms. For example, an attacker modify the drone’s data and disrupt the drone’s communication with its ground control station.

In conclusion, adversarial attack strategies are an essential and dynamic part of the ongoing endeavour to enhance the security of ML models. By actively probing and pushing these models with hostile inputs, security practitioners and researchers may uncover weaknesses, evaluate threats realistically and promote continuous development in the robustness and resilience of AI systems. Adversarial attack techniques are becoming more and more vital in preserving the reliability and integrity of AI-powered systems as ML expands into more critical industries.
