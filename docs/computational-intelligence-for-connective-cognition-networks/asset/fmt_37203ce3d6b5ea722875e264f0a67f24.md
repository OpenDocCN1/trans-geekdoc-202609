
# Visual Deepfakes

Visual Deepfakes refer to images or videos that are created by synthesizing fake images/videos or manipulating real images or videos. These visual Deepfakes involve images or videos in which the original face in the source has been replaced with someone else's face (Face Swap). Source facial expressions have been changed (Face Reenactment) or certain characteristics or attributes of the face have been changed (Facial attribute manipulation) or a complete fake has been generated (Entire Face Synthesis) or movement of the subject in the video has been changed in ways that actually never happened (puppet).

# Audio Deepfakes

Audio Deepfakes refer to audio or audiovisual media instances in which a complete fake voice has been synthesized (voice synthesis using techniques such as text-to-speech), or lip-syncing has been done by changing the voice of the subject in a video with another voice recording (voice swapping).

## 9.1.2 Categories of visual Deepfake generation methods

![Figure 9.2 Types of deepfakes and deepfake generation methods.](html/images/9781032942346_C009_002.jpg)

**Figure 9.2** Types of deepfakes and deepfake generation methods.

Visual Deepfake algorithms can be divided into four major categories (Figure 9.2): (1) face swap, (2) face reenactment, (3) entire face synthesis, and (4) facial attributes manipulation. These major categories of Visual deepfake algorithms are discussed below:

*   **Face swap**: In face-swapping methods, the identity of the person in the source video/image is replaced with that of the person in the target video. Here, the expression of the face in the source video or image is an important characteristic of this category of deepfakes. Methods such as `FaceSwap`, `Deepfakes`, and `FaceShifter` of FF++ dataset belong to this category. Recent methods of this category generate highly realistic deepfake videos.

*   **Face reenactment**: In face reenactment methods, a portion of the face is modified to change the expression or say or do something that was never done or said. Although face reenactment methods often produce high-quality and realistic fake images/frames, they lack temporal consistency in the case of videos. Methods such as `Face2Face` of FaceForensics++ (FF++) [[3]](#ch19.xhtml#r275) is an example face reenactment method. In this category, the aim of the deepfake generation methods is to reconstruct facial expressions in the source video or image of the face present in the target video while preserving the illumination, head pose, identity, and other parameters of the target video/image.

*   **Entire face synthesis**: In entire face synthesis methods, a complete face, which is fake, is synthesized after training the generating model to learn the complex latent representations and features from the face dataset. Here, the synthesized face does not belong to any real person. `ProGAN` [[4]](#ch19.xhtml#r276) belongs to this category of deepfake generation methods.

*   **Facial attributes manipulation**: This category of deepfake generation methods includes methods which manipulate certain characteristics of the face, such as skin or eye or hair color, hairstyle, gender, age, etc. An example of this category of methods is `StarGAN` [[5]](#ch19.xhtml#r277), which can perform image-to-image translation across multiple domains (or attributes of the face).

## 9.1.3 Deepfake generation process

Key technology used for creating deepfakes is generative adversarial network (GAN) [[6]](#ch19.xhtml#r278) which was proposed in 2014. GAN as shown in [Figure 9.3](#ch19.xhtml#f68) consists of two networks which make it possible to generate new synthetic fake images. These comprising networks are (1) "generator", whose purpose is to generate fake images, and (2) "discriminator", whose task is to classify if the image generated by the generator is from a dataset of real images.

![Figure 9.3 GAN architecture.](html/images/9781032942346_C009_003.jpg)

**Figure 9.3** GAN architecture.

The discriminator is trained like a binary classifier to maximize the probability of associating the correct label to both training samples as well as samples from the generator. The cost incurred by both networks is attempted to be minimized, and the back-propagation algorithm uses the derivatives of the discriminator's output with respect to the discriminator's input to train the generator to improve its output.

The first case of deepfake occurred in November 2017, when a Reddit user "deepfakes" published a pornographic video with the face of the porn actor replaced by the face of a celebrity using deep learning. Since then, there has been a rapid development in the field of deepfake such that celebrities, politicians, friends, and colleagues, and almost every person in society have been affected by it.

Since then, there has been an exponential rise in GAN methods, which are used in combination with other methods for generating deepfakes. `ExprGAN` [[7]](#ch19.xhtml#r279) introduced facial expression manipulation with expression control ability. `ProGAN` [[4]](#ch19.xhtml#r276) described a new methodology of GAN by progressively training generator and discriminator starting from low-resolution fake images and, as training progresses, adding new layers to synthesize finer quality images. `StarGAN` [[5]](#ch19.xhtml#r277) proposed image-to-image translations on multiple domains using a single model. `GANimation` [[8]](#ch19.xhtml#r280) allowed facial expression manipulation in the continuous manifold. `StyleGAN` [[9]](#ch19.xhtml#r281) made it possible to synthesize highly varied and high-quality face images with scale control variation of synthesis of fake images. `StyleGAN2` [[10]](#ch19.xhtml#r282) is an improved version of `StyleGAN`. `HomoInterpGAN` [[11]](#ch19.xhtml#r283) generates high-quality facial expression translation. `AttGAN` [[12]](#ch19.xhtml#r284) provided realistic attribute manipulation.

Various other deepfake generation methods in the category of Face swap methods such as `RSGAN` [[13]](#ch19.xhtml#r285), `FSGAN` [[14]](#ch19.xhtml#r286), `SimSwap` [[15]](#ch19.xhtml#r287), `HiFiFace` [[16]](#ch19.xhtml#r288), [[17]](#ch19.xhtml#r289), and face reenactment methods such as `ReenactGAN` [[18]](#ch19.xhtml#r290), [[19]](#ch19.xhtml#r291), `ICface` [[20]](#ch19.xhtml#r292), [[21]](#ch19.xhtml#r293) etc have also emerged.

Comparable to GANs, diffusion models [[22]](#ch19.xhtml#r294) can synthesize sophisticated and good quality images [[23]](#ch19.xhtml#r295).

## 9.1.4 Opportunities

*   **Marketing industry**: Deepfakes provide new avenues for the marketing industry with the potential to revolutionize the industry by creating more personalized marketing and multilingual and cost-effective content creation. Under ethical and legal guidelines, digital marketing can use deepfakes to advertise a product using an individual's personal favorite celebrity using his/her mother tongue to increase customer engagement. Dalí Museum, Florida used deepfake Salvador Dalí to greet visitors [[24]](#ch19.xhtml#r296)

*   **Entertainment industry**: Deepfakes has various exciting, revolutionary, and desirable offerings to the entertainment industry. Deepfakes show the ability to create cost-effective, fast, and automatic solutions for re-ageing actors or replacing stunt doubles. Existing facial reageing and replacement methods are costly and time-consuming processes as they require manual frame-by-frame work, which often takes a lot of days even by skilled workers and requires costly CGI or Makeup work. With Deepfakes, facial reageing and replacement tasks can be made cost-effective and faster than existing methods. In 2022, researchers at Disney [[25]](#ch19.xhtml#r297) presented a neural network-based production and fully automatic technique for reageing facial images in videos. It has the potential to make the arduous task of dubbing in different languages easier and cost-effective.
