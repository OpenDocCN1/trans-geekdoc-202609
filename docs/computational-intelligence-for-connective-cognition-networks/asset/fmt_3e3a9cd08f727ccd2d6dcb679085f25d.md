## 8.4.1 Training Dataset Collection

The experimental dataset is split into a test dataset and a training dataset. We use an 80%–20% training and test split. We create models with a training dataset, and we evaluate the model’s performance with a test dataset. Prior to training any algorithm, the quality of the dataset must be guaranteed in order to develop a high-performance false news detection model. We load the dataset into a data frame with the `Pandas` library. We shuffle the data before model creation to make sure the dataset is mixed up and not sticking to certain patterns according to the sorted order. We make sure that the dataset does not consist of any null values.

## 8.4.2 Attribute Selection (Step 1)

We selected the title and content of news articles for model creation (refer to Step 5 in Algorithm 1). We selected both title and content of news articles because we hypothesize that both title and content consist of important information regarding the news and including both of these attributes can be beneficial for separating fake news from real news.

## 8.4.3 Preprocessing (Step 2)

Lemmatization and stemming are popular preprocessing techniques. These techniques were utilized to enhance the performance of document retrieval precision [[39]](#ch18.xhtml#r242). According to Manning et al. [[40]](#ch18.xhtml#r243), stemming is a rudimentary heuristic process that removes the end of the word and derivational affixes from the word to achieve the goal. Compared to stemming, lemmatization is the process of employing vocabulary and morphological analysis to eliminate a word’s inflectional endings and return the word to its base or dictionary form. [Table 8.2](#ch18.xhtml#t14) shows an example of stemming and lemmatization.

**Table 8.2** Example of stemming and lemmatization [⏎](#ch18.xhtml#t14b)

| Type | Before processed | After processed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stemming | Studies, Wander News | Studies, Wand New |
| Lemmatization | Studies, Running, breathed | Study, run, breath |

In our work, we apply lemmatization to both title and content of the news articles. We did not apply stemming techniques in our dataset as lemmatization achieves improved accuracy and results in comparison to stemming, which has been shown in the study [[39]](#ch18.xhtml#r242). However, the default behavior of lemmatization that changes the word’s base to a noun might bring the possibility of reducing the accuracy of our final result outcome. Hence, to minimize the mistake brought by default lemmatization, we combine part-of-speech (POS) tagging and lemmatization in preprocessing the news title and content (refer to Step 6 of Algorithm 1). The POS tags (adjective, noun, verb, and adverb) are used to identify specific text in the dataset. So, when the lemmatizer detects a word tagged as a verb from the text, instead of changing it to a noun by default, it will return the base form of the verb. In this work, we are using the lemmatizer from `WordNet` [[41]](#ch18.xhtml#r244). `WordNet` is a huge and publicly accessible English lexical database used to create organized semantic associations between words. ## 8.4.4 Feature Extraction (Step 3)

As mentioned in Section 8.3, feature extraction is crucial to creating a high-accuracy model. We extracted features from four different domains. We believe that features extracted from different domains can be beneficial in capturing different aspects of the news and, hence, can aid in improving accuracy (refer to Step 7 of Algorithm 1). We extracted 32 features to train the FNDetector model.

## 8.4.5 Linguistic-based Feature

Linguistic-based features are used to categorize the word or phoneme specified in particular pillars such as syntax, semantics, morphology, etc. According to Mahyoob et al. [[42]](#ch18.xhtml#r245), words play a significant part in news and the style of writing the news’s contents brings a huge impact on the public. Conroy et al. [[43]](#ch18.xhtml#r246) mentioned that even though the creator of fake news tries to keep their speech under control, there will still be some verbal “leakage” that is difficult to control, such as pronoun frequencies, use of verbs, conjunction patterns, etc. Hence, to detect the “leakage” or “predictive deception indicators” in message content, we need to extract linguistic-based features from the dataset. There are two linguistic features we focus on: grammatical features and lexical features.

**Grammatical-based feature** refers to the structure of the sentences, which consists of syntax, morphology, phonology, and semantics. In this project, we extracted the count of nouns, verbs, adjectives, pronouns, and adverbs from news headlines and bodies from the dataset. To extract the grammatical features, we used the concept of POS tagging. POS tagging is also known as grammatical tagging, where each of the word sequences in a sentence is automatically assigned or tagged with a grammatical classification, which normally contains a verb, adjective, pronoun, noun, etc. [[44]](#ch18.xhtml#r247). Tagging each word with different grammar features in a large dataset is tedious and time-consuming. Hence, we took the help of the Natural Language Toolkit (`NLTK`) to perform POS tagging.

**Lexical-based feature** is another type of linguistic-based feature. Lexical refers to the relationship between a word or vocabulary of a language. In our project, we focused on lexical frequency where the frequencies of the text with correspondences occur in a large corpus, such as the number of stopwords like “the”, “is”, etc., appearing in the title or contents of the article. Besides, we also calculated the count of words in each article.
