
Kojima et al. [[4](#ch20.xhtml#r345)] mentioned in their work the success of LLMs is attributed to few-shot or zero-shot learning. Several studies have explored the use of large language models and attributed these models through prompt engineering using zero shot, few shot, conditioning few shots [[5](#ch20.xhtml#r346)], instructional zero shot [[6](#ch20.xhtml#r347), [7](#ch20.xhtml#r348)], etc. The primarily used zero-shot learning works without any explicit training data and generate a generalize outcome of tasks. On the other hand, few shots use a very limited amount of data to achieve the desired outcome. The developer implements prompt tuning as well to generate task-specific data during training [[8](#ch20.xhtml#r349)]. A significant concern in preparing lecture materials for coding courses is ensuring consistency. Literature recommends utilization of template prompts [[9](#ch20.xhtml#r350)] to assist coding learners in grasping various algorithms and problem-solving concepts in a uniform and coherent manner. This research work is a notable effort towards generation of lecture preparation material using template-based prompting and aims to address the gap in various prompt engineering techniques by investigating the effectiveness of template-based prompting. The research objectives of this study are as follows:

*   To generate lecture preparation material for programming/coding courses to serve as study material for professors.
*   To explore the feasibility of template prompting for educational content generation.
*   To evaluate performance of template prompting over conventional zero-shot and few-shot prompting for generating lecture preparation material by implementing a three-phased evaluation process.
*   To explore utilization of Gemini pro for lecture preparation material quality assessment.

To achieve these objectives, comprehensive was developed involving the exploration of various queries, categorization of the queries, generation of lecture preparation material using various prompting techniques, and evaluation of the generated material using Gemini Pro which to our best of the knowledge has not been explored before. This research has made various contributions to the contemporary literature. First, by exploring the application of template-based prompting for generating lecture preparation material expanded the scope for use of large language models in education. Second, it renders educators and researchers with meaningful insights into the effectiveness of various prompting techniques and an understanding of the most suitable approach for generating high-quality lecture preparation material specifically for programming courses. Examining the quality and relevance of generated lecture preparation material using Gemini Pro demonstrates its capabilities as a content evaluator which is also an unexplored domain. Our work discusses about the literature studied, throwing light upon how the applications of LLMs are being explored in the education domain, the framework for generation of lecture preparation material, various prompting techniques explored, utilization of Gemini Pro with RAG for ranking, the experimental setup and results obtained from the three-phased evaluation in the following sections.

## 10.2 Existing literature survey

The language models have already been used in education domain for diverse objectives such as to facilitate teachers by generating quizzes [[1](#ch20.xhtml#r342)], defining lecture objectives [[10](#ch20.xhtml#r351)], preparing lecture content [[3, 11, and 12](#ch20.xhtml#r352)], providing suggestions based on the feedback of students [[13](#ch20.xhtml#r354)], etc. Several research papers are studied in education domain specifically utilizing large language models and a few relevant research papers are summarized in this section.

One recently published most relevant research work by Spasić et al. [[2](#ch20.xhtml#r343)] focuses on the development and testing of prompting techniques to enhance the generation of lecture plan for teaching programming to preschoolers. The authors employed three prompting techniques: (1) standard prompting, (2) standard prompting with role prompt, and (3) standard prompting with role, instructions, and seed words. The results demonstrate the capacity of the model to generate human-like responses aligned with the given task. However, there were notable differences among the generated lecture plans, particularly in terms of adaptability to preschoolers and adherence to unplugged programming principles. The findings suggest that carefully tailored prompts, incorporating role definitions and seed words, can significantly influence the quality and suitability of the generated lecture plans. Sridhar et al. [[10](#ch20.xhtml#r351)] have used language model GPT-4 to automatically design learning objectives (LOs) for artificial intelligence courses. This study explores the efficiency of GPT-4 to produce high-quality LOs aligned with Bloom’s Taxonomy. The result shows that GPT-4 can generate sensible and well aligned LOs with Bloom’s taxonomy.
