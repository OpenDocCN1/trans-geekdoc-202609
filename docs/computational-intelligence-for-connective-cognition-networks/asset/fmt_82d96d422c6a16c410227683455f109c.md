
### 3. Efficient information processing

Quantum algorithms can optimize network protocols and have the tendency to reduce delay in the data transmission ([Gisin et al. 2002](#ch12.xhtml#r043); [Djordjevic 2012](#ch12.xhtml#r039)). This will overall increase the capacity of quantum networks to transmit data with minimum delays. Quantum computing and hence quantum networks can lead to more efficient processing and transmission of data.

### 4. Quantum Sensors

Quantum networks aim to interconnect the quantum devices over a long distance and hence find applications from medical imaging to environmental monitoring with enhanced sensing capabilities ([Landman et al. 2022](#ch12.xhtml#r055)).

### 5. New paradigms in network design

The advancements in quantum physics had led to the development of novel network architectures based on it. For instance, communication systems are greatly affected by the development of quantum repeaters and quantum routers which can extend the communication range and hence, reliability of quantum communication.

### 6. Scientific Research

Quantum networks can expedite the collaborative research by enabling shared access to quantum resources and linking quantum laboratories. Overall, quantum-based network paradigms hold the potential to revolutionize communication and computation that are unattainable with classical technologies.

## 2.6 Challenges

The role of quantum computing in quantum-based networks is exciting. There exist challenges also as discussed below:

1.  **Quantum decoherence**: The decay of quantum information as a result of interaction with the environment is called quantum decoherence ([Schlosshauer 2019](#ch12.xhtml#r068)). The quantum states are very much prone to decoherence on interaction with environment. This can lead to the loss of information so the main challenge is to protect the purity of quantum states by developing advanced isolation techniques and materials.

2.  **Quantum and Classical integration**: The integration of quantum networks with the existing classical networks so as to create hybrid systems brings new challenges ([Shi and Malaney 2023](#ch12.xhtml#r069); [Reilly 2019](#ch12.xhtml#r067)). The integration includes designing interfaces and protocols for the seamless interaction between quantum and classical systems ([DiAdamo et al. 2022](#ch12.xhtml#r038)).

3.  **Atmospheric Interference**: Free-space quantum communication is greatly affected by weather conditions and atmospheric turbulence.

4.  **Security**: Quantum networks need to be protected against the attacks by continuously monitoring the security protocols, developing safe cryptographic methods and doing rigorous testing.

5.  **Development of efficient quantum repeaters**: The quantum repeaters are required to extend the range of quantum communication but robust and efficient repeaters are difficult to build that can perform entanglement swapping and preserve the purity of quantum states without loss of information. Quantum repeaters rely on quantum memories to store and retransmit entangled states. However, current quantum memories struggle with limited coherence times (the duration they can reliably hold quantum states) and low storage efficiency, which restricts network scalability ([Dusanowski and Thompson 2023](#ch12.xhtml#r040)). Further, in large quantum networks, scaling up of numerous repeaters and synchronization between these repeaters is a complex challenge.

6.  **Cost and scalability for large-scale integration**: Small quantum networks, while scaling up to larger practical quantum networks require advancements in technology. This includes developing scalable architectures, standardized protocols and modular quantum network components which requires investment and infrastructure also. Quantum hardware is still in development, and real-world problems can be solved only with much more powerful hardware ([Monroe and Kim 2013](#ch12.xhtml#r058)).

7.  **Interdisciplinary collaboration**: Research and development in quantum networks requires the expertise in various fields such as quantum physics, computer science, engineering and thus fostering interdisciplinary collaboration.

8.  **Standards and protocols**: The development of quantum networks lacks the standardized protocols and regulations for quantum communication.

## 2.7 Conclusion and future directions

Quantum computing and hence quantum networks have many real-world applications. With the advent of quantum computing which leverages the principles of quantum mechanics, there occurs a paradigm shift in classical computing since quantum computing enable computations that are infeasible with classical methods. The foundation of quantum computing is based on the qubits and quantum gates, which in turn, offers the unprecedented speed and efficiency in solving complex problems. Thus, quantum-based network paradigms represent a transformative shift in computation, communication technology and information processing to achieve enhanced security, speed and computational power. The potential of quantum computing is exciting as well as challenging also. There exist many challenges with quantum networks such as preservation of entanglement over the long distance, technologies for integrating quantum nodes with existing classical infrastructure, and errors due to decoherence and noise. But the ongoing research and development efforts in this field promise to unlock the full potential of quantum networks to make quantum communication as the cornerstone of global connectivity. Key directions include hybrid quantum-classical protocols to integrate quantum systems with classical infrastructure, standardized communication protocols for interoperability, quantum machine learning ([Karthikeyan et al. 2024](#ch12.xhtml#r050)) and AI-powered network optimization ([Khang and Rath 2024](#ch12.xhtml#r051)) to adapt dynamically to network conditions. It is emphasized that the international collaboration and standards development to realize a global quantum internet infrastructure is urgently needed. A more secure, interconnected, and technologically advanced future can be embarked by embracing quantum-based network paradigms.
