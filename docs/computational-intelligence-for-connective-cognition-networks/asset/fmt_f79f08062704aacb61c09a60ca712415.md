The pivotal role of synthetic ECG generation in propelling advancements in cardiology research and innovation. By harnessing this technique, researchers can propel the development of more precise diagnostic tools, predictive models, and personalized healthcare solutions tailored to individual patient needs. The focus of the presented work has been on ECG modeling utilizing a parametric quartic spline method, complemented by machine learning classification applied to the resultant database. Through this approach, the study aimed to enhance our understanding of ECG signals and improve automated classification capabilities.

Machine learning techniques, including `KNN`, `Naïve Bayes`, and `AdaBoost`, were employed to analyze the derived ECG database. These methods demonstrated considerable potential in automating classification tasks, thereby streamlining diagnostic processes. Notably, among the three techniques, `Naïve Bayes` emerged as particularly promising, exhibiting superior accuracy compared to its counterparts. Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of combining ECG modeling with machine learning classification techniques. This integrated approach holds significant promise for revolutionizing cardiac healthcare by enabling more accurate diagnoses, predictive insights, and tailored treatment strategies. As such, continued exploration and refinement of these methodologies are imperative for driving future advancements in the field of cardiology.
