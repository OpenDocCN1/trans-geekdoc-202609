
## Frequency in Face Forgery Network (F³ net)

Frequency in Face Forgery Network (F³ net) proposed by Qian et al. [[55]](#ch19.xhtml#r327) used frequency-aware forgery clues for face forgery detection. The proposed method consists of two branches:

1. **Frequency aware decomposition branch**: Partitions input image in the frequency domain according to learnable filters, and then decomposed frequency components are transformed back into spatial domain image components, which are stacked together and fed to CNN to fine forgery clues.
2. **Local frequency statistics branch**: Mines discrepancies in local frequency statistics between real and fake images of faces by employing sliding window DCT on the input image and calculating the mean frequency values within a set of learnable frequency bands.

The two branches are then fused together using a cross-attention fusion module. The features of the two branches are then augmented using cross attention fusion module for feature interaction between the two branches and FF++ dataset is used for method evaluation and results of which show good performance in different qualities of images of FF++ with a minimum of 93.2% accuracy and 0.958 AUC score for low quality and a maximum of 99.99% accuracy and 0.9999 AUC score for raw FF++ videos.

## Hierarchical Frequency-Assisted Interactive Networks (HFI-Net)

Another frequency-based deepfake detection method, hierarchical frequency-assisted interactive networks (HFI-Net) proposed by Changtao et al. [[56]](#ch19.xhtml#r328) is dual branch network that explores mining face forgery clues of deepfakes in the frequency domain. It consists of two branches:

1. **Transformer branch**: Consists of four stages of transformer blocks (each stage has three ViT transformer blocks), captures Global forgery clues.
2. **CNN branch**: For extracting local forgery artefacts, also has four stages as transformer branch with each stage containing three separable convolution blocks and a residual connection between input and output of the stage.

At each stage of HFI-Net a global–local interaction (GLI) module fuses, global and local information and allows interaction between the two branches of the network and combines them to provide complementary features and improve generalization ability. Intradataset experimentation is done on FaceForensics++ (FF++) dataset, and cross-dataset evaluation is done on Celeb-DF, TIMIT, DFDC, and UADFV datasets. and also use GID-DF and GID-FF protocols [[57]](#ch19.xhtml#r329) for unseen manipulations experimentation and DeeperForensics-1.0 for unseen perturbations experiments. The method shows impressive performance in different experiments.

## 9.3.6 Temporal domain feature-based methods

The recurrent neural network method proposed by Xi et al. [[58]](#ch19.xhtml#r330) is based on extracting spatial, steganalysis, and temporal features. For spatial features, XceptionNet is used, and for extracting Steganalysis features, constrained convolutional filter is used on simplified XceptionNet. Both of these features are fused together and temporal features are extracted from the fused features of the consecutive frames using LSTM. The proposed method is evaluated using FF++ and GAN-based Deepfakes [[59]](#ch19.xhtml#r331) dataset. The method achieves accuracy % of 98.57% (FF++ HQ) and 90.11% (FF++ LQ) in the intradataset evaluation, and cross-dataset evaluation on GAN-based Deepfakes achieves accuracy comparatively lower than that of intra-dataset evaluation.

Xi et al. [[60]](#ch19.xhtml#r332) proposed a deepfake video detection method that uses both spatial and noise features of the spatial and temporal domain of video frame to detect deepfake. Of the k-selected video frames, each cropped facial regions are sent to two streams: RGB stream and SRM (Spatial Rich Model) stream. RGB stream derives the RGB features and SRM stream is used for extracting noise features by applying learnable SRM filters whose values are defined manually. The fusion of both segment-based and stream-based predictions provides the final detection result. Both streams use Xception as a backbone network, and the method is evaluated on the FF++ dataset and achieves the performance of 90.36% accuracy on evaluation.

## 9.3.7 Deep learning-based methods

Yuval et al. [[61]](#ch19.xhtml#r333) presented a method which aimed at identifying deepfake videos based on comparing inner and outer context regions, and the method is based on the assumption that the manipulation method only manipulates the inner part of the face and the outer context regions such as hairs, neck, ears etc are not easy to regenerate by the manipulation methods. During the preprocessing step, the face region is detected and cropped, and the inner face region and context region are obtained and fed into different Xception [[62]](#ch19.xhtml#r334)-based networks. E[f] learns on the facial region, and E[c] learns on the context region. Extracted vectors are then the difference of the two extracted vectors is computed and used as discrepancy vector which is concatenated with vectors obtained from the other two networks which map face regions from face swap and face reenactment methods. The concatenated vector is then passed to the final classifier for the final detection task. The method uses multiple datasets such as VGGFace2, Labeled Faces in the Wild (LFW), FaceForensics++, DFDC, Celeb-DFDC, and FaceForensics++ datasets for training and testing. This method achieves AUC % performance of as low as 66% (Celeb-DF v2) and as high as 99.7% (FF-DF) in images created by face swapping.

Framework for mining generalized features for the detection of AI-manipulated faces proposed by Yang et al. [[63]](#ch19.xhtml#r335) focused on mining intrinsic features in the form of channel difference image (CDI) and spectrum image (SI), which are indispensable characteristics of the camera imaging process and AI manipulation process. The framework uses the Octave convolution for mining the features as both the features are related to the frequency domain and uses an attention-based fusion module to effectively combine the extracted information and adopt a domain alignment module to improve generalizability. The dataset used for experiments is constructed by four categories (face entire synthesis, facial expression manipulation, facial attribute manipulation, and identity manipulation), and the dataset contains 10 state-of-the-art and popular manipulation techniques such as StyleGAN, StarGAN, FF++ (Deepfakes), DFDC, etc. The model outperforms other existing methods in both the same dataset analysis as well as cross dataset analysis and demonstrates good performance for different postprocessing methods such as compression, resizing, median filtering, noise addition, etc. For unseen categories, the method achieves an accuracy performance of 86.79% for entire synthesis, 89.26% for expression manipulation, 89.74% for attribute manipulation, and 81.92% for identity manipulation.
