
## 6.4 Results and discussion

Where **True Positives (TP)** refers to instances where the model correctly predicts positives, **True Negatives (TN)** represents correct negative predictions, **False Positives (FP)** represents incorrect positive predictions, and **False Negatives (FN)** represents missed positive cases.

**Precision** measures the ratio of accurate positive predictions to the total number of instances predicted as positive, calculated as:

$$
\text{Precision} = \frac{\text{True Positives}}{\text{True Positives} + \text{False Positives}} \tag{2}
$$

**Recall**, also known as sensitivity, measures the proportion of actual positives correctly identified by the model, defined by:

$$
\text{Recall} = \frac{\text{True Positives}}{\text{True Positives} + \text{False Negatives}} \tag{3}
$$

The **F1-score**, a harmonic mean of precision and recall, combines these two metrics to provide a balanced evaluation, particularly useful when both false positives and false negatives are important:

$$
F_1 \text{ Score} = 2 \times \frac{\text{Precision} \times \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}
$$

Evaluating these metrics on both training and test datasets is crucial for a complete assessment, as it allows for comparison of the model’s performance on known versus new data. This practice helps identify issues such as over-fitting or under-fitting, providing a comprehensive view of how well the model generalizes to unseen cases.
