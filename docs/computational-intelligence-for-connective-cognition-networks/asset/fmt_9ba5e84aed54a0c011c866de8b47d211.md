## 3.1 Introduction

Enhancing the clinical capabilities of medical image enhancement is critical for image analysis. The goal of this work is to apply genetic algorithms (`GA`s) to produce enhanced images. The basic idea entails feeding the initial image through `GA`s to produce a clear and high-quality reproduced image, which is then enhanced further. This work aims to create a framework for creating the finest possible images by using a cutting-edge algorithm that considers all restrictions to produce better outcomes. For accurate and efficient picture enhancement, it employs the `GA`. We began with reading an image and preparing the fitness function. This randomly generated image is continuously evolved through many generations, using crossover and mutation, and `GA` until a superior solution is found. With a dual-peaked intensity distribution, this can enhance the visual impression of a region of interest (`ROI`) in picture data.

Before processing, image enhancement enhances the quality and information content of the original data. Traditional technique includes contrast enhancement, spatial filtering, and density slicing. In contrast enhancement or stretching, a linear transformation is applied to expand the range of grey levels. Spatial filtering improves linear features that occur naturally, such as fault. Density slicing segments the continuous range of grey tone into a series of density intervals, with each interval represented by a specific color or symbol.

The demand for medical image improvement has expanded in recent years in order to assist clinicians in making accurate diagnoses; hence, medical image processing provides relief to enhance medical images for proper illness diagnosis [[1]](#ch13.xhtml#r080). One of the primary goals of medical image processing has always been to improve image quality. The clarity of images for human viewing is improved by image enhancement.

Enhancement techniques include removing blur and noise from an image, increasing contrast, and revealing details [[2]](#ch13.xhtml#r081). While capturing an image, some information is lost, resulting in a blurry or less informative image that is not always clear. Medical images are poorly lighted, and many key parts of the image are not visible, resulting in visual blur and contrast that is difficult to notice.

Medical research data contains a plethora of information that, in some situations, cannot be simply examined, if at all, using traditional statistical approaches. Physicians who could benefit from using algorithms to solve difficult medical problems are unaware of their existence. As a result, we wanted to use the `GA` and its medical applications. The employment of the `GA` in several medical specialties, including radiology, has intriguing implications. This work presents the `GA`'s uses in illness screening, diagnosis, treatment planning, pharmacovigilance, prognosis, and healthcare management, as well as allowing physicians to imagine how they may apply this metaheuristic approach in their own practice. ## 3.2 Literature Survey

The majority of extant image enhancement approaches are empirical or heuristic methods that are tied to specific types of images and try to enhance the contrast level of images that have deteriorated throughout the acquisition process. An optimization problem is determining the ideal level of greyscale that enhances each different input image. Some approaches employ Swarm Intelligence techniques. Particle swarm optimization (`PSO`) is used in multiobjective histogram equalization to improve contrast while preserving brightness. Some works use the utilize similar candidate solution encodings and histogram mapping strategies, all within an optimization framework focused on the Artificial Bee Colony (`ABC`) algorithm.
