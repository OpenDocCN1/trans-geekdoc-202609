
According to certain studies, high crossover rates of 80–95% and modest mutation rates of (0.1–1) should be used. The researcher advises utilizing a high mutation e.g. 50% with crossover and a small population in another study. Without any feedback from the search process or user involvement, the deterministic control parameter alters the parameter value during the execution. As a result, this kind of feature setting should be utilized to try to figure out which behavior the parameter should deal with in order to be effective and to discover the best or near-best answer. Using high crossover rates with low mutation rates, along with a large population size, promotes diversity within the population. However, in smaller populations, higher mutation rates are employed to introduce diversity and enhance search quality. By adjusting crossover and mutation rates, GA efficiency can be improved through a balanced approach to population diversity.

### 3.5.2 Time efficiency and scalability

To assess scalability, tests were conducted on image datasets of various resolutions. While the GA performed efficiently for smaller image sets, higher-resolution images required significantly more processing time, underscoring a limitation of the framework in terms of computational cost. Further optimization or parallel processing could help address this limitation, making the GA framework more viable for large-scale applications.

## 3.6 Limitations

One of the main limitations of the proposed GA framework is the high-computational cost associated with iteratively evolving large datasets. Each generation requires a fitness evaluation, crossover, and mutation processes that can be computationally intensive, especially for high-resolution medical images. As the dataset size increases, so does the number of generations and operations required to converge on an optimal solution. This prolonged processing time may limit real-time applications, making the framework less suitable for scenarios where immediate image enhancement is essential.

Moreover, the GA’s reliance on numerous parameters—such as mutation rate, crossover rate, and population size—demands careful tuning to maintain efficiency, which can add further to the processing time if multiple configurations need to be tested. Future work could explore optimized parallel processing techniques or hybrid models that combine GAs with other, less resource-intensive algorithms to improve efficiency without compromising image quality.
