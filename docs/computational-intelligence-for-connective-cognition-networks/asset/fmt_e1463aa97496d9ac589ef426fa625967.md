
For example, if we have 20 documents and the term “library” appears in three documents, the IDF of the term will be `loge(20/3) = 1.897`. In order to address issues like stopwords that are highly prevalent in documents but have limited significance for text mining, the researchers have suggested merging TD and IDF computations to represent a term’s relative importance to a document within the collection. The Term Frequency – Index Document Frequency (TD-IDF) will assign higher importance to the term that has higher frequency but less occurrence in the total number of documents. To calculate TD-IDF, it is just simply taken the multiplication of TD and IDF. For example, to calculate the term “library” in the collection, we will multiply 0.0025 and 1.897 which is 0.00474.

The concept of TD-IDF is playing important role in training the fake news detection model. By using the concept of TD-IDF, we can analyze what are the common terms being used in fake news to attract the public. In our work, the TD-IDF will be applied to the lemmatized title and the content of the articles. To implement the TD-IDF feature in the dataset, the `TfidfVectorizer` in the `scikit-learn` package [[50]](#ch18.xhtml#r253) (a machine learning library) is being used.

Tokenizer: The `TfidfVectorizer` from the `scikit-learn` package will return the result as a sparse matrix. However, LSTM classifier gives better performance when the sequence is used as input. Hence, for the LSTM classifier, we vectorized heading and body of news article by turning each text into a sequence of integers using `Tokenizer` [[51]](#ch18.xhtml#r254) (from the `TensorFlow` package).

Random Forest (RF) Classifier: Breiman et al. [[52]](#ch18.xhtml#r255) first presented the RF method, an ensemble learning approach for regression trees and classification. A collection of classifiers with tree structures make up the RF classifier, and each tree votes for the class that is most popular.

Compared to other machine learning algorithms, the random forest doesn’t consist of overfitting problems due to the use of the Strong Law of Large Numbers. The core method for the random forest to improve its accuracy was using the bagging method (also known as Bootstrap aggregating [[53]](#ch18.xhtml#r256)). Besides, the out-of-bag method was used to estimate the strength, correlation, and generalization error. Furthermore, based on the strength of each predictor and their correlations, the algorithm offers information about the random forest’s prediction ability.

According to Breiman [[52]](#ch18.xhtml#r255), the random forest provides significant improvement in classification from the concept of expanding the collection of trees and letting them choose the most popular class.

[Figure 8.3](#ch18.xhtml#f47) describes RF algorithm. Taking into account that the training dataset includes balls of various hues, including pink, green, blue, yellow, and red.

The dataset will be divided into subsets and assigned to one decision tree in the RF algorithm. Each of the decision trees produces one output. For example, decision tree 1 and 2 produces yellow ball as their output, and tree n produces blue ball as their output. Then, the random forest algorithm will collect the majority voting and produce the final prediction. In our example, most of the tree has selected the yellow ball as their prediction output. Hence, the final prediction of RF will be the yellow ball.

Many studies such as [[54]](#ch18.xhtml#r257) have shown that RF is among the machine learning methods with the highest performance, particularly for prediction tasks. The RF algorithm was formed by a tree-based model. The random forest algorithm is being used widely across different areas such as stock price prediction in the financial industry [[55]](#ch18.xhtml#r258). There are also lot of researchers who had used the random forest as the classifier to train fake news detection model as mentioned in Section 8.3. In our work, we tend to use different extracted features with RF algorithms to experiment with prediction accuracy.

Long-short term memory neural network (LSTM): The pioneer of the LSTM is the recurrent neural network (RNN) that utilizes consecutive or time series data. The RNN is often used in time series forecasting such as forecasting the next twelve-month energetic usage of a household. According to Hochreiter and Schmidhuber [[56]](#ch18.xhtml#r259), the existing RNN causes the vanishing and exploding of gradients when the error signals are flowing backward. To overcome this issue, Hochreiter and Schmidhuber [[56]](#ch18.xhtml#r259) has introduced the concept of LSTM. The LSTM was formed by four core elements which are forget gate, input gate, output gate, and a complex unit called memory cell [[57]](#ch18.xhtml#r260). Different from other traditional deep neural networks, the memory cell in the LSTM algorithm has the center linear unit with fixed self-connection. It goes along the whole chain and makes the selection on which information needs to be stored with the help of three gates [[56]](#ch18.xhtml#r259). This process allowed the LSTM network to identify long-term dependencies and memorize multiple time dependencies with different characteristics [[58]](#ch18.xhtml#r261).

In our work, we have created the LSTM neural network to train the model. Two embedding layers are created to store the information of the title and content of the article. Then, two LSTM layers with 128 neurons are created to hold the output of embedding layers. A concatenate layer is created to concatenate the output of the two LSTM layers. Furthermore, a dense layer (a layer of neurons that receive the input from the previous layer) is created to process the other numeric features. Another concatenate layer is created to process outcomes from previous two layers before going into another dense layer. Using the input from former layer, the final output layer produce one result as the end product.

The overall LSTM neural network model for our project can be viewed in [Figure 8.4](#ch18.xhtml#f48). We used the sigmoid function as an activation method for our final output layer. This helps network to learn which data needs to be forgotten or remembered by holding the value between 0 and 1. The information will be forgotten if the value is 0 and stays if the value is 1. Since our final result has only two outcomes which are true or false news, we used binary cross entropy function for providing better performance for the binary classification model.
