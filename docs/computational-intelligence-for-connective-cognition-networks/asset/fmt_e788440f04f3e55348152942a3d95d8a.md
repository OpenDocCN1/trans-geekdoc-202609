
## Motivation

Most of the researchers have focused on analyzing the headlines of the news articles for fake news model creation such as [[66](#ch18.xhtml#r269)–[68](#ch18.xhtml#r271)]. Some of the datasets that are used for building a fake news detection model such as the LIAR dataset [[69](#ch18.xhtml#r272)] do not contain both the title and contents of the news articles in the dataset. The dataset only contains the statement extracted from both the title and contents of the article. We believe that the content of news can provide important information for fake news detection. Thus, in this RQ, we work on analyzing the importance of both titles and contents for detecting fake news. The result of this RQ can help us understand which part of the news is more important in fake news detection.

## Approach

To answer this RQ, we build three variants of the `FNDetector` model: `FNDetector (title)`, `FNDetector (content)` and `FNDetector (title+content)`. `FNDetector (title)` model is trained using only the title of the news articles. `FNDetector (content)` is trained using the content of the news articles. `FNDetector (title+content)` is trained using both title and content of the news articles which is the original `FNDetector` model proposed in this work. We train all these variants using both `RF` and `LSTM` classifiers, hence, create a total of six variants of the `FNDetector` model.

## Results

[Figures 8.12](#ch18.xhtml#f56) and [8.13](#ch18.xhtml#f57) show the comparison of the performance between different variants for `FNDetector[RF]` and `FNDetector[LSTM]` models, respectively. [Table 8.13](#ch18.xhtml#t25) shows that the accuracy and F-measure using `FNDetector[RF] (title)` model is 70.6% and 72.7%, respectively. The `FNDetector[LSTM] (title)` gives accuracy and F-measure of 74.5% and 76.4%, respectively. We observe from [Table 8.16](#ch18.xhtml#t28) that the performance of the `FNDetector[RF]` model is improved when we use “content” of news article as compared to when we use “title” for training the model. We observe an improvement of 11.8% (accuracy) and 10.3% (F-measure) when using `FNDetector[RF] (content)` as compared to the `FNDetector[RF] (title)` model. For the `LSTM` classifier, we observe a reduction in the performance in both accuracy and F-measure when the “content” of news articles is used to train the classifier (refer to [Table 8.17](#ch18.xhtml#t29)).

We notice an improvement in the performances of both `FNDetector[RF]` and `FNDetector[LSTM]` models. Using `FNDetector[RF] (title + content)`, we notice an improvement of 17.6% (in comparison to `FNDetector[RF] (title)`) and 5.8% (in comparison to `FNDetector[RF] (content)`) in accuracy. Similarly, using `FNDetector[LSTM] (title+content)`, we notice an improvement of 3.93% (in comparison to `FNDetector[LSTM] (title)`) and 19.63% (in comparison to `FNDetector[LSTM] (content)`) in accuracy. The improvements are also shown in [Tables 8.16](#ch18.xhtml#t28) and [8.17](#ch18.xhtml#t29). Results indicate that both the title and content of news articles are important in fake news detection. For both `FNDetector[RF]` and `FNDetector[LSTM]`, we observed a significant improvement in the prediction accuracy when both title and content are used to train the model.

## RQ 4: Which features are more important to detect fake news in the FNDetector model?

### Motivation

We use 32 features to train the `FNDetector` model. These features belong to different domains and have different types, i.e., linguistic-based features (grammatical and lexical features), sentiment-based features, named entity recognition features, and textual features. The `FNDetector` model outdid the baseline line classifier significantly in both accuracy and F-measure. An analysis of the performance of the proposed `FNDetector` model with incremental feature class addition can provide insights into the importance of different feature classes.

### Approach

To answer this RQ, we perform two types of experiments. First, we created four variants of `FNDetector` models (for both `RF` and `LSTM` classifiers) and added features incrementally to observe the effect of each feature class on the performance of the `FNDetector` model. We create the following variants:

*   `FNDetector[A]^(F[S])`: `FNDetector` model trained using sentiment-based features.
*   `FNDetector[A]^(F[S] + F[L])`: `FNDetector` model trained using sentiment-based and linguistics-based features.
*   `FNDetector[A]^(F[S] + F[L] + F[N])`: `FNDetector` model trained using sentiment-based, linguistics-based features, and NER-based features.
*   `FNDetector[A]^(F[S] + F[L] + F[N] + F[T])`: `FNDetector` model trained using all the 32 features extracted.
