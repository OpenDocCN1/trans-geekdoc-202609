
## 4.3.2 Other approaches for security in drones

‘Drones for Smart Cities: Issues in Cybersecurity, Privacy, and Public Safety’ highlights public safety as a key consideration when identifying drone-related issues in future smart cities.

The study takes a progressive stance, projecting how drones will be employed in networked smart cities in the future. It outlines a wide range of possible duties, such as broadband wireless access, delivery services, and monitoring and security systems. The report also highlights the technological and social challenges associated with the widespread deployment of drones in future smart cities, notably with regard to public safety, cybersecurity, and privacy.

With an emphasis on how drone integration may affect cybersecurity, the survey article creates a comprehensive approach by reviewing and synthesising recent studies, publications, and literature on the topic of drone integration into future smart cities [[2](#ch14.xhtml#r101)].

‘The Current Opportunities and Challenges in Drone Technology’ highlights UAV applications in agriculture, healthcare, and armed-services sectors. The study examines the many applications of drone technology in agriculture, healthcare, and armed-services areas. It examines drones’ roles and contributions in various industries in depth, highlighting their advantages, potential hazards, and potential misuses. The study examines the advantages, disadvantages, possibilities, and potential risks provided by drones in agriculture, healthcare, and armed-services settings [[5](#ch14.xhtml#r104)].

‘SecureIoD: A secure data collection and storage mechanism for internet of drones’ explains the creation and testing of SecureIoD, a secure IoD data collection system.

Given the limits of drones and the open wireless medium, this study emphasizes the need for data security and privacy in the IoD scenario. SecureIoD, a secure IoD data gathering and storage solution, is introduced in the study. It consists of mutual authentication between drones and zone service providers (ZSPs), secure key creation, and blockchain-based data storage.

The miner ZSP is selected via a hybrid proof-of-work and proof-of-stake consensus mechanism. Practical tests and simulations demonstrate that SecureIoD is more secure and effective than previous systems, and security studies support its resistance to attacks [[20](#ch14.xhtml#r119)].

‘Towards fully autonomous UAVs: A survey’ highlights recent advancements in UAVs and safe autonomous navigation methods. The article starts by underlining the progress of UAVs across many applications in the armed-services and public sectors, emphasising their value in remote access, time savings, and safety advancements. The research focuses on current UAV advancements, particularly in terms of reaching full autonomy and eliminating human interaction. The study aims to assess and evaluate improvements in UAV technology, with a focus on safe autonomous navigation. The process includes a thorough assessment of current literature, technical reports, and research papers to accomplish this. Advanced approaches for 3D avoidance manoeuvres and trajectory planning address unique research issues related to UAV development, particularly in the context of safe autonomous navigation [[9](#ch14.xhtml#r108)].

‘Privacy and Security Improvement in UAV Network using Blockchain’ addresses UAV vulnerabilities and proposes using blockchain to secure UAV communication. It proposes blockchain technology for protecting UAV communication, with an emphasis on smart contracts and the establishment of a secure UAV ad hoc network that is resistant to different network attacks and intrusions. A blockchain-based secure UAV ad hoc network is envisioned to withstand different network attacks and provide a safe and dependable communication environment for UAVs [[19](#ch14.xhtml#r118)].

‘Intrusion detection systems in Unmanned Aerial Vehicles’ introduces an intrusion detection system for UAVs using PCA and single-class classifiers, tackling GPS spoofing and jamming. In an intrusion detection system developed to discover GPS spoofing and jamming threats against UAVs, PCA and single-class classifiers are used. This system operates within the UAV’s resource-constrained agent devices and aims at high accuracy rates of over 90% in detecting and perhaps neutralising GPS spoofing and jamming assaults against UAVs [[20](#ch14.xhtml#r119)].
