## 1.1 Introduction

Electrocardiogram (ECG) signals play a vital role in diagnosing various cardiac abnormalities and monitoring heart health. These signals, which represent the electrical activity of the heart, provide valuable insights into the heart’s functioning and can aid in the detection of conditions such as arrhythmias, ischemia, and myocardial infarction [[1]](#ch11.xhtml#r001), [[2]](#ch11.xhtml#r002). As such, accurate analysis and interpretation of ECG signals are paramount in clinical practice.

Traditionally, ECG signals are analyzed visually by trained medical professionals to identify abnormalities. However, this process can be time-consuming and subjective, leading to variability in diagnosis [[3]](#ch11.xhtml#r003). To overcome these limitations, there has been a growing interest in the development of automated methods for ECG analysis, leveraging advancements in signal processing and machine learning techniques. One area of focus in ECG research is the development of accurate models that can replicate the complex morphology of ECG signals [[4]](#ch11.xhtml#r004). These models are essential for various applications, including algorithm development, medical device testing, and education.
