## 4.1 Introduction

### 4.1.1 Working of drones

#### 4.1.1.1 Network infrastructure of drones

To enable successful communication, control, and data transmission, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) or drones rely on a range of network infrastructure components. Understanding these infrastructural components is critical for understanding the intricacies of drone operations:

1.  **Communication Links:**
    *   **Radio Frequency (RF) Links:** Drones often use RF links, such as 2.4 GHz and 5.8 GHz, which are common for control and telemetry.
    *   **Wi-Fi:** Consumer drones frequently utilise Wi-Fi connections for communication between the drone and a remote controller or smartphone app.
    *   **Cellular Networks:** Commercial and enterprise-level drones are equipped with cellular connectivity, allowing for extended-range communication, data transfer, and live video streaming.
    *   **Satellite Communication:** In remote or offshore areas, drones may rely on satellite communication to maintain connectivity, ensuring global coverage.

2.  **Data Links:** Drones provide a variety of data kinds, such as photos, movies, sensor readings, and telemetry data. Data connections, both cable and wireless, are used to transport data between the drone and ground systems.

3.  **GPS Receivers:** Most drones include GPS receivers, which allow them to detect their specific location and assure correct navigation. GPS data is an essential component of drone network architecture, allowing for safe and efficient flying.

4.  **Telemetry Systems:** Telemetry systems send real-time data about the drone’s status to the ground control station, including altitude, speed, battery level, and sensor readings. This information is critical for tracking and managing the drone’s performance.

5.  **Remote Controllers:** Operators utilise remote controllers or transmitters to convey control commands to the drone. These controllers communicate with the drone via RF or Wi-Fi connectivity.

These numerous components compose the network backbone of drones, allowing them to perform efficiently in a variety of applications. The infrastructural elements chosen are determined by considerations such as the drone’s function, range requirements, operational environment, and regulatory restraints. Understanding this infrastructure is critical for understanding the security issues and potential vulnerabilities that may be addressed in the context of drone operations.

Drones are susceptible to a variety of hostile assaults, such as data eavesdropping, GPS spoofing, radio frequency jamming, and unauthorised access. This emphasises the urgent need for extensive security measures to safeguard both the drones and the private information they carry. Drone network architecture often incorporates security features including encryption, authentication, intrusion detection systems, and network monitoring to prevent unauthorised access and data eavesdropping.

### 4.1.2 Adversarial attacks

A wide variety of algorithms have developed in the complex field of machine learning (ML) security, each one specifically designed to look for and take advantage of flaws in models. These algorithms primarily focus on the exciting field of adversarial attacks, which explores and manipulates the very underpinnings of ML models.

1.  **L-BFGS Algorithm:** The L-BFGS method is an aggressive optimisation approach. It is a white-box attack approach. L-BFGS generates adversarial samples by repeatedly improving perturbations on input data in order to optimise the model’s loss. This approach is successful, although it is computationally demanding.

2.  **Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM):** FGSM is a white-box attack algorithm that is both quick and efficient. It modifies the input data by performing a single step in the direction of the loss function’s gradient with respect to the input. Although FGSM produces adversarial cases fast, it does not always achieve high success rates.
