

## ORIGINAL ARTICLE

## Machine Learning Model for Atherosclerosis Evaluation and Cardiovascular Risk Prediction Based on Coronary CT Angiography-Analysis From the CREATION Registry

Ying Song, MD*; Na Xu, MD*; Jianan Zheng, MD; Sida Jia, MD; Cheng Cui, MD; Yin Zhang, MD; Lijian Gao, MD; Zhan Gao, MD; Jue Chen, MD; Lei Song, MD; Jinqing Yuan, MD; Bin Lu, MD*; Hou Zhi-hui, MD†

### BACKGROUND

Current ASCVD risk prediction tools based on traditional risk factors and the coronary artery calcium score have limitations.

### METHODS

The CREATION study includes suspected coronary artery disease patients who underwent coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA) at Fuwai Hospital between 2016 and 2019. The primary outcome was major adverse cardiac events defined as a composite end point of all-cause death, acute myocardial infarction, coronary revascularization, or stroke. Six machine learning survival models were used to create an ASCVD prediction model.

### RESULTS

Overall, 8431 participants with analyzable CCTA data were included with a median follow-up of 3.68 years, and 319 major adverse cardiac events (3.8%) occurred (mean age, 54.73±10.21 years, 48.2% were male, 50.9% with symptomatic chest pain). Among 6 machine learning models trained with 48 CCTA parameters, XGBoost showed the best performance and was selected for model development. In the training cohort (n=5901, 70%), the XGBoost model significantly outperformed the clinical risk factors and coronary artery calcium score model (area under the curve, 0.903 versus 0.830; P<0.001). Testing cohort showed similar performance (area under the curve, 0.899 versus 0.753; P<0.001). The CCTA model demonstrates consistent predictive performance across gender (female or male), onset-age (early onset or late-onset), and symptom (asymptomatic or symptomatic) subgroup analysis. The final CCTA model included diameter stenosis, lipid plaque burden and volume, total plaque volume, high-risk plaque, and vessel volume as the most important features. Lipid plaque burden was most strongly associated with major adverse cardiac event (adjusted hazard ratio per 5% increase: 2.524 [95% CI, 2.157–2.996]; P<0.001). The incremental value of machine learning CCTA features was consistent across different time points throughout the 1- to 5-year follow-up period. The findings remained unchanged when restricted to a secondary composite end point (death, myocardial infarction, or stroke).

### CONCLUSIONS

The machine learning model incorporating CCTA plaque quantification, characterization, and stenosis assessment significantly enhanced the predictive capacity for major adverse cardiac events. It provides direct visualization of coronary atherosclerosis and outperforms the traditional risk factors and the coronary artery calcium score model recommended in clinical practice.

## CLINICAL PERSPECTIVE

Among 6 machine learning survival models trained with 48 coronary computed tomography angiography parameters, XGBoost showed the best performance and was selected for model development. Based on the XGBoost algorithm, a coronary computed tomography angiography machine learning model integrating 6 key imaging features—including diameter stenosis, lipid plaque burden and volume, total plaque volume, high-risk plaque and vessel volume—demonstrated superior predictive performance for major adverse cardiovascular events compared with models incorporating traditional clinical risk factors and coronary artery calcium score across multiple time points during 1- to 5-year follow-up. This multidimensional model offers individualized, precise, and interpretable risk assessment, enabling early identification of patients with high-risk and guiding personalized prevention and intervention strategies.

Recent studies indicate that various novel drug therapies, such as PCSK9 monoclonal antibody inhibitor,<sup>1,2</sup> inclisiran,<sup>3</sup> icosapent ethyl,<sup>4,5</sup> GLP1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) receptor agonists,<sup>6</sup> and colchicine,<sup>7,8</sup> effectively reduce ASCVD risk by altering coronary plaque phenotype and composition. Nevertheless, these treatments are relatively costly. Therefore, accurately identifying high-risk populations, optimizing clinical decision-making, maximizing cardiovascular benefits, and improving health care economic efficiency are critical issues to be solved.

Current ASCVD prediction guidelines mainly rely on clinical risk scores and the coronary artery calcium score (CACS).<sup>9</sup> Risk algorithms including Systemic Coronary Risk Evaluation 2 system<sup>10</sup> and the Second Manifestations of Arterial disease 2,<sup>11</sup> Pooled Cohort Equations,<sup>12</sup> and the Framingham Risk Score<sup>13</sup> are based on traditional cardiovascular risk factors, have limited value in predicting ASCVD risk due to population-level differences, especially for Pooled Cohort Equations often overestimate risk in high-risk individuals and European populations even after recalibration, and cannot predict the complex pathophysiological processes of coronary atherosclerosis at individual-level.<sup>14-16</sup> The CACS is a noninvasive CT scan used to measure calcium deposits within coronary artery plaques. However, CACS does not provide information regarding noncalcified plaques or the extent of coronary stenosis.<sup>17</sup> Given the considerable heterogeneity in the prevalence and management of ASCVD, there is an urgent need for additional biomarkers or imaging indicators to enhance the prediction of future cardiovascular events.

Despite practical barriers to the widespread clinical adoption of coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA), such as relatively high cost, radiation exposure, and the risk of contrast-induced nephropathy, it remains a widely recognized noninvasive imaging modality. Beyond assessing the severity and extent of coronary artery stenosis, CCTA enables whole-heart visualization of adverse plaque characteristics, plaque distribution, and precise plaque quantification.<sup>18</sup> Recent studies have shown that plaque morphology and plaque quantification metrics are important risk predictors for cardiovascular events and hold great potential in improving ASCVD risk stratification.<sup>19,20</sup>

Deep learning, a subset of artificial intelligence, has experienced substantial advancements with sophisticated algorithms emerging, including the DeepHit neural network. Additionally, XGBoost, an optimized implementation of gradient-boosted decision trees, has also made significant contributions to artificial intelligence applications. These advanced machine learning (ML) algorithms enable the integration of complex, multidimensional, and interactive data sets derived from CTA, which are challenging to analyze using traditional statistical methods. In particular, models like DeepHit are designed to capture time-to-event dynamics, while tree-based methods like XGBoost can flexibly incorporate engineered temporal features. At present, a CCTA-derived risk ML model that integrates coronary stenosis, plaque qualitative, and quantitative analysis across multiple dimensions has not yet been established. Integrating CCTA with advanced ML techniques to develop an interpretable, more accurate, personalized, and dynamic cardiovascular risk prediction model will help achieve better cardiovascular risk stratification and prediction, thereby promoting the clinical application and translational value of CTA imaging.
