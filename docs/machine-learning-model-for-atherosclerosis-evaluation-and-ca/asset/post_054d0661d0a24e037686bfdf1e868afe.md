## METHODS

## Data Availability

Due to ethical restrictions related to the consent given by subjects at the time of study commencement, our data sets are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request after permission from the institutional review board of Fuwai Hospital.

## Study Cohorts

This study is derived from the Coronary Atherosclerosis Progression and Prognosis Evaluation by Coronary CT Angiography multicenter prospective cohort (CREATION, URL: https://www.clinicaltrials.gov; Unique Identifier: NCT03518437). Currently, complete and standardized coronary CT imaging data are available only from the Fuwai Hospital center, while imaging data from other participating centers remain under collection. This analysis includes 8873 patients with suspected coronary artery disease (CAD) who underwent CCTA at Fuwai Hospital between January 2016 and August 2019. Initially, all patients were referred for CCTA by their cardiologists. For the present study, we excluded subjects who have incomplete clinical information (n=33), nondiagnostic coronary images because of motion artifacts or inadequate contrast concentration on CCTA images (n=12), or have other heart diseases (eg, cardiomyopathy, n=25; valvular heart disease, n=12; congenital heart disease, n=39), and lost to follow-up patients (301 patients could not be contacted, 20 patients could not provide accurate outcome information). Eventually, 8431 patients were enrolled for final analysis (Figure S1). The study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki, all patients provided informed consent for inclusion in the registry, and institutional review board approval was obtained at each center.

## CCTA Scanning Protocols and Image Analysis

The detailed information regarding the computed tomography scanners used for the study population is available in the Supplemental Methods. The coronary CTA images were audited by the core laboratory for image analysis.

For plaque measurement, we adjusted the plaque threshold slider to separate the Hounsfield unit (HU) values that separate different plaque characteristics into lipid (<30 HU), fibrous (30–150 HU), calcified plaque (>350 HU), and lumen (350–350 HU). A color map overlay was displayed between the start and end of a plaque, highlighting lumen, calcified plaque, and noncalcified plaque in different colors determined by HU values. Adverse plaque characteristics include low attenuation plaque (pixel density ≤30 HU), positive remodeling (remodeling index ≥1.1), spotty calcification (average density >330 HU, with a diameter <3 mm in any direction), and the napkin-ring sign (a ring-like attenuation pattern with peripheral high attenuation and central low attenuation; legends detailed in Figure S2). High-risk plaque is defined as a lesion with ≥2 adverse plaque characteristics. Quantitative plaque characteristics include plaque volume (PV) and subtypes (in cubic millimeters), including total PV, noncalcified PV, calcified PV, fibrous PV, and lipid PV. Plaque burden (expressed as a percentage) is defined as the corresponding PV divided by the vessel volume, then multiplied by 100, including percent total PV, percent noncalcified PV, percent calcified PV, percent fibrous PV, and percent lipid PV. Total PV is calculated by summing the volumes of the 3 components, while noncalcified PV is calculated by summing the volumes of the fibrous and lipid components. For patients with normal coronary arteries, PV and burden are set to 0. The volumes of various plaque components were quantified using validated automated plaque software (Deep Blue, ADW4.6, GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, WI). Two radiologists worked together to determine the location and extent of the plaques and reached a consensus to derive the final results. There was high interobserver and intraobserver agreement in evaluating CCTA (k statistic: 0.97, 0.98, respectively).

## Clinical Data

A structured interview was conducted before CCTA to collect information on symptoms, demographic characteristics, and the presence of cardiovascular risk factors. Hypertension was defined as a previously established diagnosis, systolic blood pressure >140 mmHg, diastolic blood pressure >90 mmHg, or antihypertensive medication use. Hypercholesterolemia was defined according to the National Cholesterol Education Panel guidelines or by the current use of lipid-lowering medication. Diabetes was defined as a previously established diagnosis, insulin, or oral hypoglycemic therapy. Family history of CAD was defined as myocardial infarction, coronary revascularization, or sudden cardiac death for father <55 years old or mother <65 years old. Smoking was defined as any cigarette smoking within 1 year before the CCTA exam. Diabetes was defined as a fasting plasma glucose level of ≥126 mg/dL, glycated hemoglobin of ≥6.5%, or treatment with any antidiabetic drugs. Hyperlipidemia was defined as triglycerides of ≥200 mg/dL, total cholesterol of >240 mg/dL, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol of ≥160 mg/dL, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol <40 mg/dL, or treatment with any lipid-lowering medications.

## Outcome

The primary end point was major adverse cardiac events (MACE) obtained from patient telephone interviews, contact with the patients' physicians, and hospital records. A standard questionnaire was used during the telephone interview. MACE included any of the following: (1) all-cause death, (2) acute myocardial infarction (AMI), (3) coronary revascularization (percutaneous coronary intervention and coronary artery bypass graft), and (4) stroke. AMI was defined based on the criteria of typical chest pain, elevated cardiac enzyme levels, and typical alterations of the ECG. The decision to submit patients to revascularization was made by referring physicians, taking into account all available information and patient preferences. Revascularization procedures within 60 days after CCTA were considered to be triggered by the scan and were not considered events (physicians were likely to include CCTA findings in management decisions). When a patient experienced >1 MACE, the first event was chosen. When 2 MACE occurred simultaneously, the worst event was chosen (death>AMI>stroke>revascularization). An adjudication outcome panel of 2 physicians reviewed the patient data forms and verified by 2 review of medical records to determine whether a patient had MACE during the follow-up period.

The outcome panel was blinded to the findings of the CCTA. Disagreement was resolved by consensus, which included an additional senior cardiologist. If the telephone numbers were invalid and the patient could not be contacted, the patients were considered lost to follow-up. Three attempts were made using the patient's direct phone number in 1 week. If we were unsuccessful or the patient could not provide accurate information, the patients were considered lost to follow-up. The secondary end point was defined as the occurrence of death, AMI, and stroke, excluding all revascularization events.

The outcome of all prediction models is the time to MACE. Time zero is defined as the time point when the patient undergoes CCTA. All subsequent survival predictions are based on this time point.

## Feature Selection and Extraction

Potential variables were selected based on clinical experience, previous studies, and data availability. The final structured database containing 48 CCTA candidate predictive variables encompasses the presence, extent of coronary stenosis, plaque distribution across vessels, qualitative and quantitative plaque characteristics, including PV/burden and subtypes (calcified, noncalcified, fibrous, and lipid plaques; Table S1 for variable abbreviations and definitions). Multiclass nominal variables were processed using 1-hot encoding.

## Model Training and Optimization

The study outline of ML model creation and validation is shown in Figure S3. The database was randomly partitioned into a training set (70%) and a testing set (30%). An enhanced stratified data splitting strategy was implemented to maintain comparable distributions of event rates and other variables, such as time, across both data sets. Predictive models were built using the training set (70%, n=5901), and optimal features were selected. The models were internally validated with 5-fold cross-validation (Figure S4). The test set (30%, n=2530) was not used for model development but was used to evaluate the model's performance. The hyperparameters of the model were optimized using an R package called caret, combined with 5-fold cross-validation to maximize C-index (see the parameter space in Table S2).

This study employed 6 survival analysis methodologies: XGBoost, deep learning neural net, Cox Proportional Hazard, Random Survival Forests, Support Vector Machine for Survival Analysis, and Gradient Boosting Survival Analysis.23,24 The XGBoost survival prediction model is distinguished by its high computational efficiency and excellent predictive performance, as well as its robust regularization techniques and extensive hyperparameter tuning capabilities. The deep learning neural network is an advanced deep learning framework adept at processing time-series data and forecasting future events, making it particularly suitable for survival analysis. Model performance was evaluated using the Harrell concordance index (C-index). The final model is selected based on the C-index and time-dependent area under the curve (AUC), and its performance is evaluated in the testing set.

## Model Interpretation

Shapley additive explanation values were used to analyze feature importance and interrelationships, thereby optimizing feature selection. These values reflect the average contribution of each variable to the MACE risk (positive values indicate that the variable increases MACE risk). Recursive feature elimination was used to simplify the model until a reduction in model performance was observed.

In the training set, features with the highest mean absolute impact on model predictions were ranked and validated in the testing set. Additionally, the study provided 2 case examples from the test data set where MACE events were accurately predicted, offering a detailed explanation of each feature's individual contribution to the final prediction outcomes.

## Model Performance

We constructed 5 classification models: First, to evaluate the CCTA model against current clinical practice, we built a clinical risk model (model 1) using the same ML techniques as the CCTA model. Model 1 enrolled variables from well-established risk assessment scores, including sex, age, diabetes, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, smoking, obesity, family history of CAD, and non-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol levels. Model 2 was a hybrid model that combined clinical risk factors and CACS. Model 3 was the CCTA model formulated using the 6 parameters with the highest predictive value. Model 4 was a hybrid model that combined clinical risk factors, CACS, and CCTA parameters. Model 5 combined clinical risk factors, CACS, and CCTA parameters.

The model performance was evaluated in the testing cohort based on discrimination, calibration, and reclassification capabilities. Discrimination was assessed using the time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, AUC, and C-index, reflecting the model's capacity to distinguish between different categories (events and nonevents) over time. Calibration was quantitatively evaluated using the Brier score to measure the consistency between predicted probabilities and actual event outcomes. Reclassification capability was assessed using the net reclassification improvement (NRI >0), focusing on the improvement of individual risk classification. Specifically, we used time-dependent AUC to calculate the area under the ROC curve for prediction models annually over a 1- to 5-year period.

## Statistical Analysis

In the descriptive analysis, continuous variables with a normal distribution are presented as mean±SD, continuous variables with a skewed distribution are presented as median (interquartile range), and categorical variables are presented as number (percentage). The comparison of continuous variables with a normal distribution was conducted using the independent t test, continuous variables with skewed distribution were performed using the Mann-Whitney U test, and categorical variables were analyzed using the χ² test. The statistical significance between areas under the curve was determined using the DeLong method.

Exploratory subgroup analyses were performed for the primary outcome: first, by dividing the overall population into different gender groups (male and female); second, by analyzing groups with or without symptomatic chest pain. Typical angina was defined as having all of the following criteria: (1) substernal chest pain or discomfort that was (2) provoked by exertion or emotional stress and (3) relieved by rest and nitroglycerine.
