# CLINICAL PERSPECTIVE

Among 6 machine learning survival models trained with 48 coronary computed tomography angiography parameters, XGBoost showed the best performance and was selected for model development. Based on the XGBoost algorithm, a coronary computed tomography angiography machine learning model integrating 6 key imaging features—including diameter stenosis, lipid plaque burden and volume, total plaque volume, high-risk plaque and vessel volume—demonstrated superior predictive performance for major adverse cardiovascular events compared with models incorporating traditional clinical risk factors and coronary artery calcium score across multiple time points during 1- to 5-year follow-up. This multidimensional model offers individualized, precise, and interpretable risk assessment, enabling early identification of patients with high-risk and guiding personalized prevention and intervention strategies.

Recent studies indicate that various novel drug therapies, such as PCSK9 monoclonal antibody inhibitor,<sup>1,2</sup> inclisiran,<sup>3</sup> icosapent ethyl,<sup>4,5</sup> GLP1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) receptor agonists,<sup>6</sup> and colchicine,<sup>78</sup> effectively reduce ASCVD risk by altering coronary plaque phenotype and composition. Nevertheless, these treatments are relatively costly. Therefore, accurately identifying high-risk populations, optimizing clinical decision-making, maximizing cardiovascular benefits, and improving health care economic efficiency are critical issues to be solved.

Current ASCVD prediction guidelines mainly rely on clinical risk scores and the coronary artery calcium score (CACS).<sup>9</sup> Risk algorithms including Systemic Coronary Risk Evaluation 2 system<sup>10</sup> and the Second Manifestations of Arterial disease 2,<sup>11</sup> Pooled Cohort Equations,<sup>12</sup>

and the Framingham Risk Score<sup>13</sup> are based on traditional cardiovascular risk factors, have limited value in predicting ASCVD risk due to population-level differences, especially for Pooled Cohort Equation often overestimate risk in high-risk individuals and European populations even after recalibration, and cannot predict the complex pathophysiological processes of coronary atherosclerosis at individual-level.<sup>14-16</sup> The CACS is a noninvasive CT scan used to measure calcium deposits within coronary artery plaques. However, CACS does not provide information regarding noncalcified plaques or the extent of coronary stenosis.<sup>17</sup> Given the considerable heterogeneity in the prevalence and management of ASCVD, there is an urgent need for additional biomarkers or imaging indicators to enhance the prediction of future cardiovascular events.

Despite practical barriers to the widespread clinical adoption of coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA), such as relatively high cost, radiation exposure, and the risk of contrast-induced nephropathy, it remains a widely recognized noninvasive imaging modality. Beyond assessing the severity and extent of coronary artery stenosis, CCTA enables whole-heart visualization of adverse plaque characteristics, plaque distribution, and precise plaque quantification.<sup>18</sup> Recent studies have shown that plaque morphology and plaque quantification metrics are important risk predictors for cardiovascular events and hold great potential in improving ASCVD risk stratification.<sup>19,20</sup>

Deep learning, a subset of artificial intelligence, has experienced substantial advancements with sophisticated algorithms emerging, including the DeepHit neural network. Additionally, XGBoost, an optimized implementation of gradient-boosted decision trees, has also made significant contributions to artificial intelligence applications. These advanced machine learning (ML) algorithms enable the integration of complex, multidimensional, and interactive data sets derived from CTA, which are challenging to analyze using traditional statistical methods. In particular, models like DeepHit are designed to capture time-to-event dynamics, while tree-based methods like XGBoost can flexibly incorporate engineered temporal features. At present, a CCTA-derived risk ML model that integrates coronary stenosis, plaque qualitative, and quantitative analysis across multiple dimensions has not yet been established. Integrating CCTA with advanced ML techniques to develop an interpretable, more accurate, personalized, and dynamic cardiovascular risk prediction model will help achieve better cardiovascular risk stratification and prediction, thereby promoting the clinical application and translational value of CTA imaging.
