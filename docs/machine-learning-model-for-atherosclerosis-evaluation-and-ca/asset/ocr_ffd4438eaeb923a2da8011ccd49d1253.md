# Risk Assessment for Cardiovascular Events Based on Conventional Risk Factors and CACS

ASCVD risk assessment tools used in clinical practice predominantly focus on identifying population-level traditional risk factors, which pose challenges in reflecting the dynamic prevalence of disease and associated risk factors.27 CACS has substantial predictive value for cardiac events in both asymptomatic and symptomatic chest pain.28,29 Nevertheless, CACS is limited to quantifying calcified plaques within the coronary arteries and does not provide information regarding noncalcified plaques. A CAC score of zero does not equate to zero cardiovascular risk or the complete absence of atherosclerosis. Previous studies indicate that 5% to 16% of individuals with a CAC score of zero still have coronary plaques.30,31 The present study found that ≈5.9% of patients with a CAC score of zero similarly have coronary plaques. The CONFIRM study, involving 35 281 individuals with suspected CAD, demonstrated that an ML model incorporating clinical variables and CACS can improve the preoperative likelihood of detecting obstructive CAD.32

A meta-analysis that included 6 cohort studies from different countries revealed that CACS provides incremental discriminatory ability beyond traditional cardiovascular disease risk assessment equations in predicting future cardiovascular event risk, yielding an incremental C-statistic of 0.036. This increment was relatively consistent across studies.33 Consistent with previous studies, our study found that the clinical prediction model incorporating 9 well-validated traditional cardiovascular risk factors showed an AUC ranging from 0.650 to 0.788 across different subgroups. Adding CACS to the clinical model resulted in an incremental C-statistic to 0.033 to 0.085, without yielding a significant additional benefit. These findings further confirm the limitations of traditional cardiovascular risk factors in predicting cardiovascular events and highlight the modest incremental value of CACS over clinical risk factors.

# Additional Discriminatory Value of ML-CCTA During Follow-Up

Although previous literature has reported the predictive value of various CCTA variables, including anatomic stenosis severity, plaque phenotype, and plaque quantification analysis,34 however, the CONFIRM study primarily evaluated the feasibility of traditional ML models to combine clinical data with coronary stenosis severity.35 The subsequent CONFIRM2 study demonstrated artificial intelligence-derived quantitative plaque features were associated with a higher risk of MACE in women, suggesting the potential underestimation of cardiovascular risk in female patients and underscoring the need for more individualized interventions.36 In this study, we developed and validated a simplified novel model based on 6 key CCTA

features through deep learning methods, with clinical risk factors and CACS serving as reference models, demonstrating significantly enhanced performance in predicting cardiac events. The 6 features represent distinct dimensions, including absolute atherosclerotic total plaque burden, relative and absolute lipid-rich plaque burden, coronary stenosis severity, and plaque phenotype. These imaging-derived risk factors are consistent with previous studies. Analysis of the ICONIC study found that the presence of severe stenosis (≥70%), the volume of low-density noncalcified plaque, and the presence of high-risk plaque was higher in ACS cases.37 In analysis from the multicenter PROMISE trial in 4451 patients, high-risk plaque was associated with a 70% increased risk of MACE, independent of other risk factors or the presence of obstructive disease.38 Also using external validation, validated deep learning system SCOT-HEART, Lin et al99 found that diameter stenosis ≥50% and total PV above 238.5 mm3, were associated with a >3 to 5-fold increased risk of future myocardial infarction. But did not incorporate multiple ML survival models. It is noteworthy that the incremental predictability of the 6 CCTA features remains consistent across different time points throughout the 5-year follow-up period. Therefore, the CREATION study emphasizes the importance of incorporating a broader range of multidimensional CCTA variables that reflect the pathophysiology of coronary atherosclerosis into cardiovascular risk assessment in a real-world Asian population.

This study further identified the key predictors of ASCVD events and found that lipid plaque burden serves as the best imaging marker for assessing cardiovascular risk. Williams et al40 demonstrated that low-attenuation plaque burden >4% was nearly 5× more likely to have subsequent myocardial infarction in 1769 patients with stable chest pain undergoing CCTA in the SCOT-HEART trial, which is consistent with our findings. This highlights the critical role of plaque burden and phenotype in the pathogenesis of ASCVD events. Plaque rupture is associated with inflammatory plaques characterized by a thin fibrous cap and a large necrotic core.

In this study, most MACE events were revascularizations occurring after 60 days, which may be influenced by CCTA findings rather than spontaneous events, which could lead to overestimation of the model's predictive performance. To mitigate this intervention bias, we conducted a sensitivity analysis excluding all revascularizations. The findings remained unchanged when restricted to a secondary composite end point (death, myocardial infarction, and stroke), demonstrating strong robustness and applicability.
