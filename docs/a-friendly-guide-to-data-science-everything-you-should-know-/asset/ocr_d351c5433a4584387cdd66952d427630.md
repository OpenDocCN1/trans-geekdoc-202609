Figure 3-3 shows two normal distributions, the standard normal distribution and one overlaid on some real data that we know is normally distributed—the height of men (we used the fathers' heights from Galton's famous family height dataset). We left the Y-axis values off because they aren't really important for understanding the concepts of the normal distribution.

![](bbox=[0.078, 0.266, 0.852, 0.513])

**Figure 3-3.** Two normal distributions: the standard normal on the left and heights of men in a classic dataset with a normal curve overlaid on the right

The plot on the left is the standard normal distribution where μ (the mean) is 0 and σ (standard deviation) is 1. We can see the peak in the middle at 0 and the tails are not too far off, fitting with a standard deviation of 1. It's known that with a big enough sample size, the heights of adult men are normally distributed, so we plotted heights of the fathers from Galton's famous family height dataset. It's not a huge set and there are some deviations, but we can see it's mostly normal from the normal curve that's plotted over it in orange, scaled to this data.
