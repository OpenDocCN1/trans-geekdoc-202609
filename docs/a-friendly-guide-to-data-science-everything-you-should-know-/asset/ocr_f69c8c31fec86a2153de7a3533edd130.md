This algorithm starts on the right, with each individual flower data point in its own cluster. Its class is identified by color. The algorithm starts grouping them, two at a time, and moving up to generate different levels of the tree. You can see where the color changes that many of the versicolor starts getting grouped together and ultimately end up in a cluster with the virginica. But what you can do is pick where you want to cut the tree to see what the clusters are. Let's say you cut it at 3 on the X-axis. Imagine a vertical line going up from there, and it would cross four lines, each of which represents a distinct cluster. You can look at how the flowers are grouped at that point. Everything to the right under that line is in that cluster, so you can see two clusters splitting the virginica flowers, and one for the versicolor and setosa, with some in the wrong clusters.

# Association Rule Learning

Association rule learning is another class of unsupervised machine learning. You might have heard about the beer and diapers story, where men stop by the store after Friday to pick up diapers and snag a case of beer on their way to the register. This is apocryphal, but apparently it's not—a group did association rule mining at a drugstore and found that there was a strong coupling with beer and diapers, along with over 20 more pairs of products.
