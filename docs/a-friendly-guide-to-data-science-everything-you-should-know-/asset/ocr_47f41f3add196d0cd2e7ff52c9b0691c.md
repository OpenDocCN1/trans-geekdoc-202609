# CHAPTER 3 SETTING UP FOR SUCCESS: THE INFERENTIAL STATISTICS FRAMEWORK AND EXPERIMENTS

While working on the degree, Danny saw an opportunity and cofounded an agency focused on automating brand communication. But soon he realized this was a dead-end venture because large language models were becoming increasingly mainstream and available (and they’d make his business obsolete). He landed in a job at an advertising startup where he was thrown into the deep end and learned SQL and also met a bunch of old-school machine learning gurus who introduced him to the wider world of data science. That job was rewarding because he learned a lot and did some valuable work, but it was also time-consuming.

After he and his wife learned they had a baby on the way, Danny changed jobs and became a consultant. He often felt over his head in the beginning of several of his assignments, and he realized there was a lot for him to learn before he’d feel comfortable at the start of any new assignment. Specifically, he needed to better understand how to manage the full lifecycle of a project, from gathering requirements to doing a full implementation. He also found that stakeholders peppered him with fairly random questions every week data science, but there was rarely enough time with all the ad hoc queries. Everyone wanted again and again wanted more than his primary tools could provide. Still, his appetite for doing more advanced analytics grew. A lot of his time went to working on his master’s and learning the academic side, but he still found himself squeezing in some more business-focused and applied study when possible.

All that education combined with his work on a project that got him some accolades at a consulting gig helped him eventually turn into a permanent job at his current company, where he’s one of several data scientists on a dedicated data science team. Since then, his knowledge of data science and software design has grown, and he loves the work he’s doing.
