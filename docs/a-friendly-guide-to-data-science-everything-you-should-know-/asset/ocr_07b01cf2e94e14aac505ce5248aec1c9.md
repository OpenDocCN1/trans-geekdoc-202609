# ACTUARIAL SCIENCE

Actuarial science, which is simply the use of statistics and quantitative methods in insurance and similar fields to assess and manage financial risks and expected outcomes, can be thought of as an applied offshoot of traditional statistics that emerged while the modern field of statistics was still developing, but they've evolved somewhat separate fields. Actuarial science is a discipline that's most associated with insurance and finance, where rigorous techniques from math and other areas are used to assess risk. It first emerged as there was a need for people to handle large expenses down the road, and insurance was the solution. But knowing how much an individual needed to pay wasn't obvious, so they had to develop ways of quantifying risk by predicting future events (like death) and to handle the money being paid in.

A london named John Gra made the first life expectancy tables in the mid-seventeenth century (there's more on his work below), and the field of actuarial science grew from there. There were further developments in this vein, but it wasn't until the mid-eightteenth century that a life insurance company first set its rates based on modern calculations based on the anticipated entire life of the insured. These calculations were based on deterministic models but were still revolutionary in the industry.
