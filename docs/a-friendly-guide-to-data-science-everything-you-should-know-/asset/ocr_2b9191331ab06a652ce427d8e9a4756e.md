# CHAPTER 18 A NEW KIND OF STORYTELLING: DATA VISUALIZATION AND PRESENTATION

Every one of these audiences needs to know what the problem being solved is and how your solution helps the stakeholders do their jobs (steps #1 and #4, with a bit of #5 needed). For both business and technical leaders, keep both of these short and sweet, but on how the solution helps, do your best to quantify in terms of hours saved (reducing the time stakeholders spend accomplishing the original task) or a likely increase in sales or reduction in cost. Numbers are the language of leadership. Both groups will want a minimal amount of info from #2 and #3. Unless you've been told otherwise, keep #2 and #3 to a bare minimum. Don't leave it altogether, however, because some of them may have questions if you're presenting live. It can be good to put some more detail in an appendix for both a doc and a slide deck. Next steps can be good to share.

Your business stakeholders already know the problem, so you need to go into much detail on the problem for them, and depending on who the other business teams are, you may need to be detailed or quickly summarize it (if it's a problem they already have, they don't need much info, but if they work in a different area, they may want much more info). For both of these groups, “it depends” is the rule on steps #2 and #3. As mentioned above, some of these groups are also working with the data themselves, so they may want to understand about how you used it, in case that helps them. It can be good for you to spend some time on them, however, since this is the area that you are most likely to either have worked with your stakeholders or will need to work with future stakeholders (e.g., figuring out what the data means). So it's helpful to them to see that data prep is an important and time-consuming step. They're all going to be most interested in the solution, steps #4 and #5. This should be explained in detail to these audiences (even so for any other business teams that aren't familiar with your current stakeholders' work). Just like with leaders, make sure it's clear how much this benefits them in terms of time and quality. Both of these groups will also be interested in hearing about the next steps, and with some you may even want to dive deep into these if they are on the table for immediate work.
