Similarly, domains can also be specific to the company. I once worked on a project creating a system that processed Spanish text to assign a reading difficulty level, which was based on the company's existing system that did the same thing with English text. My colleague had worked on the English one and had great knowledge about how it worked. I hadn't worked on the English one, but it turned out that my intermediate knowledge of Spanish was very useful as we worked on the Spanish one. His domain knowledge on the English analyzer and my domain knowledge of Spanish meant we were the perfect team.

# Domain Experts and Subject Matter Experts

People who understand a domain really well are called domain experts. Being a domain expert does not require someone to know literally everything about an area, but does it mean they have a lot of knowledge and are consistently gaining more as they work in that area. Even a little of domain can be very valuable, like my knowledge of Spanish when I worked on a Spanish text analyzer.

For scientists, domain knowledge is also valuable because it means they are familiar with the types of data science work that is done in those domains. Forecasting sales in a retail store is not the same as forecasting fraud on credit cards, for instance. But even more important is that there are certain types of approaches in different areas that are done that wouldn't make sense at all others.
