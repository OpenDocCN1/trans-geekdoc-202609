Coders who have good math and stats knowledge can do machine learning, but it's not data science unless they understand their data at a deep level. It's common for people who've learned some coding and how to do machine learning to start trying to find interesting problems, but without having a deep knowledge of what the data represents, the work has limited value. Finally, someone's an expert in some domain and learns to code may think they know how to find things out, but without the rigor of math and stats, their results will be all over the place, which is why we label the "danger zone."

Although so many people think data science is magic and easy, people who are aware know the secret: data science is hard, and for two main reasons—first, data is messy, and second, defining the exact problem to be solved is difficult. Data is both data scientists' bread and butter and the bane of their existences.

# Data Science Values

Data science isn't a monolith, and every project is a little different from others, but there are still a lot of commonalities that require certain ways of working to be most effective. There are six core values that we see in good data science: simplicity, explainability, reproducibility, trustworthiness, understandability, and shareability.

Simplicity comes into play when you're looking for a solution—you start with the simplest ones that might work and go with more complicated choices when aren't working. Simplicity sometimes gets a bad rap, but it's actually valued in almost all science and engineering fields. If you remember that it's the opposite of complexity, you might see the advantage.
