Sometimes the Poisson distribution seems to be the right one, but when an event becomes or less likely over time, the Weibull distribution is the better option, as it models the amount of time that will pass before a particular event happens, also over time or space like Poisson. A classic example is tracking the failure of a mechanical device. Intuitively we know that the longer a device is used, the more likely it is to fail because of the continued wear and tear. The Weibull distribution can basically tell us how likely a device is to last a specified amount of time, and it requires two parameters. The first is referred to as the shape parameter and noted with β (beta), which represents how quickly the probability of failure changes over time. If it's greater than one, then the probability increases over time, and if it's negative, it decreases over time. The second parameter is the expected life of the device, noted with η (eta). One limitation to Weibull is that β itself needs to be constant over time.

# Central Limit Theorem

The central limit theorem is another concept that deals with sample statistics, where you're calculating the mean of many samples from the same population. The central limit theorem says that if you take the mean (or another statistic) of multiple samples from the same population under appropriate conditions, the distribution of those means will approach a normal distribution as the sample size (the number of means of samples taken) becomes large enough.

This is true regardless of the distribution of the population samples are taken from and the statistics being measured.
