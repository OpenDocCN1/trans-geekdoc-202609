# Trying Not to Make a Mess: Data Collection and Storage

# Introduction

Two more aspects of data that are important, even if they’re not given much thought, are data collection and data storage. Who gets the data and how do they get it? And then where do they keep it so it’s accessible? The ancient Egyptians and Sumerians sent officials out into their territory and recorded and stored their data on clay tablets. Medieval tax collectors did the same but used ink on parchment or paper.

If you’re reading this, you obviously know that there’s a lot more data collection happening now than at someone riding a horse from house to house and writing some numbers down. Also, you know data must be on computer somewhere for anyone who wants to do data science. But saying you’re storing data “on the computer” is not specific enough—there are many ways to store data digitally. We’ll look at some of the ways it is stored and how it’s collected.
