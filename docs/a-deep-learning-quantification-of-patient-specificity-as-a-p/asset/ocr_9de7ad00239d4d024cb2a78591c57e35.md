# 3. Results

# 3.1. Sample and characteristics

Data were gathered from 65,003 participants (n = 47,308 male, n = 241 excluded) from 78 IAPT sites across the UK. Participants completed an average of 5.64 (SD = 3.57; range: 1–99) sessions. Primary specificity was common across tasks (Table 1). Mean age of participants was 34.69 years (SD = 12.35, 18–94 years). Mean pre-treatment PHQ-9 scores (M = 13.15, SD = 5.90) were in the ‘moderately severe’ range (Roekeeke et al., 2001).

There are two keys that must be considered. First, this is observational data and cannot be inferred. Our goal was to identify factors of successful CBT, to guide future therapy development. Therapy specificity (associated with improvement) could be influenced by other factors (e.g., quality of CBT). Similarly, the use of change treatment services that were introduced could influence treatment specificity. Various therapeutic components, such as behavioural activation, planned number of sessions linked to randomised allocation and the impact of specificity on observed effects were seen. However, small effects were not fully demystified (Duner and Ozkins, 2013). The typical effect size of CBT (versus placebo) on depressive symptoms was not reported.
