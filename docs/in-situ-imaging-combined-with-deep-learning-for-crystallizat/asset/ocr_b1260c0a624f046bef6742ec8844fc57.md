We take the production of cephalexin (CEX, a beta-lactam antibiotic with a large annual production rate of >5,000 tons per year31) as an example where a byproduct of the reaction may crystallize and contaminate the product in the event of deviation from the process set point.32

# 2. MATERIALS AND METHODS

# Crystallization Experiments.

Industrial processes for cephalexin production typically use an enzymatic reaction between an activated acyl donor such as phenylglycine methyl ester (PGME) and the beta-lactam nucleus 7-aminodesacetoxy-cephalosporanic acid (7-ADCA).31,32 The biocatalyst used in this process is typically penicillin G acylase (PGA), which catalyzes the primary reaction of producing cephalexin as well as two hydrolysis side reactions shown in eq 1. In the reactive crystallization process, conditions are set such that cephalexin is crystallized in the reactor to produce CEX crystals, which protects the cephalexin from biocatalytic hydrolysis.32 However, the byproduct phenylglycine (PG) has a low solubility in water (~30–35 mM at 25 °C and pH = 5.5–7.533) and may crystallize and contaminate the product if generated in excess.

```
PGME + 7-ADCA
  → MeOH + CEX
  → CEX·H₂O (crystal)

PGME + H₂O → MeOH + PG → PG (crystal)

CEX + H₂O → 7-ADCA + PG → PG (crystal)    (1)
```

For the production of CEX crystals, pH manipulations were used to generate the required supersaturation (solubility of cephalexin in water as a function of pH value is presented in Figure S1 in Supporting Information). A typical crystallization experiment started with dissolving a sufficient amount of CEX solids (RIA, Hanover, NJ) in 200 mL of deionized water at pH 7.5 to prepare a saturated solution. After saturation, the resulting solution was filtered to remove any undissolved solids. The solution was then transferred to a jacketed crystallization vessel that maintained a temperature of 25 °C and was mixed using a pitched blade stainless-steel impeller (with four 45° blades and 1 cm off-bottom clearance) at 250 rpm. Crystallization was started by adding 0.05 g of ground seed crystals and adding 2 M HCl to acidify the solution and generate supersaturation. The pH value was lowered from the initial 7.5–5.5 over a period of 2–3 h using a Mettler-Toledo SP-50 dosing unit. The slurry was mixed for 3–5 h after the pH manipulation and until no significant change in the solution turbidity (measured using the EasyViewer probe) was detected. Figure 2 shows the course of a typical CEX crystallization experiment where pH and turbidity values are tracked.

PG crystals were produced in two sets of experiments. In the first, PG was crystallized using the same pH swing method described above; initial and final pH values ranged from pHinitial of 9 to pHfinal of 5 (PG solubility in water as a function of pH is provided in Figure S1 in Supporting Information). The crystals were filtered, dried at room temperature, and later sieved to remove large agglomerates (particles >300 μm).
