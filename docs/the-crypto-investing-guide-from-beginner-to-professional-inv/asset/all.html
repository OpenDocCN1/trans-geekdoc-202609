What is cryptocurrency?
Cryptocurrencies take their name from the encryption techniques that secure them, making them secure against many forms of fraud that are commonly used with traditional currencies. The security that they offer has made them extremely attractive to investors and encouraged people to buy them in recent years.
A cryptocurrency is essentially a virtual form of currency that occurs as a digital asset. It is proofed against counterfeiting or double-spending due to the cryptography that secures it. This makes it much more secure than traditional forms of currency that can easily be counterfeited with the right equipment, especially as criminals have become increasingly more sophisticated with the techniques they employ.
The vast majority of cryptocurrencies are decentralised networks that are based on blockchain technology. This means that they are enforced by a distinct group of computers that facilitates the existence of these cryptocurrencies outside of any central system of government or authority.
Cryptocurrency is an umbrella term for a variety of different currencies which includes an array of varieties including:
  • Bitcoin
  • Tether
  • Ethereum
  • Ripple
  • Dogecoin

  • Binance Coin

While these are some of the most popular forms, there are newer cryptocurrencies around that will likely increase in popularity over the forthcoming years. One of the most attractive points about cryptocurrencies to investors is the constantly evolving market which allows a range of choices and new opportunities.
In 2021, the most traded cryptocurrency was Tether, barely edging out Bitcoin to take the top spot. Tether was worth more than 150 billion US dollars in 2021, based on 24-hour volume. Tether and Bitcoin were the only two cryptocurrencies to reach a volume that exceeded more than 100 billion US dollars in 2020 and 2021, making them the market forerunners. These are consistently the two most traded virtual currencies on the cryptocurrency market.
However, when it comes to the value of a single coin, there are no current cryptocurrencies that can rival Bitcoin. Furthermore, this demonstrates the need for new investors to be well-versed in the market and have the awareness and knowledge to make predictions as to coming trends which facilitates successful investment strategies.
Records of ownership of various cryptocurrencies are kept on digital databases and are highly secured by cryptography. In laymen’s terms, computer coding is utilised to keep any information held on the databases secure and stop them from being hacked or infiltrated for nefarious means.
Cryptocurrencies, such as Bitcoin, are mined using computerised means. Essentially, this involves solving certain extremely complicated mathematical equations that take a lot of digital power to achieve. Most investors opt to stake currencies rather than mine them. For one thing, staking is much less time-consuming than mining. To mine currencies, you will need advanced hardware and software. How does investment in cryptocurrencies work?
Investing in cryptocurrencies works a lot like investing in the stock market or Forex. From the outside, it can appear to be extremely simple, especially if you employ the tried and tested plan of buying low and selling high. However, the nuances are much more complex than that and it is important to truly understand the specifics of cryptocurrency investment to stand a good chance of making profits.
Not all investors buy cryptocurrency. Some investors are simply traders who speculate on the price of the various cryptocurrencies without actually having ownership of any of them. To do this, you can use trading instruments that are known as cryptocurrency CFDs. With trading, you can trade the assets through a third-party broker without accessing the more advanced mechanisms of the currency exchanges.
There are pros and cons to trading over investing. Firstly, with trading, you do not have to pay in full for the assets but can instead sink a small percentage of your capital into the currency thereby gaining a leveraged position. Additionally, you will not be liable for any capital gains tax when you trade whereas you are liable for taxes if you buy and sell the cryptos instead. However, it is important to be wary of trading because it can widen the scope for exceptional losses in cases where it goes wrong. Trading can be more volatile than investing and although it is cheaper if you do not trade in the right way it can end up being more costly.
For anyone new to the cryptocurrency market, investment can be a wiser and safer way to make money. There are different investment strategies if you are looking to buy and sell cryptocurrencies and make a good return on cryptocurrency investment. You can employ one or more of these strategies in your routine or see which one works for you by experimenting.

As you see the prices start to rise, you can then sell the currencies when you believe they have reached their high point. One of the benefits of this strategy is that it does not matter if the prices fall because you have made a longer-term commitment for your investment and so it can be a lower stress plan. It provides scope for small and larger returns on your initial financial investment and allows you to get a good feel for the cryptocurrency market. For beginners, this is almost certainly the best way to get started.
Another method that many investors use is called shorting and this can allow you to make a substantial profit. It works by borrowing a cryptocurrency from a lender at a specific price. You can then sell the currency, assuming that the price of the cryptocurrency will subsequently fall. If this works and the price does fall, you can purchase the currency again at the new, lower price before you return the currency to the original lender. However, there are pitfalls to doing this.
Cryptocurrency markets can be volatile and have wild fluctuations within the space of a few weeks. If you bet wrongly that the price will fall, then you could end up paying more to repurchase the stake than you sold it for. On the other hand, there is a lot of room for profit if the prices of the currency do fall. Therefore, it is a good idea to get some experience in the cryptocurrency market before you employ this type of investing. The better the feel you have for the climate, the more likely you are to invest in the right currencies and make a profit rather than endure any losses.
One of the riskier strategies involved in cryptocurrency trading involves correctly timing the market to benefit from any short-term fluctuations in price. This means that you would trade the currencies frequently, rather than waiting and riding out any rises or falls in the market. You may get lucky with the timings but there is a substantial risk of losing significant amounts of money if you employ this manner of investing.
For newer investors and even for seasoned investors, it is much better to stick with the first strategy and dabble in the second if you become confident. While the third strategy has the potential to offer huge returns, it has an even bigger potential to leave you with extreme losses and become disheartened with cryptocurrency investment.

What is Blockchain and how do blockchain mechanisms function?
Blockchain is not as complex as many people perceive it to be. Essentially blockchain is a database that is designed to store information. Blockchain has its origins in the early 1990s when two researchers named Stuart Haber and W. Scott Stornetta sought to devise a system that was resistant to timestamp tampering. For many years the system remained idle until the advent of Bitcoin in 2009. From that point on, blockchain became the gold standard form of a database for cryptocurrencies to be built on as it is an inherently high-security system.
The blockchain database itself is structured into blocks so that the information and records that are stored within it can be searched or filtered. It can easily be accessed and amended by a high volume of users at a single time and is specifically designed to hold large amounts of recorded information. The data is housed on extremely powerful servers that facilitate the hefty volumes of records that it needs to cope with.
However, blockchain is different to traditional types of databases in the way that it stores the information on its systems. The data is stored on the database network in blocks that are then linked together in chains. These chains of blocks are what gives blockchain its name. All blocks are timestamped as they get added to the chain of information.
When new data comes in, this data is entered into an entirely new block and subsequently, that specific block is filled up with data. Once that has been achieved, a chain is created between this block and the previous one so they are linked together chronologically. Generally, blockchains are used to store transactional data, although they can be utilised for other uses too.
The majority of cryptocurrencies use decentralised blockchains that are not controlled by any specific authority such as a particular financial institution, government, group or individual. Collective control is retained by all network users which is a beneficial way for investors to conduct their transactions.
Due to their decentralised nature, blockchains remain immutable so the data that is entered cannot be changed or erased. Therefore, the transactions have a permanent record on the blockchain and they can be viewed by anyone who wants to access the records. Of course, some cryptocurrencies allow a higher level of privacy and provide the option for the records to be hidden so that a higher degree of anonymity can be attained.
Blockchain operates on mechanisms that make it resistant to tampering. If someone tried to tamper with a blockchain database, the cross-referencing between one block and all the other blocks in the chain would identify where the incorrect information is being stored. Subsequently, this would highlight the attempt to tamper with the system and prevent the records from being altered. While the hackers that attempt to tamper with the system can stay hidden behind a veil of anonymity, the actual transactions that they have attempted to tamper with can easily be traced.
So, for example, if someone suffered a heavy loss of their assets and then attempted to hack their personal block to amend this their hacking attempt may not be traceable but the transactions and coins that are linked to the specific block could be traced back to the individual that held them. In other cases, someone may try to hack the system to steal someone else’s coins. Again, the hacker could remain anonymous but the coins can be traced via their subsequent transactions so the hacker could be apprehended.
Security is one of the paramount features of blockchain because the blocks are stored in a chronological and linear order making it almost impossible to alter previous records. All blocks are listed and timestamped so there is a clear and transparent transaction record. Changing a record would be virtually impossible without an incredible amount of computing power, resources, money and intelligence that would allow them to amend more than half of all the blocks at the same time. The blockchain network is extremely vast for almost every cryptocurrency and trying to achieve this level of hack would not garner any profits.
There are significant advantages and disadvantages to using blockchain. Some of the pros of using blockchain include:
  • Reductions in cost as the transactions do not need to be verified by an independent third party.
  • More accuracy in database records as there is no scope for human error due to the automated nature of blockchain.
  • Tamper resistance due to its decentralisation. Therefore, if someone attempted to hack the network, they would be unable to access the entire blockchain and would only be able to hack a small section of it. This keeps the overall networks significantly more secure.
  • High levels of security and privacy for transactions.
  • Exceptional efficiency and transparency allow transactions to be completed in a matter of minutes rather than hours or days. When trading across international borders, this can be especially advantageous as traditional methods of transferring funds can be extremely slow when there are disparities in time zones.
  • The ability to be used globally regardless of the social, political or financial climate of any specific country, including in less developed or unstable nations.
However, while there are many advantages to using the blockchain network, there are also some drawbacks too. For example, it is unregulated and can only conduct a small number of transactions per second which can make it slow. It can also have negative impacts on the environment as it is essentially powered by the effects of Bitcoin and altcoin miners who use high levels of power to mine the coins.
Nevertheless, despite any drawbacks, blockchain has become the standard foundation for the majority of cryptocurrency operations. In real-world use, it is also expanding to other applications including voting, traditional banking, supply chains, smart contracts, property records and healthcare uses.
Blockchain will likely remain the standard way for recording and immortalising cryptocurrency transactions for the foreseeable future as there are simply no viable alternatives that could be applied on a widescale basis. Considering that its benefits outweigh any of its drawbacks, this allows it to be a useful and secure system for investors to put their confidence in.

Bitcoin is mined using technology and is a finite currency with a limit to how much can ultimately be mined. In many ways, this makes it worth its weight in gold. Unlike traditional currencies like Dollars, Pounds or Euros, Bitcoins have the same worth no matter which country you are in. The fluctuations in Bitcoins are global, rather than local or regional which can make them an attractive international investment.

Therefore, keeping ahead of the curve when it comes to any event that might drive an increase or decrease in prices is essential.
Ethereum
One of the world’s foremost cryptocurrencies is Ethereum and it is increasingly gaining popularity in the world of crypto investments. Ethereum is used in smart contracts so transactions can be carried out as long as specific conditions have been satisfied. Smart contracts have varying terms depending on the jurisdiction but they are enforceable by law in certain countries, such as England and Wales. A ruling was set out by the UK Law Commission in 2019 ensuring that they can now be enforced by law.
Ethereum is significantly newer to the market than Bitcoin, having only been released in 2015. Operating via blockchain technology, it is primarily used to facilitate smart contracts rather than as a currency to pay for goods and services. This sets it apart from Bitcoin and some of the other prominent cryptocurrencies and makes it extremely popular with investors. This is because it has a modicum of stability compared to some of the other cryptocurrencies. As its primary usage is not in buying goods, its value does not tend to rise and fall as wildly as some other cryptocurrencies.
In many ways, Ethereum bridges some of the gaps between traditional currencies and cryptocurrencies. Corporations are including Ethers in their transactional means and ensuring that the currency can record crypto-based transactions. One of the biggest draws to Ethereum is that all ownership is recorded via the blockchain network. Therefore, there is no potential for forgery or counterfeiting. This is extremely advantageous for companies meaning that there is a trickle-down effect for investors. While it can still be considered a high-risk investment, it provides a little more stability than Bitcoin and is an excellent way for new investors to get started in the market.
Litecoin
Created in 2011 by Charles Lee, Litecoin is a hard fork of Bitcoin. This means that this altcoin has been created due to some extreme changes to the protocol of the blockchain network concerning the validity of transactions.
It is an open-source network that facilitates global payments and shares some similarities with Bitcoin such as its decentralisation. However, it also differs in many ways too as it can generate blocks much more quickly than Bitcoin.
Over the years, since its creation, it has fluctuated wildly in value reaching the heights of being the third largest of all cryptocurrencies according to the market cap. Using peer-to-peer technology can facilitate worldwide payments.
The finite number of Litecoin coins that can be generated is significantly larger than the finite number of Bitcoins (84 million compared to Bitcoin’s 21 million.) Consequently, there is an ample supply of coins available. This can keep the prices slightly lower but makes it an easier investment for beginners. In 2021, Litecoin had a market value of US$13.1 billion.
Some of the main benefits of Litecoin include its swift transaction speed that ensures that transactions can be concluded rapidly without any detriments to the network’s security or integrity.
Litecoin offers security and longevity to investors which can be attractive features for more reticent investors and newbies. As one of only two cryptocurrencies that have never left the top ten highest value cryptocurrency projects (with the other being Bitcoin), Litecoin gives the potential for a lucrative investment project with lower risks than some other altcoins.

When it was initially designed, the intention was for one Tether to be worth US$1.
Like other stable coins, Tether provides an added dimension of security that most cryptocurrencies cannot because it is backed by an outside source. As a fiat-backed stable-coin, Tether has become extremely popular with investors, topping the tables when it comes to daily trading volume. Consequently, it is an extremely fast cryptocurrency in terms of the speed at which transactions can be performed.
Ripple also differs from many other cryptocurrencies as it cannot be mined. It is part of a centralised system in which all the coins are already in existence and belong to the creators until sold. Controlled by Ripple Lab, the Ripple currency is already in use by the Bank of America.
Ripple is an excellent option for new and veteran investors who are looking for a fast and simple cryptocurrency that offers the potential for medium to high returns on investments. It provides an affordable entry point for newer investors too.

For any investors who are concerned with high security and total anonymity, Dash is an excellent option. Although it is susceptible to the normal variations in the price of other cryptos, it offers a heavy degree of stability because it has already established itself firmly in the market. DAY SEVEN:
TIME IS OF THE ESSENCE
Knowing when to buy and sell your cryptocurrency is the crux of crypto investment. It can be a difficult part of the investment for newer investors to become accustomed to because it is something that is learned over time.
However, there are some strategies to negotiate the market that can give you the edge over other investors. Employing a variety of different strategies maximises the potential for you to become a highly successful investor. Your investment day is the first day of the rest of your life!
Firstly, do not wait too long! Waiting for Bitcoin, Ethereum or other prices to drop to an all-time low could be endless. There is no way to predict future prices, so waiting can merely mean that prices will continue to rise and you will miss out on your initial profits. Invest a small amount in the first instance and follow the progressive trends on the market. You may see rises or falls but the chances are that you will see some return on your investment. Timing the market exactly is almost impossible, so jumping in and starting your crypto investment portfolio means that you get to dip your toes in the water.
Buy when they sell and sell when they buy!
When other investors start to sell a cryptocurrency, this is a neon sign that you should buy it. As others offload the currency, the prices will naturally decline. This makes a currency ripe for purchase and allows you to get it at a much lower price than you would achieve when the market is stable.
Similarly, if you see that other investors are rapidly snapping up crypto-coins of a particular currency, this can be an excellent time to sell. When a currency is more in demand, the prices start to increase. Of course, they may cap out at a specific price but do not be tempted to sell too early. At the same time, do not wait too long. Selling at precisely the right time before there is a selling boom can get you the maximum value and secure the biggest return on your investment.
You can track the price of various coins on apps like Blockfolio, CoinCap and Coinfolio, allowing you to monitor the accumulation of cryptocurrencies. If a coin’s price has been slowly but steadily increasing for a prolonged time period, it can be a good investment that will continue to rise in the future.
Sell high and buy low!
Crypto markets regularly experience dips and troughs. This is an excellent time to buy. For example, if you buy one thousand units of a specific cryptocurrency for £0.10 and then sell it for £10 each one year later, you could make thousands of pounds worth of profits. From a small investment, you can experience an excellent return. You can then sink the money into more cryptocurrencies that are experiencing dips and repeat the cycle. Of course, you may not always experience these kinds of extreme highs and lows, but there are enough chances to invest in this way to give you substantial room for making money. It is important to remain patient, however. Buying or selling too early can hurt your profit margins.
Stay with it for the long haul!
Holding a currency for a prolonged period of time can be an extremely good way of making a profit. With this strategy, you do not need to worry as much about timing the market. Many cryptocurrency investors claim to have made significant sums of money by simply holding their currencies and waiting for them to accumulate. Holding your currency even during extreme dips can be profitable because there are inevitable rises on the horizon. In crypto terminology, investors who stick with a currency for the long-term, despite any sharp rises or falls are known as strong hands.
Only sell the profits!
One great way of playing the market is to retain your initial investment when cryptocurrencies experience an overall rise. For example, if you buy £1000 worth of Bitcoins and several months later your Bitcoins are worth £10,000, you would sell £9,000 worth of the coins and retain the rest. This is the equivalent of cashing out the interest on traditional stocks and shares whilst still keeping some of the shares as a part of your portfolio.
Once you have sold the profits, you can then use them to buy other cryptocurrencies that are falling in price or you can wait until your current cryptocurrency falls and then repurchase those same coins and wait for the cycle to repeat itself.
