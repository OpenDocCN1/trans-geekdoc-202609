## How does investment in cryptocurrencies work?

Investing in cryptocurrencies works a lot like investing in the stock market or Forex. From the outside, it can appear to be extremely simple, especially if you employ the tried and tested plan of buying low and selling high. However, the nuances are much more complex than that and it is important to truly understand the specifics of cryptocurrency investment to stand a good chance of making profits. Not all investors buy cryptocurrency. Some investors are simply traders who speculate on the price of the various cryptocurrencies without actually having ownership of any of them. To do this, you can use trading instruments that are known as cryptocurrency CFDs. With trading, you can trade the assets through a third-party broker without accessing the more advanced mechanisms of the currency exchanges. There are pros and cons to trading over investing. Firstly, with trading, you do not have to pay in full for the assets but can instead sink a small percentage of your capital into the currency thereby gaining a leveraged position. Additionally, you will not be liable for any capital gains tax when you trade whereas you are liable for taxes if you buy and sell the cryptos instead. However, it is important to be wary of trading because it can widen the scope for exceptional losses in cases where it goes wrong. Trading can be more volatile than investing and although it is cheaper if you do not trade in the right way it can end up being more costly. For anyone new to the cryptocurrency market, investment can be a wiser and safer way to make money. There are different investment strategies if you are looking to buy and sell cryptocurrencies and make a good return on cryptocurrency investment. You can employ one or more of these strategies in your routine or see which one works for you by experimenting. Firstly, you can try buying cryptocurrencies and holding them for a while. There are inevitably ups and downs in the market, as there are on any market. As you see the prices start to rise, you can then sell the currencies when you believe they have reached their high point. One of the benefits of this strategy is that it does not matter if the prices fall because you have made a longer-term commitment for your investment and so it can be a lower stress plan. It provides scope for small and larger returns on your initial financial investment and allows you to get a good feel for the cryptocurrency market. For beginners, this is almost certainly the best way to get started. Another method that many investors use is called shorting and this can allow you to make a substantial profit. It works by borrowing a cryptocurrency from a lender at a specific price. You can then sell the currency, assuming that the price of the cryptocurrency will subsequently fall. If this works and the price does fall, you can purchase the currency again at the new, lower price before you return the currency to the original lender. However, there are pitfalls to doing this. Cryptocurrency markets can be volatile and have wild fluctuations within the space of a few weeks. If you bet wrongly that the price will fall, then you could end up paying more to repurchase the stake than you sold it for. On the other hand, there is a lot of room for profit if the prices of the currency do fall. Therefore, it is a good idea to get some experience in the cryptocurrency market before you employ this type of investing. The better the feel you have for the climate, the more likely you are to invest in the right currencies and make a profit rather than endure any losses. One of the riskier strategies involved in cryptocurrency trading involves correctly timing the market to benefit from any short-term fluctuations in price. This means that you would trade the currencies frequently, rather than waiting and riding out any rises or falls in the market. You may get lucky with the timings but there is a substantial risk of losing significant amounts of money if you employ this manner of investing. For newer investors and even for seasoned investors, it is much better to stick with the first strategy and dabble in the second if you become confident. While the third strategy has the potential to offer huge returns, it has an even bigger potential to leave you with extreme losses and become disheartened with cryptocurrency investment. Whichever investment strategy you choose, when you buy cryptocurrency, you can invest in less than a single cryptocoin. For example, if a single Bitcoin is on the market for £50,000, you could buy one-tenth of a Bitcoin for £5,000. If you have a lower amount of liquid capital to invest, you can buy even smaller fractions of a single cryptocoin. This allows all investors to tailor the volume of their investments to their personal budgets and means that you do not have to sink high volumes of cash into your initial investments. An important takeaway when you start to invest in cryptocurrencies is that they cannot be held in an ISA. This means that any profits are eligible to be taxed and will need to be declared to the government. If you fail to declare any earnings, you may face penalties that can include fines and imprisonment. When you invest in cryptocurrency, it can be a lot of fun and a wild ride. As long as you do not invest more money than you have available, it is an extremely enjoyable way to try and make a profit. However, there are inevitable market crashes from time to time that can put your capital at risk. Another risk that you might discover is that this is an unregulated sector. Therefore, if a cryptocurrency or cryptocurrency exchange collapses, there is no recourse for recovering your lost financial assets. This highlights the importance of safe investment strategies and emphasises the need to stay on track and stick to a plan rather than getting carried away with large investments. Of course, always remember, fluctuations are a natural part of crypto investment!

## What is Blockchain and how do blockchain mechanisms function?

Blockchain is not as complex as many people perceive it to be. Essentially blockchain is a database that is designed to store information. Blockchain has its origins in the early 1990s when two researchers named Stuart Haber and W. Scott Stornetta sought to devise a system that was resistant to timestamp tampering. For many years the system remained idle until the advent of Bitcoin in 2009. From that point on, blockchain became the gold standard form of a database for cryptocurrencies to be built on as it is an inherently high-security system. The blockchain database itself is structured into blocks so that the information and records that are stored within it can be searched or filtered. It can easily be accessed and amended by a high volume of users at a single time and is specifically designed to hold large amounts of recorded information. The data is housed on extremely powerful servers that facilitate the hefty volumes of records that it needs to cope with. However, blockchain is different to traditional types of databases in the way that it stores the information on its systems. The data is stored on the database network in blocks that are then linked together in chains. These chains of blocks are what gives blockchain its name. All blocks are timestamped as they get added to the chain of information. When new data comes in, this data is entered into an entirely new block and subsequently, that specific block is filled up with data. Once that has been achieved, a chain is created between this block and the previous one so they are linked together chronologically. Generally, blockchains are used to store transactional data, although they can be utilised for other uses too. The majority of cryptocurrencies use decentralised blockchains that are not controlled by any specific authority such as a particular financial institution, government, group or individual. Collective control is retained by all network users which is a beneficial way for investors to conduct their transactions. Due to their decentralised nature, blockchains remain immutable so the data that is entered cannot be changed or erased. Therefore, the transactions have a permanent record on the blockchain and they can be viewed by anyone who wants to access the records. Of course, some cryptocurrencies allow a higher level of privacy and provide the option for the records to be hidden so that a higher degree of anonymity can be attained. Blockchain operates on mechanisms that make it resistant to tampering. If someone tried to tamper with a blockchain database, the cross-referencing between one block and all the other blocks in the chain would identify where the incorrect information is being stored. Subsequently, this would highlight the attempt to tamper with the system and prevent the records from being altered. While the hackers that attempt to tamper with the system can stay hidden behind a veil of anonymity, the actual transactions that they have attempted to tamper with can easily be traced. So, for example, if someone suffered a heavy loss of their assets and then attempted to hack their personal block to amend this their hacking attempt may not be traceable but the transactions and coins that are linked to the specific block could be traced back to the individual that held them. In other cases, someone may try to hack the system to steal someone else’s coins. Again, the hacker could remain anonymous but the coins can be traced via their subsequent transactions so the hacker could be apprehended. Security is one of the paramount features of blockchain because the blocks are stored in a chronological and linear order making it almost impossible to alter previous records. All blocks are listed and timestamped so there is a clear and transparent transaction record. Changing a record would be virtually impossible without an incredible amount of computing power, resources, money and intelligence that would allow them to amend more than half of all the blocks at the same time. The blockchain network is extremely vast for almost every cryptocurrency and trying to achieve this level of hack would not garner any profits.
