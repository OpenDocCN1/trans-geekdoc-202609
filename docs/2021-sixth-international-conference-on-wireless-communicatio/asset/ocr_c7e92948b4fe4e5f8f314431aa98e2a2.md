**Abstract**—With the IoT industry undergoing a boom, *Location Based Services* (LBSs) are playing an essential role in constructing smart cities. Since LBSs are in the process of being ubiquitous, it is essential to find a low-cost and low energy solution for localization. Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) technology for indoor localization is a smart choice for giving economical solutions to the industry with additional advantages like trouble-free connection to other gadgets. We propose an improved **RSSI** (*Received Signal Strength Indicator*) based fingerprinting technique in which data is first augmented and then classified using Machine Learning algorithms. This indoor localization technique facilitates us to recognize the **X-position** of the user node (UN) or tag, which receives signals from the anchor nodes (ANs). The fluctuations in the RSSI values were large, because they were affected by multipath propagation and other factors in the indoor positioning environment. The fingerprinting data was augmented to overcome these drawbacks, to decrease the computational cost, to guarantee the precision of the framework by increasing the accuracy and also to be equipped for Machine Learning calculation. The augmentation strategy was actualized by utilizing accessible RSSI estems at one location, where *Random Forest* had the highest test accuracy of 96% surpassing all existing methods.

*Index Terms*—Low-cost, Bluetooth Low Energy, Indoor Localization, Fingerprinting, Augmentation, Machine Learning

# I. INTRODUCTION

Localization has pulled in a great deal of research effort in the last decade due to the increasing use of location-based services (LBS). Wireless fingerprinting localization, specifically, has received a fair share of attention from the industry due to its high simplicity and compatibility with existing hardware [6]. There has been a rising need for indoor location information services in various industries. Some examples of implementation of indoor location information services are monitoring pedestrian flows at air terminals and stations, positions customers in stores and shopping malls and even monitoring the position of medicines in hospitals for easy access. The past decade has also bore fruit to some advances such as Wi-Fi, RFID and ZigBee. Though advances have been made in the location service industry, the localization task for indoor environments is rather complex with respect to outdoor cases. This can be attributed to the fact that in the former case there exist complex infrastructures and paths. In closed environments, the multipath effect assumes a pertinent and negative part. Most wireless signals face this sort of interference in structured environments, with numerous reflective and attenuating surfaces, such as walls and windows. Consequently, the indoor environment positioning demands undeniable levels of quality, which must be ensured by efficient exploitation of the hardware, high complexities, and extreme robustness [9]. To tackle these issues, fingerprinting method is needed to estimate indoor positions that need an estimation algorithm to guarantee accuracy of position. This technique is constructed from two phases, online and offline. During the offline phase the entire area of interest is split into a rectangular set of grid points. From each grid point the RSS is recorded from the Anchor Nodes (ANs) using the tag node. These signals are finally stored in a database system called the radio map. In the online phase, the cellphone collects the RSS from the ANs and sends it to the server to analyze the predefined fingerprint of the offline phase with the RSS in the online phase in order to assess the location on the grid map [10].

One such positioning system that can be employed is the BLE positioning system, which has become a research focus on account of its low energy consumption, low cost, high compatibility, and small sizes of BLE devices [6]. Since, nearly every smart device such as laptops, cellphones, tablets and smartwatches are enabled with a Bluetooth module there is no necessity of an extra gadget. Due to its simplicity, BLE systems depend on RSS based inputs, though there are many ways in which BLE can be implemented, such as AoA and ToF [7]. In the actual applications, the distance measurement that is dependent on RSSI is positioned by reflection, multipath, antenna gain, etc, and large positioning error is caused. In this way, the localization method based on fingerprint feature vector is utilized, where the location area will be isolated into grids, and the received RSSI values of all Wi-Fi/BLE sources in each grid will be gathered. This is followed with all the RSSI values being stored in a fingerprint database [6]. This BLE technique utilized 2.4 GHz unlicensed frequency (2402 to 2480 MHz) with 40 channels (2 MHz for each channel width).
