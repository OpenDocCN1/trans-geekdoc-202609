**Abstract**—With the IoT industry undergoing a boom, *Location Based Services* (LBSs) are playing an essential role in constructing smart cities. Since LBSs are in the process of being ubiquitous, it is essential to find a low-cost and low energy solution for localization. Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) technology for indoor localization is a smart choice for giving economical solutions to the industry with additional advantages like trouble-free connection to other gadgets. We propose an improved **RSSI** (*Received Signal Strength Indicator*) based fingerprinting technique in which data is first augmented and then classified using Machine Learning algorithms. This indoor localization technique facilitates us to recognize the **X-position** of the user node (UN) or tag, which receives signals from the anchor nodes (ANs). The fluctuations in the RSSI values were large, because they were affected by multipath propagation and other factors in the indoor positioning environment. The fingerprinting data was augmented to overcome these drawbacks, to decrease the computational cost, to guarantee the precision of the framework by increasing the accuracy and also to be equipped for Machine Learning calculation. The augmentation strategy was actualized by utilizing accessible RSSI estimates at one location, where *Random Forest* had the highest test accuracy of 96% surpassing all existing methods.

*Index Terms*—Low-cost, Bluetooth Low Energy, Indoor Localization, Fingerprinting, Augmentation, Machine Learning

## I. INTRODUCTION

Localization has pulled in a great deal of research effort in the last decade due to the increasing use of location-based services (LBS). Wireless fingerprinting localization, specifically, has received a fair share of attention from the industry due to its high simplicity and compatibility with existing hardware [6]. There has been a rising need for indoor location information services in various industries. Some examples of implementation of indoor location information services are monitoring pedestrian flows at air terminals and stations, positioning customers in stores and shopping malls and even monitoring the position of medicines in hospitals for easy access. The past decade has also borne fruit to some advances such as Wi-Fi, RFID and ZigBee. Though advances have been made in the location service industry, the localization task for indoor environments is rather complex with respect to outdoor cases. This can be attributed to the fact that in the former case there exist complex infrastructures and paths. In closed environments, the multipath effect assumes a pertinent and negative part. Most wireless signals face this sort of interference in structured environments, with numerous reflective and attenuating surfaces, such as walls and windows. Consequently, the indoor environment positioning demands undeniable levels of quality, which must be ensured by efficient exploitation of the hardware, high complexities, and extreme robustness [9]. To tackle these issues, fingerprinting method is needed to estimate indoor positions that need an estimation algorithm to guarantee accuracy of position. This technique is constructed from two phases, online and offline. During the offline phase the entire area of interest is split into a rectangular set of grid points. From each grid point the RSS is recorded from the Anchor Nodes (ANs) using the tag node. These signals are finally stored in a database system called the radio map. In the online phase, the cellphone collects the RSS from the ANs and sends it to the server to analyze the predefined fingerprint of the offline phase with the RSS in the online phase in order to assess the location on the grid map [10].

One such positioning system that can be employed is the BLE positioning system, which has become a research focus on account of its low energy consumption, low cost, high compatibility, and small sizes of BLE devices [6]. Since, nearly every smart device such as laptops, cellphones, tablets and smartwatches are enabled with a Bluetooth module there is no necessity of an extra gadget. Due to its simplicity, BLE systems depend on RSS based inputs, though there are many ways in which BLE can be implemented, such as AoA and ToF [7]. In the actual applications, the distance measurement that is dependent on RSSI is positioned by reflection, multipath, antenna gain, etc, and large positioning error is caused. In this way, the localization method based on fingerprint feature vector is utilized, where the location area will be isolated into grids, and the received RSSI values of all Wi-Fi/BLE sources in each grid will be gathered. This is followed with all the RSSI values being stored in a fingerprint database [6]. This BLE technique utilized 2.4 GHz unlicensed frequency (2402 to 2480 MHz) with 40 channels (2 MHz for each channel width). Following the experimental setup and the data collection, numerous algorithms have been employed for optimizing the accuracy of the framework such as, multilateration and fingerprinting. All things considered, there are not yet any optimized solutions for high accuracy using BLE technology [5]. The techniques employed to predict the estimated position of an object will influence the accuracy of the estimated real position of the same. There have been many algorithms that have been implemented to estimate the position of the object. Starting from traditional methods that use polynomial regression as a propagation model to using K-Nearest Neighbours and machine learning. With the traditional method, RSS received was processed using weighted sum to get the coordinates of the objects and using a polynomial regression model to get distance. Both were determined using RSS signals from 3 advertisement channels of each beacon. Then both results filtered using outlier detection to clean the outcome, by combining fingerprinting with polynomial regression model distance into combined distance. This filtered result will be handled using extended Kalman filtering utilizing filtered distance from first outlier detection. Results from extended Kalman filtering will be filtered again using outlier detection to remove false measurement. This result is then processed again with extended Kalman filtering into an estimated position that will be compared with the radio map to get the real position. This method is using distance-based measurement which gives high error and reduced accuracy due to the filter [5]. Later, researchers have implemented CNN architecture by processing the fingerprinting data into image data and using this image data to train the CNNs. This method however requires huge datasets and numbers ANs to feed to the CNN [4].

In this paper a new technique for RSSI based fingerprinting and an improved fingerprinting data augmentation technique has been proposed. This method is highly recommended when the ANs are minimal and not in large scale. To give highly accurate localization even with less data, the employed RSSI-based augmentation technique is utilized, which is robust, unique and simple to implement, which is dependent on only RSSI values collected at each grid of the location to produce augmented output. The RSSI value present at the target grid is arbitrarily selected and written in the new output. The robustness lies in the fact that the pattern of augmented output matches with the input RSSI pattern of the target grid. The following shows how the rest of the paper is organized. Section II describes the previous works done in this domain of study. The proposed localization technique utilizing the fingerprinting method is described in Section III, along with the description of the data and also the augmentation method. System model and the experimental setup are presented in Section IV. Finally, Section V concludes the paper.

## II. BACKGROUND

Indoor Localization has been a topic for research for quite a while now. Initially RSSI wireless signals were used in indoor positioning as reference data. However due to the wireless multipath influence from these signals, there exits large fluctuations in the values and these fluctuations cause large distances errors. To tackle the problem of high fluctuations and errors Aiguo Zhang et al. [6] proposed an RSSI-based fingerprint feature vector algorithm. In this algorithm, the location area is divided into a rectangular set of grids. Mobile devices were localized through the similarity matching between the real-time RSSI feature vector and RSSI fingerprint database feature vectors. Since this was highly dependent on the location, further work was required. LF however faces a disadvantage due to the prerequisite of having an updated fingerprint database. A non-parametric modelling method called Gaussian Processes (GP) was adopted to model the received signal strength indicator (RSSI) and create the updated fingerprint database. This method was dependent scarcely on the existence of training data. Perez-Cruz et al. [2,3], utilized a parametric pathloss model for the GP mean and a flexible non-parametric co-variance function, with which reliable estimates are conceivable with low fingerprinting effort. The author also explained various other pathloss models and their pros and cons. Hereafter, BLE came into the picture due to its low-cost services, high compatibility with existing softwares and small size. The improved results of employing BLE technology along with a few fundamental standards of a radio-based indoor localization technique were reviewed and documented by Pavel Kriz et al. [8]. The author executed a distributed system for collecting radio fingerprints by mobile devices with the Android operating system. The system returned a high error of 2.82m and hence had to be improved. A new system - BLE iBeacon-based indoor localization system that applies k-nearest neighbours algorithm was proposed by Ngoc-Son Duong et al. [11]. K-nearest neighbours (k-NN) is an ML that decides the user position based on training data and the nearest neighbouring data points for the same RSS values. Even so, the algorithm posed a disadvantage since the accuracy of the ML model was obscure. Following this the implementation of an Internet of Things (IoT)-based framework was achieved by Marco Terán et al. [9]. The design of the framework utilizes Wireless Networking Technologies along with a connection to Amazon Web Services cloud computing service. Lamentably, the maximum accuracy achieved by this framework was 75%. Osmah Ali Abdullah et al. [10] presented a new framework based on symmetric Bregman divergence, which incorporates kNN classification in signal space. The coordinates of the target resolved as a weighted combination of the closest fingerprints using Jensen-Bregman divergences, which bound together the squared Euclidean and Mahalanobis distances with information-theoretic Jensen-Shannon divergence measures. Yet, this technique was not celebrated even though it gave an accuracy of 90% as it had high complexity. Likewise et al.[4,5] use regression and CNNs respectively to estimate the location with exceptionally high accuracy values of 96.67% for the former. These techniques cannot be used when the anchor nodes are less than 150. Rashmi Sinha et al. [1] proposed an improved data augmentation technique dependent on RSSI values for fingerprint indoor positioning systems.
