
# Cryptocurrency Glossary

## Crypto-to-Crypto Trade
A transaction where one cryptocurrency is exchanged for another (e.g., trading Bitcoin for Ethereum). Crypto-to-crypto trades are considered taxable events in many jurisdictions, and the value must be calculated based on the fair market value of both assets at the time of the trade.

## Custodial Wallet
A type of cryptocurrency wallet where a third party (typically an exchange) holds and manages the private keys for the user. While more convenient, custodial wallets place control of the funds in the hands of the custodian.

## Non-Custodial Wallet
A wallet where the user controls their private keys, giving them full control over their cryptocurrency. Non-custodial wallets provide higher security but require users to manage their own private keys carefully.

## DeFi (Decentralized Finance)
A financial ecosystem built on blockchain technology that operates without intermediaries like banks. DeFi platforms offer financial services such as lending, borrowing, and trading through smart contracts, typically on Ethereum.

## Fair Market Value (FMV)
The price at which a cryptocurrency can be bought or sold in a free market at any given time. FMV is used to determine the value of cryptocurrency transactions, especially for tax and accounting purposes.

## FIFO (First In, First Out)
A valuation method used in accounting where the first cryptocurrencies purchased are the first to be sold or traded. FIFO is commonly used to calculate capital gains or losses for tax purposes.

## LIFO (Last In, First Out)
A valuation method where the most recently purchased cryptocurrencies are sold or traded first. LIFO may yield different tax results compared to FIFO, depending on the price fluctuations of the assets.

## Capital Gains
The profit realized when a cryptocurrency is sold or exchanged for a higher price than it was purchased. Short-term capital gains occur when the holding period is one year or less, while long-term capital gains apply to assets held for more than one year.

## Staking
The process of participating in the validation of transactions on a proof-of-stake (PoS) blockchain. In return for staking their crypto, users earn rewards in the form of additional cryptocurrency. These rewards are often considered taxable income.

## Mining
The process of verifying and adding cryptocurrency transactions to a blockchain, typically on a proof-of-work (PoW) network like Bitcoin. Miners are rewarded with newly created cryptocurrency, which is taxable income.

## Stablecoin
A type of cryptocurrency that is pegged to the value of a fiat currency (e.g., USD) or a commodity (e.g., gold). Stablecoins are designed to reduce the price volatility associated with other cryptocurrencies and are often used in trading and payments.

## Smart Contract
Self-executing contracts with the terms of the agreement directly written into code on the blockchain. Smart contracts automatically enforce the terms and conditions without the need for intermediaries, often used in DeFi applications.

## Intangible Asset
In accounting, an intangible asset is a non-physical asset, such as intellectual property or cryptocurrency, that has value. Cryptocurrencies are often classified as intangible assets on financial statements under GAAP and IFRS.

## Cost Basis
The original value of a cryptocurrency for tax purposes, usually the purchase price, plus any associated transaction fees. The cost basis is used to calculate capital gains or losses when the asset is sold or traded.

## Impairment
An accounting term referring to the reduction in the value of an asset when the market value drops below the cost basis. If the value of a cryptocurrency falls significantly, an impairment loss must be recognized on financial statements.

## Taxable Event
An event that triggers a tax obligation. In the context of cryptocurrencies, taxable events include selling crypto for fiat currency, trading one cryptocurrency for another, or using crypto to purchase goods and services.

## Anti-Money Laundering (AML)
Laws and regulations aimed at preventing the use of financial systems, including cryptocurrencies, for illegal activities such as money laundering and terrorism financing. Businesses handling crypto must implement AML protocols to comply with regulations.

## Know Your Customer (KYC)
A regulatory requirement for financial institutions and crypto exchanges to verify the identity of their users. KYC processes help prevent fraud, money laundering, and other illegal activities by ensuring that users are who they claim to be.

## Hot Wallet
A cryptocurrency wallet that is connected to the internet, allowing for quick and easy access to funds. Hot wallets are convenient for day-to-day transactions but are more vulnerable to hacking compared to cold wallets.

## Cold Wallet
A cryptocurrency wallet that is offline and not connected to the internet, making it more secure for long-term storage. Cold wallets, such as hardware wallets, are commonly used to store large amounts of cryptocurrency.

## Yield Farming
A DeFi strategy where users lend or provide liquidity to platforms in exchange for rewards, typically in the form of cryptocurrency. Yield farming can generate significant returns but comes with higher risk and complexity.

## Centralized Exchange (CEX)
A cryptocurrency exchange managed by a third party that facilitates the buying, selling, and trading of digital assets. CEXs often hold custody of user funds, making them more vulnerable to security breaches compared to decentralized exchanges.

## Decentralized Exchange (DEX)
A peer-to-peer marketplace where users can trade cryptocurrencies directly with one another using smart contracts, without relying on an intermediary. DEXs offer greater privacy and control but may lack the liquidity and ease of use provided by centralized exchanges.

## Decentralized Autonomous Organization (DAO)
An organization run by smart contracts on a blockchain, where decision-making is decentralized and governed by token holders. DAOs are used to manage DeFi platforms and other decentralized projects.

## Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC)
A digital version of a country’s fiat currency, issued and regulated by the central bank. CBDCs aim to provide a stable, government-backed digital currency for use in everyday transactions.

## Multi-Signature Wallet
A type of cryptocurrency wallet that requires multiple private keys to authorize a transaction. Multi-signature wallets are commonly used to enhance security by ensuring that no single party can move funds without approval from others.

## Weighted Average Cost
A valuation method where the cost of all purchased cryptocurrencies is averaged, and the resulting value is used to calculate capital gains or losses when the cryptocurrency is sold. This method is especially useful for businesses with high-frequency trades.

## ProveSource
A tool used to provide real-time notifications of purchases or activities, often used on e-commerce platforms. In crypto accounting, such tools are relevant for tracking activities and ensuring accurate reporting.
