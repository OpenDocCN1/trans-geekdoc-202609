# 12.3 HOW IS DIABETES DIAGNOSED

The World Health Organization (WHO) provides guidelines to diagnose diabetes as summarized in Figure 12.1. The BG value of <100 mg/dL is considered normal, BG values between 100 and 126 mg/dL are considered pre-diabetes, and BG values ≥126 mg/dL are confirmed as the development of diabetes. Alternately, ADA considers the level of HbA1c <5.7% as normal, 5.7% to 6.4% as pre-diabetes, and ≥6.5% as diabetes.

# 12.4 MANAGEMENT OF DIABETES

The proper management of diabetes refers to maintaining BG levels near normal by adjusting food intake, medication, or physical activity. In recent years, there has been much advancement in electronic health technology, which has proved to be a cost-effective method for monitoring health-related data. The advancement of e-health technology allows for a cohort of health-related data to be collected. For diabetes diagnosis and management, BG measurement plays a vital role in reducing diabetes-related health complications. Different laboratory tests such as oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) and HbA1c measures are used for diabetes management.
