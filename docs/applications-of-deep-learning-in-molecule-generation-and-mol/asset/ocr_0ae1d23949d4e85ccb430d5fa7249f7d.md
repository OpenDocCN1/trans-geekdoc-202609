# Biographies

W. Patrick Walters leads the Computation and Informatics group at Relay Therapeutics in Massachusetts. His group focuses on novel applications of computational methods that integrate simulations and experimental data to drive drug discovery. Prior to joining Relay, he spent more than 20 years at Vertex Pharmaceuticals, where he was Global Head of Modeling & Informatics. # 4. SUMMARY AND OUTLOOK

Over the past years, there has been a tremendous amount of interest, enthusiasm, and surging application of AI in drug discovery. While a group of researchers has published promising results, there have few examples of prospective application of many the techniques described above. It is important to note that drug discovery is a complex process that requires balancing multiple parameters. In order to achieve potency against a target, drug discovery must avoid potential off targets (which could bring side effects), an appropriate pharmacokinetic profile that will enable in synthesis, and ensure that a drug can be safely delivered.

In the end, one of the most critical elements in generating machine learning models is the data. In each case the quality and reliability of the predictions will depend on the data used. Unfortunately, much of the data needed to train these models reside in proprietary databases within pharmaceutical companies or in databases that be limited from publishers. Such databases as PubChem and ChEMBL¹

large amounts of experimental data are available to scale the quality, and quality can also be improved. As the field progresses, it is possible we will be able to create automated methods of identifying and predicting which drug molecule contains many errors. Although pharmaceutical companies have large datasets, the field moves forward only when these individuals have some collected datasets that are not difficult to use for model building.

It is unfortunate that the surging applications of AI in drug discovery has caused the scientific community to dismiss the discipline. This field has made a number of significant advancements in a relatively short period of time and appears to continue to advance the methods as more widely applied. While we do not hold ai as a panacea to be adopted in drug discovery, it will become another tool in the broad array of techniques used to bring medicine to patients.
