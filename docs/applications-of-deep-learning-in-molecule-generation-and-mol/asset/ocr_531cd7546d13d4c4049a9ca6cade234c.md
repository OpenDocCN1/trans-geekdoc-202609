# 3.2. Applications of Generative Modeling

While many papers have been describing generative models, very few have reported the synthesis of molecules based on generative models. One of the first papers to report on synthesis and chemical and biological testing of molecules designed by a generative algorithm was published in 2018 by Merck and coworkers who successfully designed agonists of retinoid X receptors (RXRs) or peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs).$^{46}$

![](bbox=[0.535, 0.42, 0.905, 0.54])

Figure 5. Comparison of Compound 1, designed by a generative model, and the marked drug ponatibinib.

A QSAR model was used to predict the activity of each generated molecule. Subsequent biological assays showed that the compound were dual PPAR and RXR inhibitors with $EC_{50}$ values between 60 and 13 nM.

A subset generated by Zhavorov et al. reported the molecules as potent inhibitors of DDR1, a protein tyrosine kinase family often implicated in fibrosis.$^{47}$ That paper received considerable notice in the popular press and led to reports of "a drug designed in 21 days." This work was considered notable, in order to demonstrate its selectivity in a biochemical model, the authors demonstrated that one molecule was active in cells and demonstrated reasonable pharmacokinetics in a mouse model. These results initially provided a strong response.$^{48}$ Murcko pointed out that the molecule highlighted in the hidden space bore a striking similarity to a previously published DDR1 inhibitors, including the marked drug ponatibinib (Figure 5). This controversy highlights difficulties in assessing the significance of molecules "invented" by generative methods.

A number of recent papers suggested methods for evaluating the molecules produced by generative models.$^{49}$ These benchmarks focus on evaluating the ability of generative models to explore chemical space and exploit known information. The GuacMolchem benchmark, published by Brown and coworkers, evaluates exploration through a series of tests to evaluate the validity, novelty and diversity of molecules generated by a generator. The software also has a goal-directed method to assess the ability of a generative model to focus on specific chemical space. An alternative for evaluating the diversity of molecules produced by a generative model is to compare the generated molecules with an exhaustive exploration of chemical space.

Another question for the field is where to apply generative models in the drug discovery process. Given the difficulty of exploring large chemical spaces, one can imagine in the assessment of synthesizability$^{50}$ and the use of machine learning in the synthesis of synthetic molecules.$^{51}$ the synthesis of molecules produced by generative models may end up being difficult or time-consuming.
