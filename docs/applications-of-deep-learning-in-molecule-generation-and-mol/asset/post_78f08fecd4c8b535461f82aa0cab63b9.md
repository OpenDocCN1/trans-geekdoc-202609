SPECTUS: Recent advances in computer hardware and software have led to a revolution in deep neural networks that have impacted fields ranging from translation to computer vision. Deep learning has also impacted a number of areas in drug discovery, including the analysis of chemical images and the design of new routes for the synthesis of organic molecules. While work in these areas has been incomplete, a complete review of the scope of a single Account. In this Account, we will focus on two key areas of deep learning in drug discovery: the prediction of molecular properties and the de novo generation of suggestions for new molecules.

One of the most significant advances in the quantitative development of structure–activity relationships (QSARs) has come from the application of deep learning methods to predict the biological activity and physical properties of molecules in drug discovery. Rather than using expert-derived chemical features to typically build models, researchers are now using deep learning to model complex, nonlinear representations. These representations, have led to state-of-the-art performance. While deep learning has changed the way that many researchers approach QSARs, it is not a panacea. With any other machine learning technique, the task of designing and creating a training set is still a fundamentally challenging issue, such as identifying critical areas that are still uncertain. Multiple researches have the development of methods for assessing the confidence in a model.

Deep learning has also contributed to the generation of new molecular structures. Rather than relying on manual design, deep learning has opened up new possibilities. Techniques that were originally developed for image generation and language translation have been adapted to the generation of molecules. These deep learning methods have been used with the predictive models above and are being used to generate new molecules with specific predicted biological profiles. While these generative algorithms appear promising, there have been only a few reports on the synthesis and testing of molecules proposed by generative designs. The evaluation of the diversity, quality, and ultimate value of molecules produced by generative models is still an open question. # 2. MOLECULAR PROPERTY PREDICTION

The generation and evaluation of new molecules based on data are critical tasks in the design of new medicines. In the early days of drug discovery, iteration were largely manual and intuition-driven. Scientists would test a set of compounds, examine the data produced, and use an understanding of the underlying science to synthesize molecules. The structural-activity relationships (QSAR) in the 1990s created a framework for associating compounds with properties.

### 2.1. Neural Networks for Property Prediction

Over the past years, the field of molecule and property prediction has seen a revolution, which has been driven by deep learning. Advances in computer hardware and software have made natural language processing now applicable to molecular properties including bioactivity, ADME, and interactions with targets. Rather than relying on sets of expert-defined models, deep learning has enabled researchers to develop models that can be trained directly on large datasets.
