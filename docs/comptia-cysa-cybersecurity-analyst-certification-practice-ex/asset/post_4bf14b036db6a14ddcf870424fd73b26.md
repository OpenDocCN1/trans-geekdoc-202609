- 7. What type of identity is unique in that its authorizations can vary in multiple ways?

- A. Services

- B. Roles

- C. Endpoints

- D. Applications

- B is correct. Roles are unique in that there is often a many-to-one relationship between roles and users. A user can have multiple roles, and so the challenge to administrators is to grant access according to the task fitting the role at the time.

- A, C, and D are incorrect. Services, endpoints, and applications would generally have one defined set of access. This contrasts with a role or a user, which might have multiple levels of access.

- 8. A few employees have fallen victim to having their passwords compromised. The users claim it is happening after visiting the company's own hourly billing website. The cybersecurity analyst verifies the website code and web server as clean. However, you witness the attack yourself after watching a user click through a text message that reminds them to update their timesheet. What sort of attack seems to be happening here?

- A. Man-in-the-middle

- B. Cross-site scripting

- C. Rootkit

- D. Privilege escalation

- B is correct. Considering that the problem might be originating from a link provided by text, which is extra difficult for users to scrutinize, and that the web server was verified as clean, then cross-site scripting is the likely attack method.

- A, C, and D are incorrect. There doesn't seem to be any indication of a man-in-the-middle attack. Rootkits would be significantly harder to detect or without specialized tools, and privilege escalation isn't the primary issue. However, after the XSS attack, perhaps the session information gleaned from the attack might contribute to privilege escalation layer, meaning the attacker might use the opportunity to elevate the privileges to a higher level, or to somehow gain access to others' accounts.

- 9. A vice president's workstation is actually racing, so you investigate the laptop personally. You carefully review the system's application and security logs. You confirm the antivirus is running and nothing is quarantined. You evaluate the running processes and find nothing out of the ordinary. After using command-line utilities to confirm no strange network connections, you feel somewhat frustrated to find nothing to report. What action should you take?

- A. Return the laptop to the vice president and report that all are fine.

- B. Reinstall and update the antivirus to rescan the system.

- C. Assume the laptop has a rootkit and wipe the system immediately.

- D. Use tools external to the laptop to explore the suspicion of a rootkit.

- D is correct. Despite suspicious behavior, local tools reporting nothing wrong is a strong suggestion to explore deeper with offline tools. Using tools stored on media outside the system, and if possible without the OS running, can improve visibility without allowing the potential rootkit to mask itself.

- A, B, and C are incorrect. Returning the laptop might be what the VP would like, but this is not acceptable until some closer examination. Because a rootkit is resident under the operating system and running applications, reinstalling the antivirus would only give you a false sense of security. Although a rootkit might be the suspicion, such a suspicion is typically not enough to warrant wiping the system.

- 10. Manual provisioning of accounts takes considerable time, but still allows a genuine person to exercise diligence. With regard to auto-provisioning, what is the most critical component?

- A. Consumer
- B. Identity provider
- C. Service provider
- D. Network access

- B is correct. Because the identity provider is the source of truth for user attributes, it is the most critical component to secure in an auto-provisioning workflow.

- A, C, and D are incorrect. While the consumer and service provider are involved in the process, they rely on the information provided by the identity provider. Network access is the transport mechanism, not the component providing the data.

- 11. A company recently implemented self-service password reset. Although this saves time, what is the primary security risk?

- A. Social engineering attacks
- B. Increased server load
- C. Database performance issues
- D. Lack of encryption support

- A is correct. Self-service password reset portals are frequent targets for social engineering, where attackers attempt to answer security questions or intercept codes.

- B, C, and D are incorrect. While database performance and encryption are general concerns, the most specific security risk associated with self-service reset is the vulnerability of the human element to social engineering.

- 12. Knowing the root cause and mechanics of session hijacking, what is the most effective way to mitigate this?

- A. Employing long session keys
- B. Using special characters within sessions
- C. Encrypting the session key
- D. Ensuring sessions have a very short timeout

- C is correct. Encrypting the session keys would certainly stop the keys from being captured and used for hijacking.
