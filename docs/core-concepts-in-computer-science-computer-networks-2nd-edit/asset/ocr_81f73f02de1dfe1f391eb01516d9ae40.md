# Analog Signal:

+   Continuous Values: Analog signals are continuous in nature and can take on
any value within a certain range. They are represented as varying voltage
levels or continuous waveforms.

+   Susceptibility to Noise: Analog signals are more susceptible to noise and
interference during transmission, which can lead to signal degradation and
quality loss.

+   Signal Degradation: Analog signals are prone to degradation over long
distances due to factors like attenuation and distortion.

+   Limited Processing: Analog signals are more challenging to process and
manulate directly, requiring analog circuits and specialized techniques
for amplification and filtering.

+   No Exact Reproduction: Analog signals cannot be perfectly regenerated or
reproduced without some loss of quality.

+   Applications: Analog signals are used in traditional telephony, audio
systems, radio broadcasting, analog television, and various sensors that
measure continuous physical quantities like temperature and pressure.

Digital signals offer advantages in terms of noise immunity, error correction,
and ease of processing, making them suitable for modern digital communication and
computing systems.

Analog signals, on the other hand, are essential for applications that require
continuous representations of physical quantities but are more susceptible to noise
and signal degradation. Both types of signals have their roles in today's diverse and
interconnected world of electronic communication and technology.
