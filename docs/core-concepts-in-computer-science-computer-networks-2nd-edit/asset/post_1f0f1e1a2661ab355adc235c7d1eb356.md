# Use of Computer Networks

A computer network is an interconnection of computers, printers, scanners and other hardware devices and software applications. Networks connect users within a defined physical space (such as within an office building). The Internet is a network that connects users from all parts of the world. Educational institutions, government agencies, health care facilities, banking and other financial institutions, and residential applications use computer networking to send and receive data and share resources.

The computer networks are playing an important role in providing services to large organizations as well as to the individual common man.

# Uses of Networks for Companies

- Resource Sharing: Many organizations have large number of substantial computers in operation often located far apart. Let us consider an example, a company having many factories at different locations. A computer at each location (that is in each factory) keeps the track of inventories, monitor productivity and do the local payroll. Initially each of these computers may have worked in isolation from each other, but at some point, the management may have decided to connect these computers to be able to extract and correlate the information of the entire company. The issue here is resource sharing. Its goal is to make all the programs, equipments, especially data available to anyone on the network irrespective of the location of the resource and the user.

- High Reliability: The second goal or use of networking in companies is high reliability by using alternative sources of supply. For example all the files can be replicated on two or more machines, so that in case one of them is not available (due to hardware failure), other copies can be used. This feature is used in financial institutions.

- Saving Money: The third goal is to save money. Small computers often have better price/performance ratio than the larger ones. Mainframe (room-size) computers are roughly ten faster than the personal computers, but are a thousand times costly. This imbalance caused the system designers to design a system consisting of personal computers, one per user, with data kept on one or more file server machines. In this model the users are called the clients and this whole arrangement is known as the client-server model. In client-server model, the communication generally takes the form of a request message from a client to the receiver asking for some work to be done. Server does the work and sends back the reply.

- Scalability: Another goal is scalability. Scalability is the ability to increase the system performance gradually as the workload grows, by just adding more processors.

# Use of Computer Networks in Education

Computer networks have significantly transformed the landscape of education, revolutionizing the way knowledge is accessed, disseminated, and shared. Their integration into educational settings has ushered in an era of improved learning experiences, enhanced collaboration, and increased access to educational resources. Here are some key uses of computer networks in education:

- **Access to Information and Learning Resources:** Computer networks provide students and educators with a vast repository of educational information and resources available on the internet. Online libraries, digital textbooks, academic journals, and educational websites offer a wealth of knowledge that can supplement traditional classroom materials. Students can conduct research, explore diverse perspectives, and expand their understanding of subjects beyond the confines of textbooks.

- **E-Learning and Online Courses:** Computer networks facilitate e-learning and online education platforms, enabling students to enroll in virtual courses, attend lectures, and participate in interactive learning activities. These platforms offer flexibility, allowing students to study at their own pace and access course materials from anywhere with an internet connection. E-learning has made education more accessible to individuals with varying schedules, geographical locations, or physical disabilities.

- **Collaborative Learning and Group Projects:** Computer networks promote collaborative learning environments. Students and teachers can communicate and work together on group projects, assignments, and presentations using various online collaboration tools. This fosters teamwork, communication skills, and the ability to solve problems collectively, which are essential for success in the modern workforce.

- **Communication and Interaction:** Computer networks enable seamless communication between educators, students, and parents. Through email, instant messaging, and learning management systems, teachers can provide feedback, answer queries, and share important updates. Parents can stay informed about their child's progress and involvement in school activities. Such interaction strengthens the parent-teacher-student relationship, leading to better support for student learning.

- **Virtual Laboratories and Simulations:** Computer networks facilitate the creation of virtual laboratories and simulations. Students can perform experiments and simulations online, replicating real-life scenarios without the need for physical equipment. This hands-on experience enhances understanding and critical thinking, particularly in subjects that require practical applications, such as science and engineering.

- **Global Connectivity and Cross-Cultural Learning:** Computer networks connect students and educators from diverse geographical locations and cultural backgrounds. Virtual exchange programs and online collaboration initiatives allow students to engage with peers and educators around the world, fostering cross-cultural understanding and global awareness.

- **Assessment and Evaluation:** Computer networks support various assessment tools and platforms, making it easier for educators to administer quizzes, tests, and assignments electronically. These platforms provide immediate feedback, reducing the time for evaluation and enabling educators to identify areas where students may need additional support.

- **Professional Development for Educators:** Computer networks offer professional development opportunities for teachers. They can participate in webinars, online workshops, and educational forums to stay updated on pedagogical practices, and exchange ideas with colleagues globally.

# Classification of Computer Networks

Computer networks are classified based on various factors. They include:

Inter-Connectivity: Components of a network can be connected to each other differently in some fashion. By connectedness we mean either logically, physically, or both ways.
- Every single device can be connected to every other device on network, making the network mesh.
- All devices can be connected to a single medium but geographically disconnected, creating bus-like structure.
- Each device is connected to its left and right peers only, creating linear structure.
