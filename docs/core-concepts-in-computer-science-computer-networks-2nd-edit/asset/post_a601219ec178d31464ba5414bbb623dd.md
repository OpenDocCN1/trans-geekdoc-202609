# INTRODUCTION

Data communications is the process of exchanging data or information between devices or systems over a communication medium. It serves as the backbone of modern communication, enabling seamless transmission and sharing of data across local and global networks. The field encompasses various technologies, protocols, and concepts that facilitate efficient and reliable data transfer.

At the heart of data communications are the communication mediums, which can be wired or wireless. Wired mediums use physical cables like copper or fiber-optic cables, while wireless mediums utilize radio waves or microwaves for transmission. The data is sent from a source device to a destination device through data transmission, which can be simplex, half-duplex, or full-duplex, depending on the communication needs.

To ensure effective data exchange, data communications rely on protocols, which are sets of rules and conventions defining how data is formatted, transmitted, received, and interpreted by devices in a network. Popular examples include the Transmission Control Protocol (TCP) and the Internet Protocol (IP), which form the foundation of the Internet.

In the data transmission process, errors may occur due to noise or interference. Therefore, error detection and correction techniques are employed to maintain data integrity. These methods enable devices to identify errors and request retransmission, ensuring accurate data delivery.

Networking devices like routers, switches, and modems play a crucial role in directing data traffic and ensuring proper connectivity between devices. They act as intermediaries, forwarding data packets to their intended destinations.

Bandwidth and latency are vital considerations in data communications. Bandwidth refers to the capacity of a communication channel to carry data, and latency refers to the time delay experienced during data transmission. Both factors affect the speed and efficiency of data transfer.

Security is a paramount concern in data communications. Encryption, firewalls, and all other security measures are implemented to protect data from unauthorized access, interception, or tampering.

The Internet and the World Wide Web have revolutionized data communications, providing a global network of interconnected devices. The Internet serves as a vast platform for information sharing, online services, and digital interactions on an unprecedented scale.

Data communications is a foundational pillar of modern technology and society. It enables the seamless exchange of data, drives global connectivity, and underpins the digital world we inhabit today. As technology continues to advance, data communications will remain a key area of innovation and development, shaping the way we interact and communicate with the future.

# Characteristics of Data Communication

Data communication is the active process of transporting data from one point to another. Networks are communication system designed to convey information from a point of origin to a point of destination. Note that they are communication system, not computer system. The operative word is communication, the transfer of information from one device to another.

![](img/f7819d05465fc14719b3ebbba980271a_99_0.png)

Networks come in two flavors – local as in local area network, which cover a small area and have a finite, relatively small, number of users and global or long-haul, which cover long distance and have an unlimited number of users. Telephone networks are long network. It refers to the transmission of the digital signals over a communication channel between the transmitter and receiver computers. Communication is possible only with wired and wireless connectivity of the computers with each other. The effectiveness of a data communication system depends on three fundamental characteristics:

- Delivery: The system must deliver data to the correct destination. Data must be received by the intended device or user and only by that device or user.
- Accuracy: The system must deliver data accurately. Data that have been altered in transmission and left uncorrected are unusable.
- Timeliness: The system must deliver data in a timely manner. Data delivered late are useless. In the case of video, audio and voice data, timely delivery means delivering data as they are produced, in the same order that they are produced, and without significant delay. This kind of delivery is called real-time transmission.
