+   - Direct Connection: The fundamental characteristic of point-to-point networks is the establishment of a direct link or connection between two devices. This means that the communication occurs between a single receiver and at any given time.

+   - Simplicity: Point-to-point networks are relatively simple in design and implementation. Since there are only two devices involved in communication, there is no need for complex addressing schemes or routing protocols.

+   - Dedicated Bandwidth: Each point-to-point link in the network provides dedicated and exclusive bandwidth between the connected devices. This ensures that the available bandwidth is fully utilized by the two endpoints, resulting in efficient data transfer.

+   - Full Duplex Communication: Point-to-point connections typically support full duplex communication, meaning data can be transmitted simultaneously in both directions. This enables bidirectional data transfer without the need for separate channels for sending and receiving data.

+   - Low Latency: Due to the direct nature of the connection, point-to-point networks usually exhibit low latency. The data travels directly between the two devices without passing through intermediate devices, reducing transmission delays.

+   - Privacy and Security: Point-to-point networks inherently offer a higher level of privacy and security compared to broadcast networks. Since communication occurs between specific endpoints, there is minimal risk of unauthorized access to the transmitted data.

+   - Distance Limitations: Point-to-point networks are suitable for relatively short to medium distances. As the distance between the two devices increases, the signal strength may diminish, leading to potential signal loss and degraded performance.

+   - Network Scalability: While point-to-point connections are simple and efficient for connecting two devices, they may not be scalable for larger networks with numerous devices. Each connection requires a separate physical link, which can become impractical as the number of connections increases.

+   - Application Scenarios: Point-to-point networks are commonly used in scenarios where direct communication between two specific devices is required. Examples include connecting two offices in a WAN, establishing a secure link between a server and a client, or setting up dedicated communication links from remote locations.

+   - Reliability: Point-to-point connections generally offer high reliability, as there are no intermediate devices that could introduce potential points of failure. The direct link ensures a more stable and dependable communication path.
