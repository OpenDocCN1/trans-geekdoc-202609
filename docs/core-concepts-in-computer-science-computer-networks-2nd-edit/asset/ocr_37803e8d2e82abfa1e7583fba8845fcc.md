| Ring | A network topology in which every node has branches connected to a star. A star-ring topology may appear (exactly) to be a topology. The MAU (multistation access unit) contains a wiring ring that allows information to pass from one node to another or ring, point-to-point links in a loop | Information flows in one direction around the ring until it reaches the correct destination | Cable between computers be broken to add a new computer, the network is down until the new device is online. | If there's a break in the cable or an error in the network, the rest of the ring still reaching the point of break. This makes troubleshooting easy. | One of the more expensive topologies than other topologies. Uses twisted pair. | The security of the information intended for one machine pass all all. |
| Mesh | A network topology in which there are at least two nodes with two or more paths between them. | Often used across distances. Information transfer can happen in different ways, depending on the other topology. | Used for WANs | Connection devices make combining different networks and different topologies. | Troubleshooting is most difficult in topology because of variety of technologies. | Expensive, large, and usually complicated. | Cabling depends on the types of networks. Can use twisted and coaxial cable. Also incorporates fiber optic cabling over long distances. | A mesh needs secure links, routing and forwarding | Trade with cost |
| Tree | The nodes are arranged as a tree. The tree topology is a generalization of the bus topology. The transmission medium is a branching cable with no closed loops. The tree layout has a point known as the head (Root), where one or more branches start, and each of these has branches. The branches in turn may have additional branches to allow complex layouts. | A transmission from any to all Propagates throughout the medium and can be received by all other stations.
