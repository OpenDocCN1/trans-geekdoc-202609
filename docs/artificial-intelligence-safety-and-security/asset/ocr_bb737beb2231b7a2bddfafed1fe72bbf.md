Figure 12.1 shows different models on how to trade off capability and control for a given task. The left side is for entities with little actual control, like our consulting AI physicist. The right side is for entities that have life or death level of control. The top side is for high intelligence. The bottom represents legacy “dumb” computer systems that don’t qualify as AI. The solid line on top represents the wishful thinking that AI is always safe and its control is unfettered. This is presumably supported by the possibility of the emergence of a Superintelligent Super-Responsible (SISR) AI which is not only more intelligent than humans but also more trustworthy. The burden of proof, we believe, should fall on those claiming that such SISR exists, and until such proof is provided and tested over a long time, this approach is to be avoided. Even if an SISR exists, our political history and philosophy lead us to suspect that “power corrupts and absolute power corrupts absolutely,” as Lord Acton said [22], and giving too much power to such an entity can still be rejected on that basis alone, given the very high stakes.

The solid curve (“minimal AI”) represents a conservative trade-off, in which autonomous intelligence is only encouraged for computers that have little or no control. Computers with a significant span of control should be, in this view, rule-based with the rules written explicitly by humans. We believe that trading off capability for control as our main basis for AISS, while better than “don’t ever built autonomous AI,” is still not a satisfactory solution. We need a compromise that safely enables wider use of AI.

We advocate that we should build structural and procedural AISS constraints embodied in the principles recommended throughout this chapter. The dashed curve represents the AISS approach that includes the principles. We project that using the principles will enable us to increase the range where we can use highly capable AI and still maintain a high standard of safety. At very high spans of control, even the “principled AI” model will still converge to the baseline where we do not allow autonomous (AI) systems to have life or death or major power over us or the ability to manage themselves without human participation and consent.
