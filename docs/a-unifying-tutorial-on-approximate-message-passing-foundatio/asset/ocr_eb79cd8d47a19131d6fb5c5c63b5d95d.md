Proof. Note that \( P_k \) is an \( \mathcal{S}_k \)-measurable projection matrix of rank \( r_k \le k \). Since \( \tilde{Z}^{k+1} \) is independent of \( \mathcal{S}_k \), it therefore has conditional distribution \( N_n(0, I_n) \) given \( \mathcal{S}_k \) by Remark B.4. Now let \( \xi_1, \dots, \xi_k \stackrel{\text{iid}}{\sim} N(0,1) \) be independent of \( \mathcal{S}_k \) and let \( \{\tilde{m}^1, \dots, \tilde{m}^{r_k}\} \) be any \( \mathcal{S}_k \)-measurable orthonormal basis of \( \text{Im}(M_k) = \text{Im}(P_k) \), as in Remark A.10. Recall that if \( Z \sim N_n(0, I_n) \) and \( P \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n} \) is a deterministic projection matrix of rank \( p \), then \( PZ \sim N(0, P) \), which is also the distribution of \( \sum_{i=1}^p \zeta_i u_i \) when \( \zeta_1, \dots, \zeta_p \stackrel{\text{iid}}{\sim} N(0,1) \) and \( \{u_1, \dots, u_p\} \) is any orthonormal basis of \( \text{Im}(P) \). We deduce from this and Lemma B.6(b) that \( P_k \tilde{Z}^{k+1} \) and \( \sum_{i=1}^{r_k} \xi_i \tilde{m}^i \) both have conditional distribution \( N_n(0, P_k) \) given \( \mathcal{S}_k \). This implies that \( \|P_k \tilde{Z}^{k+1}\|_{n,r} \stackrel{d}{=} \|\sum_{i=1}^{r_k} \xi_i \tilde{m}^i\|_{n,r} \).

Now for all \( x \in \mathbb{R}^n \), we have \( \|x\|_{n,r} = n^{-1/r} \|x\|_r \le n^{-1/(r \vee 2)} \|x\|_2 \) by Hölder’s inequality and the fact that \( \|\cdot\|_{p'} \le \|\cdot\|_p \) for \( 1 \le p \le p' \). Since \( \|\tilde{m}^i\|_2 = 1 \) for all \( i \) by definition, it follows from this and the triangle inequality for \( \|\cdot\|_{n,r} \) that \( \|\sum_{i=1}^{r_k} \xi_i \tilde{m}^i\|_{n,r} \le \sum_{i=1}^{r_k} |\xi_i| \|\tilde{m}^i\|_{n,r} \le \sum_{i=1}^{r_k} |\xi_i| / n^{1/(r \vee 2)} \le \sum_{i=1}^k |\xi_i| / n^{1/(r \vee 2)} \). Combining this with the conclusion of the previous paragraph yields the result.
