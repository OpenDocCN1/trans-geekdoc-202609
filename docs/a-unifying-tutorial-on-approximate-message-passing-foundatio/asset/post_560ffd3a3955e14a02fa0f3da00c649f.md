## A.9 Proofs for Section 4

The proof of Lemma 4.1 makes use of the following multivariate version of Stein's lemma.

### Lemma A.20.

Let $g : \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}$ be such that for $j = 1, \dots, d$, the function $x_j \mapsto g(x_1, \dots, x_d)$ is absolutely continuous for Lebesgue almost every $(x_1, \dots, x_{j-1}, x_{j+1}, \dots, x_d) \in \mathbb{R}^{d-1}$, with weak derivative $\partial_j g : \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfying $\mathbb{E}(|\partial_j g(X)|) < \infty$. Let $\nabla g(x) := (\partial_1(x), \dots, \partial_d(x))$ for $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$. If $X \sim N_d(0, \Sigma)$ with $\Sigma$ positive definite, then

$\mathbb{E}(Xg(X)) = \Sigma \mathbb{E}(\nabla g(X))$.

**Proof.** The result for $\Sigma = I_d$ is stated as Tsybakov (2009, Lemma 3.6). For a general non-negative definite $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$, let $\tilde{g}(z) = g(\Sigma^{1/2} z)$ for $z \in \mathbb{R}^d$. Then $\nabla \tilde{g}(z) = \Sigma^{1/2} \nabla g(\Sigma^{1/2} z)$ for all $z$ (Fourdriner et al., 2018, Theorem 2.1; Fan, 2022, Proposition E.5), so by taking $Z \sim N_d(0, I_d)$, we conclude that

$\mathbb{E}(Xg(X)) = \Sigma^{1/2} \mathbb{E}(Z\tilde{g}(Z)) = \Sigma^{1/2} \mathbb{E}(\nabla \tilde{g}(Z)) = \Sigma \mathbb{E}(\nabla g(X))$.

### Proof of Lemma 4.1.

The first assertion follows from the following standard fact: let $(X_1, X_2)$ be a Gaussian random vector with $\mathbb{E}(X_1) = \mathbb{E}(X_2) = 0$ and $\Sigma_{ij} := \text{Cov}(X_i, X_j)$ for $i, j \in \{1, 2\}$. If $\Sigma_{11} = \text{Var}(X_1)$ is invertible, then $AX_1$ and $X_2 - AX_1$ are uncorrelated and hence independent when $A := \Sigma_{21}\Sigma_{11}^{-1}$. Thus, $(X_1, X_2) \stackrel{d}{=} (X_1, AX_1 + G)$, where $AX_1 = \mathbb{E}(X_2 \mid X_1)$ and $G \sim N(0, \Sigma_{22} - \Sigma_{21}\Sigma_{11}^{-1}\Sigma_{12})$ is independent of $X_1$. For (4.8), we deduce from Lemma A.20 that

$\mathbb{E}(Z\tilde{g}_k(Z, Z_k, v)) = \Sigma_{11} \mathbb{E}(\partial_1 \tilde{g}_k(Z, Z_k, v)) + \Sigma_{12} \mathbb{E}(\partial_2 \tilde{g}_k(Z, Z_k, v))$

for all $v \in \mathbb{R}$, where $\Sigma \equiv \Sigma_k$ is as in (4.7). Thus, since $(Z, Z_k) \stackrel{d}{=} (Z, \mu_{Z,k}Z + \sigma_{Z,k}\tilde{G}_k)$ is independent of $\bar{\varepsilon}$ in (4.6), and $\mu_{Z,k} = \Sigma_{21}/\Sigma_{11}$ by the first part of the lemma,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}(Zg_k(Z_k, Y)) &= \mathbb{E}(Z\tilde{g}_k(Z, Z_k, \bar{\varepsilon})) \\
&= \Sigma_{11} \mathbb{E}(\partial_1 \tilde{g}_k(Z, Z_k, \bar{\varepsilon})) + \Sigma_{12} \mathbb{E}(\partial_2 \tilde{g}_k(Z, Z_k, \bar{\varepsilon})) \\
&= \frac{\mathbb{E}(\bar{\beta}^2)}{\delta} \{ \mu_{k+1} + \mu_{Z,k} \mathbb{E}(g'_k(Z_k, Y)) \},
\end{aligned}
\tag{A.68}
$$

which yields the first equality. Next, by the tower property of expectation, the final expression in (4.9) can be written as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}\left( \frac{\mathbb{E}(Z \mid Z_k, Y) - \mathbb{E}(Z \mid Z_k)}{\text{Var}(Z \mid Z_k)} g_k(Z_k, Y) \right) &= \mathbb{E}\left( \frac{Z - \mathbb{E}(Z \mid Z_k)}{\text{Var}(Z \mid Z_k)} \tilde{g}_k(Z, Z_k, \bar{\varepsilon}) \right).
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $Z$ is conditionally Gaussian given $Z_k$ and $\bar{\varepsilon}$ is independent of $(Z, Z_k)$, a further (conditional) application of Stein's lemma yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}\left( \frac{Z - \mathbb{E}(Z \mid Z_k)}{\text{Var}(Z \mid Z_k)} \tilde{g}_k(Z, Z_k, \bar{\varepsilon}) \Bigg| Z_k \right) &= \frac{\mathbb{E}\{(Z - \mathbb{E}(Z \mid Z_k)) \tilde{g}_k(Z, Z_k, \bar{\varepsilon}) \mid Z_k\}}{\text{Var}(Z \mid Z_k)} = \mathbb{E}(\partial_1 \tilde{g}_k(Z, Z_k, \bar{\varepsilon}) \mid Z_k),
\end{aligned}
$$

so by taking expectations, we obtain the second identity for $\mu_{k+1}$.

### Proof of Proposition 4.4.

Consider the right hand side of (4.31) and write \(\bar{J}(\bar{\beta}) := \sum_{j=1}^p J(\bar{\beta}_j)\) for \(\bar{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^p\). Using the expression for the Lagrangian (4.30), and ignoring terms that do not depend on \(\bar{\beta}\), we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\operatorname{argmin}_{\bar{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^p} \left\{ L(\bar{\beta}, \hat{\theta}^k, \hat{s}^k) - \frac{\bar{c}_k}{2} \| \bar{\beta} - \hat{\beta}^k \|^2 \right\} \\
&= \operatorname{argmin}_{\bar{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^p} \left\{ \bar{J}(\bar{\beta}) - \bar{\beta}^\top X^\top \hat{s}^k - \frac{\bar{c}_k}{2} \| \bar{\beta} - \hat{\beta}^k \|^2 \right\} \\
&= \operatorname{argmin}_{\bar{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^p} \left\{ \bar{J}(\bar{\beta}) - \frac{\bar{c}_k}{2} \left\| \bar{\beta} + \frac{X^\top \hat{s}^k - \bar{c}_k \hat{\beta}^k}{\bar{c}_k} \right\|^2 \right\} \\
&= \operatorname{argmin}_{\bar{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^p} \left\{ \bar{J}(\bar{\beta}) - \frac{\bar{c}_k}{2} \left\| \bar{\beta} + \frac{\beta^{k+1}}{\bar{c}_k} \right\|^2 \right\} = \hat{\beta}^{k+1},
\end{aligned}
\tag{A.69}
$$

where the third and final equalities follow from the definitions of \(\beta^{k+1}\) and \(\hat{\beta}^{k+1} = f_{k+1}(\beta^{k+1})\) respectively in (4.29), with \(f_{k+1}\) as in (4.24). Similarly, in view of the definition of \(\bar{g}_k\) in (4.23), we can obtain (4.32) by completing the square. For (4.33), we can apply (4.29) to see that

$$
\hat{s}^{k+1} = \frac{\hat{\theta}^{k+1} - \theta^{k+1}}{\bar{b}_{k+1}} = \frac{\hat{\theta}^{k+1} - (X \hat{\beta}^{k+1} - \bar{b}_{k+1} \hat{s}^k)}{\bar{b}_{k+1}} = \hat{s}^k + \frac{(\hat{\theta}^{k+1} - X \hat{\beta}^{k+1})}{\bar{b}_{k+1}}.
\tag{A.70}
$$

For the final assertion of Proposition 4.4, if \((\beta^*, \theta^*, \hat{\beta}^*, \hat{\theta}^*, \hat{s}^*)\) is a fixed point of the algorithm (4.29), then \(\hat{\theta}^* = X \hat{\beta}^*\) by (4.33), and (for example by considering subgradients) it follows from (4.31) and (4.32) respectively that

$$
\hat{\beta}^* = \operatorname{argmin}_{\bar{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^p} L(\bar{\beta}, \hat{\theta}^*, \hat{s}^*) = \operatorname{argmin}_{\bar{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^p} \{ \bar{J}(\bar{\beta}) - (X \bar{\beta})^\top \hat{s}^* \},
$$

$$
\hat{\theta}^* = \operatorname{argmin}_{\hat{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^n} L(\hat{\lambda}^*, \hat{\theta}, \hat{s}^*) = \operatorname{argmin}_{\bar{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^n} \{ \ell(\bar{\theta}, y) + \bar{\theta}^\top \hat{s}^* \},
$$

where \(\bar{\ell}(\tilde{\theta}, y) := \sum_{i=1}^n \ell(\tilde{\theta}_i, y_i)\). Thus, for all \((\tilde{\beta}, \tilde{\theta}) \in \mathbb{R}^p \times \mathbb{R}^n\) with \(\tilde{\theta} = X\tilde{\beta}\), we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar{J}(\tilde{\beta}) + \bar{\ell}(\tilde{\theta}, y) &= (\bar{J}(\tilde{\beta}) - (X\tilde{\beta})^\top \hat{s}^*) + (\bar{\ell}(\tilde{\theta}, y) + \tilde{\theta}^\top \hat{s}^*) \\
&\ge (\bar{J}(\hat{\beta}^*) - (X\hat{\beta}^*)^\top \hat{s}^*) + (\bar{\ell}(\hat{\theta}^*, y) + (\hat{\theta}^*)^\top \hat{s}^*) \\
&= \bar{J}(\hat{\beta}^*) + \bar{\ell}(\hat{\theta}^*, y),
\end{aligned}
$$

so \((\hat{\beta}^*, \hat{\theta}^*)\) is a solution to the optimisation problem (4.22), as required.
