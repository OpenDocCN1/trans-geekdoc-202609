
are identically distributed given $\mathcal{S}_k$. Finally, recall that $\dot{m}^k = (I - P_k) m^k = m^k - \sum_{\ell=1}^k \alpha_\ell^k m^{\ell-1}$, and that $(M_k^+)^\top M_k^\top m^\ell = P_k^\top m^\ell = P_k m^\ell = m^\ell$ for all $0 \le \ell \le k-1$ by the definition of the projection matrix $P_k = M_k M_k^+$. It follows that $P_k^\perp (\tilde{\zeta}^{k+1} m^k) = \tilde{\zeta}^{k+1} m^k$ and

$$
(M_k^+)^\top H_k^\top \dot{m}^k = (M_k^+)^\top \left( H_k^\top m^k - \sum_{\ell=1}^k \alpha_\ell^k H_k^\top m^{\ell-1} \right),
$$
$$
b_k m^{k-1} - \sum_{\ell=1}^k b_{\ell-1} \alpha_\ell^k m^{\ell-2}
$$
$$
= (M_k^+)^\top \left( b_k M_k^\top m^{k-1} - \sum_{\ell=1}^k \alpha_\ell^k b_{\ell-1} M_k^\top m^{\ell-2} \right).
$$

Thus, since $(M_k^+)^\top = M_k (M_k^\top M_k)^+$, the random variable $h^{k+1, k}$ defined in (A.7) is identical to that in (A.13), so we conclude from (A.12) that $h^{k+1} \stackrel{d}{=} |_{\mathcal{S}_k} h^{k+1, k}$, as required.

## A.4 Proof Outline for the AMP Master Theorems in Section 2.1

Recalling the definition (2.6) of the limiting covariance matrices $\bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k]} \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times k}$ in Theorem 2.3, we first outline a standard construction of a single random sequence $(\bar{G}_k : k \in \mathbb{N})$ satisfying $(\bar{G}_1, \dots, \bar{G}_k) \sim N_k(0, \bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k]})$ for each $k$. Let $\bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k], k+1} := (\bar{\mathbf{T}}_{1, k+1}, \dots, \bar{\mathbf{T}}_{k, k+1}) \in \mathbb{R}^k$ and

$$
\bar{\alpha}^k \equiv (\bar{\alpha}_1^k, \dots, \bar{\alpha}_k^k) := (\bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k]})^{-1} \bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k], k+1} \in \mathbb{R}^k \quad (A.14)
$$

for each $k \in \mathbb{N}$, where the latter is well-defined since $\bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k]}$ is positive definite under (A4) by Lemma 2.2. It is easily verified that if $(G_1, \dots, G_{k+1}) \sim N_{k+1}(0, \bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k+1]})$, then $G_{[k]} := (G_1, \dots, G_k)$ and $\xi_{k+1} := G_{k+1} - G_{[k]}^\top \bar{\alpha}^k = G_{k+1} - \sum_{\ell=1}^k \bar{\alpha}_\ell^k G_\ell$ are uncorrelated and hence independent. This means that

$$
G_{[k]}^\top \bar{\alpha}^k = \sum_{\ell=1}^k \bar{\alpha}_\ell^k G_\ell = \mathbb{E}(G_{k+1} \mid G_1, \dots, G_k)
$$

for each $k$. Moreover, since $\bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k+1]} = \text{Cov}(G_1, \dots, G_{k+1})$ is positive definite and $\xi_{k+1}$ is a non-trivial linear combination of $G_1, \dots, G_{k+1}$, it follows under (A4) that

$$
0 < \mathrm{Var}(\xi_{k+1}) = \mathrm{Var}(\xi_{k+1}|G_1,\dots,G_k) = \mathrm{Var}(G_{k+1}|G_1,\dots,G_k) = \mathrm{Var}(G_{k+1}) - \mathrm{Var}(G_{[k]}^\top \bar{\alpha}^k) = \bar{\mathbf{T}}_{k+1,k+1} - (\bar{\alpha}^k)^\top \bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k]} \bar{\alpha}^k = \tau_{k+1}^2 - (\bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k],k+1})^\top (\bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k]})^{-1} \bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k],k+1} =: \dot{\tau}_{k+1}^2
$$

for \( k \in \mathbb{N} \), so that \( \dot{\tau}_{k+1} \in (0, \infty) \) satisfies \( \dot{\tau}_{k+1}^2 = \mathrm{Var}(\xi_{k+1}) \le \mathrm{Var}(G_{k+1}) = \tau_{k+1}^2 \). Now let \( \bar{G}_1 \sim N(0, \tau_1^2) \), and for \( k \in \mathbb{N} \), inductively define

$$
\bar{G}_{k+1} := \sum_{\ell=1}^k \bar{\alpha}_\ell^k \bar{G}_\ell + \dot{\tau}_{k+1} \dot{\zeta}_{k+1},
$$

where \( \dot{\zeta}_{k+1} \sim N(0, 1) \) is independent of \( (\bar{G}_1, \dots, \bar{G}_k) \). Then \( (\bar{G}_k : k \in \mathbb{N}) \) is a random sequence with \( (\bar{G}_1, \dots, \bar{G}_k) \sim N_k(0, \bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k]}) \) for each \( k \), as desired. With the above definitions in place, we record here some key identities. In view of (2.6), we certainly have

$$
\mathrm{Cov}(\bar{G}_k, \bar{G}_\ell) = \mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_k \bar{G}_\ell) = \bar{\mathbf{T}}_{k,\ell} =
\begin{cases}
\tau_1^2 & \text{if } k = \ell = 1 \\
\mathbb{E}(F_0(\bar{\gamma}) \cdot f_{k-1}(\bar{G}_{k-1}, \bar{\gamma})) & \text{if } k > \ell = 1 \\
\mathbb{E}(f_{\ell-1}(\bar{G}_{\ell-1}, \bar{\gamma}) \cdot f_{k-1}(\bar{G}_{k-1}, \bar{\gamma})) & \text{if } k \ge \ell \ge 2,
\end{cases}
$$

where \( f_1, f_2, \dots \) are the Lipschitz functions in the AMP recursion (2.1), and \( \tau_1 \) and \( F_0 \) are as in (A2) and (A3) respectively. This fact underlies an important assertion (Proposition A.16(e) below) in our inductive proof of the master theorems. Moreover, for \( k, \ell \in \mathbb{N} \) and any Lipschitz function \( \varphi : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R} \) with weak derivative \( \varphi' \), we have

$$
\mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_k \varphi(\bar{G}_\ell)) = \mathbb{E}(\varphi'(\bar{G}_\ell))\mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_k \bar{G}_\ell) = \mathbb{E}(\varphi'(\bar{G}_\ell))\bar{\mathbf{T}}_{k,\ell}.
$$

This follows from Stein's lemma, a general formulation of which can be found in Tsybakov (2009, Lemma 3.6) and Lemma A.20.

### Lemma A.15 (Stein's Lemma)

If \( Z \sim N(0, \sigma^2) \) and \( \varphi : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R} \) is an absolutely continuous function with weak derivative \( \varphi' \) such that \( \varphi'(Z) \) is integrable, then \( \mathbb{E}(Z\varphi(Z)) = \sigma^2 \mathbb{E}(\varphi'(Z)) \).

Indeed, the first equality in (A.18) follows from Lemma A.15 upon writing \(\bar{G}_k = (\bar{\mathbf{T}}_{k,\ell}/\bar{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell,\ell})\bar{G}_{\ell} + \xi_{k\ell}\), where \(\xi_{k\ell}\) has zero mean and is independent of \(\bar{G}_{\ell}\), so that \(\mathbb{E}(\xi_{k\ell}\varphi(\bar{G}_{\ell})) = 0\).

Our choice of functions \((f_k)_{k=0}^\infty\) and \((f'_k)_{k=0}^\infty\) in (2.1) ensures that for any fixed \(y \in \mathbb{R}\), we can take \(\varphi = f_{\ell}(\cdot, y)\) and \(\varphi' = f'_{\ell}(\cdot, y)\) in (A.18) to see that \(\mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_k f_{\ell}(\bar{G}_{\ell}, y)) = \mathbb{E}(f'_{\ell}(\bar{G}_{\ell}, y))\mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_k\bar{G}_{\ell})\) for \(k, \ell \in \mathbb{N}\). We deduce from this (and Lemma B.7) that if \(\bar{\gamma} \sim \pi\) is independent of \(\bar{G}_1, \bar{G}_2, \dots\), then

$$
\mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_k f_{\ell}(\bar{G}_{\ell}, \bar{\gamma})) = \mathbb{E}(f'_{\ell}(\bar{G}_{\ell}, \bar{\gamma}))\mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_k\bar{G}_{\ell}) = \bar{b}_{\ell} \bar{\mathbf{T}}_{k,\ell} \quad (A.19)
$$

for all \(k, \ell \in \mathbb{N}\), where \(\bar{b}_{\ell} := \mathbb{E}(f'_{\ell}(\bar{G}_{\ell}, \bar{\gamma}))\). This forms part of assertion (f) in Proposition A.16 below.

To complete our technical preparations for the main derivations below, we will set up a more explicit connection between the Gaussian variables \(\bar{G}_{k+1} \sim N(0, \tau_{k+1}^2)\) in (A.16) and the random vectors \(h^{k+1,k} \equiv h^{k+1,k}(n)\) defined for \(n \in \mathbb{N}\) and \(k \in \{0, 1, \dots, n-1\}\) in (A.5) and (A.7) in Section A.2 above. For such \(n\) and \(k\), Proposition A.11 asserts that \(h^{k+1}(n)\) and \(h^{k+1,k}(n)\) are identically distributed given \(\mathcal{S}_k \equiv \mathcal{S}_k(n) = \sigma(\gamma_j, m^0, h^j : 1 \le j \le k)\), and we now write \(h^{k+1,k}(n) = \tilde{h}^{k+1}(n) + \Delta^{k+1}(n)\), where

$$
\tilde{h}^1 \equiv \tilde{h}^1(n) := \tau_1 \tilde{Z}^1 \quad \text{and} \quad \Delta^1 \equiv \Delta^1(n) := (\|m^0\|_n - \tau_1)\tilde{Z}^1 + \tilde{\zeta}^1 m^0, \quad (A.20)
$$

and

$$
\tilde{h}^{k+1} \equiv \tilde{h}^{k+1}(n) := \sum_{\ell=1}^k \bar{\alpha}_{\ell}^k h^\ell + \dot{\tau}_{k+1} \tilde{Z}^{k+1}, \quad (A.21)
$$

$$
\Delta^{k+1} \equiv \Delta^{k+1}(n) := \sum_{\ell=1}^k (\alpha_{\ell}^k - \bar{\alpha}_{\ell}^k) h^\ell + M_k(M_k^{\top} M_k)^+ \left(v^{k,k} - \sum_{\ell=1}^k \alpha_{\ell}^k v^{k,\ell-1}\right) - \|\tilde{m}^k\|_n(P_k \tilde{Z}^{k+1}) + (\|\tilde{m}^k\|_n - \dot{\tau}_{k+1})\tilde{Z}^{k+1} + \tilde{\zeta}^{k+1}\tilde{m}^k. \quad (A.22)
$$

Recall that \((\tilde{Z}^{k+1}, \tilde{\zeta}^{k+1}) \equiv (\tilde{Z}^{k+1}(n), \tilde{\zeta}^{k+1}(n)) \sim N_n(0, I_n) \otimes N(0, 1/n)\) was taken to be independent of \(\mathcal{S}_k \equiv \mathcal{S}_k(n)\) in Proposition A.11, where we also defined \(\alpha^k \equiv \alpha^k(n)\) and \(v^{k,\ell} \equiv v^{k,\ell}(n)\) for \(0 \le \ell \le k\).

In the decomposition above, we have defined \(\tilde{h}^{k+1}\) in (A.21) to mimic the expression for the limiting Gaussian variable \(\bar{G}_{k+1}\) in (A.16).
