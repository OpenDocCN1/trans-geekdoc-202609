+   (iii) \( x \mapsto \phi(x, y) \) is \(\mathcal{A}\)-measurable for all \( y \in \mathcal{Y} \) and \( Q_y \)-integrable for \( P^Y \)-almost every \( y \in \mathcal{Y} \);

+   (iv) \( y \mapsto \int_{\mathcal{X}} \phi(x, y) dQ_y(x) \) is \(\mathcal{B}\)-measurable and \( P^Y \)-integrable;

+   (v) \( \int_{\mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{Y}} \phi(x, y) dP(x, y) = \int_{\mathcal{Y}} (\int_{\mathcal{X}} \phi(x, y) dQ_y(x)) dP^Y(y) \).

This generalisation of Fubini's theorem is sometimes known as the *disintegration theorem*, and is derived from (II) using a monotone class argument; see Dudley (2002, Theorem 10.2.1(II)) and Kallenberg (1997, Theorem 5.4).

**Lemma B.6.** Let \( (\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{A}), (\mathcal{Y}, \mathcal{B}) \) be measurable spaces and let \( (\mathcal{Z}, \mathcal{C}) \) be a Borel space. Let \( \phi: (\mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{Y}, \mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{B}) \to (\mathcal{Z}, \mathcal{C}) \) be a measurable function and let \( \mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F} \) be a \(\sigma\)-algebra.

+   (a) If \( E \in \mathcal{G} \) and \( X_1, X_2: (\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}) \to (\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{A}) \) are measurable functions with conditional distributions \( P \equiv P_\omega \) and \( Q \equiv Q_\omega \) respectively given \(\mathcal{G}\), then the measurable function \( X: (\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}) \to (\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{A}) \) satisfying \( X = X_1 \) on \( E \) and \( X = X_2 \) on \( E^c \) has conditional distribution \( R(\cdot) \equiv R_\omega(\cdot) := P_\omega(\cdot)\mathbb{1}_{\{\omega \in E\}} + Q_\omega(\cdot)\mathbb{1}_{\{\omega \in E^c\}} \) given \(\mathcal{G}\).

+   (b) For \( D \in \mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{B} \) and \( y \in \mathcal{Y} \), let \( D^y := \{x \in \mathcal{X} : (x, y) \in D\} = \iota_y^{-1}(D) \), where \( \iota_y: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{Y} \) denotes the map \( x \mapsto (x, y) \). Fix \( \Omega_0 \in \mathcal{G} \). Suppose that \( X: (\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}) \to (\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{A}) \) has conditional distribution \( P \equiv P_\omega \) given \(\mathcal{G}\) on \( \Omega_0 \), and that \( Y: (\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}) \to (\mathcal{Y}, \mathcal{B}) \) is \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable. If \( Z: (\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}) \to (\mathcal{Z}, \mathcal{C}) \) is a measurable map that agrees with \( \phi(X, Y) \) on \( \Omega_0 \), then \( Z \) has conditional distribution \( \tilde{P} \equiv \tilde{P}_\omega = P_\omega \circ (\phi \circ \iota_{Y(\omega)})^{-1} \) given \(\mathcal{G}\) on \( \Omega_0 \), so that \( \tilde{P}_\omega(C) = P_\omega(\phi^{-1}(C)^{Y(\omega)}) \) for all \( C \in \mathcal{C} \) and \( \omega \in \Omega_0 \).

+   (c) Suppose that \( X, X': (\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}) \to (\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{A}) \) are measurable functions satisfying \( X \stackrel{d}{=}|_\mathcal{G} X' \), and that \( Y: (\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}) \to (\mathcal{Y}, \mathcal{B}) \) is \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable. Then \( \phi(X, Y) \stackrel{d}{=}|_\mathcal{G} \phi(X', Y) \).

The result in (b) has an intuitive interpretation. Suppose for simplicity that \( \Omega_0 = \Omega \), and fix \( \omega \in \Omega \). Let \( \mu := P_\omega \) be taken from the conditional distribution of \( X \) given \(\mathcal{G}\), and assume that \( Y \) is \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable.
