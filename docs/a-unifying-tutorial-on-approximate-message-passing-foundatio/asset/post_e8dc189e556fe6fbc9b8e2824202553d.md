
## Proof of Lemma B.10.

For each $n$, denote by $\mathcal{B}_n, \mathcal{B}'_n$ the Borel $\sigma$-algebras of $E_n, E'_n$ respectively. It follows from Dudley (2002, Theorem 2.5.7) and Kallenberg (1997, Lemma 1.2) that $\prod_{j=1}^n E'_j$ and $\prod_{j=1}^n E_j$ are Polish spaces with Borel $\sigma$-algebras $\otimes_{j=1}^n \mathcal{B}'_j$ and $\otimes_{j=1}^n \mathcal{B}_j$ respectively. Denote by $\mu_n, \pi_n$ the distributions of $Y_n$ and $(Y_1, ..., Y_n)$ on $(E'_n, \mathcal{B}'_n)$ and $(\prod_{j=1}^n E'_j, \otimes_{j=1}^n \mathcal{B}'_j)$ respectively. Since $E_n$ is a Polish space, we know from Section B.2 that there exists a regular conditional distribution for $X_n$ given $\sigma(g_n(X_n))$. Equivalently, there is a family of probability distributions $(Q_y^n)_{y \in E'_n}$ on $E_n$ satisfying conditions (I) and (II) in Remark B.5, where we take $X := X_n, Y := g_n(X_n) \stackrel{d}{=} Y_n$ and $P^Y := \mu_n$. It follows from Remark B.5(II) that $\mathbb{P}(X_n \in A) = \int_{E'_n} Q_y^n(A) d\mu_n(y)$ for all $A \in \mathcal{B}_n$, and moreover that

\[
\int_{B'} \mathbb{1}_B(y) d\mu_n(y) = \mathbb{P}(g_n(X_n) \in B \cap B')
= \mathbb{P}(X_n \in g_n^{-1}(B), g_n(X_n) \in B')
= \int_{B'} Q_y^n(g_n^{-1}(B)) d\mu_n(y) \tag{B.4}
\]

for $B, B' \in \mathcal{B}'_n$. Thus, for all $B \in \mathcal{B}'_n$, we have $Q_y^n(g_n^{-1}(B)) = \mathbb{1}_B(y)$ for $\mu_n$-almost every $y \in E'_n$.

For each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, we now define a new measure $\pi'_n$ on $(\prod_{j=1}^n E_j, \otimes_{j=1}^n \mathcal{B}_j)$ by

\[
\pi'_n(A) := \int_{\prod_{j=1}^n E'_j} \left( \int_{E_1} \cdots \int_{E_n} \mathbb{1}_A(x_1, ..., x_n) dQ_{y_n}^n(x_n) \cdots dQ_{y_1}^1(x_1) \right) d\pi_n(y_1, ..., y_n) \tag{B.5}
\]

for $A \in \otimes_{j=1}^n \mathcal{B}_j$. That this a well-defined probability measure follows from Remark B.5(III, IV) and the monotone convergence theorem. For each $n$, we claim that

- (i) $\pi'_{n+1}(A \times E_{n+1}) = \pi'_n(A)$ for every $A \in \otimes_{j=1}^n \mathcal{B}_j$;
- (ii) $\mathbb{P}(X_n \in A_n) = \pi'_n(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1} E_j \times A_n)$ for every $A_n \in \mathcal{B}_n$;
- (iii) $\pi_n = \pi'_n \circ (g_1, ..., g_n)^{-1}$, where $(g_1, ..., g_n) : (\prod_{j=1}^n E_j, \otimes_{j=1}^n \mathcal{B}_j) \to (\prod_{j=1}^n E'_j, \otimes_{j=1}^n \mathcal{B}'_j)$ denotes the measurable map $(x_1, ..., x_n) \mapsto (g_1(x_1), ..., g_n(x_n))$.

Property (i) is immediate from (B.5) and the fact that $\pi_{n+1}(B \times E'_n) = \pi_n(B)$ for all $B \in \otimes_{j=1}^n \mathcal{B}'_j$. To verify (ii), observe that

\[
\pi'_n(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1} E_j \times A_n) = \int_{\prod_{j=1}^n E'_j} \int_{E_n} \mathbb{1}_{A_n}(x_n) dQ_{y_n}^n(x_n) d\pi_n(y_1, \dots, y_n) = \int_{E'_n} Q_{y_n}^n(A_n) d\mu_n(y_n) = \mathbb{P}(X_n \in A_n),
\]

where the final equality is obtained from Remark B.5(II) as above. As for (iii), fix $B_j \in \mathcal{B}'_j$ for $1 \le j \le n$ and note that by (B.4) and (B.5), we have

\[
\pi'_n(\prod_{j=1}^n g_j^{-1}(B_j)) = \int_{\prod_{j=1}^n E'_j} Q_{y_1}^1(g_1^{-1}(B_1)) \cdots Q_{y_n}^n(g_n^{-1}(B_n)) d\pi_n(y_1, \dots, y_n) = \int_{\prod_{j=1}^n E'_j} \mathbb{1}_{B_1}(y_1) \cdots \mathbb{1}_{B_n}(y_n) d\pi_n(y_1, \dots, y_n) = \pi_n(\prod_{j=1}^n B_j).
\]

This means that $\pi_n$ and $\pi'_n \circ (g_1, \dots, g_n)^{-1}$ agree on $\{ \prod_{j=1}^n B_j : B_j \in \mathcal{B}'_j \text{ for all } 1 \le j \le n \}$, a $\pi$-system that generates $\otimes_{j=1}^n \mathcal{B}_j$, so (iii) holds.

Since the distributions $\pi'_1, \pi'_2, \dots$ on the Polish spaces $E_1, E_1 \times E_2, \dots$ satisfy the consistency condition (i), we deduce from the Daniell-Kolmogorov extension theorem (Kallenberg, 1997, Theorem 5.14) and Remark B.11 that exists a sequence $(X'_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ of random elements $X'_n : \Omega \to E_n$ such that $(X'_1, \dots, X'_n) \sim \pi'_n$ on $\prod_{j=1}^n E_j$ for each $n$. Then by (ii) and (iii) above, we have $X'_n \overset{d}{=} X_n$ and $(g_1(X'_1), \dots, g_n(X'_n)) \sim \pi'_n \circ (g_1, \dots, g_n)^{-1} = \pi_n$ for each $n$, where $\pi_n$ was defined to be the distribution of $(Y_1, \dots, Y_n)$. Thus, the sequences $(g_n(X'_n))$ and $(Y_n)$ have the same finite-dimensional distributions; in other words, their distributions agree on $\{ \prod_{j=1}^N B_j \times \prod_{j=N+1}^\infty E'_j : N \in \mathbb{N}, B_j \in \mathcal{B}'_j \text{ for all } 1 \le j \le N \}$, a collection of cylindrical sets that generate the cylindrical $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{B}'$ of $\prod_{n=1}^\infty E'_n$. (By Kallenberg (1997, Lemma 1.2), $\mathcal{B}'$ is the Borel $\sigma$-algebra of $\prod_{n=1}^\infty E'_n$.) We conclude that $(g_1(X'_1), g_2(X'_2), \dots) \overset{d}{=} (Y_1, Y_2, \dots)$ as random sequences taking values in $(\prod_{n=1}^\infty E'_n, \mathcal{B}')$.

Finally, we apply Kallenberg (1997, Corollary 5.11) with $T = \prod_{n=1}^\infty E_n, S = \prod_{n=1}^\infty E'_n, \eta = (X'_n), \xi = (Y_n)$ and $f : T \to S$ given by $f(x_1, x_2, \dots) = (g_1(x_1), g_2(x_2), \dots)$; note that $T, S$ are Polish spaces (e.g. Dudley, 2002, Theorem 2.5.7) and that $f$ is Borel measurable. Having already shown that $f(\eta) \stackrel{d}{=} \xi$, we deduce from Kallenberg (1997, Corollary 5.11) that there exists $(\tilde{X}_n) \equiv \tilde{\eta} \stackrel{d}{=} \eta = (X'_n)$ satisfying $(g_n(\tilde{X}_n)) = f(\tilde{\eta}) = \xi = (Y_n)$ almost surely, as required.

## Lemma B.12.

There exists a universal constant $C > 0$ such that the following holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $r \ge 2$ and $t \ge 0$: if $Z_1, \dots, Z_n \stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim} N(0, 1)$, $L \equiv (L_1, \dots, L_n) \in (0, \infty)^n$ and $f_i \in \mathrm{PL}_1(r, L_i)$ for $1 \le i \le n$, then

\[
\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \{f_i(Z_i) - \mathbb{E}(f_i(Z_i))\}\right| \ge t\right) \le \exp\left(1 - \min \left\{ \left(\frac{nt}{(Cr)^r \|L\|_2}\right)^2, \left(\frac{nt}{(Cr)^r \|L\|_\infty}\right)^{2/r} \right\}\right). \tag{B.6}
\]

Proof. We first consider the case $n = 1$. For arbitrary $r \ge 2$ and $L > 0$, we may assume without loss of generality that $f \equiv f_1 \in \mathrm{PL}(r, L)$ satisfies $f(0) = 0$, so that $|f(x)| = |f(x) - f(0)| \le L(|x| + |x|^r) \le 2L(|x| \vee |x|^r)$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Thus, if $Z \sim N(0, 1)$, then

\[
\mathbb{P}(|f(Z)| \ge s) \le \mathbb{P}(|Z| \vee |Z|^r \ge s/(2L)) \le e^{-\frac{1}{2} \min \left\{ (\frac{s}{2L})^2, (\frac{s}{2L})^{2/r} \right\}} \tag{B.7}
\]

for all $s \ge 0$, and

\[
\frac{\mathbb{E}(|f(Z)|)}{L} \le \mathbb{E}(|Z| + |Z|^r) = \left( \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}} + \frac{2^{r/2}}{\sqrt{\pi}} \Gamma\left(\frac{r+1}{2}\right) \right) =: v_r
\]

by direct computation. Now $\Gamma(x) < e^{1/(12x)}(x/e)^x \sqrt{2\pi/x}$ for all $x > 0$ by a non-asymptotic version of Stirling’s formula; see for example Gordon (1994, Theorem 5) and Dümbgen et al. (2021, Lemma 10). Since $r \ge 2$, we have $(r+1)/e < r$ and $(\sqrt{2}-1)r^{r/2} \ge 2(\sqrt{2}-1) > 1/\sqrt{\pi}$. Therefore,

\[
\frac{v_r}{2} \le \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}} + \left( \frac{r+1}{e} \right)^{r/2} e^{\frac{1}{6(r+1)} - \frac{1}{2}} \right) \le \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}} + r^{r/2} \right) < r^{r/2}. \tag{B.8}
\]

Thus, for \( t \ge Lv_r \), we deduce from (B.7) and (B.8) that

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{P}\{|f(Z) - \mathbb{E}(f(Z))| \ge t\} \le \mathbb{P}\{|f(Z)| \ge t - \mathbb{E}(|f(Z)|)\} \\
\le e^{-\frac{1}{2} \min\left\{(\frac{t}{2L} - \frac{v_r}{2})^2, \, (\frac{t}{2L} - \frac{v_r}{2})^{2/r}\right\}} \\
\le e^{\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2}(\frac{t}{2L} - \frac{v_r}{2})^{2/r}} \\
\le e^{\frac{1+r}{2} - \frac{1}{2}(\frac{t}{2L})^{2/r}} \\
\le e^{1 - (\frac{t}{2L})^{2/r} \frac{1}{r+1}},
\end{aligned}
\]

where the third inequality follows from the fact that \( a^{2/r} \le |a - b|^{2/r} + b^{2/r} \) for \( r \ge 2 \) as above and any \( a, b \ge 0 \). Now (B.9) holds trivially for all \( t \in [0, Lv_r) \) since \( 1 - (r + 1)^{-1}\{t/(2L)\}^{2/r} > 1 - (r + 1)^{-1}(v_r/2)^{2/r} > 0 \) by (B.8), so (B.6) holds with \( C = 3 \) when \( n = 1 \).

We now derive (B.6) for general \( n \ge 2 \) with the aid of Theorem 3.1 and Proposition A.3 in Kuchibhotla and Chakrabortty (2018); see also Theorem 1 and Corollary 2 in Bakhshizadeh et al. (2020). As in Sections 2 and 3 of Kuchibhotla and Chakrabortty (2018), we begin by defining \( \vartheta_\beta: [0, \infty) \to [0, \infty) \) for each \( \beta > 0 \) by \( \vartheta_\beta(x) := \exp(x^\beta) - 1 \). Moreover, for \( \beta, \lambda > 0 \), let \( \vartheta_{\beta, \lambda}: [0, \infty) \to [0, \infty) \) be the continuous, strictly increasing function with inverse given by \( \vartheta_{\beta, \lambda}^{-1}(t) := \log^{1/2}(1 + t) + \lambda \log^{1/\beta}(1 + t) \) for \( t \ge 0 \). For a random variable \( X \) and a strictly increasing function \( g: [0, \infty) \to [0, \infty) \) satisfying \( g(0) = 0 \), we write \( \Xi_g(X) := \inf\{\theta > 0 : \mathbb{E}(g(|X|/\theta)) \le 1\} \in [0, \infty] \), setting \( \inf \emptyset = \infty \) by convention. Note that \( \Xi_g(X) \) is precisely the \( g \)-Orlicz norm of \( X \) when \( g \) is convex, but that \( \Xi_g \) does not in general define a norm when \( g \) is not convex (for example when \( g = \vartheta_\beta \) for \( \beta \in (0, 1) \), as in the proof below).

For arbitrary \( n \ge 2 \), \( r \ge 2 \) and \( L \equiv (L_1, \dots, L_n) \in (0, \infty)^n \), let \( f_1, \dots, f_n \in \text{PL}(r, L_i) \) and \( Z_1, \dots, Z_n \stackrel{\text{iid}}{\sim} N(0, 1) \), and assume without loss of generality that \( X_i := f_i(Z_i) \) satisfies \( \mathbb{E}(X_i) = 0 \) for all \( 1 \le i \le n \). Setting \( \beta := 2/r \in [0, 1] \) and \( \theta_i := 2\{4(r + 1)\}^{r/2} L_i \) for \( 1 \le i \le n \), we
