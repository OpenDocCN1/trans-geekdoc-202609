For \( k \in \mathbb{N}_0 \), the functions \( \bar{g}_k, g_k, f_{k+1} \) in (4.23)-(4.24) are given by

\(\bar{g}_k(u, v) = v - \text{prox}_{\bar{b}_k M}(v - u) = u + S_{\bar{b}_k}(v - u),\)
\(g_k(u, v) = \frac{S_{\bar{b}_k}(v - u)}{\bar{b}_k}, \qquad f_{k+1}(w) = -\frac{w}{\bar{c}_k}.\)

Given \( \bar{b}_0 > 0 \), \( \hat{\beta}^0 \in \mathbb{R}^p \) and \( \hat{r}^0 := y - X\hat{\beta}^0 \), we now write the GAMP algorithm (4.29) in terms of \( \hat{r}^k = y - \theta^k \) and \( \hat{\beta}^{k+1} = f_{k+1}(\beta^{k+1}) = -\beta^{k+1}/\bar{c}_k \), and obtain the recursion

\(\hat{\beta}^{k+1} = \frac{\delta \bar{b}_{k+1}}{\bar{b}_k} X^\top S_{\bar{b}_k}(r^k) + \hat{\beta}^k,\)
\(\hat{r}^{k+1} = y - X\hat{\beta}^{k+1} + \frac{\bar{b}_{k+1}}{\bar{b}_k} S_{\bar{b}_k}(r^k) \qquad \text{for } k \in \mathbb{N}_0,\) (4.53)

where \( \bar{b}_{k+1} = -1/(\delta \bar{c}_k) \). Moreover, expressing the state evolution recursion (4.6)-(4.7) for (4.29) in terms of \( \tilde{\mu}_k := \delta \bar{b}_k \mu_k \), \( \tilde{\sigma}_k := \delta \bar{b}_k \sigma_k \) and \( \tau_k := \mathbb{E}((Z - Z_k)^2)^{1/2} \) with \( (Z, Z_k) \sim N(0, \Sigma_k) \), we have \( \tau_0 = \mathbb{E}((Z - Z_0)^2)^{1/2} \) and

\(\tilde{\mu}_{k+1} = \delta \mathbb{E}(S'_{\bar{b}_k}(\bar{\varepsilon} + \tau_k G_k)), \qquad \tilde{\sigma}_{k+1}^2 = \delta^2 \mathbb{E}(S_{\bar{b}_k}(\bar{\varepsilon} + \tau_k G_k)^2),\)

\(\tau_{k+1}^2 = \frac{\mathbb{E}(\bar{\beta}^2)(\tilde{\mu}_{k+1} - 1)^2 + \tilde{\sigma}_{k+1}^2}{\delta}\) (4.54)

for \( k \in \mathbb{N}_0 \), where \( \bar{\varepsilon} \sim P_\Xi \) is independent of \( G_k \sim N(0, 1) \).

Turning now to **Step 1** in Section 4.4, we seek a fixed point \( (\hat{r}^*, \hat{\beta}^*, \bar{b}_* > 0, \tilde{\mu}_*, \tilde{\sigma}_*, \tau_*) \) of (4.53)-(4.54) satisfying

\( 0 = \delta X^\top S_{\bar{b}_*}(\hat{r}^*), \qquad \hat{r}^* = y - X\hat{\beta}^* + S_{\bar{b}_*}(\hat{r}^*), \) (4.55)

\(\check{\mu}_* = \delta \mathbb{E}(S'_{\tilde{b}_*}(\bar{\varepsilon} + \tau_* G_*)) = 1, \quad \tau_*^2 = \delta \mathbb{E}(S_{\tilde{b}_*}(\bar{\varepsilon} + \tau_* G_*)^2), \quad \check{\sigma}_* = \sqrt{\delta} \tau_* , \tag{4.56}\)

where \(\bar{\varepsilon} \sim P_{\bar{\varepsilon}}\) is independent of \(G_* \sim N(0,1)\), and \(\mu_* = \check{\mu}_*/(\delta\tilde{b}_*)\) and \(\sigma_* = \check{\sigma}_*/(\delta\tilde{b}_*)\) are fixed points of the original state evolution equation (4.6). By Proposition 4.4, \(\hat{\beta}^*\) solves the M-estimation problem in (4.50). Assuming that

M is continuously differentiable and S = M' is absolutely continuous with \(\sup_{w \in \mathbb{R}} S'(w) < \infty, \tag{4.57}\)

Donoho and Montanari (2016, Lemma 6.5) showed that for any \(\tau > 0\), the map \(b \mapsto \mathbb{E}(S'_{\tilde{b}}(\bar{\varepsilon} + \tau G_*)) =: F_\tau(b)\) is continuous on \((0, \infty)\) with \(\lim_{b \to 0} F_\tau(b) = 0\) and \(\lim_{b \to \infty} F_\tau(b) = 1\), and hence that there exists \(b \equiv b_\tau > 0\) satisfying \(\mathbb{E}(S'_{\tilde{b}}(\bar{\varepsilon} + \tau G_*)) = \delta^{-1}\) for \(\delta \in (1, \infty)\). Using this, they deduced that under (4.57), there exists a unique solution \((\tau_*, \tilde{b}_*)\) to (4.56) for any such \(\delta\) (Donoho and Montanari, 2016, Corollary 4.4).

The functions \(f_*, g_*\) in **Step 2** in Section 4.4 are given by \(f_* : w \mapsto \delta \tilde{b}_* w\) and \(g_* : (u, v) \mapsto S_{\tilde{b}_*}(v - u) / \tilde{b}_*\), so for \(n \in \mathbb{N}\) and \(p \equiv p_n\), the "stationary" AMP iteration (4.35) can be written as

\(\hat{\beta}^{k+1} = X^\top S_{\tilde{b}_*}(\hat{r}^k) + \hat{\beta}^k, \quad \hat{r}^{k+1} = y - X \hat{\beta}^{k+1} + S_{\tilde{b}_*}(\hat{r}^k) \quad \text{for } k \in \mathbb{N}_0. \tag{4.58}\)

Here, \(\hat{r}^0 = y - X \hat{\beta}^0\) and \(\hat{\beta}^0 = \beta + \tilde{\sigma}_* \xi = f_*(\mu_* \beta + \sigma_* \xi) \in \mathbb{R}^p\), where \(\xi \sim N_p(0, I_p)\) is independent of the signal \(\beta \in \mathbb{R}^p\). This choice of oracle initialiser ensures that the corresponding state evolution sequence is stationary with \(\tau_k = \tau_*\) for all \(k \in \mathbb{N}_0\). Then under the conditions (G0)-(G5) of Theorem 4.2 with \(r=2\), it follows from Remark 4.3 and (4.10) that for each fixed \(k \in \mathbb{N}\), the empirical distributions of the components of \(\hat{r}^k - \varepsilon \in \mathbb{R}^n\) and \(\hat{\beta}^k - \beta \in \mathbb{R}^p\) converge completely in \(d_2\) to \(N(0, \tau_*^2)\) and \(N(0, \check{\sigma}_*^2) = N(0, \delta \tau_*^2)\) respectively as \(n, p \to \infty\) with \(n/p \to \delta \in (1, \infty)\).

We remark that this result can in fact be derived by directly transforming (4.58) into an abstract asymmetric AMP iteration of the form (2.10). Note in particular that since \(h(z, v) = z + v\) in (4.1) for the linear model and \(S_\eta\) is 1-Lipschitz for all \(\eta > 0\), the function \(\tilde{g}_k = \tilde{g}_* : (z, u, v) \mapsto S_{\tilde{b}_*}(z + v - u) / \tilde{b}_*\) in (G4) is indeed Lipschitz.

As in Section 4.5, the remaining ingredient (**Step 3** in Section 4.4) is to show that the iterates \(\hat{\beta}^k\) in (4.58) converge in the sense of (4.36)

to some \(\hat{\beta}^*\) satisfying (4.55), which is an M-estimator by Proposition 4.4. Under (4.57) and the additional assumption that M is strongly convex, i.e., \(\inf_{w \in \mathbb{R}} S'(w) > 0\), the conclusion of Donoho and Montanari (2016, Theorem 4.1) is indeed that

\(\lim_{k \to \infty} \text{c-lim}_{p \to \infty} \frac{\|\hat{\beta}^k - \hat{\beta}^*\|^2}{p} = 0.\) (4.59)

Together with the state evolution characterisation of the iterates in (4.58), this leads to the following characterisation of the asymptotic performance of the M-estimator.

**Theorem 4.7** (Donoho and Montanari, 2016, Theorem 4.2). Consider a sequence of linear models \(y = X\beta + \varepsilon\) satisfying (G0) and (G1), with \(n/p \to \delta \in (1, \infty)\) as \(n, p \to \infty\). Assume that the loss function M is continuously differentiable, and that the score function \(S = M'\) is absolutely continuous with \(0 < \inf_{w \in \mathbb{R}} S'(w) \le \sup_{w \in \mathbb{R}} S'(w) < \infty\). Let \((\tau_*, \bar{b}_*)\) be the unique fixed point of (4.56). Then

\(\sup_{\psi \in \text{PL}_2(2,1)} \left| \frac{1}{p} \sum_{j=1}^p \psi(\hat{\beta}_j^{\text{M}} - \beta_j, \beta_j) - \mathbb{E}(\psi(\sqrt{\delta}\tau_* G, \bar{\beta})) \right| \xrightarrow{c} 0\) (4.60)

as \(n, p \to \infty\) with \(n/p \to \delta\), where \(G \sim N(0,1)\). In particular, the asymptotic mean squared error of \(\hat{\beta}^{\text{M}}\) is given by

\(\text{c-lim}_{p \to \infty} \frac{\|\hat{\beta}^{\text{M}} - \beta\|^2}{p} = V(S_{b_*}; \bar{\varepsilon} + \tau_* G) = \frac{\mathbb{E}(S_{b_*}(\bar{\varepsilon} + \tau_* G)^2)}{\mathbb{E}(S'_{b_*}(\bar{\varepsilon} + \tau_* G))^2} = \frac{\tau_*^2 / \delta}{1/\delta^2} = \delta \tau_*^2.\) (4.61)

\(\sup_{\psi \in \text{PL}_1(2,1)} \left| \frac{1}{p} \sum_{j=1}^p \psi(\hat{\beta}_j^{\text{M}} - \beta_j) - \mathbb{E}(\psi(\sqrt{\delta}\tau_* G)) \right| \xrightarrow{c} 0,\)
