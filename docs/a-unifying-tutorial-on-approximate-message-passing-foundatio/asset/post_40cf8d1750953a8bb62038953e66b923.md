
$\phi \in \text{PL}_{k+2}(1)$, it follows that $n^{-1} \sum_{i=1}^n m_i^0 \phi(h_i^1, \dots, h_i^k, h_i^{k+1}) \stackrel{d}{=} n^{-1} \cdot \sum_{i=1}^n m_i^0 \phi(h_i^1, \dots, h_i^k, h_i^{k+1,k}) =: T_n$ for each such $n$. Using (A.38), we now write

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_n &= \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n m_i^0 \phi(h_i^1, \dots, h_i^k, \tilde{h}_i^{k+1}, \gamma_i)
&\quad + \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n m_i^0 \{ \phi(h_i^1, \dots, h_i^k, h_i^{k+1,k}, \gamma_i) - \phi(h_i^1, \dots, h_i^k, \tilde{h}_i^{k+1}, \gamma_i) \} \\
&=: T_{n1} + T_{n2}
\end{aligned}
\tag{A.48}
$$

for each $n > k$, and aim to prove that $T_{n1} \stackrel{p}{\to} \mathbb{E}(F_0(\bar{\gamma}) \cdot \phi(\bar{G}_1, \dots, \bar{G}_k, \bar{\gamma}))$ and $T_{n2} \stackrel{p}{\to} 0$, which together imply the desired conclusion.

For $T_{n1}$, recall once again from (A.21) or (A.38) that $\bar{h}^{k+1}(n) = \sum_{\ell=1}^k \bar{\alpha}_\ell^k h^\ell(n) + \bar{\tau}_{k+1} \tilde{Z}^{k+1}(n)$ for each $n$, where $\tilde{Z}^{k+1} \equiv \tilde{Z}^{k+1}(n) \sim N_n(0, I_n)$ is independent of $\mathcal{S}_k \equiv \mathcal{S}_k(n) = \sigma(\gamma, m^0, h^1, \dots, h^k)$. Define $\Phi: \mathbb{R}^{k+1} \to \mathbb{R}$ by $\Phi(x_1, \dots, x_k, y) := \mathbb{E}\{\phi(x_1, \dots, x_k, \sum_{\ell=1}^k \bar{\alpha}_\ell^k x_\ell + \bar{\tau}_{k+1} Z, y)\}$ with $Z \sim N(0, 1)$. Then $\Phi \in \text{PL}_{k+1}(1)$ by Lemma B.23(b), and as in (A.40) and (A.41), it follows from the inductive hypothesis $\mathcal{H}_k(d)$ and Lemma B.7 that

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n m_i^0 \mathbb{E}(\phi(h_i^1, \dots, h_i^k, \tilde{h}_i^{k+1}, \gamma_i) \mid \mathcal{S}_k) \\
&= \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n m_i^0 \Phi(h_i^1, \dots, h_i^k, \gamma_i) \\
&\stackrel{p}{\to} \mathbb{E}(F_0(\bar{\gamma}) \cdot \Phi(\bar{G}_1, \dots, \bar{G}_k, \bar{\gamma})) \\
&= \mathbb{E}(F_0(\bar{\gamma}) \cdot \phi(\bar{G}_1, \dots, \bar{G}_k, \bar{G}_{k+1}, \bar{\gamma})).
\end{aligned}
\tag{A.49}
$$

Next, we show as in (A.42) that

$$
\begin{aligned}
T'_{n1} &:= \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n m_i^0 \{ \phi(h_i^1, \dots, h_i^k, \tilde{h}_i^{k+1}, \gamma_i)
&\quad - \mathbb{E}(\phi(h_i^1, \dots, h_i^k, \tilde{h}_i^{k+1}, \gamma_i) \mid \mathcal{S}_k) \} \stackrel{p}{\to} 0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{A.50}
$$

To this end, for each $u \in \mathbb{R}$ and $v \equiv (x_1, \dots, x_k, y) \in \mathbb{R}^{k+1}$, define $\phi_{u,v}, \bar{\phi}_{u,v}: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ by $\phi_{u,v}(z) := u \phi(x_1, \dots, x_k, \sum_{\ell=1}^k \bar{\alpha}_\ell^k x_\ell + \bar{\tau}_{k+1} z, y)$ and $\bar{\phi}_{u,v}(z) := \phi_{u,v}(z) - \mathbb{E}(\phi_{u,v}(Z))$, where $Z \sim N(0, 1)$. Since $\phi \in \text{PL}_{k+2}(1)$, we deduce from Lemma B.23(a) that there exists $K' > 0$, depending only on the deterministic \(\bar{\alpha}^k \equiv (\bar{\alpha}_1^k, \dots, \bar{\alpha}_k^k)\), \(\breve{\tau}_{k+1}\) and \(r\), such that \(\bar{\phi}_{u,v} \in \mathrm{PL}_1(1, LK'|u|)\) for each \(u \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(v \in \mathbb{R}^{k+1}\). Now define the \(\mathcal{S}_k\)-measurable vectors \(v_k^{(i)} := (h_1^i, \dots, h_k^i, \gamma_i)\) for \(1 \le i \le n\), as in (A.45), and let \(\breve{E}_r\) be as in (A.34). Then by Lemma B.7 and (B.12) in Remark B.13, it follows as in (A.34), (A.44) and (A.45) that for each \(n > k\) and \(\varepsilon > 0\), we have
\[
\mathbb{P}(|T'_{n1}| > \varepsilon \mid \mathcal{S}_k) = \mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \bar{\phi}_{m_i^0, v_k^{(i)}}(\breve{Z}_i^{k+1})\right| \ge \varepsilon \bigg| \mathcal{S}_k\right)
\]
\[
\le \breve{E}_r(n, \varepsilon, LK'\|m^0\|_n). \tag{A.51}
\]

Now \(m^0 \equiv m^0(n)\) is measurable with respect to \(\mathcal{S}_0 \subseteq \mathcal{S}_k \equiv \mathcal{S}_k(n)\) for each \(n\), and \(\|m^0\|_n = O_c(1)\) by (A2), so we conclude as in (A.36) and (A.37) that \(\sum_n \mathbb{P}(|T'_{n1}| > \varepsilon) < \infty\) for all \(\varepsilon > 0\). Thus, \(T'_{n1} \xrightarrow{c} 0\) by Proposition 1.2, as claimed in (A.50).

Finally, we prove that \(T_{n2} \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}} 0\). Let \(L > 0\) be such that \(\phi \in \mathrm{PL}_{k+2}(1, L)\), and for \(n > k\) and \(1 \le i \le n\), define \(v_{k+1}^{(i)} = (h_1^i, \dots, h_k^i, h_i^{k+1, k}, \gamma_i)\) and \(\breve{v}_{k+1}^{(i)} = (h_1^i, \dots, h_k^i, \tilde{h}_i^{k+1}, \gamma_i) = (h_1^i, \dots, h_k^i, h_i^{k+1, k} - \Delta_i^{k+1}, \gamma_i)\) as in (A.47), where the final equality is obtained from (A.22). As in the proof of \(\mathcal{H}_1(d)\), we now apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the fact that \(\|\cdot\|_n \equiv \|\cdot\|_{n,2} \le \|\cdot\|_{n,r}\) to see that
\[
|T_{n2}| \le \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n |m_i^0| |\phi(v_{k+1}^{(i)}) - \phi(\breve{v}_{k+1}^{(i)})| \le \frac{L}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n |m_i^0| |\Delta_i^{k+1}|
\le L \|m^0\|_n \|\Delta^{k+1}\|_n.
\]

Since \(\|m^0\|_n \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}} \tau_1\) by (A2) and \(\|\Delta^{k+1}\|_n \le \|\Delta^{k+1}\|_{n,r} = o_c(1)\) by \(\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(a)\), we conclude that \(T_{n2} = o_c(1)\), as required. Together with (A.48), (A.49), (A.50), this yields \(\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(d)\), and hence completes the inductive step for Proposition A.16.

### Proof of Remark A.17

Under (A0), (A4) and (A5), if instead (A1)–(A3) hold with \(\xrightarrow{c}\) and \(O_c(1)\) replaced with \(\xrightarrow{p}\) and \(O_p(1)\) respectively, then as explained in the third bullet point in Remark A.1, we can make the same replacements in the proof of Proposition A.16 and most of the arguments go through as before. However, a few alterations are required in the proofs of (c, d) and (g), which we now describe.

First, in the proof of $\mathcal{H}_1(d)$, the goal in (A.33) is now to show that $T'_{n1} \xrightarrow{p} 0$ as $n \to \infty$. Instead of proceeding as in (A.36) and (A.37), we return to (A.35), where we note that if $\varepsilon > 0$ is fixed and (A2) takes the form $\|m^0\|_n \xrightarrow{p} \tau_1$, then $\mathbb{P}(|T'_{n1}| > \varepsilon | \mathcal{S}_0) \le \tilde{E}_r(n, \varepsilon, L\tau_1 \|m^0\|_n) = o_p(1)$ by Slutsky's lemma and the definition of $\tilde{E}_r$ in (A.34). Then for every $\varepsilon > 0$, it follows from the bounded convergence theorem that $\mathbb{P}(|T'_{n1}| > \varepsilon) = \mathbb{E}(\mathbb{P}(|T'_{n1}| > \varepsilon | \mathcal{S}_0)) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, so $T'_{n1} \xrightarrow{p} 0$, as desired.

In the proofs of $\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(c, d)$, the analogues of (A.42) and (A.50) can be derived from (A.45, A.46) and (A.51) respectively in much the same way; for the former, since $\|h^\ell\|_{n,r} = O_p(1)$ for $1 \le \ell \le k$ by the modified $\mathcal{H}_k(b)$, (A.46) implies that $\tilde{L}_k(n) \lesssim_{k,r} LK(1 + \sum_{\ell=1}^k \|h^\ell\|_{n,r}^k)^{1/r'} = O_p(1)$.

In addition, $\mathcal{H}_k(g)$ now reads $b_k = \langle f'_k(h^k, \gamma) \rangle_n \xrightarrow{p} \mathbb{E}(f'_k(\bar{G}_k, \bar{\gamma})) = \bar{b}_k$. To prove this, we can argue along subsequences, similarly to the proof of Corollary B.21(b).
