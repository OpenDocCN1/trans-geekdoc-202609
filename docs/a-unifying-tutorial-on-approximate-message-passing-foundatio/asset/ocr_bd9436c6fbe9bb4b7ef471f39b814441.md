Thus, for \( t \ge Lv_r \), we deduce from (B.7) and (B.8) that

```
\(\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{P}\{|f(Z) - \mathbb{E}(f(Z))| \ge t\} \le \mathbb{P}\{|f(Z)| \ge t - \mathbb{E}(|f(Z)|)\} \\
\le e^{-\frac{1}{2} \min\left\{(\frac{t}{2L} - \frac{v_r}{2})^2, \, (\frac{t}{2L} - \frac{v_r}{2})^{2/r}\right\}} \\
\le e^{\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2}(\frac{t}{2L} - \frac{v_r}{2})^{2/r}} \\
\le e^{\frac{1+r}{2} - \frac{1}{2}(\frac{t}{2L})^{2/r}} \\
\le e^{1 - (\frac{t}{2L})^{2/r} \frac{1}{r+1}},
\end{aligned}\)
```

where the third inequality follows from the fact that \( a^{2/r} \le |a - b|^{2/r} + b^{2/r} \) for \( r \ge 2 \) as above and any \( a, b \ge 0 \). Now (B.9) holds trivially for all \( t \in [0, Lv_r) \) since \( 1 - (r + 1)^{-1}\{t/(2L)\}^{2/r} > 1 - (r + 1)^{-1}(v_r/2)^{2/r} > 0 \) by (B.8), so (B.6) holds with \( C = 3 \) when \( n = 1 \).

We now derive (B.6) for general \( n \ge 2 \) with the aid of Theorem 3.1 and Proposition A.3 in Kuchibhotla and Chakrabortty (2018); see also Theorem 1 and Corollary 2 in Bakhshizadeh et al. (2020). As in Sections 2 and 3 of Kuchibhotla and Chakrabortty (2018), we begin by defining \( \vartheta_\beta: [0, \infty) \to [0, \infty) \) for each \( \beta > 0 \) by \( \vartheta_\beta(x) := \exp(x^\beta) - 1 \). Moreover, for \( \beta, \lambda > 0 \), let \( \vartheta_{\beta, \lambda}: [0, \infty) \to [0, \infty) \) be the continuous, strictly increasing function with inverse given by \( \vartheta_{\beta, \lambda}^{-1}(t) := \log^{1/2}(1 + t) + \lambda \log^{1/\beta}(1 + t) \) for \( t \ge 0 \). For a random variable \( X \) and a strictly increasing function \( g: [0, \infty) \to [0, \infty) \) satisfying \( g(0) = 0 \), we write \( \Xi_g(X) := \inf\{\theta > 0 : \mathbb{E}(g(|X|/\theta)) \le 1\} \in [0, \infty] \), setting \( \inf \emptyset = \infty \) by convention. Note that \( \Xi_g(X) \) is precisely the \( g \)-Orlicz norm of \( X \) when \( g \) is convex, but that \( \Xi_g \) does not in general define a norm when \( g \) is not convex (for example when \( g = \vartheta_\beta \) for \( \beta \in (0, 1) \), as in the proof below).
