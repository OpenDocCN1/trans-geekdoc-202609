
and \( t \ge 0 \), we have

\(\begin{aligned} \tilde{P}(n, t, v) &:= \mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \phi_{v_i, y_i}(\tilde{Z}_i^1)\right| \ge t\right) \le \exp\left\{1 - \left(\frac{nt}{CL\tau_1\|v\|_2}\right)^2\right\} \\ &\le \exp\left\{1 - \left(\frac{n^{1/2}t}{CL\tau_1\|v\|_n}\right)^2\right\} \\ &=: \tilde{E}_r(n, t, L\tau_1\|v\|_n), \end{aligned}\)

(A.34)

where \( C > 0 \) is a suitable universal constant. Recalling once again that \(\tilde{Z}^1\) is independent of \(\mathcal{S}_0 = \sigma(\gamma, m^0)\), we deduce using Lemma B.7 that \(m_i^0\{\phi(\tilde{h}_i^1, \gamma_i) - \mathbb{E}(\phi(\tilde{h}_i^1, \gamma_i) \mid \mathcal{S}_0)\} = \phi_{m_i^0, \gamma_i}(\tilde{Z}_i^1)\) for all \( 1 \le i \le n \). Thus, for each \( n \) and \( \varepsilon > 0 \), we have

\(\begin{aligned} \mathbb{P}(|T'_{n1}| > \varepsilon \mid \mathcal{S}_0) &= \mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \phi_{m_i^0, \gamma_i}(\tilde{Z}_i^1)\right| \ge \varepsilon \mid \mathcal{S}_0\right) = \tilde{P}(n, \varepsilon, m^0) \\ &\le \tilde{E}_r(n, \varepsilon, L\tau_1\|m^0\|_n). \end{aligned}\)

(A.35)

Since \(\|m^0\|_n \xrightarrow{c} \tau_1\) by (A2), Proposition 1.2 ensures that the events \(\tilde{A}_0(n) := \{\|m^0\|_n \le \tau_1 + 1\} \in \mathcal{S}_0(n)\) satisfy \(\sum_{n=1}^\infty \mathbb{P}(\tilde{A}_0(n)^c) < \infty\). Moreover, for each \( n \) and \( \varepsilon > 0 \), it follows from (A.34) that

\(\begin{aligned} \mathbb{P}(|T'_{n1}| > \varepsilon \cap \tilde{A}_0(n) \mid \mathcal{S}_0) &= \mathbb{P}(|T'_{n1}| > \varepsilon \mid \mathcal{S}_0)\mathbb{1}_{\tilde{A}_0(n)} \le \tilde{P}(n, \varepsilon, m^0)\mathbb{1}_{\tilde{A}_0(n)} \\ &\le \tilde{E}_r(n, \varepsilon, L\tau_1 C_0)\mathbb{1}_{\tilde{A}_0(n)} \le \tilde{E}_r(n, \varepsilon, L\tau_1 C_0), \end{aligned}\)

(A.36)

where we have used the fact that \(\tilde{A}_0(n) \in \mathcal{S}_0(n)\) to obtain the first equality above. Recalling the expression for \(\tilde{E}_r(n, \varepsilon, C_0)\) in (A.34), we see that \(\sum_{n=1}^\infty \tilde{E}_r(n, \varepsilon, L\tau_1 C_0) < \infty\). Thus, for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \), we conclude as in (A.29) that

\(\begin{aligned} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mathbb{P}(|T'_{n1}| > \varepsilon) &\le \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mathbb{P}(\{|T'_{n1}| > \varepsilon\} \cap \tilde{A}_0(n)) + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mathbb{P}(\tilde{A}_0(n)^c) \\ &\le \sum_{n=1}^\infty \tilde{E}_r(n, \varepsilon, L\tau_1 C_0) + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mathbb{P}(\tilde{A}_0(n)^c) < \infty, \end{aligned}\)

(A.37)

which implies (A.33) in view of Proposition 1.2, and hence that \( T_{n1} \xrightarrow{c} \tilde{\tau} \mathbb{E}(\phi(\tilde{G}_1))\) in (A.31).

As for \( T_{n2} \) in (A.31), let \( L > 0 \) be as above, so that \( \phi \in \text{PL}_2(1, L) \). For each \( n \), recalling from (A.23) that \( h^{1,0} = \tilde{h}^1 + \Delta^1 \), we now apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to see that

\( |T_{n2}| \le \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n |m_i^0| |\phi(h_i^{1,0}, \gamma_i) - \phi(h_i^{1,0} - \Delta_i^1, \gamma_i)| \ \ \le \frac{L}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n |m_i^0| |\Delta_i^1| \le L \|m^0\|_n \|\Delta^1\|_n. \)

Since \( \|m^0\|_n \xrightarrow{c} \tau_1 \) by (A2) and \( \|\Delta^1\|_n \le \|\Delta^1\|_{n,r} = o_c(1) \) by \( \mathcal{H}_1(a) \), we conclude that \( T_{n2} = O_c(1) o_c(1) = o_c(1) \). This completes the proof of \( \mathcal{H}_1(d) \).

Turning to the inductive step, we consider a general \( k \in \mathbb{N} \) and suppose that \( \mathcal{H}_k(b, c, d) \) have already been established. The assertions \( \mathcal{H}_k(e, \dots, j) \) and \( \mathcal{H}_{k+1}(a, b, c, d) \) will now be proved, in that order. Note that \( \text{PL}_{k+1}(2) \subseteq \text{PL}_{k+1}(r) \) since \( r \ge 2 \).

### $\mathcal{H}_k(e)$

In the case \( j = \ell = 1 \), we have \( |||m^0||_n - \tau_1| \xrightarrow{c} 0 \) by (A2), so \( \langle m^0, m^0 \rangle_n \xrightarrow{c} \tau_1^2 = \bar{T}_{1,1} = \mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_1^2) \) by (A.17). Now fix \( j, \ell \in \{2, \dots, k+1\} \). Then \( \tilde{\psi}_{j\ell}: (x_1, \dots, x_k, y) \mapsto f_{j-1}(x_{j-1}, y) f_{\ell-1}(x_{\ell-1}, y) \) lies in \( \text{PL}_{k+1}(2) \subseteq \text{PL}_{k+1}(r) \) by Lemma B.22 and the fact that \( f_{j-1}, f_{\ell-1} \) in the AMP recursion (2.1) are Lipschitz by assumption. Thus, by taking \( \psi = \tilde{\psi}_{j\ell} \) in \( \mathcal{H}_k(c) \), we see that

\(\langle m^{j-1}, m^{\ell-1} \rangle_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n f_{j-1}(h_i^{j-1}, \gamma_i) f_{\ell-1}(h_i^{\ell-1}, \gamma_i) \ \ \xrightarrow{c} \mathbb{E}(f_{j-1}(\bar{G}_{j-1}, \bar{\gamma}) \cdot f_{\ell-1}(\bar{G}_{\ell-1}, \bar{\gamma})) = \bar{T}_{j,\ell} = \mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_j \bar{G}_\ell),\)

where the final equalities are taken from (A.17). To handle the remaining case where \( \{j, \ell\} = \{1, k+1\} \), note that since \( f_k \) is Lipschitz, the map \( \phi_{k+1}: (x_1, \dots, x_k, y) \mapsto f_k(x_k, y) \) lies in \( \text{PL}_{k+1}(1) \). Thus, by taking \( \phi = \phi_{k+1} \) in \( \mathcal{H}_k(d) \), we deduce that

\(\langle m^0, m^k \rangle_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n m_i^0 f_k(h_i^k, \gamma_i) \xrightarrow{c} \mathbb{E}(F_0(\bar{\gamma}) \cdot f_k(\bar{G}_k, \bar{\gamma})) \ \ = \bar{T}_{1, k+1} = \mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_1 \bar{G}_{k+1}),\)

where the final equalities are again taken from (A.17).

### $\mathcal{H}_k(f)$

This proof is very similar to that of $\mathcal{H}_k(e)$. First fix \(1 \le j, \ell \le k\). By Lemma B.22, the function \((x_1, \dots, x_k, y) \mapsto x_j f_\ell(x_\ell, y)\) lies in \(\text{PL}_{k+1}(2) \subseteq \text{PL}_{k+1}(r)\), so by applying $\mathcal{H}_k(c)$ again, we deduce that

\(\langle h^j, m^\ell \rangle_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n h_i^j f_\ell(h_i^\ell, \gamma_i) \xrightarrow{\text{Law}} \mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_j f_\ell(\bar{G}_\ell, \bar{\gamma})) = \mathbb{E}(f'_\ell(\bar{G}_\ell, \bar{\gamma})) \mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_j \bar{G}_\ell) = \bar{b}_\ell \bar{T}_{j,\ell},\)

where the final equalities are taken from (A.19). For the second part of $\mathcal{H}_k(f)$, we fix \(1 \le j \le k\) and apply $\mathcal{H}_k(d)$ with the \(\text{PL}_{k+1}(1)\) function \((x_1, \dots, x_k, y) \mapsto x_j\) to see that

\(\langle h^j, m^0 \rangle_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n m_i^0 h_i^j \xrightarrow{\text{Law}} \mathbb{E}(F_0(\bar{\gamma}) \bar{G}_j) = 0\)

by the independence of \(\bar{G}_j \sim N(0, \tau_j^2)\) and \(\bar{\gamma} \sim \pi\).

### $\mathcal{H}_k(g)$

In view of Definition 1.1 of complete convergence, it suffices to show that if \((\beta_n)\) is any sequence of random variables with \(\beta_n \overset{d}{=} \langle f'_k(h^k, \gamma) \rangle_n\) for each \(n\), then \(\beta_n \xrightarrow{a.s.} \mathbb{E}(f'_k(\bar{G}_k, \bar{\gamma}))\). For any such sequence \((\beta_n)\), we first seek to construct a random sequence \(((\eta_n, \theta_n) \in \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^n : n \in \mathbb{N})\) such that \((\eta_n, \theta_n) \overset{d}{=} (h^k(n), \gamma(n))\) for each \(n\), and \(((f'_k(\eta_n, \theta_n))_n : n \in \mathbb{N}) = (\beta_n : n \in \mathbb{N})\) almost surely as random sequences. This can be done by applying Lemma B.10, where we take \(g_n : \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}\) to be the measurable function \((x, y) \mapsto \langle f'_k(x, y) \rangle_n = n^{-1} \sum_{i=1}^n f'_k(x_i, y_i)\) for each \(n\).

Since \((\eta_n, \theta_n) \overset{d}{=} (h^k(n), \gamma(n))\) for each \(n\) by construction, it follows from the inductive hypothesis $\mathcal{H}_k(c)$ that \(n^{-1} \sum_{i=1}^n \varphi(\eta_{ni}, \theta_{ni}) \overset{d}{=} n^{-1} \sum_{i=1}^n \varphi(h_i^k, \gamma_i) \xrightarrow{\text{Law}} \mathbb{E}(\varphi(\bar{G}_k, \bar{\gamma}))\) for every \(\varphi \in \text{PL}_2(r)\), where \((\bar{G}_k, \bar{\gamma}) \sim N(0, \tau_k^2) \otimes \pi =: \bar{\mu}^k\). Consequently, denoting by \(\hat{\mu}_n^k := \nu_n(\eta_n, \theta_n) = n^{-1} \sum_{i=1}^n \delta_{(\eta_{ni}, \theta_{ni})}\) the joint empirical distribution of the components of \(\eta_n\) and \(\theta_n\) for each \(n\), we deduce using Corollary B.21(a) that \(d_r(\hat{\mu}_n^k, \bar{\mu}^k) \xrightarrow{a.s.} 0\), and hence that \((\hat{\mu}_n^k)\) converges weakly to \(\bar{\mu}^k\) with probability 1. By (A5), \(f'_k\) is bounded, Borel measurable and continuous \(\bar{\mu}^k\)-almost everywhere, so we may now apply Lemma B.14 to conclude that \(\beta_n = \langle f'_k(\eta_n, \theta_n) \rangle_n = \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} f'_k \, d\hat{\mu}_n^k \to \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} f'_k \, d\bar{\mu}^k = \mathbb{E}(f'_k(\bar{G}_k, \bar{\gamma}))\) almost surely. This completes the proof of $\mathcal{H}_k(g)$.
