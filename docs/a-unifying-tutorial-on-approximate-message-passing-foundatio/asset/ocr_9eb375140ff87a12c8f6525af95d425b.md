Using (A.6), we now derive the conditional distribution of \(h^{k+1}\) given \(\mathcal{S}_k\) for \(k \in \{0, 1, \dots, n-1\}\). When \(k=0\), we have \(h^1 = W m^0\), so the associated identity in (A.5) follows directly from the first part of (A.5), Lemma A.14 and Lemma B.6(c). Turning now to (A.7) with \(k \ge 1\), we have \(h^{k+1} = W m^k - b_k m^{k-1}\), where \(b_k, m^{k-1}\) are \(\mathcal{S}_k\)-measurable, so we can deduce from (A.6) and Lemma B.6(c) that

\(\begin{aligned} h^{k+1} \stackrel{d}{=} |_{\mathcal{S}_k} & Y_k M_k^+ m^k + (Y_k M_k^+)^\top P_k^\perp m^k + P_k^\perp \breve{W}^k P_k^\perp m^k - b_k m^{k-1} \\ = & Y_k \alpha^k + (Y_k M_k^+)^\top \breve{m}^k + P_k^\perp (\breve{W}^k \breve{m}^k) - b_k m^{k-1} \\ = & H_k \alpha^k + (0 \, M_{k-1}) B_k \alpha^k + (H_k M_k^+)^\top \breve{m}^k + P_k^\perp (\breve{W}^k \breve{m}^k) - b_k m^{k-1}. \end{aligned}\) (A.12)

Since \(\breve{m}^k\) is \(\mathcal{S}_k\)-measurable and \(\breve{W}^k \sim \text{GOE}(n)\) is independent of \(\mathcal{S}_k\) (and therefore has conditional distribution GOE(n) given \(\mathcal{S}_k\)), it follows from Lemmas A.14 and B.6(b) that \(\breve{W}^k \breve{m}^k \stackrel{d}{=} |_{\mathcal{S}_k} \| \breve{m}^k \|_n \breve{Z}^{k+1} + \breve{\zeta}^{k+1} \breve{m}^k\).
