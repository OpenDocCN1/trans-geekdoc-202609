By similar (and slightly simpler) arguments to those in $\mathcal{H}_1(c)$, we will prove that \(T_{n1} \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}} \mathbb{E}(F_0(\bar{\gamma}) \cdot \phi(\bar{G}_1, \bar{\gamma}))\) and \(T_{n2} \xrightarrow{p} 0\) as \(n \to \infty\).

For \(T_{n1}\), define \(\Phi: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) by \(\Phi(y) := \mathbb{E}(\phi(\tau_1 Z, y))\) with \(Z \sim N(0, 1)\). For each \(n\), recalling once again that \(\tilde{Z}^1 \equiv \tilde{Z}^1(n) \sim N_n(0, I_n)\) is independent of \(\mathcal{S}_0 \equiv \mathcal{S}_0(n) = \sigma(\gamma, m^0)\), we deduce from Lemma B.7 that \(\mathbb{E}(\phi(\tilde{h}_i^1, \gamma_i) | \mathcal{S}_0) = \Phi(\gamma_i)\) almost surely, for every \(1 \le i \le n\). \(= \mathbb{E}(F_0(\bar{\gamma}) \cdot \mathbb{E}\{ \phi(\bar{G}_1, \bar{\gamma}) | \bar{\gamma} \})\)
\(= \mathbb{E}(F_0(\bar{\gamma}) \cdot \phi(\bar{G}_1, \bar{\gamma})).\)
