$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{mmse}(s_2^{-2}) &\le \text{mmse}(s_1^{-2})\\
&= \min_g \mathbb{E}\{(V - g(V + s_1 G))^2\}\\
&\le \mathbb{E}\{(V - g_2^*(V + s_1 G))^2\}\\
&= \mathbb{E}\{(V - g_2^*(V + s_2 G + G'))^2\}\\
&= \mathbb{E}\{(V - g_2^*(V + s_2 G))^2\} + \mathbb{E}\{(g_2^*(V + s_2 G) - g_2^*(V + s_2 G + G'))^2\}\\
&\le \text{mmse}(s_2^{-2}) + L_{s_2} \mathbb{E}(|G'|^2)\\
&\le \text{mmse}(s_2^{-2}) + C_{\pi} (s_1^2 s_2^2 \vee s_1^2)(s_2^{-2} - s_1^{-2}).
\end{aligned} \tag{A.63}
$$

To justify the equality in the third-last line, note that $g_2^*(V + s_2 G) = \mathbb{E}(V \mid V + s_2 G, G')$ by the independence of $G'$ and $(V, G)$, so for any measurable $\tilde{g}: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ with $\mathbb{E}(\tilde{g}(V + s_2 G, G')^2) < \infty$, we have $\mathbb{E}\{(V - g_2^*(V + s_2 G)) \tilde{g}(V + s_2 G, G')\} = 0$. We deduce from (A.63) that $\rho \mapsto \text{mmse}(\rho)$ is Lipschitz on $(\rho', \infty)$ for every $\rho' > 0$, and hence that it is continuous on $(0, \infty)$.

### Proof of Corollary 3.9.

Given any sequence of functions $(g_k)$ for which the corresponding AMP iterations (3.3) satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem 3.1 or 3.5, we prove (3.22) by induction on $k \in \mathbb{N}_0$. For each such $k$, it follows from (3.11) and (3.19) that as $n \to \infty$, we have

$$
\frac{|\langle \hat{v}^k, v \rangle_n|}{\|\hat{v}^k\|_n \|v\|_n} \xrightarrow{c} \frac{\sqrt{\rho_{k+1}}}{\lambda} = \frac{|\mathbb{E}(V g_k(\mu_k V + \sigma_k G_k))|}{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}(g_k(\mu_k V + \sigma_k G_k)^2)}}
\le \sqrt{\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}(V \mid \mu_k V + \sigma_k G_k)^2)} = \sqrt{1 - \text{mmse}_k(\rho_k)},
$$

where we set $\rho_0 \equiv \rho_0^* = (\mu_0/\sigma_0)^2$ and write $G_0$ for the random variable $U$ from (M1) when $k = 0$. Now $\sqrt{1 - \text{mmse}_0(\rho_0^*)} = \sqrt{\rho_1^*}/\lambda$ by (3.21), so $\rho_1 \le \rho_1^*$ and (3.22) holds when $k = 0$. For a general $k \in \mathbb{N}$, we have $\rho_k \le \rho_k^*$ by induction, so since $\rho \mapsto \text{mmse}(\rho) \equiv \text{mmse}_k(\rho)$ is non-increasing by Corollary A.19, we deduce that $\sqrt{1 - \text{mmse}(\rho_k)} \le \sqrt{1 - \text{mmse}(\rho_k^*)} = \sqrt{\rho_{k+1}^*}/\lambda$ and hence that $\rho_{k+1} \le \rho_{k+1}^*$. This completes the inductive step for (3.22). As for (3.23), we can apply (3.9), the definition of \( R_{\pi, \psi}(\rho) \), the fact that \( \rho_k \le \rho_k^* \) and Lemma 3.7 (in that order) to conclude that \( n^{-1} \sum_{i=1}^n \psi(\hat{v}_i^k, v_i) \xrightarrow{c} \mathbb{E}\{\psi(g_k(\mu_k V + \sigma_k G_k), V)\} \ge R_{\psi, \pi}(\rho_k) \ge R_{\psi, \pi}(\rho_k^*) \), as required.

### Proof of Theorem 3.10.

Under the conditions of Lemma 3.8, each \( g_k^*: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R} \) is Lipschitz and satisfies (M2), and by Corollary A.19, \( \rho \mapsto \lambda^2(1 - \text{mmse}(\rho)) =: m_\lambda(\rho) \) is non-decreasing on \( [0, \infty) \) and continuous on \( (0, \infty) \).

- (a) We will show that if either (i) or (ii) holds, then
  $$
  \rho_{\text{AMP}}^* \equiv \rho_{\text{AMP}}^*(\lambda) := \inf\{\rho > 0 : \rho = m_\lambda(\rho)\} > 0,\quad
  \rho_0^* \in [0, \rho_{\text{AMP}}^*],\quad
  \rho_1^* > 0,\quad
  m_\lambda(\rho) \ge \rho \text{ for all } \rho \in [0, \rho_{\text{AMP}}^*],\quad
  \rho_{k+1}^* = m_\lambda(\rho_k^*) \text{ for all } k \in \mathbb{N}_0. \tag{A.64}
  $$
  Note that since \( m_\lambda(\rho) \le \lambda^2 \) for all \( \rho \in [0, \infty) \), we always have \( \rho_{\text{AMP}}^*(\lambda) \le \lambda^2 \).

- (i) Non-spectral initialisation: In this case, (M1) holds with \( \mu_0 = 0, \sigma_0 = 1, \rho_0^* = 0 \) and \( U = 1 \). Since \( \mathbb{E}(V) \neq 0 \) and \( \mathbb{E}(V^2) = 1 \) by (M1), \( \rho_1^* = \lambda^2(1 - \text{mmse}(0)) = \lambda^2(1 - \text{Var}(V)) = \lambda^2 \mathbb{E}(V)^2 = m_\lambda(0) > 0 \), and therefore \( \rho_{k+1}^* = m_\lambda(\rho_k^*) \) for all \( k \in \mathbb{N}_0 \). In addition, \( m_\lambda(\rho) \ge m_\lambda(0) = \rho_1^* > \rho \) for all \( \rho \in [0, \rho_1^*) \), so \( \rho_{\text{AMP}}^* \ge \rho_1^* > 0 \) and \( m_\lambda(\rho) > \rho \) for all \( \rho \in [0, \rho_{\text{AMP}}^*) \).

- (ii) Spectral initialisation: By Proposition 3.4, (M1) holds with \( U \equiv G_0 \sim N(0, 1) \), so \( \rho_{k+1}^* = m_\lambda(\rho_k^*) \) for all \( k \in \mathbb{N}_0 \). Since \( \text{mmse}(\rho) \) is the minimum value of \( \mathbb{E}\{(V - g(\sqrt{\rho} V + G))^2\} \) as \( g \) ranges over all measurable functions,
  $$
  \begin{aligned}
  m_\lambda(\rho) &\ge \lambda^2(1 - \inf_{a,b \in \mathbb{R}} \mathbb{E}\{(V - a(\sqrt{\rho} V + G) - b)^2\})\\
  &= \lambda^2 \left( 1 - \frac{1 - \mathbb{E}(V)^2}{1 + \rho \{1 - \mathbb{E}(V)^2\}} \right) \ge \frac{\lambda^2 \rho}{1 + \rho}
  \end{aligned}
  $$
  for all \( \rho \in [0, \infty) \), where the equality above can be verified by a routine calculation. Recalling that \( \lambda > 1 \), we have
  $$
  m_\lambda(\rho) \ge \lambda^2 \rho / (1 + \rho) > \rho \quad \text{for all } \rho \in [0, \lambda^2 - 1),
  $$
  so \( \rho_{\text{AMP}}^* \ge \lambda^2 - 1 = \rho_0^* > 0 \) and \( m_\lambda(\rho) > \rho \) for all \( \rho \in (0, \rho_{\text{AMP}}^*) \). Moreover, \( \rho_1^* = m_\lambda(\rho_0^*) \ge \rho_0^* > 0 \).

  To complete the proof of (a), note that if \( 0 \le \rho_k^* \le \rho_{\text{AMP}}^* \) for some \( k \in \mathbb{N}_0 \), then by (A.64) and the fact that \( \rho \mapsto m_\lambda(\rho) \) is non-decreasing, we have \( 0 \le \rho_k^* \le m_\lambda(\rho_k^*) = \rho_{k+1}^* \le m_\lambda(\rho_{\text{AMP}}^*) = \rho_{\text{AMP}}^* \). Since \( \rho_1^* > 0 \), it follows by induction that \( (\rho_k^*) \) is an increasing sequence that converges to some \( \rho^* \in (0, \rho_{\text{AMP}}^*] \). By the continuity of \( m_\lambda \) on \( (0, \infty) \), we conclude that \( \rho^* = \lim_{k \to \infty} \rho_{k+1}^* = \lim_{k \to \infty} m_\lambda(\rho_k^*) = m_\lambda(\rho^*) \) and hence that \( \rho^* = \rho_{\text{AMP}}^* \).

- (b) Since \( g_{k, \psi}^* \) is Lipschitz by assumption, it follows from Theorem 3.1 that \( n^{-1} \sum_{i=1}^n \psi(\hat{v}_i^{\wedge k, \psi}, v_i) \xrightarrow{c} R_{\pi, \psi}(\rho_k^*) \); see the proof of Corollary 3.2. Moreover, \( \rho_k^* < \rho_{k+1}^* \) by (a) and \( R_{\pi, \psi} \) is non-increasing by Lemma 3.7, so \( R_{\pi, \psi}(\rho_k^*) \ge R_{\pi, \psi}(\rho_{k+1}^*) \).

- (c) Since each \( g_k^* \) is Lipschitz and \( \rho_k^* \nearrow \rho_{\text{AMP}}^* \) by (a), this is an immediate consequence of (3.10) and (3.11) from Corollary 3.2.\(\square\)

### Proof of Lemma 3.13.

This proof proceeds by induction on \( k \). We will first present the argument for the non-spectral initialisation in Theorem 3.10 and then outline the appropriate modifications for the spectral case.

- (i) Non-spectral initialisation: Defining the state evolution parameters \( \mu_k^*, \sigma_k^*, \bar{\Sigma}^{[k]} \) and limiting random variables as in the paragraph containing (3.33), we claim that

  $$
  \text{Cov}\left( \frac{\sigma_k^* G_k}{\mu_k^*}, \frac{\sigma_\ell^* G_\ell}{\mu_\ell^*} \right) = \frac{\bar{\Sigma}_{k, \ell}}{\mu_k^* \mu_\ell^*} = \frac{1}{\rho_k^*} \text{ and }
  $$
  $$
  V \perp\!\!\!\perp (\mu_{k-1}^* V + \bar{G}_{k-1}^*) \mid (\mu_k^* V + \sigma_k^* G_k)
  $$

  for all \( k \ge \ell \ge 1 \), where we use the notation of Section B.2 to denote conditional independence. For \( k = 1 \), we have \(\text{Var}(\sigma_1^* G_1 / \mu_1^*) = (\sigma_1^*/\mu_1^*)^2 = 1/\rho_1^* \). Moreover, \( \mu_{k-1}^* V + \bar{G}_{k-1}^* = \mu_0 V + \sigma_0 U = cU \) by condition (i) of Theorem 3.10, and \( U \perp\!\!\!\perp (V, \mu_1^* V + \sigma_1^* G_1) \) by definition in (3.31). Therefore, \(V \perp\!\!\!\perp cU \mid (\mu_1^* V + \sigma_1^* G_1)\), which verifies (A.65) when \(k=1\).

  For a general \(k \ge 2\) and \(1 \le \ell \le k\), it follows from (3.31) and (3.33) that

  $$
  \begin{aligned}
  \bar{\Sigma}_{k,\ell} &= \mathbb{E}(\boldsymbol{g}_{\ell-1}^*(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell-1}^* V + \bar{\boldsymbol{G}}_{\ell-1}^*) \cdot \boldsymbol{g}_{k-1}^*(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{k-1}^* V + \bar{\boldsymbol{G}}_{k-1}^*)) \\
  &= \mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}(V \mid \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell-1}^* V + \bar{\boldsymbol{G}}_{\ell-1}^*) \cdot \mathbb{E}(V \mid \boldsymbol{\mu}_{k-1}^* V + \bar{\boldsymbol{G}}_{k-1}^*)) \\
  &= \mathbb{E}(V \mathbb{E}(V \mid \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell-1}^* V + \bar{\boldsymbol{G}}_{\ell-1}^*)) = \mu_\ell^*/\lambda \\
  &= \mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}(V \mid \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell-1}^* V + \bar{\boldsymbol{G}}_{\ell-1}^*)^2) = (\sigma_\ell^*)^2,
  \end{aligned} \tag{A.66}
  $$

  where we have used the tower property of conditional expectation to obtain the last two equalities above. Thus, \(\rho_k^* = (\mu_k^*/\sigma_k^*)^2 = \lambda \mu_k^*\) and

  $$
  \text{Cov}\left( \frac{\sigma_k^* G_k}{\mu_k^*}, \frac{\sigma_\ell^* G_\ell}{\mu_\ell^*} \right) = \frac{\bar{\Sigma}_{k,\ell}}{\mu_k^* \mu_\ell^*} = \frac{1}{\lambda \mu_k^*} = \frac{1}{\rho_k^*}.\tag{A.67}
  $$

  Now for \(1 \le \ell \le k\), let \(Z_\ell := \sigma_\ell^* G_\ell / \mu_\ell^*\) and \(\zeta_\ell := Z_\ell - Z_k\), so that \(\text{Cov}(\zeta_\ell, Z_k) = 0\) by (A.67). Since \((\zeta_1, \dots, \zeta_{k-1}, Z_k)\) is a Gaussian random vector that is independent of \((U, V)\), it follows that \((\zeta_1, \dots, \zeta_{k-1}), Z_k, U\) and \(V\) are mutually independent, whence \(V \perp\!\!\!\perp (cU, \zeta_1, \dots, \zeta_{k-1}, V + Z_k) \mid (V + Z_k)\) by Lemma B.8. Writing \(\mu_\ell^* V + \sigma_\ell^* G_\ell = \mu_\ell^*(V + Z_\ell) = \mu_\ell^*((V + Z_k) + \zeta_\ell)\) for \(1 \le \ell \le k\), we deduce that

  $$
  V \perp\!\!\!\perp (cU, \mu_1^* V + \sigma_1^* G_1, \dots, \mu_{k-1}^* V + \sigma_{k-1}^* G_{k-1}) \mid (\mu_k^* V + \sigma_k^* G_k),
  $$

  which completes the inductive step for (A.65). An immediate consequence of this conditional independence is (3.34), which implies that \(\boldsymbol{g}_k^*\) depends only its last argument and \(b_{kj}^* = \langle \partial_j \boldsymbol{g}_k^*(v^0, \dots, v^k) \rangle_n = 0\) for \(1 \le j \le k-1\). Thus, with the denoising functions \(\boldsymbol{g}_k^*\) given by (3.34), we see by induction that the state evolution recursion (3.31) reduces to that in (3.20), and hence that (3.30) coincides with the Bayes-AMP iteration (\(v^{k,\text{B}} \equiv v^{k,\text{B}}(n) : k, n \in \mathbb{N}\)) in Section 3.3.

- (ii) *Spectral initialisation:* In this case, the initial state evolution parameters are \(\mu_0^* = \sqrt{1-\lambda^2}\) and \(\sigma_0^* = 1/\lambda\). We will show by
