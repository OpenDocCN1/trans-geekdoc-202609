As for \( T_{n2} \) in (A.31), let \( L > 0 \) be as above, so that \( \phi \in \text{PL}_2(1, L) \). For each \( n \), recalling from (A.23) that \( h^{1,0} = \tilde{h}^1 + \Delta^1 \), we now apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to see that

\( |T_{n2}| \le \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n |m_i^0| |\phi(h_i^{1,0}, \gamma_i) - \phi(h_i^{1,0} - \Delta_i^1, \gamma_i)| \ \ \le \frac{L}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n |m_i^0| |\Delta_i^1| \le L \|m^0\|_n \|\Delta^1\|_n. \)

Since \( \|m^0\|_n \xrightarrow{c} \tau_1 \) by (A2) and \( \|\Delta^1\|_n \le \|\Delta^1\|_{n,r} = o_c(1) \) by \( \mathcal{H}_1(a) \), we conclude that \( T_{n2} = O_c(1) o_c(1) = o_c(1) \). This completes the proof of \( \mathcal{H}_1(d) \).

Turning to the inductive step, we consider a general \( k \in \mathbb{N} \) and suppose that \( \mathcal{H}_k(b, c, d) \) have already been established. The assertions \( \mathcal{H}_k(e, \dots, j) \) and \( \mathcal{H}_{k+1}(a, b, c, d) \) will now be proved, in that order. Note that \( \text{PL}_{k+1}(2) \subseteq \text{PL}_{k+1}(r) \) since \( r \ge 2 \).

\( \mathcal{H}_k(e) \): In the case \( j = \ell = 1 \), we have \( |||m^0||_n - \tau_1| \xrightarrow{c} 0 \) by (A2), so \( \langle m^0, m^0 \rangle_n \xrightarrow{c} \tau_1^2 = \bar{T}_{1,1} = \mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_1^2) \) by (A.17). Now fix \( j, \ell \in \{2, \dots, k+1\} \). Then \( \tilde{\psi}_{j\ell}: (x_1, \dots, x_k, y) \mapsto f_{j-1}(x_{j-1}, y) f_{\ell-1}(x_{\ell-1}, y) \) lies in \( \text{PL}_{k+1}(2) \subseteq \text{PL}_{k+1}(r) \) by Lemma B.22 and the fact that \( f_{j-1}, f_{\ell-1} \) in the AMP recursion (2.1) are Lipschitz by assumption. Thus, by taking \( \psi = \tilde{\psi}_{j\ell} \) in \( \mathcal{H}_k(c) \), we see that

\( \langle m^{j-1}, m^{\ell-1} \rangle_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n f_{j-1}(h_i^{j-1}, \gamma_i) f_{\ell-1}(h_i^{\ell-1}, \gamma_i) \ \ \xrightarrow{c} \mathbb{E}(f_{j-1}(\bar{G}_{j-1}, \bar{\gamma}) \cdot f_{\ell-1}(\bar{G}_{\ell-1}, \bar{\gamma})) = \bar{T}_{j,\ell} = \mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_j \bar{G}_\ell), \)

where the final equalities are taken from (A.17). To handle the remaining case where \( \{j, \ell\} = \{1, k+1\} \), note that since \( f_k \) is Lipschitz, the map \( \phi_{k+1}: (x_1, \dots, x_k, y) \mapsto f_k(x_k, y) \) lies in \( \text{PL}_{k+1}(1) \). Thus, by taking \( \phi = \phi_{k+1} \) in \( \mathcal{H}_k(d) \), we deduce that

\( \langle m^0, m^k \rangle_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n m_i^0 f_k(h_i^k, \gamma_i) \xrightarrow{c} \mathbb{E}(F_0(\bar{\gamma}) \cdot f_k(\bar{G}_k, \bar{\gamma})) \ \ = \bar{T}_{1, k+1} = \mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_1 \bar{G}_{k+1}), \)

where the final equalities are again taken from (A.17).
