\( 1 - \frac{1}{\delta} = \mathbb{E} \left\{ \frac{1}{1 + \tilde{b}_* \zeta''(\text{prox}_{\tilde{b}_* \zeta}(Z_* + \tilde{b}_* Y_*))} \right\}, \quad \text{(4.70)} \)

where \( Z_* := \tilde{\mu}_* Z + \delta^{-1/2} \tilde{\sigma}_* \tilde{G}_* \) with \( Z \sim N(0, \mathbb{E}(\tilde{\beta}^2)/\delta) \) independent of \( \tilde{G}_* \sim N(0, 1) \). It turns out that there exists a unique solution \( (\tilde{\mu}_*, \tilde{\sigma}_*, \tilde{b}_* > 0) \) to (4.69)--(4.70) precisely when \( \mathbb{E}(\tilde{\beta}^2)/\delta \equiv \kappa^2 < s_{\text{MLE}}(1/\delta)^2 \) (Sur and Candès, 2019b, Lemma 7 and Remark 1), in which case \( \tilde{\beta}^{\text{MLE}} \) exists with probability tending to 1. By Proposition 4.4, \( \hat{\beta}^* \) in (4.68) is an MLE for \( \beta \) in the logistic regression model.

Proceeding as in **Step 2** in Section 4.4, we can use the fixed points in (4.68)--(4.70) to construct a stationary version of (4.66) based on \( f_*: w \mapsto \delta \tilde{b}_* w \) and \( g_*: (u, v) \mapsto v - \zeta'(\text{prox}_{\tilde{b}_* \zeta}(u + \tilde{b}_* v)) \). For each \( n \in \mathbb{N} \) and \( p \equiv p_n \), let \( \hat{\beta}^0 := \tilde{\mu}_* \beta + \bar{\sigma}_* \xi = f_*(\mu_* \beta + \sigma_* \xi) \in \mathbb{R}^p \) be an oracle initialiser with \( \xi \sim N_p(0, I_p) \) taken to be independent of the signal \( \beta \in \mathbb{R}^p \). Then setting \( \theta^0 = X \hat{\beta}^0 \), we inductively define

\(\hat{\beta}^{k+1} = \delta \tilde{b}_* X^{\top} \{ y - \zeta'(\text{prox}_{\tilde{b}_* \zeta}(\theta^k + \tilde{b}_* y)) \} + \hat{\beta}^k,\theta^{k+1} = X \hat{\beta}^k - \tilde{b}_* \{ y - \zeta'(\text{prox}_{\tilde{b}_* \zeta}(\theta^k + \tilde{b}_* y)) \} \quad \text{(4.71)}\)

for \( k \in \mathbb{N}_0 \). By the choice of \( \hat{\beta}^0 \) above, the associated state evolution recursion (4.67) is stationary, i.e., \( \tilde{\mu}_k = \tilde{\mu}_* \) and \( \tilde{\sigma}_k = \tilde{\sigma}_* \) for all \( k \in \mathbb{N}_0 \). Consequently, under the hypotheses of Theorem 4.2 with \( r = 2 \), it follows from Remark 4.3 that for each fixed \( k \in \mathbb{N} \), the joint empirical distribution of the entries of \( \hat{\beta}^k, \beta \in \mathbb{R}^p \) converges completely in \( d_2 \) to the distribution of \( (\tilde{\mu}_* \bar{\beta} + \bar{\sigma}_* G, \bar{\beta}) \) as \( n, p \to \infty \) with \( n/p \to \delta \), where \( \bar{\beta} \sim \pi_{\bar{\beta}} \) is independent of \( G \sim N(0, 1) \). On a technical note, we remark that the function

\(\tilde{g}_*: (z, u, v) \mapsto g_*(u, h(z, v)) = \mathbb{1}_{\{v \leq \zeta'(z)\}} - \zeta'\left( \text{prox}_{\tilde{b}_* \zeta}(u + \tilde{b}_* \mathbb{1}_{\{v \leq \zeta'(z)\}}) \right)\)
