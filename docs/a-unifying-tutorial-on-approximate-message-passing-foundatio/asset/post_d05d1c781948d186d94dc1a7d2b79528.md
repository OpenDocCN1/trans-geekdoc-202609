
$\mathcal{H}_k(h)$: For $1 \le \ell \le k$, it follows from $\mathcal{H}_k(e, f, g)$ that
$$
v_j^{k,\ell}/n = \langle h^j, m^\ell \rangle_n - b_\ell \langle m^{j-1}, m^{\ell-1} \rangle_n \xrightarrow{c} \bar{b}_\ell \bar{\mathbf{T}}_{j,\ell} - \bar{b}_\ell \bar{\mathbf{T}}_{j,\ell} = 0
$$
for all $1 \le j \le k$. For $\ell = 0$, we have $m^{-1} = 0$ by definition, so $v_j^{k,0}/n = \langle h^j, m^0 \rangle_n \xrightarrow{c} 0$ for all $1 \le j \le k$ by the second part of $\mathcal{H}_k(f)$.

$\mathcal{H}_k(i)$: Recall from (A.4) that $\alpha^k = (M_k^\top M_k/n)^+ (M_k^\top m^k/n) \in \mathbb{R}^k$. It follows from $\mathcal{H}_k(e)$ that $(M_k^\top M_k/n)_{j\ell} = \langle m^{j-1}, m^{\ell-1} \rangle_n \xrightarrow{c} \bar{\mathbf{T}}_{j,\ell}$ and $(M_k^\top m^k/n)_j = \langle m^{j-1}, m^k \rangle_n \xrightarrow{c} \bar{\mathbf{T}}_{j,k+1}$ for all $1 \le j, \ell \le k$. In the notation of Section A.4, this means that $M_k^\top m^k/n \xrightarrow{c} \bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k],k+1} \in \mathbb{R}^k$ and $M_k^\top M_k/n \xrightarrow{c} \bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k]} \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times k}$. Under (A4), Lemma 2.2 ensures that $\bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k]}$ is positive definite and hence invertible, we now apply the continuous mapping theorem for complete convergence (Lemma B.2) to deduce that
$$
\alpha^k = (M_k^\top M_k/n)^+ (M_k^\top m^k/n) \xrightarrow{c} (\bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k]})^{-1} \bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k],k+1} = \bar{\alpha}^k,
$$
as defined in (A.14).

$\mathcal{H}_k(j)$: Recalling (A.4) as well as the definitions at the start of Section A.2, we can write
$$
\|\hat{m}^k\|_n^2 = \|P_k^\perp m^k\|_n^2 = \|m^k\|_n^2 - \|P_k m^k\|_n^2
$$
$$
= \|m^k\|_n^2 - (\alpha^k)^\top (M_k^\top M_k/n) \alpha^k.
$$
Now $\|m^k\|_n^2 \xrightarrow{c} \bar{\mathbf{T}}_{k+1,k+1} = \tau_{k+1}^2$ and $M_k^\top M_k/n \xrightarrow{c} \bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k]} \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times k}$ by $\mathcal{H}_k(e)$, and $\alpha^k \xrightarrow{c} \bar{\alpha}^k \in \mathbb{R}^k$ by $\mathcal{H}_k(i)$, so
$$
\|\hat{m}^k\|_n^2 = \|m^k\|_n^2 - (\alpha^k)^\top (M_k^\top M_k/n) \alpha^k \xrightarrow{c} \bar{\mathbf{T}}_{k+1,k+1} - (\bar{\alpha}^k)^\top \bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k]} \bar{\alpha}^k = \tau_{k+1}^2,
$$
as defined in (A.15).

$\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(a)$: Denote by $R_{n1}, \dots, R_{n5}$ the individual summands (in the order in which they appear) in the definition (A.22) of $\Delta^{k+1} \equiv \Delta^{k+1}(n) \in \mathbb{R}^n$. To establish that $\|\Delta^{k+1}\|_{n,r} \xrightarrow{c} 0$, it suffices to show that $\|R_{ns}\|_{n,r} \xrightarrow{c} 0$ for $s = 1, \dots, 5$. Observe first that since $\alpha^k \xrightarrow{c} \bar{\alpha}^k \in \mathbb{R}^k$ by $\mathcal{H}_k(i)$ and $\|h^\ell\|_{n,r} = O_c(1)$ for all $1 \le \ell \le k$ by $\mathcal{H}_k(b)$, we have $\|R_{n1}\|_{n,r} \le \sum_{\ell=1}^k |\alpha_\ell^k - \bar{\alpha}_\ell^k| \|h^\ell\|_{n,r} = \sum_{\ell=1}^k o_c(1) O_c(1) = o_c(1)$. As for $R_{n2}$, we know from $\mathcal{H}_k(e)$ that $(M_k^\top M_k/n)_{j\ell} = \langle m^{j-1}, m^{\ell-1} \rangle_n \xrightarrow{c} \bar{\mathbf{T}}_{j,\ell}$ for all $1 \le j, \ell \le k$, so $M_k^\top M_k/n \xrightarrow{c} \bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k]} \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times k}$, which is positive definite by Lemma 2.2. We can now apply the continuous mapping theorem for complete convergence (Lemma B.2) to deduce that $(M_k^\top M_k/n)^+ \xrightarrow{\text{c}} (\bar{\mathbf{T}}^{[k]})^{-1}$; see $\mathcal{H}_k(i)$ above for a similar argument. By $\mathcal{H}_k(h)$, we have $v^{k,\ell}/n \xrightarrow{\text{c}} 0 \in \mathbb{R}^k$ for all $0 \le \ell \le k$, so
$$
\tilde{w}^k \equiv \tilde{w}^k(n) := (M_k^\top M_k)^+ \left( v^{k,k} - \sum_{\ell=1}^k \alpha_\ell^k v^{k,\ell-1} \right) \xrightarrow{\text{c}} 0,
$$
formally by Slutsky's lemma for complete convergence (Lemma B.2). Since $\|m^{\ell-1}\|_{n,r} = O_c(1)$ for $1 \le \ell \le k$ by $\mathcal{H}_k(b)$, we have $\|R_{n2}\|_{n,r} = \|M_k\tilde{w}^k\|_{n,r} \le \sum_{\ell=1}^k |\tilde{w}_\ell^k| \|m^{\ell-1}\|_{n,r} = \sum_{\ell=1}^k o_c(1) O_c(1) = o_c(1)$.

Turning to $R_{n3}$ and introducing $\xi_1, ..., \xi_k \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} N(0,1)$, we see from Lemma A.18 that $\|P_k \tilde{Z}^{k+1}\|_{n,r}$ is stochastically dominated by $\sum_{i=1}^k |\xi_i| / n^{1/r}$ for each $n > k$. By Example 1(a), $\sum_{i=1}^k |\xi_i| / n^{1/r} \le k \max_{1 \le i \le k} |\xi_i| / n^{1/r} = o_c(1)$, so $\|P_k \tilde{Z}^{k+1}\|_{n,r} \xrightarrow{\text{c}} 0$. Since $\|\dot{m}^k\|_n \xrightarrow{\text{c}} \dot{\tau}_{k+1} \in (0, \infty)$ by $\mathcal{H}_k(j)$, we deduce that $\|R_{n3}\|_{n,r} = \|\dot{m}^k\|_n \|P_k \tilde{Z}^{k+1}\|_{n,r} = o_c(1)$.

For the remaining summands $R_{n4}$ and $R_{n5}$, the arguments are similar to those in the proof of $\mathcal{H}_1(a)$. Recall that $(\tilde{Z}^{k+1}, \dot{\zeta}^{k+1}) \equiv (\dot{Z}^{k+1}(n), \dot{\zeta}^{k+1}(n)) \sim N_n(0, I_n) \otimes N(0, 1/n)$. Introducing $\zeta \sim N(0, 1)$, we have $|\dot{\zeta}^{k+1}| \stackrel{d}{=} n^{-1/2} |\zeta| \xrightarrow{\text{c}} 0$ by Example 1(a), and $\|\dot{Z}^{k+1}\|_{n,r} = (n^{-1} \sum_{i=1}^n |\dot{Z}_i^{k+1}|^r)^{1/r} \xrightarrow{\text{c}} \mathbb{E}(|\zeta|^r)^{1/r} \in (0, \infty)$ by Lemma B.12 and Proposition 1.2. By $\mathcal{H}_k(j)$, we have $\|\dot{m}^k\|_n - \dot{\tau}_{k+1} = o_c(1)$. Moreover, $\alpha^k = \bar{\alpha}^k + o_c(1) = O_c(1)$ by $\mathcal{H}_k(i)$ and $\|m^\ell\|_{n,r} = O_c(1)$ for $0 \le \ell \le k$ by $\mathcal{H}_k(b)$, so it follows from (A.4) that
$$
\| \dot{m}^k \|_{n,r} = \|(I - P_k) m^k\|_{n,r} \le \|m^k\|_{n,r} + \sum_{\ell=1}^k |\alpha_\ell^k| \|m^{\ell-1}\|_{n,r}
$$
$$
= O_c(1) + \sum_{\ell=1}^k O_c(1) O_c(1) = O_c(1).
$$

Putting everything together, we see that
$$
\| R_{n4} \|_{n,r} + \| R_{n5} \|_{n,r} = \|\dot{m}^k\|_n - \dot{\tau}_{k+1} \|\dot{Z}^{k+1}\|_{n,r} + |\dot{\zeta}^{k+1}| \|\dot{m}^k\|_{n,r}
$$
$$
= o_c(1) O_c(1) + o_c(1) O_c(1) = o_c(1).
$$

We have now shown that $\|R_{ns}\|_{n,r} \xrightarrow{\text{c}} 0$ for $s=1, ..., 5$, so $\|\Delta^{k+1}\|_{n,r} \le \sum_{s=1}^5 \|R_{ns}\|_{n,r} \xrightarrow{\text{c}} 0$.

$\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(b)$: By the inductive hypothesis $\mathcal{H}_k(b)$, we have $\|h^j\|_{n,r} = O_c(1)$ for all $1 \le j \le k$ and $\|m^j\|_{n,r} = O_c(1)$ for all $0 \le j \le k$. Now let $j = k+1$. For each integer $n > k$, recall from (A.7) in Proposition A.11 and (A.21) that
$$
h^{k+1}(n) \stackrel{d}{=}|_{\mathcal{S}_k} h^{k+1,k}(n) = \tilde{h}^{k+1}(n) + \Delta^{k+1}(n)
$$
$$
= \sum_{\ell=1}^k \bar{\alpha}_\ell^k h^\ell(n) + \bar{\tau}_{k+1} \tilde{Z}^{k+1}(n) + \Delta^{k+1}(n), \quad \text{(A.38)}
$$
where the deterministic $\bar{\alpha}^k \in \mathbb{R}^k$ is taken from (A.14) and $\tilde{Z}^{k+1} \equiv \tilde{Z}^{k+1}(n) \sim N_n(0, I_n)$ is independent of $\mathcal{S}_k \equiv \mathcal{S}_k(n) = \sigma(\gamma, m^0, h^1, \dots, h^k)$. Then $\|\Delta^{k+1}\|_{n,r} = o_c(1)$ by $\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(a)$ and $\|\tilde{Z}^{k+1}\|_{n,r} = O_c(1)$, as in the last part of the proof of $\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(a)$ above. It follows from this and $\mathcal{H}_k(b)$ that
$$
\|h^{k+1}\|_{n,r} \stackrel{d}{=} \|h^{k+1,k}\|_{n,r} \le \sum_{\ell=1}^k |\bar{\alpha}_\ell^k| \|h^\ell\|_{n,r} + \|\Delta^{k+1}\|_{n,r}
$$
$$
= O_c(1) + o_c(1) = O_c(1).
$$

In addition, letting $L' > 0$ be such that $f_{k+1}$ is $L'$-Lipschitz, we can argue as in the proof of $\mathcal{H}_1(b)$ to deduce that
$$
\|m^{k+1}\|_{n,r} = \|f_{k+1}(h^{k+1}, \gamma)\|_{n,r} \le |f_{k+1}(0,0)|
$$
$$
+ L'(\|h^{k+1}\|_{n,r} + \|\gamma\|_{n,r}) = O_c(1).
$$

$\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(c)$: We again make use of the distributional equality (A.38), which together with Lemma B.6(c) implies that $(\gamma, h^1, \dots, h^k, h^{k+1}) \stackrel{d}{=}|_{\mathcal{S}_k} (\gamma, h^1, \dots, h^k, h^{k+1,k})$ for each integer $n > k$. Thus, for any fixed $\psi \in \text{PL}_{k+2}(r)$, it follows that $n^{-1} \sum_{i=1}^n \psi(h_i^1, \dots, h_i^k, h_i^{k+1}, \gamma_i) \stackrel{d}{=} n^{-1} \cdot \sum_{i=1}^n \psi(h_i^1, \dots, h_i^k, h_i^{k+1,k}, \gamma_i) =: T_n$ for each such $n$, so it suffices to show that $T_n \rightarrow^\mathcal{C} \mathbb{E}(\psi(\bar{G}_1, \dots, \bar{G}_k, \bar{G}_{k+1}, \bar{\gamma}))$. We decompose $T_n$ as
$$
\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \psi(h_i^1, \dots, h_i^k, \tilde{h}_i^{k+1}, \gamma_i) + \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \{ \psi(h_i^1, \dots, h_i^k, h_i^{k+1,k}, \gamma_i)
$$
$$
- \psi(h_i^1, \dots, h_i^k, \tilde{h}_i^{k+1}, \gamma_i) \} =: T_{n1} + T_{n2} \quad \text{(A.39)}
$$
for each $n > k$, and seek to establish that $T_{n1} \rightarrow^\mathcal{C} \mathbb{E}(\psi(\bar{G}_1, \dots, \bar{G}_k, \bar{G}_{k+1}, \bar{\gamma}))$ and $T_{n2} \rightarrow^\mathcal{C} 0$ by imitating and extending the analogous arguments in the proof of $\mathcal{H}_1(c)$.
