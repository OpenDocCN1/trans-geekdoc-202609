
Contrasting the definitions of $h^{k+1,k}$ and $\bar{h}^{k+1}$ in (A.7) and (A.21) respectively for $k \in \{0, 1, \dots, n-1\}$, we see that the random quantities $\alpha^k$ and $\|m^k\|_n$ in (A.7) are replaced in (A.21) with the deterministic $\bar{\alpha}^k \in \mathbb{R}^k$ and $\bar{\tau}_{k+1} \in (0, \infty)$ from (A.14) and (A.15) respectively; these turn out to be the correct limiting values in Proposition A.16(i,j) below under the non-degeneracy assumption (A4).

We are now in a position to state the main result of this subsection. To ease notation, we will often suppress the dependence on $n$ of quantities such as $h^k \equiv h^k(n)$, $v^{k,\ell} \equiv v^{k,\ell}(n)$, $\alpha^k \equiv \alpha^k(n)$ and $\Delta^k \equiv \Delta^k(n)$.

## Proposition A.16.

For a sequence of symmetric AMP recursions (2.1) satisfying (A0)–(A5) as well as (A4), the following hold as $n \rightarrow \infty$ for each $k \in \mathbb{N}$:

- (a) $\|\Delta^k\|_{n,r} \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}} 0$;
- (b) $\|h^j\|_{n,r} = O_c(1)$ for $1 \le j \le k$; $\|m^j\|_{n,r} = O_c(1)$ for $0 \le j \le k$;
- (c) $n^{-1} \sum_{i=1}^n \psi(h_i^1, \dots, h_i^k, \gamma_i) \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}} \mathbb{E}(\psi(\bar{G}_1, \dots, \bar{G}_k, \bar{\gamma}))$ for every $\psi \in \text{PL}_{k+1}(r)$;
- (d) $n^{-1} \sum_{i=1}^n m_i^0 \phi(h_i^1, \dots, h_i^k, \gamma_i) \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}} \mathbb{E}(F_0(\bar{\gamma}) \cdot \phi(\bar{G}_1, \dots, \bar{G}_k, \bar{\gamma}))$ for every $\phi \in \text{PL}_{k+1}(1)$;
- (e) $\langle m^{j-1}, m^{\ell-1} \rangle_n \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}} \mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_j \bar{G}_\ell) = \bar{\mathbf{T}}_{j,\ell}$ for $1 \le j, \ell \le k+1$;
- (f) $\langle h^j, m^\ell \rangle_n = \langle h^j, f_\ell(h^\ell, \gamma) \rangle_n \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}} \mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_j f_\ell(\bar{G}_\ell, \bar{\gamma})) = \mathbb{E}(f'_\ell(\bar{G}_\ell, \bar{\gamma})) \mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_j \bar{G}_\ell) = \bar{b}_\ell \bar{\mathbf{T}}_{j,\ell}$ for $1 \le j, \ell \le k$; $\langle h^j, m^0 \rangle_n \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}} 0$ for $1 \le j \le k$;
- (g) $b_k = \langle f'_k(h^k, \gamma) \rangle_n \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}} \mathbb{E}(f'_k(\bar{G}_k, \bar{\gamma})) = \bar{b}_k$;
- (h) $v^{k,\ell}/n = (H_k^\top m^\ell - b_\ell M_k^\top m^{\ell-1})/n \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}} 0$ for $0 \le \ell \le k$;
- (i) $\alpha^k \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}} \bar{\alpha}^k$;
- (j) $\|\bar{m}^k\|_n \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}} \bar{\tau}_{k+1} = \text{Var}^{1/2}(\bar{G}_{k+1} \mid \bar{G}_1, \dots, \bar{G}_k)$.

### Remark A.17.

Under (A4) and the alternative hypotheses of Remark A.1(a), the assertions (a)–(j) above remain valid if we replace \(\xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}}\) with \(\rightarrow^p\) and \(O_c(1)\) with \(O_p(1)\) throughout.

To establish Proposition A.16, we proceed by induction on \(k \in \mathbb{N}\) and prove the assertions (a)–(i) one at a time (in that order). Here, we will give a technical summary of the inductive argument (which can be read alongside the detailed proof in Section A.5) to highlight its overall structure and key features. Henceforth, we write \(\mathcal{H}_k(\cdots)\) for parts \((\cdots)\) of the inductive hypothesis for \(k \in \mathbb{N}\).

\(\mathcal{H}_k(e, f)\): These are obtained as direct consequences of the inductive hypotheses \(\mathcal{H}_k(c, d)\) by choosing suitable pseudo-Lipschitz functions \(\psi \in \text{PL}_{k+1}(2) \subseteq \text{PL}_{k+1}(r)\) that depend on at most three of their \(k + 1\) arguments. We use \(\mathcal{H}_k(d)\) to handle the inner products that feature \(m^0\) and apply \(\mathcal{H}_k(c)\) to those that do not. In \(\mathcal{H}_k(e)\), the limiting value of \(\langle m^{j-1}, m^{\ell-1} \rangle_n = \langle f_{j-1}(h^{j-1}, \gamma), f_{\ell-1}(h^{\ell-1}, \gamma) \rangle_n\) is shown to be

```
\begin{cases}
\mathbb{E}(f_{j-1}(\bar{G}_{j-1}, \bar{\gamma}) \cdot f_{\ell-1}(\bar{G}_{\ell-1}, \bar{\gamma})) = \mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_j \bar{G}_\ell) \qquad \text{for } 2 \le j, \ell \le k + 1 \\
\mathbb{E}(F_0(\bar{\gamma}) \cdot f_{j-1}(\bar{G}_{j-1}, \bar{\gamma})) = \mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_1 \bar{G}_\ell) \qquad \text{for } 1 = j < \ell \le k + 1,
\end{cases}
```

where the two equalities are drawn from (A.17) and form the basis of the definition of the limiting covariances \(\bar{\text{T}}_{j, \ell}\) in (2.6). Moreover, for \(1 \le j, \ell \le k\), the identity \(\mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_j f_\ell(\bar{G}_\ell, \bar{\gamma})) = \mathbb{E}(f'_\ell(\bar{G}_\ell, \bar{\gamma})) \mathbb{E}(\bar{G}_j \bar{G}_\ell)\) in the first line of \(\mathcal{H}_k(f)\) comes from (A.19). These identities (A.17) and (A.19) ultimately provide the crucial link between the limiting values of \(\langle h^j, m^\ell \rangle_n\) and \(b_\ell \langle m^{j-1}, m^{\ell-1} \rangle_n\) in \(\mathcal{H}_k(h)\).

\(\mathcal{H}_k(g, h)\): This is also derived from \(\mathcal{H}_k(c)\), but since \(f'_k: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}\) need not lie in \(\text{PL}_2(r)\), we instead apply the analytic Lemmas B.10 and B.14 rather than imitate the proofs of \(\mathcal{H}_k(e, f)\). See the proof of Corollary B.21(b) for a similar argument. \(\mathcal{H}_k(h)\) follows immediately from \(\mathcal{H}_k(e, f, g)\).

\(\mathcal{H}_k(i, j)\): We see from \(\mathcal{H}_k(e)\) that the matrices \(M_k^\top M_k / n \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times k}\) converge completely to the limiting covariance matrix \(\bar{\text{T}}^{[k]} = \text{Cov}(\bar{G}_1, \dots, \bar{G}_k) \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times k}\), which is positive definite under (A4). In \(\mathcal{H}_k(i)\), we consider \(\alpha^k = (M_k^\top M_k / n)^+ (M_k^\top m^k / n) \in \mathbb{R}^k\), a vector of projection coefficients defined in (A.4). It follows from \(\mathcal{H}_k(e)\) that \((M_k^\top M_k / n)^+\) and \(M_k^\top m^k / n\) converge completely to \((\bar{\text{T}}^{[k]})^{-1}\) and \(\bar{\text{T}}^{[k], k+1}\) respectively, and hence that \(\alpha^k \xrightarrow{S} (\bar{\mathrm{T}}^{[k]})^{-1} \bar{\mathrm{T}}^{[k],k+1} = \bar{\alpha}^k\), as defined in (A.14). For \(\mathcal{H}_k(j)\), we recall the definitions at the start of Section A.2 and write

\(\|\bar{m}^k\|_n^2 = \|P_k^\perp m^k\|_n^2 = \|m^k\|_n^2 - \|P_k m^k\|_n^2\)
\(= \|m^k\|_n^2 - (\alpha^k)^\top (M_k^\top M_k/n) \alpha^k.\)

Applying \(\mathcal{H}_k(e, i)\) to the individual terms on the right hand side above, we deduce that \(\|\bar{m}^k\|_n^2 \xrightarrow{S} \bar{\mathrm{T}}_{k+1,k+1} - (\bar{\alpha}^k)^\top \bar{\mathrm{T}}^{[k]} \bar{\alpha}^k = \tilde{\tau}_{k+1}^2\), as defined in (A.15).

\(\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(a)\): It is thanks to the key fact \(\mathcal{H}_k(h)\) and the presence of the Onsager term \(-b_k m^{k-1}\) in the original AMP recursion (2.1) (and subsequently in (A.7) in Proposition A.11) that the \(\|\cdot\|_{n,r}\) norm of the second term in (A.22) converges completely to 0. Using \(\mathcal{H}_k(b, i, j)\) to handle some of the remaining terms in this definition (A.22) of the deviation term \(\Delta^{k+1}\), we conclude that \(\|\Delta^{k+1}\|_{n,r} \xrightarrow{S} 0\).

\(\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(b)\): Using the distributional equality \(h^{k+1} \stackrel{d}{=} h^{k+1,k} = \tilde{h}^{k+1} + \Delta^{k+1} = \sum_{\ell=1}^k \bar{\alpha}_\ell^k h^\ell + \tilde{\tau}_{k+1} \tilde{Z}^{k+1} + \Delta^{k+1}\) from Proposition A.11 and (A.21, A.22), we deduce from \(\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(a)\) and the inductive hypothesis \(\mathcal{H}_k(b)\) that \(\|h^{k+1}\|_{n,r} = O_c(1)\). Since \(\|\gamma\|_{n,r} = O_c(1)\) by (A1) and \(f_{k+1}\) is Lipschitz, this in turn implies that \(\|m^{k+1}\|_{n,r} = \|f_k(h^{k+1}, \gamma)\|_{n,r} = O_c(1)\).

\(\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(c)\): This is the main assertion in Proposition A.16; by Corollary B.21(b), it is in fact equivalent to the conclusion (2.8) of Theorem 2.3. We first condition on \(\mathcal{S}_k = \sigma(\gamma, m^0, h^j : 1 \le j \le k)\) and appeal to Proposition A.11, which asserts that for each \(n > k\), the conditional distribution of \(h^{k+1} \equiv h^{k+1}(n)\) given \(\mathcal{S}_k\) is identical to that of \(h^{k+1,k} \equiv h^{k+1,k}(n)\) from (A.7). With \(h^{k+1,k} = \tilde{h}^{k+1} + \Delta^{k+1}\) in place of \(h^{k+1}\) on the left hand side of \(\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(c)\), we use \(\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(a, b)\) to show that the ‘deviation’ term \(\Delta^{k+1} \equiv \Delta^{k+1}(n)\) from (A.22) has asymptotically negligible effect, so that \(h^{k+1,k}\) can in fact be replaced with \(\tilde{h}^{k+1}\) in all relevant expressions. In (A.21), \(\tilde{h}^{k+1}\) was defined as \(\sum_{\ell=1}^k \bar{\alpha}_\ell^k h^\ell + \tilde{\tau}_{k+1} \tilde{Z}^{k+1}\), where \(\sum_{\ell=1}^k \bar{\alpha}_\ell^k h^\ell\) is a deterministic linear combination of the previous iterates \(h^1, \dots, h^k\), and \(\tilde{\tau}_{k+1} \tilde{Z}^{k+1}\) is a new Gaussian variable that has i.i.d. components and is independent of \(\mathcal{S}_k\).
