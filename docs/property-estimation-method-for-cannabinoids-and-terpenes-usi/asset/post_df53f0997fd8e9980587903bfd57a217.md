```
$R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum(y_i-\hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum(y_i-\bar{y})^2}$
```

where $y$ is the average of the true parameters and the summations are performed over the entire test set. $R^2$ ranges from 0 to 1, where 1 represents perfect inference. $R^2$ will be used in this work as metric for measuring the performance of the NN Models. Finally, a downside of the standard NNs is that they are sensitive to the training process and hyper-parameter choices, finding different set of weights each time they are trained, which can produce different predictions and high variance. An approach approach to reduce variance is to train multiple models instead of a single one and combine the predictions from these models. This is called ensemble learning and it helps to reduce the variance of predictions and can also result in better performance Tao (2019).

## 3. Analysis

In order to predict the melting point in terms of the established chemical groups, we started with single NN regression models. First, we built in TensorFlow a FNN consists of some layers with 256, 128, 64 and 1 neurons, followed by an ELU activation function, a Batch Normalization to optimize and accelerate the convergence, and a Dropout layer (with a rate of 0.1), for regularization purposes. Here, we adopt Adam as the optimizer, and a layer decay with 0.01 in its initial value, with a factor of 0.08 during each 10 epochs. On the other hand, the Conv1D architecture comprises of four convolutional layers of 64, 32, 32, 16, then a MaxPooling layer is used to reduce the features of the feature map, followed by four layers of 256, 126, 64 and 1 neurons. Before, each layer is followed by an ELU activation function, Batch Normalization and Dropout (rate 0.1).

## 4. Discussion and Conclusions

In this paper, we have explored several machine learning techniques for estimating melting point. We contrasted two approaches: one of trained single NN, with ensembles of Convolutional layers. We found that ensembles outperform the single NN, specifically FNN trained with MSE, reaching R=0.88. Even if the result is good, we observed that the variance is very high. To overcome this, for future work, we suggest more robust techniques for feeding data into the model.
