The comparison results demonstrate that the multivariate imputation, specifically the IterativeImputer, yields lower MAE values than the simple imputation, even after one iteration. IterativeImputer is more effective at replacing missing values with fewer errors. To obtain better estimates, you can increase the max_iter to a higher number and leave the algorithm to decide if to stop based on the tolerance used to check if the results are stable. Increasing the max_iter will lead to longer imputation times because, as an imputation method, we use a random forests algorithm. Random forests are the most effective way to handle multivariate imputations (a method known in the R community as MissForest: https://rpubs.com/lmorgan95/MissForest). However, you can choose faster methods based on linear models or k-nearest neighbors by replacing the estimator in the IterativeImputer:

+   - BayesianRidge—Regularized linear regression using BayesianRidge(regression)
- RandomForestRegressor—For forests of random trees to create shallower trees with set n_estimators, max_depth, and max_features to accelerate the imputation process as RandomForestRegressor(n_estimators=30, max_depth=6, max_samples=0.5)
- Nystroem+Ridge—A pipeline with the expansion of a degree 2 polynomial kernel and regularized linear regression by combining different Scikit-learn commands: make_pipeline(Nystroem(kernel="polynomial", degree=2, random_state=0), Ridge(alpha=3))
- KNeighbors imputation approach where you decide the number of neighbors to consider, such as KNeighborsRegressor(n_neighbors=5)

The estimator you use will affect the quality of the results you obtain and the computation time. As a start, BayesianRidge is the default choice and also the fastest.
