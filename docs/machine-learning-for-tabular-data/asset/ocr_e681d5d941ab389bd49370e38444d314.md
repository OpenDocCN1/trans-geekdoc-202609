# 6.3.5 Manually setting hyperparameters

Despite the efficiency of the previously described optimization strategies, you may not be surprised to know that many practitioners tune the settings of their models by intuition and trial and error. Such a procedure seems well-grounded during the experimentation phase when you try to make everything work reasonably as you look for ways to improve your solution in various iterations. A thorough optimization is, therefore, left after the processing and experimentation iterations have been completed.

The book provides a comprehensive guide to the key parameters of the machine learning algorithms covered this far. We begin with linear models, such as linear or logistic regressions, which can be effectively tuned using a grid search due to their limited number of parameters and discretization. A table covers forests and randomized trees, as they share similar hyperparameters, being based on the same boosted ensemble approach.

Regarding GBDTs, we have different sets of hyperparameters depending on the specific implementation. For your convenience, we have selected the most essential ones. Feel free to use them with the proposed ranges for manual or automatic optimization. The guide starts with HistGradientBoosting and then covers XGBoost and LightGBM.
