# Prints the resulting Cramer's V

Returns the score, the p-value, degrees of freedom, and the expected table based on marginal correlation

In the code, by the chi-square score and the table itself, we call the Cramer-V function to get a 0.855 value for Cramer's V comparing MPG and the year of model. Cramer's V is a reciprocal measure to identify features with a similar role in prediction. Unidirectional measures, Cramer's V is sensitive to feature swapping. This property allows for identifying highly correlated features that can be removed from the analysis. A very high positive or negative correlation between features may indicate redundancy, and one of the two features can be dropped.

PCA and SVD have their roots in statistical analysis, and PCA has a long history of being applied for EDA purposes. Both, however, approaches based on linear combinations of features (your reduced summary are a weighted summation of your data) and, thus, are sometimes suboptimal to catch nonlinear patterns that are frequently found in real-world data. The most recent t-SNE and UMAP methods that can reduce the dimensionality of data exceptionally well, allowing you to chart a reliable representation of your data where original characteristics are maintained. There are some caveats, though, tweaking the hyper-parameters of both methods may lead to entirely different projections from the same data. This is due to the loss of information in the data when passing from multiple features to a few ones that result in different plots—some more, some less representative of the data itself. In addition, the resulting plots may sometimes be easily interpretable, especially if there are many data points or if the data is highly clustered. t-SNE and UMAP are valuable tools for data exploration and visualization, as they can identify patterns and clusters within complex datasets that may not readily visible through other methods. Of course, a core principle of EDA does not uniquely rely on them but compares their outputs with other multivariate, bivariate, and univariate methods.

Before starting with these methods, read articles as "How to t-SNE Effective" (https://distill.pub/2016/misread-tsne/) or "Understanding UMAP" (https://mng.bz/AoXQK), which will provide you with adequate confidence when using the methods approaches. Another caveat is that both are computationally intensive, and may take quite a long time to get a reduction from a large and complex dataset. Recently, NVIDIA developed its RAPIDS based on CUDA and GPU (https://developer.nvidia.com/rapids), which can dramatically cut the time necessary before getting results from UMAP and t-SNE, making them even more effective for intensive explorations.
